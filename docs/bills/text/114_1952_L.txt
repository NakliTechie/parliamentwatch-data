When the UehabiIitation Finance Administration (Amendment) Bill, 
1950 came up for consideration, suggestions were made in the House that 
'he existing provision of Rs. 8 crores under clause (b) and (0) of section 12 
for granting financial assistance through the medium of normal banking 
ohannels whioh had not been availed of by the Soheduled Banks should 
be transferred for the grant of direct loans by the Administration. The 
applications for loans still pending with the Administration further suggest 
that even if the total amount for direct loans by the Administration is 
thus raised to Rs. 10 crores, it will not be adequate for the purpose. The 
ameading bill, therefore, seeks to increase this amount to Re. 12·5 orares. 
It is also considered that though the re-discounting facilities under olause 
(b) of seotion 12 may be deleted as they are not likely to be availed of, 
some Scheduled Banks may now come forward to grant lo~s if full loss 
is guaranteed by the Administration under clause (0) of section 12. This 
olause is being amended accordingly and.provision is being made ~ permit 
of any portion of the Hs. 2 crores provided under the clause being transfer-
red for the purpose of the grant of direot loans by the Administration. It 
has also been found that olause (0.) of section 12 as at present worded 
does not permit the Administration to utilise moneys recovered from loans 
for the grant of fresh loans after the maximum limit presoribed therein 
has been reached. That clause has been amended to remove this anomaly. 
The Bill also seeks to achieve the following objects:-, 
(i) to diminish the hardship of loanees by extending the period 
of repayment of loans from 10 to 15 years; 
(ii) to remove a lacuna in the Aot regarding the powers of the 
Administrat·ion to meet the running cost out of the funds at its 
disposal; and
