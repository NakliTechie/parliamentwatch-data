(a) to citizens of India outside India;
(b) to persons in the service of the Government wherever they may be; and
(c) to persons on ships and aircrafts registered in India wherever they may be.

7.  Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force and
subject to such general or specific orders, as the Central Government may make, the Bureau
shall prevent, investigate and prosecute the offences specified in—
(i) Schedule I and also such other offences relating to matters specified in
List I—Union List of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution, as may be notified in
this behalf by the Central Government in the Official Gazette, from time to time; and
(ii) Schedule II and also such other offences relating to matters specified in
List III—Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution, as may be
notified in this behalf by the Central Government in the Official Gazette, in respect
of Union territories, from time to time.
8. (1) Save as hereinafter provided in this section, the superintendence of the Bureau
shall vest in the Central Government.
(2) The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and
Justice shall exercise oversight and  review the functioning of the Bureau once in every three
months.
(3) The Standing Committee shall present a quarterly report to Parliament with regard
to its observations and findings about the functioning of the Bureau.
(4) The Parliamentary Committee referred to in sub-section (2) shall not ordinarily go
into any matter connected with the investigation or inquiry of an offence at the inquiry stage
in order to provide complete independence and autonomy to the investigation officer or the
investigating team, as the case may be.
(5) The superintendence of Central Vigilance Commission shall be exercised only with
regard to investigation of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.
9. (1) There shall be an independent Directorate of Prosecution to be headed by a
Director of Prosecution and not below the rank of a Secretary to the Government of India.
(2) The Directorate shall employ as many lawyer as are required and necessary to
handle the work emanating out of the investigating arm of the Bureau.
(3) The lawyers employed under Sub-Section (2) shall be paid salaries/fees comparable
with the prevailing financial package structure in the private/corporate sector.
(4) The Directorate shall independently evaluate and determine the prosecutibility of
the offences investigation by the Bureau and prosecute those offences, that can, in their
opinion, stand the test of law.
(5) The Directorate of Prosecution shall throught its Director, report tot the Director of
the Central Bureau of Investigation who shall exercise administrative control on the Directorate
of prosecution only through the Director of Prosecution.
10. (1) The powers and jurisdiction of the officials of the Bureau shall extend to the
whole of India including States, Union territories and Railway areas for the prevention,
investigation and prosecution of offences specified in section 7.
(2) The prior consent of the State Government shall not be necessary for the
investigation and prosection of offences specified in section 7.
(3) If a State Government wants to preclude the Bureau from investigating and prosecuting
any offence in the territory of that State specified in List II-State List of the Seventh Schedule
to the Constitution, it can do so by enacting a law declaring that for the purposes mentioned
herein above the Bureau's jurisdiction shall not apply to that particular State.

filing of final report in the Court.
12. An officer of the Bureau may execise the powers and discharge the duties conferred
or imposed under this Act on any other officer of the Bureau who is subordinate to him.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
The Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India's Resolution No. 4/31/61-T, dated
1.4.1963 established the Central Bureau of Investigation with a view to investigate serious
crimes relating to defence of India, corruption in high places, serious frauds, cheating and
embezzlement and social crimes, particularly of hoarding, black-marketing and profiteering in
essential commodities, having all-India and inter-State ramifications.
The Central Bureau of Investigation is presently governed by the Delhi Special Police
Establishment Act, 1946 (Act No. 25 of 1946). This Act dates back to the pre-Independence
and pre-Constitutional era. As per the existing provisions, the Central Bureau of Investigation
requires consent of States to investigate offences in their jurisdiction as per the Delhi Special
Police Establishment Act. This has become a conflict area as a number of States have
challenged the powers of the Supreme Court and the High Courts to order investigation by
the Central Bureau of Investigation without their consent.
Successive Parliamentary Committees have recommended replacement of Delhi Special
Police Establishment Act by an independent Central Bureau of Investigation Act keeping in
view the above constraints and the rising challenges. The Second Administrative Reforms
Commission in its fifth report on Public Order in June 2007 recommended that a new law
should be enacted to govern the working of the Central Bureau of Investigation. This law
should also stipulate its jurisdiction including the power to investigate the new category of
crimes.
The Bill seeks to provide an appropriate legal architecture and give statutory status to
the Central Bureau of Investigation and to equip it with police power to prevent, investigate
and prosecute serious crimes pertaining to the security of the country, corruption and other
crimes having all India and inter-State ramifications in an independent and transparent manner.

FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM
Clause 3 of the Bill provides for the constitution of Central Bureau of Investigation by
the Central Government. Clause 9 provides for setting up of a Directorate of Prosecution to
undertake all work relating to prosecution, appeal and revision of cases investigated by the
Bureau. The Bill, therefore, if enacted, will involve expenditure from the Consolidated Fund
of India. It is estimated that a sum of rupees three hundred and ten crore will be involved as
recurring expenditure per annum.
No non-recurring expenditure is likely to be involved.

MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION
Clause 19 of the Bill gives power to the Central Government to make rules for carrying
out the purposes of the Bill. As the rules will relate to matters of detail only, the delegation of
legislative power is of a normal character.
