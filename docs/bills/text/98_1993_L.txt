STATEMENT OF OBJECIS AND REASONS

Following the recommendations of the Committee on the Financial
System (Narasimham Committee) the Reserve Bank of India had intre-
duced a new set of norms for income recognition and provisioning and
also for capital adequacy in relation to yisk weighted assets. These
norms were designed to pui the financial accounting and prudential
standards of Indian banks on a sound footing in line with current in-
ternational practice. Following the introduction of these norms, all
the nationalised banks ag well as the State Bank of India will have to
puildl up their capital base substantially over the next 3 years. All
panks have to ensure that their total capital equals at least 8 per cent.
of their risk weighted assets by 31st March, 1996. This norm has to be
reachid by 3lst March, 1994 in the ease of banks having overse!

operations.

2, Achievement of the prescribed capital adequacy norms is essential
for the basic financial health of the banking system. It is also essential
for its international eredibility since banks all over the world are adopt-
ing these standards which were laid down by the Committee on Banking
Regulations and Supervisory Practices set up by the Bank of Interna.
tional Settlements. :

3. State Bank of India, being a bank with overseas operations, is
required to reach 8 capital adequacy ratio of 8 per cent. by 3lst March,
1994. For this purpose, the State Bank of India is required to mobilise
substantial additional resources from the market. ‘The Bank is, there-
fore. coming out shortly with a rights-cum-public issue.

4, With a view to comply with the guidelines issued by the Securities
and Exchange Board of India and also to enable the bank to attracl a
large number of smaller individual investors and keeping in view the
current market preference, certain amendments in the State Bank of
India Act. 1955 are necessary to provide for (i) reduction in the face
value of Bank’s share from Rs. 100 to Rs. 10; (it) removal of restriction
of 200 shares on individual holdings; (iii) raising of ceiling placed
on votine rights from { per cent. to 10 per cent; (iv) doing away with
the maintenance of pranch registers at Local Head Offices; (v) mainten-
ance of central register at the Central Office and to keep that register in
computer floppies or diskettes; ‘and (vi) other consequential amendments
on account of abolition of branch registers. : -

5. As Parliament was not in session, the President promulgated the
State Bank of India (Amendment) Ordinance. 1998 (Ord. No, 38 of 19:3)
on the 15th October, 1998.

6. The Bill seeks to replace the above Ordinance.
