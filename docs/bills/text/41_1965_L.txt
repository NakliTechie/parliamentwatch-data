Clause 27 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to make 
rules to give effect to the provisions of the Act. The matters in res-
pect of which such rules m;iy be made relate to the term of office 
of members and the manner of filling casual vacancies among them, 
the allowances payable to members other than the Chairman and to 
pe~sons associated under clause 10, the disCJ..~~Iil.lcation for memb(.r-
ship of the Board and the procedure to be" fonowed in removing 
members subject to disqualitication, the conditions subject to which 
and the mode in which contracts may be entered into by or on be-
half of the Board. All these matters are comparatively of a minor 
importance and are normally the subject-matter of delegated legis-
lation, and the rules will also be laid before Parliament. 
2. Clause 28 of the Bill empowers the Board to 'miLke regulations 
not inconsistent with the provisions of the Act and the rules made 
thereunder. The power of making regulations is confined to proce-
dural matters, such' as the conditions and restrictions subject to 
which manuscripts and books in the Library may be used, the re-
cruitment and conditionS' of service of oftlcers '!nd employees of the 
Board, the time and place of meetings of the Board, the procedure 
to be followed at such meetings, the maintenance of minutes of meet-
ings of the Board and the transmission of copies thereof to Govern-
ment, the maintenance of registers and accounts, the compilation of 
catalogues and inventories of books, manuscripts, articles and things 
in the Library, the fees and other charges to be' levied, etc.. The 
power of making regulations is thus confined to matters of detail only 
and this power can be exercised by the Board only with the pre-
vious approval of the Central Government. '. .
