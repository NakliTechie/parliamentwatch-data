Integrated Housing and Slum Development Programme (IHSDP) is a
component of Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission
(JNNURM), launched in December 2005 to strive for holistic slum
development with a healthy and enabling urban environment by providing
adequate shelter and basic infrastructure facilities to the slum dwellers through
upgradation/new construction of houses and infrastructure facilities, like water
supply and sewerage in ULBs. State Urban Development Agency (SUDA)
was appointed as the State Level Nodal Agency for implementation of the
programme in the State.

The implementation of IHSDP projects was lagging behind the completion
schedule prescribed by Central Sanctioning Committee. As against 17922
sanctioned dwelling units in the State in 18 IHSDP projects, ULBs could
construct 7947 dwelling units (44 per cent) upto March 2014.

In four test-checked Municipal Corporations, Municipal Corporations were
not able to allot all the completed dwelling units and the allotment was only 71
per cent (3606 dwelling units) of the dwelling units completed. Department
attributed the reason for non-allotment of dwelling units to reluctance on the
part of the beneficiaries to shift to the newly constructed dwelling units in
Rajnandgaon and Bilaspur.

We observed that none of the test-checked Municipal Corporations created
revolving funds for future maintenance of IHSDP infrastructure. Further,
Municipal Corporations at Bilaspur and Durg raised excess loan of
X 3.88 crore.

The 74th Constitutional amendment gave constitutional status to Urban Local
Bodies (ULBs) and established a system of uniform structure, regular election
and regular flow of funds through Finance Commission etc. As a follow up,
the States are required to entrust these bodies with powers, functions and
responsibilities so as to enable them to function as institutions of
Self-Government.

Article 243Q of the Constitution envisages that there shall be constituted in
every State, Municipal Corporation for large urban areas; Municipal Councils
for smaller urban areas; and Nagar Panchayats for areas in transition from a
rural to an urban area. Further, Article 243W states that the Legislature of a
State may, by law, endow the municipalities with such powers and authority
as may be necessary to enable them to function as institutions of
Self-Government and such law may contain provisions for devolution of
powers and responsibilities upon municipalities.

The State of Chhattisgarh, with Raipur as its capital, came into existence on
1 November 2000 by separation of 16 districts of Chhattisgarh region from
Madhya Pradesh. At present, there are 27 districts in the State.

The Madhya Pradesh/ Chhattisgarh Municipalities Act, 1961 was enacted by
the then undivided Madhya Pradesh, and was adopted (August 2001) in
Chhattisgarh with the objective of consolidating and amending the law
relating to Municipalities and to make better provision for the organisation
and administration of Municipalities.

The three tier of ULBs in Chhattisgarh are Municipal Corporations, Municipal
Councils and Nagar Panchayats. In July 2014, the State Government
converted two Municipal Councils! to Municipal Corporations and 14 Nagar
Panchayats” to Municipal Councils. At present, there are 12 Municipal
Corporations, 44 Municipal Councils and 113 Nagar Panchayats. The basic
information about the State of Chhattisgarh is given in the Table 1.1 below:

The Urban Administration and Development Department (UADD) is the
administrative department for the various Municipal Corporations, Municipal

Councils and Nagar Panchayats in the State. A Directorate has been
established under UADD, with its regional offices at Raipur, Bilaspur,
Ambikapur and Jagdalpur. State Urban Development Agency (SUDA),
constituted under UADD, is responsible for implementation and monitoring of
poverty alleviation programmes in the urban areas of the State. District Urban
Development Agency (DUDA) has been established in each district under the
chairmanship of the respective Collectors. Project Officers have been
appointed in each DUDA and have been made responsible for the
management of its operations. For the management of urban administration
and fulfillment of responsibilities, each ULB has an elected Municipal
Corporation/Municipal Council/Nagar Panchayat.

Article 243X of the Constitution envisages that State Legislature may, by law,
vest power in ULBs to impose various taxes for revenue collection. This
constitutional provision has been incorporated in Clause 132 of Chhattisgarh
Municipal Corporation Act, 1956 and Clause 127 of Chhattisgarh
Municipalities Act, 1961. ULBs receive funds on monthly basis under Chungi
Compensation Grant and Passenger Tax Special Grant by the State
Government. In addition to this, at the ULBs level various types of taxes have
been imposed including Property tax, Samekit Kar, Water tax, Market fee,
Export tax etc. In urban areas, the process of self-assessment of the property
tax has been enforced by the Government to reduce the difficulties for
assessment and collection of Property tax.

1.4.1 Chhattisgarh Infrastructure Development Fund

Chhattisgarh Urban Development Fund Rules 2003 had been framed and
implemented for the systematic development of urban local bodies in the State.
Two types of accounts are maintained under this fund, viz. Devolution
Account and Infrastructure Account. Devolution account includes the grants
received under regular Chungi Compensation, Passenger Tax, Registration
fee, Bar license fee and other compensation grants, which are utilised by the
ULBs as per its discretion. The Infrastructure Account consists of the funds
received in accordance with the recommendations of State Finance
Commission (SFC) and funds received for items like repair and maintenance
of roads etc. These funds are utilised for purposes like road repair works,
schemes related to drinking water, reforms of Fire Brigade service, any special
scheme of the State Government, Solid Waste Management, and development

of basic amenities. The allocation of funds under the Chhattisgarh

The Raipur Municipal Corporation (RMC) is governed by the Chhattisgarh
Municipal Corporation Act, 1956. Its jurisdiction extends to 178.35 Square
Kilometer consisting of eight Zonal Offices (ZOs) and 70 Wards. The
population of RMC as per census 2011 was 10.47 lakh. The RMC is a
statutory body, which provides civic services and infrastructure facilities to the
citizens of Raipur and is empowered to levy and collect tax and non-tax
revenues. It also implements various Central/State Government Sponsored
Programmes in urban areas under its jurisdiction.

The Urban Administration and Development Department (UADD) headed by
Principal Secretary is the controlling department of all the municipalities for
all administrative purpose. The Directorate, Urban Administration and
Development Department, Raipur (DUADD) has also been established in
Raipur to provide technical and administrative guidance to municipal
authorities at regional level.

Under Section 69 (4) of the Chhattisgarh Municipal Corporation (CGMC) Act,
1956, the administrative and executive powers and functions of RMC are
vested with the Commissioner. One Upper Commissioner (UC) and two
Deputy Commissioners (DCs) assist the Commissioner, RMC. In addition,
Superintending Engineer (SE) assists the Commissioner RMC in respect of
Technical Works relating to development of infrastructure under the areas of
RMC. RMC has 17 sections for execution of various functions.

RMC is divided in eight Zone Offices (ZOs)— ZO No.1 (Khamtarai), ZO No.2
(Maudhapara), ZO No. 3 (Shankar Nagar), ZO No. 4 (Malviya Road), ZO
No. 5 (Idgah Bhata), ZO No. 6 (Bhatagaon), ZO No. 7 (Malviya Road) and
ZO No.8 (Mahoba Bazar). Each ZO is headed by Zonal Commissioners (ZCs).
As per the Commissioner, RMC order (February 2012), the financial and
administrative powers delegated to ZC are upto हैं 20 lakh.

Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM), launched in
December 2005, is the flagship programme of Government of India (Gol) to
address the problems of infrastructure and basic services to the poor in cities
and towns in a holistic manner. Integrated Housing and Slum Development
Programme (IHSDP) is a component of JNNURM, which is applicable to all
cities except Mission cities covered under JNNURM. The basic objective of
THSDP is to strive for holistic slum development with a healthy and enabling
urban environment by providing adequate shelter and basic infrastructure
facilities to the slum dwellers of the identified urban areas. The components
for assistance under the scheme are slum improvement/upgradation/relocation
projects including upgradation/new construction of houses and infrastructure
facilities, like water supply and sewerage.

as nodal agency for implementation of the IHSDP. Accordingly, Government
of Chhattisgarh appointed (December 2005) State Urban Development

To promote greater autonomy at the grass root level and to involve people
through Gram Sabhas in identification and implementation of development
programmes, the Seventy-third Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 was
promulgated. According to the provisions of Article 243 G of the Constitution,
the Legislature of a State may, by law, endow the panchayats with such
powers and authority as may be necessary to enable them to function as
institutions of self-government and such law may contain provision for the
devolution of powers and responsibility upon panchayat at the appropriate
level, subject to such conditions as may be specified therein.

A three-tier system of Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) had been
established in the State by Madhya Pradesh Panchayati Raj Aivam Gram
Swaraj Adhiniyam (Act), 1993 which came into force from January 1994
and adopted by Chhattisgarh State as Chhattisgarh Panchayat Raj
Adhiniyam 1993 on 7 June 2001 after creation of the State of Chhattisgarh.

The PRIs are classified into three tiers, viz., Zila Panchayat at district level,
Janpad Panchayats at block level and Gram Panchayats at village level. At
present, there are 18 ZPs, 146 JPs and 9734 GPs in the State. The last general
election for the GPs, JPs and ZPs was held during January and February 2015.

Total population of the State is about 2.55 crore with 1.96 crore people
residing in rural area constituting 76.86 per cent of the total population as per
2011 census. The population-wise classification of GPs is given in the Table

4.4.1 Zila Panchayats (ZPs) are the first tier of Panchayat at the district level.
Chief Executive Officer (CEO) is the administrative head and assisted by
departments like Public Health, Public Works, Rural Engineering etc.

4.4.2 The ZP is required to prepare the budget for the planned development of
the district and utilisation of the resources. The ZP is responsible for the

schemes entrusted by the Central or State Governments; appropriation of the
grants received from the Central or State Governments to the JPs and GPs in
accordance with the specified criteria; taking steps to ensure procurement of
resources and any other functions entrusted by the State Government under
special or general orders.

4.4.3 Janpad Panchayats (JPs) are the intermediate tier of PRIs at the Block
level. The JPs have least source of their own revenue and are mostly
dependent on the Block Grants received from ZPs. The JPs undertake
development works at the block level. It is their duty to make suitable
arrangements for rural development, agriculture, social forestry, animal
husbandry and pisciculture, health and hygiene, adult education, cooperative
work, cottage industries, social welfare, family planning, rural employment

Executive Summary

For making the fiscal decentralisation meaningful and to maintain the balance
between the functional assignments and resource availability, Chhattisgarh
Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam (CGPRA) devolved revenue raising powers to
Janpad Panchayat (JP) and Gram Panchayat (GP) in their respective
jurisdictional areas. The performance audit of receipts of Panchayats was
carried out with the objective to ascertain the extent to which GPs and JPs
levied and collected taxes and fees for which they were empowered, besides
maintenance of records of receipts. Performance Audit was conducted during
April 2014 to September 2014 covering the period from 2009-10 to 2013-14.
The major audit findings are:

¢ Out of 179 test-checked GPs, 117 GPs was not levying the Property tax,
though this has been classified as an obligatory tax assigned to GPs. Some
GPs attributed the reasons for non-levy of Property tax to opposition by
villagers/elected members, poverty, non-valuation of property, etc.

(Paragraph 5.1.5)

e Professional tax was not levied in 99 GPs, as these GPs did not pass the
requisite resolution for levy of Professional tax. In 21 other GPs, Professional

The 73rd Constitution Amendment Act, 1992 conferred a constitutional status
on the Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) considering them as institutions of
Self-Government. A three-tier system of PRIs - Zila Panchayat (ZP), Janpad
Panchayat (JP) and Gram Panchayat (GP) had been established in the
Chhattisgarh by Chhattisgarh Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam (CGPRA)!. There
are 18 ZPs in 27 Districts, 146 JPs and 9734 GPs spread across the State.

For making the fiscal decentralisation meaningful and to maintain the balance
between the functional assignments and resource availability, CGPRA
devolves revenue raising powers to PRIs in their respective jurisdictional
areas. In addition to the tax sources, the PRIs are also empowered to collect
non-tax revenues in the form of fees, fines, user charges, etc.

The Performance Audit of Receipts of Panchayats was carried out to assess
whether:

. the GPs/JPs have levied all the taxes for which they are empowered;

. assessment and collection of fees and taxes and other receipts were
regularly done;

. records of receipts (relating to assessment, levy, collection and arrears)
were maintained properly.

The main criteria to arrive at the audit conclusions were:

. Chhattisgarh Panchayat Raj Adhinivam (CGPRA) 1993 and rules
made thereunder,

. Orders and instructions issued by Government of Chhattisgarh from
time to time.

' Chhattisgarh adopted Madhya Pradesh Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam 1993 as Chhattisgarh

Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam 1993 on 7 June 2001 after creation of the State of
Chhattisgarh.
