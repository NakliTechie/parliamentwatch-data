Government commercial concerns, the accounts of which are subject to audit
by the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG), fall under the
following categories:

(i) Government companies,
(ii) Statutory corporations, and
(iii) | Departmentally managed commercial undertakings.

2. This Report deals with the results of audit of Government companies
and Statutory corporation and has been prepared for submission to the
Government of Chhattisgarh under Section 19A of the Comptroller and
Auditor General’s (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971, as
amended from time to time.

3. Audit of the accounts of Government companies is conducted by the
CAG under the provisions of Section 619 of the Companies Act, 1956.

4. Audit of accounts of Chhattisgarh State Warehousing Corporation
which is a Statutory corporation is conducted by Chartered Accountants and
supplementary audit is done by CAG. In respect of Chhattisgarh State
Electricity Regulatory Commission, CAG is the sole auditor. The Audit
Reports on annual accounts of these corporation/ commission are forwarded
separately to the State Government.

5. The cases mentioned in this Report are those which came to notice in
the course of audit during the year 2012-13 as well as those which came to
notice in earlier years, but were not dealt with in the previous Reports. Matters
relating to the period subsequent to 2012-13 have also been included,
wherever necessary.

6. The Audit has been conducted in accordance with the Auditing
Standards issued by CAG.

2. Review relating to Government Compan.

A review on procurement, storage and distribution of rice, wheat and sugar under
various schemes of Public Distribution System by Chhattisgarh State Civil
Supplies Corporation Limited was conducted. Executive summary of our audit
findings is given below.

Introduction

The Government of India (Gol) issues scheme-wise allotment of foodgrains to the States as
per the number of beneficiaries in the States for distribution of the same under Public
Distribution System (PDS). On the basis of it, Food, Civil Supplies and Consumer
Protection Department (Department) of Government of Chhattisgarh (GoCG) issues district-
wise allotment to the Chhattisgarh State Civil Supplies Corporation Limited (Company) for
taking advance action for procurement, storage and distribution of foodgrains to the

districts. The Company works as a nodal agency for PDS. The main objective of the
Company is procurement, storage and distribution of foodgrains under various schemes of
PDS so as to ensure their availability to the beneficiaries according to the schemes framed
by Gol/GoCG.

Decentralised Procurement was introduced (April 2002) in the State for procurement and
maintenance of stock of rice at State level in order to provide Minimum Support Price
(MSP) to farmers and also to ensure economy in distribution of rice. Wheat is procured by
the Company from Food Corporation of India (FCI) at subsidised rate fixed by Gol and
sugar is procured by the Company from sugar mills specified by the Gol at the prescribed
rate and are distributed to the beneficiaries.

The performance of the Company was reviewed to assess economy, efficiency and
effectiveness in implementation of various schemes of Gol/ GoCG under PDS during the
period 2008-09 to 2012-13.

Planning for procurement of rice, wheat and sugar

Keeping in view the requirement of rice to meet distribution under various schemes of PDS,
the Department procures paddy from farmers through Chhattisgarh State Cooperative
Marketing Federation (Markfed) at MSP and supplies Custom Milled Rice (CMR) to the
Company through Markfed. For this purpose, Department issues policy related to CMR
every year duly mentioning the tentative quantity of rice for procurement during Kharif
Marketing Season which is followed by the Company. For procurement of wheat and sugar,
the Company is under obligation to follow the allotment made by the Department.

Storage and transportation of rice, wheat and sugar

In the absence of norms of storage losses, data on actual reimbursable losses were not
maintained by the Company. Requirement of storage space for reservation in State
Warehousing Corporation (SWC)/ Central Warehousing Corporation (CWC) godowns was
not assessed properly thereby the Company could not utilise the reservation of godowns in
full and paid © 7.58 crore for unutilised storage space. There was huge variation in
transportation rates of different base depots in the same division and there was no
mechanism for comparison of rates of neighbouring divisions. The Company recovered
penalty at lower rate than the prescribed rates from transport contractors.

Overview

Distribution of foodgrains under various schemes of Gol/GoCG

The Company distributed less quantity of rice and wheat than the allotments made under
various schemes during the years 2009-10 and 2010-11 due to which the benefit of the
schemes had not reached fully to the intended beneficiaries. At the direction of GoCG, the
Company diverted 7.43 lakh MT rice and 1.91 lakh MT wheat from Central Schemes to
State Scheme (Mukhyamanti Khadyann Sahayata Yojana) in violation of Gol instructions
resulted in diversion of foodgrains valuing €1975.43 crore and wrong claim of
₹ 899.12 crore from Gol. The Company distributed rice without Gol allotment under Mid
Day Meal Scheme and Other Welfare Schemes which resulted in non-receipt of € 35.78
crore,

Financial Management

There were delays in finalisation of accounts of the Company due to which it could not
submit its audited accounts to Gol in time leading to blockage of subsidy claims of € 760.93
crore and consequent loss of interest of € 186.81 crore. Dues of the Company of € 1.48 crore
could not be recovered from Fair Price Shops due to ineffective action by the Department.

Deployment of Manpower

The post of District Managers in 18 districts were manned by Assistant Managers/ Assistant
Accounts Officers (AMs/AAOs). Thus, there was no supervision on the work of these
AMs/AAOs.

Monitoring and Internal Control System

There was lack of coordination among the various agencies which resulted in storage of
excess stock and consequent avoidable storage charges. Mismatch in allotted quantity of Gol
and GoCG caused excess/ short distribution of foodgrains and submission of incorrect
utilisation certificates to Gol by the Company.

A well set monitoring and internal control system prevails in the Company due to
introduction of information and communication technology. However, there is no
mechanism to conduct surprise checks of stocks at different storage points and also there is
no vigilance wing in the Company.

Conclusion

There were cases of non-utilisation of storage space and excess/less distribution of foodgrains
under various schemes. Transport contracts were finalised without comparing rates of
neighbouring divisions resulted in huge variation in transportation rates. In the absence of
norms of storage losses, data on actual reimbursable losses were not maintained by the
Company. Reserving storage space in excess of the requirement led to under utilisation of
storage space. The Company did not recover the penalty at prescribed rates from the transport
contractors. The Company diverted foodgrains from Central schemes to State scheme. Rice
was distributed in Mid Day Meal Scheme and Other Welfare Schemes without Gol allotment.
Delay in finalisation of accounts led to non-receipt of subsidy from Gol. The Company had
not prescribed any mechanism to conduct periodical surprise check at storage points.

Executive Summary

Introduction

The Government of India (Gol) issues scheme-wise allotment of foodgrains to the
States as per the number of beneficiaries in the States for distribution of the same
under Public Distribution System (PDS). On the basis of it, Food, Civil Supplies and
Consumer Protection Department (Department) of Government of Chhattisgarh
(GoCG) issues district-wise allotment to the Chhattisgarh State Civil Supplies
Corporation Limited (Company) for taking advance action for procurement, storage
and distribution of foodgrains to the districts. The Company works as a nodal agency
for PDS. The main objective of the Company is procurement, storage and distribution
of foodgrains under various schemes of PDS so as to ensure their availability to the
beneficiaries according to the schemes framed by Gol/GoCG.

Decentralised Procurement was introduced (April 2002) in the State for procurement
and maintenance of stock of rice at State Level in order to provide Minimum Support
Price (MSP) to farmers and also to ensure economy in distribution of rice. Wheat is
procured by the Company from Food Corporation of India (FCI) at subsidised rate
fixed by Gol and sugar is procured by the Company from sugar mills specified by the
Gol at the prescribed rate and are distributed to the beneficiaries.

The performance of the Company was reviewed to assess economy, efficiency and
effectiveness in implementation of various schemes of Gol/ GoCG under PDS during
the period 2008-09 to 2012-13.

Planning for procurement of rice, wheat and sugar

Keeping in view the requirement of rice to meet distribution under various schemes of
PDS, the Department procures paddy from farmers through Chhattisgarh State
Cooperative Marketing Federation (Markfed) at MSP and supplies Custom Milled Rice
(CMR) to the Company through Markfed. For this purpose, Department issues policy
related to CMR every year duly mentioning the tentative quantity of rice for
procurement during Kharif Marketing Season which is followed by the Company. For
procurement of wheat and sugar, the Company is under obligation to follow the
allotment made by the Department.

Storage and transportation of rice, wheat and sugar

In the absence of norms of storage losses, data on actual reimbursable losses were not
maintained by the Company. Requirement of storage space for reservation in State
Warehousing Corporation (SWC)/ Central Warehousing Corporation (CWC) godowns
was not assessed properly thereby the Company could not utilise the reservation of
godowns in full and paid हैं 7.58 crore for unutilised storage space. There was huge
variation in transportation rates of different base depots in the same division and there
was no mechanism for comparison of rates of neighbouring divisions. The Company
recovered penalty at a lower rate than the prescribed rates from transport contractors.



Audit Report on Public Sector Undertakings for the year ended 31 March 2013

Distribution of foodgrains under various schemes of Gol/GoCG

The Company distributed less quantity of rice and wheat than the allotments made
under various schemes during the years 2009-10 and 2010-11 due to which the benefit
of the schemes had not reached fully to the intended beneficiaries. At the direction of
GoCG, the Company diverted 7.43 lakh MT rice and 1.91 lakh MT wheat from Central
Schemes to State Scheme (Mukhyamanti Khadyann Sahayata Yojana) in violation of
Gol instructions resulted in diversion of foodgrains valuing € 1975.43 crore and wrong
claim of € 899.12 crore from Gol. The Company distributed rice without Gol allotment
under Mid Day Meal Scheme and Other Welfare Schemes which resulted in non-
receipt of €35.78 crore.

Financial Management

There were delays in finalisation of accounts of the Company due to which it could
not submit its audited accounts to Gol in time leading to blockage of subsidy claims of
& 760.93 crore and consequent loss of interest of हैं 186.81 crore. Dues of the Company
of © 1.48 crore could not be recovered from Fair Price Shops due to ineffective action
by the Department.

Deployment of Manpower

The post of District Managers in 18 districts were manned by Assistant Managers/
Assistant Accounts Officers (AMs/AAOs). Thus, there was no supervision on the work
of these AMs/AAOs.

Monitoring and Internal Control System

There was lack of coordination among the various agencies which resulted in storage
of excess stock and consequent avoidable storage charges. Mismatch in allotted
quantity of GoI and GoCG caused excess/ short distribution of foodgrains and
submission of incorrect utilisation certificates to Gol by the Company.

A well set monitoring and internal control system prevails in the Company due to
introduction of information and communication technology. However, there is no
mechanism to conduct surprise checks of stocks at different storage points and also
there is no vigilance wing in the Company.

Conclusion

There were cases of non-utilisation of storage space and excess/less distribution of
foodgrains under various schemes. Transport contracts were finalised without
comparing rates of neighbouring divisions resulted in huge variation in transportation
rates. In the absence of norms of storage losses, data on actual reimbursable losses
were not maintained by the Company. Reserving storage space in excess of the
requirement led to underutilisation of storage space. The Company did not recover the
penalty at prescribed rates from the transport contractors. The Company diverted
foodgrains from Central schemes to State scheme. Rice was distributed in Mid Day
Meal Scheme and Other Welfare Schemes without Gol allotment. Delay in finalisation
of accounts led to non-receipt of subsidy from Gol. The Company had not prescribed
any mechanism to conduct periodical surprise check at storage points.

3.1 Irregular finalisation of rate contracts

Irregular finalisation of rate contracts for vegetable seeds resulted in
placement of orders worth f 47.26 crore on ineligible bidders

The Chhattisgarh Rajya Beej Evam Krishi Vikas Nigam Limited, Raipur
(Company) procures vegetable seeds {Certified'/ Open Pollinated Truth
Level’ (OPTL)} of different crops through its District Offices for Horticulture
and Farm Forestry Department, Government of Chhattisgarh. This is done on
the basis of rate contracts finalised by it by inviting Open Ended Rate Contract
Offer (RCO) from the registered seed producers/ suppliers. The lowest rates
received through RCO are compared with the previous rates (increased by 50
per cent) finalised by the Company and prevailing rates of Madhya Pradesh
State Agro Industries Development Corporation Limited (MP Agro).
Accordingly, the Company issues counter offers to the suppliers on the basis
of lowest of the above three rates and rate contracts are finalised with the
suppliers who accept the counter offer rate. These rate contracts remain
effective for one year or upto finalisation of new rate contracts.

For the year 2010-11, the Company invited (11 February 2011) RCO for 29
and 40 types of Certified and OPTL vegetable seeds respectively. In response,
15 offers were received upto the last date 1.6. 26 March 2011. The technical
bids were evaluated by the tender evaluation committee on 26 March 2011 and
13 bidders were found technically qualified. Accordingly, price bid of these 13

Annexure - 2.1
Details of Central and State Schemes of PDS
(Referred to in paragraph 2.1)

This scheme is for distribution of foodgrains under Targeted Public
Distribution System (TPDS) introduced by Gol in 1997 and subsequent PDS
control order issued in 2001 and 2004. Under this scheme, 35 kilogram (kgs)
of foodgrains (rice and wheat together) per month is provided to the Below
Poverty Line (BPL) families at subsidised rate of
₹ 5.65 per kg (rice) and 4.15 per kg (wheat) by Gol. The beneficiaries can
lift the rice and wheat as per their choice subject to a maximum of 35 kgs.

The scheme specifically caters to the needs of the poorest of poor people living
in urban and rural areas, who are unable to buy essential commodities even at a
relatively lower price. Gol had introduced Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) in
December 2000 to ensure food security to poorest of the poor among BPL
families through provision of 35 kgs of foodgrains (rice and wheat together)
per month at 3 per kg (rice) and % 2 per kg (wheat). The beneficiaries can lift
the rice and wheat as per their choice subject to a maximum of 35 kgs.

The objective of this scheme is to provide food security to senior destitute
citizens who are above the age of 65 years and who are not included under the
National old age pension scheme. In Chhattisgarh it was introduced in October
2001. The beneficiaries receive 10 kgs of rice every month free of cost.

This scheme was introduced by Gol in 1997 to provide foodgrains to the
Above Poverty Line (APL) families. Under this scheme 15 kgs of foodgrains
(rice and wheat) per month for each family are given at subsidised rates (rice at
the rate of % 9.30 per kg and wheat at the rate of
₹ 7.50 per kg).

Mid-Day Meal Scheme under National Programme of Nutritional Support to
students of Primary Education was launched (August 1995) with a view to
enhance enrolment, attendance and retention of school children and
simultaneous improvement in the nutritional levels among them. For
implementation of the scheme, free allotment of rice at the rate of 100 gms per
child per school per day is made to schools as per the allotment of School
Education Department of the State.

Poorak Poshan Aahar scheme is a centrally sponsored scheme. In this scheme
food is provided to pregnant lactating women and children between six months
and six years of age to improve the nutritional status among them through
Aanganwadi Centres at free of cost. The Company claims from Gol the cost
of foodgrains supplied in this scheme and the Gol reimburses the same.

Under the Other Welfare Scheme (OWS), foodgrains are supplied to hostels
and ashrams for Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) students at
the rate of 15 kgs per student per month. These hostels and ashrams are
operated by SC/ST Welfare Department of GoCG.

GoCG had introduced Mukhyamantri Khadyanna Sahayata Yojna (MKSY) in
April 2007. In this scheme foodgrains are distributed to those BPL families
whose names were available in the survey of 1991 and 1997 but did not exist
in the list of beneficiaries prepared based on the survey made by the GoCG in
the years 2002 and 2007. Objective of the scheme is to distribute commodities
of PDS to poor families left out from the TPDS. Under this scheme, GoCG
distributes rice at € 2 per kg to BPL families and % 1 per kg to Antyodaya
families.
