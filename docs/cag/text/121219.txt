Preface 
This Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India  for the year ending  
31 March 2022 has been prepared for submission  to the Governor of Madhya Pradesh under 
Article 151 of the Constitution of India, for being laid before the Legislature of the State. 
The Report contains significant results of the Performance Audit of ‘Implementation of 
Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram Jy oti Yojana and Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana 
by Electricity Distribution Companies of Madhya Pradesh ’, covering th e period 2014-15 to 
2021-22 for DDUGJY and 2017-18 to 2021-22 for SAUBHAGYA (implementation period of 
the schemes).  
The instances mentioned in this Report are among those which came to notice in the course 
of test audit.   
The audit has been conducted in conformity with the Auditing Standards  issued by the 
Comptroller and Auditor General of India. 
 

  
   
     
   
   
  
  
 
   
  
  
    
  
  
  
  
       
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

Executive Summary 
Power is a critical infrastructure element for the growth of an economy. The availability  
of reliable, quality and affordable power is vital for rapid growth in agriculture, industry and 
the overall economic development of a state. For this, an efficient, resilient and financially 
healthy power sector is an essential requirement for the growth of a state and the economic 
empowerment of the common man.  Rural electrification is considered the key to accelerating 
the rural economy. The demand for electricity in rural areas is increasing day by day due to an 
increase in customer base, changes in lifestyle, and consumption patterns which require 
continual strengthening and augmentation of the distribution network to  ensure reliable and 
quality power supply.  
Under the Indian Constitution, electricity is a concurrent subject. As per Electricity Act 2003, 
it is the duty of a distribution licensee to develop and maintain an efficient, coordinated and 
economical distribution system in the mandated area of supply a s well as to supply the 
electricity in accordance with the provisions contained in the Act. However, the poor financial 
health of the power distribution companies (DISCOMs) resulted in under -investment in the 
distribution network and poor upkeep and maintenance of assets, particularly in rural areas. As 
such, there had been different schemes to support DISCOMs to augment and strengthen the 
distribution networks and provide universal electrification.  
The Ministry of Power (MoP), GoI launched (December 2014) Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram 
Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY) subsuming the targets laid down under the erstwhile Rajiv Gandhi 
Grameen Vidyutikaran Yojana (RGGVY) as a separate rural electrification sub-component by 
carrying forward the approved outlay for the RGGVY to the DDUGJY with additional 
objectives, viz. separation of agriculture and non -agriculture feeders; and strengthening and 
augmentation of sub -transmission and distribution infrastructure in rural area s, including 
metering at distribution transformers, and the feeder and consumers’ end.  
The GoI also launched ( October 2017) Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana 
(SAUBHAGYA) to achieve universal household electrification in the country. The scope of the 
Scheme included providing last -mile connectivity and electricity connections to all  
un-electrified households in rural areas, Solar Photovoltaic based standalone system for  
un-electrified households located in remote and inaccessible villages/habitations, where grid 
extension is not feasible or cost -effective, and providing last mile connectivity and electricity 
connections to all remaining economically poor un-electrified households in urban areas. 
The above two Schemes involved an expenditure of ₹ 4,694.95 crore for the creation of power 
distribution infrastructure and providing new electricity connections to 19.33 lakh households. 
All the DISCOMs / State Government certified (July/September/October 2018) that they had 
achieved 100 per cent electrification. Considering the huge financial amount involved and the 
larger social objective of the Schemes, the implementation of these Schemes has been 
examined in this Performance Audit.  
The Audit examined the system of assessing the requirement of infrastructure works, execution 
of projects under the Schemes and mechanism of monitoring the execution and quality of 
works. Of the 50 projects under the Schemes, 13 projects were sampled for detailed scrutiny. 
DDUGJY 
The DISCOMs were to formulate the project-wise DPRs based on the detailed field survey for 
sanction of projects from GoI and execute the works as per the approved DPRs. However, the 
DISCOMs prepared the DPRs without any field survey. Resultantly, huge variations occurred 
in various components of the works executed against the sanctioned quantities.

The DPRs were prepared at rates other than SOR. The nine sampled projects of MPMKVVCL 
and MPPaKVVCL were overvalued to the tune of ₹ 25.94 crore and the four sampled projects 
of MPPoKVVCL were undervalued by ₹ 2.73 crore. 
Metering of the supply of power is considered essential for energy accounting and in 
identifying high -loss pockets and initiating remedial measures for the reduction of losses. 
Against the requirement of metering for 89,310 DTRs and 2,83,777 unmetered consumers, the 
DISCOMs planned for metering of 2,400 DTRs (three per cent ) and 1,02,61 4 consumers  
(36 per cent) only. However, no DTR and only 25,982 unmetered consumers ( 25 per cent) 
were meterised.  
For strengthening the power distribution system, against the augmentation of 86 substations 
approved under the Scheme, only 55 substations were augmented. Importantly, MPMKVVCL 
augmented 17 of these substations for ₹ 7.12 crore on its own thereby losing a grant of  
₹ 4.27 crore under the Scheme. 
Timely completion of the project is essential so that the beneficiaries are connected to the grid 
at the earliest. However, it was noticed that f our projects which were awarded on a turnkey 
basis were delayed by two to 22 months. In three projects, the MPMKVVCL descoped the 
work of turnkey contractors but did not recover the liquidated damages of ₹ 6.51 crore. 
The DISCOMs were required to bring down the AT&C losses in the range of 15.50 to  
23.00 per cent by 2020-21 and as an incentive additional grant (15 per cent of project cost) of  
₹ 102.96 crore was to be received. However, AT&C losses remained high in the range of  
18.51 to 47.34  per cent in the year 2020 -21 and the DISCOMs could not avail of addition al 
grants.  
Variations were noticed in the details of projects completed, details as submitted and approved 
in the project Closure Report and details as available in the Scheme dashboard.  
MPMKVVCL and MPPoKVVCL awarded Project Management Agency ( PMA) works at 
consultancy fees of 1.5 per cent and 0.60 per cent of the project cost respectively, which was 
much higher than the approved PMA cost of 0.50 per cent. As such, the cost to the tune of  
₹ 0.31 crore in sampled nine projects was borne by the MPMKVV CL and MPPoKVVCL. 
Further, MPPaKVVCL and MPPoKVVCL did not assign the work of monitoring and 
coordination of the bidding process of projects, project planning and implementation, MIS and 
updating of the web portal to PMAs in contradiction of the guidelines issued by the REC.  
It is recommended that the DISCOMs should plan the works based on detailed field surveys 
and should implement the Scheme as per the approved plan. The GoMP and the DISCOMs 
should establish an effective system for ensuring quality work execution and project 
monitoring in adherence to the Scheme guidelines. Also, delays in the execution of works 
should be avoided and the DISCOMs should levy the LD as per the terms of the contracts. 
SAUBHAGYA 
The DISCOMs were to formulate the project-wise DPRs based on the detailed field survey for 
sanction of projects from GoI and execute the works as per the approved DPRs. However, the 
DISCOMs prepared the DPRs without any field survey, at rates other than SOR . Resultantly, 
the 13 sampled projects were valued higher by ₹ 30.66 crore. The DPRs of all 50 projects were 
submitted with delay. 
Despite assurance from GoMP to provide for ‘own fund’ of 10 per cent  of the cost of the 
Scheme, it was observed that the MPPoKVVCL availed a loan for this and had to incur 
avoidable interest of ₹ 24.65 crore up to March 2022.

Audit also noticed deficiencies at the stage of award of work as 4,080 LoAs to the tune of  
₹ 50.62 crore for the electrification of 1,38,054 households without inviting bids and five LOAs 
were issued for the electrification of 860 households on the date of completion of work or 
thereafter. The work orders included the supply of material also, which was at a rate higher 
than the SOR. 
For providing new connections,  the audit noticed that MPPoKVVCL included additional 
items/items with higher specifications at a higher rate and incurred an expenditure of  
₹ 3,514.90/ ₹ 4,461.38 per connection against the ceiling of ₹ 3,000 per connection under the 
Scheme, thereby incurring  an extra expenditure of ₹ 11.45 crore in four projects. Besides,  
the DISCOMs procured materials of ₹ 27.60 crore above the requirement, which remained 
unutilised. 
To provide power connection to unelectrified HHs located in remote and inaccessible areas, a 
solar power pack with adequate capacity and battery bank were to be installed. Considering the 
solar radiation available in the State, solar power packs of 250 WP were to be provided whereas 
the DISCOMs installed 2,964 solar power packs of 200 WP only.  
The DISCOMs declared the completion of the Scheme at an early date to claim cash  
award (MPMKVVCL and MPPaKVVCL ₹ 100.50 c rore each) and an additional gra nt of  
₹ 110.54 crore, though as per the records, some works under the Scheme were carried out after 
the declared completion dates of the Scheme.  
The Project Management Agencies were to be engaged for management and timely completion 
of Projects. The MPMK VVCL and MPPoKVVCL  did not engage any PMA whereas 
MPPaKVVCL appointed PMA at a belated stage (25 February 2019) whereas the works were 
completed by 31 March 2019.  
There was a delay of more than 16 months in the submission of closure reports. In 11 projects, 
the DISCOMs provided connections to 3.30 lakh beneficiaries but lodged claims for 4.16 lakh 
beneficiaries thus raising excess claims of ₹ 24.06 crore. 
It is recommended that the DISCOMs should prepare DPRs only after the detailed field survey 
and based on the latest SOR to avoid huge variations during execution. They should avail funds 
from the State Government for a portion of ‘own funds’ instead of going for loans. The 
DISCOMs should adhere to the tendering process and procure the material as p er the field 
requirements and avoid lodging incorrect declarations on the achievement of electrification 
targets. The GoMP and DISCOMs should establish an effective system for ensuring quality 
work execution and project monitoring in adherence to the Scheme guidelines. 
With the implementation of these Schemes, the DISCOMs / State Government could achieve 
universal electrification in the State and the people at large benefitted. However, as it was 
noticed, there continue to be many deficiencies in the metering of households as well as DTRs 
in addition to other inefficiencies le ading to a substantial increase in provisional billing i.e. 
billing on an estimation basis. Hence, unless the DISCOMs manage to realise optimum revenue 
from all its consumers including the newly added consumers through these Schemes, the 
DISCOMs will not be able to control the ever-burgeoning AT&C losses.  

  
   
     
   
   
  
  
 
   
  
  
    
  
  
  
  
       
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

  
   
     
   
   
  
  
 
   
  
  
    
  
  
  
  
       
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

Chapter-I 
 
Introduction 
 
1.1 Rural Electrification 
Electricity is an essential requirement for all facets of our life and it has been recognized as a 
basic human need. It is the key to accelerating economic growth, employment  generation, 
poverty elimination and human development . Rural Electrification is viewed as the key to 
accelerating rural development.  Electricity is an essential infrastructure for agriculture, small 
and medium industries, khadi and village industries, cold chains, health care, education, 
information technology and so on. The Rural Electrification Policy of 2006 stipulated that the 
key development objective of the power sector is the supply of electricity to all areas including 
rural areas as mandated in Section 6 of the Electricity Act of 2003. Both the Central Government 
and State Governments are to jointly endeavour in achieving this objective at the earliest. The 
demand for electricity in rural areas is increasing day by day due to an incr ease in customer 
base, changes in lifestyle, and consumption patterns which require continual strengthening and 
augmentation of the distribution network. However, the poor financial health of the power 
distribution companies (DISCOMs) has resulted in under -investment in the distribution 
network leading to poor upkeep and maintenance of assets, particularly in rural areas. 
Therefore, strengthening and augmentation of sub -transmission & distribution infrastructure 
are also considered necessary to ensure reliable and quality power supply in rural areas.  
Accordingly, the Government of India (GoI) launched various Schemes for the electrification 
of villages from time to time. In  December 2014, GoI launched a Scheme namely Deendayal 
Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY) for strengthening rural infrastructure subsuming the 
erstwhile Rajiv Gandhi Grameen Vidyutikaran Yojana (RGGVY)  which had the objective of 
electrification of the villages having a population of 100 and above . Subsequently, in 2017,  
GoI launched ano ther Scheme namely Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana 
(SAUBHAGYA) for providing electricity connections to all rural households and urban poor 
households. 
The Performance Audit (PA) has been conducted to review the implementation of the DDUGJY 
and SAUBHAGYA in the State of Madhya Pradesh. The brief introduction of these Schemes 
consisting of the S cheme objectives, funding pattern, and the details of infrastructure created 
have been discussed in this chapter along with the Audit Objectives, Audit Criteria, and Audit 
Methodology including the sample selection details.  
1.2 Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana  
To address the problem of inadequate and unreliable power supply in rural areas and to 
strengthen the distribution network in rural areas, GoI in December 2014 launched the 
“Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana” (DDUGJY) Scheme for rural electrification. To 
achieve the objective, the Scheme was formulated to execute the following components:  
➢ Separation of agriculture and non -agriculture feeders facilitating judicious rostering of 
supply to agricultural & non-agricultural consumers in the rural areas; 
➢ Strengthening and augmentation of sub -transmission & distribution (ST&D ) 
infrastructure in rural areas, including metering at distribution transformers (DTRs), 
feeders, and consumer ends; and 
➢ Completing the outlay of rural electrification laid down under Rajiv Gandhi Grameen 
Vidyutikaran Yojana (RGGVY) for the 12th and 13th Plans by subsuming RGGVY in

DDUGJY. The RGGVY was formulated to cover the electrification of unelectrified 
villages with a population of 100 and above.  
The Ministry of Power (MoP ), GoI, appointed (December 2014) R EC Limited ( formerly

implementation of the Scheme.  
The REC formulated detailed guidelines for project formulation, execution, quality assurance, 
and monitoring to achieve the objective of the successful implementation of the Scheme. The 
Scheme was to be implemented by the DISCOMs in the State. 
1.3 Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana 
The GoI launched (October 2017) another Scheme namely Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har 
Ghar Yojana (SAUBHAGYA) to electrify all the remaining un-electrified households in rural 
areas as well as in urban areas with an objective of universal household electrification in the 
country on the premise that urban u n-electrified households were economically poor. To 
achieve the objective, the Scheme was formulated to execute the following components:  
➢ Providing last -mile connectivity and electricity connections to all un -electrified 
households in rural areas; 
➢ Providing Solar Photo Voltaic-based standalone system for un-electrified households in 
remote and inaccessible villages/households; and 
➢ Providing last mile connectivity and electricity connections  to all remaining  
economically poor un-electrified households in urban areas. Non-poor urban households 
were excluded from this Scheme. 
As per the Scheme guidelines, REC was the Nodal Agency for monitoring the project 
execution. This Scheme was to be implemented by the DISCOMs in the State.

1.7 Audit objectives 
The Performance Audit of the Schemes was conducted to assess whether: 
➢ The system for assessing the requirement of infrastructural works and formulation of 
DPRs was adequate; 
➢ The execution of the projects was economical, efficient and effective; 
➢ The mechanism for monitoring the execution and quality of executed works was 
adequate; and 
➢ The objectives of the Scheme were met efficiently and effectively. 
1.8 Audit Criteria 
Audit findings were benchmarked against the following criteria: 
➢ Scheme guidelines issued by MoP and REC; 
➢ Rural Electrification Policy 2006; 
➢ Tripartite agreement among REC, State Government and DISCOMs; 
➢ Sanctions orders of DPRs; 
➢ Instructions/circulars/orders issued by MoP and REC regarding the Schemes; 
➢ Approved DPRs; and 
➢ Contract Agreements 
1.9 Audit Scope and Methodology 
The Performance Audit was conducted in all three DISCOMs to analyse the implementation 
of the Scheme  in conformity with the intended objectives and stipulated provisions of the 
Scheme. The audit was conducted during the period from April 2022 to August 2022 and 
covered the entire implementation period of both the Schemes (i.e . 2014-15 to 2021 -22 for 
DDUGJY and 2017-18 to 2021-22 for SAUBHAGYA) including the project closure. 
To assess the implementation of the Schemes , the records of the Department of the State 
Government, the H eadquarters Office(s) of the DISCOMs and the recor ds of 13 sampled

Chapter-II 
 
Implementation of DDUGJY 
 
Summary 
To address the problem of inadequate and unreliable power supply and to strengthen the 
distribution network in rural areas, the Government of India (GoI) launched the Deendayal  
Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY) for rural electrification. The audit analysed the 
project formulation and its execution in conformity with the Scheme Guidelines and noticed the 
deficiencies in the formulation of Detailed Project Reports (DPRs), award as well as execution of 
the works, financial compliances, monitoring, and closure of the Scheme. Significant issues 
highlighted in the chapter are given below: 
➢ DISCOMs had prepared DPRs without any field survey. As a result, huge variations 
ranging between (-100) to 4,368 per cent were observed in work execution as against the 
sanctioned quantity of DPRs. 
➢ There were delays in the award of the projects ranging from 10 months to 18 months, and 
in the completion of projects ranging from two months to 22 months. 
➢ While executing the works, Scheme provisions/ LoA were not adhered to. Consequently, 
extra expenditure to the tune of ₹ 0.63 crore was incurred by the DISCOMs. 
➢ DISCOMs did not frame any strategy to achieve the milestones . As  a result, the 
DISCOMs failed to avail additional grant of ₹ 102.96 crore.  
Based on the audit findings discussed in this chapter, a summary of the conclusion is given 
below: 
➢ DPRs were not prepared based on a detailed field survey. 
➢ The electrification of villages was not in conformity with the DPRs. 
➢ Project implementation was beset with delays in the award of contracts and completion of 
works. 
➢ Works were not executed in compliance with the provisions of the Scheme guidelines 
and LoA. 
➢ Milestones, as envisaged under the Scheme, were not achieved by the DISCOMs.

Summary 
The Government of India (GoI) launched Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana 
(SAUBHAGYA) to ensure last-mile connectivity and electricity connections to all un-electrified 
Households (HHs) in rural areas and all un -electrified poor HHs in urban areas to achieve the 
objective of universal household connections. Audit analysed the project formulation and its 
execution in conformity with the guidelines of the Scheme and noticed the deficiencie s in the 
formulation of Detailed Project Reports (DPRs), award as well as execution of the work, 
financial compliances, monitoring and closure of the Scheme. S ignificant issues highlighted in 
the chapter are given below: 
➢ DISCOMs had prepared DPRs without a ny field survey. Consequently, 5,09,053 
households were electrified as against the 9,77,056 household electrification envisaged 
under the DPRs. 
➢ The DISCOMs did not adhere to the provisions of the Scheme/ LoA during 
implementation. Resultantly, extra expend iture to the tune of ₹ 42.74 crore  was 
incurred by the DISCOMs. 
➢ DISCOMs awarded 4,080 orders to the tune of ₹ 50.62 crore for the electrification of 
1,38,054 households without inviting bids through tender in contravention of the 
guidelines. 
➢ MPPoKVVCL had availed loan in excess of ₹ 98.93 crore in contradiction of the Scheme 
guidelines and had to incur an avoidable interest payment of ₹ 24.65 crore.  
➢ DISCOMs incorrectly declared an early completion of 100 per cent household 
electrification under the Scheme before the REC leading to the receipt of the ineligible 
cash award and an additional grant of ₹ 250.53 crore. 
Based on the audit finding discussed in this chapter, a summary of the conclusion is given 
below: 
➢ Detailed project reports were not prepared based on a detailed field survey, which led to 
large number of variations in executed quantity. 
➢ Households were electrified without due tendering process in contravention of the 
Scheme guidelines. 
➢ The Electrification of the villages was executed without identifying the households for 
releasing the connections, in contradiction with the provisions of the Scheme guidelines. 
➢ The guidelines for arranging the fund from its own source were not adhered to by 
MPPoKVVCL, which led to the avoidable burden of the interest. 
➢ The DISCOMs received a cash award and additional grant by an incorrect claim of early 
completion of the Scheme.
