This Report is prepared for submission to the Governor of the State of Bihar

under Article 151 of the Constitution of India.

The audit of revenue receipts of the State Government is conducted under
Section 16 of the Comptroller and Auditor General's (Duties, Powers and
Conditions of Service) Act, 1971. This Report presents the results of audit of
receipts comprising commercial taxes, state excise, taxes on vehicles, land
revenue, stamp duty and registration fees and other non-tax receipts of the

Government of Bihar.

The cases mentioned in the Report are among those which came to notice in
the course of test audit of accounts during the year 2013-14 as well as those
which had come to notice in earlier years but could not be reported in previous
Audit Reports; matters relating to the period subsequent to 2013-14 have also

been included, wherever necessary.

Audit has been conducted in conformity with the Auditing Standards issued by

the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.


OVERVIEW



This Report contains 44 paragraphs including one performance audit relating
to non/short levy of tax, interest etc. involving हैं 1,403.80 crore. Some of the
major findings are mentioned below:

I. General

Total receipts of the Government of Bihar for the year 2013-14 were
% 68,918.65 crore. The revenue raised by the State Government amounted to
% 21,505.51 crore comprising tax revenue of हैं 19,960.68 crore and non-tax
revenue of & 1,544.83 crore. The receipts from the Government of India were
% 47,413.14 crore (States’ share of divisible Union taxes: ~ 34,829.11 crore
and grants-in-aid: ~ 12,584.03 crore). Thus, the State Government’s own
contribution was only 31 per cent of total revenue.

(Paragraph 1.1.1)

The number of inspection reports (IRs) and paragraphs issued up to December
2013 but not settled by June 2014 stood at 4,806 and 27,764 respectively
involving ~ 17,825.55 crore. We are yet to receive even first replies for 1,773
IRs though these were required to be furnished within one month from the
date of issue.

(Paragraph 1.6)

We conducted test-check of the records of commercial taxes, State excise,
taxes on vehicles, land revenue, non-ferrous mining and metallurgical
industries and other departmental offices during the year 2013-14 and
observed underassessment/short levy/loss of revenue of % 2,310.97 crore in
3,357 cases. During the period April 2013 to August 2014, the concerned
departments accepted underassessment and other deficiencies of ¥ 785.06
crore which were pointed out in audit during 2013-14.

(Paragraph 1.9)

2.3.1 Introduction

The levy and collection of entry tax is governed by the “Bihar Tax on Entry of
Goods into Local Areas for Consumption, Use or Sale therein (BTEG) Act,
1993” and Rules made/notification issued thereunder. Entry tax is levied on
entry of scheduled goods into a local area? for consumption, use or sale therein
for the purpose of development of trade, commerce and industry in the State,
at such rate, not exceeding twenty per cent, of the import value of goods above
a specified quantum, as may be specified by the State Government. The
Commercial Taxes Department of the Government of Bihar has been assigned
with the levy and collection of entry tax in Bihar.

It is also prescribed under the BTEG Act that where an importer of scheduled
goods liable to pay tax under the Act incurs tax liability, at the rate specified
under Section 14 of the Bihar Value Added Tax (BVAT) Act, 2005, by virtue
of sale of imported Scheduled goods or sale of goods manufactured by
consuming such imported Scheduled goods, his tax liability under the BVAT
Act, 2005 shall stand reduced to the extent of tax paid under the BTEG Act.

2.3.2 Audit Objectives
The Performance Audit was conducted to ascertain whether:

. the notifications, circulars and instructions for entry tax administration
were issued in prescribed manner and under proper authority;

. the provisions are in place and sufficient to keep effective check on
assessment, levy and collection of entry tax;

. Rules and procedures laid down are being duly observed to levy and
collection of entry tax in the State and the VAT liability is being
reduced in the manner prescribed under the BTEG Act/Rules;

. the proceeds of the levy under the Act had been appropriated to the
earmarked fund and utilised exclusively for the intended purposes and

. an adequate internal control and monitoring mechanism existed to
ensure proper realisation of entry tax.

2.3.7 Irregularities in issuing notifications at the Department level

2.3.7.1 Deviation from the Cabinet decision

Rule 18 (1) of the Rules of Executive Business, provides that when it has been
decided to bring a case before the Council of Ministers, the Department to
which the case belongs shall, prepare a memorandum, indicating with
sufficient precision the salient facts of the case and the points for decision
stating the main arguments for and against the particular course advocated,
without over-stressing the advantages or under-stressing the disadvantages to
suit the view finally recommended.

We observed during examination of file relating to a notification (S.O. 215
dated 27 November 2012) that there was proposal to increase the rate of entry
tax of all those scheduled goods whose entry tax rate was four per cent and
VAT rate was five per cent (Para no. 5.0) in the memorandum sent to the
Council of Ministers. There were 11 such commodities/group of commodities
in the schedule whose entry tax rate was four per cent and VAT rate was five
per cent. But the draft notification as well as table-I mentioned in the
memorandum had the mention of only nine commodities/group of
commodities excluding entry no. 8 “Paper of all kinds excluding newsprint but
including ...” and entry no. 26 “Petroleum coke and Bitumen” of the schedule.
No reason was found recorded on the file for omission of these two
commodities despite having fulfilled the criteria (VAT rate-five per cent and
Entry tax rate-four per cent) adopted to increase the rate of entry tax of other
nine goods. The same memorandum got approval of the Council of Ministers

and despite their approval entry tax rate of aforesaid two commodities could
not be increased. This indicates lack of adequate controls as well as thorough
preparedness by the Department while issuing the notification, which involved

entry tax revenue collected on “paper” during December 2012 to March 2014.
The revenue impact on account of Petroleum coke and Bitumen could not be
worked out due to non-availability of revenue figures of these commodities.

2.3.8 System deficiencies

2.3.8.1 Absence of provision for levy of interest under the BTEG Act

Section 8 of the BTEG Act provides that subject to other provisions of this Act
and the Rules framed thereunder the authority empowered to assess, reassess,
collect and enforce payment of tax and penalty payable by a dealer under the
Bihar Finance Act, 1981 (Bihar Act 5, 1981) shall assess, reassess, collect and
enforce payment of tax and penalty payable under this Act and for this
purpose they may exercise all or any of the powers assigned to them under the
said Act and Rules made thereunder for the time being in force including the
provisions relating to returns, assessment, reassessment, escaped assessment,
recovery of tax, special mode of recovery, maintenance of accounts,
inspection, search and seizure liability in representative character, refund,
appeal, revision and reviews, statement of cases to the High Court,
compounding of offences and other miscellaneous matter and the provisions of
the said Act shall mutatis mutandis apply accordingly.

We observed that the assessing authorities draw their power from the
provisions of Bihar Finance Act, 1981 while disposing off the cases of entry
tax as the BTEG Act had no separate set of laws to deal with the assessment,
re-assessment etc. Though the Bihar Finance Act, 1981 was repealed with
effect from 1 April 2005 and replaced with the BVAT Act, 2005, but the
above provision remained unamended. Further, many new features, such as
levy of interest, fine, scrutiny of returns and tax audit etc. came into being
after the introduction of BVAT Act, but these were not introduced under the
Section 8 of the BTEG Act. In the absence of provision for interest, it cannot
be imposed in the cases of non/delayed payment of entry tax.

Further, the Hon’ble Patna High court in the matter of Shree Shankar Ice and
Cold Storage vrs. the State of Bihar and others (CWJC no. 19512/2010) also
held that interest for non-payment of entry tax cannot be imposed as there is
no substantial provision under the Entry tax Act for passing such interest.
Therefore, in the absence of provision for interest there is no deterrent for the
dealers for payment of entry tax, though during pre-VAT period (period prior
to 1 April 2005) the deterrent measure for payment of entry tax was in
existence as there was a provision of levy of penalty for such default under the
erstwhile Bihar Finance Act, 1981 and the provision of Section 8 of the BTEG
Act was sufficient for levy of such penalty. But BVAT Act provides for levy
of interest for non-payment of tax instead of penalty and accordingly the
provision of BTEG Act was not amended to include interest in Section 8 of the
said Act. Therefore, due to absence of provision for interest in the BTEG Act,
there was no deterrent measure for the default of non/delayed payment of
entry tax.

After this was pointed out, the Government replied in the exit conference held
in July 2014 that the remedial measures were being considered.

The Government may consider bringing out suitable amendments to
make the new features relating to interest, fine, scrutiny and tax audit etc.
of the BVAT Act, applicable under the BTEG Act.

2.3.24 Internal controls

Internal controls are intended to provide reasonable assurance of proper
enforcement of Act, Rules and departmental instructions. Effective internal
control system both in the manual as well as computerised environments is a
pre-requisite for the efficient functioning of any department. The following
deficiencies were noticed in the internal control mechanism.

2.3.24.1 Absence of register of returns

We observed that there is no provision for any such register to keep a watch
on the filing of returns by the dealers for monitoring and subsequent action in
time. As a result large number of dealers were not filing returns as given in the
Para no. 2.3.12 of this Report.

2.3.24.2 DCB register

We observed that there is no provision for maintenance of Demand, Collection
and Balances (DCB) register under the BTEG Acct. In the absence of such a
control tool, the monitoring and verification of demand raised by the AAs
after the assessments made by them under various provisions, collection of
arrears of revenue and the balance to be deposited becomes very difficult.

2.3.24.3, Absence of disposal register

We observed that there is no provision for maintenance of Disposal register
under the BTEG Act/Rules. In the absence of such register, there is no
prescribed mechanism to track the cases which are assessed by the AAs for
better internal controls and monitoring.

After this was pointed out, the Government stated in the exit conference held
in July 2014 that circulars/instructions would be issued for maintenance of
these registers under entry tax.

The internal audit wing of any department is a vital component of its internal
control mechanism and enables the department to ensure itself that the
prescribed systems are functioning appropriately. Section 26 of the BVAT Act
provides for detailed audit of a dealer selected on the basis of a selection
model in the prescribed manner by the Audit wing of the department.

2.3.25 Conclusions

The control system in the Department was lacking as the provision of
Acts/Rules/Statutes etc. were not followed in issuance of notifications for tax-
administration. Due to non-amendment of the provisions of the BTEG Act,
after the introduction of BVAT Act, many new features available under the
BVAT Act was not applicable under the BTEG Act. The proceeds of entry tax
accumulated in Bihar Trade Development Fund created for development of
infrastructure was not exclusively utilised for the intended purposes. There
was lack of compliance to the provision of the BTEG Act/Rules leading to
large scale underassessment and short levy of entry tax. Large numbers of
eligible dealers were not registered under the BTEG Act and assessment of
dealers under the Act was very low. There was absence of full-proof
mechanism in out to out swvidha generation and utilisation leading to leakage
of revenue. Absence of provisions for scrutiny, regular assessment and tax
audit was indicative of the fact that the Department has not instituted proper
control checks to safeguard its revenue.

1999) clarifies that many a time the quantity to be ordered is much more than

such a manner that the purchase is done in a fair, transparent and equitable
manner.

We scrutinised the conditions of NIT for supply of country liquor in PET
bottles and observed that condition no. 2 (ix) of the NIT provided that the
entire state was divided into 17 supply zones. Further, condition no. 3 of the
NIT stipulated that the lowest tenderer (L-1) would be allotted one zone of his
first preference and thereafter remaining tenderers would be allotted one
supply zone each of their preference on the basis of their quoted rate and this
process would continue till all the supply zones were allotted. Condition no. 3
further provided that each tenderer was required to deposit differential amount
of base rate (¥ 5.78 and हैं 9.76 for 200ml and 400 ml pack size respectively)
and tendered rate. The provision of deposit of differential amount of base rate
and quoted rate tantamounts to allowing supply of country liquor at different
rates in different zones.

Condition of allotment of 17 supply zones to 17 different tenderers at different
rates was against the provision of the Bihar Finance (Amendment) Rules, 2005
and CVC instruction because (i) capacity of L-1 was not exhausted before
offering supplies to next higher responsive bidder, (ii) there was no provision
that other suppliers would have to supply liquor at the rate quoted by L-1. It is
also noteworthy that it was a departure from the established procedure adopted
in previous tenders for the periods 2005-08, 2009-12 and 2012-14 in which the
Department had fixed uniform rate for supply of liquor in the entire State.

The Department selected (February 2014) 17 tenderers for supply of liquor at
different rates in 17 supply zones and the tenderers were required to deposit
the differential amount. Thus, due to allowing payment of differential amount
on the basis of their tendered rate instead of rate quoted by L-1 the Department
would be deprived of हैं 341.32 crore during 2014-15 to 2018-19 (₹ 68.26
crore per annum) calculated on the basis of Minimum Guaranteed Quantity
(MGQ) of country liquor for the year 2013-14 as detailed in Annexure- XV.

penalty or both has not been paid in accordance with the provisions of the Act,
an officer not below the rank of Inspector of Motor Vehicles or any other
officer specially authorised by the State Transport Commissioner, may seize

Mining of minerals is governed by the Bihar Minor Mineral Concession Rules
(BMMC Rules), 1972 and Mineral Concession Rules (MC Rules), 1960
framed by the State Government under the Mines and Minerals (Regulation
and Development) Act (MMRD Act), 1957. The regulation and development

with the Commissioner-cum- Principal Secretary as its head at the
Government level. The Director of Mines is the head of the Department and is
assisted by one Additional Director of Mines and three Deputy Director of
Mines (DDMs) at headquarters level. Further there are nine Deputy Director
of Mines at Divisional offices and at the district level, 14 district mining
offices are headed by Assistant Director of Mines/Mining Development
Officers independently where as Mining Inspectors (MIs) are the in-charge of
the remaining 24 district mining offices who are under the control of the
Collector of the respective districts and are responsible for assessment, levy
and collection of royalty and other mining dues.

Bihar State has minor minerals like sand, stone and earth and a few major
minerals like Limestone, Mica, and Silica etc. Receipts from mines and
minerals in Bihar comprise royalty, dead rent, surface rent, application fee for
lease/permit/prospecting licence, pre-survey licence, penalty, fine and interest
for delayed/belated payment of dues etc.
