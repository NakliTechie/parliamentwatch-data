EXECUTIVE SUMMARY


SUMMA:

Why we did this Audit?

“Health is wealth’ is an adage of far reaching significance in a welfare State.
Delivery of public health and medical services is one of the major functions of
the State Government, with significant support from the Central Government.
The system for health care delivery in the State is organised with three layers;
PHCs providing the primary health care with greater focus of preventive care,
Community Health Centres providing secondary health care or the first level
referral services and the Government General Hospitals and Medical College
Hospitals at the top of the pyramid, providing the tertiary care or second level

referral services.

Considering the importance of infrastructure and management of health services

in ensuring a healthy nation and the priority accorded to health by both the

Central and State Governments, CAG took up this Performance Audit covering
the activities during 2016-22. The objectives of this Audit were to assess the:
(1) availability of necessary human resource at all levels, (2) adequacy of
provision of health care services (3) availability of drugs, medicines and
equipment, (4) availability and management of health care infrastructure,
(5) adequacy of the funding for the health care sector, (6) effectiveness of
implementation of centrally sponsored schemes, (7) adequacy and effectiveness
of the regulatory mechanisms for ensuring quality health care services and

(8) achievements against SDG-3 targets.

What we found?

Sanctioned strength of doctors, nurses and para-medical staff in tertiary care
institutions was inadequate with respect to the IPHS norms. The shortfall in
para-medical staff was 26 per cent as at the end of March 2022. Vacancies in
the cadre of nurses and paramedical staff at all test-checked HCFs were noticed.
The availability of doctors and nurses in GH, Puducherry was more than the
requirement and there were shortage of doctors and nurses in other hospitals

indicating uneven manpower distribution across the HCFs.

xv


PA on Public Health Infrastructure and Management of Health Services

The UT Government did not prepare any Health Policy to ensure that the
challenges relating to health sector are adequately addressed so that objective
of providing quality and improved healthcare services is attained. The average
number of patients attended to by a doctor in PHCs were higher leading to lesser
consultation time. Some of the essential services like Accident and Trauma
Ward, Burn ward, Dialysis, full range of Radiology and Pathology services were

not available in test-checked HCFs.

The savings under procurement of drugs during 2020-21 and 2021-22 were
49 and 38 per cent respectively, which had led to non- procurement of essential
drugs in the range of 13 to 54 per cent as per the essential drugs list. Equipment
for physiotherapy and occupational therapy were lying idle. Non-availability
of laboratory equipment affected the patient care offered by the hospitals.
Mammography unit at Women and Children Hospital, Puducherry was not
functioning since December 2020.

Primary and Secondary health care suffered for want of adequate number of
PHCs/CHCs. As against the requirement of 53 PHCs and 14 CHCs, there were
only 44 PHCs and 4 CHCs respectively. The sub-optimal working conditions of
HCFs was a greater cause for concern. PHCs were functioning in dilapidated
buildings and there was insufficient space in HCFs, lack of residential
accommodation, non-provision of lift/ramp, inadequate fire safety measures,

etc.

The expenditure on health care in the Union Territory of Puducherry fell short
of the allocation by 2 to 16 per cent during 2016-22. Except during 2019-20, the
capital expenditure constituted just 0.82 per cent to 2.96 per cent of total
expenditure on healthcare of the UT and the savings ranged between 6 and
92 per cent during the same period indicating that lesser priority was given for

healthcare infrastructure creation.

The institutional deliveries were more than 25,000 per annum during the period
2016-17 to 2021-22, but the coverage of beneficiaries under “Janani Shishu
Suraksha Karyakram” was less than 10 per cent, which indicates that the scheme
was not implemented effectively. There was a shortfall of 37 per cent in
coverage of beneficiaries under AB-PMJAY. There were shortfalls in the
provision of benefits to the women beneficiaries under ‘Janani Suraksha

Yojana’ and ‘Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram’ despite availability of funds.

CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION



1.1 __ Introduction

The Union Territory of Puducherry comprises of four geographically isolated
regions (viz., Puducherry, Karaikal, Mahe and Yanam) with a total population
of 12.48 lakh as per 2011 Census. The Department of Health and Family
Welfare Services functions with the objective of providing preventive and
curative healthcare to the public.

As per the Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS), 2012 guidelines, the public
healthcare delivery system is provided at the UT through a three tier health
care system viz., primary, secondary and tertiary. The primary healthcare
facilities are the first level of contact between the public and healthcare
providers. The primary healthcare is delivered through a network of Sub-
Centres (SCs) and Primary Health Centres (PHCs). The secondary healthcare
is provided by the Community Health Centres (CHCs), which function as first
referral centres. Tertiary healthcare facilities are provided by the District
Hospitals/General Hospitals, Women and Children Hospital, Medical College
Hospitals and other speciality hospitals. Healthcare is provided in UT of
Puducherry under both Allopathic and Indian System of Medicine. To achieve
the objectives of providing quality and improved healthcare services in the UT
of Puducherry, a comprehensive health policy was required to be framed in
line with the National Health Policy, which the Government of Puducherry
(GoP) has not yet prepared.

The UT of Puducherry has adopted IPHS norms, which has also been adopted
by Audit as the benchmark for assessing healthcare facilities in the hospitals.
While the IPHS guidelines stipulate the essential and desirable services that
are to be provided by the District Hospitals (DHs) and General Hospitals
(GHs), the Medical Council of India (MCI) stipulates the services that are to
be provided by the Teaching and Autonomous Hospitals.

Conclusion

The health workforce includes clinical staff such as physicians, nurses and
paramedical staff such as pharmacists, lab technicians, X-Ray technicians, etc.,
and other management and supporting staff. Sanctioned strength of doctors,
nurses and paramedical staff in tertiary care institutions was inadequate with
respect to the IPHS norms. There were vacancies in the cadre of nurses and
paramedical staff at all test-checked HCFs including ISMH hospitals. The
shortfall in paramedical staff ranged between 35 per cent and 63 per cent as at
the end of March 2022. The availability of doctors and nurses in GH,
Puducherry was more than the requirement and there were shortage of doctors
and nurses in other hospitals indicating uneven manpower distribution across
the HCFs.

2.4 Recommendations

. The UT Government should ensure that the Directorates periodically
assess the vacancy position of different categories of staff and prepare
an Annual Recruitment Calendar for recruitment of different categories

of manpower.

. The UT Government should also review the adequacy of the sanctioned
strength and should revise it in line with the IPHS norms and also
re-distribute the manpower to the needy hospitals.

delivery.

3.14 Conclusion

The UT Government did not prepare any Health Policy to ensure that the
challenges relating to health sector are adequately addressed so that objective
of providing quality and improved healthcare services is attained. The average
number of patients attended to by a doctor in PHCs were higher leading to lesser
consultation time. Some of the essential services like Accident and Trauma
Ward, Burn ward, Dialysis, full range of Radiology and Pathology services were
not available in test-checked HCFs. Patients, doctors, hospital workers were not
provided with all types of linen as prescribed in IPHS norms which may lead to
infection. The GHs, Yanam and Mahe were lagging behind in the delivery of
most of the health care services. As the time of arrival of Ambulance driver at
the scene was not recorded by the call centre coupled with non-availability of the
patients at the scene, the possibility of patients making their own arrangements to
reach the hospitals could not be ruled out.

Recommendations

e The UT Government should ensure that all essential services as per
norms are provided in HCFs through availability of required

infrastructure.

e The UT Government should ensure procurement of required radiology
equipment and availability of proper pathology services at all HCFs to
improve the level of diagnostic services as per IPHS norms for providing
evidence based treatment.

e The UT Government should study the feasibility of implementing a fully
automated GPS enabled monitoring system for ambulance service or to
explore the possibility of outsourcing the ambulance services.

August 2010 for the registration and regulation of clinical establishments in the
country and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. Under this
Act, Puducherry Clinical Establishments (CE) (Registration and Regulation)
Rules, 2014 was framed, which came into effect from 05 March 2014 and is
applicable to all the CEs in the UT of Puducherry. As per the Act, no person
shall carry on with a clinical establishment unless it is duly registered under the
Act and Rules and a certificate of registration has to be granted by the competent
authority', which is valid for one year and to be renewed every year.

8.1.1 Non-registration of clinical establishments in the UT

of provisional registration thirty days before the expiry of the validity of the
certificate of provisional registration.

8.4.1 Licences/Registration not obtained for Radiation
Equipment

As per rule 7(e) of Atomic Energy (Radiation Protection) Rules, 2004, in order
to obtain licence for operation of a radiation installation, appropriate dosimetry
devices are to be available for radiation surveillance. Thermoluminescent
dosimeters (TLD) are used for monitoring radiation doses in health workers.

It was noticed in audit that in none of the hospitals, the pocket dosimeters were
available whereas, TLD badges were available only in GH, Puducherry and
WCH, Puducherry. In the absence of dosimeters, the radiation level in health
workers handling radiation equipment such as CT Scan and high radiation
X-ray equipment went unnoticed putting the health of the workers at risk.

Rule 3(2) of Atomic Energy (Radiation Protection) Rules, 2004 provides that
no person shall handle any radioactive material or operate any radiation
generating equipment except in accordance with the terms and conditions of a
licence. A licence shall be obtained from Atomic Energy Regulatory Board
(AERB) for the operation of Computed Tomography (CT) Scan unit and
medical diagnostic X-Ray equipment. Audit noticed that the four test-checked
District Hospitals had not obtained licence in respect of two CT scan units and
12 X-Ray units as given in Appendix 8.1.

Thus, radiation safety of patients, occupational workers and members of public
could be compromised if the radiation equipment do not undergo periodical tests
and are not issued with registration certificate/licence by AERB.

Government stated (November 2023) that it would speed up the necessary
process in getting the registration done and obtaining the licenses.

Bio-Medical

Bio-medical waste is generated during procedures related to diagnosis,
treatment and immunisation in the hospitals and its management is an integral
part of infection control within the hospital premises. Bio-Medical Waste
Management Rules, 2016 (BWM Rules) inter alia stipulate the procedures for
collection, handling, transportation, disposal and monitoring of the Bio-medical
waste with clear roles for waste generators and Common Bio-Medical Waste
Treatment Facility (CBMWTF). CBMWTFs are required to function in
compliance with the standards notified under BWM Rules and the guidelines
issued by Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).

The availability of authorisation for handling of bio-medical waste, registration

8.6.1 Bio-medical waste management

The Puducherry Pollution Control Committee (PPCC) is the prescribed
authority to ensure implementation of BWM Rules. Pondicherry Solid Waste
Management Company Pvt Ltd., (PSWMPL) is the only CBMWTF authorised
by PPCC to collect, handle, transport and dispose the Bio-medical waste in
Puducherry and Karaikal regions. Audit noticed the following violations of the
BWM Rules in the succeeding paragraphs.

8.6.1.1 Unsafe storage of untreated Bio-medical waste

Rule 5(a) of BWM Rules stipulates that it shall be the duty of every CBMWTF
to ensure that the Bio-medical waste collected from the occupier’ is transported,
handled, stored, treated and disposed off without any adverse effect to the
human health and the environment.

Audit observed that PPCC in their inspection reports had pointed out that the
PSWMPL accumulated all type of Bio-medical waste in yellow, red and blue
coloured bags, in a haphazard manner throughout their facility with obnoxious
odour, in violation of the rules due to non-working of autoclave machine’.
During joint inspection, Audit confirmed that the CBMWTF continued to
violate the BWM Rules, with the bags containing the Bio-medical waste strewn
all over the facility in open space and separate covered area was not provided
for storage of the untreated waste and they were open to sunlight/rainfall.

8.6.2.1 Non-installation of Effluent Treatment Plant

Effluent Treatment Plant is used for waste water treatment to release safe water

8.7 Conclusion

The Puducherry Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Rules,
2014 was not being enforced effectively and there was no monitoring
mechanism available in the Department to ensure timely registration and
renewal of clinical establishments. None of the hospitals in the UT had applied
for accreditation by NABH. Records relating to radiation exposure of patients
and staff were not maintained. Bio-medical wastes were handled/disposed off
in violation of rules.

8.8 Recommendations

. The UT Government should ensure that all clinical establishments (both
public and private) in the UT are registered under the Puducherry
Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Rules, 2014 and
the DHFWS deploy adequate manpower to effectively enforce the
Rules.

. The UT Government should ensure that NABH accreditation/NQAS
certification is obtained to improve the credibility of hospitals.

The UT Government should ensure enforcement of Bio-Medical Waste
Management Rules, 2016 for avoiding its serious implication on
environment and human health.
