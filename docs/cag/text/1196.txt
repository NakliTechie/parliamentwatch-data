PREFAC

This Report is prepared for submission to the Lieutenant Governor of the
National Capital Territory of Delhi under Article 151 of the Constitution of
India. The Report is in two parts:

Part A presents the results of audit of receipts such as sales/value added tax,
state excise, taxes on motor vehicles, stamp duty and registration fees and
other tax and non-tax receipts of the Government of NCT of Delhi for the
financial year ended March 2012.

Part B presents the introduction on Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)
(Social and Economic Sector), Performance Audit and findings arising from
compliance audit of PSUs of the Government of NCT of Delhi for the same
year.

The Report containing audit observations on matters arising from examination
of Finance Accounts and Appropriation Accounts is presented separately.

The cases mentioned in this Report are among those which came to notice in
the course of test audit of accounts during the year 2011-12 as well as those
which had come to notice in earlier years but could not be dealt with in
previous reports; matters relating to the period subsequent to 2011-12 have
also been included wherever necessary.

The audit has been conducted in conformity with the Auditing Standards
issued by the Comptroller and Auditor General of India

Failure of senior officials to enforce accountability and
protection of the interest of the State Government

‘The Principal Accountant General (Audit), Delhi, (Pr.AG) conducts periodical
inspection of the Government departments to test check the transactions and
to verify the maintenance of the important accounts and other records as
prescribed in the rules and procedures. These inspections are followed up with
the issue of inspection reports (IRs) incorporating irregularities detected
during the inspection but not settled on the spot, to the Heads of the Offices
inspected with copies to the next higher authorities for taking prompt
corrective action. The Heads of Offices/Government are required to promptly
comply with the observations contained in the IRs, rectify the defects and
omissions and report compliance through initial reply to the Pr.AG within one
month from the date of issue of the IRs. Serious financial irregularities are
reported to the Head of Departments and the Government.

2.1 Tax administration

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a tax on value addition. It is a multi-point tax, which is
levied at every stage of sale. It is collected at the stage of manufacture/resale and
contemplates rebating of the tax paid on inputs and purchases, thereby providing
revenue to the Government on value addition at every stage.

The receipts from VAT are administered by the Commissioner of the Department of
Trade and Taxes (DTT) assisted by four Special Commissioners. There are ten zones
each headed by the Joint Commissioners/Deputy Commissioners who work under the
Additional Commissioners and supervise the work of the Value Added Tax Officers
(VATOs) and the Assistant Value Added Tax Officers (AVATOs) and the Inspectors
working in the wards under their contro]. Delhi has been divided into 108 wards
headed by the VATOs.

2.14 Application of

Section 3 (2) of the DVAT Act, 2004 specifies that every dealer shall be liable to pay
tax at the rate mentioned in schedules of Section 4 of the Act on every sale of goods
effected by him. Section 86(12) of the Act, further provides that if any person
furnishes incorrect returns, the Commissioner may assess or reassess the amount of tax
due for a tax period. Short payment of tax attracts penalty at the rate of one per cent
of tax deficiency per week or € 100 per week for the period of default, whichever is
higher and interest computed thereon on daily basis @ 15907 cent per annum.

Audit noticed during the test check of the records of four wards’ of DIT between
April 2011 and March 2012 that in four cases relating to the assessment year 2009-10,
the dealers paid tax on sale valued at € 13.07 crore at rates lower than those prescribed
in the Act. However, the assessing authority did not scrutinize the returns of the
dealers to ascertain the correctness of rate at which tax was required to be paid. This
resulted in short payment of tax of € 73.20 lakh. Besides, interest of F 21.44 lakh and
penalty off 74.67 lakh were also leviable.

Audit reported the matter to the Department and the Government in August 2012, but
has not received any reply.

The Delhi Entertainment & Betting Tax (DEBT) Act, 1996, provides for the
levy of entertainment, totalisator and betting tax in the NCT of Delhi.
Entertainment tax is leviable on admission to cinema shows, entertainment
programme i.e. exhibition, performance, amusement, cinematograph
exhibition etc. and for cable and direct-to-home (DTH) services. Totalisator
and betting tax are levied on bets and stakes placed within the NCT of Delhi
on horse races.

The Act, read with the rules made thereunder provides for submission of
prescribed forms and for imposition of interest and penalties for non-
compliance with provisions. It also provides for recovery of tax as arrears of
land revenue.

The Delhi Tax on Luxuries (DTL) Act, 1996, and Rules made thereunder
provides for levy of luxury tax on the turnover of receipts of an hotelier at a
rate not exceeding 15 per cent to be notified by the Government from time to
time. Where the charges are levied otherwise than on daily basis or per room,
then the charges for determining the tax liability shall be computed
proportionately for a day and per room based on the total period of occupation
of the accommodation for which the charges are made. Luxury tax is not
leviable in respect of turnover of receipts for supply of food and drinks on the
sale of which the hotelier is liable to pay Value Added Tax (VAT). The Act,
1640 with the rules also provides a time frame for payment of the tax dues and
for imposition of fines for contravention of the rules.

3.6.2 Organisational set-up

The Department is headed by the Commissioner of Entertainment, Betting and
Luxury Tax who is assisted by one Deputy Commissioner (Taxes). There are

3.6.8 Non-maintenance of basic records

Rules 14, 26, 26A and 50 of the DEBT Rules envisage the submission of
returns in support of payment of entertainment tax, cable tax, DTH services
and betting tax. Similarly, Rule 16 of DTL Rules provides for the submission
of return by the hoteliers. In case the tax is not paid according to return, the
concerned Luxury Tax Officer may, by notice, require him to pay the amount

due from him according to the return but remaining unpaid, by a date
specified in the notice issued under Rule 18 of the DTL Rules. Section 15 of
the DEBT Act lays down where the assessing authority is satisfied that the
proprietor of an entertainment has failed to submit true and full returns or has
fraudulently evaded tax, it should assess the amount of tax due after giving
reasonable opportunity of being heard to the concerned proprietor.

To ensure the compliance of provisions of the Act and Rules, proper handling
and scrutiny of returns and challans submitted in support of payment of tax is
essential. During Audit, returns and challans were found dumped in large
bags instead of proper filing in relevant files. It is indicative of the fact that
these returns were not scrutinised to ensure the correctness of tax due and paid
while tax paid by the assessee was simply treated as tax due. Non-
maintenance of records indicates lack of internal control in the Department.

The Government stated (February 2013) that in view of work load and
shortage of staff, records could not be maintained. However, the Department
had issued instructions to the staff concerned to keep returns and challans upto
date in concerned files.

An appraisal of the assessment, levy and collection of entertainment tax

on the amount so due at one and half percent per month from the date
immediately following the last date for payment of tax for a period of one
month and at the rate of two percent per month thereafter so long as he
continues to default in making such payment.

An Audit appraisal of 304 cable operators out of 688 registered in three

These 175 cable operators defaulted on 2335 occasions in making timely
payment of tax during three years covered under audit, whereas tax for 2127
months was not paid at all.

The Government stated (February 2013) that efforts are being made to recover

6.2 Audit mandate

Audit of Government Companies is governed by Section 619 of the
Companies Act, 1956. According to Section 617, a Government Company is
one in which, not less than 51 per cent of the paid up capital is held by
Government(s). A Government Company includes a subsidiary of the
Government Company as well. Further, a company in which not less than 51
per cent of the paid up capital is held in any combination by Government(s),
Government Companies and Corporations controlled by Government(s) is
treated as if it were a Government Company (deemed Government Company)
as per Section 619-B of the Companies Act, 1956.

The accounts of the State Government Companies (as defined in Section 617
of the Companies Act, 1956) are audited by Statutory Auditors, who are

-1 Introduction

With a view to supply reliable and quality power to all by 2012, the
Government of India (501) prepared the National Electricity Policy (NEP) in
February 2005 which stated that the transmission system required adequate
and timely investment besides efficient and coordinated action to develop a
robust and integrated power system for the country. It also, inter-alia
recognized the need for development of National and State grid with the
coordination of Central/State Transmission Utilities. Transmission of
electricity and grid operations in Delhi are managed and controlled by Delhi
Transco Limited (DTL) which is mandated to provide an efficient, adequate
and properly coordinated grid management and transmission of energy. The
DTL, a successor company of erstwhile Delhi Vidyut Board (DVB) for the
power transmission activities was incorporated on 4 July 2001 under the
Companies Act, 1956 and came into existence on 1 July 2002. After
unbundling of DVB, the power distribution sector in NCT of Delhi has been
handed over to private companies while the generation and transmission are
still with the Government. The DTL is under administrative charge of
Department of Power, Government of NCT of Delhi (GNCTD).

The management of the DTL is vested with a Board of Directors comprising
eight members appointed by the State Government including four independent
directors. The day-to-day operations are carried out by the Chairman cum
Managing Director who is the Chief Executive of the DTL, with assistance of
Director (Operations), Director (Finance), Director (Human Resources) and
Company Secretary. As on 31 March 2012, the DTL had transmission line
network of 890.534 Circuit Kilo Meter (CKM) and 31 nos. sub-stations (SSs)
with installed capacity of 12191 Mega Voltage Ampere (MVA), capable of
annually transmitting 52306 Million Units (MU)! at 70 per cent capacity at
220 KV.

cent to 99.70 per cent of the requirement) (discussed at paragraph 7.8.4). The
turnover of the DTL was & 1447.36 crore in 2011-12, which was equal to

7.8.2.2 Voltage management

As per CEA (Grid Standards) Regulations 2010, the transmission networks are
required to maintain voltages between 420 KV and 380 KV for 400 KV sub-
stations and 245 and 198 KV for 220 KV sub-stations. Normally over-drawl of
power from system results into falling of voltage level, which may affect the
electric appliances of consumers and in off peak hours results in high voltage
level which may affect equipment of the DTL. To stabilize the voltage
according to peak demand and to protect the collapse of voltage and large
scale power outages, there is a need to install adequate number of Capacitor
Banks/Shunt line reactors which provide and maintain the necessary reactive
power” in the system. Further, the loading of the lines beyond stipulated
capacity also results in voltage fluctuations, higher transmission losses and
frequent interruptions/breakdowns. As per MTPC issued by CEA, permissible
line loading cannot normally be more than the TLL. CEA prescribed the
loading limit of 697 Amp. and 546 Amp. of maximum current load for 400
KV (ACSR-Bersimis conductor) and 220 KV (ACSR-Zebra conductor)
respectively.

MIPC of CEA stipulates that reactive compensation should be provided as far
as possible in low voltage systems with a view to meet the reactive power
requirement of load close to the load points. As per general principles, the
utilities should have reactive power compensation equivalent to that of active
power demand to avoid reactive power demand from the grid during the low
voltage conditions.

As per the study conducted by Northern Regional Power Committee (NRPC)
to assess the requirement of Capacitor Banks for the year 2007-08, Delhi was
required to have the reactive energy of 3750 MVAR in 2007-08. Audit,
however, noticed that against this, Delhi was having installed capacity of
reactive energy of 3456 MVAR resulting in shortfall of 294 MVAR in
2007-08.

The GCC time and again reiterated that Capacitor Bank capacity in Delhi was
short and was required to enhance it to avoid heavy drawal of reactive power
under low voltage conditions. Further, SLDC pointed out (October 2010) that
the shortfall in reactive energy was on DISCOMs level and these entities were
required to install Capacitor Banks at their ends. Audit, however, noticed that
the position of reactive energy capacity did not improve in Delhi, as the
installed capacity of Capacitor Banks in 2011-12 was 3657 MVAR (DTL-
754.48 MVAR and DISCOMs-2902.52 MVAR) against the requirement of
4492 MVAR (DTL-754.48 MVAR and DISCOMs-3737.52 MVAR) assessed
by NRPC resulting in shortfall of 835 MVAR (DISCOMs level).

Latent Defects are inherent weaknesses which normally are not detected by
examinations or routine tests but are present at the time of manufacturing due
to some design lapses and are aggravated by use. Latent Defect Warranty
(LDW) clause is used as part of guarantee clauses in a sale contract to recover
damages from seller if defects turn up after sale. Therefore, at the time of
agreement for the procurement of costlier item like transformer such clause
should be incorporated.

Scrutiny of records relating to SSs and Lines revealed that due to non—
incorporation of LDW clause in some of the purchase orders, the DTL failed
to have transformers repaired free of cost indicating that there was no
consistency in respect of incorporation of LDW clause in purchase orders.

administered by the manufacturer.

Audit scrutiny revealed that in the procurement and installation of above three
transformers, no LDW clause was incorporated in the NIT and LOA. Asa

7.9.1 Maintenance of grid and performance of SLD!

Transmission and grid management are essential functions for smooth
evacuation of power from generating stations and grid to the
DISCOMs/consumers. Grid management ensures continuous monitoring of
power balance in the interconnected power system to take care of reliability,
security, economy and efficiency of the power system. Grid management in
India is carried out in accordance with the standards/directions given in the
Grid Code issued by CEA. The Delhi’s SLDC, a constituent of Northern
Regional Load Dispatch Centre (पारा 100)", ensures integrated operation of
power system in the State, viz., optimum scheduling and dispatch of electricity
and real time monitoring and controlling of grid operations. The SLDC levies
and collects such fees and charges from the generating companies and
DISCOMs, as specified by the DERC. The GNCTD notified (November 2002)
that the SLDC, Delhi, would be operated by the DTL.

7.9.2 Grid discipline by frequency management

As per Grid Code, the transmission utilities are required to maintain grid
discipline for efficient functioning of grid. As per regulation, all users viz.
SLDC, generation companies, DISCOMS, NRLDC should take all possible
measures to ensure that the grid frequency always remains within the 49.5 —
50.2 Hz band. Grid frequency goes below or above the permitted frequency
levels due to shortages in generating capacities, high demand, grid indiscipline
in maintaining load generation balance, inadequate load monitoring and
management.

As per Delhi Grid Code 2008, no user should cause a sudden increase in its
load by more than a limit as laid down by the SLDC (scheduled load) without
prior intimation and consent of the SLDC. SLDC may direct the concerned
users to effect manual load shedding to curtail over drawal, when the
frequency falls below the permissible limit.

To enforce the grid discipline, the NRLDC issues three types (A, B and ©)" of
violation messages to SLDC and SLDC forwards such messages to
overdrawing DISCOMs.

7.12 Disaster management

Disaster Management (DM) aims at mitigating the impact of a major break
down on the system and restoring it in the shortest possible time. As per the
Best Practices and CEA Regulations, 2010, DM should be set up by all power
utilities for immediate restoration of transmission system in the event of a
major failure. It is carried out by deploying Emergency Restoration System,
DG sets, vehicles, firefighting equipment, skilled and specialized manpower.

7.12.1 Disaster Management for SLDC

As per Delhi Grid Code, the Load Shedding Procedure and Black Start
Procedure were to be developed and submitted to DERC within three months.
However, SLDC developed the same in January 2010 ie, after lapse of 18
months. Delhi power system is an integral part of system revival of northern
region maintained by NRLDC. In the event of total black out, Delhi has to
follow the instructions and procedures of NRLDC in providing start up power
to generating stations, railway traction and other essential and emergency
services.

SCADA data is being preserved electronically in SLDC since 2004 in the form
of magnetic tape, magnified operating disk, hard disk and CDs. However,
during discussion, it was intimated by SLDC that above control data was
stored in the same premises. Since the control data is not kept at a separate
back up site, the SCADA data was vulnerable to damage in the event of
disaster.

The Management stated (December 2012) that scheme of creation of back up
SLDC was under implementation and would be completed by September
2013.

The Management in their reply (July 2012) accepted the facts and stated that
the company received funds from the Government of NCT of Delhi on 29
December 2011 and had invited bids for a period of 7-30 days and settled for
14 days under the fluid situation prevailing at that time. Calling of bids for 7 to
30 days should not be taken to indicate their intention of parking of funds for
30 days only but obviously for the period decided by competent authority by
taking into consideration all probable variables operating in the then
environment. They further stated that because of urgency of releasing the
equity funds to BYPL and BRPL, Government of NCT of Delhi released the
funds to DPCL and it could not have blocked these funds for longer period
simply with the view to earn higher interest.

The reply of the Management is not tenable as Allahabad bank had offered
highest rate of interest of 10.05 per cent for a period upto 30 days with no
penalty clause for premature withdrawal. Further, the contention of the
