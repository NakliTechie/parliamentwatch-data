BASIS AND APPROACH TO UT FINANCES AUDIT

REPORT

In terms of Section 49 of the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963
(UT Act, 1963), the Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India
(CAG) relating to the accounts of a Union Territory (UT) are to be submitted to
the Lieutenant Governor of the UT, who shall cause them to be laid before the
Legislature of the UT. The UT Finances Audit Report (UTFAR) is prepared
and submitted under Section 49 of the UT Act, 1963.

The Director of Accounts and Treasuries prepares the Finance Accounts and
Appropriation Accounts of the UT annually from the vouchers, challans and
subsidiary accounts rendered by the offices and departments responsible for
keeping of such accounts functioning under the control of the UT Government
and the statements received from the Reserve Bank of India. These accounts
are audited independently by the Principal Accountant General (Audit-II),
Tamil Nadu and Puducherry and certified by the CAG.

Finance Accounts and Appropriation Accounts of the UT constitute the core
data for this Report. Other sources include the following:

> Budget of the UT: for assessing the fiscal parameters and allocative
priorities vis-a-vis projections, as well as for evaluating the effectiveness
of its implementation and compliance with the relevant rules and
prescribed procedures.

> Results of audit carried out by the office of the Principal Accountant
General (Audit-ID, Tamil Nadu and Puducherry.

> Other data with departmental authorities

> GSDP data and other UT related statistics

> Various audit reports of the CAG of India

> Best practices and guidelines of the Government of India (Gol)

The analysis is also carried out in the context of FRBM, best practices and
guidelines of the Government of India (Gol). The draft report was sent to
Government of the UT of Puducherry in February 2026.

STRUCTURE OF THE GOVERNMENT ACCOUNT

The Accounts of the UT Government are kept in three parts.

1. Consolidated Fund of the UT (Section 47 of the Government of
Union Territories Act)

This Fund comprises all revenues received in the Union Territory by the UT
Government, all loans advanced to the Union Territory from the Consolidated
Fund of India and all loans raised upon the security of the Consolidated Fund of
the Union Territory and all moneys received by the Union Territory in
repayment of loans. No moneys out of the Consolidated Fund of the Union
Territory shall be appropriated except in accordance with law and for the
purposes and in the manner provided in this Act. Certain categories of
expenditure (e.g., salaries of Constitutional authorities, loan repayments, etc.)
constitute a charge on the Consolidated Fund of the Union Territory (Charged
expenditure) and are not subject to vote by the Legislature. All other
expenditure (Voted expenditure) is voted by the Legislature.

2. Contingency Fund of the UT (Section 48 of the Government of
Union Territories Act)

This Fund is in the nature of an imprest which shall be paid from and out of the
Consolidated Fund of the Union Territory from time to time be determined by
law made by the Legislative Assembly of the Union Territory. No advances
shall be made out of the Contingency Fund of the Union Territory except for the
purposes of meeting unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation by the
Legislative Assembly. Expenditure from the Fund is recouped subsequently by
debiting the expenditure to the functional major head concerned under the
Consolidated Fund of the UT. The corpus of this Fund in Union Territory of
Puducherry is हें 50 lakh.

3. Public Account of the UT (Section 47A of the Government of
Union Territories Act)

Apart from above, all other public moneys received by or on behalf of the
Government, where the Government acts as a banker or trustee, are credited to
the Public Account. The Public Account includes repayables like Small Savings
and Provident Funds, Deposits (bearing interest and not bearing interest),
Advances, Reserve Funds (bearing interest and not bearing interest),

Remittances and Suspense heads (both of which are transitory heads, pending
final booking). The net cash balance available with the Government is also
included under the Public Account. The Public Account is not subject to the
vote of the Legislature.

EXEC SUMMARY

This Union Territory Finances Audit Report presents an independent,
descriptive overview of the fiscal position of the Union Territory of Puducherry
for the year 2024-25.

In this report we have expressed our observations and opinion on economic
growth of the UT, receipts and expenditure, debt sustainability, budgetary
management, and financial reporting practices, with a view to present a
comprehensive picture of public finances and their management during the year.

Our macroeconomic analysis of the UT revealed that, the UT's economy
exhibited a high rebound growth of 13.05 per cent in 2021-22, after post
pandemic recovery, followed by gradual normalisation and stabilisation in
2024-25. The rate of growth of UT’s GSDP increased from 10.94 to
11.12 per cent over the previous year. This increase reflects a transition from
an economy recovering from pandemic shock to sustainable growth in Tertiary
and Secondary sectors driven by Tourism, Education and Healthcare.

During the year, Revenue Receipts increased to = 10,517 crore, registering a
growth of 4.73 per cent over the previous year, which tailed behind the GSDP
growth of 11.12 per cent. The resultant own tax revenue buoyancy of 0.59
suggests structural weaknesses in revenue generation, such as a narrow tax base
besides inefficiencies in assessment and collection.

Arrears of revenue amounted to 22.01 per cent of the UT's Own Revenue and
continued to increase over the past years. The own tax revenue of the UT
constituted 39.40 per cent of the total revenue during the current year. We noted
that non-tax revenue of the UT increased by हें 166 crore (6.54 per cent) over
the previous year and constituted 25.68 per cent of total revenue. This is largely
owing to Power receipts since electricity supply is vested with the UT
Government. Grant-in-Aid from Gol constituted 34.91 per cent of the total

revenue.

The UT recorded a revenue deficit of = 401 crore (0.77 per cent of GSDP) and
a fiscal deficit of = 848 crore (1.63 per cent of GSDP), showing sustainable

Government financing.

During the ten-year period between 2015-16 and 2024-25, 52.98 per cent of
Public debt receipts were used for repayment of public debt taken in earlier
years and 24.09 per cent of the borrowed funds were utilised for Revenue
Expenditure. Further, the UT’s Capital Receipts increased by = 561 crore
(44.21 per cent) indicating a continued and increasing dependence on Public
debt to finance the capital requirements as well as other expenditure obligations.

Returns on Government investments remained low at 0.79 per cent compared
to the average cost of borrowings of 7.25 per cent, indicating inefficiencies in
the deployment of public funds.

Over the decade, the total expenditure as a percentage of GSDP remained
broadly stable in the range of 19 to 22 per cent indicating a moderate and fiscally
conservative in Government spending. However, 96.07 per cent of the total
expenditure is consumed by Revenue Expenditure, which may limit fiscal
flexibility and leave limited space to spend on new development or growth
oriented activities. Capital Expenditure remained in the range of = 450 crore,
with = 439 crore in 2023-24, which marginally increased to = 447 crore in
2024-25.

Capital Expenditure as a share of total expenditure declined from 8 per cent in
2015-16 to 4 per cent in 2024-25, pointing to a lower capital formation and
weakened infrastructure creation.

The transport sector accounted 2161 crore (36.67 per cent ) in 2023-24 and
= 144 crore (32.21 per cent ) in 2024-25 of the total Capital Expenditure of the
respective years. Similarly, combined expenditure of two sectors i.e.,
Education, Sports, Art & Culture and Health & Family Welfare was
= 117 crore (26.17 per cent) in 2024-25, which increased from

% 63 crore (14.35 per cent) in 2023-24. A major decline was seen in the Capital
Expenditure towards Tourism, from 239 crore (8.88 per cent) in 2023-24 to
% one crore (0.22 per cent) in 2024-25.

We noted that the Government regulated the expenditure on Social and
Economic services at 38 and 34 per cent respectively. Committed expenditure
accounted for 48.84 per cent of Revenue Expenditure, thereby limiting fiscal
flexibility.

We examined the Budgetary management of the Government. During the year,
as against the original provision of % 13,438.85 crore, expenditure of
% 12,397.05 crore was incurred. The overall savings of = 1,041.79 crore worked
out to 7.75 per cent of total provision against the respective grants in 2024-25.
Further, we observed that all disbursements during the year were incurred
within the authorisations granted by the UT Legislature and no expenditure
exceeded the sanctioned grants during 2024-25, reflecting adherence to
legislative control over expenditure.

We observed that the UT did not resort to Ways and Means Advances or
overdrafts during the year and maintained adequate cash balances over the past
few years which is a healthy trend. Debt sustainability indicators also showed
large improvement, with the debt-GSDP ratio declining from 28.04 per cent in
2020-21 to 23.45 per cent in 2024-25.



Executive Summary

We reiterate our recommendations in past Audit Reports that the UT should
strengthen own-revenue mobilisation, contain committed expenditure, and
enhance returns from public investments. The Government may also take

sustained measures to strengthen revenue realisation and address the
accumulation of arrears.

file of the UT of Puduche:

set to reach 17.20 lakh (0.12 per cent of India’s population) in 2025 compared
with the country’s population of 141.33 crore with a density of 3,510 persons
per square kilometre.

1.5.6 Pathway to Fiscal Stability

Deficits can be improved by enhancing revenues and rationalizing expenditures.
This includes strengthening tax compliance, widening the tax base, revising user
charges, and monetising idle Government assets. On the spending side, better
targeting of subsidies, controlling salary and pension growth, and ensuring
proper classification of expenditure are key factors. Prioritising productive
capital investment and improving debt management through transparent and
efficient borrowing can further ease fiscal pressure. These measures collectively
create fiscal space and help reduce revenue, fiscal, and primary deficits in a
sustainable manner. These issues are discussed in the succeeding paragraphs.

1.5.6.1 Improving revenues of the UT

Untapped revenue potential, if harnessed effectively, can significantly enhance
fiscal space and reduce dependence on debt. Inefficiencies in assessment,
undervaluation, and limited enforcement mechanisms of key tax streams such

as UT GST, Stamp Duty, and Excise will lead to subdued revenue growth.
Under-realised non-tax revenues, with low user charges, poor cost recovery, and
suboptimal returns on public assets and investments also impede the fiscal
space. Timely realisation of pending arrears is (tax and non-tax) another step
towards enhancing the fiscal space.

1.6 Conclusion

The review of UT’s Finances for 2024-25 exhibited a high rebound growth of
13.05 per cent in 2021-22 followed by gradual stabilisation and normalisation
and stood at 11.12 per cent during the year. The UT’s per capita income
remaining consistently above the national average, reflecting favorable
demographic and socio-economic fundamentals.

45


Union Territory Finances Audit Report for the year 2024-25

Though UT could achieve the Fiscal Deficit to GSDP ratio and Debt to GSDP
ratio proposed in the FRBM it continued to reel under revenue deficit as it could
not achieve the target proposed in FRBM.

Despite the above achievements and macroeconomic strength, the finances of
UT did not fully translate its fiscal consolidation. Revenue Receipts grew at a
relatively modest pace (4.73 per cent), resulting in subdued revenue buoyancy
and continuation of revenue deficit (¥ 401 crore). The UT’s finances remained
characterised by a high share of Revenue Expenditure (96.07 per cent to total
expenditure) with committed expenditure constituting 48.84 per cent of
Revenue Expenditure and an expanding interest payment burden (7.45 per cent
of Revenue Expenditure), which collectively constrained fiscal flexibility.

Capital Expenditure constituted only 3.93 per cent of total expenditure
indicating less importance given for infrastructure and development. Further, a
significant portion of borrowing was utilised for debt servicing and Revenue
Expenditure rather than asset creation.

The return on investment of = 1,061.53 crore as of March 2025 in Government
Companies and Co-operative institutions was negligible (% 8.38 crore) at
0.79 per cent whereas the average interest on Government borrowing stood at
7.25 per cent.

Overall, while the UT demonstrated adherence to debt sustainability norms, the
fiscal structure continues to be marked by structural imbalances, particularly in
revenue mobilisation, expenditure composition, and the utilisation of borrowed
funds. Addressing these issues is essential to ensure durability and quality of
fiscal outcomes in future.

1.7 Recommendations

(a) The UT Government should mobilise its resources and strengthen the
machinery through improved assessment, monitoring, and enforcement
mechanism for collection of own tax and non-tax revenue and arrears of

revenue.

(ii) In view of the increasing reliance on borrowings, the UT Government
may accord priority to prudent debt management and ensure that a higher
portion of public debt is utilised for productive Capital Expenditure and asset
creation.

(iii) | UT Government needs to borrow based on its requirement and avoid

maintaining large reserves of cash.

2.5.4 Injudicious re-appropriation of funds

Re-appropriation is transfer of funds within a grant from one unit of
appropriation to another unit where additional funds are needed. During
2024-25, 1,856 re-appropriations were issued under 39 grants amounting to
% 348.97 crore which diluted the process of budgeting and expenditure control.
All re-appropriation orders were issued on 31 March 2025. It implies that
re-appropriation orders were proposed ex post facto without necessary provision
in the respective heads.

Government replied (March 2026) that re-appropriation orders are issued
throughout the financial year as and when requests are received from the
Departments concerned to facilitate utilisation of funds under the appropriate
heads. Government further stated that the order issued on 31 March is only a
consolidated order incorporating all such re-appropriations already effected
during the year. Therefore, the re-appropriation made on 31 March does not
represent fresh fund modification but only consolidation of adjustments already
carried out, and hence does not dilute the budgeting process.

Reply is not acceptable as the re-appropriation orders issued at the time of such
adjustments raises concerns regarding transparency and compliance with
prescribed financial procedures. Consolidation at the end of the financial year
limits effective legislative oversight and may weaken the credibility of
budgetary control.

Further, in 13 cases (Appendix 2.4), even after reduction of provision
exceeding % two crore through re-appropriation orders effected by various
departments proved injudicious, as there was savings of = 82.81 crore under
these cases.

During 2024-25, it was seen that in 20 cases (Appendix 2.5), augmentation of
provision through re-appropriation proved unnecessary as the expenditure
incurred under these heads was within the total provision. Further, out of these
20 sub-heads, no expenditure was incurred under four sub-heads (SI. Nos. 3, 14,
19 and 20 of Appendix 2.5) wherein provision of = 11.45 crore was made by
re-appropriation unnecessarily.

2.9 Conclusion

Budgetary management of UT of Puducherry indicated that while aggregate
expenditure remained within the authorisation of the Legislature, gaps persisted
between budget estimates, revised estimates and actual expenditure.

Expenditure of = 1.63 crore incurred during the year was without any original
or supplementary budget provision. Further, supplementary provisions of
2 738.84 crore were made but the expenditure did not come up to original
provision of = 12,700 crore. There were instances of injudious re-appropriations
under various grants through reduction of provision as well as augmentation of
provision as there were savings even after re-appropriation. Savings of more
than one crore was noticed persistently under 19 grants for the past three years.
Although, there were savings of = 637.67 crore, the entire savings were not

the expenditure were spent on last quarter of the month which indicated rush of
expenditure.

indicated that UT of Puducherry is yet to migrate to SNA SPARSH.

3.1 _ Undischarged Liabilities of the Government

Undischarged liabilities, such as non-transfer of collected cess to designated
bodies or short remittances to the National Pension System (NPS), etc., can have
significant long-term fiscal and governance implications. These unpaid
obligations accumulate over time, creating hidden liabilities that distort the true
financial position of the UT. Further, delays in cess transfer hinder the intended
development or welfare outcomes, defeating the purpose for which such levies
were imposed. Similarly, short transfers to NPS not only violate statutory
commitments but also compromise the financial security of employees. Over the
years, such practices can erode trust, trigger legal liabilities and increase future
expenditure obligations, thereby, constraining fiscal space and weakening fiscal
sustainability. Such cases are discussed in succeeding paragraphs.

1011 of Minor Head '800'

Minor Head 800 - Other Receipts/Other Expenditure are intended to be operate:
only when the appropriate Minor head has not been provided in the accounts.
Routine operation of Minor head '800' is to be discouraged since it renders the
accounts opaque. Classification of large amounts under the omnibus Minor hea:
800 affects transparency in financial reporting and distorts proper analysis of
allocative priorities and quality of expenditure.

During 2024-25, under 52 Major Heads, expenditure aggregating to
% 2,072.51 crore constituting 18.24 per cent of the total Revenue and Capita!
expenditure (= 11,365.18 crore) was classified under the Minor hea
“800 - Other expenditure” in the accounts.

On scrutiny of 52 major heads operating Minor head 800 - Other expenditure, it
was noticed that Major head 2801 constituted major share of total Minor head
800 -other expenditure (59.25 per cent). Further, it is pertinent to mention that
Revenue expenditure of “Major Head 2801-Power” for the year 2024-25 was
% 2,789.05 crore. Since the expenditure towards Power constitutes a major share
of Revenue expenditure, operating a Minor head 800 without appropriate Minor
head lacks accounting accuracy. This practice of booking under Minor head 800
for huge amount of expenditure would affect the budget proposal also.

Similarly, during 2024-25, ₹ 236.07 crore under 36 Major Heads of Account,
constituting 2.24 per cent of the total Revenue Receipts (रे 10,516.83 crore) was
classified under 800-Other Receipts in the accounts.

On scrutiny of 36 major heads operating Minor head 800, it was noticed that
Major head ‘0049’ constituted 27 per cent of the total Minor head-800 other
receipts despite having an appropriate Minor head. This indicated that UT had
no control over the booking of receipt of revenue.

The fact that such substantial proportions of the expenditure/receipts is booked
under the omnibus Minor head “800” is a cause of concern, since it adversely
impacts transparency in financial reporting as a transparent public accounting
system is not merely an administrative necessity but is a cornerstone of good
governance.

Government replied (March 2026) that specific minor heads of accounts are
identified and re-classified and action will be initiated in the forthcoming
financial years to create an appropriate minor head in place of 800-Other
Expenditure under CSS Schemes, after due consultation with the concerned
implementing Departments, the DAT and the Public Financial Management

3.8 Follow-up action on Union Territory Finances Audit Report

In every UT, the Public Accounts Committee (PAC)/Finance Department require
the line departments to provide a Suo-motu Explanatory Note (EN) on the
paragraphs featuring in the Audit Reports within one month of placing the
Reports in the Legislature. The line departments are also required to provide
Action Taken Notes (ATNs) to the Principal Accountant General (for vetting and
onward transmission to the PAC) within three months of tabling the Reports.

The UT Finances Audit Reports for the year upto 2023-24 have been tabled in the
UT Legislature. The PAC had discussed the reports till the year 2016-17 but
discussion on UT Finances Audit Reports were pending discussion by PAC from
2017-18 to 2023-24 (128 Paragraphs).

3.9 Conclusion

This chapter highlights systemic issues affecting the completeness,
transparency and reliability of the UT’s financial reporting. The UT’s financial
position was hampered due to lack of accuracy in accounting practices. Audit
observed that Utilisation Certificates amounting to = 1,405.19 crore were
outstanding in various Departments which indicated the failure of the
departmental officers to comply with the rules to ensure accountability besides
defeating the intended objective of providing these grants.

Further, Extensive use of Minor head ‘800’were noticed in receipt as well as in
expenditure during the current year. As of March 2025, 292 accounts pertaining
to Autonomous Bodies/Authorities were pending for finalisation and the extent
of arrears ranged from one year to more than fifteen years. In addition, UT
Government reported 57 cases of misappropriation, loss and defalcation upto
March 2025, involving Government money amounting to हैं 30.34 crore on
which final action was pending.

3.10 _Recommendations

. The UT Government should ensure that Controlling Officers submit
UCs and fix responsibility on those who failed to do so. Government
should consider adopting a suitable module in Budget Estimation
Allocation and Monitoring System to ensure timely submission of UCs

and adjustment of advances.

. The UT Government should ensure that the Receipts/Expenditure are
booked under the relevant Minor Heads instead of indiscriminately
booking under the Minor Head ‘800’.
