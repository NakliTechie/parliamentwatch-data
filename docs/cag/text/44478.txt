PREFACE

This report has been prepared for submission to the Government of Uttar Pradesh
in accordance with the terms of Technical Guidance and Supervision (TGS) of the
audit of accounts of Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) by the Comptroller and Auditor
General of India as entrusted by the State Government in October 2001 in terms

of Eleventh Finance Commission’s recommendation.

This audit Report for the year 2009-10 is consolidation of major audit findings

arising out of audit of accounts of ULBs in the State of Uttar Pradesh.

The purpose of this report is to give overview of the functioning of ULBs in the
State of Uttar Pradesh and to draw the attention of the Executive Departments and

ULBs for remedial action and improvement, wherever necessary.

The cases mentioned in the Report are among those which came to notice mainly
in course of test check of accounts of 153 Urban Local Bodies conducted during

the year 2009-10.

11 Introduction

Government implemented the system of democratic governance down to
grass root level in Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) through Uttar Pradesh
Municipal Corporation Act, 1959 and Uttar Pradesh Nagar Palika Act,
1916. The objective was to make the ULBs self reliant and to provide
better civic facilities to the people of the areas under their jurisdiction.
Further, the Seventy-Fourth Constitutional Amendment (1992) paved the
way for decentralization of powers, transfer and devolution of more
functions and funds to the ULBs. Consequently, more diversified
responsibilities were devolved through a three tier structure namely Nagar
Nigams 1(NNs), Nagar Palika Parishads” (NPPs) and Nagar Panchayat
3(NPs). To incorporate the provisions of the Seventy-Fourth Constitutional
Amendment, the legislature of Uttar Pradesh enacted the Uttar Pradesh
Urban Local Self Government Laws (Amendment) Act, 1994.

There were 630 ULBs in the State, governed by the elected board of their
members with normally five years’ tenure. The last election to these 630

ULBs was held in the year 2006. The population profile of the ULBs was

1.3 Database on finances

The Eleventh Finance Commission recommended that a database on the
finances of the ULBs should be developed at the district, State and Central
Government levels and be easily accessible by computerizing it and
linking it through V-SAT°. The data were to be collected and compiled in
standard formats prescribed by the C&AG of India. The objective was to
facilitate comparison of performance of local bodies among the States at
the Government of India level and the Government at the State level. Upon
the recommendation of Eleventh Finance Commission, हैं 49.41 lakh was
deposited in the Personal Ledger Account (PLA) of Nagar Nigam
Lucknow in 2000-01 for creation of database. The database was, however,
not developed up to November 2010 and action in this regard taken at the

Government level was awaited (December 2010).

Non creation of the database on finances of the ULBs deprived the
Government to assess their performances in the State by comparing it with
the performances of the ULBs of other States. Besides this, releases of
grants after reviewing their actual needs and fiscal performance were not
ensured. This was more important in terms of the recommendation of the
Twelfth Finance Commission which observed that maintenance of the
database was necessary to keep accurate information on the finances of the

ULBs for need based assessment of their requirements.

1.12 Other points

Recommendation of the State Finance Commission

Second State Finance Commission, constituted in February 2000 for the
period 2001-2006, made 107 recommendations mainly on the issues
telating to transfer of fixed share of the net tax proceeds of the State to
ULBs and PRIs, to improve their resources through license fee etc. and to
implement e-governance, computerization in local bodies etc.

It was noticed that Government accepted in toto 74 recommendations and
partially 12 and did not accept remaining 21 which mainly related to
imposing of property tax in rural areas, revision of rates of land revenue
and enhancing income of ULBs through license etc.

Third State Finance Commission was constituted in December 2004. On
the issues relating to transfer of fixed shares of the net tax proceeds of the
State to ULBs and PRIs, the commission recommended nine per cent of
the net tax proceeds to ULBs but State Government decided to devolve
only seven per cent of the total tax net receipts to the ULBs.

1.13 Conclusion

Thus, Government, on one hand, did not devolve 7.5 per cent of the
proceeds of Tax Revenue to the ULBs in terms of the recommendations of
the Second State Finance Commission and on the other hand, funds made
available to them were underutilized leading to accumulation of huge
amounts of fund depriving thereby the people of the areas under their
jurisdiction the civic basic amenities. The financial data were also not
reliable as neither the database was developed nor data of fund utilization
compiled at the State level. The status of the assets and liabilities of the
ULBs was also not available due to non maintenance of accounts in the
prescribed formats. In addition, Government decided to devolve only
seven per cent of the total tax net receipts to the ULBs against the nine per

cent, which was recommended by Third State Finance Commission.

Executive Summary

Urban Infrastructure Development Scheme for Small and Medium Towns,
a component of Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission, was
launched by the Government of India on 3rd December 2005 for the
period of seven years beginning from 2005-06 for all the cities/towns
except cities/towns covered under Urban Infrastructure & Governance.
The scheme aims to encourage reforms of Urban Local Bodies and fast
track infrastructure development. The sharing of funds would be in the
ratio of 80:10:10 among the Government of India, State Government and
Urban Local Bodies. The Government of India allocated हैं 947.92 crore
for Uttar Pradesh.

The performance audit conducted from June to September 2010 covering
the period 2005-10 revealed the following:

> Out of the 621 eligible ULBs to be covered under the scheme, only 76
ULBs were covered and 46 ULBs were sanctioned the grant for their
64 projects;

> Due to poor planning for availability of land, 14 projects
(cost: ® 119.85 crore) of solid waste management could not be started
as of March 2010;

> Detailed Project Reports of 11 projects cost 208.19 crore had been
revised to € 271.62 crore up to August 2010 due to project cost
overrun;

>

Irregular execution of 20 roads of inner city of Basti, estimated
cost रैं 23.77 crore, as no technical sanctions were obtained in
accordance with the Government order;

> Contractor was paid price escalation of हैं 7.61 lakh against the terms
and conditions of the agreement;

> Records of test checked ULBs revealed that reforms of ULBs were not
implemented in 13 ULBs out of 14, which hamper the allotment of
second instalment; and

> Programme of training to ULBs personnel and elected representative
was not organised.

2.1 Introduction

The Government of India (Gol) launched (December 2005) Urban
Infrastructure Development Scheme for Small & Medium Towns
(UIDSSMT) for the cities and town having less than 10 lakh population
except towns and cities covered under Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban
Renewal Mission (JNNURM) for improvement of infrastructure facilities
in a planned manner and to ensure implementation of urban reforms
(mandatory and optional). UIDSSMT subsumed the then existing schemes
of Integrated Development of Small and Medium Towns (IDSMT) and
Accelerated Urban Water Supply Programme (AUWSP).

Admissible items under this scheme are:

> Urban Renewal i.e. redevelopment of inner (old) city areas;

> Water Supply (WS) and Sanitation;

> Sewerage and Solid Waste Management (SWM);

> Construction and improvement of roads, highways/expressways

(Roads & Flyovers), parking lots/spaces on Public Private

Partnership basis;
> Development of heritage areas; and

> Preservation of water bodies.

Allocation of the funds to Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)'° will be on the
basis of the ratio of population of the ULB to the total urban population of
the eligible ULBs of the State. However, funds would be provided to only
those towns and cities where elections to local bodies have been held and
elected bodies are in position. The State Government may prioritise towns
and cities on the basis of their local needs i.e. existing infrastructure,
population of Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes and special

requirements. The fund share of the projects would be 80:10:10 between

10 Nagar Nigam (NN), Nagar Palika Parishad (NPP) and Nagar Panchayat (NP).

2.3. Audit objectives
The performance audit of the UIDSSMT was to assess whether:

> adequate planning for selection of eligible ULBs and projects was

carried out;

> funds were made available timely for execution of the projects and

utilized as per UIDSSMT guidelines/instructions;

> projects were executed in an efficient and transparent manner in

accordance with guidelines of UIDSSMT and financial rules;

> reforms were implemented to improve urban governance and projects

were monitored efficiently and effectively at each level; and

> evaluation of the outcomes of the scheme was done in accordance
with guidelines.

2.4. Audit criteria

Audit criteria was formed and adopted as under:

> Guideline of UIDSSMT.

> Municipal Solid Waste (Management and Handing) Rules-2000.

> Sewerage Manual and Water Supply Manual.

> Nagar Nigam Act-1959 and Nagar Palika Act-1916.

> Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) approved by State Level
Sanctioning Committee.

> Memorandum of Agreements (MoA), quality test reports, monthly
progress reports.

> Government Orders and directions issued from time to time.

conducted 13 meetings during the period from 2005-06 to 2009-10 against
the required 15 and only one meeting during 2009-10. Programme

implementation unit was not constituted till March 2010.

Thus, ineffective monitoring system was one of the factors which slowed

down the implementation of projects.

2.10. Conclusion

UIDSSMT was launched by Gol to improve infrastructure facilities in
cities and towns and quality of life of inhabitants of the small and medium
towns. The performance review conducted on the scheme revealed that
only 76 ULBs were covered out of the eligible 621. Out of the 76 ULBs,
projects of only 46 ULBs were approved to whom all the funds were
allocated. SLSC did not adhere to the Gol instruction in allocation of
additional ACA of % 700 crores. Funds were released without ensuring
availability of land and finalization of design. Internal control system was

insufficient and implementation of reforms was not ensured.

Thus, due to inefficient planning, ineffective monitoring and slow progress
in implementation of projects, inhabitants of concerned ULBs was so far

deprived of the benefits of targeted infrastructure facilities.
2.11. Recommendations

> The State Government should allocate the funds to eligible ULBs
by prioritizing the towns and cities in terms of programme

guidelines;
> DPRs should be prepared after the required survey;

> Funds to ULBs and EAs should be released after ensuring the

availability of land for implementing the projects;

> ULBs should implement the committed reforms within time

schedule.

awaited (May 2011).

3.2 Non-adjustment of Advances

Non-adherence of provisions of Accounts Code and Financial Rules
resulted in non-adjustment of advances of & 9.49 crore.

As per Rules'’, temporary advances to the officials were required to be
adjusted by the end of the financial year in which the amounts were
advanced. The Drawing and Disbursing Officer is responsible for the

adjustment/ recovery of advances.

Scrutiny of records of Nagar Nigams, Aligarh and Varanasi revealed
(January 2010) that the Nagar Nigams advanced % 9.49 crore, during 1971
to 2009 (Appendix-3.2) to the officials/departments for various purposes
such as purchase of bitumen, purchase of bleaching powder, load testing,
sample testing, electrification of slums, etc. However, the concerned
Drawing and Disbursing Officers, due to inaction, did not monitor the
recovery/adjustment of these advances. As a result, the advances were
neither adjusted in the books of accounts of the Nagar Nigams nor

recovered from the officials even after expiry of 39 years.

In reply, the Nagar Ayukt of the concerned Nagar Nigams stated
(January 2010) that action for adjustment of advances would be taken.
The reply is, however, not tenable as keeping huge amounts without
adjustment/recovery is not only a gross violation of the State Financial

Rules but is also fraught with risk of misappropriation.

Thus, due to non-observance of Codal provision, हैं 9.49 crore was lying

pending for adjustment/recovery since long.

३.३ Unfruitful expenditure on developments of plots

Due to non ascertainment of the economics of developing a plot, an
expenditure of = 8.32 lakh on them was rendered unfruitful. Besides,
the scheme objective of developing serviced sites remained
unachieved.

With a view to increasing availability of serviced sites for housing and
promoting principles of planned and orderly spatial development, the Gol
launched (1979-80) a scheme of Integrated Development of Small and

Medium Towns.

Scrutiny of records of Nagar Panchayat, Maghar, Sant Kabir Nagar district
revealed (January 2010) that it received हैं 9.01 lakh from the Gol under the
aforesaid scheme during 1999-2003 for development of 13 residential
plots measuring total area of 1,605.06 square meter. The Civil
Coordination and Monitoring Committee under the chairmanship of the
District Magistrate, Sant Kabir Nagar approved it without ensuring its
economics. The Nagar Panchayat spent हैं 8.32 lakh on their development
during 2002-03 and proposed their disposals at the rate of = 750 per square
meter with 90 years lease. However, these plots could not be disposed off

even after eight years of their development.

The Executive Officer, Nagar Panchayat, Maghar, Sant Kabir Nagar, in
reply, stated (January 2010) that the plots could not be disposed off as the

people did not show their interest in them due to availability of free hold

plots in the nearby areas at the same rate. He, however, did not spell out

the strategy for their disposals.

Thus, investing हैं. 8.32 lakh on the development of plots in Nagar
Panchayat area without ascertaining their economics led to unfruitful
expenditure thereon. Besides, the objective of increasing availability of
serviced sites for housing and promoting principles of planned and orderly

spatial development could not be achieved.

3.4 Unfruitful expenditure on purchase of generator

Due to inaction on the part of Executive Officer, Nagar Palika
Parishad, Bisauli, District Badaun, expenditure of हैं 7.46 lakh on
urchase of a generator set was rendered unfruitful.

Scrutiny of records of Nagar Palika Parishad, Bisauli, District Badaun
revealed (August 2009) that with a view to ensuring water supply to the
people living in the precincts of Nagar Palika Parishad, Bisauli, the
Executive Officer of the Parishad decided (August 2008) to purchase a
generator set of 60 kva capacity at a cost of = 7.46 lakh. The order for the
purchase was placed to a firm after inviting tender. Supply was made in
September 2008. However, within four days of the purchase of the
generator set, it was transferred to the office of the Collector of Badaun
district for office use. Scrutiny also revealed that no demand for its return
was made by the Executive Officer of the Parishad due to inaction and as

such was lying at the Collectorate even after over two years of its transfer.

Thus, due to inaction on the part of the Executive Officer of the Parishad,
the generator set costing = 7.46 lakh could not be utilized for the purpose
for which it was purchased rendering the expenditure on its purchase
unfruitful. Besides, the people of the Nagar Palika Parishad, Bisauli,
District Badaun were also deprived of the use of the generator set in water
supply.

The Executive Officer, Nagar Palika Parishad, Bisauli, District Badaun
stated (August 2009) that action would be taken for the return of the
generator set. However, the fact remained that no action was taken since

September 2008 for its return.

3.5 Idle Investment

Non allotment of 32 shops and three halls constructed by three
Nagar Panchayats resulted in idle investment of हैं 56.92 lakh on
them.

With a view to developing regional centers of economic growth and
employment the scheme of Integrated Development of Small and Medium
Towns (IDSMT) provided for creation of infrastructural facilities in small

and medium towns.

Scrutiny of records of Nagar Panchayats at Manjhanpur of Kaushambi
district, Amaraudha of Kanpur (Dehat) district and Maghar of Sant Kabir
Nagar district revealed (October 2009, December 2009, January 2010) that
the Government sanctioned construction of 32 shops and three halls in
these Nagar Panchayats under the aforesaid scheme at an estimated cost of
& 72.10 lakh (Manjhanpur: 14 shops, हैं 17.33 lakh; Amraudha: 12 shops
and three halls, ¥ 43.97 lakh; Maghar: six shops, = 10.80 lakh,). The
construction work of the shops and the halls were completed between
December 2002 and December 2006 at an expenditure of हैं 56.92 lakh.
However, these Panchayats neither conducted survey of the sites nor made

an assessment of the viability of the projects before taking up the

construction work. As a result, there were no takers of these shops and as

such were lying un disposed off even after lapse of four to eight years of

their construction.

The Executive Officer, Nagar Panchayat, Manjhanpur, stated (October
2009) that the people were not interested in shops as these were
constructed away from main road. The Executive Officer, Nagar
Panchayat, Amaraudha, stated (December 2009) that efforts were being
made to allot the shops and the Executive Officer, Nagar Panchayat,
Maghar stated (January 2010) that the people did not show their interest in
allotment of shops. The replies were not acceptable in audit as the fact

remained that infrastructure facilities created were lying undisposed.
