1.4 Planning of Audi

The primary purpose of this Report is to bring the significant results of audit to
the notice of the State Legislature. Auditing Standards require that the
materiality level for reporting should be commensurate with the nature,
volume and magnitude of transactions. The audit observations are expected to
enable the Executive to take corrective action, as also to frame policies and
directives that will lead to improved financial management, thus, contributing
to better governance.

Audit process starts with the assessment of risks faced by various Departments
of Government based on expenditure incurred, criticality/complexity of
activities, level of delegated financial powers, assessment of overall internal
controls and concerns of stakeholders. Previous audit observations are also
considered in this exercise. The frequency and extent of audit are decided
based on this risk assessment.

2.1.1 _Introduction|

The Government of Tamil Nadu (GoTN) established (1961) the Department of
Archaeology with the objective to identify, conserve and preserve ancient
monuments in the State and to conduct archaeological exploration and
excavation at the historically important places. The activities of the
Department include establishment of site museums to showcase ancient
monuments, copying, deciphering and publishing stone inscriptions and
chemical preservation of art objects.

The State Government had declared 92 historical monuments including
religious buildings as protected monuments and established 14 site museums,
as of March 2020. The Department is also in possession of 13,983 antiquities
and artefacts of historical significance found during excavation which include
gold coins, metal rings, idols, earthen pots, porcelain articles, etc.

2.1.6 Good practices adopted by the Departmen

The following good practices were adopted by the Department for
Archaeological excavations:

¢ The age of Sangam is usually considered somewhere between 37
century BCE (Before Common Era) and ३१ century CE (Common
Era). Identification of the cultural deposits and subsequent excavation
at keeladi in Sivaganga district yielded historical artefacts. The carbon
dating of these samples shift the date of Tamil-Brahmi to 6" century
BCE in Tamil Nadu.

¢ Department utilised the latest technology of scientific dating to obtain
report for the unearthed artefacts from Beta Analytic Testing
Laboratory, Florida. These reports were useful to position the State on
the global map of pre-historic to historic cultures.

2.1.7 Planning|

2.1.7.1 Identification of ancient monuments

Section 2(a) of the Act defines ‘ancient monument’ as any structure, erection
or monument, cave, rock-sculpture, efc., which is of historical, archaeological
or artistic interest and which had been in existence for not less than 100 years.
GoTN instructions (August 1988) in the Manual of duties and responsibilities
of Tamil Nadu Archaeology Department (Manual) envisaged that
Archaeological Officers shall undertake village-wise survey to identify ancient
monuments and antiques. Identification involves visiting various sites and
documenting the potential sites for further action towards notifying them as
protected monuments. Commissioner of Archaeology fixed (October 2016)
the target for identification of historical monuments by District Archaeological
Officers (DAO) as three per year.

Audit observed that DAOs identified only 14 monuments (Annexure 3) as
against the target of 72 during 2017-2020 and the shortfall was about 80 per
cent.

Government replied (July 2021) that the reason for non-achievement of target
was mainly due to diversion of DAOs to archaeological excavation work. It
was assured to undertake comprehensive survey to identify historical
monuments.

2.1.7.2 Declaration of ancient monuments

Section 3 of the Act empowered the GoTN to declare* any ancient monument
and archaeological site as protected monument by giving two months’ notice.
On the expiry of the said period of two months, the Government may declare
by notification, the ancient monument or the archaeological site to be a
protected monument or a protected area.

GoTN instructions (November 1988) in the Manual envisaged that Assistant
Executive Engineers shall submit list of monuments required to be protected

2.1.12.4 Epigraphy

Epigraphy is the study of ancient inscriptions on the rocks, pillars, temples,
walls, copper plates and other materials. It deals with the art of writing and
provides an instrument for conservation and transmission of historical
traditions from generation to generation. Inscriptions are the main source for
reconstructing the history and culture of ancient civilisations. The inscriptions
should be copied from the boulders, stone pillars, stones, temple walls and
copper plates. The copied inscriptions (estampages) are deciphered, edited and
published for use by public.

Audit observed that Department was in possession of 25,027 copied
inscriptions (March 2020) and out of which 11,083 estampages were pending
deciphering and publication.

Government replied (July 2021) that action has been initiated by engaging
retired experts on contract basis to make estampages of uncopied inscriptions.

3.1.1 Introduction]

The 74th amendment to the Constitution accorded constitutional status to
Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) and established a system of uniform structure,
regular election, regular flow of funds through Finance Commission etc. As a
follow-up, the States are required to entrust these bodies with powers,
functions and responsibilities so as to enable them to function as institutions of
local self-government.

Accordingly, the State Legislature amended the Tamil Nadu District
Municipalities Act, 1920, for transferring the powers and responsibilities to
ULBs in order to implement schemes for economic development and social
justice including those in relation to the matters listed in the Twelfth Schedule
of the Constitution.

Tamil Nadu is one of the urbanised States of India. As per the 2011 Census,
urban population of the State was 3.49 crore constituting 48.40 per cent of the
total population of 7.21 crore. Important statistics of the State regarding

Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission (TNERC) issued a

stipulates (Clause 6(1)(d)) that all interface meters installed at the points of
inter-connection for the purpose of electricity accounting and billing should be
owned by the supplier of electricity.

TANGEDCO entered into long term Energy Purchase Agreement (EPA) for
25 years with 86 SPGs under Preferential Tariff Scheme’ between March 2015
and March 2019. The EPA required that the meters should be installed and
connected at the point of inter-connection between the electrical system of the

at the cost of SPG. These 86 SPGs have installed the energy accounting meters
at generating end and connected their power plants by erecting the necessary
transmission lines at their cost with the nearest sub-stations owned by
TANGEDCO/TANTRANSCO.

In this connection, Audit observed that the SPG did not adhere to the EPA as
well as the CEA Regulations by not installing the meters at the inter-

pooling sub-station (i.e. TANGEDCO’s sub-stations) was absorbed by

As per the provisions of Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission

The SLDC based on the demand of the grid will allocate the quantum of
generation for wheeling to OA consumers. The Superintending Engineer (SE)
at generation end will allocate the approved quantum of generation both in
terms of capacity in mega watt (MW) and units to various consumers based on
the approval. In the event of outage of the generator in any particular month,

compensate loss of generation occurred during the outages. Further, all the
SEs of OA consumers’ end were to ensure the above while making other
adjustments in monthly energy bills of the consumers as this would otherwise
lead to loss to TANGEDCO.

It was further observed that despite the provisions in the Accounts Manual to

mention that, even after five years since development, 90.48 per cent of the
plots remained unsold.

TANSIDCO replied (November 2020) that the IEs were established in the
backward area and it took up the strategy of phased development in the IEs. It
further stated that, it is taking up various promotion activities to sell the plots
including reduction of plot cost for which a proposal has been sent to the
Government. The reply is not tenable for the reason that even after repeated
advertisements, TANSIDCO could not find the prospective MSME Units.
Thus, embarking on new investment without demand survey was in violation
of its own manual provisions as well as financial prudence. Besides, the
objective of the GoTN to encourage the MSME in these backward areas
remained unachieved.

Government in its reply stated (May 2021) that in order to develop industrial
activities in rural areas, these IEs were created in rural areas. The area being
backward, demand for plot was very less and the development was made in a
phased manner based on demand to initiate industrial promotional activities as
per the Government announcement. It further stated that future investment will
be made after feasibility study or demand assessment report. However, the
fact remains that initiation of development of IEs without conducting demand
