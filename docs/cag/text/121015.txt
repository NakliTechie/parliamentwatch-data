PREFACE

This Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India for the year
ended March 2022 has been prepared for submission to the Governor of
Tamil Nadu under Article 151 (2) of the Constitution of India for being
placed in the State Legislature.

Part I of this Report contains significant results of Compliance Audit of
Government Companies of the Government of Tamil Nadu under the audit
purview of the Principal Accountant General (Audit-I]), Tamil Nadu and
Puducherry, Chennai.

Part II of this Report contains significant results of the Compliance Audit
of Receipts Audit relating to Industries, Investment Promotion and
Commerce Department and Home (Transport) Department.

Part वा of this Report contains significant results of Compliance Audit of
the Departments which included the Departments of Environment, Climate
Change and Forests; Home, Prohibition and Excise; and Tourism, Culture
and Religious Endowments.

The audit was conducted in conformity with the Auditing Standards issued
by the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.

Compliance Audit on Loss incurring State Transport Undertakings of Tamil

ET

Compliance Audit on Loss incurring State Transport Undertakings (STUs) of
Tamil Nadu revealed that the STUs incurred continuous losses in all the years
covered by Audit (2017-18 to 2021-22) as the contributions generated from
operation of their buses were not sufficient to meet their fixed costs. Employee
costs alone constituted 55.20 to 63.55 per cent of total expenses during the years
2017-18 to 2021-22.

STUs did not prepare long-term plans (5 to 10 years) despite specific directions of
GoTN. Their short-term annual plans did not address any action plan to bring
back the passengers, who switched over to other modes of transport. Fleet size of
STUs reduced from 22,517 buses to 20,304 during 2017-22 as the number of
buses condemned were more than new buses procured. The percentage of
overaged buses ranged from 46.47 per cent to 69.05 per cent. STUs did not have
any specific plan to replace them in a phased manner.

STUs cancelled schedules/trips of buses citing non-availability of crew and lost
additional revenue to the extent of 7495.27 crore during the review period which
could have been avoided, if the crew were not diverted for non-essential duties.
Cost per employee of STUs of Tamil Nadu were higher than STUs of Karnataka
and the additional expenditure incurred by STUs of Tamil Nadu, as compared to
KSRTC and KKRTC were 218,077.98 crore and 216,923.81 crore respectively
during the review period.

STUs had contributed 24,498.19 crore over and above the 12 per cent fixed as per
the pension rules, which worked out to 31.42 per cent of basic and DA. Total
loans of STUs have increased three-fold during the review period (from
26,467.66 crore to %21,980.88 crore), on account of continuous loss caused by
unviable operations. Despite facilities to monitor bulk prices and retail prices of
High Speed Diesel (HSD), STUs continued to procure HSD at bulk prices which
resulted in additional expenditure of 226.06 crore.

Instead of making periodical revision, fares were steeply increased by 20 to 88.89
per cent in January 2018. This resulted in drop in passenger patronage by 15 per
cent and 10 per cent respectively during 2018-19 and 2019-20 and consequential
loss of revenue to the extent of 24,076.62 crore. There were disparities in
reimbursing concessions given to students between MTC and other STUs and
hence, other STUs received lesser revenue to the extent of 7619.35 crore.
Contributory Pension funds to the extent of %4,565.67 crore and accident
Compensation Cess funds to the extent of 492.26 crore were diverted for day to
day activities of STUs. The STUs have to bear loss to the extent of 22,512.19
crore for not remitting Provident Fund contributions collected from employees.

Compliance Audit on Environment clearance for conservation of coastal
items in the State of Tamil Nadu

Government of India (GOI) notified the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ)
Notification, 1991 to ensure the livelihood security of fishermen and other local
communities living in the coastal areas and to conserve and protect coastal
stretches besides promoting development in a sustainable manner. Compliance
Audit on Conservation of marine and coastal ecosystems in the State of Tamil
Nadu revealed that the District Coastal Zone Management level authorities were
not formed with representative from Local Traditional Coastal Communities even
after 11 years from the implementation of CRZ notification, 2011. The Tamil
Nadu State Coastal Zone Management Authority (TNSCZMA) delegated their
mandated function of grant approval/clearance for constructions to Local
Planning Authority and District CZMAs in deviation to the CRZ Notification. It
was noticed that during 2015-22, TNSCZMA incorrectly granted CRZ clearance
to 114 out of 175 projects during 2015-22 instead of recommending them to the
appropriate authority. There were instances of grant of CRZ clearance for non-
permissible activities and without erosion assessment. Joint Inspection with
departmental officials and analysis of google earth images revealed unauthorised
constructions in CRZ area and 90 cases of construction activity in No
Development Zone in violation of CRZ notification. Post clearance monitoring
was not effective as TNSCZMA did not ensure compliance to the terms of CRZ
clearances.

1.3 Authority for audit]

The authority for audit by the CAG is derived from Articles 149 and 151 of
the Constitution of India and the Comptroller and Auditor General's (Duties,
Powers and Conditions of Service) (DPC) Act, 1971. CAG conducts audit of
expenditure of the Departments of Government of Tamil Nadu under Section
137 of the CAG's (DPC) Act. CAG is the sole Auditor in respect of
Autonomous Bodies which are audited under Sections 19(3)4 and 20(1)° of the
CAG's (DPC) Act. In addition, CAG also conducts audit of other Autonomous
Bodies, under Section 14° of CAG's (DPC) Act, which are substantially
funded by the Government.

In respect of PSUs, CAG conducts Audit of the accounts of Government
Companies under the provisions of Section 139 and 143 of the Companies
Act, 2013. Detailed procedure in respect of audit of PSUs are narrated in
Paragraph Nos. 2.1.3 and 2.1.4 in Chapter II of this Report.

1.4 Planning of Audi

The primary purpose of this Report is to bring the significant results of audit to
the notice of the State Legislature. Auditing Standards require that the
materiality level for reporting should be commensurate with the nature,
volume and magnitude of transactions. The audit observations are expected to
enable the Executive to take corrective action, as also to frame policies and
directives that will lead to improved financial management, thus, contributing
to better governance.

Audit process starts with the assessment of risks faced by various Departments
of Government based on expenditure incurred, criticality/complexity of
activities, level of delegated financial powers, assessment of overall internal
controls and concerns of stakeholders. Previous audit observations are also
considered in this exercise. The frequency and extent of audit are decided
based on this risk assessment.

Audit of (i) all transactions from the Consolidated Fund of the State, (ii) all
transactions relating to the Contingency Fund and Public Accounts and (iii) all
trading, manufacturing, profit and loss accounts, balance sheets and other subsidiary
accounts.

Audit of the accounts of Corporations (not being Companies), established by or
under law made by the State Legislature, at the request of the Governor.

Audit of accounts of any body or authority on the request of the Governor, on such

Audit of (i) all receipts and expenditure of a body or authority substantially financed
by grants or loans from the Consolidated Fund of the State and (ii) all receipts and
expenditure of any body or authority where the grants or loans to such body or

Chapter I— General

Inspection Reports (IRs) containing audit observations are issued to the Heads
of the Department/field officers after completion of audit. The Departments
are requested to furnish replies to the audit observations within one month of
receipt of the IRs. Whenever replies are received, audit observations are either
settled or further action for compliance is advised. Important audit
observations arising out of these IRs are processed for inclusion in the Audit
Reports, which are submitted to the Governor of the State under Article 151 of
the Constitution of India for laying on the table of the house of the State
Legislature.

The total audit universe was 2,431 units of 15 departments including 68 PSUs.
Out of which, 179 units and 8 PSUs were audited during 2021-22. All the 35

conduct of audit under Sections 14, 19 (2), 19 (3) and 20 (1) of the CAG’s
(DPC) Act, 1971 and these 35 Autonomous bodies were also audited during

1.6 Follow-up action on Audit Reports

With a view to ensuring accountability of the Executive in respect of the
issues dealt with in the Audit Reports, the Public Accounts Committee (PAC)/
Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) directed that the Departments
concerned should furnish remedial Action Taken Notes (ATNs) on the
recommendations of PAC/COPU, relating to the Paragraphs contained in the
Audit Reports, within six months. We reviewed the outstanding ATNs on the
Paragraphs included in the Reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General of
India relating to the Departments under the Audit purview of this office and
found that 816 and 412 recommendations pertaining to the Audit Paragraphs
discussed by PAC and COPU respectively were pending as on 31 March 2022.

Outstanding replies to Inspection Reports]

On behalf of the CAG, the Principal Accountant General conducts periodical
inspections of the Government Departments to test check transactions and
verify maintenance of important accounts and other records as prescribed in
the rules and procedures. These inspections are followed up with issue of IRs,
incorporating irregularities detected during the inspection and not settled on
the spot, which are issued to the Heads of the offices inspected with copies to
the next higher authorities for taking prompt corrective action. The Heads of
the offices/Government are required to promptly comply with the observations
contained in the IRs, rectify the defects and omissions and report compliance
through replies. Serious financial irregularities are brought to the notice of the
Heads of the Departments and the Government.

2.1.2 About this part of the Report}

This part of Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)
relates to results of Compliance Audit of 68 PSUs under the audit jurisdiction
of this office. Compliance Audit covers the examination of the records relating
to revenue and expenditure of the audited entities to ascertain whether the
transactions of the Company were in compliance with the objectives of the
Company and the provisions of applicable laws, rules, regulations and various
orders and instructions issued by the competent authorities.

2.1.3 _ Accountability Framework}

The authority for audit by the CAG is derived from Articles 149 and 151 of
the Constitution of India and the Comptroller and Auditor General’s (Duties,
Powers and Conditions of Service) (DPC) Act, 1971. The procedure for audit
of Government companies are laid down in Sections 139 and 143 of the
Companies Act, 2013 (Act 2013).

Further, as per sub-Section 7 of Section 143 of the Companies Act 2013, the
CAG of India may, in case of any company covered under sub-Section (5) or
sub-Section (7) of Section 139 of the Companies Act, 2013, if considered
necessary, cause test audit to be conducted of the accounts of such Company
and the provisions of Section 19A of the CAG’s (DPC) Act, 1971 shall apply
to the report of such test Audit. Thus, a Government Company or any other
Company owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by the Central
Government, or by any State Government or Governments or partly by Central
Government and partly by one or more State Governments is subject to audit
by the CAG of India.

2.1.4 Statutory Audit

The financial statements of the Government Companies are audited by
Statutory Auditors, who are appointed by the CAG as per the provisions of
Section 139 (5) or (7) of the Act 2013. The Statutory Auditors submit a copy
of the Audit Report to the CAG of India including, among other things,
financial statements of the Company under Section 143 (5) of the Act 2013.
These financial statements are also subject to supplementary audit by the CAG
within sixty days from the date of receipt of the audit report under the
provisions of Section 143 (6) of the Act 2013.

2.2 Compliance Audit on Loss incurring State Transport

.2.1 Introduction]

The Public Transport System plays a vital role in the overall development of
a State. In Tamil Nadu, the State Transport Undertakings (STUs) provide
Town, Mofussil, Ghat and Express services as well as Inter-State services to
neighbouring States of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala and Puducherry.
Apart from the STUs, the Government of Tamil Nadu (GoTN) allows
operation of transport services by private operators in the State.

2.2.1.1 At present, there are eight STUs functioning in Tamil Nadu. Out of
these, State Express Transport Corporation Tamil Nadu Limited (SETC)
operates long distance and inter-state services. Metropolitan Transport
Corporation (Chennai) Limited (MTC) covers Chennai city and surrounding
areas with town buses only. Other six STUs* operate mixture of town, mofussil
and ghat’ services in and around their respective regions.

2.2.1.2 As on 31 March 2022, the STUs had a fleet strength of 20,304 buses.
As has been ascertained from the database of the STUs, a total number of
314.72 crore passengers were carried by these STUs during 2021-22. The
annual turnover of these STUs was % 9,661.26 crore during 2021-22. As on 31
March 2022, GoTN had made equity contribution of 26,293.06 crore.
However, the entire equity contribution was eroded by the accumulated loss
of 248,478.50 crore due to continuous losses incurred by the STUs year after
year.

2.2.1.3 The GoTN supports STUs in the form of equity contributions, loans,
reimbursement of concessions in fares and High Speed Diesel (HSD) subsidy.
Besides the Government’s direct support, STUs also get loan from Tamil Nadu
Transport Development Finance Corporation Limited (TDFC), a sister PSU,
for procurement of chassis and for building of bus body on the chassis besides
working capital requirement. The STUs get procurement and training support
from the Institute of Road Transport (IRT) and technical support from
Pallavan Transport Consultancy Services Limited (PTCS), another State PSU.

ional Structure}

The STUs function under the administrative control of the Transport
Department of the State Government. The management of each STU is vested
with a Board of Directors appointed by the State Government. The Secretary
of Transport Department also functions as the Chairman of the STUs. The day-
to-day operations of the STUs are carried out by the respective Managing
Directors, with the assistance of General Managers, Divisional and Branch
Level Managers.

Planning for efficient operations}

A comprehensive study of planning by STUs revealed the following
discrepancies:

2.2.10.1 Absence of Long-term Planning

The GoTN had directed (April 1989) that the Public Sector Enterprises shall
prepare a long term (5 to 10 years) Corporate Plan setting out their goals and
objectives, strategy to achieve them and detailed annual operational plans. The
Corporate Plan should also include forecasts for sales, production, etc., and
long-term financial plans consisting of the Projected Income Statement,
Sources and Uses of Funds Statements and Capital Expenditure Programme.
Audit observed that no long-term plans were prepared by the STUs during the
period 2017-22. Such plans would have provided better vision and autonomy
in the functioning of the STUs.

The management replied (January 2023) that as per Government direction
(February 2022), the STUs prepared the five-year Long-Term Plan and placed
(April 2022) in the Board meeting for implementation from 2022-23. The
Government (March 2023) confirmed the same in its reply.

2.2.10.2 Deficiencies in Annual Plans

STUs prepare annual plans in the form of budgets, which are approved by the
Board of Directors. These annual plans inter alia include operational and
financial plans. Audit noticed that due to inefficient operation of scheduled
services, the number of passengers in STUs was on declining trend. Annual
plans of STUs did not address any action plan to bring back the passengers
who switched over to other modes of transport, particularly personal modes.
Apart from retaining existing passengers, STUs need to focus on passengers,
who value time and comfort more than price, as envisaged in Para 16 of the
National Urban Transport Policy" in order to improve their financial
performance.

Para 16 states that fares for public transport have been kept low for poor. In order to
make the public transport viable, the other segment of public, who are better off, would
switch to public transport from personal vehicles if high quality systems are available to
them. Hence, it insists on different level of service — basic service with subsidized fares
for poor as well as high quality service for better off with higher fares with no subsidy.

2.7 Avoidable expenditur:

Failure of PSUs to restrict their share of contribution towards
Employees’ Provident Fund to the statutory limit resulted in avoidable
excess contribution of 5.52 crore (for two years)

Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF) and Miscellaneous Provisions Scheme,
1952 (Scheme) is applicable to all Public Sector Undertakings (PSU) in the
country and both the employer and employee have to contribute towards the
scheme every month. Para 29 (1), 29 (2) and 26 A (2) of the Scheme stipulates
that the contribution payable by an employer should be 12 per cent of basic
wages, Dearness Allowance (DA) and Retaining Allowance (if any) and such
contribution should be limited to the 715,000 per month, in case, the monthly
pay exceeds 215,000 per month. Accordingly, the PSUs were required to
restrict their employer’s contribution to 21,800 (12 per cent of maximum
wage). Out of 12 per cent of such limited wage (215,000), 8.33 per cent!
(1,250) has to be deposited in Employees’ Pension Scheme (EPS) and
balance 3.67 per cent (%550) has to be transferred to EPF.

Audit test checked the implementation of above Act in nine PSUs™ of Tamil
Nadu where the scheme is applicable and the PSUs having accumulated loss
for the past two-years (2020-21 and 2021-22). The verification of records
revealed that out of nine PSUs test checked, six PSUs® have correctly
transferred the 12 per cent amount (limited to 71,800 p.m.) to EPS and EPF

4.1.1 Introduction

The marine environment, which includes oceans and adjacent coastal areas,
plays a vital role in our lives. Oceans are regulators of global climate, an
important sink for greenhouse gases and serve as the reservoirs of biodiversity.
The state of Tamil Nadu has a coastline of 1,076 km (14 per cent of the total
coastal length of India) with 14 coastal districts and stretches along the Bay of
Bengal, the Indian ocean and the Arabian sea. Besides the coastal waters, the
other major ecosystems found in the coastal environment of the State are
mangroves, coral reefs, sea grass, mud flats, estuaries/backwaters, lagoons etc.
Coral reefs act as a barrier against wave action along the coastal areas thus
preventing coastal erosion. These ecosystems are home to many important and
endangered marine animals that depend on sea and ocean for their existence.
The coastline also supports a huge human population with its rich marine
resources. Increasing human population, urbanisation and accelerated
developmental activities have put enormous pressure on the coastal districts
affecting the fragile marine ecosystems in the State.

4.1.2 _ Regulation activities in coastal areas

Government of India (GOI) notified the Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ)
Notification, 1991 to ensure the livelihood security of fishermen and other local
communities living in the coastal areas and to conserve and protect coastal
stretches besides promoting development in a sustainable manner. Provisions of
CRZ notification, 2011 declared the land area from High Tide Line to 500 m on
the landward side along the sea front as CRZ area. The Notification also
imposed restrictions on setting up and expansion of industries from operating in
the CRZ areas. CRZ Notification was revised in 2011 and in 2019. CRZ
Notifications had demarcated the entire coastal areas of Tamil Nadu into zones,
with only permitted activity allowed in these zones. Under the CRZ notification
2011, the coastal regulated areas are categorised from CRZ I to CRZ IV as
detailed below:

CRZ I areas which are ecologically sensitive which play a role in
maintaining the integrity of the coast.
