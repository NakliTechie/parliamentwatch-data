2. While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted in some cases
considerable time lag between issue of notifications and their laying. The
annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications
have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by the Committee. The
Committee observed that out of total 321 delayed notifications there were 185
notifications where no delay statement was appended. However, the bulk of
delayed notifications were from Ministry of Road Transport and Highways
which cames around 79.75% of total delayed notifications.
The observations/recommendations of the Committee in respect of the
specific Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been
noticed are as follows: -
(i) Ministry of Agriculture
(Department of Agriculture and Cooperation and Department of Animal
Husbandry Dairying and Fisheries)
Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Agriculture and Cooperation)
have laid 18 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 1 to 18) out of which 2 notifications (Sl.
Nos. 1 and 16) have been delayed by 3 and 9 months respectively. There was
no delay statement attached with one notification (Sl. No. 1) which was delayed
by 3 months. Another notification (Sl. No. 16) was delayed by 9 months but
Ministry has appended delay statement indicating the reason of the delay.
Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Animal Husbandry Dairying and
Fisheries) have laid 4 notifications (Sl. Nos. 19 to 22) All of these were delayed
by 5 months to 46 months and accompanied with delay statements. The reasons
of delay given therein are as under:-
“As per consolidated guidelines of framing/amendment/relaxation of
Recruitment Rules issued by DoPT OM No. AB.14017/48/2010-
Estt.(RR) dated 31.12.2010, para 5.3 of Part-V stipulates that “Although
the Recruitment Rules are statutory in nature, the copies of the notification
of the same need not be placed on the Table of both the Houses of

6
Parliament as Article 309 of the Constitution, under which these are
framed, does not prescribe this being done”. The Coconut Development
Board (Group ‘C’ post) Recruitment Regulations, 2012 have been framed
as per above mentioned guidelines of DoPT. Hence, it was not laid on
the Table of both the Houses of Parliament. The delay may kindly be
condoned.”
The Committee is unable to accept the reason given by the Ministry
for delay in laying of notifications despite the recommendations of the
Committee in this regard in its 82nd Report (para 4.31) which clearly
says that “... There is no reason why the Rules made under Article 309 of
the Constitution should not be laid before Parliament. There have been
some cases where the Rules made under Article 309 of the Constitution have
been laid before the Parliament. The Committee, therefore, strongly feels
and recommends that in order to maintain a uniform practice all the rules
made under delegated authority whether under various act of Parliament or
Article 309 of the Constitution should be laid before the Parliament. In
case, however, Government feels that the non-existence of the laying provision
in Article 309 is the reason for not laying the Rules made thereunder, the
Committee recommends that Article 309 of the Constitution be suitably
amended, so as to ensure that all the Rules made thereunder are laid before
the Parliament.” The Committee also takes note of the inordinate delay
of approximately 46 months in laying Rules which is not just acceptable
to the Committee and it recommends that Ministry of Agriculture should
in future strictly adhere to the laying procedure and whenever a delay
occurs, the Parliament is kept informed of the reason for the same by
appending a delay statement. The Committee hopes that Ministry would
strive to lay the notifications on the Table of the House at the first earliest
opportunity available.
(ii) Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers
(Department of Pharmaceuticals)
Only one notification that the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers

7
(Department of Pharmaceuticals) (Sl. No. 23) have laid was delayed by 5
months and no delay statement appended thereto. The Committee is not
happy over the delay in laying of the only notification by the Ministry of
Chemicals and Fertilizers (Department of Pharmaceuticals) by 5 months.
Further there is no delay statement appended to that giving reason as to
what caused such delay. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon the
Ministry to take care that such instances of delay do not recur in future
and in case of any delay in laying, a delay statement should invariably be
appended.
(iii) Ministry of Civil Aviation
Ministry of Civil Aviation have laid only three Notifications (Sl. Nos. 24
to 26) on the Table of the House during the Session. There has been delay of 7
months each in case of Sl. No. 24 and 25 and delay of 6 months in case of Sl.
No. 26. The Ministry has attached delay statement with each of these delayed
notifications and cited administrative reasons for not laying the notifications in
time.
The Committee hopes that the Ministry would avoid such instances
of delay in laying of subordinate legislation in future and would remove
lacuna, if any, administrative or any other causing delay. The Committee
also impresses upon the Ministry to take note of the fact that in case
there is a delay in the laying of any Statutory Notifications, the same
should be accompanied by a delay statement while it is being laid . The
Committee also advises the Ministry of Civil Aviation to avoid recurrence
of delays due to administrative reasons in future.
(iv) Ministry of Communication and Information Technology
(Department of Information Technology and Department of Posts)
Ministry of Communication and Information Technology (Department
of Information Technology) have laid 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 37-38) and out
of these notification one was delayed by 6 months with no delay statement

8
attached to it. The Ministry of Communication and Information Technology
(Department of Posts) have laid only one Notification (Sl. No. 39) which was
delayed by 5 months. The Notification was attached with delay statement. The
reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:-
“The copies of the Gazette Notification which was published in the Gazette
of India Extraordinary, Part II – Section 3 – sub Section (i) vide No. 497
[GSR 734 (E)] dated October 01, 2012 regarding revision of Speed Post
tariff could not be laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha during the winter
Session of Parliament due to the paucity of time. The notification is now
being laid on the Table of Rajya Sabya during the Budget Session of
Parliament”
The Committee noted with serious concern that Ministry of
Communication and Information Technology (Department of
Information Technology and Department of Posts) have failed to lay the
notifications in time on the Table of the House due to paucity of time.
The laying of notifications on the Table of the House is a statutory
obligation which should be fulfilled without delay. However, the Ministry
have shown lukewarm approach towards their duty toward Parliament.
The Committee recommends that Ministry of Communication and
Information Technology should adhere strictly to the schedule as
recommended by the Committee in its 131
st Report for timely laying of
Subordinate Legislation. The Committee hope that Ministry would avoid
citing such unworthy reasons for delay in future.
(v) Ministry of Corporate Affairs
Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 20 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 51 to
70). Out of these 10 Notifications have been delayed by 3 to 5 months. Only
one delayed Notification (Sl. No. 52) was attached with delay statement and
other nine Notifications (Sl. Nos. 53 to 61) did not have any delay statement
citing the exact reasons for delay. The Ministry has stated the following reasons
of delay in regard to G.S.R. 736 (E):-

9
“The notification relating to the GSR No. 736 (E) dated 01.10.2012 on
Amendment in Rule 10(i) of Companies (Issue of Indian Depository
Receipt) Rules, 2004 was sent to Government Press for printing on
01.10.2012. The said notification could not be laid before both the Houses
of Parliament during winter session of the Parliament, 2012 due to delay
in receiving the Gazette copy of the notification by this Ministry and
when it was collected, the session was over on 19.12.2012 (Rajya Sabha)
and 20.12.2012 (Lok Sabha). Hence the said notification got delayed.”
The Committee regrets to note that Ministry of Corporate Affairs
have delayed laying of a number of notifications, many of which do not
have delay statement attached to them. The Committee invites attention
to its recommendations contained in its 135th Report that in case of
delay in laying of notifications, delay statement should invariably be
accompanied with them. As for reason of delay tendered by Ministry for
one notification (Sl. No. 52) stating that delay was caused due to late
receipt of printed copies of notifications, the Committee would like to
impress upon the Ministry of Corporate Affairs to evolve suitable
mechanism to eliminate such instances of delay in future particularly
on account of delay in receiving printed copies of the notifications. The
Committee further recommends that the Ministry should take note of
the time schedule as prescribed by it for laying of Subordinate Legislation
and take necessary corrective steps so that such instances do not recur.
(vi) Ministry of Defence
The Ministry of Defence have laid 4 Notifications and out of which two
were delayed by 3 to 8 months. One of the Notification had delay statement
attached to it whereas another did not have any delay statement with it. The
reasons for delay given by the Ministry are as under:-
“The Jalandhar Cantonment Octroi (Amendment) Bye-laws, 2012 were
issued on 12.06.2012 from the file of Jalandhar Cantonment Board under
the signature of its Chief Executive Officer (CEO). As such the File was

10
returned to Jalandhar Cantonment Board through DGDE on 13.06.2012,
but the published copy of the Notification could be received in the Ministry
only on 4.2.2013. As such, the Notification could not be laid during
Winter Session of Parliament, 2012.
The delay in laying of papers on the Table of Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha is
due to delay in getting published copies of the Notification. It is requested
that the marginal delay on the part of Ministry of Defence may kindly be
condoned.”
The Committee finds that laying of notifications was delayed due
to late receipt of printed copies of notifications in the Ministry. In this
context the Committee observes that it is Ministry’s responsibility to
ensure that Subordinate Legislation is laid before the Parliament within
the prescribed schedule and accordingly, it is for the Ministry only to
ensure that they receive the same timely from the statutory bodies under
them. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Defence may
take necessary remedial measures to ensure that papers are received
timely in the Ministry from the statutory bodies.
(vii) Ministry of Environment and Forests
Ministry of Environment and Forests have laid 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos.
75 to 79) during the Session. Out of these, three Notifications (Sl. Nos. 75 –
76 and 78) were delayed by 4 months to 6 months and moreover no delay
statement was appended to any of three delayed notifications.
The Committee notes that three of its notifications which were not
only delayed by four to six months also did not have any delay statement
attached to them. The Committee views it seriously and reiterate its
recommendations (contained in its 135th Report) that in case there is
delay in laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department
should invariably append a delay statement. The Committee further
impresses the Ministry that it is their obligation towards the Parliament

11
to invariably apprise it about the reasons for delay through delay
statement in case they are unable to lay any Subordinate Legislation on
time.
(viii) Ministry of Finance
(Department of Financial Services)
Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) have laid 31
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 101 to 131). Out of these 9 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 111,
116 – 122 and 131) were delayed. The delay period ranges from 10 months to
31 months. The Ministry had attached delay statement with 8 notifications
whereas no statement was attached/appended with one notification. The reasons
for delay given by the Ministry is as under:-
“The Copies of the Gazettee Notifications could not be laid on the Table
of the Rajya Sabha as printed copies of the same were not received from
Regional Rural Banks in time. The delay has occurred as the Regional
Rural Banks which are spread out all over the country have to send the
Regulations to Government Press, New Delhi for publication. Thereafter,
these are sent back to the concerned Regional Rural Banks which then
sent it to the Ministry. The delay is thus in the administrative procedure/
process.”
The Committee does not accept the reasons of delay given by the
Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) and expresses
its serious concern over such inordinate delay which ranges from 10
months to 31 months. The Committee wonders that how can
administrative procedural process take 10 to 31 months to receive
Notifications and complete procedural formalities. The Committee feels
that Ministry did not take adequate precaution to avoid the delay and
made no serious efforts to lay the Notifications in time which is in
contravention of its statutory obligation to Parliament. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry must take appropriate step to remove
lacuna, if any, administrative or any other, causing such long delay and

12
wish it would strive to keep delay to the minimum by playing a more
proactive role and pursuing concerned organisations to act with
promptness in future.
(ix) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
(Department of Health and Department of AYUSH)
The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Department of Health) have
laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 228 to 234). Only one Notification was delayed
by 8 months. However, no delay statement was enclosed with it.
Department of AYUSH have also laid 5 Notifications and one Notification
was delayed by 3 months and laid with delay statement attached to it. The
reasons given by the Ministry are as follows:-
“Laying of Gazette Notification of amendment made in Rule 161 of the
Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 vide GSR No. 844 (E) dated 16.11.2012
regarding “insertion of botanical names of plants based ingredients along
with plant part(s) and form of ingredients to be mentioned on label”
published by the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare was to be laid in
both Houses of Parliament. However due to the fact that in the final
notification some typographical error was observed and it had to be
rectified as per the prescribed procedure, hence, the notification could
not be laid within the stipulated time period. After rectification of the
error, now the notification is being submitted along with amended version
GSR No. 105 (E) dated 19.02.2013 before both the Houseds of Parliament.”
The Committee have noted the reason cited by the Ministry of
Health and Family Welfare that due to typographical error, the
notification could not be laid in time. The reasons given by the Ministry
seems to be justified and well taken. The Ministry should in future take
care that such instances do not recur and that notification were laid
within the time schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee,
therefore, impresses upon the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare to

13
be watchful so that such error do not occur and consequential delay is
avoided in future. The Committee also emphasises that in case of delay
in laying of notifications, Ministry should append delay statement with
the notifications.
(x) Ministry of Home Affairs
The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 21 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 240 -
260) during the Session. Out of total Notifications laid 14 Notifications have
been delayed. Out of these 12 Notifications have average delay period of 5
months whereas one Notification (Sl. No 240) was delayed by 4 months and
no delay statement was appended to 13 notifications. One Notification (Sl. No.
242) was delayed by 12 months and also had delay statement appended to it.
The reasons given by the Ministry are as follows:-
“Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Grant-in-Aid to Panchayati Raj Institutions)
(Amendment) Rules, 2012 was published in the Andaman & Nicobar
Gazette (Extraordinary) vide notification No. 08 dated 20/01/2012. As
per laid down procedure, the said Rules should have been laid as soon as
may be after it is made before each House of Parliament while it is in
session for a total period of 30 days. It could not be laid before both the
Houses of Parliament in time for want of certain clarifications from UT
of Andaman & Nicobar Administration. Clarifications sought from the
Andaman & Nicobar Administration have now been received.
The delay is regretted. These Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Grant-in-Aid
to Panchayati Raj Institutions) (Amendment) Rules, 2012 are now being
laid in both the Houses of Parliament in current session.”
The Committee expresses its unhappiness over the large proportion
of Notifications, which comes around 67% of total notifications, being
delayed by the Ministry. The Committee further takes serious note of
the fact that the Ministry in majority of cases did not even bother to
provide the reasons for such delayed laying of Notifications. In absence

14
of any delay statement with any of the delayed notifications, Committee
finds it difficult to ascertain causes of delay. The Committee invites
attention to the recommendations made in its 135th Report wherein it is
laid down that it is essential to append a delay statement in case of delay
in laying of statutory orders. The committee, therefore, impresses upon
the Ministry of Home Affairs to strictly adhere to the time schedule in
laying of Notifications and should invariably append delay statement in
each case of delayed notifications .
(xi) Ministry of Human Resource Development
(Department of Higher Education)
The Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher
Education) have laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 261 to 267). Out of which 3
notifications were delayed by 4 to 5 months. All the delayed notifications were
appended with delay statement. The reasons given by the Ministry is that it
took some time to complete the procedural formalities and therefore, papers
could not be laid in time.
The Committee is not convinced of the vague reasons of delay
given by the Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of
Higher Education) that due to time taken to complete procedural
formalities, the Ministry could not lay the notifications in time. If, it
was so, the Ministry should have foreseen the things beforehand and
would have acted promptly. The Committee castigates this lackadaisical
approach by the Ministry and advises them to take care in future in
laying of notifications so that delay could be avoided.
(xii) Ministry of Law and Justice
(Department of Justice & Department of Legal Affairs)
Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Justice) have laid one
Notification (Sl. No. 280) on the Table of the House with a delay period of one
year and five months without any delay statement appended to it. Department

15
of Legal Affairs have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 281 to 284). All the 4
notifications were delayed and delay ranges from 8 months to 17 months.
However, all the Notifications laid by Department of Legal Affairs were appended
with delay statement. The reasons of delay given by the Department is that due
to voluminous work throughout the year, work relating to laying of notifications
could not be done in time inadvertently.
The Committee accepts that Ministry of Law and Justice is laden
with voluminous work and it may happen that the work may remain
undone inadvertently. However, the Committee impress upon the
Ministry that it is their statutory obligation to lay the notifications in
time on the Table of the House. Therefore Committee hopes that Ministry
would strive to adhere to laying schedule and avoid recurrence of such
delays in future.
(xiii) Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (Department
of Personnel and Training)
Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (Department of
Personnel and Training) have laid 10 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 285 to 294). Two
of these Notifications were delayed and the delay period was 5 months and 3
month each. The delay statement was appended with one of these Notifications.
The reasons of delay is given as under:-
“A copy of the Notification could be obtained only on 26.12.2012.
However, by the date the Winter Session of the Parliament was over.”
The Committee is not convinced of the reason given by the Ministry
of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions that notification was
received on 26.12.2012 and by then Parliament’s winter Session was
already over. The Committee advises the Ministry of Personnel, Public
Grievances and Pensions to be more vigilant in future so that such delay
could be avoided.

(xiv) Ministry of Railways
Ministry of Railways have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 298 to 301).
Only one of them (Sl. No. 300) was delayed by 3 months and accompanied
with delay statement. The reasons given by the Ministry for delay is as under:-
“The Indian Railways (Penalties for activities affecting cleanliness at
railway premises) Rules, 2012 G .S.R. 846 (E) published in the Gazette
of India Extraordinary Part-II Section 3 Sub-section-I could not be laid
down on the Table of the House due to an inadvertent oversight. The
same is therefore laid on the Table of the House.”
The Committee recommends that such instances should not be
repeated in future. Ministry of Railways must endeavour to lay the
notifications on the Table of House with upmost promptness.
(xv) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways
The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways have laid highest number
of notification during the Session which come around 374 Notifications (Sl.
Nos. 302-675). Out of these 256 Notifications have been delayed. The Ministry
did not append any delay statement with 105 delayed notifications (Sl. Nos.
306–310, 313–326, 334–347, 355–56, 358, 362–369, 401–403, 407–410, 413–
414. 416–431, 434–451, 455–458, 461–463, 465–468, 472–474, 476 & 478–
479). However, 151 delayed notifications were appended with delay statements.
The delay period ranges from 3 months to 19 months. The reason of delay
given by the Ministry is that it took some time to collect the papers and complete
the procedural formalities therefore, papers could not be laid in time on the
Table of both Houses of Parliament.
The Committee does not accept the reasons of the delay given by
the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways that it took some time to
collect the papers and complete the procedural formalities. The
Committee wonders how can it take one year to receive Notifications
and to complete procedural formalities. The Committee feels that Ministry

17
did not take adequate precaution to avoid the delay. The Committee
further takes a serious view of the fact that even after delay of one year
in certain case Ministry did not feel it necessary to append a delay
statement so as to apprise the Parliament of the reasons of delay in
laying of Notifications on the Table of the House. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry should initiate urgent measures so as to
avoid delay in completing the procedural formalities so that such delay
can be eliminated.
(xvi) Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
(Department of Social Justice and Empowerment)
The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Social
Justice and Empowerment) have laid only one Notifications (Sl. No. 676)
which was delayed by 22 months and moreover Ministry did not even bother
to append a delay statement citing the reasons for such extraordinary delay on
their part.
The Committee is not happy that the only notification that Ministry
of Social Justice and Empowerment have laid, has been extraordinary
delayed. Further, there is no reason given for this delay. This is in
contravention of Committee’s recommendation given in its 135 Report
that Notification which are delayed should invariably be accompanied by
delay statement. The Committee would like the Ministry of Social Justice
and Empowerment to take necessary steps to obviate such delay and in
case of delay in laying of Notifications, delay statement should invariably
be accompanied with them.
(xvii) Ministry of Urban Development
Ministry of Urban Development have laid one Notifications (Sl. No. 683)
during the Session which was delayed by 8 months. The reasons given by the
Ministry for delay is as under:-
“Copies of the Annual Report and Annual Accounts of Delhi Urban Art

citing shortage of time. Therefore, the same are being laid on the Table of

Delay caused in laying the above documents on the Table of the Rajya

The Committee is not convinced with the reasons given by the
Ministry of Urban Development that notification could not be laid in
time due to curtailment of the Winter Session of 2012. Committee
impresses upon the Ministry of Urban Development to endeavour to lay
the notification at the first available opportunity on the Table of the
House so as to fulfill statutory obligation.
Before the commencement of each Session of Parliament Ministries
are informed about the dates of sitting of House and dates allotted to
each Ministries. In case of laying of notifications, Ministries are informed
to bring the notifications to Committee Section 3 working days before
the day/date of laying so that laying process can be completed. But the
Ministries do not adhere to the time schedule and bring the copies for
laying at the fag end of the Session. This causes a great inconvenience
to the Secretariat. The Ministry of Urban Development is advised to
stick to the time schedule to avoid such situation.
The Committee, time and again have emphasized that laying or
rules/regulations/orders is a statutory obligation and should be completed
on priority basis within specified time-frame. The Ministry of Urban
Development should understand the implication of such failures. However,
the Ministry of Urban Development has not complied with the
recommendations of the Committee in this regard. The Committee once

19
again emphasizes that Ministry of Urban Development should adhere to
the time schedule in laying of notifications as prescribed in Committee’s
131st Report.
3. Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take
immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation.
The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/
Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the
following recommendations, as contained in its 131st, 135th, 152nd , 192nd
and 201st Reports:
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if
the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following
the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the
House is in Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, during
its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication
and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days,
before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session.
All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth
comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued
under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.
(131st Report)
(Presented on 15 December 2000)

that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the

Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all Ministries/
Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that through
the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to
introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial measures”.

“In addition to the concern expressed by the Committee over delay
in laying of Notifications on the Table of the House, the Committee also
express their deep concern over delay in framing of rules/regulations
under different Acts. The Committee in its 47th report presented to the
House on 14.9.1981 has recommended that the rule-making process should
be completed within a period of six months of the coming into force on
an Act. The Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry
is not able to adhere to this time limit, it should seek extension of time
for completing the rule-making process. The recommendation of the
committee reads as follows:-
“The Committee feels that the rule-making process could well have
been completed within the time limit of six months which the
Committee has laid down for making of rules under an Act after coming
into force of that Act. Where however, a Ministry does not find it possible
to adhere to the time limit, they should inform the Committee of the
reasons why they cannot make rules within the time limit and seek
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.”
The Committee is constrained to take note of the fact that the
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are not complying with
the Committee’s recommendation to complete the process of framing of

21
subordinate legislation contemplated under various Acts of Parliament
within a period of six months. At the same time, the Committee also
does not receive requests for extension of time for completing the rule-
making process in terms of its recommendations as above from most of
the Ministries/Departments. The Committee takes a serious note of the
prevailing situation in which the Ministries/Departments are neither
able to complete the task of framing rules/regulations under different
Acts within the given six months’ period nor do they suo-motu approach
the Committee for seeking extension of time. The Committee further
notes that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/regulations it has
taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate
legislation in respect of all legislations brought about by different
Ministries/Departments of Government of India from the year 2003
onwards with a view to ensure that the framing of subordinate legislation
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from
the Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this monitoring
exercise undertaken on behalf of the Committee.
In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates
its recommendation that the Ministries/Departments should ensure
putting in place subordinate legislation positively within a period of six
months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee further
recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline,
they should invariably approach the Committee for seeking extension
of time. The Committee further recommends that in cases where the
time limit of six months is not adhered to, they should, while laying
rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement
explaining the reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed
beyond six months so as to keep the Parliament apprised of the compliance
of the Parliament’s mandate conferred upon the Executive.”
(192nd Report)
(Presented on 26 August 2011)
