REPORT 
I 
Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of Rajya Sabha 

      During the 198th Session of the Rajya Sabha, 592 Statutory Orders were laid on the 
Table of the House by various Ministries/Departments. The details of the Gazette 
Notifications in which these were publ ished and the date of their laying on the Table of 
the Rajya Sabha have been indicated in the Ministry-wise statement placed at Annexure. 
2.   During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted some cases 
where there has been a considerable time lag between the date of notification of the Order 
and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in 
cases where notifications have not been laid as per the Committee's guidelines and 
whether such notification s were accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons for 
the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay has been quite abnormal. The 
observations/ recommendations of the Committee in respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows :– 
I.    Ministry of Coal 
      The Committee notes that the Ministry of Coal have delayed the laying of 1 of their 
notifications (Sl. No. 14) out of the 4 notifications laid by them during the Session. The 
Ministry have also not appended any delay statement  with the notification. The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Coal shall adhere to the time schedule as 
recommended by the Committee in laying the Subordinate Legislation pertaining to 
them. 
II.   Ministry of Commerce & Industry 
      The Committee regr ets to note that the Ministry of Commerce & Industry have 
delayed the laying of 6 of their notifications (Sl. No. 18 & 31 to 35) out of the 22 
notifications laid by them during the 198th session. Notification at Sl. No. 18 was not even 
accompanied by a delay statement. Appropriately, notifications at Sl. Nos. 31 to 35 could 
have been laid during Monsoon (196th) Session or thereafter during Winter (197th) 
Session of 2002. The Ministry have practically laid these notifications after about one year 
of their publication. The delay statement of the Ministry is also not convincing as it simply 
states that sufficient time was not available during the Winter Session which had resulted 
in delay. The Committee is extremely unhappy to note that the Ministry of Commerce & 
Industry did not comply with the time schedule laid by the Committee for laying of 
Subordinate Legislation. The Committee recommends the Ministry of Commerce and 
Industry should stick to the time schedule as recommended by the Committee in its 131st 
Report in laying the Subordinate Legislation before the Parliament. 
III.Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution 
      The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & 
Public Distribution have delayed the laying of 2 of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 49 & 50) 
out of the 7 notifications laid by them during the Session. The notification at Sl. No. 50 
could have been laid during theWinter (197th) Session and the notification  at Sl. No. 49 
even before i.e., during the Monsoon (196th) Session, 2002. Further, the delayed 
notifications were not accompanied by delay statements. The Committee would like to 
draw attention of the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution towards 
the schedule prescribed by it in the matter of laying of Subordinate Legislation and it 
hopes that this would be complied with in future. 
IV.Ministry of Environment and Forests 
      The Committee notes that the Ministry of Environment and Forests have delayed the 
laying of 2 of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 54 & 55) out of the 5 notifications laid by them 
during the session. There had been a delay of approximately 4 & 5 months in laying the 

notifications and no delay statements had been appended  to the notifications. The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Environment and Forests would observe the time 
schedule prescribed by it for laying of Subordinate Legislation in future. 
V.  Ministry of Finance 
      The Committee is extremely unhappy to note that the Ministry of Finance have again 
not complied with the schedule for laying of the notifications as prescribed by the 
Committee. The Ministry have laid 349 rules/regulations during the 198th session and 
there has been delay in case of 38 notificat ions ranging from 3 months to 10 months. 
Besides, there are 7 cases of delay exceeding one year (Sl. Nos. 104, 296 to 301) and one 
case of delay exceeding two years (Sl. No. 193). Further there has been one case of 
exceptional delay in laying (Sl. No. 264) where there has been a delay of 6 years. The delay 
statements have been appended only in 11 cases. In case of the notification at Sl. No. 264 
where there is a delay of over 6 years, the Ministry in their delay statement have simply 
stated that the reasons for delay were "not willful". This is not an acceptable explanation 
for delay. In another case (Sl. No. 273) the statement of delay as appended by the Ministry 
states – "Notification No. 16/2002 Service Tax dated 2.8.2002 could not be laid on the 
Table of  Rajya Sabha in the last session because of certain unforeseen circumstances 
beyond the control of the section."  The reason of delay given by the Ministry is not 
convincing. In other two cases (Sl. Nos. 322 & 323), the statement of delay appended by 
the Ministry states – "the notifications were sent on 12.12.2002 for laying before the Rajya 
Sabha for 17.12.2002 and Lok Sabha for 20.12.2002. The Rajya Sabha Secretariat refused 
to accept the same for laying due to shortage of time, and the house adjourned on 
21.12.2002. Hence the delay". In this context, it is stated that the last date of receiving 
notifications for laying on 17.12.2002 was 11.12.2002 and the notifications were brought 
to the Rajya Sabha Secretariat on 12.12.2002 i.e. after the due date. Further, the date of 
publication of both these notifications were 14.8.2002 and 30.9.2002 respectively, and 
evidently these could have been laid during the Winter (197th) Session or the first half of 
the Budget Session. Hence, the reasons of delay attributed by the Ministry of Finance are 
not justified. It may be recalled that the Committee had heard the Secretary, Ministry of 
Finance on the question of non -compliance of the time schedule in the matter of timely 
laying of Subordinate Legislation on the 9th Janua ry, 2003. During the course of oral 
evidence, the Finance Secretary, while admitting delay in laying, had assured the 
Committee that they shall take appropriate remedial action to avoid delay in future. The 
Committee expects the Ministry to be cautious in such matters and would take concerted 
steps to eliminate such instances of delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation in future. 
VI.Ministry of Home Affairs 
      The Committee notes that the Ministry of Home Affairs have delayed the laying of 
one of their notification (Sl. No. 404) by 6 months and no delay statement was annexed. 
The notification could have been laid during the preceding (197th) session. The 
Committee expects the Ministry to be cautious in such matters and ensure compliance 
with time schedule for laying the notifications in future. 
VII.   Ministry of Human Resource Development 
      The Committee notes with regret that there is delay in laying of 2 out of the 3 
notifications laid by the Ministry of Human Resource Development during the 198th  
Session (Sl. Nos. 407 & 408). The notifications having been published on the 18th 
November, 2002, the Ministry could have laid them during the 197th (Winter) Session, 
2002 itself. The Ministry also failed to append the requisite delay statement with both 
these notifications. There had been instances of delay in laying in some cases during the 

194th, 195th & 197th Sessions also. The Committee would like the Ministry to take 
necessary corrective measures so as to avoid such instances of delay in future. 
VIII.Ministry of Information & Broadcasting 
      The Committee notes with regret that the only notification (Sl. No. 410) laid by the 
Ministry of Information & Broadcasting has been delayed by 4 months and no delay 
statement has been enclosed therewith. The C ommittee hopes that the Ministry of 
Information & Broadcasting shall adhere to the time schedule as recommended by it for 
laying the Subordinate Legislation before the Parliament. 
IX.Ministry of Labour 
      The Committee notes with regret that there is a delay of over two years in laying of 1 
out of 4 notifications laid by Ministry of Labour (Sl. No. 411). The Committee takes note 
of the explanation for delay as given by the Ministry in the delay statement and hopes 
that such instances of delay shall not occur in future. 
X.  Ministry of Power 
      The Committee notes with regret that there is delay in laying of 1 out of the 2 
notifications laid by the Ministry of Power by 5 months and the Ministry have not 
appended any delay statement. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would ensure 
strict compliance of the schedule for laying Subordinate Legislation as prescribed by the 
Committee in its 131st report. 
XI.Ministry of Railways 
      The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Railways have laid 7 
rules/regulations during the 198th session out of which 5 have been delayed by period 
ranging from 5 months to one year. There were instances of delayed laying during the 
197th session also. Further, the Ministry have appended delay statement only in one case 
(Sl. No. 424). The Committee expects the Ministry to be cautious in such matters and 
ensure compliance with time schedule for laying the notifications in future. 
XII.   Ministry of Road Transport & Highways 
      The Committee notes that the Ministry o f Road Transport and Highways have 
delayed in laying of 12 notifications out of the total of 143 notifications laid. The delay in 
laying range from 4-5 months and the Ministry have also appended reasons for delay in 
laying with these notifications during t he 198th session. The Committee expects the 
Ministry to be more prompt and vigilant in laying the rules/regulations within the 
stipulated period to obviate such instances of delay in future. 
XIII.Ministry of Shipping 
      The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Shipping delayed the laying of 
2 of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 570 & 577) by 5 to 6 months. The Ministry also did not 
enclose the delay statements giving reasons for delay in laying. The Committee expects 
the Ministry of Shipping to exercise caution in such matters and ensure the laying of 
Subordinate Legislation within the time schedule. 
3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate 
steps to avoid delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation. The Co mmittee, accordingly, 
wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, 
particularly, the defaulting ones as enumerated above the following recommendations, as 
contained in its 131st Report wherein a time schedule for the laying of Subordinate 
legislation has been prescribed, as follows : 

      "Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following the date of publication of the 
Order in t he Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the 
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the 
date of publication and the date of closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, 
before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply with this 
schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued under various Acts of Parliament 
on the Table of the Rajya Sabha." 
4.   The Committee also reiterates its following observations made in its 135th Report 
regarding appending of delay statement where there has been delay in the laying : 
          "The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is  essential that in case 
there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department 
should invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as 
to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committ ee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that through the 
process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identifying 
bottlenecks and devise remedial measures".
