INTRODUCTION 
 I, the Chairperson of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having 
been authorized by the Committee do hereby present on its behalf this Hundred 
and Eighty-Fifth Report of the Committee.  
1.2 The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on 
10th December, 2009 
1.3  The Notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. which were laid on the 
Table of the Rajya Sabha during its 217th Session (2nd July to 7th August, 2009) 
have been perused by the Committee in the light of the provisions of the Acts 
under which these were framed and also with a view to see whether these have 
been laid properly and within the prescribed form and time-frame. The 
Committee’s observations/recommendations are contained in the report.

2. During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted 
some cases where there has been a consid erable time lag between the date of 
Notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also 

indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifications have not been laid as per 
the schedule prescribed by the Committ ee and where such notifications were 
accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. In some 
of the cases the delay has been quite long. The observations/recommendations of 
the Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: --- 
(i) Ministry of Agriculture (Depar tment of Agriculture & Cooperation) 
 The Ministry of Agriculture (Depar tment of Agriculture & Cooperation) 
have laid 16 Notifications out of which 6 notifications (Sl. Nos. 4 to 6, 8, 9 and 
13) have been delayed. None of the de layed Notifications have been accompanied 
by any delay statements. The majority of Notifications have a delay period of 
more than one and half year. 
 The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Agriculture have 
made inordinate delay in laying of Notifications. There is no delay statement 
appended therewith either to know the reasons of such delay. The Committee 
would like the Ministry of Agricult ure (Department of  Agriculture & 
Cooperation) to take corrective measures so as to obviate such delay in future 
and in case such delay does take plac e it should invariably be accompanied 
by statements giving the reasons for the delay in laying. 
(ii) (Ministry of Civil Aviation)  
 The Ministry of Civil Aviation have laid twelve Notifications out of which 
two notifications (Sl. Nos. 18 and 29) have been delayed by five months and one 
year and one month respectively. Delay stat ements are appended to with both of 
them. The reasons cited by the Ministry for delay is given below: 
 “Though the Notification was published on 2nd February, 2009, however 
due to late receipt of notification in the Ministry, the approval of the Hon’ble 
Minister for Civil Aviation could not be obtained in time for placing in the 
Parliament. Hence there was administrative delay in laying on the Table of the 
House, the papers relating GSR No. 68(E) regarding Amendment to the Aircraft 
Rules, 1937.” 

 The Committee regrets the lapse on the part of the Ministry of Civil 
Aviation causing such delay and advises,  the Ministry of Civil Aviation to 
take necessary steps and loopholes are plugged to ensure that such delays 
does not recur. 
(iii) Ministry of Communication 
(Department of Posts) 
 Department of Post have laid two Notifications one of which (Sl. No.50) has 
been delayed by seven months. There is no delay statement attached to them. 
 The Committee impresses upon the Ministry of Communication and 
Information Technology (Department of Posts) to evolve suitable mechanism 
to avoid such delays and also to append delay statements where there has 
been a delay, in laying of the Notifications. 
(iv) Ministry of Consumer Affair s, Food and Public Distribution 
 (Department of Consumer Affairs)  
 Department of Consumer Affairs have laid five notifications one of which 
(SI. No. 52) have been delayed by five months. There is no delay statement 
appended with the delayed Notifications. 
 The Committee reiterates its recomme ndation that in case of delay in 
laying, Notifications should be acco mpanied by a delay statement. The 
Ministry should take care that such delay is avoided in future. 
(v) Ministry of Corporate Affairs  
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 33 notifications. One Notification 
(Sl. No. 73) has been delayed by six months and there is no delay statement 
appended with the Notification. 
 The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
should strictly adhere to the time sc hedule as recommended by it in its 131st 
Report in laying of the Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and in case 
of any delay in laying of the Notifica tions, a delay statement should also 
invariably be appended therewith. 

(vi) Ministry of Environment and Forests  
 Ministry of Environment and Forests have laid 16 notifications out of which 
one Notification (Sl. No. 110) has been delayed by eight months and there is no 
delay statement appended with the Notification. 
 Another Notification (Sl. No. 106) has been delayed by one year with delay 
statement appended thereto. The reasons for delay given is as under:- 
 “Notification No. GSR 559(E) could not be laid on the Table of the Houses 
as immediately after its publication in the Gazette, within the stipulated period due 
to administrative reasons.” 
 The Committee is not convinced with the reply given by the Ministry. 
The Committee directs the Ministry to give  specific reason for the delay as to 
why these Notifications were not laid in the House within the stipulated time. 
 The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Environment and 
Forests should take note of the time sc hedule as prescribed by it for laying of 
Subordinate Legislation and take necessa ry corrective step s so that such 
instances do not recur. 
(vii) Ministry of Finance  
 (Department of Economic Affairs and Department of Revenue)  
 The Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) have laid 18 
notifications. Out of which one notification (Sl. No. 131) has not been laid within 
time. No delay statement is appended therewith. 
 The Committee emphasizes that the Department of Economic Affairs 
should take steps to avoid delay in laying of Notifi cations and where there is 
delay the Notifications should be acco mpanied by delay statement explaining 
the reasons therefor. 
 Department of Revenue have laid 141 Notifications out of which five 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 208 to 212) have been delayed by five months. Delay 
statements are appended to with all of th em. The reasons cited by the Ministry for 
delay is given below: 

 “The Gazette copy of Notifications was made available only on 20.2.2009 
late evening for laying in both Houses during the last sessions of Parliament but 
could not be laid as the work of laying of the papers in both Houses was finalized 
by the Legislative Branch of both the Houses by them. Hence, the notifications 
may be considered for laying in the next session of Parliament.” 
 The Committee notes with regret th at the Department of Revenue 
(Ministry of Finance) have again failed to strictly adhere to the time schedule. 
The Committee hopes that the Department  of Revenue (Ministry of Finance) 
would put an effective mechanism in pl ace to eliminate su ch instances of 
delay in future, particularly finalization of the work of laying of paper by the 
Department of Revenue. The Committee ho pes that the Ministry of Finance 
would be able to plug the loopholes so that delays do not recur in future. 
(viii) Ministry of Home Affairs  
 The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 18 Notifications. 3 of these (Sl. No. 
303, 315 and 316) have been delayed. The delay period ranges from eight months 
to one year and one month. All 3 notifications have been attached with delay 
statement. The reasons for delay given is as under:- 
 “The Indo-Tibetan Border Police Force, Telecommunication Cadre (Group 
“A” & “B” posts) Recruitment (Amendment) Rules, 2008 were published on 7th 
July, 2008 in the Gazette of India and received from Government of India Press, 
Mayapuri, New Delhi on 17.7.2008. 
 On two days special session held on 22-23 July, 2008, no transaction of 
business was taken in the Parliament except trust vote. Thereafter, these 
Recruitment Rules were again sent for laying on the Table of Rajya Sabha on 15th 
December, 2008, but these R ecruitment Rules could not be laid on the Table of 
Rajya Sabha as the Parliament was adjourned Sine Die on 23.12.2008.” 
 The Committee regrets the delay in ‘laying of papers and recommends 
that Ministry should take the first available opportunity for laying the 
statutory Notifications and should not postpone this work for the later part of 
the session so that the eventualities like early prorogation of the House, which 

is of course rare, could be avoided. The Ministry of Home Affairs should take 
necessary corrective measures in right earn est so that they are able to cut on 
delays in laying of Notifications. 
(ix) Ministry of Huma n Resource Development 
 (Department of Higher Education)  
 The Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher 
Education) have laid 7 notifications and f our of these (Sl. No. 320, 322 to 324) 
have been delayed by five months to one and half year. There is no delay 
statement appended with them. 
 The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Human 
Resource Development (Department of  Higher Education) laid seven 
Notifications and four of them have been  delayed, The Notification at Sl. No. 
320 has a delay period of six months and Notification at Sl. No. 324 with a 
delay period of eight months. Two notifications at Sl. No. 322 and 323 have 
been delayed by one year six months a nd one year four months respectively. 
The Ministry have not appended any de lay statement with either of them. 
The Ministry of Human Resource De velopment (Department of Higher 
Education) have failed to lay the notifi cations fairly on time. If precaution 
had been taken, the Notifications co uld have been laid earlier. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry should keep vigil for laying their 
Notifications at the earliest opportuni ty during the session so that extreme 
delays are avoided. Where there is delay,  Ministry should invariably apprise 
the House about the reason of delay through delay statement, in case they are 
unable to lay them in time. Th e Committee invites attention to 
recommendation contained in its 135th Report. 
(x)  Ministry of Rural Development 
 (Department of Rural Development)  

 Ministry of Rural Development (Department of Rural Development) have 
laid a total of 7 Notifications. Out of these two (Sl. No.379 and 380) have been 
delayed by seven months each and no delay statements are appended with them. 
 The Committee recommends that th e Ministry of Rural Development 
(Department of Rural Development) should take necessary corrective steps to 
ensure that such delays are not repeate d and that in case such delays do 
occur for unavoidable reasons, the same  should be conveyed to Parliament 
without fail by giving proper and adequate reasons in a delay statement. 
(xi) Ministry of Road Transport & Highways  
 The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways have laid 194 notifications on 
the Table of the House. 18 notifications have been delayed out of which 7 
notifications have been accompanied by delay statements and 11 delayed 
notifications have no delay statements appended with them. The delay period 
ranges from 5 months to six months. The delayed notifications which have been 
accompanied with delay statements have a delay period of 5 to 7 months. The 
reasons for delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 “It took some time to collect the papers and complete the procedural 
formalities. As such the notifications could not be laid on the Table of both the 
Houses of Parliament during the last session.” 
 The Committee notes with regret that  Ministry of Road Transport & 
Highways have appended delay statement only with seven of the eighteenth 
Notifications that were delayed. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry must have a re-look into th e cases of delay and try to plug the 
deficiencies so that such instances do not recur. They should also not fail to 
append delay statement in relevant cases in future. 
(xii) Ministry of Shipping  
 The Ministry of Shipping have laid nine Notifications out of which three 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 585 to 587) have been delayed by five months. Delay 
statements are appended to with all of th em. The reasons cited by the Ministry for 
delay is given below: 

 “Due to delay in receiving the papers from Government Press and 
completing the formalities of authentication etc., the Notifications could not be 
sent on 20.2.2009 to Rajya Sabha Secretariat.” 
 The Committee observes that laying of Notifications in time on both the 
Houses of Parliament is a statutory obli gation on the part of the Ministry of 
Shipping. Being aware of that, the Mini stry should have taken immediate 
necessary steps to complete the formalit ies for laying of the papers. But the 
Ministry kept the matter pending t ill laying time approched. Had the 
Ministry been vigilant, the unnecessary delay would have been curbed. The 
Committee observes that the Ministry of  Shipping should review the cases of 
delay and devise remedial measures to av oid such instances of delay in laying 
of subordinate legislation. 
3. The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take 
immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation. The 
Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the defaulting 
ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 131st, 135th and 152nd 
Reports:- 
 “Orders required to be laid before th e House should be laid (i) if the 
House is not in Session, during the Sessi on immediately following the date of 
publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in 
Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, during its continuance 
and in case the time lag between the da te of publication and the date of the 
closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the 
Session immediately followin g the said Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply 
with this schedule in the matter of layi ng of the Orders issued under various 
Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. 
(131st Report) 
(Presented on 15 December 2000) 
 “The Committee has, time and again , emphasized that it is essential 
that in case there is a de lay in the laying of Stat utory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with 

the notifications being laid so as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. 
The Committee desires all Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since 
the intention is that through the proces s of formulation of a delay statement 
the Ministry is able to introspect, id entify bottlenecks a nd devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135th Report) 
(Presented on 27 July 2001) 
 “In the opinion of the Committee, ever y notification should be assigned 
a distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no 
notification should be published/laid  which does not carry a notification 
number. The Committee expects all Mini stries/Departments of Government 
of India to exercise caution in this regard. In case where there are more than 
one notification under a single file, th e Ministries/Departments should ensure 
that the file number on each notificati on should be suitably distinguished 
from one another.” 
(152nd Report) 
(Presented on 26 August 2004)
