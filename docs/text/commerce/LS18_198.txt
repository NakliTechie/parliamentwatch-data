2. During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee noted 
some cases where there has been a considerable time  lag between the date of 
notification of the Order and the date of its layin g. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where notifi cations have not been laid as 
per the schedule prescribed by the Committee and wh ere such notifications 
were not accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in 
laying. The observations/recommendations of the Com mittee in respect of the 
specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public D istribution 
 (Department of Food and Public Distribution) 
 
 Ministry of Consumer Affairs  (Department of Food and Public 
Distribution) have laid 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 2 1 to 25). Only 1 notification 
(Sl. No. 25) has been delayed by 6 Months and there  is no delay statement 
appended to it.  
  
   The Committee notes that there has been delay in la ying of one 
Notification and no delay statement has been attach ed to it. The 
Committee reiterates its recommendation contained i n its 135 
th  Report 
that in case of delay in laying Notifications etc.,  invariably a delay 
statement should be attached. The Ministry should e nsure to do that in 
future.  
 
(ii) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 18 notific ations (Sl. Nos. 26 to 
43), out of which 5 Notifications have been delayed and Four Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 28, 31-33) have delay statement appended to th em.  The delay period 
ranges from  4 months to 1 year and 3 months. There  is no delay statement  
appended to 1  Notification (Sl. No. 38). The reaso ns given by the Ministry in 

 
regard to delay in one Notification (Sl. No. 31) wh ere maximum delay of 15 
months has been noticed is as under:- 
 
 “ The  Notifications mentioned above could not be lai d before both the 
Houses of Parliament within the prescribed period a s the required number of 
copies of the Notifications could not be made avail able by the Institute due to 
misplacement and oversight on account of elections of the Central Council and 
Regional Councils, Elections held in December, 2010.” 
 
 The Committee is not convinced with the reason giv en by the 
Ministry of Corporate Affairs that delay occurred d ue to misplacement 
and oversight on account of elections of the Centra l Council and Regional 
Councils, held in December, 2010. It is surprising how the delay could 
extend upto 15 months due to the said reason. This reflects the 
carelessness and negligence  on the part of the Min istry in laying of the 
Notifications on the Table of the House within pres cribed time.  The 
Committee therefore, invites attention of the Minis try to the 
recommendations of the Committee as contained in it s 131
st   Report for 
timely laying of Notifications in Parliament.  
 
(iii)  Ministry of Defence 
 
Ministry of Defence have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 44 to 47), out of 
these, 2 Notifications have been delayed by 4 month s each. One Notification 
(Sl. No. 47) has delay statement appended to it whe reas no delay statement  
was appended with other Notification (Sl. No. 44). The reasons given by the 
Ministry is that it took some time to get the publi shed copies of the 
Notification from the Government of India Press. By  the time the Ministry 
received the printed copies of the Notification, th e Sittings of the Eighth (8 
th ) 
Session of Fifteenth Lok Sabha and Two Hundred and Twenty Third (223 rd ) 
Session of Rajya Sabha were concluded on 8 th  September, 2011. Due to this, 
the Notification could not be laid during Monsoon S ession of Parliament in 
2011. 
 
The Committee does not accept the late receipt of p rinted copies of 
Notifications from the Government of India Press as  a valid reason for a 
delayed laying of Notifications etc. The Ministry o f Defence  should in 
future ensure that all its papers are laid before t he House within the 
timeframe as prescribed in the Committee’s 131 
st  Report. Ministry should 
take it up with the Ministry of Urban Development f or late receiving of 
printing copies of the Notifications. 
 
 
 
 
 

 
(iv)  Ministry of Finance 
 (Department of Revenue) 
 
Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) have la id 74 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 79 to 152) during the Sessi on. Out of these, 7 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 107 – 113) have been delaye d by 4 to 5 months. There 
is no delay statement appended to any of these. 
 
The Committee appreciates the fact that the Ministr y of Finance 
(Department of Revenue) has to lay huge volume of s tatutory papers 
during Session,  while only 7 Notifications have be en delayed, none had 
delay statement appended to it.  As a result Commit tee does not know the 
reason for this delay. The Committee reiterate its recommendations 
contained in its 131 
st  and 135 th  Reports emphasizing timely laying of  
Notifications  on the Table of the House and append ing a delay statement 
where there has been a delay. No delay statement wi ll henceforth will be 
construed as negligence of the Ministry in this regard. 
 
(v) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare  
            
  The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare  have lai d 17 Notifications
 
(Sl. Nos. 153 to 169) out of which 8 Notifications have been delayed (Sl. Nos. 
160-162, 166-169). Seven Notifications  have been a ppended with delay 
statement and one Notification has no delay stateme nt appended to it. The 
delay period ranges from 9 months to 1 year and 1 month. The reason for delay 
in laying of various Food Safety and Standards Auth ority of India regulations 
is given as under:- 
  
 “These Regulations, being issued for the first tim e, could not be laid 
within the prescribed time frame in view of the fac t that FSSAI being a newly 
created statutory body, the required administrative  structure was not in place. 
The organization is working on the strength of larg ely technical officers 
transferred from different Ministries/Departments i n December, 2008/January, 
2009 for implementing the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.” 
 
  The Committee does not accept the reason given by  the Ministry 
that the Food Safety and Standards Authority of Ind ia (FSSAI) being a 
newly created  statutory body did not have  the req uired administrative 
structure due to which the Notifications could not be laid in time.  The 
organization has been working since December, 2008 and how it has taken 
more than one year for place the Notifications on t he Table of the House. 
Moreover, laying is basically Ministry’s obligation  and if the Ministry had 
framed the rules and modified it; why would it not lay on the Table of  
Parliament as well. Thus the  Committee feels that no serious efforts were 
made by the Ministry to lay the Notifications in ti me which is in 
contravention of its statutory obligation to Parlia ment. The Committee 

 
views it seriously and impresses upon the Ministry to take note of the fact 
that in case there is a delay in the laying of any Notifications, the same 
should be accompanied with a delay statement while it is being laid. The 
Committee also directs  the Ministry to avoid recur rence of such delays in 
future and append delay statement so as to  keep Pa rliament apprised of 
the reasons of delay.  
 
(vi)      Ministry of Home Affairs  
 
The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 25 Notificat ions (Sl. Nos. 
170-194) out of which 21 Notifications have been de layed. The delay period 
ranges from 4 months to 10 months. One delayed Noti fication (Sl. No. 194) 
has no delay statement attached with it and 20 Noti fications are appended with 
delay statement. (Sl. No. 170 - 189). The reasons f or delay given is that due to 
adjournment of the House these Notification could n ot be laid in the Monsoon 
Session, 2011.  
 
 The Committee takes serious note of the fact that d elay was caused 
due to adjournment of the House. Ministry of Home A ffairs should have 
taken the matter on priority basis so that Notifica tions would have been 
laid well before the adjournment of the House. This  possibility  should 
have been assessed well before and adequate arrange ments should have 
been made in respect of this. The Committee call up on the Ministry of 
Home Affairs to ensure that such delays do not recur in future. 
 
(vii) Ministry of Human Resource Development 
  (Department of School Education and Literacy) 
 
The Ministry of Human Resource Development (Departm ent of School 
Education and Literacy) have laid 5 Notifications. Out of these 3 Notifications 
(Sl. Nos. 195-197) have been delayed. The delay per iod ranges from 5 months 
to 9 months. None of the delayed Notifications have  been appended with delay 
statement.  
 
The Committee invites attention of the Ministry of Human 
Resource Development to its recommendations made in  135 
th  Report, 
wherein it is laid down that it is essential to app end a delay statement in 
case of delay in laying of statutory orders. The Co mmittee therefore, 
impresses upon the Ministry of Human Resource Devel opment 
(Department of School Education and Literacy) to ap pend delay 
statement in case of any delay and strictly adhere to the time schedule in 
laying of Notifications so as to avoid any delay. 
 
 
 
 

 
(viii) Ministry of Labour and Employment  
  
The Ministry of Labour and Employment have laid 2 N otifications (Sl. 
Nos. 200 - 201). Out of these 1 Notification (Sl. N o. 201) has been delayed by 
4 months. There is no delay statement appended to it. 
 
The Committee would like the Ministry of Labour and  
Employment to take necessary steps to obviate such delay and in case of 
delay in laying of Notifications, delay statement s hould invariably be 
accompanied with them. 
 
  
(ix) Ministry of Law and Justice 
 (Department of Legal Affairs)  
  
The only Notification that the Ministry of Law and Justice (Department 
of Legal Affairs (Sl. No. 203) have laid is delayed  by 4 months. There is no 
delay statement attached to it. 
 
The Committee never expects the Ministry of Law and  Justice 
(Department of Legal Affairs) to delay laying of pa pers and also not 
appending the delay statement. Appropriate steps be  taken to prevent any 
delay in future for laying of Notifications on the table of the  House and 
append delay statement. 
 
(x) Ministry of Power 
 
The Ministry of Power have laid only one Notificati on (Sl. No. 209) 
during this Session which has been delayed by 5 mon ths. The Ministry has not 
appended any delay statement to it.  
 
The Committee hopes that the Ministry would avoid s uch instances 
of delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation in fu ture. The Committee 
also impresses upon the Ministry to take note of th e fact that in case there 
is a delay in laying of any Statutory Notification,  the same should be 
accompanied by a delay  statement while it is being laid. 
 
  
(xi) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways have la id 56 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 213-268). There has been si gnificant number of 
Notifications which have been delayed. Out of 54 de layed Notifications only 1 
Notification (Sl. No. 236) has been attached with d elay statement and 53 
Notifications have no delay statement appended. The  delay period ranges from 
5 Months to 18 Months.  

 
 
The Committee is not happy over such large number o f delayed 
Notification. This is just not acceptable to the Co mmittee that 53 out of 56 
Notifications laid by the Ministry did not have the  delay statements, delay 
period being  more than one year in the case of som e notifications. While 
there may be a reason for the delay, but there cann ot be any reason for 
not furnishing reason for that. The Ministry must u nderstand the 
implications for not doing so regularly and moreove r in such a large 
number of cases. 
 
The Committee does not accept the reasons of the de lay given by 
the  Ministry of Road Transport and Highways that i t took some time to 
collect the papers and complete the procedural form alities. The 
Committee wonders how can it take one year and thre e months to receive 
Notifications and to complete procedural formalitie s. The Committee feels 
that Ministry did not take adequate precaution to a void the delay. The 
Committee further makes a point that Ministry shoul d append a delay 
statement so as to apprise the Parliament of the re asons of delay in laying 
of Notifications on the Table of the House.  
 
(xii) Ministry of Urban Development 
 During the Session, the Ministry of Urban Developm ent have laid 15 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 277 – 291). Out of which 6 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 286 
– 291) have been delayed by 6 to 8 months without a ny delay statement 
appended thereto.  
  
 The Committee time and again have emphasized that l aying of 
rules/regulations/orders is a statutory obligation and should be completed 
on priority within specified time-frame. The Minist ry of Urban 
Development should understand the implication of su ch failures. 
However, the Ministry of Urban Development has not complied with the 
recommendations of the Committee in this regard. Th e Committee once 
again emphasise that Ministry of Urban Development should adhere to 
the time schedule in laying of notifications as pre scribed in Committee’s 
131 
st  Report. The Committee also lay emphasis to append a delay 
statement alongwith Notifications that have been de layed while laying on 
the Table of Parliament House.  
 
3. The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/ Departments to take 
immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation. 
The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the defaulting 
ones, the following recommendations, as contained i n its 131 
st 
, 135 
th 
, 152 
nd    
and 192 nd  Reports: 

 
 “Orders required to be laid before the House shoul d be laid (i) if the 
House is not in Session, during the Session immedia tely following the date 
of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette  and (ii) if the House is 
in Session, on the date of the publication of the O rder, during its 
continuance and in case the time lag between the da te of publication and 
the date of the closing of the Session is less than  15 clear days, before the 
expiry of the Session immediately following the sai d Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India shoul d henceforth 
comply with this schedule in the matter of laying o f the Orders issued 
under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. 
(131 st  Report) 
(Presented on 15 December 2000) 
 
 
 “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized tha t it is essential 
that in case there is a delay in the laying of Stat utory Orders, the 
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably app end a delay 
statement along with the notifications being laid s o as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee de sires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, sinc e the intension is that 
through the process of formulation of a delay state ment the Ministry is 
able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial measures”. 
(135 th  Report) 
(Presented on 27 July 2001) 
 
 “In the opinion of the Committee, every notificati on should be 
assigned a distinctive number in order to give it a  separate identity and 
similarly, no notification should be published/laid  which does not carry a 
notification number. The Committee expects all Mini stries/Departments 
of Government of India to exercise caution in this regard. In case where 
there are more than one notification under a single  file, the 
Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number on each 
notification should be suitably distinguished from one another.” 
(152 
nd 
 Report) 
(Presented on 26 August 2004)  
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay 
in laying of Notifications on the Table of the Hous e, the Committee also 
express their deep concern over delay in framing of  rules/regulations 
under different Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report presented to the 
House on 14.9.1981 has recommended that the rule-ma king process 

 
should be completed within a period of six months of the coming into force 
on an Act. The committee has further recommended th at in case a 
Ministry is not able to adhere to this time limit, it should seek extension of 
time for completing the rule-making process. The re commendation of the 
committee reads as follows:- 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould 
well have been completed within the time limit of s ix months which 
the Committee has laid down for making of rules under an Act after 
coming into force of that Act. Where however, a Min istry does not 
find it possible to adhere to the time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the 
time limit and seek extension of time for completin g the rule-
making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are n ot complying with 
the Committee’s recommendation to complete the proc ess of framing of 
subordinate legislation contemplated under various Acts of Parliament 
within a period of six months. At the same time, th e Committee also does 
not receive requests for extension of time for comp leting the rule-making 
process in terms of its recommendations as above fr om most of the 
Ministries/Departments. The committee takes a serio us note of the 
prevailing situation in which the Ministries/Depart ments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts 
within the given six months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the 
Committee for seeking extension of time. The Commit tee further notes 
that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/reg ulations it has taken an 
initiative to monitor the progress of framing of su bordinate legislation in 
respect of all legislations brought about by differ ent 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India from the year 2003 
onwards with a view to ensure that the framing of s ubordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 

 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise 
undertaken on behalf of the Committee.  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates 
its recommendation that the Ministries/Departments should ensure 
putting in place subordinate legislation positively  within a period of six 
months of coming into force of the Act. The Committ ee further 
recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they 
should invariably approach the Committee for seekin g extension of time. 
The Committee further recommends that in cases wher e the time limit of 
six months is not adhered to, they should, while la ying rules/regulations 
before the Parliament invariably lay a statement ex plaining the reasons 
why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed be yond six months so as 
to keep the Parliament apprised of the compliance o f the Parliament’s 
mandate  conferred upon the Executive.” 
(192 
nd 
 Report) 
(Presented on 26 August 2011)
