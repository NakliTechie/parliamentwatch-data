2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note d that there was a considerable time 
lag between issue  of Notifications and their layin g in some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 117 delayed 
Notifications, there were 110 Notifications where n o delay statement was appended to. However, 
the bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministry of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 54.7% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 

 
(i)  Ministry of Communications and Information Technology 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
The Ministry of Communications and Information Tech nology (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 25 – 28). Out of these, 2 Notifications 
have been  delayed by 4 to 6 months. There is no de lay statement appended to one Notification 
(Sl. No. 27) although the other Notification has a delay statement appended (Sl. No. 26) to it. The 
reason for delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
  
 “The TRAI Regulation No. 2/2016 – Prohibition of D iscriminatory Tariffs for Data 
Services Regulations, 2016 dated the 8 
th  February, 2016 could not be laid down on 13 th  May, 
2016 on the table of Rajya Sabha due to the decisio n taken in the leaders meeting with the 
Hon’ble Chairman on 12 th  May, 2016 that no other business would be transacted.” 
 
 The reason of delay given in case of one Notificat ion by the Ministry is acceptable. 
But in so far as the one where no reason has been a ssigned, the Committee hopes that the 
Ministry would avoid such instances of delay in lay ing of subordinate legislation in future 
and would keep off situations, intentional or unint entional, causing delay. The Committee 
expects them to keep more vigil to avoid such unreasonable delays in future. 
 

 
(ii) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 The Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 21 Not ifications (Sl. Nos. 32 to 52). Out of 
these, one Notification (Sl. No. 41) has been delay ed by 4 months with delay statement appended 
thereto. The reason for delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “The Notification relating to the G.S.R. No. 397 ( E), dated 04.04.2016 on the Companies 
(Filing of Documents and Forms in Extensible Business Reporting Language) Amendment Rules, 
2016, was required to be laid before both the House s of Parliament during the Budget Session, 
2016. 
 The above Notification G.S.R. No. 397 (E) dated 04 .04.2016 on the Companies (Filing of 
Documents and Forms in Extensible Business Reporting Language) Amendment Rules, 2016 was 
sent to Government Press for printing. The said not ification could not be laid before both the 
Houses of Parliament during budget Session of the P arliament, 2016 due to delay in receiving the 
Gazette copy of the said notification by this Minis try and when it was collected, the Rajya Sabha 
session was over on 10.05.2016 and Lok Sabha on 13. 05.2016. The said notification got delayed 
and hence it may kindly be condoned.” 
 
 The reason furnished by the Ministry is unacceptab le. This is becoming a recurring 
feature in the responses by the Ministries. The Com mittee have time and again pointed out 

that delay in receiving printing copies of Notifications from the Government Press, is a lame 
excuse. Ministry of Corporate Affairs should plan b eforehand that ample time is given to 
printing press  so that printed copies are received  timely. The Committee suggests the 
Ministry of Corporate Affairs to take adequate prec aution to avoid such delay in future. In 
this regard Committee wants to draw the attention o f the Ministry to its 213 th  Report the 
Committee directs all the Ministries that in case t here is a delay in procuring printed copies 
in time, they may obtain G.S.R. and S.O. numbers fr om the Government of India Press and 
prepare requisite number of copies in cyclostyled f orm for laying in the Parliament. This 
had been even suggested by the controlling Ministry  (Urban Development) of Government 
of India Press so as to avoid such unwanted delays. 
 
(iii) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate C hange 
 
The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Cha nge have laid 9 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 56 to 64) on the Table of the House during the  Session. Out of these, 3 Notifications  (Sl. 
Nos. 61 to 63) have been delayed. The delay period is of about 4 months in each case and, 
moreover, no delay statement has been attached to any of these delayed Notifications.  
 
The Committee takes a strong view of the delay occu rred in tabling the Notifications 
in time and emphasise that the Ministry should avoi d such instances of delay in laying of 
Subordinate Legislation in future. The Committee al so impresses upon the Ministry to take 

note of the fact that in case, there is a delay in the laying of any Statutory Notifications, the 
same should be accompanied by a delay statement whi le it is being laid. The Ministry may 
clarify the reasons for delay in laying and also wh y the delay statement has not been 
appended to the Notifications. 
 
  
(iv) Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Af fairs) 
 
 The Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Af fairs) have laid 50 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 66 to 115).  Out of these. 36 Notifications (S l. Nos. 73 – 108) have been  delayed by 5 
months to 15 months. There is no delay statement appended to any of these delayed Notifications. 
 
This Committee regrets to say that there is delay i n laying of more than  70% of the 
total Notifications that the Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) have laid. 
Further, 10 Notifications have a delay period of mo re than one year and  9 Notifications 
have delay period of more than eleven months. This is not comprehensible to the Committee 
for it is left pondering as to what caused this ino rdinate delay. Furthermore, there is no 
delay statement appended to any of these delayed No tifications. No delay statement coupled  
with inordinate delay is quite enigmatic to the Com mittee. The Committee makes a point 
that Ministry should have appended a delay statement so as to apprise the Parliament of the 
reasons of delay in laying of Notifications on the Table of the House. However, the  Ministry 

of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) should also strive to cut on the excessive delay 
period in laying of the Notifications. 
 
(v) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
 (Department of Health and Family Welfare)  
 
 The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Departm ent of Health and Family Welfare)  
have laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 224 to 230). Ou t of these, 3 Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 224 to 
226) have been delayed by 7 to 9 months. The  Minis try did not even bother to append a delay 
statement to any of these.  
 
 The Committee is saddened to see that 3 out of 7 No tifications that the Ministry of 
Health and Family Welfare (Department of Health and  Family Welfare) have laid, have 
been delayed. Further, there is no reason assigned for this delay.  This is in contravention of 
Committee’s recommendation given in its 135 Report that Notifications which are delayed 
should invariably be accompanied by a delay stateme nt. The Committee would like the 
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Department of Health and Family Welfare) to take 
necessary steps to obviate such delays, and, in cas e of delay in laying of Notifications, delay 
statement should invariably be appended to them. 
 
(vi) Ministry of Home Affairs 

  
The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 20 Notificat ions (Sl. Nos. 231 to 250) and only 3 
of them have been delayed, with delay of 4 months i n each case (Sl. Nos. 240-242) accompanied 
with delay statement. The reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:-  
 
“(i) The National Disaster Response Force, Group “B ” Gazetted,   Non-
Combatised Posts Recruitment (Amendment) Rules, 2015   (GSR 54); 
(ii) The National Disaster Response Force, Group “A ” Gazetted,  
 Combatised Posts Recruitment (Amendment) Rules, 20 15 (GSR   55); and  
(iii) The National Disaster Response Force, Group ‘ B’ and Group ‘  C’ 
Non-Gazette, Combatised Posts Recruitment (Amendment)   Rules, 2015 (GSR 56). 
  
 As per sub-section (1) and clause (f) of sub-secti on (2) of section 75 of the Disaster 
Management Act, 2005 (53 of 2005) the said Rules we re framed & published in the Gazette of 
India. As defined in the procedure all Rules or ‘Or ders’ required to be laid before the House 
should be so laid within a period of 15 days after their publication in the Gazette, if the House is 
in Session, and, if the House is not in Session, th e Rules or ‘Orders’  should be laid on the Table 
of the House as soon as possible (but in any case w ithin 15 days) after the Commencement of the 
following session. 

2. However, the extant Rules (GSR 54 to GSR 56) pub lished in the Gazette on 21 st  March, 
2015 could not be laid before both the Houses of Pa rliament in time due to late receipt of the 
Rules from the Press and inadvertent reasons. 
3. The delay in laying the said Notifications befor e the House is deeply regretted and it is 
requested to condone the delay which occurred due to inadvertent reasons.” 
 
 The  Committee notes with regret that the Ministry  of Home Affairs have failed to 
strictly adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in its 131 
st  Report. In so far as the 
procurement of printed copies from Government of In dia Press is concerned, they may 
resort to the recommendation of the Committee given  time and again, by procuring a 
G.S.R./S.O. number from the Government of India Pre ss and lay it. The Committee hopes 
that the Ministry of Home Affairs would put an effe ctive mechanism in place to eliminate 
such delays in future, particularly on account of d elay in receiving printed copies of 
Notifications. The Committee hopes that such delays would not recur in future.  
 
(vii) Ministry of Power 
 
The Ministry of Power have laid 7 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 284 – 290) out of which two of 
them (Sl. Nos. 284-285) have been delayed by 8 to 9  months respectively. Delay statements are 
appended to both of them. The reasons cited by the Ministry for delay is given below:- 
 

“Notifications Nos. 08/01/2015-BBMB, dated 20 th  October, 2015 and 09/01/2015-BBMB, 
dated the 11 th  December, 2015 were issued by Ministry of Power fo r amendment in BBMB 
Rules, 1974 allowing CPSU officials to join BBMB in Board Level Capacity. 
The above Notifications were  published in the Gaze tte of India (Extraordinary) and 
weekly in the month of October, 2015 and December, 2015 respectively and should have been 
laid on the table of House during the Budget Sessio n of Parliament, 2016. However, the same 
could not be laid on the tables of Parliament due t o change in procedure at Government of India 
Press for obtaining copies of official gazette. The  stabilization of the system change from manual 
to digital publication has taken some time.” 
 
The Committee is constrained to note  the lapse on part of the Ministry of Power 
causing such delay and advises the Ministry to take  necessary anticipatory and advanced 
steps so that loopholes are plugged in so as to ensure that such delays do not recur. 
 
(viii) Ministry of Railways 
  
  The Ministry of Railways have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 291 to 294). One 
Notification (Sl. No. 293) has been delayed by six months and there is no delay statement 
appended with the Notification. 
 

The Committee desires that the Ministry of Railways  should strictly adhere to the 
time schedule as recommended by it in its 131st Rep ort in laying of the Subordinate 
Legislation before Parliament. The Ministry is remi nded that in case of any delay in laying 
of the Notifications, a delay statement should also invariably be appended therewith so as to 
apprise the Parliament of the real causes for the same. 
 
 
 
 
 (ix) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
 The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways have lai d 75 Notifications
 on the Table of 
the House during the Session. Out of these, 64 Noti fications have been delayed. None of these 
have delay statements appended thereto and  the delay period ranges from 4 months to 14 months.  
   
 The Committee takes note of the fact that the Mini stry of Road Transport and 
Highways have to lay  a huge volume of Notification s on the table of the House.  The 
Committee, however, finds that the percentage of de layed Notifications, out of total 
Notifications laid, is quite high i.e. as high as o ver 84.7%. This is simply unacceptable. 
Further, the Ministry have not appended any delay s tatement with any of the  delayed 
Notifications/Rules. Ministry of Road Transport and  Highways should look into the matter 

seriously to eliminate recurrence of such delays in  completing the procedural formalities. 
The Committee desires that Ministry of Road Transpo rt and Highways should adhere to 
the time schedule in laying of Notifications as pre scribed in the Committee’s 131 st  Report 
and append a delay statement without fail in case t here is  delay in laying. This has, sort of 
become a matter of routine with this Ministry, its seems. The Ministry has been reminded 
on this issue on umpteen number of times in the pas t. The Ministry may clarify the reasons 
for delay in laying and also why the delay statemen t has not been appended to the 
Notifications. 
 
(x) Ministry of Textiles 
  
 The Ministry of Textiles have laid 2 Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 379 – 380) and one of these 
(Sl. No. 379) has been delayed by 6 months. There i s no delay statement appended to the 
Notification. 
  
The Committee notes with concern the delay in layin g of one of its Notification on 
the Table of the House. The Committee notices  the Ministry of Textiles   had communicated 
the circumstances causing delay in laying of Notifi cation and also to have appended a delay 
statement therefore. But the Committee finds that t he Ministry of Textiles   have not only 
delayed the laying of one Notification but also did  not append any delay statement thereto. 
The Committee, therefore, advises the Ministry   to strictly adhere to the time schedule in 

future in laying of the Notifications and append a delay statement where there is delay in 
laying of Notification on the Table of the House. T he Committee recommends that the 
Ministry may clarify the reasons for delay in layin g and also why the delay statement has 
not been appended to the Notification. 
 
 
 
 
(xi) Ministry of Urban Development 
 
 Ministry of Urban Development have laid 3 Notifica tions (Sl. Nos. 381 – 383) out of 
which one Notification (Sl. No. 382) is delayed by 4 months and  no  delay statement  is attached  
thereto. 
 
 The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendatio n, that in case of delay in laying, 
Notifications should be accompanied with delay stat ement. Attention to recommendation 
contained in 135 
th  Report of the Committee is drawn here. The Ministr y of Urban 
Development should take care so that repetition of such delay is avoided in future. They 
should take such matters of delay very seriously.

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Committee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force of an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Mi nistry is not able to adhere to this 
time limit, it should seek extension of time for co mpleting the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 

 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are n ot complying with the Committee’s 
recommendation to complete the process of framing of subordinate legislation contemplated 
under various Acts of Parliament within a period of  six months. At the same time, the 
Committee also does not receive requests for extens ion of time for completing the rule-
making process in terms of its recommendations as a bove from most of the 
Ministries/Departments. The Committee takes a serio us note of the prevailing situation in 
which the Ministries/Departments are neither able t o complete the task of framing 
rules/regulations under different Acts within the g iven six months’ period nor do they suo-
motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. The Committee further notes 
that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/reg ulations it has taken an initiative to 
monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legi slation in respect of all legislations 
brought about by different Ministries/Departments o f Government of India from the year 
2003 onwards with a view to ensure that the framing  of subordinate legislation is not 

delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the res ponses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its 
recommendation that the Ministries/Departments shou ld ensure putting in place 
subordinate legislation positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the 
Act. The Committee further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this 
deadline, they should invariably approach the Committee for seeking extension of time. The 
Committee further recommends that in cases where th e time limit of six months is not 
adhered to, they should, while laying rules/regulat ions before the Parliament invariably lay 
a statement explaining the reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond 
six months so as to keep the Parliament apprised of  the compliance of the Parliament’s 
mandate  conferred upon the Executive.”

  Rules/Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are laid by dif ferent Ministries on the Table of 
the House under various provisions of parent Acts p assed by Parliament. These Rules/ 
Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are published in the of ficial Gazette before being laid on the 
Table of the House. Even after they are laid, it is  very difficult to get access to these 

Notifications. The Committee recommends that such R ules/Regulations/Orders, etc. laid on 
the Table of the House should be invariably uploade d on the websites of respective 
Ministries immediately on the same day when they ha ve been laid on the Table of the 
House, for information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access. 
201 st  Report 
(Presented on 14 th  December, 2012)  
 
 
The Committee observes that the whole process of en acting laws is defeated if rules 
are not framed and laid on the Table of the House i n time.  A reasonable delay could be 
understood but unjustifiably long delays should be avoided and the Ministries should work 
towards improving this position.   The Committee hopes that Ministry would strive to k eep 
the delay to the minimum and take care that Notific ations are laid on the Table of the 
House at the first available opportunity after publication of Notifications. 
  
 
The Committee would like to impress upon the Minist ry that it is their responsibility 
to ensure that copies of the Notifications are prin ted timely and for this purpose, they need 
to evolve a suitable mechanism to ensure that notifications are laid in time.   The Committee 
is happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  

The Committee hopes that it would take action accor dingly in order to comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications. 
 
The Ministry must take appropriate step to remove l acuna, if any, administrative or 
otherwise, causing such long delay by being little proactive and prompt.  Further, the 
Ministries should take it up with the Ministry of U rban Development for late receiving of 
printed copies of the Notification.
