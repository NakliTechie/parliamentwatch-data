2. While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted 
that there was a considerable time lag between issue  of 
Notifications and their laying in some cases. The annexed 
statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where 
Notifications have not been laid as per the time schedule 
prescribed by the Committee. The Committee finds that out 
of the 67 delayed Notifications, no delay statement was 
appended to 49 Notifications. The bulk of delayed 
Notifications were from the Ministry of  Home Affairs and 
Ministry of Finance, (both 13 Notifications) which comes to 
around 19.4% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee 
in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments where delay 
in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
 
 
 
 
(i) Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare 
 (Department of Agriculture, Cooperation and 
Farmers Welfare) 
 
 The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare 
(Department of Agriculture, Cooperation and Farmers 
Welfare) laid 9 Notifications (Sl. No. 1 to 9) during the 
Session. Out of these, two Notifications (Sl. Nos. 2 & 9) have 

been delayed by 7 and 6 months respectively and no delay 
statement was attached to any of these Notifications. 
 
 The Committee have clearly mentioned in its 135th 
Report that delay statements should invariably be 
appended where there is delay in laying of Notifications. 
The Committee, in the absence of delay statements, are 
not able to know the reasons behind the delay in laying of 
these Notifications. The Committee, therefore, impresses 
upon the Ministry to strictly adhere to the Committee’s 
recommendations  that in case there is  any delay in 
laying, a delay statement should invariably be appended 
so that the valid reasons for such delay are brought to its 
notice of the Committee.  
(ii) Ministry of Coal 
 
The Ministry of Coal have laid only one Notification 
(Sl. No. 19) which was delayed by 7 months and, moreover, 
Ministry did not even bother to append a delay statement 
citing the reasons for such delay on their part. 
 
 The Committee notes that the only Notification 
that Ministry of Coal have laid, has been delayed by seven 
months. Further, there is no reason given for this delay.  
This is in contravention of Committee’s recommendation 
given in its 135th Report that Notifications which are 
delayed should invariably be accompanied by a delay 
statement. The Committee would like the Ministry of Coal 
to take necessary steps to obviate such delays and, in case 
of delay in laying of Notifications, a delay statement 
should invariably be accompanied with them. 
 
(iii) Ministry of Communications and Information 
Technology 

 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
 The Ministry of Communications and Information 
Technology  (Department of Telecommunications)  have laid 
11 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 31 to 41). Two of them (Sl. Nos. 31 
& 32) have been delayed by 5 months each and are 
accompanied with delay statement. The reason given by the 
Ministry for delay is as under:- 
  
"The TRAI Regulation No. 5/2018 - 
Telecommunications Consumers Education 
and Protection Fund (Fourth Amendment) 
Regulations, 2018, dated 18.07.2018  and 
TRAI Regulation No. 6/2018 - The Telecom 
Commercial Communications Customer 
Preference Regulations, 2018, dated 
19.07.2018 could not be laid down in 
Monsoon Session, 2018 due to some 
unavoidable administrative delay." 
 
 The Committee is not satisfied with the reasons  
'unavoidable' stated for delay by the Ministry of 
Communications and Information Technology  
(Department of Telecommunications) and recommends 
that more elaborate statement giving reasons for delay 
needs to be appended with the notification. To obviate 
such instances of delay in laying of subordinate legislation 
in future, the Ministry should take necessary steps, 
administrative or otherwise, to address the issues causing 
delay. 
 
(iv) Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public 
Distribution 
 (Department of Consumer Affairs) 

 
 The Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public 
Distribution (Department of Consumer Affairs) have laid 8 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 42 – 49) and one of these (Sl. No. 49) 
has been delayed by 9 months. There is no delay statement 
appended to the Notification. 
  
The Committee notes with concern the delay in 
laying of one of the Notification of the Ministry of 
Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution 
(Department of Consumer Affairs) on the Table of the 
House. The Committee notes that  the Ministry had not 
communicated the circumstances causing delay in laying 
of the Notification as no delay statement has been 
attached thereto. The Committee, therefore, advises the 
Ministry  to strictly adhere to the time schedule in future 
in laying of the Notifications and append a delay 
statement where there is delay in laying of Notification on 
the Table of the Council.  
 
(v) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate 
Change 
 
 The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate 
Change have laid 9 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 96 to 104).  Out of 
these, 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 96 & 97) have been delayed 
by  4 months each and no delay statement appended  to any of 
them. 
 
 The Committee is constrained to note that there 
has been delay in  laying of the two Notifications by the 
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. 
Moreover, there is no delay statement appended to throw 
light on the factors which caused this delay. Further, the 

Committee desires that delay statements should 
invariably be appended where there is delay in laying of 
Notifications. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon 
the Ministry to strictly adhere to the time schedule as 
prescribed in Committee’s 131st Report and,  in case of 
any delay in laying, a delay statement needs to be 
appended. 
 
(vi) Ministry of Finance 
 (a)  (Department of Economic Affairs) 
 
 The Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic 
Affairs) have laid 47 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 105 to 151).  Out 
of these, 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 111 & 112) have been  
delayed by 8 months and 5 months respectively and no delay 
statement, were appended to any of these delayed 
Notifications.  
 
 The Committee notes that out of 47 Notifications 
laid by the Ministry of  Finance (Department of Economic 
Affairs) two Notifications were delayed and delay 
statements were not attached. The Committee would like 
to impress upon the Ministry of Finance (Department of 
Economic Affairs) that it is obligatory on the part of the 
Ministry to communicate the circumstances causing delay 
in laying of Notifications and also to append delay 
statement thereto. The Committee would, therefore, 
advise the  Ministry  to strictly adhere to the time 
schedule in laying of the Notifications and if there is delay 
then to append a delay statement. 
 
(b)  (Department of Financial Services) 
 

 The Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial 
Services) have laid 16 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 152 to 167), out 
of which, 7 Notifications have been delayed.  Out of 7 
delayed Notifications,  6  Notifications (Sl. Nos. 152 to 156 & 
164) had no delay statement appended to them, while delay 
statement had been attached to one notification (Sl. No. 160). 
The reasons forwarded by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 “The Notification dated 17.2.2018 regarding amendment 
in Corporation Bank Officers' Service Regulations, 1982, 
published in the Gazette of India, vide No. 15 on 14.4.2018 - 
20.4.2018. 
 As the Bank did not furnish the requisite copies of 
Gazette Notification within the stipulated period, clarification 
was sought from Corporation Bank. 
 The bank has reported that due to communication gap 
from the Publication Department with the bank, requisite 
copies of Gazette Notification regarding amendment in 
Officers' Service Regulations could not be forwarded for 
laying within stipulated time. It has further expressed regret 
for inadvertent omission. 
 In view of the above, the said Notification could not be 
laid on the Table of the both the Houses on earlier occasions. 
Delay in this regard is deeply regretted and may please be 
condoned." 
 
 The Committee notes the time taken by the 
Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) in 
laying of their Notifications, which ranges from seven 
months to twenty months.  The Committee is unable to 

accept the reasons given by the Ministry of Finance 
(Department of Financial Services) for the delay in laying 
due to communication gap from the Publication 
Department with the bank, and that requisite copies of 
Gazette Notification regarding amendment in Officers' 
Service Regulations could not be forwarded for laying 
within the stipulated time. Further, the Committee is 
astounded by the fact that the remaining 6 Notifications 
had no delay statements attached thereto. It only reflects 
the lack of seriousness in the part of the Ministry in 
fulfilling its constitutional responsibility towards the 
Parliament. The Committee directs the Ministry to be 
careful about laying of its Subordinate Legislation in time. 
 
 
(c) (Department of Revenue) 
 
 The Ministry of Finance (Department Revenue) have 
laid 151 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 168 – 318). Out of these, 4 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 214, 215, 217 and 218) have been  
delayed with delay periods ranging from  5 months to 7 
months and no delay statement has been attached  to any of 
the notifications so delayed. 
 
 The Committee takes a serious view not only for 
the delay in laying of the four Notifications  on the Table 
of the Council,  but also for its failure to append the 
required delay statement thereto. The 
Ministry/Department must realise the possible 
implications of failing to meet this requirement that 
tantamounts to diluting Executive’s accountability to the 
Parliament. It must, therefore,  ensure to lay the 
Notifications well within the time frame and if there is a 

delay, it must append a delay statement in all cases in 
future. 
 
 (vii) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
 (Department of Health and Family Welfare) 
 
 The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
(Department of Health and Family Welfare) have laid 22 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 323 - 344), out of  these 9 
Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 323 to 329 and 335 & 336) have been 
delayed. The delay period ranges from 8 months to 11 months 
and no delay statement has been attached to any of these 
delayed Notifications. 
 
The Committee finds it astounding that 40.9% (9 
out of 22) Notifications laid by the Ministry were delayed 
and none of these notifications have any delay statements 
attached to them. While there may be a reason for the 
delay, there cannot be any reason for not furnishing the 
statement therefor. The Ministry of Health and Family 
Welfare (Department of Health and Family Welfare) must 
understand the implications for not doing so regularly 
and that too in such a large number of cases. The 
Committee finds this callous approach of the  Ministry 
unacceptable and directs that in future, it should avoid 
delay and if it happens, then invariably append a delay 
statement. 
 
 (viii) Ministry of Home Affairs 
  
The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 33 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 345 –377). Out of  these,  13 
Notifications  have been delayed. Whereas 12 Notifications 
(Sl. Nos. 345, 347 to  351, 365 to 359  & 370) have been 

delayed for periods ranging from of 5 months to 7 months and 
are not accompanied with delay statement. However,  one 
Notification (Sl. No. 372) has been delayed by 5 months and 
a delay statement has been appended to it. The reasons of 
delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
“As per Section 155 of  sub-section (2) of the 
Sashastra Seema Bal Act, 2007 (53 of 2007), the Recruitment 
Rules for the posts of Head Constable (Electrician), Constable 
(Mason), Constable (Carpenter), Constable (Plumber), 
Constable (Black Smith) and Constable (Painter) namely 
Sashastra Seema Bal (Combatised) Engineering Cadre Group 
'C' posts Recruitment Rules, 2018 notified vide GSR 220 
dated 17.07.2018, under sub-section (3) of Section 155 of 
SSB Act, 2007, were required to be laid before the House of 
Parliament while it is in Session for a total period of 30 days 
soon after these were made. The hard copy of the notification 
was downloaded from web-site (E-gazette) of the 
Government of India Press on 02.08.2018. The Recruitment 
Rules, however could not be laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha 
during the previous Monsoon Session, 2018 before closure of 
the Session due to the fact that by the time of its submission 
on 07.08.2018 the laying schedule was already over. 
 
2. Accordingly, Recruitment Rules are now being laid 
down on the Table of Rajya Sabha in the current Session.” 
 
The reasons cited by Ministry of Home Affairs,  i.e. 
'Notification could not be laid on the Table because the 
copies of Notification was downloaded from web-site (E-
gazette) of the Government of India Press on 02.08.2018  
during the previous Monsoon Session, 2018 before closure 
of the Session due to the fact that by the time of its 
submission on 07.08.2018 the laying schedule was already 

over,'  is not a convincing reason. Further, no effort has 
been made to lay it on the Table of the Council during the 
247th and 248th Session. However, the more serious  issue 
is that 13 Notifications were delayed and no delay 
statements were appended in case of 12 of these delayed 
Notifications. It is obligatory on the part the Ministry to 
lay the notification within the prescribed time schedule. 
The Committee, therefore, impresses upon  the Ministry 
to  devise suitable mechanism to avoid such instances of 
delay in future, particularly, on account of delay in getting 
printed copies of Notifications from Printing Press so as to 
adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in Committee’s 
131st Report. 
 
(ix) Ministry of  Housing  and Urban Affairs 
 
The Ministry of Housing  and Urban Affairs have laid 
5 Notifications (Sl.Nos. 378 - 382), out of these only one 
Notification (Sl.No.382) has been delayed and that too by 8 
months and is accompanied by a  delay statement. The reason 
given by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 
 "In accordance with Section 86 (1) of the Real Estate 
(Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, every rule made by 
the Central Government, every regulation made by the 
Authority under the Union Territory of Delhi and the Union 
Territories without Legislature and every notification issued 
by the Central Government under this Act, shall be laid, as 
soon as may be after it is made before each House of 
Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty 
days which may be comprised in one session or in two or 
more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the 
session immediately following the session or the successive 
sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any 

modification in the rule of regulation or in the notification, as 
the case may be, or both Houses agree that the rule or 
regulation or the notification should not be made, the rule or 
regulation or notification, as the case may be shall thereafter 
have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as 
the case may be, so however, that any such modification or 
annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of 
anything previously done under that rule or regulation or 
notification, as the case may be. 
 As per above provision, the above mentioned 
Notification was to be laid in both the Houses of Parliament 
during the Budget Session of 2017, but it got delayed 
inadvertently." 
 
The Committee notes the reason given by the 
Ministry of Housing  and Urban Affairs that caused delay 
in laying of the Notifications and finds it very cryptic as 
no specific reason for the delay has been furnished. The 
Committee, though, appreciates that a delay statements 
has been attached. But the Committee, feels that tabling of 
Gazette Notification is of utmost public importance and it 
is prime responsibility of the Ministry to discharge its 
duty to Parliament and getting it delayed inadvertently is 
inexcusable. 
   
 
(x) Ministry of Labour and Employment 
 
 The Ministry of Labour and Employment  have laid 5 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 406 – 410). Only 1 Notification (Sl. 
No. 406) has been delayed by 9 months and there is no delay 
statement appended to it.  
  

  The Committee notes that though there has been 
delay in laying of one Notification of the Ministry, but  no 
delay statement has been attached to it. The Committee 
expects the Ministry to be careful and responsible in these 
matters henceforth.  The Ministry of Labour and 
Employment should ensure such compliance in future 
with due caution so that such recurrence does’nt take 
place. 
 
 
(xi) Ministry of Law and Justice  
 (Department of Legal Affairs) 
  
The Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of 
Legal Affairs) have laid 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 411 – 415). 
Out  of these, 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 411 to 414) have  been 
delayed. The delay period ranges from 5 months to 8 months 
and no delay statement have been attached to any of these 
delayed Notifications.  
 
The Committee is astounded by the fact that  the 
Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Legal 
Affairs) did not  lay its papers on the Table of the Council 
in time. The Committee expects a lot from this Ministry 
and being the custodian of Law and Justice of the country, 
it is their primal duty  to follow the established law and 
moreso to lead by example. Further, the Ministry of  Law 
and Justice did not append any delay statement to these 
delayed notifications. The Committee finds this 
unacceptable. In all the delayed cases, the delays ranges 
from 5 months to 8 months, and the Ministry has not 
taken any responsibility to inform the Council of the 
reasons for the delays. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that appropriate steps may be taken to 

obviate any such delay in future for laying of Notifications 
on the Table of the  House by the Ministry of Law and 
Justice (Department of Legal Affairs) and in case of 
delays, a delay statement should invariably be appended 
thereto. 
 
 
(xii) Ministry of Power 
 
The Ministry of Power have laid 10 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 424  – 433). Out of  these,  8  Notifications  have been 
delayed. Whereas 6 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 424  to  429) have

months to 3 years and 5 months, these were accompanied by 
delay statements. However,  2 Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 430  
and 431) has been delayed by 11 months and one year 
respectively and no delay statement has been appended to 
them. The reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
(i)  "The Bureau of Energy Efficiency (Particulars

(iii) The Bureau of Energy Efficiency (Particulars

(iv) Energy Conservation (Energy Consumption

(v) Energy Conservation (Energy Consumption

(vi) Energy Conservation (The Form & Manner for

Amendment Rules, 2015 - published in the Gazette  of India, 
Extraordinary, Part-II - Section 3, sub-section (i)  dated 
 19 th November, 2015. 
 
The above said Regulations at Sl. No. (i) to (iii) have 
been published/notified in the Gazette of India Extraordinary 
in exercise of the powers conferred under Section 58 of the 
Energy Conservation Act, 2001. Similarly, the above said 
Regulations at Sl. No. (iv) to (vi) have been 
published/Notified in the Gazette of India Extraordinary in 
exercise of the powers conferred under Section 56 of the 
Energy Conservation Act, 2001. 
 

2. Under the provisions contained in the sub-section (1) 
of the section 59 of the EC Act, 2001, every rule/regulation 
made by the Central Government and every regulation made 
under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is 
made, before each House of Parliament. 
 
3. It is submitted that the files of the respective 
notifications were inadvertently sent for recording without 
completing the requirement of laying them in the Parliament. 
 
4. It is requested to kindly condone the delay in laying 
the above notifications." 
  
 The Notifications could not be laid on time due to 
inadvertence and the notifications were sent for recording 
without completing the requirement of laying them in the 
Parliament, is not acceptable to the Committee. The 
Committee wonders as to how it has taken such a  long 
period of time ranging from 2 years and 4 months to 3 
years and 5 months for the Ministry to realize their 
mistake. It only reflects the lack of seriousness on the part 
of the Ministry in fulfilling its responsibility to Parliament 
on one hand and also to deny the rights of Members to 
analyses/study such Notification and move motion for 
modification/annulment etc. The Ministry should find 
some suitable mechanism to eliminate such instances of 
delays in future. The Committee expects the Ministry to 
be more careful about laying of its papers and see that 
persons responsible for this are held accountable so that 
there are no such recurrences. 
 
(xiii) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 

 The Ministry of Road Transport and  Highways have 
laid 67 Notifications on the Table of the House (Sl. Nos. 441 
– 507).  A total of 9 Notifications have been delayed out of 
which 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 491 to 497) are accompanied 
by delay statements and 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 441 & 442) 
have no delay statements appended with them. The delay 
period ranges from 5 months to 11 months. The reasons given 
by the Ministry is that it took some time to complete the 
procedural formalities and, therefore, papers could not be laid 
in time.. 
 
 The reasons for delay given by the Ministry of 
Road Transport and Highways that it took some time to 
collect the papers and complete the procedural 
formalities, is not convincing. Instead, it shows the 
Ministry in very poor light and its careless attitude 
towards discharging its statutory obligation. The 
Committee wonders as to how can it take 5 months to 11  
months to receive Notifications and to complete 
procedural formalities. The Committee feels that Ministry 
did not take adequate precaution to avoid such  delays. 
The Committee further takes a serious view of the fact 
that even after delay of five months and nine months in 
certain case, Ministry did not feel it necessary to append a 
delay statement so as to apprise the Parliament  of the 
reasons for delay in laying of Notifications on the Table. 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should put 
in place measures to avoid delay in completing the 
procedural formalities in time so that such delays are 
avoided.

(7th  Report)

Formula for laying of Statutory Rules 
 
 The Committee approve the following draft revised 
formula for laying of Statutory Rules before both Houses 
of Parliament which the Ministry of Law and Justice 
propose to incorporate in all future legislation which 
provide for making of rules by the Central 
Government:— 
 
 "Every rule made by the Central Government under 
this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is 
made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in 
session, for a total period of thirty days which may be 
comprised in one session or in two or more successive 
sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session 
immediately following the session or the successive 
sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any 
modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the 
rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter 
have effect only in such modified form or be of no 

effect, as the case may be; so, however that any such 
modification or annulment shall be without prejudice 
to the validity of anything previously done under that 
rule." 
  
The Committee recommend that the revised formula 
should be incorporated not only in new legislation but also 
in the existing Acts as and when Bills to amend them are 
brought before Parliament. The Ministry should also 
incorporate the new formula in the Bills pending before 
Parliament. This will, in the opinion of the Committee, 
bring about uniformity in the matter expeditiously. 
 
(10th  Report) 
(presented on 24th May, 1971) 
 
Time period for laying 
 
 “Orders required to be laid before the House should 
be laid (i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session 
immediately following the date of publication of the Order 
in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, 
on the date of the publication of the Order, during its 
continuance and in case the time lag between the date of 
publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less 
than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session 
immediately following the said Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should 
henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of 
laying of the Orders issued under various Acts of 
Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.

Time limit for rule making process

could well have been completed within the time 
limit of six months which the Committee has laid 
down for making of rules under an Act after 
coming into force of that Act. ................. Where

(47th Report) 
(Presented on 14th  September 1981) 
 
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the 
Committee over delay in laying of Notifications on the 
Table of the House, the Committee also express their deep 
concern over delay in framing of rules/regulations under 
different Acts. The Committee in its 47th report presented 
to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended that the rule-
making process should be completed within a period of six 
months of the coming into force of an Act. The Committee 
has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not 
able to adhere to this time limit, it should seek extension 
of time for completing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 

  “The Committee feels that the rule-
making process could well have been completed 
within the time limit of six months which the 
Committee has laid down for making of rules 
under an Act after coming into force of that Act. 
Where however, a Ministry does not find it possible 
to adhere to the time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make 
rules within the time limit and seek extension of 
time for completing the rule-making process.” 
  
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the 
fact that the Ministries/Departments of Government of 
India are not complying with the Committee’s 
recommendation to complete the process of framing of 
subordinate legislation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the same 
time, the Committee also does not receive requests for 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process 
in terms of its recommendations as above from most of 
the Ministries/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which the 
Ministries/Departments are neither able to complete the 
task of framing rules/regulations under different Acts 
within the given six months’ period nor do they suo-motu 
approach the Committee for seeking extension of time. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure 
timely framing of rules/regulations it has taken an 
initiative to monitor the progress of framing of 
subordinate legislation in respect of all legislations 
brought about by different Ministries/Departments of 
Government of India from the year 2003 onwards with a 
view to ensure that the framing of subordinate legislation 

is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the 
responses from the Ministries/Departments have not been 
prompt in this monitoring exercise undertaken on behalf 
of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once 
again reiterates its recommendation that the 
Ministries/Departments should ensure putting in place 
subordinate legislation positively within a period of six 
months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet 
with this deadline, they should invariably approach the 
Committee for seeking extension of time. The Committee 
further recommends that in cases where the time limit of 
six months is not adhered to, they should, while laying 
rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a 
statement explaining the reasons why the framing of 
rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to 
keep the Parliament apprised of the compliance of the 
Parliament’s mandate  conferred upon the Executive.” 
(192nd Report) 
(Presented on 26th  August 2011)  
 
 
The Committee observes that the whole process of 
enacting laws is defeated if rules are not framed and laid 
on the Table of the House in time.  A reasonable delay 
could be understood but unjustifiably long delays should 
be avoided and the Ministries should work towards 
improving this position.  The Committee hopes that 
Ministry would strive to keep the delay to the minimum 
and take care that Notifications are laid on the Table of 

the House at the first available opportunity after 
publication of Notifications. 
 
The Committee would like to impress upon the 
Ministry that it is their responsibility to ensure that copies 
of the Notifications are printed timely and for this 
purpose, they need to evolve a suitable mechanism to 
ensure that notifications are laid in time.   The Committee 
is happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal 
mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  The Committee 
hopes that it would take action accordingly in order to 
comply with the prescribed schedule in the matter of 
laying of statutory Notifications. 
 
The Ministry must take appropriate step to remove 
lacuna, if any, administrative or otherwise, causing such 
long delay by being little proactive and prompt.  Further, 
the Ministries should take it up with the Ministry of 
Urban Development for late receiving of printed copies of 
the Notification.

Uploading Notifications on websites 
 
  Rules/Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are laid by 
different Ministries on the Table of the House under 
various provisions of parent Acts passed by Parliament. 
These Rules/ Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are published 
in the official Gazette before being laid on the Table of the

that such Rules/Regulations/Orders, etc. laid on the Table

for information of Members of Parliament and people at

3. The Committee during the course of it discussion 
on the 239th and 240th Reports took note of the delay in 
laying of rules/regulations under different Acts. The 
Committee was of the opinion that the 
Ministries/Departments should strictly follow the 
recommendations of the Committee regarding the laying 
of rules/regulations under different Acts as contained in 
131st Report of the Committee. The Committee thereupon 
decided to recommend that in cases where the prescribed 
time limit for laying of Subordinate Legislation is not 
adhered to by the Ministries/Departments, they should, 
while laying the rules/regulations before the Parliament, 
also lay a statement explaining the reasons for the said 
delay so as to keep the Parliament apprised of the reasons 
for the delay. 
4. Thereafter, the Committee adopted the reports 
unanimously. The Committee authorized the Chairman 
and in his absence Shri V. Vijayasai Reddy and Shri 

Amar Patnaik to present the reports to the House on the 
12th December, 2019.
