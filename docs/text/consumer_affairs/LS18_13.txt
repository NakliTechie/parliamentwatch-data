CHAPTER I 
INFTODUCTORY 
 
(i) An overview 
  The Department of Consumer Affairs (DoCA) is one of the two 
Departments under the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public 
Distribution. The mandate of the Departments is consumer advocacy. India 
was pioneer in consumer advocacy with the Consumer Protection Act (CPA), 
a path breaking legislation at the time, enacted in 1986 and the establishment 
of a separate government department dedicated to consumer affairs as early 
as in 1997. 
  The new Consumer Protection Ac t, 2019 came into force on July 20th, 
2020 in India, replacing the previous enactment of 1986. The new Act 
overhauls the administration and settlement of consumer disputes in India. It 
provides for strict penalties, including jail terms for adulteration an d for 
misleading advertisements. More importantly, it now prescribes rules for the 
sale of goods through e -commerce. Translating this mandate into action 
entail: 
• Enabling consumers to make informed choices;  
• Ensuring fair, equitable and consistent outcomes for 
consumers; and 
• Facilitating timely and effective consumer grievance redress

2.13 On being askedto clarify reasons for the variations between Budget Estimates 
(BE) and Revised Estimates (RE) during the year 2025 -26. The Department of 
Consumer Affairs furnished the following reply. 
“The Price Stabilization Fund (PSF) was set up in 2014 -15 to tackle 
inflationary trends of some agri -horticultural commodities to protect the 
interests of consumers. The basic objective of P SF scheme is to protect 
the consumers from extreme volatility in prices of agri - horticultural 
commodities and to make essential food commodities available at 
reasonable prices.  
Under the PSF scheme, strategic buffer stock of pulses and onion 
are maintain ed by procuring from domestic as well as imported sources 
for calibrated release to discourage hoarding, unscrupulous speculation 
and for supplies to consumers at reasonable prices. PSF interventions 
have also been undertaken in perishable commodities like  Onion, Tomato 
by procuring from source markets and for retail distribution in major 
consumption centres at discounted prices to nudge the market for bringing 
prices down. 
In order to bring about convergence between procurements to 
provide remunerative prices to farmers and market interventions to protect 
the consumers, PSF and Price support Scheme (PSS) have been brought 
under the Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM -
AASHA) schemes. As per the convergence framework, procurement of 
pulses at M SP would be under PSS of DAFW and requirement towards 
building suitable PSF buffer would be transferred to PSF buffer stock on 
payment of the cost. Procurement of pulses at prices other than MSP 
(market price) for the buffer and procurement of other agri -horticultural 
commodities like onion for price stabilization interventions are done under 

10 
 
PSF. Further, the activities and expenditure incurred under daily 
monitoring of prices have been met from the PSF. 
In addition to the annual budgetary support, the Min istry of Finance 
allows DoCA to utilise the accrued sale proceeds of pulses and onions 
buffer stocks for price stabilisation interventions. The fund requirement 
under PSF is, therefore, met from two sources, viz., annual budgetary 
allocation and sale proce eds realized from the disposal of buffer 
stocks. Accordingly, the Budget Estimates are formulated after duly 
factoring the expected receipts from such sales which accrued to the 
Corpus fund. As per DoE’s directives, the PSF scheme is on -boarded in 
CNA Mode l-2 of fund flow and fund are disbursed to Implementing 
Agencies on Just-in-Time basis. 
In so far as SCC scheme is concerned, the RE has been kept in 
view of the expected proposals to be received from various State 
Governments/ U.T Administrations in the current F.Y. 
In so far as CONFONET scheme is concerned, the RE has been 
kept low depending upon the manpower and hardware/software 
demands furnished by the Implementing Agency i.e NIC.”

A. CONSUMER PROTECTION 
(i) Price Stabilisation Fund 
The Price Stabilization Fund (PSF) was set up in 2014 -15 to tackle inflationary 
trends of some agri -horticultural commodities to protect the interests of consumers. 
The basic objective of PSF scheme is to protect the consumers from extreme 
volatility in pr ices of agri - horticultural commodities and to make essential food 
commodities available at reasonable prices.  
 
3.2  Under the PSF scheme, strategic buffer stock of pulses and onion are 
maintained by procuring from domestic as well as imported sources for  calibrated 
release to discourage hoarding, unscrupulous speculation and for supplies to 
consumers at reasonable prices. PSF interventions have also been undertaken in 
perishable commodities like Onion, Tomato by procuring from source markets and 
for retail distribution in major consumption centers at discounted prices to nudge the 
market for bringing prices down. 
 
3.3  In order to bring about convergence between procurements to provide 
remunerative prices to farmers and market interventions to protect the consumers, 
PSF and Price support Scheme (PSS) have been brought under the Pradhan Mantri 
Annadata Aay Sanrakshan Abhiyan (PM-AASHA) schemes. As per the convergence 
framework, procurement of pulses at MSP would be under PSS of DAFW and 
requirement towards building suitable PSF buffer would be transferred to PSF buffer 
stock on payment of the cost. Procurement of pulses at prices other than MSP 
(market price) for the buffer and procurement of other agri -horticultural commodities 
like onion for price stabiliza tion interventions are done under PSF.  Further, the 
activities and expenditure incurred under daily monitoring of prices have been met 
from the PSF. 
 
3.4  In addition to the annual budgetary support, the Ministry of Finance allowed 
DoCA to utilise the accr ued sale proceeds of pulses and onions buffer stocks for 
price stabilisation interventions. The fund requirement under PSF is, therefore, met 
from two sources, viz., annual budgetary allocation and sale proceeds  realized from 
the disposal of buffer stocks.  Accordingly, the Budget Estimates are formulated after 
duly factoring the expected receipts from such sales which accrued to the Corpus 
fund. As per DoE’s directives, the PSF scheme is on -boarded in CNA Model -2 of 
fund flow and fund are disbursed to Implementing Agencies on Just-in-Time basis.

3.56 Under the sub -scheme ‘Strengthening of the Legal Metrology Regulation and

for strengthening infrastructure in holistic way namely constructio n of laboratory

for verification of weights and measures used in any transaction & protection and

(c) Time Dissemination [ Dissemination of Indian Standard Time 
(IST)]:  
 
 The Time Dissemination Project is a critical support mechanism for 
legal metrology, providing a standardized and accurate time reference that is 
essential for a wide range of measurement processes, legal compliance, digital 
systems, and trade practices. By ensuring that all time -dependent activities are 

44 
 
based on a reliable and uniform tim e standard, the project helps maintain the 
integrity of measurements, supports fair trade, and ensures legal and regulatory 
compliance. 
 
 There are seven base units for any quantitative measurement, which 
in the international systems of units (SI unit) are   kilogram for mass, meter for 
length, second for time, Ampere for electric current, Kelvin for temperature, 
Candela for light intensity and mole for amount of substance. Provisions for 
units of weights and measures are provided under the Legal Metrology A ct, 
2009.  
 
 In India, dissemination of Time, one of the seven base units, is being 
maintained at only one level which is at NPL, New Delhi. The Group of 
Secretaries on Science & Technology, constituted by the Cabinet Secretariat in 
2016, recommend that, “Presently, Ind ian Standard Time (IST) is not being 
adopted mandatorily by all Telecom Service Providers (TSPs) and ‘Internet 
Service Providers’ (ISPs). Non -uniformity of time across different systems 
creates problems in investigation of cyber -crime by the law enforcemen t 
agencies (LEAs). Hence, synchronization of all networks and computers within 
the country with a national clock is a must, especially for the real time 
applications in strategic sector and national security.  
 
   Accurate time dissemination as well as pre cise time synchronization 
has significant impact on all societal, industrial, strategic and many other sectors 
like monitoring of the power grid failures, international trade, banking systems, 
automatic signaling in road & railways, weather forecasting, di saster 
managements, searching for natural resources under the earth’s crust requires 
robust, reliable and accurate timing systems.  
 A MoU with CSIR - NPL for executing the project for setting up of 
atomic clocks has been signed.  Under the MoU, the hardwar e and software for 
setting up the time ensembles will be procured, installed and commissioned by 
NPL.   NPL will handhold LM and shall provide requisite technical support.  The 
MoU also envisages setting up of a Disaster Recovery Centre (DRC) at RRSL, 
Bengaluru.      
 Precise time is essential for country’s strategic and non -strategic 
sectors for navigation, tele-communication, internet, power grid synchronisation, 
banking systems, digital governance, transport systems, financial transactions, 
defence syste ms, cyber physical systems, and upcoming technologies of 5G, 
artificial intelligence and internet of things. Precise time with nanosecond 
accuracy is also required for high -end research in science, namely, 
measurement of fundamental physical constants, det ection of the gravitational 
waves, geodesy, deep space navigation, radio telescopes etc. 
 Synchronisation of all networks and computers within the country with 
a national clock is a must, especially for the real time applications in strategic 
sector and national security.   
 Considering the importance of dissemination of Indian Standard Time 
(IST), the project has been undertaken by the Department of Consumer Affairs. 
The project aims to create technology and infrastructure to disseminate IST from 

45 
 
five site s across India to all the telecom operators, power grids, financial 
institutions, data centres, common citizens etc. These locations are the Regional 
Reference Standard Laboratories of this Department at Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, 
Bhubaneswar, Faridabad and Guw ahati. A Disaster Recovery Centre (DRC) of 
the Primary Time Scale generating IST at NPL is also being setup at Bangalore.

the country. The data is analysed to guide decisions on release of 
buffer stocks, retail interventions and other policy responses. 
 
The Committee recognise the value of such a nationwide monitoring 
system. However, they observe tha t the effectiveness of price 
stabilisation measures depends on how quickly the Department is able 
to respond to emerging price pressures. 
 
The Committee therefore recommend that the Department continue to 
streamline its monitoring and decision -making systems to ensure that 
timely and proactive interventions under the Price Stabilisation Fund 
are undertaken to effectively curb abnormal price fluctuations and 
protect consumer interests.

6. 3.94 The Committee note that retail interventions under the PSF, including 
the sale of Bharat -branded pulses, have been carried out through 
government outlets, cooperative societies, authorised retailers and 
mobile vans to ensure wider availability of essential commodities. 
 
The Committee appreciate the D epartment’s efforts to reach 
consumers through multiple distribution channels. At the same time, 
they observe that the benefits of such interventions must reach 
consumers in smaller towns and rural areas where market fluctuations 
often have a stronger impact on household budgets. 
 
The Committee recommend that the Department continue to 
strengthen the distribution network for PSF -supported commodities 
and ensure that retail interventions are implemented across a wider 
geographical area. 
(Recommendation 6) 
 
7. 3.95 The Committee note that the e -Jagriti platform has facilitated more 
than 1.62 lakh case filings and around 1.5 lakh disposals during year 
2025. The platform provides online filing, digital fee payment, real -time 
case tracking and hybrid hearing facilities. 
 
The Committee welcome the digital transformation of consumer 
grievance redressal systems. They observe that such platforms can 
significantly improve transparency and accessibility if supported by 
adequate institutional capacity. 
 
The Committee recommend the Department to continue strengthening 
digital grievance redressal platforms while also ensuring that 
consumer commissions have the necessary staffing and infrastructure 
to handle increasing digital case flows. 
(Recommendation 7) 
 
8. 3.96 The Committee note that as on 31 January 2026 there are 18 vacancies 
of Presidents and 79 vacancies of Members in State Commissions and 
179 vacancies of Presidents and 397 vacancies of Members in District 
Commissions. The Department has stated that appointmen ts to these 
positions fall under the jurisdiction of the respective State 
Governments. 
 
The Committee observe that such a large number of vacancies can 
affect the efficiency of consumer dispute resolution and may 
contribute to delays in disposal of cases. 
 
The Committee recommend that the Department continue engaging 
with State Governments and Union Territory administrations to ensure 
that vacancies in consumer commissions are filled at the earliest so 
that timely redressal of consumer grievances, enhance a wareness 
about consumer rights, and promote fair business practices.

11. 3.99 The Committee note that against the projected demand of ₹88.80 crore 
for the National Test House (NTH)  during 2026 –27, an allocation of 
₹67.00 crore has been provided in the Budget Estimate. The 
Department has stated that the allocation, though lower than the 
projected demand, has been assessed as adequate to meet priority 
requirements and that ongoing pro jects, implementation capacity, 
statutory functions, infrastructure works, procurement of equipment 
and capacity-building activities will continue without disruption. 
 
The Committee believe that the National Test House plays a critical 
role in strengthenin g the country’s quality infrastructure by providing 
testing and certification facilities across a wide range of industrial 
sectors. In view of the growing demand for reliable testing facilities in 
emerging areas such as electric mobility, renewable energy 
equipment, electronics and advanced manufacturing, the need for 
strengthening and expanding testing infrastructure is expected to 
increase significantly. 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommend the Department to closely 
monitor the implementation of infrastructure expansion and laboratory 
modernisation projects under the National Test House to ensure that 
planned capacity augmentation is not delayed due to resource

constraints. The Committee also urge the Department to explore 
avenues for phased expan sion of NTH facilities in emerging sectors

meeting evolving quality and safety standards. The Committee further 
recommend that the National Test House should pursue internationally 
recognised accreditations and strengthen alignment with global 
testing and certification frameworks so as to enhance international 
acceptance of its test results and support Indian industry in accessing 
global markets.

12. 3.100 The Committee note that the Budget Estimates under the Consumer 
Awareness Scheme have remained almost stagnant at around ₹19 
crore since 2023 –24, despite higher Revised Estimates and actual 
expenditure in certain years. While the Committee appreciate the 
Department’s efforts to utilise social media and other low -cost digital 
platforms for wider dissemination of consumer awareness messages, 
they are of the view that digital outreach alone may not b e sufficient, 
particularly in rural and backward areas. 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommend the Department to review the 
adequacy of the present allocation and consider enhancing it in future, 
while adopting a balanced approach combining digital campaigns with 
on-ground awareness activities through States/UTs, local institutions 
and public representatives to ensure wider and more effective 
outreach. The Committee also desire the Department to emphasise on 
the potential of social media outreach which needs to be further 
expanded and strategically utilised to reach a wider and younger 
audience.  
(Recommendation 12)
