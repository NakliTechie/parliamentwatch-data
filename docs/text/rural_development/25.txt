(i). Introduction 
Standing Committee on Rural Development and Panchayati Raj is one of the 
sixteen Departmentally Related Standing Committees of the Lok Sabha primarily 
entrusted with the mandatory task of examination of Demands for Grants for each 
financial year sought by the Ministry/Department under its purview while also 
scrutinizing the schemes under the administrative control of the concerned 
Ministry/Department. The present Report is on the examination of the Demands for 
Grants of the Department of Rural Development (Ministry of Rural Development) for 
the ensuing financial year 2026-27 under Rule 331E(1)(a) of Rules of Procedure and 
Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha. 
 
(ii).  Brief about the Ministry of Rural Development  
 The Department of Rural Development which came into existence in October, 
1974 as a part of the Ministry of Food and Agriculture was converted into a new 
Ministry of Rural Reconstruction on 18th August, 1979. The Ministry was 
renamed as the Ministry of Rural Development on 23rd January 1982. The 
Ministry of Rural Development was again converted into a Department in 
January 1985, under the Ministry of Agriculture & Rural Development. The 
Department was again upgraded as the Ministry of Rural Development on July 
5, 1991. Another Department viz. Department of Wasteland Development was 
created under this Ministry on 2nd July 1992. The Ministry was renamed in 
March 1995 as the Ministry of Rural Areas and Employment with three 
departments namely Department of Rural Employment and Poverty Alleviation, 
Rural Development and Wasteland Development.  
 
 The Ministry was again renamed as the Ministry of Rural Development in 1999 
with three Departments i.e. Department of Rural Development, Department of 
Land Resources and Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation. The 

2 
 
 
Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation was separated from this Ministry 
on 13th July, 2011.  
 
 At present, the Ministry of Rural Development consists of two Departments, 
namely, (I) Department of Rural Development, and (II) Department of Land 
Resources. 
 
(iii). Objectives of Department of Rural Development   
The main objectives of the Ministry are classified as:  
i. Uplifting the deprived rural households through multidimensional 
support including for assets, livelihoods, infrastructure and services. 
Adopting a saturation approach and ensuring resilience to the families 
and communities.  
ii. Convergence of programmes and schemes.  
iii. Raising institutions of poor and forging partnerships with PRIs and 
other drivers of transformation.  
iv. Integrated and sustained monitoring of measurable outcomes.  
 
 The vision and mission of the Ministry is sustainable and inclusive growth of 
rural India through a multipronged strategy for eradication of poverty by 
increasing livelihoods opportunities, providing social safety net and developing 
infrastructure for growth. This is expected to improve quality of life in rural India 
and to correct the developmental imbalances, aiming in the process, to reach 
out to most disadvantaged sections of the society.

(ii). Trend of Expenditure during 2025-26 
The data provided by the Department revealed that an amount of Rs. 1,758.92 
crore was decreased at the RE stage during the Financial Year 2025-26. On being 
queried upon the reasons for such decreased requirement at the RE stage, the 
Department in their written replies have submitted as under:- 
“MGNREGA:- Mahatma Gandhi NREGA is a demand driven Scheme. 
RE is based on anticipated demand for wage employment based on 
current pace of demand. Accordingly, additional funds were sought from 
the Ministry of Finance depending upon the change in demand of wage 
employment from the beneficiaries. The provision for providing additional 
funds of Rs.2,000 crores has been made under RE for the FY 2025-26 
for MGNREGA Scheme. 
PMAY-G:- The BE allocation for the current financial year for PMAY-G 
has been Rs.54,832.03 crore, and the RE will be Rs.27,332.14 crore. 
The expenditure incurred under the scheme is Rs.15,563.62 as on 
23.02.2026. 
 
The comparatively lower expenditure is primarily due to the ongoing 
migration from the existing Single Nodal Agency (SNA) model to the 
SNA-SPARSH platform. This transition involves integration with multiple 
stakeholders, including State IFMIS, NIC, PFMS and NPCI, which has 
led to a temporary disruption in fund flow mechanisms. 
 
Despite these transitional challenges, significant progress has been 
achieved, 23 States have already been onboarded onto the SNA-
SPARSH platform under PMAYG, while 06 others are at various stages 
of integration. Notably, expenditure routed solely through SPARSH under 
PMAYG has already reached Rs.5,319.90 crore, indicating steady 
stabilization of the new system and improved fund flow alignment. 
 
Additionally, the monsoon season and flood conditions prevailing in 
several parts of the country have significantly affected on-ground 
construction activities. Inaccessibility of sites, displacement of 
beneficiaries, and unavailability of labour and materials have further 
slowed down physical progress 
 
DAY-NRLM:- As far as DAY-NRLM is concerned, even though this 
Division has proposed to keep the RE amount at the same level of BE 

10 
 
 
provision of Rs.19005.00 for the current year, the Ministry of Finance has 
reduced an amount of Rs.3000.00 Crore and RE has been finalized at 
Rs.16,000.00 Crore. The reason for reduction is the slow pace of 
expenditure during the current year, due to the lengthy procedure for 
implementation of SNA-SPARSH in all the States/UTs (except UTs 
without legislature). 
 
PMGSY:- The budget allocation of Pradhan Mantri gram Sadak Yojana 
(PMGSY) for financial Year 2025-26 was Rs. 19,000 Crore at BE stage 
which has been revised to Rs. 11,000 Crore at RE stage. The reasons 
for the decreased requirement of funds under PMGSY at RE stage are 
as below:- 
 
(a) Transition to SNA SPARSH: The most significant technical reason for 
the reduction is onboarding of SNA SPARSH. Many States had 
substantial unspent balances in their Single Nodal Accounts (SNA). Under 
the previous system, Central releases were made even if States had not 
fully exhausted previous tranches. The Ministry has now fully transitioned 
to the SNA SPARSH. This platform provides real-time visibility of 
funds. Many States had first exhausted the existing "float" or "SNA 
balances" before onboarding on the SNA SPARSH. Hence the actual 
requirement for new Central releases decreased at the RE stage. It could 
be seen that against the release of Rs. 4311.95 crore of central funds 
actual expenditure on the ground is Rs. 11,026. 23 Rs. crore (including 
state share) . This additional funds over and above actual release of this 
year is the unspent available balances with the states of the previous year. 
The release is thus less but expenditure on the ground is substantial.  
  
(b)  Sun-setting of Verticals: The decrease also reflects that the legacy 
phases of PMGSY are nearing completion, while new phases are in the 
initial mobilization stage. PMGSY-I & II phases have reached over 95% 
physical completion nationwide. PMGSY-I has been extended only for 
specific regions like Chhattisgarh (till March 2026) due to LWE (Left Wing 
Extremism) challenges. 
  
(c)  PMGSY-III Restrictions: While PMGSY-III was extended to March 
2026, the government imposed a condition that no new works should be 
taken up if they were sanctioned but not yet tendered. This effectively 
capped new expenditure for this vertical. Many sanctioned works could not 
be awarded due to this restrictions and this extension with the rider came 
only in the month of July. By that time working season was over due to 

11 
 
 
onset of the Monsoon in many parts of the country. Also contractors 
slowed down the works as the payment was not released due to late 
receipt of the extension of the timeline of PMGSY-II, RCPLWEA and III.  
  
(d)  Transition to PMGSY-IV: The launch of PMGSY-IV (targeted for 
2024-2029) is currently in the "indicative allocation" and planning phase. 
Large-scale fund absorption typically happens after the tendering and 
initial mobilization of new projects, which shifted the bulk of the financial 
requirement to the next fiscal year. 
  
(e)  Systemic and Operational Constraints: Field-level challenges 
impacted the pace of work, leading to lower-than-anticipated 
expenditure. Inclement weather, including extended monsoons and 
extreme heatwaves in 2025, significantly shortened the "working season," 
leading to temporary work stoppages. In the Himalayan states and LWE-
affected areas, projects faced persistent delays due to forest clearances 
and law and order issues.  Some States faced a "limited contracting 
capacity" where the volume of sanctioned work under PMGSY-III 
exceeded the number of qualified contractors. 
 
NSAP:- Based on the anticipated demand for the current year from the 
States/UTs, the BE 2025-26 of Rs. 9652.00 Crore under NSAP was 
reduced by Rs 454.90 Crore  to Rs.9197.10 at RE stage.

(v). Unspent Balances 
When asked about the reasons for the accrual of such amounts and the 
strategy to avoid such occurrence, the Department of Rural Development submitted 
their reply as below:- 
“PMAY-G:- The implementation of SNA-SPARSH has been completed 
in 23 States under PMAYG, while the remaining States are at various 
stages of integration. Therefore, the concept of unspent balance is not 
presently applicable, as funds are being routed through the revised fund 
flow mechanism. 
The mother sanctions amounting to Rs.17,354.61 crore have been 
issued, out of which Rs.5,319.90 crore has been spent under PMAYG 
through SNA Sparsh. The opening SNA balance at the beginning of the 
financial year was Rs.11,092.01 crore, which has reduced to 
Rs.1,241.92 crore as the closing balance. 
 
Further, the Ministry has issued letters to States regarding the refund of 
the remaining SNA balance. 
 
DAY-NRLM:- Considering the implementation process which requires 
fund flow from State to District and Blocks, the guidelines had allowed 

18 
 
 
retention of around 25% of the allocated funds by permitting release of 
next tranche/instalment after utilization of 75% of available funds. This 
was the reason for accrual of unspent balances.  
 
However, with the implementation of Single Nodal Bank account 
concept the parking of funds does not arise at lower levels. This has led to the 
reduction in the accrual of unspent balance. 
 
Furthermore, the implementation of SNA-SPARSH ensures a 'just-in-
time' release of funds, effectively ensuring that no unspent balance remain with 
the States. 
 
DDU-GKY:- However, States/UTs has utilized their Unspent balance 
wherever there were heavy floats lying in their SNA account. But, Specially 
the above Reported States may be having several reasons for the accrual 
of unspent balances under the Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Grameen 
Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY), whereas few reasons for Unspent 
Balances are mentioned below: 
i. Delays in sanctioning the project and revision of the projects 
multiple times lead to delayed commencement of training, that 
resulting in unutilized funds. 
ii. Capacity Constraints: Insufficient training capacity, inadequate 
infrastructure, and limited geographical reach hinder fund 
utilization. 
iii. Low Enrolment: Lower-than-expected enrolment of candidates 
affects lower fund utilization. 
iv. Delay in achievement of milestones by PIAs results in delay in 
release of instalments leading to accrual of unspent balances with 
the States/UTs. 
 
PMGSY:- The SNA-SPARSH module has  been introduced recently to 
ensure just in time release to the States/ UTs, which has resulted in 
reducing  the unspent balance to a large extent. Additionally, the State of 
Bihar possesses an unspent balance of Rs. 390.27 crore in the SNA 
account, from a total unspent balance of Rs. 401.15 crore under the 
PMGSY. Since the State of Bihar has also onboarded on SNA SPARSH, 
this amount will be returned to the Consolidated Fund of India and the 
Consolidated Fund of the State by the end of this Financial Year. 
Therefore, there will be minimal unspent balances with the Union 
Territories without Legislatures, which have been excluded from the 
SPARSH module.

NSAP:- The funds are released in advance to the States/UTs for 
providing central assistance to the beneficiaries identified under NSAP 
after following the due procedure. The release of funds to the States/

States/UT normally report the expenditure of the last installment along 
with the release proposal of next installment. States/UTs disburse the 
pension to the beneficiaries on time however, the time gap between 
making expenditure and reporting the same to this department is the 
reason for reflecting unspent balance lying with States/UTs. 
  
In pursuance of the revised procedure for release of funds 
issued by Department of Expenditure, the following strategy is followed 
by this department for better fund management:- 
 
• Funds are now being release to the States/UTs in four equal installments

• Funds for next installment are released to the States/UTs only when

SNA of the implementing department and expenditure of 75% thereof.

CHAPTER - III 
Analysis of Schemes 
A. Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 
(MGNREGA) was notified on September 7, 2005. 
(i).  Mandate  
The mandate of the Act is to provide at least 100 days of guaranteed wage 
employment in a financial year to every rural household whose adult members 
volunteer to do unskilled manual work.  
(ii). Objectives  
The core objectives of the Scheme are as below:  
 Providing not less than one hundred days of unskilled manual work as a 
guaranteed employment in a financial year to every household in rural 
areas as per demand, resulting in creation of productive assets of 
prescribed quality and durability. 
 Strengthening the livelihood resource base of the poor;  
 Proactively ensuring social inclusion and  
Thus, Mahatma Gandhi NREGA is a powerful instrument for ensuring inclusive 
growth in rural India through its impact on social protection, livelihood security and 
democratic empowerment.  
 
(iii). Coverage 
  
The Act was notified in 200 districts in the first phase with effect from February 
2nd 2006 and then extended to an additional 130 districts in the financial year 2007-
2008 (113 districts were notified with effect from April 1st 2007, and 17 districts in Uttar 
Pradesh (UP) were notified with effect from May 15th 2007). The remaining districts 
have been notified under Mahatma Gandhi NREGA with effect from April 1, 2008. 
Thus, the Mahatma Gandhi NREGA covers the entire country with the exception of 
districts that have a hundred percent urban population. 
 

24 
 
 
 
(iv). Funding Pattern 
 Section 22(1) and (2) speaks of the funding pattern of the Schemes taken up 
under Mahatma Gandhi NREGA. As per Section 22(1), the Central Government shall 
meet the cost of following namely:- 
(a) The amount required for payment of wages for unskilled manual work 
under the Scheme; 
(b) Up to three-fourths of the material cost of the Scheme including 
payment of wages to skilled and semi-skilled workers subject to the 
provisions of Schedule II; 
(c) Six percentage of the total cost of the Scheme towards the 
administrative expenses. 
      
As per Section 22(2), the State Government shall meet the cost of the following, 
namely:- 
(a) The cost of unemployment allowance payable under the Scheme. 
(b) one-fourth of the material cost of the Scheme including payment of 
wages to skilled and semi-skilled workers subject to the provisions of 
Schedule II. 
(c) The administrative expenses of the State Council.

(vii). Compensation for delay in payment of Wages 
(a) As per the provisions mentioned in Schedule-II of the Mahatma Gandhi National 
Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 wage seekers are entitled to receive payment 
of compensation for the delay at the rate of 0.05% of the unpaid wages per day for the 
duration of delay beyond the sixteenth day of closure of muster roll. As per Para 29 (1) 
of Schedule-II of Mahatma Gandhi NREGA, 2005 lays down that: 
  
(1) “In case the payment of wages is not made within fifteen days from 
the date of closure of the muster roll, the wage seekers shall be 
entitled to receive payment of compensation for the delay, at the rate 
of 0.05% of the unpaid wages per day of delay beyond the sixteenth 
day of closure of muster roll. 
 
(a) Any delay in payment of compensation beyond a period of 
fifteen days from the date it becomes payable, shall be 
considered in the same manner as the delay in payment of 
wages. 
(b) For the purpose of ensuring accountability in payment of 
wages and to calculate culpability of various functionaries or 
agencies, the States shall divide the processes leading to 

31 
 
 
determination and payment of wages into various stages such 
as – 
i. Measurement of work; 
ii. Computerizing the must rolls; 
iii. Computerizing the measurements; 
iv. Generation of wage lists, and  
v. Uploading Fund Transfer Orders (FTOs)’ 
and specify stage-wise maximum time limits along with the 
functionary or agency which is responsible for discharging the 
specific function. 
  
(c) The computer system shall have a provision to automatically 
calculate the compensation payable based on the date of 
closure of the muster roll and the date of deposit of wages in 
the accounts of the wage seekers. 
(d) The State Government shall pay the compensation upfront 
after due verification within the time limits as specified above 
and recover the compensation amount from the functionaries 
or agencies who is responsible for the delay in payment. 
(e) It shall be the duty of that District Programme Coordinator or 
Programme Officer to ensure that the system is 
operationalised. 
(f) The number of days of delay, the compensation payable and 
actually paid shall be reflected in the Monitoring and 
Information System and the Labour Budget. 
 
The payment of the compensation is made by the State Government 
upfront after due verification. Ministry has issued Standard Operating 
Procedure (SoP) on Timely Payment and as per this SoP the timely 
payment process has been divided into two stages namely Stage-I (FTO 
generation within 8 days by State Government) and Stage-II (process of 
FTO at Central Level by different stake holders). The entire system is 
designed such that the wages are credited within the stipulated time. 
Sometimes the Stage-I may overshoot its prescribed time but Stage-II 
may finish earlier resulting in overall timely payment of wages and vice-
versa and also in some cases PFMS fails to send the FTO to the bank 
for payment.”

 The core objectives of the Scheme shall be as follows:—(a) The

 To modernise governance, accountability and citizen engagement through a 
comprehensive digital ecosystem, including biometric authentication at various 
levels, global positioning system or mobile based worksite monitoring, real time 
management information system dashboards, proactive public disclosures and 
the use of Artificial Intelligence for planning, audits and fraud risk mitigation.

C. Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana – Gramin (PMAY-G) 
(i). Overview 
(a) The Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana – Gramin (PMAY-G) is one of the flagship 
schemes with Government’s commitment to provide “Housing for All”. The scheme 
Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana – Gramin (PMAY-G) came into effect from 1st April 
2016. The aim of PMAY-G is to provide a pucca house with basic amenities to all 
houseless households and households living in kutcha/ dilapidated houses in rural 
areas of the country. Initially, a target of 2.95 crore was set to provide pucca houses 
with essential amenities to eligible houseless households. However, realizing the 
growing housing needs in rural areas, Union Cabinet granted an extension to the 
scheme on 9th August 2024 to construct additional 2 crore rural houses during FY 
2024-25 to FY 2028-29, over and above the previous target of 2.95 crore. Out of the 
cumulative target of 4.95 crore houses, a total of 4.14 crore houses have been 
allocated among States/UTs out of which 3.86 crore houses have been sanctioned by 
States/UTs and 2.93 crore have been completed as on 31st December 2025. The total 
financial outlay for this initiative is ₹ 3,06,137 crore, allocated for the period from FY 
2024-25 to FY 2028-29. 
 
 (b)  Identification of Beneficiaries: 
The identification of beneficiaries in the scheme is based on the housing deprivation 
parameters as per Socio Economic Caste Census – 2011. Further, to provide benefit 
of the scheme to the left out eligible households, the Ministry decided to identify such 
households, and Awaas+ survey was conducted by States/UTs on directions of the 
Ministry in 2018. As a result of the exercise of allocation of targets, the SECC 2011 
based Permanent Wait List (PWL) and the finalized Awaas + 2018 lists of 29 States 
and UTs are saturated. To meet the additional requirement of houses in States/UTs as 
well as achieve the target of construction of 2 crore more houses, the Awaas+ list is 
being updated via the Awaas+ 2024 survey. The identification of additional eligible 
rural houses through survey is currently underway and is expected to be completed by 
March 2025 as per the revised exclusion criteria of the PMAY-G.  
 
(ii). Key Features 
i. Providing assistance for construction of 2 crore houses in rural areas over the period 
2024-25 to 2028-29. 
ii. The minimum unit (house) size under the PMAY-G is 25 sq. mt, including a 
dedicated area for hygienic cooking. 
iii. Unit assistance of Rs. 1.20 lakh in the plains and Rs. 1.30 lakh in North Eastern 
States, and 2 Hill States of Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh and Union Territories 
of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. 

40 
 
 
iv. The cost of unit (house) assistance is shared between Central and State 
Governments in the ratio 60:40 in plain areas and 90:10 for North-Eastern and 2 Hill 
States of Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand and UT of Jammu and Kashmir. In 
respect of other Union Territories, 100% cost is borne by the Government of India. 
v. Provision of assistance (Rs.12,000/-) for toilets through convergence with Swachh 
Bharat Mission – Gramin (SBM-G), MGNREGS or any other dedicated source of

D. Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) 
(i). Brief Outline 
PMGSY was launched on 25th December, 2000 with the objective to provide 
single all-weather road connectivity to all eligible unconnected habitations in rural 
areas of country. The PMGSY was launched as 100% Centrally Sponsored Scheme. 
However, subsequently, on the basis of the recommendations of the Sub-Group of 
Chief Ministers on Rationalization of Centrally Sponsored Schemes the fund sharing 
pattern of PMGSY was changed. 
Later on, PMGSY-II was launched in 2013, with a target to upgrade 50,000 Kms 
of the existing rural road network. In the year 2016, Road Connectivity Project for Left 
Wing Extremism Affected Areas (RCPLWEA) was launched for construction and 
upgradation of strategically important roads in the 9 LWE affected states. In the year 
2019, Government launched PMGSY-III for consolidation of 1,25,000 Km through 
routes and major rural links connection habitations, inter-alia, to Gramin Agricultural 
Markets (GrAMs), Higher Secondary Schools and Hospitals. 
The Hon’ble PM launched the Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha 
Abhiyan (PM-JANMAN) on Janjatiya Gaurav Divas on 15th November, 2023 at Khunti 
District of Jharkhand. Under Road Connectivity component of PM-JANMAN, 8000 Km 

47 
 
 
of road length including bridges is to be constructed across 15 states with cost norms 
of Rs.1.00 Cr/Km. 
Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana-IV (PMGSY-IV) has been launched in 
2024 for construction of 62,500 km of all-weather roads (single lane) to provide 
connectivity to about 25,000 unconnected habitations of population size 500+ in plains, 
250+ in NE & Hill states/UTs, special category areas (Tribal Schedule V, Aspirational 
Districts/ Blocks, Desert areas) and 100+ in LWE affected districts, as per Census 
2011. The survey work is presently undergoing to identify the eligible unconnected 
habitation as per population norms. 
Under PMGSY-I, 1,64,581 roads (6,44,735 km) & 7,454 bridges have been 
sanctioned, out of which, 1,63,665 roads (6,25,115 km) & 7,210 bridges have been 
completed as on 31.12.2025. 
Under PMGSY-II, 6,665 roads (49,795 km) & 759 bridges have been 
sanctioned, out of which, 6,612 roads (49,086 km) & 749 bridges have been 
completed as on 31.12.2025. 
Under RCPLWEA, 1,344 roads (12,211 km) & 701 bridges have been 
sanctioned, out of which, 1020 roads (9,771 km) & 536 bridges have been completed 
as on 31.12.2025. 
Under PMGSY-III, 15,966 roads (1,22,376 km) & 3,211 bridges have been 
sanctioned, out of which, 12,627 roads (1,02,571 km) & 1699 bridges have been 
completed as on 31.12.2025. 
Under PM-JANMAN, 2,495 roads (7,324 km) & 163 bridges have been 
sanctioned, out of which, 247 roads (1230 km) have been completed as on 
31.12.2025. 
Under PMGSY-IV, 2,490 roads (8,655km) & 1 bridge have been sanctioned as 
on 31.12.2025.  
(ii). Funding Pattern 
The scheme was started as 100% Centrally Sponsored Scheme. PMGSY-II 
was started in the year 2013, on sharing basis between the Centre and the States / 
UTs (75:25 for the plain areas and 90:10 for North-Eastern States and hill areas). 
Subsequently, on the recommendation of the Sub-Group of Chief Ministers on 
Rationalization of Centrally Sponsored Schemes, the fund sharing pattern of PMGSY 
was changed to 60:40 between the Centre and States for all States except for 8 North 

48 
 
 
Eastern and 3 Himalayan States (Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, the then Jammu & 
Kashmir) for which it was decided to be 90:10 with effect from the financial year 2015-
16. It is 100% central share for Union Territories (UT) except UT of Jammu and 
Kashmir.

E.  The Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana — National Rural Livelihoods Mission 
(DAY-NRLM) 
(i). Background  
Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM) 
was launched in June 2011. The objective of DAY-NRLM is to organize rural poor 
women into Self Help Groups (SHGs), and continuously nurture and support them till 
they have an appreciable increase in incomes over a period of time. This will enable to 
improve their quality of life and come out of abject poverty. The Mission seeks to reach 
out to all rural poor women, estimated at about 10 crores in a phased manner.  
The core financial support under the programme is the Revolving Fund (RF) 
and Community Investment Fund (CIF) provided to Self Help Group (SHGs) and their 
Federations to facilitate and augment their livelihood activities.  
In order to make bank loans affordable for women Self Help Groups, DAY-
NRLM has made provisions for providing Interest Subvention to women SHGs 
borrowing from banks. Women SHGs can avail loans up to Rs.3.00 lakh from banks at 
subvented interest rate of 7% per annum. For this, banks are provided uniform 
subvention of 4.5% per annum. 
Women SHGs can avail loans above Rs.3 lakhs and up to Rs.5 lakhs, at 
subvented interest rate equivalent to respective lending bank’s 1-year Marginal Cost 
Based Lending Rate (MCLR) or 10% per annum whichever is less. For this, banks are 
provided uniform subvention of 5% per annum. 
Mahila Kisan Sashaktikaran Pariyojana (MKSP) a Sub Scheme of DAY-NRLM.

F. National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) 
 
(i).  Background 
 
National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) is a 100% centrally funded 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) of Ministry of Rural Development, implemented 
as a social security programme for old aged, widows, disabled persons and bereaved 
families on death of primary bread winner, belonging to below poverty line household.  
(ii). Components of NSAP 
 
The NSAP at present includes five sub-schemes as its components: - 
o National Old Age Pension Scheme - assistance of ₹ 200/- per month to 
persons in the age group of 60-79 years and ₹ 500/- per month to persons of 80 
years and above. Currently, more than 221 lakh beneficiaries are covered. 

59 
 
 
o National Widow Pension Scheme - assistance of ₹300/- per month to widows 
in the age-group of 40-79 years and ₹ 500/- per month to widows of 80 years 
and above. Currently, more than 67 lakh beneficiaries are covered. 
o National Disability Pension Scheme - assistance of ₹300/- per month to 
persons aged 18-79 years with severe or multiple disabilities and ₹ 500/- per 
month to persons of 80 years and above. Currently, more than 8.8 lakh 
beneficiaries are covered. 
o National Family Benefit Scheme - one-time assistance of ₹ 20,000/- on the 
death of primary breadwinner aged between 18 and 59 years. It is a demand-
based scheme. The upper limit of households is 3.58 lakh.  
o Annapurna Scheme - 10 kg of food grains per month free of cost to those 
senior citizens who, though eligible, are not receiving old age pension. It is a 
demand-based scheme. The upper limit of beneficiaries is 8.32 lakh. 
As per NSAP guidelines, the State/UTs are encouraged to provide top up 
amounts at least an equivalent amount to the assistance provided by the Central Govt. 
so that the beneficiaries could get a decent level of assistance.

PART – II 
 
OBSERVATIONS / RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
Demand Number 87 of the Department of Rural Development under the 
Ministry of Rural Development was laid in the Lok Sabha on 12th February, 2026 
and stood reference to the Committee on Rural Development and Panchayati Raj 
in exercise of the powers conferred upon the Departmentally Related Standing 
Committees, Standing Committee on Rural Development and Panchayati Raj 
have thoroughly scrutinized the Demands for Grants of the Department of Rural 
Development keeping in view primarily the previous and ongoing 
financial/physical performance and in light of their findings have made the 
following Observations/Recommendations:-  
1.  Optimum Utilization of Allocated Funds  
The Department of Rural Development (DoRD) under the aegis of the 
Ministry of Rural development have a huge responsibility upon them to create a 
conducive environment wherein the vast rural populace living in the nooks and 
corners of the country realise the succor of holistic development. The areas of 
concern range from Rural Development, Rural Housing, Rural Connectivity to 
Rural Livelihoods to name a few. For a nation of such rich heritage as ours, the 
rise from ‘developing’ to ‘developed’ status is not at all feasible until and unless 
the majority population of the country residing in villages march side by side 
with their urban counterparts. The Committee resonate with this idea and 
acknowledge the paramount importance of rural development in the country. 

66 
 
 
However, in this scenario, the Committee is not able to understand the approach 
of the Department that as against the funds of Rs. 1,86,995.61 crore allocated at 
the RE stage of 2025-26, actual expenditure was Rs. 99,090.47 crore only, which 
is 47.01% less than the funds allocated at the RE stage. This indicates that either 
the budgetary planning of the Government was not prudent enough or there is a 
problem at the actual implementation stage of the schemes. Non-utilization of 
allocated funds means that deprivation of the targeted people from availing 
benefits from the scheme, and the same could have been meaningfully utilized 
for the other scheme where funds were direly needed. At the fag end of the 
completion of the financial year, the Committee desire to know how the DoRD 
plans to utilize the allocated funds fully within the short span of time. Therefore, 
the Committee, recommend the DoRD to chalk out quarterly and monthly 
expenditure plans well in advance in consultation with Stakeholder particularly 
the State Governments in respect of all of its schemes/programme and should 
undertake all possible measures for optimum utilization of funds and at the 
same time also keep a close monitoring on the availability of funds so as to 
raise supplementary demands or re-appropriate at opportune moment in 
collaboration with the Ministry of Finance.  
2.  Need for Increased Budgetary Allocation  
The Committee note that the Department of Rural Development (DoRD) 
have been entrusted with a huge task of implementing and overseeing plethora 
of schemes of the Government of India which are aimed at the upliftment of rural 
masses of our country. It is pretty evident that vast population of our country 

67 
 
 
still reside in the villages among which a substantial quantum belongs to 
marginalised and economically downtrodden section of our society. Thus, it 
becomes imperative on the part of the Department of Rural Development to 
ensure that the visionary flagship schemes of the Government of India are not 
left in the lurch for want of better maneuvering and are provided with ample 
support in terms of finances for keeping the schemes healthy and running. The 
Committee find that Rs. 2,28,768.81 crore have been allocated to the Department 
of Rural Development at the BE stage for 2026-27, which is 21.20% more than 
the funds at BE stage in Financial Year 2025-26. The Committee acknowledge 
this and hope that it would be fully utilized for the rural development schemes 
as conceived by the Government in order to extend benefits to the targeted 
beneficiaries. However, the Committee note with concern that out of the total 
allocation of Rs. 2,28,768.81 crore at BE stage for 2026-27, a major amount of 
Rs. 95,692.31 crore has been allocated to VB-GRAMG. Therefore, barring VB-
GRAMG, funds for other major schemes like PMGSY, PMAY-G, DAY-NRLM and 
NSAP, by and large, have been kept static and moreover, funds for MGNREGA 
has been drastically reduced to Rs. 30,000 crore. The Committee are of the firm 
opinion that such allocation is inadequate and totally unacceptable. Therefore, 
in order to accelerate the sustainable momentum of rural progress, adequate 
budgetary allocation is necessary particularly when the popular schemes like 
MGNREGA, NSAP, DAY- NRLM, PMGSY and PMAY-G are also not immune to the 
inflation and rising cost of raw materials involved. Further, the Committee hope 
that allocated funds would be utilised fully on various rural development 
schemes in order to yield desired results for the overall welfare of the rural 

68 
 
 
population. The Committee are of the firm opinion that such allocation is 
inadequate and may be suitably enhanced, particularly when the scheme 
continues to face substantial pending liabilities from previous years. The 
Committee note that pending liabilities under MGNREGA are expected to 
approach nearly Rs. 25,000 crore by the end of the financial year, which would 
leave very limited fiscal space for meeting fresh demand for work during the 
current financial year. In a demand-driven programme such as MGNREGA, such 
a situation may severely constrain the ability of the scheme to respond to 
genuine demand for employment. Therefore, in order to accelerate the 
sustainable momentum of rural progress, adequate budgetary allocation is 
necessary, particularly when popular schemes like MGNREGA, NSAP, DAY-
NRLM, PMGSY and PMAY-G are also affected by inflation and the rising cost of 
raw materials and services. The Committee further hope that the allocated funds 
would be fully utilised under various rural development schemes in order to 
yield the desired outcomes for the overall welfare of the rural population. 
3.  Liquidation of Unspent Balances  
The Committee note with concern the omnipresence of unspent balances 
across all the schemes of rural development which is not acceptable and 
justifiable. The Committee painfully note that during the financial year 2025-26, 
Rs. 4999.54 lakhs in MGNREGA (as on 22.02.2026), Rs.1,24,192.48 lakhs in 
PMAY-G (as on 22.02.2026), Rs. 401.15 crores (as on 19.02.2026) in PMGSY, Rs. 
819.41 crores in NSAP (as on 23.02.2026), Rs. 382.82 crores in NRLM (up to 
20.02.2026), Rs. 506.99 crores in DDU-GKY (as on 23.02.2026) and Rs. 57.37 

69 
 
 
lakhs in RSETI (till 23.02.2026) lie unspent. The Committee, although note the 
various constraints being faced and measures adopted by the DoRD in tackling 
and mitigating the issue of unspent balances are still in a quandary on the 
meagre increase in fund allocation for the Department on one hand, existing 
pendencies in schemes like MGNREGA as reflected through wage and material 
component liabilities or delay in installment releases in PMAY-G on the other 
hand along-with non-utilization of full funds. In situations, wherein beneficiaries 
are kept awaiting for their wages or installments, the accrual of unspent 
balances is completely unacceptable. Therefore, the Committee strongly 
recommend the DoRD to take appropriate and judicious approach so that 
unspent balances do not keep piling up in the various schemes and that each 
penny allocated against the schemes is fully utilized for the welfare of poor and 
marginalized section of the rural populace across the country.  
4.  Expediting Stabilisation of SNA-SPARSH and Communication to States  
The Committee note that the Ministry has cited difficulties arising from the 
implementation of SNA-SPARSH as one of the factors affecting fund flow and 
utilisation under various schemes. The Committee are of the considered view 
that any digital public finance system ought to have been introduced only after 
adequate preparedness, training and handholding support to the implementing 
agencies. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry urgently 
address operational bottlenecks in SNA-SPARSH, ensure complete onboarding 
and handholding of all States and implementing agencies, and issue clear 

70 
 
 
operational guidance so that delays in fund release and utilisation do not 
continue on account of procedural or technical deficiencies.  
5.  Enhanced Allocation of Funds under MGNREGA  
The Committee are concerned to note that the Budget Estimates under 
MGNREGA for the Financial Year 2026-27 have been drastically reduced to 
Rs.30,000 crore which is 65.12% less than the Budget Estimates for the previous 
financial year, i.e. 2025-26. In this scenario, the Committee is not able to reason 
the approach of the Department in seeking/obtaining funds which prima-facie do 
not seem to be adequate enough to fuel the sustainable momentum of the rural 
progress because most of them will be utilized to clear pendencies. The 
Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) 
provides a legal right to work for the most economically disadvantaged sections 
of the rural population who are willing to work. It is a last resort of succour for 
the jobless section of the society who have no other means of livelihood to feed 
their family members. The scheme serves as a critical safety net, particularly for 
those facing extreme poverty and unemployment. Therefore, the Committee are 
unable to comprehend the rationale for such a huge reduction in the allocation 
of fund under MGNREGA for 2026-27. Since the scheme ensured employment to 
masses of rural poor and deprived section of the society, it is very much 
essential that adequate Budgetary Allocation ought to be made for the effective 
implementation and running of the Scheme. The Committee note that significant 
pending liabilities continue to persist under MGNREGA. As per the information 
furnished by the Department of Rural Development, as on 22.02.2026, liabilities 

71 
 
 
amount to Rs.5,940.54 crore under the wage component and Rs.10,921.59 crore 
under the material component, aggregating to Rs.16,862.13 crore. The 
Committee further observe that with the overall allocation of only Rs.30,000 
crore for the year, the existing and anticipated liabilities together are likely to 
rise to nearly Rs.25,000 crore by the end of the financial year, leaving very little 
fiscal space for fresh works under the scheme. The Committee are of the view 
that such a situation undermines the demand-driven character of MGNREGA 
and constrains its ability to respond to genuine employment demand. The 
Committee, therefore, urge the Department of Rural Development to reassess 
the requirement of funds more realistically and press upon the Ministry of 
Finance for enhanced allocation, particularly considering that additional funds 
can be provided at the Revised Estimates stage for a demand-driven programme 
like MGNREGA. 
6.  Low Wage Rate under MGNREGA  
An oft-repeated concern of the Committee pertaining to MGNREGA has 
been the inadequacy of wage rates under the programme. Despite several 
recommendations in this regard, there has been no noticeable shift in the stance 
of the Department of Rural Development (DoRD). While the Department has 
consistently indicated that wage rates are revised annually, the Committee is of 
the view that the magnitude of such revisions remains insufficient in the context 
of rising inflation and the increasing cost of living in both rural and semi-urban 
areas. The Committee note with concern that the notified wage rates in several 
States continue to hover around Rs.200 per day, which is significantly lower 

72 
 
 
than the prevailing market wage rates for similar categories of labour in those 
very States. Such a disparity undermines the fundamental objective of 
MGNREGA as a programme intended to provide livelihood security and fair 
compensation for rural labour. The Committee are of the view that the existing 
methodology for wage determination warrants a comprehensive review. In 
particular, the continued reliance on an outdated base year and the absence of a 
robust inflation-linked mechanism have resulted in wage levels that do not 
adequately reflect present economic realities. The Committee, therefore, 
reccomends that the Department of Rural Development undertake a thorough 
reassessment of the wage structure under MGNREGA and consider instituting a 
minimum wage floor of  Rs. 400 per day under the programme. The Committee 
further recommend that the wage rates under MGNREGA be systematically 
indexed to an appropriate inflation indicator to ensure periodic and meaningful 
adjustments. The Committee note that the base year of 2011 continues to be 
used as the reference for determining wage calculations under MGNREGA, 
which has remained static for a considerable period. The Committee are of the 
view that in the present context, when the country is well beyond 2025, reliance 
on such an outdated base year fails to adequately capture the prevailing 
economic conditions, inflationary trends and changes in rural wage structures. 
The Committee, therefore, recommend that the base year be revised at the 
earliest so that wage determination under MGNREGA more accurately reflects 
current economic realities and ensures that the notified wages remain 
meaningful and relevant to rural workers.  

73 
 
 
7.  Wage and Material Pendencies under MGNREGA  
The Committee are concerned about the continuous delays/pendencies in 
the disbursement of the Centre’s share of funds under the wage and material 
components of MGNREGA across multiple States and Union Territories. As per 
the information provided by the Department of Rural Development, as of 
22.02.2026, the pending liabilities stand at Rs.5,940.54 crore in wages, 
Rs.10,921.59 crore in material components and total pending liabilities are 
Rs.16,862.13 crore. This accounts for 56.20% of the current budget, meaning 
that more than half of the allocated funds will be used to clear previous years' 
dues. Consequently, the actual working budget for the current financial year is 
reduced to Rs.13,137.87 crore, significantly limiting the scheme’s capacity to 
function effectively and meet its primary objective of preventing rural distress 
and ensuring livelihood security. The Committee further note that with an overall 
allocation of around Rs.30,000 crore for the year, the existing and anticipated 
liabilities together may rise to nearly Rs.25,000 crore, leaving very little fiscal 
space for fresh works under the scheme. Given the demand-driven nature of 
MGNREGA, it is therefore imperative that adequate and realistic budgetary 
allocations are provided to ensure uninterrupted implementation of the 
programme. The Committee feel that both the aspect of MGNREGA, i.e. a 
demand driven nature of the scheme and creation of assets under the scheme 
are severely hampered. Any administrative or procedural lapse causing delays 
in wage payments and material fund release in the context of a scheme of such 
enormous proportion like MGNREGA which caters to the nook and corners of 

74 
 
 
the country and has millions of beneficiaries enrolled as job card holders would 
only deter the needy person from availing the benefits of the scheme. Therefore, 
the Committee recommend DoRD to spruce up its financial management of the 
scheme and tighten its oversight on the systemic gaps that may have crept into 
the implementation of MGNREGA at the ground level, so as to ensure the 
earliest eradication of pendencies in wage payments and material components. 
The Committee further emphasise that, given the demand-driven nature of the 
scheme, adequate additional budgetary provisions should be made as and when 
required to meet emerging demand, and no legitimate demand for work under 
MGNREGA should be curtailed or suppressed on account of shortage of funds 
8.  Non-Discrimination in Provision of Wage Employment Across Panchayats  
The Committee are of the view that the level of development already 
achieved in a Gram Panchayat should not become a ground for denial or 
restriction of wage employment support to eligible rural households residing 
therein. The Committee note that pockets of deprivation, vulnerable labour 
households and distress migration may continue to exist even within relatively 
better-performing Panchayats. The Committee, therefore, recommend that 
programme implementation should remain sensitive to household-level 
vulnerability and actual demand for employment, and that no eligible household 
should be denied access to wage employment merely on the ground that the 
concerned Panchayat is considered relatively developed.  
  

75 
 
 
9.  Eligible Beneficiaries under PMAY-G  
The Committee note that a persistent challenge in the implementation of 
PMAY-G has been the identification of genuine beneficiaries, particularly 
homeless individuals and those residing in kutcha houses. The eligibility criteria 
for PMAY-G were determined based on housing deprivation parameters from the 
Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC), 2011. However, considerable time has 
passed since the base year, and it has been widely observed that many eligible 
beneficiaries have become ineligible while a whole lot of eligible beneficiaries 
have surfaced and waiting to avail the benefits. This gap in beneficiary 
identification is a serious deficiency in the scheme’s implementation and 
requires urgent rectification. The Committee recommend that the Department of 
Rural Development (DoRD) undertake a comprehensive review of the existing 
list of eligible beneficiaries at the earliest. Additionally, the policy framework 
must be revised to ensure that newly emerging needy households—who were 
not covered under the SECC 2011 Census—are duly included in the PMAY-G 
scheme. This will ensure that no eligible and deserving beneficiary is left out. 
Furthermore, the Committee also recommend that the eligibility criteria be 
expanded to include households with semi-permanent structures, such as those 
with pucca walls or tin roofs, to better reflect the current housing realities and 
ensure comprehensive coverage under PMAY-G.  
  

76 
 
 
10.  Increase in Per Unit Assistance under PMAY-G  
The per unit assistance under PMAY-G for plain areas is Rs.1.20 lakh and 
for hilly areas is Rs.1.30 lakh which has remained static for a considerable 
period of time. With rising inflation having a detrimental effect on the cost 
associated with raw materials, transportation and labour, constructing a new 
house of the requisite size under PMAY-G for poor and needy beneficiaries with 
the present level of assistance appears to be an arduous task. The vision of 
“Housing for All” may not achieve its envisaged target unless beneficiaries are 
provided adequate financial handholding in terms of assistance of appropriate 
value and at the right juncture. Moreover, there are several instances where 
houses remain incomplete for want of adequate financial resources, thereby 
causing delays in achieving the targets under the scheme. The Committee, 
during their interactions with stakeholders and based on feedback received from 
various States, have observed that the actual expenditure incurred by 
beneficiaries for construction of a basic pucca house significantly exceeds the 
present assistance, with beneficiaries often compelled to mobilise additional 
resources through borrowings or personal savings to complete construction. In 
many cases, the total cost of construction has been reported to be substantially 
higher due to the rising cost of construction materials, transportation in remote 
areas, and increased labour charges. In view of the foregoing, the Committee 
find it necessary that a comprehensive review of the per-unit assistance under 
PMAY-G be undertaken on priority, particularly when the scheme has been 
extended up to March, 2029. The Committee are of the considered view that the 

77 
 
 
per-unit assistance under PMAY-G needs a substantial revision and recommend 
that the assistance be enhanced up to minimum Rs. 4 lakh per unit, with 
appropriate differentiation for difficult and hilly areas, so as to ensure that 
beneficiaries are able to construct a durable and dignified dwelling without 
being subjected to financial distress. Such a revision would not only reflect the 
prevailing construction costs but also strengthen the objective of the scheme in 
delivering safe and permanent housing to rural households. The Committee also 
recommend there should be parity in unit assistance provided under Pradhan 
Mantri Awas Yojana – Urban and Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana – Gramin. 
11.  Inclusion of Landless Households under PMAY-G  
The Committee note that apart from the inadequacy of the present unit 
assistance under PMAY-G, a serious challenge also persists in relation to 
families who are houseless but do not possess land for construction of a 
dwelling unit. The Committee are of the considered view that the objective of 
rural housing cannot be fully realised unless the concerns of the landless poor 
are addressed in an integrated manner. The Committee, therefore, recommend 
that the Department evolve a suitable policy framework for providing housing 
support to landless eligible households, including through land allotment, 
community land models or other viable arrangements in coordination with the 
State Governments. The Committee further recommend that the per unit 
assistance under PMAY-G be reviewed comprehensively in light of the 
prevailing cost of construction so that the assistance provided is realistic and 
adequate for completion of a dignified dwelling unit.  

78 
 
 
12.  Reconstruction Support for Houses Damaged by Fire and Natural 
Calamities under PMAY-G  
The Committee note that in many rural areas houses of poor families are 
rendered uninhabitable due to fire accidents, floods and other natural 
calamities, leaving affected households in immediate distress. The Committee 
are of the view that such households require urgent housing support and should 
not be left to await prolonged procedural processes. The Committee, therefore, 
recommend that the Department consider evolving an appropriate provision or 
priority window under PMAY-G for reconstruction of houses belonging to 
eligible rural households that have been destroyed or severely damaged due to 
fire, floods or similar calamities, in coordination with the concerned State 
Governments.  
13.  Consultation and Institutional Role of Members of Parliament under 
PMGSY 
The Committee are fully aware of the inbuilt mechanism under PMGSY 
which mandates consultation with the concerned Members of Parliament during 
the finalisation of road plans. However, despite these provisions, the Committee 
continue to receive frequent complaints from Members of Parliament regarding 
non-compliance with the consultative procedures in both letter and spirit. 
Instances have been brought to the notice of the Committee where consultation 
is either not undertaken at all or reduced to a mere formality, such as obtaining 
consent without sharing adequate details or without facilitating proper ground-

79 
 
 
level verification of proposed road alignments. The Committee are of the view 
that Members of Parliament, being elected representatives and the primary point 
of contact for grievances of the local population, possess valuable insights 
regarding the developmental needs of their constituencies and are therefore well 
placed to provide meaningful inputs during the planning stage of rural 
connectivity projects. The Committee also reiterate that the role of Members of 
Parliament should not be confined merely to post-facto information, but should 
extend to meaningful consultation and appropriate institutional association in 
the planning, oversight and approval processes relating to major rural 
development projects. In this regard, the Committee are of the view that suitable 
arrangements may be evolved to ensure due participation of Members of 
Parliament, or their authorised representatives where necessary, in relevant 
State-level or district-level oversight and approval mechanisms. The Committee 
are further concerned to note that the prescribed guidelines relating to 
foundation laying and inauguration ceremonies of PMGSY roads are also not 
being consistently followed. As per extant provisions, the foundation stone and 
inauguration of PMGSY roads are to be carried out by the concerned Member of 
Parliament (Lok Sabha) along with other elected representatives of the area. 
However, several instances of deviation from this protocol have been reported, 
wherein Members of Parliament were either not invited or were informed only 
after the event. The Committee are of the firm view that the presence of 
Members of Parliament at such events strengthens transparency and 
accountability in the execution of projects funded from the public exchequer. In 
view of the above, the Committee recommend that the Department of Rural 

80 
 
 
Development should ensure strict compliance with all consultative and protocol 
provisions relating to Members of Parliament under PMGSY and impress upon 
the State Governments and implementing agencies the necessity of adhering to 
these provisions in both letter and spirit. 
14.  Reassessment of “Saturated” Districts under PMGSY  
The Committee note that although several districts have been shown as 
having achieved saturation under the Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana 
(PMGSY IV), concerns continue to be received that a number of habitations, 
particularly those situated in forest-fringe, hilly and otherwise difficult areas, still 
remain without all-weather road connectivity. The Committee are of the 
considered view that mere classification of a district as saturated cannot be a 
ground for overlooking habitations that remain genuinely unconnected due to 
terrain, forest-related clearances or administrative exclusion. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Department of Rural Development undertake a 
fresh and detailed reassessment of such districts, with special focus on 
habitations left out on account of forest and difficult-area constraints, and 
ensure that districts where genuine connectivity gaps continue are not treated 
as fully saturated till universal road access is actually achieved.  
15.  Timely Approval and Selection of Roads under PMGSY  
The Committee observe that delays in appraisal, approval and sanction of 
road proposals under PMGSY adversely affect execution timelines, escalate 
costs and delay the intended benefits of rural connectivity to the local 

81 
 
 
population. The Committee are of the view that avoidable procedural delays 
defeat the core objective of the scheme, especially in remote and underserved 
regions where road connectivity remains a basic developmental necessity. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that the Department streamline the approval 
process under PMGSY through clearly defined timelines, closer coordination 
with the State Governments and regular monitoring of pending proposals so 
that eligible roads are sanctioned and executed without undue delay.  
16.  Revision of Population and Eligibility Norms for Rural Connectivity  
The Committee note that the determination of eligibility and prioritisation 
under PMGSY has continued to rely upon older population benchmarks and 
legacy data, which in many areas no longer reflect the present demographic 
realities on the ground. The Committee are of the view that dependence on 
outdated population parameters may result in exclusion of deserving 
habitations that have grown over time or whose connectivity needs have 
changed significantly. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Department undertake a review of the existing norms and criteria used for 
identification and prioritisation of habitations under PMGSY so that present-day 
population realities and actual connectivity deprivation are more accurately 
captured.  
17.  Allocation of Funds and Revision of Assistance under NSAP 
The National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP) is a 100% Centrally 
Funded Centrally Sponsored Scheme providing social security assistance to 

82 
 
 
senior citizens, widows, persons with disabilities, and bereaved families upon 
the death of the primary breadwinner. The Committee note that the Budget 
Estimates for NSAP have remained almost static at around Rs. 9,652 crore for 
the last three financial years and Rs. 9,671 crore for the current financial year. 
The Committee are unable to comprehend the rationale behind the continued 
stagnation in allocation since 2022–23, particularly when the programme caters 
to approximately 3.09 crore BPL beneficiaries (including about 2.97 crore 
pensioners) across the country. Given the importance of ensuring financial 
protection to vulnerable sections of society, the Committee are of the view that 
the existing level of allocation requires urgent review. The Committee also 
observe that the assistance amounts provided under the major components of 
NSAP, namely the Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme (IGNOAPS), 
Indira Gandhi National Widow Pension Scheme (IGNWPS) and Indira Gandhi 
National Disability Pension Scheme (IGNDPS), which range from Rs. 200 to Rs. 
500 per month, remain extremely low and inadequate in the present economic 
context. The Committee find such meagre assistance wholly insufficient to 
provide meaningful support to beneficiaries belonging to highly marginalised 
and economically distressed households, especially when the cost of living has 
increased substantially over the years. In view of the above, the Committee 
recommend that the Department of Rural Development submit a comprehensive 
proposal for Cabinet consideration seeking both an enhanced budgetary 
allocation for NSAP and a substantial upward revision of the assistance 
amounts under all its components. The Committee further emphasise that the 
adequacy of pension support under NSAP must be reassessed in light of 

83 
 
 
prevailing cost-of-living conditions and a structured mechanism for periodic 
revision of pension amounts linked to appropriate inflation indices should be 
introduced so as to preserve the real value of assistance over time. Such 
measures would ensure that the programme continues to provide meaningful, 
predictable and dignified income support to vulnerable rural households. 
18.  Ensuring Universal and Hassle-Free Access under NSAP 
The Committee note with concern that under the National Social 
Assistance Programme, eligible beneficiaries, particularly the elderly, widows, 
disabled persons and residents of remote tribal areas, often face avoidable 
difficulties in accessing benefits on account of banking formalities, biometric 
authentication failures, KYC-related issues and weak mobile connectivity. The 
Committee are of the view that a social assistance programme meant for the 
most vulnerable should be guided by ease of access and not by cumbersome 
procedural barriers. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Department 
evolve alternative and simplified mechanisms for delivery of assistance to 
eligible beneficiaries, especially in remote and connectivity-challenged regions, 
and ensure that no eligible person is denied or delayed benefits on account of 
procedural or technological hurdles. The Committee further recommend that the 
existing cap or quota-based constraints under NSAP be revisited so that every 
eligible beneficiary may receive assistance without undue waiting. The 
Committee also note that in several States new beneficiaries are often enrolled 
only when an existing beneficiary exits the scheme, typically upon death, due to 
fixed ceilings on beneficiary numbers. The Committee are of the view that such 

84 
 
 
a practice defeats the purpose of a social security programme. Any person who 
fulfils the eligibility criteria should be able to receive assistance without having 
to wait for vacancy under the capped list, and the Department should move 
towards maintaining a universal and dynamic list of eligible beneficiaries under 
NSAP. 
19.  Promotion of Products by Self-Help Groups (SHGs)  
Self-Help Groups (SHGs) under the scheme of DAY-NRLM are a boon to 
the rural women who work together for means of livelihoods. However, the 
Committee note that the women of SHGs may be better served in terms of 
generating revenue for their livelihoods if products prepared by them like dairy 
products, agriculture, handicrafts etc. have a proper platform for their marketing 
and income generation. Although SHGs have access to village mandis, the 
limited operational days of these mandis restrict their ability to sell their 
products effectively. Therefore, continuous promotion and marketing of SHG 
products is essential to provide them with the much-needed opportunity to 
thrive. This includes free connectivity for SHG products to online marketplaces 
like Amazon, Flipkart, etc. Additionally, SHG members should be provided with 
specialized computer training to help them launch their local products online 
through websites and social media platforms, enabling them to generate 
revenue through their hard work and skills. The Committee would like to be 
apprised of the status of such efforts along with the revenue generated by the 
SGHs. The Committee therefore recommend that the DoRD develop a robust 
marketing structure that allows SHG products to gain better visibility, leading to 

85 
 
 
wider local and international customer reach, ultimately enhancing revenue 
generation and ensuring sustainable incomes. The Committee also recommend 
that the DoRD should strengthen market linkage initiatives through expansion of 
digital marketplaces, partnerships with e-commerce platforms and promotion of 
producer collectives that can aggregate products and negotiate better prices.  
20.  Enhancing Financial Literacy among SHG Members  
The Committee note that under Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National 
Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY–NRLM), substantial progress has been 
achieved in mobilising rural women into community institutions. More than 10 
crore rural women have been organised into over 90 lakh Self-Help Groups 
(SHGs), and the SHG–Bank linkage programme has facilitated access to 
institutional credit of approximately Rs.11.95 lakh crore. While the Committee 
acknowledged the remarkable scale of financial inclusion achieved under the 
programme, the Committee also expressed concern during the course of 
deliberations that many SHG members, particularly in remote and economically 
vulnerable regions, lack adequate understanding of loan management, interest 
obligations, market dynamics, bookkeeping practices and business planning, 
which may affect the sustainability of SHG-based enterprises and could lead to 
sub-optimal utilisation of credit resources The Committee feel that 
strengthening financial capability at the grassroots level has become essential 
to ensure that SHGs evolve into viable income-generating institutions rather 
than remaining primarily credit-linked collectives. The Committee therefore 
recommend that the DoRD strengthen financial literacy and entrepreneurship 

86 
 
 
development initiatives for SHG members through structured training 
programmes covering areas such as basic financial management, enterprise 
planning, bookkeeping, digital financial transactions, market linkage strategies 
and risk management. The Committee further recommend that such initiatives 
be implemented in close coordination with banks, State Rural Livelihood 
Missions, training institutions and community resource persons, and be 
integrated with ongoing capacity-building efforts so as to ensure responsible 
utilisation of credit, sustainability of SHG enterprises and long-term economic 
empowerment of rural women.  
21.  DISHA Meetings and Overall Monitoring of Schemes  
Time and again, the Committee have taken note of the casual conduct of 
District Development Coordination and Monitoring Committee (DISHA) Meetings. 
The sole aim of such meetings is overseeing the effective utilisation of 
Government money for the welfare of the common’s masses and as such these 
meetings assume paramount importance and a tool of great value in the 
implementation of various rural schemes. However, the purpose for holding 
such Meetings is defeated with the non-appearance of responsible Officials at 
the helm of affairs, in lieu thereof, junior officers with no knowledge of the 
projects attending such DISHA meetings has become the order of the day. As on 
20 February, 2026, 992 meetings at District Level are conducted in 2025-26 in 
comparison to 645 in 2024-25 while only seven States (Chhattisgarh, Haryana, 
Karnataka, Kerala, Mizoram, Tamil Nadu and Uttarakhand) have conducted their 
State level DISHA Committee meetings in the year 2025-26. The Committee 

87 
 
 
therefore, implore upon DoRD to take serious note of this approach of District 
Development and Monitoring officials in the matter and ensure that the Meetings 
of DISHA Committees are held regularly (at least two meetings per year should 
be mandatorily conducted) as per the guidelines and also sufficient number of 
senior level representatives from the concerned Department is present there in 
these committees.  
22.  Addressing Administrative Constraints in Holding District-Level DISHA 
Meetings  
The Committee were informed during the course of deliberations that in 
certain districts DISHA meetings are not being convened regularly due to lack of 
awareness among District Collectors and district-level officials regarding the 
availability of reimbursement for meeting-related expenditure. The Committee 
pointed out that this administrative gap has, in some instances, resulted in 
reluctance on the part of district authorities to convene meetings. The 
Committee are of the considered view that such issues should not impede the 
functioning of an important monitoring platform intended to strengthen 
accountability in the implementation of rural development programmes. The 
Committee therefore recommend that the DoRD issue comprehensive 
instructions to all State Governments and district administrations clearly 
outlining the reimbursement provisions and procedural requirements for DISHA 
meetings. The Committee further recommend that the Ministry undertake 
awareness and orientation initiatives for District Collectors and other 
implementing authorities, and consider developing a simplified and timely 

88 
 
 
reimbursement mechanism, so that financial and administrative constraints do 
not discourage district authorities from convening DISHA meetings on a regular 
basis.  
23.  Inclusion of DMF in DISHA Review Mechanism and Wider Communication 
of Meeting Support Provisions  
The Committee note that effective monitoring of development expenditure 
at the district level requires convergence across major funding streams, 
including those which have substantial bearing on local infrastructure and 
welfare outcomes. The Committee are of the view that the District Development 
Coordination and Monitoring Committee (DISHA) would be more effective if 
important district-level funds and programmes, including District Mineral 
Foundation (DMF) review, are also brought within its review ambit. The 
Committee further note that the lack of awareness regarding reimbursement and 
expenditure support for DISHA meetings continues to impede their regular 
conduct in some places. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry 
issue explicit and widely communicated instructions to all States and district 
authorities regarding the admissible meeting expenditure, including the 
sanctioned ceiling for conduct of DISHA meetings, and also consider including 
DMF-related review in DISHA proceedings wherever relevant so as to strengthen 
district-level coordination and accountability.

2. At the outset, the Chairperson welcomed the Members to the sitting of the 
Committee convened for taking oral evidence of the representatives of the Department 
of Rural Development (Ministry of Rural Development) in connection with the 
examination of Demands for Grants 2026-27 relating to Department of Rural 
Development.     
 [Thereafter the representatives from the Ministry of Rural Development (Department of 
Rural Development) were called in] 
 
3. After welcoming the representatives, the Chairperson in his opening remarks 
stressed upon scheme-wise total budget estimate of the Department of Rural 
Development for the financial year 2026-27. The Chairperson, then asked the 
representatives of the Ministry to introduce themselves and place their views on the 
subject. The Secretary, Department of Rural Development (Ministry of Rural 
Development) after taking permission from the Chairperson directed the Joint Secretary 
to make a Power Point Presentation on the above-mentioned subject. 
 
4. During the PPT, Ministry discussed about the allocations viz. utilization of funds, 
i.e. percentage increase/decrease in various major schemes/heads over the last three 
years along with the plan allocation for the 2026-27. 
 
5. Subsequently, Members raised their individual queries. In response to the query 
of the Members, Ministry highlighted the initiatives taken under different schemes like 
MGNREGA, PMGSY, DAY-NRLM, PMAY-G, NSAP, etc. and also stressed upon the 
various important aspect of the newly introduced VB-G RAM-G, Act, the framework for 
its operationalization across the country, its transition provisions, timeline and funding 
pattern etc.  
 
6. During the discussion, Members, inter-alia, raised the points namely, transition 
from MGNREGA to VB-G RAM G, the rules and operational guidelines under the new 
Act, the issue of possible overlap between livelihood infrastructure proposed under VB-
G RAM G, reassessment of saturated districts under PMGSY, issue of wage payments 
and livelihood support under new framework, issues related to gaps in programme 
coverage and its monitoring and coordination. 
   
7. The Chairperson then thanked the representatives of the Department of Rural
