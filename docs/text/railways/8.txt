authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on their behalf, present this Eighth

I.  INTRODUCTORY 
 
1.1  Indian Railways continues to serve as the backbone of the nation’s 
transportation system, connecting diverse regions and driving economic growth 
through its extensive and integrated network. Reflecting the Government’s sustained 
commitment to infrastructure expansion and modernization, the Capital Outlay for 
2026–27 has been pegged at Rs. 2,93,030 crore, which is Rs. 27,830 crore higher than 
the Revised Estimates for 2025 –26. The Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) has been 
fixed at Rs. 2,78,030 crore, supplemented by Rs. 3,000 crore from Internal Resources 
and Rs. 12,000 crore under Extra Budgetary Resources (Partnership).  
1.2 The Annual Plan for 2026 –27 places continued emphasis on removal of traffic 
bottlenecks, capacity augmentation and strengthening of safety infrastructure across 
the railway network. In pursuit of these objectives, substantial allocations have been 
made under Budgetary Sources in BE 2026 –27, including Rs. 36,722 crore for New 
Lines, Rs. 37,750 crore for Doubling works, Rs. 22,853 crore for Track Renewals, Rs. 
800 crore for Safety Works at Level Crossings, Rs. 8,225 crore for Road Over/Under 
Bridges (ROBs/RUBs) and Rs. 7,500 crore for Signal and Telecommunication works.  
The overall Capex allocated for capacity augmentation stands at Rs.1,13,670 crore, for 
customer amenities at Rs. 11,900 crore and for rolling stock and inventories at Rs. 
64,397 crore. 
1.3 Indian Railways have formulated the National Rail Plan (NRP) with the 
objective of creating a “future -ready” railway system by infrastructure augmentation. 
The Plan envisions meeting the growing demand for passenger and freight transport, 
segregating freight and passenger traffic on High Density Networks through 
Dedicated Freight Corridors, enhancing speeds of both freight and passenger trains, 
expanding connectivity to ports, mines and industrial clusters, and increasing the 
network to about 72,000 rou te kilometers. It also emphasizes induction of modern 

2 
 
rolling stock, redevelopment of 1,337 stations under the Amrit Bharat Scheme, 
implementation of advanced safety systems such as KAVACH, elimination of level 
crossings, adoption of predictive maintenance practices, 100% electrification and 
achievement of Net Zero Carbon Emissions by 2030. 
1.4 Indian Railways have completed several landmark railway infrastructure 
projects aimed at enhancing regional connectivity and national integration. The 
Udhampur–Srinagar–Baramulla Rail Link (272 km) has been dedicated to the nation, 
ensuring seamless and all -weather connectivity of the Kashmir Valley with the rest of 
the country. The commissioning of the 51.38 km Bhairabi –Sairang New Line project 
through challenging hilly terrain has provided direct rail connectivity to Aizawl, the 
capital of Mizoram. Also, the inauguration of the New Pamban Bridge, the country’s 
first vertical lift sea bridge connecting Rameswaram Island to the mainland, marks a 
significant engineering achievement and strengthens connectivity in the southern 
region.

3.3 The main objectives and thrust areas of Annual Plan 2026 -27 are expanding, 
modernizing and enhancing the operational efficiency of the railway. The Ministry 
while detailing the main objectives and thrust areas remarked as under: 
 
A. Capacity Augmentation and Network Expansion: 
• New Lines (PH11) :  Construction of new rail lines presents a strategic 
avenue for enhancing last -mile connectivity. It contributes significantly 

7 
 
to economic development, particularly in remote areas, such as the 
Northeastern states and Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and 
Uttarakhand. New rail lines are planned to make travel easier within the 
country, helping people reach and leave areas that are difficult to 
access. Construction of new railway lines helps reduce congestion on 
existing routes by providing additional capacity for passenger and 
freight services. Improved transportation infrastructure attracts 
investment, support tourism, and enhance regional development. It also 
provides environmental benefits such as reduction of greenhouse gas 
emissions and contributes to a more sustainable and green 
transportation system by providing an alternative to road transportation. 
• Gauge Conversion (PH14)  :  Gauge conversion projects provide a 
uniform gauge for the smooth and fast flow of traffic, which is 
necessary for strategic and operating point of view. It improves the 
operational efficiency. 
• Doubling (PH15) :  The plan prioritizes the expansion of rail network 
capacity to meet the increasing demand for passenger and freight 
services. Projects for track doubling, tripling, and even quadrupling are 
underway to enhance line capacity. 
• Double-track railways use each track exclusively in one direction. This 
arrangement simplifies signaling systems. When one track of a double -
track railway is out of service due to track maintenance work or a train 
breaks down or during an unusual (Accident or ACF), all trains may be 
concentrated on a single usable track.  Maintenance 
and engineering work are easier on tracks in quadruple line with little 
resulting delay because standard double-track service can continue even 
if the other two double tracks are halted during the work. 
• Traffic Facilities (PH16) – Yard remodeling helps reduce congestion 
and improve the movement of passenger and freight trains. It involves 
interlocking of tracks to increase safety and capacity. Old yards were 
built for past needs, so remodeling removes issues like short shunting 

8 
 
necks and inadequate lines for cleaning or stabling trains. It also 
allows for more platforms to handle more trains, improving efficiency 
and reducing delays. Overall, yard remodeling is essential for 
improving railway infrastructure and making train services more 
reliable and efficient. 
• Dedicated Freight Corridors (PH61)  - Completion and 
operationalization of key sections of the Western Dedicated Freight 
Corridors to decongest the existing network. 
B. Modernization and Technological Upgradation: 
• Computerization (PH17)  : Updating mobile app services for 
passengers to improve the booking process and offer real -time train 
tracking.  Freight digitalization by online Indent placement, Supply 
information, Freight calculation and payment, Movement of receipt, 
Tracking of consignment, Detention charges (wharfage, demurrage) 
application. 
• Rolling Stock (PH21)  – Operationalization of coaches like; Vande 
Bharat, Amrit Bharat, LHB and Vande Sleeper, equipped with GPS, 
Wi-Fi, bio -toilets, and real -time information systems and upgraded 
interiors for enhancing passenger services. Acquisition of modern 
locomotives, exemplified by the WAG -12, represents a remarkable 
advancement in terms of power output and capabilities. Introduction of 
modern age wagons like Flat Multi -purpose Wagon, BCATIC and 
BLSS. 
• S&T Works (PH33)  - Advance signaling technologies such as 
Automatic Block Signaling (ABS) for  increased train frequency , 
KAVACH to avoid train collision etc. are useful in improving safety 
and efficiency. 
• Customer Amenities (PH53)  - Station Modernization by 
redevelopment of major railway stations under Amrit Bharat Station 
Scheme to provide world -class facilities to passengers. Modern Goods 

9 
 
Shed with high level platform, cover over platform, lighting and 
concrete pathways. 
• National High -Speed Rail Corporation Limited (PH61)  – Notable 
progress on Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train project. 
C. Safety and Security: 
• Road Safety Works - LC/ROB/RUB (PH29/30) - Continuing efforts to 
eliminate all unmanned level crossings across the broad -gauge network. 
The closure of level crossings and the construction of Road Over 
Bridges (ROBs) or Road Under Bridges (RUBs) offer numerous 
benefits, contributing to enhanced safety, efficiency, and overall 
effectiveness of railway and road transportation systems. 
• Track Renewal/Bridge Works, Tunnel Works & Approaches  (PH31/32) 
- Significant investments in renewing old tracks, bridges and tunnels to 
enhance safety, Capacity, Operational Efficiency, Long -Term 
Durability and Passenger Experience. 
• Workshops including Production Units (PH42) – Workshops and PUs 
contribute to the overall performance of the rail network by facilitating 
timely repairs, maintenance, and production of rolling stocks. This, in 
turn, enhances the reliability and efficiency of the railway system, 
fulfils operational needs, enhance safety standards, and boost capacity 
and throughput. 
D. Electrification: 
• Electrification Projects (PH35) - Indian Railways aims to achieve 100% 
electrification. This is in line with the goal of reducing dependence on 
fossil fuels and making railways a greener, more sustainable mode of 
transportation. 
E. Sustainability and Green Initiatives: 
• Other Specified Works (PH64) - Projects include installation of solar 
power plants, rainwater harvesting systems and water recycling plants at 
stations and maintenance depots. 
F. Human Resource Development: 

10 
 
• Training/HRD (PH65) - Training programs to equip railway personnel with 
up-to-date skills and knowledge, preparing them to handle the complexities 
of modern railway operations. It leads to a more competent and efficient 
workforce to insure safety and operational efficiency. 
G. Urban Transport Integration: 
• Metropolitans Transport Project (PH81) - Expanding and improving 
suburban rail networks in major metropolitan areas to ease congestion and 
provide better connectivity for commuters. 
H. Freight Sector Development: 
• Encouraging public -private partnerships (PPPs) in freight operations, 
development of private freight terminals, and wagon leasing. 
I. Financial Sustainability and Resource Mobilization: 
• Monetization of Assets: Indian Railways plans to monetize assets like land, 
stations, and other infrastructure through long -term leasing and 
partnerships with private players. 
• Non-Fare Revenue: Increasing non -fare revenue through advertising, real 
estate development, and leasing of railway infrastructure.

• Despite substantial fixed and rising obligations, Indian Railways

Modernization and Improvement/up -gradation of Rolling Stock to enhance 
safety, convenience and comfort of passengers is a continuous and ongoing 
process on Indian Railways. 
The following new technologies have been adopted in locomotives in recent 
years:  
i) Head on Generation Technology: In HOG system electrical power is 
fed to the coaches directly from the Overhead Equipment (OHE) through 
the Locomotive. It eliminates the need for separate diesel fuelled power 
cars in trains and thus reduces the need for pulling extra coaches and also 
increases efficiency further resulting in reduction in carbon footprint.  
Indian Railways have a total of 2342 HOG compliant locomotives as in 
January 2026. 
ii) Provision of RMS on electric locos: Remote Monitoring System helps in 
remote diagnostic of the failures in the locomotives bringing down the 
failure investigation time. As in January 2026, it has been commissioned 
on about 2233 electric locomotives and 2910 diesel locomotives. 
iii) Kavach (loco equipment): Kavach is an indigenously developed Automatic 
Train Protection (ATP) system. Kavach aids the loco pilot in train running 
within specified speed limits by automatic application of brakes in case it is 
exceeded to ensure safely. 
iv)  High Reach Pantographs: High Reach Pantographs are being provided to 
for seamless working in high -rise OHE territory. Till date, 5655 
locomotives have been provided with High Reach Pantographs 
pantographs. 
v) Waterless urinals: Waterless Urinals are being provided in locomotives as 
an amenity to the loco crew.  These are robust in design considering riding 
shocks and vibrations and can be retrofitted without many alterations to 
layout and without interference with working of other equipment of 
locomotive.  Till date more than 2000 locomotives have been provided 
with Urinals as in January 2026. 

44 
 
vi) LED headlight:   LED Headlights are being provided in locomotives to 
improve visibility to Loco Pilots during train operations.  7362 locomotives 
have been provided with LED Headlights. All new locomotives are being 
manufactured with LED Headlights.  Total 8718 locomotives are provided 
with LED Headlights as in January 2026.   
vii) Energy saving mode in electric locomotives:   To save Electrical Energy in 
3-phase freight locomotives (WAG9/WAG9H/WAG9HC) modification is 
being carried out in existing scheme of load distribution and ‘Energy 
Saving Mode’ has been included in software of Auxiliary converter to put 
off the auxiliary blowers of locomotives, when cooling is not required 
during the idling of locomotives.  3028 locomotives have been modified 
with this feature as in January 2026. 
Technological improvement is a continuous process. As a step towards the 
modernization of Indian Railways (IR), many technology driven projects are 
being introduced to improve upon the Safety & Productivity and to enhance 
availability, reliability, operability, maintainability of the Rolling Assets like 
Wheel Impact Load Detector (WILD) and Hot Axlebox Hot Wheel (HAHW) 
Detector/Hot Box Detector (HBD). WILD is a way -side inspection system that 
measures the impact of wheel on tracks to automatically identify the defective 
wheels in Rolling Stock and HAHW/HBD are automatic systems consisting of 
infrared sensors installed on wayside to measure the temperature of axle 
bearing box/wheel of rolling stock while in motion. 
Further, as a step towards inducting latest technology on IR, the pilot projects 
viz. Machine Vision based Inspection Systems (MVIS) and Automatic Wheel 
Profile Measurement System (AWPMS) have been taken up. The MVIS will 
generate alerts on detecting any hanging, loose or missing components of 
moving trains and AWPMS allows automatic non-contact measurement of train 
wheel profile ensuring measurement of wheel geometry and wear. 
In addition, installation of CCTV cameras in coaches and locomotives has been 
taken up to strengthen passenger security.

14.1 Strengthening, upgradation, modernisation  and improvement of track 
infrastructure is a continuous and ongoing process over Indian Railways. The 
following measures as detailed by the Ministry are being taken by Indian Railways to 
upgrade railway tracks:  
i. Modern track structure consisting of 60kg, 90 Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS) 
rails, Wider and heavier Pre -stressed Concrete Sleepers (PSC) with elastic 
fastening, fan -shaped layout turnout on PSC sleepers and H -beam Sleepers on 
girder bridges are being used while carrying out primary track renewals.  
ii. The Thick Web Switches and Weldable CMS Crossings are being used in 
turnout renewal works.  
iii. Supply of 260m long rail panels have been increased to avoid welding of joints, 
thereby improving safety and riding quality  
iv. Thick Web Switch Expansion Joints are being used in place of earlier 
Conventional/Improved SEJs. 
v. Adoption of better welding technology for rails i.e. Flash Butt Welding.  
vi. Adoption of mechanized system for track maintenance using high output plain 
tampers and points & crossing tampers for improved maintainability & 
reliability of track.   
vii. Deployment of state -of-the-art modern machines including Rail Grinding 
Machines to further improve asset reliability. 
viii. Mechanisation of track laying activities through use of track machines like 
PQRS, TRT, T-28 etc. 
ix. Interlocking of Level Crossing (LC) Gates for enhancing safety at LC gates. 
x. Use of advanced Phased Array technology of testing of rail and welds. 
xi. Deployment of Integrated Track Monitoring Systems (ITMS) and Oscillation 
Monitoring System (OMS) for comprehensive health assessment to ascertain 
optimal maintenance requirements. 

71 
 
xii. Adoption of portable Track Measuring Trolley for continuous recording of track 
parameters in yards. 
xiii. Using web enabled Track Management System (TMS) for integration and data 
analytics of the track inspection records received through various sources to 
enable precise maintenance inputs.

16.2 The Ministry have provided the following details of research and development 
initiatives undertaken by Indian Railways/RDSO and tangible outcomes achieved in 
terms of safety, efficiency and cost reduction: 
1. Development of Kavach System for Automatic Train Protection in Indian 
Railways which is comparable to World Standards and is cost effective.  
2. New Modern Rolling Stock like Vande Bharat, Trains, VB Sleeper trains, 
Amrit Bharat Trains, and Vande Metro Trains have been developed.  
3. Other New Rolling Socks have been Developed  like Double Decker Cargo 
Liner, Treasury Vans, Linke Hofmann Busch (LHB) Parcel Van with rear 
opening, Automobile Carriers Vistadome Coaches and Dining Cars. 
4. Development of 2.4 MW first Hydrogen Fuel Cell DEMU has been done by 
Research, Designs and Standards Organisation (RDSO). The rake is ready and 
is awaiting Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organization  (PESO) clearance at 
Northern Railway for the Hydrogen Fueling infrastructure. 
5. Semi Automatic Couplers with Crash Tube is being developed to provide 
slack free coupling in Amrit Bharat trains. This will reduce Jack -Knifing and 
jerks in trains. 
6. Comprehensive review/ framing of guidelines on issues related to Disaster 
Management leading to more reliable equipment, coordinated response and 
time sensitive response to unforeseen circumstances. 
7. Development of Emission kit for achieving US Environmental Protection 
Agency (EPA) Tier 0+ emission standards in HHP Diesel locomotives 
Presently High Horse Power (HHP) Diesel locos do not meet any emission 
norms. In June 2022, Railway Board entrusted RDSO to float EOI for supply of 
Emission kits to upgrade HHP diesel Locomotives to US EPA Tier 0+ emission 
standards. After floating Expression of Interest (EOI), RDSO developed the 
specification for the emission kits for achieving Tier 0+ emission standards.  
8. Diesel locomotive operation in USBRL (Udhampur - Srinagar - Baramulla 
Rail Link) Section 
Original Equipment Manufacturers  (OEMs) of Diesel locomotives viz M/s 
WABTEC and M/s Progress Rail were approached by Northern Railway to 

91 
 
provide a solution for running of Diesel Locomotives in harsh climatic 
conditions of USBRL section. Due to low volumes of the number of 
locomotives to be modified for the new section, both the OEMs didn't provide 
any solution. NR approached RDSO to provide a scheme for modification of 
HHP Diesel locomotives to enable them run in sub zero temp. & high altitudes. 
A detailed study was carried out and each and every system of the locomotive 
was analysed for suitability to the climate. A scheme for modification was 
developed and provided to NR enabling operation of HHP Diesel locomotives 
over USBRL. Trials were successfully conducted in December 2024 over 
USBRL with WDG4D loco and presently 20 Diesel locomotives are operating 
in USBRL section. 
9. Modification in Diesel locomotives to enhance safe operation of trains in 
ghat sections RDSO has issued instructions for modification in Diesel 
locomotives for provision of Audio Visual indication in Banker/Rear Diesel 
Locomotives for Brake Pipe (BP) pressure drop. This modification in the 
Diesel locomotive will provide additional alert to the loco pilot of the 
Banker/Rear locomotive when Braking is initiated by the loco pilot of the 
leading locomotive so that the loco pilot in Banker/rear loco can also initiate 
braking simultaneously. This enhances the safety of train operation in Ghat 
section of Railways. 
10. Three-phase Propulsion Technology  has been introduced in Distributed 
Power Rolling Stocks (DPRS) i.e EMUs/MEMUs/Vande Bharat Trainsets 
having potential for regeneration of energy to the extent of 30% resulting in 
saving of overall energy consumption and operating efficiency. 
11. Fire safety  has been improved in terms of use of HL2/HL3 compliant 
items/components as per EN 45545 for Rolling stock application.. 
12. Development of Reverse cycle Roof Mounted Packaged Unit (RMPU)  has 
been undertaken to improve the fire safety, reliability and energy efficiency.  
13. Improvement in specifications:  More than 60 Specifications have been taken 
up for upgradation during 2025 -26 to improve Safety, Operational reliability, 
efficiency and indigenization leading to cost control.

PART-II 
OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS 
 
ANALYSIS OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANT (2026-27) 
1. Indian Railways, widely regarded as the backbone of India’s transport 
system, continues to strengthen national connectivity and fuel economic growth 
through its expansive network. The 2026 –27 budget underscores the 
Government’s firm commitment to modernization and expansion, reflecting a 
strategic focus on infrastructure development and long -term sustainability.  The 
annual plan emphasizes removal of traffic bottlenecks, capacity augmentation, 
and safety improvements, with major allocations for new lines, doubling works, 
track renewals, and significant investments in safety works, bridges, and 
signaling. Overall, a substantial amount  has been earmarked for capacity 
augmentation, customer amenities and rolling stock. The National Rail Plan 
envisions a future-ready system with dedicated freight corridors, enhanced train 
speeds, expanded connectivity to industrial hubs, redevelopment of 1,337 
stations, advanced safety systems like KAVACH, elimination of level crossings, 
predictive maintenance, full electrification, and net zero carbon emissions by 
2030. Landmark projects already completed include the Udhampur –Srinagar–
Baramulla link connecting Kashmir, the Bhairabi –Sairang line linking 
Mizoram’s capital Aizawl, and the New Pamban Bridge, India’s first vertical lift 
sea bridge.  The Demands for Grants for the year 2026 –27 of the Ministry of 
Railways were tabled in the House on 2 nd February, 2026. The Committee 
thereafter undertook a detailed scrutiny of these Demands, examining both 
revenue and capital provisions in light of the objectives of modernization, 
capacity augmentation, and safety enhancement across the railway network. 
Following its deliberations, the Committee have formulated a set of 
recommendations, which are summarized in the succeeding paragraphs. 

107 
 
 
ANNUAL PLAN 
2. The Committee note that the Ministry has set the Annual Plan for the year 
2026-27 at Rs. 2,93,030 crore which is more than the Revised Estimates for 2025 -
26 by Rs.  27,830 crore . This includes Rs. 2,78,030 crore as Gross Budgetary 
Support (GBS)  including Rs. 10,000 crore under the Rashtriya Rail Sanraksha 
Kosh (RRSK). Internal Resources contribute Rs. 3000 crore including Rs.1000 
crore Railway’s contribution for RRSK, while Rs. 12,000 crore comes from Extra 
Budgetary Resources (EBR).   The Committee further note that the focus of the 
Annual Plan 2026 –27 continues to be on expanding, modernizing and enhancing 
the operational efficiency of railway.  Accordingly, allocations have been made 
under key plan heads such as New Lines (Rs. 36,722 crore), Doubling (Rs. 37,750 
crore), Track Renewals (Rs. 22,853 crore), Rolling Stock (Rs. 65,497 crore), 
Safety Works of Level Crossings (Rs. 800 crore), Road Over/Under Bridges (Rs. 
8,225 crore), Customer Amenities (Rs. 11,972 crore) and Signal & Telecom 
Works (Rs. 7,500 crore) under Budgetary Sources in BE 2026 –27. While noting 
that utilization of the 2025 –26 budgetary allocation stands at around 90% till 
January, 20 26, t he Committee recommend full and optimal utilization of 
budgetary resources and urge the Ministry to ensure timely execution of projects 
to avoid time and cost overruns.  The Committee emphasize an adoption of safety 
first and technology driven infrastructure approach.  The Committee further 
recommend that priority be accorded to new lines, gauge conversion, doubling 
works, and timely completion of Dedicated Freight Corridors to ensure 
expansion of the railway network. Alongside these measures, modernization of 
rolling stock and enhancement of customer amenities should remain a key focus. 
The Committee also emphasize the need for a forward -looking approach that 
integrates sustainability and green initiatives, human resource development, 
urban transport integration, and resource mobilization, while ensuring financial 
sustainability at the core of the annual targets set by Indian Railways. 
 

108 
 
 
INTERNAL RESOURCES 
3. The Committee note the persistent shortfall in internal resource 
generation over the past few years. Against the Revised Estimates of Rs. 3000 
crore for 202 3–24, the actual generation stood at Rs. 2943 crore; for 202 4–25, 
actual receipts were Rs. 1988 crore and in 2025 –26, Rs. 2028 crore has been 
realized up to January, 2026 against RE of Rs.  3,000 crore. Notwithstanding this 
trend, the target for internal resource generation has been retained at Rs. 3,000 
crore at the BE stage for 2026 –27. The Committee, while appreciating the 
various measures undertaken to augment internal resources despite nearly 90% 
of expenditure being classified as inelastic and committed, are of the considered 
view that any shortfall in internal resources may compel the Ministry to curtail 
or reallocate budgetary provisions from planned heads. Such adjustments, in 
turn, could adversely impact the timely execution of projects for which 
allocations were originally envisaged, thereby impacting the overall pace of 
modernization and expansion of the railway network.  The Committee further 
observe that inadequate internal resource generation increases reliance on Gross 
Budgetary Support.  Therefore, the Committee recommend the Ministry to 
undertake a comprehensive review of all potential avenues for resource 
mobilization, with focused efforts on improving operational efficiency , enhancing 
returns from commercially viable initiatives , augmenting non -fare revenue  and 
optimization of terminal and logistics infrastructure  for freight earning.  The 
Committee note that expanding network capacity, removing bottlenecks, 
developing freight corridors and cargo terminals, and introducing freight -
specific policies are important steps. Improving procurement and maintenance of 
rolling stock, tracks, and signaling is also necessary. These measures should be 
carried out in a time -bound and outcome -oriented manner. Doing so will help 
Indian Railways generate more internal resources  which will protect the 
financial stability of the Railways and ensure steady funding for both ongoing 
projects and future growth initiatives. 

109 
 
 
EXTRA BUDGETARY RESOURCES (EBR)  
4. The Committee observe that resource generation through Extra Extra 
Budgetary Resources  (Public Private Partnership) over the past few years has 
met or surpassed the targets and note that for 2023–24, actual generation was Rs. 
16,625 crore against RE of Rs. 17,000 crore, in 2024–25, with actual generation of 
Rs. 15,049 crore against RE of Rs. 10,000 crore, and in 2025–26, where Rs. 10940 
crore has been realized up to January, 2026 against RE of Rs.  10,000 crore. The 
target for EBR (P) generation has now been kept at Rs. 12,000 crore at the BE 
stage for 2026 –27. The Committee note that internal revenue generation has 
remained largely stagnant in recent years, necessitating reliance on GBS in 
addition to EBR for financing ongoing and expansion projects. The Committee 
appreciate that the Ministry has exercised financial prudence by limiting 
borrowings under EBR in order to avoid additional lease liabilities and is 
prioritizing GBS to augment capacity without increasing repayment burdens. In 
this context, the Committee recommend the Ministry to adopt a calibrated and 
project-specific approach towards PPP -based investments, ensuring that critical 
and capacity -enhancement projects are not delayed due to funding constraints. 
The Committee note and appreciate the renewed focus of Indian railways on 
attracting private investments via different models, the results of which shall be 
visible in coming years. The Committee therefore desire the Ministry to identify 
commercially viable and revenue -generating projects for private participation, 
explore the feasibility of evolving appropriate risk -sharing mechanisms to 
safeguard the interests of private investors,  and streamline approval and 
regulatory processes to attract credible private investment.  
NET REVENUE 
5. The Committee observe that the net earnings of the Railways for 2024 –25 
were estimated at Rs. 2800 crore at the Budget Estimates (BE) stage, which was 
subsequently revised downward to Rs. 1341 crore at the Revised Estimates (RE) 

110 
 
stage, while the actual realization stood at Rs. 2660 crore. For 2025 –26, the BE 
was pegged at Rs. 3041 crore, later revised downward to Rs. 1957 crore at the 
RE stage. For 2026 –27, the target has been fixed at Rs. 3000 crore.  The 
Committee note that passenger services continue to be operated with a 
substantial social obligation component, with an average subsidy of about 45 per 
cent, and that several uneconomical routes are maintained in order to ensure 
connectivity to remote and rural regions of the count ry. The Committee further 
observe that since 2022 –23, the Railways has undertaken record levels of capital 
expenditure, with significant investments directed towards new line construction, 
track doubling, electrification, augmentation of rolling stock and adoption of 
modern signalling systems. The Committee acknowledge that such capital 
investments are not merely routine expenditures but constitute long -term 
strategic assets aimed at enhancing capacity, efficiency, safety and sustainable 
growth of the Indian Railways . However, t he Committee are of the view that 
improving financial sustainability in Indian Railways requires a balanced and 
result driven  approach to both revenue augmentation and expenditure control. 
The Committee further stress that the substantial capital outlay on new lines, 
doubling, rolling stock, and Dedicated Freight Corridors must be effectively 
translated into measurable outcomes, particularly in terms of traffic growth and 
revenue enhancement.  Therefore, t he Committee desire the  Ministry to take 
concrete steps to narrow the gap between passenger earnings and operating costs 
without compromising affordability for vulnerable sections  through a segment-
wise review of passenger operations to identify services that can be commercially 
strengthened through better ca pacity utilisation, demand -based fare 
management and targeted introduction of premium services . Further 
monetisation of non -fare avenues  through measures like 
advertisements/hoardings at stations and inside trains, leasing land for 
commercial development and setting up ATMs  should be further explored .  In 
freight segment, the Committee desire the Ministry to focus on increasing freight 
capacity and market share . While continuing with the impetus on infrastructure 
strengthening and modernization, focus needs to be to reduce maintenance costs.  

111 
 
The Committee also recommend i mplementing of modern IT and AI based 
systems to improve asset utilization and reduce, for instance, energy costs and

EXPENDITURE AND OPERATING RATIO (OR)  
6.  The Committee note that the revenue expenditure of Indian Railways has 
increased substantially over the years, rising from Rs.1,38,236 crore in 2020 –21 
to a projected Rs.2,99,500 crore in 2026 –27. However, the Committee appreciate 
the proactive steps being taken by Indian railway by adopting the two -pronged 
approach of increasing revenue and reducing expenditure and recommend  that 
sustained efforts be made to reduce Ordinary Working Expenses by promoting 
energy efficiency, expanding the use of predictive and technology -driven 
maintenance systems including AI system to minimize repair and breakdown 
costs, and strengthening skill development initiatives to enhance workforce 
productivity and operational efficiency.  The Committee further observe that the 
Railways have taken several initiatives to  improve the Operating Ratio, such as 
enhancing freight capacity and efficiency through network expansion, 
modernization of rolling stock, rationalizing repair and maintenance of rolling 
stock, development of cargo terminals under the Gati Shakti Multi-Modal Cargo 
Terminal (GCT)’ policy, etc . The Committee also note that from the year 2022 -

showing an upward trend. For the year 2025 -26, Operating Ratio is estimated to 
be 98.82%. The Committee observe that for 2026 -27 the Railways have projected 
a target of 98.40% for its Operating Ratio which continues to be above 98%.   
Therefore, the Committee desire the Ministry to boost freight revenue through 
better utilization of DFCs and expansion into non-bulk cargo, strengthening non-
fare revenue through structured asset monetization, station redevelopment and 
commercial use of railway land. The Committee emphasize t imely completion of 
capacity augmentation projects to avoid cost overruns and need for linking 
capital investment to measurable revenue outcomes  to ensure sustainable 
financial improvement.

DOUBLING OF RAILWAY LINES 
7. The Committee notes that removal of traffic bottlenecks has been 
accorded priority in the Budget 2026–27, with a significant increase in the throw -
forward for doubling works, rising to Rs. 37,750 crore over the previous year.  
However, the Committee observe that against a target of 2,600 km for 2025 –26, 
573 km (22.04%) was completed up to December 2025, while financial utilization 
stood at Rs. 21,457.27 crore, i.e., 73.92% of the Revised Estimate of Rs. 29,026 
crore. The Committee acknowledge that doubling projects are long -gestation 
assets involving land acquisition, statutory clearances and complex execution in 
high-density corridors which effect the completion time as well as cost estimates.  
However, the Committee  stress the  need to minimize the gap  between 
expenditure incurred and corresponding physical achievement. Doubling of lines 
is a critical capacity augmentation measure with direct implications for 
operational safety, congestion reduction and  revenue growth . The Committee 
therefore expect that in 2026 –27, budgetary allocations will be optimally utilized 
with commensurate achievement of physical targets.  Accordingly, the Committee 
recommend that Ministry of Railways strengthen pre-construction preparedness, 
particularly in respect of land acquisition and statutory clearances, improve 
coordination with State authorities and other allied agencies to avoid delays, and 
ensure stage-wise monitoring of doubling projects.  The Committee further urge 
the Ministry to prioritize the implementation of doubling projects on capacity -
constrained and freight -intensive routes in a focused mission mode  manner, 
while leveraging modern mechanized construction technologies to accelerate 
execution.

high-impact connectivity projects , focused execution of corridors with economic 
impact and  seamless financing to ensure timely completion/achievement of 
targets established. The Ministry may also study the applicability of the model of 
National Highways Authority of India (NHA) on project execution and PPP 
models in planning and undertaking key railway projects for new tracks 
including Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs) and High -Speed Rail corridors.  
The Committee further desire the Ministry to adopt a  targeted and scientific  
approach for systematic consideration of proposals for new railway lines 
received from State Governments, Central Ministries, public representatives, as 
well as those arising from Railways own operational requirements. While 
examining such proposals, due weightage may be accorded to factors such as 
traffic projections, bridging of missing links, provision of alternate routes and 
augmentation of congested or saturated corridors. 
ROAD SAFETY WORKS AND ELIMINATION OF MANNED LEVEL 
CROSSINGS 
 
9. The Committee note that safety works related to Level Crossings (LCs) 
and Road Over/Under Bridges (ROBs/RUBs) are key focus areas in the Annual 
Plan for 202 6-27, with allocations of Rs. 800 crore and Rs. 8225 crore, 

114 
 
respectively. During 202 5-26, allocation had been kept as Rs. 7 00 crore at the 
Revised Estimate (RE) stage for LC safety works  and Rs. 471.78 crore has been 
utilized as of December, 2025 . Similarly, for ROBs/RUBs, allocation had been 
kept as  Rs. 7734 crore at the RE stage, with actual utilization standing at Rs. 
5637.59 crore (till December, 2025 ). The Committee note that the targets fixed 
for elimination of manned level crossings have not been fully met and emphasize 
the necessity for renewed focus and strengthened execution to ensure timely 
completion of these critical safety works.  The Committee, while acknowledging 
the progress made by Indian Railways in road safety works, construction of 
Road Over Bridges (ROBs)/Road Under Bridges (RUBs) and elimination of 
manned Level Crossings, emphasize the need for full utilization of allocated 
funds and adherence to physical targets. The Committee note the persistent 
challenges such as land acquisition, utility shifting, statutory clearances, cost -
sharing issues with State Governments, local resistance, inadequate approach 
roads, traffic congestion and recurring waterlogging in RUBs  while stress that 
these obstacles must be addressed through a time -bound, mission -mode 
approach with strengthened coordination among Railways, State Governments 
and road owning authorities  and streamlined approvals. The Committee  
recommend to prioritize ROB/RUB works on high -density routes, incorporating 
site-specific engineering solutions and improved drainage systems at the design 
stage to avoid water logging in RUBs , shifting towards condition -based or 
predictive maintenance, and establishing a robust grievance redressal 
mechanism with active involvement of people’s representatives and local 
authorities to ensure timely completion of these critical infrastructure projects. 
ROLLING STOCK 
 
10. The Committee appreciate the introduction of advanced rolling stock such 
as Vande Bharat Express (Sleeper) and Amrit Bharat Express, incorporating 
enhanced safety features and modern passenger amenities aimed at inducting 
high-capacity, lightweight, sturdy and faster trains. The Committee note that 

115 
 
during 2025–26 (up to December, 2025), production of Locomotives, Coaches and 
Wagons stood at 1404, 5,855 and 25,326 against annual targets of 1826, 9665 and 
26,000 respectively. The Budget Estimate (BE) for Rolling Stock for 2025 –26 was 
enhanced to Rs. 63,373 crore at the Revised Estimate (RE) stage, while actual 
expenditure stood at Rs. 50,135.40 crore up to December , 2025. For 2026–27, the 
BE has been kept at Rs. 65,496.6 crore.  Considering the shortfall against annual 
targets, the Committee recommend augmentation and modernization of 
Production Units to ensure timely achievement of rolling stock targets  and 
strengthening indigenisation and skill development aligned to next -generation 
technologies. 
The Committee also observe that while manufacturing capabilities have 
advanced significantly, the maintenance ecosystem in Zonal workshops must be 
strengthened commensurately. Th erefore, the Committee desire the Ministry to 
prioritise workshop modernization through induction of advanced diagnostic 
equipment, digital maintenance systems and structured technical training 
modules. A formal mechanism for seamless transfer of technology and technical 
know-how from Production Units to workshops should be institutionalised to 
ensure reliability, safety, reduction in maintenance time and cost for optimal 
lifecycle performance of rolling stock. 
The Committee further with regard to rolling stock recommend for 
modernization efforts  beyond core mechanical systems to ancillary and 
passenger-facing components so that coach interiors, finishing and overall 
aesthetics are aligned with the technological standards of modern rolling stock, 
thereby enhancing passenger experience and service quality. 
REDEVELOPMENT OF RAILWAY STATIONS 
11. The Committee note that the Ministry of Railways have launched the 
Amrit Bharat Station Scheme  for phased redevelopment of railway stations 
across the country under which 1,337 stations have been identified  and 172 
stations have been completed while work is in progress at 1,0 72 stations.  The 

116 
 
Committee desire that station redevelopment works be implemented in a phased 
manner under a structured project monitoring framework for each station, 
incorporating physical and financial progress tracking, periodic field inspections, 
reporting of shortfalls against prescribed targets, identification of underlying 
causes, and time -bound corrective measures. The Committee further emphasise 
the establishment of robust institutional mechanisms to prevent cost overruns 
and avoidable delays through improved project planning and inter -agency 
coordination.  The Committee also recommend that comprehensive traffic 
management plans and stringent safety protocols be mandatorily implemented to 
ensure minimal disruption to passenger movement and train operations during 
the construction phase.  Further, the Committee underscore the need for periodic 
assessment of passenger satisfaction and post -redevelopment impact evaluation, 
particularly with regard to amenities, accessibility, cleanliness and safety 
standards. The Committee also recommend that  feedback and grievance 
redressal mechanisms be formed as an integral part of the station redevelopment 
process to ensure continuous improvement. 
The Committee further recommend that station redevelopment plans be 
based on assessment of passenger traffic patterns, projected demand and future 
growth potential of the city or region concerned, so as to ensure that the 
upgraded infrastructure remains scalable, demand -responsive and aligned with 
local development needs. 
RAIL FREIGHT STRATEGY UNDER THE NATIONAL RAIL PLAN 
12. The Committee observe that freight remains the backbone of Indian 
Railways, contributing about 65% of its revenue . The Committee note that Coal, 
Iron Ore, Cement , Iron and Steel constitute about 70% of freight loading and 
revenue generation through freight operation.  The Committee note that 
Dedicated Freight Corridors Corporation of India Limited is running 406 trains 
per day and handling approximately 13% of Indian Railway traffic. The 
Committee view that announcement of the Dankuni –Surat Dedicated Freigh t 

117 
 
Corridor in Budget, 2026 represents a strategically significant expansion of the 
freight network and its timely execution and cost control will ensure economic 
and operational benefits, including decongestion of conventional routes and 
improved freight efficiency. The Committee are aware that Railways have 
implemented various policies for promoting non -bulk cargo segments such as 
automobiles, containers, parcels and fast -moving commercial goods. The 
Committee appreciate the product specific policies being designed which are 
enhancing freight earnings and recommend further strengthening of transport 
policy/model for light and low-density cargo to increase market share for Indian 
railways in commodity transportation. The Committee desire the Ministry to 
take concerted efforts in minimizing transit delays, lowering inventory holding 
requirements, reducing wastage and degradation of goods during transit  and 
further simplify business processes. The Committee recommend the Ministry to 
accord priority for integration of freight corridors with industrial corridors, 
ports and multimodal logistics facilities to maximize logistics efficiency and long -
term financial sustainability.   The Committee also recommend the Indian 
Railways to scale up the Joint Parcel Product –Rapid Cargo Service (JPP -RCS) 
on high -demand routes and expand the model to other commercially viable 
corridors across the country. The Committee urge the Ministry to strengthen 
partnerships with other logistics aggregators on similar lines to enhance 
connectivity and augment asset utilisation. 
13. The Committee note that Gati Shakti Multi Modal Cargo terminal policy 
issued in December, 2021 and subsequently revised in December, 2022 and 
January, 2026, provides framework for development of rail freight terminals 
equipped with modern facilities including mechanized handling. The Committee 
also note that till date, a total of 124 GCTs have already been commissioned, 290 
are in process . Besides  846 goods sheds of Railway are being improved and a 
work of Rs.14,500 crore has been sanctioned for development of customer 
amenities at the goods sheds and parcel offices during 2023 -24, 2024 -25 and 
2025-26. The Committee further note that in order to improve the efficiency of 
rail freight handling at the terminals, Indian Railway has adopted two -pronged 

118 
 
approach of encouraging private sector to develop the modern rail freight 
terminals and augmenting/upgrading the infrastructure at railway owned goods 
sheds. While appreciating these efforts, the Committee recommend the Ministry 
to accord higher priority to increasing rail’s modal share in freight through 
creation of an extensive and commercially viable network of privately operated 
and railway-supported GCTs. The Committee further recommend simplification 
and standardization of siding approval procedures, along with ensuring time -
bound rail connectivity from the nearest railhead to upcoming terminals. From 
an operational perspective, the Committee emphasize the need for technology -
driven mechanized loading and unloading systems, assured and predictable rake 
supply and seamless multimodal integration with ports and major road corridors 
to reduce dwell time and enhance reliability of freight movement. The Committee 
also recommend instituting a performance -based monitoring framework for 
terminals, incorporating measurable indicators such as turnaround time, rake 
availability and utilization, degree of mechanization, safety compliance and 
customer satisfaction, so that the policy not only expands infrastructure capacity 
but also leads to higher freight earnings and improved asset utilization. 
UPGRADATION OF TRACKS AND SIGNALLING SYSTEMS IN INDIAN 
RAILWAYS 
 
14. The Committee observe that Indian Railways has undertaken substantial 
measures towards upgradation of track and signalling infrastructure, including 
adoption of modern track structures, mechanised maintenance, deployment of 
Electronic Interlocking, Automatic Block Signalling and large -scale rollout of the 
indigenously developed Kavach system. The financial outlay under Signalling & 
Telecom has shown a consistent upward trend, with actual expenditure exceeding 
budget estimates, reflecting prioritisation of safety and capacity enhancement.   
During the year 2025 -26, Rs. 6 500 crore (RE) have been allocated for Signalling 
& Telecom related works, out of which Rs. 5354.30 crore has been spent till 
January, 2026. The allocation is Rs. 7500 crore for the year 2026 -27.  For Kavach 
works, funds utilized so far up to December, 25 is Rs. 2,573.36 crores. The 

119 
 
Committee note that Kavach version 4.0 has been successfully commissioned on 
1297 Route Kilometers, covering the high density Delhi - Mumbai and Delhi -
Howrah routes. The track side Kavach implementation work has been taken up 
on 23,360 RKM covering all GQ, GD, HDN and identified sections of Indian 
Railways. Tender has been finalised for equipping 6,300 Electric Locomotives 
with Kavach version 4.0 and another tender for equipping 2,679 Diesel 
Locomotives is under finalization.  The Committee desire the Ministry to ensure 
time-bound completion of ongoing track renewal and signalling projects through 
project monitoring mechanisms with clearly defined physical and financial 
targets. The Committee also desire  the Ministry to expedite large-scale 
deployment of Kavach across high -density and high -risk routes, strengthen 
indigenous manufacturing capacity and ensur e adequate training of personnel to 
maximise safety outcomes. The Committee further recommend that impact 
assessments be undertaken for measures implemented towards the upgradation 
of track and signalling infrastructure to evaluate reduction in accidents, 
improvement in punctuality and enhancement in line capacity. 
 
HIGH SPEED RAIL NETWORK IN INDIA  
 
15. The Committee note that Mumbai Ahmedabad High Speed Rail Line 
(MAHSR) (508 km) project was approved in 2015 at Rs.  97,636 crore. The 
Committee also note that the cost has increased mainly due to addition of taxes 
and cess (Rs. 29,330 crore), rolling stock and signalling (Rs. 16,500 crore), 
inflation      (Rs. 19,084 crore) and higher land and resettlement and 
rehabilitation cost          (Rs. 16,695 crore) and the revised estimate is under 
sanction. The Committee further note that the development of E10 ser ies of 
Shinkansen is underway . Further the Committee appreciate the  decision to start 
the operation using B28 (Bharat made Bullet Trainset) and efforts are underway 
to start operation using B28 (Bharat made Bullet, 280 kmph) for Surat to Vapi 
(97 km) in August, 2027.  The Committee recommend that the Ministry adopt a 
robust execution framework to ensure effective inter -agency coordination, early 

120 
 
identification and resolution of bottlenecks, and expeditious completion of utility 
shifting works. Such measures are essential to prevent time and cost overruns 
and to secure timely delivery of projects.  The Committee also note the various 
steps taken by the Railways under Make in India Initiative in indigenising soft 
and hard skills amongst other innovations.  The Committee recommend 
accelerated indigenisation of high  speed rail components  and institutionalised 
capacity-building efforts through expansion of training programmes at the High 
Speed Rail Training Institute, Vadodara, and by leveraging international 
expertise. While pursuing indigenisation of high -speed rail components and 
associated technologies, the Committee recommend that the Railways undertake 
a comprehensive study of successful high -speed rail networks in countries where 
such systems are operating efficiently. The Committee are of the view that 
drawing upon international best practices in planning, execution and operations 
would help in expediting project completion, minimise cost overruns and 
facilitate the development of a world -class high -speed rail network in the 
country. 
   The Committee appreciate the forward -looking vision of Indian Railways 
in announcing new High -Speed Rail corridors in the Budget 2026 –27. For future 
corridors, the Committee emphasise that land acquisition and other statutory 
clearances should be completed prior to the sanctioning of projects, so as to 
ensure time-bound implementation and safeguard financial viability.   
RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT 
16. While observing the trend of budgetary estimates and actual expenditure 
on Railway Research in recent years, the Committee feel  the need to enhance the 
pace of expenditure in research and development activities . Such efforts are 
essential to support rapid modernization, indigenisation, safety enhancement and 
technological self-reliance. The Committee desire that the Budget Estimate of Rs. 
86.72 crore for 2026 –27 should be effectively utilized to meaningfully advance 
these objectives and accelerate innovation -led transformation in the Railways.  
The Committee recommend that researc h initiatives be strategically aligned with 

121 
 
emerging and future -oriented priorities, including advanced signalling and train 
control systems ; energy efficiency and decarbonization ; resilient construction 
and predictive maintenance of RUBs, ROBs , railway tunnels and bridges ; next-
generation rolling stock; high-speed rail technologies and digital transformation. 
The Committee further emphasize the need to establish clearly defined, outcome -
oriented targets with timelines for each major R&D project, supported by a 
monitoring and evaluation framework to ensure accountability and measurable 
impact. The Committee also desire that sustained collaboration be fostered with 
premier national and international institutions and industry partners working in 
these domains, so that research investments result in scalable outcomes. 
INDIAN RAILWAYS ROADMAP TO NET ZERO CARBON EMISSIONS 
 
17. The Committee note the wide range of policy measures, infrastructure 
initiatives, technology adoptions and institutional mechanisms undertaken by 
Indian Railways to achieve the target of Net Zero Carbon Emissions by 2030, 
including near -complete electrification of the broad -gauge network, large -scale 
deployment of solar and wind energy, introduction of energy -efficient rolling 
stock and propulsion systems, promotion of green station redevelopment, solid 
waste management, bio -toilets, hydrogen train technology and enhanced rail 
share in automobile and freight transportation . The Committee appreciate  the 
setting up of Environment and Housekeeping Management (EnHM) Directorate 
in Railway Board in year 2015 and EnHM wings in all Zones to deal with the 
issues related to Environment as well as Housekeeping Management of Indian 
Railways. The Committee also note development and deployment of technologies 
by Indian Railways with organizations such as Automotive Research Association 
of India (ARAI), Council of Scientific & Industrial Research -Central Institute of 
Medicinal and Aromatic Plants (CSIR -CIMAP), Internat ional Centre for 
Automotive Technology (ICAT), Pune . While appreciating the energy efficiency 
and carbon reduction initiatives of Railways, the Committee recommend the 
Ministry to establish defined targets, rational timelines, and measurable 

122 
 
performance indicators. The Committee further recommend undertaking 
periodic impact assessments of these initiatives in collaboration with research 
organizations and technical institutions, so as to objectively evaluate actual 
reductions in energy consumption and carbon emissions. Such assessments would 
also help identify areas for improvement and enhance the overall effectiveness of 
the measures adopted.
