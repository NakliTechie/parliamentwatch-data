4. DEPARTMENT’S INITIATIVES ON DRUG DISCOVERY FOR MALARIA 
 4.1    The Committee, recognising the need for scientific interventions in the control and 
treatment of malaria, probed the Department about its initiatives in this re gard. The 
Department, in a written reply, brought to the attention of the Committee that CSIR has long 
standing programme/s on malaria drug discovery and development as well as parasite biology 
and drug target identification. One of the most successful outcomes of the program me 
includes discovery and development of α - β – Arteether for severe P. falciparum malaria 
including cerebral malaria as a second line treat ment for chloroquine resistant cases. Drug is 
being marketed as E MAL by the CSIR -CDRI licensee in more than 20 African & Asian 
countries. Apart from this licensee, the  α - β – Arteether is also being marketed with nearly 
400 different brand names.  
 4.2    The Committee was also informed that CSIR laboratories in collaboration with other 
Departments of the Ministry of Science and Technology are pursuing projects  related to 
development of novel oral combination formulation for the treatment of resistan t malaria, 
repurposing of Antican cer Drugs for the treatment of m alaria and other DNA & Genomic 
studies of the Malarial Parasite.  
 4.3   The Committee is cognizant of the fact that since malaria is a disease common to 
the entire country, especially in the  densely populated pockets , low cost and effective 
treatment is absolutely essential to save poor pepole’s life, loss of productive man hours 
and decrease the need for hospitalisation. In this backdrop, the Committee applauds the 
efforts of the Department in research and development of drugs for the treatment of 
malaria. The Committee observes that all scientific advancements serve their purpose 
only when those are  implemented in the welfare of the larger populace  and, therefore, 
recommends that the Department should initiate a programme on a mission mode, 
along with other stakeholder Department s/Ministries, with a target of reaching out to a

sizeable population in the most affected areas  and create awareness about the various 
interventions of the Department in treatment of Malaria.  
     5.   SICKLE CELL ANAEMIA (SCA) MISSION 
5.1 The Committee enquired from the  Department about its initiatives in respect of 
diagnosis, treatment and management of Sickle Cell Anaemia (SCA), especially in the tribal 
belt of the country , including Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha 
and Jharkhand. It was brought to the attention of the Committee, that a consortium of seven 
CSIR laboratories headed by CSIR - Centre for  Cellular and Molecular Biology (CSIR -
CCMB), Hyderabad is involved in research and development of technologies/interventions in 
addition to screening and testing of SCA patients using in -house developed methods and 
techniques under CSIR Sickle Cell Anaemia  Mission since 2017.  The CSIR Mission also 
involves collaboration with State Medical Colleges who are actively involved in the Mission . 
Hence, CSIR is able to reach the affected population to help the Medical Colleges with their 
expertise in screening, diagnosis and application of technological interventions that are being 
developed under the Mission.  
5.2 In a written reply to the Committee, the Department listed out the objectives of the 
Sickle Cell Anaemia Mission as follows: 
(i) Population screening, genetic testing, carrier testing, prenatal diagnosis and 
genetic counselling, treatment of identified patients, etc. 
(ii) Procurement of DCGI/CDSCO approval for dedicated use of Hydroxy urea 
therapy for patients of Sickle Cell Anaemia 
(iii) Identification of new drug molecular for the management of Sickle Cell Anaemia 
patients 
(iv) Development of genome editing approaches towards development of curative 
treatment of Sickle Cell Anaemia 
(v) Development of cheap and robust, point of care device for rapid screening of 
such patients. 
5.3  The Committee was pleased to note that about 25 lakh children have been screened in 
Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh and hundreds of target couples were identifie d; and, through 
prenatal diagnosis and counselling, birth of affected kids has been prevented. The Committee 
was also informed that one of the major achievements under the CSIR -Sickle cell Anaemia 
Mission has been in  getting the DCGI/CDSCO approval for dedicated use of Hydroxy urea 
therapy for Sickle Cell Anaemia  patients, thereby setting the stage for development of new 
formulations for effective treatment and better compliance to the Hydroxy urea usage.  
5.4  The Committee observes that the activities of SCA Mission remain confined to 
two states, i.e.  Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra in the Phase I undertaken during the 
period from 2017-2020. The SCA Mission has now been extended to Madhya Pradesh in 
Phase II  with plan s to extend the same model t o Jharkhand.  The Committee also 
observes that a programme on SCA is also executed by the Department of 
Biotechnology and, therefore, recommends the Department of Scientific and Industrial 
Research to coordinate  with the Department of Biotechnology to ensure  synergy of 
efforts and avoidance of duplicity.   
5.5 The Committee notes and appreciates the efforts of the Department towards 
early screening, treatment and management of Sickle Cell Anaemia. Since the disea se is 
endemic to many tribal areas, cost effective and efficient scientific interventions are the 
need of the hour to prevent any further prevalence of this disease  and improve the 
patient’s quality of life. The Committee, feels that identifying the carriers and avoidance 
of marriage of carrier couples may -be successful in a limited manner  to manage the 
disease. The Committee strongly recommends that appropriate interventions backed by 

6 
 
effective policy/guidelines will go a long way in not only improvi ng the quality of life of 
the SCA patients but also prevent and reduce the number of children born and affected 
by this disease. The Committee  firmly recommends that the Department should engage 
with other national and international agencies undertaking research on gene therapy 
and gene editing based treatment of Sickle Cell Anaemia.  
5.6 The Committee feels that the Department needs to take more proactive steps in 
increasing on-ground presence and collaborate with local and State level stakeholders 
so th at the solutions developed by the Department can actually reach to the affected 
masses and other intended stakeholders . To this effect, the Committee strongly 
recommends the Department to aggressively promote the use of Hydroxy urea therapy 
for the treatment of Sickle Cell Anaemia.

DEPARTMENT’S INITIATIVES ON DRUG DISCOVERY FOR MALARIA 
            The Committee is cognizant of the fact that since malaria is a disease common to 
the entire country, especially in the densely populated pockets, low cost and effective 
treatment is absolutely essential to save poor pepole’s life, loss of productive man ho urs 
and decrease the need for hospitalisation. In this backdrop, the Committee applauds the 
efforts of the Department in research and development of drugs for the treatment of 
malaria. The Committee observes that all scientific advancements serve their pur pose 
only when those are implemented in the welfare of the larger populace and, therefore, 
recommends that the Department should initiate a programme on a mission mode, 
along with other stakeholder Departments/Ministries, with a target of reaching out to a  
sizeable population in the most affected areas and create awareness about the various 
interventions of the Department in treatment of Malaria.                                     (Para 4.3) 
     SICKLE CELL ANAEMIA (SCA) MISSION 
 The Committee observes th at the activities of SCA Mission remain confined to 
two states, i.e. Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra in the Phase I undertaken during the 
period from 2017-2020. The SCA Mission has now been extended to Madhya Pradesh in 
Phase II with plans to extend the same model to Jharkhand. The Committee also 
observes that a programme on SCA is also executed by the Department of 
Biotechnology and, therefore, recommends the Department of Scientific and Industrial 
Research to coordinate with the Department of Biotechnology t o ensure synergy of 
efforts and avoidance of duplicity.                                                                            (Para 5.4) 
 The Committee notes and appreciates the efforts of the Department towards 
early screening, treatment and management of Sickle Cell Anaemia. Since the disease is 
endemic to many tribal areas, cost effective and efficient scientific interventions are the 
need of the hour to prevent any further prevalence of this disease and improve the 
patient’s quality of life. The Committee, feels that identifying the carriers and avoidance 
of marriage of carrier couples may -be successful in a limited manner to manage the 
disease. The Committee strongly recommends that appropriate interventions backed by 
effective policy/guidelines will go a long way in not only improving the quality of life of 
the SCA patients but also prevent and reduce the number of children born and affe cted 
by this disease. The Committee firmly recommends that the Department should engage 
with other national and international agencies undertaking research on gene therapy 
and gene editing based treatment of Sickle Cell Anaemia.                                     (Para 5.5) 
 The Committee feels that the Department needs to take more proactive steps in 
increasing on-ground presence and collaborate with local and State level stakeholders 
so that the solutions developed by the Department can actually reach to  the affected 
masses and other intended stakeholders. To this effect, the Committee strongly 
recommends the Department to aggressively promote the use of Hydroxy urea therapy 
for the treatment of Sickle Cell Anaemia.                                                               (Para 5.6)
