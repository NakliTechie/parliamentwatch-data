R E P O R T
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Ocean Development on the
 recommendations contained in the One Hundred-fifteenth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee
 on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2003-2004) of the Department of Ocean
 Development which was presented/laid to both Houses of Parliament on 2
nd May, 2003.  There are twenty one
 recommendations contained in One Hundred- fifteenth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations
 of the Committee were received from the Department on 31st July,2003.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the
 Department of Ocean Development are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR 2003-04
Recommendation
The Committee feels that the Department during 2002-03 had to scale down its activities due to reduction in its B.E.
 from Rs.175 crores to Rs. 150 crores.  The Committee is constrained to note that in many cases   major  disproportionate
 variations is due to delay in getting the necessary Government approvals.  The Committee feels that these kind of hassles should
 be removed and strongly recommends higher allocations for activities of the Department which is doing a commendable job. 
 The Committee also feels that there is an urgent need to review the whole planning and budgeting process in the Department so
 that realistic physical and financial targets are projected in the Budget and planned projects are not hampered due to drastic cuts
 at R.E. stage.  It is equally important to prioritise the projects in the light of their potential benefit to the Nation.
                                                                                                                      (Para 8)
Action Taken by the Department
            The budget projection for various Plan and Non-Plan Schemes is made on the basis of inputs received from the
 Programme officers controlling the Schemes.  The Ministry of Finance following  exhaustive  discussions  between  the 
 Department  and the Planning Commission makes final Budget allocation for the Plan Scheme available.  The Budget allocation
 and actual expenditure for the year 2002-03 was;
Scheme
                        B.E                              R.E.                             Actual Expenditure
Plan Schemes:              Rs.175.00 Crore          Rs.150.00 Crore          Rs.139.58 Crore
Non-Plan Schemes       Rs. 24.28 Crore           Rs. 24.28 Crore           Rs. 27.42 Crore
Comments
While the Committee notes the reply, it reiterates that Department should review the whole planning and budgetary
 process by keeping in mind the long term policy perspective so that any reduction during R.E. stage can be avoided.
 
POLAR SCIENCE (ANTARCTIC RESEARCH ) PROGRAMME
Recommendation
Commencement of the Antarctic Programme in the eighties and its evolution and growth represent the excellence of our
 scientific and technological capabilities beyond the frontiers of our country.  The study and research undertaken during
 Antarctic expeditions have produced a repository of data and  information  which are of immense importance not only for India
 but for the entire world.  Pursuing this mission as part of the mainstream of the international endeavour to explore the icy
 continent has made invaluable contribution to promote international understanding.  The Committee, therefore, feels that the
 Antarctic programme should not be allowed to suffer for want of funds.  Besides, with a view to strengthening our scientific
 base in Antarctica, there is a need for expanding the area of activities by installing many more research stations in different
 regions of Antarctica.

DRUGS FROM SEA  
Recommendation
The committee notes with satisfaction that under the Drugs from Sea Programme the Department has done commendable
 work.  The Committee trusts that the Department would continue the exploratory work relating to collection, identification and
 bio-evaluation of the marine flora and fauna from the Indian Coastal waters  for the purpose of extracting drugs.  
                                                                                                                                   (Para 27)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department is continuing its activities intensively for exploration of the entire coastline and collections made from
 deep sea as well for identification of more flora and fauna having potential for extraction of drugs and chemicals.  Large-scale
 collection of active materials for characterization and structural determination studies are being carried out with updating of the
 computerized database.
 
 
Comments 
The Committee appreciates the efforts being made by the Department in augmenting its efforts in collection, identification
 and bio-evaluation of marine flora and fauna from Indian Coast.
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that possibly from marine resources some medicines could be extracted which otherwise
 are not available at present to treat some of the diseases affecting mankind.  Therefore, the committee recommends that marine
 resources must be used in a sustainable way for the benefit of our people.                                 
                                                                (Para 28)
Action Taken by the Department
The ocean living resources provide ample scope for extraction of drugs and chemicals for medicinal and other purposes. 
 The likelihood of marine biota yielding a number of compounds particularly for curing liver disorders, diabetes, healing of
 wounds, anti-ageing, etc.  is very high.  Having achieved  the earlier aims and objectives of the programme, it is now proposed
 to focus on product development in seven major areas (anti-diabetic/anti-diarrhoeal, anti-hyperlipidaemic; anti-anxiety;
 antihyper-glycaemic; anti-bacterial/anti-fungal; anti-tumour/anti-cancer and spermicidal), besides continuing with the ongoing
 exploratory activity.
Comments

COASTAL OCEAN MONITORING AND PREDICTION SYSTEM (COMAPS)       
Recommendation
The Committee recommends that the Department may augment its ongoing Coastal Ocean Monitoring and Prediction
 System to regularly monitor the condition of the coastal waters and adjoining areas.       
(Para 31)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department monitors the health of our seas on a long-term basis to facilitate the Pollution Control Authorities for
 management of marine pollution related issues, like prescription of appropriate disposal standards, as per the assimilation
 capacity of the receiving water bodies, water quality criteria etc.  During the X Plan period, the Department has augmented the
 programme with following additional activities: (1) Pesticide residues analysis in organisms at selected stations along the
 coastline; (2) Physical oceanographic studies leading to determination of Waste assimilation capacity; (3) Development of GIS
 based data base for source of pollutants; and (4) Establishment of a Microbial reference Centre.
Comments  
The Committee observes that there is a need for greater co-ordination between the Department and respective State
 Pollution Control Boards for a continued monitoring of marine pollution levels and data generated through Geographical
 Information System (GIS) must be utilized to the fullest extent to achieve desired results.
MARINE RESEACH AND  CAPACITY BUILDING
Recommendation
For strengthening ocean research centers in coastal universities, the Department is emphasizing on quantum jump in our
 approach towards the ocean.  To achieve that purpose the Committee recommends that the Department should formulate an
 integrated approach and explore the possibilities of bringing all maritime educational institutions under one body such as a
 Maritime University.  Since ocean sciences are multi-disciplinary sciences such an integrated approach, the Committee feels,
 will be of vital significance.  The Committee recommends that the Department should establish Oceanariums in different parts
 of the country.  If planetariums could promote knowledge and awareness about space, the Committee feels that Ocenariums
 could also create awareness about oceans and their resources.                            
(Para 36)
Action Taken by the Department
The   Department through nine Ocean Science and Technology Cells (OSTCs), which have been established to act as
 nodal coordinating agencies in various universities/institutes, has been providing funding support to research projects in selected
 disciplines of marine science and technology.  This is being done in addition to the marine science courses that these and other
 universities are having.  The department has established three specialist institutes – the National Institute of Ocean Technology
 (Chennai), the     National Centre for Antarctic and Ocean Research (Goa) and the Indian National Centre for Ocean
 Information Services (Hyderabad) and two attached offices – Integrated Coastal and Marine Area Management (ICMAM)
 Project Directorate (Chennai) and the Centre for Marine Living Resources and Ecology (Kochi) to conduct research, training
 and provide specialized facilities in marine science & technology sector.  However, as suggested, the possibility of bringing
 organizations/institutes set up in the marine sciences & technology field under one umbrella agency for better coordination, will
 be actively explored.
Department has taken initiative by developing exhibits and through national and state level expositions it is promoting
 awareness about oceans and their resources amongst people and students.  Development of Oceanariums, which are very
 specialized permanent exhibits containing live specimens under artificial conditions, will be taken up in consultation with State
 Governments and technical institutes such as NIOT, CMFRI and NIO, considering the high level of capital investment and
 technical skills required for establishing and maintaining such Oceanariums.
Comments  
The Committee commends the efforts being made by the Department in achieving programme objectives and hopes that
 desired results would be achieved.

INTERNATIONAL   CO-OPERATION
Recommendation
The Committee notes that India is a signatory to the Ocean Charter which is not a legal entity, but which stipulates that
 all nations shall act in unison to protect the oceans.  India being a major maritime country in the Indian Ocean rim, it has a
 greater responsibility to take initiatives for bringing together all littoral countries for the development, protection and
 conservation of oceans.  The Committee notes with trepidation the observation of Dr. R. Sengupta, renowned oceanographer,
 “Of the three greatest water bodies, the Atlantic, the pacific and the Indian ocean, the latter was the cleanest.  But that is history
 now”.  The Committee therefore recommends that India must take a lead to protect its own interests and the interests of
 developing countries vis-a-vis their oceanic resources.
(Para 47)
Action Taken by the Department
Noted.  It may be mentioned that Inter governmental Oceanographic Commission(IOC), UNESCO has established
 Global Ocean Observing System(GOOS).  GOOS did not exist so far for Indian Ocean.  India has taken lead in establishing
 IOGOOS, involvingh several Indian Ocean Rim  Countries.  India has also taken lead in establishing Argo floats in Indian
 Ocean.  It may be mentioned that Indian is pioneering the cause of developing Indian Ocean Rim countries.
Comments  
The Committee notes the reply and appreciates the efforts of the Department.
OCEAN OBSERVATION & INFORMATION SERVICES (OOIS)
Recommendation
The Committee recommends that in order to improve Oceanography services, the Department should strengthen the
 observation platforms.  The moored buoy network should also be augmented to improve generation of data useful for the
 Coastal and off shore developmental activities.  The Department should also process the data collected from Array for Real-
time Geotropic Oceanography (ARGO) Floats for obtaining temperature and salinity profiles from the Indian Ocean to study the
 long-term climate and Monsoon variability.
(Para 52)
Action Taken by the Department
In order to strengthen the observation platforms to cater to the needs of coastal and off shore developmental activities,
 the Department has proposed to augment from the 12-buoy network established during 9
th Plan to the 40-buoy network during
 the X Plan period.  Besides, about 150 ARGO Floats would be deployed in the Indian Ocean region for acquiring temperature &
 salinity data profiles, to understand the upper ocean processes for studying the long-term climate and Monsoon variability.  The
 temperature salinity profiling data being collected under ARGO project would be utilized for studying the large scale
 oceanographic process to address issues relating to climate variability and monsoon with active participation of reputed
 institutes like, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, NIO, Goa etc.
Comments
            While the Committee appreciates the Department’s efforts for undertaking programmes aimed at developing Ocean
 Observation Information Service for different states, it is of the view that the data collected must be timely disseminated so that
 the same can be utilized to predict cyclones and prevent loss of life and property.

NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF OCEAN TECHNOLOGY
Recommendation
The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation to the Department to build energy plants of higher capacity as it
 feels that this might be cost effective in the long run and lessen the dependence on fossil fuel for power generation.
(Para 57)
Action Taken
National Institute of Ocean Technology(NIOT) had completed all subsystem qualification and pre-commissioning trials
 of OTEC plant off Tuticorin.  The deployment of 1 km long HDPE pipe and complete mooring system   were completed in
 March 2003.  However the commissioning and demonstration of 1 MW OTEC plant is deferred due to parting of transfer hose
 flange and deterioration of weather.  Based on the experience gained a brief   feasibility report for establishing a 5 MW plant is
 under preparation.
 
Comments
The Committee hopes that after examining the feasibility report, sustained efforts should be made to build higher
 capacity energy plants. The Department should devise better ways and means to achieve the objective.
