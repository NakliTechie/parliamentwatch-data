Action Taken by the Ministry 
  
The Ministry of Environment and Forests (MoEF) shares the concerns of the Committee. At the 
same time, it would like to inform the Committee that due to innovative efforts of the Central and State 
Governments especially in enlisting the  active participation of the local communities in forest 
conservation and development, appreciable changes in forest cover have been reported by the Forest 
Survey of India: 
 
Report, Forest Survey of India 2001) 
  
            The Committee may also be pleased to note that the focus of the forestry management has shifted
from State-controlled production to sustainable management with people’s pa rticipation in the past two 
decades. The Ministry of Environment and Forests vide letter no. 6.21/89-PP dated 1.6.1990 outlined the 
principles of Joint Forest Manage ment (JFM) and conveyed  to State Government s for creating massive
people’s movement through involvement of the vill age communities in the protection, regeneration and
development of degraded forests on “Care and Share” basi s. Further, through it s letters No. 22-8/2000-
JFM (FPD) dated 21.2.2000 and 24.12.2002, this Ministry issued the gui delines for strengthening of the
JFM programme. These letters provided the framework for the Stat e Governments to ensure meaningful
participation of women in JFM programme, extension of JFM in good forests areas, dovetailing o f
village-level forest management microplan under JFM with the Work ing Plan of the forest division, and
formalizing relationships with local village  community by way of signing of Memorandum o f
Understanding (MoU) for JFM.    
            As on date, all 28 State Govern ments have adopted JFM as their key policy relating to
conservation and development of forests.  As per available in formation on 10.9.2003, there were 84632
JFM committees in the country looking after 17.33 million ha. of forest lands (except Meghalaya, which
adopted JFM after 10.9.2003), which is approximately 23% of the total forest area of the country.  
            Tribal and women are the special focus groups under JFM. Almost 60percent of the forest cove r
in the country as per assessment by Forest Survey of India 2001 is availa ble in the tribal districts of the
country, and the tribal families ar e automatically covered in the JFM programme in these areas. As on
10.9.2003, approximately 8.4 million fa milies were involved in JFM programme out of which
approximately 2.7 million were Schedule Tribe families.  
            To ensure participation of wome n in the JFM programme, the guidelines issued vide this
Ministry’s letter dated 21.2.2000 st ipulate that 50% of the member s in General Body of the JFM
committee at the village level shoul d be women and in the Executive  Body their representation should
be 33%.  
            As per this Ministry’s Guidelines issued vide letter dated 1.6.1990, the beneficiaries of JFM are
to be given to a share in usufructs,  like grasses, lops a nd tops of branches, minor  forest produce, and a
portion from the sale of trees when they mature, to  the extent, and subject to the conditions prescribed
by the State Governments in this behalf. Each State Government has prescribed these shares to suit local
conditions. For example, the Madhya  Pradesh Government has provide d 100% share of the net profit o f
the harvesting of timber and ba mboo to the Joint Forest Mana gement Committees. The Policy
Guidelines issued by Government  of India vide letter dated 24.12.2002 also stipulate the signing o f
MOU by the State Forest Department with the JFM committees  to inter-alia, include roles and 
responsibilities of the two sides and the details of usufruct sharing.  
            In order to support JFM programme , this Ministry is implem enting National Afforestation
Programme (NAP) Scheme in the X Plan which starte d as a Pilot Scheme, i.e., Samanvit Gram
Vanikaran Samridhi Yo jana (SGVSY) Scheme, in the latter half of the IX Plan.  This Scheme is

fixing the annual tree planting targets under 20-Point Programme, this Ministry also takes into account
all related factors, e.g. existing fo rest and tree cover in the State/ UT, the extent of potential lands
available as depicted by wa steland statistics, the past performance of the State/ UT with respect to tree
planting, etc.

Recommendation 
The Committee has observed that given the present state of Wild Life Conservation in India,
there are a lot of work to be done in the field to gi ve the word ‘Conservation’ a real meaning.  Over the
years, the Committee has noted that  the basic requirements for conserva tion of wild life have not been
fulfilled to its best by the Minist ry of Environment & Forests and neither much importance has been
attached for the preserva tion of endangered species of animal and flora which constitute the heart o f
nature in India. 
            In order to give a thrust to the real  understanding of the meaning of  the term ‘Conservation’, the 
term Wild Life will have to be interpreted in a different perspectiv e in the broadest sense possible to
include scientific work, field wo rk, planning and manage ment, control of poach ing and illegal trade,
awareness, education and extensi on of work, eco-development, captive breeding and zoo management 
etc. 
            In order to give a more thrust  on the crux of the above definition, the Committee feel that
following priorities for wild life conservation should be designed by the Ministry during the current plan
period:- 
1.                  Control of poaching and illegal trade in wild life and wild life produc ts made of wild life
parts; 
2.                  Development of protected areas network.; 
3.                  Welfare measures for wild life staff; 
4.                  Eco tourism; 
5.                  Wild life conservation outside protected areas; 
6.                  Prevention and reduction of damage to wild life;  
7.                  Establishment of second home for the Asiatic lion in Gir. 
So long as the Asiatic lion population remains restri cted to a single location, it is at constant risk
from catastrophic natural events li ke forest fire, flood, earthquake et c.  Accordingly, efforts should be

objective of effectively controlling poaching and illegal trade in wildlife and its derivatives. This has
been amended in January, 2003 and puni shment an penalty for offences  under the Act have been made
more stringent.  
Offences pertainin g to huntin g of endan gered species and alterin g of boundaries of protected
areas. 
  
            For offences relating to wild an imals (or their parts a nd products) included in schedule- I of part 
II of Schedule –II  and those relating to hunting or altering the boundaries of a sanctuary or national park
the punishment and penalty have been enhanced, th e minimum imprisonment pres cribed is three years
which may extend to seven years, with a minimum fine of Rs. 10,000/-. For a subsequent offence of this 
nature, the term of imprisonments shall not be less than three years but may extend to seven section (51-
A) has been inserted in the Act, making certain conditions applicable while granting bail: 
            When any person accused of the commi ssion of any offence relating to Schedule I or Part II o f
Schedule II or offences relating to hunting inside the boundaries or National Park or Wildlife Sanctuary
or altering the boundaries of such parks and sanctuaries, is arrested under the provisions of the Act, then
not withstanding anything containe d in the Code of Cr iminal Procedure, 1973, no such person who had
been previously convicted of an offence under this Act shall be released on bail unless- 
            (a)  The public Prosecutor has been given an opportunity of opposing the release on bail; and  
            (b) Where the Public Prosecutor oppos e the application, the Court is  satisfied that there are
reasonable grounds for believing that he is not guilty or such offences and that he is not likely to commit
any offence while on bail.

PROTECTION OF TAJ MAHAL 
Recommendation 
The Taj Mahal at Agra is  one of the most important monuments  of not only in India but also the
whole world.  In recent years owing to  growth of satellite industries, the ambient air quality particularly
within the Taj Trapezium Area (TTZ) has become more and more vulnerabl e to severe pollution
generating a serious threat to the qua lity of white marble stones of this  remarkable structure. This most
celebrated monuments viewed from  a distance, looms on the banks of  a stinking river almost always
enveloped in a haze of dust and smog from be lching smokestacks and exhaust fumes from the
surrounding industries.  The Committeefeels with a painful heart that this soot and fumes will eventually
turn the gleaming white building black. 
It is, therefore, obvious from the above, that in the recent year s the corrosive impact of the
polluted air on Taj Mahal ha s become a matter of grave concern.  Accordingly, the committee feels that
corrosive impact of pollutants and emissions from surroundings industrial conclave and running vehicles
within the vicinity of the monument is required to be addressed on extreme priority basis. 
(Para 82)
Action Taken by the Ministry 
For improvement in the environm ental quality in Taj Trapezium Zone(TTZ) and  particularly 
around Agra where the World famous  monument of Taj Mahal is lo cated, various steps have been
initiated,  which inter-alia include the following : 
 In pursuance of the Vardarajan Committee Re port on the Environmenta l Impact of Mathura 
refinery,  the thermal power plants  have been closed and Dieseliz ation of Railway yard was taken 
up;  
 TTZ was declared covering an ar ea of 10,400 sq. km. for prohibiti on of establishment/expansion 
of polluting industries;  
 In compliance with the order of the Hon’ble Supreme Court dated 10 th May, 1996, approximately 
450 brick kilns have been closed and the license d brick kilns are locate d beyond 20 kms. radial 
distance from Taj and other significant monuments;

 Mathura Refinery has been directed to comply with recommendations of NEERI Report such as 
use of Natural Gas, improved Sulphur Recovery  Unit, setting up of Hydro cracking unit and 
development of green belt around the Refinery.   
 New vehicles are being registered which are fitted with catalytic converters;  
 Natural gas, LPG and Propane is being used by industries in Agra;  
 The Petha industries, which are presently usi ng coal,  have been directed by District 
Administration to switch over to LPG at the earliest.  Shifting of these industries in Kalandi Vihar

 For reducing noise levels from  DG sets, an Action Plan is under preparation and all

Comments 
            The Committee is happy to not e that various steps have been initiated for improving 
environmental quality in Taj Trapez ium Zone (TTZ). But what is more important is the final outcome
and success of these steps. For in stance, District Administration ha s directed the Petha industries to
switch over to use of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) at the earlie st. However, the Ministry has not
informed whether they have actuall y switched over to LPG. The Comm ittee is of the view that the
compliance of administrative order in right earnest is more important  than its issuance and therefore a
periodical review by the Ministry of its steps would act as a deterrent against the polluters. 
            The Committee has expressed its se rious concern over the hazardous effects of pollution on the
Taj Mahal in its previous Reports but only now, Gas Authority of India Limited (GAIL) has been asked
to prepare a CNG feasibility repor t. The Committee recommends th at the proposal prepared by GAIL
should be earnestly examined within a limited ti me-frame stipulated  by the Ministry and be made 
operational to avoid pollution engulfing the entire area.
