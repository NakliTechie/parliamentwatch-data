4.2 The Committee was informed that there had been a substantial reduction to the tune of 
12.19 percent in the budgetary allocation of the Ministry at RE Stage in      2021-22.  On being 
asked by the Committee  about the reasons  for this reduction , t he Ministry informed that the 
COVID-19 pandemic impacted the implementation of few schemes and th e utilization of funds 
thereof resulting in  reduction in the total outlay of the Ministry in 2021 -22 at RE Stage. The  
Committee desired to know the impact of  COVID-19 on the budgetary allocations and the  
schemes/programmes of the Ministry.   In response, it was informed that the Ministry’s budget 
allocation was reduced by ₹349.93 crore at RE  stage due to COVID -19 pandemic.  Thus, the 
various programmes and schemes run by the Ministry face d issues in achieving all the outcomes 
envisaged for the year 2020-21 including key s chemes such as National Coastal Mission  and 
Control of Pollution. The reduction in funds will  also lead to non -release of funds for all 
proposed activities mentioned in the Annual Pl an of Operations (APOs) of various schemes.  The 
Ministry further informed that changes in procedure for release of funds under Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme and monitoring utilization of funds  led to delay in release of funds to Green 
India Mission (GIM). 
 
4.3 The Committee notes the adverse effect of COVID-19 pandemic on the budgetary 
allocation of the Ministry due to which  the BE allocation of the Ministry for 2021-22 was 
substantially reduced by Rs. 349.93 crore, a decrease of  12.9% at RE stage, thus resulting 
in non -release of funds for all proposed activities mentioned in the Annual Plan of 
Operations (APOs) of various schemes.   The Ministry has been able to spend 71 % of the 
RE allocation up to 31st January, 2022.  The Committee is satisfied with the util isation of 
funds and recommends the Ministry to ensure its full utilisation by the end of the financial 
year.  
4.4 The Committee desired to know th e budgetary allocation demanded  by the Ministry and 
the amount actually allocated to it for  2022-23.  The Committee was informed that the Ministry 
had requested for ₹3795.17 crore and had been allocated only ₹3030.00 crore for 2022-23.  The 
reduction would  definitely impact allocation under all the key schemes/programmes of the 
Ministry during the year.   When asked as to how the Ministry proposed to deal with the 
challenges due to this reduction of funds, the Ministry informed that it will seek an increase in 

6 
 
budgetary allocation  at Supplementary / Revised Estimates (R E) stage after due analysi s of 
expenditure trends of the programmes/schemes.  
 
4.5 The Committee expressing concern over the shortfall in allocation and its impact on the 
Ministry’s programmes and initiatives asked the Ministry about  the absolute bare minimum 
increase it needed in the budgeta ry allocations for the year 2022 -23 to sustain its key 
programmes.  In response, the Ministry informed that a minimum additional allocation of at least 
Rs.370 crore over and above BE 2022 -23 was immensely required for implementation of its key 
programmes/schemes. This amount includes provisions to undertake envisaged activities under 
schemes such as Green India Mission -National Afforestation Programme, Forest Fire Prevention 
and Management, National Coastal Mission (NCM), Project Tiger , Development of Wildl ife 
Habitats and National Mission on Himalayan Studies.  
 
4.6 The Committee notes that the reduction in funds of the Ministry at RE 2021 -22 was 
mainly due to the situation caused by  the COVID-19 pandemic.  However, the BE 20 22-23 
of the Ministry is still less than the BE 2020-21 (Rs.3100 crore). The Committee is of the 
view that  the programmes for which the Ministry has sought additional funding  are of 
utmost importance in the present day context, and must be given the topmost priority. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry must be provided the requisite 
allocation for timely and efficient implementation of the programmes. At the same time, 
the Ministry should also review the capital requirement of the programmes/schemes and 
explore internal budgetary reallocations to ensure availability of funds for the projects. 
The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should make proactive efforts to ensure 
that the physical targets of the Ministry in respect of its various schemes/programmes are 
on schedule and the allocation made at BE is utilised optimally so that it is on a firm 
ground to seek additional funds at the Supplementary/ RE stage to achieve its physical and 
financial objectives during the financial year.

6.2 The Committee observed that the budgetary allocation for ‘National Coastal Mission’ 
(NCM) was drastically reduced from Rs.200.00 crore in BE 2021-22 to Rs.100.90 crore at RE  
stage which shows a decrease of more than 49 percent. On being asked the reasons for such 
drastic reduction in the budgetary allocations at the RE Stage, the Ministry informed the 
Committee that while proposing the Budget E stimate (BE) for 2021 -22 under E xternally Aided 
Project component, it was construed that the ENCORE project will be approved by the  Cabinet 
Committee on Economic Affairs  (CCEA), and the initial activities will be taken up for 
implementation in the current year. Since the approval of the ENCORE Project was still awaiting 
the CCEA approval the BE was reduced to have realistic implementation target and associated 
financial requirements for the remaining six months of FY 2021-22. This impacted the activities 
to be taken up under the ENCORE Project. 
6.3 The Committee  understands under this programme, annual Management Action 
Plan (MAP) for conservation and management of mangroves are formulated and 
implemented in all the coastal States and Union Territories . In view  of the same the 
Committee recommends that the approval of CCEA may be sought at the earliest to avoid 
further delay in activities to be taken up under the ENCORE Project. 
6.4 The Committee further noted that even after the drastic curtailment of budgetary 
allocations under ‘National Coastal Mission’ (NCM) , the Ministry managed to utilise only Rs. 
14.87 crore from out of 100.9 crore  up till January, 2022. The Committee desired to be apprised 
of the reasons for such poor/under -utilisation of the funds . The Ministry informed  that under-
utilisation of funds under the Cen tral Sector Scheme for coastal S tates was mainly due to (i) 
delay in implementing advance activities which were to be undertaken by the S tates; (ii) setting 
up of P roject Monitoring Units by the States and (iii) creation of O peration Manuals. Further, 
delays in implementation of  some of the activities were observed  due to COVID related 

8 
 
regulations and lockd owns which did have an impact on  the scheme. The Ministry assured the 
Committee that necessary arrangements have now been put in place and are  in final stages of 
preparation. It is expected that the National Coastal Mission  will be approved in the next 
Financial Year and the project is likely to be completed in five years . Secretary, Ministry of 
Environment, Forest and Climate Change informed that the committed financial and physical 
progress against the propo sed budget under Externally Aided Project (EAP) and Direct Budget 
Support (DBS) will be met subject to approval of CCEA. 
6.5 The Committee notes with concern the poor utilisation of funds under the ‘National 
Coastal Mission’ (NCM) . The Committee while acknowledging the impact of COVID -19 
pandemic which led to delay in activities envisaged under the programme  during the 
financial year 2021 -22, however urges upon the Ministry to stream line the activities/ 
projects which are to be undertaken in the current  Financial Year  2022-23 and also to 
impress upon the coastal States, which are involved in the Mission , the urgency of 
implementation of the Mission. In this regard, t he Committee recommends that the 
Ministry should monitor the sta tus of Project  Monitoring Units setup by the State 
Governments/UTs on regular basis and defaulters may be dealt with firmly.

7.2 The Committee note d that the budgetary allocation for ‘Control of Pollution’ having 
‘National Clean Air Programme ’(NCAP) as its sub -component has been reduced to Rs.390.1 
crore at RE Stage 2021 -22, a decrease of 17 percent in the allocation.  The Committee notes that 
an amount of Rs.290 crore was allocated for 82 cities in 2021-22 from budget allocation of 
Control of Pollution S cheme. The same allocation has been made for 2022 -23. On being asked 
the reasons for reduction in budgetary allocation at RE 2021 -22 Stage, the Ministry replied that 
COVID- 19 impacted utilization of funds in State Pollution Control Boards/PCCs and in 
implementation of city action plan across most of the Non -attainment cities (total 82) identified 
under National Clean Air Programme. The  physical and financial progress was  impacted 
adversely, at ground level implementation of proposed activities.  The Committee further 
observed that only Rs.239.71 crore was spent till January, 2022 out the total budgetary allocation 
for 2021 -22.  The Ministry cited the impact of COVID -19 on und er-utilisation of funds. The 
Ministry further informed that Control of Pollution Division has inte nsified NCAP in city level 
and State level through constant meeting with stakeholders of the implementation committee in 
all level s which would help in achiev ing the physical and financial targets sets under the 
programme in 2021-22 and as such there was no shortfall. 
7.3 The Committee desired to know the allocation under ‘Control of Pollution’ Scheme 
demanded by the Ministry and the amount actually allocated to it for the Financial Year 2022-23.  
In response, the Committee was informed that the Ministry had sought funds to the tune of ₹470 
crore for the NCAP S cheme and they have been allocated ₹460 crore for 2022-23.  In response 

9 
 
to a query whether the reduced allocation will affect the targets of the Ministry under the 
scheme, the Ministry informed the Committee that the reduction will not affect the 
targets/achievements envisaged under variou s components of the schemes as major programme 
under the scheme is National Clean Air Programme (NCAP). This is a c omprehensive time 
bound action programme at national level for reduction of air pollution levels, both according to 
regional and urban scales , in non-attainment cities. The implementation of city action plans for 
reduction of air pollution is based on convergence of activities from other 
Departments/Ministries. Though the allocation for the year 2022-23 has been kept at ₹460 crore 
which is somewhat at the level of BE 2020 -21, additional funding for the activities is supported 
by grants provided to 42 'million plus cities' ( Rs.4400 crore in the year 2020 -21 and Rs.2217 
crore in the year 2021 -22) on the recommendation of Fifteenth Finance Commissi on. Further, 
provisions made under Urban Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0 ( Rs.1,41,678 crore  over 5 years 
through Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) with a focus on air pollution 
reduction by effectively managing waste from construction and demolition activities and bio -
remediation of all legacy municipal solid waste dump sites, will also augment funding for 
achieving NCAP objectives. 
7.4 The Committee notes that the Ministry has not categorically mentioned the actual 
utilisation made under the sub- component National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) during 
the year 2021-22.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry should invariably furnish 
the information to the Committee to facilitate a more detailed analysis of Demand for 
Grants. Funds allocated to NCAP and its utilisation thereof in future, needs a transparent 
elucidation.  The Committee feels that the Ministry should make more  efforts to get 
increased budgetary allocation for the NCAP considering the impact of increase in air 
pollution on climate change. The Committee, therefore, recommends that concerted efforts 
should be made by the Ministry for optimum utilisation of funds under NCAP .  The 
Committee expresses its satisfaction over the initiative s of the Ministry in extending  
incentives and motivat ing the agencies at municipal level towards the target of city -wise 
reduction of PM 10 concentration. 
7.5 The Committee also noted that a budgetary  allocation of Rs.470 crore was made under 
the Head ‘Control of Pollution’ in BE 2021-22 which was reduced to Rs.390.10.00 crore at RE 
stage. The Committee desired to know the actual amount spent under the scheme till 31.1.2022

11. Forest Fire Prevention and Management 
11.1 Forest fire is a major point of concern and occurs in almost every State in the country and 
some districts are more vulnerable than others.  The Committee therefore desired to know the 
mechanism put in place for detection and prevention  of forest fires. The  Ministry informed that 
Forest Survey of India (FSI) is playing a pivotal role in forest fire monitoring in the country since 
2004 when satellite data based alerts of near real time detection of active forest fires were started.  
FSI is providing various se rvices for monitoring of forest fires some of which are: (i) Pre Fire 
Alert (one week advance based on Forest Fire Danger Rating) , (ii) Large Forest Fire Monitoring, 
(iii) Near Real Time Forest Fire Monitoring and (iv) Van Agni Geoportal . Through the use of 
satellites, under a completely digital integrated system namely Near Real Time Forest Fire 
Monitoring (MODIS & SNPP – VIIRS), early warning signs can be detected which helps in 
prevention of forest fires and also aids in managing the damage caused by such fires. 
11. 2 While the Committee acknowledges the use of satellite technology for detecting 
forest fires, the Committee feels that there is need to strengthen  the ground mechanism and 
develop a well equipped and trained workforce to fight forest fires. The Committee , 
therefore, recommends strengthening post -fire assessments, inter -state knowledge sharing 
and research to understand the longer -term impacts of  forest fire which will enable to 

14 
 
identify gaps in implementation of the existing management system  and finding solutions  
thereon. 
11. 3 Tackling forest fires is important for improving the sustainability of the country’s 
forests. The Committee is happy at the increased allocation of Rs. 44.19 crore, for the 
Financial Year 2022 -23 for efficient utilisation and sufficient funding to the Ministry for 
implementation of its initiatives/ programmes for forest fire prevention and management at 
the central and State level . The Committee also urges upon the Ministry to promote 
awareness pro grammes in regions around forest areas for sensitising the concerned 
stakeholders to prevent accidental cases of forest fires.  Proper fencing of forest areas,  real 
time monitoring of illegal activities in forest areas and punitive action against the 
miscreants would further help the agencies in preventing forest fires.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should take into consideration national and 
international best practices for forest fire management. 
12. Man-Animal Conflict 
12.1 During the meeting of the Committee , a number of Members pointed out the increase in 
instances of man-animal conflict which were resulting in loss of life of both humans and animals 
and loss of property . Instances of human killing and damage to the crops  were unequivocally 
referred to by many Members.  Members also pointed out that a number of wild animals, 
especially , elephants, nil gai , wild pigs, leopards  etc, were venturing out of their territory and 
entering into the surrounding areas of human habitat resulting in damage to crops and increase in 
cases of man-animal conflict . The Committee , therefore,  desired to be appr ised about the 
mechanism that has been put  in place for compensation, treatment of victims of man -animal 
conflict, early settlement of compensation and prevention of such conflicts.  
 
12. 2 Responding to the issues raised in this regard, the representative of the Ministry of 
Environment, Forest and Climate Change submitted that this is a very serious issue and needs 
immediate attention. The Committee was informed that increase in cattle population is one of the 
major reasons for activities like grazing of cattle, collection of fire wood , etc, in forests, which 
often leads to attack on humans and cattle by the wild animals.  Therefore, work is being carried 
out for demarcation of forest area and its boundaries and also electric fencing of the boundaries. 
The Committee was also informed that efforts are being made to treat man -animal conflict as a 
disaster so that early resolution to such  conflicts could be made in a coordinated manner at 
district level. Efforts are also being made to evolve a mechanism for formulating a uniform 
compensation policy for the country  and that the inclusion of NDMA into the scheme for 
prevention and management  of National Disaster at national level would further help in early 
resolution of man-animal conflict and its prevention. 
 
 
12. 3 The Committee is of the view that the cases of man -animal conflict are on the rise  
and it still remains an area of concern.  As such, there is an urgent need to minimize the 
instances of man-animal conflict that was resulting in both human and an imal killings. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should take proactive steps to 
sustainably manage and minimis e man-animal conflict in a balanced manner , whereby the 
wildlife habitat is improved.  The Committee urges upon the Ministry to evolve a 
mechanism at the national level for uniform compensation, ea rly settlement of 
compensation and rehabilitation of  the victims of man -animal conflict. The Committee 
recommends that efforts should be made by the Ministry at local level and especially 
among the communities living in peripheral forest areas to sensitis e them towards 
ecological balance through co-existence with wild life. The Committee urges for creation of 

15 
 
grasslands for grazing of cattle outside the forest area to minimis e man -animal conflict.  
Further, State Forest Departments should also be advised to improve availability of fodder 
and adequate water sources in the forests, especially during summers, which would reduce 
the chances of the wildlife venturing out of their habitat in search of these basic needs.
