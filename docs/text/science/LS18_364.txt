the Ministry was allotted higher funds in respect of „Atmosphere & Climate

2.3 The Committee finds that against the total budgetary support of                  
Rs.2369.54 crore for the year 2021 -22, the Ministry could utilise Rs.1379.98 crore only 
which is just 58.23 per cent utilisation of the funds made available.  The Committee, thus, 
summarises that despite having higher allocation in certain components at RE st age, the 
overall utilisation of the same by the Ministry has not been very encouraging.   The 
Committee observes that had the increased funds allocated to the Ministry at  RE stage 
were given atBE stage itself then the utilisation by the Ministry could have been better. 
 
2.4 The Committee further finds that the utilisation of the funds has been significantly 
low under the Head „Establishment Expenditure of the Centre‟ where the Ministry could 
manage to spend 43.64 per cent only of the total funds made availab le.  Further, utilisation 
under „Central Sector Schemes‟ has been to the tune of  66.99 per cent.  Given the 
budgetary allocation utilisation capacity of  the Ministry,  the Committee hopes that the 
Ministry would increase its absorption capacity in order to utilise the total budgetary 
allocation made for the year 2021-22 optimally.  The Committee, therefore, desires that the

23 stage would be sufficient initially to take care of the financial requirement of the 
Ministry under its various schemes/projects/programmes and if required,  the Ministry 
would seek higher allocation at RE stage specifically under the Deep Ocean Mission (DOM) 
and Polar and Cryosphere Research (PACER) components so that the objectives envisaged

4. AVAILABILITY OF HUMAN RESOURCES  
4.1 Responding to the query about the availability of manpower, the Ministry submitted that 
over the past few years many new activities including R&D in understanding of the earth science 
processes, development of applications for new services based on the requirements of the 
stakeholders, development of technology for exploration and harnessing of ocean resources, 
exploration of Antarctica, Arctic, Southern Indian Ocean and the Himalayas and scientific 
studies have been undertaken under the six major umbrella Schemes without  any 
commensurate increase in staff.  It was also informed that based on the current activities of the 
Ministry about 500 additional manpower (Scientific/Technical/Administrative) would be 
required.       
4.2 The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry is facing challenges on account 
of availability of trained manpower to further strengthen it s R&D activities.  The 
Committee is of the firm opinion that even if the Schemes/programmes/activities of the 
Ministry are adequately funded, the desired results cannot be achieved without having 
trained manpower to take up R&D at par with international st andards.  Therefore, it is 
imperative to take necessary interventions on immediate basis so that the required 
manpower (scientific/technical/administrative) could be made available to the Ministry.  
The Committee, accordingly, recommends the Ministry to ta ke necessary action so as to 
ensure that the R&D activities of the Ministry are not halted for want of skilled workforce.  
The steps undertaken by the Ministry for the recruitment and training of the additional 
500 manpower may also be intimated to the Committee.         
5. WEATHER BASED AGRO ADVISORY SERVICES  
5.1 On being enquired about the weather based Agro Advisory Services of the Ministry for 

 
6 
 
the farmers of the country, it was submitted by them that growing uncertainties of weather and 
climate pose a major threat to food productivity and security of the country which warrant for 
farmers empowerment through informed decision making in agricultur al risk management.  
While all other physical factors, inputs and agronomic practices can be manipulated, vagaries of 
weather cannot be controlled.  But, adverse effects on cro ps can often be mitigated and risk in 
agricultural operations can be minimised b y providing accurate and timely weather information 
along with proper interpretation thereof implying the agricultural significance of the 
forecast/nowcast; issuing advisories for farm operations; and disseminating all relevant 
information well in advance about the impending weather.  The Committee was informed that 
the India Meteorological Department (IMD), Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) in collaboration 
with Indian Council of Agriculture Research (ICAR) and State Agriculture Universities (SAUs) 
and oth er institutions is rendering weather forecast based  District/Block level                            
Agro-meteorological Advisory Services (AAS) for the benefits of farmers in the country under 
the scheme “Gramin Krishi Mausam Sewa (GKMS)”.  The Ministry su bmitted that presently 
agromet advisories are being prepared on every Tuesday and Friday for all the agriculturally 
important districts and around 3100 blocks by  130 Agromet Field Units (AMFUs), co -located 
with State Agricultural Universities (SAUs), institutes of Indian Council of Agriculture Research 
(ICAR), Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) etc., and 199 District Agromet Units (DAMUs), 
newly established in the premises of Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) under the network of 
ICAR, in the country.  Along w ith the biweekly bulletins, daily weather forecast and now cast 
information are also disseminated to the farmers by Regional and State Meteorological centers of 
IMD.  The Committee was given to understand that the main emphasis of the existing AAS 
system is to collect and organise climate/weather, soil and crop information, and to amalgamate 
them with weather forecast to assist farmers to take decisions on day -to-day farm operations.  In 
AAS, the Ministry has been making efforts t o continuously improve the existing District level 
Agromet Advisory Services and extend them further to block level.  The Ministry aims to 
establish 530 District Agro -Met Units (DAMUs), complimentarily with already operating 130 
AMFUs, in close cooperation with ICAR, Department of A gricultural Research and Education 
(DARE), at Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) to support block level AAS and to expand the 
existing channels of communication of weather based agro-met advisories to the farmers, so as to 
expand the outreach to cover all farmer  households.  The Ministry is also making efforts to 
promote Research & Excellence in Agro -meteorology in support of targeted improvement to the 
operational services. 
5.2 The Committee while making note of the efforts being made by the Ministry under 
its w eather based agro advisory services expresses its satisfaction towards the overall 
progress achieved against the physical targets for the year 2021 -22.  The Committee, 
however, observes that the progress of the Ministry on many counts such as number of 
District Agro -meteorological Field Units (DAMU);  installation & commissioning of 
Doppler Weather Radars; installation of Automatic Weather Stations (AWS); number of 
Climate Reference station set up by IITM/IMD; number of X -band radars 
installed/commissioned; establishment of Doppler Weather Radars over North Eastern 
Region; etc ., has been sluggish.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to revisit its 
performance in terms of realisation of physical targets set  under ACROSS Scheme and 
strives hard to improve it.  Steps taken in this regard may be intimated to the Committee.                                
The Committee also recommends that climate hazards and vulnerability atlas will be 
prepared State/UT-wise for the entire country and disseminated to the farmers at ground 

 
7 
 
level on priority basis.   
5.3 On the issue of the Indian Antarctic Programme, the Ministry informed the Committee 
that Ice Class Vessel is the backbone of the Indian Antarctic Programme for tran sportation of 
man and annual supplies for the two research bases, i.e., „Maitri‟ and „Bharati‟ and also as a 
platform for Helicopter support for science and logistics operations in Antarctica.   It was 
submitted that currently the annual Antarctic expeditio ns are being executed with the available 
Ice Class Cargo Vessels from the global market.  The Committee was given to understand that 
availability of such facilities are also dwindling in the recent years.  It was submitted that non -
availability of chartere d vessel for any reason or failure to hire a suitable vessel during any 
particular Antarctic summer cannot only seriously jeopardize the Antarctic programme but also 
endanger the life of the personne l manning these two stations.  Sharing the major problems with 
the chartered cargo vessels, it was submitted that apart from having limited maneuverability 
these vessels are unable to serve as a research platform and, therefore, it is imperative to acquire 
our own PRV.  It was also brought to the notice of the C ommittee that due to one or another 
reason, not much headway could be gained in the direction of acquiring a PRV.  The Committee 
was also apprised of the debilitating conditions and the efforts being undertaken by the Ministry 
for the upgradation of Maitri Station at Antartica.    
5.4 The Committee appreciates the sentiments expressed by the Ministry and the 
problems being faced by it in acquiring a PRV.  It is painful to learn that despite having 
two stations in Antarctica and one in the Arctic of greater strategic significance and 
importance with specific reference to the climate change, India does not have a PRV.  The 
reasons for delay in acquiring a PRV are well understood but the Ministry should not lose 
sight of its determination of acquiring one.  The  Committee strongly supports the Ministry 
in the matter and recommends it to tighten the loose knots and make earnest efforts in 
getting the required funds for the purpose.   The Committee , therefore,  recommends the 
Ministry of Earth Sciences to take up the  matter appropriately with the Ministry of 
Finance to make the funds available so that the purpose of having these stations could be 
achieved fully. 
5.5 Further, the Committee notes the impressing need for replacement/upgradation of 
the Maitri Station especially in light of the fact that the Station has not only outlived its life 
and its structural integrity is very much compromised which needs immediate 
replacements but also the Station is not environment friendly and the same has been 
commented upon by various international committees, as informed by the Ministry.                                 
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry is already seized of the matter and 
making efforts in this direction.  The Committee hopes that the proposed new Station 
referred to as “Maitri-II” would be completed as per the timeline fixed for the purpose and 
the Ministry would be duly supported financially to meet this target.

AVAILABILITY OF HUMAN RESOURCES  
 The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry is facing challenges on account 
of availability of trained manpower to further strengthen its R&D activities.  The 
Committee is of the firm opinion that even if the S chemes/programmes/activities of the 
Ministry are adequately funded, the desired results cannot be achieved without having 
trained manpower to take up R&D at par with international standards.  Therefore, it is 
imperative to take necessary interventions on i mmediate basis so that the required 
manpower (scientific/technical/administrative) could be made available to the Ministry.  
The Committee, accordingly, recommends the Ministry to take necessary action so as to 
ensure that the R&D activities of the Ministr y are not halted for want of skilled workforce.  
The steps undertaken by the Ministry for the recruitment and training of the additional 
500 manpower may also be intimated to the Committee.                                             (Para 4.2)
