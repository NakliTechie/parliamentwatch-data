INTRODUCTION 
 The Government of India has set up the Space Commission and the Department of 
Space (DOS) in 1972. Department of Space is responsible for implementing the policies 
framed by the Space Commission. These are carried out through the Indian Space Research 
Organisation (ISRO), Indian National Space Promotion & Authorization Centre (IN-SPACe) 
and the Grant-in-aid institutions viz., Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National 
Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-
SAC), Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL) and Indian Institute of Space Science and 
Technology (IIST). 
1.2 The Secretariat of the Department of Space and the Headquarters of ISRO are located 
in Bangalore. The Programme Offices at ISRO Headquarters co-ordinate the overall 
programmes like Satellite Communication, Satellite Navigation, Earth Observation, Space 
Transportation Systems, Space Science and Planetary Exploration, Climate & Atmospheric 
Programme, Disaster Management Support, National Natural Resources Management System 
(NNRMS), International Co-Operation, Capacity Building, Media & Public Relations, 
Budget & Economic Analysis and Human Resources Development. 
1.3 The activities of Department of Space (DOS) are broadly organized under four 
umbrella schemes namely 
 Space Technology - development of launch vehicles, satellites, manned space mission, 
R&D in Space Technology such as propulsion, navigational satellite systems, 
indigenization of Space materials & components, etc.  
 
 Space Applications - major Projects/activities such as Earth Observation Navigation 
Missions, Tele-medicine, Tele-education, Disaster Management Support, etc are 
undertaken. 
 
 Space Sciences - projects towards Space sciences and planetary exploration are 
undertaken. Major projects undertaken under the scheme include Indian Lunar Mission - 
Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1 mission, XPoSat, Space Docking Experiment.  
 
 INSAT Satellite Systems- This programme is envisaged to provide satellite based 
communication services in the country.  
1.4 Major Centres / Units of DOS/ISRO: 
a) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, provides the 
technology base for the country’s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development 
b) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities located at Bangalore and 
Thiruvananthapuram, is responsible for development of liquid propulsion and 
cryogenic propulsion systems for satellite and launch vehicles 
c) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, is the 
spaceport of India providing launch base to indigenously built launch vehicles.  Also, 
it provides the facilities for processing of solid rocket motors 

2 
 
d) Human Space Flight Centre (HSFC) is created with the responsibility to act as the 
lead centre for Human Space Flight Program. This centre is responsible for the 
execution of Gaganyaan programme that envisages sending a crew of astrounauts to 
space and bring back safely to earth.   
e) U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC), Bangalore, is entrusted with the prime 
responsibility for development of satellites     
f) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad 
g) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad is responsible for satellite data 
acquisition and processing, data dissemination, aerial remote sensing and decision 
support for disaster management 
h) The Laboratory for Electro-Optics Systems (LEOS), Bangalore  
i) ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC), Mahendragiri  
j) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking & Command Network (ISTRAC) 
k) Master Control Facility (MCF), Hassan 
l) Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), Dehradun  
m) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram  
1.5 Organisations receiving Grants-in-aid: 
a) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous institution 
supported mainly by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences. 
b) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL) set up at Gadanki near 
Tirupati, conducts atmospheric research. 
c) The North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC) established as an 
autonomous body under the Department of Space provides space technology inputs 
for developmental activities in the North-Eastern Region.  
d) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) is an autonomous body 
under the Department with the objective of creating quality human resources tuned to 
suit the requirements of state-of-art space research programme.    
1.6 Government Companies under DOS:  
a) The Antrix Corporation Limited, Bangalore, established in 1992, is a wholly owned 
Government Company for marketing space products and services.  
b) The NewSpace India Limited (NSIL), Bangalore, is another commercial arm of 
Indian Space Research Organization, with the primary objective of enabling Indian 
industries to scale up high-technology manufacturing and production base for Indian 
space programme.  
1.7 Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Center (INSPACe) 
Recently, the space sector was opened up to promote, handhold, regulate and 
authorise private enterprises and start-ups to undertake space activities by creation of an 
independent nodal agency under Department of Space - the Indian National Space Promotion 
and Authorization Center (IN-SPACe). This will enhance the contribution of space 
technology towards boosting space economy within the country.

2.10 The Committee observes that the Joint ISRO-NASA Mission to the ISS faced a 
substantial budget cut at the R.E. stage, reducing its allocation from ₹715 crore to ₹412 
crore, reflecting a ₹303 crore reduction. The actual expenditure till December 2024 was 
₹413.14 crore, exceeding the R.E. allocation. The Department in written reply stated that 
the reduction in RE 2024-25 was due to the mission being rescheduled to next Financial 
Year. However, the B.E. for 2025-26 has been drastically reduced to ₹135 crore, 
reflecting the deferral of the mission to a later date. The Committee expresses concerns 
over further delays in India's collaboration with NASA and recommends that DoS 
maintain strong international engagement to ensure mission stability. The DoS informed 
the Committee that while the reduction was necessary due to scheduling constraints, the 
bilateral collaboration with NASA and Axiom Space remains intact, and necessary funds 
would be allocated at a later stage.

2.12 The Committee observes from Chart 4 that the budget for IN-SPACe (Indian 
National Space Promotion and Authorization Centre), which is tasked with promoting 
private sector participation in space activities, was reduced from ₹96 crore (B.E. 2024-
25) to ₹58 crore (R.E. 2024-25), reflecting a cut of ₹38 crore. The actual expenditure till 
December 2024 was ₹30.19 crore, significantly below the allocated funds. However, B.E. 
2025-26 has been set at ₹70 crore, showing partial restoration of funding. The 
Department in a written reply informed the Committee that The BE 2024-25 allocation of ₹96 
crore for IN-SPACe included establishment and operational expenditure as well as provision 
towards institutionalization of various schemes such as establishment of technology adoption 
fund, establishment of common manufacturing clusters in various states. However, in 2025-
26 the budget provision of ₹70 crores is provided towards establishment and operational 
expenses of IN-SPACe. IN-SPACe subhead being a non-scheme item, the budget provision 
of ₹165 crore for promotional activities / schemes has been provided under the minor head 
‘Space Technology’- Promotional activities of IN-SPACe. Hence, the total allocation for 
operational and promotional activities of IN-SPACe has been enhanced to ₹235 crores in BE 
2025-26. The Committee recommends that the Department should provide clear 
demarcation in future budget proposals between establishment and operational 
expenses and promotional activities for IN-SPACe. The Committee also recommends 
that a separate budgetary subhead under IN-SPACe should be considered to ensure 
transparency in fund allocation and utilisation, preventing confusion in financial 
reporting.

2.17 The Committee strongly recommends that additional funds be allocated to Space 
Sciences at the R.E. 2025-26 stage, ensuring that critical planetary and interplanetary 
missions proceed without disruption. The Committee also recommends that the 
₹1,488.60 crore reductions in Space Technology be re-evaluated, as it could slow down 
key advancements in satellite launch capabilities, propulsion systems, and indigenous 
space innovation. The Committee recommends that the Department should improve its 
financial forecasting for major space research and technology projects to avoid 
significant funding shortfalls at the final allocation stage. The Committee also 
recommends that while cuts in Establishment and Other Central Expenditures are 
reasonable, critical infrastructure and capacity-building expenses should not be 
compromised.

4.1    IN-SPACe 
  The Department in a written reply informed the Committee about the vision, mission 
and objectives of INSPACe. IN-SPACe aims to boost private sector participation in space 
activities, strengthen India’s space economy, and promote self-reliance in space technology. 
As a single-window agency, it facilitates start-ups, MSMEs, industries, and academic 
institutions in innovation and commercialisation within the space sector. Its key functions 
include:  
 
(i) Enabling private sector involvement in launch vehicle and satellite development. 

20 
 
(ii) Granting access to ISRO’s infrastructure for research, manufacturing, and testing. 
(iii) Overseeing the establishment of new space facilities by private players while ensuring 
compliance with safety and statutory guidelines. 
(iv) Facilitating launch campaigns and satellite registration for Indian private entities. 
(v) Encouraging technology sharing and collaboration to strengthen India’s space 
ecosystem. 
(vi) Managing an integrated launch manifest to align priorities of ISRO, public sector 
units, and private entities. 
(vii) Providing affordable access to ISRO’s high-tech facilities for private sector research

5.3   National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL)  
NARL conducts advanced research in atmospheric and space sciences, focusing on 
monitoring atmospheric processes, high-resolution weather modelling, and scientific 
observations to support space missions and other applications. It has developed high-power 
radio and optical instruments, many of which have been patented and transferred to industry. 
The Department informed that observatories in Hyderabad and Kolkata have been established 
to study key national issues, while the Venus Advanced Radar and Differential Absorption 
Lidar have been developed for planetary and atmospheric studies. NARL has also introduced 
AI/ML-based techniques for weather forecasting and atmospheric data analysis, built GNSS 
receiver networks for ionospheric and meteorological research, and improved rocket launch 
weather forecasting models. The airglow laboratory in Hyderabad and spectrograph in 
Gadanki contribute to studying atmospheric dynamics and space weather. Over 6.8 TB of 
climate data has been shared with scientists, and 184 research papers have been published in 
peer-reviewed journals. The institute has produced 17 PhDs and 97 dissertations, with many 
faculty members receiving national and international honours, including recognition from 
Stanford University, INSA, and the International Commission for Optics. Looking ahead, 
NARL aims to expand its research facilities, setting up more observatories across the country 
and establishing a national-level observational network. It seeks to develop indigenous 

26

vapour and ionospheric mapping, and study the impact of rapid urbanisation on atmospheric 
conditions. The institute is also committed to strengthening collaboration with academic and 
national laboratories to achieve significant advancements in atmospheric and space research.

exploration, and the human spaceflight program. He then presented the major 
accomplishments of the Department of Space over the years, starting from the first sounding 
rocket launch in 1963 to the recent successful missions such as Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1, 
and the XPoSat satellite. He highlighted that India has launched 4,002 sounding rockets, 
accomplished 100 launch vehicle missions, and built 131 satellites, including the heaviest 
satellite, GSAT-11, weighing 6,000 kg. He also mentioned that India has launched 433 
satellites for 34 foreign countries, demonstrating its capability in the global space arena. He 
elaborated on the recent achievements of ISRO, including the successful launch of the 
XPoSat satellite on January 1, 2025; INSAT-3DS meteorological satellite and  the successful 
docking experiment conducted in January 2025, which positioned India as the fourth country 
to achieve this feat. He stated that this experiment is crucial for future missions, including the 
development of a space station and the Gaganyaan human spaceflight program. He further 
highlighted the progress in the Gaganyaan program, including the human rating of launch 
vehicles, the development of the orbital module, and the crew escape system. He mentioned 
that the first uncrewed mission is planned for 2025, followed by two more uncrewed missions 
and a crewed mission. While informing about the development of a Next-Generation Launch 
Vehicle (NGLV) capable of placing 30,000 kg into low Earth orbit, he explained that the 
NGLV would use a liquid oxygen and methane propulsion system, which is reusable and 
more cost-effective. Additionally, he highlighted the development of atomic clocks and 
Traveling Wave Tube Amplifiers (TWTAs), which are critical for navigation and 
communication satellites. He also informed about the Department’s plans for India's first 
space station, with the first module targeted for launch by 2028 and the complete space 
station by 2035. He further elaborated on the Chandrayaan-4 and Chandrayaan-5 missions, 
which aims to bring back lunar samples and conduct more advanced lunar exploration. 
10. Thereafter, the Chairman and Members of the Committee raised several queries such 
as need for indigenization of electronic components and carbon composites, which are critical 
for space missions, role of the National Atmospheric Research Laboratory in Tirupati in 
monitoring greenhouse gas emissions and weather conditions, concerns about space 
congestion and pollution, particularly with the increasing number of satellites being launched
