REPORT 
 The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research 
on the recommendations contained in the One hundred and Fifty-sixth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2006-07) of the 
Department of Scientific & Industrial Research, which was presented to Parliament on 18th May, 2006. There were 12 
recommendations contained in the Report. 
 Action Taken Note on the recommendations of the Committ ee and further clarifications thereon were received 
from the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research on 8th August, 2006 and 13th September, 2006, respectively. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon by the Department and comments of the Committee 
thereon are set out in the succeeding paragraphs. 
BUDGETARY  ALLOCATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department  has made efforts to keep the pace of expenditure 
uniform except for the first quarter for which funds were partially released. The Committee has also been assured that 
the funds for last quarter will be fully utilized. The Committee in its previous report had recommended that the 
Department evolve a mechanism to maintain uniform pace of expenditure, to the extent po ssible, throughout the year. 
The Committee hopes this healthy trend will continue in future.  
(Para 10) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 As desired by the Committee, Department would make all efforts to keep the pace of expenditure uniform during 
2006-07 also. 
 During the first quarter, the expenditure would be slightly on lower side, as the new projects are sanctioned only 
after intensive evaluation of each proposal. The expenditure in  respect of ongoing projects is based on submission of 
utilization certificates and evaluation of progress of each pr oject. After the end of the financial year, the institutions 
take time to complete the auditing. Only after auditing, they can submit the utilization certificates. 
 The funds allocated for DSIR have been utilized during the last quarter of 2005-06. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and expects the Depart ment to ensure that allotted funds are utilised 
optimally. 
IT INFRASTRUCTURE RENOVATION AND REFURBISHMENT  
Recommendation 
 The Committee would like to emphasize the importance of indigenous technologies and the crucial role they are 
going to play in an increasingly globalized world. Cutting edge technologies and patentable innovations are the only 
hope for wealth generation in a cut-throat competitive envir onment. Scientific establishments of the country like the 
CSIR have a huge responsibility of training human resource to that effect and creating an enabling environment for 
such innovations to take place. The Committee is hopeful that the Council will leave no stone unturned to show 
concrete results, which can help create wealth and contribute in improving people’s life. (Para 25) 
Action Taken by the Department 

 Department appreciates the faith reposed on it by the Hon’ble Committee. At this stage CSIR is in the midst of 
formulating its Eleventh Plan and would dovetail the proposals wherever feasible for enhancing the innovation 
environment. 
Comments 
 The Committee is hopeful that CSIR in the Eleventh Plan will take due  consideration of the Committee’s 
concerns. The Committee has time and again emphasized the importance of qualitative improvements in human 
resource supply, as in a scenario where  knowledge frontiers are expanding at an exponential speed, no scientific 
establishment can afford to remain complacent. 
NATIONAL  LABORATORIES 
Recommendation 
 The Scientific research in various fields is mainly dependent on the availability of modern environment and 
major research facilities. With the global competitiveness in different fields there is a need to focus on the issues 
relating to modernization of our national laboratories. The Committee in its 142nd Report had recommended building 
of new infrastructure and renovation of the existing ones should be taken up in right earnest. The Committee would like 
to reiterate the same and would like to see renovation carried out as early as possible. The Committee recommends that 
the Council should draw a time-frame for such programme and s hould finish the task within the prescribed time-limit. 
Since the CSIR laboratories are located all over the countr y, having people’s interface in a much better way, the 
Committee recommends that there is need for setting up a special Technology Dissemination Cell in all the CSIR 
laboratories so that the technology support can be given to Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) also. (Para 31) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The concern of the Committee is taken note of. In the Tenth Plan, separate schemes on Infrastructure Renovation 
& Refurbishing (Civil) and IT Infrastructures are being implemented in a time bound manner. 
 With reference to the query on Technology Dissemination Cell in all the CSIR laboratories, it is important that a 
Unit for Science Dissemination coordinates such activities. Moreover, all the laboratories have a business development 
cell to provide technology support and transfer. 
 The Department further clarified that the Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) has recommended the project 
“Building a Science knowledge Grid & ICT Infrastructure and services” on 24.10.06 and the financial sanction was 
approved by Department of expenditure on 27.01.2006 at a cost of Rs. 97.50 crore. 
 Major deliverables of the project include network enable d Desktop computers to Scientist/ staff of CSIR Labs for 
seamless integration with E-Grid / Data Centre; to facil itate the implementation of operation of e-management 
packages like e-procurement, e-finance, e-administration etc.; to facilitate Data Centre equipment & facilities for Grid 
computing infrastructure and linkage to CSIR Labs.; to faci litate the State of the art Video Conferencing Facility for 
CSIR Labs and related Scientific departments. 
 Achievements till date includes budget allocation to pr ocure Hardware such as PCs, Servers & LAN for creation/ 
modernization of ICT infrastructure, VC facility and ERP applications; preparation of common tender; specifications 
for Data Centre equipments. 
 Infrastructure Renovation & Refurbishing (Civil):  Most of the CSIR laboratories are more the 25 years old. 
For good quality research to be carried out, it is necessary to have conformance and compliance of Good Laboratory 
Practices and of various parameter standards set up by ag encies like NABL, ISO etc. These buildings should also 
adhere to the fire an d safety standards. In order to meet these requirements, a scheme namely ‘Infrastructure 
Renovation& Refurbishing (Civil)’ was put in place with an aim to upgrade the infrastructural facilities of CSIR 

laboratories. Since the approval of the scheme was received only in 2005-06. Selective renovation could only 
commence in 2006-07. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. It also  takes note that Unit for Scie nce Dissemination coordinates the 
activities to help technology dissemination . The Committee is still of th e opinion that though all the laboratories 
have a business development cel l to provide technology support and tran sfer, there is a need for a nodal agency 
to keep track of development taking place in different laboratories for better coordination and dissemination. 
RESEARCH SCHEMES, SCHOLARSHIPS AND FELLOWSHIPS 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that there is a need to improve quality  of science education in th e country. The start has to 
be made right at the school level. Quality of science education should not only generate keen interest in children but 
also be able to fire their imagination in order to make them stick to the science stream. A holistic ‘start- to-finish’ 
approach is the need of the hour, where children once initia ted in science are not left confused after their schools but 
are provided a link to higher research institute via universities. Improving quality of research courses in Universities 
and their affiliation with research institutes, can be followed according to our own requirements. The Committee is of 
the opinion that an ambitious programme, which caters to both  the supply of rich human resource right from the school 
level and the consequential rich dividends gained by way of intellectual property finally is what should be aimed at.  
  (Para 33) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department is running a programme “CSIR Programme on Youth for Leader ship in Science (CPYLS)” to 
attract meritorious school students towards science and help them in building scientific temper and expose the young 
minds to exciting world of science. Under the programme, the first 50 students (toppe rs) from each Secondary Board 
are chosen after passing the X examination and brought to CSIR laboratories for two days for the exposure of its 
activities. Among the students who pursue science after cla ss X are being provided support and facilities to do their 
project work for class XI/XII at one of the CSIR laboratories. They are free to discuss the selection and progress of 
their project with the scientists of the laboratories and also have the access to the R&D facilities available. Amongst 
these students who secure over 90% marks in natural science subjects (physics, chemistry, biology and mathematics) in 
the Senior Secondary Examination and continue to pursue  science studies, are supported for another three years till 
graduation. They are designated as “CSIR Student Associates ” and given the facility to work on a project, in a CSIR 
laboratory of their interest during thei r vacations. Further, the Department has taken an integrated approach for the 
national human resource development for S&T by encourag ing and promoting research in the universities and 
institutions of higher learning. 
 Ministry of Human Resource Development (MHRD) had set up a Task Force for basic scientific research in 
universities during March 2005. The Task Force had recommended inter alia: To create 10 networking centres in basic 
sciences (Physical Sciences, Chemical Sciences, Life Scie nces, Material Sciences and Mathematical Sciences) in 
leading departments of universities in different parts of th e country to provide collaborative research etc.; formal 
linkages between the universities and national level institutions including CSIR laboratories. The recommendations of 
the Task Force were accepted by MHRD and directed UGC to implement the recommendations of the Task Force for 
which an Empowered Committee has been constituted. The Empowered Committee is seized with the mechanisms of 
implementation of the recommendations of the Task Force. 
 
 

Comments 
 The Committee is hopeful that the recommendations of the task force will be taken up in the right earnest 
and the Empowered Committee appointed by the UGC will come out with an effective blue print to kick start an 
integrated programme to network high -end research that makes use of all th e available resea rch inputs in the 
country and provides necessary exposure to budding scientists. 
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY & TECHNOLOGY MANAGEMENT 
Recommendation 
 In a world where a substantial share of wealth produ ced is intellectual and a major portion of economy is 
knowledge-based, definition of wealth and property ha s undergone a sea change. India, being a fast-growing 
knowledge economy with a huge human resource potential, is well poised to be a leader in such a scenario. Property is 
no longer just natural and physical but increasingly intellectual. To exploit the huge intellectual capital we will have to 
ensure certain fundamentals lik e awareness and an understanding of market mechanism so that we do not lose out to 
other competitors in an envi ronment of patents and copyrights. The Comm ittee appreciates the good work done by the 
Department and hopes more results will follow in the future. (Para 41) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 In order to operate in a competitive environment, CSIR is trying to enhance awareness on some of the critical 
aspects of IP Management, namely, IP valuation, infringement detection, freedom to operate analysis, licensing, 
valorization and portfolio development. These objectives are planned to be achieved through the organization of 
workshops, conferences and training programmes for the scientists. 
Comments 
 The Committee is hopeful that the steps taken by the Department will help save our intellectual rights and 
will also ensure that products of both  modern research and traditional knowledge are not lost as a result of lack 
of awareness or knowledge of the patenting process. Th e Committee feels that the commercial interests of our 
research, including traditional knowledge, need to be protected at all costs. 
NEW MILLENIUM INDIAN TECHNOLOGY LEADERSHIP INITIATIVE (NMITLI) 
Recommendation 
 Progress of human civilization is based on innovation of one kind or the other. Material civilization progresses 
only with the progress in technology. Innovations require ‘out-of-the box thinking’, flexibility and an enabling 
environment. The Committee feels the above programme is a step in  the right direction. It is of the opinion that there is 
a need to take care of grass root innovations as small beginni ngs have big results. Beside this, in an era of intellectual 
property, innovations and weal th are necessarily linked and,  therefore, the stress on innovation cannot be over 
emphasized. (Para 44) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department appreciates the Standing Committee’s comments on innovation particularly the grass root 
innovations to generate wealth. The National Innovation Foundation (NIF), Ahmedabad, a registered society, is 
engaged in scouting, documenting, augmenting and adding value to the innovations and traditional knowledge of the 
innovations at the grass root level. NIF is also preparin g a national register of outstanding traditional knowledge and 
contemporary green grass root innovations. 
 CSIR and NIF have entered into an MoU to institute the “CSIR-NIF Innovation Fellowship” to provide hand 
holding support to grass root innovators and traditional knowledge holders. The selected innovations are being taken up 

by CSIR laboratories for value addition with a view to transform such innovations and knowledge into employment and 
wealth generating opportunities. 
Comments 
 The Committee feels that the country requires more and more institutional support systems like the 
National Innovation Foundation (NIF), Ahmedabad, to support grass root scientific breakthroughs so that such 
groundbreaking discoveries are not lost on account of lack of support. What is also required is awareness about 
the availability of such support so th at maximum use of such infrastructure is made for generating wealth for 
individual innovators in particular and society in general.

 The Committee feels that a developing country like India ne eds basic infrastructure to serve the needs of a fast

 The Expenditure and Finance Comm ittee (EFC) has approved the project  “Building a Science knowledge Grid &

 It is expected after the successful completion of the project the information flow and decision support system in

 The Committee is of the view that the Knowledge Grid should be comple ted as soon as possible so that

TECHNOLOGY PROMOTION, DEVELOPMENT AND UTILIZATION (TPDU) PROGRAMMES 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the Department should make efforts to encourage more participation from the Industry 
with a good track record for distribution of both risks and rewards. Industry with a rich experience of market can help 
scientific establishments feel the pulse of the market and make scientific research commercially viable. (Para 55) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Technology Development and Demonstration Progra mme (TDDP) of DSIR is specifically intended to 
encourage participation of industry in developing products/ processes and absorption of imported technologies. The 
Scheme supports lab scale/ bench scale technologies developed by CSIR, National Laboratories/ Institutions and 
Universities for scale up and commercialization by Indian industries. 
 The projects are submitted, for partial financial support, by the industry on its own or in association with national 
laboratories like IITs, IISc. and Universities. The scheme has been instrumental in establishing linkages amongst 
national laboratories, scientific institutions and industries and making scientific research commercially viable. 

 As suggested by the Committee, the Department would make further efforts by giving wide publicity to the 
programme for enhancing the interaction with Scientific establishments and industrial units to formulate joint 
technology development and demonstration projects to benefit from each other’s expertise. 
 With this in view, the Department periodically inserts advertisements in national dailies (a) covering all 
departmental schemes and also (b) covering individual schemes. 
 Last year 12 TV programmes were developed and broadcasted through Doordarshan. 
 Apart from 2-days national conference on research and de velopment in industries organized every year in Delhi, 
eleven one-day seminars with exhibition on innovations were held in smaller towns with industry clusters. These events 
always had a large draw. About ten such events will be held in 2006-07. 
 DSIR has been arranging exhibition spaces in associ ation with ITPO in the IITF every year for industries. 
Besides, in association with CII, facilities were provided to Indian industries to exhibit their technologies abroad. This 
year, DSIR facilitated participation of Indian industries in the Hannover Fair also. 
Comments 
 The Committee appreciates the effort made by the Department to comme rcialize infant technologies and 
involve industry in such an endeavour.  The Committee is of the view that ultimate test of a newly developed 
technology is it’s commercial viability. 
DEVELOPING NEW GENERATION FUELS 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the view that the research on altern ative sources of energy is still at infant stage. Where 
success has been achieved, commercial feasibility is a big problem. Given the volatile situation of fossil fuels, spiraling 
prices of crude oil; environmental concerns and expensive nature of nuclear fuel sooner or later the country will have to 
turn to non-conventional energy sources for its future needs.  The efforts made in this direction may be a good start but 
there is a long way to go. A non-conven tional source of energy, wh ich is commercially viab le, environment-friendly 
and capable of changing the present pattern of fuel consumption, is the only solution in the long run. (Para 59) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department has initiated a network programme on Development of new generation fuels and lubricants 
during Tenth Five Year Plan. CSIR through its various laboratories has been working on biofuels (ethanol) and 
biodiesel. The technologies are at various stages of developments. Biodiesel developed by the Department conforms to 
Euro emission norms. 
 The Department further clarified that CSIR has initiated a programme in the Tenth FYP for developing liquid 
fuels and ethanol produced from biomass. This is one of the major activities of the network project ‘Developing new 
generation fuels and lubricants’ approved with a total co st of Rs.24.05 crores. The programme envisages use of 
agriculture wastes such as wheat straw, rice straw, bagasse  etc. instead of molasses whose availability in India is 
predicted to be insufficient. CSIR is also working on performance evaluation of new generation automotive fuels to 
meet the emission norms. 
Achievements so far: 
 Ethanol produced from rice, wheat flour using commercial thermostable enzyme and yeast at 10L scale. 
 Isolation and identification of thermophilic strains that grow at 50°C. 
 Produce ethanol from starch by simultaneous liquification, saccaharification and fermentation by using 
thermophiles. 

 Analysis of fermented hydrolysate containing poly-, tr i-, di- and monosaccharides with ethanol in single 
HPLC run. 
 14 fold increase in cellulase production by SSF process using T. reesei was achieved. 140 FPUs of cellulase 
produced per gm of wheat bran compared to global average of 200 FPUs /g pure cellulose or lactose. 
 Novel substrate (Water Hyacinth) used in cellulase production. 
 New Strain with improved BGL activity isolated. 
 Strategy for genetic improvement of T reesei formulated and works initiated. 
 Sugar fatty acid ester, sugar amine, cellulose ester and starch esters prepared as lube base stock. 
Bio fuels 
 Indian Institute of Petroleum (IIP), a constituent of CSIR has carried out field studies with ethanol-gasoline 
blends with IOC R&D, Faridabad. These studies on ethanol we re carried out in earlier (1980s) technology vehicles and 
fuels. 
 In early nineties, Ministry of Non-Conventional Energy  Sources (MNES) sponsored a study on use of ethanol in 
diesel bus fleet of DTC running on dual-fuel mode using IIP developed retrofit kit for induction of ethanol. The results 
of this study showed feasibility of replacing 14.9% of diesel by ethanol and smoke reduction of up to 40%. 
Biodiesel 
 CSIR has been investing on development of ‘Biodiesel’ for quite some time and have established competencies 
in many areas of its production and use. These include en d-to-end use i.e., growing of bioresource as well as its 
extraction and conversion into diesel as fuel. The detail of the work carried out by CSIR is given below: 
    a.  Generation of Plantation material: 
 Inadequate seed availability is a major problem. Jatropha Curcus was never grown in organized sector and there 
is very little information available on germplasm, if at all, besides the problem of composition and consistency. One 
germplasm, which grows in one part of India, may not fruit in other region. Also, there are wide variations in seed yield 
and oil content. 
 NBRI, Lucknow has made a concerted effort in collecti ng germplasm from various states, creating nursery trails 
of germplasm, nursery experiments and raising successful nurse ries of selected materials. It has raised 3 lakh plants 
from its selections. 
 RRL, Jorhat in the hilly terrain of North-East and CSMCRI, in the waste lands of Gujarat and Orissa have also 
made efforts in identifying elite varieties of Jatropha Curcus in these regions. 
    b.  Extraction of oil 
 This is an important area wherein the oil purity and yi eld will have an impact on the cost of biodiesel and the 
byproduct. NBRI, has developed protocols for extraction of oil from Jatropha. 
    c. Storage and transportation 
 No authentic data is available on shel f life of Jatropha seeds, oil and biodiesel in India. NBRI has been engaged 
in generating data on storage of oil under various conditions. IICT has expertise and experience in similar activities, 
which can be used to generate data on shelf life of seeds, oils and biodiesel. 
    d.  Biodiesel production/ transesterification/ blending 
 CSIR laboratories have been doing R&D on transeterification and have successfully converted jatropha oil to 
biodiesel matching international specifications. IIP, Dehradun has developed three batch processes for low Free Fatty 

Acid (FFA) as well as for high FFA contents. Efforts are on to develop continuous transeterificiation process. IICT is 
doing R&D on catalyst free process as well as on solid catalys t. CSMCRI has designed plan ts for rural cooperatives. 
Blending of biodiesel with diesel is another area where expertise exists in IICT and IIP. 
    e.  Testing of blends 
 IIP, Dehradun and CSMCRI have already initiated testing blends of Diesel/Biodiesel for emissions and engine 
performance. While IIP is testing blends on engine performance vis-a-vis emissions, CSMCRI, has studied the engine 
performance of vehicle run over 6000 km. 
    f.  Effective generation / utilization of by products 
 Value addition to the Jatropha oil cake, which is a no n-edible matter, is another important area. CSMCRI in 
collaboration with Anand Agriculture University, has establis hed that cake has dual role of manure-cumbiopesticide. 
Besides developing cake as fertilizer, NBRI is collaborating wi th industry to make it a substitute for coal. Glycerine, 
which is a byproduct of transesterification needs to be of high purity. liP and CSMCRI are working on this aspect in the 
transesterification technology development. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Depa rtment will continue to make vigorous efforts in 
this direction. 
SETTING UP A WORLD CLASS DRUG RESEARCH INSTITUTE 
Recommendation 
 A country still not free from diseases which acquire epidemic proportions, a research programme for drug 
development cannot be overemphasized. Given the fact that major chunk of people suffering from such diseases are the 
ones with least purchasing power makes the challenge doubly complex. Government funded research institutes, not 
being solely driven by profit motive, are not left with a choice but to shoulder the responsibility, along with other 
departments of the Government, of providing affordable drugs  as no multi-national company will venture into an area 
where rich returns are not possible. The Committee is of the vi ew that what is required is  a close coordination between 
different arms of the Government as individual departments cannot be expected to bring down the cost of drugs below 
a level. The Committee feels that innovative ways like distribu tion of cost by providing cheap insurance for diseases to 
people have to be devised, as cost of manufacturing of drugs cannot be brought down below a point. (Para 61) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The suggestions of the Committee are under consideration of  Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers which is the 
administrative ministry for this issue. It is hoped that new Pharmaceutical and Drugs Policy will address this issue 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Department would pursue the Committee’s suggestion 
with the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers, and also keep the Committee apprised of such follow up. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is also of the opinion that the traditional medicine is popular, accepted and used by most of the 
country’s population. Moreover, there is growing importance in  traditional health systems in providing health care for 
wider population. Inspite of the Department’s efforts in formulating Golden Triangle, there is a need for giving boost to 
the herbal medicines. The Committee feels that with the abu ndant resources of medicinal plants all over the country 
there is a need to set up a Herbal Research and Developm ent Institute in a location where all the variation of our 
biodiversity can be tapped. (Para 62) 

Action Taken by the Department 
 There are over 7 lakhs registered Prac titioners of Ayurveda System in the country who are providing Health Care 
facilities in the country. On an average, for 1600 po pulation there is one A YUSH doctor. Department of AYUSH is 
committed to promote and propagate Ay urveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani,  Siddha and Homoeopathy (AYUSH) 
system of medicines in India and abroad. Some of our products like Isabgol, Senna etc. and Panchkarma therapy are 
doing very well at International level also. Department is following strategy of providing seats for foreign national in

INDIAN INSTITUTE OF CHEMICAL BIOLOGY, KOLKATA (IICB) 
 The Committee held discussions with the officials of Indian Institute of Chemical Biology, Kolkata on 5th 
February, 2005. During the course of the discussion, the Committee was informed that the Institute has taken up 

research in the field of cancer and sign ificant progress has been achieved as a herbal molecule has been identified to 
kill chronic meylogenous lukemic cells. The Committee, was informed that: 
 “The molecule identified by IICB Scientists from Paan has shown in experimental conditions a beneficial affect 
for chronic meylogenous luikemic cells (CML), a kind of blood cancer. The institute has to do a lot more work on the 
molecule so that it can be passed to pharmaceutical sector for production.” 
Recommendation 
 In pursuance of the above statement of  the IICB and also in view of the discussion it had with the management of 
IICB, Kolkata, the Committee strongly recommends that since cancer is the most difficult disease to treat in medical 
science and different types of cancer require different types of medicines, the Institute should put optimum effort to 
develop this molecule of paan to a final drug stage as soon as possible so that it could be passed on to the 
pharmaceutical sector for production in the near future thereby transmitting a lot of benefit to medical sector in treating 
chronic cases of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML.) (Para 64) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 A total number of 21 patent applications in different countries on the crude Piper betel leaf extract and the 
purified molecule have been licensed out to Nicholas Piramal India Ltd (NPIL). NPIL is working on the two 
approaches: Development of Piper betel leaf extract and the pure molecule for the treatment of chronic myeloid 
leukemia (CML) patents. According to NPIL, the extract sh owed significant anti-tumor activity in drug-sensitive and 
drug-resistant cells obtained from CML patients. NPIL is going to conduct GLP toxicity studies and human clinical 
trials with the extract soon. The pure compound showed co mparable activity to the only drug available, Gleevee in in 
vivo assays. However, the bioavailability of the compound in oral route is poor. NPIL is working upon development of 
formulations to improve the bioavailability for developing marketable oral product.
