R E P O R T
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Biotechnology on the Recommendations
 contained in the One Hundred-sixteenth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science &
 Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2003-2004) of the Department of Biotechnology which was
 presented/laid to both Houses of Parliament on 2 nd May, 2003.  There are twenty–three Recommendations contained in One
 Hundred- sixteenth Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the Recommendations of the Committee were received
 from the Department on 30
th July, 2003.
The Committee’s Recommendation, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the
 Department of Scientific and Industrial Research are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
Recommendation
            The Committee was informed that one of the recurring problems of the Department is the shortfall of the non-plan
 budget.  Recurrence of such problems on year to year basis, the committee was apprised, has created a compelling situation for
 the autonomous bodies whereby they had to cut down from plan funds to pay salaries to the scientists.  The Committee is
 constrained to note that scientific institutions engaged in research in frontier areas of science have to confront such a situation. 
 The additional requirement of the Department of non-plan expenditure is to the tune of Rs.5.00 crores.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that sufficient funds may be provided to the Department to overcome this problem.  It further underlines
 that the Departments dealing with science and technology should be accorded priority for allocation of funds and if necessary,
 special provisions may be made for them so that they do not face such problems.  Biotechnology era is waiting to dominate the
 World economy in the next twenty years.  Investment in this field is not commensurate with the potential of this branch of
 Science.
(Para 8)
Action taken by the Department
The Department has corresponded with Ministry of Finance, Government of India, on additional non-plan requirement as
 per recommendations. The matter is being pursued.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Department will pursue the Ministry of Finance to take cognizance of
 the matter.

PLANT TISSUE CULTURE
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that efforts should be made to make use of the tissue culture and floriculture technology in
 rural area.  Additional incentives should be given to persons willing to work in these areas.
(Para 20)
Action taken by the Department
            Department of Biotechnology has been supporting the area of plant tissue culture through a number of R&D projects and
 large-scale production facilities.  The two Micropropagation Technology Parks supported by DBT along with the six Regional
 Hardening Units in different parts of the country are making a concerted effort to promote this technology for application at the
 grass root level.  There is an enormous domestic market potential and efforts are being made to demonstrate the feasibility of this
 technology to farmers and other end users so as to take up this technology on a large scale.  Micropropagation technologies have
 been standardized for a large number of forest trees, horticulture and other economically important crops.  Demonstrations have
 been organized for agro forestry and agriculture sector through the help of state departments, corporations, NGOs and other
 extension agencies.
            A Consortium on Micropropagation Research and Development has now been established and programme support is
 being provided specifically for socio-economic development.  Different sites have been identified in the states of Maharashtra,
 Haryana and Punjab where grass root rural workers are being given extensive training.  Training is also provided through the
 National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) and Small Farmers Agri Consortium (SFAC) sponsored
 programmes on agro techniques and agri business ventures.  This is mainly for the educated youth who wish to take up
 agriculture as an enterprise. 
            The demonstration plots established in the agro forestry sector have yielded very good results at the farmer’s field in
 association with the Agro Forestry federation.  42% higher net profit has been achieved in the case of Eucalyptus and this
 technology has now been taken up by a commercial firm to cater to the needs of the farmers.
            Another important activity is training of the farmers through the Joint Forestry Management projects especially in the
 state of Haryana.  Special training programmes have been organized for potato and sugarcane, since, both these crops have a
 large demand.
            Special initiative has been taken by the Department was for rehabilitation of the Earthquake victims  of Gujrat in Bhuj. 
 Under a project coordinated by the Science Ashram, Baroda a green house has been established in collaboration with one of the
 local NGOs for producing vegetables all year round.  This has been very successful and an increase in the income specially of
 women has been noted.  Large-scale plantation of important forest trees such as Bamboo and Salvadora has also been initiated
 for the afforestation programme.  Bamboo plantation has been promoted in this region which would ultimately lead to socio-
economic upliftment of the people. 
            Application of tissue culture for floriculture is also being promoted through the DBT supported programmes specially for
 large scale production of important flowering and non-flowering plants which will lead to direct income generation.  A
 coordinated programme on orchids is now being evolved which would help in developing important novel varieties for large
 scale multiplication to cater to the domestic and export market.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the efforts at dissemination of the Micro propagation techniques of tissue culture and
 floriculture in different areas and would like to see more such programmes coming up in different parts of the country.

JAI VIGYAN NATIONAL S&T MISSION
Recommendation
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department in vaccine research and development.  The Committee
 hopes that the research done by the Department on microbial genomes, especially pathogens, in view of the burden of infectious
 diseases on our people, will result in the elimination of diseases like Malaria and Tuberculosis in a time bound manner.  The
 Committee understands that India is on the threshold of producing the first ever DNA vaccine in the world, for rabies.  It is
 appropriately said that “In the area of infectious diseases, drugs, vaccines and diagnostics are the ultimate products expected out
 of genomics information”.  While the Committee commends the efforts of the Department and notes such achievements with
 satisfaction, it hopes that vaccines/medicines would be priced reasonably so that these are within the reach of common people.
(Para 30)
Action taken by the Department
In the medical biotechnology and health-care areas, the Department has major emphasis towards development of suitable
 diagnostic assay systems for quick and accurate diagnosis of the important and major diseases considering the disease burden,
 morbidity and mortality. Under the National Jai Vigyan Science & Technology Mission, the oral recombinant cholera vaccine is
 in the advanced stages of human clinical trials with encouraging results. The other vaccines for malaria and HIV/AIDS are
 undergoing preclinical animal experimentations and likely to enter human clinical trials after successful completion of the same.
 The DNA Rabies Vaccine for animals is likely to be available in near future. Current programmes either have been initiated or
 being initiated to study the microbial genomics as the information will be useful towards better understanding of the
 microorganisms, development of diagnostics and vaccines including the disease process and pathogenesis. During the current
 financial year, the Department has emphasis towards development of new multicentric interactive programmes on respiratory
 diseases, leishmaniasis and cardiovascular diseases/disorders. As a policy matter, it is intended to keep the cost of the
 vaccines/diagnostics within the reasonable and affordable limit so that they can be available to the common people. This aspect
 has always been taken into consideration while negotiating with the industries towards commercialization. However, the
 marketing is done by the Industry, thus only efforts can be made. Final price will depend upon industries own marketing
 strategy.
Comments
The Committee commends the endeavour by the Department towards development assay systems for the quick and
 accurate diagnosis of major diseases and various programmes initiated to study different diseases.  The Committee would like to
 see more such steps to be taken to make it affordable to common people.

SERI-BIOTECHNOLOGY
Recommendation
The Committee feels that the country has the advantage of production of all four commercial silk varieties viz., mulberry,
 tusser, eri and muga.  The committee notes with satisfaction that the Department is according priority on Seri Biotechnology
 with special focus on non-mulberry silk.  It is heartening for the Committee to know that the Indian Institute of Science,
 Bangalore has, for the first time, done the important job of adopting control measures for pests of silk worm.  As sericulture is
 suitable for marginal, small and landless farmers, it has tremendous scope for expansion in view of its employment generation
 potential. Efforts should, therefore, be made to strengthen the linkages between extension and R&D units.  The technology
 delivery systems should be streamlined by providing need based training and effective field demonstration.  Various
 communication channels should also be used to disseminate information about the new measures to improve to safeguard the
 rights of those who develop it.
(Para 40)
Action taken by the Department
The Department is actively interacting and co-ordinating with Central Silk Board (CSB) with a view to strengthen the
 linkage between user agencies and R&D institutions. Field demonstration projects have also been carried out jointly with CSB
 to disseminate the technology packages for controlling the major diseases/pest of silkworm towards improving the silk yield.
 Training programmes were also an integral part of such demonstrations at the field level. Representatives of CSB have been
 closely associated with the monitoring, review and implementation of the project, supported by DBT in this area.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
MICROBIAL AND INDUSTRIAL BIOTECHNOLOGY
Recommendation
The Committee recommends that the technology developed at the laboratory stage under the programme should be
 brought to the level of pilot plant scale and should be transferred to the industry.  Moreover, the technology developed should
 be patented so as safeguard the rights of those who develop it.
(Para 43)
Action taken by the Department
The department has established sophisticated biotech facilities for scale-up and validation of technologies available at
 laboratory stage. Further, adequate mechanisms have been evolved for the purpose through public- private partnerships and
 technology transfer. Major thrust of the department is to convert research leads into products and processes through technology
 transfer and commercialization involving industry. About 51 technologies from R&D leads transferred to industry and products
 such as HIV diagnostic kits, drug delivery system, Leprosy vaccine (1
st of its kind in the world), 14 diagnostic kits for detection
 of Pregnancy and contraceptive problems, packages for bio-remediation of petroleum oil spills, several bio-fertilizers, bio-
pesticides formulations are already in the market resulting in import substitution and value addition. A patent facilitation cell has
 been established to promote national and international patents of indigenous innovations. So far, 112 patents have been filled
 and 7 International patents and 6 National patents have been granted. To facilitate technology transfer, Biotechnology
 Consortium India Limited (BCIL) is also actively involved.
Comments
The Committee notes with satisfaction the attempts to transfer and commercialize biotech products and processes for use
 in industry.

 the NBRC, the Department should undertake activities to network with the existing neuroscience groups/institutions in the

Action Taken by the Department (64 & 65)
The National Brain Research Centre, an autonomous institution of the Department of Biotechnology, through its
 networking with 42 institutions has helped to promote neuroscience in the country. Multi-institutional collaboration projects are

The Committee endorses the vision of the Department to make agriculture sustainable by using biotechnology.  To get
 better yields, we need plants and methods of cultivation which will be in harmony with biodiversity and eco-system and,

 tomato) resistant to biotic stresses.  Similarly, some R&D projects are also being supported in this direction.  Another project on
 salinity and dehydration stress tolerance in rice has been supported recently. The project would ultimately result in raising of
 rice transgenic employing candidate genes for resistance against salinity/dehydrations stress.   In an attempt to solve the
 problem of malnutrition in rural areas, a multi-institutional coordinated programme to enhance quantity/quality of protein and

USE OF TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE
Recommendation
India is rich with traditional knowledge.  Our people with centuries of experiences have emerged as practitioners of such
 knowledge.  This rich reservoir of such resources must be understood scientifically and safeguarded.  The Committee was
 informed by a Member that Ganga Jal, Gou Mutra and Gober have anti-bacterial properties.  It was also told that Gober is
 radiation resistant.  It was told that when hospital wastes were treated with layers of Gober for five months, it was found that all
 bacteria, toxin and poison had been neutralised.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that steps must be taken by the
 Department to explore the anti-bacterial properties of Ganga Jal, Gou Mutra and Gober, specially the effectiveness for treating
 hospital waste.
(Para 79)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department has noted the recommendations of the Committee on Ganga Jal, Gou Mutra and Gobar. At present, there
 are no specific projects in these areas. However, other agencies like Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) have
 conducted investigations and experimentations on the utilities of the above-mentioned products. Similarly, Ministry of Health &
 Family Welfare and Ministry of Environment and Forest are implementing projects/programmes on regulation and treatment of
 hospital waste in which the department is not directly involved. However, in future, if any such proposal is submitted to the
 department, it will be considered on scientific merit.
Comments
        The Committee feels that the department should initiate efforts on its own with regard to exploring the anti-bacterial
 properties of Ganga Jal, Gou Mutra and Gobar.
