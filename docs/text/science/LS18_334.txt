Government on the recommendations/observations contained in Three Hundred Twenty Sixth Report 
of the Committee on 'Demands for Grants (2020 -21) of the Department of Atomic Energy’.  The 
Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 6th March, 2020. 
2.  Action Taken Note (ATN) has been received from Department of Atomic Energy in respect of 
the seven recommendations/observations containe d in the Three Hundred Twenty Sixth Report.  This

BUDGETERY DETAILS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2019 -20 AND ALLOCATION 
FOR 2020-21 
 
Recommendation/Observation  
1.1 The Committee was informed that the Department had made a projected demand of                          
Rs. 40,259.05 crore for BE 2020 -21 against which an amount of Rs.26,691.70 crore has been 
allocated, registering an overall reduction of Rs.13,567.35 crore i.e. 33.70% less than the projected 
demand of the Department.  Of the seven Ministries/Departments  under the purview of the Committee, 
it is only the budgetary allocations for the DAE in BE 2020 -21 that is cause for grave concern.  
Approximately 41% of this shortfall amount of Rs.13,567.35 crore is the revenue expenditure and 59% 
affects the projected capital expenditure.                                                                                     (Para 2.2)                                      
                                                                                                                        
Action Taken 
1.2 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee in respect of para 2.2 of the 326 th Report are 
noted.  The Department will take up the matter with Ministry of Finance at RE 2020-21 stage.  
Recommendation/Observation  
1.3 The Committee is of the considered opinion that there are expenditures such as salaries to 
employees, payment of utility bills, etc. under the Revenue head which are obligatory in nature and any 
reduction/shortfall in funds under these components would be akin  to denying oxygen to life, and will 
severely affect the overall functioning of the Department of Atomic Energy.  The Committee is, 
therefore, fully convinced that expenditure of obligatory nature must not be subjected to any kind of 
reduction/shortfall whatsoever.  The Committee, accordingly, recommends that, at the very minimum,  
adequate funds to meet such expenditure of obligatory nature should be allocated by the Ministry of 
Finance at the RE stage.                                                                                                             (Para 2.5)                                                               
Action Taken 
1.4 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee in respect of para 2.5 of the 326 th Report are 
noted.  The Department will take up the matter with Ministry of Finance at RE 2020-21 stage.  
SPECIAL OBSERVATIONS 
Recommendation/Observation  
1.5 The Committee notes that the country's nuclear energy programme is at a very sensitive 
crossroads.  From the point of view of emi ssions of greenhouse gases, nuclear power is a clean source 
of electricity.  However, its costs are not coming down.  In fact, they are going up due to greater 
concerns on safety, following the Fukushima disaster in May 2011.  On the other hand, prices of solar 
power have come crashing down and the relative economics of nuclear and solar power has changed 
dramatically.  This cannot be ignored totally, even though presently solar power is intermittent power 
and conversions efficiency is low.  The Committee believes India cannot afford to do away with a base-
load source of electricity like nuclear power.  India's nuclear energy programme has both a strategic 
and energy dimension, and both of these have to be kept in view while considering the Department of 
Atomic Energy.                                                                                                                             (Para 3.1)                                                  
 

3 
 
Action Taken 
1.6 The Hon. Committee’s observation is noted. NPCIL is very conscious of the competitiveness of 
nuclear power and its efforts to reduce its tariffs are ongoing. These efforts include optimizing the 
capital cost of nuclear power projects by standardization of design, reducing gestation period, constan t 
monitoring at multiple levels with timely interventions to ensure timely completion of projects and 
having a fair tariff mechanism. 
Recommendation/Observation  
1.7 The Committee hopes that the DAE would be in a position to commission the fast breeder 
reactor at Kalpakkam by the end of 2021.  Even though it would have taken almost two decades when 
commissioning takes place, this is a pioneering initiative of which India can be justifiably proud.  It 
will transform our nuclear energy programme.  In addition , the Committee hopes that the DAE will 
take forward the plans to set up the advanced thorium reactor on an engineering scale, which is the 
ultimate objective of the three -phase nuclear energy programme, as envisaged by Dr. Homi J. Bhabha 
in the early 1950 s.  The Committee also wishes to draw the attention of the DAE to a recent study of 
the Center for Study of Science, Technology and Policy (C -STEP), Bengaluru, which argued for large -
scale induction of thorium -based reactors ahead of the second stage fast breeders for gaining early 
experience with thorium.                                                                                                              (Para 3.2)                                                                                   
Action Taken 
1.8 Owing to its first -of-a-kind indigenous effort towards technology development, the on -going 
PFBR project has been facing both time & cost escalation. The initial delays were essentially due to the 
delays in systems & equipment development/fabrication by manufacturers. This was owing to the need 
for development of the required materials with specific metallurgical compositions & manufacturing 
processes for sodium applications and also owing to the complexity & precision in manufacturing/ 
fabrication of the various systems, structures & equipment (SSE) of PFBR. There have been further 
delays owing to the need for specialized erection & installation methodology/techniques/handling -
equipment for extra -large & slender components, viz. Main -Vessel, Safety Vessel & Inner -Vessel, 
Primary Sodium Pumps, Intermediate Heat -Exchangers, Steam-Generators, etc. Again, during the on -
going commissioning process, which commenced in the beginning of 2015, many difficulties have 
been encountered, due to first -of-a-kind design complexities of the SSEs in high -temperature 
environment.  
1.9 During the past four years, while  commissioning activities of  the various SSEs (Systems, 
Structures & Equipment) of PFBR are progressing, a large number of technical challenges as  well as 
design inadequacies (owing to the first -of-a-kind status of the PFBR) are being encountered at each 
stage, thereby resulting into delays. The initial milestones pertaining to (a) pre -heating (b) reduction in 
the leak-rate of the reactor containmen t building and (c) reduction in the total leak -rate of the reactor 
assembly, required a large number of corrective measures, since there were differences noticed in 
actual field conditions, as against the earlier estimated values. After effective design mo difications and 
in-situ corrections, pre-heating of the reactor assembly could be carried out and the secondary sodium 
loops were started. However, there were a number of failures in the electro -magnetic pumps, which 
took a lot of time to understand and re ctify. Further to it the secondary sodium pump had also failed 
due to discrepancies between theoretical analysis and as observed behavior of the piping reactions.  
1.10 All these could be successfully rectified and both the secondary loops could be re -started in 
March 2019 and operated for eight months.  During this operation of the secondary sodium loops, the 
failure-zones of the electro -magnetic pumps, had been identified and dynamically adjusted based on 
vibration parameters and the performance of both t he secondary sodium pumps could also be 
characterized. In addition, the Transfer Arm (which forms a part of the fuelling machinery), also had to 
undergo certain modifications (since deviations were noticed in its performance during the trial tests at 

4 
 
high temperatures). Further, during this period, a snag had been observed in the indigenously 
developed Large Rotatable Plug Bearing (which forms a part of the fuelling machinery for location of 
the sub-assembly coordinates) and the same is presently undergoing rectification, based on a number of 
design reviews and experiments on a prototype.  
1.11 It may be noted that many of these failures are occurring during the commissioning of the 
equipment/components in the plant, at high temperature conditions, essentially owing to their first-of-a-
kind (FOAK) design. Consequently, all these equipment failures have been a set -back to the earlier 
targets and it is estimated that, typically, two more years may be required for making the PFBR 
operational.  The present additi onal time requirement has been based on the difficulties faced, failures 
encountered & performance deficiencies observed, in the various Systems Structures & Equipment 
(SSEs), during commissioning, which progressively required further design reviews & in -situ changes 
and thereby has been requiring much more time than originally estimated. 
1.12 Presently, testing & commissioning of the various systems & equipment, required for filling 
sodium in the Main Vessel (MV), is underway.  The initial fuel loading can  commence, only after MV 
filling, followed by purification of sodium & commissioning of the primary sodium pumps. 
Considering the number of stage -wise commissioning activities, requiring adequate in -stage trials and 
regulatory clearances, it would not be p ossible to achieve the first approach to criticality (FAC) before 
March 2022. Further, based on the experience feedback of the commissioning activities and the faults 
being encountered, it would be prudent to have a graded approach, thereby providing adequ ate time at 
the end of each step for temperature stabilization & performance evaluation. Such a graded approach 
would necessarily result in increased commissioning time, thereby resulting in this delay of achieving 
criticality and subsequent power operation.

Recommendation/Observation  
 
1.14 The Committee is also aware of the fact that apart from helping India acquire badly needed 
natural uranium from other countries, the Indo -US Nuclear Agreement of 2005 has not yet resulted in 
new commercial projects with foreign assistance.  Negotiations with  the French and American 
companies have been going on for over a decade.  The Committee feels that at this point of time it 
would be better for the DAE to adopt a standardised 700 MW heavy -water reactor and use that 
standardised design for its expansion programme in an aggressive manner.                             (Para 3.3) 
                                                  
Action Taken 
1.15 Following the fruition of the international cooperation in nuclear energy, work has commenced 
on two projects with f oreign cooperation – KKNPP 3&4 (2X1000 MW) and KKNPP 5&6 (2X1000 
MW) at Kudankulam, Tamilnadu, being set up in technical cooperation with Russian Federation. 
Techno-commercial discussions with foreign companies to arrive at project proposals have been long  
drawn involving complex techno -commercial, legal and regulatory issues. In addition to above, setting 
up of the indigenous 700 MW Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor (PHWR) is the primary focus of 
India’s nuclear power programme and is being pursued. NPCIL ha s evolved a standardized 700 MW 
PHWR design and presently six (6) 700 MW PHWRs are under construction. The first of the standard 
700 MW PHWRs (KAPP-3) is poised to be operationalised in the current financial year. Apart from the 
six (6) 700 MW PHWRs under construction, ten (10) more 700 MW PHWRs have been accorded 
administrative approval and financial sanction by the Government, to be set up in fleet mode. Pre -
project activities are in progress in respect of these 10 PHWRs.   
Recommendation/Observation  
1.16 The Committee believes that by the year 2030, India should aim to at least double the present 
proportion of electricity generated by nucl ear power plants.   It is a hugely ambitious target but it 
needs to be planned and budgeted for and achieved.                                                                   (Para 3.4)  
Action Taken 
1.17 To increase the share of nuclear power generation, a large nuclear power capacity addition 
programme is being pursued. The present installed nuclear power capacity of 6780 MW is expected to 
reach 22480 MW by 2031 on completion of projects presently under construction and accorded 
sanction progressively (including 500 MW PFBR being implemented by BHAVINI).  
1.18 The direction from the Hon Committee is noted and all effor ts will be made for efficient 
implementation of the sanctioned projects.

SPECIAL OBSERVATIONS  
Recommendation/Observation  
3.1 Finally, the Committee recalls that the very first Report it submitted on November 11, 2019 was 
titled “An Expand ed Role for the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) in Cancer Treatment in India 
through and Enlarged Network of the Tata Memorial Centre (TMC)”.  The Committee reiterates the 
significance of that Report and would like to express its earnest hope that the re commendations made 
in that Report would be implemented by the Government of India at the earliest. This will call for 
coordinated actions by the DAE, Ministry of Health, Ministry of Finance and the Prime Minister‟s 
Office.  The existing fund allotment to D AE will however be grossly insufficient to meet the needs of 
expanding TMC in various parts of the country.  Further, if the existing funds of DAE are used for 
TMC expansion, it will divert funds from R&D projects of DAE, which is not desirable. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that separate funds allocation may be made to DAE for the expansion of the 
TMC network based on the „hub and spoke‟ model as discussed in detail in its Report of November 
2019.  Unfortunately, the Budget for 2020-21 has not considered these recommendations although they 
were made well in time so as to be able to be considered and incorporated in the Budget.       (Para 3.5)                                                                  
 
Action Taken 
3.2 The construction of 350 bedded cancer hospital at Mullanpur, Punjab is in the advanced stage of 
completion. The hospital is expected to be completed in all respects within the scheduled date of 
completion by 31.03.2021.  A cancer hospital is already functioning at Sangrur in Punja b. The Tata 
Memorial Centre, Punjab Cancer project has grown in stature. It is a comprehensive cancer care centre 
in the state of Punjab having registered over 16,000 patients over the past 5 years. Patients are now 
coming in huge numbers from all parts of  Punjab  as well as from neighbouring states with an actual 
growth of 25% registrations. 
3.3 Tertiary care and private hospitals in Punjab are now referring patients to the centre for 
specialized surgery, radiotherapy and TMC is among the few centres with IHC and telepathology. 
TMC has developed the Cancer Registry Programme and started technician courses in laboratory, 
radiology, teletherapy, anesthesia and operating theatre technology. TMC will now initiate the MD 
course in radiotherapy and Diploma in Oncology Nursing which will enhance the availability of human 
resources for cancer care in Punjab. A prevention programme in the two tehsils of Sangrur and 
Bhawanigarh has covered a population of 2 lakhs. 
3.4 The cancer project will be enhanced with the commi ssioning of the Mullanpur hospital which 
will add human resources and infrastructure to be able to treat more than 20,000 patients annually in 
Punjab taking it towards self- reliance in cancer care. 
3.5 A 100 Bedded cancer hospital at Visakhapatnam, Andhra  Pradesh is presently functioning from 
temporary Porta Cabin. The construction of hospital building and other services building are 
progressing well. 95% of the works are completed and this hospital is also expected to be 
commissioned by December, 2020. Si milarly, there are certain other projects of TMC such as 
Augmentation of Health care services and construction of hospital building at Haffkine Parel, Mumbai, 
Women and Children Cancer Wing, National  Facility for Handron Beam Therapy, Advanced Facilities 

8 
 
for Diagnostics and Services at ACTREC, Kharghar Campus, Navi Mumbai which are in various 
stages of completion are also required to be completed expeditiously. 
3.6 A statement indicating the details of Projects in hand and fund requirement for the year 2020-21 
of TMC is given as Annexure. The availability of fund to the extent of Rs.657.40 Crore is  require to be 
made available for taking up the activities of projects and its completion as per schedule. 
Further recommendation/observation  
3.7 The Committee while reiterating inter alia  the significance of 
recommendations/observations contained in its 325 th Report on “An expanded role for the 
Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) in cancer treatment in India through an enlarged network 
of the Tata Memorial Centre (TMC)” would like to highlight the need for separate funds in the 
budgetary allocation of the Department for the year 2020 -21 for the expansion of the TMC 
network based on „hub and spoke‟ model.  However, the Department has not furnished any detail 
of the action taken by it in the matter.  The Department in its reply has only submitted the details 
about the cancer hospitals at Mullanpur, Punjab and Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh apart 
from sharing the funds requirement for the year 2020 -21 of TMC.  The Commi ttee, therefore, 
would like to reiterate that the Department should take up the issue of fund requirements with 
the Ministry of Finance and impress upon them to consider their demand for higher allocation in 
right earnest and take necessary action in the matter.
