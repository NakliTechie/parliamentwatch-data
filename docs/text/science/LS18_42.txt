REPORT
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the
 Committee to submit the report on its behalf present this 42nd Report of the Committee. The report is based on the 'Mid-Term
 Appraisal' of the Programmes and Functioning of the Department of Space, undertaken by the Committee.
2. In the meeting of the Committee held on the 16th January, 1997, the Secretary and other officials of the Department of
 Space gave an overview of the activities of the Department and the Committee sought clarifications on the various !aspects on the
 performance and functioning of the Department during the year 1996-97 particularly in the fields of Research and Development,
 application of Satellite Technology for Rural Uplift and allied areas and its financial aspects.
3. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the officers of the Department of Space for their co-operation and answering
 the queries raised by Members on various aspects of the programmes and initiatives taken by the Department.
4. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on the 5th March, 1997.
5. The Secretary, Department of Space in his presentation before the Committee, gave a graphic account of the performances
 and achievements of the Department of Space during the year 1996-97 and an overview of the thrust areas in which the
 Department is presently engaged in pursuance of the national objectives of attaining the goal of total self-reliant use of space
 technology for National Development, which has not only revolutionised the present but promises to shape our future. Since the
 modest beginning in 1972 the Department of Space and the autonomous institutions functioning under the Department have been
 making consistent efforts in formulation of policy statements on guidelines on Space programmes which are carried out through
 the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), the National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), the Physical Research
 Laboratory (PRL), the National Mesosphere-Stratosphere-Troposphere Radar Facility (NMRF) and other agencies. Programmes
 taken by the Department are: exploration and promotion of new avenues for application of space technology; co-ordination of
 inter-departmental space programmes and subsequent application efforts including research and development efforts; support
 R&D programmes in Universities/Laboratories/Institutions; support application oriented advanced research in autonomous
 bodies; international co-operation in sharing space technology; increasing role of space technology in academia and industry;
 catalysing space programmes for social development through satellite communication, rural uplift, tribal development, monitoring
 of rescue and warning system in cyclone prone areas, etc.; commercialization of space technology and .services offered in
 international market; providing satellite services in the areas of surveying and mapping, locating natural resources, etc.; and
 support to meteorological users, especially in weather forecasting.
6. The Committee considered the performance of the Department in various fields during the current year and its major
 thrust areas identified for the 9th five year plan in light of the presentation made by the Secretary, Department of Space.

7. With the state-of-the-art technology for remote sensing satellite data applications, drought monitoring and assessment,
 disaster management, wasteland management, water resources management, ocean/marine resource management, mineral
 prospecting, forests resources surveying and management, etc. have become scientifically possible. Thus IRS has been the
 integrated mission for sustainable development. Therefore, it is necessary that the multi-purpose activities of the Department
 should reflect the aspirations of the people. The Committee feels that the space programmes should be more and more benefit
 oriented and the Department should earnestly help to percolate the benefits of space technology to different sections of the
 society.
8. The Committee observes that remote sensing is catching up as a major source of activity for national development which
 was, by the large, until recently a Government activity. The Department of Agriculture, Environment and Forests,
 Oceanography, Science and Technology and Other Departments have been using it for .their individual requirements. There is a
 large magnitude of value addition to the remote sensing data, which can be used in marketable form in several disciplines, both
 Government and Non-Government. Therefore, more and more private entrepreneurs are to be associated in the process of remote
 sensing data utilization programme.
 
9. The Secretary informed the Committee that the development of Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) for
 launching INSAT class satellites which was to take place during the 8th plan, got procrastinated due to a delay in the transfer of
 cryogenic engine technology from Russia. However, with the mechanical mug up from Russia and successful firing of the upper
 stage engine of GSL V, the first test flight of GSL V is targeted for launching in 1997-98 i.e., during the 9th plan period.
10. The Committee is of the view that because of the non-availability of the cryogenic engine technology tile Indian space
 programme has got delayed. Therefore, the Department of Space should take initiative to develop indigenous technology
 required for launching of GSL V.
11. The Secretary explained the question of "brain-drain" in ISRO. In the wake of the economic reforms introduced in 1991-
92 and the subsequent increase in the activities of and remuneration for personnel in the private sector, there has been a flight of
 talent in technical fields towards more remunerative sectors. The Committee was informed that in the period between 1990-94
 about 85 people left ISRO every year. During 1995-96 the figure was 135. Roughly 42% of these people who left ISRO, had the
 computer technology area as their field of specialisation, and 27% of them had electronics and teleco!l1munication as their field
 for specialisation. Particularly the exodus of computer scientists, especially software experts, from various centres of ISRO have
 become fairly significant over the last couple of years. The problem was more pronounced among junior scientists.
12. The Committee finds that occasions did arise when scheduled critical work slowed down owing to 
8 number of young
 scientists/engineers leaving ISRO en-block. The Committee recommends that the problems must be addressed urgently to
 prevent the flight of talent. Government should provide pay perks and amenities that help to arrest the trend.
13. The Secretary also apprised the Committee of the progress made in regard to GRAMSAT Satellite Network Project. The
 concept of GRAMSAT is to promote awareness among the village population through various programmes being devised by the
 local rural development agency. It is a system which is being tested at Jhabua, M.P., aimed at the problems of the local
 population, which is predominantly tribal. The present problems in Jhabua ,concern literacy, agricultural practices, health,
 hygiene and the role of women in panchayat raj. The GRAMSAT project is also targeted to cover atleast 25-30 districts, during
 the 9th Five year plan. The project aims to produce population relevant development programmes.
14. The Committee feels that the GRAMS AT project needs to be made economically viable because the area, it covers is
 mainly inhabited by people having low per capita income. The GRAMSAT project should also draw up a time schedule to cover
 the villages in India for rural development on time bound basis.  
15. The Secretary explained about INSAT- II’s disaster warning system and the role it played during the recent Andhra Pradesh
 cyclone. The INSAT-II is capable of analysing the cyclonic formation as it gradually approaches the coastal area. At present there are
 250 receivers located at various places on the coastal side for the purpose of detecting the cyclone.
16. The Committee recommends that in order to avoid disasters in the coastal region, the INSAT-II Satellite Disaster Warning
 System should be augmented and designed to cover the whole of coastal region in order to avoid further human casualties, loss of
 livestock etc.
17. The Secretary explained that IRS data were being used for various socio-economic development programmes, including
 hydrology, underground water exploration with greater than 92% accuracy and for mapping surface water bodies for optimal
 implementation of irrigation schemes, improving agricultural production, wasteland development, urban plan­ning, environmental
 monitoring, identification of appropriate location for establishing industries, etc.
18. The Committee is strongly of the view that there is need to exploit remote sensing technological capabilities commercially,
 specially In Asia-Pacific and West Asian Region where most of the world's undernourished and underdeveloped population live. The
 Government should explore the possibility of Asian countries using our space technology for the progress of their space programmes
 including launching of their own satellites.
19. The Secretary also informed the Committee that the actual allocation for space programmes for the Eighth plan period was Rs.
 3,160 crores. It is Rs. 11,000 crores for the Ninth plan period, which is between Rs. 7,000 to 7,500 crores at the 1991-92 prices. The
 Department of Space, therefore, needed and enhanced outlay for its space programmes.
20. The Committee is satisfied with the progress made by the Department of Space in respect of the Space related programmes. The

 view that the space programmes should not be allowed to stagnate or suffer. Adequate advance planning in this
