REPORT 
INTRODUCTION 
 The Government of India set up the Space Commission and the Depa rtment of Space (DOS) in 
1972. Department of Space is responsible for implementing the policies framed by the Space 
Commission. These are carried out through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), Indian 
National Space Promotion & Authorization Centre (IN -SPACe) and the Grant -in-aid institutions viz., 
Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), North -
Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC), Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL) and Indian Institute 
of Space Science and Technology (IIST). 
1.2 The Secretariat of the Department of Space and the Headquarters of ISRO are located in 
Bengaluru. The Programme Offices at ISRO Headquarte rs co -ordinate the overall programmes like 
Satellite Communication, Satellite Navigation, Earth Observation, Space Transportation Systems, 
Space Science and Planetary Exploration, Climate & Atmospheric Programme, Disaster Management 
Support, National Natur al Resources Management System (NNRMS), International Co -Operation, 
Capacity Building, Media & Public Relations, Budget & Economic Analysis and Human Resources 
Development. 
1.3 The activities of Department of Space (DOS) are broadly organized und er four umbrella 
schemes namely 
 Space Technology - development of launch vehicles, satellites, manned space mission, R&D in 
Space Technology such as propulsion, navigational satellite systems, indigenization of Space 
materials & components, etc.  
 
 Space Applications - major Projects/activities such as Earth Observation Navigation Missions, Tele-
medicine, Tele-education, Disaster Management Support,etc are undertaken. 
 
 Space Sciences - projects towards Space sciences and planetary exploration are undertaken. Major 
projects undertaken under the scheme include Indian Lunar Mission - Chandrayaan-3, Aditya -L1 
mission, XPoSat, Space Docking Experiment.  
 
 INSAT Satellite Systems - This programme is envisaged to provide satellite based communication 
services in the country. 
1.4 Major Centres / Units of DOS/ISRO: 
a) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, provides the technology base for 
the country‟s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development 
b) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities locat ed at Bengaluru and 
Thiruvananthapuram, is responsible for development of liquid propulsion and cryogenic 
propulsion systems for satellite and launch vehicles 
c) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, is the spaceport of 
India providing launch base to indigenously built launch vehicles.  Also, it provides the facilities 
for processing of solid rocket motors 
d) Human Space Flight Centre (HSFC) is created with the responsibility to act as the lead centre for 
Human Space Flight Progra m. This centre is responsible for the execution of 
Gaganyaanprogramme that envisages sending a crew of astrounauts to space and bring back 
safely to earth.   
e) U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC), Bengaluru, is entrusted with the prime responsibility for 
development of satellites

f) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad 
g) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad is responsible for satellite data acquisition 
and processing, data dissemination, aerial remote sensing and decision s upport for disas ter 
management 
h) The Laboratory for Electro-Optics Systems (LEOS), Bengaluru  
i) ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC), Mahendragiri 
j) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking & Command Network (ISTRAC) 
k) Master Control Facility (MCF), Hassan 
l) Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), Dehradun  
m) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram  
1.5 Organisations receiving Grants-in-aid: 
a) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous institution supported mainly 
by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences. 
b) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL) set up at Gadanki near Tirupati, 
conducts atmospheric research. 
c) The North -Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE -SAC) established as an autonomous body 
under the Department of Space provides spac e technology inputs for developmental activities in 
the North-Eastern Region.  
d) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) is an autonomous body under the 
Department with the objective of creating quality human resources tuned to suit the requirements 
of state-of-art space research programme.    
1.6 Government Companies under DOS:  
a) The Antrix Corporation Limited, Bengaluru, established in 1992, is a wholly owned 
Government Company for marketing space products and services.  
b) The NewSpace India Limited (NSIL), Bengaluru, is another commercial arm of Indian Space 
Research Organization, with the primary objective of enabling Indian industries to scale up high-
technology manufacturing and production base for Indian space programme.  
1.7 Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Center (INSPACe) 
Recently, the space sector was opened up to promote, handhold, regulate and authorise private 
enterprises and start-ups to undertake space activities by creation of an independent nodal agency under 
Department of Space - the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Center (IN -SPACe). 
This will enhance the contribution of space technology towards boosting space economy within the 
country.

3. MAJOR ACHIEVEMENTS IN THE RECENT PAST 
 The Committee, with a view to gather information and the importance of the key achievements 
of the Department, enquired for the same. The Department of Space informed that   the Indian Space 
Research Organization (ISRO) is involved in science, engineering and technology to harvest the 
benefits of outer space for India and the mankind. ISRO is pursuing multidisciplinary Research and 
Developments (R&D) in various areas to fulfil the various national needs: 
3.1 Space Transportation System 
3.1.1. During the period 2020 -2022, the Department has performed nine launch vehicle missions 
including three dedicated commercial missions for NewSpace India Limited (NSIL), a CPSE under the 
Department. In  addition, the spare capacity available onboard the national missions of PSLV was 
utilized to launch 16 customer satellites as co -passengers along with experimental payloads from Indian 
space start-ups.  
3.1.2. LVM3, the heaviest launch vehicle of India, p erformed its first dedicated commercial mission 
for launching 36 satellites for the OneWeb constellation. 
3.1.3. ISRO completed the first developmental flight of Small Satellite Launch Vehicle (SSLV), which 
has a payload capability of 500 kg to planar Low Earth Orbit.  SSLV is designed to be a quick 
turnaround, launch on demand launcher for launching small satellites.   
3.1.4. The margin demonstration tests for the solid, liquid and cryogenic propulsion systems of the 
human rated launch vehicle were also co nducted during this period, thereby building confidence for the 
upcoming Gaganyaan mission. In addition, the Department has developed a Test Vehicle based on liquid 
propulsion to function as a platform for testing out critical technologies such as the Gaga nyaan Crew 
Escape System and stage recovery systems. The Test Vehicle is expected to have its first developmental 
flight in 2023.  
3.1.5. The Department has progressed in the development of engine subsystems for the high thrust 
Semicryogenic engine that wi ll power the Semicryogenic stage for LVM3, thereby resulting in higher 
payload capability. The engine is ready for testing in the intermediate configuration.  
3.1.6. With regard to the development of critical technologies for a Reusable Launch (RLV), the 
Department has completed several ground tests, simulations and helicopter sorties towards the RLV 
Landing Experiment, that is scheduled in the first quarter of 2023.  
3.2 Space Infrastructure 
3.2.1 In recent times, ISRO has realized advanced spacecraft for various applications ranging 
from Communications services, Earth Observation to Navigation. High throughput satellites, 
Earth Observation satellites for day night imaging, sub -meter resolution, multi -spectral 
capability, micro satellites with reduced reali zation time, and navigation satellites for accurate 
Position, Navigation & Timing Services with multiple frequencies. Further to that advanced 
technologies have also been developed for Deep space missions to facilitate multi wavelength 
observations, in-situ scientific experiments, sensors and soft landings, etc. 
3.3 Space Science and Inter-planetary missions 
3.3.1 The Chandrayaan-2 missions has facilitated hydration mapping on lunar surface at extended IR 
wavelength. This mission also achieved the first -ever global mapping of exospheric Ar -40, and global 
surface distribution of Na. Chandrayaan -2 observations have also led to the detection of trace elements 
like Cr and Mn. This mission houses the highest resolution (~0.25 m) camera for lunar surface studies.  

12 
 
3.3.2 The Mars Orbiter Mission, which served till 2022, studied the Martian exospheric neutral 
composition, which expanded our knowledge on the Solar forcing on evening side of the Martian 
exosphere. The mission has also conducted surface studies, including the study of the Martian polar caps 
and their variabilities. The mission is also credited with the imaging of the Martian natural satellites 
Phobos and Deimos.  
3.3.3 The AstroSat satellite, which is India‟s multi -wavelength astronomical observatory in space, is 
credited with several significant discoveries, which include the discovery of Ly -alpha photons from a 
galaxy 9.3 billion years away, solving the puzzle of a source bright in both UV and IR thereby leading to 
the discovery of a hidden white dwarf, discovery of extended emission (~3 times more) in butterfly 
nebula, FUV observation revealing hot stars in Stephen‟s quintet, complementing JWST observations, to 
name a few.  
3.3.4 ISRO has also made the planetary and astronomy data available for public, through its portal of 
the Indian Space Science Data Centre. The data from the Indian space science missions are used by 
several national and international scientists. 
3.3.5 In future, missions are being conceptualized to study the surface and atmosphere of Venus, and 
the upper atmosphere of Earth. Missions are also getting conceptualized in the domains of lunar polar 
volatile research, surface studies, as well as Astronomy missions in UV wavelengths.

4. GLOBAL SPACE ECONOMY – INCREASING INDIA’S SHARE  
 Acknowledging the size of the Global Space Economy to be around $ 350 billion and the 
immense potential, economic and strategic position, that India can attain through increasing its share 
and participation, the Committee sought information from the Department about its vision and the steps 
being taken in this regard. The Committee was informed that through its various ag encies and 
especially through Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Centre (INSPACe) and throug h 
New Space India Limited (NSIL), the Department is working out a strategy for increasing India‟s share 
in global space economy from the current 2 percent to 10 percent by 2030.  
4.2 INSPACe: 
4.2.1 The vision of IN-SPACe is to enable private sector participation in space activities, boost space 
economy of India and develop space force frontiers for a resurgent Aatmanirbhar Bharat. The 
mission of IN-SPACe is to act as the single window agency, to promote, enable, supervise and 
authorize Non -Governmental Entities (Private industries, start -ups, MSMEs, State/Central 
PSUs and academic institutions) in the country to take up innovations in space sector . The 
objectives of the IN -SPACe are to promote, permit and oversee the following activities of 
NGEs: 
 a) Space activities including building of Launch Vehicles & satellites and providing space 
based services as per the definition of space activities legislation. 
 b) Sharing of space infrastructure and premises under the control of DOS/ISRO with due 
regard to the on-going activities. 
 c) Establishment of temporary facilities under DOS/ISRO‟s control based on safety norms 
and feasibility assessment.  
 d) Establishment of new space infrastructure and facilities, by NGPEs, in pursuance of 
space activities based on safety norms and other statutory guidelines and necessary clearances. 
 e) Initiation of launch campaign and launch, based on readiness of launch vehi cle and 
spacecraft systems, ground and user segment 
 f) Building, operation and control of spacecraft for registration as Indian Satellite by 
NGPEs and all the associated infrastructure for the same. 
 g) Usage of spacecraft data and rolling out of space ba sed services and all the associated 
infrastructure for the same.  
 h) IN-SPACe shall draw-up an integrated launch manifest considering the requirements for 
ISRO, CPSEs and NGPEs based on priorities and readiness level. 
 i) IN-SPACe shall work out a suitabl e mechanism for promotion and hand holding, 
sharing of technology and expertise to encourage participation of NGPEs in space activities.   
 j) In order to carry out the space activities, capital intensive, high technology facilities will 
be required by NGP Es. These facilities, spread across ISRO centres, shall be permitted for use 
by NGPEs at a reasonable cost/free of cost. 
 k) The decision of IN-SPACe shall be final and binding on all stakeholders

 l) IN-SPACe will be chaired by a Secretary Grade officer and will have technical experts 
for space activities, safety experts, experts from Academia, Legal & Strategic experts from 
other department of Government of India.

6. RESOLVING IMPORT DEPENDENCIES – ACHIEVING STATE OF THE ART 
INDIGENIZATION  
 Noting that one of the main reasons for reduction in allocation during the R.E. 2022 -23 was 
delayed procurement of critical component due to g lobal supply chain disruptions, the Committee 
gathered information about the key import dependencies of the Department and the steps bein g taken 
for its indigenization as the schedule of satellite projects is dictated by the manufacturing lead time and 
availability of these components since the technologies are available with only a few global players. 
6.2 The Department informed that with regard to space transportation systems, continuous efforts 
by the Department has resulted in a reduction of the import content of 25 -30% in the development 
phase to about 8 -9% currently. Presently, the major import depende ncies are related to electronic 
components and high strength carbon -carbon fibres. With regard to satellite programme, the major 
import dependencies are for sourcing EEE components, space qualified Solar Cells, Detectors, Optics 
and Power Amplifiers. Currently, the import dependency for satellite programme is about 50-55% for a 
typical satellite. 
6.3 Some of the Measures taken to reduce dependency in realization of space systems include:  
 a) Careful planning of procurement activities and some amount of sto ckpiling has helped 
to mitigate the schedule risk.  
 b) Department has a continuous programme for indigenization and has achieved success in 
indigenizing some passive electronic components as well as critical components like processors 
and data converters in association with Semiconductor Laboratory, Chandigarh. 
 c) Indigenization programs for critical technologies such as Travelling wave Tube 
Amplifiers, Atomic Clock, Electronics packaging including LTCC (Low temperature co -fired 
ceramic) & HTCC (High temp erature co -fired ceramic) package technology for RF, Optical, 
and Digital systems, ASICs(Application Specific Integrated Circuits). 
 d) Indigenization targeted for Electro -optical Detectors development using indigenous 
design, RF high power GaN HEMT technology foundry development. 
 e) The imported items are published in the Procurement Live Register(PLR) portal of 
ISRO website to invite Expression of Interest (EoI) from Indian Industries for indigenous 
development. 
6.4 The Committee notes that the current import dependencies in satellite systems are to the 
tune of 45 -50 percent and that the Department is making earnest efforts in achieving higher 
indigenization. The Committee recommends that the Department collaborates more intensely 
with the Indian industry as well as with the Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology in 
finding out solutions towards creating the support ecosystem that enables domestic 
manufacturing of critical electronics components. This shall not only save expensi ve foreign 
reserves but also shuttle India into the orbit of semiconductors and related critical technologies.
