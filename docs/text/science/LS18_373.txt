INTRODUCTION 
 The Department of Atomic Energy (DAE), came into being on August 3, 1954, and has been 
engaged in the development of nuclear power technology, application of radiation technologies in the 
fields of agriculture, health care, industry and basic research. 
1.2  The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) is the apex body for formulation of policies and 
direction of the programmes. The Commission implements its policies and programmes through the 
Department of Atomic Energy (DAE). The programmes of  DAE have been divided into four sectors 
viz. Research and Development, Industries, Minerals and Power Sector. 
1.3  The programmes being followed by DAE, can be briefly summed up as follows: 
 Increasing the share of nuclear power through deployment of indig enous and other proven 
technologies and also developing fast breeder reactors and thorium reactors with associated fuel 
cycle facilities; 
 Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes; developing 
radiation technology applications in the field of health care, agriculture and industry, cancer 
care, water related technology, waste management etc.; 
 Designing and developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, supercomputers, 
advanced materials and instrumentation and encouraging transfer of technology to industry; 
 Providing support to basic research in nuclear energy and related frontier areas of science; 
interaction with universities and academic institutions; supporting academic programmes 
having a bearing on DAE’s activities; and International co -operation in related advanced areas 
of research and contribution to national security. 
1.4 DAE’s programmes are multidisciplinary in nature. For their execution, DAE has evolved 
abroad based organizational structur e comprising research centres, industrial units, public sector 
undertakings and fully aided institutions, along with service organizations. These organizations are 
engaged in research, technology development and commercial operations in the areas of nuclea r power 
and associated technologies, radiation technologies and their applications, basic research and academic 
programmes. 
1.5 The Department is also engaged in the development of radiation technologies and their 
applications for better crop varieties, te chniques for crops protection, radiation based post -harvest 
technologies, techniques for radio -diagnosis and radiotherapy of diseases particularly cancer, 
technologies for safe drinking water, better environment and industrial growth. 
1.6 Over the years, t he Department has been able to establish a network of institutions engaged in 
R&D as well as industrial activities and acquired expertise in all aspects of the nuclear fuel cycle. The 
first stage of the nuclear power programme is in commercial domain and t he second stage has been 
launched with the ongoing construction of 500 MWe Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR). 
1.7 Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) formed in 1987, is a Public Sector 
Enterprise under the administrative control of DAE. NPCIL is responsible for siting, design,

7. TATA MEMORIAL CENTRE  – IN NATION’S SERVICE AS A PRIMER CANCER 
CARE INSTITUTE IN THE COUNTRY 
The Committee, with  a view of highlighting the activities and impact of the Tata Memorial 
Centre, sought a note on the same from the Department. The information received from the Department 
is as below: 
The Tata Memorial Centre, a grant -in-aid organization under the Departme nt of Atomic 
Energy (DAE) is the oldest and largest cancer centre in the country. Registering close to 80,000 new 
patients and over 650,000 follow ups annually at Mumbai alone, it provides high quality cancer care to 
patients from across the country regard less of socio economic status and their ability to pay; more 
than 60% of patients are treated almost free of cost. The centre runs an active Bone Marrow 
Transplant (BMT) program with a 6 bedded BMT unit performing 60 to 70 transplants every year. 
Twenty-five percent of all transplants are funded by the institute so that patients who otherwise would 
not have been transplanted undergo this expensive but life saving treatment. 
7.2 TMC is the largest centre for cancer education in the country and trains close t o 200 students 
every year in various aspects of cancer including surgical, medical and radiation oncology, pathology, 
radiology and anesthesiology amongst others. TMC’s educational activities are affiliated to the Homi  
Bhabha National Institute (HBNI) Mumb ai – a deemed university of the DAE. Besides academic 
courses such as MCh, DM, MS, MD, PhD, MSc, etc. the center organizes various training programs, 
fellowships, and short courses for continuing education.  Some innovative courses started at TMC in 
the pa st 5 years include Masters in clinical research, patient navigation (Kevat) and physician 
assistants. These courses have had major impact on the conduct of cancer research and the holistic 
approach to patient care. 
7.3 The focus of TMC’s research remains t he identification of gaps in cancer management, 
addressing them by conducting high quality clinical trials, and finding cost effective solutions to cancer 
problems relevant to the region. While the Tata Memorial Hospital remains at the forefront of clinica l 
cancer research, ACTREC, the research wing of TMC houses several basic and translational scientists 
working towards unravelling the biology of cancer. The Centre for Cancer Epidemiology conducts field 
studies on various aspects of causation of cancer in addition to organizing regular cancer awareness 
and prevention programmes. 
7.4 Responding to the national need for more centres of excellence for cancer treatment like TMH 
and ACTREC in Mumbai, TMC embarked on a major expansion plan that will quadruple its  patient 
care capabilities as well as broaden its geographic presence in the country. TMC has now expanded to 
six other hospitals located in Varanasi (2), Guwahati, Sangrur, Vishakapatnam, Chandigarh and 
Muzaffarpur. ACTREC, which had 100 beds till 2020, i s now a 500-bedded facility, and will expand to 
900 beds by end -2023, offering cutting -edge treatment with dedicated facilities for solid tumor 
chemotherapy, hematolymphoid cancers management, treatment with radionuclide isotopes, and the 
first Proton Beam therapy unit in the government sector.  
7.5 Starting from 840 beds in 2017, TMC has grown to 2200 beds (in 2022) and will further 
increase its capacity to 2800 beds by the end of 2023, and 3400 beds by the end of 2026. Currently, 
TMC treats about 125,000 new patients with cancer annually (approximately 10% of all of India’s

 The Committee is perturbed to note the low percentage of physical targets achieved by the 
Atomic Minerals Directorate especially under Heliborne Survey and Augmentation of Uranium. 
While under Heliborne Survey, it could achieve only 40 percent, 65 percent and 56 percent of 
targets in the last three year s respectively, under Augmentation of Uranium this figure 100 
percent, 68 percent and 41 percent respectively. AMD plays a crucial role in the front end fuel 
cycle and is tasked with key objectives relating to survey and exploration of atomic minerals. In 
this backdrop, for AMD to miss its targets shall have direct impact on nuclear fuel sufficiency

should take necessary steps in ensuring that the physical targets f or the year 2023 -24 set by 
Atomic Minerals Directorate are achieved.                                                                            (Para 5.3)

second quarter of F.Y. 2023-24 be furnished.                                                                          (Para 5.4)
