REPORT 
The Action Taken Report (ATR) of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change  deals with the 
action taken by the Government on the recommendations/observations of the contained in its 
„Three Hundred Sixty Fourth Report‟ on “Demands for Grants (2022-23) of the Ministry of Earth 
Sciences”.  The Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 
15th March, 2022. 
2. Action Taken Notes (ATNs) have been received from Ministry of Earth Sciences  (MoES) 
in respect of all the thirteen recommendations/observations contained in the „Three Hundred Sixty 
Fourth Report‟. The replies of the Ministry of Earth Sciences have been categorized as follows:- 
Chapter I: Recommendations/Observations which have been accepted by the 
Government: Para Nos.:- 2.3, 2.4, 2.7, 3.4, 3.5, 3.6, 3.8, and 5.2  
 Total recommendations – 08 accepted 
The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry has accepted its eight 
recommendations/observations as mentioned in Chapter-I of the Report. 
Chapter II: Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to 
pursue in view of the Government’s replies: Para Nos.:-3.7 and 6.4  
  Total recommendations – 02 Not to be pursued further.  
 The Committee is convinced with the explanations furnished by the Ministry and 
therefore, does not want to pursue the recommendations further. 
Chapter III:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the 
Government have not been accepted by the Committee: Nil 
Chapter IV:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the 
Government are still  awaited: Para  Nos.:- 4.2, 5.4 an d 5.5  To be pursued 
further 
 Total recommendations – 03 
The Committee desires that the Ministry should furnish pointed and detailed 
Action Taken Notes in respect of recommendations/observations made in 
Chapter IV of the Report. 
3. The details of the ATNs have been analyzed in the succeeding Chapters.

WEATHER BASED AGRO ADVISORY SERVICES 
Recommendation/ Observation 
1.26 The Committee while making note of the efforts being made by the Ministry under its 
weather based agro advisory services expresses its satisfaction towards the overall progress 
achieved against the physical targets for the year 2021 -22.  The Committee, however, observes 
that the progress of the Ministry on many counts such as number of District Agro -meteorological 
Field Units (DAMU); installation & commission ing of Doppler Weather Radars; installation of 
Automatic Weather Stations (AWS); number of Climate Reference station s set up by IITM/IMD; 
number of X-band radars installed/commissioned; establishment of Doppler Weather Radars over 
North Eastern Region etc., has been sluggish.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to revisit 
its performance in terms of realisation of physical targets set under ACROSS Scheme and strive 
hard to improve it. Steps taken in this regard may be intimated to the Committee.                                
The Committee also recommends that climate hazards and vulnerability atlas be prepared 
State/UT-wise for the entire country and disseminated to the farmers at ground level on priority 
basis.                                                                                                                            (Para 5.2 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.27 Establishment of District Agro-metrological Field Units (DAMUs): In the year 2021-22, a 
total of 3 DAMUs were established at Kupwara in J& K and Almora and Pithoragarh in 
Uttarakhand against the target of 20 DAMUs. As of now IMD has established a total of 199 
DAMUs in the country. The pace of growth of DAMU network  was affected due to limited funds 
available for this. Keeping funds availabili ty into consideration, it has been planned  to set up 
additional 100 DAMUs in the premises of Krishi Vigyan Kendras ( KVK) at district level in next 
four years i.e. 2022 -23-2025-26. This expansion will help to improve the quality of weather 
services for farmers by rendering block-level advisories in these districts. 
1.28 Installation & commissioning of Doppler Weather Radars (DWRs): Installations of Radars 
have been taken up on priority basis in Himalayan States. 6 DWRs have been installed at 
Mukteshwar, Kufri, Jammu, Leh, Surkanda Devi and Ayanagar , Delhi. DWRs will be installed 
soon at Lansdowne, Murari Devi, Jot and Banihal top.   
1.29 Initiatives have been taken for Radar coverage of North-Eastern States. Tender has been 
issued for commissioning of 08 Nos. X -Band DWRs for North East region and technical 
evaluation of bids is under process.  Revised tender for 03 Nos. SSPA based C -Band DWRs has 
been issued and technical evaluation of bids is under process.   An expert committee has been 
constituted for eva luation of choice of technology (SSPA or Klystron) for further weather radars 
in IMD. As per decision, tender process for procurement of 08 Nos. C -Band DWRs will be 
initiated. Process has also been initiated for replacement of 04 Nos. S -Band DWRs at Chenna i, 
Kolkata, Vishakhapatnam  and Machhilipatnam. 
1.30 Installation of Automatic Weather Stations (AWS):  IMD‟s existing Automatic Weather 
Station (AWS) and Automatic Rain Gauge (ARG) network comprises of 727 AWS and 1382 
ARGs. Further augmentation of network is underway on a large scale. The installation of 200 
Agro-AWS was completed on 27 th April, 2022. The procurement process for another 330 Agro 
AWS is in progress. Another project for installation of 400 AWS all over India is under process. 
IMD is establishing a network of 10 AWS in Ladakh UT.  Besides, process has been initiated for 
installation of AWS, ARG and ASG in NW Himalayan region, major Urban centres and Schools. 
1.31 Expansion of observational network over North East India: Tendering process is underway 
for procurement of 8 DWRs for NE India. Sites are already selected at Jorhat, Tezpur, Lengpui, 
Kohima, Guwahati, Imphal, Silchar. Site selection is underway at Itanagar/ Holongi airport.  For 
augmentation of AWS network, one AWS is already installed in Guwahati city. 10m mast & 
accessories (15nos) for installation of AWS has been sent to Imphal for installation at Manipur, 
Mizoram & Nagaland. Procurement of AWS/ARGs for Arunachal Pradesh and other sites in NE  

6 
 
 
India is being carried out through rate contract. Besides, 7 AWS/ ARG are likely to be installed at  
Indo-China border during June/July, 2022. 
1.32 Climate hazards and vulnerability Atlas: From the web version of climate hazards and 
vulnerability Atlas, State-wise Atlas has been prepared in soft form (pdf). Already it has been sent 
to some of the States like Maharashtra, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Jharkhand etc. The Atlas will be sent 
to all the regional offices of IMD shortly for onward communication to State authorities and other 
users.

Rs.120 crore was re-appropriated to Deep Ocean Mission vide Om dated 17th February, 2022. Out 
of the total Rs.150 crore provided, Rs.119 crore was utilized by 31st March 2022.  
2.3 The remaining Rs.30 cr ore was for purchase of land in Goa for the proposed marine 
station activities under Deep Ocean Mission. ONGC was approached to transfer/lease out the land 
at the campus of Institute of Petroleum Safety Health and Environment Management ( IPSHEM), 
Goa to Ministry of Earth Sciences. The issue was discussed in the ONGC Board and few queries 
were raised. After clarification and discussion, the ONGC Board approved the lease of land only 
on 30 th March 2022.  Subsequently, a detailed survey of the land allocated by ONGC at the 
campus of IPSHEM, Goa was undertaken before finalization of lease deed. It was found that 
about more than 60% of land come s under steep slope and is absolutely not suitable f or any 
purpose. The said issue was brought to the notice of the officials of ONGC. Hence, the matter 
needs to be discussed and resolved with ONGC on providing suitable land to an extent of                       
40 acres. If not possible, M inistry of Earth Sciences will explore alternative location of Marine 
Station.

AVAILABILITY OF HUMAN RESOURCES 
Recommendation/ Observation 
4.1 The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry is facing challenges on account of 
availability of trained manpower to further strengthen its R&D activities.  The Committee is of the 
firm opinion that even if the Schemes/programmes/activities of the Min istry are adequately 
funded, the desired results cannot be achieved without having trained manpower to take up R&D 
at par with international standards.  Therefore, it is imperative to take necessary interventions on 
immediate basis so that the required man power (scientific/technical/administrative) could be 
made available to the Ministry.  The Committee, accordingly, recommends the Ministry to take 
necessary action so as to ensure that the R&D activities of the Ministry are not halted for want of 
skilled workforce.  The steps undertaken by the Ministry for the recruitment and training of the 
additional 500 manpower may also be intimated to the Committee.                           (Para 4.2 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
4.2 The Ministry has taken up the case for sanctioning of the permanent manpower, as per the 
approved schemes, with Dep artment of Expenditure, Min istry of Finance. In the meantime, the 
required manpower is hired under project mode. 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
4.3 The Committee while taking note of the action taken by the Ministry  desires that it 
should make sincere efforts to complete the process in a time bound manner.  
WEATHER BASED AGRO ADVISORY SERVICES 
Recommendation/ Observation 
4.3 The Committee appreciates the sentiments expressed by the Ministry and the problems 
being faced by it in acquiring a PRV.  It is painful to learn that despite having two stations in

PRV are well understood but the Ministry should not lose sight of its determination of acquiring 
one.  The Committee strongly supports the Ministry in the matter and recommends it to tighten the 
loose knots and make earnest efforts in getting the required funds for the purpose.  The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry of Earth Sciences to take up the matter 
appropriately with the Ministry of Finance to make the funds available so th at the purpose of 
having these stations could be achieved fully.                                                             (Para 5.4 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
4.4 Ministry carried out revised cost estimation as per prevalent market conditions through 
Indian Maritime University (IMU) and arrived at an estimated cost of USD 317 million USD. 
Currently, Ministry is in the process of preparing a Note for in -principle approval of Dep artment 
of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance. 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
4.5 The Committee notes that the Ministry is in the process of preparing a Note for        
in-principle approval of Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance. The Committee 
would like the Ministry to intimate it of the further development in the matter.

Recommendation/ Observation 
4.6 Further, the Committee notes the impressing need for replacement/upgradation of the 
Maitri Station especially in light of the fact that the Station has not only outlived its life and its 
structural integrity is very much compromised which needs immediate replacements but also the 
Station is not environment friendly and the same has been commented upon by various 
international committees, as informed by the Ministry. The Committee is happy to note that the 
Ministry is already seized of the matter and making efforts in this direction.  The Committee hopes 
that the proposed new Station referred to as “Maitri -II” would be completed as per the timeline 
fixed for the purpose and the Ministry would be duly supported financially to meet this target. 
                                                                                                                                     (Para 5.5 ibid) 
Action Taken 
4.7 Maitri, the second Indian Research Station which was commissioned in 1988, has outlived 
its useful life but continues as the mainstay for Indian scientists till date.   It was designed for an 
estimated life of 10 years. Much beyond the duration of the origi nal design, the station has served 
as a permanent and continuous Indian Antarctic base for the last 34 years. It is proposed to
