INTRODUCTION 
 I, the Chairman of the Depa rtment-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and 
Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its 
behalf, present this One Hundred Seventy-ninth Report on the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-
technology on the recommendations contained in the One Hundred Seventy-second Report of the Committee 
on Demands for Grants (2007-08) of the Department of Bio-technology. 
2.  The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology on the 
recommendations contained in the One Hundred Seventy-second Report of the Department-related 
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for 
Grants (2007-08) of the Department of Bio-technology which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on  
26th April, 2007. 
3.  There were seventeen recommendations contained in the One Hundred Seventy-second Report of the 
Committee. Action Taken Notes on the recommendati ons of the Committee were received from the 
Department on 17th August, 2007. 
4.  In the meeting held on 15th November, 2007, the Committee considered the draft Action Taken Report 
and adopted the same.

REPORT 
 The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology on the 
recommendations contained in the One Hundred Seventy-second Report of the Department-related 
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for 
Grants (2007-08) of the Department of Bio-technology, which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament 
on 26th April, 2007. There were seventeen recommendations in the One Hundred Seventy-second Report of 
the Committee. Action Taken Notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the 
Department on 17
th August, 2007. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the 
Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:–  
1. Recommendation  
 The Committee takes note of the gap between the projected demand and the actual allocation made to the 
Department. It is of the view that the Department should make projections that are realistic so that they are not 
reduced at the time of allocation.                      (Para 3.2) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has noted the advice of the Committee. However, the department has prioritized the 
activities to keep the pace of progress within the allocated budget. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and desires that the pace of progress made including outcomes be 
intimated. 
2. Recommendation  
 The Committee desires that the Department should make good use of money allocated for the new 
programmes. It also strongly recommends that the De partment should draw a reasonable time-frame for the 
new programmes and devise a mechanism to monitor their progress so as to prevent themselves from over-
running their schedules.              (Para 3.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The advice has been noted. All the programmes have inbuilt time-frame  and mechanism to monitor the 
progress. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and desires  that the progress made be intimated. 
3. Recommendation  
 The Committee takes serious note of  the underutilization of funds and the consequent time over-runs such 
projects will suffer from. The Committee desires that the problem should be rectified in the coming year as 
promised by the Department.            (Para 3.6) 

Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has taken steps to ensure timely implementation of the projects. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and feels that th e Department give a brief account of the steps 
taken to check underutilization of funds and to ensure timely implementation of the projects. 
4. Recommendation  
 The Committee desires that the reorientation of some of the above programmes, which could not be 
completed, should give them a sharper focus and higher probability of success in the coming years. 
(Para 3.8) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Process and Product Development schemes ha s been reoriented and named as Programme for 
Promotion of Excellence and Innovation which includes schemes on Centres of Excellence and Technology 
Management. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like to be apprised of the e ffect of reorientation in implementation of the 
various programmes. 
5. Recommendation  
 After examining the above proposal  the Committee is of the view that  the above additional requirements 
of the Department, considering the importance of above programmes, should be provided to enable the 
Department to complete its programmes on schedule.        (Para 3.10) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department will make efforts to enhance the provision for these schemes at RE stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would like to be apprised of th e further developments in this 
regard. 
6. Recommendation  
 The Committee congratulates the Department for meeting its targets set for the Tenth Plan and desires 
that the Department should continue the good work in the next plan.        (Para 4.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department is thankful to the Committee for appreciating the effective and proper management 
during Tenth Plan. The department assures to maintain the pace. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 

7. Recommendation  
 The Committee appreciates the Department for establishment of International Depository Authority at 
IMTECH, Chandigarh for patent deposits. The Committee has been informed that it is first such facility in 
India, 7th in Asia and 34th in the World. The Committee is of the opinion that in a world of fierce competition 
where piracy of intellectual property is commonplace,  especially in developing countries where awareness 
about wealth generation through intellectual efforts is low, such facilities should be put to maximum possible 
use to save our intellectual property.            (Para 8.3) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department is thankful to the Committee for ap preciating the establishment of IDA. The department 
is committed to safeguard the intellectual property rights in the area of biosciences. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view that safeguarding our intellectual property will remain an important 
area of priority. 
8. Recommendation  
 The Committee would like the Department to expedite its efforts in areas of cancer biology, development 
of drought tolerant crop varieties and research on tuberc ulosis. While the cases of cancer in the country are on 
the rise, we are still far from being a tuberculosis free Nation. A large section of our people belonging to the 
lower strata of the society, who also happen to fo rm a substantial number of the workforce, suffer from 
tuberculosis. The Committee also recommends the Department to work harder to develop drought tolerant crop 
varieties as Indian agriculture is still dependent on th e vagaries of monsoon and crop failures and consequent 
agrarian distress is a recurrent phenomenon.          (Para 8.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has supported a centre of excellence on research on tuberculosis. Drought and salinity 
stress mostly experienced by crops grown in arid and semi - arid regions are some of the most critical factor 
effecting agricultural productivity. Research over the past two decade has provided a better understanding of 
molecular biology of stress responses in plants. Many genes and promoters have been identified and 
biotechnological tools have been applied to transfer some of these  useful genes implicated in stress tolerance. 
In DBT supported programme on development of salinity and drought stress tolerance in rice, over 900 stress 
regulated gene sequences were obtained and sequenced and specific potential genes have been identified. Rice 
and wheat lines transformed with a number of genes to c onfer stress tolerance. Projects are in progress for 
breeding drought resistant sorghum, pearl millet and finger millet through DNA marker assisted selection. 
Comments 
 The Committee while taking note of the work done by the Depart ment to develop stress tolerance 
in rice and wheat also notes that the Department has not reported any wo rk done in the area of cancer 
biology. 
9. Recommendation  

 The Committee welcomes the idea of capacity building in agriculture biotechnology and recommends 
that the Department should carefully choose such agriculture universities so that over a period of time they 
could develop core competency in this area developing centres of excellence to handle most of the agriculture 
biotechnological research in future.             ( P a r a  9 . 3 )  
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has supported four Agriculture Universities for upgradation research facilities & faculty 
member viz. Punjab Agriculture University, Ludhiana, GB Pant University of Agriculture & Technology, 
Pantnagar, University of Agricutural Sciences., Dharwad and Tamil Nadu Agriculture University, Coimbatore. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and th e action taken by the Department. 
10. Recommendation  
 The Committee has been informed that several technologies for vaccines development have been 
transferred to industries. The Committee is hopeful that such vaccines will soon come in the market and will 
be available at affordable prices to the people.           (Para 9.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 Vaccines for rotavirus, diarrhoea, typhoid and ma laria are presently at clinical evaluation stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view that after clinical trials such vaccin es should be made available to 
people at affordable prices. 
11. Recommendation  
 The Committee is happy to note that the Department has successfully decoded the genome information of 
the rice chromosome number 11. 1t is hopeful that transformation technologies for rice developed for salinity 
and drought tolerance and GM mustard with higher yields is in field testing which showed 45% and 28% yield 
advantages would Soon reach the farmers and help rais e their income lessening so me of the distress Indian 
farmer is presently undergoing.         (Para 10.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 Indian scientists have participated in the International Rice Genome Sequencing Project (IRGSP). The 
Indian scientists have successfully decoded the genome information of the rice chromosome number 11 as part 
of the IRGSP. A network project has been sanctioned last year on development and evaluation of salt and 
drought tolerant transgenic rice. The main objective of the programme is development of rice lines with 
genetically modified stress tolerance serving as pre-bree ding material for transfer of drought and salt tolerance 
trait. 
 The University of Delhi, South Campus (UDSC), Ne w Delhi developed mustard male sterile and restorer 
lines making use of barnase and barstar genes respectively. The male sterility and restorer capacity were 
subsequently transferred to suitable combiners through  recurrent backcrossing for development of DMH-11 
hybrid. The National Research Centre of Rape Seed  Mustard, Bharatpur along with UDSC have been 

conducting the multi-location trials of this hybrid with the permission of regulatory committee to see the yield 
advantages. During the last crop season maximum yield advantage of ( 28%) was recorded at certain locations. 
Further field tests are at more locations worked out. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the reply and woul d like the Department to make available stress 
tolerant rice varieties as soon as possible. 
12. Recommendation  
 The Committee understands the importance of Animal Hu sbandry to the rural sector and is of the view 
that continuous research efforts have to be made to improve the quality of our livestock capital in order to raise 
the income levels of our rural population. The Committee is happy to know that embryos of superior animals 
are being continuously used to pres erve and improve indigenous breeds of cattle and buffalo. The Committee 
desires that the Department should perfect ‘Ovum pick up technology’ making the cost of Embryo Transfer 
affordable.           (Para 14.6) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department is making efforts to enhance the productivity of livestock animals. Emphasis is being 
given to improve the health status of livestock by developing newer animal vaccines & diagnostics. 
Productivity enhancement is being approached throu gh improvement of nutrition and breed improvement 
through various molecular and reproductive techniques. The Department would strengthen and standardize 
various aspects of Ovum Pick up technology for producing a large number of elite animals. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would lik e to be apprised about the progress made. 
13. Recommendation  
 The Committee is of the opinion that the new Gra nd Challenge programmes started by the Department 
which is of interdisciplinary nature should be able to tackle the problem of synergies between diverse areas of 
higher science as scientific research can no long er be divided into water-tight compartments. The 
developments world over in the field of science suggest that the conventional approach of exclusive research in 
clearly defined. scientific areas is no longer relevant. The new target-oriented approach of getting results 
through a combination of inputs from various fields has gained currency. The Committee is of the opinion that 
the above programme should be able to take some of the challenges thrown by higher science.         (Para 15.2) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The programmes to be taken up are: 
 Food and nutrition science and technology 
 Molecular breeding of agricultural crops 
 Molecular breeding of silk worm 
 Microbial prospecting of microbes for industrial, agricultural, environmental, medical and 
therapeutic purposes. 

 Integrated Tuberculosis Research 
 National network for microbicide development 
 Health Science Technology Mission 
- New generation human and animal vaccines and delivery systems 
- Diagnostics for health care 
Stem cell biology and regenerative medicine Bio-design: Implants and Medical Devices 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and the proposed programmes. 
14. Recommendation  
 The Committee expresses its concern over the under-u tilisation of funds allocated for the programme. 
The Committee would like the Department to seriously seek solutions for the problem and draw an effective 
mechanism to monitor progress during the financial year.        (Para 17.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department is engaged in the establishment of biotech parks and incubation centre at a number of 
places in India. These biotech park s and incubation centres provide an excellent template for promotion of 
biotech startup companies. The biotech park at Lucknow  has been established and has attracted several new 
partners in setting up of ventures. The projects at Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Himachal Pradesh and Karnataka 
are under various stages of establishment and likely to emerge as models of Public Private Partnership. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would lik e to be apprised about the progress made. 
15. Recommendation  
 The Committee supports the idea of  lending a helping hand to existing private industrial units whose 
product development is based on in-house innovative R&D. The Committee believes that financial and 
technical support should be given to individual innovators as good innovations many a times come from the 
unorganised sector. The Committee welcomes the idea of creating opportunities for starting new technology-
based or knowledge-based businesses by science entrepreneurs. Such a step can go long way in spreading the 
culture of knowledge based entrepren e u r s h i p .           ( P a r a  
18.5) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has launched the scheme “Small Business Innovation Research Initiative (SBIRI)” in 
September 2005 to boost public private partnership effort in the country in the areas of biotechnology. The 
SBIRI aims to strengthen private industrial units whose product development is based on in-house innovative 
R&D. The SBIRI scheme operates in two phases viz. SBIRI Phase I support is for highly innovative, early 
stage, pre-proof of concept research, while SBIRI Phase II support is for late development and 
commercialization of innovative research leads. The key objectives of the scheme is to nurture and mentor 

innovative and emerging technologies /entrepreneurs, and to forge appropriate linkages with academia and 
government. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply a nd the initiatives introduced and would like to be apprised about 
the progress made. 
16. Recommendation  
 The Committee believes that the Department should engage in maximum possible cooperation in the field 
of bio-technology with other countries in consonan ce with our national priorities. Opportunities for 
technological collaboration in the fields of agriculture biotechnology, disease resistance and finding new 
vaccines should be sought by the Department to find solutions to our problems in these areas.   (Para 19.5) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 Recommendation have been noted. The Department is already engaged in bilateral cooperation in 
biotechnology which includes the fields of agriculture biotechnology, disease resistance in plants and vaccine 
development. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the progress made in the field of agricultural bio technology. 
17. Recommendation  
 The Committee is of the opinion that there is a lot to learn from the developed countries in the field of 
genetic engineering to address the health needs of ou r country. The Institute can play a pivotal role in 
coordinating scientific and technological exchanges with the west so as to obtain vital inputs for our health 
r e s e a r c h .                ( P a r a  2 0 . 3 )  
Action Taken by the Government 
 International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology (ICGEB) has a focused programme on 
infectious diseases, vaccine and diagnostics development in collaboration with both developed and developing 
countries. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view th at more institutes should collaborate in international research 
projects as modern research by its very nature is complex and require varied inputs.
