REPORT 
 The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Bio-technology (DBT) on the 
recommendations contained in the One hundred and Fifty-seventh Report of the Department-related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2006-07) of 
the Department of Bio-technology, which was presented to bo th the Houses of Parliament on 18th May, 2006. There 
were 14 recommendations contained in the Report. 
 Action Taken Note on the recommendations of the Committ ee and further clarifications thereon were received 
from the Department of Bio-technology on 14
th August. 2006 and 25th September, 2006, respectively. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon by the Department and comments of the Committee 
are set out in the succeeding paragraphs. 
BUDGETARY  ALLOCATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes that the Department has been able to make optimum use of the funds allocation in carrying 
out its various programmes/activities. It also notes the assura nce of the Secretary of the Department that the remaining 
amount of the last quarter of 2005-06 will be fully utilized by March, 2006. (Para 21) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department is thankful to the Committee for appreciating the effective and proper management of funds. The 
Department has fully utilized the allocated funds during 2005-06. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and desires compliance. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes the efforts made by the Institute in the areas of stem cell biology, molecular neuroscience 
etc. The Committee is hopeful that the Institute will do its best to develop human embryonic stem cell lines and further 
translational research by transplanting embryonic stem cell lines in animal models of spinal cord and brain injury. The 
knowledge derived from the above is extremely important considering its impact on clinical situations. (Para 25) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 National Brain Research Centre has initiated a major programme with DBT support in Stem Cells, which 
involves both the development of Stem Cells line(s) as well as  their transplantation into animal model with spinal cord 
injury, brain injury and retinal defects. It is held that in the next two years this initiative will provide valuable 
information. The DBT has funded this programme. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like to see that information ge nerated by the National Brain Research Centre 
through development of Stem Cells line(s) as well as th eir transplantation into animal model will go a long way 
in breaking new grounds in the field of therapeutic engineering to cure sp inal cord injury, brain injury and 
retinal defects. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee further notes the importance of research in areas of computational neuroscience, system and 
cognitive neuroscience, developmental neurobiology and basic research towards understanding of neurological and 
psychiatric disorders, as such cases are on the rise in the country owing to the stressful demands of the modern 
civilization which take heavy toll of psychological well-being. (Para 26) 

Action Taken by the Department 
 National Brain Research Centre has initiated a progra mme on neurodegenerative diseases with a view to 
understand both the pathogenesis as well as develop potential therapies for these disorders. The Department has further 
clarified that molecular and cellular neuroscience group is  actively engaged in understanding molecular mechanisms 
involved in the pathogenesis and progression of neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s and 
polyglutamine diseases using cellular and animal models. Several critical partners that mediate break down of 
aggregated proteins have been identified which can be useful as potential drug targets for these diseases. The molecular 
mechanisms underlying the behavioral learning deficit in neurodevelopmental diseases such as Angelman’ s syndrome, 
is also being investigated. Importantly, the neuropharmacological effects of plants that are used in traditional system of 
medicine for improving higher mental function are being examined as potential treatment for senile dementia including 
Alzheimer’s disease. Traditional systems of medicine such as Ayurveda offer a knowledge base that can be utilized for 
development of therapeutic intervention strategies for tr eatment of these disorders. Recent advances in molecular 
biology and neuroscience offer us the tools such as cell lines expressing human neurotransmitter receptors and 
transporters. DNA  microarray technology, transgenic animal  models and rationally defined, validated methods for 
testing higher cognitive functions such as memory are used suitably for assessing the therapeutic potential of the plants 
used in ancient systems of medicine. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinion that  the programme initiated by the National Brain Research Centre on 
neurodegenerative diseases should be  taken in all seriousness keeping in  mind the increasing demand of such 
cures as neurological and psychiatric disorders are on  the rise. The Committee would also like to see the 
optimum use of traditional systems of  medicine, such as Ayurveda and ot her available traditional methods for 
development of therapeutic intervention for treatment of these disorders. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the development of new vaccines is extremely important. New vaccines and 
immunodiagnostic kits will be required by the country to check  spread of bacterial and viral diseases which either have 
become immune to old vaccines or are still un-tackled. (Para 31) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 National Institute of Immunology, New Delhi is working on newer insights/strategies for developing better 
vaccine as well as diagnostics. Third generation JE vaccine using Adeno virus recombinant has shown promising lead. 
New target has been identified for intervention of the growth of Mycobacterium tuberculosis. New strategies to 
enhance immune response specially TH I type of immunity have been worked out that may work towards paradigm 
shift in overcoming resistance. NII has also shown good leads in the area of cancer based on hCG and SPAG9 that can 
be modulated for cancer therapeutic as well. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like the Department to bring JE vaccine to its logical conclusion so that it can 
benefit the people as soon as possible. Preventing and curing tuberculosis is yet an other area where vigorous 
efforts are required as people suffering from this disease come from poor sections of the society, who in most 
cases are dependent on physical labour for their survival which get hindered as a result of this affliction. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee also feels that in this age of market economics, fundamental high-end research being a high-risk 
venture requires participation from industry for distribution of risks between the research institute and the industry. 
Interaction with industry for manufacture of products deve loped from the research leads is extremely important to 

become commercially viable. The Committee hopes effective participation from the industry, which is also in long-
term interest of quality research, will be encouraged by the Department as far as possible. (Para 32) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 Public-Private partnership is being encouraged by the Department. A new scheme on Small Business Innovation 
Research Initiative (SBIRI) has been launched for supporting Small and Medium Enterprises and Private companies for 
pre-proof of concept Research. Joint partnership resear ch programme for product development have also been 
supported with Industry. The Department through NII has on going collaboration with several industries, namely: 
» Cadila Pharmaceutical Ltd. in tuberculosis and immunity related research 
» NPRL, Mumbai on inhibitor design 
» Panacea Biotech in the area of tissue culture based JEV vaccine 
» Dabur on compounds from traditional system of medicine. Efforts to involve more industries are going on 
for early stage tie up. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the effort made by the Department to make research more and more 
professional and increasingly cost e ffective so that money put in researc h projects not only is recovered but 
surplus is generated which, in turn , can be put in other research project s. Institutions of Public-Private 
partnership should be strengthened further so that research becomes result-oriented and does not remain 
merely academic. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee takes note of Studies that have been initiated to understand the process of differentiation of 
embryonic stem cells into cardomycytes and also into dopaminergic neurons which could be utilized for replacement of 
damaged neurons in neurological disorders such as Parkinson’s. The Committee feels that new discovery about bone 
marrow stem cells is of therapeutic importance and can prove important in finding better cure for diabetes.(Para 36) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 Several programmes have been supported to isolate, propagate and characterize the adult stem cells population 
from various sources; to study the process of differentiation/transdifferentiation of adult stem cell population into 
pancreatic beta cell lineage and to understand the mechanism involved in diabetes reversal in-vivo upon transplantation 
of these stem cells.

Comments 
 The Committee would appreciate if the above-mentioned programmes are concluded successfully so that 
the findings can be of use to patients of  diabetes who, of now, have no oth er option but to be fully dependent on 
expensive and constant medication. It would like to be kept informed of the progress made in this area. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee would also like the Institute to make more efforts for understanding the molecular basis of 
.cancer using cell line and animal models as this can help  understand this deadly disease better. The Committee would 
also like to emphasize the importance of studies on sequenc ing of the midgut cDNA library  of Anopheles Stephensii, 
the mosquito responsible for spread of malaria in India as malaria is still prevalent in backward areas of the country and 
still require a lot of effort for total eradication. (Para 37) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Institute has identified cancer as a thrust area and is already developing several cancer animal models using 
transgenic lines from within the Institute and outside. In the 11th  plan, the Institute proposes to convert its activities into 
Centre of Excellence for cancer biology and application. 
 Further Research efforts on Expressed Tag analysis of Anopheles stephensii are being continued. Further 
emphasis has been laid on sequencing of the library fro m uninfected mosquito midguts and construction of Library 
from Plasmodium infected mosquito midgets and sequencing. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the Institute should continue to focus on cancer biology and should 
make full use of available biotechnologica l research in improving cancer treatmen t. It is also happy to note that 
the Department has a plan to conv ert the Institute into a Centre of Excellence for cancer biology and 
application, in the Eleventh Plan. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the opinion that automated DNA sequ encing, comprehensive in silico modeling studies and 
microarray based approaches, which have already been initiated, should be brought to their logical conclusion. The 
Committee also feels that Data bases of genetic disorders prev alent in India and of the pathogen that causes TB should 
be completed as soon as possible as they can serve as an important referential source for further studies and research. 
  (Para 44) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The super computing facility created at IIT, Delhi was made accessible on biogrid lndia and via Internet to 
scientists and students from different parts of the country.  A website of the supercomputing facility has been launched 
(www.scfbio-iitd.res.in). which summarizes the major R&D and other academic activities at the facility. The 
indigenous suits of programmes have been developed for activ e site directed lead design (Sanjeevini), protein structure 

analysis & prediction (Bhageerath) and Genome analysis (Chemgene). Total 25 modules have been developed under 
this project, which include active site directed lead design, template library, molecule generator, drugs like filters, 
docking, structural analysis, energy analysis, binding affiniti es, protein structure analysis and prediction, generation of 
3 D structure, secondary/tertiary structure generation, sampling protein conformational space. Persistence length, radius 
of gyration, hydrophobicity index. Packing fraction, clash removal, scoring function, Monte Carlo optimizer, 
characterization of structure, free energy evaluation an d chemgene. An Inter Agency programme on Tuberculosis 
Research is contemplated. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply a nd would like a report on the progress made in this connection. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that the research programme of  National Centre for Plant Genome Research (NCPGR) 
aims at contributing to the understanding of the structure, expression and function of genes and arrangement of genes 
on plant genomes and manipulation of plant genes/genomes to breed improved varieties of food and industrial crops for 
high yields of better quality products. The mandate of the Department becomes extremely crucial in the light of the fact 
that India is on the threshold of second Green revolution. The technological advances made  in the field of agriculture 
bio-technology should, in the ultimate analysis, translate into increased farm productions, which can raise per-capita 
income in the farm sector. (Para 49) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 Bio-technological methods including recombinant technology, marker assisted selection methods and molecular 
breeding can supplement our crop breeding efforts. The emphasis of the Agricultural Biotechnology research 
programmes is on improvement of yieid in crops through development of resistance against pest and diseases, tolerance 
to abiotic stresses ( like drought, salinity, etc.) and engineering male sterility in them for hybrid seed. In addition, 
efforts are being made to improve their nutritional quality. 
 The Department is already pursuing programmes on development of transgenic rice tolerant to drought and 
salinity, resistant to tungro virus, brinjal resistant to hit and shoot borer, hybrid mustard through barnase/arstar gene 
technology, pyramiding genes for leaf and strip rust resistance in wheat, development of markers for wheat quality 
improvement, biotechnological interventions improving important millet crops, etc. In addition. recently a programme 
on crop bio-fortification has been taken up for improving iron  and zinc micronutrients in our three major staple food 
crops viz. Wheat, Rice and Maize. These efforts are likely to benefit farmers as well as consumers. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view that  bio-technology can play an important role in increasing farm produce 
and thus substantially raise farm income. Agriculture biotechnology can also shoulder the responsibility of 
ensuring food security and the Department has an important task cut out in this respect. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the Department has taken steps to carry toxicological evaluation of 
genetically engineered products and development of protocol s/guidelines to evaluate various GM products for its 
toxicological aspects. A state-of-the-art facility for generating toxicological data necessary for the certification and bio-
safety approval of all GM products and to aid research la bs and Industry in evaluating the biotech products in the 
country has been created. The Committee feels issues of genetically modified seeds and their impact on agriculture in 
general and on farmers in particular are profound and have  to be taken seriously. Environmental issues and other 
externalities have to be taken into account before agriculture bio-technology is allowed fully in the field. Besides this, 

the frequency of toxicological evaluation of drugs, vaccin es, and other pharmaceutical and GM products will increase 
in the future and will require optimum utilization of these facilities. (Para 54) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The toxicological evaluation facilities are created in th e country in the public and private sectors and some of 
them meet the global standards. The Department supports these facilities technically by way of designing the relevant 
protocols to address the biosafety concerns as per the regul atory norms depending upon the nature of the product. The 
Department has supported some of the institutions for upgrading their facilities. The Department directs the 
investigators to develop information on the transgenic cr ops which includes impact on Indian agricultural system, 
farming community and environmental safety before they are recommended for cultivation. The Department will 
continue to provide necessary support as and when required for optimum utilization of these facilities for the safety 
evaluation of GM products in Healthcare and Agriculture. The Department has further clarified that the following 
projects and programmes based facilities for GM detection, testing, toxicology etc. have been supported: 
 1. Animal facility at National Institution of Nutrition, Hyderabad 
 2. Animal facility at DRI, Lucknow 
 3. GM detection facility at CFTRI, Mysore 
 4. Toxicological testing centre at ITRC, Lucknow 
 All these facilities serve the public and private sector institutions for GM food testing and evaluation. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view th at biosafety concerns are of i mmense importance. Agriculture 
biotechnology should not be contrary to ecological principles. Any such  research should take into account the 
long-term interest of farmers as they  are the ones who should benefit from such research and not greedy 
commercial interests looking for short-term benefits ca using externalities like ecolo gical imbalances and other 
hidden costs, which the poor farmers will have to pay later. 
Recommendation 
 In its interaction with the Secretary of the Departme nt, the Committee expressed concern regarding updating of 
curriculum especially of inter-disciplinary courses like those of biotechnology in various Central Universities. The 
courses have not kept pace with the recent developments in the discipline. The Committee feels that there is a gap 
between the target fixed and goals achieved due to non-availability of qualified scientists. To cope with the 
technological innovation and achieving major break-through, there is a need to develop a new strategy which can meet 
the international standards by way of quality training and education. The Committee is of the opinion that the 
Department should moot an idea of creating a pool of scientists in various fields by encouraging scientists not only 
from various parts of the country but also attracting Indian Scientists working abroad by way of special incentives and 
creating good working environment. It also recommends improvement in syllabi of universities to keep tune with times 
along with improvement in teaching standards. (Para 56) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department of Biotechnology has held workshops to consider the curriculum taught in DBT supported M.Sc/ 
M. Tech. teaching programmes and model curricula for M.Sc. (Animal Biotechnology, medical biotechnology, 
agriculture biotechnology, general biotechnology) and M. tech. (Biochemical engineering and biotechnology) have 
been developed and adopted. The Department is formulating an award scheme corresponding to Acharya J.C. Bose 
award and Ramanujam Award instituted by DST to attract NR I scientists working abroad to Indian laboratories for 
specific assignments. 

 The Department has further clarified that that a proposal for National Pool of jobs for scientists has been mooted 
and is under consideration. A proposal for recruiting Resear ch Scientists on a contractual basis under the innovative 
scheme of Centres of Excellence is under preparation. The strategy for the same is being planned in consultation with 
the Planning Commission. 
Comments 
 The Committee is hopeful that th e Department would do the needfu l to bring academic programmes 
relevant to the discipline in tune with the latest developments in the field. It is also hopeful that the schemes 
proposed by the Department would be ab le to attract Indian scientists in the country and abroad to research in 
the country, when implemented. 
Recommendation 
 The problem of inadequate supply of quality researchers for scientific research is dangerously growing to a level 
where it can severely hit scientific progress. Given the fact that metropolitan centres are no longer happy hunting 
grounds to enroll science students as business studies an d information technology provide more lucrative careers, 
potential scientists are to be found in small centres and then appropriately groomed. The Committee strongly 
recommends improved quality of science t eaching in small centres and exposing tale nted students from such centres to 
the bigger world of innovation and discovery. (Para 57) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department is awarding DBT -JRF and DBT PDF fello wships every year to enable students to work for 
doctoral and post doctoral work in Indian laboratories. Curriculum revision exercise and replacement of obsolete 
equipment for better Laboratory facilities, visiting faculty programme are being strengthened to Improve quality of 
teaching, in the Universities. M.Sc. Biotechnology Programme is being supported in about 65 universities. which are 
mostly outside the main cities. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the efforts made by the Department will result in availability of quality 
researchers. It also hopes that such efforts will also help in popularizing science as a career option. 
Recommendation 
 The advent of new molecular and cellular techniques of genetic modifications of micro organisms has led to the 
continuing emergence of products including organisms of bio-technology that promises substantial benefits and 
importance to the quality of life. These techniques are available but to be safely and effectively used, they must be 
applied according to a number of priniciples and with th e support of a National Bio-safety Information Network and 
Advisory Service. The Committee is of the opinion that since newly introduced organisms have the potential for trans-
frontier impacts, there is an urgent need to develop national  code of conduct/practice to establish a general frame work 
and guidelines to ensure safety in research, development,  production, trade and use of these Genetically Modified 
Organisms (GMOs). The Committee feels that with the development of Comprehensive Code of Conduct and 
Guidelines for Bio-safety, the safe application of bio-technology in orderly manner would be possible. (Para 58) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department of Bio-technology has already prepared guidelines for safe use, application and release of GMOs 
in the environment under the provisions of Rules 1989 of EPA, 1986. These guidelines include “Recombinant DNA 
Safety Guidelines-1990”. “Revised Guidelines for Safety in Bio-technology-1994”. Revised Guidelines for Research in 
Transgenic Plants and Guidelines for T oxicity and Allergen city Evaluation of Transgenic Seeds, Plants and Plant 
Parts-1998" and “Guidelines for Generating preclinical and Clinical data for r-DNA based Vaccines, Diagnostics and 
other Biologicals 1999". All these guidelines provide compre hensive code for conduct fo r safety in research and 

development. production, and use of GMOs and guide the applicants to develop Bio-safety data for assessing safety of 
the biotechnology products in orderly manner before they are approved for use by the society. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply.  
Recommendation 
 Allocation to Public Private Partnership has been increa sed from Rs.20.00 crores in B.E. and Rs.16.54 crores in 
R.E. in 2005-06 to Rs.30.00 crores in B.E. 2006-07. The Committee has been informed that the increased grants are for 
a new Public-Private partnership scheme SBIRI that has been launched during September, 2005. The Committee 
observes that the private companies in different sectors of Biotechnology have shown a good response. Since the 
scheme has been initiated only in the second half of the y ear 2005-06, it hopes a much bett er response during the year 
2006-07. It appreciates the new initiative taken by the Department in this regard and in view of its importance to 
promote innovation for product development, this scheme needs to expand in a big way. (Para 62) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The scheme “Small Business Innovation Research Initiative (SBIRI) has been launched during September, 2005. 
In view of the good response received, it is proposed to expand the scheme during Eleventh  Plan. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply.
