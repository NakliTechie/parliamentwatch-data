The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science & Technology on the recommendations
 contained in the One Hundred-Second Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science &
 Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2002-2003) of the Department of Science & Technology which was
 presented to the Rajya Sabha on 7th May, 2002.  There are Twenty-three recommendations in the  Report of the Committee. Action
 Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee was received from the Department on 5th August, 2002.
On scrutiny of the Action Taken notes as received from the Department of Science and Technology, the Committee finds that all
 the Twenty-three recommendations have been fully implemented. 
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the Department
 of Science and Technology are given in the succeeding paragraphs: -
 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS
Recommendation
The Committee would like that the Department should make an effort to reduce the non-plan expenditure by way of generating
 the Internal Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) through training programmes and transfer of technology to the industry etc. so that
 Department would not depend solely on budgetary allocation.
(Para 8)
Action taken by the Department
The Recommendation of the Committee has been noted.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
SCIENCE AND ENGINEERING COUNCIL (SERC)
Recommendation 
The Committee notes with satisfaction the increased allocation for SERC. The Committee feels that there should be a periodic
 review of the Programmes in order to avoid wasteful expenditure.
(Para 13)
Action taken by the Department
The Science and Engineering Research Council (SERC) is an apex body through which the Department of Science and
 Technology promotes R & D programmes in newly emerging and frontier areas of science and engineering. The council is composed
 of eminent scientists, technologists drawn from various Universities / National Laboratories and industries, SERC supports various
 types of activities which include project support to scientists, core groups and centres of excellence, national research facilities and
 training programmes and fellowships.
The performance of the R & D activities is continuously monitored through various Programme Advisory Committees (PAC) and
 Monitoring Workshops. Specific evaluation of sanctioned projects is performed by different core groups composed of identified
 experts. In addition, high value projects are continuously subjected to on-site monitoring by experts in the field. These rigorous
 monitoring mechanisms generate development of appropriate models and methodology for the improvement of the field and avoid
 wasteful expenditure.
 
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
 
OPPORTUNITY FOR YOUNG SCIENTISTS
Recommendation 
The Committee observes that very few talented students are motivated to come forward to take-up R&D as career. It is necessary
 that efforts should, therefore, be made not only to increase the number of schemes/projects, the schemes should also be innovative
 and students should be provided with the state of art laboratory facilities and expert care should be provided to encourage them to test
 their ideas. In case there is dearth of ideas, suggestion could be invited from all & sundry and a list could be drawn up to keep it
 handy for those who need it.
(Para 15)
 
Action taken by the Department
Development and nurturing of manpower for R&D has been the focus of new initiatives taken by the Department recently. The
 launch of fellowship schemes such as the Kishore Vaigyanik Protsahan Yojana, DST-JNC Student Summer Research fellowship etc.
 aim at identifying students at 10+2, under-graduate and post-graduate level and providing them placement in labs under an expert and
 with state-of-the-art facilities, in order to inculcate a motivation for taking research as a career. In addition to these schemes, other
 complimentary programmes such as FAST Track Scheme for Young Scientists, SERC Visiting Fellowship, integrated Science
 Olympiad Programme, Contact programme, SERC Schools etc. also facilitate in providing a thrust for motivating students in science
 discipline as well as the faculty.

Recommendation 
The Committee feels that in order to avoid chances of research being stolen/copied etc. care should be taken by the Department in

 instruments developed should be transferred to industries for commercial production.

 institutions, R&D institutions and industries for sensitizing Indian scientists about intellectual property rights and related aspects.

 and original work in India and elsewhere. PFC has already filed 176 patent applications and registered some copyrights and designed
 as well. PFC also provides support for maintaining patents. As a result of efforts of PFC, the number of patent applications emanated
 from the education sector has gone up considerably in the last few years. This activity would continue in the 10
th Plan.

 Technology and Universities.  The projects are initiated in consultation with the industries.  After completion of the projects, the

PATENT FACILITATING CELL
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that Patent Facilitating Cell should be reorganized into a vibrant nodal and referral agency for
 developing countries in Asia and Africa which could provide consultancy services for industry, universities, R&D institutions,
 government, NGOs and individuals, databases, video and multi-media products, undertake and support research and futuristic studies
 on IPR related topics. Besides, efforts should be made to develop Patent Facilitating centres at state levels in State S&T Councils,
 universities, colleges; industry association for easy access to IPR related information at the local level.
(Para 25)
Action taken by the Department
Patent Facilitating Centre (PFC) has been recognized by some countries as a possible model for them to create IPR awareness and
 provide support to their scientific community for protecting their inventive work. The countries like Bangladesh, Indonesia, Sri
 Lanka and Namibia have already had consultation with PFC under different programmes. The country like Mauritius have also
 shown interest for utilizing the services of PFC. Most of these actions have emanated from S&T collaborations/S&T dialogue with
 these countries. This activity will continue to grow and it is proposed to strengthen this activity during the 10
th Plan and budget for
 the same is being requested.
PFC has already set up 10 Patent Information Centres in the States of Punjab, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Manipur, Gujarat, West Bengal,
 Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh. Steps are being taken to set up similar Centres in the
 remaining States and Union Territories preferably with the cooperation of State S&T Councils. The budget provisions for the same
 are being made during the 10
th Plan.
 
Comments
 
The Committee notes the reply.

Recommendation 
 
The Committee observes that given the recent scare of anthrax and reappearance of plague in some parts of India efforts must be

 diseases.

TECHNOLOGY INFORMATION, FORECASTING AND ASSESMENT COUNCIL (TIFAC)
 
Recommendation 
            The Committee is of the view that effective technology transfer process to industry may also help in challenging the
 technologies coming from abroad. Besides, the technology transferred to the industry is to be cost effective and also retain certain
 safeguards to prevent industry from exploiting the research.
(Para 34)
Action taken by the Department
The department has taken effective steps in smooth transfer of technology to industry and also to make it cost effective. Under the
 technology related programme on Drugs and Pharmaceuticals, industry association is ensured right at the inception of the
 programme. The department contributes towards financial requirements of the institutions and in this manner the
research programme is subsidized to the industry. Industry is given the first option to commercialize the technology.
The department under the programme on Home Grown Technology implemented by TIFAC, also provides financial assistance for
 technology upgradation to the industries. The financial assistance is provided on very soft terms in order to make the technology cost
 effective. Similarly, the newly created Technology Development Board which is under the administrative control of the department
 also provides loan on soft terms to industries to commercialize the technologies.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the department and hopes that effective and adequate safeguards have been initiated
 to prevent the industries from misusing the funds/loans made available to them.

NATURAL RESOURCES DATA MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (NRDMS)
Recommendation 
The Committee observes that even though the country has made technological advances there are frequent cases of landslides. In
 order to prevent this, it is necessary to have a complete hazard zonation map clearly demarcating landslide prone areas similar to the
 one
for earth quake prone areas. Besides GIS data received form GRAM ++ should be utilized for the agricultural development.
(Para 48)
Action taken by the Department
Under NRDMS, a sub-programme on Landslide Studies is operational over the last one decade. DST had supported more than
 dozen projects for preparation of landslide hazard zonation mapping in parts of Himachal Pradesh, Garhwal and Kumon Himalaya,
 North Eastern States, Western Ghats and Nilgiris Hills. In addition to this, close interaction has been made with the other central
 agencies like Department of Space and Geological Survey of India who have also done landslide hazard zonation mapping as their
 in-house R&D activities. DST has also constituted an Expert Committee to oversee the R&D activities. Further, efforts are being

 made to identity the gap areas where landslide hazard zonation mapping has not been done. During Current Financial Year, five
 R&D projects on Landslide Hazard Zonation were supported under ‘Jai Vigyan Mission’ Project on “Natural Disaster Mitigation”.
 The LHZ mapping will be carried out in part of North Eastern States. Apart from this, DST has initiated site specific studies to

Comments

NATIONAL CENTRE FOR MEDIUM WEATHER FORECASTING (NCMRWF)
Recommendation 
The Committee feels that the thrust of weather forecasting should be on its accuracy. The Committee observes that Department
 should consider enhancing the spatial resolution, range and accuracy of forecasts and advisories by constantly improving forecast
 models with use of available satellite data and techniques.
(Para 54)
 
Action taken by the Department
India Meteorological Department (IMD) is solely responsible for issuing weather forecast in different time-space scales. Since
 1988, IMD has been successfully using 16 parameteric power regression model for issuing Long Range Forecast of South West
 Monsoon rainfall for the entire country as a whole.
For day-to-day weather forecast IMD is equipped with adequate infrastructure. Concerted efforts are being made to enhance the
 forecasting capabilities of IMD by constantly updating the resources/technology so that improved and more reliable weather
 forecasts/warnings/advisories could be provided to various user agencies. Recently, Quasi-Langrangian Model has been
 operationalised for tropical cyclone forecasting. It is providing higher resolution outputs and is assimilating the necessary satellite
 derived information. A new workstation “ORIGIN-200” has been commissioned at IMD Hqrs. It has enhanced our capabilities to run
 numerical models whose output for 24 hrs. forecast is now available within 2 hrs. after submission of the input data.
 
Comments
The Committee is of the view that the predictions made for monsoon should be precise and accurate. All efforts of the Department
 should be geared to making the forecasts accurate.

SEISMOLOGY
 
Recommendation 
 
The Committee has been given to understand that exact prediction of earthquakes has not been possible so far. Nevertheless, the
 Committee feels that attempts should be made to use all technological advances available to unravel the mysteries which the Nature
 has so far not permitted the mankind to penetrate into. Perhaps the day might not be far when the Mother earth might relent and
 impart some insight to human beings about its fury expressed through earth quakes. The Committee therefore feels that efforts should
 continue in that direction.
(Para 65)
Action taken by the Department
 
An Expert Group has been set up by DST under the Chairmanship of Prof. V. K. Gaur, Distinguished Professor, IIA to prepare a
 comprehensive document as well as to identify priority projects for implementation under the mission mode project. The first
 meeting of this group was held on May 21
st, 2002 and discussed the various issues. Apart from the several recommendations, it was
 agreed to establish multi-parametric geophysical observatories at a few selected locations in the country. The proposed observatories
 will house the various geophysical instruments, including magnetometer, gravitymeter seismograph, GPS resistivity meter, Gas
 emanometers and ULF/VLF equipments. The data collected by these instruments may help in possible correlation of precursory
 phenomena with the earthquake occurrence.
 
Comments
 
The Committee notes the reply.
