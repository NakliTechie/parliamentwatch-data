REPORT 
 The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Ministry of Earth Sciences on the 
recommendations contained in the One Hundred Fifty-eighth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2006-07) of the Ministry of 
Ocean Development, which was presented/laid to/in Parl iament on 18th May, 2006 . The Ministry of Ocean 
Development has been reorganized as Ministry of Earth Sciences vide Government of India’s Notification dated 12th 
July, 2006. There were sixteen recommendations contained in the Report of the Committee. Action Taken Notes on the 
recommendations of the Committee were received from the Ministry of Earth Sciences on 24th July, 2006. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken 
by the Ministry of Earth Sciences are set out in the succeeding paragraphs : 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR 2006-07 
Recommendation 
 The Committee while noting the reason given by the Mini stry regarding the non-uniform pace of expenditure for 
the third quarter of the financial year 2005-06 would like to know the expenditure incurred by the Ministry on the 
annual Indian Scientific Expedition to Antarctica and whether taking into consideration this expenditure, the 
expenditure in the third quarter is more or less uniform as compared to the previous  two quarters. The Committee, 
however, finds that the pace of the expenditure by the Ministry has not been uniform during the first two quarters 
either. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Ministry must strive for th e regular infusion of funds in various 
projects/schemes in all quarters of the financial year and thereby maintain the uniformity in the pace of expenditure 
throughout the year. (Para 9) 
Action Taken by Government 
 The expenses related to Indian Antarctic Programme te nd to be non-uniform during the various quarters of any 
financial year on account of the following reasons: 
(i) The Indian Antarctic Expedition is undertaken durin g the third and fourth quarters of the financial year 
(typically between December and March/April). A majo r portion of the expenses related to the Programme 
is towards the chartering of the expedition vessel a nd the two helicopters. Therefore, the bulk of the 
expenses would be by and large during the third and fourth quarters only, corresponding to the periods of 
charter-hire. 
(ii) The Expenses during the second quarter primarily relate to the procurement of items for the expeditions, 
while the first quarter expense pertain to the settlement of the expenses incurred during the return of the 
expedition including the charter charges for March/April. 
 However, efforts are made to utilize the funds in a unif orm manner, starting from the fi rst quarter. Over last few 
years the unspent balance at the end of a financial year, ha s been progressively brought down. From this year strict 
monitoring of monthly and quarterly expenditure in various  projects/programmes per the Action Plan with specific 
physical and financial targets has been started.

Research activities during the year 2005-06 and upto June, 2006.

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry and opines  that the Ministry should stick to its objective of 
strict monitoring of monthly and qua rterly expenditure in various projects/pr ogrammes as per the Action Plan. 
It also opines that Ministry must strive for the timely implementation of its projects. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that the reasons advanced by the Ministry for non-utilization of the budgetary support in 
respect of Polymetallic Nodules-Programme, Comprehensive Swath Bathymetric Survey of  Indian EEZ, Gas Hydrates 
Exploration and Technology Development for Exploitation and Acquisition of new Research vessel programmes 
mainly related to delay in acquiring the clearances and finalizing the contract, non-availability of vessel and other such 
procedural delays. The Committee expresses serious concern on such delays and is of the opinion that such delays 
could have been avoided. The Committee, therefore, recomme nds to the Ministry to evolve a suitable mechanism to 
monitor the budgetary planning and its allocations and so as to avoid such delays in future. (Para 12) 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. Slow pace of utilization of funds is due to delay in clearances from various Ministries in respect of 
deployment of research vessels in our various projects is EEZ Survey, Gas Hydrate, PMN etc. Revised cost estimate for 
Acquisition of new Research vessel cleared in December, 2005, order will be placed during the end of December, 2006. 
 The Ministry has initiated regular dialogues with the Ministries concerned to ensure timely clearances. 
Comments 
 The Committee finds the reply of the Ministry evasive as nothing in detail has been mentioned about the 
steps being undertaken or pr oposed to be undertaken by it in the di rection of suitable mo nitoring of budgetary 
allocations. The Committee reiterates that the Minist ry should chalk-out a time-bound action plan for 
budgetary planning and urges the Ministry to uphold its commitment to avoid any delays in future. 
POLAR SCIENCE/ANTARTIC RESEARCH 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the view that in addition to on-going programmes in various domains of polar sciences, 
National Centre for Antarctic O cean Research (NCAOR) should also initiate cutting-edge experiments in such frontier 
areas as assessment of biotechnological potential of Anta rctic microbes, biodegradation of organic- wastes using 
microbial consortium and productivity and ecosystem dynamics in the Antarctic lakes It should also envisage initiating 
meaningful logistics operation with other countries which could lead to operational and financial cooperation.
 (Para21) 
Action Taken by Government 
 In pursuance of the observations of the Committee, National Centre for Antarctica & Ocean Research (NCAOR) 
has already initiated action for mounting cutting-edge experiments in Antarctica in the disciplines of Polar 
Microbiology, biotechnology and ecosystem dynamics in Antarctica in the disciplines of Polar Microbiology, 
biotechnology and ecosystem dynamics in Antarctic lakes. As the first major step in this direction, the following 
studies were initiated during XXV Antarctic Expedition (2005-06): 

 (a) Microbial studies by Nicholas  Piramal Research Centre (NPRC) 
 For the first time a scientist from a Research Laboratory in the private sector participated in the Antarctic 
Programme. 
Following are the specific objects of this particular research programme are outlined: 
 Investigation of microbial diversity with the long-term aim of utilizing them for various biotechnology 
purposes. 
 Isolation and culture of microorganisms from different Antarctic habitats. 
 Phylogenetic analysis and other microbiological studies of the collected microbial isolates. 
 Exploration of microorganisms for the their bioactive potentials in different therapeutic areas. 
 Application of molecular biological tools to investigate the uncultured microbial diversity of the region. 
(b) Antarctic Lake studies 
 Bathymetric survey of Antarctic lake s in the Schirimacher Oasis (by NCAOR) 
 Paleoclimatic studies from proxy records preserved in the Antarctic lake sediments (by NCAOR, BSIP, 
GSI). 
 NCAOR plans to expand on the above studies during the next expedition (2006-07). 
II. Ministry through MEA is in the pr ocess of entering into collaboration on joint research in the polar Science, 
through other countries working in the Antarctica and Polar Science. 
Comments 
 The Committee expresses its satisfaction on the sustained efforts on the part  of the Ministry in the field of 
Polar Science and desires that these on-going programmes should be regularly monitored. The Committee also 
hopes that Ministry would keep enhancing its performance in various studies on regular basis. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the view that the National Centre  for Antarctic Ocean Research (NCAOR), being the nodal 
organization to execute the Antarctic Programmes should strive to further expand its infrastructure facilities for 
carrying out research in the realms of Polar Sciences and Southern Ocean Oceanography. (Para 22) 
Action Taken by Government 
(i) National Centre for Antarctica & Ocean Research (NCAOR) has already initiated action for augmenting its 
existing infrastructure for catering to the research activities in the frontier domains of Polar Sciences and 
Southern Ocean Oceanography. Following the commissio ning of the unique ice-core archival processing 
and analytical facilities at the Centre, the Centre is now fully equipped to undertake precise chemical 
analyses of ice cores collected from Antarctica. In addi tion to the state-of-the-art  laboratory, equipment for 
undertaking analyses of ice samples. NCAOR has estab lished the requisite facilities (such as microscopes, 
SEM, wet geochemistry laboratory, stable isotope ma ss spectrometer, etc.) for carrying out analyses of 
water and sediment samples from the Southern Ocean and Antarctic lakes as well. In continuation of the 
Pilot Expedition mounted by NCAOR to the southern Ocean during 2003-04, a special multi-disciplinary 
expedition to the Southern Ocean realm was undertaken under the aegis of NCAOR during 2005-06, 
dovetailing the studies with the activities in the Larsernann Hills. 

(ii)  To further strengthen infrastructure at Maitri Station, NCAOR has already sent 10 living modules, and 3 
toilet modules with the XXV Indian Antarctic Expedition in December, 2005. 
Comments 
 The Committee Commends the initiative  being undertaken by the Ministry  in augmenting its existing 
infrastructure for catering to the res earch activities in the domains of Polar Sciences and Southern Ocean 
Oceanography. Besides, the Ministry sh ould ensure that all the existing technological facilities are utilized to 
their utmost capacity.

OCEAN OBSERVATION AND INFORMATION SERVICES 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes with satisfaction that the programmes under the Ocean Observation Information Service 
have been progressing in the right track. However, the Committee feels that the National Centre for Ocean Information 
Services (INCOIS), being the no dal agency for providing coastal and ocean da ta and value added products to the users 
should monitor its performance and work-mechanism to process and disseminate ocean data and data products to user 
communities like fishries, weather, climate, ports, shipping etc. on a regular basis so that ocean weather forecasting is 
timely and precise. The Committee hopes that the High Pe rformance Computing system at INCOIS, Hyderabad would 
be in place at the earliest for which a budgetary allocation of Rs. 10 crores has been made for the year 2006-07. (Para 
31) 
Action Taken by Government 
 INCOIS activities, ocean information and advisory services and their performance are being monitored 
periodically by the Governing Council and Finance Committee. Various high level Committees (Project Management 
Council/Committees, Steering Committee and Expert Committ ees) are in place for reviewing and monitoring the 
progress of INCOIS programmes. 
 The Progress of the projects is also reported to the Planning Commission on halfyearly basis for monitoring 
performance. Further, the Research Advisory Committee (R AC) was constituted recently to monitor the progress and 
provide advice on the activities of INCOIS. For the High Performance Computer, a Technical Committee has been 
constituted to (i) draw specifications, (ii) prescribe the method of procurement, (iii) evaluation of technical and 
financial bids and (iv) evaluate the systems and furnishing recommendations for acceptance. 
Comments 

 The Committee appreciates that the Ministry has undert aken various measures to review and monitor the 
activities and performance of INCOIS. However, it  has not mentioned any steps being undertaken to 
disseminate ocean data and data products to user communities. The Committee wishes that the sustained efforts 
of the Ministry in the direction of  attaining the High Performance Computer are not get entangled in 
administrative and procedural delays and feels that some target date shou ld be fixed for completing the task 
undertaken on time, so that it may not lose importance and urgency that is called for. 
MARINE RESEARCH AND TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT 
Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that the efforts of the Ministry  to monitor pollution in the coastal areas on vigorous 
basis should not be restricted to hot spots only. The Ministry should keep on identifying locations for periodical 
monitoring alongwith stipulated parameters for diffusion and dispersion characteristics. Besides, the samples collected 
at these locations should be forwarded to State Pollution Control boards for taking remedial measures.(Para 37) 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendations of Committee are well taken and the Ministry will identity new locations for periodical 
monitoring under COMAPS programme. 
 Identification of locations for periodical monitoring is a continuous process and the locations for monitoring are 
being continuously updated based on the results obtained from the previous years. When the pollution levels of a 
particular location other than hotspots are found to be incr easing, these locations will be brought under the category of 
periodical monitoring. The results obtained from such locations will also be sent to the State Pollution Control Boards 
apart from the results being referred to these. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would like that it be informed about the action taken by the State 
Pollution Control Boards. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the Ministry should also focus its efforts in assisting Universities to create 
infrastructure facilities and building up a skilled human resource base in Marine Sciences. Besides, the Ministry should 
also participate and showcase its activities in national and international level exposition, exhibition, fairs etc. from time 
to time. Inter-active computer generated information on O cean Science & Technology could perhaps help educate the 
public on the ocean and its resources. (Para 38) 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Ministry has established basic centralized facilities, instruments and equipment, viz. and requisite 
infrastructure for Marine Scie nce under Ocean Science & Technology, Cells (OSTCs) in various Universities/Institute 
by providing funding support to about 50 research project under which over 100 research personnel & about 40 
fellowships given in the last 5 years. These OSTCs are enc ouraged to initiate special academic & technical courses and 
Andhra University and Annamalai University have already st arted such courses in ‘Coastal Aquaculture’ and ‘Ocean 
Science and Technology’. Also the Mi nistry has its own Institutes. The Na tional Institute of Ocean Technology, 
Chennai, the National Centre for Antarctic and Ocean Re search, Goa and the Indian National Centre for Ocean 
Information Services, Hyderabad where marine science studies are pursued. 
 The Ministry has been participating in the important National & International exhibitions since 1984 onwards. It 
had a permanent exhibition stall at Pragati Maidan, New Delhi till 1994 and had been displaying its achievements of 
that year. 

 During the year 2005-06 the activities of the Ministry have been displayed on Tsunami early warning system, 
Desalination Plant to produce one lakh liter freshwater per day at Lakshadweep, Antarctica Programmes, Argo-gloats, 
data Buoy & its Position, Potential Fishing Zones Ocean In formation Services, Fleet of Research Vessels, Drugs from 
the Sea, Continental Shelf, Gas Hydrates, Inter National Co operation, Ocean Science & Technology Cell and Centre of 
Excellence in Universities/IITs, exploitation of Nodules from sea bed, extraction of Metals from Pilot plant, computer 
based Ocean Quiz etc; through Panels, Models, Translights, films, Videos etc. 
 This Ministry has. participated in one International Exhibition in China, two International Exhibitions in India, 
one Science Congress, three Rural Exhibition, two Scientific Education Exhibitions and one Technology Exhibition. 
 In addition, the Ministry has been supporting to the National and International seminars, symposia, Workshop, 
training programmes etc. on topics concerned to Ocean Science & Technology to create an Environment for bringing 
various people for exchange of views and also to create scientific temperament amongst the public and school children. 
During 2005-06, fifty three Seminar/symposium etc. were supported. 
Comments 
 The Committee appreciates the Ministry’s participatio n in various national and international exhibitions. 
Besides, the Committee recommends that apart from Andhra and Annamalai Universities, the Ministry may try 
to further expand its association with other universities as well through its Ocean Science & Technology Cells in 
the near future. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that the sea’s potential as a medicine storehouse remains largely in the realm of 
experimentation. It commends the Ministry’s ongoing efforts towards transferring the technology to pharmaceutical 
industry for commercialization of the products. The Committ ee recommends that under this programme, the extraction 
and development of other potential drugs and chemicals from the depth of the o cean should be the priority of the 
Ministry. (Para 39) 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Ministry has already initiated action towards extraction and development of other potential drugs in a 
focused manner on a priority basis. 
 The Ministry has already initiated action for extraction & development of other Potential drugs (such as Cancer, 
anti HIV, etc.) through the participation of other institutes/organizations including Dabur Research Foundation, 
Ghaziabad, U.P. 
 Also for the first time samples have also been collected from Antarctica for screening organisms for 
Drug/Chemicals. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry and would like to receive a status report of the action taken 
regarding potential drugs from the ocean. 
NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF OCEAN TECHNOLOGY (NIOT) 
Recommendation 
 The Committee commends the efforts of the Ministry on the successful commissioning of the first ever Low 
Thermal Desalination Plant in Kavaratti, Lakshadweep by National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT). The 
Committee, however feels that despite above initiatives by the Ministry, there is still enough scope left to commission 

such Plants in other coastal areas of the country. The Committ ee, therefore, feels that it should be one of the prioritized 
areas of the Ministry. (Para 43) 
Action Taken by Government 
 National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT) is continuing its efforts towards desalination. 
 The Lakshadweep administra tion would like to install Low, Temperature Thermal Desalination Plants (LTTD) in 
eight more islands with the help of NIOT which will be taken up shortly. 
 The location identified by th e Administration for proposed  setting lip of LTTD  plants in the eight islands of 
Lakshadweep are (1) Minicoy; (2) Andrott, (3) Amini; (4) Agatti, (5) Kalpeni, (6) Kadmat; (7) Kitan;  
(8) Chetlat. 
 Regarding setting up of the plants in other coastal areas, development of higher capacity desalination plant which 
is to be set up on a barge, is underway. An LTTD barge m ounted plant of 1 million lt/d ay capacity plant has been 
designed, developed and demonstrated on experimental basis. After successful operational demonstration of such high 
capacity plant, the Ministry would consider the request, if any, from the coastal States for setting up of this LTTD plant 
depending on the feasibility 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and commends the efforts of the Ministry in iden tifying eight more islands 
in Lakshadweep for installation of Low Temperatu re Thermal Desalination Plan ts after the successful 
installation of LTTD Plant in Kavaratti and hopes that success would be achieved in these islands too. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC) has a rich potential to provide 
pollution free renewable energy to meet the power needs of coastal region as well as is land groups. Therefore, it 
recommends to the Ministry to build energy plants of higher capacity as this might lessen the demand on fossil fuel for 
power generation. (Para 44) 
Action Taken by Government 
(i)  OTEC technology is not yet ready for commercial exploitation Technology for OTEC plant is yet to be 
demonstrated economically. 
(ii)  At this stage National Institute of Ocean Tech nology (NIOT) is trying to combine desalination with 
renewable energy like OTEC so as to make desalination cost effective Efforts are on in this direction. 
Comments 
 The Committee urges the Ministry to continue its efforts in this direction as OTEC is a very promising 
alternative energy source for island co mmunities and could provide them wi th much-needed power as well as 
desalinated water and a variety of mari-culture products. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee would like the National  Institute of Ocean Technology (NIO T) to ensure that its activities 
especially in the area of Deep Sea Mining should not pollu te the ocean floor and depletion of fish stocks from their 
habitats, which could affect the fishing communities. (Para 45) 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. 

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry. 
COASTAL RESEARCH VESSELS 
Recommendation 
 The Committee would like to suggest that since the assessment of the sea pollution and subsequent action plan 
for dealing with the level of pollution is extremely importa nt from the point of view of maintaining an optimal sea 
ecology, it is necessary that in-depth re search should be undertaken for a comp lete monitoring of the ocean pollution 
and initiating subsequent steps to control the same. The Co mmittee, therefore, strongly advocates the Ministry to 
initiate comprehensive programme under ocean pollution studies and control. (Para 47) 
Action Taken by Government 
 Ministry of Earth Sciences being the scientific organization, monitors systematically in a comprehensive manner 
the health of coastal waters and generate data on 25 parameters related to physical, chemical biological and 
microbiological characteristics including pesticide residues in sea food. The information on the health of coastal waters 
are periodically provided to the concerned coastal State Po llution Control Boards and Central Pollution Control Board 
for mitigation measures if, any. 
Comments 
 The Committee observes that co-ordination between th e Central and State Pollut ion Control Boards for a 
continued monitoring of marine pollution levels be utilized to the fullest extent to achieve the desired results.

GAS-HYDRATE EXPLORATION AND TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT FOR EXPLOITATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the view that Fossil Fuel reserv es are continuously depleting and will not last long. 
However, Gas Hydrates have the potential of providing energy security to the country, as it is an alternative source of 
energy. The Committee is however , constrained to observe that budget that was allocated under this programme was 
not fully utilized. The Ministry should encourage excellence in national laboratories to focus on niche areas of hydrate 
research with special emphasis on re source extent evaluation and environmental impacts and development of 

technology for detection of gas hydrates in sediments. The Committee, therefore, feels that this programme should be a 
high priority area for the Ministry in the years to come. (Para 52) 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. Slow pace of project implementation is due to no n-availability of defence clearances for deployment of 
chartered research vessels in our EEZ for assessing the resource extent. The Mi nistry is pursuing the R&D endeavour 
on resource extent evaluation and environmental impact and development of technology for detection of gas hydrated 
in sediments with participation of National Institutes like NIO, Goa, NGRI, Hyderabad, NIOT, Chennai. 
 The Ministry gives the highest priority to this research endeavour however pending due clearances from Ministry 
of Defence has slowed the pace of this project. 
Comments 
 The Committee is distressed to note the half-hearted  approach of the Ministry towards such a major 
programme and expresses serious concern on delays in ac quiring clearances from the Ministry of Defence. It 
feels that the Ministry could have pl anned its programme in a manner that it would have well taken in advance 
the time taken for such administ rative matters and could have av oided such slow pace of project 
implementation.

Comments
