REPORT 
The Department of Ocean Development, since its inception in July, 1981, has directed its 
programmes towards development and use of science and technology for the sustainable and 
environment friendly exploration and exploitation of marine resources for the socio­
economic benefit of the country. Its technology development programmes are for future 
application~ with strategic, industrial and economic significance. 
2. The Department of Ocean Development is a nodal Department for orgamzmg, 
coordinating and promoting ocean development activities in the country. The national level 
R&D laboratories, educational institutions and industries assist the Department in carrying 
out its multi-disciplinary and multi-institutional plans and programmes of national 
importance. Further, marine research and capacity building is one of the key areas of the 
Department to promote basic research in marine science for self-reliance. 
3. The programmes of the Department which have been reoriented and revamped during 
the Ninth Five Yeur Plan period cover Polar (Antarctic) Science, Marine Living Resources, 
Marine Non-Living Resources, Ocean Observation & Information Services, Marine and 
Coastal Area Management, Marine Research and Capacity Building, Coastal Community 
Programmes, Ocean Awareness and International Cooperation. It has been stated that while 
formulating the research and development programmes, stress is laid on accelerated 
development of applied technology so that benefits continue to accrue to the society.

NON-PLAN EXPENDITURE 
8. The Non-Plan expenditure broadly falls under four heads, namely (i) ORY Sagar 
Kanya; (ii) FORV Sagar Sampada; (iii) Secretariat and Economic Services; and 
(iv) Administrative infrastructure. The major increase in the Non-Plan expenditure is against 
ORV Sagar Kanya which was acquired in 1984. It is a sophisticated research vessel which 
has been carrying out survey and exploration of marine non-living resources particularly in 
the field of physical and chemical oceanography, marine biology, meteorology, marine 
geophysics, marine geology, etc. It provides a common platform to the National Institute/ 
Laboratories in the field of Oceanographic research. 
9. The Committee was informed that during 1998-99 the ORY Sagar Kan ya conducted 
13 scientific cruises both in the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal. The scientific investigations/ 
research carried out by ORY Sagar Kanya during 1998-99 include data collected from sea 
for validating satellite derived oceanic parameters; deployment, retrieval and servicing of the 
shallow and deep water data buoys in the Arabian Sea under the National Data Buoy 
Programme; bathymetric data collection in Arabian Sea for delineation of outer limits of 
continental shelf; multidisciplinary experiments under the INDOEX programme; BOBMEX 
pilot project in the Bay of Bengal for Monsoon Experimental studies; trial testing of various 
sub-systems of the crawler at shallow depth; and Lar}d Ocean Interaction in Coastal Zone 
(LOICZ) studies for comprehending climate change induced reactions in the coastal regions. 
10. The Secretary, DOD, informed the Committee that an enhanced budget provision in 
the Non-Plan sector would be required for operation and maintenance of ORV Sagar Kanya 
and FORV Sagar Sampada which were about 15 years old necessitating replacement of both 
the deck machinery and other essential equipment/instruments. The allocation of Rs. 9.48 
crore and Rs. 7.50 crore made for operation and management of ORV Sagar Kanya and 
FORV Sagar Sampada respectively during 1999-2000 is the barest minimum as against the 
required amount of Rs. 43.00 crore. This has resulted in accumulation of huge amounts of 
payments over the years to be made to Shipping Corporation of India, which run s the 
vessels. 
11. Th~ task of survey is to locate and map the resource potential of living and non-living

station , Dakshin Gangotri was commissioned in 1983 and replaced by the indigenously 
designed second Indian permanent station 'Maitri' in the Schirmacher Oasis in 1988. The 
Station 'Maitri' has adequate infra-structural facilities for conducting multi-faceted scientific 
research in Polar (Antarctic) Science::. While using An~m:tic a!\ a platform for conducting

naturo.: of thi!\ remote and unique continent. In order to uphold thi!> commitment, India, an

sciem:t:, including an expedition to the weedell Sea and one for assessment of Antarctic 
Krill. During the year under reference the broad scientific and logistic activities planned to 
be undertaken are in continuation of studies on ozone, trace gases and aerosols of the 
Antarctic atmosphere using various state-of-the-art equipment; archival of meteorological 
parameters that can be employed for climatic modeling and prediction; experiments on solat 
terrestrial phenomenon like optical aurora and geo-magnetic perturbations; studies on 
adaptation techniques of human beings in adverse conditions; application and utiliz<ltioh of

DRUGS FROM THE SEA 
18. The Na tional Project on "Drugs from the Sea" initiate d during 1990-91 is a multi­
institution al proje ct on devel opment of p oten tial dru gs and chemicals from marine organisms 
which was funded under R & D prog ramme till the end of Phase- 11 (1995-96). The 
Comm ittee was informe d tha t during the period 1998-99, 15 new marine flora and .53 fauna 
were collected for general biologica l s creening. I n order to confi rm biological activity and 
follow-up stud ies, repeat collect ion of 7 6 flora and 40 fauna was made which has yielded 
valuab le clues for develop ing potent dru gs. 
19. T he Depa rtment propose d an outlay of Rs. 3.00 crore for 1999-2000, it was reduced 
to Rs.1.60 crore due to financial crunch. The project has completed Phase-I II . It will enter 
the crucial Phase- IV from 1999-2000 onwards. In Phase-III, the resea rch activ ities have led 
to ide ntification of five marine organisms having potent ial properties as anti-d iabetic, anti­
viral, anti-anxiety, anti-cholesterol and larvicidal agents for develop ing new drugs du ring the 
Ninth Plan period. In Phase -IV, steps would be taken to investigate further and release new 
drugs developed from marine flora and fauna under Phase- Ill. Chemical trials are expected 
to be complete for anti-diabetic (CDR -134) drug. It has now been observed that the same 
lead has antidiabetic as well as anti-diarrhoeal propert ies. The Drug is as good as the 
standard antidiabetic drug like Phonformin and better than Tolbutamide. Also commercial 
work for patenting anti-cholesterol lead would be initiated. 
20. While the Committee appreciates the work being done by the Department in 
developing the drugs from Sea, it observes that a large number of multinational as well as 
Indian companies are engaged in researc h in this field in different systems of medicines. 
Medicines coming to market from these companies have largely a profit motive at th~ir back. 
The Committee therefore feels that a mere addition of a drug in the market would not serve 
the purpose. The country has a large potential of resources, be they in the form of herbs 
grown in forests or in other forms of natural deposits in the vast sea bed. A large number of 
diseases should be curable once for all. It should also be possible ~0 prevent the diseases by 
either preserving the natural force in the body or by restoring it with the help of medicines, 
as Hahneman, the founder of Homoeopathy put it. Our belief in Hahneman's principle of 
Similia Similibus Curenter (i.e. likes are treated by likes) is further strengthened because of 
the vast amount of natural resources available in our country. The Committee, therefore, 
observes that whatever number of drugs are taken up for research and development they 
should aim at excellence by curing the diseases once for all. The programme on research for 
these medicines should be time bound, well-knit to avoid delays and should not he allowed to 
suffer for want of funds otherwise it may lose its significance in the fast changing world. 

POLYMETALLIC NODULES PROGRAMME (PNP) 
21. In 1987, India was accorded the status of Registered Pioneer Investor and started 
the Polymetallic Nodules Programme (PNP). Considerable progress has since been made in 
the country in resource identification and evaluation, technology and manpower 
development towards exploitation of these resources. However, presently, this programme 
has been expanded to develop technologies for deep-sea mining and metallurgy for recovery 
of metals, etc . 
22. The major activities proposed to be undertaken during the current financial year are 
Survey & Exploration in the Central Indian Ocean Basin (CIOB) for spot sampling of 
nodules using grab sampler and updating of geo-statistical resource evaluation in the pioneer 
areas, ·Benthic disturbance and impact assessment studies in the Indian Pioneer Area', 
prepared on baseline benthic conditions. Besides, under Technology Development (Mining) 
programme activities would be undertaken for demonstrating shallow bed mining system 
along the coast. Also the work relating to development of manipulator for underwater 
operation and testing of materials for deep-sea operation would be undertaken including 
initiating a joint programme between NlOT and Russian Academy of Science, Moscow for 
design, development and testing of unmanned submersibles operational unto 6000 meter 
depth. An outlay of Rs.8.35 crore has been provided for this programme. 
23. Also initiatives will be made under Technology Development (Metallurgy) for 
carrying out demonstration campaigns to validate the data obtained from earlier campaigns 
and designing and development of semi-continuous demonstration pilot plant of 500 kg/day 
capacity at Hindustan Zinc Ltd. (Udaipur). Due to resource limitation, financial outlay for 
the Pilot Plant is kept at Rs.5.15 crore with a request to HZ Ltd. to advance the money and 
go ahead with the project. However, a final understanding is yet to be reached since the 
matter is under consideration of HZ Ltd. Thus, the Department will have to meet the entire 
expenditure of HZ Ltd. subject to the ceilings for which EFC approval exists. The activities 
proposed to be undertaken during the current year are part of the long-term activities 
planned under the programme fot which the tot ·1l financial outlay is Rs.14.05 crore. 
24. The Committee was apprised that Polymetallic Nodule Programme is still in 
experimental stage. Therefore, commercial exploitation of polymetallic nodules may not be 
feasible for some more time. However , the technology for exploration and exploitation has 
been demonstrated. Thus, development of technologies for nodule mining purposes for the 
deep sea may be viewed as contribution to a long term objective . 
25. The Committee observes that the long gestation period, rapidly changing technology, 
economic priorities of the countries and economic importance of the material likely to be 
extracted as also the cost of extraction are some of the factors which are liable to affect the 
viability of the programme. A critical assessment of all these factors was necessary before w 
planning the programme. At this stage, the Committee feels that the programme having · 
commenced, it should not be allowed to suffer because of the reduction in the budgetary 
support for such a reduction would disrupt continuity, may render some of the assets idle and 
thus, in turn, push up the cost of the . project.

ISLAND DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME 
44. ln the Ninth Five Year Plan, the Island Development Programme envisages to give 
impetus for achieving various charters oi the Department of Ocean Development in the fielj 
of demonstration of technology having a bear ing on the socio-economic aspects of the Island 
community. Accordingly, the set object ives of the programme are to demonstrate the

fattening, sea ranching and sea farming of lobster; and introduction of technology 
development programmes for sea farming of sea bass, ornamental fish, mud crab, etc. 
45. With the commissioning of a sea-front laboratory at Neelankarai, Chennai, and with

MARINE RESEARCH AND CAPACITY BUILDING 
47. The Department has been implementing the Marine Research and Manpower 
Development Programme with the active participation of educational institutions, National 
R&D Laboratories, etc. The objectives of the programme are to encourage research on 
physical and chemical processes, which govern the seas of India; impact of natural 
anthropogenic sources on global climate change; to strengthen the infrastructure facilities in 
selected universitiesltnstitutes to carry out basic research in Marine Sciences; to create 
centres of excellence on ocean sciences at selected institutes/universities; to create a code of 
high class ocean scientists and trained personnel/users; and to create awiireness among the 
school children, public and coastal community on the usefulness of ocean resources and 
create scientific temper among the people on ocean science. 
48. The ongoing projects would be integrated for generation of a national project under 
Marine Living Resources Programme. Besides, funding of about 10 socially beneficial 
projects proposed to be taken up with a view to develop suitable technologies. Also, action 
wouid be initiated to bring uniformity in course curriculum for offering courses on Ocean 
Science & Technology in Colleges/Universities and to evolve a mechanism for upgrading and 
bringing uniformity in academic standards in all marine related course, including 
introduction of Ocean Science & Technology at secondary school level. 
49. The Committee observes the need to induct Ocean Science related courses at various 
levels of studies to mak~ the students more conversant with the developments taking place in 
the field of Ocean Sciences.
