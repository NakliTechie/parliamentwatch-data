OVERALL BUDGETARY DETAILS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2024-25 (BE, RE & 
ACTUAL EXPENDITURE) AND ALLOCATION FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2025-26 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.1 The Committee recommends that the DAE should adopt a more robust approach to 
financial planning and forecasting by incorporating detailed risk assessments and contingency 
allocations into the initial budget estimates. The Committee notes that the Revised Estimates 
have consistently exceeded the Budget Estimates. The Committee therefore urges the Department 
to implement regular internal and external audits and to ensure transparent reporting of any 
mid-year adjustments, with clear justifications for deviations from the initial allocations. 
Furthermore, it is recommended that standard operating procedures for urgent procurement and 
maintenance should be established to pre-empt emergency expenditures, while also integrating 
anticipated costs for global collaborations and new technologies into the budgetary framework. 
(Para 2.5 of the 387th Report) 
Action Taken  
1.2 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee shall be complied with. 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.3 The Committee recommends that the Department continue to streamline fund disbursal 
processes to ensure optimal utilization of allocated resources, particularly for multi-year atomic 
energy projects. The Committee also recommends that a more robust financial strategy be 
adopted, incorporating contingency planning to mitigate the impact of unexpected budget cuts. 
Additionally, the Committee urges the Ministry of Finance to consider the critical nature of the 
Department’s commitments, particularly those related to nuclear fuel procurement and 
international obligations, and to ensure that adequate funding is provided in a timely manner. 
The Committee urges upon the Department to implement these measures with immediate effect to 
restore the expenditure trajectory to match both the budget estimates and the requirements of 
multi-year projects.                                                                                                     (para 2.7 ibid)

Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.6 The Committee observes with concern that the Research & Development (R&D) Sector is 
the most impacted amongst all, with the R&D sector suffering the largest reduction of 
Rs.3,289.60 crore, from a proposed BE of Rs.14,516.38 crore to the approved BE Rs.11,226.78 
crore. The Committee observes that the shortfall ofRs.3,289 crore would impact long-term 
research initiatives under BARC, IGCAR, and RRCAT, slowing down innovations in nuclear 
reactor safety, radioactive waste management, and new reactor technologies. The Mega Science 
projects, which include research on fusion energy and directed research, may also face delays.            
                                                                                                                                  (Para 2.11 ibid) 
1.7 The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends immediate action to restore essential 
funding, support nuclear research, and ensure uninterrupted progress in India’s nuclear energy 
and medical applications. The Committee recommends urgent restoration of at least Rs.5,933.62 
crore, particularly for the R&D and I&M sectors, to ensure continued progress in nuclear 
technology development and fuel production.                                                         (para 2.13 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
1.8 As recommended by the Hon’ble Committee the additional funds for R&D and I&M 
Sector shall be sought at RE stage, to ensure continued progress in nuclear technology 
development and fuel production. 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
1.9 The Committee supports DAE’s initiative to attract private investment. However, the 
Committee recommends that clear regulatory frameworks and risk mitigation strategies should 
be developed to ensure private sector participation in the critical and sensitive nuclear 
infrastructure projects. While the Committee acknowledges DAE’s efforts to optimize its 
expenditures, it recommends a detailed financial audit to ensure that budgetary constraints do 
not result in inefficient cost-cutting measures that could compromise long-term technological 
advancements.                                                                                                           (Para 2.14 ibid)

Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.13 The Committee observes from Chart 3 that the total budget allocation for the Department 
of Atomic Energy (DAE) in B.E. 2025-26 stands at Rs.37,482.93 crore, with Rs.22,668.29 crore 
earmarked for revenue expenditure and Rs.14,814.64 crore for capital expenditure. The 
Committee observes that while the proposed capital expenditure was Rs.16,468.59 crore, the 
approved allocation was Rs.1,653.95 crore lower, whereas the revenue allocation saw a steeper 
decline of Rs.4,279.67 crore from the proposed Rs.26,947.96 crore.                     (Para 2.18 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
1.14 As observed by the Hon’ble Committee there are budgetary cuts in the BE allocations. 
The current shortfall of Rs.5,933.62 crore alongwith any further additionality, based on the 
budgetary requirements, shall be sought at RE stage. 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
1.15 The Committee notes a significant disparity in proposed budgetary allocations against 
the actual allocations for 2025-26, between capital and revenue expenditure across key sectors 
of the Department, particularly in I&M and R&D sectors. A comparative assessment of the 
proposed and approved allocations (Table-2) highlights that while capital expenditure across 
sectors has been significantly curtailed, revenue expenditure has remained relatively stable.       
         (Para 2.19 ibid) 
1.16 The Committee notes that the I&M sector saw a capital allocation reduction from the 
proposed Rs.6,386.07 crore, in BE 2025-26, to Rs.5,293.18 crore, reflecting a 17.1% shortfall, 
while the revenue allocation was curtailed by 22.9% from the proposed Rs.6,762.58 crore to 
Rs.5,217.27 crore. The Committee notes with concern that R&D sector experienced an even 

5 
 
substantial reduction in capital expenditure, reduced by Rs.560.89 crore, a reduction of over 
27.3%, while the revenue component saw a reduction of Rs.2,728.71 crore (21.9%), indicating a 
prioritisationof operational expenses over infrastructure investments. The Committee notes that 
the Power sector has faced only a marginal reduction in capital allocation, with the approved 
amount standing at Rs.8,028.84 crore against the proposed allocation of Rs.8,029.01 crore. 
Revenue allocation for the sector also remained largely unchanged at Rs.7,631.72 crore.             
                                                                                                                                  (Para 2.20 ibid) 
1.17 The Committee feels that capital expenditure disparity is expected to have a negative 
impact on several high-priority projects. The Committee observes that the reduced capital 
allocation to the R&D sector could affect long-term research initiatives under Bhabha Atomic 
Research Centre (BARC), Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR), and Raja 
Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT), potentially delaying innovations in 
nuclear safety, reactor technology, and radioactive waste management. The Committee 
emphasizes that while operational (revenue) expenses are necessary for maintaining institutional 
stability, the underfunding of capital investments may hinder advancements in nuclear energy 
infrastructure and indigenous technology development. To address these concerns, the 
Committee recommends a reassessment of expenditure priorities within the DAE, advocating for 
a more equitable distribution of funds between revenue and capital components. (Para 2.21 ibid) 
Action Taken  
1.18 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee shall be complied with. 
Recommendations/Observations 
1.19 The Committee acknowledges that the predominance of revenue expenditure is partly due 
to salary commitments, facility maintenance, and recurring operational costs. However, it 
underscores the need for a balanced allocation to ensure continued infrastructure expansion and 
modernization of nuclear energy facilities.                                                       (Para 2.22 ibid) 
 
1.20 The Committee urges the Department to recalibrate its financial strategy by enhancing 
capital allocations and ensuring a robust implementation framework for major nuclear projects. 
The Committee notes that the underfunding of capital projects could result in execution delays 
and inefficiencies in scaling nuclear infrastructure, ultimately impacting India’s long-term 
energy security. The Committee understands that a sustained focus on capital investment will be 
crucial for maintaining India’s leadership in atomic energy, fostering self-reliance in nuclear 
technology, and ensuring long-term energy security.                                              (Para 2.24 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
1.21 Necessary steps shall be taken to evolve a framework as desired by the Hon’ble 
Committee to recalibrate its financial strategy by enhancing capital allocations and ensuring a 
robust implementation framework for major nuclear projects.

Recommendations/Observations 
1.28 The Committee is concerned that the budgetary shortfall of Rs.633.62 crore for 2025-26 
may negatively impact the progress of key nuclear research and infrastructure projects. The 
shortfall is likely to delay advancements in isotope production, nuclear safety technologies, and 
research in fast breeder reactor technology. Moreover, the reduction in funds may slow down 
ongoing work in nuclear waste disposal, a critical area for India’s nuclear sustainability. The 
Committee recommends that the Government should reconsider BARC’s budgetary allocation in 
the Revised Estimates (RE) for 2025-26 to bridge the funding gap, particularly in areas of 
nuclear safety, waste management, and advanced reactor research.                       (Para 3.4 ibid) 
 
1.29 The Committee taking into note the under-utilisation of funds in 2024-25, recommends 
BARC to streamline its procurement and project execution mechanisms to ensure timely 
implementation and optimal fund utilisation. The Committee urges BARC to prioritise high-
impact projects in nuclear safety, isotope production, and advanced reactor technology, 
ensuring that critical research is not disrupted due to budgetary constraints. The Committee 
recommends the DAE to ensure quarterly monitoring of fund utilisation and project progress so 
as to prevent delays in key infrastructure and R&D initiatives.                                (Para 3.5 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
1.30 The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee to reconsider BARC’s budgetary 
allocations in the Revised Estimates (RE) 2025-26 to bridge the funding gap, particularly in 
areas of nuclear safety, waste management and advanced reactor research are noted.  
1.31 Major challenge in R&D Project is procurement of high and imported instruments/ 
components. These items are not available in India and are essential for new technology 
development. These items are needed in very less quantities and require sophisticated 
technologies and know-how therefore, not economically viable to develop and produce in India. 
Hence, procurement of such items from the global market will be required which necessitates 

8 
 
raising global tender enquiry (GTE). To streamline the process, BARC has compiled a 
comprehensive list of such imported items required in near future for R&D Projects and their 
exemption from existing procedures of GTE is being pursued with Department for Promotion of 
Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT). To ensure timely execution of R&D projects, pre-project 
activities like designs, tender specifications, drawings, regulatory clearances etc. are completed 
to maximum possible extent before the financial sanction of the projects to expedite the project 
execution. Specifications are framed to have participations of multiple vendors for 
competitiveness and streamlined processing. Mechanisms for quarterly monitoring of on-going 
projects is already in place. The financial progress of Projects is being regularly monitored by 
project review committees to prevent delays in project execution.  
1.32 Further, as recommended by the Hon’ble Committee all the Heads of Accounting Units 
have been advised vide OM dated 18.06.2025 to ensure quarterly monitoring of fund utilization 
and realistic budget formation. 
Recommendations/Observations 
1.33 The Committee notes that IGCAR had sought Rs.1,035.94 crore, but only Rs.800.39 crore 
was allocated, resulting in a shortfall of Rs.235.55 crore. TheCommittee believes that the 
reduced allocation may hamper R&D in fast breeder reactor technology, fuel reprocessing, and 
safety enhancements. The Committee also observes that capital allocation has been reduced 
from Rs.373.55 crore to Rs.204.77 crore, affecting infrastructure and equipment upgrades.       
(Para 3.7 ibid) 
 
1.34 The Committee observes that insufficient funding may slow down accelerator research, 
high-brightness synchrotron radiation sources, and laser technology development.   (Para 3.9 
ibid) 
1.35 The Committee notes that during the years 2023-24 and 2024-25 AMD focused on

and Geophysical surveys and drilling in high-potential regions and expansion of isotope 
hydrology applications for groundwater resource management.                            (Para 3.10 ibid) 
1.36 The Committee notes with concern that the budget shortfall for 2025-26 could 
significantly impact these institutions’ ability to meet their research objectives. The reduced 
allocations may delay crucial reactor safety upgrades and fast breeder reactor development at 
IGCAR. The Committee notes that slowdown in critical laser and accelerator research at 
RRCAT will affect India’s capability inmedical, industrial, and defence applications. The 
Committee recommends that IGCAR, RRCAT and AMD should increase collaboration with 
industry partners to generate additional revenue streams and reduce reliance on government 
funding. The Committee recommends quarterly reviews of fund utilisation to ensure that 
available resources are effectively deployed and that additional funding is sought wherever 
necessary.                                                                                                                 (Para 3.13 ibid)

1.37 As suggested by the Hon’ble Committee, the options to increase industrial collaboration 
to generate additional revenue streams would be explored. However, it is felt that still 
government funding would be relied upon for R&D activities. Also it is to be emphasized that

Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.38 The Committee notes that budget constraints will slow down geophysical surveys, deep 
drilling operations, and resource assessment, delaying efforts to expand India's nuclear fuel 
reserves considering the fact that Uranium is essential for pressurised heavy water reactors 
(PHWRs), while thorium is key to India's Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (AHWR) programme.                
                                                                                                                                  (Para 3.14 ibid) 
 
1.39 The Committee recommends that AMD should focus resources on high- potential 
uranium and thorium reserves in Jharkhand, Meghalaya, Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan and 
special attention should be given to monazite-bearing coastal areas in Odisha, Kerala, Tamil 
Nadu, Andhra Pradesh for rare earth and thorium extraction. The Committee also expressed 
concerns over illegal mining of Atomic minerals and its impact on the cost of exchequer and 
national security. In parallel with exploration, the Committee recommends enhancing uranium 
ore processing plants at Jaduguda, Tummalapalle, and Narwapahar to improve the efficiency of 
domestic fuel production. The Committee recommends expedited approvals for the construction 
of AHWR and associated fuel reprocessing infrastructure. The Committee urges upon the DAE to 
have collaboration with international nuclear agencies for technological exchange on thorium 
reactor operations.                                                                                                  (Para 3.16 ibid) 
Action Taken

Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.44   The Committee understands that given China’s dominance on production of global Rare 
Earth Elements (REE), India's lack of investment in domestic exploration and processing will 
keep the country vulnerable to supply chain disruptions. The Committee recommends the DAE to 
fast scale the exploration to reduce dependence on China-dominated REE supply chains. Given 
the increasing demand for nuclear and REE-based technologies, the Committee recommends 
establishing a strategic reserve for uranium, thorium, and REEs. The Committee observes that 
this will ensure long-term security and prevent supply disruptions in India’s nuclear and high-
tech industries.                                                                                                        (Para 3.18 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
 
1.45 AMD has established uranium, monazite, Beach Sand Minerals (BSM) and Rare Earth 
Elements (REE) as follows: 
 AMD established first hard-rock hosted REE deposit of the Country in Ambadungar, 
Gujarat. Besides, AMD has also established HREE enriched REO deposits in

1.119 Below are some of the research studies that have made an impact on routine treatment 
guidelines of the cancer patients and provided evidence of high quality affordable treatment 
1) Low-Dose Immunotherapy in Head and Neck Cancer: A Randomized Study 
Immunotherapy treatment for treatment of cancer has been one of the key 
developments with two nobel prizes. Due to cost there are only 3-4 % of patients in 
India who can use it. With the research project done at Tata memorial Hospital the 
cost for this treatment has come down by almost one tenth of original cost which 
has led to use by the majority of patients of head and neck cancer. This also has 
opened the avenue to explore the similar concept and develop low dose cost 
effective immunotherapy in other cancers. This is part of routine care in our country 
and developing world. This paper had an editorial in JCO and was covered by NIH-
NCI, MEDSCAPE etc.  
2) Gefitinib vs Gefitinib Plus Pemetrexed and Carboplatin Chemotherapy in EGFR-
Variant Lung Cancer-Long-Term Results of a Randomized Clinical Trial:  Modern 
targeted therapy has improved outcomes in lung cancer with EGFR mutation but 
only 4-5% of patients in India can use it due to cost. Tata Hospital used an 
innovative approach of combining two relatively cost effective drugs and showed in 
a randomised study that their efficacy is similar to modern targeted therapy and 
accessible to almost every patient in India with lung cancer with EGFR mutation. 
This is part of international and national guidelines for lung cancer management eg 
- PAN ASIA ESMO guidelines, UpToDate guidelines, NCG guidelines etc. 
3) Low-cost oral metronomic chemotherapy versus intravenous cisplatin in patients 
with recurrent, metastatic, inoperable head and neck carcinoma: an open-label, 
parallel-group, non-inferiority, randomised, phase 3 trial: Chemotherapy is an 
integral part of treatment of Head and Neck cancer but has toxicities and is also of 
limited benefit. Tata Hospital used an innovative approach of low dose, oral 
administration of chemotherapy in head and neck cancer. TMC was able to prove in 
our research work that in platinum sensitive and resistant patients they are effective 
and also cost effective and almost every patient can use it and this also eliminates 
the IV administration of drugs. This research has been published in various 
prestigious journals. This is part of various guidelines - UpToDate, NCG etc. 
4) Late Toxicity After Adjuvant Conventional Radiation Versus Image-Guided 
Intensity-Modulated Radiotherapy for Cervical Cancer (PARCER): A Randomized 
Controlled Trial: This study was conducted by Tata Memorial Centre in women 
with cervical cancer who needed post-operative radiotherapy. It compared, in a 
randomized controlled trial, two techniques of giving radiotherapy to these women 
- intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) or conventional 3D conformal 
radiotherapy. The results showed that IGRT markedly reduced the radiotherapy 
related short-term and long term side effects on the bowel and bladder. Thus, 

26 
 
although it is more resource intensive, it is now accepted that IGRT is a better way 
of delivering radiotherapy to these patients. 
5) Evaluation of 1-Year vs Shorter Durations of Adjuvant Trastuzumab Among 
Patients With Early Breast Cancer: An Individual Participant Data and Trial-Level 
Meta-analysis:Targeted cancer therapies are effective but very expensive. Once 
such expensive targeted therapy for breast cancer patients is an antibody drug called 
trastuzumab. Ordinarily it is given for a duration of 1 year. An individual-patient 
data meta-analysis conducted by Tata Memorial Centre, involving about 12000 
patients from six randomized controlled trials which compared 3-6 months of 
Trastuzumab with 1 year of Trastuzumab showed conclusively that shorter 
durations of this drug resulted in similar long-term cure rates. Another study 
conducted in collaboration between researchers of Tata Memorial Centre and PGI 
Chandigarh showed that shorter durations of Trastuzumab were cost-effective but 1 
year of Trastuzumab was not cost-effevtive, at current prices of this drug, in India. 
As a result of this study the PMJAY has rolled out short duration Trastuzumab as 
the standard treatment regimen in this scheme. 
6) Effect of Peritumoral Infiltration of Local Anesthetic Before Surgery on Survival in 
Early Breast Cancer: Clinical researchers at Tata Memorial Centre conceptualized 
and led a study wherein they injected the inexpensive local anesthesia drug 
lidocaine all around the breast cancer tumor, just prior to surgical removal of this 
tumor. This was a randomized controlled trial in 1600 women with breast cancer, 
wherein half the patients received this injection prior to surgery and the other half 
underwent surgery without this injection. The results showed that the recurrence of 
cancer and death were substantially reduced by this simple intervention which costs 
less than Rs 100. The results of this recently published study have been 
implemented in TMC and many other centres in India and in some other parts of 
the world.

1.122 Accessibility to all sections of society- One of the problems of our country is accessibility 
of the treatment.  In order to overcome the problem TMC has offered hub and spoke model. This 
is a distributed model of cancer care to offer patients high quality cancer care at their doorsteps. 
Common and less complex cancer care should be provided close to patients’ homes (spokes) to 
create minimum disruption in their lives and that of their families. Treatment of uncommon 
cancers and those with complex treatment protocols require management in expert centres of 

27 
 
excellence (hubs). However, to avoid over-burdening the hubs, the spokes need to be reasonably 
capable of handling less complex and common cancer types in the region. With an aim to 
decentralize cancer care and to make it more affordable and accessible. TMC registered 1,28,000 
new cancer patients in 2024 (Calendar year) of which about 70% are in general and nominal 
charges category.

Recommendations/Observations 
2.36 The high vacancy rates across DAE institutions raise serious concerns about workforce 
planning, recruitment efficiency, and the overall sustainability of nuclear and research projects 
in India. The Committee expresses its concerns over these vacancies, especially in key 
operational areas such as nuclear power generation, research, and mineral exploration. The 
shortage of skilled personnel could lead to delays in research and development, inefficiencies in 
plant operations, and constraints in nuclear fuel production, ultimately affecting the country's 
nuclear energy programme. To address this issue, the Committee strongly recommends an 
immediate recruitment drive to fill the existing vacancies. It is imperative that recruitment 
processes be streamlined to ensure timely hiring of personnel in technical, scientific, and 
operational roles. A review of existing workforce policies should be undertaken to identify areas 
where delays occur in the hiring process, and necessary reforms should be implemented. The 
DAE must adopt modern human resource management strategies to attract and retain talent, 
ensuring competitive salaries, career progression opportunities, and research grants for 
professionals in nuclear science, energy, and advanced research.                           (Para 8.6 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
 
2.37 Department of Atomic Energy follows Merit based Promotion Scheme for Scientific and 
Technical personnel which is delinked from availability of vacancies and rewards them with time 
bound promotions. This merit-based career progression scheme is implemented through a well-
established mechanism of minimum residency requirements, performance assessment, screening, 
review and evaluation. The Merit Promotion Scheme has so far been regulated through norms 
and practices based on decisions taken by the Trombay Council and further approved by the 
DAE. 
2.38 In addition, the modern human resource management strategies as adopted by DAE, is 
followed in the Units to attract and retain talent by way of following measures: 

43 
 
i. Advance increments are given to the Scientific and Technical Personnel on their initial 
appointment. 
ii. Scientists are encouraged to carry out further studies like M Tech, MSc. Engg., PhD. After 
PhD, opportunities are also given to carry out Post-Doctoral Research works at reputed 
International laboratories.  
iii. DAE also grants Performance incentives in the form of Performance Related Incentive 
Scheme (Organization) [PRIS-O], Performance Related Incentive Scheme (Group) [PRIS-
G] and Performance Related Incentive Scheme (Individual) [PRIS-I] for timely completion 
of major activities related to DAE programmes. 
iv. DAE also grants Professional Update Allowance to Scientific / Technical Officers in the 
grade of SO/C and above, every year. 
v. DAE has collaboration with International Agencies like ITER/CERN Projects where they 
get opportunity to work on these projects on deputation. 
vi. To promote research and capacity building in the field of nuclear science and energy, 
grants are provided to young talented scientists for research projects and scientific events 
aligned with the departmental mandate through Board of Research in Nuclear Sciences 
(BRNS) an extramural agency of DAE through various schemes such as Young Scientist 
Research Project (YSRP) scheme, research fellowship for (JRF/ SRF/ RA) and DAE 
Graduate Fellowship Scheme (DGFS-PhD).  
2.39 In Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd. (NPCIL), Policy changes have been proposed 
and approved for recruitment of Technicians on Fixed Term Appointment basis so that locals get 
the opportunity for employment on Fixed Term basis. 
2.40 Electronics Corporation of India Ltd (ECIL), is constantly reshaping policies such as 
Promotion Policy with better norms, Medical Insurance Policy with Cashless facility and 
Recruitment Policy with an aim to create a conducive work environment to employee group and 
in line with the market trends.  
2.41 Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), an Aided Institution,actively seeks out 
talented Indian nationals with proven high credentials in emerging areas of scientific research 
from around the world to apply for advertised positions through continuous engagement via 
visits, collaborations, and conferences. They are provided with competitive initial start-up grants 
based on the research program’s requirements and salaries consistent with other premier 
academic institutions in the country.   
2.42 Tata Memorial Centre (TMC) has robust Training & Developmentsystem. Medical & 
Scientific Staff are encouraged to attend conferences, workshops, research programmes in India 
and abroad.  They are also supported with extramural and intramural research grants for various 
projects. 

44 
 
2.43 In order to retain talented and experienced medical staff, TMC has taken up a proposal 
for increasing the age of superannuation in case of Medical Staff from 60 to 65 years. 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.44 The Committee observes with great concern contractual employment and temporary 
staffing, particularly in research institutions such as TIFR. The Committee urges a review of 
these employment practices and recommends converting long-term contractual positions into 
permanent roles wherever necessary. The Committee recommends that regularisation of 
employment will not only enhance job security for researchers and scientists but also ensure 
continuity in long-term research projects. Additionally, for institutions such as TMC, where 
medical professionals are in high demand, the Committee recommends expanding recruitment 
efforts and increasing budgetary support for healthcare staff and researchers.       (Para 8.7 ibid) 
 
Action Taken  
 
2.45 With regard to employment on a contractual or short-term basis, it is stated that 
contractual appointments are made only for support services, execution of Plan projects for 
research activities and creation of assets and not for core scientific or technical roles. 
 
2.46 In Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), the contractual positions are primarily 
used for administrative tasks that are suitable for outsourcing. Short-term contractual positions 
for research-related tasks are only utilised for projects which have a specific time and scope 
delimitation. This is in keeping with standard practices being followed at all leading research 
institutions at the national and international levels. It is highlighted that temporary Research 
Associate / Research Fellow positions offer valuable training opportunities for fresh Ph.D. 
holders, enabling them to grow as independent researchers and thereafter take up tenured 
positions in academia/research institutions/industry while at the same contributing to advance 
scientific research in the Institute.
