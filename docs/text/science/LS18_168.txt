REPORT 
 The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science & Technology on the 
recommendations contained in the One hundred and FiftyF ifth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2006-07) of the 
Department of Science & Technology, which was presented to Parliament on 18th May, 2006. There were 13 
recommendations contained in the Report. Action Taken Note on the recommendations of the Committee was received 
from the Department of Science & Technology on 16th November, 2006. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken by the Department and Comments of the Committee thereon 
are set out in the succeeding paragraphs. 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee has considered the reas ons given by the Department for maintaining uniform pace of expenditure 
throughout the year. It does not, however, feel satisfied and convinced by them. The Committee would like that the 
Department may evolve suitable mechanism so that the expend iture should not reach the desired level at the close of 
the financial year but it should be uniform, to the extent possible, throughout the year. The reasons given show lack of 
coordination in decision-making and indicate that an ad-h oc approach is being adopted. The Committee therefore, 
strongly recommends that a blue print be drawn for the purpo se and process should not be delayed if externalities are 
not involved. (Para 10) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 Expenditure under the Revenue Section shows a fairly even trend of expenditure. Regarding the pattern of 
expenditure under the Capital Section not being uniform and leaning towards the later two quarters of the financial 
year, it is submitted that the low expenditure during the firs t two quarters is mainly due to the time taken for purchase 
procedure for equipments; viz., finalizing the technical specifications, draw ing-up of the tender document, invitation of 
technical and financial bids, evaluation of the tender bids, placing of the orders and time factored for delivery 
schedules. The entire purchase process involves a minimum of  5-6 months. Thereafter, the payments are released and 
expenditure booked during the third and fourth quarters. Ho wever, efforts are being made to ensure that uniform 
pattern of expenditure is followed both in Revenue and Capital sections. 
Comments 
 The Committee, even while considering the reasons given by the Department for uneven expenditure on 
capital account, is still of the opinion that a mechanism to ensure even expenditure throughout the financial year 
is possible and the Department should make efforts to evolve such a system. 
INDIAN ASSOCIATION FOR CULTIVATION OF SCIENCE, KOLKATA 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes with satisfaction about the additional financial provision to the country’s oldest research 
Organisation for replacing obsolete equipments and improve its working condition. However, the Committee is of the 
opinion that this premier orga nization needs a new building and more funds for buying new instruments for its better 
functioning. The Committee suggests that the Department may take up the matter with the concerned State Government 
to acquire land for the said purpose. (Para 14) 
Action Taken by the Department 

 This Institute was requested for finalizing the plans for new building and also consult the State Government to 
acquire land for this purpose. Institute has finalized the building plans within the existing space and has incorporated 
the same in the Eleventh Five-Year Plan. The institute is also exploring the possibility of getting additional land from 
the State Government. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinio n that the ‘new building proposal ’ for the Indian Association for 
Cultivation of Science, Kolkata, as suggested by the Committee, should be pursued in all seriousness and the 
Committee hopes that the institute will get a new building as soon as possible. 
BOSE INSTITUTE, KOLKATA 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should draw a long-term time schedule and accordingly 
make long-term investments in the institutions rather than doing it in an ad-hoc manner. It, therefore, strongly 
recommends to the Department to undertake studies of individual institutions to find out areas where they can excel and 
make investments accordingly rather than following piecemeal approach. (Para 19) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department of Science & Technology is discussing the Eleventh Five Year Plan proposals and 
corresponding investments to capitalize on the strengths of each institute in consultation with Experts and Planning 
Commission. As regards release of funds, it is done based on requirements and availability of utilization certificates 
from the grantee institutions as per the provisions of the General Financial Rules, 2005. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the recommendations made by it  in the above context will be kept in mind while 
drawing long-term plans for individual institutes so that the allocated funds could be utilized optimally. 
SREE CHITRA TIRUNAL INSTITUTE FOR MEDICAL SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY, 
THIRUV ANANTHAPURAM 
Recommendation 
 The Committee notes with satisfaction that additional grants at R.E. stage were provided to help the Institute to 
provide reduced cost of treatment for the needy population an d the hospital provides nearly 50% beds to poor patients 
at reduced cost or no cost. It also notes that additional gran ts will be provided to the Institute during 2006-07 as per its 
requirement. (Para 23) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department has provided additional grants as recommended by the Committee for providing free and 
subsidized treatment to the poor patients, based on their income levels. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would like the Depart ment to ensure such facilities for the poor strata 
of society in future as well. 
RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT SUPPORT, SCIENCE AND ENGINEERING RESEARCH COUNCIL 
(SERC) 
 

Recommendation 
 The Committee notes the steps taken by the Council to further basic research. The Committee is of the opinion 
that technological advances cannot be made without pathbreaking research in basic sciences, which, in-turn, need 
world-class laboratories and quality human resource. The Committee takes note of the various scholarships and 
fellowships being given by the Council and is hopeful that the present gap between the re quirements of quality human 
resource and its supply will be abridged in the near future. (Para 25) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department has noted the Recommendation of the Committee. The Department is confident that the various 
fellowships, research grants and other incentives provided  under various schemes will help reduce the gap between 
requirements of quality human resource and its supply. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hope s that the benefits of the sche me would continue to flow for 
improving the quality of our human resource, for which we feel proud of. 
TECHNOLOGY FOR BAMBOO PRODUCTS (NATIONAL MISSION FOR BAMBOO APPLICATION) 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the objective of producing wood substitutes to reduce pressure on our forests is of 
utmost importance. Given the amount of de-forestation that has taken place over the years and the rising demand of 
urban material culture which takes heavy toll of our limited natural resources, alternate arrangements to meet the 
demand of modern times without doing ecological damages have to be evolved. The Committee would also like to 
emphasize that such technologies should also be disseminated to spread awareness among the masses and raise their 
sensitivities towards natural environment to a level, which can make modern life-styles sustainable. (Para 31) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The National Mission on Bamboo Application (NMBA) has developed several technologies for use of Bamboo 
as wood substitute in construction purposes. The Mission, in consultation with Central Public Works Department and 
Ministry of Urban Development, has developed Standards for use of Bamboo material in construction works. The 
NMBA has erected pre-fabricated Bamboo Houses in ear thquake-affected areas in Jammu & Kashmir and some 
schools in Leh. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinion th at the technologies developed by the National Mission on Bamboo 
Application (NMBA), if meetin g the requisite standards, should be in troduced on an increasing scale in the 
earthquake affected areas so as to mi nimize the damage done by calamity. The Committee is of the opinion that 
more and more such eco-friendly technologies should be  encouraged to strike a balance between development 
and environment. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee has been informed that there was a shor tage of programmes during 2005-06 and this resulted in 
reduced grants at RE level. The Committee strongly r ecommends that assessment of ‘ready for implementation 
programmes’ should be done at the B.E. stage itself so as to reflect realistic budgeting. The Committee also desires that 
the Department should study the reasons for shortage of  programmes during 2005-06 and take suitable remedial 
measures in that regard. (Para 32) 

Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department has implemented the suggestion made by the Committee to assess the fund requirement at BE 
stage based on ready to implement programmes. The Departme nt has examined the reasons for shortage of ready to 
implement programmes. The shortage has resulted because of technologies envisaged at BE stage could not pass 
through the testing and certification proce ss to meet the specifications prescribed  by the Bureau of Indian Standards 
(BIS). These programmes needed further Research and Deve lopment for developing acceptable materials meeting BIS 
specifications. Secondly, there was also a high percentage of mortality of tissue culture raised plants during hardening 
and transportation to meet the targets of afforestation. This aspect is being further examined by the Institute from where 
the technology was sourced. These resulted in reducing the grants at RE Stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the Department will take nece ssary remedial measures in this regard. It also 
hopes that more care will be taken while making estimates at the budgetary stage itself.  
S&T PROGRAMME FOR SOCIO-ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT (SCIENCE & SOCIETY DIVISION) 
Recommendation 
 The importance of science is ultimately attested by the basic changes it brings in day-to-day lives of people in 
general. Science has to shoulder social responsibility and cannot remain confined to academic research and idle 
curiosity. It is in this context that the importance of the above programme has to be seen. Women Component Plan, 
Special Component Plan for Scheduled Caste and Tribal Sub-Plan are no doubt, a good initiative but they need to give 
encouraging results in future. The above schemes are potential vehicles of social change and, therefore, deserve special 
attention. (Para 42) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department is in total agreement with the recommendation. The Special Component Schemes; such as, 
Science and Technology (S & T) for Women, Scheduled Cast es Sub-Plan and Tribal Sub-Plan have been implemented 
and these schemes have achieved good success. For example, 15 Women Technology Parks have been set up in 
different agro-climatic zones and under a coordinated project on “Medicinal Plants” in seven States products; such as, 
nutraceuticals, medicated oils and creams have been deve loped which are being currently marketed. A special washer 
pump has been developed which is very helpful in drawing water up to a depth of 15 metres from deep wells. About 
650 water filters which are capable of removing virus have been installed in different locations in the country. About 
500 filters have been installed in Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland. Under a coordinated project related to fodder and 
feed for benefiting rural women, projects have been implemented in 12 different locations and they have yielded good 
results. A special project has been successfully completed for educating school children in science through a mobile 
van in schools in Tamil Nadu where the population of school  children is predominantly of Scheduled Caste children.. 
In an effort to make SC youth movable in one of the projects, 180 SC youths from some villages in North Goa have 
been trained in trades like plumbing, carpentry, automobile repair and electrician. 
 Some projects are field-based and invo lve large scale community participation. New initiatives having large scale 
programme will be taken up during the Eleventh Plan. Tw o important areas, being looked into are (1) Integrated 
development of 10 villages which are predominantly occupied by Scheduled Castes population in Himachal Pradesh, 
Uttaranchal and Jammu & Kashmir; and (2) A programme that will be launched for upgrading the technical standards 
of artisans in these areas. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinion that in an era of globalisation when mu ltinational products are sweeping 
the markets, village artisans, who predominantly belong to the socially backward sectio ns of the society, need a 

helping hand in the form of upgradation of skills so as  to face market forces. It is in this context that 
programmes launched for upgra ding the technical standards of arti sans acquire importance. More such 
programmes needs to be initiated in future to face global challenges. 
Recommendation 
 In a country where efforts have been made during the last  fifty years to achieve total literacy, spreading scientific 
awareness, remove superstition and inculcate scientific thinking, the success has been modest. Beyond the success 
islands of new India, which are generally metropolitan, there is  an India on the other side of the digital divide that is 
largely rural, backward, steeped in regressive social values and superstitious thinking. The challenge before the 
Department is to take the offensive against backwardness in this part of India, which has not been able to keep pace 
with the rest of the country. The various awareness programmes run by the Department should not only continue but, in 
fact, increase and cover different parts of the country. What is also required is to keep track  of the results achieved by 
way of feedback mechanism so that effectiveness of such programmes could be evaluated from time to time.(Para 49) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The recommendations of the Committee are unexceptionable. Intensive and extensive campaigns are being drawn 
up to address the needs of different population groups. Existi ng and new partners are being drawn into a programme to 
make a measurable impact on the level of scientific literacy. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like to see a holistic approach and a detailed programme on the above subject 
having an in-built mechanism of self-evaluation and measurable impact on the target group. 
NATIONAL CENTRE FOR MEDIUM RANGE WEATHER FORECASTING (NCMRWF) 
Recommendation 
 The Committee recommends that the upgradation of co mputing and infrastructure facilities, visualization, 
networking, I.T. infrastructure should be done as soon as possible. Since the objectives of the Centre is to generate 
location specific forecasts for providing weather based agro-a dvisory services to farmers, and NWP guidance to other 
user agencies, including India Meteorological Department and Defence, upgradation of existing technologies is the 
need of the hour. Weather forecasting needs constant im provements both in technology as well as expertise. The 
Country still has substantial number of farmers caught in  the vagaries of unpredictable monsoon and other natural 
phenomenon. They can immensely benefit from better forecasting in the future. (Para 54) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 NCMRWF upgrades its computing and associated infrastru cture facilities from time to time in a phased manner. 
Taking into account the development of technology and fast obsolescence witnessed in the technology, the upgradation 
of CRA Y SVI system procured in May, 2001 was finalized during 2005-06. Major part of payment was released in 
first quarter of 2006-07 after the acceptance of the machine. This has consumed the allocated budget for the year 2006-
07. Due to this, other major and critical projects such as implementation of web based data service, replacement and 
upgradation of desktop computers, of NCMRWF have had to be put on hold. The budget required for these projects is 
estimated to be around Rs.9 crores. The matter will now be taken up by Ministry of Earth Sciences. 
 The recent upgradation of the main computer system and the indigenous computer system PARAM PADAMA 
have helped the Centre in initiating the efforts towards increasing the resolution- of its global atmospheric general 
circulation model which is expected to lead to increased efficiency in generating the op erational forecasts and also 
possibly the quality and range of the loca tion specific forecasts provided to the fa rmers. It is also expected that the 
improved forecasts will benefit other sections of economy a nd society as well. The recent upgradations are also likely 

to help us in improving the accuracy of  forecast in case of high impact weather,  and early warnings of weather based 
disaster. 
 The projects, which have been on hold such as web based data service, are of critical importance and form part of 
the national commitment of NCMRWF. Once the service is  available it will give research and academic community 
and other stake holders access to NCMRWF’s  current and fast data and products and in turn give a boost to research 
efforts in the field of NWP in the country. This web based data service will also help the user agency to do value 
addition to our works. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and ho pes that the Department would cont inue its efforts in removing all 
bottlenecks including financial constraints to ensure operation of web based data service on a priority basis. 
TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT BOARD 
Recommendation 
 As the mandate of the Department is to produce and disseminate indigeneous technologies, the Committee is of 
the opinion that the Board has a big responsibility to promote grass-root innovations and give such innovators as much 
help as possible. The Committee feels that though the Government has taken institutional initiatives to the above effect, 
there have not been many success stories on the ground. Commercialization is yet another area which has to be tackled. 
India, with a large number of small farmers and a huge population in small towns with modest purchasing power, can 
easily be a good market to such small scale technologies. (Para 60) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Government of India has set up TDB under the Technology Development Board Act 1995 (the legislation 
setting up the TDB has been brought in to effect from 1st September, 1996) with adequate funds to consolidate further 
technological development in the country bringing together efforts of industry and R&D institutions for achieving 
integrated excellence. The fund primarily provides financ ial assistance to industrial concerns and other agencies 
attempting commercial application of indigenous technol ogy or adapting imported technology for wider domestic 
application. Under its mandate, the Board is encouraging innovators to commercialize their innovations. TDB as on 
31st March 2006, has supported 160 projects of technology commercialization under the initiative of industry, 
individual and R&D institutions and has leveraged Rs.2735 crores with financial support of Rs. 750 crores. 
 In its 10 years of existence, TDB ha s been accredited for disseminating afford able technologies at national level 
with special reference to Hepatitis vaccines by M/s Shantha Biotech and M/s Bharat Biotech International; diabetic foot 
growth factor by M/s Virchow Lab and M/s Bharat Biotech; and renewable energy from municipal solid waste by M/s  
Selco International and M/s  Shriram Energy. TDB has also supported fo ur Irradiation plants which are used for 
agriculture products, pharmaceutical, food products, etc., resulting in better revenue to farmers and industry. 
 Further efforts are being made to encourage grass root in itiatives as well as small scale technologies due to which 
small towns would be benefited as envisaged by the Committee. 
Comments 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the above success stories should be well publicized so that they serve 
as inspiration to other innovators and become a self-propagating system help ing in development of a culture of 
innovation and ‘out-of-the-box’ thinking. 
SYNERGY PROJECTS 

Recommendation 
 Given the limited amount of funds available for research and technology development and the need to initiate 
research studies in various emerging fields, the need for sy nergizing and proper networking of various projects is of 
utmost importance not only to avoid duplication but also for appropriate channelising of  scarce resources. As these 
projects relate to national development or national security, they acquire much more importance. The Committee feels 
that formulation of National Energy Ma p, providing direction to face the challe nges of emerging international patent 
regime and coordination of mission-oriented projects should be under taken with the seriousness they deserve, as they 
will determine the emerging contours of future development.  (Para 66) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Office of the Principal Scientific Adviser to the Government of India has awarded a project ‘National Energy 
Map for India - Technology Vision 2030’ to The Energy and Resources Institute (TERI) in 2004. The project has now 
been completed and the final report has been submitted in October, 2006. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply a nd would like the implementation of the project at the earliest. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee recommends to the Department to explore the prospects of introducing a full-fledged Science 
Channel on T.V. to increase awareness about scientific development among the masses. This Channel can also help 
bringing masses to the fold of the mainstream and build their potential to participate in national development and get 
benefited by them. (Para 67) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The endorsement of the Committee for a full-fledged science channel on television is valuable. The techno-
economic feasibility study of the proposal is being commissioned. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and expects the Departme nt to expedite the techno-economic feasibility 
study in a time bound manner.
