  REPORT 
 Over the past few decades, a major cause of concern for countries across the 
world has been the rapid deterioration of  the environment caused mainly due to 
massive deforestation, ever increasing po llution resulting in global warming and 
climate changes.  Fast pace of developm ent and industrial growth has offset the 
delicate balance of nature.  To address these concerns, Ministry of Environment & 
Forests was created in 1985 as the nodal agency for planning, promotion and 
coordination of environment and forestry programmes. The Ministry of Environment 
& Forests is engaged in implementation of policies and programmes relating to 
conservation of the country' s natural resources, including lakes and rivers, its 
biodiversity, forests and wild life, ensuring the welfare of animals and prevention and 
abatement of pollution.  These tasks ar e being carried out through environment 
impact assessment, eco-regeneration, as sistance to organizations implementing 
environmental and forestry programmes, en actment of environmental legislation, 
formulation of environmenta l policies, promotion of environmental and forestry 
research, education and training to  augment the requisite manpower, and 
dissemination of environmental awarenes s among all sections of the country’s 
population.

3. MONITORING AND REVIEWING THE PROGRESS OF ONGOING 
SCHEMES 
3.1 The Committee was informed that the Ministry has a well developed 
monitoring mechanism.  Its schemes/projects are monitored regularly in Headquarters 
and by on the spot field visits by officials. Senior officers visit the states regularly and 
monitor progress of scheme s/programmes through the mechanism of nodal officers 
under which each state/UT has been assigned  a senior officer as its nodal officer for 
regular interaction with stat e/UT.  Monitoring process, inter alia, include mandatory 
audit of accounts of grantee organizati on, submission of audited utilization 
certificates, expenditure statement and a progress report on the activities conducted 
by the grantee organization besi des field visits by officers of the Ministry. Research 
projects are monitored/ reviewed by th e concerned Expert Review Committees. 
Regional offices of the Ministry also review/monitor and follow up on various 
issues/programmes in the environment, forestry and wildlife sectors.   
3.2 Under forestry, a multi level monitori ng and evaluation system of projects is 
being pursued under National Afforestatio n Programme (NAP). This includes 
intensive review of projects at the state and central levels by the State Level 
Coordination Committee and National Leve l Coordination Committee. Performance 
of the schemes is also reviewed regula rly by the Secretary by convening meetings 
with senior officers of the Ministry.  Pla nning Commission also conducts half yearly 
review meetings with the Ministry, to moni tor progress of vari ous schemes/projects. 
Proposals received in the Ministry are thor oughly examined, both in-house and also 
through external evaluators like independent experts, peer review, expert committees 
etc., before being posed for funding to the Planning Commission.  
3.3 The Committee observes that despit e such a strong looking monitoring 
mechanism, the utilisation trend of the approved outlay for the 10 th Five Year Plan, 
i.e., 2002-2007 of the Ministry has not been very encouraging.  The actual 
expenditure of the Ministry during this Plan  Period remained at Rs. 5114.58 crores as 
against the actually allocated outlay of Rs . 5723.84 crores.  Although, the overall 
utilisation was 90 per cent, it varied from 86 .85 per cent in 2004-05 to 95.76 per cent 
in 2002-03.  The reasons attributed to th e gap between outlays and expenditure are 
more or less procedural and administrativ e in nature, i.e., delays in approval of 
schemes, change in financial pattern of schemes, implementing agencies being State 
Governments/various Departme nts, delays in receipts of proposals, acquisition of 
land, submission of utilisation certificates, etc.

9. NATIONAL AFFORESTATION AND ECO-DEVELOPMENT 
PROGRAMME 
  9.1 The National Afforestation and Ec o-development Board was set up in 1992 
with the principal aim of promoting afforestat ion, tree planting, ecological restoration 
and eco-development in the country.  Sp ecial attention is being given to the 
regeneration of degraded forest areas and la nds adjoining forest areas, national parks, 
sanctuaries and other protected areas as well  as the ecologically fragile areas like the 
Western Himalayas, Aravallis, Western Ghats, etc.  Main schemes under this 
programme are National Afforestation Sche me, Greening India and National Action 
Programme to Combat Desertification. Th e implementation of the United Nations’ 
Convention to Combat Desertification and So cial Forestry are also covered under the 
ongoing schemes. 
9.2 The total-plan and non-plan budgetary allocation for this programme during 
the year 2009-10 was Rs. 330.62 crores at  B.E. stage which was reduced to Rs. 
279.12 crores at R.E. stage. The budgetary allocation for the year 2010-11 has been 
further reduced to Rs. 274.50 crores.  When the Committee enquired about the 
reasons for reduction in outlays, the Ministry  informed that due to less allocation of 
funds to the Ministry the allocation for National Afforestation Programme (NAP) was 
reduced at the R.E. in 2009-10, similarly, for the year 2010-11 the allocation for NAP 
has been curtailed on account of budgetary constraints.  
9.3 When the Committee asked about the current position of the forest and 
tree cover in the country, the Ministry replied that it is 23.84 per cent of the total 
geographical area of the country as per the India State of Forest Report, 2009 
which includes 21.02 per cent forest cover and 2.82 per cent tree cover.  When 
the Committee enquired from the Ministry as to whether the present forest 
cover of 23.84 is inclusive or exclusive of the forest land diverted during the year 

  
2009,  it was informed that the position of forest cover given is as in December, 
2007, so it does not include the area which is lost or going to be lost by way of 
diversion.  The Committee is of the opin ion that two years time lag in working 
out the state of forest report is too long a period.  On a query that whether it is 
possible to reduce this two years period  to one year, the Ministry agreed to 
reduce this period to one year. 
10.  CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY  
10.1 The Committee feels that massive afforestation is very important. Every 
year, the Ministry of Environment and Forests should have a target of increasing 
the forest cover. But for this massive a fforestation, the huge financial resources 
are required and the Ministry cannot afford to undertake afforestation on a 
large scale in the absence of funds/resources. The Committee finds that in our 
country, Banks, Public Sector Undertakings, major industries in public and 
private sectors have the corporate social responsibility as an obligation to spend 
certain resources out of their profits as Corporate Social Responsibility towards 
the welfare of the society. The Committee feels that if the Government makes it 
mandatory that 50 per cent of the mone y being spent towards corporate social 
responsibility for the massive afforestation   under the guidance of the Ministry 
of Environment and Forests, the millions of hectares of land will be covered by 
forests. At the same time, the Government need not worry for any resources and 
the Ministry of Environment and Forest also not to worry about the funds since 
the Corporate world is already spending by way of corporate social 
responsibility towards welfare activities. Therefore, the Committee strongly 
recommends to the Government of India th rough the Parliament that they must 
make it mandatory to spend 50 percent of their profit on the afforestation under 
the control of the Ministry of Environment and Forests.

12. AFFORESTATION IN CHINA VIS-À-VIS INDIA 
12.1 The Committee enquired the Ministry regarding the China' s massive plan of 
bringing millions of he ctares of land under afforestat ion scheme and its comparative 
study with that of corresponding programme in India.  
12.2 The Committee was informed that China promulgated a Law in 1981 which 
requires that every citizen over the age of 11  plant at least one tree per year. As a 
result, China currently has the highest affore station rate in the world i.e., about 4.55 
million hectare per year.   

  
12.3 The Committee feels that in or der to achieve a task of gigantic 
proportions, i.e., massive afforestatio n some bold and dynamic decisions are 
required.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry may also workout 
some modalities to ensure voluntary participation of citizens in the afforestation 
programme.  The Ministry may also thin k in terms of making it a fundamental 
duty of every citizen of the country over the age of 18, to plant one tree every 
year.  Massive awareness pr ogramme particularly among the children to make 
them understand the imperatives of planting and protecting trees would also go 
a long way in realizing the national goal of  bringing one third of the area under 
forest and tree cover.   
13. WILDLIFE PRESERVATION 
13.1 Under Wildlife Preservation, the Mi nistry provides fina ncial and technical 
support to the State Governments for the development of National Parks & Wildlife 
Sanctuaries under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme, i.e., ‘Assistance for Development 
of National Parks and Sanctu aries’. Financial assistan ce is also provided for 
management and conservation of habitat of wildlife in protected areas and for wildlife 
research to institutions, universities and NGOs. Grants are also provided to strengthen 
four Regional Offices located at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata & Chennai and three Sub-
regional Offices located at Ko chi, Guwahati & Amritsar which have been set up to 
check illegal trade in wildlife and wildlif e products and for better enforcement of 
Wildlife (Protection) Act, Convention on Trade in Endangered Species on Wild fauna 
and Flora (CITES), etc. 
13.2 For the year 2010-11, a total budgetary provision both plan and non-plan, 
Rs.301.36 crores has been made for this programme of wildlife preservation. The 
Committee enquired from the Ministry that whether the activities pursued under this 
programme are proving productive in preservi ng the wildlife. In re sponse to this, the 
Committee was informed that the Wildlife scheme, particularly, the Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme-'Integrated Wildlife of Development Habitats' was modified 
during 2008-09 by adding more components like Protection of Wildlife Outside 
Protected Areas and Recovery Programmes for saving critically endangered species 
and habitats to strengthen Wildlife Conservation, habitat improvement and 
infrastructure development. During 2008- 09 the Ministry initiated recovery 
programme for endangered species like Ha ngul, Snow leopard and Vulture in Jammu 
& Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Madhya Pradesh,  Haryana and Punjab. During the year 
2009-10, three endangered species namely Manipur brow, antlered deer, Swiftlet Nest 
and Nilgiri Tahr have been covered under th e scheme. It is proposed to include other 
endangered species in the financial year 2010-11. Financial assi stance was provided 
for Protection of wildlife outside protecte d areas in States namely Kerala, Tamil 
Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab last year.   However, due to reduction of fund at R.E. 
stage this year no financial assistance could be provided to States for protection of 
wildlife outside protected areas.   
13.3 The Committee observes that the over all budgetary provision under this head 
for this year is less as compared to the last year B.E. allocation of Rs. 340.77 and R.E. 
allocation of Rs. 329.39 crores.  The Committee fails to u nderstand that at a time 
when wildlife related crimes are on the rise, wildlife animals are coming out of the 
protected areas and mercilessly killed, th e number of endangered species is on the 
rise, reduction in budgetary alloca tions does not augur well.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that this programme may not be allowed to suffer due to 
shortage of funds and the Ministry of Environment & Forests should vigorously 

  
take up this matter with Ministry of Fi nance to increase allocation under this 
head appropriately at the R.E. stage.   
13.4 The National Tiger Conservation Au thority (NTCA), a statutory body under 
the Ministry of Environment and Forests has been constituted under the Wildlife 
(Protection) Act, 1972, as amended in 200 6, for strengthening tiger conservation.  
‘Project Tiger’ is a Centrall y Sponsored Scheme of the Mi nistry of Environment and 
Forests, providing funding support to tiger range States, for in-situ conservation of 
tigers in designated tiger reserves. 
13.5 The Committee has been informed that the National Tiger Conservation 
Authority, constituted in 2006 has been successful in fulf illing its mandate.  Project 
Tiger has put the endangered tiger on an a ssured path of recovery by saving it from 
extinction, as revealed by the recent findi ngs of the All India tiger estimation using 
the refined methodology.  This independent report also highlights the achievement of 
Project Tiger by showing that  viable tiger population exis ts only in Project Tiger 
areas under the jurisdiction of Project Tige r, while outside populations are highly 
depleted.  Many major policy initiatives have been taken on the tiger front for 
improving the infrastructure in tiger reserves, besides strengthening the field delivery.   
13.6 During the year 2009-10, the budgetary allocation for Project Tiger was of Rs. 
184.83 crores at B.E. stage and was reduced  to Rs. 179.53 crores at R.E. stage. For 
the year 2010-2011, it has been reduced fu rther to Rs. 154.20 crores. The Ministry 
was enquired in this regard and it info rmed that the National Tiger Conservation 
Authority (NTCA) receives funding support for performing its functions through the 
ongoing Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Project Tiger.  Reduction in allocation of RE 
2009-10 and BE 2010-11 in respect of the sche me is due to reduction in overall RE 
2009-10 and BE 2010-11 of the Ministry.  It was informed further that Annual Plan 
(2010-11) demand for the scheme was project ed to Rs. 609.30 crores against which 
reduced funds have been allocated.  
13.7 The Committee has been further informed that the Ministry has taken up 
number of steps – legal, administrative a nd financial for protection of tigers.  8 new 
tiger reserves have been declared and for 4 new reserves, namely, Sahyadri in 
Maharashtra, Pilibhit in Uttar Pradesh, Rata pani in Madhya Pradesh and Sunabeda in 
Orissa, in principal approval has been accorded.  The relocation/rehabilitation 
package for people living in core or critical  tiger habitats has been enhanced from Rs. 
1 lakh/family to Rs. 10 lakhs/ family for 13 tiger reserves.  Crea tion of Special Tiger 
Protection Force (STPF) has also been approved and Rs. 93 lakhs each has been 
released to Corbett, Ranthambore and D udhwa Tiger Reserves for creation of STPF 
during 2008-09.  The Ministry further informed the Committee that Central assistance 
will be provided to States for creation of STPF in remaining 10 tiger reserves during 
2009-10.  The Committee was also informed that the guidelines of the STPF have 
also been revised for deploying various pers onnel in place of police with scope for 
involving local people like the Van Gujjars.   However, the actual expenditure 
physical targets and achievements for the year 2008-09 and 2009- 10 does not reflect 
anywhere if the Special Tiger Protection Force for which Rs. 93 lakhs each had been 
released in 2008-09 and the Cen tral assistance proposed to be released in remaining 
10 tiger reserves in 2009-10 has been created  or not.  As such the Committee fails to 
ascertain the actual position as regards the creation of Special Tiger Protection Force.   
13.8 The Committee observes that in the recent past the Similipal Tiger Reserve in

placing the buffer of Simlipal under the administrative control of the Field Director.

14. PREVENTION OF POLLUTION OF NATIONAL RIVERS  
14.1 The Committee has been informed that the scope of the erstwhile scheme 
“Prevention and Control of pollution of Ri ver Ganga” has been widened to cover all 
national rivers and lakes and hence this sc heme has been renamed as “Prevention of 
Pollution of National Rivers and Lakes”.  U nder this scheme, an action plan has been 
drawn up for cleaning the polluted stretches of National Rivers and to oversee its 
implementation.  The major work relates to (i) interception a nd diversion of raw 
sewage water from flowing into rivers through  open drains; (ii) treatment of sewage 
water through setting up of sewage treatment plants; (iii) other sanitation measures, 
such as construction of low cost san itation, electric crematorium, river front 
development works, afforestation on rive r works, etc.  The National Ganga River 
Basin Authority (NGRBA) has been set up as an empowered planning, financing, 
monitoring and coordinating authority for the Ganga River.  

  
14.2  The Committee observes that the Budget allocation for the year 2009-10 was 
revised from Rs.562.33 crore at BE stage to  Rs.413.28 crore at RE stage.  When the 
Committee enquired as to why this happened,  it was informed by the Ministry that 
this revision was due to reduction in allocation for Nationa l Ganga River Basin 
Authority (NGRBA) from Rs.250.00 crore at BE stage to Rs.100.00 crore at RE 
stage.  This revision took place mainly because the Budget announcement on 
allocation for this authority was made in  July 2009 and the detailed Budget head was 
created in August 2009. 
14.3 The Committee finds that for the year  2010-11, an increased allocation of Rs. 
729.64 crore has been made.  The increase as  informed by the Ministry is mainly due 
to increase in the allocation for NGRBA from Rs.100 crore (RE) for the year 2009-10 
to Rs.500 crore for the year 2010-11.  Th e Committee was also informed that the 
Budgetary allocation for National Lake C onservation Plan (NLCP) has also been 
marginally increased from Rs.45 crore (RE)  for the year 2009-10 to Rs.50 crore for 
the year 2010-11.  
14.4 When the Committee enquired about th e status of the constitution of the 
National Ganga River Basin Au thority, it was informed that it has already been 
constituted.  It is chaired by the Prime Minister and Members include some Union 
Ministers, as well as, the Chief Ministers of five States.  This is a high level body and 
is a part of National River Conservation Plan.   
 14.5 The Committee further desired to know from the Ministry as to how much 
money has been spent on Ganga Action Pl an and in what manner National Ganga 
River Basin Authority is different from Ganga Action Plan.  The Ministry replied that 
the Ganga Action Plan was very narrow in its focus as it concentrated on point source 
of pollution, i.e., industrial effluent and sewage and sewerage.  The present 
programme will have a river basin approach  based on four principals, i.e., a river 
must flow, a river is not a drain, a river mu st have space and a river is an ecological 
entity.  It was also informed that roughly an amount of Rs. 900 crores have been spent 
on Ganga Action Plan I and Ganga  Action Plan II.  It was also informed that some 
spillover projects under Ganga Action Plan II are still going on. 
14.6 The Committee feels that despite a huge amount of money spent and whatever 
efforts made thus far under Ganga Action Plan I & II to clean the river Ganga, there 
has not been any considerable improvement in  the water quality of the river Ganga.  
The Committee is happy to note that the Government has initiated a new action to 
clean the river Ganga and allocated a substa ntial amount for this purpose.  It is, 
however, of the opinion that only money is not going to matter much unless and until, 
there is a major change in the strategy.  So  far the Ministry’s main focus had been on 
setting up of sewerage treatment plants, common effluent plants, but the Committee 
feels that this strategy has not worked to the desired level.  Flowing of untreated 
sewage and sewerage, open dr ains and industrial effluent  are still continuing on a 
large-scale.  Sewage treatment plants are not working to their optimum capacity due 
to one reason or the other.  With the ev er increasing load of population on urban 
areas, this problem would further aggravate.   The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that the Ministry must work mainly on the principal that river must flow.  The 
Committee, therefore, further recommends that besides taking remedial 
measures like setting up of sewage trea tment plants, common effluent treatment 
plants, etc., it must also ensure that the flow of the river Ganga is maintained on 
the level of the flow that was there 10 or 20 years back.   

  
15.  YAMUNA ACTION PLAN (YAP) 
15.1 The Yamuna Action Plan (YAP), a b ilateral project between the Government 
of India and Japan to carry out the task of pollution abatement works of the river 
Yamuna is in its second phase.  Its first phase has been completed with the creation of 
a total of 753 mld sewage treatment capacity in  2003.  It is one of the largest river 
restoration projects in India.  The Committee has been informed that YAP II has been 
approved at an estimated cost of Rs. 624 crores for abatement of pollution of river 
Yamuna in Delhi, Uttar Prad esh (eight Towns) and Haryana (six towns). The cost of 
the works is to be shared between Central and the State Governments in the ratio of 
85:15. As per the Ministry a sewage treatme nt capacity of 189 mld is targeted to be 
created besides major rehabilitation/replacement works for sewers and other pollution 
abatement works. So far thirty one pollution abatement schemes have been sanctioned 
at a cost of Rs. 647.86 crore, out of whic h seven schemes have been completed and 
others are in different stages of progress. 
15.2 The Committee observes that the path etic condition of Yamuna River is for 
everybody to see despite all the claims of the Ministry and completion of Yamuna 
Action Plan-I and ongoing Yamuna Action Plan -II and 100 crores of rupees having 
been spent on Yamuna Action Plan.  The Committee feels that the answer to clean the 
Yamuna is not in building more sewage tr eatment capacity or more drains because it 
is expensive to build sewerage drainage a nd even more expensive to maintain them.  
Most of these treatment plants remain gro ssly underutilized because the city does not 
have drainage to convey all sewage to th e treatment plants and hence these plants 
remain grossly underutilized.  The Committee is of the view that despite best of the 
efforts it would not be possible to treat th e entire sewerage flowing into the river and 
hence recommends that the Ministry should look for alternative measures to clean the 
river.  The Committee is of the view that a steady supply of fresh water in the 
river is urgently required.  The Committee in its 188
th Report on Demands for 
Grants, 2008-09 of the Ministry had rec ommended that a proper system should 
be setup for recharging the river by creating manmade water bodies like 
reservoir and catchment areas which can store the monsoon water which usually 
goes into drains and the stored water can be released into the river during the 
lean season.  The Committee also note that most of the catchment area of the 
river Yamuna has been diverted for purposes other than creating reservoirs.  
The Committee, therefore, recommends that a result oriented time bound 
approach should be adopted with regard to the cleaning of river Yamuna.   
16. CLIMATE CHANGE  
16.1 The Committee observes that recen tly Intergovernmental Panel on Climate 
Change (IPCC) Report on projected loss of Himalayan glaciers by 2035 had created a 
lot of confusion and finally it was settle d that the projection made by IPCC in its 
Report cannot be corroborated by scientific evidences.  But the Committee finds that 
the Department of Space has in it s Annual Report 2009-10 mentioned that 
investigations on glacial retreat were es timated for 1317 glaciers in 10 sub-basins 
from 1962 and this has shown an overall reduction in glacial area from 5866 Sq. 
Kms. to 4921 Sq. Kms. showing an overall decrease of 16 per cent.  When the 
Committee enquired in what manner the Ministry would be able to handle the climate 
change project with a paltry allocation of Rs. 7.00 crores, it was informed that this 
project is meant to protect India’s intere sts on climate change issues in various 
international fora and faci litate India’s participation in various international 
negotiations.  Thus, the Committee finds that there is no specific and exclusive 
programme on climate change with the Minist ry which is dealing with environment.  

  
The Committee, therefore, recommends that since the Ministry of Environment 
and Forests is the nodal Ministry, it mu st have a programme on climate change 
with sufficient budgetary allocations.

18. HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCE MANAGEMENT  
18.1 Hazardous waste, bulk of which is generated by the industries, can cause 
environmental pollution and adverse hea lth effects if not handled and managed 
properly. Its effective management, with em phasis on minimizati on of its generation 
and recycling/reuse taking in to account economic aspects, is therefore essential. 
Various actions have been taken to manage  hazardous wastes in the country, which 
include establishing a regulatory and institutional framework, preparation of technical 
guidelines, development of indi vidual and common facilities for recycle/ recovery/ 
reuse, treatment and disposal  of hazardous wastes, prepar ation of an inventory of 
hazardous wastes generation, identification and assessment of hazardous waste dump 
sites for the purpose of preparing remedi ation plans and creating awareness amongst 
various stakeholders. 
18.2 The Committee was informed that during 2009-10, under Hazardous 
Substance Management Schemes, the BE was Rs.17.50 crores which was decreased 
to Rs.7.75 crores at RE stage. It was because under this scheme a provision of 
Rs.10.00 crore was kept in the BE for the Externally Aided Project namely Capacity 
Building for Industrial Pollution Manageme nt Project (CBIPMP). This provision was 
predominantly for implementation phase of  the project. However, the process of 
seeking approval of CCEA and negotiati on with the World Bank could not be 
completed as scheduled during 2009-10. In view of this, RE fo r this project was 
reduced to Rs.0.25 crores, to be used for preparation phase only. Hence the total RE 
is Rs.7.75 crores.   
18.3 It was informed further that the budget allocation for 2010-11 has been 
increased to Rs.32.50 crores against Rs.7 .75 crores RE of 2009-10 in respect of 
Hazardous Substance Management Scheme. The reason for increasing the allocation 
during 2010-11 as compared to the year 2009-10 is the provision of Rs.25.00 crores 
for implementation of CBIPM Pr oject. All pending issues re lated to the project have 
now been resolved and a Note for approva l of CCEA has been sent to the Cabinet 
Secretariat. Implementation of the project will start during 2010-11.  
18.4 For maintaining the eco-system and k eeping in view the climate changes 
and protection of environment and also conservation of forests, the Ministry 
must emphasise and make all efforts to collect full informations and 
clarifications by the concerned Departments and Ministries and if necessary, 
hold regular meetings with the stakeholders in the interest of the Indian 

  
economy. In solving the various issues pending with the Ministry, better 
coordination and cooperation is need of the hour and at the same time the 
Ministry must take final decisions as early as possible while giving permissions 
for the proposals/projects or  rejecting the same. While doing so the Ministry 
must take pains to minimise the delays in giving the clearances to other 
Ministries as quickly as possible. 
18.5 The Committee observes that the rapid industrial developments have led 
to the generation of huge quantities of hazardous wastes which have further 
aggravated the environmental problems  in the country by depleting and 
polluting natural resources. Rational an d sustainable utilization of natural 
resources and its protection from toxic releases is vital for sustainable socio-
economic development. Hazardous substance management involves planning 
and overseeing the implementation of  the policies and programmes. The 
Committee recommends that to attain success under this programme Ministry 
should ensure implementation of efficient plans for management of hazardous 
wastes, environmentally sound manageme nt and active promotion of cleaner 
technologies. 
18.6 The Committee is of the view th at increasing population, rapid 
industrialization, growing urbaniza tion, etc. over the years has made 
tremendous impact on environment.  Th e prevention and control of pollution 
measures have not been commensurate with the pace of adverse impact on 
environment.  The responsibility and wo rkload of the Ministry of Environment 
& Forests whose primary concern is the implementation of policies and 
programmes relating to conservation of the country's natural resources, 
biodiversity, forests and wildlife, the welfare of animals and prevention and 
abatement of pollution has also increased accordingly manifold.  Hence, there is 
a need to correspondingly enhance the manpower, scientific capabilities and 
expertise of the Ministry.  The Committee is of the view that these issues need to 
be urgently addressed. Therefore, it is of the view that the Ministry's manpower 
and infrastructure must be strengthened and augmented.  The Committee, 
therefore, strongly recommends that th e Ministry of Environment and Forests 
should be properly and adequately funded.
