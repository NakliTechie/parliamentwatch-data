        The Committee further feels that for be tter understanding of the moon’ s resources, environmental

Action Taken by the Department 
        It is to bring to the ki nd attention of the Committee that several of the sc ience objectives suggested 
above will be fully or partially achieved during the Chandray aan-1 mission. The following are brought 
to the kind attention of the Committ ee with specific reference to the various suggestions made above by
the Committee: 
        Sl. No. 1, 2 and3: The Chandrayaan-1 mission will carry a terrain mapping camera and a lase r 
ranging instrument to achieve these particular obj ectives and will produce a three dimensional map o f
the lunar surface with a spatial resolution (~5 metr e) that is better than  those achieved earlier.
Determination of size distribution of small craters that will be f easible with the improved spatial
resolution is also one of the science objectives of the Chandrayaan-1 mission. 
        Sl. No. 4: The ages of surface matter can be determined by using dati ng technique once these 
samples can be returned to earth. This would re quire lunar landin g and sam ple return missions, which

Chandrayaan-1 will attempt to ma p Uranium (U) and Thorium (Th) c oncentrations in the lunar pola r
region. 
        Sl. No. 6: There are pos sibilities of modeling the chemical evolution of the l unar crust along with

evolution of the moon and formation of lunar crust. The determinati on of both these elements requires

        Sl. No. 7: The abundance of Heli um is planned to be measured  by one of the international
experiments selected for Chandr ayaan-1 through announcement of o pportunity. However it would be

particle flux in the lunar environm ent and fulfil the above objective.  However more detailed particle

REMOTE SENSING APPLICATIONS MISSION (RSAM) 
Recommendation 
        Although the Committee is satisfied with  the progress achieved by De partment in IRS technology,
it however feels data generated by IRS technology needs proper interpreta tion in view of recent failure
in accurate prediction of the ad vancement of monsoon in Northern
India.                                                                                                      (Para 47) 
Action Taken by the Department 
        In regard to prediction of monsoon, the Depar tment has been providing  valuable inputs from 
INSAT series of satellites to I ndia Meteorology Department (IMD) towards improving its capability fo r
providing accurate short-term weat her forecasting. The data from Kalp ana and INSAT-3A satellites are 
operationally received by IMD, and along with the numerical models available at the National Centre for
Medium Range Weather Forecasti ng, the data is operationally us ed for weather forecasting. Fo r
example, the 3-level wind information available a nd assimilated effectively in the numerical models
improves the accuracies of  short-term weather forecasting. With the proposed INSAT-3D system with 
imagers and sounders, providing temperature and humidity prof iles, further improvements in
understanding the complex tropical weat her systems should be possible. In addition, the data from IRS
satellites such as Oceansat-1 and the planned Oceansat-2 and Megha-Tropiques provide add-on inputs to 
improve the forecasting capability of IMD. 
Comments 
        The Committee believes that a gr eat deal can be done for the perf ection in use of remote sensing 
data as the recent Tsunami disaster ha s revealed that the remote sensing data is not being utilised to the
optimum level. 
Recommendation 
        The Committee, therefore, is of the str ong opinion that in order to in crease accuracy of IRS data
and also for more realistic interpretation of retrieved data, the DoS needs to the following:— 
           (a)  Integration of GIS, GPS and IRS technology to enhance accuracy of derived data; 
           (b)  Production and distribution of satellite data; 
           (c)  Design of different data base by taking the help of the latest technology; 
           (d)  Processing of remote sensing data  using functional approach towards difficulties being faced
by Department for accurate interpretation of the data retrieved; 
           (e)  Carrying out constant latest re search on remote sensing tec hnology facilities and equipment
including sensors and ground stations; 
           (f)   Updating of technology at par with world standards; 
           (g)  Promotion of remote sensing appl ications in co-ordination wi th universities and research 
centres; 
           (h) Holding adequate training c ourses for advanced handli ng of data generation and
interpretation; 
           (i)   On the job training of DoS personnel to considerably enhance their scientific capabilities; 
           (j)   Co-operation and co-ordinati on with international society relating to space science and 
remote sensing technologies;
