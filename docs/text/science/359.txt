schemes is lesser than even the R.E. 2021 -22 allocation by about 23 percent. When enquired

further submitted that the budgetary outlay for schemes for the period from 2021 -22 to 2025-26 
(15th Finance Commission circle) has been kept at 5.5 times of actual expenditure of all schemes 
in the year 2019 -20 of the Department of Bio technology, amounting to a total of Rs. 8447.48 
crore and according B.E. 2022-23 allocation of Rs.1680 crore has been made.   
2.6 The Committee takes note of the fact that particularly, budgetary reduction under 
the Head “Industrial and Entrepreneurship Development” is quite significant. It can be 
seen that the R.E allocation for the Scheme got reduced by 27 percent approximately in 
comparison to the B.E. allocation of the year 2021 -22.  Moreover, the current allocation 
made in B.E. 2022 -23  for the same has been reduced by 50 percent in comparison to the 
already reduced R.E. allocation of 2021 -22. The Committee is at dismay as to how  the 
Department of Biotechnology would be able to carry out its activities with such meager 
allocation. The Committee observes that since the Department has not been able to utilise 
the R.E. 2021 -22 allocations to its fullest, allocation in B.E. 2022 -23 ha ve been reduced 
substantially. The Committee expresses its worry on this constant under -utilisation of 
funds especially under the Central Sector Scheme –„Biotechnology Research & 
Development‟ and strongly recommends the Department to ensure full utilisatio n of the 
funds allocated to it in B.E. 2022-23.

3.2 On the basis of the information provided by the Department in the above -mentioned 
table, the Committee observes that while the achievement of physical targets is satisfactory 
in case of providing support to post graduate teaching programmes and fellowship to 
Junior Researchers (DBT -JRF), the performance of the Department is unsatisfactory in 
awarding fellowships to research associates (DBT -RA). The Department has been able to 
provide Research associateship to only 38 candidates as against its set target of 100, for the 
year 2021-22.   
3.3 The Committee also observes that the reported number of fellows who got job 
placement is very low as compared to the total number of fellowships awarded under 
different categories. While this may be due to under -reporting a nd the fact that many 
fellows may not have apprised the Department about them getting a job placement, the 
Committee strongly recommends the Department to devise a mechanism of keeping the 
record updated in order to enable a much better assessment of the o utcomes and industry 
acceptance of the Department‟s fellowship programmes. In addition, the Committee 
recommends that the Department should form a high level research direction committee 
comprising of experts from academia and industry whose main purpose s hould be to steer

5.4 The Committee notes the data mentioned in the table above and expresses its deep 
dissatisfaction and discontent over the execution of programmes of the Department under 
UMMID initiative. It is agonizing to note that these programmes, though are well planne d 
on the paper, lack actual ground level execution. Identification of rare and genetic diseases 
has a far reaching consequence, not just to the patient and its family but also on the 
biotechnology and bio -pharmacology research and treatment infrastructure.  The 
Committee, therefore, strongly recommends the Department to  identify and resolve the 
lacunae in execution and speed up its efforts to achieve the set targets for the year 2021-22.   
5.5 The Committee also observes that a programme on Sickle Cell Anemi a  is also 
executed by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research and, therefore, 
recommends the Department of Biotechnology to coordinate with the Department of 
Scientific and Industrial Research to ensure synergy of efforts and avoidance of duplicity.   
5.6 The Committee is also perturbed to see that the physical outreach of the three 
components under the UMMID initiative are limited to only few States of the Country. The 
Committee expresses its displeasure over this regional imbalance and recommends the 
Department to expedite its processes and engage with different stakeholders in other 
under-served regions as well. The Department should proactively reach out to District

7. BIOREMIDIATION – FUTURE OF INDIA‟S WASTE MANAGEMENT  
7.1 The Committee, keeping in view the urgent need to treat urban and rural waste and 
remedy the hazards of landfills, sought information about the initiatives of the Department with 
regards to treatment of waste through Biotechnological interventions. The Department submitted 
before the Committee that it has developed various technology platforms to convert different 
solid, liquid and gaseous wastes into renewable fuels, energy and value added products. The 
Department informed that Landfill Leachate is a major source of pollutants and pathogens which 
are lethal for public and environmental health. Treatment of leachate is also mandatory to 
mitigate the risk of the spread of AMR and pathogens in the environment and community. 
7.2 The Committee was informed that the Department has supported waste to energy 
demonstration plants with capacity between 500 Kg/ Day to 10 tons/day, in Telangana, Gujarat, 
and Goa, where organic waste (vegetable market waste, kitchen/hotel waste, organic fraction of 
MSW) has successfully converted to energy. Following biotechnological interventions could be 
made for management of sanitary landfills- 
(i) Anaerobic Digestion, Composting and Bioremediation 
(ii) Recovery of fuel in terms of Methane from stored organic waste 
(iii) Leachate treatment to convert it to compost 
(iv) Risk mitigation for AMR, pathogens spread to community 
7.3 The Committee enquired from the Department, whether bioremediation solutions can be 
implemented at giant sanitary landfills such as the Ghazipur and Bhalswa landfill in Delhi. The 
Committee also probed whether biotechnology can be used for the treatment of waste effluents 
and cleaning of rivers. The Department submitted in a written reply that Bioremediation is a 
natural process that may be a solution for the restoration of organic C arbon Contaminated sites 
including soil/water. In the Bioremediation process, secondary pollutants will not be 
generated. Department of Biotechnology is supporting Research, development and 
demonstration projects on remediation and reclamation of xenobioti c pollutants, degraded soil 
reclamation, oil -based soil contaminants, dye degradation and industrial wastewater, tannery 
waste, etc.  
7.4 The Department informed that the f ollowing focused interventions for waste management 
and pollutant removal are being focused on- 
(i) Phytoremediation, Algal Bioremediation,  
(ii) Microbial-Bioremediation/Geomicrobial bioremediation;  
(iii) Microbial degradation of dumpsite leachate, and, 
(iv) Biologically assisted treatment for crude oil pollutants;  
(v) Waste paper recycling, Bio-mineralization;  
7.5 The Committee, given the potential applications of Biotechnological interventions in 
finding scalable and sustainable solutions to waste treatment, enquired whether Bioremediation 
can prove to be the future. The Department replied that Bioremediation is a futuristic technology 

13 
 
 
in resolving the current and upcoming challenges for cleaner and productive management of 
waste including sanitary landfills and degradation of pollutants in India vis-à-vis mitigating the 
impact of associated climate change. Hence, these technologies may be promoted for scale up to 
provide environment friendly and sustainable solutions. 
7.6 The Committee observes the efforts of the Department in promotion of research and 
development of technological platforms towards finding solutions for waste treatment. The 
Committee therefore recommends that the Department should promote its efforts in a 
holistic manner and involve other stakeholders as well so that the country can take lead in 
using c heap, effective and environmentally sustainable methods in dealing with the 
problem of waste treatment and management.
