INTRODUCTION
The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change is the nodal agency in the administrative
structure of the Central Government for planning, promoting, co-ordinating and overseeing environmental,
ecological, forestry and wildlife policies and programmes. Its main activities include conservation and
survey of natural resources including flora, fauna, forests, wildlife, aquatic bodies and water resources
and eco-systems, prevention and control of pollution and management of hazardous substances, protection
of the environment and forests, including their biodiversity, mitigation and adaptation to climate change
through measures that include afforestation, regeneration of degraded areas, and adoption of cleaner
technologies, and promoting sustainable development and better environmental governance while protecting
human, plants and animals life, health and welfare.
1.2 The main tools used to discharge its mandate include, inter alia,  conducting surveys; collecting,
compiling and disseminating data and information; undertaking scientific research, extension services and
analyses; carrying out impact assessment of developmental projects and programmes; regulating use of
natural resources; undertaking regeneration programmes; extending technical, scientific, financial and
administrative support to organisations and institutions in the subject fields; imparting education, training,
building capacity and generating awareness among stakeholders and the public at large; and promoting
international co-operation through bilateral, regional and multilateral initiatives in environment and ecology.
1.3. The institutional set up of the Ministry includes a number of Directorates and Divisions assembled
around the main thematic sectors of environment and ecology, national river conservation, forestry and
wildlife, afforestation and eco-development, and animal welfare. The Ministry has ten Regional Offices;
twelve Attached/Subordinate Offices (e.g. Botanical, Zoological and Forest Survey of India, and Directorate
of Forestry Education); Boards and Authorities (e.g. Central Pollution Control Board, National Biodiversity
Authority, National Tiger Conservation Authority, Central Zoo Authority and Animal Welfare Board of
India); Autonomous Institutions ( e.g. Indian Council for Forestry Research and Education and Wildlife
Institute of India); and one Public Sector Undertaking, namely, the Andaman & Nicobar Islands Forest
Plantation Development Corporation Ltd. at Port Blair. Provision has been made in the Demands for

4. FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE IN 2016-17
4.1 The Committee notes that against the RE allocation of `````2060.23 cr. in 2016-17, the Ministry
has been able to spend only `````1459.58 cr. upto 13th February, 2017, which is nearly 71%. Since
the Ministry will be required to utilise nearly 29% of the Budget allocation in about one and a
half months time left in the completion of financial year, the Committee is afraid that the
Ministry will not be able to fully utilise the amount allocated to it for the financial year
2016-17.
4.2 The Committee desired to know the reasons for lower expenditure in 2016-17 as well as the whole
12th Five year Plan period. The Ministry informed the Committee that the lower utilistion in 2016-17 was
mainly on account of general administrative and procedural reasons, like delays in receipt of proposals,
lack of adequate proposals, non/late submission of Utilisation Certificates, revalidation of unspent balances,
completion of works, etc. In many schemes, implementing agencies are State Governments/Forest
Departments, whose performance depends on timely release of matching grants. Time lag in release of
funds by the Ministry and actual availability of funds to such implementing agencies also impacts on the
performance of these schemes. It was also informed that regular meetings are held with the Divisional
Heads in-charge of the Programmes/Schemes to expedite utilization levels. Instructions have been issued
to the Programme Divisions/autonomous bodies/ attached offices from time-to-time to improve the pace
of utilization.
4.3 The Committee notes the reasons for shortfall in utilisation of funds furnished by Ministry
of Environment, Forest & Climate Change. The Committee had come across identical reasons
for underutilization while examining the Demands for Grants 2016-17 of Ministry of Environment,
Forest & Climate Change and had recommended the Ministry to take a review of its financial
performance. In this context, the Committee would like to reiterate the recommendation made
in its 283rd Report on Demands for Grants (2016-17) of Ministry of Environment, Forest &
Climate Change which is as under:-
"Most of the reasons cited by the Ministry for under-utilisation of funds are administrative and
procedural which could have been avoided. This also shows lack of planning and foresightedness
on part of the Ministry. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should undertake
a review of its financial performance during the last few years and try to identify the bottlenecks
responsible for not being able to optimally utilise the funds at its disposal so as to improve its
performance on this front in the future".

8. ANIMAL WELFARE
8.1 The Ministry is mandated to undertake activities relating to animal welfare which is intended to
prevent the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering on animals, for which a 3-pronged approach is
followed covering regulation, development and education. Animal Welfare Division, entrusted with
implementation of provisions of Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960, has set up two statutory
bodies viz., Animal Welfare Board of India (AWBI) and Committee for the Purpose of Supervision and
Control of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) under the Act.
8.2 The Committee observed that in the activities relating to Animal Welfare, `2.54 cr. could be spent
upto 31.1.2017, out of the total allocation of `6.75 cr. in RE 2016-17 and desired to know the reasons
for underutilization. In response, the Ministry informed the Committee that funds could not be released
under the Animal welfare schemes due to the condition that funds can be released to those Animal
Welfare Organizations only who are registered with NITI Aayog portal and against whom no Utilisation
Certificate is pending. At present, proposals amounting to `2.31 cr. are also under examination in
consultation with Integrated Finance Division of the Ministry. However, the division is making efforts
to utilise the available budget before the end of the current financial year.
8.3 The Committee is of the view that animal welfare must get due attention of the Ministry.
The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry for underutilisation of funds and recommends
that while following due procedure for release of funds, the Ministry should ensure that there
is no shortfall in the funds meant for preventing the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering
on animals.

15. CHIRU GOAT (TIBETAN ANTELOPE)
15.1 During the meeting of the Committee held on 14th February 2017, the Committee, while discussing
Wildlife Conservation, observed that Chiru Goats were being used by certain communities in Jammu and
Kashmir for extracting wool and making shahtoosh shawls. The Committee desired to know about
wildlife status of the chiru goats. In response, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Environment,
Forest and Climate Change that Tibetan antelope (Panthelop hodgsoni)  from which the shahtoosh is
derived is included under Schedule-I of Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. This species is highly endangered
and any commercial trade is strictly prohibited. There are only few animals left in the wild and as it is
critically endangered, the species is listed in Schedule-I of the Act, thereby according it highest degree
of protection. Hunting of the species and trade of its parts and products, including shawls made of Chiru
wool are prohibited under the Act. Any contravention of the provision of the Act, attracts stringent
penalties. As Chiru cannot be used for extraction of wool for shahtoosh wool as per the prevalent Act,
alternate livelihood for the affected communities needs to be taken up by the State Government. The
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change would extend support for alternative livelihood for
the affected communities.
15.2 The Committee feels that Jammu and Kashmir has been the conflict region for long and
many women there are unable to leave their homes and go out for livelihood. The shawl walas
who live there have been accused of killing the chiru goat in order to procure the wool for
making a shahtoosh shawl. However, the shawl makers say that they do not kill the goats
because if they kill a goat for every shawl, where will the shawls be produced from. The
Committee understands that China and Mongolia are breeding the chiru goats and Mongolia is

23
known for its finest wool. The Committee feels that livelihood opportunities are very low for the
people of Jammu and Kashmir because of the extreme climatic conditions there and shawl
making contributes a lot towards sustainable livelihoods of these people. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should conserve and
breed the chiru goat and vast tract of land should be utilised for conserving the chiru goats.
These goats can then be given to the shawl makers for collecting hair. This would not only
increase the number of these goats but would also add to the sustainable livelihood opportunities
of the people of Jammu and Kashmir , who are a lot dependant on the handicraft of embroidered
shawls.
16. THE CAMPA FUNDS
16.1 During the meeting of the Committee held on 14th February 2017, the Secretary of the Ministry,
in his presentation, informed the Committee that the Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act 2016 was
passed on the 3rd August 2016. The Act provides for the establishment of funds under the public
accounts of India and the public accounts of each State and crediting thereto the monies received from
the user agencies. The money is to be utilized for undertaking artificial regeneration (plantations), assisted
natural regeneration, protection of forests, forest related infrastructure development, Green India
Programme, wildlife protection and other related activities. In response to a query whether plantations
under CAMPA can be used for commercial viability, the representative of the Ministry informed the
Committee as under:
"No, Madam. It is not like that. There are two parts of CAMP A funds. One is about compensatory
afforestation. In that, either an area equal to the piece of land from where trees were removed for
non-forestry purpose, or, in some cases, double the degraded forest land has to be planted. It is not
necessary that these plantations will be of single species. It is not like that. It is not meant for
commercial purpose. This is basically compensation for the loss of the forest which had been
diverted for non-forestry purpose. The sentence here means compensatory afforestation. Also, we are
doing artificial regeneration as a part of the NPV (Net Present Value) funds. It is for the loss of
ecosystem services. It is not commercial plantation."
16.2 The Committee expressed apprehension that some reports suggested that Ministry of Road Transport
and Highways intended to do tree plantations along the National Highways using CAMPA funds. It was
also brought out that during its study visit to some States, the Committee was informed that CAMPA
funds were being used for many activities which otherwise do not come under the ambit of CAMPA or
had no relation whatsoever with the afforestation activities. In response, the Ministry informed the
Committee that the CAMPA funds are utilized as per the Guidelines of the Adhoc CAMPA which was
constituted by the Hon'ble Supreme Court. CAMPA has come into being with effect from 3rd August,
2016 and the Ministry is now in the process of framing rules in consultation with the States and
concerned stake holders so as to provide uniformity in the utilization of compensatory levies collected

18. CENTRE OF EXCELLENCE - CENTRE FOR ECOLOGICAL SCIENCES, (INDIAN INSTITUTE
OF SCIENCE), BENGALURU.
18.1 The Scheme Centre of Excellence was initiated in the Year 1983. The objective of the Scheme is
to provide 'selective support' to Universities, reputed institutions including NGOs, professional organisations
and other scientific and technical institutions to enable them to develop into advanced Centres at the
national level in that particular subject area. The broad objectives of the scheme are as under:
• To enhance and strengthen the country's human, scientific technological, organizational,
institutional and resource capabilities with a view to achieving economic development without
damaging the environment.
• To encourage and support universities/institutions, NGOs to undertake activities to develop or
strengthen the capacities and capabilities of the identified units so that they may contribute to
the process of the sustainable development.
• To enhance and upgrade existing skills, knowledge and technical know-how of the universities/
research institutions/NGOs so that may be able to integrate environment and development.
• To enhance the efficiency of existing universities, institutions and NGOs for devising national
sustainable development strategies in partnership with the Ministry of Environment, Forests
and Climate Change.
• To create awareness among various sections of the population of the country regarding
environmental issues with a view to encourage and enhancing public participation in the
programmes and activities of the MoEF&CC.
18.2 At present, there are eight Centres of Excellence which are operational under different themes. CES,
IISc, Bengaluru is one of them. The CES, IISc, functions under the Indian Institute of Science and
carries out projects agreed to and approved by the Research Advisory and Monitoring Committee
(RAMC) constituted by MoEF&CC for which the financial assistance is provided by the MoEF&CC. The
CES, IISc undertakes research with practical applications in frontline areas of ecology/environment of
the Western Ghats under the following themes:
(a) Community, forest and wildlife ecology
(b) Animal Behaviour and Evolution
(c) Human and Landscape Ecology
(d) Climate Change and its Impact
(e) Conservation Outreach, Training and Education
18.3 CES is engaged in high quality original research on the biodiversity of the Western Ghats which is
one of the global hotspots of biodiversity. This research ranges from microfauna such as ants and

26
termites to megafauna such as elephants, as well as the floristics and ecology of tropical forests. Unique
research being conducted in CES involves such subjects as (1) acoustic sampling of the biodiversity of
tropical forests, (2) reconstruction of the palaeoclimate of the southern Western Ghats with bearing on
present scenarios of climate change, (3) investigating the impact of atmospheric pollution such as
tropospheric ozone on the half-life of chemical signals emitted by plants to attract their pollinating insects,
and (4) long-term research on tropical forest dynamics and carbon sequestration. Research in CES has
not solely been concentrated on the terrestrial ecology of the Western Ghats but has also been focused
on freshwater ecosystems including freshwater fish and molluscs. Over the years, since its inception,
this research has resulted in 1024 research papers, 25 books, and 122 technical reports. The research
papers of CES have been published in international and national scientific journals of high repute. In
recognition of their outstanding caliber, CES faculty have also been elected to national and international
Science Academies such as the National Academy of Sciences (USA), The World Academy of Sciences,
Indian Academy of Sciences and the Indian National Science Academy. Members of the CES faculty have
also received prestigious awards such as the Order of the Golden Ark and the International Cosmos Prize
for their conservation-related research.
18.4 The scientific output/information/data collected by the Centre of Ecological Sciences are used by
the various departments/research institutions of the country. The information is disseminated through
publishing technical research papers in the national and international journals, organizing conference/
seminar/symposia, publishing of books and putting the achievements on the website on CES/IISc. The
information is also shared with ENVIS for use by the various departments of the Ministry.

19. INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF WILDLIFE HABITATS
19.1 The Committee was informed that this Centrally sponsored Scheme has the following components:-
(a) Project Tiger
19.2 India launched the holistic "Project Tiger" in 1973 to conserve our critically endangered national
animal - the tiger. Based on the recommendations of the Tiger Task Force, the Wildlife (Protection) Act,
1972 was amended in 2006 for creating the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) to give
statutory support for strengthening tiger conservation. The coverage of "Project Tiger" has increased
from 9 reserves since inception to 48 tiger reserves spread out in 18 States of the country in 2015.
Though the focus is on the flagship / umbrella species 'tiger' the project strives to maintain the stability
of ecosystem by fostering other tropic levels in the food chain thereby ensuring healthy forests, clean
air, clean water and a clean environment. NTCA retains oversight through advisories / normative guidelines,
based on appraisal of tiger status, ongoing conservation initiatives and recommendations of specially
constituted Committees. All India Tiger Estimation, 2014 results shows that India now has 2226 tigers
which are 30% increase over the last count and is 70% of the total global tiger population.
(b) Project Elephant
19.3 Project Elephant was launched in 1991-92 as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme with following
objectives:
(i) To protect elephants, their habitat and corridors;
(ii) To address issues of man-animal conflict; and
(iii) Welfare of domesticated elephant.

elephant bearing States in the country. Under the scheme, 100% financial assistance is provided to the
concerned State Government for undertaking various activities for scientific management of elephant

proposed Elephant Reserves in the country, which are still to be notified by the concerned State
Governments, covering approximately 65,000 sq. km. area.

21.2 The Committee was informed by the representatives of the State Government that Kutch district
in Gujarat state covering an area of 45,674 sq. km. is the largest district of India. The population of
Kutch is 2,092,371 (2011). The district consists of 10 Talukas, 939 villages and 6 Municipalities. Kutch
District is surrounded by the Gulf of Kutch and the Arabian Sea in south and west, while northern and
eastern parts are surrounded by the Great and Little Rann (seasonal wetlands) of Kutch. Bhuj is the
district headquarters of Kutch District. The land of Kutch which supports forest vegetation mostly
consists of undulated plains with scattered hillocks therein. Some patches are in plains, some with deeply
cut riverbeds and larger tracts are of pasturelands.
21.3 The Committee was further informed that the Rann is a salt impregnated and wilderness area and
is often referred as 'saline waste'. The Rann is only a few feet above the mean sea level and is spread
over 23309 sq. kms. Rann of Kutch is divided into two parts; one to the north known as the Greater
Rann of Kutch and the other towards the east is known as the 'Little Rann of Kutch'. The Greater Rann
of Kutch consists of a vast expanse of tidal mud flats with saline efflorescence. Strata of northern strip
of Kutch belongs to the Jurassic period with recent alluvium on the low land. In the southern portion
of the Greater Rann of Kutch, there is a large stretch of alluvium called the 'Banni' having area of about
2497 sq. kms. It was informed that this tract seems to have been once a part of the Rann but later on
was covered by fresh alluvial deposits brought down by rives. In the hilly areas, the soil is very shallow
often existing only in the crevices of rocky areas.
21.4 Kutch is rich in biodiversity and is represented by diverse flora and fauna. The varied geographical,
geological and climatic conditions has blessed Kutch district with diverse forest areas ranging from grass
lands, arid tropical forests to mangrove vegetations. The forests are mainly distributed between two
divisions, Kutch east and Kutch west division which are a part of the Kutch forest circle formed for
administrative reasons. The details are as under:

22. PRIVATE LAND PARTICIPATION IN PRESERV ATION AND CONSERV ATION OF FORESTS
22.1 During the meeting of the Committee held on the 14th February 2017, the Committee desired to
know as to whether forests can be raised on private lands. The Committee had also received request
to this effect from some persons. Responding to the query of the Committee, the representative of the
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change responded as under:
"Madam, there was also a question on whether forests can be raised on private lands. Y es, they
can be raised on private lands. The issue of permission for tree-felling depends on the State as
well as on the species. Different States have different regulations. But the local regulations would
apply and we welcome if anyone wants to raise tree plantations on their land. It is going to add
to the tree cover of the country."
22.2 Some of the suggestions received by the Committee that should form the fundamental framework
in this context are as under:
• This should apply to only private lands and restriction of 'Rural land Ceiling' should not apply.
• Participation should be a voluntary process, which cannot be reversed for perpetuity.
• The owner's easement on the entire land should remain intact. This includes sale or transfer
without any change in the nature of the land i.e. permanent preservation of the forest.
• Restriction on area that can be developed (Not exceeding 5% for instance).
• Encouragement of Eco-friendly agriculture, nature/wildlife tourism, flora and fauna reintroduction
programs and research.
• Monetary compensation only in form of Income Tax Breaks over a long period of time, say
20 to 30 years (To bring in sincere participation and not make it another tax break tool).
• 'Cooperation framework' to work with the forest departments.
22.3 The Committee feels that as the forest cover of the country has been drastically and
constantly depleting, there is a need to involve private sector in increasing the forest cover of
the country by encouraging private land participation in preservation and conservation of forests.
There are a lot of people in the country who are having large landholdings and that they would
be happy to contribute to increasing the forest cover of the country. This would also give a boost
to the shrinking forest cover of the country. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of
Environment, Forest and Climate Change should consider the suggestions received by the
Committee as mentioned above. The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should
consider giving tax exemptions and compensations to those who proposed to raise forests on the
big areas of land available with them. They should be incentivized only when the land available
with them grows as forest, so that the green area cover of the country grows. This will not only
increase India's forest cover, meet some of the green India mission requirements but also bring
in much needed private participation in conservation and preservation of forests.
