REPORT 
 
The Action Taken Report (ATR) of the Committee deals with the action taken by the 
Government on the recommendations/observations contained in Three Hundred Thirty -first Report of 
the Committee on 'Demands for Grants (2020 -21) of the Ministry of En vironment, Forests & Climate 
Change'. The Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of  Lok Sabha on the 6 th 
March, 2020. 
2.  Action Taken Note (ATN) has been received from Ministry of Environment, Forests & Climate 
Change in respect of all the ten recommendations/observations contained in the Three Hundred Thirty -
first Report. This has been categorized as follows:- 
Chapter I: Recommendations/Observations which have been accepted by the Government:         
Para:-  2.2, 2.5, 6.2, 8.4 and 8.5  
(Total –05) 
Chapter II:  Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue 
in view of the Government’s replies:

(Total –04). 
The Committee is convinced with the explanation furnished by the Ministry and, therefore, does 
not want to pursue above-mentioned recommendations further. 
Chapter  III:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the Government 
have not been accepted by the Committee:    Nil. 
Chapter IV:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the 
Government are still awaited:   
 Para 5.4  
(Total –01).

BUDGETARY DETAILS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2019 -20 AND ALLOCATION FOR 
2020-21 
Recommendation/ Observation  
1.1 The Committee notes that the BE 2020 -21 allocation of the Ministry has increased by Rs.442 
crores over RE 2019 -20 and hopes that the Ministry would be able to make optimum utilisation of 
funds allocated during the year.                                                                                                  (Para 2.2)           
Action Taken 
1.2 Recommendation of the Committee for making optimum utilization of funds allocated during 
the year is noted for complianc e and it is also informed that due to COVID -19 pandemic, the Finance 
Ministry has kept the Ministry in Category 'C' for the first two quarters of 2020 -2021 restricting 
budgetary allocation by 10% per quarter under austerity measures. 
Recommendation/ Observation  
1.3 The Committee is of the view that both the above programmes, especially National Clean Air 
Programme, are of utmost importance in the present day context, and controlling air pollution must be 
given the topmost priority.  The allocation of addit ional funds would help support additional 22 non -
attainment cities under ‘Control of Pollution’ scheme.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the 
Ministry must be provided the requisite allocation as sought by the Ministry with respect to these two 
programmes at the RE stage.                                                                                                       (Para 2.5)       
                                                                                                            
Action Taken 
1.4 Recommendation of the Committee for providing the requisite allocation as sought by the 
Ministry will be requested to the Ministry of Finance.  The funds to both the programmes and including 
more cities in addition to the existing cities under Control of Pollution Scheme will be considered at the 
RE stage. 
GREEN INDIA MISSION - NATIONAL AFFORESTATION PROGRAMME  
Recommendation/ Observation  
1.5 The Committee acknowledges that the National Afforestation Programme is one of the 
important programm es of the Government for protection and conservation of natural resources 
through the active involvement of the people.  However, the utilisation of funds under the scheme falls 
short of the allocation made for the purpose, and is highly unsatisfactory.                                (Para 6.2)             
                                                  
Action Taken 
1.6 With regard to Committee’s observation on utilization of allocated funds, it is submitted that the 
budgetary allocation of Rs. 157.50 cro re for 2018 -19 and Rs.140.00 crore during 2019 -20 is the 
combined allocation for both the schemes i.e. for both National Afforestation Progrogramme (NAP) and 
Green India Mission (GIM). Out of the total combined budgetary allocation, NAP utilized/released 
funds to the tune of Rs.95.38 crores in 2018 -19 and Rs.21.98 crores in 2019 -20 respectively. Under 

3 
 
GIM an amount of Rs.80.75 crores and Rs.116.75 crores were released during 2018 -19 and 2019 -20 
respectively. Hence, total budgetary allocation has been fully utilized. 
 
MAN-ANIMAL CONFLICT 
Recommendation/ Observation  
1.7 The Committee notes the compensation available for human/cattle/crop loss in case of man -
animal conflict and the financial assistance provided by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and 
Climate Change to the States/Union Territories in such cases.  The Committee observes that the 
amount of compensation varies a lot from state to state.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry 
of Environment, Forest and Climate Change must take initiative to carry out a comprehensive review 
of the compensation being provided in different states and frame guidelines to align the compensation 
at an equivalent and realistic level, so that the affected persons are suitably compensated for the loss 
suffered by them equitably all over the country.                                                                         (Para 8.4)                                              
Action Taken 
1.8 In the past, there were different  ex-gratia payment for loss of life or injury , crop damage or 
properties by wild animals existed across the states. In order to streamline the  ex-gratia payment 
mechanism, the Ministry has reviewed the system and enhanced the ex -gratia relief payable to the 
victims under the centrally sponsored schem es in 2018 and communicated to all States/UTs vide letter 
dated 09.02.2018 (Annexure I). The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change  also 
provides funding assistance to States/UTs for ex -gratia to the victims of Human Wildlife Conflicts 
through Centrally Sponsored Schemes of Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitat(IDWH), as per 
the activities envisaged in the Project Tiger, Project Elephant and Development of Wildlife Habitat in 
sanctuaries and national parks. The Ministry is constantly review ing periodically to make the 
compensation equivalent and realistic so that the affected persons are suitably compensated for the loss 
suffered by them. 
Recommendation/ Observation  
1.9 The Committee also takes a serious view of the cases of revenge killing  of animals by the 
affected persons.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry must impress upon the State 
Governments/Union Territories to ensure that prompt payment of compensation is made to the persons 
affected by man -animal conflicts on time, which would deter people resorting to retaliatory killing of 
wild animals.                                                                                                                                (Para 8.5)                         
Action Taken 
1.10 States/UTs have been advised to make prompt payment of ex -gratia to the persons affected by 
Human Wildlife Conflict vide this Ministry's letter dated 10.07.2020(Annexure II). Through this letter 
the States/UTs have also been requested to review the rates of ex -gratia applicable in case of Human 
Wildlife Conflicts in their States/UT's and increase the same to bring t hem at par with other States/UTs 
and other types of conflicts in the States/UT, undertake systemic reforms for making prompt payment, 
and continuous re view at the higher levels to ensure prompt payment by resolving inter -departmental 
issues. The Ministry will be reviewing with the States/UTs this item on a continuous basis.

ALLOCATION FOR CONTROL OF POLLUTION TO MoHUA  
Recommendation/ Observation 
 
2.1 The Committee observes that the allocation of  Rs.4,400 crore for pollution control to Ministry 
of Housing and Urban Affairs is more than the entire budget allocation for 2020 -21 for the Ministry of 
Environment, Forest and Climate Change. The Committee notes that there are 46 cities in India, with a 
population above 1 million.  The Committee recommends that these 46 cities can be kep t out of the 102 
non-attainment cities identified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change to take up 
measures for the abatement and control of air pollution .  This would also help the Ministry reduce the 
shortfall of funds being sought by them for their Control of Pollution scheme.                         (Para 3.2)                                                                          
 
Action Taken 
2.2 The operational guidelines for the implementation of the recommendations on Urban Loc al body 
grants contained in Chapter 5(Local Body Grants) of the Fifteen th Finance Commission (FC -XV) 
Report has been issued by the Ministry of Finance, Dept. of Expenditure vide report 15(2)FC -
XV/FCD/2020-25 dated 01.06.2020.   
As per observation of the Pa rliamentary Standing Committee, Ministry of Environment, Forest 
and Climate Change has decided to  limit the funding to cities which are not covered under XV -FC 
recommendations. 
 
BUDGETARY SUPPORT & ACTUALLY UTILISED DURING THE LAST TWO YEARS 
Recommendation/ Observation 
2.3 The Committee notes the pattern of utilisation of allocated amount by the Ministry during the 
last two financial years and finds the same satisfactory.  However, the Committee notes that during 
2019-20, whereas only 50% of the allocation has been utilised with respect to Schemes, the expenditure 
under the Non -Scheme has gone beyond the RE allocation.  The Committee also notes that the BE 
allocation of the Ministry was reduced by nearly Rs.300 crores at RE stage in the current financial 
year, which indicates a slow start to the schemes in the initial phase, thereby leading to the reduction in 
allocation at the RE stage.  The Committee is of the view that the Ministry needs to take note of this 
issue and take corrective measures to avoid the same in the future.  The Committee urges the Ministry 
to optimally utilise the allocation made to it during the remaining period of the financial year 2019-20.   
                                                                                                                                                     (Para 4.2)

4 which is r e-produced as table 4.1 of the said report. The Actual Expenditure as on 31.01.2020 for 
Scheme is Rs 1391.71 crore out of 1645.62 crore and for Non -Scheme, Rs 828.09 crore out of Rs 
1012.32 crore. The expenditure in both Scheme and Non -Scheme is in proport ion to the budget 
allocation.

R&D Scheme for Conservation and Development 
Recommendation/ Observation 
2.6 The Committee finds that during the last three years, the allocation under the above scheme has 
been utilised to the tune of only 25%.  The Committee urges the Ministry to introspec t on the poor 
usage of funds allocated to Research and Development, and take measures to ensure that such funds 
that are vital, are not grossly underutilised in future.                                                                 (Para 7.3)                                                                                      
                                                                                           
Action Taken 
2.7 The Scheme on Research & Development had ended as on 31 st March 2017 with t he end of 
XIIth Five Year Plan. However, there were a large number of on -going projects under the old scheme 
which required to be completed. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change had also 
begun the process of preparing a new scheme on R&D for Conservation & Development. The SFC for 
the new Scheme was approved only in last quarter of 2017. The approved new scheme required that the 
entire scheme was implemented on a web portal from receipt of on -line applications, their appraisal, 
processing for funding and approval of releases of expenditure. This work was entrusted to NIC and the 
scheme was started online in August 2018 for receipt of online applications. However, the applications 
received could be considered only during 2019 as the Technica l & Financial Appraisal Committee 
(TFAC) was constituted only in February 2019. With the constitution of the Technical and Financial 
Appraisal Committee, a number of meetings were held and a total of 16 new projects were approved by 
December 2019 for fundi ng.  As a result, Budget Estimates of R&D Scheme for Conservation & 
Development was reduced for the FY 2019 -20, from Rs. 10 cr to Rs 4.5 cr. at RE stage, which was 
fully utilised. Also, with the Budget cut in all schemes of the GOI beyond 31 st December 2019, many 
of the approved projects could not be funded during 2019-2020.  
2.8 The new R&D Scheme is now fully functional on -line on a dedicated web portal and a number 
of applications have been received for funding during 2020-21.

UTILISATION OF CAMPA FUND 
Recommendation/ Observation 
3.1 The Committee observes that CAMPA is a huge fund.  However, its guidelines have been 
drafted in such a manner that its resources cannot be used for the schemes/programmes of the Ministry 
with similar objectives where it requires the money most.  The Committee is of the view that CAMPA 
funds have accumulated due to deforestation.  There is, there fore, no reason that the Green India 
Mission, which primarily aims at afforestation, cannot be funded out of the CAMPA fund.  The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should explore 
the possibility of allocating CAMPA funds for those schemes/programmes of the Ministry having 
similar objectives as envisaged in the activities to be undertaken by the CAMPA fund, even if it 
requires amendment to the CAMPA Act/Rules and the Committee would support the Ministry in this 
matter.  This would also, to an extent, reduce the financial burden on the Ministry and it would be able 
to utilise the fund for its schemes with similar objectives.                                                          (Para 5.4)
                                                                                                                            
Action Taken 
3.2 The combined release of funds to the States/UTs under the National Afforestation Programme 
(NAP) and the Green India Mission (GIM) was to the tune of Rs. 138.73 crore during financial year 
2019-20. 
3.3 Further, a number of items of work that are permissible under the GIM are also permissible 
under the CAF Act, 2016 and CAF Rules, 2018. Such activities are being funded already under the 
State fund as per Annual Plan of Operations (APOs) being submitted by the State Governments/UT 
Administration under the CAF Act, 2016 and CAF Rules, 2018 on a regular basis. Thus, under 
CAMPA during 2019-20, Afforestation and regeneration activities over 4 lakh  hectares were approved 
at a total cost Rs 1261.10 crore in the APOs of 20 States. The Ministry is examining the issue under 
CAMPA of enhancing synergy amongst its different schemes and programmes related to 
improvement/expansion of forest and tree cover i ncluding restoration of degraded forests which is one 
of the major objectives of the Green India Mission. Progress on this will be communicated through 
Supplementary ATR within a period of three months.  
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
3.4 The Committe e notes the reply of the Ministry that it is examining the issue under 
CAMPA of enhancing synergy amongst its different schemes and programmes related to 
improvement/expansion of forest and tree cover including restoration of degraded forests which 
is one of the major objectives of the Green India Mission. The Committee hopes that the progress 
on this issue will be communicated to the Committee through Supplementary ATR within three 
months from submission of this Action Taken Note, as promised by the Ministry.
