1.  INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Department of Science & Technology (DST) is the nodal agency of the country  
for the promotion, coordination and strengthening of Science, Technology and Innovation 
(STI) in the country. It plays a central role in identifying critical gap areas across diverse 
S&T sectors and formulating appropriate policies, programmes and strategic interventions to 
address emerging societal, industrial and national developmental needs. The Department is 
also entrusted with promotin g frontier and emerging areas of science and technology in 
anticipation of future demands, technological disruptions and global advancements. 
1.2 In addition to its policy and planning functions, DST acts as a key interface linking 
the science and technolo gy ecosystem with various Ministries and Departments of the 
Government (both at the horizontal and vertical levels), as well as with academia, national 
laboratories, research and development institutions, and industry. Through this integrative 
approach, th e Department facilitates collaboration, convergence and knowledge exchange 
across stakeholders, thereby strengthening the overall innovation value chain in the country. 
1.3 With the overarching objective of enhancing national S&T capacity and capability, 
DST provides the largest share of extramural research and development support in India. This 
support is extended to scientists and researchers across institutions and disciplines through a 
competitive and merit -based mechanism. Earlier structured under thre e umbrella schemes  
namely (i) Science and Technology (S&T) Institutional and Human Capacity Building, (ii) 
Research and Development, and (iii) Innovation, Technology Development and Deployment , 
these programmes collectively aimed at reinforcing the country’s higher education system, 
scientific research base, industrial R&D ecosystem, and the broader Science, Technology and 
Innovation landscape. 
1.4 In order to enhance administrative efficiency and enable greater flexibility in the flow 
and utilizatio n of funds across programmes, these three umbrella schemes have been 
consolidated into a single unified scheme titled ‘Vigyan Dhara’, with the approval of the 
Union Cabinet. The restructuring is intended to promote optimal resource allocation, foster 
inter-programmatic synergies, and ensure responsive implementation aligned with national 
priorities. 
1.5 The Department continues to pursue sustained efforts toward strengthening and 
deepening the national STI ecosystem, with a view to positioning India as a gl obal forerunner 
in science and technology. Through its strategic initiatives and policy interventions, DST 
seeks to facilitate positive societal transformation, contribute to the creation of a safe, secure 
and inclusive society, and build resilience to eff ectively address future challenges and 
disruptions.

3.2 It was further submitted by  the Department that the establishment of the Anusandhan 
National Research Foundation (ANRF) and the operationalisation of the Research, 
Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund constitute a significant restructuring of India’s 
national research funding architecture. ANRF has been mandated to support mission -oriented 
and translational research through convergence across academia, industry and government, 
while the RDI Fund is designed to promote commercialisation, industry -led R&D, DeepTech 
validation and high  Technology Readiness Level (TRL) innovation. In this evolving 
ecosystem, the Department of Science and Technology (DST) is required to anchor the 
foundational tier of the research system. 
3.3 Together, the three institutions create a complementary, tiered  R&D architecture , 
which constitutes about 83.51% of the total BE 2026 -27 (₹28,049.32 crore) of the 
Department: 
 RDI Fund: High-TRL, market-ready innovation and commercialization 
 ANRF: Mission-driven translational research   
 Vigyan Dhara: Foundational science, human capital, research infrastructure, STI 
data, State ecosystems

4.  RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT & INNOVATION (RDI) FUND 
4.1 The Department informed that the RDI Fund was established with a significant corpus 
of ₹1 lakh crore  to be deployed over a six -year period, following formal approval by the 
Union Cabinet on July 1, 2025. The Department of Science and Technology (DST) serves as 
the nodal ministry for this scheme, which is central to the national goal of raising R&D 
expenditure to 2% of GDP by 2030. By focusing on commercializing high -technology 
breakthroughs, the fund aims to align India’s innovation output with that of other leading 
global economies. 
4.2 The fund is specifically designed to support high -risk, high-reward projects in sunrise 
industries, including Green Hydrogen, Quantum Technologies, and Semiconductors. A 
critical technical objective of the RDI scheme is to support research at Technology Readiness 
Level (TRL) 4 and above, which involves taking prototypes de veloped in laboratories and 
scaling them into market -ready commercial products. To further this, a Deep Tech Fund of 
Funds is being established under the initiative to catalyze venture capital and private equity 
investment into next-generation startups. 
4.3 Unlike traditional schemes, the RDI Fund operates under a unique governance 
structure. DST has formulated and finalized comprehensive implementation guidelines and 
Special Financial Rules (SFRs) in close consultation with the Department of Economic 
Affairs (DEA) and the Department of Expenditure (DoE). These guidelines have also 
received the formal approval from the Executive Council of the Anusandhan National 
Research Foundation (ANRF), ensuring the fund operates within a cohesive national research 
framework. 
4.4 On being asked about the disbursal of funds, the Department submitted that the 
disbursement of funds is managed through a multi-tier system. The Technology Development 
Board (TDB) and the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC)  were 
nominated as the initial Second Level Fund Managers (SLFMs) based on Cabinet approval. 
Both organizations have already initiated the operational rollout, with TDB launching its first 
call for project proposals on February 4, 2026, followed by BIRAC o n February 13, 2026. 
The department is also in the process of appointing additional SLFMs from the private and 
financial sectors to expand the fund's reach. 
4.5 While examining the financial performance of the fund under the scheme and its 
utilization, the Committee observed that while the initial BE for 2025 -26 was ₹20,000 crore, 
it was revised down to ₹3,000 crore (RE) as the guidelines and operational frameworks were 
being finalized. However, for the FY 2026-27, a full allocation of ₹20,000 crore  has again 
been made. The Department submitted that it expects the scheme to be in full disbursement 
mode during the coming year, focusing on accelerating private -sector-led R&D and fostering 
a robust deep-tech ecosystem. 
 

9 
4.6  The Committee acknowledges that the nomination of TDB and BIRAC as 
Second Level Fund Manag ers (SLFMs) is a positive start under the RDI Fund.  The 
Department could not utilize any amount out of the RE allocation of ₹3,000 crore in the 
current FY 2025-26 (as on 31.01.2026). The Committee recommends that in FY 2026-27, 
the Department must make an early start and expedite the selection and appointment of 
additional SLFMs from the private sector ; financial institutions ; and venture capital 
firms. Diversifying the fund management pool will enhance the scheme's reach across 
different industry segments and ensure that the evaluation process for projects is both 
technically rigorous and commercially viable.  
4.7 The Committee also recommends that the Department establish a quarterly 
disbursement and progress review mechanism. This will ensure that the massive corpus 
is utilized effectively throughout the year, preventing mid-year surrenders and ensuring 
that the momentum of industrial R&D is maintained and the funds allocated for the 
RDI are also utilized to the maximum. 
4.8 Given that the RDI Fund specifically targets Technology Readiness Levels (TRL) 
4 and above, the Committee recommends that the Department should define clear 
commercialization metrics for the projects it funds. The success of the RDI Fund should 
be measured not just by the amount of capital disbursed, but by the number of 
indigenous technologies that successfully transition from the laboratory to the 
commercial market. The Department should prioritize projects with a clear path  to 
production, especially in ‘Sunrise sectors’ like semiconductors and green energy. 
 
5. ANUSANDHAN NATIONAL RESEARCH FOUNDATION 
5.1 The Department informed the Committee that t he Anusandhan  National Research 
Foundation (ANRF) was established to catalyze a robust research culture and bridge the gap 
between academia, industry, and the government. Its primary mandate is to uplift the Science 
and Technology (S&T) ecosystem across India’s vast ne twork of universities and colleges, 
particularly those with limited existing research infrastructure. By replacing the SERB, the 
ANRF aims to create a more integrated and agile funding mechanism that supports multi -
institutional and multidisciplinary research aligned with national priorities. 
5.2 The department highlighted that a  cornerstone of the ANRF's institutional capacity -
building effort is the Partnership for Accelerated Innovation and Research (PAIR), launched 
in November 2024. Operating on a hub -and-spoke model, PAIR connects well -established 
research institutions (hubs) with emerging ones (spokes) to foster mentorship and 
collaborative growth. To date, seven networks involving 7 hubs and 42 spokes have been 
approved, with each network eligible for up to ₹100 crore in support over five years. 
Complementing this is the Prime Minister Professorship, launched in July 2025, which has 
already approved 21 positions across 20 States and one UT to embed eminent scientists in 
less-endowed institutions for strategic leadership and mentorship.

5.7 The Committee asked the Department about the reasons why the fund utilization 
remained at zero for last two consecutive years (2023 -24 and 2024 -25). The department 
replied that the FY 2023 -24 largely pertained to the legislative finalisation and preparator y 
phase preceding operationalisation of ANRF. As the statutory provisions came into force 
only in February 2024, no programmatic approvals or financial sanctions were undertaken 
during that year. In FY 2024 -25, the emphasis was on establishing the institut ional, 
administrative and financial architecture necessary for a newly constituted statutory body.

and a roadmap for the next generation of Exascale computing may be drawn. 
(Para 2.10)

RESEARCH, DEVELOPMENT & INNOVATION (RDI) FUND 
 The Committee acknowledges that the nomination of TDB and BIRAC as 
Second Level Fund Managers (SLFMs) is a positive start under the RDI Fund. The 
Department could not utilize any amount out of the RE allocation of ₹3,000 crore in the 
current FY 2025-26 (as on 31.01.2026). The Committee recommends that in FY 2026-27, 
the Department must make an early start and expedite the selection and appointment of 
additional SLFMs from the private sector; financial institutions; and venture capital 
firms. Diversifying th e fund management pool will enhance the scheme's reach across 
different industry segments and ensure that the evaluation process for projects is both 
technically rigorous and commercially viable.       (Para 4.6) 
 The Committee also recommends that the Dep artment establish a quarterly 
disbursement and progress review mechanism. This will ensure that the massive corpus 
is utilized effectively throughout the year, preventing mid-year surrenders and ensuring 
that the momentum of industrial R&D is maintained an d the funds allocated for the 
RDI are also utilized to the maximum.      (Para 4.7) 
 Given that the RDI Fund specifically targets Technology Readiness Levels (TRL) 
4 and above, the Committee recommends that the Department should define clear 
commercialization metrics for the projects it funds. The success of the RDI Fund should 
be measured not just by the amount of capital disbursed, but by the number of 
indigenous technologies that successfully transition from the laboratory to the 
commercial market. The D epartment should prioritize projects with a clear path to 
production, especially in ‘Sunrise sectors’ like semiconductors and green energy. 
(Para 4.8) 
ANUSANDHAN NATIONAL RESEARCH FOUNDATION 
 The Committee notes the work initiated by the Department under ANRF. 
However, the Committee also observes that for the current FY 2025 -26, the Department 
has been able to utilize only ₹1,191 crore (as on 31.01.2026) out of RE allocation of 
₹1,948 crore, wh ich is merely 61.13% of the RE. The Committee is apprehensive 
whether the Department will be able to utilize the allocated amount during the current 
financial year. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department must make 
efforts to optimally utili ze the funds made available to it during FY 2025 -26 in order to 
strengthen the research framework of the country.     (Para 5.9) 
 The Committee appreciates the successful launch of the Partnership for 
Accelerated Innovation and Research (PAIR)  and its hub -and-spoke model. However, 
to truly democratize science, the Committee recommends that the Department set a 
target to expand the ‘Spoke’ network to include at least one state university or college 
from every aspirational district. This will ens ure that the mentor -mentee relationship 
transcends elite institutions and reaches the grass -roots level of the national education 
system.                    (Para 5.11)
