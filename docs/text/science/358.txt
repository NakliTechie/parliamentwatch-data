4. DELEGATION OF ENHANCED POWERS FOR  RE-APPROPRIATION OF 
FUNDS TO SECRETARY, DEPARTMENT OF ATOMIC ENERGY 
4.1 During the meeting , the Department apprised the Committee that a t present there are 
restrictions for re -appropriating funds from “Major Works” to any other primary  units like 
“Minor Works”, “Repairs & Maintenance”, “Tools & Plants”, etc.   It was further brought to the 

7 
 
 
notice of the Committee that presen tly the powers of re -appropriation for “Administrative 
Expenses” (viz. under the primary units salaries, wages, travel expenses, etc.) is  limited to 25% 
or Rs. 1 crore, whichever is less.  The Department, accordingly, proposed that full powers may 
be given to the Secretary, Department of Atomic Energy, for re-appropriation of above expenses 
within the same grant which will enable the Department to utilize the budgetary grant to the 
optimum level. 
4.2 The Committee takes note of the proposal and feels that proper care must be taken 
in the matter by the Department  without losing sight of financial prudence.  Granting 
power for re -appropriation of the above mentioned expenses to the Secretary may on the 
one hand solve the problems being faced by the Department but on the other hand this will 
also bring greater amount of responsibility and accountability so as to ensure strict 
adherence to the relevant rules and guidelines laid down for the purpose.                                            
In view of the proposal submitted by the Department, the Committee recommends that full 
powers may be given to the Secretary, Department of Atomic Energy, for re -appropriation 
of „Administrative Expenses‟. The Committee sincerely fee ls that granting such powers for 
re-appropriation would surely help the Department  to make optimum utilisation of the 
budgetary allocations made.

6. CANCER CARE IN THE COUNTRY – ROADMAP FOR FUTURE 
6.1 Responding to the query of the Committee about the availability of cancer care  facilities 
in various parts of the country with specific reference to Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, 
North Karnataka and South Rajasthan , it was informed  by the Department  that the Tata 
Memorial Centre  (TMC) has already functional centres at Mumbai,  Navi Mumbai, Varanasi, 
Guwahati, Sangrur, Vizag, and Muzaffarpur.   Centres at Mullanpur and augmented services at 
Vizag would be functional later this year.  Sharing the plans and suggestions for the States where 
cancer care is not readily available , the following was submitted for the perusal of the 
Committee:- 
(i) It was submitted that Tata Trusts has initiated construction o f the Cancer Hospital 
at Ranchi, Jharkhand which would be completed by July , 2022 and  TMC is 
providing technical support as well as training for the staff.   TMC has a unit at 
Muzaaffarpur also which can support this initiative. 
(ii) It was suggested that the facility at AIIMS, Raipur could be upgraded to function 
as a Hub.  AIIMS, Raipur already has an Oncology Department which can be 
supported and expanded.  TMC would be able to provide technical assistance and 
support, if necessary.  In addition, BALCO Cancer Centre is in existence already 
and TMC has been providing technical support and it should be completed in 18 
months.  
(iii) It was brought to the notice of the Committee that senior faculty from 
TMC had visited the North Bengal Medical College and Hospital, Siliguri  to 
assess the oncology services there and supported the construction and made 
departmental recommendations .  TMC would be providing continued technical 
support and training for this centre. 
(iv) TMC is supporting the temple trust at Mantralayam to develop a  cancer facility in 
Mantralayam which is at the intersection of three States – (i) North -East 
Karnataka; (ii) Telangana; and South Maharashtra. 
(v) TMC could develop a cancer facility at Karwar which would serve the population 
of North-West Karnataka and Goa, both of which currently lack a cancer 
centre. 
(vi) TMC could develop a cancer facility at or nearby Kota  as there are no cancer 
centres in this region and currently patients from South Rajasthan travel to 
Ahmedabad for cancer treatment. 
(vii) TMC is in advanced stage s of planning a cancer hub at the National Institute of 
Science Education and Research (NISER) campus near Bhubaneswar in Odisha.  
This will be built with partial support from Tata Trusts as well as DAE. 
6.2 The Committee was also apprised by the Department that o ne of the most important 
aspects to be addressed is the availability of qualified and trained human resource in all these 
facilities, which are currently not available.   It is important to make the Homi  Bhabha National 
Institute an  “Institute of National Importance” to enable the existing TMC centres at various 
States to start super specialty courses in oncology.  This would ensure that there is convergence 
of human resource with the development of these cancer centres.

cancer care facilities are made available in these areas.  It is also heartening to note that the

 The Committee hopes that the Department would make earnest efforts and make 
optimum utilisation of the budgetary support provided for the year 2021 -22 in the

to a query observes that the Department was allotted Rs. 27.15 crore in BE 2021 -22 under 
the component „Heavy Water Production Facility‟ which was increased exponentially to 
Rs. 212.86 crore  at RE stage. The Committee is constrained to note that even though 
exponential allocation was made to the Department at RE stage, the utilisation of the 
Department has been to the tune of Rs. 52.53 crore only, upto January, 2022.                                   
The Committee takes serious note of this under -utilisation of the budgetary support by the

have led to such poor utilisation of funds.                                                                     (Para 2.4)

particularly under Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) and Bharatiya 
Nabhikaya Vidyut N igam Limited (BHAVINI) under the administrative control of the

utilisation of the funds made available for NPCIL; and BHAVINI where the Department 
could utilise 35.25 per c ent; and 38.03 per cent, respectively under these components.  The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to apprise the Committee of the 
detailed reasons for such under-utilisation of funds.                                                   (Para 2.5) 
 The Committee is also dismayed to note that zero utilisation of funds was made 
under Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL), which was allocated Rs. 21.70 crore 
at RE stage 2021 -22.  The Committee would, therefore, like to be informed about the  
reasons due to which zero utilisation of funds was made by the Department.          (Para 2.6) 
 The Committee takes a serious note of the cut in the budgetary allocation of the 
Department of Atomic Energy in BE 2022 -23 and its likely impact on the activit ies of the 
Department.  The Committee also notes with concern that out of the total reduction of     
Rs. 8,177.12 crore, Rs. 3,318.50 crore is under revenue budget and Rs. 4,858.63 crore under 
the capital budget.                                                                                                         (Para 2.10) 
 The Committee further notes that the Department would need additional funds to 
ensure that its R&D activities along with other major importa nt activities are not 
negatively impacted.  The Committee, therefore, desires the Department to prepare a 
detailed note on the additional fund requirement and impress upon the Ministry of Finance 
for the same. The Committee also hopes that the Department w ould make sincere efforts to 
utilise the budgetary support given for the year 2022 -23 optimally and strive hard to 
achieve the envisaged targets both physically and financially.                                  (Para 2.11)

FLEET MODE REACTORS 
 The Committee takes note of the progress achieved by the Department with regard 
to construction of ten indigenous 700 MW Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs) in 
Fleet mode.  The Committee learns that moving of reactors from „pre -project stage‟ (which 
also includes excavation), to „under construction stage‟ is marked by the event of placement 
of First Pour of Concrete (FPC) and based on the present status of projects,                                        
FPC of Kaiga 5&6 is expected in 2023; FPC of GHA VP 3 & 4; MBRAPP 1 to 4 is expected 
in 2024; and that of CMPAPP 1 & 2 in 2025.                                                                (Para 3.7) 
 The Committee recommends the Department to make sincere efforts to ensure that 
the timeline fixed for movement of these above mentioned reactors from „pre-project stage‟ 
to „under construction stage‟ are adhered to as far as possible and zero tolerance policy be 
adopted towards any kind of procedural delay.                                                           (Para 3.8) 
DELEGATION OF ENHANCED POWERS FOR RE -APPROPRIATION OF FUNDS TO 
SECRETARY, DEPARTMENT OF ATOMIC ENERGY 
 The Committee takes note of the proposal and feels that proper care must be taken 
in the matter by the Department without losing sight of financial prudence.  Granting 
power for re -appropriation of the above mentioned expenses to the Secretary may on the 
one hand solve the problems being faced by the Department but on the other hand this will 
also bring greater amount of responsibil ity and accountability so as to ensure strict 
adherence to the relevant rules and guidelines laid down for the purpose.                                            
In view of the proposal submitted by the Department, the Committee recommends that full 
powers may be given to the Secretary, Department of Atomic Energy, for re -appropriation 
of „Administrative Expenses‟. The Committee sincerely feels that granting such powers for 
re-appropriation would surely help the Department to make optimum utilisation of th e 
budgetary allocations made.                                                                                           (Para 4.2) 
GRANTS TO OTHER INSTITUTIONS 
 The Committee is satisfied to note the financial performance of the Department 
under this Head.  The Committee, however, notes that the total number of grants issued by 
the Department during the year 2021 -22 (upto 16.02.2022) is 75 which is less than half i.e. 
154 of grants issued during the year 2020 -21.  It is also observed that even though the 
number of institutions for which the Department had given the grants during the year was 
81 but grants issued were to the tune of 154 during the year 2020 -21, nonetheless, during 
the year 2021 -22 the total number of institutions supported by the Department w as 107 
against which only 75 grants have been issued.  The Committee finds it difficult to 
understand that even when the number of institutions supported by the Department got 
increased during the year 2021 -22 with reference to 2020 -21, then in what manner  the 
grants issued have  been decreased. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Department to furnish a detailed note containing the list of the institutions supported by 
the Department during the last five years; specific number of grant under which that are 
covered and the amount of financial support provided to them by the Department.
