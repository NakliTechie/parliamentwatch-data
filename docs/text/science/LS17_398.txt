Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.1 The Committee observes that the Budget Estimate (BE) for 2024-25 was ₹13,042.75 
crore, but this was revised downwards substantially by 10.09% to ₹11,725.75 crore at the 
Revised Estimate (RE) stage. As of December 2024, actual expenditure stood at ₹8,296.38 
crore, a mere 71% utilisation of RE allocations. For the year 2025-26, the Department 
projected a requirement of ₹15,983.37 crore, but was allocated ₹13,416.20 crore, reflecting 
a shortfall of ₹2,567.17 crore that could impact some planned initiatives. While the increase 
in allocation over the previous year suggests continued government support for space 
activities, the reduction from the projected requirement raises concerns about the 
Department’s ability to execute all planned projects efficiently. (Para 2.2 of the 391st Report) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.2 At the end of the FY 2024-25, the Department was able to utilise 98% of the RE 
allocations – expenditure of ₹11,518.64 crore incurred against RE allocations of ₹11,725.75 
crore.  
 
1.3 An amount of ₹13,416.20 Crore has been allocated to the Department in BE 2025-26 
against the projection of ₹15,983.37 crore. The Department has ensured that key projects / 
programmes are sufficiently funded based on the budgetary resources made available in 
2025-26. Depending on the progress of on-going missions, R&D projects and facility build-
up, the Department would approach Ministry of Finance for additional funds at RE stage and 
through the mechanism of supplementary Grants. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.4 The Committee observes from Chart-2 & 3, for RE 2024-25, an overall utilization 
rate of 70.75% across all schemes. Space Technology, a key sector, has utilized 70.95% of its 
allocated funds while Space Sciences shows the lowest utilization rate at 46.10%, indicating 
significant underutilization. The Committee notes that Space Applications, which includes 
Earth Observation, GIS-based services, and disaster management applications, has a 
utilization rate of 73.09%. This suggests a relatively strong performance in project execution, 
particularly in satellite-based services for agriculture, urban planning, and environmental 
monitoring. INSAT Satellite Systems has seen 60.10% utilization, reflecting slower spending. 
Establishment Expenditure stands at 65.53%, pointing to stable spending on administrative 
functions, infrastructure maintenance, and research facility operations. Other Central Sector 
Expenditure, including initiatives supporting private sector participation and space-sector 
reforms, has the highest utilization at 75.76%.                                         (Para 2.7 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.5 Department incurred expenditure of ₹11,518.64 crore against RE allocations of 
₹11,725.75 crore during FY 2024-25. (~98% of RE allocations).

Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.7 The Committee observes from Charts 4 & 5 that there has been a significant 
reduction in revised allocations for 2024-25 under some of the crucial schemes of the 
Department. The Scheme- ‘Gaganyaan Programme’ India’s human spaceflight mission, 
experienced a significant budget reduction at the R.E. 2024-25 stage, from ₹1200 crore to 
₹847.35 crore, indicating a cut of ₹352.65 crore. The actual expenditure till December 2024 
stood at ₹627.05 crore, reflecting delays in hardware realisation and milestone-based 
payments. However, B.E. 2025-26 has been restored to ₹1200 crore, suggesting that higher 
spending is expected in the coming year. The Committee expressed concern over the repeated 
fund reductions and emphasised that predictable financial allocations were crucial  for 
maintaining mission timelines.                                                                             (Para 2.9 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.8 At the end of the financial year, the Department has utilised an amount of ₹826.96 
crore under the Gaganyaan programme, which is about 98% of RE allocations. 
 
1.9 Gaganyaan Programme involves development of several new technologies such as 
Human Rated Launch Vehicle, Crew Escape System, Crew Module, Service Module, 
Environment Control & Life Support System and Human Centric Products. The budgetary 
projections and expenditure patterns have been affected by the complex technology 
development cycle involving multiple design iterations and extensive flight/ ground testing to 
meet the stringent human rating requirements. In addition to these, extrinsic factors such as 
global pandemic and disruptions in supply chain of semiconductors & raw materials have 
also impacted the programme in recent past. 
 

4 
 
1.10 As ISRO is preparing for the first 4nscrewed mission after the completion of some of 
the major ground qualification tests, there is greater clarity on the overall outlay plan and 
yearly budgetary requirements. This is expected to result in higher expenditure in the coming 
years for flight hardware realisation and mission related activities. Further, expenditure 
monitoring through regular reviews is being carried out. Based on this cash-flow assessment, 
the BE 2025-26 provision for the programme is restored to ₹1200.00 crore. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.11 The Committee observes that the Joint ISRO-NASA Mission to the ISS faced a 
substantial budget cut at the R.E. stage, reducing its allocation from ₹715 crore to ₹412 
crore, reflecting a ₹303 crore reduction. The actual expenditure till December 2024 was 
₹413.14 crore, exceeding the R.E. allocation. However, the B.E. for 2025-26 has been 
drastically reduced to ₹135 crore, reflecting the deferral of the mission to a later date. The 
Committee expresses concerns over further delays in India’s collaboration with NASA and 
recommends that DoS maintain strong international engagement to ensure mission stability.
                                                                                                                 (Para 2.10 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.12 The mission has been rescheduled by NASA from FY 2024-25 to FY 2025-26 due to 
its internal scheduling constraints. Accordingly, the associated payment milestones and 
expenditure plan has been revised. The mission was successfully launched on June 25, 2025. 
 
1.13 ISRO and NASA teams continue to have regular bilateral engagements to plan and 
review the collaborative activities being undertaken as part of this mission. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.14 The Committee observes that the Scheme –‘Semi- Cryogenic Engine Development’ 
was allocated ₹190 crore (B.E. 2024-25) which was revised to ₹115 crore (R.E. 2024-25), 
reflecting a ₹75 crore cut. The actual expenditure till December 2024 was ₹76.67 crore, 
highlighting slower-than-expected project execution. However, B.E. 2025-26 has been 
increased to ₹150 crore, indicating renewed funding support for this critical propulsion 
technology.                                                                                                     (Para 2.11 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.15 The BE 2024-25 allocation for ‘Semi-cryogenic Engine Development’ & ‘Semi-
cryogenic Stage Development’ was ₹190.00 crore. Against RE 2024-25 allocation of ₹115.00 
crore, the actual expenditure was ₹120.63 crore. The reasons for reduced budget utilisation 
with respect to BE is mainly due to the geopolitical situation in Ukraine as a result of which 
the engine tests are now being conducted at IPRC, Mahendragiri.  Further, payments 
pertaining to SIET fluid servicing system, Strain gauge equipment for tank proof pressure 
tests spilled over to the next financial year.  
 
1.16 The BE 2025-26 allocation under ‘Semi-cryogenic Engine Development’ & ‘Semi-
cryogenic Stage Development’ is ₹150.00 crore to meet the project milestones for 
commissioning of SIET stage test bay, Engine and stage sub-systems realisation, spill over 
payments etc.

CENTRAL SECTOR SCHEMES OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SPACE 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
1.39 The Committee notes that the allocation under the Scheme- ‘Space Technology’ has 
increased from ₹8,985.60 crore in R.E. 2024-25 to ₹10,230.21 crores in B.E. 2025-26. 
 (Para 3.3 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.40 In BE 2025-26, an amount of ₹10,230.21 crore has been allocated under the head 
‘Space Technology’. The increased allocation in 2025-26 is towards the following: 
a) Realisation of flight hardware, testing infrastructure and progress towards test 
vehicle flights and first unmanned flight under Gaganyaan follow-on missions 
leading to precursor for the Bharatiya Antariksh Station. 
b) Realisation of operational flights of PSLV, GSLV and GSLV MkIII towards 
providing launch services for Earth Observation, Communication, Navigation and 
Commercial missions. 
c) Initiate activities towards development of Next Generation Launch Vehicle

Advanced propulsion systems: 
Hathor Rockets- Semi Cryo Engine- 
 
Hathor Rockets is a space propulsion startup incubated at the Indian 
Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), focused on the 
development of advanced semi-cryogenic and cryogenic rocket engines for 
reusable launch vehicles and affordable access to space. The start-up is 
currently developing the Bharath-5P engine - a 5 kN class, regeneratively 
cooled semi-cryogenic engine that uses ATF and liquid oxygen (LOX). In 
the long term, Hathor Rockets aims to scale its propulsion systems and 
develop a fully cryogenic engine, contributing to India’s self-reliant 
capabilities in launch vehicle propulsion technologies. 
 
ISRO’ Scramjet Project- HAVA- IIST’s involvement- 
 
ISRO is actively pursuing the development of horizontal-flight scramjet 
engines with longer test durations, with the intention of integrating them 
with other propulsion systems to enhance launch vehicle reusability. The 

25 
 
practical implementation of scramjet flights remains challenging due to 
issues such as combustion stability, rapid fuel-air mixing, and reliable 
ignition under extreme aerodynamic conditions. A critical component of the 
scramjet engine is the fuel injection system, which must deliver a finely 
atomized liquid fuel to ensure efficient evaporation and mixing within the 
millisecond-scale residence times available in the combustor. The 
effervescent strut injection strategy is one of the successful configurations 
implemented by ISRO as well as other agencies worldwide to sustain 
supersonic combustion in real time scramjet flights. The spray 
characteristics of the actual flight strut hardware is investigated and tested 
at APLD Lab, IIST using advanced optical based diagnostic techniques like 
high-speed shadowgraph, Mie scattering and phase Doppler measurements. 
IIST is fully involved in the optimizing the Scramjet injector in ISRO’s 
mission.

that for full utilization of the potential of private enterprises in the space sector, a well-
structured incentive framework should be introduced to support start-ups and industry 
players engaged in space applications, satellite development, and launch services. The 
Committee urges upon the Department that IN-SPACe should create dedicated incubation 
hubs within ISRO centres, where private firms can collaborate with ISRO scientists to refine 
their technologies.                                                                                         (Para 2.23 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.5 IN-SPACe has created a limited version technical centre phase-1 attached to IN-
SPACe within the SAC/ISRO campus to provide the private firms access to space systems’

design and analysis software tools, electronics systems assembly, integration and testing 
facilities including climatic chambers, thermovac & vibration test facilities, cleanrooms. The 
private firms are provided with mentorship through retired ISRO scientists. Currently, we are 
working on phase-II of establishing  full-fledged dedicated Technical centre collocated with 
SAC campus which is expected to be commissioned in 2027. The objective of this centre is to 
nurture private space 31tilize3131 and entrepreneurs by providing technical and legal

2.6 IN-SPACe has launched the Pre-Incubation Entrepreneurship development program, 
where the identified start-ups can be incubated/guided by the experts from 
industries/academia/ research institutes/incubators. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
2.7 In view of the fact that NSIL has now become the primary commercial arm of ISRO, 
the Committee recommends that it should proactively engage with international clients, and 
secure more commercial contracts for satellite-building and launch services. The Committee 
expresses concern over the sharp decline in INSAT/GSAT transponder lease earnings. The 
Committee recommends that the Department should identify market demand, explore 
partnerships with telecom operators and OTT service providers, and secure long-term 
transponder leasing agreements. The Committee recognizes that the launch services market 
remains a key revenue generator. The Committee recommends that ISRO should strengthen 
its commercial launch portfolio, offer competitive pricing, and leverage reusable launch 
vehicle (RLV) technology to reduce costs and attract global customers.          (Para 2.25 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.8 NSIL has been very proactively engaging with international customers for securing 
launch service contracts for launching customer satellites onboard PSLV, SSLV and LVM3. 
Global Launch Service market has been extremely competitive as some of the US launch 
service providers have been offering services at much reduced cost in comparison to Indian 
launchers.  
 
2.9 Inspite of the stiff competition, NSIL could secure several dedicated PSLV and 
LVM3 launch contracts.  As on date, NSIL has successfully accomplished 5 dedicated PSLV 
missions and 2 dedicated LVM3 missions for international customers, besides launching 
several international customer satellites as co-passengers.   Till date, NSIL has launched 64 
customer satellites onboard PSLV. NSIL has three Dedicated launch contracts in-hand, one 
for launching US customer satellite onboard LVM3, one for launching Singapore customer 
satellite onboard PSLV and other for launching Australian customer satellite onboard SSLV.  
 
2.10 In addition, NSIL has several co-passenger satellite launch service contracts in-hand. 
NSIL is making all efforts to secure many more dedicated launch service contracts.  
 
2.11 DTH market is undergoing disruption due to increase in OTT services and NSIL is 
putting its efforts to improve the Satcom business by providing end-to-end solutions to its 
customers in addition to working towards long term leasing agreements and newer 
partnership models with operators.

ESTABLISHMENT EXPENDITURE OF THE CENTRE 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
 
2.15 The Committee acknowledges the significant strides made by IN-SPACe in promoting 
private sector participation in the Indian space sector. The Committee recommends that IN-
SPACe should develop a structured framework for private sector access to ISRO’s 
infrastructure, including transparent guidelines on facility usage charges, eligibility criteria, 
and priority allocation for research and commercial activities. In view of the fact that 75 
Technology Transfer (ToT) agreements have been signed since 2020, the Committee 
recommends that IN-SPACe should expand its industry outreach, conduct awareness 
programs, and streamline ToT processes to increase private sector adoption of ISRO-
developed technologies. The Committee notes that IN-SPACe has issued 56 authorisations 
and received 480 applications from 382 Indian entities. To streamline processes and ensure 
timely approvals, the Committee recommends that IN-SPACe should establish clear 
timelines, a digital tracking system, and a public dashboard for real-time updates on 
authorisation requests and approvals.                                                                (Para 4.5 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.16 IN-SPACe has put in place Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) to process the 
applications from private entities for various support from IN-SPACe. A dashboard has been 
created in the IN-SPACe digital platform giving the status of the applications of NGE and 
real time updates on authorisation requests and approvals. 
 
2.17 IN-SPACe released the Norms, Guidelines and Procedures for implementation of the 
Indian Space Policy 2023 (‘ISP-2023’) in respect of Authorization of Space Activities 
(“NGP”) on May 3, 2024 which is readily available online on IN-SPACe Digital Platform 
(IDP www.inspace.gov.in). The Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) to process the 
Authorization applications is clearly brought out in the NGP and provides clarity on the 
timelines established & transparency in the authorization process. 
 

33 
 
2.18 Also, all applications to IN-SPACe are received digitally through IDP portal where 
their progress can also be seen. Additionally, a separate page is hosted publicly on the 
dashboard of IDP, displaying details of all Authorization issued by IN-SPACe. 
 
2.19 IN-SPACe is not able to meet the desired timelines.  A detailed review is being 
undertaken to further streamline the process, identify the reasons for delay and thus speed up 
the process.  There is a dependency on several Departments for doing so. 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
 
2.20 The Committee notes IN-SPACe’s ongoing efforts to establish space-based Earth 
Observation (EO) services under a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model and the 
development of common technical facilities in space manufacturing clusters with a funding 
cap of ₹100 crores per state. The Committee recommends that modalities for these initiatives 
should be finalised at the earliest and that a structured implementation roadmap with 
periodic progress reports should be made available. The Committee also recommends that 
the Investment Incentive Scheme for private-sector manufacturing of space systems should be 
formulated with clear eligibility criteria and incentive structures.                   (Para 4.8 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
2.21 IN-SPACe is following a due EOI/RFP process for identifying an Indian Entity to 
establish of space based EO services under a Public Private Partnership. The final selection of 
Indian Entity is targeted by 15 August 2025.  
 
2.22 IN-SPACe has communicated to all the state governments seeking interest in 
establishing space manufacturing clusters (SMC). As on 01 June 2025, four states have 
communicated their willingness to establish SMC (viz., Government of Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, 
Karnataka and Chhattisgarh). IN-SPACe has signed framework MoU with Governments of 
Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka and discussions have been initiated for establishing 
Common Technical Facilities within the SMC. A Detailed Project Report for each Common 
Technical Facility would be prepared by IN-SPACe and submitted to the empowered 
committee for final approval for implementation. 
 
2.23 IN-SPACe has prepared a Detailed Project Report for the Investment Incentive 
Scheme detailing the scope, incentive structure, eligibility and evaluation criteria. As 
recommended by the Space Commission, the DPR will be submitted to Department of 
Expenditure for approval. 
 
2.24 Based on the direction of the PSC, IN-SPACe will work with the companies to better 
understand how we can support them better and implement initiatives.
