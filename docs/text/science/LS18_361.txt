3. NATIONAL MISSION ON INTERDISCIPLINARY CYBER PHYSICAL 
SYSTEMS (NM-ICPS)  
3.1 The Department informed the Committee that it is implementing multi -stakeholder 
National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber Physical System (NM -ICPS).  The mission was 
approved in 2018 with a total outlay of ₹3660 crore for a period of five years. The Mission aims 
at convergence of stakeholders by establishing strong linkages between academia, industry, 
Government and International Organisations. The Mission is implemented through 25 number of 
Technology Innovation Hubs (TIHs) established in the top academi c Institutes. Each TIH will 
have four major activities i.e., Technology Development, Human Resource & Skill Development 
(including development of CPS technology application tools for education at elementary and 
high school level), Innovation, Entrepreneurs hip & Start -Up Ecosystem and International 
Collaborations.  
3.2 The Committee observed that despite the importance and criticality of NM -ICPS, no 
budget was allocated to the Mission in 2021 -22. The Committee enquired the reason and impact 

5 
 
of this to which the Department submitted that as per approved expenditure plan for NM -ICPS, 
Rs.123.66 crore was allocated for the Year -one (2019-20) and the same was released on March 
27, 2020 at the end of financial year which was available for utilization in next FY 202 0-21. 
Also,      Rs.271.27 crore was allocated for the year 2020 -21 and approximately same amount 
was released. Since, ample amount of fund was available with supported 
organisations/institutions under the scheme NM -ICPS and there was no scope of releasing  any 
further grant to the host institutes for the financial year  2021-22, allocation for the current year 
was withdrawn at R .E. 2021-22 stage. Since fund was available with host institutions under the 
scheme with Implementing Agencies, there is no hindrance in execution of the scheme.  
3.3 The Department also informed that the set targets of the Mission were impacted adversely 
by not allocating budget as all TIHs had drawn up their strategies and tie -up with industries and 
other stakeholders for development  of advanced technologies. The lack of funds and timely 
release of budget has impacted the Hubs adversely in executing the on-going activities.  
3.3 The Department further submitted that for the year 2022 -23, an allocation of Rs.350 crore 
has been made in B.E. 2022 -23 against the requirement of Rs.560 crore. However, the allocated 
Rs.350 crore will be utilized in augmenting the hubs towards creation of start -ups, R&D 
Infrastructure, Technology Development, Human Resource and Skill Development, 
Entrepreneurship Development and International Collaborations in a scaled down manner.

4. SUPPORT TO STARTUPS UNDER NSTEDB AND TDB 
4.1 The Department informed the Committee that under it established the  National Science & 
Technology Entrepreneurship Development Board (NSTEDB) was established as an institutional 
mechanism to help promote knowledge driven and technology intensive enterprises. The Board 
aims to foster innovation and Start -Up ecosystem through Science, Technol ogy & Innovation 
(ST&I) interventions, capacity building, pre -incubation, incubation, acceleration and engaging 
private sector.  
4.2 The Department informed the Committee that the National Initiative for Developing and 
Harnessing Innovations  (NIDHI) is NST EDB's flagship programme for nurturing ideas and 
innovations into successful startups. NIDHI aims to nurture start -ups through scouting, 
supporting and scaling of innovations. The key stakeholders of NIDHI include various 
departments and ministries of the central government, state governments, academic and R & D 
institutions, mentors, financial institutions, angel investors, venture capitalists and private 
sectors. While NSTEDB is the funding agency, the NIDHI programmes are implemented 
through Technology B usiness Incubators (TBIs) available around the country.   The Department 
further informed that NIDHI has the following components with their corresponding 
achievements: 
i. NIDHI4COVID2.0 initative: 34 startups are being funded by DST to support startup -led 
Covid solutions making our country stronger on multiple fronts in the ongoing fight 
against COVID. 
ii. i-TBI: To increase outreach of incubation and entrepreneurship programs of DST, a new 
program has been rolled out in name of ‘Inclusive -Technology Business Incu bator’ 
(iTBI). The mandate of iTBI is to increase outreach of incubation program in terms of 
geographies, gender etc. 
iii. NIDHI-TBI: ‘National Initiative for Developing and Harnessing Innovations’ - 
Technology Business Incubators to support and nurture knowled ge driven innovative 
start-ups into successful enterprises. 4 new TBIs are being established in the country. 
iv. NIDHI COE: NIDHI - Centre of Excellences to strengthen existing capacities of 
Technology Business Incubators (TBIs) and supports potential start -ups. 2 new COEs are 
being established at FITT, IIT Delhi and T-Hub, Hyderabad.  
v. NIDHI- PRAYAS (PRomoting and Ac celerating Young and ASpiring technology 
entrepreneurs) to support young innovators turn their ideas into Proof of Concept 
(PoC)/Prototype. PRAYAS is implemented by 43 PRAYAS Centres, with 13 new 
PRAYAS centres established in FY 21-22. 
vi. NIDHI EIR (Entrepren eurs-in- Residence) to inspire and motivate graduating students to 
take entrepreneurship by providing support as fellowship. EiR is being implemented 
through 32 TBI’s, including 12 new TBI’s implementing EiR. 
vii. NIDHI Seed Support Programme to ensure timely a vailability of the seed support to the 
deserving incubate startups within an incubator. During this FY 2021 -22, a total of about 
65 startups have been supported so far.

viii. NIDHI Accelerator program to provide startups to introduce to entrepreneurial 
development activities.

5 EXTRAMURAL RESEARCH SUPPORT PROVIDED BY THE DEPARTMENT  
5.1 The Department informed the Committee that it provides the largest extramural research 
and development support in the country to strengthen national S&T capacity and capability 
through a competiti ve mode to scientists cutting across institutions and disciplines. The 
Department supports and encourages the whole gamut of S&T ecosystem from seeding and 
capacity building in the cutting -edge research areas; translational research; technology 
development and deployment; innovation and startup ecosystem; and international 
collaborations. This is achieved by supporting S&T infrastructure, scientists and students in 
academic and R&D institutions across the country.

6. SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY INNOVATION (STI) HUBS 
 
6.1 The Committee enquired the Department about the initiatives of the Department towards 
disseminating science and technology education, research and technological interventions at the  
grass root levels, especially in the rural and backward region s. The Department submitted before 
the Committee that it has initiated a programme for holistic development of Scheduled Caste 
(SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) Communities in the Country through s ystemic interventions by 
establishing Science Technology and Innovation (STI) Hubs in different regions of the country 
with the following objectives:  
(i) To address the weakest linkages in the predominant livelihood systems through Science 
& Technology (S&T) interventions. 
(ii) Creation of social enterprises based on the strengths in livelihood systems. 
(iii) To improve the Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS) through inputs of S&T  
 6.2 The STI Hubs follow a livelihood centric approach for strengthening liv elihoods and 
livelihood assets rather than techno -centric approach driven by the inquisitiveness of principal 
investigators. The STI Hubs will thus develop, nurture and ensure the delivery of appropriate and 
relevant technologies for inclusive socio -economic development through creation of sustainable 
livelihoods for the SC and ST population in tune to their growing aspirations. The STI Hubs are 
established in collaborative, networked and partnership framework to resolve the last mile 
problem in the introduction, delivery, management, use and extension of technology applications 
for development of target population. 
6.3 The Department further informed the Committee that so far, 14 STI Hubs for Scheduled 
Caste - Maharashtra (1), Rajasthan (1), Tamil Nadu (2),  Telangana (2), Andhra Pradesh (3), 
Punjab (1), Uttar Pradesh (1), West Bengal (1), Madhya Pradesh (1) and Karnataka (1) and 6 STI 
Hubs for Scheduled Tribe West Bengal (1), UT of Ladakh (1),  Odisha (1), Meghalaya (1), 

12 
 
Jharkhand (1) and Telangana (1) - were established. The Department also informed that it is 
targeted to establish 75 STI Hubs (including the existing ones) by March 2023 as part of 
Celebrations of 75th Year of Indian Independence.

percent higher as compared to R.E. 2021-22.                                                               (Para 2.5)

utilisation of funds especially under the Central Sector Schemes and strongly recommends 
the Department to ensure optimal utilisation of the funds allocated to it in B.E. 2022-23.
