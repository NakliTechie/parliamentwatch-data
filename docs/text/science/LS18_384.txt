on Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change deals with the action taken by

Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.1 The Committee makes note of the variation in budgetary allocations across key schemes of the 
Department and the reasons pertaining to disruption of the global supply chain. The Committee also 
makes note of the fact that Semi -Conductor Laboratory wh ich is responsible for design and 
development of crucial electronic components has been transferred to the Ministry of Electronics and 
Information Technology. The Committee highlights that a significant inventory of components used in 
satellites and launch vehicles are imported as they are not manufactured in India.  
        (Para 2.13 of the 377th Report) 
1.2 While the challenges to domestic manufacturing of electronic chipsets, solar panels, atomic 
clocks and other crucial components are varied, ranging f rom availability of raw material to expertise 
in design and high scale-cost effective and precise manufacturing, the Committee recommends that the 
Department should take necessary steps in maintaining an inventory of these crucial components and 
engineer the supply chain in such a manner that it remains immune to such geo-political hurdles, to the 
largest extent possible. Further, the Committee also recommends that steps be taken to achieve higher 
indigenization in production of some key components.                                                    (Para 2.14 ibid) 
Action Taken 
1.3 As recommended by the Committee, the Department shall ensure the inventory of key 
components/materials required for its on -going projects/programmes to mitigate the effects of 
disruptions in supply chain in future.  
 
1.4 The Department is pursuing Indigenis ation programs for critical technologies such as 
Travelling Wave Tube Amplifiers, Electronics packaging including LTCC (Low temperature co -fired 
ceramic) & HTCC (High temperatur e co -fired ceramic) package technology for RF, Optical, and 
Digital systems, ASICs (Application Specific Integrated Circuits), detectors. In addition, currently 
imported items are published in the Procurement Live Register  (PLR) portal of ISRO website to i nvite 
Expression of Interest (EoI) from Indian Industries for indigenous development. The indigenously 
developed atomic clock has been realized and flown in recent navigation satellite (NVS-01).

Recommendations/Observations 
2.1 The Committee observes that the B.E. allocation to the Department for the year 2022 -23 was 
Rs. 13,700 crore which was reduced substantially by almost 23 percent to Rs. 10,530.04 crore in R.E. 
2022-23. Further, the Committee also obse rves that though the B.E. 2023 -24 allocation of the 
Department (Rs. 12,543.91 crore) is higher by about 20 percent as compared to the revised allocation 
in 2022-23, it is still lesser than the B.E. 2022-23 allocation.    (Para 2.2 of the 377th Report) 
Action Taken 
2.2 The substantial reduction in RE 2022 -23 to Rs. 10,530.04 Crore from B.E. of Rs. 13,700 Crore 
is mainly attributable to programmatic realignment of satellite and launch vehicle programmes due to 
revised mandate of the Department, global supply chain issues, shortage in semiconductors and geo -
political issues faced during the financial year. 
2.3 The allocations made in BE 2023 -24 is adequate to meet the committed liabilities and for 
funding its on -going projects and activities. Depending on the p rogress of on -going missions, R&D 
projects and facility build -up, the Department shall review the budgetary requirements and approach 
Ministry of Finance for additional funds at RE stage and through the mechanism of supplementary 
Grants. 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.4 The Committee observes that even though the budgetary allocation for B.E. 2022 -23 (about Rs. 
13,700 crore) is almost similar to that in B.E. 2021 -22 (Rs. 13,950 crore), the R.E. 20 22-23 allocation 
(about Rs. 10,500 crore) is much less as com pared to R.E. 2021-22 (about Rs. 12,600 crore). Further, 
the B.E. 2023-24 allocation of Rs. 12,543.91 crore is the least in three years. This is also suggestive of 
the fact that the Department is transitioning from a research based institution to a more co mmercially 
oriented agency with buoyant internal revenues.                                                                (Para 2.6 ibid) 
Action Taken 
2.5 As per the reformed mandate and Indian Space Policy – 2023, ISRO, as the National Space 
Agency, will focus primarily on research and development of new space technologies and applications, 
and for expanding the human understanding of outer space. M/s New  Space India Limited (NSIL), as 
the Public Sector Undertaking under DOS, shall be responsible for commercializing space technologies 
and platforms created through public expenditure.

present activities of ISRO.

MAJOR ACHIEVEMENTS IN THE RECENT PAST 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.18 Considering the immense potential of downstream data applications across various fields of 
governance and public service delivery such as MG -NREGA, DBT, Survey and Monitoring of land, 
location based services, domestic navigation, disaster warning, etc. the Committee recommends that 
the Department collaborates with more stakeholders, service providers and data distribution platforms 
so that more people can benefit from data based service  delivery and also add to the revenue of the 
Department.                                                                                                                          (Para 3.9 ibid) 
Action Taken 
2.19 Following steps have been taken by the Depar tment to ensure broad -based penetration of 
downstream services:  
(a) For greater interaction amongst various user departments, an inter-ministerial mechanism called 
Space Applications Management System (SAMS) has been established by Government, for identif ying 
the requirements of space based services, and to recommend necessary space technology interventions 
to ensure its maximal utilisation in the respective thematic domains. SAMS framework comprises of a 
Planning Committee (PC -SAMS) as its apex body and i s supported by ten theme -specific Standing 
Committees (SCs). PC -SAMS oversees the SAMS activities, provides policy guidelines and 
recommends suitable funding mechanism to realize space assets & associated ground segment, in line 
with the space sector reforms announced by the Government. 
(b) As per the Space Policy 2023, ISRO’s remote sensing data of spatial resolution >=5m is to be 
made accessible on free & open basis to all, while remote sensing data of resolution <5m is to be made 
available free of cost t o Government entities and at fair & transparent pricing to non -governmental 
users. ISRO has established a web -based data dissemination system called Bhoonidhi Geoportal for 
hassle-free dissemination of remote sensing data to the users. 
(c) Interaction with non-governmental entities and academia with reference to Earth Observation 
data utilization & applications is promoted through the IN-SPACe mechanism.  
(d) Capacity building of the stakeholders is also being carried out regularly, for enabling their 
significant involvement in the utilisation of space based inputs.  
(e) In order to provide PNT service to strategic users, Department of Space is working closely with 
the users for building robust space and ground segments and for ensuring comprehensive deploym ent 
in strategic user segment. 
(f) In transportation sector, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) has mandated 
panic button and location tracking device in all public and commercial vehicles to ensure safety of 

10 
 
passengers and timely help during distress. NavIC has been identified as an essential PNT system for 
vehicle location tracking. Additionally, MoRTH is in consultation with the Department for establishing 
the overall framework of NavIC/GNSS-based tolling in the country. Indian Railways has been utilising 
NavIC and GAGAN based devices for Real -time Train Information System (RTIS). Department is in 
consultation with the stakeholders in maritime domain, including Ministry of Shipping (MoS), for 
introducing NavIC in the maritime equipments. Depa rtment had successfully demonstrated NavIC 
based vessel positioning system to MoS. Recognition of NavIC by the International Maritime 
Organisation (IMO) has been possible due to combined efforts of the Department and MoS. 
Department is in regular consultation with Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA) for adoption of GAGAN 
in civilian aircrafts. Also, MoCA has recommended NavIC as the PNT system in Drone Rules 2021. 
(g) Department is working closely with the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) for 
integrating NavIC in Aadhaar enrolment devices. Department has facilitated successful conduction of 
field trials, and is providing technical expertise in finalisation of procurement specification of the 
devices.  
(h) Department is working with Department o f Consumer Affairs (DoCA) and National Physical 
Laboratory (NPL) for the implementation of country -wide IST dissemination system. Department is 
providing technical expertise and in-house developed software for building the system.  
(i) Department is in con sultation with telecom OEMs and chipset manufacturers for ensuring 
seamless adoption of NavIC in mobile handsets in the country. The interaction is facilitated by 
Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY) and Department of Telecom (DoT). Industry has already 
indicated willingness and readiness for incorporating NavIC in 5G handsets.  
(j) Department has supported National Disaster Management Agency (NDMA) for utilising NavIC 
as an alert dissemination system for major natural disasters like landslides, earthquak es, floods, 
avalanches, etc. Indian National Centre for Ocean Information System (INCOIS) already utilises 
NavIC to broadcast cyclones, high waves, and tsunamis alert messages for fishermen venturing into 
deep sea. 
(k) Department is engaged with the Indian  industry, startups, and academia for promoting NavIC -
based PNT ecosystem in the country. This engagement is through technology transfers, test facility 
support, startup challenges, student hackathons, and workshops. Department also works in close 
coordination with the industries in formulating various standards applicable to the PNT domain. Some 
of the standards bodies that are actively working on NavIC standards are the Bureau of Indian 
Standards (BIS), Telecom Standards Development Society (TSDSI), Telec om Engineering Centre 
(TEC), Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP), Radio Technical Commission for Maritime 
Services (RTCM), International Electrotechnical Committee (IEC), and International Standards 
Organisation (ISO).

INDIGENISATION 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.23 The Committee notes that the current import dependencies in satellite systems are to the tune of 
45-50 percent and that the Department is making earnest efforts in achieving higher indigenization. 
The Committee recommends that the Department collaborates more intensel y with the Indian industry 
as well as with the Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology in finding out solutions towards 
creating the support ecosystem that enables domestic manufacturing of critical electronics 
components. This shall not only save  expensive foreign reserves but also shuttle India into the orbit of 
semiconductors and related critical technologies.                                (Para 6.4 ibid) 
Action Taken 
2.24 Department has taken cognizance of the recommendations of the Committee to collaborate 
more intensely with the Indian industry as well as with the Ministry of Electronics & Information 
Technology in finding out solutions towards creating the support ecosystem that enables domestic 
manufacturing of critical electronics componen ts. Towards this, some of the measures taken to reduce 
import dependency are as follows: 
(a) Department has a continuous program me for indigenis ation and has achieved success in 
indigenizing some passive electronic components as well as critical components  like processors and 
data converters in association with Semiconductor Laboratory, Chandigarh. 
(b) Indigenisation programs for critical technologies such as Travelling wave Tube Amplifiers, 
Electronics packaging including LTCC (Low temperature co -fired cer amic) & HTCC (High 
temperature co -fired ceramic) package technology for RF, Optical, and Digital systems, ASICs  
(Application Specific Integrated Circuits). 
(c) Indigenisation targeted for Electro -Optical Detectors development using indigenous design, RF 
high power GaN HEMT technology foundry development. 
(d) The imported items are published in the Procurement Live Register  (PLR) portal of ISRO 
website to invite Expression of Interest (EoI) from Indian Industries for indigenous development.

Recommendation/Observation 
3.1 The Committee, at this juncture, would like to reiterate its views of t he importance of making 
BHUVAN a widely adopted -widely used -commercial application. BHUVAN has the potential of 
catering to multiple stakeholders across different verticals and the Committee strongly recommends 
that the Department should explore ways throu gh strategic partnerships with the Private Sector in 
popularizing BHUVAN application. This shall not only help the subscribers of BHUVAN take data 
backed-better informed decisions but shall also act as a source of revenue for the Department.  
        (Para 3.10 ibid) 
Action Taken 
3.2 Bhuvan has been conceived and developed as a platform for hosting the Geospatial applications 
of Government Ministries/ Departments, in addition to having provisions for visualisation of satellite 
data, and derived thematic inf ormation. Today, Bhuvan hosts a number of Geospatial applications of 
various Ministries/ Departments such as MoRD (MGNREGA, IWMP, PMGSY etc.), MoJS (NHP, 
AIBP, Ground Water Information System, etc.), DoA&FW (RKVY Asset monitoring, Horticulture 
Development, etc.), MoEF&CC (ENVIS), MoH&UA (PMAY-HFA-Urban, Urban Information System 
etc.), MHA (National Database for Emergency Management; NDEM, Island Information System, 
Disaster Management Support Service for Flood, Landslide, Forest Fire, etc.), MEA (portal for  islands 
under FIPIC), Ministry of Culture (Heritage Sites & Monuments) etc. 
  
3.3 Department has taken cognizance of the direction given by the Committee to explore strategic 
partnership with Private Sector for popularizing Bhuvan and exploiting its poten tial for wider use, 
including commercial exploitation. Steps have already been taken in this direction, such as ISRO 
signing MoU with CE Info Systems (Map My India; MMI), for mutual exchange of geospatial services 
of MMI & ISRO’s Geoportals.  
 
3.4 Further, modalities are being worked out for extending the capabilities of Bhuvan in terms of 
Geospatial Platform, Data, Decision Support Tools, Software etc. for its utilisation by wider audience 
for decision support and also for commercial exploitation. 
 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
 
3.5 While the Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department in popularizing the 
BHUVAN application, the Committee expects tha t the Department would take concrete  steps to 
explore the potential of BHUVAN especially in  the day to day life of the citizens of the country. 
The Department has also informed that ISRO has signed  a MoU with CE Info Systems. The 
Committee desires that the Department may furnish the detailed information regarding the 
revenue being realized from this MoU. The Committee recommends that ISRO should explore 
more such partnerships with private players to reach larger sections of the society.
