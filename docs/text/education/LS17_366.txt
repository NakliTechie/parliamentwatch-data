INTRODUCTION 
 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, having been 
authorized by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby 
present this Three Hundred and Sixty Sixth Report of the Committee on the 
Demands for Grants (2025-26) (Demand No.102) pertaining to the Ministry of 
Youth Affairs and Sports. 
2.  In accordance with the constitutional requirement under Article 113, 
the estimated expenditure of Ministries/Departments of Government of India 
projected under various Demands for Grants for the upcoming Financial Year 
needs to be voted by Parliament. As a sequel thereto, Demands for Grants of 
the relevant Ministries/Departments stand referred to Department-related 
Parliamentary Standing Committee concerned to make a close scrutiny thereof 
under Rule 270 of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council 
of States. While making scrutiny of the Demands for Grants, the Committee 
has made an appraisal of the performance, programmes, schemes and policies 
of the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports vis-à-vis expenditure made out of 
Consolidated Fund of India in the current Financial Year. 
 
3. In this Report, a scrutiny of Demand No.102 pertaining to the 
Department of Youth Affairs and Department of Sports of the Ministry of 
Youth Affairs and Sports for the Financial Year 2025-26 has been made by the 
Committee.  
 
 
4. During the course of examination of Demands, the Committee heard 
the views of the Secretary, Department of Youth Affairs and Department of 
Sports along with representatives of the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan 
(NYKS), National Service Scheme (NSS); Directors-General of            the Sports 
Authority of India (SAI) and National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) and 
Vice-Chancellor of the                                National Sports University in its meetings held on 17th 
February, 2025. 
 
5. The Committee, while making its observations/recommendations, has 
mainly relied upon the following: - 
(i) Presentations made by the Secretary of the Department of Youth 
Affairs and Department of Sports of the Ministry of Youth Affairs 
and Sports covering Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), 
National Service Scheme (NSS), Sports Authority of India (SAI), 
National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) and National Sports 
University under the Administrative Control of the Ministry of 
Youth Affairs and Sports;      
 
(ii) Detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Youth Affairs and 
Sports for the year 2025-26;

REPORT Department of Youth Affairs 
1. INTRODUCTION 
 1.1 India has the largest youth population in the world. To optimally tap their constructive and 
creative energies, the Department of Youth Affairs (DoYA) pursues the twin objectives of personality 
- development and nation building that is, developing the personality of youth and involving them in 
various nation-building activities constructive leadership skills to promote the spirit of community 
service through its organisations. They participate in various initiatives and provide support to all 
Ministries, bringing in Janbhagidari-se-Janvishwas.  All organisations work through both their field 
presence and My Bharat portal. The objective of this entire organisation is to provide exposure to 
youth in diverse work settings, cutting across departmental silos, so as to facilitate building vast youth 
network.  The Department has also recognized "Adolescents" as an important segment of the Youth. 
The role of the DoYA is to act as a facilitator and catalytic agent for youth development. Since youth 
is a very diverse category, the Department of Youth Affairs synergies their efforts with multiple 
Departments like Education, Health and Family Welfare etc. to cater to the interests of youth. The 
State Governments and a number of other stakeholders are also working to support youth development 
and to enable productive youth participation. The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports was bi-furcated 
into two Departments in April, 2008, namely, Department of YouthAffairs and Department of Sports. 
1.2 The Department of Youth Affairs formulated the National Youth Policy (NYP), 2014, which 
proposes a holistic ‘vision’ for the youth of India and “To empower youth of the country to achieve 
their full potential, and through them enable India to find its rightful place in the community of 
nations".In order to realize this Vision, the NYP-2014 identifies 5 clearly defined ‘Objectives’ which 
need to be pursued and the ‘Priority Areas’ under each of the Objectives. The objectives and priority 
areas identified under the NYP-2014 are summarized below:

1.3  Vision: To act as a key enabler in the process of all-round development and empowerment of 
Youth of the country, to enable them to realize their full potential and thereby contribute to the nation-
building process. To develop strong and healthy generations. To instill social values and promote 
community service, facilitate participation and civic engagement, support youth at risk and create 
equitable opportunity for all. 
1.4 Mission: 
 To formulate policies aimed at promotion and development of Youth of the Country. 

2 
 
 To formulate and implement policies and programmes aimed at facilitating the all round 
development of the personality of youth through community service. 
 To formulate and implement policies and programmes aimed at involving the youth in 
various nation-building activities. 
 Strengthen engagement programmes for youth. 
 Support organizations working towards spreading social values and harmony 
 Leverage community development organization 
 Engage youth outside the political system 
 To leverage the large number of youth organizations  toward community development, to 
generate positive outcomes at the grassroots level 
 Promote volunteerism by involving youth in community development activities.

The detailed of schemes/initiatives implemented by DoYA, is as under: 
2.2. RashtriyaYuva Sashaktikaran Karyakram: 
2.2.1  Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan: Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS) is one of the largest 
youth organisations in the world. NYKS has presence in 623 districts of the country through Nehru 
Yuva Kendras (NYKs). The objective of the programme is to develop the personality and leadership 
qualities of the youth and to engage them in nation-building activities.  
2.2.2 The areas of focus of the NYKS activities include literacy and education, health and family 
welfare, sanitation and cleanliness, environment conservation, awareness on social issues, women 
empowerment, rural development, skill development and self-employment, entrepreneurship 
development, civic education, disaster relief and rehabilitation, etc. The youth associated with Nehru 

9 
 
Yuva Kendras are not only socially aware and motivated, but are also inclined towards social 
development work through voluntary efforts. 
2.2.3 In BE 2024-25 Rs 426.00 crores was allocated but in RE 2024-25 the amount was reduced to 
Rs 412.00 crores. The projection of MoF for BE 2025-26 is Rs 556.50 crores and BE 2025-26 
allocation stands at Rs 423.50 crores.The variations of percentage in allocations from BE 2025-26 
over RE 2024-25 stands at 2.8%. BE 2025-26 over projections of MoF stands at -23.9%. While 
variations of percentage in allocations from BE 2025-26 over BE 2024-25 stands at -0.6%.  
2.2.4 The Committee was informed that for BE 2025-26, an additional requirement are proposed 
because of the cited reasons - (i) for payment of Salary of existing 1146 employees, (ii) for payment of 
salary of 674 new recruits staff expected to join in next FY, (iii) for meeting the retirement dues of 
retired employees in FY 2025-26, (iv) for recruitment exercise to be conducted in next FY, (v) for 
core programmes and operations of project management unit. 
2.2.5 Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan, National Youth Corps, National Programme for Youth and 
Adolescent Development (NPYAD), Youth Hostels (YH), National Young Leaders Programme 
(NYLP), International Cooperation, Assistance to Scouting and Guiding Organisations, these seven 
programmes are under the RYSK.   
2.2.6 The Committee observes that the sanctioned strength for Nehru Yuva Kendra 
Sangathan is 2,273, but that the number of positions filled is only 1,184. There are therefore, 
1,089 vacant positions. The Ministry in its response noted that before Covid, three initiatives 
were taken through which District Youth Officers were given employment in NYKS. The 
Committee was informed that these vacancies are no longer filled through the UPSC or the 
Staff Selection Commission but through the Institute of Banking Personnel Selection (IBPS), 
as per the decision of the Governing Board. The Committee recommends that these vacancies 
must be filled at the soonest.  
2.2.7 The Committee notes with concern that of the sanctioned post of 560 District Youth 
Officer, a total of 240 posts are lying vacant, and that the last direct recruitment process was 
carried out in the year FY 2019-20. The NYKS designs its core programmes keeping the 
District as a base unit for conduct of the programme. The Committee was informed that in 
the absence of regular District Youth Officer, additional charge of the said districts has been 
assigned to officials from neighboring Kendras. The fact that the existing DYOs have been 
tasked with running multiple Nehru Yuva Kendras is a constraint on their ability to 
effectively run the programs under this scheme. Therefore, the Committee recommends that 
the Department conduct recruitment to fill all existing vacancies. An additional demand of 
funds may be submitted to the Finance Ministry at RE stage of FY 2025-26.  
2.2.8 The Committee notes that a separate NYKS has not been established in every district of 
the country. It has been created only in 623 districts, while the number of districts in the 
country totals 788 districts. The new districts do not have staff positions sanctioned yet nor a 
Nehru Yuva Kendra.  Therefore, taking a comprehensive view of staff positions, the 
Committee observes that the current sanctioned positions may be insufficient for the 
discharge of the NYKS responsibilities across all districts in the country. Therefore, in 
addition to the filling of vacancies, the number of sanctioned posts in the NYKS may need to 
be enhanced, and funds need to be provided for expansion of the organization in all districts. 
2.2.9 The Committee queried as to why the RE for the FY 2024-25 was not utilized and as to 
why the BE for the FY 2025-26 was reduced when the resources were direly needed. The 
Committee notes that there is an increase in the budget outlay for the current financial year 
and recommends that the Department take all steps to ensure that the same is not repeated. 
 

10 
 
2.2.10 The Committee was informed that the Nehru Yuva Kendras ran at least 42 core 
programmes and 11 special programmes in the last few years. The Committee is concerned 
that the proliferation of programmes and activities dilutes the brand of the NYKS. The 
Committee was informed that each year, the NYKS core programmes are set by the Annual 
Action Plan approved by the Board of Governors. The Committee recommends that the 
NYKS adopt a few flagship programs that are conducted every year without fail, so as to 
provide a focus to the organization and build its brand positioning among youth. Additional 
special programmes may be instituted as per the decision of the Board of Governors.  
2.2.11 The Committee was informed that the NYKS programs are designed to instill a sense of 
civic responsibility and build personality traits that help participants in their academic and 
professional journeys. However, the Committee was informed that there is currently no 
mechanism to track the professional trajectories of NYKS participants beyond their 
association with the NYKS. The Committee believes that tracking the alumni of the programs 
can help inform the Department about the long-term utility of the program, as well as create 
an alumni network that can benefit the existing volunteers. The same provision can be 
extended to NSS volunteers and NYVs as well. The Committee notes that the MY Bharat 
Platform has provided a dedicated profile page for each youth to describe their professional 
interest.  For Experiential Learning Program conducted on the portal, a certificate of 
participation is also reflected on the youth profile page as an incentive to youth to update 
their profiles from time to time.  This facility can be used to track the NYK volunteers, NSS 
volunteers, and NYVs after their association with the Department and its activities ends.  
2.2.12 The Committee noted with satisfaction that more than 2,000 Padyatras were organised 
across the country to commemorate the Constitution Day 2024 celebrating 75 years of Indian 
constitution, in which more than 1.5 lakh youth and others participated where oath of the 
Preamble to the Constitution was integral part of the Padyatra. The theme of these padyatras 
was Humaara Samvidhaan, Humaara Swabhimaan. On the same lines, the Committee 
recommends that all youth programs must begin with oath taking of the Preamble to the 
Constitution, and the recitation of the National Pledge. Similarly, all NYKS building and 
other structures operated by the Department must feature a copy of the Preamble to the 
Indian Constitution to build a culture of Constitutional morality among our youth. 
 
2.2.13 The Committee recommends that the Department should work more closely with the 
District Collectors, the State administration, and elected representatives to involve them in 
programmes being conducted at the district level so that they can leverage their own networks 
to enhance youth participation in the schemes.  
 
2.2.14 The Committee notes the work done by NYKS, NSS, and local Youth Clubs in 
promoting peace and social harmony in Manipur, and in bringing internally Displaced 
Persons into relief camps. The Committee encourages the Department to continue supporting 
these groups in these endeavours and to provide them with capacity building support to 
promote social harmony at the grassroots level.  
2.3 National Youth Corps: 
2.3.1 The Scheme of National Youth Corps (NYC) is being implemented through NYKS. The main 
objectives of the Scheme are – 
i. To set up a group of disciplined and dedicated youth who have the inclination and spirit to 
engage in the task of nation-building; 
ii. To facilitate the realization of inclusive growth (both social and economic); 

11 
 
iii. To act as focal points for dissemination of information, basic knowledge in the community; 
iv. To act as group modulators and peer group educators; and 
v. To act as role models for the younger cohort, especially towards enhancement of public ethics, 
probity and dignity of labour.  
2.3.2 Under the Scheme, youth in the age-group of 18-25 years are engaged as volunteers to serve 
up to a maximum of 2 years in nation-building activities at block level. 
2.3.3 Further, the DoYA submitted that the BE 2024-25 for the National Youth Corps was ₹75 
Crores.However, due to administrative reasons, the target number of NYV volunteers could not be 
deployed resulting in under-utilization of the budget allocation made under the scheme. Thus, the RE 
2024-25 was reduced to ₹ 18.25 Crores. Further, for 2025-26, it has been planned to deploy around 
13,000 NYV volunteers throughout the country for which enhanced allocation of Rs 65.00 crore shall 
be utilised for their training and for payment of monthly honorarium. These volunteers work as a 
bridge between the youth and the field officers of NYKS/MY Bharat and play an active role in the 
implementation of the programs of the Department. As of now, the volunteers are paid an honorarium 
of ₹ 5000/- each on monthly basis. There is no proposal for increasing the honorarium. 
2.3.4 The Committee notes with concern that there is a steady decrease in the RE of the 
National Service Scheme as compared to its BE for the last four financial years – the RE in 
2021-22 was 89.48% of the BE; in 2022-23 it was 87.91%, in 2023-24 it was 72.07% and in 2024-
25 it was 72.05%. The Committee recommends that the Department look into the reasons for 
the same and ensure that the enhanced budget for FY25-26 is utilized fully.  
2.3.5 The Committee notes that the National Youth Volunteers (NYVs) are the key 
implementers of this scheme since they work as a bridge between the youth and the field officers 
of the NYKS and MY Bharat. However, in the FY 2024-25, the Department was not able to 
reach the target number of NYVs and the scheme was implemented only in eight States and 
UTs. This must be avoided in the future.  
2.3.6 The Committee notes that during the EFC meeting for the continuation of RYSK scheme 
for the period from 2021-22 to 2025-26, all the seven schemes of RYSK including NYC were 
deliberated upon. The proposal of the Ministry for enhancing the honorarium of NYVs from Rs. 
5000/- to Rs. 15000/- was also discussed but not agreed to by the EFC. However, the Committee 
recommends that the Department continue to advocate for an increased honorarium of Rs. 
15,000 per month for NYVs. 
2.4  National Programme for Youth and Adolescent Development:  
2.4.1 National Programme for Youth and Adolescent Development (NPYAD) is an “Umbrella 
Scheme” of the Ministry under which financial assistance is provided to Government/ non-
Government organisations for taking up activities for youth and adolescent development. The 
assistance under NPYAD is provided under 5 major components, namely, 
a) Youth Leadership and Personality Development Training 
b) Promotion of National Integration (National Integration Camps, Inter-State Youth Exchange 
Programmes, Youth Festivals, multi-cultural activities, etc.) 
c) Promotion of Adventure; Tenzing Norgay National Adventure Awards 
d) Development and Empowerment of Adolescents (Life Skills Education, Counseling, Career 
Guidance, etc.) 
e) Technical and Resource Development (Research and Studies on Youth issues, 
Documentation, Seminars/ Workshops) 

12 
 
2.4.2 The DoYA has submitted that under the scheme, ₹ 65.00 crores has been allocated for BE 
2025-26. For 2024-25, the BE was ₹ 22.00 crores, the RE was ₹ 45.00 crores, and the expenditure was 
₹ 10.45 crores only. It may be observed that the reason for variation was given as the funds are 
required for the activities:- National Youth Festival, Grant to HMI, JIMWS, NYKS & NGOs for 
adventure/ scheme activities, conducting of Tenzing Norgay National Adventure Award (TNNAA) & 
National Youth Award (NYA) and North East Youth Festival.National Youth Festival (NYF) is 
organized in collaboration with one of the host State/UT. For FY 2024-25, NYF was restructured and 
called Viksit Bharat Young Leaders Dialogue (VBYLD). There was no host state this time and the 
expenses were borne by the department. As such the expenditure for the festival has been increased 
and thus provision for additional funds at RE stage was made for the NPYAD Scheme.

problem-solving exercise;

d) To utilize their knowledge in finding practical solutions to individual and community 
problems; 
e) To gain skills in mobilizing community participation; 
f) To acquire leadership qualities and democratic values; 
g) To develop capacity to meet emergencies and natural disasters; and  
h) To practice national integration and social harmony.

OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SPORTS 3.1 The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports was initially set up as the Department of Sports in 1982 at the time of the organization of the IX Asian Games in New Delhi.  Its name was changed to the Department of Youth Affairs & Sports during celebration of the International Youth Year, 1985.  It became a Ministry on 27th May, 2000.  Subsequently, the Ministry has been bifurcated in Department. of Youth Affairs and Department of Sports under two separate Secretaries w.e.f. 30th April,2008.   The specific subjects being dealt with by these two Departments are contained in the Order of the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961. 3.2 Sports promotion is primarily the responsibility of the various National Sports Federations which are autonomous.  The role of the Government is to create the infrastructure and promote capacity building for broad-basing sports as well as for achieving excellence in various competitive events at the national and international levels.  The Department schemes are geared towards achieving these objectives. Vision and Mission 3.3 Sports and games are crucial for holistic human development, serving not just as entertainment and physical exercise, but also fostering a spirit of healthy competition and community bonding. Athletic accomplishments on the global stage have long been a source of national pride.  3.4 The significance of sports for the nation has transcended from merely showcasing excellence on the global stage to playing a multifaceted role in contributing to the holistic growth of the country by promoting healthy lifestyles, driving economic activities, fostering community engagement, and providing opportunities for national pride through international achievements. Recognizing this multidimensional importance in nation-building, the Government of India has implemented several key schemes and initiatives through the Department of Sports, aimed at supporting athletes and stakeholders in reaching their full potential across disciplines. With flagship programs like Khelo India, the National Sports University, and the National Centre for Sports Science and Research, the aim is to develop India's sporting infrastructure and capabilities at par with major nations. While sports garnered attention for decades, it received dedicated push after hosting the 1982 Asian Games, leading to the National Sports Policies of 1984 and 2001 which outlined an organized framework for 

26 
 
development and promotion of sports, with the twin pillars of "Broad-basing" and "Achieving Excellence" reflecting the commitment to fostering a vigorous sporting culture and elite talent. As modern sports have become highly competitive, the use of advanced infrastructure, equipment, and scientific support has transformed the international sporting landscape. Recognizing the growing demands for such state-of-the-art resources, the Government of India has also undertaken several initiatives to assist athletes by providing training, exposure to international competitions, and access to scientific backing and cutting-edge equipment. This comprehensive support aims to equip Indian sportspersons with the tools necessary to excel on the world stage. Proposed National Sports Policy (NSP), 2025: 3.5 In terms of the National Sports Policy, 2001, the Central Government, in conjunction with the State Government, the Olympic Association (IOA) and the National Sports Federation will concertedly pursue the twin objectives of "Broad-basing" of Sports and "Achieving Excellence in Sports at the National and International levels". Sports activities, in which the country has potential strength and competitive advantage, need to be vigorously promoted. Towards this end, Sports and Physical Education would be integrated more effectively with the Education Curriculum. 3.6 While the broad-basing of Sports will, primarily remain a responsibility of the State Governments, the Union Government will actively supplement their efforts in this direction and for taping the latent talent, including in the rural and tribal areas. The Union Government and the Sports Authority of India (SAI), in association with the Indian Olympic Association (IOA) and the National Sports Federations, will focus specific attention on the objective of achieving excellence at the National and International levels. 3.7 Various sports disciplines will be prioritized on the basis of proven potential, popularity and international performance. Particular emphasis will be placed on the development of such priority disciplines and the prioritization reviewed, from time to time. The IOA and the State Governments would also accord higher priority to such disciplines. In planning the development of various disciplines, the genetic and geographical variations within the country would be taken into account so that in areas of potential, in particular disciplines, timely steps may be taken to harness the existing and emerging talent. Centers of excellence will be set up to identify and train outstanding sportspersons including sports academies where young and talented sports persons will be groomed to achieve higher levels of performance in the international sports arena. 3.8 The Department of Sports is in the way of finalizing the National Sports Policy (NSP), 2025. The draft policy was circulated to various stake holders for their comments. The comments received from various stake holders such as various Departments/Ministries of Central Government, various National Sports Federations 

27 
 
and general public have been incorporated. NSP 2025 has been submitted to Cabinet Secretariat for the approval of the Cabinet.  3.9 DEPARTMENT OF SPORTS – ORGANOGRAM:  (a) Sports Authority of India (SAI) (i) 12 Regional Centres (RCs)  24 National Centres of Excellence (NCOEs) and 69 SAI Training Centres (STCs (ii) Academic Centres  Netaji Subhash Institute of Sports (NSNIS), Patiala  Lakshmibai National College of Physical Education (LNCPE), Trivandrum (iii) 5 Stadias (b) National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) (c) National Dope Testing Laboratory (NDTL) (d) National Sports University (NSU) (e) Lakshmibai National Institute of Physical Education (LNIPE) (i) Main Campus (Gwalior) (ii) Guwahati Campus

5.10 NATIONAL ANTI-DOPING AGENCY 
5.10.1   The National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) is the apex body in India responsible for 
promoting, coordinating and monitoring the doping control program in sports. Its mandate is to 
ensure that all sports activities in the country are free from doping practices, aligning with the 
international code established by the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA). 
5.10.2  The primary functions of NADA are as follows: 
i. Doping Control: Conduct in-competition and out-of-competition testing of athletes. Collect 
and analyze samples in accredited laboratories. 
ii. Results Management: Handle the process of identifying violations and determining 
sanctions. Ensure compliance with international standards for testing and results. 
iii. Anti-Doping Education Awareness: Organize workshops, seminars, and campaigns to 
spread awareness about anti-doping rules and the dangers of performance-enhancing drugs. 
Disseminate educational materials to athletes, coaches, and support staff. 
iv. Regulation and Enforcement: Formulate and implement anti-doping rules and regulations in 
India. Monitor compliance with anti-doping code of WADA. 
 
5.10.3  The key challenges faced by NADA are as follows - 
the National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) faces several challenges in its efforts to maintain 
doping-free environment in sports. These challenges arise from various factors, including the 
evolving nature of doping practices. Many athletes, especially at the grassroots level, are unaware 
of banned substances and inadvertent doping risks through contaminated supplements. Every efforts 
are being made to reach the athletes for anti-doping education awareness at the grass root level i.e 
school/college etc.  
5.10.4  The Committee was informed that NADA has taken the following measures to 
ensure the accuracy and integrity of its testing procedures.These measures are designed to adhere to 
international standards and guidelines set by the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA). The Details 
are as under: 
i. Compliance with WADA Code. 
ii. Doping Control Officers (DCOs) and chaperones are thoroughly trained and certified 
to handle sample collection, ensuring standardized and professional procedure 
iii. A strict chain of custody is maintained to prevent tampering or mismanagement of 
samples 
iv. Samples are collected under standardized conditions, with athletes observed by 
trained personnel to prevent sample adulteration or substitution. 
v. Sample collection kits are tamper-proof and sealed in the athlete's presence. 
vi. Athletes are educated about the testing process to ensure they understand their rights, 
responsibilities, and the importance of compliance. 
5.10.5  By implementing above measures, NADA strives to ensure that its testing procedures are 
accurate, fair, thereby upholding the integrity of sports and protecting clean athletes. 
5.10.6  The National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) plays a crucial role in educating athletes, 
coaches, and support staff about anti-doping rules to promote fair play and compliance. The details 
of such measures are as under:  
i. NADA conducts workshops, seminars, and training programs across various regions 
and sports disciplines. These sessions are tailored for athletes, coaches, medical staff, 
and other support personnel. 

52 
 
ii. NADA runs campaigns to promote a doping-free culture, emphasizing the importance 
of ethical sportsmanship. 
iii. NADA offers e-learning modules and resources accessible through its official website. 
iv. NADA encourages the use of the Anti-Doping Education and Learning (ADEL) 
platform for global anti-doping education. 
v. NADA publishes materials such as the Anti-Doping Rulebook, TUE guidelines, and 
supplement advisories. 
vi. These materials are often translated into regional languages to ensure broader reach. 
vii. NADA disseminates the updated WADA Prohibited List annually. 
viii. Athletes are educated about the risks of contaminated or unverified supplements and 
encouraged to consult experts before use. 
ix. Athletes and medical staff are informed about the process of applying for TUEs for 
medications containing prohibited substances through workshops. 
x. NADA partners with national sports federations, state associations, and educational 
institutions to organize joint awareness initiatives. 
xi. NADA leverages social media platforms to disseminate information quickly and 
effectively. 
5.10.7  The National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) collaborates with other anti-doping agencies 
and international organizations to strengthen its efforts in ensuring fair play and clean sports. These 
collaborations allow NADA to align with global standards, share resources, and combat doping 
effectively.  The initiative taken in this regard, are as under: 
(i) Partnership with the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) 
(ii) NADA through regional coordination works with RADOs in South Asia to harmonize anti-
doping efforts and share expertise. 
(iii) NADA participates in international awareness campaigns like Play True Day, promoting 
clean sports globally. 
5.10.8  NADA coordinates with WADA and other agencies to align its TUE processes with 
global standards, ensuring fairness in granting exemptions. As a member, NADA benefits from 
shared best practices, policies, and anti-doping strategies among NADOs worldwide. NADA’s staff 
and Doping Control Officers (DCOs) receive training from WADA and other NADOs on advanced 
testing and investigation techniques. 
5.10.9   The increase in Anti-Doping Rule Violations (ADRVs) from 123 in 2022 to 249 in 
2024 is primarily attributed to the expansion of the testing programme and the result is as under: 
 2022: 4,260 tests conducted → 123 violations (2.88% violation rate) 
 2024: 7,466 tests conducted → 249 violations (3.33% violation rate) 
5.10.10  The reason for increase in cases of anti doping rules violations can be attributed to 
the followings:  
(i) The number of tests increased by 75% (from 4,260 to 7,466) in two years. 
(ii) Continued expansion of dope testing is key to maintaining clean sport for fair play. 
(iii) Use of advanced analytical techniques and targeted testing has led to higher detection 
rates.

41. The Committee noted that the Khelo India Games being organized by theDepartment of 
Sports are enabling talented young sportspersons to get experience of international 
standard events. A total of 16 editions of these Games have been hosted so far, where more

ensure maximum participation of sportspersons in these games. The Committee further 
recommends that the name and eligibility of the Khelo India University games may be 
shifted solely to the age of the participant, to ensure a level playing field for all 
sportspersons who any not be enrolled in HEIs.

the age of 8 years and 14 years. The talent so scouted should be supported through

55. The Committee noted that the National Sports University offers a Masters in Physical 
Education course for the duration of two years, with the capacity of thirty seats. There 
is also a Bachelors in Physical Education with fifty seats, and a Master of Science in 
Sports & Coaching course with twenty seats. The Committee recommends that the 
number of seats available under these programs be increased. The Committee also 
recommends that the University be accompanied by a sports school to provide 
educational and residential facilities to sportspersons below the age of 18 years. 
Further, the Committee appreciates the progress in the completion of building NSU’s 
permanent campus in spite of the current disturbances in the state of Manipur. 
(Para 5.11.13) 
56.  The Committee notes that the National Sports University in Manipur is the only 
institution of its kind in the country and was established by an Act of Parliament in 2018. 
It has been running from a temporary facility at Khuman Lampak in Imphal while 
construction activities for the permanent campus at Koutruk are ongoing. The Committee 
notes that the University does not currently have a regular Vice-Chancellor or the 
required faculty. The Committee recommends that the construction of the campus be 
expedited, and that a permanent, full-time Vice Chancellor be appointed so that the 
recruitment of required faculty members may also occur. 
(Para 5.11.14) 
57. The Committee notes that the Department has provisioned for post-retirement support for 
ex-sportspersons who have brought laurels to the country, and that over 850 sportspersons 
have been awarded lifelong pension ranging up to Rs. 20,000/- per month. The Committee 
observes that the career of sports persons and their pensions should be made lucrative so 
that the required talent is attracted and retained properly. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends that the pension due to meritorious sportspersons be not less than the 
pension accorded to a retired Class 1 Officer. 
(Para 5.12.1) 
58. The Committee recommends that the Department take proactive steps to train high 
quality coaches within the country. Furthermore, the Committee recommends that the 
Department institute a protocol that medical professionals should compulsorily 
accompany sports persons in all Games overseas. 
(Para 5.15.1) 
59. The Committee notes that the National Games are typically organized by the Indian 
Olympic Association (IOA) biennially, but that they weren’t organized between 2015 
and 2022, partly due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The Committee recommends that 
the Department work more closely with the Indian Olympic Association to ensure that 
the same is not repeated in the future. The Committee also recommends that the 
Department consider supporting the IOA in organizing the National Games annually. 
The Department may assist the IOA with identifying cities with the requisite sporting 
infrastructure and host the games there so as to avoid having to incur high expenses on 
capital expansion.
