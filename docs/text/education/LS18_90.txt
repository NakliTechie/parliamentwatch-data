I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       India with just 2.4% of the world landmass constitutes 16% of the present world population.  The census of 1991
had indicated the population of India as 846.3 million.  In the decade of 1981-91, 163 million persons were added to the
population of India.  On 11th May, 2000, India is projected to have 1 billion (100 crore) population.  If the present rate
of annual growth of population  of 1.74% continues, India may overtake China in 2045 to become the most populous
country in the world.
1.2      With a view to check the growth of population, the Family Welfare Programme was taken up with the
introduction of Planning in the country.  The objective of the Family Welfare Programme for the country has been to
stabilise population at a level consistent with the needs of national development.  To achieve this goal the Department
continues to implement the three-pronged strategy of :
(i)         improvement in the health status of women and children;
(ii)        fulfillment of the unmet needs of contraception with a special focus on widening the range of choice
available to the people; and
(iii)       professionalising and broad-basing the Information, Education    and Communication Campaign on
Family Welfare, by not only using the mass media, but also involving the community, NGOs, women’s
organisations in the local specific IEC work at the district, sub-district and village level.

II.         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The Committee notes that the allocation for the Department has been substantially enhanced from Rs. 2940.60
crores in  BE 1999-2000 to Rs. 3541.47 crores in 2000-2001.  Out of  Rs. 3541.47 crores, Rs. 3520.00 crores form the
Plan component and Rs. 21.47 crores is the non-Plan component.
Commenting on the allocation made to the Department, the Secretary stated that in the Annual Plan proposal for the
year 2000-2001 sent to the Planning Commission, a requirement of Rs. 6056.78 crores was projected.   However, an
outlay of only Rs. 3520 crores  for 2000-2001 has been fixed by the Planning Commission, which is grossly insufficient
and is far below of even the Department’s minimum requirement. 
2.2       The Committee was further informed that the annual plan allocations, though increasing each year, have not
been adequate to meet the existing cost of maintenance of infrastructure.  There has been no increase in the
infrastructure since 1980s.  Besides that, huge arrears on account of committed expenditure of salary etc. have been
building up over the years.  During 1999-2000, additional funds by re-appropriation and supplementary grants of the
order of Rs.400 crore had to be provided to clear the arrears certified on the basis of audit till 1996-97 and 1997-98 in
most of the cases.  The statements of 1998-99 are yet to be received.  Projected requirement for this specific purpose is
Rs.900 crores for 2000-2001.
2.3       Adverse impact of non-settlement of arrear claims of state govts. on the Family Welfare Programme, which is a
100 per cent centrally funded programme has been engaging the attention of the Committee for considerable period. 
Last year, the Committee had recommended that the Planning Commission may be approached for one time grant.   In
response, the Department has informed that a Committee has been set up to solve this problem.   As per the latest
information, recommendations of the aforesaid Committee are yet to be finalised in consultation with the States.   The
Committee feels that setting  up a Committee and then awaiting its recommendations is indirectly an effort to evade the
issue.   Settlement of arrear claims of State Governments  is a long-standing problem which needs a  solution at the
earliest. The Committee reiterates that the Planning Commission should be approached for one-time grant.
2.5       The Committee is not in agreement with the proposal placed by the Department before the XI Finance
Commission in September, 1998 for transfer of liability of maintaining the health infrastructure set up in the States over 
the earlier Plan periods to the State Governments’ budget.  The Committee  fails to understand as to how majority of the
states, being already in a very bad shape so far as mobilisation of resources is concerned, would be able to bear this

of Family Planning.    Provision  under this head shows a declining trend from Rs. 140.00 crores in BE 1999-2000 to Rs.

in RE 1999-2000 and BE 2000-2001 has been reduced on the basis of requirements as per the current norms.   The 
Committee enjoins upon the Department to expedite the approval of revised norms of compensation.

8.1       The Information, Education and Communication (IEC) component of  National Family Welfare Programme is
mainly to create an effective communication strategy to inform the masses about the  means and measures of Family
Welfare programme, educate them about the perils of over population and motivate and persuade them to adopt small
family norm, using all possible channels of media.  A multi-pronged strategy to carry out IEC activities is being
implemented by the Department through respective Mass Education and media set ups created in the states and media
units of the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting.   The activities listed  are –
i)          RCH-IEC activities through Zila Saksharata Samitis
ii)         Mahila Swasthya Sanghs
iii)         Sensitisation of Opinion Leaders Scheme.
iv)        Preparation of IEC Print material
v)         Production of Audio-Visual Software
vi)        Intensification of IEC activities in 135 demographically weak districts etc.
 
Allocation of Rs. 30.00 crores is proposed in BE 2000-2001 for this scheme.   This is in addition to the provision made
in RCH of Rs. 130.00 crores for IEC activities.
8.2       The Committee  finds that a number of activities are being carried out for awareness generation in the masses
about Family Welfare measures. Local agencies are also being actively involved and 135 demographically weak
districts have also been specifically focussed  in this regard. There is no dearth of funds also for this purpose.   The
Committee however, fails to understand that inspite of so many measures, effect of IEC activities is still not reaching the
targetted sector.   The  Committee is of the view  that  in states where level of health and population indicators is much
below the average level, there is a need of having  area specific IEC activities.   In weak pockets and remote,
inaccessible and tribal areas of such states, intensive  campaigns in local dialect and based on local perspectives in
active cooperation with local agencies should be taken.  Mere initiation/launching of such activities would not be
sufficient. Follow up action with modifications and corrective measures as and when necessary would also be required. 
The Committee feels that in states where there is lack of initiative on the part of administration at whatever level,
Department can take steps to associate the local MPs/MLAs in the family welfare measures.   Another effective measure
can be convergence of IEC activities of all the concerned Departments at the Centre.   The Committee feels that in states
also, this kind of exercise would lead to better achievements in family welfare measures.  The Committee recommends
that the Department should take necessary measures in this regard.

as to hasten their development at par with nation’s average, area projects have been taken up with partial foreign
assistance from external agencies.
10.2     Allocation of Rs. 100.00 crores was made for ‘Area Projects’ in 1999-2000 which has been substantially
increased to Rs. 180.00 crores in 2000-2001.   The Department     has stated that this increase was to provide necessary
funds for extending the VIII India Population  Project to 94 additional cities and towns in the states of West Bengal,
Karnataka and  Andhra Pradesh and to take up logistic Development Projects in UP and Tamil Nadu and also to meet
the  escalation cost of IPP-VIII Project in the existing cities of Delhi, Calcutta, Hyderabad and Bangalore and to
complete the project activities of IX India Population Project being implemented in the states of Assam, Karnataka and
Rajasthan.
10.3     The Committee notes that Bihar, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Kerala, Himachal Pradesh and Orissa are the states 
where implementation of Area Projects is reported to be slow.   The progress of implementation of Area Projects is
being assessed on a regular basis through a process of discussion/interaction at different levels and field visits .  The
Committee is of the view that alongwith  regular field visits, surprise inspections/random surveys in weak pockets
should be carried out by the central teams.   Attempts may also be made to have a system of involving the local
MLA/MP in this exercise.

and 1998-99.  However,  as per the latest information, the actual project expenditure was much more than the actuals for

XII.      NATIONAL POPULATION POLICY
12.1     The Committee notes that after many years of deliberation and consultation, the National Population Policy,
recently approved by the Cabinet, has been presented to Parliament.   The Committee has been informed that the
immediate objective of the National Population Policy is to address the unmet needs of contraception, health
infrastructure,  health personnel and to provide  integrated service delivery for basic reproductive and child health care.  
The medium term objective  is to bring the total fertility rates to replacement level by 2010 through vigorous
implementation of intersectoral operational strategies.  The long term objective is to achieve a stable population by
2045, at a level consistent with the requirement of sustainable economic growth, social development and environmental
protection.
12.2     The Committee notes that a number of new structures, i.e. National Commission on Population, State/UT
Commissions on Population, Coordination Cell within the   Planning Commission  and a Technology Mission are
proposed.   The Committee is of the view that although objectives behind these proposed structures are laudable,  states
lagging behind in the implementation of Family Welfare Programme need special focussing.  Only with the active
participation of dedicated personnel both Govt./NGOs at state and central levels,  desired goals can be achieved. Since
programme implementation vests with the state govts., considerable efforts are required to monitor and guide the
planning and implementation of the comprehensive, multi-sectoral national population policy.  The Committee would
also like to  point out that the issue of Family Planning should be given top priority as various development projects
would have no meaning in view of high rate of population growth.  The Committee strongly feels that a meeting of all
political parties should be convened by the Prime Minister to discuss this problem threadbare and arrive at a consensus.

XIV.    COMMITTED LIABILITY
14.1     The issue of committed liability has been engaging the attention of the Committee since 1997-98.  At the end of
the 8
th Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission had issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the committed
expenditure from Plan to the non-plan side .   A lump sum provision of Rs. 500 crores in the Ministry of Finance Budget
for 1997-98 was provided for this purpose.   As a result of this exercise,  however, only a sum of Rs. 150 crores was
transferred from Plan to non-Plan for the various Ministries as committed expenditure during 1997-98.   Rest of the
amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable.   Secretary (Expenditure) while deposing before
the Committee on 27
th March, 1999 had informed that the Tenth Finance Commission had given a breathing time till
1999-2000 for this transition.
14.2     The Department has now informed that the issue of transfer of committed liabilities to non-plan was taken up
with the Ministry of Finance in 1997-98 but it was decided that no provision could be made for committed liability of
the scheme under non-plan as it was not permissible under the Planning Commission guidelines on the subject which
reads as follows :-
“The committed expenditure liability is to be borne by the Central Govt. in respect of Central Plan schemes and by the
State govts. in respect of state Plan schemes.   As regards all centrally sponsored schemes, the state govts. will provide
for the committed expenditure liability”
14.3     The Committee notes with serious concern that while grants for Plan Schemes are getting reduced, non-Plan
allocation and expenditure are steadily  increasing.   What is happening is that the committed expenditure which should
appropriately be charged to the Pool, is being passed on to the respective Departments which is totally against the
guidelines of Tenth Finance Commission/Planning Commission.  If the committed expenditure is charged to the Pool, it
will not have any burden on the specific schemes of the Department.  Secretary (Expenditure), who appeared before the
Committee last year was in full agreement with the stand taken by the Committee.   The Committee  is, however,
constrained to observe that in spite of Committee’s repeated efforts/directives to assist the Departments, no progress has
been made in the matter.   It seems that breathing time till 1999-2000 given by  the Tenth Finance Commission has not
been availed of by any Department.    There is total lack of initiative on the part of the Departments.  The Committee
again emphasises  that the Department should get this matter resolved without any further delay.   The way the 
Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the
Finance Commission and the recommendations made by the Committee.  This tendency, the Committee is constrained
to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.   The Committee again reiterates that the
Department should pursue the matter and report to the Committee about the outcome thereof.
