REPORT
           
I.            INTRODUCTION
 
1.1       Growth of the economy has to be not only faster but also inclusive to achieve overall human development.   The flow
of benefits should be sufficiently wide spread and equally distributed among all sections of society, especially women and
children.              As per 2001 Census of India, of the total population of 102.9 crores, there are 49.6 crores (48.3%) women.
Women and children (child 0-14 years) constitute 68.6 crores (66.7%).  Development of Women and children is thus of
paramount importance and sets the pace for overall development.
 
1.2       The Ministry of Women and Child Development is the nodal ministry for all matters pertaining to the welfare,
development and empowerment of women and children in the country.  A separate Ministry for Women and Child
Development came into existence from 30
th January 2006 with the enhanced mandate of dealing with not only policy relating
to children and women but also a comprehensive range of programmes relating to child and women development and
protection.
 
1.3            Secretary, in her presentation before the Committee pointed out that plan allocation of Rs. 5793 crore for 2007-08
showed an approximate increase of 20 per cent over allocation of Rs. 4893 crore for 2006-07. This increase was reflected in
five or six major areas, the foremost being the new schemes.  The Integrated Child Protection Scheme (Rs. 95 crores),
Conditional Cash Transfer Scheme for the Girl Child (Rs. 15 crores) Priyadarshini (Rs 10 crores) and Gender Budgeting (Rs.
3 crores). Besides that, additional provision of Rs. 749 crore was proposed for ICDS, for Swayamsiddha Rs. 20 crores, Creche
Scheme and  the Working Women’s Hostel Scheme- additionality of Rs. 10 crore each and lastly Swadhar getting Rs. 7 crore.
Increase in terms of percentage was 19 per cent for Children Schemes and 40 per cent for Women Schemes.

1.4       The Committee was, however, given to understand that this enhanced allocation was much below the projected
requirements of Rs. 9463.58 crores made out by the Ministry.  The Secretary clarified  that it was mainly due to the fact that
procedural formalities for the proposed new schemes were yet to be completed.  Secondly, Planning Commission was of the
view that some ongoing schemes needed to be evaluated in order to decide their continuance in the Eleventh Plan.  The
Committee was informed that by the middle of the financial year, there were chances of additional funds being provided by
the Planning Commission.
 
1.5       The Secretary also informed the Committee that in the overall Budget of Govt. of India for 2007-08, there was an
increase of one per cent in the Gender Budget of the country.  4.8 per cent of the total budget provision for the country was
earmarked for gender budgeting which came to Rs. 31,000 crores. 
 
II.
                BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
 
2.1       Plan allocation for the Ministry has been made at Rs.5793.00 crore in BE 2007-08 against the projected requirement of
Rs.9463.58 crore for Annual Plan 2007-08 which was going to have an adverse impact on sixteen schemes of the Ministry.
 Important among them are ICDS, Gender Budgeting, Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS), Rashtriya Mahila Kosh,
Swadhar, Comprehensive Scheme for Combating Trafficking of Women and Children; and Relief and Rehabilitation of Rape
victims. The Committee would, however, like to point out that under all these schemes, funds have remained more or  less
under-utilised, if the performance during the last three years is taken into account. Underutilization is in those areas where
need is most.  There appear to be great laxity in implementation of these schemes.   The Committee strongly feels that an
assessment of schemes falling behind their targets should be carried out without any further  delay so as to make a justifiable
proposition before the Planning Commission. The Committee understands that this advice has already been given by the
Planning Commission.
 
2.2
              The plan allocation for the year 2006-07 was Rs. 4839.85 crore.  The actual expenditure as reported upto 31 st
December 2006 is Rs. 3140.64 crore which is 58.99% of plan allocation.   The expenditure upto 16 th March, 2007 is Rs.
4286.78 crore which is 88% of plan allocation.   The expenditure on 29 th March, 2007 is 97% of the allocation as reported by
the Secretary during her evidence.
 
2.3       The year 2007-08 marks the beginning of Eleventh Plan period.   The Committee observes that overall performance of
the Ministry during the Tenth Plan can be considered satisfactory, keeping  in view utilization of Rs. 14656.25 crore against
the approved outlay of Rs. 13,780.00 crore. However, separate assessment of women and children-related schemes leads to a
somewhat different position.   Whereas in the case of schemes relating to child development, both physical and financial
targets could be achieved, status of women related schemes having lesser allocation is very discouraging. The Committee
notes that under Central Schemes for women, utilization figures are less than half of the approved outlay.  Against an outlay of
Rs. 971.11 crore, expenditure reported is only Rs. 434.47 crores. Similarly, for central sponsored schemes for women, only
Rs. 130.48 crores could be utilized by the end of Tenth Plan against an allocation of Rs. 275.01 crore.   The Committee is also
constrained to observe that performance   of six schemes of Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment transferred to the
Ministry of Women and Child Development during the Tenth Plan was far from satisfactory. Against an approved   outlay of
Rs. 245.35 crore, only Rs. 166.70 crore could be spent.   The Committee would also like to point out the under the children
sector also, the only exceptions are the Flagship Scheme of ICDS and Creche Scheme.  In case of Training Programme under
ICDS, National Nutrition Mission, allocated funds remained under-utilised/ unutilized.
 
2.4            Committee’s attention has also been drawn by the CAG observations contained in Report No. 1 of 2006, according
to which Test Check of appropriation accounts with reference to re-appropriation orders for 2004-05 revealed that heavy re-
appropriation of funds amounting to Rs. 462 crore were made between the different primary units of re-appropriation
defeating the original purpose/activity as authorized by Parliament.   The Committee apprehends that things may have
remained unchanged in the subsequent years of Tenth Plan also. The Committee thus would like to know the details of
reappropriation of funds made during the last three years and whether the approval of Ministry of Finance was given or not.
The Committee recommends that the emphasis should not be on the quantity of expenditure but on the quality of output that is
coming out of the Schemes/Programmes which is the ultimate objective of all the Schemes/programmes.   The Committee also
emphasizes that re-appropriation is a bad practice of financial management and the Ministry should try to utilize the funds for 
the purposes for which the funds are allocated.
 
2.5       In the light of feedback received about the performance   level during the Tenth Plan period, the Committee would like
to emphasise that every conceivable   measure needs to be taken by the Ministry so that shortfall and deviations from the
prescribed financial norms do not occur during the Eleventh  Plan Period.

2.6       The Committee expresses its serious reservation on the trend of Utilisation of allocated funds during 2006-07. About
38 per cent of fund utilization during the last quarter of the financial year with 15 per cent to be spent during the last month,
i.e. March  goes against the prescribed norms of only 33 per cent utilization during the last quarter. The Committee would,
therefore, like to emphasise that every conceivable attempt should be made for strict compliance of financial norms.
 
2.7       The Committee   has been voicing its concern about the declining trend in both allocation and utilization of funds for
women welfare schemes. For 2006-07, also, inspite of enhanced allocation of Rs. 130.70 crore, expenditure reported upto 16
th
March, 2007 is only Rs. 91.40 crores. Not only this, the Committee notes that it was only in the last quarter that utilization
picked up as expenditure reported upto 31st December, 2006 was only Rs. 58.75 crore.  
 
2.8            Pendency of Utilisation Certificates   under the various schemes handled by the Ministry continues to be an area of
concern for the Committee. The Committee was informed that a total of 6765 UCs have been pending as on date.   The year-
wise position is as under : -

The Committee is deeply disturbed by the status of outstanding Utilisation Certificates. As many as 4697 UCs outstanding for
the period 1986-87 to 2001-02 compels the Committee to conclude that role of the Ministry is perhaps confined to only
disbursement of funds. Year after year, Central Funds are being released to State Govts./ implementing agencies.   Ministry’s
efforts for pursuing the matter with the State Governments as well as the State nodal agencies for submission of the pending
UCs on priority basis seem to have made no impact.   The Committee is also not inclined to believe the contention of the
Ministry that  no further grant is released to any NGO/ implementing agency where UCs are pending. This is borne out by the
pertinent  observations made in the  Audit Report No.3 of 2006 (Para No. 1.4) that
“the Certificates of utilization of grants were required to be furnished by the Ministries/Departments concerned
to the Controller General of Accounts in respect of grants released to statutory bodies, non-government
organizations etc. to ensure that grants had been properly utilized for the purpose for which they were
sanctioned. In the case of Ministry of Women and Child Development, 5934 Utilization certificates (UCs)
involving an amount of Rs. 316.04 crore in respect of grants released up to March 2004, which were due by
31st March 2005 are stated to be outstanding at the time of audit.
Audit has observed that the Ministries/Departments before releasing grants to statutory bodies and non-
government organizations did not satisfy themselves about utilization of grants in 85% cases involving 59% of
the total grant released.”
 
2.9       The Committee wonders as to why inspite of guidelines being issued by the Ministry of Finance from time to time and
also Audit observations, pendency of  UCs has been allowed to continue since long.  Pendency for more than two decades
clearly indicates that fiscal management has not been given its due importance so far.  The Committee would once again
emphasize that this issue  should be taken up with the highest authorities at the State level so that old cases are cleared and
also for identification of  the inherent drawbacks/hindrances--systematic, procedural and others.  The Committee may be
furnished details of implementing agencies (state-wise, schemewise) who are yet to submit UCs and the steps taken to
minimise the pending UCs.
 
2.10            Committee has also been observing the availability of unspent balances with the implementing agencies under
different schemes. As a test case, the Committee would like to point out that under the two Centrally Sponsored Schemes,
ICDS and Swayamsiddha, there were unspent balances amounting to Rs. 61.00 crore and Rs. 13.70 crore available
respectively as on 01.04.06. By 31
st December, 2006, these figures reached to Rs. 156.91 crore and Rs. 15.34 crores under the
two schemes respectively.   State-wise analysis about availability of unspent balances raises doubt about adherence of the
financial norm of releasing of funds only after adjustment of unspent balance from the previous year.
 
2.11     The Committee has been given to understand that to deal with the unspent balance as well as the committed
expenditure, while releasing funds to the grantee institutions (State Governments/UTs/ NGOs), relevant provisions/guidelines
contained in the GFRs and DFPRs as also the instructions dated 8
th August, 2006   issued by the Department of Expenditure, -
which inter-alia stipulate that the unspent balances available with the States and implementing agencies must be taken into
account before further releases are made, are always taken into account.
 
2.12     The Committee may be apprised about the details of those agencies and organizations who are in receipt of funds from

III.
             INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES SCHEME (ICDS)
 
3.1
              The ICDS scheme/launched in 1975 aims at holistic development of children below six years and pregnant and
lactating mothers from disadvantaged sections by providing a package of integrated services, comprising of supplementary
nutrition, immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school, non-formal education and nutrition and health
education.
 
3.2       Plan allocation under ICDS constituted about 80% of the budget of the Ministry in 2006-07.  Plan allocation of Rs.
4454.52 crore was made in BE 2006-07, reduced to Rs. 4331.48 crore at RE stage.  Against this, expenditure of Rs.3874.86
crore  has been incurred upto 16
th March, 2007. Funds amounting to Rs.123.04 crores from ICDS were reappropriated to the
World Bank assisted ICDS projects.   Against an approved outlay of Rs. 10391.75 crore for ICDS during the Tenth Plan, 
expenditure reported is Rs. 11,883.05 crore.  Trend of allocation indicates that major provision has been made during the last
two years of the Plan Period.  Enhanced Plan allocation of Rs. 5204.00 crores has been made during 2007-08, the first year of
the Eleventh Plan. While appreciating the trend of optimum utilization of allocated funds during the Tenth Plan, the
Committee observes that quantum of unspent balances has also been showing an upward trend.  Unspent balances of Rs. 61.00
crores were available with different States as on 1 st April, 2006 which have increased upto Rs. 156.91 crores by the end of 
December, 2006.
 
3.3       State-wise information made available by the Ministry indicates that following States have the maximum amount of
unspent balances available with them:-

3.4       Besides that, States like Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat and West Bengal have exceeded the allocated funds, thereby
indicating that funds from the previous year were also available with them.   Both the situations indicate that some inherent
problem-areas continue to persist under the Scheme.   Either the Central funds are not reaching the implementing agencies in
time or States simply do not have the capacity to utilize the enhanced allocation.   The Committee understands that there
already exists a comprehensive four-level monitoring mechanism for ICDS.  The Committee can only emphasise that there is a
need for strengthening the monitoring mechanism so as to regulate the flow of funds and judicious utilization thereof.
 
3.5       The Committee notes that there has been continuous progress in the sanctioning of both ICDS Projects and Anganwadi
Centres.  The number of ICDS Projects  sanctioned upto 31

AWCs sanctioned upto 31 st March, 2005. Against the total 10,52,786 sanctioned AWCs, 7,81,208 AWCs had become
operational as on 30th  September, 2006.

3.7       On a specific query about the status of AWC buildings in N-E Region, the Committee was informed that while during
2001-02 and 2002-03, the N-E States could achieve the targets set up for construction of AWC buildings, position has
deteriorated from 2004-05 onwards.  Out of 7600 AWC buildings sanctioned for construction during 2004-05, only 3177
could be constructed and out of 16370 and 11502 buildings sanctioned during 2005-06 and 2006-07, not even one building
could be constructed.
 
3.8       The Committee notes that inspite of no provision for construction of AWC buildings under the Scheme, in view of
special needs of N-E States, an amount of Rs. 1.25 lakh per AWC has been sanctioned for 20,000 AWCs.  However, inspite of
funds being duly released, N-E States have failed to benefit from this special dispensation.
 
3.9            Committee’s attention has been drawn by an evaluation Study entitled ‘Three Decades of ICDS: an Appraisal’
conducted by the National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD) (Report published in December,
2006)-As per this Study, a definite improvement was noticed in the building structure of AWCs over the past 14 years.  It is
not known as to any observation about the specific constraints being faced by N-E States in this vital component of ICDS has
been commented upon in the aforesaid study.  If done, the Committee hopes that remedial steps would be initiated   at the
earliest so as to improve the position in N-E Region. The Committee also notes that the following gap areas have been
identified in the implementation of ICDS in the NIPCCD Study -
-
         60 per cent Anganwadi Centres having no toilet facility and in about 17 per cent Anganwadi Centres, facility

-         Lack of space for conducting outdoor and indoor activities

-     37 per cent of Anganwadi Centres reported non-availability of materials/ aids for nutrition and health

3.10     The Committee strongly recommends that immediate corrective measures need to be initiated on the gap areas
highlighted in the NIPCCD Study.  If this is the state of affairs after more than thirty years of ICDS being launched and crores
of funds being provided under the Flagship Scheme of the Ministry, then it would be better if the so-called multi-level
monitoring mechanism is simply done away with.

3.13     The Committee is aware that the ICDS was envisioned as a community participative programme and the components
of the scheme do not provide for the creation of infrastructural facilities.   However, in view of the present dismal status of
AWC buildings and also chronic problem of fund constraints being faced by majority of States, it is now time that providing
infrastructural facilities like construction of pucca building and other related aspects form part of the scheme so that over a
period of time all the AWCs must have pucca building to function.   If the Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan can provide 30% of its
allocation for infrastructure development, the Committee does not find any reason why a particular percentage of the fund
could not be allocated for infrastructure creation under the ICDS. 
 
3.14     The Committee notes that Ministry is doing all out efforts to universalize the ICDS Scheme.  The Secretary submitted
before the Committee that there seems to be a lack of motivation at the grass-root level and there is a case for a much stronger
community participation.              The Committee feels that the Ministry should come forward to revamp, redesign and recast
the ICDS scheme in all possible ways.
 
3.15     It was submitted before the Committee that   delays  happen  in the allocation of funds due to the involvement of RBI,
State Governments and implementing agencies.  When the money is the released by the Central Government first it goes to the
State Government’s account in the RBI.  The State Government in turn takes its own time to send it down, and depending
upon its ways and means position they keep the money for a month or two or three, and then, further pass it down.
 
3.16     The Committee recommends that since the flow of money from the Centre to the Implementing agencies is taking lot
of time, efforts may be made for quicker and expeditious dispersal of money.  If need be, the ICDS scheme may be converted
into a Mission Mode Scheme so that intermediate agencies can be removed.
 
3.17            Supreme Court, vide its order dated 7.10.2004, had inter-alia, directed all the States/UTs that the contractors shall
not be used for supply of nutrition in Anganwadi Centres and funds shall be spent by making use of SHGs and Mahila
Mandals for buying of grains and preparation of meals.  The Committee was informed that 21 States/UTs have so far
furnished information on system of procurement, storage, distribution of supplementary nutrition, involvement of Corporates/
SHGs and duties/responsibilities thereof under ICDS Programme in their States/UTs.  The Committee may like to know
whether any assessment of the impact of the changeover is being studied. The Committee also recommends that the remaining
states should be directed to furnish their feedback at the earliest.
 
3.18     From 2005-2006, the Government of India were to support states upto 50% of the financial norms or 50 % of the
expenditure incurred by thereon for supplementary nutrition whichever is less. Financial norms for supplementary nutrition
fixed in 1991 have been revised in October, 2004, for children from 6 months to 6 years, Rs. 2.00 per day per child and for
severely malnourished children, Rs. 2.70 per child per day.  On  a specific query about the number of children covered under
ICDS and getting nutrition, the Committee was informed that as per Census 2001, total number of children in the age group 0-
6 years is 15.78 crores. Of these, 10.49 crore children have been reported as child population as per Anganwadi Survey
Register under ICDS as on 30.09.06.  The Committee is, however, surprised to note that only 5.43 crore children out of 10.49
crore children under ICDS were getting supplementary nutrition as on 30.09.06.  Such a huge gap between targeted children
and those covered under ICDS and between children under ICDS and getting supplementary nutrition depicts a very dismal
scenario.  If the State-wise figures are analysed, shortfall and gaps are noticed across the country. The Committee is
extremely  disturbed to note that there are States where need is the maximum, but out of less than half of targeted children
covered under ICDS, only 50 per cent are getting the benefit of supplementary nutrition.

V.        RAJIV GANDHI NATIONAL CRECHE SCHEME FOR THE CHILDREN OF WORKING MOTHERS
 
5.1       The scheme aims to provide day care services for children in the age group of 0-6 years, belonging to economically
weaker sections of society, whose family income does not exceed Rs. 12,000 per month.  The crèches running under the
scheme provide health care, supplementary nutrition, medical check-up and immunization, etc. to the children whose parents
are away at work sites or are incapacitated due to sickness and are unable to look after them. 
 
5.2       The Committee notes that Plan funds being released under the Scheme are being optimally utilized by the
implementing agencies. Allocation   of Rs. 90.00 crores for 2007-08 is at an enhanced level due to increase in the financial
norms from Rs. 18,480/- to Rs. 42,384/- per crèche per annum.  There has been increase in the honorarium for crèche workers
and  component of supplementary nutrition also.  Under the scheme, 90 per cent of the total expenditure is released as grant to
the implementing organization, except the honorarium of crèche workers.
 
5.3       The scheme is implemented through the Central Social Welfare Board and two other national level organizations, i.e.,
Indian Council for Child Welfare and Bhartiya Adim Jati Sevak Sangh. Out of total 30,902 creches running in the country,
24,101 creches are being maintained by the Central Social Welfare Board, 3279 creches by ICCW and 5137 by BAJSS.
 
5.4       The Committee was informed that all these three organizations have experience of implementing crèche programme
across the country for almost three decades and have a nation-wide presence and network for running crèches.   These
organizations are well equipped with field units and staff.  Hence no constraints are being faced for implementation of the
scheme through these organizations. The Committee recommends that to meet the growth in number of creches other
organization/NGOs may also be involved in the implementation of these projects. 
 
5.5       While not disputing the considerable experience of the three implementing agencies handling the Crèche Scheme, the
Committee would like to draw attention to two problem areas.  First is the uneven spread of Creches across the country. Out of
total 30,902 creches in the country, maximum concentration is in Andhra Pradesh (3902), M.P.(2740), Maharashtra (2292),
Tamil Nadu (1928), U.P.(1868) and West Bengal (2203). The Committee strongly feels that there is a need for conducting a
survey across the country so as to bring the uncovered cities under the scheme.   This task can be easily handled by the State
monitoring agencies like State Women Development Corporations, NIPCCD etc.
 
5.6       The other area of concern identified by the Committee is the pendency of settlement of accounts. The Committee notes
that Andhra Pradesh State Board having the maximum number of crèches (3902) has settled accounts upto 1995-96 only. For
States like Assam, Manipur, Rajasthan, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram and A&N Islands, accounts have been settled upto 2003-
04 only.  Very few States have settled accounts upto 2005-06.  Evidently, funds are released without settlement of previous
accounts thereby violating the financial rules.  Details about crèches being run by the two other implementing agencies are not
known to the Committee.   The Committee apprehends a similar status with regard to 8659 creches   being run by them.  The
Committee would like to point out that with the enhanced financial norms and levying of user charges, financial management
should be as per the prescribed norms. The Committee wonders about the factors behind non-settlement of accounts by an
experienced body like CSWB having a country-wide network of State Boards for running the crèches.
 
5.7       The Committee notes that the monitoring of crèches which is a continuing process has been assigned to States/Women
study centres of the Universities and State Women Development Corporations during the current financial year.  So far,
feedback has been received from monitoring             agencies in respect of 276 creches being run in the States of Delhi,
Haryana, Jammu & Kashmir, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Nagaland, Orissa, Tripura and Uttar Pradesh. The Committee may
be apprised about the feed back on the implementation of the scheme.
 
5.8       The Committee notes that there are a large number of working mothers in unorganized sector who are in great need of
creches to look after their children when they go to work.  The safety of the children is utterly neglected and they are prone to

implementing agencies.

agencies under the scheme.   Adoption agencies monitor the case of domestic adoption through regular follow up for at least a
period of 1 year or as determined by the Court as per Guidelines for In-country Adoption, 2004.

X.            CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AGENCY (CARA)
 
10.1     Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA) was set up in 1990 and has been registered as an autonomous body under
the Registration of Societies Act, 1860. 
 
10.2     It is assigned the role of regulating inter-country adoptions from India and promoting domestic adoption.   For this
purpose, CARA at present has recognized 65  Indian Placement Agencies(RIPAs) to carry out in-country and inter-country
adoptions of children, who are either relinquished by biological parents/unwed mother/guardians or are abandoned/destitute

finding Indian parents for their children.  There are no regional offices of CARA at present.  CARA regulates inter-country
adoption through “Guidelines for Adoption from India-2006”.  CARA issued 867 NOCs in the year 2005 and 853 NOCs in the
year 2006 for inter-country adoption.

18.3            Committee’s attention has been drawn by a Study entitled ‘Poverty, Gender and Micro-Credit: A study of the

            The following observations made in the above study are worth noting –
“RMK seeks to extend micro credit, with this over all objective, to women in poverty households.  However,
despite this mandate to focus on poor as defined/ accepted in public policy on cut off household income level,
only a small minority of the assisted borrowers is in BPL income level and only a small percentage (3.9%) is

upper poor.  This exclusion of the poorest, the hard-core poor, results in most of the intended target group
remaining outside the micro credit net.”
          
“The implementation of RMK credit delivery is so NGO specific in respect of the identification and location
details of the borrowers that unless the NGO and its staff are available and they cooperate and provide the
details of the borrowers including their location, it is almost impossible to contact the borrowers and
interview them. There is no visibility of RMK programme as such and the borrowers are quite often not
aware that the funds they have received are RMK funds.”
 
18.4     The Committee may be apprised of the follow-up action initiated on the recommendations of the above study. The
Committee also notes that another study is to be conducted to assess the impact of credit scheme on women beneficiaries
during the last five years. The Committee hopes that this study would be completed at the earliest so that benefits under RMK
reach maximum beneficiaries.
18.5     The Committee in its earlier Report had pointed out that RMK charges interest @ 8% on reducing balance from the
borrowing agency which in turn charges any rate on or above 8% on reducing balance from the ultimate beneficiaries subject

charging 24% as interest rate from the beneficiaries. The Committee recommends that this issue need to be looked into and
check should be prevented from doing so and a cap should be put on the interest charged, under this scheme.
 
18.6     The Committee was informed that on the directions of the Governing Board of RMK, a 10 member Sub-Committee
was formed on 21.03.06 which besides other issues, also examined the issue of lowering of interest-rate of RMK.
Recommendation of the Sub-Committee was that the maximum upper limit currently at 18% on reducing balance to 15% on
reducing balance be brought down for RMK loans.  However, the Committee notes that this recommendation of the Sub-
Committee  was not agreed to by the Governing Board. Board was of the view that the position may be reviewed after two
years when RMK may have expanded business.
 
18.7     The Committee is not inclined to agree with the view taken by the Governing Board of RMK.  The Committee
reiterates its earlier recommendation.  The Committee would like to be  apprised about different rates of interest charged on an

XXI.            GENDER BUDGETING
 
21.1     Gender Budgeting is understood as a dissection of the Government budget to establish its gender-differential impacts
and to translate gender commitments into budgetary commitments.Gender Budgeting lends itself to strengthening
administrative processes and actions to achieve the targets for improvement in the position of women.  It not only entails a
look at allocation of resources for women but goes beyond to cover tracking the utilization of allocated resources, impact
analysis and beneficiary incidence analysis of public expenditure and policy from a gender perspective.
 
21.2     The Committee was informed that the Sub-Group on Gender Budgeting was set-up as part of the Working Group on
Empowerment of Women for 11
th Five Year Plan.
Main recommendations of the Working group are:
-         Important National  macro-economic policies to be engendered
-                Review of and effective implementation of all legislations and
-                laws with a gender perspective to eliminate all forms of discrimination
-                and violence against women.
-                Gender mainstreaming to be extended to corporate sector and other
-                civil society organizations
-                Estimation of value addition by women in the unorganized, informal
-                and rural non farm sector and systemized mechanism in place to
               monetize the labour, efforts and output and include the same in the GDP
-                Gender Outcome Assessment
-                Strengthening of Gender Budget cells in all Ministries/ Departments.
 
21.3     So far  50 Ministries/Departments have set up Gender Budgeting (GB) Cells. Detailed training manuals were needed to
be prepared which can be used by the Trainers. These manuals will give the approach and the methodology to be taken in the
engendering policies, schemes, programmes and assessing their gender outcomes. The Committee was informed that a Task
Force for developing a Gender Budgeting Manual is being constituted by the Ministry.
 
21.4     To ensure Gender Budgeting in true sense, in 2006-07 the Ministry adopted a multi pronged strategy to effectively
wield gender budgeting as a tool for women’s empowerment. Initiatives ranged from advocacy, training and capacity building
to strengthening partnerships, supporting policy dialogues and strategy sessions. However, the Committee notes that more pro-
active action is required to be taken to effectively handle the concept of Gender Budgeting. Preparation of training manual
which is indispensable tool of the concept is under formulation by sub-committee. It seems it will take another 2-3 years to
finalise the things. The Committee recommends that immediate step should be taken to finalise the training manual in a time-
bound manner. The Ministry should evolve a monitoring mechanism to ensure that all the Government
Ministries/Departments/Undertakings are taking necessary action for resorting to Gender Budgeting.
 
21.5     It has come to the notice of the Committee that many expenditures being incurred by different Ministries/Departments,
which are not directly women specific were being shown in the Gender budgeting.  This, Committee notes, results in inflated
figures that does not reflect the actual allocation for women.  The Committee recommends that this practice should be stopped
immediately and the Gender budgeting should reflect only those expenditure/allocation that are cent percent women specific.

XI.
              NEW SCHEMES
 
Comments of the Committee
 
            Committee’s experience about new schemes proposed to be launched by the Ministry has not been very encouraging.
Committee has been observing that new schemes are proposed without any pre-liminary exercise about formulation of their
components.  A token provision is duly made which continues for years together due to a number of procedural formalities to
be completed at different levels. New schemes should be announced only after the text of the scheme is ready. 
Comprehensive Scheme for Combating Trafficking of Women and Children and Scheme for Relief and Rehabilitation of Rape
Victims are two such schemes. Comprehensive Scheme for Combating Trafficking of Women and Children was introduced
during the Tenth Plan.  As per the information available with the Committee, there was an allocation of Rs. 3.00 crore for the
Scheme in 2004-05, reduced to Rs. 30 lakhs in 2005-06 and Rs. 50 lakhs provided in 2006-07. Provision for 2007-08 is Rs.
1.00 crore. The only progress which seems to have been made is the change in its name. The very fact that it is proposed to be
launched as a new scheme inspite of being in the formulation stage during the Tenth Plan indicates that there is not going to be
any progress in the near future.  Scheme for Relief and Rehabilitation   of Rape Victims is the other scheme which was
introduced in 2006-07 with a token provision of Rs. 1.00 crore. Same provision is there for 2007-08 and it continues to be at
the proposal stage.                                                                                                                           (Para 11.2)
Comments of the Committee
            Rajiv Gandhi Scheme for Empowerment of Adolescent Girls is another new scheme proposed to be launched after
merger of two schemes, Kishori Shakti Yojana and Nutrition Programme for Adolescent Girls. The Committee observes that
NPAG was implemented on a pilot basis in 2002-03 and 2003-04 by the Planning Commission It continued on a pilot project
basis in 51 identified backward districts in 2006-07 also. Kishori Shakti Yojana has been there under ICDS for a few years. It
was only in 2006-07 that the scheme has been expanded from 2000 ICDS projects to 6118 ICDS projects. Committee is not
aware whether there is going to be a significant modification by bringing these two schemes under a new scheme.   The
Committee would, however, like to emphasise that there is a need for having a comprehensive review of all the Schemes
relating to nutrition for women and children being run not only by the Ministry of Women and Child Development but by
other Ministries like Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Rural Development etc.   The Committee strongly feels that there
should be an umbrella scheme for provision of nutrition to women and children under which different small schemes run by
other Ministries can be brought together.                                                                                               (Para 11.3)

XX.
           PREVENTION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT AT WORKPLACE
 
Comments of the Committee
 
            The Committee further notes that the judgment of the Supreme Court in the Vishaka case is applicable to all
workplaces/employer’s organizations throughout the country, whether in the public or private sector. Details of the complaints
committees set up in the country in terms of the said judgement are not centrally maintained by the Ministry as it has not been
found feasible to do so. However, in the Action Taken Note the Committee was informed that the NCW has issued reminders
to all the organisations and Universities which have not set up complaint committees or which have not furnished any
information in this regard. The Committee would like to be apprised about the information received from various States.  The
Committee recommends that the directions contained in the Supreme Court Judgement in Vishaka case may be implemented
in all the Central Government Departments, and its autonomous and subordinate offices and organizations immediately.   The
Committee may be apprised about the organizations/Departments of the Central Government which have not taken steps to
implement the direction of the Supreme Court.                                                                          (Para 20.2)
XXI.
         GENDER BUDGETING
 
Comments of the Committee
 
            However, the Committee notes that more pro-active action is required to be taken to effectively handle the concept of
Gender Budgeting. Preparation of training manual which is indispensable tool of the concept is under formulation by sub-
committee. It seems it will take another 2-3 years to finalise the things. The Committee recommends that immediate step
should be taken to finalise the training manual in a time-bound manner. The Ministry should evolve a monitoring mechanism
to ensure that all the Government Ministries/Departments/Undertakings are taking necessary action for resorting to Gender
Budgeting.                                                        (Para 21.4)
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee recommends that this practice should be stopped immediately and the Gender budgeting should reflect
only those expenditure/allocation that are cent percent women specific.                                                                                (Para
21.5)
