REPORT 
I INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development came into existence as a separate Ministry 
w.e.f 30 th January, 2006.  It has the nodal responsibility to advance the rights and concerns of 
women and children who constitute 67.7 per cent of the country’s population as per 2011 census.  
The Ministry was constituted with the prime intention of addressing gaps in state action for women 
and children and for promoting inter-ministerial and inter-sectoral convergence to crea te gender 
equality and child-centred legislation, policies and programmes.  The function of the Ministr y is to 
promote the empowerment and protection of women and children and to ensure their equitable and 
wholesome development.  
1.2 The role of the Ministry includes policy formulation, programme implementation, 
coordination with other sectors, child and gender budgeting, data management, training and 
capacity building, reporting and monitoring on the status of women and children and their rights,   
The Ministry is the prime mover of inclusive programmes, policies, schemes and legislations for 
women and children and stands solely for securing the best interest of women and children.  
Promoting social and economic empowerment of women and ensuring survival, development, care 
and protection of children drive the Ministry’s agenda.  Principles of equity, justice and non -
discrimination guide the Ministry’s actions concerning women and children.  
1.3 The Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development, in her presentation before the 
Committee on 17 th February, 2017 gave an overview of the various programmes/schemes of the 
Ministry and achievements thereof.  She informed the Committee that the budget allocation for the 
year 2017-18 has been kept at Rs.22,094.67 crore, whereas the projected demand of the Ministry 
was for Rs.25,425.38 crore.  She pointed towards insufficiency of funds for the implementation of 
Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme on account of recommendations of the 
Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) which has recommended discontinuation of the salary 
component (Rs.7,950 crore) of the regular ICDS field staff and has also disallowed construction of 
Aaganwadi Centres (AWCs) buildings (Rs. 4,662 crore) by the Ministry from ICDS funds causing 
a short fall of Rs.12612 crore under ICDS scheme.  According to the Secretary,  this would 
adversely affect the implementation of the scheme as the States/UTs may not be willing to take 
additional financial burden and that there would be infrastructural gaps in the construction  of 
Anganwadi Centre buildings and related facilities. The Secretary requested for 0.5 per cent 

budgetary support from Swachch Bharat Kosh for construction of toilet’s in AWCs without toilet 
facilities and for ensuring fund flow for infrastructure from convergent Ministries. 
1.4 Other important issues highlighted by the Secretary were the prevalence of underweight 
children in the 0-5 years age group, problem of anaemia, especially in the pregnant women and 
lactating mothers in the age group of 15-49 years and the declining child sex ratio (CSR) in the 
certain parts of the country.  According to the Secretary, serious attempts were required to be made 
to deal with these important issues.  The Committee was also informed of the initiatives relating to 
child protection services such as emergency outreach service-child line, railway child line, web 
portal for missing and found children, strengthening implementation of Beti Bachao Beti Padhao 
(BBBP) in the low CSR districts, expansion in operationalization of One Stop Centres (OSCs) and 
women helpline schemes and important proposals received under Nirbhaya Fund.

III INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES  (ICDS) SCHEME 
3.1 The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme is one of the flagship 
programmes of the Government of India and represents one of the world’s largest and unique 
programmes for early childhood care and development.  The beneficiaries under the scheme are 
children in the age group of 0-6 years, pregnant women and lactating mothers.  The ICDS Sc heme 
offers a package of six services, viz, : 
(i) supplementary nutrition; 
(ii) pre-school non-formal education; 
(iii) nutrition and health education; 
(iv) immunization; 
(v) health check-up; and 
(vi) referral services. 
  
Immunization, health check-up and referral services are related to health and are provided 
by the Department of Health and Family Welfare, Ministry of Health and Family W elfare through 
National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) and health system.  Convergence is one of the key 
features of the ICDS Scheme for better governance in the delivery of the scheme.  This 
convergence is inbuilt in the scheme which provides a platform in the form of Anganwadi Centres 
for providing  all services under the scheme.  The ICDS scheme has been gradually universali zed 
and currently 7076 projects and 14 lakh Anganwadi Centres (AWCs) have been approved.  This 
also includes a provision of 20,000 AWCs ‘on demand’.  As on 30. 9.2016, out of 14 lakh 
approved AWCs about 13.49 lakh AWCs are operational. 

3.2 The Committee was given to understand that in order to address various programmatic, 
management and institutional gaps and to meet administrative and operational challenges, th e 
Government had approved the strengthening and restructuring of ICDS Scheme with an allocation 
of Rs.1,23,580 crore during the 12 th Five Year Plan.  The budget allocation and expenditure under 
the ICDS Scheme during the 12 th Five Year Plan and the budget allocation of 2017-18 is as shown

3.4 The Ministry listed the   following problem areas in the implementation of Integrated 
Development Child Services (ICDS) Scheme due to fund shortage:- 
- The Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) while considering continuation of ICDS 
scheme during the Fourteen Finance Commission (FFC) period i.e. 2017-18 to 2019-20 has 
recommended  payment of entire salary of regular field staff by the States/UTs and the 
construction of AWC buildings from sources other than ICDS.  No fund has bee n 
earmarked for this purpose.  These are ongoing components and their discontinuation 
would adversely affect  the scheme. 

- Construction of 4 lakh AWCs is to be done through convergence with MGNREGA with 
their contribution of Rs.5 lakh per unit and Rs. 2 lakh contributed  by MWCD.  
Discontinuation   of this activity will stop the units  already under construction  and further 
sanction of units will be hampered.  It is needless to say that provision of pucca building is 
pre-requisite  for delivery of child related services. 
- Ministry of WCD has requested Ministry of Finance  to allocate  additional funds of Rs. 
12612 crore for (FFC) period 2017-18 to 2019-20 for paying salary to the regular  field 
level staff and for construction of AWC buildings. 
- There  are 2.86 lakh AWC government  owned buildings without toilet facilities and 1.25 
lakh government owned buildings  without drinking water facility.  Provision of funds for 
providing these services is very essential. 
- Ministry has requested the Government to provide a supplementary grant of Rs. 896.40 
crore for the current financial year 2016-17 as the allocated amount is not sufficient to meet 
the cost. 
3.5 The Committee while taking note of the above problems observes  that ICDS scheme 
is one of the biggest and most unique scheme of the Ministry aimed at improving the 
nutritional and health status of children in the age group of 0-6 years and laying the 
foundation of their proper psychological, physical and social development.  It is of utmost  
importance that adequate funds  should be provided under the scheme to cov er all the 
targeted beneficiaries who are children in 0-6 years of age, pregnant women an d lactating 
mothers.    The  Committee notes that  the Ministry  has got around 15 per ce nt less 
allocation in BE 2017-18 than their projected demand . It is Committee’s concerted view that 
shortage of fund should not hamper the proper implementation of the scheme  and  the 
Committee desires the Ministry to ensure adequate funds for proper implemen tation of the 
ICDS Scheme.

Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP) 
3.11 Supplementary Nutrition is one of the six services being delivered at AWCs under the 
ICDS scheme. Provision of supplementary nutrition is primarily made to bridge the gap between 
Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) and the Average Daily Intake (ADI) of children and 
pregnant and lactating women. Under the revised nutritional and feeding norms which have been 
made effective from February 2009, State Governments/UTs have been directed to provide 300 
days of supplementary food to the beneficiaries in a year which would entail giving more than one 

meal to the children from 3-6 years who come to AWCs. This includes morning snacks in the form 
of milk/banana/egg/seasonal fruits/micro-nutrient fortified food followed by a Hot Cooked Meal. 
For children below 3 years of age and pregnant and lactating mothers, age appropriate Take Home 
Rations (THRs) in the form of pre-mix/ready-to-eat food are provided. Besides for severely 
underweight children in the age of 6 months to 6 years, additional food items in the form of 
micronutrient fortified food and/or energy dense food as THR have been recommended.

IV INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS) 
4.1 The Integrated Child Protection Scheme was revised in the year 2014-15 and has come into 
effect from 1st April, 2014. Some of the key features of the revised scheme are increased 
maintenance grant for children in homes, from existing Rs.750/- to Rs.2000/- per child per month, 
enhanced cost of construction from existing provision from R600 per sq. Feet to Rs.1000/- per sq. 
Feet, flexibility in the staffing pattern and in service delivery structures, depending on the size and 
need of State/UT, to provide child protection services in a more cost effective manner; and 
flexibility to States to source additional funds from corporate/private donors for any component of 
the Scheme.  
 
4.2  The scheme provides financial support to State Governments/UT Administrations for 
running services for children either themselves or through suitable NGOs. These services include 
(a) Homes of various types for children; (b) Emergency Outreach services through Childline; (c) 
15 Open Shelters for children in need of care and protection in Urban and Semi Urban Areas; (d)  
Family Based Non-Institutional Care through Sponsorship and Foster Care and Adoptions. 
 
4.3 Financial Assistance under the Scheme is also provided for setting up of statutory bodies 
under the Juvenile Justice Act namely; Child Welfare Committees (CWCs) and Juvenile Justice 
Boards (JJBs). As reported by the State Governments/UT Administrations, so far 675 CWCs and 
669 JJBs have been set up across the country.  The budget allocation under the ICPS for the 12 th 
Plan period was Rs. 2350.00 crore.  Against the allocated BE of Rs.400.00 crore for  2016-17, 
amount of Rs.335.58 crore has been released upto 31.12.2016.

efforts be made  by all the stakeholders i.e. Central Government, State/UTs Goverments, 
police and other law enforcement agencies in coordination with each other to tack le the issue 
on the priority basis.  The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation for e stablishing 
separate cells in all the police stations for handling the missing children cases.  F urther, as 
the issue of missing children requires sensitive handling, efforts to locate them ne ed to be 
synergized to build an effective and functional system where all the stakeholders work  in a 
concerted, coordinated and integrated  manner. The Committee feels stron gly about 
utilisation of advanced technologies, skills and training for the same. 
 
4.9 On a specific query regarding efforts made by the Ministry to strengthen the mechanism 
for monitoring of quality services at the Juvenile Homes so as to prevent abuse or exploitation of 
children, the Ministry submitted that Section 41 of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of 
Children) Act, 2015 has made registration of Homes mandatory within six months of the Act 
coming into force. There are stringent penalties in case of non-compliance.  Under Section 8, the 
Juvenile Justice Board is required to conduct at least one inspection visit every month of 
residential facilities for children in conflict with law and recommend action for improvement in 
quality of services to the District Child Protection Unit and the State Government.  Further, under 
Section 54, State Government is required to appoint inspection committees for the State and 
district.  The inspection Committees are required to mandatorily conduct visits to all facilities 

housing children, at least once in three months.  The Act also includes detailed rehabilitation and 
re-integration services that are to be provided to institutions registered under the Act such as 
education, vocational training, skill development, mental health interventions including 
counselling, recreational activities, etc. 
 
4.10 The Committee, while noting the reply of the Ministry would like to reiterate that the 
well meaning laws alone would not make any change at ground level.  There is n o denying 
the fact that the living conditions at most juvenile homes in the country are so worse that 
they could be termed as violation of both human and child rights.   There are persistent 
problems of inadequate space, bathrooms, toilets, cleanliness, lack of recreational activities, 
life skills and vocational training and staff shortage.   
 
4.11 The Committee was  given to understand that the staff attrition rate was very high due to 
low salaries under ICPS scheme.  The Ministry has since enhanced the staff salary norms.  Prior to 
the revision, the lowest salary was of Ayahs, cooks etc at Rs. 2000/- per month, it has been 
enhanced to Rs.6000/- per month.  Similarly, the highest salary of the programme managers has 
been enhanced from Rs. 20,000/- per month to Rs. 35,000/- .  The Committee while noting the 
above reply recommends that the Ministry being the nodal authority for all child-wel fare 
measures has to be pro-active in sensitizing the states/UTs  to strive for improving the living 
conditions of the Juvenile homes as per the mandate of the Juvenile (Care and Protection of 
Children) Act,  2015. 
 
V. BETI BACHAO BETI PADHAO (BBBP) 
5.1 Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (BBBP) was launched by the Prime Minister on 22 nd January, 
2015 at Panipat, Haryana.  BBBP addresses the declining Child Sex Ratio (CSR) and related issues 
of women empowerment over a life-cycle continuum.  It is a tri-ministerial effort of Ministries of 
Women and Child Development, Health and Family Welfare and Human Resource Development.  
The key elements of the scheme include Enforcement of Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic 
Techniques Act (PCPNDT), nation-wide awareness and advocacy campaign and multi-sectoral 
action in select 100 districts (low on CSR) in the first phase.  There is a strong emphasis on 
mindset change through training, sensitization, awareness raising and community mobilization.

 The scheme is well received in the selected 100 districts and seeing the criticality of the 
CSR issue, it has been expanded  in additional  61 districts covering 11 States/UTs. 
 
5.2 The Ministry informed the Committee about the achievements of BBBP scheme as under: 
- since its inception, the scheme has resulted in hundreds  of local level innovative initiatives 
to promote the girl child; 
- the preliminary reports of 100 districts  indicate that for the time period between April-
March, 2014-15 & 2015-16, an increasing trend in Sex Ratio at Birth (SRB) is visibile in 
58 BBBP districts; 
- 69 districts have reported progress  in the first trimester registration, against the reported 
ANC registrations and status of institutional deliveries have improved in 80 districts; 
- the gir ls’ enrolment in secondary education has increased to 80.67  per cent  as per the 
Unified District Information System for Education in 2015-16 against 76 per cent in 2013-
14. 
- construction of girls’ toilets in every school in selected 100 low CSR district s (Phase-I) has 
been completed. 
 
5.3 The Committee notes that the Child Sex Ratio (CSR), an indicator of gender discrimination 
towards girl child stands at 918 girls per 1000 boys in the age group of 0-6 years, much lower than 
the figure of 927 in 2001 Census.  The unabated decline in CSR since 1961 is a matter of grav e 
concern as it reflects the low status of women in our society and indicates her disempowerment.  
There are many areas/pockets in the country where the child sex ratio has been below the nati onal 
average or declining and such areas/pockets  have been expanding due to various socio-economic 
factors.  As per the information available  to the Committee by  the Ministry, out of 640 districts 
across the country, 429 districts have declining CSR.  The Committee feels that the declining 
CSR is a major social problem requiring urgent remedial action before it is too late.   
Accordingly, the Committee recommends  that the Ministry should make concerted efforts to 
check this declining CSR in a mission mode manner and also to put more aggre ssiveness, 
energy and resources to reverse this trend.

XI NATIONAL MISSION FOR EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN (NMEW) 
11.1 The National Mission for Empowerment of Women (NMEW) Scheme was  approved with 
a mandate to strengthen inter-sectoral convergence  of programmes, policies and schemes  for 
women both at the centre and at the State/UT level.  The scheme is meant to strengthen efforts  
through inter-sectoral coordination amongst different ministries and programmes with the Ministry 
of Women and Child Development as the nodal Ministry.  It also endeavours  to facilitate  the 
reach of various services  and programmes of the Central and State Governments to the target 
group  of women.  Training and Capacity building to enhance  and strengthen understanding of 
gender issues  and bridge the gap between knowledge and practice  is the other focus are a of 
NMEW. 
11.2 National Mission for Empowerment of Women (NMEW) was  approved as a centrally 
sponsored  scheme during the terminal year of 11 th Five Year Plan with 100 per cent central 
funding.  Following restructuring of Centrally Sponsored schemes in July, 2013 the scheme was 

revised.  NMEW scheme is now being implemented on the basis of 60:40 centre-state sharing 
except North-Eastern and special category states where the cost sharing ratio is 90:10. 
11.3 On a specific query regarding the achievements of the NMEW scheme, the Ministry has 
informed that the scheme has been able to make progress  towards addressing holistic 
empowerment  of women in terms of bridging gender issues centre-stage  across  different 
States/UTs.  The gender experts engaged  as part of technical support to the Ministry of WCD in 
the fields of Poverty Alleviation, Social Empowerment, Health and Nutrition, Gender Budgeting, 
Gender Rights, Empowerment of Vulnerable and Marginalized Women and Women in Difficult  
Circumstances, Media and Communications etc. are supporting various women centric 
schemes/programs of the government while engaging consistently and continuously with a wide 
range of stakeholders.  The scheme has played an important role towards  enhancing the capacity 
of functionaries through  training and capacity building.  Several consultations at regional level 
have been held with State Governments and civil society organizations to take the discourse on 
gender issues to a wider audience as well as identify key issues that need immediate action.  In 
pursuance to the same, NMEW has been working towards engendering institutions and 
strengthening Gender Focal Points within institutions at various levels including Central 
Ministries, State Governments, Local Self Governments (LSGs) etc. for  enabling these institutions 
to identify and respond to gender issues. Developed participatory tools such as Nari ki Chaupal to 
create open fora to discuss gender issues and sharing   of experience and best practic es.  Nari ki 
Chaupal are being organized on regular basis. 
11.4 The State Resource Centre for Women (SRCW) established  under the respective State 
Government/UT Administrative to facilitate convergence of schemes and programmes meant for 
women has been strengthened by timely disbursement of funds and approval of the State Action 
Plans during Financial Year 2016-17.  SRCWs are functional with dedicated staff in 25 
States/UTs. 
11.5 In order to facilitate convergence of schemes/programs at the ground level and enable 
greater access for women to government schemes/programs and create awareness on gender issues 
Poorna Shakti Kendra (PSK) project was conceived in a pilot mode for selected districts.  The PSK 
project entails setting up of convergence centres at the district, block and Gram Panchayat (GP 
level).  Each centre/Kendra at GP level has two Mahila Gram Samanvyaks (Village Coordinators) 
who act as motivators for the women in the area and are also responsible for providing 

support/facilitation on various issues that affect women's empowerment.  The first pilot project to 
demonstrate convergence of programmes and schemes was launched in District Pali, Rajasthan on 
September, 16, 2011.  NMEW  has released/revalidated  funds for 20 PSK  projects during 2016-
17. 
11.6 The PSK projects have been instrumental in bringing about greater access and awareness 
related to women centric schemes/programs at the village level.  With the learning from this 
project, Village Convergence and Facilitation Service (VCFS) gas been extended to cover 200 
high burden districts in the  country.   VCFS was initially  approved for 100 BBBP districts during 
2015-16 and extended  to additional 61 BBBP districts during  2016-17.

XIII CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AUTHORITY (CARA) 
13.1 The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA), which was an Autonomous Body of 
the Ministry of Women and Child Development, has now become a statutory body as per the 
provisions of Section 68, Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.  It 
functions as a nodal body at National level on adoption related matters of Indian children.  The 
mandate of CARA is to promote in-country adoptions in coordination with state agencies, regulate 
inter-country adoptions, frame regulations on adoption and to carry out the functions of the of the 
Central Authority under the Hague  Convention on Protection for Children and Co-operation in 
respect of inter-country adoption.  CARA has framed the Adoption Regulations 2017 which have 
been operationalized w.e.f 16th January, 2017.  It has also been informed that adoption procedure 
has further been simplified as it is entirely facilitated through on-line system.  CARA has engaged  
Helpline staff (toll free) to assist all prospective adoptive parents.  One counselling centre at 
CARA is operational and there is a proposal  to extend it to the State and district level to ensure 
that Prospective Adoptive Parents (PAPs) have excess to counselling help. 
13.2 The Committee has been made to understand that the adoption regulations, 2017 have been 
framed keeping in mind the issues and challenges faced by CARA and other  stakeholders 
including the adoption agencies and the prospective adoptive parents (PAPs).  This will further 
strengthen adoption programme in the country by streamlining the adoption process.  

Transparency, early deinstitutionalization of children, informed choices for the parents, ethical 
practices and strictly defined  timelines in the adoption process are the salient aspects of the 
adoption regulations. 
13.3 On a specific query regarding promotion of inter-country adoptions and monitoring 
mechanism to check exploitation of children in inter-country adoptions, the Committee was 
informed  that the adoption regulations 2017 has provisions for placing the children with eligible 
and suitable foreign parents if they are not being placed with domestic parents within the stipulated 
time-frame.  The inter-country adoption procedure has adequate safeguards to protect the interest 
of adopted children and is being undertaken as per the Hague Convention on Inter country 
Adoptions to which India is a signatory.  Various recognized agencies and authorities are involved 
to ensure that such children should be de-institutionalised as early as possible and strict adoption 
protection is in place to check any possible exploitation of children.  The eligibility/suitability of 
the Prospective Adoptive Parents is determined on the basis of their Home Study Report (HSR) 
prepared by professional Social Worker in the country of their residence.   The same are further 
assessed by the concerned Authority Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA) and their  application is 
approved by the concerned Central Authority.  The applications are initially scrutinized  by the 
screening  Committee  and are examined by the NOC Committee in CARA at later stage.  The  
Court  concerned also looks  into the suitability of the inter-country Prospective Adoptive Parents, 
before issuing  Adoption Order in their favour.

XIV NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN (NCW) 
14.1 The National Commission for Women (NCW) was constituted in 1992 as a statutory body 
under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990 at the  national level to safeguard and 
promote the rights and interests of women.   It has  a wide mandate covering almost all aspect of 
women’s development.  The Commission investigates and examines the legal safeguards provided  
for women under constitution  and other laws  and recommends  to the government, measures for 
their effective implementation.  The Commission also reviews the existing provisions of the 
constitution  and other laws affecting women and recommends amendments to meet any lacunae 
or inadequacies in such laws.  It also looks into complaints and takes suo-motu cognizance  on 
matters  relating to deprivation of women’s  rights.  The Commission takes up studies/research on 
issues of relevance  to women, participates  and advises in the planning process for socio-
economic development of women, evaluates the program made thereof, inspects jails, remand and 
shelter homes etc. where women are kept under custody and seeks remedial action wherever  
necessary. The Commission also focuses on public awareness regarding laws and schemes relating 
to women. 
14.2 During the period from 1 st April to 31 st September, 2016, the Commission sanctioned 6 
studies, 26 seminars/consultations on identified subjects and two expert committees.

complaints/cases were registered at the Complaint and Investigation Cell of the Commission.

14.5 On a specific query regarding rising crime against women and steps taken by the Ministry 
to curtail this trend, the Ministry stated that a number of measures have been taken by the 
Government to address the issue of violence against women.  Special laws for women to tackle  
violence in the domestic  sphere, in the workplace and public are already in place.  In 2013 , 
comprehensive amendments in the Indian Penal Code, Code of Criminal Procedure and the Indian 
Evidence were made making the punishment more stringent for rape and sexual offences.  New 
offences such as acid attack, stalking, sexual harassment, voyeurism and disrobing have also been 
included.  The Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013 also mandates all the hospitals, public or 
private to provide free medical treatment to all victims of acid attack and rape.  Under the Juvenile 
Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 special provisions  have been made to tackle 
child offenders committing heinous offences such as gang rape, rape of minor, acid attack causing 
permanent or partial damage/deformity, etc., in the age group  of 16-18 years.  Also, the Juvenile 
Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 has special provisions to tackle child offenders 
committing heinous offences  such as gang rape, rape of minor, acid attack causing permanent or  
partial damage, deformity etc in the age group of 16-18 years. 
14.6 The Committee while taking note of the above details would like to state that inspite 
of all the interventions, the crime against women have ever been rising violating their right  
to life and other  freedoms guaranteed under the constitution as equal citizen.  It cannot be 
denied that women have been at the receiving end most of the time, be it gen der based 
violence and discrimination or sexual assault, rape honour killing and trafficking etc.  T he 
Committee is of the view that proper structure should be put in place not only to curtail 
crime against women but also to provide justice and help the victims on time.  For this NCW  
should come forward to play a  more pro-active role in preventing crime against women by  
undertaking vigorous  awareness generation programmes, co-ordinating with state agencies 
and sensitizing the police and other enforcement agencies dealing with crime against women.
