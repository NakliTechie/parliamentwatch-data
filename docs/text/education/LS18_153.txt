R E P O R T
 
I           Introduction
 
1.1       The words 'gender' and 'sex' are commonly referred to in respect of women and men.  While sex is a biological
concept, gender is mainly a social and cultural concept; it refers not only to women and men but also to the relations of
social, economic and political powers enjoyed by them in a society.  This distribution of power had never been even in
any society.  Gender equality, therefore, has been made an integral part of the developmental planning and efforts, by
not only the national governments but by multinational agencies as well.  Efforts made by the UN during the 1990s, led
to a global consensus in integrating a gender perspective in all policies and budgetary dimensions of different countries.
 
1.2       For achieving the goal of gender equality, Governments take affirmative actions in favour of women and girl
children with a view to enabling them not only to share the benefits of development proportionately but also contribute
their bit to national construction.  Any effort of gender empowerment cannot succeed, unless there is an effective
targeting of national resources to women's cause.  And this will be possible only if we know precisely how much of our
resources are being spent on women and girl children, both scheme-wise and Ministry-wise.   
 
1.3       Generally, budgeting involves four components: (i) the budgetary allocation of resources to various heads, (ii)
the actual Government outlays on various heads, (iii) an accounting of how resources are utilised for a particular
purpose and (iv) an evaluation of the effectiveness of the resources utilised in delivering the intended results.  Gender
Budgeting involves looking at all the four components from the point of view of women as beneficiaries.
 
1.4       Gender Budgeting thus refers to the process aimed not only at capturing/highlighting gender sensitivities of
budgetary allocations but also to help identify gaps in access to and distribution of resources on gender basis.

Budgeting allocations using traditional macro-economic practices with no regard to different needs and requirements
that men and women may have in a society transmit and breed gender biases in that society.  Gender budget analysis,
therefore, is critical in fostering a more open democratic and just society where government's accountability and
transparency in its working are more real than apparent.  Gender aware budgets, in fact, promote equity as well as
efficiency.
 
1.5       Gender Budgeting also involves carrying out an impact analysis of Government programmes and its budgetary
allocation on the overall socio-economic status of women in the country with a view to targeting of public spending and
offsetting any undesirable gender-specific consequences of previous budgetary measures.
 
1.6       The aim of gender budgeting is thus not to have a separate budget for women but to look at public expenditure
for assessing relative benefits/costs accruing to men and women.  Apart from the public expenditure and its impact on
gender balance, it also concerns about the adequacy, affectivity and implementation etc. of public policies from gender
perspective.
 
II         International Initiatives
 
2.1       Gender Budgeting is gaining increasing acceptance as a tool for engendering macro-economic policy-making. 
The Fourth World Conference of Women was held in Beijing  in September 1995 and the 'Platform for Action' that it
adopted called for a gender perspective in all macro-economic policies and their budgetary dimensions.  The Outcome
Document of the UN General Assembly Special Session on Women held in June 2002, also called upon all the nations
to mainstream a gender perspective into their key macro-economic and social development programmes.  Emphasis on
gender budgeting was also placed by the Sixth Conference of Commonwealth Ministries of Women's Affairs held in
New Delhi in April 2002.
 
2.2       Many countries, both in the developed and the developing world have undertaken the exercise of gender budget
analysis.  Australia , Canada, UK, South Africa, Sri Lanka  and some of the African nations such as Tanzania and
Mozambique - are practicing gender budgeting in different forms.  The practices vary from gender audit in Australia  to
cross-sectoral gender sensitive analysis of expenditure in South Africa and to gender conscious planning and gender-
segregated data collation in Sri Lanka and presentation of Gender Impact Statement alongwith the budget in the U.K. 
Gender budget initiatives are currently being attempted in 35 countries following diverse trajectories in terms of the
process and partners involved in undertaking the activity. 
 
III        Gender Budgeting in India
3.1       In India, women constitute about half of the population who have traditionally been at disadvantage in any area
of life.  Efforts for removing this disparity, no doubt, has been made from the beginning by the government as well as
by the civil society in one form or the other.  The Planning Commission of India has always focused on women's issues
as per the perceptions of their members on the status of women in the country.  The Central Social Welfare Board
(1953) was the first major step in this direction during the period of First Five Year Plan.  The Second Five-Year Plan
(1956-1960) adopted the development of mahila mandals to undertake work at the grassroots level i.e. upto village
level. The Third, Fourth and Interim Plans (1961-74) carved out a special provision for women's education, pre-natal
and child health care, supplementary feeding for children and nursing of expectant mothers. The Fifth Five-Year Plan

(1974-1978) marked a major shift in the approach towards women, from welfare to development. The Sixth Five-Year
Plan (1980-85) accepted women's development as a separate economic agenda. It took a multi-disciplinary approach
with a three-pronged thrust on health, education and employment. The Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-1990) declared as
its objective of bringing women into the mainstream of national development. Another landmark was the enactment of
73
rd & 74 th Amendment Bill providing reservation of 1/3 rd of seats for women in the units of local self-Government
across the country.
 
3.2       The Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-1997), in fact, marked a paradigm shift from development to empowerment of
women and promised to ensure the flow of benefits to women in the core sectors like education, health and employment.
The outlay for women was raised substantially to Rs 2,000 crore in this Plan. The Eight Plan highlighted for the first
time the need to ensure a definite flow of funds from the general developmental sectors to the area of women
development.  The Plan document made an explicit statement that "the benefits of development from different sectors
should not by pass women and special programmes on women should complement the general development
programmes.  The latter, in turn, should reflect greater gender sensitivity".  Although this approach could not make
much dent in ensuring adequate flow of funds and benefit to women, the Eighth Plan marked the beginning of a gender
based budgeting, which for the first time, stressed upon the need for a gender sensitive perspective in the planning
process. 
 
3.3       The Ninth Plan (1997-2002) adopted 'Women Component Plan' as one of the major strategies and directed both
the Central and State Governments to ensure "not less than 30 per cent of the funds/benefits are earmarked in all the
women related sectors.  Special vigil advocated on the flow of the earmarked funds/benefits through an effective
mechanism to ensure that the proposed strategy brings forth a holistic approach towards empowering women.  The
National Policy for Empowerment of Women 2001 envisaged introduction of a gender perspective in the budgeting
process as an operational strategy.
 
3.4       Tenth Plan reinforces commitment to gender budgeting to establish its gender-differential impact and to translate
gender commitments into budgetary commitments.  Therefore, the Tenth Plan will initiate immediate action in tying up
these two effective concepts of Women Component Plan (WCP) and Gender Budgeting to play a complementary role to
each other, and thus ensure both preventive and post-facto action in enabling women to receive their rightful share from
all the women-related general development sectors.
 
3.5       In the context of India, where the gender bias is inherent in a male-dominated society, Gender Budgeting
becomes all the more relevant.  The Committee observes that the trends in sex ratio and other socio-economic indicators
clearly show, how the bias is getting accentuated in many parts of the country.  Over the years, during the course of
examination of various women and girl child related programmes/schemes, one factor which has been constantly
drawing the attention of the Committee is that inspite of well-conceived programmes and the funds allocated, there has
been no noticeable improvement in the status of women/girls.  Recently released State specific sex ratio figures of
Census 2001, do not leave any doubt in this regard.  The Committee is of the view that it is high time that the Budget of
the Government provides a clear indication of how much is earmarked specifically for the benefit of women so as to
ensure effective implementation of women/girl related programmes.  Department of Women & Child Development is
not only the nodal agency for such programmes but also the nodal Department assigned with carrying out the task of

IV        Issues for Gender Budgeting
 
4.1       The Committee is of the opinion that in view of integration of gender equality with sustainable development,

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
need and importance of gender budgeting can hardly be over emphasised.  The aim of gender budgeting is to
generate/capture the gender based data/information, which will be relevant input for assessing the gender specific
impact of budgetary allocations.  The Committee found that currently gender segregated macro-economic data were not
available in India.  In the absence of such data from different sectors of our economy, the Committee feels, it will be
difficult to analyse the budgetary allocations on the basis of gender.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
process of gender budgeting should necessarily contain not only the segregation of gender based data but also aspects
like quantification of contribution of women in the economy; impact of Government budget on women; and policy
alternatives for evolving a gender sensitive social order.
 
V         Role of Department of Women and Child Development
 
5.1       The Committee heard the Secretary, Department of Women and Child Development, which has the mandate to
monitor the actions taken by different government agencies for the promotion of gender equality.  The Committee was
informed that the Department had taken the following initiatives in the recent years to advocate gender sensitivity in
planning and budget-making:-
 
q                   Consultations with State and Union territory Administrations through a workshop on 'Gender Issues and
Indicators' in November 1998, followed by another workshop on 'Developing Gender Development Index' (GDI)
and 'Gender Empowerment Measures' (GEM) and Establishing Indicators for States and Districts held on
December, 1998.
q                   A workshop on 'Engendering National Budgets in the South Asia Region' was held in July 2000 in New Delhi
in collaboration with the UNIFEM in which government representatives, UN agencies, media, NGOs, research
institutions, civil society and members of the Planning Commission participated.
q                   National Institute of Public Finance and Policy (NIPFP) was commissioned to study the gender-related
economic policy issues.  Based on the interim report of the Institute, the Economic Survey 2000-2001
highlighted for the first time aspects like, gender inequality and status of women, to be continued in its future
editions.  The NIPFP analyzed the Union Budget 2001-2002 from a gender perspective.
q                   Follow up Workshops in October and December, 2001 were organized, culminating in initiation of measures to
undertake analysis of State Budgets broadly adopting the methodology suggested by the NIPFP report on the
Union Budget.
q                   Taking the study done by NIPFP as a base, the Department of Women and Child Development carried out
concurrent Gender analysis of Union Budget for the financial years 2002-2003 and 2003-2004.
 
5.2       The Department of Women and Child Development informed the Committee that after extensive consultations
with gender experts and research institutions, it has initiated an exercise for State Gender Budget Analysis under the
coordination of the National Institute for Public Cooperation and Child Development.  Gender Budget studies have been
commissioned for 25 States through 19 social research scientists and research organizations to provide an analysis of the
changing status of women in the selected areas.  The exercise covers the years: 2000-2001, 2001-2002 and 2002-2003.
 
5.3       After looking at these efforts made by the Department, the Committee notes that so far, this exercise had
remained confined mainly to holding Seminars/Workshops since November, 1998.  The Committee is of the opinion

that the exercise of gender budgeting was not being carried out with the seriousness that it deserved.  The Committee
feels that the exercises undertaken by the Department so far, were hardly enough to sensitize the other Ministries and
Departments of the Government of India for undertaking this important activity.  Perhaps, the idea of making the
Department of Women & Child Development as a nodal agency for undertaking this exercise was to do the ground
exercise and evolve methodologies, etc.  to be later on adopted by other Ministries/Departments for gender budgeting in
an organized and comprehensive manner.  The Committee is constrained to say that unfortunately this has not happened
and we have not been able to evolve till today the required tools, methodologies and institutional mechanisms for
segregating gender based data, leave alone the quantification of contribution of women in the economy and the impact
of budgetary allocations on the women in our society.  Unless it is done, the Committee firmly believes, the goal of the
gender budgeting shall remain elusive.
 
5.4       The Committee in this regard would like to point out that the Department of Women and Child Development
which had started publishing a separate chapter titled "Gender Budget Initiatives" in its Annual Reports from the year
2001-02, has discontinued the same in 2003-04.  The Annual Report of the Department for the year 2003-04, in other
words, did not contain that chapter.  This fact was pointed out to the Secretary of the Department who explained that she
had joined the Department after the Annual Report had been published.  She, however, assured the Committee that not
only an explanation in writing would be taken from those responsible, but also the entire information for the current
year as well as for the year 2003-04 would be included in the Annual Report for 2004-05. 
 
5.5       It is not clear to the Committee, whether the decision to discontinue the chapter on Gender Budget Initiatives
was advertent or inadvertent.  If it was advertent, the Committee would like to know the reasons for this; and if it was
inadvertent, the Committee expresses its unhappiness in the matter.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
chapter on Gender Budget Initiatives should be restarted in the Annual Report from the year 2004-05, which should also
list the developments/steps taken in the year 2003-04.
 
VI        Gender Budgeting in other Ministries/Departments
 
6.1       The Committee was informed that the Cabinet Secretary had directed all the Ministries and Departments to
devote a chapter on gender issues in their Annual Reports. The Committee, however, observes that hardly any Ministry
and Department had started this exercise so far.  The Committee feels that the Department had not pursued this matter
with other Ministries and Departments effectively. The Committee recommends that the Department should make all
out efforts to persuade the other Ministries and Departments to include a chapter on gender budgeting in their Annual
Reports highlighting new initiatives/policies on gender related issues, resources available and their utilization and
gender disaggregated statistics for promoting gender awareness within and outside Government.
 
6.2       The Committee was informed that 18 Ministries /Departments which have women related specific schemes have
been identified to monitor for effective gender budgeting and expenditure. To a specific query as to the rationale behind
identifying only 18 Ministries for gender budgeting, the Department informed that bulk of the government's budget goes
to these 18 Ministries/Departments identified by it. 
 
6.3       The Committee appreciates the idea of identifying some Ministries/Departments for impressing upon them to
undertake the process of gender budgeting on a wider scale.  The Committee, however, feels that to the best of its

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
knowledge, the identified Ministries/Departments have apparently not been able to take up the exercise of gender
budgeting in their respective areas.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take up with
these Ministries/Departments to start the exercise of gender based analysis of their allocations/expenditure from the next
year 2005-06 positively.
 
6.4       The Committee is of the opinion that gender based analysis of allocations should be done for the entire Budget
of the government. This exercise, therefore, will have to be taken up by all the Ministries/Departments of the
Government of India, not only by those identified for this purpose so far.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
the remaining ones should also be sensitized to infuse gender aspects into their allocations/expenditure without further
delay.  This is necessary to have holistic idea about gender based allocations of the entire Budget of the Government of
India. The Department of Women and Child Development being a nodal agency should assume greater responsibilities
for undertaking this exercise of the gender budgeting by all the Ministries/Departments without further delay.
 
6.5       The Committee notes that the objective of gender budgeting is there, the Ministries/Departments are identified,
but the results are not forthcoming in absence of any guidelines for this purpose.  The Committee fails to understand the
Department’s limitations in this regard.  The Committee recommends to have specific guidelines laid down regarding
gender budgeting to be followed by all the Ministries/Departments.  This would still be quite possible on the basis of
experiences/feedback received on the problems being faced in implementation of gender budgeting.
 
VII      Lack of Inter-Ministerial Coordination
 
7.1       The Committee notes that creation of an inter-ministerial body has been recommended by a Task Force headed
by Shri K.C. Pant in April 2002.  Creation of an Inter-Departmental Steering Committee has also been suggested by the
Expert Group on Gender Budgeting.  Main task of such a Committee will be to develop an overall time-bound
programme for Gender Budgeting in identified Ministries and also to monitor and review the progress.  The Task Force
has also suggested that the Secretary, Department of Women & Child Development should be the Chairperson of the
Inter-Departmental Steering Committee.
 
7.2       The Committee appreciates the idea of having a monitoring and coordination mechanism for the purpose of
gender budgeting exercise.  Such a machinery assumes greater relevance in view of the fact that gender budgeting has to
be ultimately taken up not only by all the Departments/Ministries but by the State governments and local bodies as
well.  The Committee, however, fails to understand as to why no such inter-ministerial body could be created so far,
despite the specific suggestions made by the two important groups mentioned above.  The Committee is not aware
whether any initiative has been taken by the Department so far.  In view of the importance and urgency of such inter-
ministerial coordinating mechanism, the Committee urges upon the Government to take immediate steps to put such a
machinery in place.
 
7.3       The Committee feels that apart from having an inter-ministerial body for coordinating and monitoring, it is
equally important to have intra-ministerial/Departmental cells for coordinating/facilitating this exercise within each
Ministry/Department.  Such cells would certainly provide required impetus for this exercise.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that such monitoring cell should be created in all the Ministries/Departments.

VIII     Developing suitable Methodology
 
8.1       The Committee notes that we are yet to evolve suitable methodologies, tools, etc. for doing an effective Gender
Budget Analysis.  The Department of Women & Child Development has, with the help of NIPFP, undertaken some
exercise in this regard, which is limited to only few Departments/Ministries.
 
            The Committee emphasizes the need for developing/refining methodologies and gender tools like gender audit,
gender impact assessment, gender analysis and gender budgeting to monitor implementation and impact of gender
policies. 
 
8.2       The Committee also emphasizes the urgent need for undertaking research activities in different aspects of gender
budgeting including gender benefit incidence analysis.
 
IX        Need for Training/Capacity Building
 
9.1       The Committee notes that the exercise of Gender Budgeting, which is in the process of evolving suitable tools,
techniques and methodologies pre-supposes the need for orientation/training for the officials responsible for the job in
different Ministries/Departments.  They can undertake this exercise effectively and efficiently only if they are familiar
or have been adequately trained for the purpose.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the training and capacity-
building efforts should be made for the officials working in the Government Departments both at the Centre as well as
in States.  Possibility of arranging orientation/training for the people's representatives in local bodies should also be
considered.
           
9.2       The Committee recommends that government functionaries and financial specialists should be sensitized since it
is with the help of theses people that budget is made and auditing of expenditure is done at the end of financial year. If
they would do their job effectively, it will help in further streamlining the gender budget analysis. This should,
therefore, be specifically trained/sensitized to the importance of the task as their knowledge and motivation becomes
critical in determining the accuracy and validity of such an exercise.
X         Need for Impact Assessment
 
10.1     The Committee notes that there are still a number of core issues like the increasing burden of poverty on women;
inaccessibility and unequal access to health, family welfare and educational services; inequality in access and control
over assets and resources, both within and outside the family; inequality and inaccessibility to work, employment and
decision-making; and violence against women including domestic and otherwise, which have apparently some direct or
indirect bearing on framing policy relating to gender budgeting.
 
10.2     In view of this, the need for impact assessment of gender allocations, the Committee feels, assumes greater
importance. This impact assessment should not only be restricted to women-specific and pro-women programmes but
also should extend to impact of mainstream expenditure on women. 
 
10.3     The integration of gender in budgeting policies, Committee feels, would not only ensure better transparency and
accountability in the allocations meant for women, it would, at the same time, help evaluate the effectiveness of
resources utilized in delivering the intended results.  Simply focusing on how much money has been allocated for

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
women-specific and pro-women schemes could be counter-productive, if the outcome and performance of schemes were
far from the desired level.  This would, in fact, help us monitor output as well as input.
           
10.4     The Committee, therefore, would like the Department to explore the possibility of including a comparative
'Gender Impact Statement' as a separate part in Annual Budget of the government.
 
XI        Gender Budgeting in States
 
11.1     The exercise of gender budgeting, the Committee is of the view, would remain woefully incomplete without
extending it to State Budgets as well, because the States and Union territories account for bulk of expenditure on social
sector which relate to welfare/empowerment of women.  The Committee is happy to note that the exercise of State
budget from gender perspective was already on.  The Committee was informed that data from only ten States- Assam,
Bihar, Meghalaya, Gujarat, J&K, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, M.P. Orissa and Manipur were received.  The Committee has
learnt that due to wide variation in nomenclature of schemes/programmes across the States and discrepancies in data,
which require further validation, authentic collection of results and a comparative analysis is difficult to be made.  The
Committee, therefore, urges upon the government to formulate uniform procedures pertaining to gender budgeting and
analysis and disseminate these to the States.  The Committee also emphasizes the need for standardizing nomenclature,
terms, etc. with a view to avoiding possible ambiguities and confusions across the States.
 
11.2     The Committee is aware of the practical difficulties involved in having a meaningful analysis of State budgets,
but at the same time, it cannot ignore to emphasize about the necessity of undertaking and refining the exercise of State
budget analyses.
 
11.3     The Committee notes that of all the States and UTs, only ten have supplied data regarding their budgets, which is
not a very encouraging sign in this regard.  The Committee does not know the reasons behind it.  The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the States, which have not responded should be approached and persuaded to undertake/start
the exercises at the earliest.  The Department should act as a facilitator and provide necessary help, expertise, etc. for
this purpose to the States.
 
XII      Gender Budgeting in Local Bodies
 
12.1     The Committee emphasizes the need for taking up gender based analysis not only for the States but also for local
bodies - nagarpalikas, panchayats, etc. which also undertake social expenditure having gender specific benefits. 
 
XIII     Gender Budgeting in Private Sector
 
13.1     The Committee emphasizes the need for undertaking the exercise of gender analysis of allocations/expenditure
in private sector also.  The Committee would like to point out that of the total women workforce, their share in private

OBSERVATIONS/ RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
III        GENDER BUDGETING IN INDIA
 
3.5       In the context of India, where the gender bias is inherent in a male-dominated society, Gender Budgeting
becomes all the more relevant.  The Committee observes that the trends in sex ratio and other socio-economic indicators
clearly show, how the bias is getting accentuated in many parts of the country.  Over the years, during the course of
examination of various women and girl child related programmes/schemes, one factor which has been constantly
drawing the attention of the Committee is that inspite of well-conceived programmes and the funds allocated, there has
been no noticeable improvement in the status of women/girls.  Recently released State specific sex ratio figures of
Census 2001, do not leave any doubt in this regard.  The Committee is of the view that it is high time that the Budget of
the Government provides a clear indication of how much is earmarked specifically for the benefit of women so as to
ensure effective implementation of women/girl related programmes.  Department of Women & Child Development is
not only the nodal agency for such programmes but also the nodal Department assigned with carrying out the task of
Gender Budgeting, the Committee, accordingly decided to make a study on work done so far in this emerging area.
 
IV        Issues for Gender Budgeting
 
4.1       The Committee is of the opinion that in view of integration of gender equality with sustainable development,
need and importance of gender budgeting can hardly be over emphasised.  The aim of gender budgeting is to
generate/capture the gender based data/information, which will be relevant input for assessing the gender specific
impact of budgetary allocations.  The Committee found that currently gender segregated macro-economic data were not
available in India.  In the absence of such data from different sectors of our economy, the Committee feels, it will be
difficult to analyse the budgetary allocations on the basis of gender.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
process of gender budgeting should necessarily contain not only the segregation of gender based data but also aspects
like quantification of contribution of women in the economy; impact of Government budget on women; and policy
alternatives for evolving a gender sensitive social order.
 
V         Role of Department of Women and Child Development
 
5.3       After looking at these efforts made by the Department, the Committee notes that so far, this exercise had
remained confined mainly to holding Seminars/Workshops since November, 1998.  The Committee is of the opinion
that the exercise of gender budgeting was not being carried out with the seriousness that it deserved.  The Committee
feels that the exercises undertaken by the Department so far, were hardly enough to sensitize the other Ministries and
Departments of the Government of India for undertaking this important activity.  Perhaps, the idea of making the
Department of Women & Child Development as a nodal agency for undertaking this exercise was to do the ground
exercise and evolve methodologies, etc.  to be later on adopted by other Ministries/Departments for gender budgeting in
an organized and comprehensive manner.  The Committee is constrained to say that unfortunately this has not happened
and we have not been able to evolve  till today the required tools, methodologies and institutional mechanisms for
segregating gender based data, leave alone the quantification of contribution of women in the economy and the impact
of budgetary allocations on the women in our society.  Unless it is done, the Committee firmly believes, the goal of the
gender budgeting shall remain elusive.
 
5.5       It is not clear to the Committee, whether the decision to discontinue the chapter on Gender Budget Initiatives
was advertent or inadvertent.  If it was advertent, the Committee would like to know the reasons for this; and if it was
inadvertent, the Committee expresses its unhappiness in the matter.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
chapter on Gender Budget Initiatives should be restarted in the Annual Report from the year 2004-05, which should also
list the developments/steps taken in the year 2003-04.
VI        Gender Budgeting in other Ministries/Departments
 
The Committee, however, observes that hardly any Ministry and Department had started this exercise so far. 
The Committee feels that the Department had not pursued this matter with other Ministries and Departments effectively.
The Committee recommends that the Department should make all out efforts to persuade the other Ministries and
Departments to include a chapter on gender budgeting in their Annual Reports highlighting new initiatives/policies on
gender related issues, resources available and their utilization and gender disaggregated statistics for promoting gender
awareness within and outside Government. (Para 6.1)
 
6.3       The Committee appreciates the idea of identifying some Ministries/Departments for impressing upon them to
undertake the process of gender budgeting on a wider scale.  The Committee, however, feels that to the best of its
knowledge, the identified Ministries/Departments have apparently not been able to take up the exercise of gender
budgeting in their respective areas.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take up with
these Ministries/Departments to start the exercise of gender based analysis of their allocations/expenditure from the next
year 2005-06 positively.
 
6.4       The Committee is of the opinion that gender based analysis of allocations should be done for the entire Budget
of the government. This exercise, therefore, will have to be taken up by all the Ministries/Departments of the

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
Government of India, not only by those identified for this purpose so far.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that
the remaining ones should also be sensitized to infuse gender aspects into their allocations/expenditure without further
delay.  This is necessary to have holistic idea about gender based allocations of the entire Budget of the Government of
India. The Department of Women and Child Development being a nodal agency should assume greater responsibilities
for undertaking this exercise of the gender budgeting by all the Ministries/Departments without further delay.
 
6.5       The Committee notes that the objective of gender budgeting is there, the Ministries/Departments are identified,
but the results are not forthcoming in absence of any guidelines for this purpose.  The Committee fails to understand the
Department’s limitations in this regard.  The Committee recommends to have specific guidelines laid down regarding
gender budgeting to be followed by all the Ministries/Departments.  This would still be quite possible on the basis of
experiences/feedback received on the problems being faced in implementation of gender budgeting.
 
VII      Lack of Inter-Ministerial Coordination
 
7.2       The Committee appreciates the idea of having a monitoring and coordination mechanism for the purpose of
gender budgeting exercise.  Such a machinery assumes greater relevance in view of the fact that gender budgeting has to
be ultimately taken up not only by all the Departments/Ministries but by the State governments and local bodies as
well.  The Committee, however, fails to understand as to why no such inter-ministerial body could be created so far,
despite the specific suggestions made by the two important groups mentioned above.  The Committee is not aware
whether any initiative has been taken by the Department so far.  In view of the importance and urgency of such inter-
ministerial coordinating mechanism, the Committee urges upon the Government to take immediate steps to put such a
machinery in place.
 
7.3       The Committee feels that apart from having an inter-ministerial body for coordinating and monitoring, it is
equally important to have intra-ministerial/Departmental cells for coordinating/facilitating this exercise within each
Ministry/Department.  Such cells would certainly provide required impetus for this exercise.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that such monitoring cell should be created in all the Ministries/Departments.
 
VIII     Developing suitable Methodology.
 
8.1       The Committee notes that we are yet to evolve suitable methodologies, tools, etc. for doing an effective Gender
Budget Analysis.  The Department of Women & Child Development has, with the help of NIPFP, undertaken some
exercise in this regard, which is limited to only few Departments/Ministries.
            The Committee emphasizes the need for developing/refining methodologies and gender tools like gender audit,
gender impact assessment, gender analysis and gender budgeting to monitor implementation and impact of gender
policies.  
8.2       The Committee also emphasizes the urgent need for undertaking research activities in different aspects of gender
budgeting including gender benefit incidence analysis.
IX        Need for Training/Capacity Building
9.1       The Committee notes that the exercise of Gender Budgeting, which is in the process of evolving suitable tools,
techniques and methodologies pre-supposes the need for orientation/training for the officials responsible for the job in
different Ministries/Departments.  They can undertake this exercise effectively and efficiently only if they are familiar

153rd Report of HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/153rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:21:27]
or have been adequately trained for the purpose.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the training and capacity-
building efforts should be made for the officials working in the Government Departments both at the Centre as well as
in States.  Possibility of arranging orientation/training for the people's representatives in local bodies should also be
considered.
           
9.2       The Committee recommends that government functionaries and financial specialists should be sensitized since it
is with the help of theses people that budget is made and auditing of expenditure is done at the end of financial year. If
they would do their job effectively, it will help in further streamlining the gender budget analysis. This should,
therefore, be specifically trained/sensitized to the importance of the task as their knowledge and motivation becomes
critical in determining the accuracy and validity of such an exercise.
X         Need for Impact Assessment
10.1     The Committee notes that there are still a number of core issues like the increasing burden of poverty on women;
inaccessibility and unequal access to health, family welfare and educational services; inequality in access and control
over assets and resources, both within and outside the family; inequality and inaccessibility to work, employment and
decision-making; and violence against women including domestic and otherwise, which have apparently some direct or
indirect bearing on framing policy relating to gender budgeting.
 
10.2     In view of this, the need for impact assessment of gender allocations, the Committee feels, assumes greater
importance. This impact assessment should not only be restricted to women-specific and pro-women programmes but
also should extend to impact of mainstream expenditure on women. 
 
10.3     The integration of gender in budgeting policies, Committee feels, would not only ensure better transparency and
accountability in the allocations meant for women, it would, at the same time, help evaluate the effectiveness of
resources utilized in delivering the intended results.  Simply focusing on how much money has been allocated for
women-specific and pro-women schemes could be counter-productive, if the outcome and performance of schemes were
far from the desired level.  This would, in fact, help us monitor output as well as input.
           
10.4     The Committee, therefore, would like the Department to explore the possibility of including a comparative
'Gender Impact Statement' as a separate part in Annual Budget of the government.
 
XI        Gender Budgeting in States
 
11.1     The exercise of gender budgeting, the Committee is of the view, would remain woefully incomplete without
extending it to State Budgets as well, because the States and Union territories account for bulk of expenditure on social
sector which relate to welfare/empowerment of women.  The Committee is happy to note that the exercise of State
budget from gender perspective was already on.  The Committee was informed that data from only ten States- Assam,
Bihar, Meghalaya, Gujarat, J&K, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, M.P. Orissa and Manipur were received.  The Committee has
learnt that due to wide variation in nomenclature of schemes/programmes across the States and discrepancies in data,
which require further validation, authentic collection of results and a comparative analysis is difficult to be made.  The
Committee, therefore, urges upon the government to formulate uniform procedures pertaining to gender budgeting and
analysis and disseminate these to the States.  The Committee also emphasizes the need for standardizing nomenclature,
terms, etc. with a view to avoiding possible ambiguities and confusions across the States.
