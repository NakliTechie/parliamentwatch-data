CHAPTER-I
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS WHICH
HA VE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE GOVERNMENT
Recommendations/Observations
1.4 The Committee appreciates the lofty ideals and objectives envisaged through the vision
Statement of the Ministry. In the first instance, it appears as if the vision covers expansions
of review of some of the existing schemes of the Ministry. This is a positive thinking on the
part of the Ministry. Regular evaluation of the schemes indicate that feedback is being
obtained, monitoring is done and corrective measures taken. Most of the schemes under the
Ministry are being implemented through State Governments or NGOs. It is, therefore,
imperative that implementing agencies are kept in loop during the entire exercise of review
and revamping to be done after thorough consultation with field agencies. The Committee
hopes that the Ministry had done all such preparatory exercises as were necessary in respect
of its schemes meant to be reviewed, expanded and revamped. The Committee is optimistic
that Ministry would strive hard to achieve its mandate for development of women and
protection of children through focused schemes envisaged in their Vision Statement.
Action Taken
The observations of the Committee have been noted for compliance. This Ministry continues
to review and if required revamp the schemes in consultation with all stake holders. All efforts will
be made to achieve the mandate for development of women and protection of children.
However, the Ministry is proposing to universalize the Swayamsidha Project as
Swayamsidha Phase-II to cover all districts/blocks in the country. An amount of Rs.20.00 crores
has been allocated for the year 2009-10 for Swayamsidha Phase-II and Rs.500 crores for the
Eleventh Plan period. The draft EFC memo has been prepared and is circulated for soliciting
comments of Departments/Ministries.
As envisaged under the scheme, the Support to Training and Employment Programme for
Women (STEP) has been evaluated by an independent agency. The evaluation agency has made field
visits across the country to assess the impact of STEP projects on the status of women beneficiaries.
Furthermore, the agency has the made interaction with beneficiaries to know the difficulties, if any,
being faced by them and also the bottlenecks, if any, being faced by the implementing agencies. The
evaluation agency has also organized a seminar on 09.05.2007 in which representatives from various
quarter including implementing agencies were invited to offer their views. Taking into consideration
all these issues, the evaluation agency has submitted its final report with certain suggestions/
recommendations which was examined by a Core Group constituted in the Ministry for the purpose.
Based on these recommendations, the STEP scheme is going to be revamped shortly.
Central Social Welfare Board has informed that steps are being taken to revamp the schemes
envisaged in its Vision Statement, in consultation with all stake holders.

and Livelihood Programme in the Mid- Gangetic Plains/Priyadarshini Project is concerned. The loan

Ministry is in the process of fulfilling the conditions for loan effectiveness viz. Setting of CPSU,
signing the MOU with Lead Programme Agency setting up of NPCU taking Lead Project Agencies

III. ICDS
Recommendations/Observations
3.7 Construction of 47,872 AWC Buildings in NE-S tates was sanctioned as a special case out
of the NER component of the Budget of the Ministry from 2001-02 to 2006-07. latest status made
available to the Committee reveals a very unsatisfactory position. Targets in respect of construction
of AWCs for 2001-02 and 2002-03 have been achieved. However, there was slow progress during
2004-05, but during 2005-06 and 2006-07 there has been no progress in the construction of AWC
Buildings. The Committee is aware of the problems/constraints being faced in the North-East
region in construction of AWC buildings due to its peculiar conditions. It however, fails to
understand the absence of any progress in this regard during the recent years. It appears
that all activities for this purpose have come to a standstill. The Committee was informed
that the States were not submitting the UCs of funds released to them for constructing
AWCs. It appears that they are not able to contribute even 10% of their share for this
purpose. The Committee is not happy with this state of affairs in the region, which needs
special attention. It therefore, recommends that it must be taken at the highest levels to
sort out the problems at the earliest, so that the work could proceed at the desired pace.
Action Taken
This Ministry shares the concern of the Hon’ble Standing Committee over the slow
progress of construction of Anganwadi buildings in North Eastern States. The Ministry is pursuing
the matter at higher level regularly. Hon’ble MOS(IC), WCD had taken up the matter with the Chief
Ministers of Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur and Sikkim in 2008. Secretary, WCD had also taken up
this matter with the Secretaries of NE States to expedite the construction of Anganwadi buildings
and their utilization.

IV. RAJIV GANDHI NATIONAL CRECHE SCHEME FOR THE CHILDREN OF WORKING
MOTHERS
Recommendations/Observations
4.4 The Committee has been informed that Rs.550 crores has been earmarked of RGN Creche
Scheme for the Eleventh Plan Period out of which Rs.100 crores is the approved outlay for the year
2008-09. The Committee was informed that the Ministry proposes to open 5000 new creches per
annum for an expanded and effective scheme for child care facilities. The Committee is happy to
learn that 5000 creches are proposed to be operated every year during the Eleventh Plan period.
It, however, feels that the allocation for this scheme will be highly inadequate. Besides, the cost
norms for financial assistance, supplementary nutrition for child, emergency medicines,
honorarium for crèche worker, etc, are also very low, in view of the existing inflationary rate.
It may be very difficult to manage the services to be provided by these creches. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that keeping in view the shortage of creches in the country, the
Ministry should open more creches preferably in the areas where these are needed most and
also in uncovered areas. The Committee further recommends that the allocation for this be
enhanced appropriately with a view to meet the required expenditure meant only for the
proposed expansion but also for enhancing cost norms under the Schemes suitably.
Action Taken
The proposal of opening up of more number of Creches in the existing area and also in
uncovered areas would be considered depending upon the requirement and availability of funds. The
proposal to the conversion of the Scheme from Central Sector to Centrally Sponsored Scheme and
the revision in the norms is under consideration in consultation with State Governments and
Planning Commission.
Recommendations/Observations
4.7 As per Ministry’s version, a grant of Rs.20.96 lakhs has been sanctioned upto 01.01.2008 to
monitoring agencies for monitoring of creches. State-wise independent monitoring agencies have been
identified in consultation with the State Governments. These agencies are like State Women
Development Corporations, Women Study Centres in the Universities, National Institute of Public
Cooperation and Child Development, New Delhi and other agencies recommended by the State
Governments. The Committee appreciates this in-built component of monitoring of creches which
would help in the better delivery of services in the creches. The Committee is, however, of the
view that monitoring mechanism needs to be made more effective and extensive. It only hopes
that the findings in this regard are studied and mid course corrections adopted regularly.
Action Taken
The Ministry analyzes and examines the monitoring reports received from monitoring
agencies and the deficiencies found in the functioning of crèches are forwarded to the
implementing agencies to take remedial steps and rectify the deficiencies. So far monitoring reports
of 6113 creches have been received and concerned implementing agencies have been requested to
take corrective action.

Recommendations/Observations
7.6 The Committee is surprised to know that the Ministry does not have any database
on the street children in the country, the target group of this scheme. The Committee
would like the Ministry to start preparing a data-base entailing the number of children on
the streets (State-wise) and the number of children benefiting from this scheme. The
Committee also lays emphasis on evolving a mechanism to monitor and ensure that the
children from the centres, fit into the mainstream; and do not go back to streets again. The
Committee believes that the real success of the scheme lies in this. A feedback in this
regard would be of great help to access the success/failure of the scheme in the true sense.
Action Taken
There is no definite estimate of the number of street children in the country as they are
generally floating/mobile in nature and it may not be possible to maintain the data-base. However,
Ministry of Women and Child Development has formulated a new centrally sponsored scheme
namely ‘Integrated Child Protection Scheme [ICPS]’ under which the ‘Street Children Scheme’ is
merged. Under this scheme, provision has been made for setting up of ‘Open Shelters’ in urban
and semi-urban areas to cater the children in need of care and protection including ‘street children’
to encourage them to keep away from a life on the street. One of the programme components and
activities of the ‘Open Shelters’ is to maintain electronic data of each child and furnish the details
to the District Child Protection Society [DCPS] as a part of the child tracking system.
Recommendations/Observations
7.10 The Committee would also like to draw the attention of the Ministry towards the
‘Study on Child Abuse – India 2007’ which depicts the deplorable condition of child in our
country. The Committee believes that the Ministry taking note of the findings of the study
on child abuse should make pro-active efforts for providing safe environment for children
and take stern measures against the persons accused of abusing children. The Ministry
should send out a strong message to persons engaged in child abuse through TV , radio and
print media. The Commission for Protection of Child Rights should be involved in taking
initiatives to stop the problem of child abuse rampant in our society. The Committee hopes
that through networking of various agencies working for the cause of children, the Ministry
can contain and address the problem of child abuse through social education, stringent laws
and the integrated scheme implemented in the mission mode.
Action Taken
National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) has constituted a Working
Group for Child participation in TV/Reality Show. The Working Group is in the process of
consultation and has not submitted its report. The Ministry is also working on a draft Bill on
Prevention of Offences against Children, a comprehensive legislation to cover all offences against

8
children. The Bill is presently being revived in consultation with NCPCR. The child victims of
abuse are presently protected under JJ Act under the category of children in need of care and
protection. Such children are also covered under new Integrated Child Protection Scheme.
IX. SCHEME OF ASSISTANCE TO HOME FOR CHILDREN (SHISHU GREH) TO
PROMOTE IN-COUNTRY ADOPTION
Recommendations/Observations
9.4 The Committee is glad to know that the Ministry realizes the importance of training of
functionaries of the Shishu-Grehs and special focus on awareness campaigns. The Committee
hopes that the Ministry would make concerted efforts for making this scheme popular and
ensure effective implementation of the scheme through training and awareness programmes.
Action Taken
Ministry of Women and Child Development has formulated a new Central sponsored scheme
–‘Integrated Child Protection Scheme [ICPS]’ under which the existing scheme ‘Shishu Greha’ has
been merged. However, Central Adoption Resource Agency [CARA] has been conducting various
training and development programmes for capacity building of Shishu Grehas, efforts have been
made recently for multimedia campaign to create awareness about adoptions in the country. Under
the ICPS, efforts will be made to cover the districts in the country. ICPS also provides for
strengthening of CARA in order to provide better adoption services in the country.
Recommendations/Observations
9.5 The Committee notes the findings of evaluation of the scheme by NIPCCD-2007 as
mentioned below:–
– Not all the organisations maintained a unit of 10 children at a given time.
– About 88% child care workers (ayahs) getting honorarium/salary between Rs.1000-2500
p.m.
– 53% organisations faced delay in release of grants and another 32% found terms/
conditions of the scheme complicated.
– Difficult procedure and non-uniformity of law.
– Delay in release of grants.
– Difficult in finding families for in-country adoption.
– Lack of budget for salaries of staff.
– 47% child care workers had no training.
The Committee recommends the Ministry to take note of these shortcomings and
adopt measures for improving the condition of Shishu Greh. The Committee hopes that
performance of this scheme also will improve during Eleventh Plan after it would be merged
under the new umbrella scheme of ICPS.
Action Taken
Under ICPS it has been visualized to strengthen Shishu Grehs and address the gaps
mentioned above. CARA shall monitor functioning of all adoption agencies through its state level
offices such as SARAs. It has also been felt that timely disbursement of grants can also be made
possible through ICPS.

II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
The Committee is still not convinced with the trend of utilization of funds. Major
chunk of the funds have been utilized between the period from 1.02.2008 to 31.03.2008 with
respect to many schemes like ICDS, Training Programme under ICDS, STEP, Short Stay
Homes, Swayamsidha and Swadhar. For the scheme of Rashtriya Mahila Kosh, the entire
allocated funds of Rs. 12.00 crore could be utilized only in the last two months of the year.
The hurried utilization of funds at the end of the financial year is not a healthy pattern
of financial discipline. More so, the expenditure still appears short of financial targets in
respect of those schemes. Expenditure should be evenly spread throughout the year to
maintain transparency as well as justification in utilization with a view to establish financial
accountability at the implementation level. The Committee is not aware about the reasons
for funds allocated under the schemes of ‘Conditional Cash Transfer Scheme for the Girl
Child with Insurance Cover’ and the Scheme for Combating Trafficking remaining unspent
at the end of the year. The Committee hopes that position would be better in 2009-10.
(Paras 2.2 & 2.3)
The Committee agrees in principle with the procedure followed by the Ministry with
respect to release of funds to States/UTs. However, it feels that the money released to the
State Governments needed to be properly accounted for. The Committee recommends that
the Ministry should work in tandem with the concerned State Governments and guide them
regularly on the optimum and judicious utilization of the public money on such crucial
social sector schemes. (Paras 2.4, 2.5 & 2.6)
The Committee would like to draw the attention of the Ministry to the targets set
and achievement level with regard to number of beneficiaries under Awareness Generation
Programme. Similarly, against the target of organization of 6000 Awareness Generation
Camps, achievement level as on 31
st December, 2007 was only 1259 and by the end of the
year (31.3.08), 5436 camps could be organized. These figures clearly indicate that the last
quarter of the year saw the maximum activity. The Committee is also surprised to note that
upto 31
st December, 2007. 31.250 beneficiaries were reported to have attended 1250 camps.
This figure was 1,32,150 beneficiaries having attended 5436 camps as on 31 st March, 2008.
Not only this, there is also mismatch in the targets set under the Awareness Generation
Programme and their achievement level. The Committee therefore, recommends that a view
of both the above-mentioned components of Awareness Generation Programme may be
undertaken so as to ensure their implementation in the real sense. The Committee finds
that the information under the Shishu Greh Scheme is maintained for a calendar year.
Achievement level for 2007-08 (upto 31.3.08) was actually for the period from 1.1.2007 to
31.12.2007. The Committee fails to understand the rationale for maintaining the
information for one single scheme calendarwise as compared to maintenance of information
for all the other schemes financial year wise. The Committee is of the view that for the
Shishu Greh Scheme also the same accounting mechanism should be put into place.
(Paras 2.7 & 2.8)

inability of the Government in getting the accounts cleared are major hindrances in the

III. ICDS
The Committee can hardly emphasise the significance of construction of AWC
buildings with basic amenities like safe drinking water, sanitation facilities etc. for efficient
delivery of services. The Ministry’s dependence for the support of local community for
infrastructural development of AWCs over the years and making no fund provisions
therefor has not brought any substantial results.  The scheme continues to be plagued with
infrastructural deformities. The Committee, therefore, reiterates that a percentage of
allocation for ICDS be fixed for construction of AWC buildings.  This would be a one time
expenditure which would lead to betterment of services given by these A WCs. The
Committee would also like to be apprised about construction status of AWCs in North
Eastern States, keeping in view the special provision of Rs. 1.75 lakh per AWC since
2005-06. (Para 3.4)
The Committee has noted the shortcomings pointed by NIPCCID in the existing
Management Information System on ICDS Scheme; it would be more interested in the
corrective initiatives taken to ameliorate the problems encountered in providing
supplementary nutrition. The State Governments needed to be sensitized about the
importance of supplementary nutrition to children, pregnant & lactating mothers. They
should be motivated to provide supplementary nutrition on regular basis and that too as per
financial norms. The Committee believes that the Management Information System needs
to be reviewed in the light of disruptions pointed out in the study undertaken by NIPCCD.
(Para 3.5)
No feedback has been reflected by the Ministry about lack of Pre-School Education
Kits.  The Committee is greatly concerned at the high percentage of A WCs who do not have
Pre-School Education Kits. It would like the Ministry to initiate special steps to ensure
availability of these kits. This is crucial for achievement of literacy targets and towards this
end, the Ministry should work out a time bound action programme. The Committee hopes
that all the basic amenities would be arranged to facilitate better delivery of services by
AWCs. The Committee would also like to be apprised about the other probable changes/new
initiatives in the restructured ICDS scheme. (Para 3.6)

VI. THE INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS)
The Committee notes that the Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS) got the
approval of EFC in its meeting held on the 20 th March, 2008. It took almost a year for
getting the clearance from the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs on 26 th February,
2009. The Committee also observes that response of States/UTs to the communication dated
28th April, 2009 for signing a MOU with the Central Government under the scheme has not
been very encouraging. By 27 th August, 2009, only three States namely, Andhra Pradesh,
Chattisgarh and Orissa have signed the MOU. ICPS was to be implemented during the
Eleventh Plan. Every year funds are being allocated for it.  Rs.180.00 crore were allocated
during 2008-09 also. The Committee, however, notes with concern that it is already third
year of the plan period and only three states have come forward to sign the MOU so far.
The Committee has little hope of this much awaited scheme taking off in the real sense
during the Eleventh Plan. The Committee, therefore, urges upon the Department to get this
scheme implemented at the earliest. (Paras 6.2, 6.3 & 6.4)
