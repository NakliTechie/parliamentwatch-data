REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION    
1.1              The main objectives of the Department of Health are:-
 
(i)         to undertake national programmes of health and to intensify measures for the prevention, control and
eradication of communicable diseases;
(ii)        to promote education, research and training in various medical disciplines, to reorient the medical
colleges in the delivery of health services in rural areas;
(iii)       to prevent adulteration of food as well as drugs;
(iv)       to provide primary health care at the doorsteps of the people and to train the required number of
personnel for this purpose;
(v)        to take steps for better implementation of Health Care Programmes for tribal and other weaker sections
of the Society; and
(vi)       to collaborate with  member countries of United Nations and International Agencies like WHO,
UNICEF in matters relating to health promotion.
1.2       The Department of Health incurs expenditure either directly or through various institutions and establishments
run by it and by way of grants given to autonomous and voluntary organisations.  Grants to States and Union Territory
Administrations are mainly for Centrally Sponsored Health Schemes being implemented through the State Governments
and Union Territory Administrations.  They include national programmes for prevention, control and eradication of
diseases.
1.3       The Department finances, wholly or substantially, the following autonomous institutions engaged in patient care
and/ or research:
 
(i)         All India Institute of Medical Sciences and Dr. Rajendra Prasad Centre for Ophthalmic Sciences,  New
Delhi;
(ii)        Post-Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh; 
(iii)       All India Institute of Speech and Hearing Sciences, Mysore; and
            (iv)       Indian Council of Medical Research, New Delhi.

II.        BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       A Budgetary provision of Rs. 2167.86 crores has been made for the Department of Health for 2000-2001.  Rs.
1277.86 crores is the Plan component and Rs. 890.00 crores have been provided under Non-Plan.
2.2       On a specific query as to the projected requirements of the Department for 2000-2001, the Department submitted
that as against the original projected demand of Rs. 1350 crores, the amount finally approved is Rs. 1300 crores
(including a component of Rs. 22.14 crores  under Capital Works). The requirements originally proposed for Annual
Plan 2000-2001  were keeping in view the Ninth  Plan priorities and likely  expenditure.   The major programmes/
institutions where outlays have been reduced following reduction by the Planning Commission are Malaria, TB, AIDS,
Cancer and the National Institute of Biologicals (NIB).
2.3       The Committee has been given to understand that there has been no substantial increase in the allocation for
health sector.   The Committee notes that out of the total investment of 6% of GDP for the health sector, 1.3% is
contributed by the State sector, i.e. the State Governments and the Central Government.   However, due to the
constraint-ridden financial position of majority of the States, there has not been any major addition to the Plan outlay of
the State Governments.   The Committee is of the opinion that  in view of the weak financial position of majority of the
States the  Central Government has no other alternative but to bear the main responsibility in this regard.
2.4       The Committee, at the same time, is also distressed to note that the Department has not been able to utilise the
funds allocated to it for the past few years.   The Committee finds that allocations made are invariably being reduced at
the RE stage and even this reduced allocation is not fully utilised.   Provision of Rs. 2361.69 crores was made in 1998-
99 which was reduced to Rs. 2067.56 crores.   Actual  expenditure is  only Rs. 1901.69 crores.   Similarly, provision of
Rs. 2449.23 crores in BE 1999-2000 has been reduced to Rs. 2231.88 crores at the RE stage.   Details of actual
expenditure  when made available would again indicate under-utilisation of allocations.   The Committee finds that
maximum saving is due to surrender of funds under EAC component of various National Health Programmes in 1997-
98 and 1998-99.   The Committee takes a serious view of this.
2.5       In fact, it is an irony that on one hand we have funds constraint and on the other whatever allocation is made,
despite funds constraint, is not fully utilised.   The Committee is of the considered view that this is one of the reasons for
non-effective and improper implementation of most of the schemes in our country.  Last year, the Committee had
extensively dwelt on this issue and had observed that the Department of Health, being the nodal agency, has to take
remedial steps.  The Committee would like to be apprised about the measures  taken by the Department. The Committee
would also like to know about the utilization of fund under EAC component of various National Health   Programes
during 1999-2000. 
2.6       The Committee has been informed that foreign direct investment  (FDI) upto 10%  is allowed in health sector.  
FDI policy has been further liberalised thereby putting almost all items/activities except a small negative list under the
automatic route.   Further, a special Subject Group to look into the policy framework for private investment in
Education, Health and Rural Development has been recently re-constituted by the Prime Minister.   The Committee

2.7       Performance Budget should clearly reflect both the financial provision and corresponding physical achievements
of different schemes/programmes during the financial year.  The Committee, however, observes that Performance

III.      CENTRAL GOVERNMENT HEALTH SCHEME(CGHS)
3.1       Rs. 10  crores have been provided under Plan for CGHS for  2000-2001 against an allocation of Rs. 9 crores in
BE 1999-2000 reduced to Rs. 8 crores at the RE stage.  To a question as to  in what manner  this enhancement in budget
allocation is proposed to be utilized, the Department replied that the allocation is proposed to be utilized for the new
schemes based on the proposals contained in the Ninth Plan which included extension of CGHS to new cities and also
opening of dispensaries/polyclinics/ Labs. etc. in the existing CGHS cities.
3.2       The Committee observes  that during the Ninth Plan, it was targetted  to extend the CGHS  network  by covering
seven new cities.   However, the Committee is constrained to note that no achievement could be made during the first
three years of the Ninth Plan as the  SIU Report of the Department of Expenditure on the Norms study of CGHS
(Allopathic)  dispensaries submitted on 2.11.99 stated that creation of additional/new posts under CGHS can be done
only on completion of six months from the date on which action as given in, the SIU Report has been completed.  The
Committee finds that as the SIU Report  is, at present under examination of the CGHS ,  it will take some more time for
the CGHS to implement the recommendations contained in the report.  It is doubtful whether the creation of additional
new posts under CGHS would be taken up during 2000-2001.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Department  should make concerted efforts to expedite all the formalities,   so that Ninth Plan target for upgrading
CGHS network is achieved.
3.3       Apart from expansion of CGHS, the Committee is of the view that there is a need for improvement in the quality
of services provided by the  CGHS Dispensaries.   The Department should take effective measures  in this regard.
3.4       The Department has informed that the report given by a Review Committee constituted on 17
th February, 1999
for formulating lists of Patent Proprietary drugs for inclusion in the combined formulary for CGHS/VMS is yet to be
approved.   To a query by when  this exercise is likely to be completed, the Department has stated that initially a
Committee under the Chairmanship of Dr. P. Das Gupta, the then DCG (I) was constituted to prepare a combined drug
formulary of CGHS and MSO.  The formulary recommended by this Committee excluded drugs where purchases were
less than Rs. 5 lakhs.  The report of the second committee headed by Dr. Ranjit Roy Choudhary was not found feasible
as it excluded most of the old drugs finding place in the earlier formulary.   It was also observed that small and medium
scale manufacturers would be hit if this formulary was accepted.  The reports of both the above Committees were
reviewed by a Committee under the Chairmanship of DGHS and  after going through all the  reports a fresh Committee
for formulating a single comprehensive formulary for CGHS and MSO is under consideration of the Department.   The
Committee expresses deep anguish over the delay in finalising the combined formulary due to contradictory
recommendations of the various committees appointed by the Department and recommends that the formulary be
finalised without any further loss of time and the Committee be apprised thereof.
 IV.      NATIONAL MEDICAL LIBRARY

National Resource of Biomedical and Health Science Information.  It attempts to reach all professionals and
practitioners of Medicine all over the country through its information products and services. It also attempts to support
various plan activities and programmes of the Directorate General of Health Services and the Ministry of Health .
4.2       Allocation for National Medical Library for 2000-2001 is Rs. 7.85 crores.  Provision was almost the same (Rs.
7.81 crores) in 1999-2000.  To a query  as to what was the projected demand of the Library  and whether the allocation
for the Library, which functions as national Resource of Bio-Medical and Health Science Information, is sufficient to
meet its requirement,  the Department replied that the National Medical Library  had proposed allocations of Rs. 903
lakhs (Rs. 630 lakhs Plan +Rs. 273 lakhs (non-Plan)for  the year 2000-2001.  However, actual allocation has been
restricted to Rs. 785 lakhs (Rs. 540 lakhs Plan + Rs. 245 lakhs Non-Plan) which may not be sufficient to meet all its

V.        SAFDARJUNG HOSPITAL
5.1       The allocation for Safdarjung Hospital  for the year 2000-2001 is Rs. 86.80 crores which shows a marginal
increase in comparison to  RE allocation of Rs. 84.35 crores for 1999-2000.
5.2       The Committee notes that Plan allocation of Rs. 25.93 crores for Safdarjung Hospital in 1999-2000 was
enhanced to Rs. 26.54 crores at the RE stage.  However, the Committee is constrained to observe, that against this
enhanced allocation, expenditure reported upto 10.3.2000 is only Rs. 19.27 crores.  What is more disturbing is that
against a provision of Rs. 8 crores for Machinery and equipment, there has been expenditure of only Rs. 1.13 crores
upto 10.3.2000.  The Committee has been pursuing the progress made in the purchase of equipments required for
upgradation of the Burns and Plastic Surgery Department of the Hospital.  Equipments worth Rs. 90.30 lakhs against a
sanction for procurement of equipment worth Rs. 115.86 lakhs have been purchased upto 1999-2000.  The Committee
hopes that the process of procurement of the remaining equipments would be completed during 2000-2001.
5.3       Safdarjung Hospital,  the largest hospital under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, provides  medical
services in all basic specialties including super specialties  and also provides round the clock casualty and emergency
services.  The number of indoor patients is more than the capacity of the hospital, almost at any point of time. 
Furthermore, the Hospital is run with the policy of not denying  admission to any one who comes to it for treatment. 
The Committee is, however, constrained to observe that most of the services provided by the Hospital are 
unsatisfactory, there is total lack of cleanliness and behaviour of the  staff  is  wanting.   This is true about other
Government Hospitals too.  Some drastic measures need to be taken at the earliest.   The Committee understands that at
one stage, the Department had decided to entrust the work of maintaining cleanliness at least in the emergency wards to
private agencies.   The Committee fails to understand the reasons for non-implementation of this decision.   The
Committee is of the view that the Department should find out ways and means of maintaining cleanliness in all
Government Hospitals.
VI.       DR. RAM MANOHAR LOHIA HOSPITAL
6.1       Plan allocation for the Dr. RML hospital, New Delhi has been substantially increased from Rs. 12.47 crores in
1999-2000 to Rs. 24.50  crores in 2000-2001.  To a question as to in what manner this allocation of additional funds is
proposed to be utilised, the Department submitted that the enhanced allocation is meant for payment of salary for newly
created posts in various Departments, purchase of equipments and new schemes like Nephrology, Hematology and
Pediatric Surgery. 
6.2       To a query as to by when the task of building of a Trauma Centre is likely to be completed, the Department
replied that the work of building a Trauma Centre has been entrusted to the Hospital Services Consultancy Corporation. 
A draft E.F.C. proposal is likely to be submitted shortly. After the E.F.C. proposal is approved by the competent
authority and no objection/approvals are received from the various authorities, the construction work will start.  The
project is likely to take five years to complete.   The Committee feels that considerable time has already been taken at
the proposal stage.  The Committee is, therefore, of the view that the Department should expedite the completion of all
procedural formalities so that the construction work can start at the earliest.   The Committee feels  that all preliminary
exercise should be completed  before provision for any scheme is made.
6.3       The Committee understands that Nursing Home facilities are available only in Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital
at Delhi which are meant for Central Govt. employees drawing certain level of salary and other VIPs.  However, certain
constraints are being faced in availing of this facility.   It has been reported that upkeep of the rooms is also generally
lacking.   At present, rooms in the Nursing Home are being allotted only during specific time on recommendation of the
doctors authorised to do so.  The Committee fails to understand the rationale behind this procedure as the illness does
not come with an advance notice.  The Committee would like to be apprised about the rules/guidelines governing the
allotment of rooms.  The Committee is of the view that a proper chart displaying the availability of rooms should be
displayed at the Nursing Home.   The Committee also recommends that in the event of a room not being available, on
the basis of recommendation of the doctor on duty, the patient should be free to go to one of the CGHS recognised
private hospitals.

VII.      CENTRAL INSTITUTE OF PSYCHIATRY, RANCHI
7.1       The Plan allocation for this Institute in BE 1999-2000 was Rs. 3.80 crores, which was reduced to Rs. 2.71 crores
in the RE.  For 2000-2001, the Plan allocation is Rs. 3.00 crores.
7.2       Last year, the Committee was informed that the allocation of Rs. 2.80 crores in 1998-99 for the Institute was
enhanced to Rs. 3.80 crores in 1999-2000 for filling up 260 vacant posts.  However, the Committee finds that  posts
could not be created on account of ban orders issued by the Department of Expenditure.  This issue has been continuing
for a considerable time as  an IWSU study conducted for the Institute had recommended creation of 260 posts on
16.1.97, i.e. about three years ago.  The Committee is of the view that given the nature of the ailment, the patients of this
Institute need special treatment.  Doctors and para-medicals with specialized skills should be appointed to treat the
patients more efficiently.  It is an undesirable situation specially in view of that CIP, Ranchi is a teaching institute of a
national level.  The Committee is,  therefore, of the view that creation of additional posts is a necessity which cannot be
avoided.
7.3       To a query as to  the latest position in respect of the Draft Cabinet Note conferring autonomous status to the CIP,
Ranchi , the Department replied that  the Note  was circulated to the concerned Ministries and their comments received
are being examined. The Department of Expenditure vide their O.M.  dated 5.3.99 desired to clearly bring out the
financial implications on the upgradation and to come up with a proposal for rationalization of the manpower in the
Institute.  Some information has been received from the Institute which is being examined.  The Committee is of the
view that the issue of granting autonomous status to CIP, Ranchi has been unduly delayed.  The response of the
concerned Ministries/Departments has also not been quick and encouraging.  The Committee recommends that a
decision may be taken without any further delay.
VIII.     ALL INDIA INSTITUTE OF MEDICAL SCIENCES (AIIMS)
8.1       The All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi is a premier medical institution of the country.  The Plan
allocation for the Institute in BE 1999-2000 was Rs. 81 crores, which was reduced to Rs. 70 crores in RE.  The plan
allocation for 2000-2001 is Rs. 91.30 crores.  Asked about the reasons for reduction in RE, the Department stated that
the EFC memo relating to the setting up of an Organ Bank, procurement of PET Scan, and setting up of Trauma Centre
at AIIMS could not be cleared during 1999-2000 and hence the reduction in RE.
8.2       The Committee is worried to note that despite its earlier recommendation, no progress seems to have been made
in respect of setting up a Trauma Centre.  The workshop on Trauma Centre was held in the Institute on 9-10 July, 1999. 
The Committee, however, finds that recommendations made at the workshop are still in the process of examination. 
The Committee recommends that the Department should make concerted efforts for completion of all the procedural
formalities prior to setting up the Trauma Centre.  It should be ensured that allocation made for this purpose should not
be left under utilised in 2000-2001 like the previous year.
8.3       It came to the notice of the Committee that Dr. R.C. Anand, Medical   Superintendent of the Institute who had
earlier been suspended on corruption charges was reinstated pending inquiry.  The Committee has come to know that
the reinstatement of Dr. Anand has not been approved by the Governing Body of the Institute.  The Committee fails to
understand as to why Dr. Anand was reinstated before the outcome of the inquiry instituted against him.  The
Committee feels that an institute of repute like AIIMS should avoid such controversy.
IX.       JAWAHARLAL INSTITUTE OF POST GRADUATE MEDICAL EDUCATION AND RESEARCH,
PONDICHERRY (JIPMER)
9.1       The plan allocation for Jawaharlal Institute of Post Graduate Medical Education and Research, (JIPMER)

vacant posts could not be filled up due to the following reasons:-
 
a)         lengthy recruitment process in case of group `A’ and `B’ posts; and
b)         candidates selected by the UPSC not joining the posts in Pondicherry.
9.2       The Committee has been informed that against 3003 sanctioned posts as on 31.3.99, 2707 staff members

X.        NATIONAL MALARIA ERADICATION PROGRAMME
10.1     In the case of National Malaria Eradication Programme, the plan provision of Rs.240.00 crores in BE 1999-2000
was reduced to Rs. 195.00 crores at the RE stage.  To a specific query as to why it was reduced  at the RE stage it was
stated that it was due to  economy measures imposed by the Ministry of Finance.  Against this provision, Rs. 178.61
crores are earmarked as grants-in-aid to the States/UTs.  An expenditure of Rs. 100.29 crores has been incurred upto
31.12.99.  The Committee finds that the marginal shortfall in utilisation of the provision for grants-in-aid is attributable
to the decline in the prices of some of the commodities, time-consuming procedure involved in the procurements of the
commodities under the programme etc. An enhanced allocation of Rs.245.00 crores has been proposed for  2000-2001.
10.2     The Committee notes that to protect the population from transmission of malaria, every year target for spraying
of insecticides is worked out in respect of each State. Spray coverage during the year 1997-1998 was around 60% in the
States of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Gujarat, Haryana, Jammu & Kashmir, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura, Uttar
Pradesh, Chnadigarh and Daman & Diu.  168.04 million population were targetted to be covered by residual insecticidal
spray in 1998-99, but only 79.26 million population could be covered.  The Committee is of the view that corrective
measures need to be taken in this regard.
10.3     The Committee has been informed that the major constraints affecting the effective implementation of the
National Malaria Eradication Programme, such as vacancies in key positions, inadequacy of vehicles, frequent transfer
of officials even at the supervisory and grass root level were taken up with the State Govts.  from time to time.  States of
Rajasthan and Maharashtra have initiated action for recruitment of Multi-Purpose Workers/Surveillance Workers. 
Other States are also being advised to remove the constraints. The Committee is of the view that besides a regular
channel of interaction with the State Governments for removal of constraints, the Department should also closely
monitor the implementation of the National Malaria Eradication Programme.
10.4     To a question as to which states are covered by the National Dengu Control Programme,  the Department
informed that  12 States/UTs namely Delhi, Haryana, U.P., Rajasthan, Punjab, Chandigarh, Maharashtra, Karnataka,
Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and West Bengal have been considered to be endemic as reports of more
than 10 cases of Dengue Fever and Dengue Haemorrhagic Fever have been received from these States in the past.  The
Directorate of National Anti Malaria Programme propose to intensify the Dengue Control measures in these endemic
States in the first phase although the Programme would ultimately cover all the States/UTs.
10.5     The Department further informed that the endemic States were requested to convey their formal concurrence for
implementation of the programme on 50.50 cost sharing basis.   However, response have been received only from the
States of Haryana, Rajasthan, U.P. and Chandigarh and the remaining States have been  reminded to expedite their
views/comments.  The Committee recommends that the Department should pursue this matter with the remaining States.

XII.      NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR CONTROL OF BLINDNESS
12.1     The aim of the National Programme for Control of Blindness launched  in 1976 is to reduce the blindness rate
from 1.49% to 0.3% by 2000 A.D. Besides, a World Bank Assisted Cataract Blindness Control Project is being
implemented since April, 1994 in  seven States i.e. Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Orissa, Rajasthan,
Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu,  where prevalence rate is very high.  Physical achievements have been – Cataract
Surgery – 100%, training – 80%, OTs – 70%, IOL – 133%.
12.2     The Committee is  constrained to note that in the States of Goa, J&K, M.P., Nagaland, Orissa, Rajasthan and
Chandigarh, the prevalence rate of blindness is much more than the national average.  The Committee, therefore, feels
that special efforts should be made for the above mentioned States.
12.3     The Committee notes that  Epidemiological Blindness Survey has been conducted on a pilot basis  in the two
districts i.e. Bharatpur in Rajasthan and Sivaganga in Tamil Nadu in 1998-99 to assess the prevalence of blindness. 
Prevalence of blindness in illiterates is as high as 14.90% in Bharatpur District of Rajasthan.  Major cause of blindness
is cataract with 57.3% and 53.4% rate of prevalence in the two districts. The Committee feels that given the vastness of
the country such surveys should be conducted in more districts.
12.4     The Committee is constrained to note that  under the National Trachoma and Blindness Control Programme as
against a provision of Rs. 700 lakhs as grant-in-aid to State Governments during 1997-98, actual expenditure amounted
to only Rs. 110.48 lakhs due to the poor performance of the States in developing the service units.  During 1998-99 
also, the position was somewhat similar. States of MP, Orissa, UP, Assam, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana, HP,  J&K,
Karnataka, Kerala, Meghalaya, Punjab were having unspent  balances lying with them.  The main reason for unspent
balances is the reluctance on the part of the State Govts. to create the posts due to the liability at the end of the plan
period.  Situation is more worrying in the case of Orissa as funds  were not released by the Finance Department of the
State Govt. to the executing Agencies.  The Committee recommends that the implementation of the Programme in the
States should be closely monitored  and in the event of any problem, the matter should be taken up with the concerned
State Government.

beneficiaries who take treatment in Government hospitals like G.B.  Pant Hospital, AIIMS , Dr. RML Hospital,
Safdarjang Hospital and Lady Hardinge Medical College and Hospital are reimbursed full medical expenditure, except
the inadmissible items like TV Charges, telephone charges and diet charges, etc.

Government hospitals/CGHS dispensaries, the Department of Consumer Affairs has intimated as under:-
“…… The intention of the Health Ministry is to provide an alternative forum for redressal of grievances of
patients who receive  free treatment at Government hospitals.   As you are aware, Civil Courts are already for
seeking redressal of such grievances but this procedure entails delay.   We are of the view that instead of the
Consumer Protection Act, it would be better that the Ministry of Health, being the administrative Ministry

having control over Government hospitals, may consider establishing an effective alternate redressal machinery
perhaps at individual hospitals so that grievances/genuine problem of patients are sorted out at that level without

Accordingly, instructions have been given to the Additional Directors/Joint Directors/Deputy Directors of CGHS to the
