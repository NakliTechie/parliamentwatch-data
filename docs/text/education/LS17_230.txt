III.  INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME  
Special intervention for construction of AWC building  
Recommendations/Observations  
3.5.3 Short working season, non-availability of con struction materials at site, difficult terrain and carriage by head loads in 
remote locations, low capacity of village-based con tractor are the main factors cited as responsible f or this state of affairs. The 
Committee while appreciating the region-specific co nstraints of North - East States would like to poin t out that urgent steps 
need to be taken by the Centre in co-ordination wit h the State Governments so as to resolve/lessen the se problem areas. The 
Committee also strongly feels that local community along with PRIs may be motivated like the Young Miz o Association in 
Mizoram for taking part in this exercise. The Commi ttee also recommends that this over-delayed constru ction work of AWC 
buildings may be got completed in a time-bound manner.  
Action Taken  
  The matter has been taken up with the NER states for completion of all the AWC buildings sanctioned till 2009-10 in the 
State Secretaries Review Meeting held on 28th October, 2010. It was also clarified to the NE states th at further funds would be 
released only after completion certificates are rec eived. This has also been followed up through a D.O . letter dated 2nd 
November, 2010 from the Ministry to all the Secreta ries of NE States urging them to furnish pending DC  and progress of 
construction of AWCs. 
Recommendations/Observations  
3.5.4  The Committee has been emphasizing on infras tructure development of AWCs with special reference  to construction of 
AWC building in the past. Majority of AWCs do not h ave their own building. The Committee was informed that the Planning 
Commission in its Review Meeting of ICDS held on 13th and 20th November, 2009 had suggested that appropriate investments 
in infrastructure at the AWC level needed to be explored. The Ministry had also requested the Thirteenth Finance Commission’ 
to provide for the construction of Anganwadi Centres in the State allocation and a decision was awaited. While appreciating the 
efforts of the Ministry, the Committee would like t o draw its attention to the fact that the Report of  the Thirteenth Finance 
Commission had already been presented to the Parlia ment. If the proposal has not been recommended by t he Commission, the 
Committee strongly feels that the Ministry needs to  approach the Planning Commission so as to ensure t hat this proposal is 
substantially included in the approach to the Twelfth Five Year Plan. 
Action Taken  
  The 13th Finance Commission has recommended const ruction of AWCs with an estimated cost of Rs. 1982 Crore in six 
states which demanded for it, namely Chhattisgarh ( Rs.l50 Crore), Jharkhand (Rs.432 Crore), Madhya Pradesh (Rs.400 Crore), 
Maharashtra (Rs.300 Crore), Orissa (Rs.400 Crore) and West Bengal (Rs.300 Crore). 
  Construction of AWC building as an activity was p roposed in the XI Five Year Plan, but could not be approved by GOI. 
However, in the Mid-Term Appraisal for the XI Five Year Plan, Planning Commission has recommended adeq uate 
infrastructure for AWCs. The issue of construction will be pursued in XII Five Year Plan. 
VI.  INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME 
Recommendations/Observations  
6.6  Committee’s attention has been drawn by the Mo del Rules framed under the Juvenile Justice (Care a nd Protection of 
Children) Act, 2000 which provide for constitution of Inspection Committees by the State Governments /  UTs at State, District 

or City Level. These Committees are required to vis it and oversee the conditions of the institutions/h omes and appropriateness 
of the processes for safety, well being, review the standards of care and protection, look into the functioning of the Management 
Committee and Children’s Committee and give suggest ions for improvement. The Committee finds that the Inspection 
Committees are being set up by the respective State  Governments under ICPS. The Committee apprehends t hat so far this very 
important monitoring mechanism envisaged under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2000 was not 
there in position or not effective, if existing in majority of the States. The Committee strongly feel s that in the absence of 
constant and rigorous monitoring, the very objectiv e. If all such homes, i.e. , protection of vulnerable children would remain on  
paper inspite of lofty schemes and substantive fund ing. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Centre has to play a 
pro-active role in getting the real sense. The Committee would appreciate if publicity to services provided by these Child Lines 
is given in the cities from where these are operating on a regular basis through both print and electronic media. 
Action Taken  
  To strengthen the monitoring mechanisms of the St ate Governments further, provision has been made un der the 
Integrated Child Protection Scheme for supervision and monitoring of all child care organizations/ institutions/ agencies, at both 
State and District levels. The Programme Manager (Child Protection) of the State Child Protection Society at the State level and 
the Protection Officer (Institutional Care) under D istrict Child Protection Society, at the District l evel, have been given the 
responsibilty to ensure supervision and monitoring all the institutions/agencies housing children in t he State and ensure 
implementation of minimum standards of care. 
  Publicity to the Child line services is being pro vided both through the electronic and print media from time to time. Child 
line India Foundation, Mumbai is also undertaking p ublicity and awareness campaigns in the cities wher e Child line service is 
available. 
VII.  SCHEME OF HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN 
Recommendations/Observations  
7.7  The Committee would like to draw the attention  of the Ministry towards areas where there are nume rous industries 
forming an industrial hub but there are no provisio n of hostels for working women due to which women d o not opt to work in 
such areas which offer ample employment opportunities to them. Further, only 887 hostels sanctioned do  not cater to the needs 
of all the working women across the country. Therefore, the Committee recommends that additional hostels for working women 
should be sanctioned especially in areas left uncovered so that more and more number of women are benefited from the scheme. 
Action Taken  
  Recommendation of Hon’ble Committee has been note d for compliance.

XI.   CONCLUSION 
Recommendations/Observations  

15.1  The Committee while reviewing the allocation, utilization trends and performance of various schemes administered by the 
Ministry, finds that utilization of allocated funds  has been more or less satisfactory. The Ministry, however, needs to monitor 
the implementation of schemes as many schemes could not accomplish their set targets, with special focus on the implementing 
agencies like the NGOs. The target groups of the sc hemes of the Ministry are women and children and th ey are the most 
vulnerable section of our society and they need to be addressed with utmost care and sensitivity. The Ministry needs to lay 
emphasis on the timely implementation of its new sc hemes and the revamping of the schemes under proces s. The Committee 
believes that concerted efforts of the Ministry wou ld certainly lead to the evolution of an efficient delivery mechanism with 
minimum hindrances. 
Action Taken  
  The recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee have  been noted for compliance.

III.  INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME  
Recommendations/Observations  
Funding for ICDS  
3.2.1 Allocation for ICDS for the year 2010-11 is R s.8700.00 crore representing a substantial increase when compared with BE 
allocation of Rs.6705.00 crore and a marginal incre ase against RE provision of Rs. 8162.00 crore. The Committee notes that 
allocation during the Eleventh Plan period has been  showing an upward trend. Provision of Rs.5204.00 c rore in 2007-08, the 
first year of the Plan has reached to Rs.8700.00 cr ore in 2010-11, fourth year of plan. However, utili zation status has failed to 
match the increasing allocation, During 2009-10. Plan allocation was enhanced from Rs.7350.00 crore to Rs.8500.00 crore. This 
increase was required on account of upward revision  of cost and nutrition norms. However, the proposal s for release of grants 
under ICDS were processed only after obtaining appr oval for supplementary grants of Rs.1200.00 crore i n January, 2010. In 
addition, savings from Non-ICDS schemes (about Rs.250.00 crore) were also re--appropriated to ICDS because of its additional 
requirement. The Committee was given to understand that due to these factors, expenditure by the end o f January, 2010 was 
only Rs.5969.90 crore. However, pace of utilization  picked up thereafter reaching to Rs.6826.04 crore by 28th February, 2010 
and more than 99 per cent by the end of the year.  
Action Taken  
  The release for ICDS is made in 4 or more install ments as per extant instruction of Ministry of Fina nce. Therefore, the 
release is distributed over the year in nearly equal proportion. There was an underprovisioning in the beginning of the year. This 
was made good by reappropriation and through Supple mentary Grants which became available for release i n the month of 
January, 2010. This resulted in more releases in la st quarter of 2009-10 under ICDS. Had these funds b een available as B.E., it 
would have been released evenly through the year. 
Recommendations/Observations  
3.2.2 Trend of utilization during 2009-10 clearly i ndicates that substantive quantum of allocated funds could be released during 
the last quarter of the year. The Committee notes t hat cost norms for Honoraria of Anganwadi Worker an d Helper and 
Supplementary Nutrition Provision were revised w.e.f.  1st April, 2008 and 7th November, 2009 respectivel y. Nutritional and 
Feeding norms for Supplementary Nutrition were revi sed from 24th February, 2009. The Committee is of t he opinion that gap 
between revision of norms and release of enhanced f unds should be the minimum. The approval process fo r the revision of 
norms and making available the required funds shoul d not be a protracted exercise. All efforts should also be made to avoid

Action Taken  
  As per the instructions of Ministry of Finance, g rants under ICDS are released in four or more insta llments in a year. The 
first two installments are released on indicative b asis (based on norms, number of beneficiaries, oper ational projects and 
operational AWCs). Subsequent installments are rele ased on the basis of Statement of Expenditure as on  30th June and 30th 
September. Any unspent balance or excess expenditur e reported as on 31st March in a particular year is  adjusted in the 
following financial year.  

  The indication of balance on 31st December is par t of releases & expenditure made upto 3rd quarter a nd part of a 
continuous process. It is therefore not considered as ‘unspent balance’ for the purpose of release of funds during the financial 
year. The actual position of excess expenditure/uns pent balance as on 31.3.2010 (Annex 1)  will be taken into account and 
adjusted while releasing grants in 3rd installment of current year.

three installments. However, as on 31.3.2010 there was an unspent balance of Rs.47.18 crore only under  ICDS(General). This 
has been adjusted from the amount due for releasing 3rd installment of funds to Uttar Pradesh in the year 2010-11. 
  Similarly in the case of Gujarat, the State made an excess expenditure 24.62 crores as on 31.3.2010 which was adjusted 
while releasing 3rd installment to Gujarat in the year 2010-11. 
  The balances with State Governments (Plus or Minu s) are therefore, not taken up with State Governments on the basis of 
availability of funds with them as on 31.12.2009. H owever, the States are exhorted to submit Statement  of Expenditure timely 
for the purpose of financial progress.

Child Welfare Committees have been set up in 465 districts, Juvenile Justice Boards in 445 districts and Special Juvenile 
Police units in 478 districts. While appreciating t he increasing number of State Governments signing t he MOU for the 
scheme, the Committee believes that an uphill task is yet to be completed. The State Governments need to put in place 
the child protection network like Juvenile Justice Boards, Child Welfare Committee, Special Juvenile P olice Units etc 
for effective implementation of the scheme. The Com mittee is happy to note that the Ministry is extend ing its 
cooperation to the States in framing financial prop osals and also assessing their available resources and actual 
requirements. The Committee recommends that setting up of other structures such as the Societies at St ate and district 
levels, State Adoption Resource Agencies etc in the  States should be expedited so that these agencies are ready for 
implementation of the scheme. An inbuilt effective monitoring mechanism should also be evolved so that  the scheme 
remains fully functional in the real sense, caterin g to the needs of targeted beneficiaries. The Commi ttee would also like 
to be apprised about the expenditure details of Rs. 300 crore allocated for the scheme in 2010-11.     (Paras 6.3 & 6.4)  
  As per the information received from the Ministry , total number of Homes supported under the Integra ted Child 
Protection Scheme in 2010-11 (upto 31st October, 20 10) is 191 and the number of beneficiaries is 10, 3 77. Proposals for 
Homes for the year 2010-11 have not been received f rom Chhattisgarh, Nagaland and Rajasthan and propos als for 
States like Andhra Pradesh, Haryana, Karnataka, Ker ala, Manipur are under consideration. The Committee  finds the 
number of Homes supported and the number of benefic iaries to be too less thereby indicating the limite d outreach of 
these Homes. The Committee is of view that preparat ory exercises like the assessment of requirement of  such Homes in 
the States should have been completed beforehand so  as to present comprehensive proposals for their ma intenance, up 
gradation and construction. The Committee is also of the opinion that since such Homes cater to the needs of vulnerable 
children in need of protection and care, a monitori ng mechanism should be devised for their smooth and  proper 
functioning.    (Para 6.5)  
VII.   SCHEME OF HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN 
  The Committee has been monitoring the implementat ion of the Scheme of Hostels for Working Women for the last 
many years. The Scheme has never been implemented a s envisaged due to inherent problem areas and opera tional 
difficulties. Unfortunately, the process of revisio n of scheme has been unduly long with almost an ent ire plan period 
being spent therefor. As per the latest feedback ma de available to the Committee, the last ambiguity i n the revised 
scheme, i.e.  definition of the term ‘Public Land’ has been clar ified and the revised scheme is being notified. The  
Committee hopes that by the end of 2010-11, it woul d be notified so that it may become operational fro m the beginning 
of 2011-12 itself.     (Paras 7.5 & 7.6)  
VIII.   CARA 
  The Committee would like to emphasize that the pr ocess for having an enabling legislation for the Ha gue 
Convention on Inter-country Adoptions should be expedited so as to ensure removal of all procedural delays being faced 
in the adoption of children. (Para 8.4)  
IX.  RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK) 
  A very critical component of the Scheme i.e  performance of NGOs implementing the scheme was hi ghlighted by 
the Committee with special reference to 15 NGOs bla cklisted under RMK during 2008-09 and 79 NGOs black listed 
during 2009-10. The Committee had recommended that a viable mechanism of inspections at periodic inter vals 

involving both State authorities and officers from the Ministry needed to be evolved with stipulated t ime frame for 
completing the proceedings against the ensuing NGOs . The Committee is, however, disappointed to find n o comments 
from the Ministry in this regard. The Committee onl y wants to emphasize upon the principle of financia l accountability 
of the NGOs involved in the implementation of the Scheme.  (Para 9.6)  
X.   CONDENSED COURSES OF EDUCATION FOR WOMEN 
  The Committee notes that out of 1763 courses sanc tioned during 2007-08, 2008-09 and 2009-10, the num ber of 
non-performing NGOs is 122. These NGOs were given s how cause notices and their grants cancelled and ac tion to 
obtain the refund of grant was initiated. The Commi ttee finds that out of 33 States, only 19 States ha d furnished 
information about the blacklisted NGOs in the year 2007-08. Amongst the 19 States, Maharashtra had the  maximum 
number of blacklisted NGOs i.e  16. With information still awaited from many State s, the overall assessment cannot be 
made. The Committee is of the view that concerted e fforts are required to be made for getting the comp lete details. 
More show cause notices would not serve the purpose  as stern action should be taken against the erring  NGOs. They 
should be made financially accountable and refund the grant released to them without any further delay. As regards the 
placement position of States under the Scheme, only  ten States have furnished the data in this regard.  Of these ten 
States, the placement position of candidates could not be ascertained as results have not been receive d from the 
NGO/State Board or are still awaited. The Committee is not inclined to agree with the Ministry’s contention that almost 
all the candidates are placed in one way or the oth er way. The Committee would like to point out that the placement 
position of candidates is the real indicator of the  success of the scheme. Given the small number of b eneficiaries in the 
ten States, the Committee feels that a lot has to be done for translating the scheme into a successful venture.  
       (Para 10.3)  
XI.   SCHEME FOR RELIEF TO AND REHABILITATION OF RAPE V ICTIMS 
  The Committee has been given to understand that t he Scheme for Relief to and Rehabilitation of Rape Victims has 
been renamed and revised as a Centrally Sponsored S cheme and the Scheme has now been finalized and app roved by 
the EFC on19.9.2010. The Scheme, inter-alia , provides for the setting up of District Criminal Inquiries Relief and 
Rehabilitation Board which would consider claims fo r assistance under the scheme. Given the fact that the scheme is a 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme, this Board would have t o be constituted in each district of the country wh ich is an uphill 
task in itself. The Committee would like the Minist ry to play a pro-active role in ensuring the consti tution of this Board 
in all the districts of the country. Further, care should be taken to see that the composition of the Board includes ample 
representation of women keeping in view the fad tha t the Board has to deal with vulnerable women who w ould be rape 
victims and need to be rehabilitated suitably. The Committee believes that given the nature of crime a gainst these 
victims, utmost sensitivity is required to handle their cases. (Paras 11.1 to 11.3)  
XII.  NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN (NCW) 
  The Committee takes note of the problems encounte red by the NCW in disposal of complaints registered  at the 
agency. Given the increase in the number of complai nts filed, a restructuring proposal for examining t he staffing 
requirement of NCW as suggested by the Ministry is a step in the right direction. The Committee feels that NCW plays 
an important role in redressal of grievances of wom en suffering various kinds of atrocities and theref ore it needs to 
widen its scope and structure so as to strengthen i t for efficient disposal of complaints. There shoul d be an increase in 
the number of staff including the counsellors who have to deal with ever increasing complaints due to introduction of the 
system of online filing of complaints. The Committee is of the view that adequate allocation of funds along with increase 
in the number of staff should be taken up by the Ministry on a priority basis.  (Para 12.1 & 12.2)  
XIV.  COMPREHENSIVE SCHEME FOR COMBATING TRAFFICKING (UJJAWALA) 
 The Committee has been informed that from April to  October, 2010, three meetings of Project Sanctioni ng 
Committee were held in which 99 projects were consi dered and of these, only 18 proposals were approved  after careful 
examination for implementation of Comprehensive Sch eme for Combating Trafficking 73 Rehabilitation Hom es were 
also sanctioned under the scheme upto 30 October, 2 010 where facilities such as food, clothing, medica l care, legal aid, 
education and vocational training are provided to t he affected women and children. The Committee obser ves that only 

18 out of 99 proposals were approved for the implementation of the scheme. This means that the State Governments are 
not clear about the scope and objectives of the sch eme and therefore are not coming up with viable pro posals. The 
Committee feels that the Ministry should work in ta ndem with the State Governments helping them in mak ing suitable 
proposals for the scheme. Selection of NGOs should be done very carefully so as to avoid non-performin g or poor-
performing ones as learnt from the experiences of t he implementation of the other schemes of the Minis try. Efforts 
should also be made to operationalise the sanctione d Homes as early as possible for the benefit of the  affected women 
and children. Further, proper monitoring along with  periodic inspections of the Rehabilitative Homes s anctioned so far 
should be carried out.         (Paras 14.1 to 14.4)
