REPORT 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development (MWCD) is the nodal 
Ministry for all matters pertaining to development of women and children who as 
per the 2001 census constitute 71.44 per cent of the country’s population.   The 
Ministry of Women and Child Development, a Department under the Ministry of 
HRD earlier was elevated as a separate Ministry w.e.f. 30
th January 2006.  On 
acquiring the status of a Ministry, the role and responsibility of the Ministry has 
increased manifold.   
1.2 The vision of the Ministry is to have empowered women living with 
dignity and contributing as equal partners towards the development of the country 
in an environment free from violence and to provide all children with full 
opportunities for their growth and deve lopment in an environment free from 
exploitation.  All the schemes/programmes and institutional set-ups are envisaged 
to  promote social and economic empowerment of women, mainstream 
gender concerns, create awareness about th eir rights and fac ilitate institutional 
and legislative support for enabling them to develop to their full potential.  
Similarly, child specific schemes/programmes seek  to ensure holistic 
development and protection of children, spread awareness about their rights, 
facilitate access to learning and supplement ary nutrition for targeted groups and 
institutional and legislative support for enabling them to grow and develop to their 
full potential. 
 

1.3  The Secretary of the Ministry in his presentation before the Committee gave an 
overview of the various schemes handled by the Ministry, achievements made during 
2009-10 along with the problem-areas and the tasks ahead.  Committee’s  attention was 
drawn to the fact that the Ministry besides being the nodal Ministry for women and child 
welfare has the dual responsibility of ensuring that all the programmes run by other 
Ministries also reflect the concern of  women and children.

III. INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME 
3.1 ICDS is the most important flagship scheme of the Ministry which seeks to 
provide a package of services comprising of supplementary  nutrition, immunization, 
health checkup and referral services, pre-school non-formal education for children upto 
six years of age, pregnant and lactating mothers.  In the recent past, the Ministry has 
undertaken the task of universalization of the Scheme by increasing the number of AWCs 
to 14 lakh with a provision of 20,000 AWCs on demand with special focus on coverage 
of SC/ST and minority population.  Cost sharing has been introduced between Centre and 
State, from 2009-10 in the ratio 90:10 for all components including SNP for North-East 
and 50:50 for SNP and 90:10 for all other components for all States other than North-
East.  Enhancement of honorarium for AWWs/Helpers, provision of Flexi funds at 
Anganwadi level, revard mechanism for ICDS functionaries are some of the major 
interventions made under ICDS.  The Committee appreciates the efforts of the 
Ministry in making the delivery mechanism more effective by taking such 
interventions.  The Committee hopes that all these initiatives of the Ministry would 

have a positive impact on the operationalization and effective functioning of AWCs 
in all the States/UTs, specially those not performing well. 
FUNDING FOR ICDS 
3.2.1 Allocation for ICDS for the year 2010-11 is Rs. 8700.00 crore representing a 
substantial increase when compared with BE allocation of Rs. 6705.00 crore and a 
marginal increase against RE provision of Rs. 8162.00 crore.  The Committee notes that 
allocation during the Eleventh Plan period has been showing an upward trend.  Provision 
of Rs. 5204.00 crore in 2007-08, the first year of the Plan has reached to Rs. 8700.00 
crore in 2010-11, fourth year of the plan.  However, utilization status has failed to match 
the increasing allocation. During 2009-10, plan allocation was enhanced from Rs. 
7350.00 crore to Rs. 8500.00 crore.  This increase was required on account of upward 
revision of cost and nutrition norms.  Howeve r, the proposals for release of grants under 
ICDS were processed only after obtaining approval for supplementary grants of Rs. 
1200.00 crore in January, 2010.  In addition, savings from Non-ICDS schemes (about Rs. 
250.00 crore) were also re-appropriated to ICDS because of its additional requirement.  
The Committee was given to understand that due to these factors, expenditure by the end 
of January, 2010 was only Rs. 5969.90 crore.   However, pace of utilization picked up 
thereafter reaching to Rs. 6826.04 crore by 28
th February, 2010 and more than 99 per cent 
by the end of the year. 
3.2.2 Trend of utilization du ring 2009-10 clearly indicates that substantive quantum 
of allocated funds could be released during the last quarter of the year.  The Committee 
notes that cost norms for Honoraria of Anganwadi Worker and Helper and 
Supplementary Nutrition Provision were revised w.e.f. 1
st April, 2008 and 7 th November, 

2009 respectively.  Nutritional and Feeding norms for Supplementary Nutrition were 
revised from 24th February, 2009.  The Committee is of the opinion that gap between 
revision of norms and release of enhanced funds should be the minimum.  The 
approval process for the revision of norm s and making available the required funds 
should not be a protracted exercise.  Al l efforts should also be made to avoid 
substantive utilization of allocated funds during the last quarter of the year.   
 
3.2.3 The Committee was also give n to understand that allocation of Rs. 8700.00 
crore under ICDS for 2010-11 falls short of  projected requirement of Rs. 11,227.00 
crore.  The Committee is of the opinion that the required funds need be made 
available  for this Flagship scheme of the Ministry.  At the same time it has to be 
ensured both by the Ministry and the implem enting agencies in the States that there 
is optimum but judicious utilization of the allocated funds. 
 
3.2.4 From 2009-10, cost sharing between Centre and States has been introduced in 
the ratio of 90 : 10 for all components inducing SNP for North East and 50 : 50 for SNP 
and  90 : 10 for all other components for all States other than North-East.  On a specific 
query about States’ acceptance of the revised norms, the Committee was informed that 
State Government of Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir have requested that 
they may be treated at par with North-East States for 90 :10 considering their topographic 
conditions.  The Committee strongly feels that the requests of the two States need to 
be looked into in the proper perspective and the needful done at the earliest. 
  
 

 UNIVERSALIZATION OF ICDS 
3.3.1 The ICDS scheme has been expanded in three phases in 2005-06, 2006-07 and 
2008-09.  During the third phase of expansion, 792 additional projects and 2,13,859 
AWCs and 77,102 Mini AWCs and provision for 20,000 Anganwadi on Demand have 
been approved in October, 2008. With this approval, cumulative total of approved 
projects and Anganwadi Centres increased to 7076 and 14 lakh, thus fulfilling the 
directions of Supreme Court for universalisation of ICDS.   Committee’s attention  has 
been drawn to a somewhat disturbing development thereafter. Committee was given to 
understand that some States reduced their requirements for AWCs/Mini AWCs 
subsequent to approval by the Government.  Eventually the cumulative total of 
sanctioned AWCs/Mini AWCs as on 31
st March, 2009 is 13.56 lakh.  The remaining 
44,000 AWCs/Mini-AWCs, including 20,000 Anganwadis on Demand are yet to be 
sanctioned.  Not only this, requests have come from some States for further reduction. 
 
3.3.2 The Committee fails to comprehend the basis for this reversal in the 
requirement for AWCs in respect of some  States.  Not only this, Supreme Court’s 
directions for universalisat ion of ICDS thereby opening of 14 lakh AWCs were 
based on an extensive survey as per th e prescribed population norms along with 
State-specific requirements.  In such a scenario, request of some States for reduction 
not only goes against the Apex Court directions but also seems to negate the very 
objective of ICDS.   
 
3.3.3 The Committee was informed  by the Secretary that Chattisgarh  and 
Uttrakhand were the two States which had asked for substantive reduction.   The 

Committee is happy to note that the request  of these two States has not been agreed 
to so far.  The Committee feels that revi sion in the cost norms may perhaps be one 
reason for this development.  The Committ ee recommends that the Ministry should 
pursue this issue with the concerned States vigorously so that no habitation in these 
two States remains bereft of positive gains of ICDS.

raised by some of the members.  It was pointed out that AWCs at present have been 
opened at Panchayat level only in States like Madhya Pradesh.  Normally, jurisdiction of 
a Panchayat extends upto a number of villages.  As a result, pregnant women and small 
children, finding it difficult to cover the distance between the AWC at Panchayat and 
villages covered by it remain deprived of Supplementary Nutrition.  Similarly, attention 
of the Ministry was also drawn to  women workforce working in tea gardens and their 
children not getting the benefit of ICDS.  Population norms of 800 for one AWCs or 
minimum requirement of habitations having 40 children were not helping the labour 
population, e.g. those employed for sugarcane harvesting or very small habitations of 
labourers and uneducated class having less than 40 children.  The Committee strongly 
feels that all the problem areas pointed ou t by the Members, based on their first 
hand experience of ground realities need to be looked into immediately.  The 
Committee would appreciate if  a survey of these areas as well as other similar areas 
is be taken up by the Minist ry in co-ordination with th e concerned States.  Present 

norm of 40 children for Anganwadies on De mand may also be reviewed, specially in 
case of specific demands for relaxation of the same.   
 
PRE-SCHOOL EDUCATION KITS    
 
3.4.1 Pre-School Education is a critical co mponent under the ICDS Scheme to ensure 
the holistic development of children.  Pre-School Education Kits are procured and 
supplied by States/UTs as per norms.  To strengthen this component of ICDS, cost norms 
for these kits have been revised from Rs.500 to Rs.1000 per AWC per annum from  
2009-10.  The Committee is, however, surprised to note that State-wise status of 
procurement of Pre-School Kits for AWCs in 2008-09 and 2009-10 reveals that large 
number of States/UTs i.e. Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu 
and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Kerala, Maharashtra, Chandigarh, Delhi, Puducherry, 
Meghalaya, Mizoram and Sikkim have so far failed to provide this vital component to the 
children in AWCs in their States/UTs.  What is  more disturbing is that in States/UTs like 
Chattisgarh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Chandigarh, Dadra and 
Nagar Haveli, Delhi, Arunachal Pradesh and Tripura, procurement process for the Kits is 
continuing for long. In Maharashtra, Kits  for 2009-10 as well as 2008-09 are being 
procured.  In respect of the remaining States, number of procured kits for 2009-10 falls 
short of the requirement or no response has been forthcoming so far. 
 
3.4.2 The Committee is very disappointed with th is state of affairs.  It seems that 
States are not interested in procuring the Pre-school Education Kits for their 
Children.  The Committee would like to point out that these kits are a replacement 
for pre-parental education in nursery school .  They can be considered an important 

tool for enhancing literacy at  the end of class V.  World- wide, it is an established 
fact that children having a certain minimum vocabulary before entering school have 
a higher chance of achieving literacy at the end of class V.   The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that concerted efforts need to be made so as to convince the 
States to include this very crucial component financial assistance for which is 
provided by the Centre.

VI. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME 
6.1 The Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS), a Centrally Sponsored Scheme, 
has been launched during the year 2009-10 by merging three existing schemes i.e. 
Programme for Juvenile Justice, Integrated Programme for Street Children and Shishu 
Greh and has also incorporated new interventions. 
 
6.2 The Scheme seeks to improve access to  and quality of child protection services 
(institutional services through shelter ho mes, children’s homes etc.) create public 
awareness about child rights, clearly ar ticulating responsibilities and enforcing 
accountability for child protection, provide  statutory and support service to children in 
difficult circumstances at all government levels like shelters for children, emergency 
outreach services and set up an evidence based monitoring and evaluation system.  The 
Scheme is being implemented mainly through the State Governments/UT 
Administrations, Child Line India Foundation, NIPCCD and CARA throughout the 
country.  So far, only 17 States namely , Andhra Pradesh, Assa m, Chhattisgarh, Goa, 
Gujarat, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur,  Nagaland, Orissa, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, 
Tripura and West Bengal etc. have signed the MOU with the Ministry.  The State 
Government of Bihar has also agreed to sign the MOU under the scheme. 
 
6.3 BE 2009-10 of Rs. 60.00 crore for the Scheme was reduced to Rs.50.00 crore at 
the RE stage. This reduction was because most of the  States signed the MOU in the 
second half of the financial year 2009-10 and submitted their proposal thereafter. As a 
result, only Rs.3.00 crore could be released by the Ministry by the end of January, 2010. 
As per the latest information furnished by the Ministry, central share of Grant-in-aid of 

Rs.33.31 crore to 14 States for implementation of the Scheme and Rs.9.33 crore to the 
Childline India Foundation, Mumbai for running the Childline service in the country has 
been released.

crore has been allocated for the Scheme in 2010-11 mainly for  the setting up of Juvenile 
Justice Board and Child Welfare  Committee, Special Juvenile Police  Units, Child   
Protection Societies  and Open  Shelters, Child Line Service in 163 cities in the country  
for 300 districts of 25 States/UTs.  The Committee appreciates the lofty targets set for 
the implementation of the Scheme.  Howeve r, getting the remaining States/UTs to 
sign the MOU specially due to the Scheme being on a cost sharing basis and setting 
up of Juvenile Justice Board, Child Welf are Committees, Special Juvenile Police 
Units, Child Protection Services in 300 distri cts of at least 25 States would not be an 
easy task.  Besides that, implementation of  the Scheme in the 17 States which have

formal approval of the scheme from competent authority will also have to undergo a 
number of stages which again is a protracted exercise.  The Committee, therefore, 
would like to impress upon the Ministry  to make all conceivable efforts for

XIII. NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN (NCW)  
12.1 The National Commission for Women (NCW) set up in 1992 is a statutory apex 
national level body under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990.  NCW 
reviews the provisions relating to the safeguards for women under the Constitution as 
well as in other laws.  It takes up petitions from women for redressal of their grievances, 
and does promotional research for the advancement of women, takes suo moto notice of 
matters relating to deprivation of women’s rights etc.  and takes up the issues with 
appropriate authorities. 
 
12.2 The Committee is aware that the National Commission for Women is the 
only  national level authority having the mandate of protecting the rights of women 
across the country.   The most crucial task assigned to the Commission is to 
investigate specific problems or situations arising out of discrimination/atrocities 
against women.  However, the Commit tee feels that the Commission has not 
succeeded fully in carrying out this mandate.  This is borne out by the status of 

complaints received by the Commission.  As per the information made available to 
the Committee, out of 15,854 complaints  registered with the Commission during 
2007-08 and 12,895 complaints during 2008-09, 1781 and 1166 complaints are 
pending respectively.   The Committee finds  the details of complaints on which 
Action Taken Reports from concerned authorities are still pending as more 
disturbing.  Out of 8640 and 7509 complaints registered in 2007-08 and 2008-09 
respectively, ATRs, are pending on 5610 and 4679 complaints respectively.  This 
clearly shows that the matter has not pr oceeded beyond the complaint registered 
with the Commission and sent to the conc erned authorities for necessary action.  
Therefore, it would not be wrong to conclude that no relief whatsoever has been 
provided to the victim in such a case.  The very fact that a very large number of 
complaints from 2007-08 and 2008-09 conti nue to remain pending defeats the very 
purpose of mandate of safeguarding the ri ghts and interests of women folk of the 
country, with which the Commission has been entrusted.  The Committee can well 
imagine the fate of 13,696 complaints registered with NCW (as on 31.01.2010) 
during 2009-10.   The Committee strongly feels that the National Commission for 
Women needs to be empowered so  as to be in a position to take conclusive action in 
cases relating to redressal of grievances received from victims.    
 
12.3 Another very important mandate of NCW is to review the provisions relating to 
safeguards for women under the Constitution as well as in other laws, and recommend 
suitable amendments as and when required.  Committee’s attention has been drawn to a 
seminar on the Immoral Traffic Prevention Act, 1956 conducted by the National Law 
School.  It was suggested that a Central Anti-Trafficking Nodal Authority on the lines of 

Narcotics Control Bureau needs to be set up.  Nobody can deny the fact that the problem 
of exploitation of young girls specially from remote and tribal areas has become very 
serious and needs to be tackled effectively.  The Committee therefore, strongly feels 
that the National Commission for Women has to play a proactive role in such 
matters.     
 
XIII. MERGER OF SCHEME OF SWADHAR AND SHORT STAY HOME 
13.1 The Scheme of Swadhar was introduced in 2001-02 with the objective of 
addressing the requirements of women in difficult circumstances.  It is a shelter-based 
scheme for providing relief and rehabilitatio n to women in difficult circumstances.  It 
seeks to rehabilitate widows, victims of tr afficking, victims of natural calamities, 
mentally challenged and destitute women.  The scheme provides for support like food, 
skill development, legal support, medical faciliti es helpline service etc.  At present, there 
are 317 Homes and 237 help-lines are functional under the Scheme. 
 
13.2 The Scheme of Short Stay Homes seeks to protect and rehabilitate women and 
girls who are facing social ostracism, exploitation or other causes.  The Scheme provides 
services like medical care, skill developm ent training, education, vocational and 
rehabilitation services and re-adjustments in  family/society.  The Ministry has also 
anchored help-line services for women in distress in some Short Stay Homes.  There are 
414 Homes and 30 new Homes are anticipated for sanction during 2010-11 
 
13.3 The Committee has been informed that these two schemes would be merged.  The 
Ministry proposes to launch a new scheme to replace the existing two schemes during the 
year 2010-11.  The merged scheme is expected to provide a network of shelter homes all 

over the country to take care of the needs of one of the most marginalized and vulnerable 
sections of the society i.e. women without family or societal support and independent 
means of income.  The scheme is still unde r formulation and the Ministry is making 
efforts to provide enhanced financial norms for every component, emphasizing on 
generation of livelihood options through vocational training, simplify application 
procedures and putting in place a stricter monitoring regime. During the transition of 
switching from the two schemes to one scheme, each home under the existing schemes 
shall be reviewed with respect to its performance, need assessment  of the number of 
homes in an area vis-à-vis the existing number, ability and willingness of the 
implementing organizations to comply with the revised criteria and other relevant factors. 
 
13.4 The Committee is of the view that the merger of these two schemes with 
almost similar objectives is a step in the right direction.  However, many aspects like 
financial norms, new compon ents, monitoring mechanism etc. would need to be 
taken into consideration for the implem entation of the me rged scheme The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry evalua tes all  aspects of both the schemes 
carefully before its implementation.  The Committee is doubtful about the 
implementation of the merged scheme in the year 2010-11.  The Committee also 
wonders by when the process of signing of  MOUs  with State Governments will be 
undertaken.  The Committee can only hope th at the new scheme  will be finalized at 
the earliest and all formalities will be  completed and approvals obtained so that 
there is no undue delay in the implementati on of this merged scheme which is the 
only support for many marginalized women in the country.

with the cost of living increasing day by day, interest of these grassroot level 
workers performing a very crucial task in taking care of young mothers and little 
children needs to be given the required importance. For this, more contribution 
from both the States and Centre is required.                                                (Para 3.8.2) 
 
Monitoring Mechanism 
 
One issue which was raised again a nd again by the members during the 
discussions was lack of visible presence of MPs/MLAs, the public representatives in 
the district level monitoring committees of ICDS. Consensus of the Committee was 
that the very presence of MPs/MLAs in such bodies will not only make all the 

implementing agencies alert but would go a long way in ensuring the outreach of 
this Flagship Scheme to the targetted bene ficiaries.  The Committee, accordingly, 
recommends that the Ministry may take necessary action in this regard. (Para 3.9.1) 
 
IV. RAJIV GANDHI NATIONAL CRECHE SCHEME FOR THE CHILDREN 
OF WORKING MOTHERS. 
 
On a review of the achievement level of the physical targets under the 
scheme, the Committee notes that outreach of  this scheme has shown no expansion. 
Number of crèches and coverage of children have remained at the same level.  The 
Committee has been persistently advocating for expansion of this scheme which is 
primarily meant to take care of children of working mothers with meagre income. 
The Committee is disturbed to note that instead of increasing, the coverage of this 
scheme has started showing a downward trend. The Committee finds that during 
2009-10, against a target of 31,718 crèches, only 26,177 crèches could be opened up 
to 31 st December, 2009. As a result, number of  child beneficiaries also stood at 
6,54,425 against a target of 7,92,950 children.                                                  (Para 4.2) 
 
The Committee is of the view that deficiencies like improper maintenance of 
records and registers, non-provision of  supplementary nutrition, unsatisfactory 
coverage of children, irregular payment of honorarium to crèche workers, doctors 
not visiting the crèches regularly noticed by the monitoring agencies in the crèches 
being run by BAJSS are of a very seriou s nature. The Committee finds that inquiry 
into complaints against the NGO is yet to be completed by the Vigilance Wing of the 
Ministry. The Committee is of the firm opinion that the inquiry should be completed 
at the earliest, followed by action against all the concerned agencies/authorities.      
                                                                                                                             (Para 4.3) 
 
The Committee finds that charge of 5522 crèches being run by BAJSS was 
temporarily transferred to CSWB on 18th November, 2008. It has been more that 16 
months since then and CSWB which is alrea dy handling 20,893 crèches continues to 
have the additional charge of 5522 crèches. The Committee wonders whether the 

CSWB has the required manpower to effectively handle the additional 
responsibility. The Committee is somewhat su rprised to note that as per the Annual 
Action Plan 2010-11, around 26,000 creches  benefitting 4,12,875 children are 
targeted to be covered. This target shows a considerable decline when compared 
with achievement of 26,177 crèches with coverage of 6,54,425 children as on 31 st 
December, 2009. This confirms Committe e’s apprehensions about 5522 crèches 
earlier run by BAJSS being non-functional. No other plausible reason seems to be 
there for such a situation. The Committee would like to be apprised about the status 
of all the functional crèches.                                                                              (Para 4.4) 
 
The Committee notes that NIPCCD which was requested to prepare a 
Concept Paper suggesting revision in the existing scheme with a view to restructure 
the scheme, has submitted the paper on 2 nd February, 2010. At present, an internal 
Committee of the Ministry is examining the Report. The Committee notes that the 
Concept Paper has recomme nded major changes in the cost norms, manpower 
requirement, inclusion of new components like Play and Sleeping Material Kit (PSE 
Kit), rent for crèche building, extensive tr aining for the worker s and monitoring on 
six monthly basis.  The Committee welcomes these suggestions for improvement 
which were long awaited. The Committee wo uld appreciate if the final decision on 
the Concept Paper is taken at the earliest and approval process is also expedited. 
The Committee notices a major change suggested in the Concept Paper in the 
implementing agencies from CSWB a nd two NGOs to SHGs, Mahila Mandals, 
Women’s’ Organizations, Panchayats a nd Tribal Associations etc. under the 
supervision of the State Governments. The Co mmittee feels that this is a step in the 
right direction.                                                                                                     (Para 4.5) 
 
V. SCHEME FOR WELFARE OF WORKING CHILDREN IN NEED OF 
CARE AND PROTECTIONS. 
 
The Committee hopes that with Rs. 1.20 crore to be reimbursed on account

The Committee has been informed that NIPCCD has submitted its final draft 
Evaluation Report on the Scheme for Welf are of Working Children in need of Care 
and Protection to the Ministry on 1 st January, 2010.  The Committee is happy to 
note that as per the Report, more than 90  percent of the centres have been found to 
be providing all important services envisa ged under the scheme.  As a result, about 
50 per cent of children have withdrawn from active work after joining the centre 
and 28.5 per5cent have joined formal education.  The Committee hopes that 
suggestions for improvement like need for strengthening the infrastructure, capacity 
building measures, community involvement, enhancement of financial norms etc.  
would be looked into and necessary corrective measures initiated at the earliest. 
                                                                                                                               (Para 5.4) 
VI. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME 
 
The Committee appreciates the lofty targets set for the implementation of the 
Scheme.  However, getting the remaining States/UTs to sign the MOU specially due 
to the Scheme being on a cost sharing basis and setting up of Juvenile Justice Board, 
Child Welfare Committees, Special Juvenile Police Units, Child Protection Services 
in 300 districts of at least 25 States wo uld not be an easy task.  Besides that, 
implementation of the Scheme in the 17 St ates which have signed the MoU will also 
have to be closely monitored, so as to tackle the initial problem areas. The 
Committee hopes that the assurance of the Ministry with regard to the speedy and 
accelerated implementation of the scheme holds true.                                    (Para 6.4) 
 
The Committee would like to be apprised of the number of 
beneficiaries/children these Homes in the 7 States are catering to. The Committee 
hopes that during 2010-11, substantive nu mber of such Homes/Shelters will be 
covered under the Scheme.                                                                                 (Para 6.5) 
 
   The Committee finds that the Inspection Committees are being set up by the 
respective State Governments under ICPS. The Committee apprehends that so far 

this very important monito ring mechanism envisaged under the Juvenile Justice 
(Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2000 was not there in position or not 
effective, if existing in majority of the States. The Committee strongly feels that in 
the absence of constant and rigorous monitoring, the very objective of all such 
homes, i.e., protection of vulnerable childr en would remain on paper inspite of lofty 
schemes and substantive fundi ng. The Committee, therefor e, recommends that the 
Centre has to play a pro-active role in getting the monitoring mechanism of 
Inspection Committee and activated in the real sense. The Committee would 
appreciate if publicity to services provided by these Child Lines is given in the cities 
from where these are operating on a regular basis through both print and electronic 
media.                                                                                                                   (Para 6.6) 
 
Attention of the Committee has been draw n to the plight of children of sex 
workers, child labourers and child beggars.  It has been observed that these children 
are a vulnerable lot without proper housing, food and schooling.  Rehabilitation of 
such children becomes important.  The Committee is of the view that special 
provisions and reservations should be made for including these children in the list of 
beneficiaries of the Integrated Child Pr otection Scheme. While welcoming this 
proposed expansion, the Committee would like to point out that ideal position would 
be when his facility is made available across the country. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that concerted efforts may be made by the Ministry in persuading all 
the State Governments to give maximum coverage to Child Line Services.  
                                                                                                                   (Para 6.8) 
 
VII. SCHEME OF HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN 
 
 
  The Committee has been given to understand that Ministry has started a case 
by case review of all incomplete projects and has advised the State Governments in 
13 cases to recover the amount of grant di sbursed  along with prescribed interest 
failing which they have been advised to take over the premises for use of any activity 
related to welfare of  women and children.  The Committee is satisfied with the pro-
active action initiated by the Ministry against the defaulting organizations. The 

Committee recommends that action against al l defaulting organizations should be 
initiated.  Efforts should be made to complete the incomplete projects so that the 
objective of the scheme is not defeated.                                                             (Para 7.4) 
   
The Committee is of the view that three years of the Eleventh Plan are 
already over and the process of revision of the Scheme still remains incomplete. 
Concerted efforts have to be made to overcome procedural difficulties and 
ambiguities. The Committee hopes that with new components of maintenance grants, 
grant for hostels in rented buildings, accre ditation of PG like accommodation, grant 
for initial furnishing etc., the revamped Sc hemed will go a long way in reaching the 
targetted beneficiaries in the real sense. Th e Committee is also happy to note that 
under the new Scheme, endeavour would be to  streamline the process of scrutiny and 
approvals so that the time-lag between the receipt of applications and their 
approval/rejection is cut down significantl y. The Committee would also like to point 
out that a mechanism whereby both surprise  and regular inspections of functional 
hostels are carried out, needs to be evolve d. This would ensure that the Scheme is 
benefitting the targetted beneficiaries.                                                                 (Para 7.6) 
 
The Committee would like to draw th e attention of the Ministry towards 
areas where there are numerous industries forming an industrial hub but there are no 
provision of hostels for work ing women due to which women do not opt to work in 
such areas which offer ample employment opportunities to them.  Further, only 887 
hostels sanctioned do not cater to the needs of all the working women across the 
country.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that additional hostels for working 
women should be sanctioned especially in areas left uncovered so that more and more 
number of women are benefitted from the scheme.                                            (Para 7.7) 
 
VIII. CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AGENCY (CARA) 
 
The Committee was given to understand that the budget for the years 2008-
09 and 2009-10 could not be utilized due to human resource deficit in the 
organization.  Moreover, the Action Plan for the year 2009-10 was approved by the 

Management Committee of CARA only in th e month of January, 2010 because it was 
not in place since April, 2009.  Hence, no activities could be carried out prior to 
January, 2010.  The Committee has been informed that the post of Secretary, CARA 
has been filled on a regular basis after almost five years. The Committee is not 
convinced with the justification given by the Ministry.   The Committee expresses its 
doubt whether CARA would be able to fu lfill its obligations in view of human 
resource deficit in the organization. The Committee also finds the allocation for 
CARA inadequate for carrying out a number of functions and therefore recommends 
for increase in allocation for the organization.                                                    (Para 8.2) 
 
The Committee would like to know the reasons for reductions in number of 
adoptions over the last three years.  The Committee observes that such a state of 
affairs was probably due to human resource deficit in CARA and delay in 
finalization of Action Plan of 2009-10 and its inability to carry out its most 
important function i.e. of facilitating adoptions.  The Committee hopes that with the 
post of Secretary being filled up and the Action Plan ready for the year 2010-11, 
CARA would be able to utilize the allo cated funds and the year 2010-11 would 
witness an increase of inter-country and in-country adoptions.                     (Para 8.3) 
 
The Committee  hopes that the hindrances faced by CARA at present in 
cases of inter-country adoption like judicial delays, clearances from CWC declaring 
children legally free for adoption and ille gal adoption/direct adoption will be taken 
care of in the revamped scheme.                                                                        (Para 8.4) 
 
VIII. RASTHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK) 
 
The Committee appreciates the initiative of the Government regarding 
restructuring RMK so that its corpus is ra ised to Rs. 500 crore which is likely to 
benefit many more poor women who woul d be able to earn their living by 
themselves.  The Committee would like to be  a p p r i s e d  a s  t o  w h e n  t h i s  p r o c e s s  o f  
restructuring would be comple te and what new components more beneficial for the 

targeted beneficiaries (like the rate of in terest at which the loan would be given 
would be included in the restructured RMK.                                                   (Para 9.3) 
 
In view of restructuring of RMK, the Committee would like to point out that 
the need of the hour is to focus on better management of the portfolio of the poor 
women and improving the overall chain which converts the viable ones into genuine 
women entrepreneurs.  Micro-finance organizations should identify the needs of the 
poor women, their saving capacity and time  period and mode of repayment of loan.  
It is time to explore more about the whole philosophy of micro financing and 
successful experiences in the international context.                                         (Para 9.4) 
 
As per the details furnished by the Ministry, out of Rs. 30.30  crore 
sanctioned in 2008-09, Rs. 26.47 crore was disbursed to Rs. 36, 166 beneficiaries.  
Similarly, during 2009-10, against a sanctioned amount of Rs. 9.01 crore, Rs. 11.91 
crore were disbursed, number of beneficiaries being only 9904.  State-wise details 
indicate that during these two years, ther e are States where funds were sanctioned 
but there were no disburseme nts inspite of beneficiaries reported.  There are also 
States where no sanction was there but  disbursements were duly made with no 
details of beneficiaries.  It seems that exact figures of beneficiaries during 2008-09 
and 2009-10 inspite of disbursement of loan s have not reached the Ministry so far.   
The Committee apprehends that position about the  previous years may also be on 
the same lines.  The Committee would like to have full details about funds 
sanctioned, loan disbursed, number of beneficiaries, number of implementing 
agencies involved, non-disbursed amount available with the NGOs at the beginning 
of the years for the last three years (State -wise).  The Committee would also like to 
understand in which States reach of RMK is not evident so far.                   (Para 9.5)  
 
The Committee believes that there should be  a stipulated time frame for completing 
the proceedings against these erring NGOs.  Also, strict monitoring of the NGOs 
should be an important comp onent for the real assessment of the performance level 
of the scheme.  The Ministry is contemplating to adopt the system of on-line 
monitoring of the projects sanctioned .  The Committee believes that on-line 

monitoring of the projects would help in bringing about transparency in the whole 
process and detailed information about disbursements, releases to NGOs, utilization 
figures, number of beneficiaries etc. sh ould be updated on the website of the 
Ministry at regular intervals.  Simultaneously, keeping in view the blacklisting of a 
large number of NGOs, the Committee is of the view that a viable mechanism of 
inspections (both regular and surprise) at periodic intervals involving both States 
authorities and officers from the Ministry  needs to be evolved.  Local community, 
PRIs etc. can also be involved in this exercise.                                                 (Para 9.6) 
 
IX. CONDENSED COURSES OF EDUCATION FOR WOMEN 
  
The Committee wonders whether this utilization is towards clearance of past 
liabilities only and would like to be apprised of the number of beneficiaries in 2009-
10.  The Committee would like to draw the attention of the Ministry towards the 
utilization status of allocated funds during 2007-2008 and 2008-09.  Against a

7.60 crore could be utilized.  The Committee is, therefore, not fully convinced by the 
clarification given by the Ministry for targeted beneficiaries not being covered 
during 2009-10. The Committee is of the view that funds should be fully utilized in 
an optimum and judicious manner and set ta rgets should be achieved within the 
specified time period.                                                                                        (Para 10.2) 
 
The Committee hopes that this alloca tion would be fully utilized and 800 
courses would be conducted during 2010-11.  It also hopes that unlike 2009-10, this 
financial year would not just witness th e clearance of past liabilities.  The 
Committee finds monitoring of the scheme is  being done both by State Boards and 
CSWB.  The Committee would like to have a Status Report on the NGOs not 
performing well during the last three years identified by the Monitoring Officers 
along with follow-up action (State-wise)  The Committee has been given to 
understand that on an average 50 per cent beneficiaries under the Scheme are being 
provided placement.  The Committee would li ke to have an idea about the States 
showing good results in this vital component of the school.                          (Para 10.3) 

 
XI. SCHEME FOR RELIEF TO AND REHABILITATION OF RAPE 
VICTIMS 
 
 
The Committee would like to point out that considerable time has already 
been taken in the formulation of the scheme.  Almost one plan period has been 
taken for this exercise.  The Committee apprehends that  formal approval of the 
scheme from competent authority will al so have to undergo a number of stages 
which again is a protracted exercise.  The Committee, therefore, would like to 
impress upon the Ministry to make a ll conceivable efforts for expeditions 
completion of all the preparatory activities so as to ensure the launch of this much-
required scheme by the end of the Eleventh Plan.                                         (Para 11.2)   
 
The Committee feels that a beginning has to be made for launch of this 
scheme even if at a small scale.  In the meantime, the Ministry has to make all out 
efforts for getting the funds as required under the scheme.   The Committee also 
recommends to the Ministry for working out a set of guidelines for ascertaining 
whether the cases brought forward are ge nuine and assistance is provided to the 
victims as envisaged under the scheme.                                                          (Para 11.3) 
 
XIII. NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN (NCW)  
 
The Committee is aware that the National Commission for Women is the 
only national level authority having the mandate of protecting the rights of women 
across the country.   The most crucial task assigned to the Commission is to 
investigate specific problems or situations arising out of discrimination/atrocities 
against women.  However, the Commit tee feels that the Commission has not 
succeeded fully in carrying out this mandate.  This is borne out by the status of 
complaints received by the Commission.  As per the information made available to 
the Committee, out of 15,854 complaints  registered with the Commission during 
2007-08 and 12,895 complaints during 2008-09, 1781 and 1166 complaints are 
pending respectively.   The Committee finds  the details of complaints on which 
Action Taken Reports from concerned authorities are still pending as more 

disturbing.  Out of 8640 and 7509 complaints registered in 2007-08 and 2008-09 
respectively, ATRs, are pending on 5610 and 4679 complaints respectively.  This 
clearly shows that the matter has not pr oceeded beyond the complaint registered 
with the Commission and sent to the conc erned authorities for necessary action.  
Therefore, it would not be wrong to conclude that no relief whatsoever has been 
provided to the victim in such a case.  The very fact that a very large number of 
complaints from 2007-08 and 2008-09 conti nue to remain pending defeats the very 
purpose of mandate of safeguarding the ri ghts and interests of women folk of the 
country, with which the Commission has been entrusted.  The Committee can well 
imagine the fate of 13,696 complaints registered with NCW (as on 31.01.2010) 
during 2009-10.   The Committee strongly feels that the National Commission for 
Women needs to be empowered so  as to be in a position to take conclusive action in 
cases relating to redressal of grievances received from victims.                   (Para 12.2) 
   
   The Committee therefore, strongly feels that the National Commission for 
Women has to play a proactive role in such matters.                                    (Para 12.3)  
 
XIII. MERGER OF SCHEME OF SWADHAR AND SHORT STAY HOME 
 
The Committee is of the view that the merger of these two schemes with 
almost similar objectives is a step in the right direction.  However, many aspects like 
financial norms, new compon ents, monitoring mechanism etc. would need to be 
taken into consideration for the implem entation of the me rged scheme The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry evaluates all aspects of both the schemes 
carefully before its implementation.  The Committee is doubtful about the 
implementation of the merged scheme in the year 2010-11.  The Committee also 
wonders by when the process of signing of  MOUs  with State Governments will be 
undertaken.  The Committee can only hope th at the new scheme  will be finalized at 
the earliest and all formalities will be  completed and approvals obtained so that 
there is no undue delay in the implementati on of this merged scheme which is the 
only support for many marginalized women in the country.                        (Para 13.4) 
 

XIV. COMPREHENSIVE SCHEME FOR COMBATING TRAFFICKING 
(UJJAWALA) 
 
The Committee is unhappy over under-utilization of funds for this scheme 
for the last three years.  The Committee is  of the view that problem-areas in the 
implementation of the scheme should be id entified and solutions looked for.  The 
Committee believes that valuable inputs and help can be taken from NIPCCD which 
conducts orientation courses, sensitization programmes etc. with respect to this 
scheme.  The Committee hopes that the Mi nistry would take concrete steps for 
optimum and judicious utilization of fu nds for a vital scheme like this.  The 
Committee hopes that the allocation of Rs. 10.00 crore for 2010-2011 will not remain 
under-utilized.                                                                                                             (14.2) 
The Committee finds that achievement of targets was comparatively better in  
2008-09 than in 2009-10.  In 2009-10,  against the target of 12,850, only 2800 
benefited from the scheme.  Also, against th e target of 150 (new) and 107 (existing)  
proposals for projects to be approved, only 40 (new) and 39 (existing) proposals 
were approved.                                                                                                  (Para 14.3) 
 
The Committee believes that the scheme  is the only scheme to prevent 
trafficking, rescuing and rehabilitating victims etc. This scheme should be 
implemented with a vigour as it helps in givi ng new life to the victims of trafficking. 
Concerted efforts should be made to ac hieve the targets set and benefit maximum 
number of women and children. Care shou ld be taken to handle the cases with 
utmost sensitivity and avoi d harassing of victims.  The Committee, further, 
recommends to the Ministry to set up a Spe cial Cell to monitor human trafficking, 
particularly among girls and women.  Provision for taking care of children of 
trafficked women should be made so as to give these children basic education, 
housing etc. to enable them to lead a normal life.                                          (Para 14.4) 
 
 
 
 

XV. CONCLUSION 
 
The Committee while reviewing the allocation, utilization trends and 
performance of various schemes admini stered by the Ministry, finds that 
utilization of allocated funds has been mo re or less satisfactory.  The Ministry, 
however, needs to monitor the implementation of schemes as many schemes 
could not accomplish their set targets, with special focus on the implementing 
agencies like the NGOs.  The target group of the schemes of the Ministry are 
women and children and they are the most vulnerable section of our society and 
they need to be addressed with utmost care and sensitivity.  The Ministry needs 
to lay emphasis on the timely implementation of its new schemes and the 
revamping of the schemes under process.  The Committee believes that 
concerted efforts of the Ministry would certainly lead to the evolution of an 
efficient delivery mechanism with minimum hindrances.                           
                                                                                                                    (Para 15)
