PREFACE
 
            I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human Resource Development, having been authorised by the
Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Hundred Tenth Report on Action Taken by Government on the Recommendations
contained in the 74 th   Report on Vocational Education of  Department of Secondary  Education and Higher Education,  Ministry of Human Resource
Development.

Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on 8th December, 1998.  Replies of the Government on the recommendations contained in the Report, received from
the Department of Secondary  Education and Higher Education, Ministry of Human Resource Development were considered by the Committee.  Clarifications
were also sought by the Committee from the representatives of the Department.  .

EPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the Government on the recommendations contained in its Seventy-fourth Report on the
Vocational  Education which was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on 8th December,1998. Action Taken Notes have been received
from the Government in respect of the recommendations contained in the Report.  They have been categorized as follows:-
(i)                  Recommendations/Observations which have been accepted by the Government: Para 2.2, 2.6, 2.10, 2.12, 2.14, 2.16, 2.21, 2.23 and 2.28 (Chapter-
I)
(ii)                Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the Government’s replies: 2.3, 2.5, 2.7, 2.8, 2.17, 2.18,
2.27 and 2.29  (Chapter-II)
(iii)               Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the Government have not been accepted by the Committee : Para-2.1, 2.4, 2.9, 2.11,
2.13, 2.15,  2.19, 2.20, 2.22, 2.24, 2.25, and 2.26   (Chapter-III)
(iv)              Recommendations/Observations in respect of which  replies of the Government have not been received.  –Nil, (Chapter-IV)
CHAPTER-I
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE
BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE GOVERNMENT
Recommendation
2.2       The Committee is deeply anguished to note that only 5% of the targets fixed has been achieved.  The Committee is of the view that even if this figure is
to be relied on, there remains another 20% to be covered by 2000 AD for diversion of students at +2 level which stands as a tough task ahead.  The Committee,
therefore, strongly recommends that the Deptt. should make serious efforts to give a meaningful thrust to the scheme.  The Committee also feels that the
programme needs to be totally reoriented.
Action Taken
            In 1999-2000, the programme was evaluated by the Centre for Planning, Research and Action and the Centre in its report emphasised that there should
not be any tie up of the educational plans with hypothetical targets of diversion at +2 level to the Vocational Education Programmes.  In fact,  the report termed
the NPE-1986 target of 25% diversion of students to V.E. stream as “absurdly unrealistic and should never have been included in the document.   Even 10%
target for the year 1995 was not achievable and has not been achieved.  It is necessary to examine this fetish of fixing targets in the cut and dry manner adopted
so far, and learn from the experience of  Family Welfare planners who also went on fixing unrealistic targets year after year till it was given up on the World
Bank initiative.”  However, as per the information available, the total enrolment under the programme is about 4 lakhs (23 states/UTs.) which works out 5.17%
compared to the figure of 3.18 lakhs (4.82%) given in ORG Report in 1995 (22 states/UTs.).  In real terms, the increase in enrolment for vocational courses
amounts to about 80,000.  Reorientation process, which includes exploring the possibility of linkages with Industrial Training Institutes and community
polytechnics for maximum use of infrastructure, has been initiated.
Recommendation
2.6       As the scheme of Vocational Education is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme and funds allocated depend on proposals received from the States, the
Committee is of the view that sufficient awareness is required to be created in this regard.  The Committee is also of the view that proper initiative is lacking on
the part of the Central Government due to which the problems are arising.  The Committee stresses that the Department should take all necessary steps to
activate the State Directorates and increase awareness among the States about the utility and importance of the scheme.

Action Taken
            The steps taken to reactivate the scheme include intimation sent to all the Education Secretaries about the reactivation and continuation of the scheme as
before, inviting fresh proposals from them for expansion of the scheme to be considered during the current year, holding of a meeting of State Education
Secretaries in November, 1999, augmentation of Budget provision for the scheme to Rs.35 crores during the current year from Rs.10.5 crores last year, holding
a meeting of North Eastern State Education Secretaries in June, 2000, spending a major portion of budgeted amount by September this year itself, issue of a
letter to all State governments requesting them to replicate the Madhya Pradesh Government model, issue of another letter to all the States/UTs to strengthen
their management structure for better implementation and monitoring of the scheme and a proposal to hold a another meeting of North Eastern State Education
Secretaries in the near future.
Recommendation
2.10     The Committee feels that there is a necessity on the part of the vocational schools to formulate updated packages which must be multifunctional
through trades/specialised skills from secondary school level.  There is also necessity of a placement cell to establish, maintain and disseminate placement
status record at the school and provide a sense of direction to the students.  The Principals of the Vocational Schools and Heads of the respective Departments
should be in
constant liaison with sector specific industry personnel/ representative deputed by the collaborating industry.
Action Taken
Several states have made provisions for placement and Vocational Education Guidance Cells.  Through these cells, vocational pass-outs are counselled to take
apprenticeship programme under Board of Apprenticeship Training.  At the Central Government level, pilot project with NIFT and those in pipeline with
FICCI, CII will also help placement of Vocational pass-outs.
Recommendation
2.12     Looking at the present scenario, the Committee feels that there is a need to devise a system of imparting vocational education which while building a
sound theoretical base, could also ensure connectivity to the market and job security.
Action Taken
            Committee’s observations to the effect that vocational courses should ensure connectively to the market and job security will be considered at the time
of revising the existing scheme.   It may not be out of place to mention that the present curriculum has a component on entrepreneurial development, guidance
and counselling.   This step, to some extent, will obviate the need for job security.
Recommendation
2.14    Similarly, many students who are good at practical and vocational courses may be weak in the regular subjects such as language, mathematics, science
etc.  Such students may not be able to pass out the regular examination to get the schools certificate.  It is, therefore, necessary that a separate certificate for
vocational course is given to enable such students to get gainful employment.  In case a student could not complete the other subjects but secured good marks
in vocational papers, another year of practical training may be given and a certificate in vocational course could be given to him.  There could also be two
levels of vocational certificate course.  The lower level should cover the students who take up Vocational Education after class VII or class VIII and Higher
level certificate could be given to those who take up Vocational Education after class X.  The certificate from Vocational Education centres would act as a
proof of acquired knowledge in skill.  There can be separate institute or Board for issuing the certificate for vocational education.  The certificate can be
recognised one just like the ITI certificate.
Action Taken
New vocational courses are being designed in modular form.  In this format after completing each module a certificate indicating the competency will
be issued by the Central Board of Secondary Education.  National Open School also offers 49 vocational courses in 7 broad disciplines for different durations. 
Credit transfer is also being implemented by NOS for award of certificates at X and XII level.  The existing courses will be taken up for designing in modular
form at the time of their revision.  Plans are afoot to introduce multilevel entry and exit with certificate being issued for each course at all levels.
     A committee has been constituted to advise on vertical mobility of vocational pass-outs.  Representatives from NIEPA, UGC, PSSCIVE and this
Department’s technical bureau are members.  The committee had held its 1
st meeting and come out with its initial findings/recommended action.
Recommendation
2.16     The Committee is constrained to note that even if seats are reserved for the Dalits and other deprived sections in Vocational Institutes, many of them are
not in a position to finally clear the courses offered.  The Committee also observes that in rural areas, due to poor infrastructural facilities and lack of other
development facilities many a student fail to compete with the urban educated students.  Hence, the Committee is of the view that unless some sort of coaching
classes are provided to these groups, it would be difficult to achieve the desired goal.  The Committee,  therefore,  recommends that coaching classes be
arranged for the disadvantaged sections of society.
Comments of the Department
            The concerns of the Committee have been communicated to the State Governments for consideration while running vocational courses in schools and
other areas.
Recommendation
2.21     The Committee is of the opinion that Voluntary Organisations must submit their audited report year wise.  The Committee desires that the evaluation by
Joint Evaluation Team be conducted every year and voluntary agencies must be made accountable to the local community for their acts of omissions and
commissions.
Action Taken
            A copy of the sanction order is being endorsed to the local Panchayat, MLA, MP and state government concerned to keep a watch over thefunctioning
of these organisations. Release of grants to the NGOs (2
nd installment) is linked to the report from local District Magistrate or visit by Joint Evaluation Teams.
Recommendation
2.23     The Committee was given to understand that under the Vocational Education Scheme, Certificate is issued by the Board after the completion of two-
year course.  The Deptt., however, clarified that there were some courses which did not require two years to complete it.  The Committee is of the view that the
courses which can be completed in less than two years should not be prolonged purposelessly and permitted to be completed within the required period only. 
By putting a strict range of two years for Vocational Education for a course which could be completed much earlier, the scheme itself has created a barrier for
the students.  Hence, the Committee recommends that there should be flexibility in the term of the courses and necessary changes in the Vocational Education
scheme may be effected accordingly.
Action Taken
            Steps have been taken to introduce flexibility/modularity in the design of the vocational courses to provide for multiple entry/exit.  This flexibility will
enable those who want to upgrade their knowledge through studies after a break or who want to pursue the course in a limited manner for one reason or the
other.  Under the system, certificates will be issued for the courses successfully completed.  Steps are being taken to link the prior learning through issue of
certificates or equating them with the known standards.
Recommendation
2.28     The Committee is constrained to note that although physical achievements so far as training aspects of both the Schemes is concerned are quite
satisfactory, this is not the position if we look at the figures relating to number of Youths self-employed/wage employed.  The Committee would like to
emphasise that generation of employment, both self and wage-employment, is the ultimate objective of both these schemes.  The Committee, accordingly,
recommends that some pilot projects should be started on trial basis where all kinds of facilities are made available to trainees so as to make them gainfully
employed.

           This Department has undertaken a pilot project in collaboration with National Institute of Fashion Technology.  Under this project, facilities including

CHAPTER-II
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH THE COMMITTEE DOES
NOT DESIRE TO PURSUE IN VIEW OF THE GOVERNMENT’S REPLIES
Recommendation
2.3       The Committee is also not happy to note that in spite of 9 years of implementation of the programme, the need and area based approach has not yet
been strictly followed.  The Committee recommends that community participation and need and area based approach must be followed to accelerate the
process of diversion of students at the +2 level so as to achieve the target.
Action Taken
            So far, 320 districts have been got surveyed by various State/UT governments and vocational courses have been introduced on the basis of these survey
reports.  On their own assessment, they have introduced need/area based courses.  More projects in consultation with FICCI, CII, CBSE and other stakeholders
to develop and operationalise more need/area based courses are in the pipeline.
Recommendation
2.5       The Committee finds that in many a case, the Central assistance released for vocational education is used by the states as a ways and means expenses
and/or diverted for the purpose other than the one for which the assistance was released.  The Committee feels that this is not a healthy trend.  The Committee
is of the considered view that this problem can be tackled if separate funds are earmarked for Vocational Education  by the Planning Commission and it is
impressed upon the State Govts. that non-utilisation of funds for Vocational Education would be treated as misappropriation, for which the concerned State
Government would be held responsible.  The Committee also recommends that for proper monitoring, there should be a Joint Monitoring Mechanism which
would have representatives from Central Govt., State Govt. concerned, Members of Parliament from the particular State and MLAs of that area to ensure
successful implementation of the scheme.
Action Taken
            State Governments have been advised of the recommendation of the policy regarding diversion of funds for the purpose other than the prescribed one
and non-utilisation of funds for the allocated purpose.  The monitoring is done by the area officer scheme, personal visits of the officers concerned with the
scheme and review of the progress of each scheme, state-wise by the Secretary himself.  The details are given as under:
(1) Area Officer Scheme: Under this Scheme, each Deputy Secretary/ Director level officer has been allocated one or two states.  The Area Officer prior
to his visit to the state concerned collects information from the various divisions in regard to the issues to be sorted out with the State Government.
(2) Education Secretary has recently instructed all the Divisions dealing with the schemes to endorse a copy of the sanction to Block Development
Officer, Panchayat President, M.L.A. & M.P. representing the area and to the Webmaster for putting up on the website for the general information of
the public.  Accordingly, copies of sanctions have been endorsed.
 
Recommendation
2.7      The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction over the neglect of District Vocational Surveys.  These surveys are an important ingredient, because the
need and area based approach of the programme depends mainly thereon.  The yearly District Vocational Surveys should be made compulsory and community
participation in it should be encouraged so that assessment of manpower requirements of the area, range of available occupations, trend of emerging vocations
etc. are properly recognised.  Without this, it is difficult to introduce area and need based courses.
Action Taken
Realising the importance of District Surveys, effective steps were taken to ensure district surveys throughout the country.  Considerable progress has
been achieved, as survey has been completed in 320 districts.  State Governments have been advised to ensure the completion of surveys in the remaining
districts.
Recommendation
2.8       The Committee finds that the courses offered are generally not very popular in many of the states. If out-dated courses are offered, the people would
have very limited choice. Similarly, if a course which is not suitable to an area is introduced, students would not be attracted  by such courses. It is, therefore,
essential that the programme should include the courses which are useful in the tertiary sector. The Committee is,  however, constrained to note that the
approach of the Department is very peripheral in this regard. There are many examples such as plumbing, carpentry, agriculture related skills, pump set repairs,
para medical courses which can be introduced on requirements.  There is a tremendous scope and demand for such courses. The Department should keep  in
mind the fact that the vocational education programme is meant for improving the skills of the people who are not able to go for higher education, rather than
making people engineers/architects/scientists, etc. 
Action Taken
            The scheme requires only energisation through meetings/ conferences.  A committee has been constituted to review the linkages of the vocational
courses to the tertiary sector.  The scheme provides for periodical updating of vocational courses.  Pt. Sunderlal Sharma Central Institute of Vocational
Education (PSSCIVE) reviews the vocational courses in consultation with other agencies.  Several obsolete courses have been weeded out.  New courses in
several areas including Information Technology have been introduced.  Central Apprenticeship Council also reviews vocational courses periodically.  The steps
outlined above will hopefully rectify the situation.
Recommendation
2.17     The Committee is of the opinion that mentally and physically handicapped children and other deprived sections of  the society need to be given special
attention.  A more humanitarian approach should be followed while dealing with the neglected and disabled sections of society whereoutput need not be judged
by the adherence to law rather  than by the number of people benefited and settled.
Action Taken
            The State/UT Governments have been advised to remove infrastructural barriers and to provide special facilities like toilets, ramps etc. in the schools
for the welfare of the disabled children studying therein.  Under Integrated Education of Disabled Children (IEDC) scheme disabled children are provided
school uniform, books, stationery, equipment to overcome disability, transport allowance, hostel allowance, etc.  The IEDC Scheme endeavours to promote
inclusive education in general/mainstream schools.  A scheme of Community Polytechnics for persons with disabilities has been envisaged under the ambit of
Community Polytechnics to provide institutional, technical, vocational and educational support to children with disabilities.  Fifty polytechnics have been
selected which would benefit every year 1250 students with disability in diploma level courses and 5000 students with disability in short duration Technical/
Vocational courses.  Also after the promulgation of the Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act, 1995,
all government educational institutions and other educational institutions receiving aid from the government, shall reserve not less than 3% of seats for persons
with disabilities.  The State/UT Governments, KVS, NVS, UGC, NOS and other Bureaux in the Ministry of HRD have been advised accordingly.  Relaxed
standards are also applied to such students taking examinations.  All sanctions to State Governments/NGOs would highlight and emphasis 3% reservation in
admissions for children with disabilities.

2.18     The Sub-Committee was informed during its visit to Chennai (24 th Sept. 1997) that in Tamil Nadu Vocational Education is being used as a launching
pad for securing admission into Polytechnics.  Students who fail to qualify the entrance test of Polytechnics, join Vocational Education andafter one year of its
completion, through the quota fixed for Vocational Education, get admission in 2nd year of Polytechnics.  In this way, the basic aim of Vocational Education to
espouse the concept of self employability goes into the background.  Steps should, therefore, be taken to see that Vocational Education fulfils its objectives like
individual self- employability, supplying skilled manpower and acting as an alternative to those pursuing higher education without any aim.  The Committee is,
therefore, of the view that only those students from Vocational Education who are  very brilliant and secure the qualifying standard for joining Polytechnics
should be allowed to go for it.
Action Taken
            The curriculum for vocational education contained a component for entrepreneurship development.  Provision also exists for guidelines and counseling
in the training under Vocational Education.  Committee’s suggestions will block the vertical mobility of vocational pass-outs.  This is not desirable especially
in the area of higher education components such as Polytechnics, etc.  The lack of freedom for vertical mobility (there will only be small percentage of such
students) might actually act as a disincentive for the potential candidates for Vocational Education.  Since the ultimate goal being employability any avenue
open may not be blocked.
Recommendation
2.27     The Committee finds that under the Training of Rural Youth for Self-Employment (TRYSEM) Scheme, there exists no exhaustive list of trades.  The
State Govts. have the option to select their trades in accordance with the illustrative list of trades furnished by the Central Govt.  The Committee, however,
feels that area specific and need based approach has to be developed to generate the interest of Rural Youth in these courses.  The Committee was given to
understand that in most of the cases funds were being released directly  to the implementing agencies.   Chances of diversion of funds by the States were thus
very less.
Action Taken
Training of Rural Youth for self employment (TRYSEM) relates to the Department of Rural Development.  Action taken by Ministry of Rural
Development is given as under:-
Under the new programme of Swarnjayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana (SGSY) area- specific and need-based approach has been adopted.   Under SGSY
each block identifies 4-5 key activities based on the resources, occupational skills of the people and availability of the markets.   The Swarozgaris will be
imparted minimum skill training as per the requirement of the identified activity.
            Hence, this Department is not in a position to comment.   As far as this Department is concerned, funds are released to  the NGOs directly for
implementing the Vocational Education.   A proposal regarding release of funds directly to the implementing agencies instead of through State Governments
will be taken up when the modification of scheme of Vocational Education is taken up for consideration.
Recommendation
2.29     The Committee finds that no recent evaluation of both the Schemes of I.T.Is. & TRYSEM has been carried out.  Therefore, the Committee recommends
that the Department should carry out survey so as to find out the inherent lacunae in the schemes and take necessary remedial action.  The Committee would
also like to point out that effective monitoring mechanism should be evolved so as to carry out effective implementation of the Vocational Schemes.  This
monitoring Committee may consist of Central/State Govt. officials and M.P./MLA and other local representatives.
Action Taken
This para relates to the Ministry of Labour and the Department of Rural Development.  Accordingly, they have been requested to frame the replies.
Reply given by the Ministry of Rural Development is as under
TRYSEM was a facilitating component of IRDP.  The fourth round of IRDP  Concurrent Evaluation has revealed that only 3.38% of IRDP
beneficiaries received training under TRYSEM.  Quick Evaluation of TRYSEM conducted during 1993 has shown that only 20% of the TRYSEM trainees
received loans under IRDP and 47% of the TRYSEM beneficiaries were unemployed.  To overcome these deficiencies the self-employment programmes have
been restructured and a new programme known as Swarnjayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana (SGSY) has been launched from 1 st April, 1999.  Under this
programme, training needs of the beneficiaries are ascertained with reference to Minimum Skill Requirement both in terms of technical and managerial skills. 
The beneficiaries are to be given training according to the required need for the identified key activity.  There is effective mechanism of monitoring the
programme of SGSY.  The State level, District level and Block level vigilance and monitoring committees have been constituted, which have representation for
Central/State Govt. officials and MPs/MLAs and other local representatives.
Reply given by Ministry of Labour
     The Vocational Training in India is a concurrent subject, both of Central and State Governments and the recommendations of National Council for
Vocational Training (NCVT), an advisory body, are not binding on the State Govts., implying that a number of concerns are beyond the control of the Central
Govt.  Therefore, the governance and the management of the system need to be strengthened.  The number of I.T.Is. under the Craftsmen Training Scheme
have been rapidly increasing and during the last one decade, the numbers have almost doubled The administrative set up at the State level has virtually
remained the same.  In order to strengthen the National Vocational Training System (NVTS) in the country, a Workshop was held at NVTI, Noida, in 1995, in
which various weaknesses and strengths of the system were analysed.  Based upon the outcome of this workshop a proposal for the formation of All India
Council for Vocational Training (AICVT), a statutory autonomous body was put up to the NCVT in its meeting held in December, 1996 and was supported by
the council.  The proposal for the formation of AICVT has also been discussed with various Central Govt. and National bodies, Employers Organisations,
Labour Union etc.   In order to bring qualitative improvements in the system and to execute regulatory or policy making function, the Council would have
different statutory Bodies like Trade Testing, Affiliation, Apprenticeship Boards, etc.
     For the planning or restructuring of NVTS, German Government has come forward to provide the necessary assistance.  Under the Planning of NVTS
various studies, workshops and seminars are planned to be conducted to work out micro level details.  These studies would include essential aspects of training
including lacuna in the existing training system and remedial actions for bringing in improvement.
     As per the prescribed monitoring system each training institute is required to be inspected quarterly by an Inspector of State Directorate.  In addition, one
center in each State is required to be inspected once in a year by a tripartite team comprising of representatives of State Directorate of Training, Industry and
Labour Organisations.  However, because of paucity of staff at the State Directorate level, effective monitoring/inspection of I.T.Is has not been possible as
envisaged.  The Sub-committee of NCVT in its meeting held on 24
th June,  1998  reiterated  that   inspection on random basis by tripartite teams comprising of
members from Central/State Governments and CII should be carried out.  It also recommended that there should be monitoring cell at each State Directorate
for carrying out inspection on regular basis so as to ensure quality of training.  At the time of restructuring of NVTS, as stated above, the proper monitoring
system would be evolved.

Recommendation
2.1       The Committee emphasises that vocational education has very ancient roots in our country.  There used to be ‘craft schools’ in villages for training in
crafts.  The Committee appreciates the emphasis given to vocational education by various Commissions and the National Policy on Education 1986.  Even
though all these efforts culminated into launching the scheme in 1988, yet the scheme has not made much headway so far.  The Committee is constrained to
note that the scheme has not been given due weightage and has also not been implemented to the extent it should have been done.
Action Taken
            Since its inception in 1988 till 1996 the expenditure under the scheme was incurred depending upon the viable proposals received for expansion of the
Scheme.  In July 1996, in the Chief Ministers’ Conference, a decision was taken to transfer some of the Centrally Sponsored Schemes to States.

Vocationalisation of Secondary Education Scheme was categorised one for transfer along with funds to the States.  National Development Council constituted
a sub-committee of Chief Ministers to arrive at a decision in the matter.  However, no concrete decision has been arrived at, with regard to the budget for the
scheme by the Central Government.  The funds were reduced from Rs.49.77 crores in 1996-97 to Rs.2.58 crores in 1997-98.  The uncertainty was removed
through clarification issued by the Planning Commission stating that the scheme would continue in the existing form and that the Ministry could go ahead for
the implementation of the Scheme.  Consequently, Education Secretary convened a meeting in August 1999 to review and reactivate the scheme.  In the
meeting, it was decided to give an impetus to the implementation of the scheme.  The scheme provides for sufficient flexibility in designing courses in various
areas such as agriculture, banking and commerce, engineering and technology, health & para-medical, Home Science & Humanities and others.  Accordingly,
the following measures were taken:
1.           Letters were written to the State/UT governments for reactivation of the scheme and for generating fresh proposals from them for expansion of
the Scheme.
2.   Budget for the scheme was raised from Rs.10.65 crores in 1999-2000 to Rs.35 crores in 2000-2001.  The figure has been increased to Rs.45 crores in
the R.E. 2000-2001.
3.   It is proposed to increase the budget allocation for the next financial   year   2001-2002  to Rs.55 crores.
4.   Due to persistent efforts, the unspent balance lying with the State/UT governments was reduced from Rs.182 crores in 1996 to about Rs.45 crores till
March, 2000.
5.   Standing Committee of Joint Council of Vocational Education was convened on 21.7.2000 and two committees were constituted to look into policy
matters such as norms for financial assistance for various components, course design, introduction of flexibility and modularity in the courses,
certification, providing equivalence vertical mobility, etc. and to suggest specific changes.
6.   During 2000-2001, proposals received from State/UT governments were speedily dealt with and funds to the extent of Rs.31 crores approximately
out of Rs.35 crores have already been released.
As can be seen from the above, during the past one year significant progress has been achieved in reactivation and implementation of the Scheme.
Further Recommendation
            
The Committee expresses its concern over the slow pace of progress in the implementation of the Scheme and the indecisiveness persisting over its
funding.    Initially there was a proposal for transferring this scheme to the State Governments  for   implementation.   However, at present Planning
Commission has directed to continue the scheme in the existing form. Due to this sort of confusion, implementation has been very tardy.   The Committee
understands that no final decision has yet been taken with regard to the fate of this scheme leading to half-hearted implementation of the scheme.  It, therefore,
recommends that the issue be finally resolved at the earliest.
Recommendation
2.4       The Committee is pained to note that the programme has not received adequate priority in most of the States.  There is also a considerable time-lag
between release of grants from the Centre and its disbursement to the schools.  The Committee is of the view that when no response is received from State
Govts. on yearly basis, it should be followed up and accountability fixed.  The Committee was given to understand that funds are generally released to
reimburse the expenditure made, which is a major hurdle in the successful implementation of the scheme.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
funds be released in advance on six monthly basis.
Action Taken
A  decision has been taken to release funds in advance   from the current financial year i.e. 2000-2001itself. Accordingly, advance grants have been
released this year.  Some States viz. Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Assam, Mizoram, Goa are the recipients of financial assistance to the
extent of Rs.31 crores during the current year already.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee is happy to note that some States viz. Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Assam, Mizoram and Goa have received
advance grants.   However, other States/UTs have not perhaps received the grants in advance.   The Committee understands that unspent balances with other
States/UTs might have the been reason for not releasing advance grants to them.
Recommendation                                                                                 
2.9       The Committee is of the view that unless the gap is bridged in regard to enrolment of girls and boys, the country would be losing the talent of the major
chunk of its population.  Hence, the Committee recommends that steps must be taken to encourage more and more number of girls to be enrolled in Vocational
Education in general and in Engineering and Technology and Health courses in particular.
Action Taken
            The enrolment of girls in vocational education in percentage terms is of the order of 30.3% (in 10 States of the total).  Efforts are being made to bring it
to the level of 50% at least and also to collect regular information on girl enrolment from all the States.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee is of the considered view that enrolment of girls still remains very low in vocational education.   At present, as per the information
given only 30.3% of girls are in Vocational Education.   Concerted efforts are necessary to increase the enrolment of girls in Vocational Education.  The
Committee, therefore, recommends that necessary steps must be taken in this direction.
Recommendation
2.11The Committee further recommends that industrial sector should be involved in curriculum preparation and renewal.  Demonstration of basic skills should
be done by industrial personnel also.  Faculty visits to the industry for specialised skill training, nominating an industrial representative for the monitoring and
inspection team, deputation of the external examiner from the industry/ involvement of industry in evaluating students performance, on-the-job training
provision for about 4-6 weeks, apprenticeship training for one year on completion of the course (based on selection criteria) by the collaborating industry and
above all a profile of job requirements by the industry for the purpose of placement to be provided by the collaborating industries and their recommendation for
absorption in organisations located elsewhere, are the aspects which should be seriously considered in collaboration with the industry.  The Committee,
however, feels that as there is a lack of proper planning in this regard, there is an urgent need for perspective planning.  Not only this, efforts should be made to
assimilate the agro-based industries in the curriculum of vocational education, so as to make the task of achieving the goals easier.
Action Taken
             Pandit Sunderlal Sharma Central Institute of Vocational Education have representatives from 36 major industries including agro-based Indus-tries in
their Advisory Boards.  These Boards offer advice in development of curricula for vocational courses, on the job training of vocational pass-outs and provide
linkages with the industries for perspective planning.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee is constrained to observe that the present arrangement with ragard to collaboration with industrial sector  does not seem to be
efficacious and it, therefore, recommends that  coordinated efforts by Centre, State, Institute & Industry are required to be made in this direction.
Recommendation
2.13     The Committee is of the opinion that since skill is a major requirement for vocational education, the 10
th standard as essential qualification can be
brought down to class 8 th.  The Committee is constrained to note that about 52.25% of the children drop-out are in the classes I-VIII (as per the 1995-96,
figures).  Many of those drop-outs are quite vulnerable and indulge in undesirable activities.  This manpower can be utilised for constructive purpose if
education is made attractive and relevant.  Vocational education is the best alternative.  The parents will also be interested in sending their children to schools
because of the usefulness of courses.  The Committee expresses its concern over the school drop-outs, who form a major chunk of the rural masses and urban
poor, and desires that special courses and literature be developed for this target group.  In the rural areas there can be greater need for multi-disciplinary courses

like agriculture-related vocational subjects which include repairing of pump sets, agricultural machines/implements etc. Similarly, fisheries can also be given
due attention.  Small trades like TV Mechanic and Radio can also generate employment in rural areas and these courses can also be incorporated under the
Scheme.  The Committee is of the view that in urban areas there are greater number of industries and job facilities and the industrial units should be linked to
the courses offered which can take into account the requirements of the industrial units.  If this is done, larger number of students including drop-outs can be
accommodated after the completion of their courses in the jobs of the industrial units.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that such trades be considered
for differential needs of urban and rural areas.  The Committee, in this connection, takes note of the Pre-Vocational course which is being implemented.  But it
is going at a snail’s pace and is required to beaccelerated.  This scheme may also be modified slightly to establish proper linkage with the main scheme.
Action Taken
National Policy on Education (NPE) 1986 provides for vocational education as a distinct stream intended to prepare students for identified occupations
spanning several areas of activity.  These courses should ordinarily be provided after the secondary stage, but flexibility has been provided to start Vocational
Education after Class-VIII.  Accordingly, there is no hindrance in starting vocational education programme after class VIII also.  The emphasis on
diversification to vocational education should only be after ten years of schooling is based on the minimum level of general education required to change from
childhood to adulthood.  Pre-vocational courses, which are generic, are being imparted in classes IX and X.  There already exist Community Polytechnics
wherein training is given in skills without insisting on formal qualifications.  In addition, Industrial Training Institutes are offering some of the trades for those
who have qualified up to 8
th standard.  National Open School (NOS) also provides to the school dropouts and disadvantaged groups through 6 months and 1
year vocational courses in seven broad disciplines like Radio & TV engineering, Electrical, technician course, refrigeration, librarianship, etc.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee is constrained to observe that the basic purpose of Vocational Education i.e. to provide skilled knowledge to earn livelihood through
works has not helped the Drop outs to a great extent.   The Committee is of the view that there is a need for greater coordination between Industry-Institute and
also need-based courses to be developed.   
Recommendation
2.15     The Committee is also constrained to note that lack of trained teachers is one of the weakest links.  The Committee desires that the States should be
asked to give priority to teachers’ training as this programme cannot be  successful without trained teachers.  The Committee is also concerned on the lack of
willingness on the part of the old teaching staff to accommodate new courses which stand as a stumbling block in the progress of Vocational Education.
Students are attracted only when new and up-to-date courses are offered.  The Committee feels that there is an urgency of Reorientation Training for existing
teachers to adapt themselves to new courses and also induction of more new teachers for updated courses.  However, the Committee feels that an element of
indecisiveness with regard to continuance of this scheme as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme persists.  This has created a sense of scepticism in the minds of the
State authorities implementing this scheme.  That is why teachers taken under this scheme are given only an ad-hoc appointment.  In this respect, the
Committee is of the view that once the teachers are appointed on a permanent basis, they would be actively involved in the training programmes offered.  In
this way, the Teachers training would be more meaningful.  The Committee is of the opinion that by this course of action the unemployment problem can be
solved to some extent.
Action Taken
            The status with regard to discontinuation of the scheme by the Centre and its transfer to States has since been removed in consultation with Planning
Commission and accordingly grants are now being released to States for expansion of the Scheme.  Some of the vocational courses have been updated and
courses on Information Technology have also been introduced in many States.  CBSE has introduced Vocational courses on Information Technology  from
Class X onwards.  Apart from the above, efforts have been made for training of more number of teachers by various Boards & PSSCIVE.  In an effort to be in
tune with times, encouragement of employed professionals as part-time teachers is being emphasised.  Visiting faculties, adjunct teachers from the industry/
corporates are also encouraged so that they bring  updated knowledge and new methodologies with them.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee understands that the fate of the Scheme is yet to be finally decided at the end of the 9
th Plan.  The Committee feels that implementation
of the scheme with all seriousness is not possible till a decision regarding the fate of the scheme is taken.  Though the Committee is inclined to be in agreement
with the engagement of part time professionals from Industry with the object of bringing updated information/technology to the scheme, the Committee has no
idea about the usefulness of this practice.  The Committee, therefore, opines that the idea of engaging outside professionals on part time basis for teaching
needs to be reviewed.  The Committee also reiterates its earlier observation of there being an urgent need for providing Re-orientation Training for existing
teachers to adapt themselves to new courses.
Recommendation
2.19     The Committee expresses concern over the under utilisation of the seats for the Apprenticeship training by vocational passouts. This has stood as a
weak link and has adversely affected the acceptability of the vocational trainees.  The Committee is of the opinion that steps must be taken to encourage the
students for going through Apprenticeship Training after completing Vocational Education which would make the Vocational Trainees more and more
acceptable to the industries.  There should be proper placement of students.  Hence, the Committee is of the view that the placement of students under
Apprenticeship Training needs to be vigorously implemented.  There should also be proper monitoring of it.
Action Taken
Central Apprenticeship Council has been advised to take up review of the guidelines with a view to accommodate more vocational pass-outs under the
apprenticeship programme.  Advance payment of stipends to apprentices and the industries is being contemplated.  This step is expected to make more and
more industries to volunteer for the apprenticeship programme.  A Co-ordination Committee under the chairmanship of Additional Secretary has been
constituted to review all issues related to implementation of the scheme including apprenticeship programme.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee hopes that the above exercise would be completed expeditiously, thereby leading to optimum utilization of seats for the Apprenticeship
training by Vocational passouts. 
Recommendation
2.20     The Committee has been given to understand that many Institutions running the vocational courses in the private and voluntary sectors make use of the
courses for financial exploitation and exorbitant fee is charged from the students.  Steps are, therefore, required to be taken to stop such exploitation and misuse
of vocational courses by the Management.
Action Taken
            Consequent to the inspections carried out during 1999-2000 some Non-Governmental Organisations were found to be non-performing.  As a result,
financial assistance to them have been restricted to the extent that only reputed organisations with innovative programmes are considered and extended
financial assistance.  Further, a copy of the sanction order is being endorsed to the local Panchayat, MLA, MP and concerned  state government, to keep a
watch over the functioning of these organisations.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee notes that in the inspections carried out during 1999-2000, some NGOs were found to be non-performing and action was taken against
them.  The Committee, however, is of the view that these inspections should be a regular feature.  Merely endorsement of sanction orders of NGOs to the local
Panchayat/MLA/MP and concerned State Govt. would not serve the purpose.  Area Officer should also be in touch with these authorities during his State visits
to get proper assessment of the functioning of a particular NGO.
Recommendation
2.22     The Committee has come to know that in the Chief Ministers’ Conference held in July, 1996, a decision was taken to transfer the scheme of Vocational
Education at +2 stage to the States.  However, as this decision has no endorsement of the National Development Council, the implementation of the scheme is
presently held up.  The Committee feels that this state of indecisiveness has adversely affected the scheme.  The Committee finds that Rs.60 crores were

allocated for this scheme in the BE of 1997-98.  However, in the light of the decision of the Planning Commission to transfer the scheme to States, no funds
were released to the States/UTs.  As a result, budget provision was brought down to Rs.20 crores and actual expenditure was only Rs.4 crores during 1997-98. 
Allocation for 1998-99 is Rs.10 crores only.  The Committee is of the view that the drastic cut in funds in the first two years of the 9 th Five Year Plan has
adversely affected the scheme of Vocational Education in general.  It is of the considered view that since the scheme of Vocationalisation of Secondary
Education is of special significance, it should continue as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme and drastic cuts in funds for this scheme should not be effected.
Action Taken
            The indecisiveness/ uncertainty has been removed through a clarification from the Planning Commission to continue  with the Scheme as before.  As a
result of review in the Ministry, the budget allocation for the current year has been increased from Rs.10.5 crores (1999-2000) to Rs.35 crores.  For 2001-02 the
proposed amount is Rs.55 crores.  Implementation of the scheme is being reviewed periodically in the Standing Committee of Joint Council of Vocational
Education (JCVE).  In the case of North Eastern Region, the implementation of the scheme has been reviewed twice in the last one year.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee understands that a final decision regarding the transfer of the scheme to States is yet to be taken by the Planning Commission.  This
needs to be expedited.
Recommendation
2.24     The Committee is of the considered view that the Vocational Education Scheme is still continuing as a Pilot Project.  It has come to the notice of the
Committee that four external agencies/ institutions were entrusted the work of conducting a region-wise evaluation of the Vocational Education Scheme and
the final report has been submitted by Operation Research Group (ORG).  The Committee recommends that in the light of the findings of this report, the
States/UTs should be approached by the Deptt. for taking corrective measures for successful implementation of Vocational Education Scheme.
Action Taken
            Instructions have been issued to State/UT governments for taking corrective measures, such as to create proper management structure in the state for
proper monitoring of the implementation of the scheme.  In fact, the scheme is being extended for implementation during the Tenth plan period also.
     The Report of ORG was discussed by NCERT in its Working Group meeting held during 23-25 July, 1999.  In the Working Group State Government
representatives were also associated.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee recommends that the issues/findings of which there was consensus in the meeting of working group of NCERT should be
communicated to the States/UTs for implementation and followed up.
Recommendation
2.25     The Committee finds that at present a number of vocational schemes are being handled by different departments like  Education, Rural Employment
and Poverty Alleviation & Ministry of Labour.  However, Committee feels that there being no coordination among these Departments, achievement rate of
these schemes has not been quite satisfactory.  The Committee is, therefore, of the considered view that a common strategy should be evolved so as to integrate
all these Vocational Schemes and take the maximum advantage of the facilities available with the different departments.
Action Taken
            Representatives of other Central Government Departments like Agriculture, Health, Personnel and Training, etc. have been included in the Standing
Committee of the JCVE.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee is not convinced with the reply of the Department and reiterates its earlier recommendation.
Recommendation
2.26     The Committee observes that there has been regional disparity so far as setting up of ITI is  concerned.  Eastern region, Jammu & Kashmir and also the
Backward and Tribal areas are the most neglected lot.  The Committee feels that such areas need to be given special assistance.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that steps should be initiated for provision of 100% Central Assistance for developing the institutional mechanism in these areas.  The Committee
finds that the organised sector has been the major beneficiary of ITIs.  The unorganised sector comprisingabout 90% of population mainly from rural areas
which also includes large number of Drop-outs and Non-Formal Education students has been totally left out.  This is due to the fact that there is a mismatch
between the labour market requirement and the trades and skills being offered in ITIs.  There being no proper outlet, average students are left with no
alternative but to opt for higher studies.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that urgent steps need to be taken for broad-basing the Vocational Education
Scheme by inducting area specific courses under I.T.Is.  
Action Taken
Efforts are underway to lay stress on area based and locally demand driven courses to benefit the rural youth and to reduce the regional disparity in
implementing the scheme.  For example, 516 Community Polytechnics, which are mostly located in rural areas conduct need based and locally demand driven
courses.  National Open School/State Open School also conducts courses throughout the country without insisting on formal qualifications.  In addition, Jana
Sikshan Sansthan, previously known as Shramik Vidya Peeth, also conducts vocational education programme.  Ministry of Labour has issued instructions to
the Directors of Industrial Training Institutes to allow their infrastructure to be used for vocational education purposes by the schools.
Further Recommendation
            The Committee feels that the present efforts on the part of the Department are not sufficient particularly with regard to balancing regional imbalances in
setting up technical institutes/ITIs in the country.  Although, 516 Community Polytechnics may be functioning in rural areas, but the Committee is conscious of
the fact that very few of them are located in backward, remote and hilly areas.  The Committee, is, therefore, of the view that such areas deserve special
attention in this regard.
