I. INTRODUCTION
1.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development is the nodal agency to advance the rights
and concerns of women and children and promote their survival, protection and development in a
holistic manner. The Ministry was constituted in 2006 with the intention of addressing gaps in State
action for women and children and for promoting inter-Ministerial and inter-sectoral convergence
to create gender equitable and child centered policies, programmes, schemes and legislation. The
Ministry is the prime mover of inclusive programmes, policies, schemes and legislations for women
and children and aims to secure the best interest of women and children. Principles of equity,
justice and non-discrimination guide the Ministry’s actions concerning women and children.
1.2 The Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development in her presentation before the
Committee on 10 April, 2012 gave an overview of the achievements, requirements and programmes
of the Ministry for the year 2012-13. She highlighted the fact that women and children together
constituted a substantial portion of the population and there were many challenges or areas of
concern which were the focus of the Ministry. The Ministry being the nodal agency for advancing
the rights and concerns of women and children, its programmatic interventions were in three broad
areas such as
– Child Survival, Development and Protection
– Holistic empowerment of women and social support
– Support to women and child related programmes.
The strategy of the Ministry was to strengthen the on-going schemes/programmes of the
Ministry and restructure the ICDS Scheme with special emphasis on monitoring and improvement
in the quality of citizen-centric service delivering system, encourage innovative approaches and
skill development. She further submitted that a High Level Committee on Status of Women had
been constituted which would look into the various issues relating to women’s development and
empowerment. Also, a Child Policy was being prepared and was under consultation. A Policy on
Early Childhood and Education was also on the Ministry’s web site for consultation with the
public.
1.3 Another important issue highlighted by the Secretary, Ministry of WCD was the launching
of a multi-sectoral programme on attacking malnutrition in 200 high burden districts as decided by
the Prime Minister’s Council. A proposal regarding the same had been sent to the Planning
Commission for in-principle approval of this multi-sectoral programme. Further, the Prime
Minister’s Council decided to launch an advocacy programme against malnutrition for which EFC
approval was being obtained after which it would be launched in May/June 2012.
1.4 The Ministry of Women and Child Development identified certain thrust areas seeking
special attention:–
(i) Intensify implementation of ICDS to achieve the stated objectives.
(ii) Multi-sectoral programme to address the maternal and child malnutrition programme
in selected 200 high burden districts.
(iii) Implementation of SABLA in 200 selected districts.

(iv) Implementation of In dira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana (IGMSY) on pilot basis in 52
selected districts.
(v) Setting up of dedicated delivery structures at State and District level under the
Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS).
(vi) Expansion of Creche and Day Care Programme (Rajiv Gandhi National Creche
Scheme) with enhanced financial norms.
(vii) Conversion of Swadhar and Short Stay Home into ‘Swadhar Greh’ with revised norms.

(ix) To achieve empowerment of women by securing convergence of schemes/programmes

Governments under National Mission for Empowerment of Women.

the Ministry. Also, the Ministry needs to strategise and prioritise its targets and also take
steps for optimum and judicious utilization of allocated funds.

2.6 The Committee is dismayed to find many schemes still on paper with no further
development taking place in their implementation like the Relief to and rehabilitation of
Rape Victims, Swayamsiddha, National Nutrition Mission and Conditional Cash Transfer
Scheme for Girl Child with Insurance Cover. The Committee finds that the reasons given
for under-utilization of allocated funds generally have been due to non-receipt of final
approval from the Planning Commission, non-receipt of viable proposals from the State
Governments, restructuring of funds or lack of a system in place for implementation and
monitoring. The Committee, time and again, has been reiterating upon the fact that the
necessary approvals for the schemes should be taken well in time so that their
implementation does not suffer. The Committee finds that inspite of allocations set aside
for such schemes, there was no utilization or under-utilization because of procedural delays
mainly due to pending approvals. The Committee believes that such reasons of under
utilization should ngt be allowed to surface as taking approvals for implementing schemes
is a routine affair. The Committee hopes that the necessary approvals for the above
mentioned schemes and putting in place a system for implementation and monitoring is
expedited so that these schemes see the light of the day and their fruits reach the targeted
beneficiaries as early as possible.
2.7 Attention of the Committee has been drawn to the new schemes for women proposed to
be implemented in 2012-13. The new schemes and budgetary allocations for them are as follows:-
Women’s Helpline ( `2.00 crore) Development of Distance Learning Programme on the Right of
Women ( `10 lakhs) Assistance to States for implementation of Protection of Women from
Domestic Violence Act ( `20.00 crore) and One Stop Crisis Centre ( `5.00 crore). The Committee
finds that the new schemes proposed have been provided paltry allocation in this year. Even
for the existing schemes for women, the outlays for the current year seem to be on the
lower side with respect to schemes like Hostels for Working Women, STEP, Central Social
Welfare Board and National Mission for Empowerment of Women. The Committee is
surprised to find that no funds have been allocated for Swayamsidha which was supposedly
the main vehicle of women’s empowerment in the Eleventh Plan. The Committee feels that
launching of new schemes without adequate allocations for them is nothing but a cosmetic
measure for addressing the concerns of women. Improvement in the condition of women
would only be seen if concerted efforts of policy making are matched with substantial funds
for witnessing positive outcomes for the welfare of women.

2.8 The Committee would like to highlight another area of concern in the budgetary
allocations for children in the Union Budget. The ‘Child Budget’ as a proportion of total
budget outlay by the Union Government shows a slight upturn from 4.6 per cent in 2011-
12 to 4.8 per cent in 2012-13. The Committee finds this per centage to be grossly inadequate
for addressing the various needs of children. Not just this, sector wise prioritization of the
Child Budget continues to be highly skewed in favour of Child Education and Child
Development where around 72 per cent and 23 per cent of funds were allocated out of the
total Child Budget respectively. Allocation for Child Health and Chiid Protection have been
accorded low priority as only 3.8 per cent and 0.93 per cent have given for these two sectors
respectively. Given the poor status of children who are the most vulnerable section of our
society, the Committee feels that adequate budgetary allocation with emphasis more on
Child Health and Child Protection needs to be prioritized and given urgent attention.
2.9 Attention of the Committee has also been drawn to the issue of unspent balances with the
State Governments in respect of major Centrally Sponsored Schemes viz ICDS, ICPS, SABLA and
IGMSY. The maximum unspent balances under ICDS Scheme was found with State Governments of
Bihar ( `23.60 crore), Jammu & Kashmir ( `46.63 crore) Jharkhand ( `92.33 crore), Orissa ( `40.77

amounted to `285.38 crore with respect to Supplementary Nutrition, `265.15 crore with respect to

2.10 The Committee has been emphasizing the issue of unspent balances since long and
has always dwelt upon the importance of monitoring of funds, especially with respect to the
funds of ICDS Scheme. The Committee would like to reiterate its earlier recommendation
in its previous reports that the issue of unspent balances be taken up with the State
Governments on a priority basis and the matter of unspent balances be resolved so that the
implementation of ICDS Scheme or any other Scheme like SABLA or IGMSY does not
suffer. Further, the Committee feels that a strict monitoring mechanism should be there for
ensuring the timely release of funds to State Governments and the adjustment of unspent
balances for the judicious utilization of allocated funds. The Committee would like to point
out that funds meant for Research, Publication and Monitoring have remained grossly
under-utilised year after year. The Committee would appreciate that funds meant for
monitoring are put to good use.

Committee has been given to understand that most of pending UCs are quite old and their
liquidation is difficult because the relevant records are not easily available. The Committee
can only say that special measures need to be taken to liquidate the old pending UCs. The

III. INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME
3.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development has been implementing the world’s largest
and most unique and outreach programme of Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)
providing a package of six services comprising supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check
up, referral service and pre-school non-formal education. The ICDS Scheme has been universalized
with the recent third phase of expansion of the scheme by increasing the total approved number
of ICDS Projects and AWCs including Mini-AWCs to 7076 and 14 lakh respectively with a
provision of 20,000 Anganwadis on demand.
3.2 The Committee has been given to understand that in 2009, Planning Commission had
undertaken a nation-wide evaluation of ICDS in 1500 selected AWCs across 35 States/UTs through
the National Council for Applied Economic Research (NCAER). Preliminary findings of the study
were presented by NCAER before the Planning Commission in August, 2010. Some of the key
findings on functioning of AWCs are as under:–
(i) About two third (64%) of the children received supplementary nutrition out of total
children recorded in the delivery register by AWWs. Against the norm of 25 days a
month, on an average they received food for 16 days in a month.
(ii) In general, the practice of breast feeding within an hour of birth is found to be more
widespread among ICDS beneficiaries;
(iii) 93.6 per cent of sampled A WWs reported to have been adequately trained to conduct
PSE;
(iv) Average attendance of no. of children 3-6 years based on three sudden visits by the
Research Team was found to be 14;
(v) ICDS has also positively influenced formal school enrolment and reduction in early
discontinuation among beneficiaries;
(vi) About 70 per cent of the community leaders felt that the ICDS programme was very
useful to the community;
(vii) 51.2 per cent children (12-23 months) were found to be fully immunized.
(viii) 49 per cent of children were observed to be able to write alphabets/words;
(ix) Overall 42.5 per cent of sampled AWCs have their own buildings, 17.4 per cent are
in rented buildings, 17.3 per cent are located in primary schools and other 22.9 per
cent are running from AWW/AWH house, panchayat and community buildings.
(x) Space for indoor activities was found to be adequate in most AWCs (71.8 per cent).
Almost all States had similar dimensions of the AWC buildings.
(xi) Country-wide, a total of about 87 per cent AWCs were found to have drinking water
supply. Drinking water source was available within the premises in 65 per cent AWCs.
About half the AWCs across the states had toilet facilities in the premises.
(xii) 69 per cent of sampled AWCs having functional baby weighing scale; 40 per cent of
AWCs having adult weighing scale; 49 per cent of AWCs having utensils; 48 per cent
of AWCs having cooking vessels; only 44 per cent of AWCs having PSE kits and 62
per cent of AWCs having medicine kits.

3.3 The Committee hopes that the gaps indicated in the NCAER Study would have been
pursued with the concerned implementing agencies. The Committee would like to be
apprised about the follow-up action taken on all the shortcomings identified in the NCAER
Study. The Committee would like to be apprised about the final findings of NCAER Study,
if received.
3.4 The Committee has been given to understand that this flagship scheme is to be restructured
and strengthened. As per the decision of PM’s National Council for India’s Nutritional Challenges,
a proposal for restructuring of ICDS in a mission mode at the cost of `l,83,000 crore had been
prepared and the proposal was under consideration of the EFC. Even the Approach Paper to the
Twelfth Plan emphasized on ICDS being a critical link between children and women and health care
system as well as with elementary education system on children in the 0-3 years of age. For
strengthening and restructuring of ICDS, Annual Programme Implementation Plan (APIP) for ICDS
had been introduced from 2011-12. To take ICDS delivery to its next level through the APIP format
which would serve as a precursor to setting ICDS in a Mission Mode, the APIP document for
ICDS had been circulated to State/UT Governments. The idea is to strengthen, plan and improve
programme management. This would help in capturing the diversity across the States and facilitate
the States to develop state specific strategies interventions. As per Ministry’s targets under the
Results Framework Document (RFD), the Ministry had finalized APIPs for 10 States.
3.5 The Committee is in full agreement with the initiative of the Ministry to restructure
the flagship scheme of ICDS so that several implementation issues of the scheme such as
lack of proper infrastructure, lack of technical expertise at field level, non-indexation of
cost of Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP), weak early childhood care and education
component, poor monitoring due to lack of staff, lack of support to AWWs on nutrition
education and monitoring of under nutrition etc. are taken care of so that the benefits of
the scheme reach to the maximum number of women and children. The Committee notes
the findings of nation-wide evaluation of ICDS in 1500 selected AWCs by the Planning
Commission and would like to highlight that even though the benefits of ICDS Scheme are
reaching the targeted beneficiaries, there is a need to fine tune the service delivery system
with better infrastructure and resourses. The Committee hopes that the re-structuring of
ICDS would increase the outreach of the Scheme and benefit women and children in far-
flung areas and remote regions also.

3.7 As for the physical targets and their achievements during the Eleventh Plan under ICDS is
concerned, the Committee was given to understand that the targeted number of sanctioned AWCs
was 14 lakh of which 13.70 lakh AWCs were sanctioned to States/UTs. Out of these, 13.03 lakh

3.8 The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry for satisfactorily fulfilling its
financial and physical targets. However, there are many areas of concern in the ICDS
Scheme which halt the progress and implementation of the scheme like inadequate
infrastructure, reach of SNP to women and children, availability of pre-school kits to
children, poor monitoring, lack of staff etc. Although the set targets have been more or less
achieved in the Eleventh Plan, a lot more remains to be done. Taking into consideration the
initiative of the Ministry to restructure the ICDS Scheme, the Committee hopes that all the
challenges in the implementation of the ICDS Scheme would be tackled in a much more
efficacious manner in the revamped Scheme.
Funding of ICDS
3.9 BE for ICDS Scheme in the year 2010-11 was `10,000 crore which was increased to
`14048.40 crore at the RE Stage. Justification for this increase given by the Ministry was that
additional allocation was required for the operationalisation of 57 projects and 41033 AWCs,
increase of about 8 lakh beneficiaries during the year, two fold increase in the honorarium of
AWWs/AWHs and increase in the salary of ICDS functionaries. Out of `14048.40 crore, the
Ministry released total funds amounting to `13695.00 crore to various State/UTs till 31 March,
2012. However, there were many States with unspent balances lying with them under this scheme.
The major ones being Bihar, Jharkhand, Jammu & Kashmir, Orissa, Uttar Pradesh, Assam, Tripura
etc.
3.10 Allocation for ICDS for the year 2012-13 is `15,850.00 crore which is about 11 per cent
higher than the actual expenditure of the last year i.e. 2011-12. The Secretary submitted before the
Committee that this allocation would only sustain the programme at current levels. She also
informed that the Ministry had proposed `30,000.00 crore as their requirement during annual plan
discussion in the light of proposed strengthening and restructuring of ICDS.
3.11 The Committee believes that any exercise of revamping or restructuring a scheme
like ICDS would require adequate fund support and other resources. The Committee notes
that against the projected requirement of `30,000.00 crore by the Ministry for ICDS for the
current year, the allocated sum is only half of it. The Committee wonders as to how the
restructuring exercise would then be undertaken by the Ministry keeping in view the wide
gap between requirement of funds and actual allocation. ICDS being a flagship scheme of
the Ministry has a widespread network through which its various components like SNP,
immunization, health check-up, referral services, nutrition and health education and non-
formal pre-school education have to be implemented. It has the capability of reaching out

9
to a large number of women and children who are benefited by the Scheme. Inadequate
allocation would be the biggest bottleneck in any such exercise of restructuring. Given the
immense importance of the nature of the Scheme, the Committee feels that all assistance
by way of funds and resources should be given to this flagship scheme. The Committee,
therefore, recommends to the Ministry to approach the Planning Commission for adequate
funding for the whole exercise of restructuring of ICDS Scheme.

IV. RAJIV GANDHI NATIONAL CRECHE SCHEME FOR WORKING MOTHERS
4.1 The Rajiv Gandhi National Creche Scheme for the Children of Working Mothers provides
Day Care facilities to the children in the age group of 0-6 years from families with monthly income
of less than `12,000.00. In addition to being a safe place for the children, the creches provide
services such as supplementary nutrition, pre-school education and emergency health care etc. The
scheme envisages implementation through the Central Social Welfare Board (CSWB), Indian Council
for Child Welfare (ICCW) and Bhartiya Adim Jati Sevak Sangh (BAJSS).
4.2 The Committee observes that there are 23,785 functional creches assisted under the
Scheme. Out of the total 23,785 creches, 16,075 creches are under CSWB, 2519 under
BAJSS (temporarily shifted to CSWB) and 5191 under the ICCW. As for the quantifiable
deliverables, number of creches funded in 2010-11 and 2011-12 was 22,599 and 23,785
respectively. The number of child beneficiaries in 2010-11 and 2011-12 was 5.65 lakh and 5.94
lakh respectively.

15
4.3 Budgetary allocation for this scheme has been enhanced from `85.00 crore in 2011-12 to
`110.00 crore in 2012-13. It has also been proposed to revise the scheme to enhance the financial
norms and lay down stricter monitoring procedures for improved delivery. A Committee had been
set up by the Ministry to suggest changes in the scheme for improving its coverage and quality.
The Committee had suggested enhanced financial norms, stringent monitoring and fulfillment of
certain preconditions before setting up of the creches. On recommendation of the Committee, 4
model creches have been set up in Delhi on pilot basis with enhanced financial norms and revised
procedure for the remaining period of the current financial year with a view to determine whether
the revised norms and procedures are appropriate and sufficient to provide better environment and
facilities to the children in the creches before the new model is replicated or existing creches are
strengthened. Further, the Standard Operating Procedure for operationalizing the scheme was
prepared by CSWB and approved by the Ministry in November, 2010.
4.4 The Committee appreciates the initiative of the Ministry to revise the scheme for
better delivery of services by having new set of norms and stricter monitoring mechanism.
The Committee is of the view that SOP would help in making the monitoring of the scheme
efficient with respect to sanctioning of funds, feedback and general functioning of the
creches. The need of the hour is to take up the issue of monitoring with the State
Governments so that the scheme functions successfully without any hurdles. The Committee
would also like to know about the feedback from the four model creches set up in Delhi
so that their experiences could be used in other creches for better delivery of services.
4.5 The Committee has highlighted the issue of complaints regarding irregularities in
management of creches by BAJSS due to which 5522 creches were temporarily transferred to

functional. Based on the findings of the CVO, the case has been referred to CBI for further

be transferred to other institutions by the CSWB. The Committee feels that children of
these creches are the biggest sufferers for no fault of theirs. The Committee hopes that the
CBI gives its inquiry report on the matter as soon as possible. The Committee recommends
to the Ministry to make concerted efforts for transfer of remaining 3003 creches from
BAJSS to other institutions so that they become functional and benefit the children of
working mothers.
V. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS)
5.1 ICPS is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of the Ministry launched with a view to create a
safe and secure environment for comprehensive development of children who are in need of care
and protection, children in conflict with law and other vulnerable children. The objectives of the
Scheme are to contribute to the improvement in the well being of children in difficult
circumstances as well as the reduction of vulnerabilities to situations and actions that lead to abuse,
neglect, exploitation, abandonment and separation of children from their families. The scheme is
being implemented from the financial year 2009-10 through the State Governments/UT
Administrations on a cost-sharing basis. The Programme components include Institutional Services
like Shelter Homes, Children’s Homes, Observations Homes, Special Homes, Dedicated Service
Delivery structure at Central, State and District levels, Family based non-institutional care through
sponsorship, foster care, adoption, after care programme; Emergency outreach services through
Childline and Chile Tracking System. ICPS has also brought into its fold existing child protection

16
programmes namely (i) A Programme for Juvenile Justice (ii) An Integrated Programme for Street
Children and (iii) Scheme for Assistance to Homes (Shishu Greh) to promote in country adoptions,
all under one umbrella and also initiated new interventions.
5.2 The Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development submitted before the Committee
that child protection was being brought to the centre stage through the implementation of ICPS.
Increased reporting of child abuse and trafficking necessitated the need for strong implementation
of the provisions of Juvenile Justice Act, setting up of statutory structures, recruitment of
dedicated staff and for capacity building. She apprised the Committee that MOUs had been signed
with all States/UTs except Jammu & Kashmir. So far 588 Child Welfare Committee and 561
Juvenile Justice Boards had been set up in the districts of various States. Reach of Childline (1098)
service improved considerably from 83 in the year 2009 to 193 locations at present.
5.3 The Committee has been informed that out of 34 States/UTs which have signed the MOU,
27 States/UTs have been provided financial assistance under the scheme and the remaining were
yet to avail the financial assistance as they were still in the process of need assessment. States
like Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa and UTs like Dadar & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu,
Lakshadweep and Andaman & Nicobar Islands had not been given financial assistance as
comprehensive proposals were yet to be received from them. So far State Child Protection
Societies (SCPSs) have been set up in 23 States/UTs and District Child Protection Units (DCPUs)
set up in 438 districts in the country.
5.4 As regards budgetary allocation for ICPS and utilization thereof is concerned, the Committee
observes that there was decrease in allocation for this scheme from `43.00crore in BE 2011-12 to
`186.40 crore at the RE stage. Expenditure incurred in the 2011-12 was `158.91 crore Reasons
given by the Ministry for decline in allocation at the RE stage in 2011-12 were lower number
of States submitting viable financial proposals (seven States did not submit financial proposals in
2011-12); delivery structure not established under ICPS and recruitment of staff by States/UTs
under ICPS did not materialize in some States during 2011-12. The Ministry explained that the
scheme was complex and comprehensive one with various types of components and the States
were taking time to build a perspective. There was inadequacy of in-position staff and
infrastructure and a review of existing facilities needed to be undertaken for the implementation of
the Scheme.

5.8 The Committee feels that there is a need for stricter control and monitoring of the

Committee recommends to the Ministry to take up this matter with the State Governments
on a priority basis as the interest of the young and innocent is at stake. Further, there
should be certain criteria laid down for the selection of caretakers and staff of these
institutions. Further, the staff and other workers should be trained to deal with and take
good care of children. The Committee believes that a legislation in this regard would be a
welcome step for the running of such institutions for the welfare of children with strict
monitoring and control.
5.9 The Committee would also take the opportunity to express its concern about the
status of Child Welfare Committees and Juvenile Justice Boards spread across the country.
Feedback given by the Secretary during her presentation before the Committee confirms its
apprehensions about these bodies which work as watchdogs at the ground level. Lack of
functionaries and those in position not sensitized to the special needs of vulnerable children
clearly indicates that much needs to be done for effective implementation of the
restructured ICPS Scheme. The Committee feels that with State Commissions for Protection

18

VI. CONDITIONAL CASH TRANSFER SCHEME FOR THE GIRL CHILD WITH
INSURANCE COVER (DHANLAKSHMI)
6.1 The Scheme ‘Dhanlakshmi’ was launched on 3.3.2008. This is a Central Sector scheme
being implemented on a pilot basis in 11 blocks of 7 States viz, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand,
Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Chhattisgarh and Orissa. The scheme is aimed at eliminating discrimination
against girl child which is there at every step of her life in terms of foeticide, infanticide, little or
no access to education, health and other services as well as violence and abuse. Cash transfer is
provided to the family of the girl child (preferably the mother) on fulfilling certain conditionalties
for the girl child viz. birth and registration of the girl child, immunization, enrolment in school and
retention in school.
6.2 The Scheme is implemented by the State Governments through district authorities. From
time to time, internal review meetings were held with the States which inter-alia also discussed
the Dhanlakshmi Scheme. Certain problems flagged by the States in such review meetings for
implementation of Dhanlakshmi included the number of cash transfers being too frequent for small
amounts to make administration cumbersome, addressing issues of migration, lack of clarity about
place of birth or domicile to determine eligibility for assistance, and also the long gestation period
of 18 years for the beneficiary to receive the insured amount of `1 lakh.
6.3 Analyzing the utilization trends of the funds allocated to this scheme, the Committee finds
a very discouraging picture as given below:–

6.4 The Committee observes that even though the objective of the scheme was a noble

Literacy. The Committee believes that a study of the on-going schemes with the same

expedite the evaluation of the scheme with special emphasis on the convergence of schemes
with similar objectives and also look into the other problem-areas encountered in its

VII. RAJIV GANDHI SCHEME FOR EMPOWERMENT OF ADOLESCENT GIRLS
7.1 The Rajiv Gandhi Scheme for Empowerment of Adolescent Girls (RGSEAG)-SABLA a
Centrally Sponsored Scheme had been approved by the Government in 2010. The Scheme is being
implemented in 200 districts across the country on pilot basis. SabIa is being implemented through
State Government/UTs with 100 per cent financial assistance form the Central Government for all
inputs other than nutrition provision for which 50 per cent central assistance to States is provided.
AWC is the focal point for the delivery of the services. The Scheme aims at an all-round
development of adolescent girls of 11-18 years by making them self reliant by facilitating access
to learning, health and nutrition through various interventions such as health, education, vocational
training etc. The Scheme has two major components-Nutrition and Non-Nutrition Component.
Nutrition is given in the form of Take Home Ration or Hot Cooked Meal for 11-14 years ‘Out of
School’ girls and 14-18 years of all adolescent girls (out of school and in school girls). In the
nutrition component, the out of school adolescent girls (11-18 years) are provided IFA
supplementation, health check up and referral services, nutrition and health education, counseling/
guidance on family welfare, adolescent reproduction sexual health, child care practices, life skill
education and accessing public services and vocational training for girls aged 16-18 years.
7.2 The Committee has been given to understand that the scheme was proposed to be
strengthened and expanded in the Twelfth Plan period across the country. As per the feedback
received from States/UTs, the progress of the scheme had been quite satisfactory as far as nutrition
component was concerned. Against the target of 1 crore, the coverage of beneficiaries under this
segment had been 84.82 lakh. It was the non-nutrition component of the scheme where the pace
of implementation was slow as it involved the convergence with various line Ministries, in addition
to the limited financial resources provisioned for the various non-nutrition premises. V ocational
training had been a non-starter in most of the States/UTs as it was to be linked with the National
Skill Development Initiative (NSDI) of the Ministry of Labour. The problem-areas highlighted by
the States/UTs in establishing linkage with NSDI for vocational training were non-availability of
information on available registered VTPs, allotted modules, specific areas of training under Modular
Employable Skills (MES) identified for Adolescent Girls and non-accessibility of VPTs in close
proximity to the AWCs. The Ministry had been making efforts in the regard by holding regular
convergence meetings etc. Also, availability of limited financial resources provisioned for the
various Non-nutrition service under the scheme was a constraint in the smooth implementation of
the scheme. Hence, the scheme required strengthening and the proposed efforts to be made in the
Twelfth Plan inter-alia included:–
– revision of nutritional norms
– revision of financial norms for non-nutrition
– strengthening vocational training
– provision for performance linked incentive to AWWs & AWHs involved in
implementation of SABLA and
– evaluation and studies.

IX. INDIRA GANDHI MATRITV A SAHYOG YOJANA (IGMSY)
9.1 Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana (IGMSY) - Conditional Maternity Benefit (CMB) is
a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for pregnant and lactating women to contribute to better enabling
environment by providing cash incentives for improved health and nutrition to pregnant and nursing
mothers. It is being implemented initially on pilot basis in 52 selected districts using the platform
of ICDS. Pregnant women of 19 years of age and above for first live birth are entitled for benefits
under the Scheme. The beneficiaries are paid `4000/- in three instalments per P&L women between
the second trimester till the child attains the age of 6 months on fulfilling specific conditions related
to maternal and child health to partly compensate for wage loss to mothers/women during
pregnancy and period of lactation. The cash incentives through provision of Conditional Cash
Transfer (CCT) would not only contribute to better enabling environment but also encourage and
promote health and nutrition seeking behavior. Nearly 12.5 lakh P&L women per annum form 52
selected districts are expected to be benefitted under the scheme.

XI. RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK)
11.1 The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK), an apex micro-finance organization under the Ministry
of Women and Child Development exclusively for women was set up in 1993 for the purpose of

25
providing micro-loans to poor women through Government and Non-Government organizations,
women federations and cooperative banks. The initial corpus of `31.00 crore has grown over to
`180.00 crore due to additional budgetary allocation, credit, investment and recovery management.
Credit is provided to the poor women through Intermediary Micro financing Organizations (IMOs)
working at grass root level such as NGOs, Women Federations, Co-operatives etc. RMK has also
taken a number of promotional measures to popularize the concept of women empowerment by
way of micro-financing, thrift and credit, capacity building and marketing linkages through SHGs
format and also enterprise development for poor women.
11.2 Utilization of allocated funds for RMK was nil in the years 2010-11 and 2011-12 against the
BE of `15.00 crore and `100.00 crore respectively. The reason given by the Ministry was that the
entire provision remained unutilized because restructuring of fund as Non-Banking Finance
Company (NBFC) was under process. The idea was to restructure and strengthen RMK from
existing single office society to Government-owned Non-owned Non-Deposit Taking Systematically
Important Non-Banking Finance Company (NBFC-ND-SI) of the Companies Act with a Pan-Indian
office network backed by need-based capital. In order to provide micro finance to women
beneficiaries on soft terms and conditions of low interest rates, longer repayment period and
collateral loans, the proposed NBFC would act as a single window facilitator with backward and
forward linkages; focal point for convergence of various schemes of Central and State
Governments; facilitator of women micro-enterprise development; wealth creation and
empowerment of women’; to act as a nodal agency for national and international collaboration on
issues concerning micro finance; and to undertake consultancy and research studies. On the
directions of the Governing Board of RMK, a Committee headed by the Additional Secretary,
Ministry of Women and Child Development was set up and action initiated for engaging legal
consultants to help RMK in incorporation of Government-owned NBFC ND – SI and engage
professional consultants for new recruitment policy and regulations, manuals for credit and
monitoring accounts, audit, MIS and IT and software for accounting and MIS system.
11.3 The Committee is dismayed to note the slow pace of the restructuring exercise which is
going on since the last two years. This has led to non-utilization of funds in 2010-11 and 2011-
12 leading to no sanctioning of funds to the States/UTs. The Committee can only hope that the
revamped scheme of RMK is implemented at the earliest with its new features making it
simpler for poor women to get microfinance for their projects. The Committee recommends to
the Ministry to expedite the restructuring exercise and begin its implementation from this
financial year itself and utilize `l00.00 crore allocated for the scheme in 2012-13.
11.4 The Committee has been given to understand that two impact assessment studies of
beneficiaries of RMK had been carried out in 2007-08 and in 2010. The first agency for carrying
out the study in 2007-08 was Indian Society for Agribusiness Professionals (ISAP), New Delhi.
They covered the States of Andhra Pradesh, Orissa and Tamil Nadu and surveyed around 1000
beneficiaries. The second agency was BT Moon Consultants Pvt. Ltd, Noida which covered the
State of Rajasthan in 2010 and surveyed 200 beneficiaries. The impact of the RMK on these
beneficiaries were many like their monthly income increased which saw an increase in household
expenditure and improvement in food consumption pattern, increase in household assets, access to
medical facility, improvement in standard of living, decrease in domestic violence and improvement
in their relationship with the spouse increase in self confidence and security, increase in awareness
towards health issues, tendency to save increased etc.
11.5 The Committee is happy to note the positive outcomes of the scheme of RMK which
has led to economic empowerment of poor women in the identified States as highlighted in
the impact assessment studies. Not just this, economic empowerment of these women also
resulted in increasing their self – confidence and awareness and has given them respect and

26
a say in decision making leading to their social empowerment. The Committee believes that
in order to sustain and increase the reach of the scheme to the targeted women, sustained
efforts need to be made by the Ministry to utilize the allocated funds and closely monitor
the progress of the restructuring exercise of the Scheme.
11.6 The Committee is also of the view that a sample study of 1200 beneficiaries confined
to four States of Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan cannot be considered
an assessment in the real sense of beneficiaries scattered in 35 odd States. The Committee
would also like to point out that both Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu can be considered
to be performing well States so far as this scheme is concerned. The real test would be
benefits of the scheme reaching to most vulnerable women residing in remote and under-
developed corners of the country. The Committee would appreciate if a country-wide survey
of the Kosh by a reputed agency is carried out at the earliest.
11.7 An area of concern which the Committee has time and again highlighted is the blacklisted
NGOs implementing the Scheme. As for the action taken against earring NGOs, the Committee has
been given to understand that after blacklisting the NGOs, their details are sent to all the Central
Government Ministries/Departments, concerned State Governments and District Authorities and to
all the banks and financial institutions so that these are debarred from getting grants or loan
assistance. Their names are also displayed on the website of RMK. Also, a court case is filed under
the Negotiable Instruments Act if their cheque is dishonoured and finally legal action is taken
through recovery civil suit or arbitration wherever required.
11.8 The Committee finds that the number of blacklisted NGOs has been decreasing over the last
3 years from 69 in 2009-10 to 48 in 2010-11 and finally to 27 in 2011-12. The maximum number
of blacklisted NGOs has been from Andhra Pradesh with 12 such NGOs in the year 2011-12. The
Committee would like to emphasize again on the strict monitoring of the performance of
the NGOs implementing the Scheme through regular inspections by the State authorities
and even by the Ministry. The Committee hopes that the Ministry has started the online
monitoring of the projects sanctioned so as to identify the erring NGOs easily.

XVII. GENDER BUDGETING
17.1 Gender Budgeting is a process that entails maintaining a gender perspective at various stages
like programme/policy formulation, assessment of needs of target groups, review of existing
policies and guidelines, allocation of resources, implementation of programme, impact assessment,
reprioritization of resources etc. Gender Budgeting involves dissection of the Government budget
to establish its gender-differential impacts and to translate gender commitments into budgetary
commitments.
17.2 Analysing the total magnitude of Gender Budget, there has been a marginal increase from
5.8 per cent in 2012-13. However, if the Budget Estimates figures of 2011-12 are compared with
the Revised Estimates, the magnitude of Gender Budget shows a decline from 6.2 per cent to
5.8 per cent. Also, new schemes launched by the Ministry do not get reflected in the Gender
Budget Statement.
17.3 The Ministry has submitted that so far 56 Ministries/Department which have confirmed
setting up of a Gender Budgeting Cell/nominating a nodal person including non social sector
Ministries like Power, Petroleum, Culture, Heavy Industries, Science and Technology,
Telecommunications, Defence etc. When the Eleventh Plan recommendations were drawn up, the
estimates for the Gender Budgeting Scheme were made for (i) Grants for Research &

31
Documentation and (ii) Grants for Training. However, the requirement of budgetary support for
Gender Budgeting Scheme was found to be much smaller for the reason that the expenditure under
the GB Scheme has been incurred primarily on training and capacity building of the State and
Central Government Officials on Gender Budgeting.
The objective of Gender Budgeting work was to institutionalize the process at the Central
and State Governments in the first instance by organizing various workshops/training programmes
for Officers of Central Government Ministries/Departments and State Government Officers for
capacity building.
17.4 The Ministry of Women and Child Development has undertaken several initiatives by
emphasizing on and advocating for setting up of gender budgeting structures/mechanisms in all
Ministries/Departments, capacity building to undertake gender mainstreaming of policies/schemes/
programmes and initiating the exercise of gender auditing. The Gender Budgeting Scheme of the
Ministry provides support for organizing workshops and disseminating the concepts, strategies and
tools of Gender Budgeting to the Central Government Ministries/Departments, State Government
Departments and State Commissions for Women, State Institutes of Rural Development etc. and
preparation of resource/training manuals for facilitating adoption of Gender Budgeting by various
stakeholders. The Scheme also provides for setting up a Gender Budgeting Bureau in the Ministry.

objective should have been undertaken before launching the scheme. Inputs from the
schemes administered by other Ministries and State Governments should have been taken
and a way of convergence of such schemes could have been found out. This would have
helped in strengthening the on-going schemes for welfare of the girl child and also avoided
over-lapping and duplication. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to
expedite the evaluation of the scheme with special emphasis on the convergence of schemes
with similar objectives and also look into the other problem-areas encountered in its
implementation. (Para 6.4)
VII. RAJIV GANDHI SCHEME FOR EMPOWERMENT OF ADOLESCENT GIRLS
The Committee is happy to note that the new scheme for the empowerment of
adolescent girls is yielding good results, especially with respect to the nutrition component.
Proper nutrition for the adolescent girls is of vital importance for their state of health,
hygiene of the girl-child and family and child care. The non-nutrition component, on the
other hand, needs to be strengthened as envisaged by the Ministry because of the slow pace
of implementation. The Committee is of the view that the non-nutrition component of the
scheme is very important for the development and empowerment of adolescent girls. This
component would help the adolescent girls to upgrade their home-based skills, life skills and
even vocational skills. It would make them aware about the existing public services and
even guide them in times of need. The Committee hopes that this component is
strengthened so as to benefit maximum number of adolescent girls. (Para 7.4)

40
Committee’s attention has also been drawn to the fact that the allocation of `750.00
crore for this scheme has not witnessed any increase in the year 2012-13. The Committee
wonders as to how the Ministry would cope up with such revisions in nutrition and non-
nutrition component without additional funds. The Committee hopes that the Ministry does
not face any fund constraint for this upward revision in norms. Further, the Committee
takes note of the fact that the States need to put in place system of implementation and
monitoring of the Scheme. The Committee hopes that the initial difficulties in the
establishment of required structures and system would be overcome at the earliest for the
successful implementation of the scheme so that the target set for extending benefits to
1 crore adolescent girls in the year 2012-13 is achieved by the Ministry and the budgetary
allocation of `750.00 crore for the year 2012-13 is judiciously utilized. (Para 7.7)
The Committee would like to point out. that since A WC is the focal point for
implementation of the SABLA Scheme and AWWs and AWHs are to be the agents of the
implementation process, the delivery structure is already present in the States/UTs. The
Committee, however, wonders whether the space in the AWC would be enough for delivery
of services, especially the non-nutrition component of the scheme. There are many AWCs
where there is no building or Kutcha building or being run from a school etc. Also, the
AWWs and AWHs already perform various tasks under the ICDS Scheme and get a meagre
honorarium. The Committee feels that these functionaries of the ICDS Scheme should only
be given additional duties if their honorarium is increased in proportion to the tasks
assigned to them. The Committee, therefore, reiterates that honorarium of the AWWs and
AWHs should be increased and focus of the Ministry should be on the development of
infrastructure of the AWCs as these would be the agencies for the implementation of not
just ICDS Scheme but also the SABLA Scheme. (Para 7.8)
VIII. HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN
The Committee is dismayed to find that even after the revision of the scheme, there
is no good response from the States/UTs for constructing working women’s hostels. In the
year 2011-12, only one viable proposal was received from the Government of Kerala for
construction of working women’s hostels at Vanchikkavala, Idukki District by Kerala State
Women’s Development Corporation Ltd. The reason for a lackluster response is attributed
mainly on the condition of the revised scheme of providing financial assistance for
construction of hostel building to be given only on public land. The Committee believes that
this condition limits the scope of the scheme. Getting public land for the construction of
hostels for women is not easy. The Committee takes note of the fact that the Ministry has
also admitted that it might be necessary to re-consider the decision to allow construction
only on public land. The Committee believes that the Ministry has ruled out the
contribution and participation of the private players. In the age of Public-Private
Partnership (PPP) model, this condition laid down by the Ministry is not in tune with the
practices of the present times where contribution and participation of the private sector in
the development processes cannot be ignored. The Committee, therefore, recommends to
the Ministry to rethink and review the revised features of the scheme in consultation with
the States/UTs to come to a solution as early as possible to remove the stumbling blocks
in the effective implementation of the scheme. (Para 8.4)
IX. INDIRA GANDHI MATRITV A SAHYOG YOJANA (IGMSY)
The Committee observes that this Scheme is yet to take off properly. The Committee
finds that the preparatory exercises are also not complete and a lot needs to be done by

41
the States/UTs to establish the required structures and complete various procedural
formalities. The Committee hopes that the year 2012-13 being the third year of the Scheme
witnesses the completion of all formalities and establishment of the structures so that the
scheme is implemented in a proper and efficient manner benefiting pregnant and lactating
women in the 52 districts. With the successful implementation of the Scheme, it may then
be launched across the country. Given the poor state of health of pregnant and lactating
mothers leading to malnourished and anaemic women and children, the scheme assumes a
great importance for providing incentive to pregnant and lactating women for their
improved health and nutrition. (Para 9.3)
The Committee finds that the objective of the Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana
of the Ministry of Women and Child Development is similar to the objective of the Janani
Suraksha Yojana of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Even though there are few
difference in the components of the two schemes, both aim to provide cash incentives to
pregnant and lactating mothers for their improved health and nutrition and also safe
motherhood. The Committee believes that with some kind of co-ordination mechanism
between the two Ministries, a convergence of the scheme for the welfare of pregnant and
lactating women could be made. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to
make conscious efforts for convergence of the two schemes administered by two different
Ministries to avoid duplicacy and ensure the reach of benefits to the targeted women. Both
these schemes are to be handled by AWWs and ANM functionaries of AWCs and Primary
Health Centres respectively. Here Panchayati Raj Institutions can play a very vital role, not
only in supervision of the schemes to ensure their effective implementation but also benefit
the targeted beneficiaries. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that all the
stakeholders and implementing agencies at the ground level may devise a mechanism for
providing maximum benefit to needy mothers. (Para 9.5)
X. SUPPORT TO TRAINING AND EMPLOYNMENT PROGRAMME (STEP)
The Committee observes that this scheme of the Ministry is quite old and inspite of
a revision of norms in order to strengthen the implementation and monitoring of the
scheme, the state of affairs with respect to submission of proposals by the States/UTs is
disturbing in the year 2011-12 which led to reduction of allocation at the RE stage. Not just
this, the trend of utilization with respect to the scheme in 2011-12 has been abysmally low
as the expenditure incurred was only `1.43 crore out of `11.50 crore. The Committee notes
that inspite of broadening the scope and coverage of the scheme by introducing the locally
appropriate sectors of employment and income generation for women, the Scheme has failed
to revive. The Committee is of the view that an evaluation of the Scheme needs to be done
with special focus on the problems areas, if any, with the formulation of viable proposals
at the level of States/UTs and other implementation issues. (Para 10.4)
The Committee believes the STEP Scheme needs to be revitalized and popularized in
the States/UTs not submitting any proposals or submitting non-viable proposals. States/UTs
need to be reminded and directed about the proper formulation and submission of the
project proposals for the scheme. The Committee would like to emphasize upon the
concerted efforts to be made by the Ministry for reinvigorating the STEP Scheme in co-
ordination with the States/UTs because this Scheme is the only hope for ensuring
sustainable employment for the poor and asset less rural and urban women across the
country. (Para 10.6)

42
XI. RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK)
The Committee is dismayed to note the slow pace of the restructuring exercise which
is going on since the last two years. This has led to non-utilization of funds in 2010-11 and
2011-12 leading to no sanctioning of funds to the States/UTs. The Committee can only hope
that the revamped scheme of RMK is implemented at the earliest with its new features
making it simpler for poor women to get microfinance for their projects. The Committee
recommends to the Ministry to expedite the restructuring exercise and begin its
implementation from this financial year itself and utilize `100.00 crore allocated for the
scheme in 2012-13. (Para 11.3)
The Committee is happy to note the positive outcomes of the scheme of RMK which
has led to economic empowerment of poor women in the identified States as highlighted in
the impact assessment studies. Not just this, economic empowerment of these women also
resulted in increasing their self – confidence and awareness and has given them respect and
a say in decision making leading to their social empowerment. The Committee believes that
in order to sustain and increase the reach of the scheme to the targeted women, sustained
efforts need to be made by the Ministry to utilize the allocated funds and closely monitor
the progress of the restructuring exercise of the Scheme. (Para 11.5)
The Committee is also of the view that a sample study of 1200 beneficiaries confined
to four States of Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan cannot be considered
an assessment in the real sense of beneficiaries scattered in 35 odd States. The Committee
would also like to point out that both Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu can be considered
to be performing well States so far as this scheme is concerned. The real test would be
benefits of the scheme reaching to most vulnerable women residing in remote and under-
developed corners of the country. The Committee would appreciate if a country-wide survey
of the Kosh by a reputed agency is carried out at the earliest. (Para 11.6)
The Committee finds that the number of blacklisted NGOs has been decreasing over
the last 3 years from 69 in 2009-10 to 48 in 2010-11 and finally to 27 in 2011-12. The
maximum number of blacklisted NGOs has been from Andhra Pradesh with 12 such NGOs
in the year 2011-12. The Committee would like to emphasize again on the strict monitoring
of the performance of the NGOs implementing the Scheme through regular inspections by
the State authorities and even by the Ministry. The Committee hopes that the Ministry has
started the online monitoring of the projects sanctioned so as to identify the erring NGOs
easily. (Para 11.8)
XII. RESEARCH, PUBLICATION AND MONITORING INFORMATION, MASS EDUCATION
AND PUBLICATION
It has been admitted by the Ministry that the coverage of potential beneficiaries
under the different schemes is not as much as would be desirable. Also, the awareness of
the masses and the communities in the country about the sensitivity of issue concerning
women and children is low. The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for
having a re-look of different activities to be undertaken for better implementation as well
as expanding the reach of different schemes. (Para 12.2)
XIII. ONE STOP CRISIS CENTRE
The Committee feels that merger of the schemes Swadhar and Short Stay Homes is
a wise step. But the Committee is quite concerned to note that while on the one hand two

43
already existing schemes are proposed to be merged for more visible impact, the Ministry
proposes to launch another new scheme. The Committee is of the view that Swadhar Grehs
equipped with all envisaged facilities can very well serve as a One Stop Crisis Centre. It
is all the more intriguing as already an exercise to consolidate two similar schemes is on,
another new scheme with similar objectives but with a new fancy name is in the offing. The
Committee would like to point out that any new component of the news scheme, One Stop
Crisis Center can be easily made part of the scheme of ‘Swadhar Greh’. (Para 13.3)
XIV. IMPLEMENTATION OF PROTECTION OF WOMEN FROM DOMESTIC VIOLENCE
ACT
Under this new scheme proposed to be launched during the Twelfth Plan, the Central
Government will support States in the appointment of independent Protection Officers by
sharing their salary with the State Governments on 50:50 basis. It will also provide support
for establishing Counselling Facilities/Family Counselling Centres and infrastructural
support like transport and mobile costs. While appreciating the initiative taken by the
Ministry, the Committee views with serious concern the non-implementation of the
Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act even after six years of its enactment. The
Committee hopes that now with the support of the Central Government, things would
finally be in place. The Committee would, however, appreciate if a well-structured
monitoring mechanism is also activated from 2012-13 itself otherwise at the ground level
scenario would remain grim for victims of domestic violence. (Para 14)
XV. CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AUTHORITY (CARA)
The Committee finds that the number of in-country adoptions has increased and has
picked up since 2010. As for inter-country adoptions, the numbers do not seem to be
encouraging. The Committee is of the view that the number of adoptions would increase
considerably if a simple system is put in place for prospective parents without much hassle.
The decreasing number of inter-country adoptions may possibly be due to reasons like
delays in completion of legal and procedural formalities before adopting the child. The
Committee believes that the whole process of adoption, be it in-country or inter-country
should be made parent-friendly and child-friendly. The facilitators of the adoption process
should be sensitive to the sentiments and vulnerability of orphaned or abandoned children
and also of prospective parents. (Para 15.3)
The Committee would like to highlight that the unauthorized adoption agencies
functioning in the country need to be controlled. Children of some of these institutions
often become victims of trafficking, illegal adoptions and even child labour. The Committee
believes that stern measures need to be taken by the Ministry and CARA against such
unauthorized institutions functioning at present. Either they should mandatorily register
themselves with the State Government or face penal action. This would certainly help in
curbing the instances of harassment and exploitation of children staying in these
institutions. (Para 15.5)
XVI. NA TIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
The Committee observes that cases of domestic violence followed by police apathy top
the category of complaints received by NCW. Complaints related to Domestic Violence are
forwarded to concerned authorities under the provisions of the Protection of Women against
Domestic Violence Act, 2005. Inspite of the implementation of the said Act, the situation

44
seems to be grim for women who face domestic violence. The Committee has been
emphasizing upon the proper implementation of the Act with respect to the proper structure
to be put in place along with the required manpower. The Committee notes that during the
Twelfth Plan period, a new scheme is proposed to be launched whereunder State
Governments will be provided assistance for appointment of Protection Officers and
infrastructural support. The Committees feels that with such an initiative, victims of
domestic violence will be getting the required relief. (Para 16.3)
Apart from this, the Committee believes that a vigorous awareness generation
programme through various mediums, especially television should be undertaken by NCW
with special focus on offices, colleges/universities, schools etc. Women need to know their
rights and the platform for redressal of their grievances. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that a pro-active role should be played by NCW not just for redressal of
complaints but also their prevention. Secondly, short-training and awareness generation
programme should be organized by NCW for the police personnel in various States/UTs so
as to sensitize and educate the police personnel about the assistance to be given to women
who are victims of various crimes etc. (Para 16.4)
The Committee appreciates the efforts of NCW in addressing the issues/problems
pertaining to NRI marriages. The Committee finds that maximum number of such cases
were reported from Delhi, U.P., Haryana, Punjab. The Committee believes that NCW
should organize awareness programmes with respect to rights of women married to NRIs
and the redressal mechanism available if they found themselves in any kind of trouble.
(Para 16.6)
XVII. GENDER BUDGETING
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Women and Child Development
being the nodal agency for gender budgeting should focus upon the proper allocation to
and full utilization of funds for the Gender Budgeting Scheme. Gender Budgeting as tool
for translating the gender commitments into budgetary commitment is gaining wide
importance in recent times. The Committee observes that the allocation for this scheme
is quite small. Not only this, the allocation has witnessed a decreasing trend in 2010-11
and 2011-12 and only `1.00 crore was allocated in both these years. The Committee,
therefore, recommends to the Ministry for making conscious efforts for utilization of
funds for the scheme. Not just this, emphasis of the Ministry should be on mainstreaming
of gender concerns which is the objective of the Ministry in its Strategic Plan Document
in 2011-12. (Para 17.6)
XVIII. CONVERGENCE OF SCHEMES
It has been the observation of the Committee that there are several schemes for
both women and child welfare administered by different Ministries or State Governments
with similar objectives. This leads some times to over-lapping of schemes which may not be
fruitful for the targeted beneficiaries. The Committee feels that there is a need for
convergence of schemes with more or less same vision and objectives. Co-ordination with
different Ministries/Departments at various levels is required for such convergence of
services with various schemes/programmes of the different implementing Ministries. This
convergence of services would help in better delivery of services and also help in increasing
the out-reach of the schemes to more number of beneficiaries. The Committee, therefore,
recommends to the Ministry to have an evaluation done of its schemes which may require

45
convergence and co-ordination with schemes of other Departments/Ministries/State
Governments and make concerted efforts in this regard. (Para 18.1)
XIX. CONCLUSION
The year 2012 marks the start of the Twelfth Plan and the end of the Eleventh Plan
Period. The Committee finds .the performance of the Ministry to be satisfactory in the
Eleventh Plan Period with respect to utilization of funds. However, there are many
challenges before the Ministry to be focused upon in the Twelfth Plan, fund constraints in
view of projected requirement being one of them. The Ministry would be striving towards
restructuring of ICDS and strengthening of the on-going programmes for efficient service
delivery and meeting the set physical targets. The Committee would like to emphasize upon
vigorous monitoring of the process of implementation of the schemes. The Committee hopes
that the Ministry being the nodal agency for Women and children would be sensitive to the
needs and interests of these vulnerable sections and cater to them with an efficacious
system for delivery of services. (Para 19)
