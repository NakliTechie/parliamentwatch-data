REPORT 
1.1 The affirmative action by the Governments in a welfare State, particularly through 
reservation, is an intervention designed to address historical and ongoing socio-economic 
backwardness and discrimination experienced by certain groups in society, to promote socio-
economic equality by providing increased opportunities and representation and thereby ensuring 
human and overall national development. Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution, enacted 
through the 93rd Amendment in 2005, empowers the State to make special provisions for 
socially and educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes (SCs), and Scheduled Tribes 
(STs) in admissions to educational institutions, including private ones, except minority 
institutions. This provision is a significant step toward addressing historical inequalities and 
promoting inclusive education by enabling affirmative action policies, such as reservations, to 
ensure equitable access to opportunities for marginalized communities. The rationale for its 
implementation lies in addressing deep-rooted social inequalities perpetuated by historical 
disadvantages faced by these communities. By enabling affirmative action through reservations, 
Article 15(5) seeks to ensure equitable access to education, a critical tool for socio-economic 
mobility, thereby fostering inclusivity and reducing disparities in opportunities. This provision 
aligns with the Constitutional commitment to social justice, aiming to level the playing field for 
groups marginalized by caste and socio-economic factors, thus promoting their integration into 
mainstream educational and professional spheres. 
1.2 The implementation of Article 15(5) is further justified by the need to correct systemic 
barriers that have historically limited access to quality education for SCs, STs, and OBCs. It 
recognizes that merit cannot be judged solely on academic performance without accounting for 
structural inequalities like lack of access to resources, quality schooling, or socio-economic 
support. By mandating reservations in Central and Private educational institutions, as seen in the 
Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006, the provision ensures that 
these communities can access higher education and compete in an increasingly competitive job 
market. Despite challenges like debates over merit and institutional capacity, the rationale 
remains rooted in creating a more equitable society where education serves as a catalyst for 
empowerment and social transformation. 
1.3 In view of the above, the Committee heard the views of Department of Higher Education, 
Ministry of Education, Vice-Chancellors of Ashoka University, Sonipat, O.P. Jindal Global 
University, Sonipat, Shiv Nadar University, Greater Noida and Birla Institute of Technology and 
Science (BITS), Pilani etc. on 5thAugust, 2025. The Committee also heard the representatives of 
Azim Premji University, Bengaluru and Manipal Academy of Higher Education (MAHE), 
Manipal, Karnataka during its Study Visit to Bengaluru, Karnataka on 27thDecember, 2024 on 
the subject.

2. Views of the Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education on Implementation 
of Article 15 (5) of the Constitution:- 
2.1 During the meeting of the Committee on 5th August, 2025, the Secretary, Department of 
Hogher Education informed that the Constitution (Ninety-third Amendment) Act, 2005, adjoined 
a sub-clause 5 to Article 15, which states “ Nothing in this article or in sub-clause (g) of clause 
(1) of article 19 shall prevent the State from making any special provision, by law, for the 
advancement of any socially and educationally backward classes of citizen or for the Scheduled 
Castes or the Scheduled Tribes in so far as such special provision relate to their admission to 
educational institutions including private educational institutions, whether aided or unaided by 
the State, other than the minority educational institutions referred to in clause (1) of article 30.)” 
 
2.2 Accordingly, the Parliament enacted The Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in 
Admission) Act, 2006 (notified on 4th January, 2007) and the Central Educational Institutions 
(Reservation in Admission) Amendment Act, 2012 (notified on 20th June 2012), to provide for 
reservation in admission of the students belonging to the Scheduled Castes, the Scheduled Tribes 
and the Other Backward Classes of citizens, to certain Central Educational Institutions 
established, maintained or aided by the Central Government, and for matters connected therewith 
or incidental thereto. 
2.3 The Parliament also enacted the Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in 
Teachers’ Cadre) Act, 2019 to provide for reservation of posts in appointments by direct 
recruitment of persons belonging to the Scheduled Castes, the Scheduled Tribes and the Other 
Backward Classes and the economically weaker sections, to teachers’ cadre in certain Central 
Educational Institutions established, maintained or aided by the Central Government, and for 
matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. “Central Educational Institutions”, as defined 
in these two Acts, means:- 
(i) a university established or incorporated by or under a Central Act; 
(ii) an institution of national importance set up by an Act of Parliament; 
(iii) an institution, declared as a deemed University under section 3 of the 
University Grants Commission Act, 1956 (3 of 1956), and maintained by or 
receiving aid from the Central Government; 
(iv) an institution maintained by or receiving aid from the Central Government, 
whether directly or indirectly, and affiliated to an institution referred to in 

3 
 
clause (i) or clause (ii), or a constituent unit of an institution referred to in 
clause (iii); 
(v) an educational institution set up by the Central Government under the 
Societies Registration Act, 1860(21 of 1860); 
2.4 Department has also informed that as per the above definition, Reservation Policy 
stipulated by the Government of India is followed only in Universities/Institutions given as 
under:- 
(i) Central Universities (CUs)  
(ii) Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs),  
(iii)Indian Institutes of Information Technology (IIITs),  
(iv) Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs),  
(v) National Institutes of Technology (NITs),  
(vi) Indian Institute of Engineering Science and Technology (IIEST) Shibpur,  
(vii) Indian Institutes of Science Education and Research (IISERs), and  
(viii) All Centrally Funded Institutes (CFIs).  
2.5 As informed by the Department, the Private Universities, which are established by the 
Act of State Legislatures, are not qualified as Central Educational Institutions.Reservation Policy 
stipulated by above mentioned Acts is not applicable in following Universities/Institutions:- 
(i) Universities established by the State Government (Reservation as per 
State Government Act shall be applicable here); 
(ii) University whether aided or unaided by the State Government 
(Reservation as per State Government Act shall be applicable here); 
(iii) Institution deemed to be University declared by the Central 
Government under Section 3 but not receiving grants-in-aid (fully or 
partially) from the Central Government, whether directly or indirectly; 
(iv) Minority Educational Institutions (under Article 30 (1) of Indian 
constitution) 
2.6 Department has further informed the Committee that Ashoka University was established 
under the Haryana Private Universities Act, 2006 vide notification no. Leg. 24/2014 dated May 
2, 2014.  As Education is in the concurrent list of Indian Constitution, it is also the responsibility 
of the State Government to make suitable legislative provisions for providing reservation in the 
Higher Educational Institutes established by them. Private Universities are also established by 
the Act of State Legislature. Institutions of Eminence deemed to be Universities (Birla Institute 

4 
 
of Technology & Science Pilani; O. P. Jindal Global University and Shiv Nadar University) do 
not receive grants from the Central Government. In view of this above, the Central Government 
has not made any statutory provisions for implementing Article 15 (5) of the Indian Constitution.

2.8 In response to another question asked by the Committee regarding consideration of social 
composition of students in NIRF framework , the Department of Higher Education has informed 
that the National Institutional Ranking Framework (NIRF) includes social-caste composition as a 
key element under its "Outreach and Inclusivity" parameter, which carries 100 marks and 
contributes 10% to the institution’s overall ranking score. Under this parameter, a sub-parameter 
which carries 20 marks, evaluates how many students from socially and economically 
disadvantaged backgrounds receive full tuition fee reimbursement from Institutional funds, 
Central/State Government schemes and Private sponsorships. If 50% students of the total student 
intake receive such reimbursement, the institution earns full 20 marks in this sub-parameter. 

5 
 
2.9 Further, while replying to a question asked by the Committee regarding use of social-caste 
composition in National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC), the Department has 
informed that the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC) takes into account the 
social-caste composition of the institution in its accreditation framework. In the existing Revised 
Accreditation Framework (RAF), NAAC includes the following components:  
i. Percentage of seats filled against reserved categories (SC, ST, OBC, etc.) as per 
applicable reservation policy for the first year admission year-wise during the last five 
years; 
ii. Number of actual students admitted against the reserved categories in the first year of the 
programme year-wise during the last five years; and 
iii. Total number of seats earmarked for reserved category as per GOI or State Government 
rule and number of seats filled against reserved category year-wise during the last five 
years. 
 
2.10 Apart from this, the University/HEI is also required to upload the specific document as per 
description given below: 
i. Institutional data in the prescribed format (data template); 
ii. The relevant information in institutional website as part of public disclosure; 
iii. Copy of the letter issued by the State Government or Central Government indicating the 
reserved categories (SC, ST, OBC, Divyangjan, etc.) to be considered as per the State rule 
(Translated copy in English to be provided as applicable); and  
iv. Final admission list indicating the category as published by the HEI and endorsed by the 
competent authority.

3.1.2 In response to a question asked by the Committee, whether BITS, Pilani follows any 
policy of reservations for the SC, ST, and/or OBC communities in admission of the students and 
appointment of teaching staffs, it was informed that the BITS Pilani does not follow any 
reservation policy in student admissions or faculty recruitment. As per Clause 15.0 of the UGC 
(Institutions of Eminence, Deemed to be Universities) Regulations, 2017, the applicability of 
reservation policies pertains specifically to public IoEs and does not extend to private unaided 
institutions such as BITS Pilani. In accordance with the autonomy granted under the Institutions of 
Eminence (IoE) framework, admissions and faculty appointments at BITS Pilani are conducted 
strictly based on academic credentials, through transparent, competitive, and identity-neutral 
selection processes.

3.7 The Committee notes that in the case of Ashok Kumar Thakur vs. Union of India 
(2008) the validity of the 93rd Constitutional Amendment, which introduced reservations 
for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in educational institutions, was examined. The 
Supreme Court upheld the Constitutional validity of the Amendment and the Central 
Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006, which provided for 27% 
reservation for OBCs in central educational institutions. The court also emphasized the 
need to exclude the "creamy layer" within the OBCs from these benefits to ensure that 
benefits reach the most disadvantaged sections of OBCs and balanced the need for social 
justice through reservations with the principle of merit. By upholding this reservation, the 
Supreme Court of India reinforced the State’s power to implement affirmative action for 
Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs). Further, in IMA v Union of India, 
the Supreme Court on May 12, 2011, upheld Article 15 (5) for private unaided non-
minority educational institutions. Finally, in Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v 
Union of India, on Jan 29, 2014, the Supreme Court for the first time explicitly upheld the 
entirety of Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution. It is clear therefore that reservations for 
Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Socially and Educationally Backward Classes of 
citizens in private educational institutions is constitutionally permissible. 
3.8 The Committee notes that as per the All-India Survey of Higher Education (AISHE) 
2021-22, there were 685 Government managed Universities/University-level institutions 
(i.e., 240 Central Government institutions and 445 State Government institutions) and 473 
private un-aided Universities. Since then, the number of private universities has only 
grown, with the UGC currently counting 517 private universities in the country.  Among 
degree colleges, according to the AISHE Report 2021-22, only 21.5% of India’s 45,473 
colleges are government institutions, 13.2% are private aided institutions, and 65.3% are 
private unaided institutions. The Committee observes that in view of these numbers, as well 
as the country’s growing population and number of students and the NEP’s targets for 
50% GER in higher educational institutions, it is clear that the public sector educational 

19 
 
institutions will be unable to accommodate all students and that private HEIs shall play a 
vital role in accommodating students from each category of society. 
3.9 The Committee reaffirms its observation from its 364th Report on the Demand for 
Grants of the Higher Education Department 2025-26, where it noted that “there is an 
increasing trend of higher education in India being seen in two separate spheres–one is that 
of private and select Government institutions with better faculty and resources, and the 
other are the majority of the HEIs which are not able to keep pace with the former.” The 
provision of reservation for SCs, STs, and OBCs in public higher educational institutions 
(HEIs) and the absence of such provision in the private HEIs may result in students from 
these communities disproportionately taking admission in public institutions that are not 
able to keep pace. The Committee strongly emphasizes that education must be a key 
instrument of attaining social justice in this country, and notes that the current absence of 
reservations in private HEIs may be an impediment to the same.  
3.10 The Committee notes that the fees per annum as provided by private universities are 
substantial and that students from marginalized sections of society may not be able to 
afford these fees. The Committee, therefore, feels that it is imperative for the State to take 
affirmative steps through legislation to accommodate students from these categories and to 
fully implement Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution. 
3.11 The Committee notes that Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution explicitly empowers 
the State, as defined in Article 12 of the Indian Constitution, to include private aided and 
unaided institutions of higher education in the scheme of reservations for SCs, STs, and 
SEBCs. However, private educational institutions are currently not legally obliged to 
implement reservation policies since there is no statute mandating them to do so. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution be 
implemented in full across the country through legislation by Parliament. The Committee 
recommends that 27%, 15% and 7.5% seats should be reserved for OBCs, SCs, and STs 
respectively in private higher educational institutions. 
3.12 The Committee however notes that in the interest of fairness, any introduction of 
reservations for SCs, STs, and OBCs in private HEIs must be fully covered financially by 
the Government. The Committees notes that the introduction of 25% quota in private 
schools under Section 12(1)C of the Right to Education Act is fully reimbursable by the 
schools from the Government – this is the model which the Government must follow in 
private HEIs as well.

3.14 The Committee also observes that expanding reservation will require increasing 
seats. However, many private institutions lack infrastructure like classrooms, hostels, 
faculty, etc., to accommodate additional students from SC, ST and OBC communities 
without compromising quality and reduction in General category seats. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that Central and State Government should allocate dedicated funds 
for private Educational Institutions/HEIs to increase seats, build infrastructure, and hire 
faculty in institutions implementing reservations. The Central and State Government 
should also supplement resources through Public Private Partnerships (PPP) in education. 
The Department of Higher Education through Higher Education Funding Agency (HEFA) 
should provide low-interest loans to HEIs/ Universities for infrastructure expansion, 
ensuring no reduction in General category seats to avoid reducing General category 
opportunities.

therefore, strongly recommends enforce strict anti-discrimination policies/laws in 
institutions with grievance redressal cells to address caste-based issues. Also, sensitization 
programs for faculty and students in this regard should be conducted regularly.   
 
3.17 The Committee further observes that many students from marginalized sections 
especially in rural and remote areas are unaware of reservation benefits or they lack access

Committee also recommends that the Department undertake an awareness campaign

among these communities to ensure wider dissemination of the opportunities available to

3.18 The Committee also recommends the Department to enforce the creamy layer 
principle for OBCs while implementing Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution, to ensure 
that benefits reach the most disadvantaged OBCs. Further, income and eligibility criteria 
should be updated regularly. 
 
3.19 The Committee observes that Article 46 of Indian Constitution directs the State to 
promote educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes 
(STs), and other weaker sections of society and Article 15(5) serves as a tool to achieve this 
constitutional mandate. The Committee, therefore, recommends that implementation of 
Article 15(5) should be aligned with Directive Principles of State Policy contained in Article 
46 to promote holistic upliftment SEBCs, SCs, STs and EWS categories. The Committee 
also recommends the Department to take effective steps to provide reservation benefits to 
EWS category from General castes in private educational institutions to ensure holistic 
development of each section of the society.

OBSERV ATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
1. The Committee notes that in the case of Ashok Kumar Thakur vs. Union of India 
(2008) the validity of the 93rd Constitutional Amendment, which introduced reservations 
for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in educational institutions, was examined. The 
Supreme Court upheld the Constitutional validity of the Amendment and the Central 
Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006, which provided for 27% 
reservation for OBCs in central educational institutions. The court also emphasized the 
need to exclude the "creamy layer" within the OBCs from these benefits to ensure that 
benefits reach the most disadvantaged sections of OBCs and balanced the need for social 
justice through reservations with the principle of merit. By upholding this reservation, the 
Supreme Court of India reinforced the State’s power to implement affirmative action for 
Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs). Further, in IMA v Union of India, 
the Supreme Court on May 12, 2011, upheld Article 15 (5) for private unaided non-
minority educational institutions. Finally, in Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v 
Union of India, on Jan 29, 2014, the Supreme Court for the first time explicitly upheld the 
entirety of Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution. It is clear therefore that reservations for 
Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Socially and Educationally Backward Classes of 
citizens in private educational institutions is constitutionally permissible.            (Para 3.7) 
2. The Committee notes that as per the All-India Survey of Higher Education (AISHE) 
2021-22, there were 685 Government managed Universities/University-level institutions 
(i.e., 240 Central Government institutions and 445 State Government institutions) and 473 
private un-aided Universities. Since then, the number of private universities has only 
grown, with the UGC currently counting 517 private universities in the country.  Among 
degree colleges, according to the AISHE Report 2021-22, only 21.5% of India’s 45,473 
colleges are government institutions, 13.2% are private aided institutions, and 65.3% are 
private unaided institutions. The Committee observes that in view of these numbers, as well 
as the country’s growing population and number of students and the NEP’s targets for 
50% GER in higher educational institutions, it is clear that the public sector educational 
institutions will be unable to accommodate all students and that private HEIs shall play a 
vital role in accommodating students from each category of society. 
(Para 3.8) 

25 
 
3. The Committee reaffirms its observation from its 364th Report on the Demand for 
Grants of the Higher Education Department 2025-26, where it noted that “there is an 
increasing trend of higher education in India being seen in two separate spheres–one is that 
of private and select Government institutions with better faculty and resources, and the 
other are the majority of the HEIs which are not able to keep pace with the former.” The 
provision of reservation for SCs, STs, and OBCs in public higher educational institutions 
(HEIs) and the absence of such provision in the private HEIs may result in students from 
these communities disproportionately taking admission in public institutions that are not 
able to keep pace. The Committee strongly emphasizes that education must be a key 
instrument of attaining social justice in this country, and notes that the current absence of 
reservations in private HEIs may be an impediment to the same.  
(Para 3.9) 
4 The Committee notes that the fees per annum as provided by private universities 
are substantial and that students from marginalized sections of society may not be able to 
afford these fees. The Committee, therefore, feels that it is imperative for the State to take 
affirmative steps through legislation to accommodate students from these categories and to 
fully implement Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution. 
(Para 3.10) 
5. The Committee notes that Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution explicitly 
empowers the State, as defined in Article 12 of the Indian Constitution, to include private 
aided and unaided institutions of higher education in the scheme of reservations for SCs, 
STs, and SEBCs. However, private educational institutions are currently not legally obliged 
to implement reservation policies since there is no statute mandating them to do so. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution be 
implemented in full across the country through legislation by Parliament. The Committee 
recommends that 27%, 15% and 7.5% seats should be reserved for OBCs, SCs, and STs 
respectively in private higher educational institutions. 
(Para 3.11) 
6. The Committee however notes that in the interest of fairness, any introduction of 
reservations for SCs, STs, and OBCs in private HEIs must be fully covered financially by 
the Government. The Committees notes that the introduction of 25% quota in private 
schools under Section 12(1)C of the Right to Education Act is fully reimbursable by the 
schools from the Government – this is the model which the Government must follow in 
private HEIs as well. 

26 
 
(Para 3.12) 
7. The Committee observes that as per the data furnished by Department of Higher 
Education, Private HEIs have representations of 40% OBC, 14.9% SC and 5% ST students 
out of total students studying in Private Universities/HEIs. However, as per the data 
furnished by Private Universities/HEIs mentioned above, the numbers of OBC students 
admitted therein are considerably low while the numbers of SC and ST students in above 
mentioned Universities are abysmally low.  In BITS, during the year 2024-25, out of total 
students of 5,137, OBCs are about 514, SCs are 29 and STs are 4, which is around 10 
percent in case of OBC, 0.5 per cent in case of SCs and just about 0.08 per cent in case of 
STs, though some students have not declared their category.  Similarly, in O.P. Jindal 
Global University, out of total 3,181students, 28 are SCs, 29 are STs whichis less than 1 
percent. Moreover, in case of Shiv Nadar University, out of a grand total of 3,359, SCs are 
48 and STs are 29 which is 1.5 per cent and approximately 0.5 per cent, respectively.  The 
Committee therefore, recommends the Department of Higher Education to implement 
reservation quotas in educational institutions, including private ones, proportional to the 
population of SCs, STs and Other Backward Classes (OBCs). Also, the Department should 
ensure its compliance through a central oversight/monitoring body like UGC, National 
Commission for Backward Classes and SC/ST Commissions to monitoring implementation 
of Article 15(5) of Indian Constitution, ensuring these institutions report admission data 
annually. 
(Para 3.13) 
8. The Committee also observes that expanding reservation will require increasing 
seats. However, many private institutions lack infrastructure like classrooms, hostels, 
faculty, etc., to accommodate additional students from SC, ST and OBC communities 
without compromising quality and reduction in General category seats. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that Central and State Government should allocate dedicated funds 
for private Educational Institutions/HEIs to increase seats, build infrastructure, and hire 
faculty in institutions implementing reservations. The Central and State Government 
should also supplement resources through Public Private Partnerships (PPP) in education. 
The Department of Higher Education through Higher Education Funding Agency (HEFA) 

27 
 
should provide low-interest loans to HEIs/ Universities for infrastructure expansion, 
ensuring no reduction in General category seats to avoid reducing General category 
opportunities. 
(Para 3.14) 
9. The Committee observes that students from marginalized sections of the society 
often face challenges competing in entrance exams or coping with rigorous curricula due to 
poor foundational education in rural or underfunded schools. High drop-out rate among 
reserved category students in premier institutions highlight this gap. The Committee notes 
that certain universities like Ashoka University and Azim Premji University have already 
implemented orientation and bridgeprograms to support students from underrepresented 
backgrounds. Ashoka University’s Academic Bridge Program (ABP) consists of a three-week bridge 
course and year-long support during the student’s first year. The Committee recommends that 
the Department study such models and develop certain model programs (bridge courses, 
free coaching for entrance exams, etc.) that private HEIs can adopt to support effective 
implementation of Article 15(5). Similarly, the Department may also provide scholarships 
which can bear the burden of living costs in hostels to students taking admission through 
quotas in private HEIs. 
(Para 3.15) 
10. The Committee notes that caste based discrimination in premier institutions have 
been reported very often. Students admitted under reservation quota, particularly 
SC/STbackgrounds also face stigma and discrimination from peers and faculty in premier 
HEIs which negatively impacts their performance and their mental health. The Committee, 
therefore, strongly recommends enforce strict anti-discrimination policies/laws in 
institutions with grievance redressal cells to address caste-based issues. Also, sensitization 
programs for faculty and students in this regard should be conducted regularly.   
(Para 3.16) 
11. The Committee further observes that many students from marginalized sections 
especially in rural and remote areas are unaware of reservation benefits or they lack access 

28 
 
to application processes due to digital divides and bureaucratic hurdles. The Committee 
notes that certain universities, like Ashoka University in Sonepat, have implemented 
partnerships with Jawaharlal Navodaya Vidyalayas and other organisations to support 
admissions for students from underrepresented backgrounds. The Committee recommends 
the Department to collaborate with NGOs and community leaders to reach remote areas, 
ensuring students know about available opportunities and application processes. The 
Committee also recommends that the Department undertake an awareness campaign 
among these communities to ensure wider dissemination of the opportunities available to 
them in private HEIs following any law that the Parliament may pass for the same. 
(Para 3.17) 
12. The Committee also recommends the Department to enforce the creamy layer 
principle for OBCs while implementing Article 15(5) of the Indian Constitution, to ensure 
that benefits reach the most disadvantaged OBCs. Further, income and eligibility criteria 
should be updated regularly. 
(Para 3.18) 
13. The Committee observes that Article 46 of Indian Constitution directs the State to 
promote educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes 
(STs), and other weaker sections of society and Article 15(5) serves as a tool to achieve this 
constitutional mandate. The Committee, therefore, recommends that implementation of 
Article 15(5) should be aligned with Directive Principles of State Policy contained in Article 
46 to promote holistic upliftment SEBCs, SCs, STs and EWS categories. The Committee 
also recommends the Department to take effective steps to provide reservation benefits to 
EWS category from General castes in private educational institutions to ensure holistic 
development of each section of the society.  
(Para 3.19) 
14. The Committee also recommends that the Department take steps to improve the 
quality of data currently collected on the composition of the student population in private

Report of 2021-2022, i.e., three years ago. Further, as the Committee noted in its 364th
