REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
The Department of Women and Child Development was set up in 1985 as a part of the Ministry of Human Resource
Development to give the much-needed impetus to the holistic development of women and children.
The Department formulates plans, policies and programmes, moots proposals to enact and amend legislation affecting
women and children and guides and coordinates the efforts of both governmental and non-governmental organizations
working in the field of women and child development.   Apart from this, the Department implements certain innovative
programmes for women and children which are in the areas of training and capacity building, employment and income
generation, welfare and support services, awareness generation and gender sensitization. 
II          BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
            During the Tenth Five-Year Plan total    outlay for the Department is Rs. 13,780.00 crores.  Out of the total
allocation of Rs. 2653.91 crores for 2003-2004 Plan allocation for the year 2003-2004 is Rs. 2600.00 crores as against
Rs. 2200.00 crores and Rs. 2085.00 crores in BE and RE 2002-2003 respectively.  The non-plan allocation is Rs. 53.91
crores.
The Department has informed that the additional allocation of Rs. 400.00 crores in the financial year 2003-2004 is
proposed to be utilized for the following:
1.   To cover the expenditure on the ICDS programme assisted by World Bank which has been increased due to
enhancement in honorarium and restructuring of the ICDS III Project to include base cost, innovative and civil works
for 461 new ICDS blocks in four states and   provision for construction of model Anganwadi centres. Also, the project is
to be closed in September 2004 and it is expected that substantial residual liabilities   will be discharged in the financial
year 2003-2004.
2.  To meet the expenditure required for achieving the targeted expansion in number of operational projects in the ICDS
General scheme.
3.    To augment training of ICDS functionaries.
4.   To provide adequate funds under the Swashakti Project, which is scheduled for closure in May 2004 and the
performance of this Project is at peak.
5.  To give a major fillip to counselling services through upgradation and expansion of the scheme of Family
Counselling Centres at present being run by the Central Social Welfare Board.  
6.   To revise the norms for the scheme of Crèches and Day Care Centres to accommodate the escalation in cost of
provision of services due to inflation.
7.    To make provision of Rs 15 crores for Balika Samridhi Yojana against a nil provision in the BE 2002-03 as it was
understood in 2002-03 that the scheme was to be transferred to State Governments.  However, the decision of the

The Department has further informed that the projected demand for the year 2003-2004 was Rs. 3240.32 crores but the

efforts to achieve the targets both physically as well financially and also utilize the existing allocation judiciously and
should not allow any scheme either being dropped or left neglected due to this curtailment in allocation.
At the same time the Committee feels that the women and children continue to feel neglected and insecure in the
society.  In fact, they are focus of all criminal activity that takes place.  Even otherwise there are many issues that
continue to be the cause of concern affecting women and children, be they within the four walls of homes, places of
work or schools or elsewhere, that need to be addressed.  The Department should, therefore, continue to review
periodically the effect of its programmes and Schemes on these sections of the society and modify them as per the
requirements of the situation with a view to ensure welfare and security of Women and Children.

situation of last moment spending was somewhat under control in 2001-2002, and it has again surfaced in 2002-2003,
with unspent balance of Rs. 308.02 crores.  It indicates that the Department has geared itself only at the fag end of the
Financial Year, which is not a happy situation, so far as achievement of the targets are concerned. The Committee had
observed noticed the trend of last minute spending, more or less, in each year of the Ninth Five-Year Plan and
commented upon this in  earlier reports.
             The Committee notes that the trend to utilize the funds in the last month of financial year is a regular feature and
observes that it is not a healthy trend and reflects adversely on the credibility of planning to utilize about 3% of the total
allocation in 15 days or a month before the close of the financial year despite the fact that the Committee recommended
in its earlier Reports that the Department should take corrective measures to avoid last moment spending so that the
funds could be judiciously utilized.

VI        DAY CARE CENTRES
 
The Scheme of Crèches/Day Care Centres was started in 1975. It aims to provide day care services for the children (0-5
years) of casual, migrant, agricultural and construction labourers. The children of those women, who are sick or
incapacitated due to sickness or are suffering from communicable diseases, are also covered under the scheme. The
Scheme is designed to cater to low-income groups and, therefore, only those children whose parents' total monthly
income does not exceed Rs.1800/- per month are covered.  The Scheme is being implemented by the Central Social
Welfare Board through voluntary organisations and by two other National leve1 voluntary organisations namely, Indian
Council for Child Welfare and Bharatiya Adimjati Sevak Sangh' all over the country. The scheme covers 12,470 crèches
benefiting about 3.11 lakh beneficiaries.
 
Allocation for BE 2003-2004 is Rs. 18.00 crores whereas the BE and RE 2002-2003 were Rs. 12.00 crores and Rs. 8.00
crores respectively.  The actual expenditure upto Feb. 2003 is Rs. 6.75 crores.  The amount of unspent balance under the
scheme with CSWB for the year 2002-2003 as on 5
th March 2003 is Rs. 1.08 crores.  The Committee has been informed
that the budget provision in 2002-2003 was enhanced in view of the revision of schemes. However, the proposed
revision could not match availability of funds in the Tenth Five-Year Plan. Hence it was decided to restrict revision of
Schemes to upgradation of norms in line with increase in cost of delivery of services, which would be processed in

140thReportofHRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/Report140th.htm[01-07-2016 16:17:55]
2003-2004.
 
The Committee observes that the Scheme at present caters to low-income groups in respect of which income does not
exceed Rs. 1800/- p.m. This aspect needs to be looked into whether the Scheme is in conformity with minimum wages
fixed under the law. Perhaps the minimum a construction labourer say a beldar gets is more than Rs. 100/- per day. In
these circumstances, it is doubtful whether the Scheme serves target income groups or whether the children from the
prescribed income groups are available.  Probably, this could also be the cause for low utilization of funds.
The Department has informed that it has initiated necessary action for introducing ISO approved standards for Crèches. 
Consultations have been held with BIS and Experts in this regard during 2002-2003 and a working group has been set
up for standardization of services based on experience gathered from running existing crèches and need assessment
service.
The Committee feels there is tremendous need to introduce ISO standards. At the same time, it is necessary to keep
these crèches free from all kinds of infection for which utmost cleanliness is necessary as children are more prone to
infection. The Committee, therefore, recommends that top priority be accorded for maintaining cleanliness.

for those women and girls who are facing social insecurity due to family problems with a view to protect and
rehabilitate these women. 
 
The plan allocation in respect of 2003-2004 is Rs. 13.50 crores. The allocation in BE & RE 2002-2003 were Rs. 13.50
crores and Rs. 10.00 crores respectively. The allocation in BE & RE 2001-2002 was Rs. 10.00 crores whereas the
expenditure was Rs. 5.32 crores only.
 
The Department has informed that in 2001-2002 funds were released to the Central Social Welfare Board after taking
into account the unspent balances available with them and closure of certain existing Short Stay Homes. In 2002-2003,
the provision under Plan had been reduced in view of delay in settlement of accounts by certain SSHs and withdrawal of
support to certain SSHs due to non-satisfactory performance.  Out of 332 Short Stay Homes, 41 were issued show cause
notice in 2002-2003 due to unsatisfactory performance and sanction / release of grants to these Homes were suspended.
19 Short Stay Homes were discontinued / closed.  In 2002-2003 the Project Sanctioning Committee sanctioned 21 new
Short Stay Homes.  Sanction of further proposals could not be carried out as it was observed that voluntary
organizations did not have sufficient experience / expertise to run the Short Stay Homes, and many such proposals were
rejected at the examination stage.
 
While the Committee appreciates the concept of Short Stay Homes, it appears not much publicity is given to their
locations for intended users. The Committee, therefore, recommends that adequate publicity be given in this regard.
CSWB should also make special efforts to run the Scheme. Besides, the cooperation of trustworthy organizations or
NGOs could be enlisted in the field.

XV       SWAWLAMBAN
The scheme earlier known as  "Training-cum-Production Centres", has been changed to " 'Swawlamban". It provides
training to women belonging to weaker sections in the traditional and non-traditional trades  than employing them on
sustained basis. The scheme is partially funded by Norwegian Agency for International Development Cooperation
(NORAD). Emphasis on modem skills- IT related projects, medical transcription has been made and  new trades  like
canteen management, house-keeping has also been introduced. Norms for Call centre training were being finalized.
 
The Committee welcomes the new initiatives but recommends that efforts should be made to ensure that  women are
employed in various organisations and the employment, as far as possible,  should be on a regular basis. 
 
Members were of the view that the Department was replacing old courses like tailoring, embroidery etc. by  new courses
like IT in rural areas. The Committee recommends that new programmes  may be added in Urban areas, but  traditional
courses in tailoring, embriodery should continue in rural areas and gradually new courses may be added, depending on
the pace of requirements. 
 
XVI     SWADHAR
The  Scheme was launched in 2001-2002 in the Central Sector for the benefit of women in difficult circumstances.
Under the scheme, diverse projects were sanctioned for rehabilitation of widows in religious places,  Riot affected
women and children, victims of terrorist violence, mentally disturbed women  and victims of trafficking.  Besides this,
54 Helplines have been sanctioned for women in distress.
 
The Monitoring of the Scheme was being done at  three levels. Biannual Monitoring at District Level under the
chairmanship of District Magistrate, who would  submit his report to Secretary, Social Welfare/Women and Child
Development of the concerned State Government within 15 days. Secretary in-charge of Social Welfare, of the State in
consultation with other member of the committee shall meet at least once a year and monitor the project. At the Central
Level, Department of Women and Child Development shall also review the functioning of the Scheme.
           
The Committee has been informed that pension was being provided to very few `widows’.  There was a long waiting list
of aspirants.  Although the pension was granted by Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, the Committee
recommends that the Department should take up the matter with that Ministry and have this amount suitably enhanced
so that those in need could get the pension.
 
XVII    TRAFFICKING IN WOMEN AND CHILDREN
The Committee’s attention has been drawn to the problem of Trafficking in Women and Children which has
considerably increased in the recent years. The Committee recommends that preventive measures should be undertaken
in accordance with the following strategies:
Prevention of trafficking through the legal and criminal system, training of law enforcement officers,
Control and suppression of prostitution through  legal system,
Rescue and rehabilitation for women and girl victims of trafficking,
Protection for and awareness-raising among women and girls to prevent trafficking,
Enhance public awareness, particularly in relation to forced and child prostitution and other measures,
Awareness-raising among parents and guardians,
Provision of alternative employment and income-earning opportunities for women and girls, and
Through campaigns targeting parents on the long-term advantages of girls education.
XVIII   GENERAL
The Committee notes that every year new schemes are being added/ modified for the welfare of Children and Women.
However, the Committee recommends that instead of framing new schemes every year the Department should
concentrate on strengthening the existing ones with a view to enhance their utility and acceptability.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
II          BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
The Committee expresses its serious concern over reduction in allocation of funds for 2003-2004. It observes that the
Planning Commission should take a realistic view of the basic requirements of various Schemes before restricting the
allocation of funds from the projected Demands. The Committee recommends that the Department should make all
efforts to achieve the targets both physically as well financially and also utilize the existing allocation judiciously and
should not allow any scheme either being dropped or left neglected due to this curtailment in allocation. (Para 2)
             The Committee notes that the trend to utilize the funds in the last month of financial year is a regular feature and
observes that it is not a healthy trend and reflects adversely on the credibility of planning to utilize about 3% of the total
allocation in 15 days or a month before the close of the financial year despite the fact that the Committee recommended
in its earlier Reports that the Department should take corrective measures to avoid last moment spending so that the
funds could be judiciously utilized.(Para.2)
III        INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
            
The Committee notes that there has not been any significant improvement in the number of Projects, which were
operationalised.  The states of Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Uttaranchal have not shown any increase in the
number of operational projects even after gap of an year.  This shows the indifferent attitude of the State Governments
towards the operationalisation of the sanctioned projects.
            The Committee recommends that the Department must renew its efforts and approach the State Governments to
persuade them to operationalise these projects.(Para.3)
 
The Committee notes with concern the findings of the Report which indicate that the maximum benefit of the proposals
made by the Department is almost out of reach of the targeted sections of population as it is evident from the low rate of
utilization of facilities by the beneficiaries. The Department should find out the reasons for the reluctance of the people
to avail of benefits of Schemes and take remedial steps accordingly. (Para.3)
 
The Committee recommends that there is an urgent need to sanction additional ICDS projects for the uncovered
blocks/urban slums.  In addition to this more anganwadi centres should be opened and feasibility for relocation of
existing centres should be explored.(Para.3)
 
IV        WORLD BANK ASSISTED ICDS PROJECT
            The Committee takes a serious note of under utilization of resources placed at the disposal of the Department
and impresses upon the need for proper monitoring of the Schemes by both the Central and State Governments in future
and ensuring effective implementation. (Para. 4)
 
V         ICDS TRAINING PROGRAMME – UDISHA PROJECT
The Committee welcomes the revision of syllabi for job training and revision of allocation subject to `Performance
Based Review’.  However, the Committee recommends that setting up of STIs should be expedited so that the training
programmes do not suffer a setback. The States should be given time frame to set up these Institutes and the Department
should constantly monitor the progress. (Para.5)
The Committee observes that the Department should look into this inconsistency in the allocation of funds and the
targets fixed as well as realized and takes steps as are necessary to ensure harmonious combination of the resources and
the targets.  (Para.5)
