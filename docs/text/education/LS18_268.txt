I. INTRODUCTION
1.1 The Department of Women and Child Development was set up in 1985 as a part of the
Ministry of Human Resource Development to give much needed impetus to the holistic development
of women and children. It was upgraded to an independent Ministry on 30
th January, 2006. The
Ministry of Women and Child Development focuses on addressing the needs of over 70 per cent
of the people. It aims to empower women to live with confidence, dignity and economic and
educational abilities and also aims to nurture children to develop to their full potential, make them
safe and healthy in protective environment.
1.2 The Ministry is the prime mover of inclusive programmes, policies, schemes and legislations
for women and children and stands solely for securing the best interest of women and children.
Promoting social and economic empowerment of women and ensuring survival, development, care
and protection of children drive the Ministry’s agenda. Principles of equity, justice and non-
discrimination guide the Ministry’s actions concerning women and children.
1.3  The Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development, in his presentation before the
Committee on 24
th March, 2015 gave an overview of the various programmes/schemes handled by
the Ministry and achievements thereof. He apprised the Committee that the funds allocated for the
current year i.e. 2015-16 is substantially short as compared to previous year 2014-15 fund
allocation. As compared to last year’s fund allocation of `21,000 crores, the current year’s fund
allocation has been reduced to `10,287 crores which is approximately 50 per cent less than that
of previous year. However, the Secretary pointed out that the Finance Minister has announced in
this year’s budgetary speech (Para 55) that extra resources of around `1500 crores would be
provided, depending on additional resources. In accordance to the recommendations of the
14
th Finance Commission, there will be significant increase in devolution of resources to the States
and to local bodies. Hence the States/UTs will be now in a position to bear a substantial (and
increased) share of expenditure under the Centrally Sponsored Schemes. Therefore, cost sharing
ratios need to be revised appropriately.
1.4 An important issue highlighted by the Secretary was the steeply declining child sex ratio.
Child sex ratio had been declining over the last four or five censuses and particularly a steep
decline in the report of 2011 from 927 girls per 1000 boys in the age group of 0-6 years to 918
per 1000 boys had been reported. More alarming was the fact that the phenomena had spread to
areas which were not known for disparity in the child sex ratio including tribal areas and eastern
states. The trend was particularly acute in more developed areas of the country including the
metropolitan areas. The Ministry, therefore, targets this issue in right earnest.
1.5 Another important issue discussed was the launching of the new scheme Beti Bachao Beti
Padhao by the Prime Minister at Panipat, Haryana on 22
nd January, 2015 to address the adverse and
declining child sex ratio. This is a programme introduced to address the issue of decline in Child Sex
Ratio (CSR) through a mass campaign across the country and focussed intervention and Multi-
Sectoral Action in 100 focussed Districts. It is a joint initiative of the Ministry of Women and Child
Development, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and Ministry of Human Resource Development.

III.  HIGHLIGHTS OF WORKING GROUP REPORT ON CHILD RIGHTS
3.1 The Ministry informed that a Working Group on Child Rights was constituted by the
Planning Commission to recommend priorities and strategies for children in the Twelfth Five Year
Plan 2012-2017. It recommended that the Twelfth Plan represent a new “Child Rights Paradigm”
that mandates the fulfillment of children’s rights to survival, development, protection and
participation, as the foundation of human development and as the driver of faster, more inclusive
and sustainable growth. India is home to the largest number of children in the world. 19 per cent
of world’s children live in India. It is estimated that 40 per cent of children are in difficult or
vulnerable circumstances. The Report of the Working Group on Child Rights highlights the
various parameters which underlines the condition of children in India which are mentioned
below:–
● Child sex ratio (0-6 years) as per census 2011 is 914 girls for 1000 boys.
● Child mortality (under 5 mortality rate is 60 per 1000 births for boys and 69 per 1000
births for girls.
● Anaemia among children (6-59 months) is 69.5 per cent and while only 30 per cent of
boys in age-group of 15-19 years are anaemic, 56 per cent of girls in the age-group
of 15-19 years are anaemic.
● Nearly 22 per cent newly born children have Low Birth Weight (LBW) i.e. below
2.5 kgs.
● While 55 per cent of total boys are fully immunized in the age of 12-13 months only
52 per cent of girls are fully immunized.

7
● 54.5 per cent of children (9 months and above) received at least 1 dose of Vitamin-A
supplement.
● 40.5 per cent children Breast fed within 1 hour of birth.
● 47 per cent adolescent girls and 58 per cent adolescent boys of 15-19 years have less
that 18.5 kgs/m 2 of Body Mass Index.
● As per NFHS-3 data, about 11.8 per cent children are found to be engaged in work.
Amongst major States, Gujarat has the highest proportion of children working at 32 per
cent followed by Rajasthan which has 20 per cent working children.
3.2 The Ministry also submitted that an ambitious and well-articulated agenda has been set in
the area of child rights and protection in Eleventh Plan. A process of systematic transformation
towards attainment of the goal of inclusive growth has been initiated and there has been
considerable progress made. However, there is need to strengthen the institutional framework and
administrative machinery for programme/scheme implementation along with clear norms, standards
and guidelines for safeguarding the interests and rights of children. Unmet targets of the Eleventh
Plan in areas of law and scheme implementation related to child protection, malnutrition, child sex
ratio, school drop-out rates and maternal mortality, indicate that there is still a long distance to
cover, to bridge the gap between what was envisioned and what exists as of now.
3.3 Keeping in view of the present position of children in the country, the Committee
believes that efforts are required to ensure that resources are judiciously utilized towards
implementing new schemes and that right preparations should be made for a better and
efficient implementation of these Schemes. There is a need for efforts to provide flexibility
of norms to address critical needs at the community level by creating a flexi-pool of
resources and the need for funding schemes with realistic cost norms. Policy formulation,
strengthening programmes execution, enforcement of laws, plan implementation, capacity-
building and scheme monitoring are some of the areas that need immediate attention and
strengthening. Convergence between various sectoral policies and programmes that impact
the lives of children and inter-sectoral coordination are vital for bridging the shortfalls in
achievements.
IV. INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES SCHEME (ICDS)
4.1 The Ministry submitted that, for the holistic development of children, it has been
implementing the world’s largest and most unique and outreach programme of Integrated Child
Development Services (ICDS) providing a package of six services comprising
(i) Supplementary nutrition
(ii) Immunization
(iii) Health check-up
(iv) Referral services
(v) Pre-school non-formal education
(vi) Health and Nutrition Education
The Ministry further added that health related services are provided through convergence
with Health Ministry. The ICDS scheme has been universalized with 7076 ICDS Projects and
14 lakh Aanganwadi Centres (AWCs) including Mini AWCs. The scheme presently operates through
a network of about 7067 operational projects and about 13.42 lakh AWCs as on 30.09.2014.

8
4.2 In order to address various programmatic, management and institutional gaps and to meet
administrative and operational challenges, Government approved the proposal for Strengthening and
Restructuring of ICDS Scheme with an over-all budget allocation of `1,23,580 crore during
Twelfth Five Year Plan. The key features of Strengthened and Restructured ICDS inter-alia include
addressing the gaps and challenges with (a) special focus on children under 3 years and pregnant
and lactating mothers (b) strengthening and repackaging of services including care and nutrition
counseling services and care of severely underweight children (c) a provision for an additional
Anganwadi Worker cum Nutrition Counselor for focus on children under 3 years of age and to
improve the family contact, care and nutrition counseling for P&L Mothers in the selected 200
high-burden districts across the country, besides having provision of link worker, 5 per cent crèche
cum Anganwadi centre (d) focus on Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) (e) forging strong
institutional and programmatic convergence particularly, at the district, block and village levels
(f) models providing flexibility at local levels for community participation (g) introduction of APIP
(h) improving Supplementary Nutrition Programme including cost revision (i) provision for
construction and improvement of buildings of Anganwadi centres (j) allocating adequate financial
resources for other components including Monitoring and Management and Information System,
Training and use of Information and Communication Technology (ICT), (k) to put ICDS in a
mission mode etc. and (l) revision of financial norms etc.
4.3 Restructured and Strengthened ICDS has been rolled out in all the districts in three phases
as per following details:
(a) In 200 high burden districts in the first year (2012-13);
(b) In additional 200 districts in second year (2013-14) ( i.e. w.e.f.  01.04.2013) including
districts from special category States and NER;
(c) In remaining districts in third year (2014-15) ( i.e. w.e.f.  01.04.2014).

V. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS)
5.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development is implementing a Centrally sponsored
scheme “Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS)” with a view to create a safe and secure
environment for comprehensive development of children who are in need of care and protection,
children in conflict with law and other vulnerable children. This scheme is being implemented from
financial year 2009-10 mainly through the State Governments/UT Administrations. The financial
norms have been revised w.e.f. 1 st April, 2014 with enhanced financial norms.
5.2 The programme components include:–
● Support to Statutory Structures such as Child Welfare Committees (CWCs) and Juvenile
Justice Boards (JJBs).
● Institutional Services: Shelter Homes, Children’s Homes, Observation Homes, Special
Homes and Specialized services for children with special needs (disabled & children
affected by HIV/AIDS).
● Dedicated Service delivery structures at Central, State and District levels.
● Family based non-institutional care through: Sponsorship, Foster care, Adoption and
After Care Programme.
● Open shelters for children in need in urban and semi-urban areas.
● Emergency outreach service for children in difficult circumstances through childline.
● Child Tracking System including a website for missing children.
5.3 The Committee was informed that considerable progress has been made under ICPS in
establishing statutory bodies. 636 Child Welfare Committees (240 before introduction of the
Scheme) and 635 Juvenile Justice Boards (211 before introduction of the Scheme) have so far been
established all over the country. To ensure that proper focus is maintained on child protection and
quality of services is up to the mark, the scheme envisages creating a service delivery network,
exclusively for child protection which has been provided with setting up and maintenance of 34
State Child Protection Societies, 32 State Project Support Units, 32 State Adoption Resource
Agencies and 636 District Child Protection Units. Upto 31st December, 2014, under the scheme,
317 Specialized Adoption Agencies (SAAs) and 1501 Homes of various types have been funded. In
the financial year, 2014-15 proposals of 33 States/UTs and Childline India Foundation have been
considered by the inter-Ministerial Project Approval Board (PAB), set up for appraisal and approval
of the financial proposals received under the Scheme. In 2014-15, `329.50 crores have been
sanctioned and released upto 31 st December, 2014 under the Scheme as against the previous year’s
expenditure of `263.38 crores. The budget allocation for the year 2015-16 is `402.23 crores as
against the projected demand of `700 crores. The status of the Scheme with respect to budgetary

5.5 Committee’s attention has been drawn to the various concerns raised regarding the number

facilities etc. Funds are provided to the State Governments for running these open shelters by

5.6 Working Children are identified as children in need of care and protection under the Juvenile
Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2000, amended in 2006. Therefore even though the
Ministry of Labour is the nodal Ministry dealing with child labour, MWCD is also implementing a
‘Scheme for Welfare of Working Children in Need of Care and Protection’ as well under which

facilitate their entry into the mainstream education system. This programme is implemented in urban
areas not covered by the Ministry of Labour under its National Child Labour Project.
5.7 The Scheme for Welfare of Working Children in Need of Care and Protection was
evaluated by National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD) and the
Scheme was found to be achieving desired results of mainstreaming the beneficiary children.
However, subsequent to the evaluation, a Committee was set up in the Ministry to suggest
changes in the Scheme, based on outcome on the evaluation. It was recommended that, as the
Open Shelter component of the Ministry’s newly introduced scheme, ICPS provides similar
services under its Open Shelter component, albeit with better norms and procedures, the projects

phased out.
5.8 Attention of the Committee is being drawn to the increasing incidents of rampant sexual
abuse of children in orphanages/shelter homes involving in-house caretakers. The Ministry, in this

and Protection of Children) Act, 2000 which is mandatory for establishing a juvenile justice system

17
based on the principle of a child-friendly approach and is in the best interest of children. The Act
focuses on providing for proper care, protection and treatment by catering to their development
needs of the children for their ultimate rehabilitation and prevention of all kinds of abuse. The

5.9 As per Section 34(3) of the JJ Act, which provides for mandatory registration of all
institutions housing children in need of care and protection with the intent of enforcing minimum
standards of care for the services provided for children in these Homes, the Ministry has been
strongly advocating the States/UTs to identify and register all Child Care Institutions under the Act
at the earliest.
5.10 The Ministry has also enacted the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012,
under which the abuse of children by management or staff of any hospital, educational or religious
institution or a jail, remand home, protection home, observation home, or other place of custody
or care and protection is regarded as an aggravated offence with stringent punishments.
5.11 The provisions of registration of Child Care Institutions (CCIs) and their inspections
have been made stringent in the JJ Bill, 2014 that has been presented in the Parliament on
12
th August, 2014. Under the JJ Bill, 2014, the Ministry has made it mandatory for registration of
institutions whether run by State Government or by voluntary or non-Governmental organizations,
which are meant, either wholly or partially, for housing children in need of care and protection of
children in conflict with law are to be mandatorily registered under the Act within six months from
the date of commencement of the Act.
5.12 The inspection committees constituted under Section 55 of the Bill are required to conduct
visits to all facilities housing children in the area allocated, at least once in three months and submit
reports to the State Government. The Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) under Section 9 and the Child
Welfare Committee (CWC) under section 31 are required to mandatorily conduct at least two
inspection visits per month of residential facilities for children in conflict with law and children in
need of care and protection. In case of abuse of child in an institution, the JJB can order the police
for registration of FIR.
5.13 The Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS) provides for hiring of contractual staff at
State and District level. In order to appoint competent and professionally qualified personnel, the
contractual posts are to be advertised through national/local dailies and personnel recruited on the
basis of qualifications, experience, high degree of motivation and commitment to children’s causes.
Individual terms of reference (TOR) are to be drawn up for each post by the State Government.
All recruitments shall be made as per the guidelines of the respective Governments.

VII. RAJIV GANDHI SCHEME FOR EMPOWERMENT OF ADOLESCENT GIRLS
(RGSEAG)-SABLA
7.1 Rajiv Gandhi Scheme for empowerment of adolescent girls-SABLA was introduced on a
pilot basis in August 2010 and is operational in 205 selected districts across the country. SABLA
aims at all round development of adolescent girls of 11-18 years (with a focus on all out-of school
adolescent girls) by making them ‘self-reliant’ by improving their health and nutrition status,
promoting awareness about health, hygiene, nutrition, Adolescent Reproductive and Sexual Health
(ARSH), family and child care and facilitating access to learning about public services through
various interventions such as guidance and counseling as well as V ocational Training. It also aims
towards mainstreaming ‘out of school adolescent girls into formal/non-formal education. The
scheme has two major components Nutrition and non Nutrition Component. SABLA is an
intervention which addresses the health needs of Adolescent Girls(AGs) and will not only lead to
a healthier and more productive women force but will also help to break the intergenerational cycle
of malnutrition.
7.2 The Ministry submitted that nutrition is being given in the form of Take Home Ration or
Hot Cooked Meal for 11-14 years out of school girls and 14-18 years to all Adolescent Girls (out
of school and in school girls). Each adolescent girl is given 600 calories and 18-20 grams of
protein and micronutrients (which is approx. 1/3 of recommended dietary allowances) per day for
300 days in a year. The out of school adolescent girls (11-14 years) attending AWCs and all girls
(14-18 years) are provided Supplementary nutrition in the form of Take Home Ration/Hot Cooked
Meal. The nutrition provided is as per the norms for P&L mothers.
7.3 While the nutrition component aims at improving the health and nutrition status of the
adolescent girls the non-nutrition component addresses the developmental needs. In the Non
Nutrition Component, out of school adolescent girls of 11-18 years are being provided IFA
supplementation, health check-up and referral services, nutrition a nd health ed ucation , ARSH
counseling/guidance on accessing public services and vocational training (only 16-18 year old
adolescent girls.).

to be continued in 2015-16 with revised nutrition (@ `.7 per beneficiary per day for 300 days in

per annum against the present rate of `3.8 lakh/project/annum).

th July, 2013 as per the provisions of the

specific conditions related to maternal and child health to partly compensate for wage loss to
mothers/women during pregnancy and period of lactation. The cash incentives through provision

also encourage and promote health and nutrition. The Scheme is now covered under Direct Benefit
Transfer (DBT) programme and under which 16 districts have been included under Phase-I and

IX. CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AGENCY (CARA)
9.1 Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA) is an autonomous body under the Ministry of
Women and Child Development, Government of India. It functions as the nodal body for adoption
of Indian children and is mandated to monitor and regulate in-country and inter-country adoptions.
CARA is designated as the Central Authority to deal with inter-country adoptions in accordance
with the provisions of the Hague Convention on Inter-country Adoption, 1993, ratified by
Government of India in 2003.
9.2 Functions of CARA:
● To function as a nodal body in the country on matters related to adoption;
● To evolve a structure for non-institutional child care services in the country and to
standardize, streamline and implement such services;
● To build an environment that provides an opportunity for every adoptable child in the
country to find a suitable family.

24
● To frame rules and guidelines for adoption of Indian children and ensure their proper
implementation;
● To regulate and monitor adoptions and maintain a database on adoptions done in the
country;
● To prepare a centralized database of all children and PAP(s) with the help of its
associated agencies for the purpose of establishing central and state level information
pool on orphan, abandoned and surrendered children and waiting parents;
● To carry out research and documentation on adoption in order to continuously improve
the adoption system;
● To arrange capacity building programmes for adoption agencies, and other stakeholders
engaged in child welfare activities;
● To carry out advocacy, awareness and IEC activities for promoting non institutional
child care services either by itself or through its associated bodies.
● To coordinate with all State Government, State Adoption Resource Agencies (SARAs)
and support them in adoption related matters;
● To monitor the status of children placed adoption and to maintain information and
records of all such children for the purpose of follow up and root-search.
● To liaison with the other Central Authorities and foreign Missions to ensure post-
adoption follow-up and care for the adopted children.

X. RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK)
10.1 The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK), an Apex micro-finance organization under the Ministry
of Women and Child Development, exclusively for women was set up in 1993 for the purpose of
providing micro-loans to poor women through Government and Non-government organizations,
women federations and cooperative banks.  RMK extends loans through a client friendly, without
collateral and hassle-free mechanism for livelihoods, micro-enterprises, housing and family needs in
both rural and urban areas. RMK has taken a number of promotional measures to popularize the
concept of micro financing, thrift and credit, formation and stabilization of Self Help Groups
(SHGs) and also enterprise development for poor women.
10.2 RMK works as a single-window organization. Apart from giving micro-credit, it also builds
the capacities of Self Help Groups/women members and partner organizations through trainings in
financial management, enterprise development, skills and modern management practices, as well as
exposure visits and assistance in marketing. In addition, the partner organizations that take loans
from RMK are instructed to impart literacy, legal education, health awareness etc. to the women
beneficiaries to bring about their holistic empowerment. RMK views micro-finance as development
finance. RMKs micro finance programs for the socio-economic upliftment of poor women are
dispensed through NGOs/SHGs. These NGOs may be voluntary organizations, Government
autonomous bodies; Co-operative societies etc. to work as Financial Intermediary Organization
(IMOs).
10.3 The RMK has extended its IMOs network across the Nicobar Island and the border areas

XVII. GENDER BUDGETING
17.1 Gender Budgeting is a powerful tool for addressing gender based disparities and achieving
women empowerment through mainstreaming gender corners across sector. Gender budgeting
enables in maintaining a gender perspective at all stages, including formulation of policies and
schemes, allocation of policies and schemes, allocation of resources, implementation, monitoring
and review. It leads to translation of gender commitments into budgetary commitments.
17.2 A Plan Scheme for Gender Budgeting was launched in the year 2008 during the Eleventh
Plan period, for conducting, training/workshops, capacity building, research surveys etc. Under the
Scheme, inter alia , the Ministry undertakes many programmes as well as provides financial support
for the purpose. An outlay of `1.30 crore (at RE stage) was allocated for the Scheme in the year
2014-15, for conducting various training programmes and workshops on Gender Budgeting. Out of
which, an amount of `1.07 crore have been incurred till January, 2015. An amount `2.00 crore

33
(BE) has been allocated for the year 2015-16. The Committee notes that so far 57 Ministries/
Departments have set up Gender Budgeting Cells. A total of 18 States and 3 UTs have undertaken
various Gender Budgeting Initiatives.
17.3 The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Women and Child Development
being the nodal agency for gender budgeting should focus upon the proper allocation to and
full utilization of the Gender Budgeting Scheme. There has been a considerable progress
made in this area which have concerns across the Ministries both at the Central and State
level from the perspective of women specific schemes. The focus should be aimed at
strengthening and empowering the Gender Budgeting Cells to enable them to undertake
their envisaged role effectively.
17.4 Like Gender Budgeting, Child Budgeting can be considered another major initiative
in sensitizing and institutionalizing child-related concerns. For this purpose, the Committee
notes that the Ministry will have to build capacities to analyse the Central and State
budgets and their impact on the betterment children.
XVIII. CONCLUSION
18.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development was set up as a nodal agency with
the prime aim to empower and promote holistic development and welfare of women and
children who are considered the vulnerable sections of society. The Ministry has formulated
many women and child centred legislations, plans, policies and programmes to ensure
women’s empowerment as well as gender equality to the extent possible and to ensure
children’s holistic development and growth. The Committee is aware that the Ministry has
to cater to more than 70 per cent of the total population and that too the most vulnerable
ones. It, therefore, has indeed a massive responsibility. The Committee finds the
performance of the Ministry satisfactory but still a lot more needs to be done, particularly
for arresting the rising crimes against women and children. The Committee strongly feels
that the Ministry should ensure safety and security to women including at the workplace,
by ending all forms of discrimination, exploitation, harassment and violence against women
by strictly implementing the relevant laws promulgated for the purpose. Further, the
Ministry should strive towards ensuring zero tolerance to all kinds of exploitation of
children including child labour, sexual abuse and violence, by providing legislative and
institutional support. The Committee is of the view that the Ministry would find it very
difficult to fulfill the objectives of empowerment of women in all walks of life due to
massive cut in funds this year. The Committee also feels that it should have better
coordination with the State Governments and various agencies, NGOs for implementing
women/children centric schemes for more better results.

different activities to be undertaken for better implementation as well as expanding reach
of different schemes. A comprehensive advocacy and awareness generation policy and plan
of action has to be developed and implemented that will ensure public awareness about the
existing policies and programmes for women and children at all levels. Hence, the
Committee recommends that all out efforts be made to create public awareness about the
schemes/programmes of the Ministry. (Para 16.2)

41
XVII. GENDER BUDGETING
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Women and Child Development
being the nodal agency for gender budgeting should focus upon the proper allocation to and
full utilization of the Gender Budgeting Scheme. There has been a considerable progress
made in this area which have concerns across the Ministries both at the Central and State
level from the perspective of women specific schemes. The focus should be aimed at
strengthening and empowering the Gender Budgeting Cells to enable them to undertake
their envisaged role effectively. (Para 17.3)
Like Gender Budgeting, Child Budgeting can be considered another major initiative
in sensitizing and institutionalizing child-related concerns. For this purpose, the Committee
notes that the Ministry will have to build capacities to analyse the Central and State
budgets and their impact on the betterment children. (Para 17.4)
XVIII. CONCLUSION
The Ministry of Women and Child Development was set up as a nodal agency with
the prime aim to empower and promote holistic development and welfare of women and
children who are considered the vulnerable sections of society. The Ministry has formulated
many women and child centred legislations, plans, policies and programmes to ensure
women’s empowerment as well as gender equality to the extent possible and to ensure
children’s holistic development and growth. The Committee is aware that the Ministry has
to cater to more than 70 per cent of the total population and that too the most vulnerable
ones. It, therefore, has indeed a massive responsibility. The Committee finds the
performance of the Ministry satisfactory but still a lot more needs to be done, particularly
for arresting the rising crimes against women and children. The Committee strongly feels
that the Ministry should ensure safety and security to women including at the workplace,
by ending all forms of discrimination, exploitation, harassment and violence against women
by strictly implementing the relevant laws promulgated for the purpose. Further, the
Ministry should strive towards ensuring zero tolerance to all kinds of exploitation of
children including child labour, sexual abuse and violence, by providing legislative and
institutional support. The Committee is of the view that the Ministry would find it very
difficult to fulfill the objectives of empowerment of women in all walks of life due to
massive cut in funds this year. The Committee also feels that it should have better
coordination with the State Governments and various agencies, NGOs for implementing
women/children centric schemes for more better results. (Para 18.1)
