REPORT
 
I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1.      The National Health Policy  of  1983 visualised the importance of Indian Systems of Medicine and
Homoeopathy and its role in the national health care system. India has  a  large resource  of ISM&H practitioners whose
services need to be fully utilised in the national health care delivery   system. The main objective of the Department   of 
Indian Systems of Medicine and  Homoeopathy (ISM&H) is to develop and promote  the  indigenous systems of
Medicine (Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani,  Yoga  and  Naturopathy) and Homoeopathy  in an organised and scientific manner
in the country.
1.2       While deposing before the Committee, the Secretary stated that inspite of there being  quite a number of
hospitals and colleges of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy, funding for this indigenous sector has been
extremely low.   Level of acceptance has also been very low.   The Secretary   emphasised that the main thrust of the
Department would be strengthening of educational institutions.    Another thrust area was to increase the availability  of
medicinal plants.    Other areas which need special attention are prevention of patenting of Indian Medicinal
Plants/Herbs drug standardisation, modernisation of State Pharmacies, preservation of extinct plants.  
1.3       The Committee has been informed that clinical trial of a contraceptive – Pippalyadi Yoga comprising of four
plant-based drugs conducted on 2000 women during the last three years has proved successful in  ninety per cent cases.  
At present, final trial of this contraceptive  is going on.  The Committee has been given to understand that research work
under ISMH has to undergo clinical trial which is a very cumbersome and time-consuming procedure.    There is no
other alternative as clinical material is available on a large scale only in allopathic hospitals.   The Committee is of the
opinion, that due to the prejudiced approach of allopathic doctors towards ISMH, a separate body having eminent
persons from ISMH should be set up for trying the efficacy of drugs.

2.2       For the year 2000-2001, the Department has been provided Rs. 143.50 crores.  Significant increase has been
made on plan side by almost doubling the allocation of last year.  The Department has stated that   out of the Plan
Allocation of Rs. 100.00 crores for BE 2000-2001, Rs. 65.00 crores has been allocated for on-going schemes and Rs.
35.00 crores for new initiatives for quality control and standardization, for medicinal plants, and preparation of ISM&H
Drugs.  The new initiatives inter-alia  include setting up of National Board for Medicinal Plants, Innovative Scheme for

Committee would like to emphasise   that concerted efforts are required to be made for optimum and judicious

since 1997-98/1998-99 should not be delayed any further.

of different schemes/programmes during the financial year.  The Committee, however, observes that Performance

The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take corrective steps in this regard, if the
Performance Budgeting is to have any meaning.
III.       AYURVEDA
3.1       The allocation for Ayurveda under Plan in BE 1999-2000 was Rs. 12.09 crores which was enhanced to Rs. 12.54
crores at RE stage.  For 2000-2001, Rs. 13.20 crores have been provided under Plan.  On a specific query as to in what
manner, the enhanced allocation was proposed to be utilised,   the Department submitted that the increase in plan
provision in BE 2000-2001 for Ayurveda is mainly to meet increased liability of salary, NPA and capital expenditure for
construction.
3.2       The Committee was informed that there were 13 posts lying vacant in the National Institute of Ayurveda, Jaipur
for which advertisements have been given in the newspapers. The Committee notes that out of 79 teaching posts in the
Institute, 66 have been filled.   Although considerable improvement has taken place when compared to the situation few

years ago, the Committee is of the view that sustained efforts should be made to expedite the filling up of these 13
teaching posts.
3.3       Last year, the Committee had recommended that an action plan should be drawn for popularising the Ayurvedic
system of treatment in and outside India.  The Department has informed that an International Exchange Programme to
propagate ISMH abroad has been launched under which financial assistance is given for the exchange of
delegations/experts with other countries, setting up the Ayurveda & Siddha or Yoga Therapy centres for demonstration
purpose in foreign countries and organising International Conferences/National   Conferences and Seminars. The
Committee would like to emphasise that only established professionals of ISM&H should be covered under this
programme.  The Department has signed MOU with the Govt. of Russia for collaboration in the field of traditional
medicine.   MOUs with other countries are also planned. The Committee hopes  that the Department would continue
making efforts in this direction.  The Committee was also informed that the Department was  exploring the possibility of
setting up   Health Resorts in conjunction with ITDC to attract foreign tourists.  Govt. of Himachal Pradesh has shown
some interest in a  project for using existing built-up capacity in their hotels for arranging  Panchkarma treatment for
foreign tourists. The Committee hopes that there  would be more initiatives of this kind. 
3.4       The Committee notes that a number of measures have been taken by the Department for preventing misleading
advertisements regarding Ayurvedic medicines.  There is a proposal to amend the Drugs & Magic Remedies
(Objectionable Advertisement) Act to make it more effective and a Committee appointed for the purpose has submitted
its recommendations which are at present under the examination of  the Department of Health. While appreciating the
move, the Committee is of the view that the Department of ISMH should be fully  involved in the process of amending
the Act.
3.5       The Department has informed that   160 misleading advertisments have  been forwarded to various State
Governments.  6 Advertisements were forwarded to ‘Arogya Dham’ and 7 to ‘Nav Bharat Times’ as these appeared in
these magazines/ newspaper. However, no feedback from the States has so far been received. The Committee finds that
out of 160 misleading advertisements, maximum number  of advertisements are reported to be from Uttar Pradesh ,
Madhya Pradesh and Delhi.   The Committee recommends that the Department should  intensify its efforts and if
necessary, intervention at the higher level in the State Govt. be made.  The Committee is of the view that in the event of
any misleading advertisement appearing in a magazine/newspaper, contradiction by the Department should invariably
follow.
3.6       The Committee is perturbed   to note that due support to individual practitioners   is not there as, at present,
financial assistance  is being provided by the Department to Govt. Organisations and NGOs only due to limited
resources. The Committee, however, feels that  there is a genuine need for providing support to individual practitioners
doing good work for  the growth of Ayurveda or  ISM, as a whole. Department should make all efforts  to convince the
Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance to get the funds allocated for the purpose.
3.7       The Committee observes that    there   was a nominal provision  of Rs. 1 lakh as Grant-in-aid for Advanced
Ayurveda Centre for Mental Health in  NIMHANS in BE 1997-98.   However, allocation was increased to Rs. 25 lakhs
at the RE stage which was fully utilised.  In 1998-99 also, allocation of Rs. 20 lakhs was fully utilised.   However, the
Committee is surprised to note that provision of Rs. 20 lakhs in 1999-2000 had to be reduced to Rs. 1 lakh as the issue
of sharing of project cost between the Department of Health and the Department of ISMH is pending, hence, no further
grants were required to be released.    The Committee is not convinced by the explanation given by the Department and
expresses its serious concern over such an element of uncertainty in starting a new Scheme.    The Committee, therefore,
is of the view that decision in the matter  should be taken at the earliest.
3.8       The Committee observes that the Siddha System is not getting proper attention by the Department.    The
Committee notes that there has been inordinate delay in the construction of building for the  National Institute of
Siddha, Chennai.  Funds are being allocated since 1997-98 for construction.  During the year 1999-2000, construction of
boundary wall was proposed and draft project report for construction of the building   is still under consideration.    The
Committee desires that the construction of the building be initiated at the earliest.           The Committee observes that so
far Siddha System has not gained popularity specially in northern part of the country.   The Committee desires that
suitable steps  for propagation of Siddha System be taken.    Measures taken in this regard may include inter-alia
strengthening of the existing units of the Siddha system in northern parts of the country.

V.        HOMOEOPATHY
5.1       For Homoeopathy, the plan provision was Rs. 6.27 crores in BE 1999-2000 which was increased to  Rs. 6.95
crores at RE stage.  It has been substantially increased to the level of Rs. 8.20 crores in BE 2000-2001. The Department
has informed that the enhanced allocation in BE 2000-2001 is proposed to be used for salary, NPA, stipend for students,
maintenance of institutes and capital expenditure in the National Institute of Homoeopathy.
5.2       The Committee is happy to note that the Postgraduate Course in Homoeopathy has been introduced in the 
National Institute of Homoeopathy, Calcutta during the academic year 1998-99 in three subjects.  Last year, the
Committee was given to understand that the increase in the bed capacity for in-patient department of NIH, Calcutta was

action in this regard would also be taken  during 2000-2001.  The Committee would like to be apprised in this regard.
VI.       YOGA AND NATUROPATHY
6.1       For Yoga and Naturopathy, the BE for 1999-2000 on the plan side was Rs. 6.20 crores, which was reduced to
Rs. 5.83 crores at the RE stage.  Allocation for the year 2000-2001 has been brought back to the level of BE of last year
i.e. Rs. 6.20 crores.   According to the Department, the allocation of Rs. 6.20 crores would be adequate for the Central
Council for Research in Yoga and Naturopathy (CCRYN), Morarji Desai National Institute of Yoga and the National
Institute of Naturopathy (NIN).
6.2       The Committee has been informed that the Planning Commission has not supported the establishment of a
separate campus comprising of a Degree College of Yoga with an attached hospital to MDNIY, New Delhi and NIN,
Pune.   The Committee   notes that the main reason given by the Planning Commission for not supporting these
proposals is that the Central Government should not set up its own Degree or PG Courses or hospitals.  If the demand
for such courses emerge, institutions should be set up by the State Government within the State Plan as Health is
basically a State Subject.   Central Government's role should only be to provide guidance, create standards undertake
research and form linkages.  The  Committee is of the view that this matter should again be taken up with the Planning
Commission.

bodies, the Central Government will set up a suitable apex body at the central level to regulate education  in Yoga and
Naturopathy at Central level, it has been informed.    The Committee is constrained to observe that response from
AICTE is still awaited.  The Committee feels that while emphasizing for establishment of suitable   body at States level,
the Department should not wait for completion of this work and it should initiate action to set up a regulatory body at
central level. 
7.5       Regarding need to check huge amounts of donation/capitation fee being taken by some colleges under ISM&H,
it was informed the draft regulations in the matter prepared by CCIM were under consideration of the Department.    In
the case of Homoeopathy colleges, a draft regulation has already been circulated to the State Govts.   However, only a
few states have so far responded.   The Committee is of the view that finalisation of fee structure for ISMH should be
expedited so that exploitation of students by private colleges is put to an end.

IX.       NON-AVAILABILITY OF HERBS AND MEDICINAL PLANTS
            The efficacy of  most of the ISMH drugs is dependent upon the use of pure and genuine raw material obtained
from medicinal plants.  However, due to over exploitation, unscrupulous collection and denuding of forests, supplies
have been dwindling.   The Department informed the Committee that it has initiated various steps to enhance the supply
of medicinal plants.  A special scheme to provide assistance to research organisations for development of agro-
techniques has  been introduced and a  component of R&D has also been included under the scheme to achieve better
results.  The Department further informed that it has taken initiative to set up a National Board on Medicinal Plants as a
statutory body.  The Committee would like to impress upon that the proposal be actively pursued and the Board be set
up during the current year. The Committee has also noted that the Planning Commission has set up a Task force on
conservation, cultivation, sustainable use and legal protection of medicinal plants.  The Task Force has set up a ‘Core
Group’ to conduct a study of demand and supply position of medicinal plants in the country and  export.  The
Committee was informed that there is no authentic data on the demand, consumption and availability of medicinal
plants material and for this purpose the Department propose to undertake a separate study at national level .  The
Committee feels that this kind of study can be entrusted to the proposed National Board of Medicinal Plants. 
X.        INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
10.1     The Department informed the Committee that a major area of concern is the keen interest being taken by  foreign
countries in the development of herbal products and intellectual property rights.  Some of the well known medicinal
plants of India have been patented abroad and heritage of Ayurveda needs to be protected. In order to put a check on
these attempts, a Patent Cell was  set up in the Department.   The Task Force of the Planning Commission decided to set
up a `Core Group’ for consolidation of information on the botanical and the medicinal use of plants referred to in the
Ancient Texts.  The objective is to place this in the public domain- a  pre-requisite to prevent patenting of plant based
medicinal plants.   The Committee has also noted that necessary steps on creating Traditional Knowledge Digital
Library (TKDL)  to prevent the patenting of documented knowledge of ISM has been initiated. The Department has
already taken up the matter in conjunction with the Department of Industry, NIC, CSIR, ICAR and a core group has
been set up for this purpose.  
10.2     The Committee is, however, constrained to note that the Patent Cell is still functioning with inadequate
manpower and many posts are lying vacant.    The Committee has been informed that the Department’s efforts to create

XI.       PUBLICITY
It has been informed that the Department has evolved an Information, Education and Communication (IEC)
Scheme with the objective to create awareness about the efficiency of various pathies under ISM&H, their cost
effectiveness, treatment of common ailments etc. by using various media channels.  For this purpose, the Department
organises seminars and workshops, training programme at various levels for involving various health functionaries and
also uses Doordarshan and All India Radio to spread information. The Committee hopes that various IEC activities
undertaken by the Department would result in giving wider publicity to ISM&H.
XII.      COMMITTED LIABILITY
12.1     The issue of Committed Liability has been engaging the attention of the Committee since 1997-98.  At the end
of the Eight Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission had issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the
Committed Expenditure from Plan to the Non-Plan side.  A lump sum provision of Rs. 500 crores in the Ministry of
Finance Budget for 1997-98 was provided for this purpose.  As a result of this exercise, however, only a sum of Rs.150
crores was transferred from Plan to Non-Plan for the various Ministries as committed expenditure during 1997-98.  Rest
of the amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable.  The Secretary (Expenditure) while deposing
before the Committee on 27
th March, 1999 had informed that the Tenth Finance Commission had given a breathing time
till 1999-2000 for this transition.
12.2     The Department has  informed that the Planning Commission had mentioned that the outlay for the Department
had been increased from Rs.108 cores in the Eighth Plan to Rs.266.35 crores in the Ninth Plan and the outlay for Annual
Plan 1997-98 was Rs.35.30 crores which was increased to Rs. 50.00 crores in 1998-99 and Rs.59.13 crores in 1999-
2000. According to the Planning Commission the Ninth Plan outlay included provision for such incremental liabilities.
12.3     The Committee notes with serious concern that while grants for Plan Schemes are getting reduced, Non-Plan
allocation and expenditure is steadily increasing.  What is happening is that the committed expenditure which should
appropriately be charged to the Pool, is being passed on to the respective departments which is totally against the
guidelines of Tenth Finance Commission/Planning Commission.  If the committed expenditure is charged to the Pool, it
will not have any burden on the specific schemes of the Department. Secretary (Expenditure), who appeared before the
Committee last year was in full agreement   with the stand taken by the Committee.   The Committee is, however,
constrained to observe that in spite of Committee’s repeated efforts/directives to assist the Departments, no progress has
been made in the matter.   It seems that breathing time till 1999-2000 given by the Tenth Finance Commission has not
been availed of by any Department.   There is total lack of initiative on the part of the Departments.   The Committee
again  emphasises that the Department should get this matter resolved without any further delay.   The way the
Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the
Finance Commission and the recommendations made by the Committee.   This tendency, the Committee is constrained
to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.   The Committee again reiterates that the
Department should pursue the matter and report to the Committee about the outcome thereof.
XIII.     GENERAL
            The Committee notes with concern that some Departments are not furnishing the required information/papers
and Hindi version thereof sought by the Committee on time and sometimes the papers are not even properly arranged
and indexed.  This results in a lot of inconvenience to the Committee.   In such a situation the Members either get very
little time or no time at all to go through the papers and actively participate in the deliberations of the Committee. The
Committee, is therefore, constrained to observe that the communications sent to the Ministries or the Departments by
the Secretariat on behalf of the Committee should be given due attention and all efforts should be made to furnish the
requisite information/ paper to the Committee expeditiously and within the time limit.
