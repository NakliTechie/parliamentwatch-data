PREFACE
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human Resource Development,
having been authorized by the Committee, do hereby present this Hundred Ninty-ninth Report of the Committee on North
Eastern Hill University, Shillong.
2.         The Committee had earlier presented its 172
nd Report on University and Higher Education wherein the problems
and challenges before the higher education system in India have been discussed in detail.   This Report, in fact, reflected
the Committee's concern for making our educational system, both world-class as well as accessible.   While holding
consultations for 172 nd Report and also while examining a number of Bills in recent times providing for central
universities in North East, the Committee felt the need for ascertaining the special needs and problems before universities
in this region.
3.         The Committee, accordingly, visited the North Eastern  Hill University, Shillong.  The Committee held extensive
discussion with the Vice-Chancellor, Registrar, teaching faculty, non-teaching staff and the students and attempted to find
the ways and means to help the NEHU become a centre of educational excellence in the country.
4.         The Report was considered and adopted by the Committee at its meeting held on 15
th November, 2007.

November 15, 2007
Kartika 24, 1929 (Saka)

REPORT
 
            The Committee on HRD being fully conscious of the need and importance of making the higher education
available, accessible and affordable had presented its 172 nd Report on 'University and Higher Education' dealing
comprehensively with almost all the relevant aspects.  This report infact reflected the Committee's strong desire to see the
country become knowledge superpower having a world-class higher education system. It was also conscious of the special
higher education requirements and aspirations of the people of North East. It, therefore, decided to visit the North-Eastern
Hill University, Shillong, one of the greatest and the oldest universities in the region, with a view to have first hand idea
about the existing reality in this regard.
2.         At the Campus of NEHU, the Members of the Committee were received by the Vice-Chancellor, Registrar and
other representatives of the University.  The Committee had a wide-ranging and intense interaction with the Vice-
Chancellor, Registrar, teaching faculty, non-teaching staff and the students.  Several suggestions and proposals were
received for making NEHU a world-class university, with proper infrastructure for teaching and research in various
disciplines including those relevant to local conditions and needs.
3.         The Vice-Chancellor, NEHU, informed the Committee that NEHU was established in 1973 mainly with a view to
pay special attention to the improvement of the social and economic conditions and welfare of the people of the hill areas

of north-east region for their intellectual, academic and cultural advancement.  The university with thirty-two academic
departments and five centres of studies had recently been declared as "University with Potential for Excellence" based on
its performance in academics and administration.   He further informed that it ranked among the top nine universities that
had been given this status in the country.  The University had demonstrated that it was fulfilling the objectives for which it
was set up by the Government of India, he said.  The faculty members have received many distinctions by way of awards
and fellowships.  Its students were occupying important positions in the government, private sector and teaching &
research institutions within the country and abroad.  The university has 59 affiliated colleges including minority
educational institutions located in Guwahati (Assam) and Surat (Gujarat).  The Vice-Chancellor informed that the
university had plans to implement complete e-governance and was also committed to provide clean and transparent
administration.
Budgetary Issues
4.         The Registrar of the University presented the physical progress and financial utilization of the grants received by
the university during the Xth Plan period.  He informed that the university had satisfactorily utilized the grants sanctioned
under the non-lapsable pool of the Ministry of DoNER as well as the additional grants provided during the Xth Plan period
for the infrastructure development.   It was also informed that despite the advice of PMO not to impose financial cuts on
the University’s recurring budget as it was located in North-East, it still had to face the financial crunch in the last few
years. 
The Committee has, however, been informed by the Department that there had been no advice to the UGC
regarding financial cuts on the NEHU’s budget.  The UGC releases the funds to the universities out of grants received by
the Ministry of HRD.  Only 5% cut was applied to non-plan expenditure (excluding salaries, pension, interest payments
and repayment of debt) that was mandatory in case of any Ministry and Department.  The Committee is of the opinion that
in view of special requirements of the north-eastern region, even this 5% mandatory cut may be done away with.
5.         The Committee was further informed that the University did not have sufficient funds for repair and maintenance
of its buildings, increasing cost of health care and furnishing of newly constructed buildings, etc.  In the year 2007-08, its
plan funds had been drastically reduced as compared to the previous year.  It was submitted that the university needed
more funds and grant for nurturing and harnessing the true academic potential.  The Department admitted to the
Committee that NEHU deserves much greater financial support from the UGC/Government of India.  However, it denied
that the Plan funds of the university have been reduced over the Plan period or during the current financial year.  The
Department pointed out that against the IXth Plan allocation of Rs.11.34 crores, the original allocation for the university
during the Xth Plan period (2002-2007) was Rs.14.18 crores.  However, with a substantial increase in allocation during the
year 2006-07, the revised Xth Plan allocation stood at Rs.42.52 crores.  For the year 2007-08, i.e. the first year of the XIth
Plan period, an amount of Rs.4147.12 lakh has been earmarked by the UGC for the development of NEHU which
includes: -
i)
                    Development Grant – Rs.2026.32 lakhs.
ii)                   Unspent Balance – Rs.1503.80 lakhs.
iii)                 Revalidation of earlier sanction – Rs.617.00 lakhs.
6.         The Committee appreciates the increase in allocation of funds but it strongly feels that this allocation needs to be
further enhanced substantially to make NEHU a world class institution.   It recommends the Department and the UGC to
favourably consider the budget requirements of NEHU. 
7.         The Committee was informed that NEHU had recently submitted a number of projects for infrastructural
development such as construction of hostels, classrooms, staff quarters, indoor multipurpose gymnasium, etc. particularly
in view of the recently launched Faculty of Engineering and Faculty of Management in the University.  The Department
has suggested that in the XIth Plan the University may take up these proposals with the UGC's XIth Plan Committee.   The
Committee would like the UGC to consider these proposals of the NEHU favourably and sufficient fund may be provided
to the University for these purposes.
Accommodation
8.         Highlighting other problems faced by the NEHU, the Vice-Chancellor informed that despite several new initiatives
taken by the Government to provide infrastructural support, it was still far from adequate.   The academic departments
needed adequate buildings.  The shortage of housing at the campus for all students, teachers and staff was quite acute. 
Students came from far-off and remote places in North East and the university was not able to provide them hostel
facility.  At present it could accommodate only 500 students against the total strength of more than 3500.  The Vice-
Chancellor felt that the university could become academically more vibrant, if hostel facility for all were provided on the
campus itself. 
9.         When the Committee put the problem of shortage of hostels for students and residential quarters for teachers and
staffs before the Department, it replied that the UGC had been providing funds for the construction of Hostels under
General Development Assistance and Special Scheme for Women’s Hostel.  It was submitted before the Committee that

the University may bring the proposals before the XIth Plan.  The Committee, however, was informed neither about the
quantum of funds being provided by the UGC for the purpose nor about the adequacy of the funds released under General
Development Assistance/special scheme for Women’s Hostel to NEHU. The shortage of accommodation was infact,
raised by all sections of the university community who interacted with the Committee mainly on the plea that the forest
and hilly terrain of the area did not allow long distance commuting everyday.   The Committee, therefore, feels that the
shortage of accommodation needs to be tackled on priority basis.  The Committee feels that there was no serious effort
made by the Department or the UGC in this regard.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should
help the University overcome the accommodation problems of its students as well as teaching and non-teaching staff.
Transport facilities
10.       The Committee noted that the university was located far from the city, and a large number of faculty, non-teaching
staff and students had to commute from city to the university campus every day.  The Vice Chancellor informed that there
was hardly any public transport system available and the university's efforts for the last several years requesting the State
Government to ply adequate buses have yielded no results.  On being asked if the Department of Higher Education can
help the university in this regard, the Department submitted that the problem of inadequate public transport system for the
university was primarily a responsibility of the State Government and the university may take up the matter with the State
Government, and the issue, if necessary, will also be followed up by the Central Government.   The Committee feels
concerned to note that the Central Government has not taken this issue as seriously as it deserved.  That City transport in
and around Shillong, is virtually non-existent is known to all of us.  Suggesting to approach the State Government for this
facility, is nothing but passing the buck.  The Committee recommends that to help improve the transport system of the
university, the Department, apart from taking up the matter with the State Government should also ensure that transport
facilities of the University are adequately improved.
New Initiatives
11.       The Vice-Chancellor informed that an important area that was emerging in the region was tourism.  The university
had a proposal to start courses in this field as well.  The Committee learnt that the University had been allowed by the
Department of Tourism, Government of Meghalaya, to set up an Institute of Hotel Management. Indira Gandhi National
Open University and the Indian Council of Social Science Research had also proposed to set up their regional centres on
its land.  The Sports Authority of India has been given permission to set up its Centre with modern sporting facilities in the
University.  The Committee is happy to know that the university was embarking upon newer and relevent areas of study
and was getting favourable help/response from the many agencies such as SAI, IGNOU, ICSSR, State Tourism
Department etc.   For example, the DG, SAI who accompanied the Committee when it visited the SAI Centre being built
in the campus, informed the Committee that encouraged by the university's response, SAI was going to provide more
resources to create world class infrastructure there.   The Committee also after having seen the sprawling and beautiful
campus, is of the opinion that NEHU has the potential for being developed as an important educational-hub of our
country.  The Committee, therefore, calls upon the Department and the UGC to render all necessary help and guidance to
the university in this endeavour.
12.       The Committee was informed that the Honourable Prime Minister had announced the setting up of a College  of
Horticulture/Agriculture and also an Institute of Mass Media at NEHU.  On this issue, the Department clarified that NEHU
had submitted a proposal under Xth Plan for a School of Mass Communication and Information Technology consisting of
a Department of Mass Communication and Information Technology at Tura campus of the University to the then
Department of North Eastern Region (DoNER), Government of India and the University Grants Commission.  While there
has been no response from the DoNER, the proposal was not approved by the UGC during Xth Plan period.  The
Department has suggested that the University might revive the proposal and place the same for consideration by the
UGC’s XIth Plan Committee. The Committee finds that the Department in its reply has chosen to remain silent on the
PM’s announcement about setting up of the two institutes as informed by the Vice Chancellor.  The reason for the DoNER
not responding and for the UGC not accepting the proposals of the NEHU for these institutes, also have not been conveyed
to the Committee.   The Committee only hopes that NEHU would have placed these proposals before the XIth Plan
committee of the UGC that was scheduled to visit the campus.  The Committee would like to have a note on all these
aspects for its consideration. It at the same time calls upon the Departments of Higher Education and DoNER to consider
these proposals favourably.
Issues concerning Non-teaching staff
13.       During its visit the Committee interacted with the non-teaching staff also who submitted a memorandum on their
grievances.  The Non-Teaching Staff Association of NEHU requested for immediate implementation of the proposal of
first financial upgradation to the next higher scale on completion of 8 years of service and for grant of second upgradation
on completion of total 16 years of service in the grade with prospective effect to the eligible group B, C and D grade
employees.  The Committee urges upon the Department/UGC to look into these aspects of staff’s demands/proposals with
the help of NEHU authorities with a view to arrive at a decision in favour of the B, C and D group employees.

14.       The Committee’s attention was also drawn to the fact that non-teaching posts have not increased commensurately
with the increase in the number of faculty members and students and research scholars.  It was alleged that the ban on
creation of non-teaching posts despite heavy increase in their workload has left the non-academic side of the University
overburdened rendering them disappointed and discontented.  To this issue, the Department also submitted that as against
the prescribed teaching-non teaching ratio of 1 to 1.5, the current ratio in NEHU is 1:3, which is substantially on the higher
side.  The Committee, however, would like to draw attention to the special conditions prevailing in NEHU in terms of hard
terrain and other factors, such as the big sprawling campus, twin campuses at Shillong and Tura to be taken into
consideration while deciding about the strength of non-teaching staff at NEHU.  The Committee urges the Department to
reconsider the issue favourably and urgently.
15.       The issue of repatriated staff of the erstwhile Nagaland and Mizoram campuses of NEHU was also raised by the
Association.  They argued that the manner in which these repatriated staff is accommodated has not only adversely
affected the promotion prospects of the non-teaching staff originally appointed at NEHU but has also resulted in the
decrease in the actual number of sanctioned posts.          In this connection, the Department submitted that when Nagaland
University was established, many regular employees of the erstwhile Nagaland Campus of NEHU opted to join NEHU,
Shillong.  In accordance with the policy decision taken by the UGC/MHRD, 39 such repatriated staff were adjusted in
NEHU against regular vacant posts according to their qualifications.   For the remaining staff, who could not be
accommodated against regular vacancies, their posts were treated as personal to them. Therefore, the contention of the
Non-teaching Staff Association NEHU, that there has been decrease in the number of sanctioned posts is not factually
correct.  According to the University, no non-teaching staff from Mizoram Campus has been adjusted against any regular
post.  The Committee while agreeing with the Department, wishes to emphasise that it should properly address all the
welfare related issues of the staff of NEHU.
Need for Women Studies Centre
 
16.       There are certain areas where NEHU needs to take sincere initiatives.   The Committee found that there is no
Women Study Centre in the University.  The Committee strongly recommends the University to establish a Women Study
Centre on urgent basis.  Likewise, the Committee also feels that study in emerging areas like bio-technology, nano-
technology as well as traditional knowledge system of North East must be encouraged and pursued more vigorously.
Need for Physical Education
17.       The Committee further noticed that Physical Education had not received the desired importance by the University. 
The University, the Committee feels, needs to give due emphasis to Physical Education.
18.       The Committee also appreciates the role of Department of Higher Education and the UGC in the development of
NEHU.  The Committee finds it worth mentioning that while the existing guidelines laid down by the Department of
Expenditure/Ministry of Finance do not envisage any relaxations for utilization of funds under special conditions like
heavy rainfall & poor construction facilities available, the UGC has extended the time limit of completion of
infrastructural development by two years i.e. March, 2009.  The Committee compliments the Department and the UGC
and expects them to retain their positive outlook and hopes they would do their best to help make NEHU what it deserves
to be - a Center of Educational Excellence in the country.
