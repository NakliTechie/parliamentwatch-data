I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       India with just 2.4% of the world landmass constitutes 16% of the present world population.  The census of 1991
had indicated the population of India as 846.3 million.  In the decade of 1981-91, 163 million persons were added to the
population of India.  On 11 th May, 2000, the population of India has already crossed 1 billion mark.   If the present rate
of annual growth of population  of 1.74% continues, India may overtake China in 2045 to become the most populous
country in the world.
1.2      With a view to check  the growth of population, the Family Welfare Programme was taken up with the
introduction of Planning in the country.  The objective of the Family Welfare Programme for the country has been to
stabilise population at a level consistent with the needs of national development.  To achieve this goal the Department
continues to implement the three-pronged strategy of :
(i)         improvement in the health status of women and children;
(ii)        fulfillment of the unmet needs of contraception with a special focus on widening the range of choice
available to the people; and
(iii)       professionalising and broad-basing the Information, Education     and Communication Campaign (IEC)
on Family Welfare, by not only using the mass media, but also involving the community, NGOs,
women’s organisations in the local specific IEC work at the district, sub-district and village level.

1.6.      The Committee notes that though there is a continuous decline in various demographic indicators over the years,
they are still very high in comparison to other developing countries like Sri Lanka, Indonesia, China and Cuba.  The
Committee, therefore, feels that rural and inaccessible areas are still outside the reach of Family Welfare Programme 
and unless these  areas are covered by the various Family Welfare Programmes, there cannot be any significant decline
in the abovementioned indicators and the growth of population will remain unabated.
II.         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The Committee notes that the allocation for the Department has been reduced from Rs. 3541.47 crores in  BE
2000-2001 to Rs. 3221.37 crores at the RE stage.  However, a provision of Rs. 4232.28 crores has been made in BE
2001-2002 which represents an increase of Rs. 690.81 crores over the previous year.  
2.2       Commenting on the allocation made to the Department, it has been stated that in the Annual Plan proposal for
the year 2001-2002 sent to the Planning Commission, a requirement of Rs. 5922.72 crores was projected.   However, an
allocation of only Rs. 4210 crores  for 2001-2002 has been made by the Planning Commission.  As a result of the cut in
the Plan proposal, there is likely to be an accumulation of arrears and inadequate funding on infrastructure side of
various programmes and activities like RCH Programme, contraceptive supplies and maintenance of expenditure on
Rural Family Welfare Centres, etc.  
2.3       The Department has further informed that in the case of Rural Family Welfare Centres  the outlay proposed by it
was Rs. 700.00 crores and the outlay approved was Rs. 430.00 crores.  In the case of Sub-Centres the outlay proposed
was Rs. 1237.00 crores and the outlay approved was Rs. 675.00 crores.  In the case of maintenance of Urban Family
Welfare Centres, outlay proposed was Rs. 53.33 crores and the outlay approved was Rs. 35.00 crores. 
2.4       The Committee notes that the Eleventh Finance Commission agreed with the views of the Committee that the
transfer of cost of maintenance of infrastructure under Family Welfare Programme to the States would impose
enormous liability on them and weaken their resources and also there is no guarantee that the transferred funds would be
used for Family Welfare Programme.   The Committee, therefore, reiterates  its earlier recommendation that status quo
in this regard should be maintained.

areas. There are three types of Urban Family Welfare Centres viz. Type I, Type II and Type III, depending upon the
staff sanctioned and the population covered by them. In all, there are 1083 Urban Family Welfare Centres functioning in
the country.
4.2       The total budget allocation at all India level including allocation for NE States is  Rs. 60.00 crores and Rs. 75.00

V.           REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH  PROGRAMME (RCH)
5.1.         The concept of RCH Programme is to provide need-based, client-oriented, demand-driven, high quality and
integrated RCH services to the beneficiaries. The RCH programme is a composite programme incorporating the inputs
of the Government of India as well as funding support from external donor agencies including the  World Bank and the
European Commission. 
5.2.         The Programme aims at meeting the unmet needs of Family Welfare Programme by improving health status of
women and children, especially the poor and underserved, by reducing infant, child and maternal mortality and
morbidity through:-
 
(a)
          Sustaining and strengthening the ongoing Child Survival and Safe Motherhood Programme to ensure the
essential services as per package of services;
(b)         Improving the performance of the Family Welfare System in implementing policy change;
(c)          Strengthening  the quality and effectiveness of the services under essential package of Reproductive and
Child Health Services;
(d)         Improving project implementation through the application of performance based criteria for continued
funding; and
(e)          implementing and managing project activities by following certain fixed benchmarks.
5.3.         Allocation of Rs.951.00 crores was made in BE 2000-2001 which was reduced to Rs.801.00 crores at the RE
stage. Allocation in BE 2001-2002 has been substantially enhanced to Rs.1126.95 crores. On being asked as to how the
additional allocation will be utilized, the Department informed that the additional allocation is because of release of
funds direct to SCOVA Committees formed in the States to avoid delay while routing funds through the State Finance
Departments. The increase is proposed to be utilized in the following components.

category C districts.
(ii)                Dais Training

another 100 districts covering thereby 150 districts in all.
(iv)              Hepatitis-B Project – A new Pilot Project on Hepatitis B at a cost of Rs.9.27 crores proposed to
be taken up during 2001-2002.
(v)                Financial Envelope – Currently, provided to 4 States only, proposed to extend it to 2 more
States during the next year.

5.4.         Reproductive and Child Health Programme is the flagship programme of the Department on which around
25% percent of the allocation is spent. The Committee notes that the restructuring of the World Bank aided component
of the Programme was undertaken during October-November, 2000 in order to further tailor it  to the needs and capacity
of the States.  Also, focused attention was paid on initiatives of Dais’ Training, Border District Charter Project, RCH
Camps and RCH out-reach services.
5.5.         The Department has informed that in order to remove the difficulties in transmitting RCH funds to the
implementing agencies in the States, the mechanism of fund flow through specifically created society known as SCOVA
was adopted by all the States and UTs except Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. The Committee notes that the
SCOVA societies have been vested with decision making powers for implementation of the RCH Programme in the
State and for this purpose, the Society is headed by Chief Secretary of the State/UT and has representatives from
Departments of Finance, Planning etc.  The Committee, is of the view that the Chief Secretaries of the States due to
their innumerable other engagements may not be able to devote the kind of attention these societies deserve for effective
implementation of RCH Programmes in the States. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department may
examine whether these Societies could be headed by the Secretary,  Department of Family Welfare of the respective
States.
5.6.         The Committee observes that situation in the States of Assam, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Nagaland, Orissa,
Rajasthan and U.P. continues to be a cause of concern as more than 75% deliveries  are still conducted at home and as a
consequence MMR in these States is high. The Committees notes that following initiatives have been taken to
encourage institution delivery:-
               -        Scheme of Referral Transport to Pregnant women belonging to Indigent Families through Panchayats.
               -        Provision of additional honorarium to the staff to encourage round the clock delivery  System of PHCs
and CHCs.
               The Committee is, however, constrained to observe that although Rs.326.30 lakhs have been released to 12
States under the first initiative, feedback from the States suggests that funds are not being utilised by Panchayats.
Similarly, against a release of Rs.959.15 lakhs to States till now under the second initiative, an expenditure of only
Rs.33.80 lakhs has been reported. The Committee feels that proper awareness seems to be lacking at the level of
Panchayats and PHC/CHC functionaries about such initiatives. Wide publicity, therefore, needs to be given to make
optimum use of such initiatives.
5.7.         The Department has also informed that the Government of India and World Bank has reviewed the programme
in the year 2000 and based on the performance and capacity to implement and manage project activities, certain
benchmarks were fixed and are being followed vigorously. The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Department in
overcoming the deficiencies noticed in the RCH Programme. The Committee, however,  notes that  no surveys which
track the movement of access and reach of health services are conducted on a year to year basis. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that such surveys should be made a regular phenomenon for effective implementation of the
programme.

VI.          VILLAGE  HEALTH GUIDE SCHEME
               A Committee of experts had reviewed the Village Health Guide Scheme looking into various aspects such as
its usefulness, work done by VHGs, capability of VHGs in the context of institutional arrangements available in the

VIII.     COMPENSATION      
8.1.     Allocation under the head ‘Compensation’ is meant to compensate acceptors of terminal methods (sterilization)
of Family Planning against loss of wages and to meet the costs of diet, dressing, drugs, transport etc.   The States/UTs
have the flexibility to apportion the amount of compensation among various heads of expenses subject to guidelines
given by  Government of India.   An allocation of Rs. 120.00 crores was made in BE 2000-2001 which has been
increased to Rs. 125.00 crore in BE 2001-2002. 
8.2      The proposal to revise norms of compensation was under consideration of the Department of Family Welfare
since 1996.  The Committee has been informed that the reason for delay in the matter is late receipt of the proposal to
revise norms of compensation from States/UTs.  The CCEA in its meeting held on 7
th February, 2001 approved the
proposal for increasing the amount of compensation for loss of wages for undergoing Tubectomy from Rs. 200 to Rs.
300 from Rs. 180 to Rs. 200 for vasectomy and from Rs 16 to Rs. 20 for IUD insertion. The same is  applicable from
1.4.2001.  The Committee notes that though there is an increase in the amount of Compensation the increase is not
sufficient.   The increase should be adequate enough to inspire people to accept family planning methods/norms.

XI        DISTRIBUTION OF CONTRACEPTIVES
11.1.    An allocation of Rs. 108.71 crores was made under the head ‘Free Distribution of Conventional Contraceptives’
in BE in 2000-01 which was reduced to Rs. 95.71 crores at the RE stage.  However, allocation in BE 2001-2002 has
been increased to Rs. 117.00 crores.  Explaining the reasons for this variation, the Department has informed that
reduction at RE 2000-2001 was mainly due to unexpected less expenditure in the procurement of Copper T, less
procurement of  Mala N on account of less demand from the States, and limited number of Laparoscopes procured. 
However, allocation in BE 2001-2002 has been increased mainly due to more demand of free supply of Nirodh from the
State Governments. The increase in allocation in 2001-2002 is also due to the fact that cost of raw material has been
added in the proposed BE as UNFPA  has discontinued the supply of  raw material as commodity assistance for
manufacturing  of Oral Contraceptive pills.
11.2.   The Committee has been informed that according to the studies conducted by the International Institute of
Population Studies, Mumbai in Haryana, Maharashtra and UP revealed that around sixty percent of condoms under free
distribution system was unused and wasted.  Hence, in order to minimize wastage and promote distribution of
comdoms, a scheme of service charge of ten paise per condom, to be retained by the health worker, was introduced
during 1997-98.  However, subsequent survey revealed that this new incentive did not reach most of the doctors and
health workers in PHC/ Sub Centres.
11.3.    The Committee feels that the scheme of service charge of ten paise per condom to be retained by the health
worker is  good except for the fact that ten paise per condom is too meagre an amount to attract the enthusiasm of the
health workers.  The Committee is also disturbed to know that this new scheme did not reach most of the doctors and
health workers in the sub centres.  This means that the funds  earmarked for the purpose are being squandered by the
middlemen instead of reaching the health workers.  The Committee views this as a serious lapse in monitoring the
utilization of funds by the Department and recommends that  corrective measures may be taken in this regard.
XII.         STERILISATION BEDS
12.1.       Under this Scheme, maintenance grant @ Rs.4500/- per annum is paid to each institution subject to a condition
that a minimum of 75/60 tubectomics per bed per annum are achieved by the Government/Voluntary Agencies
respectively. At present 3115 beds are functioning which is 58 below the last year’s figure of 3173.
12.2.       The Committee notes that the allocation under ‘Sterilisation Beds’ for 2000-2001 was Rs.1.70 crores.   The
Department has informed that a proposal to enhance the maintenance grant to Rs.6500 has not been implemented, so, far
and once they are revised the requirement shall be around Rs.2.02 crores.  The Committee  observes that since 1998-99
same level of allocation is being made under this scheme which indicates that there has been no increase in the numbers
of Sterlisation Beds during this period.   What is more disturbing that during 2001-2002, a reduced provision of Rs. 1.35
crores is proposed.
12.3.
              The Committee has been given to understand that after vigorously pursuing with the State Governments  for
augmentation of Sterlisation Beds, proposals from 16 NGOs for 54 Beds have been received from Tamil Nadu, West
Bengal, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Kerala in 2000-2001 which  have been sanctioned by the
Department.
12.4.              The Committee recommends that the Department should take necessary steps to  increase the number of
sterlization beds, particularly in the more populated states of the country.   The Committee also recommends that the
proposal of revision of maintenance grant from Rs. 4500/- per bed per annum for   Rs.6500/-  per bed annum which is
under consideration for long may be expedited.
XIII.        NATIONAL POPULATION POLICY
13.1.       The Committee notes that after many years of deliberation and consultation, the National Population Policy,
was presented to Parliament last year.  The Committee has been informed that the immediate objective of the National
Population Policy is to address the unmet needs of contraception, health infrastructure, health personnel and to provide
integrated service delivery for basic reproductive and child health care.  The medium term objective is to bring the total
fertility rates to replacement level by 2010 through vigorous implementation of intersectoral operational strategies. The
long term objective is to achieve a stable population by 2045, at a level consistent with the requirement of sustainable
economic growth, social development and environmental protection.

            The Committee notes that percentage coverage in 1999-2000 in the case of TT for pregnant women  and Measles

            The Committee notes that the Eleventh Finance Commission agreed with the views of the Committee that the

used for Family Welfare Programme.   The Committee, therefore, reiterates  its earlier recommendation that status quo
