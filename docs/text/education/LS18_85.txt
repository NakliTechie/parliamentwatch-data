REPORT
I. INTRODUCTION
1.1. The Department of Women and Child Development is engaged in the giving the much needed impetus
to the holistic development of women and children. The Department, as the national machinery for the
advancement of women and children, formulates plans, policies and programmes; enacts/amends
legislation; guides and coordinates the efforts of both Governmental and non-governmental organisations
working in the field of Women and Child Development. The Department also implements certain
innovative programmes for women and children covering welfare and support services, training for
employment and income generation, awareness generation and gender sensitisation. They play a
supplementary and complementary role to the other general developmental programmes in the sectors of
health, education, rural development etc. For the holistic development of the child, the Department
implements the Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) providing a package of services

comprising supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health check-up and referral services, pre-school and
non-formal education and health and nutrition education.
1.2. The other major schemes implemented by the Department include funding of Hostels for working
women, Support, Training and Employment Programme, Scheme of Training-cum-Employment-cum
Production Centres (NORAD), Socio-Economic Programme, the Programme of Condensed Courses of
Education and Vocational Training etc. Most of the programmes of the Department are run through non-
Governmental organisations.
II OBJECTIVES AND APPROACH OF THE NINTH PLAN
2.1 The Approach Paper to 9
th Five Year Plan, while laying emphasis on empowering women, under
scored harmonization of various efforts on different fronts, i.e., social, economic, legal and political. The
Approach Paper also emphasises the need to make reservation of seats for women in the Parliament and
State Legislatures, to fulfil the goal of education for women equality, to adopt a life cycle approach to
women’s health. As regards child development, the Approach paper proposes to take the convention of
child’s rights ratified in 1992 as the guiding principle and would like to review the national policy for
children. In order to promote a community based approach, action will be initiated to decentralise women
and child programme involving Panchayati Raj Institutions and local women’s self-help groups.
2.2 The Department informed that it is continuously making efforts for better delivery of services to the
targeted groups under the various schemes. As was emphasised in the approach paper, the Department is
already striving for the social, economic, legal and political empowerment of women. The Department is
also trying to get 33% reservation of seats for women in Parliament and in State legislatures. Department is
taking various steps to speed up the implementation of the Schemes for empowerment of women which
includes (I) computerisation of administration of NORAD and to some extent STEP and Working Women
Hostel Scheme; (ii) monitoring and implementation of projects under various schemes (iii) Nutrition
Education of the community in rural, urban and tribal areas through the field infrastructure of Food and
Nutrition Board; (iv) organisation of demonstration programmes/orientation training courses/integrated
nutrition education camps; and (v) involvement of Panchayti Raj Institutions and women co-operative
societies in the implementation of ICDS Scheme.
2.3. While welcoming the initiatives taken by the Department, the Committee would
also like to emphasise that sustained, dedicated and sincere efforts are the pre-requisite
to fulfil the commitments and for this purpose, cooperation of other concerned
Governmental bodies whether it be in Centre or State as well as non-Governmental
organisations is a must.

IV INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS) SCHEME
4.1. The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme aims to improve the nutritional and
health status of vulnerable groups including pre-school children, pregnant women and nursing mothers
through providing a package of services including Supplementary Nutrition, Pre-School Education,
Immunisation, Health Check-up, Referral Services and Nutrition and Health Education. In addition, the
Scheme envisages effective convergence of inter-sectoral services in the Anganwadi Centres.
4.2. The allocation made for ICDS during 1998-99 was Rs.603.4 crores which was enhanced to Rs.768.41
crores at RE stage. The Committee has noted that the actual expenditure under this scheme upto February,
1999 has been Rs. 767.14 crores. For the year 1999-2000, against the projected requirement of funds at the
level of Rs.1059.43 crores, a provision of Rs. 855.76 crores has been made. This provision is also meant to
cover expenses arising due to higher rate of honorarium payable to Anganwadi Workers and Helpers and
also increased project cost.
4.3. The Committee has been informed that in pursuance of its recommendation regarding according top
priority to the coverage and outreach of ICDS projects in the country, the Department has been included
for the implementation of Special Action Plan proposed by the Planning Commission. An additional
amount of Rs.450 crores has been allocated by the Planning Commission for the remaining 3 years of
Ninth Five Year Plan for ICDS. The Committee appreciates the decision taken by the Planning
Commission. However, the Committee finds that this has not been reflected in the budget allocation
for 1999-2000, as it is much below the projected requirements.
4.4. The Committee has been given to understand that the most important impact of the scheme is clearly
reflected in significant decline in the levels of severely and moderately malnourished children in the
country as also in significant decline in IMR. The percentage of children suffering from severe
malnutrition has significantly declined from 15.3% during 1976-78 to 8.7% during 1989-90. The IMR has
significantly declined from 80 per 1000 live births in 1991 to 71 during 1997.
4.5. While not disputing the decline in the average level of malnutrition in children, the Committee would

4.6. The Committee’s attention has been drawn to the element of discontent being observed in Anganwadi
Workers due to unsatisfactory benefits/facilities available to them. The Committee notes that AWW, a

community identify and meet the needs of their children and women. The Committee finds that in addition
to the job responsibilities of AWW originally envisaged, their services are also vital in the implementation
of RCH (Reproductive Child Health) Programme and National AIDS Control Programme. The
Committee strongly feels that noticeable improvement in the service conditions of AWWs is called
for if ICDS Programme is to be made a total success.
4.7. The Committee is happy to note that a national Committee has been recently set up to take stock of the
service conditions and duties of AWWs and Helpers and to identify Ministries/Departments/States etc.
which can provide additional remuneration to these ICDS functionaries. The Committee hopes that this
Committee would come out with its recommendations at the earliest.
4.8. The Committee notes that the ICDS programme is characterised by a built in monitoring system for
promoting assessment, analysis and action at different levels. There is a regular flow of information and
feedback between each Anganwadi and the project, between the ICDS project and the State Government
and between the State Government and Government of India. Quarterly status reports and performance
reports are sent to the State’s nodal departments in taking necessary corrective action. The Committee
hopes that the existence of an effective monitoring system for ICDS would lead to better
achievement of physical targets.

malnourished in pre-dominantly tribal and backward areas in a number of States.

social and emotional needs of the children. Since 1997-98, this scheme is being implemented only in non-
ICDS areas. 6.2. The allocation for Balwadi Nutrition Programme in BE 1998-99 was Rs. 6.17 crores

VII Crèches/Day Care Centres
7.1. Children in the vulnerable age group of 0-5 and whose parent’s total monthly income is not more than
Rs. 1800/- p.m. are given essential day care services under the Creche Scheme started in 1975 and the
National Creche Fund set up in 1994. While the Creche Scheme is being implemented through three major
organisations, i.e. (i) Central Social Welfare Board (ii) Bharatiya Adimjati Sevak Sangh (iii) Indian
Council for Child Welfare, the National Creche Fund provides assistance to registered NGOs and Mahila
Mandals throughout the country to run crèches.
7.2. The Committee last year had observed that there was a greater need for extending the network of
Creches and Day Care Centres. The Department vide its Action Taken Report has clarified that the Creche
Scheme is non-expanding and efforts are underway to increase the corpus of the National Creche Fund so
that more creches can be set up.
7.3. The National Creche Fund set up in 1993-94 with a corpus of Rs. 19.90 crores, covers 1856 General
Creches and 599 Anganwadi-cum-Creches. The Committee is, however, perturbed to note that a
proposal to increase the corpus amount has not yet been approved and a provision of Rs. 1 crore
made in 1998-99 had to be reduced to Rs.1 lakh. Similar token provision has been made in 1999-
2000 also. The Committee notes that against an estimated requirement of 8 lakh creches for children
in the country, only 14,925 creches have been set up under these two schemes so far, leaving an

VIII. Hostels for Working Women
8.1. This scheme aims to provide suitable, safe and inexpensive accommodation to single working women
in lower income group. This scheme is implemented though voluntary organisations, women’s
development corporations, universities, schools/colleges of social work and local bodies etc. Allocation for
this scheme has been constant at Rs.7.75 crores at BE and RE 1997-98, 1998-88 and also BE 1999-2000.
The Committee, however, observes that against a target of 25 Hostels to be sanctioned during 1998-99,
only 5 could be sanctioned. During 1998-99, expenditure upto Feb., 99 has been only Rs. 4.68 crores.
8.2 The Department informed the Committee that since 1997, only 819 hostels have been sanctioned so
far. During the period 1994-97, 106 hostels were sanctioned out of which 16 were completed within the
stipulated time period. In view of the pace of construction of Hostels, the Committee is of the view that
there is a need to step up efforts.
8.3. The Committee feels that this scheme should not be restricted to working women only.
Wherever vacancies are available, girls pursuing higher studies/training programme can be
accommodated in working women hostels.
IX. INDIRA MAHILA YOJANA
9.1. Indira Mahila Yojana was launched initially on pilot basis in 200 blocks. Indira Mahila Yojana is a
strategy to create an organisational base in all parts of the country for women to come together and analyse
their needs and understand the processes of fulfilling the needs through existing developmental
programmes of the State and Central Governments. As per the information available from the states, more
than 38,000 women’s groups have been formed under this scheme.
9.2. The Department has informed that on account of very high operational cost of implementing Mahila
Samriddhi Yojana, it was not found feasible to continue the scheme after March, 1998. Amount yet to be
paid to the Department of Posts is Rs. 313 crores. As a result of this, it has been decided to merge erstwhile
MSY with IMY. However, in view of non finalisation of this proposal, an amount of Rs. 10 crore provided
in 1998-99 had to be reduced to Rs. 0.01 crore at the RE stage. The Committee observes that an allocation
of Rs. 10 crore has been made for IMY in 1999-2000. As per the latest information made available to the
Committee, the draft proposal for merger of these two schemes and strengthening the IMY is under
circulation to various departments. The EFC is likely to consider the proposal once the views of the
departments are available.
9.3. The Committee has noted that a rapid evaluation study of IMY by the Planning Commission and

X SHORT STAY HOMES
10.1. Under this scheme, grants-in-aid are given to voluntary organisations for establishing and running
Short Stay Homes for rehabilitating women and girls, facing social and moral danger due to family
problems, mental strain, exploitation etc. The Committee has observed that against a plan provision of
Rs.10 crores in BE 1998-99 reduced to Rs.4.00 crore at RE stage, expenditure figures stand at Rs.2.50
crore upto February, 1999.
10.2. The Committee has been informed that since the inception of the scheme in 1969, 361 Short Stay
Homes have been established so far. Inspection of approximately 185 Homes has been organised during
the last two years. 83 Homes were, thereafter issued show cause notices, 13 Homes were closed down and
10 NGOs. were blacklisted. As a result of this exercise, the number of existing Homes was brought down
to 272.
10.3. The Committee hopes that inspections of remaining homes would also be carried out by the
Department as early as possible. The Committee welcomes the decision of the Department to
transfer the implementation of this scheme to Central Social Welfare Board from next year. The
Committee, however, has a word of caution. Alongwith the system of routine inspection, there is a
need for carrying out surprise inspections for better and real assessment of functioning of Short Stay
Homes. This becomes all the more necessary in view of the fact that assistance to a Short Stay Home
has been increased from Rs.1.87 lakhs to Rs.4.01 lakhs per annum with effect from 1
st April, 1999.
10.4. The Committee is shocked to note that as a result of a sample study of 37 Homes undertaken by the
Planning Commission, 6 Homes were found to be non-existing/non-functional. Major drawbacks as
enumerated below have been pointed out in this evaluation study :-
expenditure on establishing appeared to be on a higher side;
very little linkage with the local Government;
Greater publicity of the scheme is necessary to create public awareness;

XIV. GENERAL
15.1. Allocation of an amount for a specific programme at the beginning of a financial year, its
subsequent reduction at RE stage, failure in expenditure of the same for want of approval from the
competent authority is a trend which is being noticed in a number of cases so far as launching of a
new scheme/revision of a scheme is concerned. The Committee would like to point out that there is
an urgent need for streamlining the entire procedure. The Committee, therefore, urges upon the
Department to give serious consideration to this aspect.
15.2. The Committee finds that most of the projects are being implemented through NGOs, some of
the NGOs are doing good work. However, the Committee views with serious concern that there are
reports also about a number of NGOs involved in financial irregularities. Committee’s attention has
been drawn to CAG Report (1 of 1998) wherein it has been observed that 6321 utilisation certificates
relating to Rs. 725.76 crore in respect of grants released upto September, 1995 by the Department
were outstanding at the end of March, 1997.
15.3. The Committee notes that a ‘National Evaluation and Monitoring Agency’ is proposed to be
launched during 1999-2000 for proper identification of NGOs involved in various schemes of women
and child development and also for evaluating and monitoring the implementation of various
schemes. The need for launching such an Agency is proof enough that the various schemes being
implemented were either not having an inbuilt mechanism for monitoring or the same was not
effective enough. The Committee hopes that there would be no delay in the launching of the National
Evaluation and Monitoring Agency.
