REPORT
The Department of Heavy Industry deals with many PSEs in the engineering, power and
heavy industry sectors which are critical for nation-building, industrialisation and economic growth.
It also deals with the auto industry which is booming and which has got a lot of potential. Broadly,
the PSEs falling within the following sectors are under the jurisdiction of the Department:–
(i) Heavy Engineering Equipment & Machine Tool Industry (Capital Good Sector);
(ii) H eavy Electrical Engineering Industry; and
(iii) Auto Industry including Tractors and Earth Moving Equipments.
2. DHI aims at ensuring steady growth of all the three above sectors and sub sectors of the
Industry and assists the PSEs in their endeavours to bolster capacity utilization and profitability and
generate resources and reposition their strategies/policies to make them more competitive. The
Department also acts as an interface between PSEs and other agencies for the purpose of long term
policy formulation.

National Automotive Testing and R&D Infrastructure Project (NATRiP)
7. NATRiP was approved by the Government on 25
th July, 2005 and notified by the Department
of Heavy Industry on 31 st August, 2005. The project envisages setting up of World-class automotive
testing and homologation facilities in India, with a total investment of Rs. 1,718 crore in six years
from the date of it being notified. Thus, the original timelines set for completion of NATRiP was
September, 2011. However, land could not be made available in Manesar, Indore, ARAI Pune and
Rae-Bareilly for the project within the stipulated time-frame. Therefore, the project could not be
completed as per the schedule. There were several other factors such as handing over of land at
various sites by the state governments, obtaining various clearances, shifting of utilities,
rehabilitation of project affected people, etc., contributed to the revision of the timeline for its
completion. In April, 2011, the Government approved a revised cost estimate of Rs. 2288.06 crore
due to escalation in cost to the tune of Rs. 570 crore due to Foreign Exchange variation, Statutory
Levies, rise in input costs etc. The project is now expected to be finished by December, 2012,
as per the revised timeline.

Modernisation of Capital Goods Sector
11. Widespread concerns have been expressed to strengthen and modernise the capital goods
sector which is critical for industrial growth and greater economic output. The Committee was
informed by the Department that “A Scheme was proposed for enhancing competitiveness of the
Capital Goods (CG) Industry in 2009-10 and Planning Commission accorded ‘in principle’ approval
to the same. DHI had mandated a study by Confederation of Indian Industry (CII) and a number
of its recommendations are proposed to be pursued through the scheme. Initially, this effort would
cover five major CG sectors, viz., Heavy Electrical Equipment, Process Plant Machinery, Mining
and Construction Equipment, Textile Machinery and Machine Tools Industry, which together
account for nearly 65% of the total production in the capital goods sector. Meanwhile, an amount
of Rs.24 crore was allocated in the Plan Budget of DHI in the BE 2010-11 for the scheme.
Subsequently, the draft Scheme was considered by the Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) in
January, 2011 where the Department was advised to come up with a revised Scheme for the next
Five Year Plan (Twelfth Financial Year Plan 2012-17). Accordingly, no funds were provided for this
scheme for the year 2011-12. Clarifying this non allocation, the Department has informed the
Committee that as advised by EFC, a draft Scheme is being formulated for consideration of the
Planning Commission”.
12. The Department of Heavy Industry while answering to a question of the Committee,
wrote, “A Working Group on “Capital Goods and Engineering Sector” constituted by the
Planning Commission has projected a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 16.85% for
next five years in this sector. This will result in doubling of investment, production and
employment. On the basis of finding and recommendations of the said Report a Scheme for
enhancement of competitiveness in Capital Goods Sector has been proposed with an outlay of
Rs.2360 crore to be implemented during the Twelfth Five Year Plan. The Scheme envisages,
inter alia, setting up Product Development Centres, Common Facility Centres and Sector
Specific Industrial Cluster Parks and modernisation/upgradation of select PSEs to become
national champion. During the year 2012-13 a token budget of Rs.2.50 crore has been allocated
pending scheme approval”.
13. During the discussion on Demands for Grants of DHI for 2012-13, the representatives of
the Department submitted that second hand goods from abroad and cheap imports were retarding
the growth of Capital Goods Industry in the country. The zero percent customs duty on heavy
engineering products was encouraging developed countries to provide only manufactured
equipments and made them less interested in transferring technology to Indian industrial
establishment. The Department was of the opinion that a level playing field is not available to the
Indian industry due to zero customs duty for imported technology in the capital goods sector. The
industry associations who also had an interface with the Committee on the subject had rued the
fact that there was much to be done to prop up the Capital Goods Industry. During these

5
interactions, the Committee sensed unanimity in the opinion about the absence of any proactive
scheme from the part of the Government.
14. The Committee is disheartened to note that the capital goods sector is almost
handicapped due to free flow of imported technology. In fact, import dependence is truly
worrisome. The absence of countervailing duties and elevated export duty structure seem to
be adding to the vows of the sector. In this circumstance, the Committee recommends that
the Government may look into re-imposition of countervailing duties on import of plants
and machinery and rationalize the excise duty structure so that the indigenous industrial
units are not put in a disadvantageous position vis-a-vis  the foreign players. Technology
transfer should be incentivised and programme initiatives for R&D, joint-technology
development and acquisition, etc., should be prioritized.
15. The Committee noted that with the economic reforms and liberalisation, technology
flow has come down drastically. It is given to understand that the Planning Commission set
up a Committee headed by Mr. Arun Maira to study the situation. The Report of the Arun
Maira Committee recommended for imposition of customs duty to a certain extent. The
Department also inform the Committee that the Minister for Department of Heavy Industry
also took up the matter. The Department was expecting that there would be some budgetary
provision to remedy the situation. However, the unfavourable situation for the capital goods
sector continues to haunt the entire Indian industry. It is tragic that our capacity to
manufacture power plants and other capital goods equipments are not being harnessed due
to a liberal import regime with zero custom duty.
16. Further, the Committee strongly recommends that import of second hand goods
should be discouraged to the extent possible, if not banned. HEC, HMT and other  national
level companies have to be promoted for the cause of capital goods sector and bigger
companies like BHEL should be made fountainheads of Capital Goods Exports. A level
playing field which would entail imposition of customs duty on the imported technology
and favourable duty structure for the indigenous industry would promote competitiveness
among domestic and foreign players. The scheme Enhancement of Competitiveness in
Capital Good Sector was proposed by the Working Group on Capital Goods and
Engineering Sector set up by the Planning Commission. The Committee feels that
adequate measures must be taken to approve the scheme and implement it during the
Twelfth Five Year Plan. The Committee also recommends that maximum budgetary
support must be provided for this purpose.
Integrated Coal Gasification Combined Cycle (lCGCC)
17. This is a project which is going on for quite some years. In response to a question of the
Committee on this project the following was stated:–
“BHEL had prepared a Detailed Project Report (DPR) for a 125 MW IGCC plant to be set
up at Dr. Narla Tata Rao Thermal Power Station, Vijayawada, which was accepted by Andhra
Pradesh Power Generation Corporation Limited (APGENCO). Towards this, BHEL and APGENCO
entered into a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) in May, 2008 to set up a 125 MW IGCC
Power Plant at Vijayawada as a Technology Demonstration Project. In order to select a gas turbine
with proven experience on low calorific value gas, BHEL had proposed to enhance the capacity of
the plant from 125 MW to 182 MW, which was also approved by APGENCO. In this regard the
Committee has been informed that the techno-commercial discussions with APGENCO had been
completed. The estimated EPC cost of the project is Rs. 1665.36 crore APGENCO has agreed to
pay Rs. 812.45 crore, which is based on the cost of a commercial natural gas based combined

6
cycle plant of equivalent capacity. The project will be ushering in clean coal technology to India.
In order to partially cover the cost, BHEL had sought a grant of Rs. 350 crore from the
Government of India, with the balance of Rs. 502.91 crore being provided from BHEL’s own
internal resources as R&D funds. The Principal Scientific Adviser to the Government of India had
also supported BHEL’s request and written to the Hon’ble Prime Minister of India for a grant of
Rs. 350 crore in July 2008”.
18. On being queried on the current status of the project, DHI submitted that detailed design
and engineering of the proposed plant and further R&D efforts to improve the process are in
progress. In addition, for its portion of funding, APGENCO has requested Andhra Pradesh
Government for equity participation, who in turn has forwarded the proposal to Ministry of
Environment and Forests (MoEF) for funding for this portion under Clean Energy Fund. BHEL is
awaiting response from APGENCO to move further on the project. Coal Linkage for the Project
is also awaited by APGENCO.
19. Notwithstanding the explanation furnished by the Department, the Committee  notes
with concern that a project which has immense potential for clean power generation and
minimal environmental impact has been progressing at snails pace  since the inking of MoU
in the first half of 2008.  The Committee, therefore,  recommends that apart from the equity
participation of the Andhra Pradesh  Government. The Ministry of Environment and Forest
may be approached at the  highest level possible for the clearance of the funding proposal
under clean energy fund.
Scooters India Ltd. (SIL)
20. The SIL was incorporated in September, 1972, and is presently engaged in the  designing,
manufacturing and marketing of three wheelers, their spares and engineering goods. The Company
has been incurring losses since 2006-07. The BIFR declared the Company sick on 18
th February,
2010 and referred it to BRPSE. The BRPSE recommended  that either a possible JV partner for
revival of the Company or the Company has to close down. The Government had decided to
identify a strategic partner for SIL after introducing a resolution in Parliament.
21. When the Committee wanted to know about the progress made towards the introduction
of the resolution, the DHI submitted that the Cabinet in its meeting held on 19.05.2011 approved
the proposal for revival of SIL by transfer of the entire Government equity to a suitable identified
strategic partner through Department of Disinvestment (DoD). The process involved introduction
of a resolution in both the Houses of Parliament. Accordingly, a resolution seeking Parliamentary
approval for the revival of SIL was moved and listed during the Monsoon Session, 2011 of
Parliament. Subsequently, as per several representations received and the direction of the PMO,
the resolution was withdrawn. No date has been fixed for the introduction of resolution in
Parliament.
22. The Committee would like to have detailed explanation in this regard.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE — AT A GLANCE
The Committee finds that the Plan funds allocated for 2011-12 have been well utilized
by DHI. It is the lower rate of utilization of Non-plan funds that has pulled down the overall
utilization figure for the year. In this circumstance, the Committee views the spurt in Plan
allocation for 2012-13 to be encouraging and observes that the Department should be able
to continue the efficient utilization of funds during the current year also. This will go a long
in augmenting the performance of CPSEs under DHI and the schemes of the Department.
(Para 4)
The Committee notes that the Department has been able to secure only 15.68% of
the Plan funds they sought for the year 2012-13. The funds for all major schemes have been
drastically curtailed. The Committee feels that it will be difficult for DHI to implement the
schemes efficiently. Taking into account the efficient utilisation of plan resources by DHI
during 2011-12, the Committee feels that the Department deserves a substantially higher
allocation for 2012-13. The Committee expects that the DHI with its proven record of good
work would be able to persuade and convince the Ministry of Finance to provide more funds
at the time when the revised estimates are finalised. The Committee also strongly
recommends for higher allocation of funds for the Department of Heavy Industry.
(Para 6)
The Committee hopes that a path breaking project like NATRiP which is of great
significance for the automobile sector is completed according to the revised time schedule.
It is sad to note that the project has received only about 45% of the budgetary support
sought. The Committee observes that no stone should be left unturned to complete the
project as per the revised time line of December, 2012. The officials of the DHI informed
the Committee that it would not be possible to complete the project by December, 2012 due
to inadequate funds. NATRiP is a project of national significance and of critical relevance
to the automobile sector in India. Given the fact that India is now emerging as a global hub
for the automobile industry, it is imperative that NATRiP is extended all facilities including
the necessary monetary support for its completion as per the revised schedule. The
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Finance and the Planning Commission must
be supportive for this project which has been delayed so long due to insufficient allocation.
Therefore, it is strongly recommended that more funds must be released for this project.
(Para 10)
The Committee is disheartened to note that the capital goods sector is almost
handicapped due to free flow of imported technology. In fact, import dependence is truly
worrisome. The absence of countervailing duties and elevated export duty structure seem to
be adding to the vows of the sector. In this circumstance, the Committee recommends that
the Government may look into re-imposition of countervailing duties on import of plants
and machinery and rationalize the excise duty structure so that the indigenous industrial
units are not put in a disadvantageous position vis-a-vis  the foreign players. Technology
transfer should be incentivised and programme initiatives for R&D, joint-technology
development and acquisition, etc., should be prioritized. (Para 14)
The Committee noted that with the economic reforms and liberalisation, technology
flow has come down drastically. It is given to understand that the Planning Commission set
