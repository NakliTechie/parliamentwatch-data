REPORT
          The Small Scale Industries (SSI) Sector is a vibrant segment of the Indian economy contributing over 39% of the
manufacturing sector output and 34 per cent of the nation’s exports and providing employment to over 270 lakh persons
through more than 115 lakh units, which are spread both in rural and urban areas across the country. The major
contribution of the SSI sector lies in creation of employment opportunities at lower capital cost. While one unit of
employment requires investment of  Rs. 0.72 lakh in SSI sector, the same is as high as Rs. 5 lakh in the large units.
During 2003-04, the SSI sector registered a growth rate of 7.6 per cent as against a growth rate of 6.9 per cent for the
overall industrial sector. The SSI sector contributed 6.81 per cent towards GDP in 2002-03, while the contribution of
manufacturing sector (including SSI) to GDP was around 25 per cent.

3.0     The Ministry of SSI informed the Committee that Ministry had projected a sum of Rs. 717.81 crore for the year
2004-05. Planning Commission after discussion with this Ministry had fixed the annual plan outlay at Rs. 362.25 crores.
The major reduction is under Credit Guarantee Fund Scheme, which was proposed at Rs. 495.00 crore and was fixed by

are Tool Rooms, Integrated Infrastructural Development Scheme, Small Industry Cluster Development Programme and

3.1     The Committee observes that SSI sector is a major manufacturing sector contributing substantially to the export
and employment generation. This sector needs a boost which can largely come from the Government. A reduction of
allocation to the extent of 57 per cent of the Ministry’s requirement will adversely affect the growth of SSI sector. The
Committee therefore impresses upon the Government to provide necessary funds at RE stage.

16.0   CREDIT LINKED CAPITAL SUBSIDY SCHEME
16.1   The purpose behind introduction of CLCSS is to motivate and facilitate SSI units to upgrade their technology
through infusion of institutional credit. The strategy adopted for this purpose is to provide 12 per cent upfront capital
subsidy on institutional finance availed to facilitate technology upgradation in small scale sector. The ceiling of loans
eligible under the scheme is presently Rs. 40 lakh. The corpus fund created under the scheme is Rs. 600 crore for years

26.0   CREDIT RATING
26.1   The Finance Minister in his Budget speech (2004-05) declared that “SSI units will be encouraged to obtain credit

unit or group of SSI units along with the criteria for assigning weightage in the credit rating. The Ministry elaborated
that NSIC in consultation with the various stake holders i.e. Small Industries Associations, Indian Banks Association,
and various Rating Agencies namely CRISIL, ICRA, Dun & Bradstreet (D& B) and ONICRA, has formulated a Rating

performance factors including parameters measuring operational, financial, business and management risks.

examination of the Ministry and on approval, this Scheme shall be implemented and Rating Agencies shall be
empanelled.

performance factors. The Committee accordingly recommends the Ministry to expedite the process of initiating
and popularizing the Credit Rating System for SSI units.

28.0   POLICY OF DE-RESERVATION IN SSI SECTOR
28.1   During the course of interaction with the Committee the representatives of CII stated that the Policy of
Reservation discourages investment competition and Technology up-gradation in the small sector. The representatives
of CII further added that a gradual and phased de- reservation from the list of reserved items, enable this sector to
become globally competitive.
28.2   The Committee desired to know as to what extent the de-reservation of items, as identified by the Ministry in
regard to 85 items as declared by the Finance Minister in the General Budget (2004-05) is in the interest of SSI sector.
The Ministry of Small Scale Industries replied that Small-scale sector manufactures more than 7500 items out of which
only 675 items are reserved for exclusive manufacture in small-scale sector. These items mainly are labour-oriented
with very less investment and low technological requirements. Most of these items are produced in self-employment
ventures and do not attract big players in the field. However, the Ministry of Small Scale Industries clarified that some
hi-tech export oriented items like hosiery, hand tools, drugs & pharmaceutical and stationery, have been provided with a
higher investment limit in plant and machinery, i.e. up to Rupees five crores to enable them to meet the requirements of
technology upgradation and face the global and domestic competition.
28.3   The Ministry of Small Scale Industries further added that as such, the reservation policy does not discourage
investment. On the contrary the policy is instrumental in employment generation and meeting the concern of regional
imbalances. Where ever it is felt that the reservation of any particular item needs to be dispensed with in the interest of
growth and development of the small scale sector, the issue is discussed with the stake holders and the respective
administrative Ministries, and depending upon the general opinion, the item is de- reserved with the recommendation of
the Advisory Committee constituted under ID&R Act, 1951. Accordingly, the list of reserved items has come down
from 873 in 1984 to 675 as on date. There is a proposal to de-reserve 85 more items during this year as announced in the
Budget Speech of 2004-05, which will further shorten the list of reserved items. De-reservation of 85 items, as
announced in the Budget (2004-2005) will help the small scale sector to meet the demand for technological up-
gradation, encourage research and development and make the sector competitive to face the domestic and global
competition.
28.4   The Committee is of the view that reservation of items to SSI sector should not become a restriction to the
growth of this sector as far as investment and technology upgradation is concerned. The Committee, however,
strongly, feels that Government should take precaution while taking out an item from the reserved list so that
labour oriented units remain protected.

38.0   IMPACT OF WTO PROVISIONS ON VARIOUS SECTORS OF SMALL SCALE INDUSTRIES
38.1   Government is constantly assessing and monitoring the impact of the removal of Quantitative Restrictions (QRs)
and Globalization on the growth and development of Small Scale Industries (SSIs) in the country. Though removal of
QRs have exposed the SSIs to a greater competition, yet the SSI sector has continued to grow. However, even after the
removal of QRs, protection to the industries is available in the form of raising custom duties upto the bound levels,
imposing anti-dumping duties, taking safeguards measures in case of surge in imports, etc.
38.2   Removal of QRs does not automatically remove all the protection available to the domestic producers. India can
regulate imports through adjustments in tariff within the bound rates. Apart from tariff, action under anti-dumping and
safeguard measures can be taken to provide protection to the domestic producers. For initiating anti-dumping action and
imposition of safeguard duty, separate Directorates are already working under the Ministry of Commerce and the

153rd Report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/153rdreport.htm[04-07-2016 12:50:22]
Ministry of Finance respectively.
38.3   As on May, 2004 final anti-dumping duty has been imposed on 143 items, preliminary duty has been imposed on
5 items and 10 cases are under investigation. DG (AD) has imposed final duties on dry batteries and sports goods after
initiating suo moto investigations. The Director General of Safeguards has taken up action in respect of 15 products for
investigations. So far, safeguard duties in respect of 8 cases have been imposed. The Government are also in the process
of amending the provisions of Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992 so that safeguard action in the
form of Quantitative Restrictions (QRs) could be applied if there are sudden and massive imports. The Government can
also specify standards, impose conditions for reasons of public, plant & animal health, security and public morality on
goods entering India.
38.4   The Government has put in place several measures to help small enterprises become globally competitive. These
include special focus on areas such as technology upgradation, infrastructure assistance through the cluster approach,
timely availability of credit, adoption of modern management practices, use of electronic infrastructure and other IT
applications, marketing and timely information dissemination, including sensitisation of small industries to the emerging
challenges of trade liberalization. Government is fully aware of the WTO environment and is taking appropriate
measures for preparing the Small Scale Sector to meet the challenges of liberalization/globalization. Taking a view of
the whole situation, a new SSI policy including certain new schemes/incentives were announced during the National
Conference on SSI held at New Delhi 30-31 August 2000. Some of the major incentives/ measures include raising the
excise exemption limit from Rs. 50 lakhs to Rs. l crore, a scheme of Capital Subsidy of 12 per cent for investment in
technology upgradation in select sectors, raising the eligibility limit for coverage under the Credit Guarantee Scheme
from Rs.10 lakhs to 25 lakhs, continuation of financial support (up to 75,000) for obtaining ISO 9000/ISO14001
certification up to the end of 10th Plan.
38.5   The WTO Agreement contains a number of provisions and annexes that have an impact on the industry. It has not
been found feasible to monitor the detailed impact of these agreements sector-specific and cluster-specific. However,
industrial units are encouraged to bring to the notice of Office of the DC(SSI) either of their-own or through the
associations so that the aspects of the WTO agreement that are not found suitable to them are brought to the notice of
Ministry of Commerce and Industry to take appropriate action in the matter.
38.6   The Committee is pleased to note that the Government is well aware of the WTO environment and taking
measures for preparing SSI sector to meet the challenges of globalization and liberalization under the WTO
regime. However, the basic concern of the Committee is the protection and promotion of domestic SSI sector
under the prevailing global market economy. Therefore, the continuous and concrete efforts should be made by
the Government to protect the domestic Small Scale Industries from the on-slaught of adverse impact of
globalization and liberalization through appropriate tariff adjustment, anti-dumping measures and other
safeguards. Simultaneously the process of strengthening SSI sector and acquiring the requisite competitive
resilience should be enhanced on the ground level.
39.0   LEGAL FRAMEWORK
39.1   At present Small Scale Industries are governed by Industries (Development & Regulation) Act, 1951. However
the Acts/rules and regulations are formulated by various authorities at the Central Government, State Government level
and Local authorities. These legal regulations can be classified into, labour related issues, tax related issues, finance
related issues, location related issues, product related issues, environment related issues and safety, security, hygiene,
health and welfare related issues, etc.
39.2   The regulatory frame work, particularly pertaining to Labour related Laws is cumbersome and in this regard the
plethora of laws, rules and regulations that have accumulated over the last several years of control regimes have become
truly oppressive. The burden of inspections under various laws has proved to be too onerous to bear. They have adverse
bearing on the promotion and development of Small Scale Industries.
39.3   All the industries including Large, Medium and Small Scale mentioned in schedule I of the ID&R Act, fall under
the purview of Industrial Development and Regulation (IDR) Act, 1951. However, those units employing 50/100 or less
workers , with /without power do not fall under the purview of ID&R Act. A plethora of laws, rules and regulations and
the burden of inspections have crippled the SSI Sector. Therefore with a view to simplify these laws, rules and
regulations and streamline the system of inspections, Small Enterprises Development Bill (Single Law for SSIs) is
proposed to be enacted. It will apply only to Small Scale Sector and contains inter-alia, provisions to simplify existing
Labour Laws and streamline inspections, to wipe out the tyranny of Inspector Raj.
39.4   The Committee desires that legal framework affecting the Small Scale Industries must be satisfactory in order to
give fillip to the promotion and development of Small Scale Industries. The following issues may be taken up on

priority basis and the Ministry of SSI should pursue with the concerned Ministries :-
          (i)    A single Unified Act governing the rules pertaining to retrenchment and layoffs and social benefits for
employees.
          (ii)    Enactment of Single Comprehensive Small Enterprises Development Act for SSEs similar to the

          (iii)   Comprehensive Social Security Scheme for widening the social safety nets.

40.0   INDIAN MODEL OF SSI SECTOR VIS-A-VIS MODEL OF SSI SECTORS IN THE WORLD

ownership term or on lease or by hire purchase come under the purview of this Ministry. This limit in respect of 41
items covering hosiery, Pharmaceuticals, Stationery and hand tools has been enhanced to Rs. 5.00 crore. Further in 1991
Government also decided to extend the benefit being enjoyed by the SSIs to some of the industry-related service and
business enterprises with investment in fixed assets, (excluding land and building) upto Rs. 10.00 lakhs irrespective of
the location of the units.
40.2   The Expenditure Reforms Commission (ERC) had recommended that the inclusion of services and the
enlargement of the scope of the Ministry to medium establishment are important in the context of the emergence of
sunrise knowledge-based industries as the harbinger of a new industrial revolution. New knowledge-based industries
like pharmaceuticals and biotechnology industries have to be internationally competitive and will require
encouragement and support in the initial period of their development. The emergence of IT and of software in a large
way in this country also suggests the need for bringing the service sector prominently into the ambit of encouragement
and support given to small and medium establishments and the Ministry may be renamed as the Ministry of Small and
Medium Establishments.

industrial establishments (including the service sector) be divided into the following four categories on the basis of
investment limits.

growth, therefore, SSI units must be given the space to grow into medium enterprises. In the context of globalisation
there is a growing feeling in certain quarters that India should also adopt the SME Concept World over, policies are
devised to meet the requirement of small and medium enterprises (SME). The Committee desires that the steps should
be initiated for formulating policies for Medium Enterprises and adequate institutional arrangement and mechanism
should be setup for working of medium enterprises in the national
economy.                                                                                                                            (Para 40.6)
          The Committee is in agreement with the recommendation of ERC and directs that the Government should
consider for raising the limit of SSI sector from existing Rs. 1 crore to Rs.5 crore for all items keeping in view inflation
rate and the requirement for setting up small scale units.                                                                           (Para 40.7)
          The Committee further recommends that for streamlining and strengthening the contribution of SSI sector
in the national economy, the Government the should focus on the following issues:----
          (i)    Providing full credit, technology up-gradation, modernization and marketing support to the SSI
sector;
          (ii)   Infrastructure upgradation in general particularly in the major SSI industrial clusters;
          (iii)  Freedom from the plethora of rules and regulations existing in the SSI sector, which is known as
Inspector Raj;
          (iv)  Full support to the service industry to fulfill its true growth and employment generation potential; and
          (v)   Training of existing and potential entrepreneurs and workers on a massive scale. Modern management
practices and skill up-gradation through advanced training facilities for capacity building in the SSI
sector.
                                                                                                                                           (Para 40.8)
