REPORT              
 
India has a thriving Heavy Industry Sector that is an integral part of the economy. Heavy 
Industry in India comprises of the heavy engineerin g industry, machine tools industry, heavy 
electrical industry, industry machinery and auto-in dustry. These industries provide goods and 
services for almost all sectors of the economy. The se are involved in the production of goods that 
are critical for the infrastructure development of the country and have a considerable share in the 
total manufacturing capacity of the country. The Go vernment of India has launched various 
initiatives such as Atmanirbhar Bharat, National In frastructure Pipeline, and National Green 
Hydrogen Mission, etc.  that are expected to further boost demand for the heavy products. In the 
Union Budget 2023-24, the Government of India has p resented seven priority areas “ Saptarishi ” 
and put greater emphasis on Investment in Infrastru cture and Green growth. To give further 
impetus to green mobility, the Government has allocated Rs 5171.97 crore for the flagship Scheme 
of MHI- Scheme for Faster Adoption of Manufacturing  of EVs (FAME) and also put forth 
measures such as Viability Gap Funding for Battery Energy Storage System (BESS), Support to 
Central and State Governments for Scrappage of old Vehicles and ambulances, Custom duty 
exemption on import of specified capital goods and machinery required for manufacture of 
lithium-ion batteries etc.   
 
2. After the presentation of the Union Budget (2023 -24), the Demands for Grants (2023-24) 
of the Ministry of Heavy Industries were referred t o the Department-related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Industry for examination and report thereon. The Committee considered 
and examined the Demands for Grants (2023-24) of the Ministry of Heavy Industries in its meeting 
held on the 20 th  February, 2023 during which the Secretary, M/o Hea vy Industries and Officers of 
the Ministry gave power-point presentation on the Demands for Grants (2023-24) of the Ministry. 
 
Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI) 
3. The Ministry promotes the development and growth  of three sectors i.e . Capital Goods, 
Automobile and Heavy Electrical Equipment in the Co untry; and administers 21 CPSEs and 4 
autonomous bodies. Out of 21 CPSEs, 16 CPSEs are op erational and remaining 5 are under 
closure. The CPSEs under the Ministry are engaged i n manufacturing, consultancy and contracting 
services. The Ministry also looks after the Machine Building Industry and caters to the requirement 
of equipment for Basic Industry such as Steel, Mini ng, Non-ferrous Metals, Power, Fertilizers, 
Refineries, Petrochemicals, Shipping, Paper, Cement , Sugar, etc . The Ministry also supports the 
development of range of Intermediate Engineering In dustry such as Castings, Forgings, Diesel 
Engines, Industrial Gears, and Gear-Boxes.  
 
4. The Ministry maintains a constant dialogue with various Industry Associations in the 
Capital Goods, Automobile and Heavy Electrical Equi pment Sector and encourages initiatives for

the growth of industry in these areas. The Ministry  also assists the industries in achieving their 
growth plans through policy support, and other inte rventions such as advocating rationalization of 
tariff, promoting technological collaboration, research & development activities etc .

Overview of the Automotive Industry  
18. The automotive industry is one of the key drive rs of the Indian economy. Since the 
liberalization of the sector in 1991 and allowing 1 00 per cent FDI through automatic route, Indian 
automobile sector has come a long way. In its writt en submission to the Committee, the Ministry 
informed that today, there is a presence of major g lobal auto manufacturers in the country. All 
categories of vehicles like two-wheelers, three wheelers, passenger cars, light commercial vehicles, 
trucks, buses, tractors, heavy commercial vehicles,  etc. are produced in India and that India is the 

8 
 
second largest manufacturer of 2-Wheelers, the larg est manufacturer of 3-Wheelers and the 3rd 
largest manufacturer of passenger cars in the world. The Ministry further stated that manufacturing 
of automobiles including trucks, buses, cars, three  wheelers/ two wheelers etc . in India has risen at 
a very high pace. The industry produced about 22.9 million vehicles including Passenger Vehicles, 
Commercial Vehicles, Three Wheelers, Two Wheelers a nd Quadricycles in financial year 2021-22 
and about 19.8 million vehicles have been produced during the months of April to December 2022. 
 
19. As regards the industry’s domestic automobile s ales for Passenger Vehicles, Commercial 
Vehicles, Three Wheelers, Two Wheelers and Quadricycles, the Ministry submitted that the sale in 
financial year 2021-22 was 17.5 million and 16 mill ion for April  to December 2022. The Ministry 
also apprised the Committee that the Indian auto co mponent industry, with a well evolved 
manufacturing ecosystem, produces a wide variety of  products including engine parts, drive 
transmission and steering parts, body and chassis, suspension and braking parts, equipment and 
electrical parts, besides others to service the dyn amic automobile industry. According to the 
National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC), the auto industry provides direct employment to 
over 42 lakh people and indirect employment to 2.65  crore people. The automotive industry 
contributes 6% to India’s GDP and 35% to Manufacturing GDP. 
 
20. The Committee notes that the passenger car sect or of India grew significantly as India 
became the 3 
rd  largest manufacturer of passenger cars in the worl d.  At the same time, in 2-
Wheeler sector, India has slipped to 2 nd  position from the 1 st  position as compared to the 
FY2021-22. While the Committee commends the efforts  of the Ministry in respect of 
manufacture of passenger cars, it however notes wit h concern the decline in manufacturing 
of 2-Wheelers in view of the extensive use of 2-Whe elers in the country. The Committee is of 
the view that the Ministry should conduct a holisti c study and look into the shortcomings 
that have caused India to slip down to 2 nd  position in manufacturing of 2-Wheelers so that 
corrective measures can be taken timely to enable India to regain its position. 
 
Agricultural Machinery & Tractor Sector 
21.  Agricultural Machinery mainly consists of Agri cultural Tractors, Power Tillers, Combine 
Harvesters and other Agriculture Machineries & Impl ements. In its reply, the Ministry informed 
the Committee that due to negligible production of Power Tillers, Combine Harvesters and other 
Agricultural Machineries, this sector is mainly dom inated by Agricultural Tractors. Indian Tractor 
Industry is the largest in the world (excluding sub  20 HP belt driven tractors used in China),

Safety of Electric Vehicles 
 54.  During the course of deliberations in the mee ting on the 20 
th  February, 2023, the 
Committee raised its concerns about incidents of fi re in the Electric Vehicles and suggested 

18 
 
installation of adequate safety measures in the Ele ctric Vehicles like fire extinguishing system in 
case of fire. The Ministry apprised the Committee t hat Ministry of Road, Transport and Highways 
looks after the safety aspect of the Electric Vehic les and that an expert committee has also been 
constituted and the committee has also given a repo rt. The Ministry informed the Committee that 
manufacturing guidelines for Electric Vehicles have  also been changed and after following these 
guidelines, there is less likelihood of such fire incidents taking place in the Electric Vehicles.  
 
55.  The Committee notes that the incidents of fire in E lectric Vehicles is a cause of concern 
and needs to be addressed properly. Most of the fir e catching incidents takes place in EVs when 
battery is damaged or punctured.  Apart from technical faults and manufacturing defec ts of 
batteries, many of the fire catching incidents may also be attributed to personal negligence of users 
by not taking proper precautions . The Committee recommends that the Ministry should  in 
consultation with the stakeholders analyse and asce rtain the root cause of fire incidents in 
EVs and find a solution for it so that such inciden ts do not take place so as to avoid any 
casualty. Besides, proper guidelines should be issu ed for the users and adequate awareness 
should be generated among the people regarding the proper usage of Electric Vehicles.  
 
56. The Committee recommends that adequate and inte rnationally accepted safety 
standards should be adopted to ensure safety of the  vehicles and its users. A proper venting 
mechanism for overheated cells in the battery must be put in place. Besides, the following 
safety measures to prevent fire hazards in the Electric Vehicles may be followed:  
(i) Use of fire-resistant materials such as aluminum, stainless steel, and other non-
combustible materials for the body of the electric vehicles.  
(ii) Use of fire-resistant insulation materials in the b attery compartment and other 
areas where the electric vehicle components are located.  
(iii) Installation of fire-extinguisher system in the bat tery compartment to 
extinguish any fires that may occur. 
(iv) Installation of fire-resistant wiring and other ele ctrical components to reduce 
the risk of fire due to short circuits.  
(v) Installation of fire-resistant brakes and other saf ety components to reduce the 
risk of fire due to friction.  
(vi) Installation of temperature sensors in the battery compartment to detect any 
overheating and activate the fire suppression system.  
(vii) Installation of battery management system to monito r the temperature and 
charge levels of the battery and alert the driver if any anomalies are detected.  
(viii) Installation of cooling system in the battery compa rtment to keep the 
temperature of the battery within a safe range.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
 
1. The Committee notes that the Budgetary allocation o f Rs 6171.63 crore for the 
Financial Year 2023-24 is the highest ever enhancem ent in comparison to the BE of the 
previous years. The Committee notes the significant enhanced fund allocation to the Ministry 
and hopes that the allocation would be utilized eff ectively in achieving the targets set by the 
Ministry for the Financial Year 2023-24. 
 (Para 7) 
 
2. The Committee also notes that the Ministry has been  able to utilize more than 99% of 
the funds provided at the RE Stage in the previous two Financial Years i.e.  2020-21 & 2021-
22. However, in the Financial Year 2022-23, the Min istry has been able to utilize only Rs. 
1428.71 crore, which is 44.36% of the RE allocation s of Rs. 3220.33 crore till 31.01.2023. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry should ensur e that the entire fund allocated at the 
RE stage is utilized effectively in consonance with  the financial policy and henceforth should 
rationalize the expenditure throughout the year eve nly to avoid any uneven spending during 
the last quarter of the financial year in future. 
 (Para 8) 
 
3. The Committee observes that the expenditure of the Ministry under most of its minor 
heads was sluggish throughout the Financial Year 20 22-23, and even at the fag end of the 
financial year, expenditure under “ Revenue Section ” was barely 43.6% of the RE and most 
of the funds under “ Capital Section ” was surrendered at the RE stage, which reflects 
imprudent financial management and planning. The Co mmittee recommends that the 
Ministry should draw up a clear and comprehensible plan for the Financial Year 2023-24 so 
as to avoid accumulation of the unspent funds in th e last quarter of the financial year and 
refrain from spending more than 33% of the allocate d amount during the last quarter of the 
financial year contrary to the General Financial Rules.  
(Para 10) 
4. The Committee observes that for the Financial Year 2023-24, the Budget Projection of 
the Ministry was Rs 6574.93 crore against which Min istry of Finance has allocated Rs 
6171.63 crore, which is about 93.87% of the project ion, as such, there is a shortfall of Rs 
403.30 crore in allocation. The budgetary allocatio n for the major Schemes of Ministry such 
as FAME India and Scheme for Competitiveness in Cap ital Goods Sector have also shortfall 
in allocation for the FY2023-24. The Committee reco mmends that the Ministry should take 
all-out effort to strategize and utilize the major part of the allocations timely and if need be, 
allocation for more funds should be projected at th e RE stage to avoid any hindrance  in the 
effective implementation of these Flagship Schemes. 
 (Para 12)

Covid-19 Pandemic induced constraints, etc. due to which the major portions of the funds

the major and important Scheme for Enhancement of C ompetitiveness in Capital Goods

(ii) Use of fire-resistant insulation materials in the b attery compartment and other

(iv) Installation of fire-resistant wiring and other ele ctrical components to reduce

(v) Installation of fire-resistant brakes and other saf ety components to reduce the 
risk of fire due to friction.  
(vi) Installation of temperature sensors in the battery compartment to detect any 
overheating and activate the fire suppression system.  
(vii) Installation of battery management system to monito r the temperature and 
charge levels of the battery and alert the driver if any anomalies are detected.  
(viii) Installation of cooling system in the battery compa rtment to keep the
