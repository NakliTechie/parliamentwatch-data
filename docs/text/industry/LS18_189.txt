REPORT
           
1.0       Introduction
 
1.1       In most developed and developing countries of the world such as the USA, France, Germany, China, South Korea, Malaysia and
Thailand, small and medium enterprises are not treated as separate entities but are viewed as one sector in need of almost similar policy
interventions and legal framework. It is only in our country that we have all along targeted small enterprises, especially small scale industries
as a special sector in exclusion of the medium enterprises whose existence we do not recognize neither legally nor other wise.  
 
1.2       Small Scale Industry however, continues to be  defined as an industrial undertaking in which the investment in fixed assets in plant &
machinery, whether held on ownership terms or on lease , or by hire-purchase, does not exceed rupees  one crore. It inter-alia includes, tiny
(micro) enterprises having investment in plant and machinery not exceeding Rs.25 lakh, and  Small Scale Service & Business (industry
related) Enterprises (SSSBE ) with investment in fixed assets, excluding land & building, upto rupees 10 lacs. However, to enhance their
competitiveness both in domestic and global markets by facilitating technological upgradation and adoption of international quality standards,
the investment ceiling in 140  High-Tech And Export Oriented items  and drugs and pharmaceutical sector as a whole,   has been raised from
Rs. 1 crore to Rs.5 crore.
 
1.3       Various sectors and sub-sectors of the SSI are Ancillary Industrial Undertakings, High-Tech And Export Oriented Units, Tiny
Enterprises, Small Scale Service And Business (Industry Related) Enterprises .A Comprehensive Policy Package was announced in the year
2000 which incorporates the schemes and programmes for the promotion of various sectors / sub sectors of SSI. The small enterprises sector
is a vibrant segment of the Indian economy which contributed over 39% of the industrial sector output and 33% of the nation’s exports, and
provided employment to over 282 lakh persons through 118 lakh units (2004-05) spread both in rural and urban areas of the country. The
major contribution of the small enterprises sector lies in creation of employment opportunities at lower capital cost.
1.4       To meet the global requirements and competition both domestically and internationally, the small and medium sector requires 
immediately a promotional package to enhance its capabilities both in terms of quality and quantity and a proper comprehensive legislative
back up.  Accordingly, Government has introduced a Small and Medium Enterprises Development Bill, 2005 to be able to provide statutory
backing to the SME Sector  on 12
th May 2005, and has also envisaged a Policy Package for the promotion of Micro and Small Enterprises,
providing legislative back up, easy access to credit and fiscal incentives, technology development and infrastructure development through
cluster development programmes, entrepreneurial and managerial development, etc.

3.0       Policy iniatitves taken by Government  during the financial year 2005-06
 
3.1       Though the State Governments are primarily responsible for the promotion and development of SSI, the Central Government took
several policy initiatives for small enterprises sector during 2005-06:
193 items reserved for exclusive manufacture in the SSI sector have been dereserved in FY 2004-05. The total number of reserved items now
stands at 506.
As announced in the Budget 2005-06, the turnover eligibility limit under the General SSI Excise Exemption Scheme has been raised from
Rs.3 crore to Rs.4 crore.
With a view to integrating small and medium enterprises, facilitating their growth and enhancing their competitiveness (including measures to
reduce the rigours of the  “Inspector Raj” faced by the sector), the ‘Small and Medium Enterprises Development (SMED) Bill, 2005’ has been
introduced in the Lok Sabha.
A ‘Policy Package for Stepping up Credit to Small and Medium Enterprises’ has been announced by the Government.
Under the Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS) for Technology Upgradation, amendments have been made with effect from 29
th
September 2005 which, inter alia, include raising ceiling on loans from Rs.40 lakh to Rs.1 crore and the rate of subsidy from 12 per cent to 15
per cent.
The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has formulated the scheme of Small Enterprises Financial Centres (SEFC) under which banks will be
encouraged to establish mechanisms for better co-ordination between their branches and branches of Small Industries Development Bank of
India (SIDBI) in the identified clusters for more efficient credit delivery.
To facilitate technology upgradation and enhancing competitiveness, the investment limit (in plant and machinery) has been raised from Rs.1
crore to Rs.5 crore in respect of 69 additional items reserved for manufacture in the small scale sector and for all items in the drugs and
pharmaceuticals sector.
A new “Package for Promotion of Micro and Small Enterprises” is being formulated. This would include supplementary measures to
encourage adequate credit flow, further incentives for technology upgradation, infrastructural and marketing facilities, capacity building of
micro and small enterprises, support to women entrepreneurs, etc.
 
3.2       The small scale industries (SSI) constitutes one of the vibrant sectors of the Indian economy in terms of employment generation, the
strong entrepreneurial base it helps to create and its share in industrial production and exports.  During 2004-05, the small enterprises sector
registered a growth rate of 10 per cent as against a growth rate of 8.4 per cent for the overall industrial sector. During 2003-04, the
contribution of Small Enterprises Sector in the Gross Domestic Products (GDP) was 6.71 per cent.
 
3.3       The Committee understands that along with agriculture and organized large industries, the small enterprises sector including both SSI
and services provide the largest employment generation potential in the country.  The committee is of the view that Government should make
comparative assessment of Small Scale Industries vis-à-vis other Industries on the basis of growth rate, and chalk out strategy for   enhancing
further the employment generation potential, maxmising contribution to national exechequer, in order to  combat the problem of poverty and
improving  the standard of living and thus making real contribution to the National Economy.

4.0 Charter of functions of  Ministry of Small Scale Industries
 
4.1 The Government created the Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Agro and Rural Industries (SSI&ARI) in October, 1999 as the   nodal
Ministry for formulation of policy and co-ordination of Central assistance relating to promotion and development  of the small scale industries
in India. The Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Agro and Rural Industries (SSI&ARI) was bifurcated into two separate Ministries,
namely, Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries in   September, 2001. Taking into account the high
potential for growth in the SSI sector in terms of output, employment and exports, the role of the Ministry of Small Scale Industries is to
strengthen the SSI sector, to enable it to remain competitive in market-led economy and generate additional employment opportunities. For
achieving these objectives, the endeavor of the Ministry is to provide the SSI sector proper and timely inputs like adequate credit from
financial institutions/banks; funds for technology upgradation and modernization; adequate infrastructural facilities; modern testing facilities
and quality certification laboratories; modern management practices and skill upgradation through advanced training facilities;  marketing
assistance; and level playing field at par with the large industries sector.
 
4.2 The Committee understands that the Ministry of SSI designs policies, programmes, projects and schemes in consultation with its
organizations and various stakeholders and monitor their implementation with a view to assisting   the promotion and growth of small scale
industries. The committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should not only  advocate the  policy on behalf of the SSI sector with
other Ministries/Departments of the Central Government and the State and Union Territories for the promotion and proliferation of SMEs but
also co-opreate and  co-ordinate with them in the implementation of schemes projects prepared in this regard.
5.0 Organizational structure of Ministry of SSI
 
5.1 The Ministry of Small Scale Industries designs and implements the policies through its field organizations for the promotion and growth
of small scale industries.  The implementation of policies and various programmes/schemes for providing infrastructural and support services
to small enterprises is undertaken through its attached offices, namely Small Industries Development Organisation (SIDO), its field offices,
and National Small Industries Corporation Limited (NSIC), a public sector undertaking under the Ministry of SSI. Secretary (SSI) reviews
and monitors the functioning of organisations under the Ministry, implementation of plan schemes and various developmental activities on
fortnightly basis. The present monitoring mechanism has been found to be effective.
 
5.2 The Committee is of the view that monitoring mechanism of the Ministry of SSI should aim at developmental supervision which would
lead to carrying out the schemes and programmes efficiently and effectively in time and without cost over-run. 
 
6.0       Proposal of re-christening of the Ministry of SSI into the Ministry of Small and Medium Enterprises (SME) 
 
6.1       The Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Agro and Rural Industries (SSI & ARI) was carved out of the erstwhile Department of
Industrial Development (DID) on 14
th October, 1999 with a view to providing focused attention for promotion and development of small
scale industries.   The Ministry of SSI & ARI, thereafter on 1st September, 2001 was bifurcated into the Ministry of Small Scale Industries
(SSIs) and the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries. As per the Allocation of Business Rules notified on 6th September, 2001 the Ministry of
SSI, now, has the entries which include  coordination of the Development of Small Scale Industries, other than cottage and Village Industries
and Tiny/Micro Enterprises and Small Industries Board and National Small Industries Corporation Limited.
      
6.2       The Ministry is the nodal Ministry for policy formulation, promotion, development and protection of Small Scale Industries (SSIs). At
present all industries with an investment upto Rs. 1 (one) crore in Plant and Machinery whether held on ownership term or on lease or by hire
purchase come under the purview of this Ministry.  This limit in respect of 41 items covering hosiery, Pharmaceuticals, Stationery and hand
tools has been enhanced to Rs. 5.00 crore.   Further in 1991 Government also decided to extend the benefit being enjoyed by the SSIs to some
of the industry related service & business enterprises with investment in fixed assets, (excluding land & building) upto Rs. 10.00 lakhs
irrespective of the location of the units. 
                       
6. 3      The Expenditure Reforms Commission (ERC) had recommended that:

the initial period of their development. The emergence of IT and of software in a large way in this country also suggests the need for bringing
the service sector prominently into the ambit of encouragement and support given to small and medium establishments.  The scope of 
Ministry of SSI  should be enlarged to cover the service sector in addition to the manufacturing sector and to define and include medium

7.0 Action Plans set for the Ministry for the year 2006-07
 
7.1 The Action Plan for the Ministry for the year 2006-07 is stated  as under:-
 
Formulation of a single legislation to promote the growth of the sector and free it from  ‘Inspector Raj’;
Formulation of a Package for Promotional of Micro and Small Enterprises;
Examination of problems facing Enterprises in the Unorganised / Informal Sector through the National Commission set up for this purpose;
Facilitate credit flow to the SSI Sector – by effective implementation of ‘Policy Package for Stepping up Credit to SMEs’;
Infrastructure upgradation in major industrial clusters; and
Institutional reforms in terms of: (i) Decentralisation; (ii) Simplification; (iii) Transparency; (iv) Accountability; and (v) E-governance.
 
7.2 The Committee recognizes that the  organizational structure of Ministry of SSI have proved effective as an instrument of implementation
of various  policies, programmes/schemes relating  to Ministry of Small Scale Industries during 2005-06. The Committee is pleased to note
that  the present organisational set up has been quite effective and efficient however, the Ministry of SSI, keeping in view  the emergence of
SMEs, which has been well recognized in its Action Plan,    would need some re-orientation of form and retraining of staff to enable it to
make full use of the opportunities and challenges in the future. The Committee, accordingly , recommends that following iniatives may be
undertaken :-
More regular appraisal of policy initiative for the SME sector in similar countries;
Ascertaining best practices within the Central Government and also abroad for promotion and development of SME sector and their
adoption/adaptation in the Indian SME context;
Closer and more structural interaction with the stakeholders;
Improving synergies among the constituent organizations of the Ministry and the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries;
Promoting constructive competitiveness among field level organizations based on well defined objectives and action plans;
Retraining of staff, as appropriate.
 
7.3 The Committee hopes that once the requisite  steps are taken and adequate   precautionary measure are undertaken to achieve the Action
Plan subsequent to the  constant review and monitoring of the Action Plan, there should not be any problem in achieving the targets set in the
Action Plan 2006-07.

8.1.1 The Committee is constrained to express its displeasure  over the reduced budgetary allocation to the Ministry of SSI during the year
2006-07. Against the  projected  sum of Rs. 574.68 crore  (inclusive of Rs. 3.60 crore for works Outlay provided in the Demands of Ministry
of Urban Development) for the year 2006-07, the  Planning Commission has fixed the Annual Plan Outlay at Rs. 469.93 crores (inclusive of
Rs. 3.60 crore for works Outlay provided in the Demands of Ministry of Urban Development). The Committee wonders as how the Ministry
of SSI would manage the budgetary gap while achieving the originally envisaged objectives and action plan.
 
8.1.2    The Committee is anguished to observe that the budgetary allocation has been reduced under the Ministry’s demands for   Credit
Guarantee Fund Scheme where the proposed demand was Rs. 206.00 crore but the Planning Commission has approved only to the tune of  
Rs. 118.10 crore.  Similarly, under the Head Science & Technology, Research and Development, the proposed demand was Rs.  21.85 crore
while the same was approved to the tune of Rs.  16.85 crore  only. 
 
8.1.3    The Committee understands that Credit Gurantee Scheme is significant so far the credit to the SSI sector is concerned. And the net
credit to the SSI sector by the Public Sector Bank as the percentage to the total credit is dwindwling to the level of 9.8%.  The Finance
Minister , on the one hand understands the need for gearing up the SSI sector, considering its  importance as the “Engine of Growth” and
having the employment –generation potential but on the other hand,  the Planning Commission has  reduced the budgetary allocation under
the Credit Gurantee Scheme against the  projected demand by the Ministry of SSI for the year 2006-07.  The Committee is unable to
understand  the basic logic behind such budgeting, where there remains enormous gap between the policy and finance required to  to support
the  programme/scheme under the policy.
 
8.1.4    The Committee is of the considered view that Science & Technology, Research and Development is very much beneficial for any
industrial activities so far as  the cost-reduction, product-development, product - design etc. is concerned, therefore, reducing the budgetary –
allocation against the projected demand by the Ministry of SSI would amount to cutting the R&D activities, thereby, the margin of the profits
.
 
8.1.5    The Committee, therefore, desires that the Planning Commission would pay due attention to the proposals of the Ministry of SSI at the
R.E. stage and accordingly allocate the demands as per the requirement of the SSI sector.

18.0     Legal framework affecting the Small Scale Industries
 
18.1     Consequent upon  its introduction in the Parliament on 12 May, 2005, the “Small and Medium Enterprises Development Bill 2005”
was considered by the Department – related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry. Based on the Committee’s report, the Bill” was 
modified. The Note for the Cabinet on amendments to the “Small and Medium Enterprises Development (SMED) Bill, 2005” was considered
by the Cabinet in its meeting on 18. 8.2005. The Cabinet decided that the matter be, in the first instance, considered by a Group of Ministers
(GOM). Meetings of the GOM were held on 25.10.2005 and 10.11.2005. Based on the recommendations of the GOM,  a Note for Cabinet
was prepared and the same was approved by the Cabinet in its meeting on 24.11.2005.  The notice for intention to (i) move the amendments to
the Small and Medium Enterprises Development (SMED) Bill, 2005 and (ii) for consideration and passing of SMED Bill, 2005 had been
forwarded to the Secretary General, Lok Sabha, on 2.12.2005.However the Bill could not be placed in the Lok Sabha due to paucity of time.

In the meanwhile some amendments have been drafted and approved by the Cabinet in its meeting held on 9 March 2006.
 
 18.2    At present Small Scale Industries are governed by Industries (Development  & Regulation) Act 1951.However the Acts/rules and
regulations are formulated by various authorities at the Central Government State Government level and Local authorities. These legal
regulations can be classified into labour related issues,tax related issues finance related issues ,location related issues ,product related issues
,environment related issues and safety, security, hygiene, health and welfare related issues, etc.
 
18.3     The regulatory frame work, particularly pertaining to Labour related Laws is cumbersome and in this regard the plethora of laws, rules
and regulations that have accumulated over the last several years of control regimes have become truly oppressive. The burden of inspections
under various laws has proved to be too onerous to bear. They have adverse bearing on the promotion and development of Small Scale
Industries.
 
18.4     All the industries including Large, Medium and Small Scale mentioned in schedule I of the ID&R Act, fall under the purview of
Industrial Development and Regulation (IDR) Act 1951 .However, those units employing 50/100  or less workers , with /without power do
not fall under the purview of ID&R Act. As has been already explained in the first para , plethora of  laws rules and regulations and the
burden of inspections under these, have crippled the SSI Sector. Therefore with a view to simplify these laws, rules and regulations and
streamline the system of inspections, Small  and Medium Enterprises Development Bill,2005is proposed to be enacted. It contains,  inter-alia,
provisions to simplify existing Labour Laws and streamline inspections, to wipe out the tyranny of Inspector Raj.
 
 18.5    The Committee understands that legal framework affecting the Small Scale Industries is not conducive and satisfactory, therefore, the
Committee feels that  in order to give fillip to the promotion and development of Small Scale Industries, the following immediate measure
may be undertaken :- 
 
A single Unified Act governing the rules pertaining to retrenchment and layoffs and social benefits for employees.
 
Enactment of SMED Bill, 2005.
 
Comprehensive Social Security Scheme for widening the social safety nets.
 
Since the Inspector Raj is causing severe harassment to Small Scale Units, therefore, the provision made in the proposed legislation for giving
good bye to Inspector Raj to wipe out the tyranny  of Inspector Raj.
 
18.6     Since the issues relates to Ministry of Labour and Employment, therefore, the committee recommends that the Ministry of SSI should
coordinate with Ministry of Labour and Employment at the earliest and effectively, and furnish the Committee outcome of the efforts so
undertaken by the Ministry.
 
19.0     Formulation of National Policy for Small Scale Industries.
 
19.1     The Comprehensive Policy Package      aims to strengthen the SSI Sector to be able to meet the fast changing economic scenario and
be able to face the challenges of the domestic and global competition due to liberalization of economy and introduction of WTO regime.  The
main emphasis is on easy credit availability, technological, modernization, infrastructural development, marketing support, etc.  The existing
policies /schemes have been framed keeping in view the ground realities and are being reviewed from time to time with stake holders to
enforce the corrective/supportive measures  required to make them suitable, sufficient and  effective for supporting the promotion and
development of Small Scale Industries .
 
19.2     The emphasis in existing policies and schemes  has accordingly been laid on easier access to credit, availability of collateral free
composite loans up to Rs.25 lakhs to assist small/tiny units through Credit Guarantee Schemes, introduction of Laghu Udyami Card Scheme,
marketing support through the schemes like SSI-MDA Schemes, providing subsidy for technological upgradation through the Credit Linked
Capital Subsidy Scheme for Technology Up gradation, encourage obtaining of ISO-9000/4001 Certification by providing 75% (not exceeding
Rs.75000/-) of the cost,  infrastructure development through IID scheme, since extended to all areas with 50% reservation each for tiny sector
and rural areas, increase in project limit under National Equity Fund to Rs.50 lakhs,  and cluster development schemes, etc.  The funds are
also provided to State Governments to develop and upgrade the Mini Tool Rooms and to industry associations for setting up of Testing
Centres and sub-contracting exchanges.
 
19.3     The Committee  feels that there is the need for Comprehensive  National Policy for strengthening the Small Scale Sector, although the
process has already started  since 1991 in the sporadic manner, the need is to consolidate and streamline the national policy.  Keeping in view
of the dynamic situation of the Indian SSI sector vis-à-vis domestic & international industrial & economic environment, the Committee feels
that  that SSI policy must have been  revisited and revisied incorporating components viz programmes for infrastructure development,
technological upgradation, marketing support, easier access to creditand scientific management for inter-sectoral cooperation for ensured
promotion and proliferation of SMEs.

20.6     Working of cluster approach to the promotion and    proliferation of Small Scale Industries
           
20.6.1  The Ministry of SSI Stated that the present approach of Ministry of SSI for cluster developments is a holistic one, focusing on
interventions in various areas like technology, marketing, skill upgradation etc. selected on the basis of assessment of felt needs of the
Cluster.  The Programme is implemented with active involvement of various Cluster Actors such as Small Scale Industrial Units, Industry
Associations, Business Development Services Providers, Policy Makers etc. The process of cluster development usually consists of the  steps
as Selection of the Cluster;  Selection of a Cluster Development Agent ; Diagnostic Study ; Preparation of Action Plan for intervention ;
Approval of the project and release of funds through leveraging ; Implementation of Trust Building between Cluster Actors and Cluster
Development Agent, and also among Cluster Actors; Monitoring and Evaluation and Self  Management Phase.      The Ministry of Small
Scale Industries, through its successful collaborative efforts with UNIDO, and also independently on its own, has initiated and completed a
series of Cluster Development initiatives spread all over the country.  Till date, 81 clusters have been selected for interventions in this
manner. 
 
20.6.2  Cluster Development Programme aims at selection of suitable clusters  for development under a holistic approach.  The criteria for
selection of Clusters are based on its significance in terms of nature of products and its contribution to production, export and employment. 
Existence of critical gaps in respect of technology, quality, marketing support, common facility etc. are also taken into account. The Ministry
of Small Scale Industries has recently circulated detailed guidelines for selection and implementation of interventions to small industry
clusters under the SICDP. Cluster Development is being implemented either through the   field network of SISIs or in collaboration with
reputed  agencies like Entrepreneurship Development Institute at Ahmedabad and National Resource Centre for Cluster Development,
Hyderabad.  The results are highly encouraging in general. 
 
20.6.3  The Committee notes that under Cluster Development approach, the key problem faced by Micro Small & Medium Enterprises
(MSMEs) are being addressed. The Committee understands that the isolated Enterprises are unable to achieve economies of scales, lack
negotiating power  and have limited access to credit, strategic information, technology and market etc, therfore, such   enterprises especially
the Small & Micro ones can significantly increase their comparative advantage by mutual- cooperation and building linkages with private &
public service providers.  The Committee  views that there is the need to     build competitive strength through cost reduction, value chain
upgradation and utilization of collective economies of scale. The committee, therefore, recommends that   Cluster Development  must focus
on reducing the isolation faced by MSMEs by strengthening the linkages among all cluster stakeholders to coordinate action and pool
resources for a common development goal.

34.0     Credit  linked Capital Subsidy Scheme
 
34.1     The Ministry of Small Scale Industries (SSI) is operating a scheme for technology upgradation of Small Scale Industries (SSI) called
the Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS). The Scheme aims at facilitating technology upgradation by providing  upfront capital
subsidy to  SSI units, including tiny, khadi, village and coir industrial units, on institutional finance (credit) availed of by them for
modernisation of their production equipment (plant and machinery) and techniques.  The Scheme (pre-revised) provided for 12 per cent 
capital subsidy to SSI units, including  tiny units, on institutional finance availed of by them for induction of well established and improved
technology in selected sub-sectors/products approved under the Scheme. The eligible amount of subsidy calculated under the pre-revised 
scheme was based on the actual loan amount not exceeding Rs.40 lakh. 
 
34.2     Due to insufficient investment and lack of awareness of both the quality standards and access to modern technologies, a large
percentage of SSI units continue with outdated technology and plant & machinery.  With increasing competition due to liberalisation of the
economy, the survival and growth of the SSI units are critically dependent on their modernisation and technological upgradation. 
Upgradation of both the process of manufacture and corresponding plant and machinery is necessary for the small enterprises  to reduce the
cost of production and remain price competitive at a time when cheaper products are easily available in the global market. 
 
34.3     It is in this background that the Finance Minister made an announcement in the Budget Speech of 2004-05 to raise the ceiling for loans
under the Scheme from Rs. 40 lakh to Rs. 1 crore and rate of subsidy from 12 per cent to 15 per cent.  Further, in the light of the experience
gathered in implementing the Scheme,  certain other modifications were also required  to make it  more useful to  the  SSI  units, including
tiny, khadi, village and coir industrial  units,  in taking up technology upgradation on a larger scale. After considering these issues, the CLCSS
has been amended with effect from September 29, 2005 and the ceiling on loans under the Scheme has been raised  from Rs. 40 lakh to Rs. 1
crore and the  rate of subsidy has been enhanced from 12 per cent to 15 per cent;
 
34.4     The target group for the scheme are SSI units, including tiny, khadi, village and coir industrial units (falling under the purview of the
Ministry of ARI). The scope of the scheme has been widened to include tiny,  khadi, village and coir industrial units (falling under the
purview of the Ministry of ARI) to cover khadi and village industrial units and coir industrial units.  The basis for the selection of the target
group is that the scheme has primarily been conceived for providing subsidy to the small and tiny enterprises in India. However, industry 
graduating from small scale to medium scale on account to sanction of additional loan under CLCSS are also eligible for assistance under the
scheme. 
 
34.5     Presently, the scheme is in operation up to March 31, 2007 or till the time sanction of aggregate capital subsidy disbursed by the nodal
agencies/banks reaches Rs.600 crore, whichever is earlier.   The amount of subsidy sanctioned between 2001-02 and 2005-06 is Rs.36.65
crore (approx.).  However, most of it ( Rs. 18.14 crore approx.) has been sanctioned during 2005-06. This shows that the amendments made in
the existing CLCSS guidelines has enabled more and more   tiny and small scale  industrial units to undertake modernisation through availing
of  capital subsidy under the CLCSS.

36.0     Problems being faced by SIDO in the implementation of programmes encouraging SMEs
           
36.1     The Committee has been  apprised that SIDO is implementing several schemes/programmes for the promotion and development of the
SSI sector in the country.  Most of these schemes/programmes are aimed at augmenting the credit flow to the SSI sector and are linked with
the banks/financial institutions.  The banks are, however, not favourably inclined to finance the SSI sector for various reasons like adverse
risk perception,  lack of credit rating facilities, higher transaction cost in processing of large number of loan applications, etc.  Moreover,
some of the schemes are optional for the banks to participate in their implementation. Furthermore, the implementation of the schemes/
programmes is also dependent on the willingness of the beneficiaries (i.e. the SSI units/ entrepreneurs) and the State Governments. The State
Governments have a major role to play in the implementation of some of the schemes/ programmes by way of providing matching financial
contribution, infrastructural facilities, fiscal incentives/ concessions etc.            The Ministry of SSI has  launched  a scheme on credit rating,
which includes providing financial assistance to the SSI units for obtaining credit rating. The Ministry of SSI has taken up the matter with all
the Public Sector Banks for dissemination of information on schemes/programmes at the branch level and among the SSI entrepreneurs. The
SSI Associations have also been asked to play a pro-active role in increasing awareness about the schemes/ programmes/ facilities being
implemented for the SSI sector among the SSI entrepreneurs.
 
36.2     The Committee is of the view that working of SIDO  must be strengthened so much so that it can encourage emerging SMEs to catch
up the global trend . There is a need to encourage SSI units for obtaining credit rating and setting up of exclusive credit rating agencies for the
SSI sector. There is further need for wider and effective dissemination of information on the schemes/ facilities being provided by the banks
to the SSI sector at the branch level and among the entrepreneurs.  In addition, the State Governments need to provide an enabling
environment for development and promotion of the SSI sector through provision of fiscal concessions/incentives to the sector, better
infrastructure facilities etc. for facilitating smooth implementation of the schemes of SIDO. The Committee is of the considered view that the 
Small and Medium Enterprises Development Bill, 2005, would  strengthen SIDDO.  Besides, SIDO may be entrusted with the effective  role
in the  implemetaion of  promotional package for the Small scale sector which  is under formulation.

programmes on WTO, Intellectual Proprietary Rights (IPR), information and encouragement sunrise industries, cluster development and

39.1     The Objective of the Credit Guarantee Scheme to ensure better flow of credit to small and tiny enterprises by minimizing the risk
perception of financial institutions. This will give greater comfort level to banks and also help financing without collateral security. The
mechanism of the scheme is by setting up a corpus fund with contribution from the Government of India and the Small Industries
Development Bank of India (SIDBI) in the ratio of 4:1.   At present, the size of the corpus fund is Rs. 1181.55 crore.  As the Operating
Agency  a trust has been  set up by SIDBI/GOI and serviced by SIDBI. CMD, SIDBI is the Chairman of the Trust; AS & DC (SSI) is the
Vice-Chairman of the Trust; two other members are Chairman, Indian Banks’ Association (IBA) and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of the
Trust. The lending institutions can have loans guaranteed by payment of one-time guarantee fee of 2.5 per cent and an annual service fee of
0.75 per cent of the credit sanctioned to the Trust, the implementing agency. The Eligible Institutions  
 are Commercial Banks – public and
private, select regional rural banks (RRBs), North East Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi), National Small Industries Corporation
(NSIC) and SIDBI. So far the Claim Settlement is concerned ,  after 2-year lock-in period; settlement up to 75 per cent pending recovery
proceedings, balance 25 percent on completion of recovery proceedings.  The Extent of Guaranteeis75% of loans up to Rs.25 lakh. The
Maximum Risk cover is  75% of the risk cover subject to a ceiling of Rs.18.75 lakh.  Interest rate payable by borrower not to exceed 3%
beyond  PLR of lender.  The Scheme was announced on 30August 2000 and became operational in January 2001.
 
39.2     The scheme was launched with a view to alleviate the problems of collateral security faced by the small enterprises, particularly first
generation entrepreneurs, and to remove the impediments in flow of credit to Small enterprises sector. The problems in the implementation of
the scheme pertains to low level of awareness about the scheme amongst the field level officers of MLIs, negative mindset of the MLIs due to
greater volume of administrative work in the small enterprises lending and may be their bad experience in the past on a similar scheme,
bankers’ traditional/usual comfort with collateral security and perceived high risk nature of small enterprises sector. These problems of the
bank branches need to be seriously addressed by the management of the banks.  By enhancing the awareness about the scheme among the
field level officers of MLIs through seminars/training, the progress under the scheme could be improved. Furthermore, enhancement of the

41.13   Sub-Contracting Exchange for Ancillary  Development
 
41.13.1            The term “Sub-contracting Exchange (SCX) for Ancillary development” is an  activity for providing marketing support to
small scale units and for proliferation of industrial activities in the backward areas in different parts of the country by motivating large, public
and private sector units and other organizations to get their requirement of spares/sub-assemblies and other items from the small scale units
which result in ancillary development. Common funds is provided for SCX and Vendor Development Programme ( VDP).    A Sub-
contracting Exchange is define as an entity which builds information on expertise & infrastructure etc. to connect Small , Medium and Large
enterprises (SMEs) and facilitate building up comprehensive relationship amongst them. Such an exchange is a Store House of Data with
regard to the capacities of the small- scale units in terms of products manufactured/services rendered, idle capacities available on particular
processes/machines on one hand and storing data with regard to the requirements of the buyers which could be product/components/sub
assemblies/services.  The exchange also stores data about the specifications, class of accuracy, quantities, etc.

41.23   National Commission on Enterprises  in the unorganized/informal sectors
41.23.  The National Commission for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector (NCEUS) has been constituted to examine the problems
confronting the enterprise in the unorganised/informal sector and make appropriate recommendations to provide technical, marketing and
credit support to these enterprises. The NCEUS is proposing the formation of Growth Poles in different parts of the country with a view to
integrating within a geographical location a number of clusters of production units engaged in manufacturing services and non-farm activities
and facilitating the expansion of production and employment in small and micro enterprises. The Growth Poles would incorporate the concept
of the Scheme of Provision of Urban Amenities in Rural Areas (PURA) that has been advocated by Dr. A.P.J. Abdual Kalam, President of
India.
           
41.23.2            In the first instance, the Commission is proposing a few pilot Growth Poles for consideration by the Government as mentioned
in the Finance Minister’s Budget Speech, 2005-06. For the conduct of the proposed pilots, a number of multi-product
industrial/artisanal/handloom and handicrafts clusters that have the potential to evolve into Growth Pole are being identified. The NCEUS
proposes to delineate Growth Poles in the NER and Sikkim Region to give a fillip to the unorganized sector enterprises in this region. The
Plan outlay of Rs 3.30 crore in be 2006-07 is proposed to be utilized for conducting detailed technical and feasibility studies and preparation
of Detailed project reports for Growth Poles in the region and commence the projects in the region.
           
41.23.3            The idea of Growth Poles is an extension of the idea of clusters based on the economics of inter-dependence of different
producing units in a particular geographical concentration, creating the scope for duplication and extension of those activities in larger
contiguous areas that would eventually cover the whole of the country. These Growth Poles would also combine effectively the essential
features of the proposed scheme of PURA of providing urban amenities in rural areas. The inter-dependence of these units allows them to
enjoy external economies from their simultaneous and complementary functioning. Such positive externalities are derived from (a) forward
and backward linkages in productive activities, such as units using each other’s demands for inputs and production of output, both within and
outside the area; (b) use of common facilities for social overhead capital like road, other forms of transport, communications and marketing
connections, both within the country and for exports; (c) utilizing common services of education, health, sanitation, housing, water etc.;  (d)
allowing inter-linkages of different services to develop a network with the clusters of activities.  The main idea is that if the units were to
function in isolation from each other, the costs of these units would be much higher than when they are acting together.  If nothing else, the
fixed costs are spread over a broader base.  Indeed, the issue is not only one of exploiting positive externalities; it also becomes more cost
effective to cope with negative externalities, like generation of waste and other forms of pollution. The Commission is of the view that by
addressing the problems of the industrial clusters as a group, these clusters can take advantage of on-going programmes and bring about a
convergence and synergy through the Growth Poles. This would benefit the unorganized/ informal sector as this sector would be thus in a
position to take advantage of the generation of these externalities.

51.0     The Policy of Reservation
 
51.1     The Committee was informed that Small scale sector manufacturers more than 7500 items out of which only 506 items are reserved
for exclusive manufacture in small scale sector. These  items mainly are labour oriented with very less investment and low technological
requirements. Most of these items are produced in self employment ventures and do not attract big players in the field. However, some hi-tech
export oriented items like hosiery, hand tools, drugs & pharmaceutical and stationery have been provided with a higher investment limit in
plant and machinery, i.e. upto rupees five crores to enable them to meet the requirements of technology upgradation  and face the global and
domestic competition. As such, the reservation policy does not discourage investment. On the contrary the policy is instrumental in
employment generation and meeting the concern of regional imbalances. Where ever it is felt that the reservation of any particular item needs
to be dispensed with in the interest  of growth and development of the small scale sector, the issue is discussed with the stake holders and the
respective administrative Ministries, and depending upon the general opinion, the item is de-reserved with the recommendation of the
Advisory Committee constituted under Section 29B of Industries (Development & Regulation) Act, 1951. Accordingly, the list of reserved
items have come down from 873 in 1984 to 506 as on date.  There is a proposal to dereserve 180 more items during this  year as announced in
the Budget Speech 2006-07, which will further shorten the list of  reserved items.
 
51.2     The Committee notes the stand of the Government that  the Policy of Reservation discourages investment competition & Technology
up-gradation in the small sector. The Committee also takes note that  dereservation of 180 items, as announced in the Budget (2006-07)
would  help the small scale sector to meet the demand for technological upgradation, encourage research and development and make the
sector competitive to face the domestic and global competition. The committee would like to be apprised of the overall impact of the
dereservation of reserved items.

58.0     Impact of WTO provisions on various sectors of Small Scale Industries
 
58.1     The Committee was informed that Government is constantly assessing and monitoring the impact of the removal of Quantitative

already working under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry   and the Ministry of Finance respectively.

cases have been imposed.  The Government is also in the process of amending   the provisions of Foreign Trade (Development and
Regulation) Act 1992 so that safeguard action in the form of QRs could be applied if there are sudden and massive imports. Government can
also specify standards, impose Sanitary and phyto-sanitary( SPS )measures and other conditions for reasons of public, plant & animal health,
security and public morality on goods entering India.

61.       Recognizing SMEs in the service sector
61.1     In the recent years the services sector has emerged as a significant contributor  to the economy displaying a high rate of growth and
possibility of generation of employment for  millions of workers.  Therefore, it has become necessary, as is the practice worldwide, to address
the concerns of both the small scale industries and services together and recognize them as “small enterprises”.  As stated above, at present,
small scale industries have been dealt in the Industries (Development & Regulations) Act, [I(D&R)Act], 1951 in two sections.  However, this
Act also does not define “Medium Industries”.  Thus, neither “small enterprises” nor “medium enterprises” have been defined in any statutes.
 On the other hand, the worldwide practice today is to view “Small and Medium Enterprises” (SME) as a composite sector. Recognising this
fact, the Government has  drafted the Small and Medium Enterprises Development (SMED) Bill, 2005 which has been introduced in Lok
Sabha on 12 May 2005.  SMED Bill, 2005, inter alia, contains the definition of small enterprises and medium enterprises in clause 7. 
Detailed criteria for defining manufacturing sector and the services sector in both the categories i.e. small enterprises and medium enterprises
have been provided in this clause.  Treating the small scale enterprises in the services sector at par with small scale enterprises in the
manufacturing sector will facilitate fast growth of the services sector in India thereby improving the employment scenario and the GDP of our
country.  This would also enable the Government to plan and implement schemes/programmes for promoting the growth of the services
sector.
 
61.2     The Committee welcomes the announcement of the Finance Minister in the Budget  (2006-07) and treating the small scale enterprises
in the services sector on par with the small scale enterprises in the manufacturing sector. The Committee would like to know the   impact  and
implication of recognizing SMEs in the service sector.
