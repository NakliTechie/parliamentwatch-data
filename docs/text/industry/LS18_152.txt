REPORT
          The Agro and Rural Industries Sector has been envisaged as an important avenue for creation of employment in
the rural non-farm sector for the balanced economic growth of the country. The importance of this sector lies in the fact
that it seeks to establish village industries at low cost based on the local raw materials, skills and technology for
providing employment opportunities to the local population and artisans who have been traditionally engaged in village
level economic activities. The main contribution of ARI sector to the National Economy has been in terms of production
and employment generation through setting up of village industries in the rural and semi-urban areas. Over the past five
decades, there has been a big growth in Village Industries (VI) Sector in terms of production and employment.
2.0     CHARTER OF FUNCTIONS OF MINISTRY OF AGRO AND RURAL INDUSTRIES

152nd Report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/152ndreport.htm[04-07-2016 12:48:21]
2.1     The Ministry has a multi-pronged role to play in the quality implementation of policies, programmes and
schemes. In the role of a policy maker, the Ministry collects, collates and derives inferences, which act as critical input
to further policy formulation/amendments. In the role of an advocate, the Ministry advocates and puts up ‘felt needs’ of
the stakeholders involved in the sector as a whole. In the role of a facilitator, the Ministry facilitates, coordinates and
supports its field agencies in implementing the programmes and schemes both quantitatively and qualitatively. The
Ministry acts as a nodal agency for promotion and development of agro and rural industries in small towns and rural
areas, The Charter for the Ministry is as follows:----
          (i)    Coordination of the development of village and cottage industries, and Tiny/Micro Enterprises in both urban
and rural areas.
          (ii)    Coordination of matters relating to all Rural Industries.
          (iii)   Coir Industries including the Coir Board.
          (iv)   Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY).
          (v)   Khadi and Village Industries Commission.
2.2     The Ministry guides and coordinates with the implementing agencies to ensure development and achievement as
per the set mandate.
2.3     The Committee wanted to know the Monitoring mechanism working presently to oversee the functioning of
various organizations under the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries The Ministry stated that the monthly performances
are reviewed in the case of KVIC and Coir Board whereas the Ministry makes a quarterly monitoring, As per the
Management Information System approved by the Cabinet Secretariat for Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries,
implementation of PMRY, coir exports, increase in utilization of raw material in coir sector and employment generation
under REGP and Khadi Package are reviewed. The activities of KVIC, PMRY and Coir Schemes are also reviewed by
Secretary on a monthly basis and a Brief Note is sent to the PMO and Cabinet Secretary. On the basis of monthly and
quarterly performances of progress in various sectors of development, appropriate instructions are issued to KVIC, Coir
Board and State Governments, This is being done on a regular basis.
2.4     The Committee hopes that the creation of independent Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries would reinforce and
strengthen the functioning of the ARI sector as a whole, by highlighting its present status and ensuring timely policy
interventions when needed. The contribution of this sector can be further streamlined and strengthened by injecting
more professionalism in the areas of finance, marketing, HRD, technology and manpower upgradation in the KVI
sector, revival of sick khadi institutions, renovation and modernization of marketing outlets.
2.5     The Committee desires that the Government should conduct service auditing of the organizations working under
the Ministry of ARI with the purpose of improving their functioning in the better interest of the Agro and Rural
Industries.

gainful employment opportunities over the Xth Plan. Accordingly, a target of 1.65 million person per year over the Xth
Plan or an annual target of employment of 3.3 lakh persons or 2.20 lakh units per annum has been envisaged under the
PMRY.

12.0   PROBLEMS BEING FACED BY KHADI & VILLAGE INDUSTRIES
12.1   In the market economy, quality is an important aspect. As such, to make khadi products competitive in the market
it is necessary to enhance the quality of the products as such to enhance the budget support for such schemes. Similarly,
shabby and dilapidated conditions of sales Outlets of khadi institutions is a weak area which require improvement for
attracting the customers. A scheme has been launched for this purpose by formulating a plan for modernization and
renovation of sales outlets. The proposal for renovation of other DSOs and Institutional Sales Outlets need adequate
budgetary support. Similarly, the efforts initiated by KVIC by launching the Product Design Innovation and Packaging
(PRODIP) for improving the Product Design and Packaging also taken up for vigorous follow up with adequate
financial support.
12.2   The Committee appreciates KVIC for launching Product Design Innovation and Packaging (PRODIP) and
directs KVIC to apprise the progress made in this regard.
12.3   Shortage of working capital is another major problem blocking the progress of khadi programme. Earlier, the
institutions were enjoying working capital assistance from KVIC’s budgetary support at zero rate of interest.
Subsequently, in view of budgetary crunch, the khadi institutions have been directed to avail of funds from banking
sector, against which KVIC issues Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC). However, the financial institutions are
not honouring the ISEC issued by KVIC to its full extent as such there is shortage of working capital in the Khadi
Sector. Further, large amount of pending rebate with various State Governments adds to the burden.
12.4   The Committee expresses its concern over the lack of support from the financial institutions to KVIC and
recommends that the Ministry of ARI should play pro-active role in enabling  KVIC to get sufficient credit, flow from
the banking sector and ensure that the financial institution honour the ISEC issued by KVIC. The Committee desires
that the Ministry of ARI should take up the issue of large amount of pending rebate with various State Governments so
that KVIC get some relief from the shortage of working capital.
12.5   Moreover, as on 31-3-2004, Rs. 238.02 crores was lying as outstanding balance with Khadi Implementing
Agencies under Consortium Bank Credit (CBC) for which they have to pay interest @ 4.5 per cent in addition to
refunding the same in instalments whereas under the working fund system adopted for Khadi programme the institutions
are permitted to keep the working capital in the form of working fund as long as they maintain certain level of
production.
12.5   The Committee is constrained to express its displeasure that Rs. 238.02 crore is lying as outstanding balance with
Khadi implementing agencies under Consortium Bank Credit(CBC) and recommends that the Government should take
initiative at the earliest to release the fund.
12.6   Another area which requires immediate assistance to Khadi institutions is tools and implements. The fact remains
that the charkhas and looms available with them are mostly outdated, which require replacement or renovation. A
separate fund may be made available for this purpose.
12.7   The Committee expresses its anguish and observe that tools and implements used for Khadi production have
become obsolete and outdated. The Committee therefore recommends that adequate funds should be earmarked for
modernization of the charkhas and looms for continuation and proliferation of Khadi.
12.8   Labour law is another important problems being faced by the khadi sector. A separate labour law exclusively for
khadi sector, considering its nature of operation, funding patterns etc, is very much essential.
12.9   The Committee desires that the Ministry of ARI should come out with the specific details of labour reforms in the
interest of Agro and Rural Industries and take initiatives for the legislation.

sanction.

27.0   IMPACT OF GLOBALISATION AND LIBERALIZATION ON THE GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT
OF AGRO AND RURAL INDUSTRIES
27.1   The impact of liberalization has enhanced the competitive nature of the whole industrial
set up as such. Open market mechanisms have come in place of regulated and protected nature of
pre-liberalized economy. The challenge posed by globalized economy has enhanced and enabled
nurturing of the inherent competitive and resilient nature of the Agro and Rural Industries sector as such. The sector
mainly consists of Khadi, Village Industries, cottage industries (handicrafts), Coir, Tiny and Micro enterprises. The
products of this sector by their very nature are traditional, location specific and exclusively indigenous. This adds to the
specialized and exclusive nature of the sector as such and gives it a dynamic and resilient character. For example, herbal
products, organic manure, organic food, honey production, traditional crafts items, etc. are so exclusive by nature that it
offers immense export potential which is almost non-competitive in the world economy. This defines the future outlook
of the sector. The emphasis would be to highlight the exclusivity of ARI products and its suitability to specific local area
both for production as well as marketing as it addresses the felt local needs and to develop quality diversification and
aggressively enter the world market.
27.2   The Committee recommends that the Ministry of ARI should carefully study the adverse impact of globalisation
and liberalization on the growth and development of Agro and Rural Industries and take suitable remedial measures for
protecting the domestic Agro and Rural Industries.
28.0   PROBLEMS BEING FACED BY AGRO AND RURAL INDUSTRIES
28.1   The Major problems faced by the Agro and Rural Industries are primarily relating to lack of infrastructural
facilities, marketing linkages and modern technology. The same is being addressed by way of providing emphasis on the
development of market linkages, modernization of outlets, diversification of products, exposure to foreign markets and
infrastructural back up through cluster development, The impact of liberalization and WTO regime on the market
scenario are being responded by the Sector through policy initiatives which enhances the resilient nature of the sector as
a whole.
28.2   The Committee observes that there is need for a market-oriented approach to the overall sector and to inject
competitive edge which inter alia must include Market Development Assistance, aggressive marketing, brand building,
cluster development, technological upgradation, design and packaging facilities etc. Policy initiatives should be taken in
the ARI sector for envisaging massive product diversification and development based on collaborative research effects.

30.0   REVIVAL OF THE RURAL INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT FUND (RIDF)
30.1   The Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) being operated by NABARD under
the Ministry of Rural Development is for the purpose of financing projects like irrigation, flood protection, watershed
management, construction of rural roads and bridges etc. in the rural areas, whereas the Ministry of ARI is concerned
with providing employment in the non-farm sector through setting up of agro and rural industries As such, the RIDF is
not directly concerned with this Ministry. However, because of better connectivity to be created in the rural areas by
way of roads and bridges under RIDF, the setting up agro and rural industries would definitely be helped in so far as
quicker availability of raw materials and marketing of the products are concerned. This will have spiraling effect on
further growth of agro and rural industries.
30.2   The Committee is of the view that even if the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) is not directly
concerned with the Ministry of ARI but for the better connectivity to be created in the rural areas by way of roads and
bridges under RIDF, the measure undertaken would be helpful in the setting up agro and rural industries.
31.0   “NEW DEAL” FOR RURAL INDIA
31.1   The Prime Minister in his address to the Nation has laid stress on overall growth of the national economy.
Employment generation has been identified as a tool to banish poverty, hunger and homelessness and improve the
standard of living of the mass of our people. The employment should be generated through environmentally sustainable
rural industries. The Prime Minister has also focused on expanding opportunities for gainful employment in agriculture
and in non-farm rural activities.
31.2   Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries through its various employment generation programme is already focusing
on setting up of industries in the rural areas in the non-farm sector. The Rural Employment Generation Programme
(REGP) being implemented by this Ministry through KVIC since 1995 has successfully generated employment
opportunities to 22.73 lakh persons. Similarly, under Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) since 1993, 29.05 lakhs
educated persons have been provided employment.
31.3   Since the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries is functioning in the rural sector, the interaction between farmer
and agro industries has greatly picked up. To improve interaction between farmers and agro industries, KVIC has taken
several measures to strengthen activities in the rural areas like bee- keeping, food processing industries, handmade paper
industries etc. These programmes along with other activities of the Ministry in the agro and rural sector will continue to
receive focus in the Ministry in line with the message enshrined in PM’s speech with a view to empower disadvantaged
sections of the society.
31.4   The Committee is pleased to note that the Government is extending adequate attention to the rural sector and
especially the Agro and Rural Industries. The Committee, however, desires that the Government should come out with
pragmatic planning for Agro and Rural Industries keeping in view the twin objectives of employment generation and
poverty alleviation. The Committee wishes to highlight that the Agro and Rural Industries has the potential of
employment generation and since large chunk of Indian population dwells in rural areas under the abject poverty
therefore, holistic and comprehensive planning for rural sector and its effective implementation is the need of the hour.

largely dependent on Khadi rebate, announced on 2nd October every year. Major sale of Khadi items takes place during
the period of rebate. The Committee feels that there is an urgent need for change in the marketing strategy which does
not depend on sales rebate borne by the Government. The Committee therefore, directs the Ministry of ARI and KVIC
to work upon a suitable marketing strategy and allocate more funds for R&D and raising working capital which would
benefit the KVIC in long run.(Para 11.6)

          The Committee observes that it is necessary to recruit professionals, familiar with project preparation, analysis,
consultancy etc. or avail their services from any other professional agencies. While enhancing employment target it is
imperative to proportionately increase the margin money component and corresponding funds for backward forward
linkages and place them with field officers of KVIC well in advance as per estimated requirements. The State KVI
Boards are major agencies involved in the implementation of REGP. Their weak infrastructure/organizational condition
often hinders the expeditious implementation of the scheme. KVIBs should be revitalized by the respective State
Governments.(Para 22.9)
BETTER WORKING OF REGP
          The Committee recommends that the Government should take the following steps for the better working of
REGP:—
          (i)    At present the lock in period of Margin Money is for 2 years. Government may extend it to 3 years so that
the sustainability and continuity of project as well as employment can be ensured.
          (ii)    Fiscal concessions in terms of tax exemption relating to establishment and running of the units, Octroi
exemption, Transport and Power subsidy may be provided at least for the period of 3 years.
          (iii)   In each backward block of the District, a Rural Industrial Estate may be established with all infrastructure
support, so that REGP units can grow without any hurdles.

          The Committee is of the view that since the National Programme for Rural Industrialization has the potential for
real development and growth of rural economy, the Government should undertake concrete measures to combat the
problems in achieving the objectives, targets, credit-flow, implementation and monitoring mechanism and the “Key
Concern Areas” of NPRI.                                                                                                                                 (Para 23.4)
FUND FOR MODERNISATION OF TRADITIONAL INDUSTRIES
          The Committee appreciates budgetary allocation made in 2004-05 to the tune of Rs. 100.00 crores for the
modernisation and regeneration of Traditional Industries. Since the artisans are facing various problems including non-
availability of timely credit and raw material, lack of market opportunities, the Committee hopes that the budgetary
provision so made would be able to solve the problems faced by the artisans. Since Traditional Industries are

LUMPSUM PROVISION FOR PROJECTS SCHEMES FOR THE BENEFIT OF NORTH EASTERN REGION

          The Committee is of the view that the development of the NER region requires the special attention of the

backwardness. Since the region has lagged behind in progress as compared to other regions of the country, special

          The Committee desires that Government should initiates concrete steps for effective regularization and regulation
of Agro and Rural Industries.                                                                                                            (Para 26.3)

          The Committee recommends that the Ministry of ARI should carefully study the adverse impact of globalisation

protecting the domestic Agro and Rural Industries.                                                                               (Para 27.2)
PROBLEMS BEING FACED BY AGRO AND RURAL INDUSTRIES
          The Committee observes that there is need for a market-oriented approach to the overall sector and to inject
competitive edge which inter alia must include Market Development Assistance, aggressive marketing, brand building,
cluster development, technological upgradation, design and packaging facilities etc. Policy initiatives should be taken in
the ARI sector for envisaging massive product diversification and development based on collaborative research effects.
(Para 28.2)
CREDIT FLOW TO AGRO AND RURAL INDUSTRIES
          The Committee recommends that constant attempts should be vigorously made for recovery of dues of finances
advanced for ensuring adequate circulation of credit to ARI units and at the same time making easy credit accessibility
to new entrepreneurs. The Committee further recommends that adequate steps should be taken for removing the
problems being faced by ARI entrepreneurs relating to unviable projects, cutting down financial size of the projects,
lengthy procedural formalities while accessing credit for their units.                                                                    (Para
29.2)
REVIVAL OF THE RURAL INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT FUND (RIDF)
          The Committee is of the view that even if the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) is not directly
concerned with the Ministry of ARI but for the better connectivity to be created in the rural areas by way of roads and
bridges under RIDF, the measure undertaken would be helpful in the setting up agro and rural industries.    (Para 30.2)
“NEW DEAL” FOR RURAL INDIA
          The Committee is pleased to note that the Government is extending adequate attention to the rural sector and
especially the Agro and Rural Industries. The Committee, however, desires that the Government should come out with
pragmatic planning for Agro and Rural Industries keeping in view the twin objectives of employment generation and
poverty alleviation. The Committee wishes to highlight that the Agro and Rural Industries has the potential of
employment generation and since large chunk of Indian population dwells in rural areas under the abject poverty
therefore, holistic and comprehensive planning for rural sector and its effective implementation is the need of the
hour.
