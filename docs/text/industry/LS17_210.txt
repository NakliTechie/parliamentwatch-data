1.         The MSME sector constitutes a vibrant segment of our economy contributing significantly to the manufacturing output, employment and national exports.
Based on the 3
rd All-India Census (reference year 2001-02), it is estimated that there are about 128.44 lakh MSEs spread across the country as on 31 March
2007, providing estimated employment to over 312 lakh persons. Further, the MSE sector contributes 39% of the manufacturing output and 33% of the nation’s
exports. However, with the ten fold increase in the permissible investment limit, it is estimated that the redefined MSME sector may now account for as high as
45 per cent of the manufacturing output of India and nearly 40 per cent of the national exports. The most significant contribution of the micro and small
enterprises sector lies in creation of larger employment opportunities at lower capital cost.
 
2.         In keeping with the announcement made in the last Budget Speech, the Ministry of MSME has formulated first phase of a Central Sector Scheme on
‘Rejuvenation, Modernisation and Technology Upgradation of the Coir Industry’ at a total cost of Rs.243 crore, involving a budgetary support of Rs.99 crore
during the XI Plan. The Scheme envisages providing financial assistance for replacing the age old outdated ratts and looms and also to provide work shed in the
spinning and Tiny Household sectors, which are the most vulnerable sectors in the coir industry. The Scheme anticipates covering 4000 spinning units consisting
of 4 ratts each with 8 beneficiaries and 3200 Tiny/Household units consisting of 3 looms each with 6 beneficiaries. It is anticipated to generate 36,800 new
employment opportunities during XI Plan.
 
3.         Although, the primary responsibility for promotion and development of MSMEs lies with the State Governments, the Government of India, in recognition
of potential of these enterprises both in terms of creation of wealth and employment as well as the need for country-wide framework of policies and measures for
their promotion and development, has always taken active interest in supplementing the efforts of State Governments in a variety of ways.  The role of the
Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises is mainly to assist the States in their efforts to promote growth and development of MSMEs, for enhancing
their competitiveness in an increasingly market-led economy and for generating additional employment opportunities.   Besides, the Ministry also attempts to
address common concerns of these enterprises and undertakes policy advocacy on behalf of the sector on issues critically affecting their sustenance and growth. 
 
4.         In order to achieve the stated objective the Ministry and its organisations have been implementing various schemes / programmes that seek to facilitate /
provide adequate flow of credit from financial institutions/banks; support for technology upgradation and modernisation; integrated infrastructural
facilities;modern testing facilities and quality certification; access to modern management practices, entrepreneurship development and  skill upgradation through
appropriate training facilities; support for product development, design intervention and packaging; welfare of artisans and workers; assistance for better access
to domestic and export markets; and cluster-wide measures to promote capacity-building and empowerment of the units and their collectives, in addition to all or
some of the above-mentioned supports.
 
5.         In addition, the Ministry has been implementing two nation-wide employment generation programmes, namely, Rural Employment Generation
Programme (REGP) and Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY). Both these programmes are credit-linked capital subsidy schemes, which are implemented
through commercial banks. While the REGP is implemented by the Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), the PMRY is implemented by the State
Governments through the District Industries Centres (DICs).
 
6.         The formulation and implementation of policies and programmes/projects/schemes is undertaken by the Ministry with the assistance of its attached office,
public sector enterprise, statutory bodies and autonomous organisations, namely, Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Organisation (MSMEDO),
the National Small Industries Corporation Ltd. (NSIC), Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), Coir Board and three national level entrepreneurship
development institutes, viz. National Institute for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (NIMSME), Hyderabad; National Institute for Entrepreneurship and
Small Business Development (NIESBUD), NOIDA   and Indian Institute of Entrepreneurship (IIE), Guwahati. Government has set up the National Commission
for Enterprises in the Unorganised Sector (NCEUS) to suggest policies and programmes for addressing the wide range of issues affecting the productive potential
of the large segment of unorganised micro and small productive units.
 
7.         Pursuant to enactment of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 and framing of Rules the reunder, the Government has also
established a National Board for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (NBMSME) which is chaired by Minister(MSME), and interalia includes,
Member(VSE), Planning Commission as Deputy Chairperson and six ministers of state Governments, three MPs, Administrator of a Union Territory, five
Secretaries to Government of India, Chairman of the Board of Directors, NABARD, SIDBI, and Indian Banks Association, one officer of the RBI and two

17.       The Ministry of MS & ME  informed the  Committee On the Credit Support Programme which consists of the following three Schemes:
(a) Credit Guarantee Scheme: To ensure better flow of credit to micro and small enterprises by minimizing the risk perception of financial institutions and
banks in lending without collateral security, the Scheme was launched in August 2000. Under the scheme, guarantee cover is provided to collateral free credit
facility extended by member lending institutions (MLIs) to the new as well as existing small enterprises on loans up to Rs.50 lakh. The guarantee cover available
is up to 75% of the loans extended. The extent of guarantee cover is 80 per cent for (i) micro enterprises for loans up to Rs.5 lakh; (ii) MSEs operated and/or
owned by women; and (iii) all loans in the North-East Region. The lending institutions availing guarantee from the Trust have to pay one time guarantee fee of
1.5% and service charges of 0.75% per annum of the credit facility sanctioned. For all loans in the North-East Region, the one-time guarantee fee is only 0.75%.
For loans up to Rs.5 lakh, the one time guarantee fee is 1% and service charges is 0.5% per annum of the credit facility sanctioned. The scheme is operated by
the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), set up by the Government of India and Small Industries Development Bank of
India (SIDBI).
(b) Scheme of Micro Finance Programme
: The scheme was launched in March 2004 and has been tied up with the existing programme of Small Industries
Development Bank of India (SIDBI), which is under operation since January 1999, by way of contributing towards security deposits required from the Micro
Finance Institutions (MFIs)/ Non-Government Organisations (NGOs) to get loan from SIDBI. The Government of India provides funds for Micro Finance
Programme to SIDBI under the ‘Portfolio Risk Fund’ (PRF), which is used for security deposit requirement of the loan amount from the MFIs/NGOs. At present,
SIDBI takes fixed deposit equal to 10% of the loan amount. Of this, the share of MFIs/NGOs is 2.5% of the loan amount (i.e. 25% of security deposit) and
balance 7.5% (i.e. 75% of security deposit) is adjusted from the funds provided by the Government of India. The scheme is implemented by the Small Industries
Development Bank of India (SIDBI).
(c) Scheme on Trade Related Entrepreneurship Assistance and Development (TREAD) for Women
: The scheme has been designed to empower women
economically through the development of their entrepreneurial skills by eliminating constraints faced by them. It includes assistance in the form of loans, grants,
trade related training, trade information, counseling and extension activities relating to product and market development. Under the scheme, women
entrepreneurs from any part of the country may take up any non-farm activities in a group through reputed Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs).
 
18.       The Committee notes that the TREAD envisages the development of Micro/Tiny   Women Enterprises both  in rural   and urban areas. To make
this scheme  operational in real sense it feels that the number of  participating Banks  should be increased. It also feels that the associations who have
availed grants from M/o MS& ME should be made eligible for proper implementation after their evaluation. Even in Credit Guarantee Fund  Scheme
specific time limit by Banks for appraisal of project once the application is accepted by the banks , as the Scheme allows additional comfort to bank
through the CGTSI. The Guarantee cover under the Scheme should be enhanced  to Rs. 50 lakhs and priority should be given to women entrepreneurs
who are not able to provide security.
 
19.       The Committee also feels that the associations in MSME sector should be benchmarked by the Government of India and only the benchmarked

22.       It  was informed to the Committee that under the Credit Guarantee Scheme, guarantee cover is extended to any collateral free credit facility (both term
and working capital loan) of up to Rs.50 lakh provided by the Member Lending Institutions (MLIs) to the new as well as existing micro and small enterprises. At
the end of February 2008,  67 eligible institutions comprising 28 Public Sector Banks, 13 Private Sector Banks, 23 Regional Rural Banks, National Small
Industries Corporation (NSIC), North Eastern Development Finance Corporation (NEDFC) and SIDBI have become MLIs of Credit Guarantee Trust Fund for
Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE). All the eligible proposals received from the aforesaid MLIs are provided guarantee cover under the Scheme by the
CGTMSE.
 
23.       The Committee appreciates the effort of the Government to bring more Banks/Financial Institutions under MLIs but it desires that more

MLIs. The Committee would like to be informed about the measures and steps taken in this regard.

31.       The Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS) aims at facilitating technology upgradation by providing 15 per cent upfront capital subsidy w.e.f.
29 September, 2005 to Micro and Small Enterprises on institutional finance availed by them for induction of well established and improved technologies in 48
specified sub-sectors / products approved under the scheme. The upper limit of the institutional finance eligible for subsidy under CLCSS is Rs.1 crore w.e.f.
29.09.2005. The scheme is being implemented through 11 nodal agencies/ banks including the SIDBI and NABARD. Khadi & Village Industries and Coir
Industries are also covered under CLCSS. The scheme was valid upto 31.03.2007 i.e. end of X Plan. After obtaining approval of the CCEA, order for
continuation of CLCSS during XI Plan has been issued on 29.02.2008. Sanctions for Rs.76.30 crore out of BE of Rs.85.00 crore have been issued upto February,
2008. After completing the required formalities, the funds are being released shortly to nodal agencies appointed for implementing the scheme. It is expected that
the objectives and targets fixed for 2007-08 will be achieved.    
 
32.       The Committee notes that the CLCSS   launched for the technology upgradation of Small industries sector in selected products. This scheme is
implemented through SIDBI and NABARD. With the increasing competition due to liberalization of the Economy, the survival and growth of the SSI
units are critically dependent on their modernization and Technological upgrdation.  It feels that the  ceiling of Loan amount should be enhanced to Rs.
1 Crore and the  rate of Subsidy should be  15%.

assisted amounting to Rs. 13.38 Cr. In the current year, 2007-08 (up to Feb, 08) 1450 cases amounting to Rs.5.86 Cr have been assisted.
 
34.       The Committee takes a serious note that against a  total budget allocation of 1000 lakhs only 138 lakhs was sanctioned upto August, 2007.  As the

35.       The Committee was pleased to note that ‘Technology Mission’ has been established, as a component of a Promotional Package for Micro, Small and

was told to the Committee that the estimated cost of the programme during XI-Plan period is Rs. 300 lakhs. The programme is being implemented by
establishing a ‘Technology Mission Unit (TMU)’ at MSME-Development Institute, New Delhi. The Technology Mission is monitored by a Governing Body
under the Chairmanship of Secretary (MSME). During 2008-09, the budgetary allocation made for Technology Mission is Rs.50 lakhs.

Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Clusters Development Programmes and Growth Poles
 
85.       Within the overall budget outlay of Rs.6300 lakh for MSME Cluster Development Programme and MSME growth pole scheme for 2007-08, the Budget
Estimate (BE) for MSE-CDP component was Rs. 4300.00 lakh (including Rs. 430.00 lakh for NE Region), which was subsequently revised to  Rs. 900.00 lakh
(including Rs. 100.00 lakh for NE Region) . Against the revised target, a total   sanction of Rs. 620.40 lakh have been issued so far and another two proposals for
release of  Rs. 179.60 lakh is under submission.  The corresponding physical  targets include interventions in 82 clusters and  conduct of diagnostic studies in 89
clusters and the same has also been achieved. 
 
86.       There was no major shift in plan/policy for 2007-08.  Although the physical targets for the year 2007-08 have been achieved, the funds utilization under
MSE-CDP has been low.  This is mainly due to the fact that the major portion of funds under this scheme are utilised for ‘hard interventions’ i.e. creation of
fixed assets in the form of setting up of Common Facility Centres (CFCs) etc.  In case of hard interventions, besides GoI assistance, contribution of Special
Purpose Vehicle (SPV) and State Government is also required. GoI assistance for hard interventions are released only after necessary infrastructure has been
created, state government/SPV has confirmed their contribution in the project and purchase orders have been placed.   Hence, there is always a time lag between
approval and disbursement of funds. Since in most of the CFCs, the necessary infrastructure is at various stages of completion, there was lesser demand for the
funds than originally envisaged at the beginning.  While 21 Common Facility Centres (CFCs) are in the pipeline for disbursement of funds with approval of GoI
assistance of Rs.4592.33 lakh, an amount of Rs.3302.98 lakh is yet to be released for these CFCs, because necessary information/action at the level of state
government/SPV is awaited.
 
87.       Under MSE Cluster Development Programmes and MSME Growth Pole Scheme (with an outlay of Rs.4580 lakh for 2008-09), the BE for MSE-CDP
component is Rs. 3700.00 lakh (including for NE Region). The corresponding physical target consist of interventions in  55 new clusters and diagnostic studies
for about 100 clusters . Presently there is no proposal for enhancing ceiling for project cost.
 
88.       The projects under MSE-CDP are being implemented by Ministry of MSME through its field offices/autonomous organizations, state government and
their autonomous bodies, National and International Institutions etc. or any institution/agency approved by the Ministry.   Two types of interventions are under
taken in the cluster under MSE-CDP, soft interventions and hard interventions.   Soft interventions are basically capacity building activities in the cluster where
no fixed assets are acquired or formed.  Soft interventions are implemented by a Cluster Development Executive (CDE) normally for a period of three years. 
Hard interventions involve creation of tangible assets like setting up of Common Facility Centres (CFCs), Mini Tool Rooms, Design Centres, Testing/Training
Centres, R&D Centres etc.  For hard interventions, it is necessary to form a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), which could be a registered society, or a cooperative
society, or company, or a trust or any other legal entity.  Contribution of the Ministry of MSME will not exceed 80% of the total project cost (90% in case of
clusters owned and/or  managed by women entrepreneurs) subject to a ceiling of Rs. 10.00 crore per project including Rs. 10.00 lakh for soft activities.   The
proposals for seeking financial assistance from Ministry of MSME are considered and approved by the Steering Committee of MSE-CDP under the
Chairmanship of Secretary (MSME) for project costing Rs.1.00 crore and above and of the Development Commissioner (MSME) for project costing less than
Rs. 1.00 crore. 
 
89.       In case of project implemented by the State Governments, their autonomous bodies and SPVs with the State Government as one of the stakeholders,
monitoring of the projects to ensure satisfactory and time bound implementation of the activities is the responsibility of the State Government concerned.   For
other projects, Ministry of MSME is directly monitoring the progress through is State level offices, i.e., MSME-DIs.
 
90.       The Committee recommends that the Ministry should take proactive action to ensure that State Governments and other stake holders take
action in time bound     manner.

126.     Earlier, financial assistance to the Khadi institutions were provided by the KVIC from its budgetary support provided by the Government. of India. 
However, with the expansion of khadi activities, it was felt necessary to avail bank loan and the Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme was
launched since 1977.  Under this scheme, funds requirement of khadi institutions is worked out by the KVIC and Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC)
are issued in favour of them to mobilize loans from banks. The institutions are to pay only 4% of the interest and the difference between the 4% and the actual
rate of interest charged by the banks is paid as interest subsidy by the KVIC under its budgetary source. The difference in the ISEC issued by the KVIC and its
availment is mainly due to the difference in the assessment of Working Capital requirement by the khadi institutions.  The issue has been studied by a Committee
appointed by Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in 1994 and it was suggested that if the difference in the assessment made by KVIC and Banks is less than 10%, banks
are to accept the fund assessment made by KVIC and finance the institutions accordingly. KVIC has taken up the issues related to ISEC in all seriousness and
entrusted a study on the scheme, to an expert outside agency.  It is expected that the study report will be available shortly and the future course of action will be
taken on the basis of study report.

Workshed Scheme for Khadi Artisans
 
129.     It has been noticed that generally  the artisans, especially the spinners normally fall under the category of low-income group or below poverty line (BPL)
group.  The ‘Workshed Scheme for Khadi Artisans’ has been formulated for the artisans working in khadi sector on the lines of a similar scheme being
implemented by the Ministry of Textiles (Office of the Development Commissioner for Handlooms) for the handloom weavers. .  It aims at  providing a
workshed/place having about 20 square meters space per unit  to these artisans  which would considerably improve their work environment which in turn will not
only enhance their productivity and earnings but also prove  a major step in improving the quality of life.
 
130.     Around 38,000 khadi artisans are estimated to be provided facility of workshed during XI Plan. During 2008-09, it is proposed to provide facility of
worksheds to around 10000 kahdi artisans . The beneficiaries will be selected from all the Sates/UTs of the country, keeping in view the number of institutions
functioning in a State/UT
           
131.     The main objectives of this scheme to provide financial assistance for constructing workshed for khadi artisans to provide better ambiance by providing a
better workplace to enable them to carry out their spinning and weaving work efficiently, provide more space for housing their implements and storing
accessories, yarn, cloth, etc. ,provide a better, airy working place, thereby providing better hygienic conditions at the workplace, improve both quality and
productivity through improved facilities including electrical connection and lighting to the workshed and,provide adequate room for housing improved looms
and the design equipment like dobby, jacquard, etc.
           
132.     Since the scheme has not so far been finalized, the amount of Rs. 5 crore earmarked in BE 2007-08 has been reduced to Rs. 2.00 crore (including NER) in
RE 2007-08. As the scheme is expected to be fully operationalised during 2008-09, Rs. 25 crore (Non NER Rs. 24.95 crore and NER Rs. 0.05 crore) has been
earmarked for 2008-09   
 
133.     In order to be eligible for being covered under the scheme, the beneficiary must earn 50% of his/her income from spinning or weaving of khadi and
registered with any khadi institution financed by KVIC / KVIBs. Further, he/she should have completed at least five years from the date of enrolment with the

Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP
)
 
146.     Both PMRY and REGP are credit linked subsidy programmes with no financial contribution from State Governments to support entrepreneurs to set up
micro enterprises.  PMRY with its comparatively inferior design/subsidy parameters and lower recovery rate is proposed to be merged with REGP.  With slightly
higher level of subsidy to the projects at lower range of cost (upto Rs.5 lakh), focused identification in targeting of beneficiaries, improved monitoring,
implementation and verification processes, the merged scheme is expected to lead to increased participation of beneficiaries from rural areas in particular, setting
up Micro Enterprises leading to creation of entrepreneurs as well as indirect wage employment.   The proposal for merger and forming Prime Minister’s
Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) has been recommended by Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC). PMRY and REGP are to be merged in 2008-
09.  PMEGP which  seeks to remove the anomalies between PMRY and REGP, targets marginalized sections of beneficiaries by earmarking the quantum of
Government subsidy, allocates higher proportion of  units to the marginalized sections for promoting inclusive growth, introduces electronic tracking of
applications, expand the EDP training (duration and content), associates Gram Panchayats and reputed NGOs for improved targeting and selection of
beneficiaries, updates project profiles in consultation with Banks, seeks aggressive publicity of Credit Guarantee Trust Fund Scheme and 100% verification of
projects that would be set up.    Rs. 823 crore have been provided in BEs 2008-09, out of which 740 crore would be towards subsidy and Rs. 83 crore would be
utilized for various backward forward linkages and meeting the residual claims of PMRY and REGP.

151.     A scheme titled the “Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI)” has been launched by the Ministry in October, 2005 with a

152.     Implementing Agencies (IAs) under SFURTI are Non-Government Organizations (NGOs), institutions of the Central and State Governments and semi-

Coir Sector/Coir Board
 
164.     India is the largest coir producer in the world accounting for more than 80 per cent of the total world production of coir fibre. The coir sector in India is
very diverse and involves households, co-operatives, NGOs, manufacturers and exporters.        
 
165.     The coir industry employs more than 6.4 lakh persons of whom a majority are from rural areas belonging to the economically weaker sections of society. 
Nearly 80% of the coir workers in the fibre extraction and spinning sectors are women.           
 
166.     The development of coir industry has all along been in areas where there is a concentration of coconut trees and availability of coconut husk. 
Historically, the coir industry started and flourished in Kerala which has a long coast line, lakes, lagoons  and backwaters providing natural conditions required
for retting.  However, with the expansion of coconut cultivation, coir industry has picked up in the States of Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa,
West Bengal, Assam, Tripura, Pondicherry and the Union Territories of Lakshadweep and Andaman & Nicobar Islands through the efforts of Coir Board.
 
167.     It was informed to the Committee that the Coir Board is a statutory body established under the Coir Industry Act, 1953 for promoting the overall
development of the coir industry and uplifting of the living conditions of the workers engaged in this traditional industry.  The Coir Board consists of a full-time
Chairman and 39 Members representing all stakeholders in the coir industry.          
 
168.     The functions of the Coir Board for the development of coir industry include undertaking scientific, technological and economic research and
development activities; collection of statistics relating to exports and internal consumption of coir and coir products; development of new products and designs;
publicity for promotion of exports and internal sales; marketing of coir and coir products in India and abroad; preventing unfair competition among producers
and exporters; assisting in the establishment of units for the manufacture of products; promoting co-operative organizations among producers of husk, coir fibre,
coir yarn and manufacturers of coir products; ensuring remunerative returns to producers and manufacturers, etc.
           
169.     In order to continue existing and implement new schemes an outlay of Rs.55 crore has been kept for 2008-09 as per the details given below:-
 
            Plan General-Coir Board is looking after the promotion, growth and development of the coir industry.  The Coir Board is implementing a number of
schemes for the coir sector which include training extension, quality improvement, Mahila Coir Yojana (MCY) and welfare measures.  MCY envisages
distribution of motorized ratts to the trained women artisans.  Under the scheme of development of production infrastructure, financial assistance is provided for
setting up new coir units and for modernization of existing units.  Coir Board also undertakes number of activities for domestic and export market promotion.  
An outlay of Rs.23 crore has been kept for the year for these activities.   It is expected that with these initiatives, production of coir fibre will be increased from
4,70,000 MT (target for the year 2007-08) to 5,00,000 MT during the year 2008-09.  Employment opportunities will be provided to 6.90 lakh persons
(cumulative) at the end of the year in comparison to 6.60 lakh target fixed for the previous year.  The export of coir and coir products will be enhanced from
Rs.680 crore to Rs.750 crore at a growth rate of 10.2% over the previous year.
            Plan S&T -     The fund under this head are utilized for the Researach & Development activities of the Coir Board which are carried out through its
Research Institutes. In the Science and Technology projects undertaken by Coir Board, emphasis is given on process improvement in the extraction of fibre,
pollution free retting process, reduction in the period of retting, modernization of production infrastructure, product development, product diversification etc. 
These projects will demonstrate possibility of reducing drudgery in work, improving the quality of Coir products and introducing new products/processes.  Rs.7
crore has been kept under this head for the year 2008-09.
