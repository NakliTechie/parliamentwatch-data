Overview of the Tyre Industry in the country
1.         Tyre Industry was delicensed  in the year 1989.  There are 43 tyre companies in India.  MRF, Apollo, JK and Ceat  account for
88% of sales.  Bus and Truck tyres account for 65% of production.  The tyre industry has been growing at the rate of 11% per annum. 
The total export of tyres during 2006-07 was 54.49 lakh  an increase by 1.15% over previous year.
 
2.         The Committee was informed about the SWOT analysis of the domestic tyre industry as under:-
 
Strength:
 
1. Availability of qualified manpower and technical professionals
2. Domestic demand can be fully met from the domestic supplies
3. Export of tyres from India has increased acceptance in the world market
4. Competitive domestic market with multiple brands
5. Indian tyres conform to product and safety standards
 
Weaknesses
 
6. Lower spending on basic R&D in comparison to global tyre majors
7. Lower productivity of labour in comparison to world standards.
8. Infrastructural and related bottlenecks.
9. Higher cost of raw-materials.
 
Opportunities
 
10. Robust growth in economy and automobile industry.
11. More export opportunities.
12. Increased investment on highway projects and other roadways.
 
Threats

Background of Acquisition
 
18.       The Committee sought to know the objective and the rationale of the nationalization as the Company could never become
commercially viable even after its nationalisation.
 
19.       The Committee was informed of the background of the acquisition by the DHI. Prior to acquisition by the Government of
India, M/s. National Rubber Manufacturer Limited (NRM) at Tangra was engaged in the field of industrial product like Conveyer
Belt, Transmission Belt, Hoses, V-Belts etc. and M/s. Inchek Tyre Limited (ITL) at Kankinara was engaged in the field of
manufacturing automotive tyres and tubes. These two private sector companies were chronically sick and their management was taken
over under Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951 in the years 1977 and 1978 respectively. The total number of
employees was 4400 and they were not getting their salaries regularly. 
 
20.       At the time of nationalization, the two units were in poor financial condition.   The poor condition of machinery, non-
availability of drawings and molds, uneconomic production, labour problems and constraints in the availability of working capital
were main causes for the sickness of these units. 
 
21.       The Committee was further informed that the purpose of the nationalization was to secure proper management of the two
undertakings and to sub-serve the interest of the general public by ensuring continued manufacture, production and distribution of
tyres, tubes and other rubbers goods which are essential to the needs of the economy of the country. 
 
22.       An amount of Rs.4.91 Crore was paid by Government of India to the Commissioner of Payment (COP) as compensation to
settle the liabilities of the two companies at the time of nationalization in 1984.

24.       The modernisation of the Kankinara unit of TCIL was undertaken in 1987 at a cost of Rs. 125.80 crore.  The project was

25.       The Committee took note of the fact that the modernization of Kankinara Unit took place 1987 but the unit commenced
commercial production only in 1994.  In the meantime in 1992, the Company had already been referred to BIFR.  The Committee
sought to know the reason for such delays in commencing the commercial production and factors that led to Company being referred
to BIFR even after costly modernization.
 
26.       In its reply the DHI informed that the modernization scheme was recommended by PIB in 1987 at a cost of Rs. 66.71 crores
with foreign exchange component of Rs. 13.22 crores.  The envisaged implementation schedule was 42 months from the date of
Government approval.  The Foreign Investment Board cleared the proposal in 1988.  Due to exchange rate adjustment of July, 1991
and other changes in the Finance Act, 1992-1993 the cost of the project was revised to Rs. 102.98 crores (FE Rs. 27.54 crores) in June,
1992.  There was also delay in receipt of equipment from the suppliers of hard currency areas and delay in receipt of foreign currency
loan.  The technical commissioning of the project took place in 1993 after delay of six months from the scheduled commissioning
date. 
27.       Consequent upon, the amendment of Sick Industrial Companies Act (SICA) to include public sector enterprises within its
purview, TCIL had to be referred to BIFR in 1992 on account of its accumulated losses and erosion of net worth.  The banks insisted
on revival package from BIFR before releasing additional finance for working capital.   Commercial production could therefore, start
only in April, 1994 after an adhoc release of bank finance for holding on operation.
 
28.       The Committee took note of the DHI’s assertion that in view of the growth in auto sector in 70’s and in absence of any
Government Company in tyre sector,  the “Nationalisation took place with an aim that we should have a viable tyre Company in our
country.’  However the Committee wondered if this objective was at all achieved given the fact that the Company was referred to
BIFR in 1992 even before it could commence commercial production, its technological upgradation was never considered as viable,
and that the Company could never actually earn any profit since its nationalization.
 
29.       In its reply the DHI accepted that the Company did not make profit throughout its existence since nationalization.   However,
the purpose of nationalization has been served in so far as the socio-economic objectives are concerned.   The Company has been
continuously manufacturing tyres and providing employment to workers thereby making its own contribution to the economy.

Cost of Delay
 
31.       The Committee sought to know the comparative net worth of the Company in 1992 and its current net worth and if the
Government gave any Budgetary support to revive the Company during the intervening period. 
 
32.       The Committee was informed that the net worth of the company in 1992 was (-) Rs. 36.21 crores whereas the current net worth
of the Company has come down to (-) Rs. 638.55 crores.  The cost of delay of last fifteen years has been about Rs 602 crores. Both
Planned and Non-Planned loans were extended to the Company since 1984 for the purposes of modernization,
repairs/replacements/renewal and for meeting cash loses, payments of salaries, wages and VRS dues to the employees of the Company
including the Tangra Unit.  The Committee was further informed that since 1992, Government had provided plan and non- plan
support to the extent of Rs. 188.54 crores. Government of India also waived non-plan loan, accrued interest and guarantee fees
amounting to Rs. 100.29 crores.  The outstanding loan of Rs. 65.74 crores was rescheduled from 1999 and the interest rate was
reduced to 8%.

Reconstruction recommended by the BRPSE
 
35.       The Committee noted that as per the financial reconstruction proposed by the BRPSE, existing loan of Rs. 202 crores is to be
converted into equity.  However, the increased equity component of Rs. 295.60 crores is to be brought down to 10% (i.e. Rs. 29.65

into unsecured equity – which the Government has to eventually disinvest. The DHI informed the Committee that accumulated loss of
the Company had eroded the capital structure of the company.  The total capital structure of the Company by converting loan into
equity and then writing down the increased equity to 10%, has been proposed to reflect the current capitalization of the business. 
 
36.       The Committee enquired about the rationale regarding financial restructuring of Company including huge waivers of dues and
conversion into equity, only to enter into a JV which may even change the ownership of the Company.   The Committee wondered if
Government were to agree to such advise, such decision could have been taken at earlier instances of failed JV, thereby saving on the
accumulated liabilities of the intervening years.  Further, if Company was already doing jobbing work for the big tyre manufacturers
would the Company’s clients at all enter into JV.
 
37.       The DHI submitted that the recommendations of BRPSE did not envisage any fresh cash infusion.  It involves total sacrifice of
Rs. 616.28 crores.  Unless the balance sheet of the Company was cleaned up no private partner will come forward to enter into JV
with the Company.  The DHI further informed that the Company was free from all the debts and major liabilities except the liabilities
towards the Government.   The operations of the company had improved by reduction of manpower through VRS, curtailment of
overhead expenses and closure of unviable Tangra Unit. 
 
38.       Further, there was continued demand for Bias cut Bus / Truck tyres in the market since progress in radialisation in Bus / Truck
tyres group are slow due to poor road condition, overloading factors and lastly OEM segment has not pushed radialisation  for cost
factor. Radial tyres cost close to 20% more than Bias tyre.
     
39.       As the tyre industry is gradually switching over from bias cut technology to radial technology,  the present jobbers of TCIL
mainly Apollo, Ceat, JKTIL & BTL may find it profitable to form a strategic alliance with TCIL or takeover of the unit rather than
invest in a new Green Field project for bias cut tyres.  In so far as, the facilities of TCIL are concerned, there is scope for increasing
the plant capacity with little investment for DE BOTTLENECKING of the Plant. This factor goes in favour of TCIL for attracting its
jobbers as joint venture partners.
 
40.       However, the Committee also took cognizance of the views of the Director General, Automotive Tyre Manufacturers’
Association ( ATMA), who expressed his apprehensions over the possibility of a JV. He stated :
 
“ there is  complete delicensing that the Government has done. Anybody can set up a tyre plant anywhere. So generally, the tyre
companies will look for their own tyre plants for expanding capacities. The attraction for JV which was there earlier has become much
less from the tyre company’s point of view.”
He further agreed that the jobbing work done by the TCIL was of good quality but the JV will eventually depend upon the valuation
and percentage of participation.
 
Modernisation
 and Radialisation of the TCIL
 
41.       The Committee noted radialisation was never considered as a part of modernization. The modernization scheme implemented
at Kankinara was confined only to increasing the capacity from 1.23 lakh tyres and tubes per annum to 5 lakh pieces per annum. The
company discontinued production of own brand tyres in the year 2002 due to non-availability of working capital from the banks on
account of its reference to BIFR.  As a result the Company lost its Brand identity in the market. 
 
42.       The DHI informed that Radialization involved huge capital investments and also location of a technology partner who will be
willing to share the technology.  Even if radialization were to be undertaken the Company would have to invest in a marketing set up
which again called for capital investments.  With four of the major tyre manufacturers occupying 88% of the market, it would have
been difficult for TCIL to create a new brand image to sell its projects.  In view of this, the present restructuring and revival package
was considered to be viable.

43.       The Committee took cognizance of the Valuation methodology proposed by the Department of Disinvestment in a strategic

General Observation and Recommendation of the Committee
 
1.   The Committee has taken a serious view of the chronology of revival efforts and found that right from the beginning the
Company had suffered due to crippling delays at the crucial stages.  After the nationalization in 1984, it took three years till 1987
to recommend modernization plan which lacked long term perspective in respect of technology and market requirements.
Considering that the Company was already suffering from sickness as it was taken over and subsequent foreign exchange crisis in
early 90’s had sever impact over the sustainability of the Company.   The Committee feels that the cost of delay was never taken
into account while deciding upon its revival. The long gaps in above chronology indicate the lack of sensitivity on the part of
Government and management towards ever escalating price of revival and accumulating losses and liabilities that eventually
sealed the fate of the Company.
 
2.   The Committee feels that as envisaged the TCIL was never in a position to breach the oligopoly in tyre industry, which was
one of the stated objectives of Public Sector. The Company lacked resources and technology to harness the growth in the auto
sector domestically and internationally. Despite repeated though  delayed revival no serious effort was made by the Government to
make the Company viable with a long term strategy for technology upgradation or reviving the marketing network. Instead the
Government had initiated process of locating JV partner in 1997. A process that is likely to fructify now after a decade long delay
with the passage of this Bill. In the meantime the Company has accumulated huge liabilities in its balance sheets which need to be
cleared at a huge cost on the Government and creditor Banks. The Committee feels that the Bill as introduced in the Parliament
does not elaborate the obligations for the prospective JV partner. The Committee is of the view that while the conditions on which
the JV proposals were rejected in 1997, have been substantially relaxed in the current Bill, the cost of delay by a decade has only
mounted for the Government and creditors. The Committee takes a serious view of the delay in decision making in the
Government.  
 
3.   The Committee also takes a serious view of the practice of seeking Parliamentary approval as suggested by Committee of
Secretaries, without providing requisite   information regarding valuation of the Company, terms of proposed JV and the prospects
of the Company. Such information is essential for a considered parliamentary scrutiny of the legislation. The Committee therefore
recommends that such information should be furnished by the Government to the Parliament as and when it takes up the Bill for
its consideration and passage. The Committee sought the rationale of bringing the Bill without providing any substantive
information. It questioned if the objectives of the Bill could have been better served with an Amendment to the existing Inchek
Tyres Ltd and National Rubber Manufacturers Limited (Nationalisation ) Act, 1984. The Committee also wondered if the Bill
could coexist without making such amendments to the existing Act.  The Committee accordingly recommends that necessary
Clauses should be incorporated in the Bill to avoid any conflict with existing Bill/Ordinance/Act.
 
4.   The Committee also recommends that the Bill should incorporate broader provisions regarding the valuation of lands, assets
and liabilities that are aligned with the valuation methodology prescribed by Department of Disinvestment instead of confining the
valuation on the basis of book value.
 
5.   The Committee further recommends that the relevant provisions must be incorporated in the Bill to prevent diversion of usage
of land as per the norms of valuation prescribed by the Department of Disinvestment.
 
6.   The Committee is in consonance with the recommendation of the Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Finance which had found the asset valuation guidelines inadequate and vague on the issue of valuation of land and had
accordingly recommended “the land should be valued separately.”

7.   The Committee discussed the issue of employees at length. It took into cognizance the views of the State Government of West
Bengal that the protection of the existing service conditions of the employees   should be extended to three years. However, the

the existing service condition.
