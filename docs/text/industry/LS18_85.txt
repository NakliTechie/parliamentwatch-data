REPORT
 
1.Hindustan Steel Works Construction Limited (HSCL) is an offshoot of the Construction Division of Steel Authority 
of India Ltd. ( SAIL). The company was incorporated in 1964 with the prime objective of creating in Public Sector an
organisation capable of-taking-up   the construction of  modern  Steel Plants in the country. HSCL has executed works
in Steel Plants right from inception till commissioning, viz, Bokaro Steel plant, Vizag Steel Plant, Salem Steel Plant and
was  associated  with the expansion and modernisation activities of Bhilai Steel Plant, Durgapur Steel Plant, IISCO
(Burnpur)  as  also Bhadravati Steel Plant.  With the decline  in traditional work-order , the company  diversified its
activities  in  other sectors like  power , coal, oil and  gas and also  infrastructural facilities like  roads/highways,
bridges, dams, underground-communication and transport system besides  industrial and  township  complexes
involving  high degree of planning, coordination and modern sophisticated techniques. The Company gained experience
in the areas like civil works of plant building, structural fabrication and erection in industrial plants, and erection of
Equipment in the Steel Plants. As SAIL expanded its activities, activities of HSCL also expanded.  Apart from activities
in SAIL, the company expanded its activities in other areas, like civil works for buildings, other than SAIL, Dam
Construction'. Bridge construction, coal handling plants, road construction, stadium complexes, school buildings and
industrial townships etc. The company also gained experience in planning & coordination in steel & non-steel sectors.
1.2       The authorized capital of the company has been raised from Rs.20.00 crore to Rs.150.00 crore on approval of 
Government of India through the financial restructuring-cum-financial assistance package sanctioned to the company. 
Similarly, the paid  up  capital  has also been increased from Rs.20.00 crore to rs.117.10 crore.
1.3       The Ministry of Steel  informed the Sub -Committee that  HSCL suffered   in 1970s primarily   due to two
reasons . The first factor was the sudden  increase in work force between 1970-79 from 4,100 to 26,537.  Of this,
Bokaro unit alone   accounted for 12,600.  This  was due to the  crisis created when a large number of public sector and
private  companies engaged by Bokaro Steel   failed and left their  work sites leaving behind  their workforce.  Due to
pressure, both from the Government and from the steel plants.  HSCL  had to take over these works along with the
workforce, in order to  complete the works within the   stipulated time.  The other major factor responsible for the
company's decline was the lack of   substantial activity in the  steel sector.   The works awarded to HSLCL by the steel
plants  after 1974 were  not adequate  to sustain the additional recurring liabilities, mainly due to increase wage bills on
account of statutory hikes including  revision of pay and increase in the number of workforce.  As a result of the above
factors,   the company began to incur continuous losses from 1978-79 onwards (except 1985-86) and  as on 31.3.1999
the accumulated loss stood at Rs.1383.28 crore.  Another  factor  which  also contributed to the downgrading of the 
company is the huge  loss suffered by HSCL on Libyan works, undertaken under  Government  of India  protocol,  due
to reasons beyond the control of the  company .
 
1.4       The Committee notes that the reasons as placed  by the Ministry of Steel, for the suffering of  HSCL.  The
Committee is of the  opinion  that  lack of vision for  human resource management and  lack of a concrete policy  led to
and sudden increase in work force in HSCL.  The Committee further  opines  that HSCL's present suffering is due to
error of judgment and some elements were beyond  the  control of   management   and Government due to prevailing 
circumstances, lack of managerial vision and manpower policy added to the woes  of HSCL.

2.2.      The Committee, while taking note of the current financial performance of HSCL  expresses its concern over its
pathetic conditions,  and is of the view  that  HSCL  needs  improvement for  better financial   performance.  HSCL 
requires a  new vision, new managerial  will and effective strategy for the  continuance  of  its existence.
3.         FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE -CUM-RESTRUCTURING PACKAGE
3.1       With a view to bail out the company from its financial crisis, a comprehensive financial restructuring proposal
was approved by Government of India/CCEA in  9
th July, 1999.  The revival  package provided  several financial
assistance/exemptions  which, inter-alia, included :-
Conversion of plan loans  of Rs.97.10 crore as on 31.3.99 into equity
Grant of moratorium on repayment  of and interest holiday on all Government  of India    loans upto 31.3.99 for 10
years.
Waiver of interest accrued and outstanding on all GOI loans as on 31.3.99 amounting to around Rs.975.17 crore
Non-plan loan of  Rs.79.33 crore during 1999-2000  for payment of statutory dues with moratorium on repayment and
interest holiday for five years subject to a review at the end of  five years.
Exemption of corporate tax in 1999-2000 on  account of windfall profit due to waiver of interest on loans.
Continuance of Government  guarantee of Rs.92.00  crore, Rs12.00 crore  cash credit  and  Rs.80.00 crore bank
guarantee  facilities, with waiver of guarantee  commission of one per cent .
It is expected that HSCL will rationalise its manpower through VRS scheme and necessary funds for the same will be 
raised from banks.  The Government will provide guarantee and full interest subsidy  for raising loan of Rs.318.36
crore   during the  three  years  i.e. 1999 -2000 to 2001-02 for separating  2000 employees per year.
 
3.2       The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Government for approving the  financial restructuring
proposal.  Considering the  fact that resources are limited the  sanctioning    of revival  package  having financial
implication is a  considered decision of the Government.  Committee also appreciates the Ministry of Steel for its
consistent    efforts in persuading the Ministry of Finance for  giving   revival package  to  HSCL.   The  Committee
notes the provisions  of revival package for  HSCL.

5.2       The Committee observes that financial assistance-cum-restructuring package  as proposed by the Ministry of
Steel and so approved by the Government was not as  comprehensive so as to mitigate the accumulated loss and total 
liability of HSCL.  The company continues to rein in adverse financial scenario and disadvantages in  general because
implementation of the revival package would  respond  only  if approved  measures are  taken in time for total  and
comprehensive restructuring of the company.  HSCL  needs total  overhauling  and complete and comprehensive 
restructuring in one  go as partial  package and  delayed implementation  defeats   the whole  idea, approach and  action.
5.3       At present, the company has 5997 skilled technical and non-technical personnel on its roll.  The expenditure
towards salary  and wages in 2001-02 is estimated to be about Rs.63 crore against Rs.146.09 crore in 1998-99.  The
actual loss of Rs.172.55 crore in  2000-01 was expected to come down to Rs.133 crore in 2001-02.  The  company
cannot stand on its own with wage  and statutory dues accumulating  to around Rs.205 crore as on 1.2.02 .   A proposal
has been put up to the Finance Ministry  by the Administrative Ministry for favourable consideration.  It is a harsh fact
that with gradual reduction in margin due to fierce   competition in the  present   market economy situation, the
company can not sustain the salary burden of 6000 employees at the turnover level of Rs.275.00 crore.  In view of the
above, the  company proposes to obtain Government guarantee for a further loan of Rs.250.00 crore to separate another
5000 employees.  This would enable the company to manage with a manpower figure of around 1000 at an annual
expenditure of  about 12 crore towards salary and wages.
5.4       The Committee is of the view that once the comprehensive restructuring package is effectively implemented
HSCL  would acquire  sufficient   resilience   and synergy to work even against fierce competition.  The Committee
recommends that HSCL management should strive in this regard. 
5.5       The Sub-Committee wanted to know the reasons as to why, despite the implementation of Financial Assistance
cum Restructuring package as sought by the Ministry of Steel in tandem with HSCL management and as approved by
Government in July, 1999, the accumulated loss of HSCL stood at Rs. 532 crore as on March 2000, and the expected
loss for the year 2000-2001 was Rs.65 crore. The HSCL management stated that the loss of Rs. 532 crore as on
31.3.2000 and another Rs.65 crore as on 31.3.2001 is mainly attributable to the insurmountable factors which include:-
Turnover could not be raised to the level required as per the restructuring proposal; 
Lack of work orders from Steel Plants due to continuing decline in Steel Industry;
Recession in construction Industry;
 Delay in getting loan for VRS;
Under-utilisation and idling of manpower and resources at Steel Plants.
5.6       The Committee is constrained to express its concern over the fact that financial assistance given for 
restructuring  package  could not be implemented in the  expected   manner and the outcome    thereof   is not  as
expected.   The Committee notes the  reasons  for failure of revival package,  although it is not fully envisaged with the
reasons for  failure  of  revival package  as advocated by the Ministry of Steel.  For the  revival of the company, HSCL
management has to procure work order despite ups and downs in Steel Industry and HSCL management  should not
face  much  difficulties as Government   is giving  due importance  to the construction sector.  The committee  is  of the 
view that optimum utilisation   of manpower and  resources at the  disposal of company is the responsibility of HSCL
management.

6.         Reasons for  partial failure of  Financial Assistance -cum- restructuring package
6.1       The Ministry of Steel  informed the Sub-Committee that  the Restructuring package could not yield the desired
result and advocated  the under mentioned detailed reasons for the partial failure of the restructuring package: -
Delay of about eleven months by commercial bank in the sanction and disbursement of the first phase of loan for VRS
amounting to Rs.210 Crore, forcing the company to carry idle wages.   In the revival plan it was envisaged to separate
2000 employees per year beginning with 1999-2000 but the loan amount was only disbursed in June, 2000 due to late
sanction. As a result, the actual separation under VRS could be effected only thereafter.
The projections in terms of profit & loss as contained in revival package were made with regard to the prevailing market
condition at that time. Since then, the market condition have shown a down turn and even big companies like SAIL have
been incurring losses.
Most of the works undertaken by HSCL are being sub-contracted primarily because of the age profile of its employee
and also the difficulties faced in their redeployment on work sites which are scattered all over India. Most of the receipts
of HSCL are passed on as payments to Sub contractor, thereby leaving a very marginal amount for utilisation towards
meeting overheads like salaries /wages etc.
HSCL has diversified its operations in the non-steel sector as jobs from the steel sector had virtually not coming up. The
margins in the diversified areas are very low, and this  means that HSCL would continue to incur losses.
HSCL has been replacing its  obsolete equipment in order to execute the works efficiently at site. During the years
1999-2000 and 2000-2001 equipments for an amount of Rs.35 lakhs and Rs.2.38 crore respectively were purchased.
Further in 2000-01, order for one excavator cum loader for Bokaro Unit for Rs.71 lakhs have been placed during the
year.
 
6.2       The Committee,  while taking note of the detailed reasons for partial failure of  Financial Assistance
restructuring package, is constrained to express its concern.  The Committee  is of the view that HSCL  management
could  have well  managed and controlled  the problems being faced by  HSCL. The Committee views that replacement
of obsolete equipment in HSCL  should have  positive  impact on the operational efficiency of company  rather than  
deterring   the implementation of revival package.  Moreover, HSCL management should chalk out  a strategy for better
margin of profit. The Committee opines that HSCL management while implementing VRS should keep in mind the age 
profile in the company  so as to retain capable and   efficient  the work  force  to undertake  the job.
 
6.3       The Sub-Committee wanted to know the reasons for delay of about eleven months by Commercial Bank in the
sanction and disbursement of the First Phase of the loan for VRS to Rs.210 crore and steps which were taken by HSCL
management to avoid the delay.  The management stated  that immediately after receiving the communication for

rd September 1999.  Finally, SBI  LHO, Patna
communicated the sanction of VRS Loan for Rs.209.82 crore on 25th March, 2000 and the amount was drawn on 2nd
June, 2000.  The main reasons for delay in sanctioning and disbursement of the fund by SBI as informed by Ministry of
Steel , are mentioned below ;-

7.0       HSCL becoming a sub-contracting company
7.1       Replying to the Sub-Committee's query regarding HSCL becoming a sub-contracting company from a
construction company resultant and the possibility of sustenance of company under changed circumstances, the
management stated that the company has to resort to sub-contracting to augment the order book position in the face of
stiff competition from the construction sector.  It also has to offset difficulties like severe cash crunch to deposit
EMD/SD etc. and funds for procurement of raw materials and other inputs.  Sub-contracting is also necessitated due to
the fact that shifting manpower and other resources from steel plants to short duration projects is not only expensive but
also invites social and other local resistance.  Moreover, the age profile of the employees is also a deterrent in carrying
out the works departmentally.  Low margins due to competition and inadequacy of related expertise and skill are also
strong factors compelling adoption of sub-contracting system.
7.2       The HSCL management clarified that the company has neither become, nor has any plans to become out and out
a sub-contracting company.  It has a rich team of experienced engineers, workers, technicians and a strong fleet of
construction equipments, as its backbone.  In most cases, the company executes the jobs giving maximum possible
departmental inputs combining with it the strengths of the sub-contractors.  The extent of departmental inputs varies
according to location and nature of work.  As of now, the downward trend has been witnessed and therefore, given the
difficult circumstances enveloping the Company, the process is expected to remain the way it is for some more time. 
Till then, the primary concern of the company would be the payment of salary and wages to its employees as well as
liquidation of all other liabilities.  Once out of this predicament, the company's performance is bound to increase
through better utilization of equipments, supervision, design, procurement and monitoring besides direct execution as
much as possible.  Under the changed scenario and market economy, the system of sub-contracting (with maximum
departmental inputs) cannot be altogether discarded.  The execution arrangement, as elaborated above, is practical and
sustainable, if the liabilities reduce. 
7.3       The Committee understands the  compelling  circumstances under which HSCL has to adopt the system of  sub-
contracting  business pattern but   it is also an open secret that sub-contracting   gives low margin for HSCL than if the
work done by the company itself.  The  Committee  is of the view  that VRS  offer  by HSCL  to its employees would
take care of keeping with itself the workforce consisting of  productive age profile and  willing to undertake
work/assignment from whatever concern its coming.  The Committee feels that  direct undertaking of work by HSCL
staff would give larger margin to HSCL and thus opening scope for profitability of HSCL, therefore, it would be better
that the  system of sub-Contracting  is replaced by direct involvement of HSCL employees,  sooner than later.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE
COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE
 
The Committee notes that the reasons as placed  by the Ministry of Steel, for the suffering of  HSCL.  The Committee is
of the  opinion  that  lack of vision for  human resource management and  lack of a concrete policy  led to and sudden
increase in work force in HSCL.  The Committee further  opines  that HSCL's present suffering is due to error of
judgment and some elements were beyond  the  control of   management   and Government due to prevailing 
circumstances, lack of managerial vision and manpower policy added to the woes  of HSCL.
 
( Para 1.4)
 
The Committee, while taking note of the current financial performance of HSCL  expresses its concern over its pathetic
conditions,  and is of the view  that  HSCL  needs  improvement for  better financial   performance.  HSCL  requires a 
new vision, new managerial  will and effective strategy for the  continuance  of  its existence.
( Para : 2.2)
 
The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Government for approving the  financial restructuring proposal. 
Considering the  fact that resources are limited the  sanctioning    of revival  package  having financial implication is a 
considered decision of the Government.  Committee also appreciates the Ministry of Steel for its consistent    efforts in
persuading the Ministry of Finance for  giving   revival package  to  HSCL.   The  Committee notes the provisions  of
revival package for  HSCL.
 ( Para 3.2)
 
Implementation status
 The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry of Steel has taken action  on all provisions contained in the  revival
package.  The Committee hopes that efforts  of the Ministry of Steel in  persuading   the Department of Revenue  for
exempt  of  corporate  tax for HSCL would have a positive impact on the financial performance of HSCL.  The
Committee further  hopes that  consequent upon the  separation of  7,106  employees and reduction in the wage bill, the
financial ratio of the company  would further improve.
( Para 4.2)
 
The Committee understands that it is but natural that the accumulated loss of the company would come down once the
Government  grants waiver of interest Government loans, so far the same is a book-entry and it hardly has any reflection
on the actual financial performance  of the company.
 ( Para 4.4)
 
The Committee is happy to note the  savings on the specified  overheads due to  measures undertaken by HSCL 
management to rationalise administrative expenditure.   The Committee hopes that the efforts to rationalise
administrative  expenditure would continue.
( Para 4.8)
 
The Committee observes that financial assistance-cum-restructuring package  as proposed by the Ministry of Steel and
so approved by the Government was not as  comprehensive so as to mitigate the accumulated loss and total  liability of
HSCL.  The company continues to rein in adverse financial scenario and disadvantages in  general because
implementation of the revival package would  respond  only  if approved  measures are  taken in time for total  and
comprehensive restructuring of the company.  HSCL  needs total  overhauling  and complete and comprehensive 
restructuring in one  go as partial  package and  delayed implementation  defeats   the whole  idea, approach and  action.
 ( Para 5.2)
 
The Committee is of the view that once the comprehensive restructuring package is effectively implemented HSCL 
would   acquire  sufficient  resilience   and synergy to work even against fierce competition.  The Committee
recommends that HSCL management should strive in this regard. 
 
( Para 5.4)
