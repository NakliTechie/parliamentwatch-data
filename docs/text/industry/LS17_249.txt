REPORT  
 
 Indian economy is predominantly agrarian and agric ulture in the 
country is heavily dependent on fertilizers.  Becau se of this, the optimal 
production of fertilizers, its marketing and use re mains important variables 
in the economic performance of the country and its food security.  The 
worrying part of the story is the crippling depende nce of the nation on 
imports for raw materials to manufacture fertilizer s.  This precious nature 
of  raw materials make it even more critical to hav e the most advanced 
production processes and technology which gets the most out of the acquired 
raw  materials. Technology contributes to greater m anufacturing of 
fertilizers and makes these affordable for our farm ers.  In this backdrop, it is 
evident that CPSEs can play decisive roles in augme nting fertilizer 
production  and making our agricultural sector more  vibrant. India’s first 
large scale fertilizer unit, Fertilizers and Chemic als Travancore Ltd (FACT) 
was incorporated in 1943 as a private company promoted by M/s Seshasayee 
Brothers. It began its operations in 1947 by produc ing Ammonium 
Sulphate.  Little over a decade since its incorpora tion, FACT became a 
Kerala State PSU in 1960 and the Government of Indi a became its major 
shareholder by the end of 1962. It has been an even tful journey since then 
and FACT has emerged as a major industrial unit of Kerala.  Because of its 
location the  people Kerala have developed a specia l affinity with the 
Company. At the all India level it has played a muc h larger role.  The 
products manufactured and marketed by FACT have con tributed 
significantly to increase   agriculture output of s outh India and strengthen 
agrarian economy.  
 
2. The Mission statement of FACT encapsulates all t hat the Company 
stands for and gives a bird’s eye view of its poten tiality.  FACT’s mission is 
:- 
“To be a market leader in Fertilizers/Petrochemical s and a 
significant player in all its other businesses incl uding 
Engineering/Technology services, providing maximum customer satisfaction 
and reasonable reward to shareholders, adhering to business ethics and 
professionalism with adequate concern for the commu nity and the 
environment .”                     
                                                        
3. The Company currently has 3 divisions viz. Udyog amandel complex, 
which is the parent division, Cochin Division and t he Marketing 
Division.  Apart from these divisions, FACT has 2 s ister organizations – 

FACT Engineering & Designs Organization (FEDO) & FA CT Engineering 
Works (FEW). The manpower of the Company as on 31.03.2012 is 3157 and 
it plans to reduce the strength to 2850 by 2014 thr ough retirement and 
redeployment. The issued and subscribed share capit al of FACT is Rs. 
647.07 crore, while the authorized share capital is  of Rs. 1000.00crore.

5. The performance of FACT over the years has witne ssed varying 
degrees of high and lows. For about a decades and a  half from 1983-84 to 
1997- 98 FACT made profits. However, the financial performance of the 
Company went into the red from 1998-99. The Committ ee is given to 
understand that the reasons for negative performanc e were beyond the 
control of the Company, like inadequate compensatio n for its fertilizer 
products due to price concession scheme of the Gove rnment of India, 
stoppage of urea operations, etc. Besides, there ar e other reasons some of 
which are highly avoidable led to the loss suffered  by the Company. In an 
article entitled "Figures Fail, but FACT Remains a Favourite" authored by 
Shri K.A.Martin and published in the Hindu on 15 May 2013 the reasons for 
the failure of the Company have been outlined. It s tates "A system of 
management that does not facilitate timely decision s on crucial issues such 
as purchase of raw material; difficulties associate d with the payment of 
fertilizer subsidies; use of a costly and often pri ce-sensitive feedstock such 
as naphtha, and the lack of full-time heads for pro tracted periods have 
hampered the company’s progress. For example, the company did not have a 
full-time head between November 2009 and April 2013 . It was a period 
during which no significant decision could be taken."  
6. The Committee is happy to note that many measure s have been 
taken to address the problems including the appointment of a Chairman 
cum Managing Director who has prepared a practical and hands on 
plan to revive the Company and make it financially sound and 
profitable. The Committee strongly recommends that the management 
must go beyond core activity of the Company and con solidate, 
strengthen and augment its subsidiaries, the FACT D esign and 
Engineering Organisation (FEDO) and FACT Engineerin g Works 
(FEW) which can fetch profits and greatly add to it s financial health. In 
doing so the Company can be made a vibrant and profitable Company. 

 
7. Despite FACT’s financial performance taking a hi t, the physical 
performance of FACT, in terms of productivity, has been claimed to be 
excellent. The lowest point came in 2004-05, when t he Company became 
potentially sick and a proposal for improving the f inancial status of the 
Company being submitted to the BRPSE. A year later,  the Government 
approved a financial restructuring package for FACT  based on the 
recommendations of BRPSE.   The package had the fol lowing features as 
submitted to the Committee: 
 1. Conversion of 50% of the outstanding GOI loan a mounting to Rs. 
292.30 crore as on 31 March 2005 into equity capital. 
 
 
 2.  Write off the non-plan loan of Rs. 60.00 crore  given for Voluntary 
retirement Scheme and the balance outstanding GOI loan of Rs. 232.30 crore 
as on 31 March 2005. 
 
 3.  Write off all interest aggregating to Rs. 85.7 7 crore as on 31 
March, 2005 and penal interest thereof. 
 
8. Though the bailout helped FACT escape from being  branded as a sick 
Company, a turnaround still seemed a mirage.  This situation arose due to 
unanticipated spike in the prices of raw materials,  which went beyond the 
projections made of BRPSE and the anomalies in the price concession 
scheme for complex fertilizer.  In the aforemention ed article "Figures Fail, 
but FACTS remain  Favourite" it has been stated tha t "one of the features 
that went against the company was the averaging sys tem of providing 
subsidy for complex fertilizer components. 

 
 
9. FACT, having significantly higher cost of produc tion, did not stand to 
gain from the system that paid subsidy on the basis  of the average cost of 
production of a particular fertilizer." The Committee recommends that the 
above stated problems must be resolved  by the Gove rnment of India 
for the purpose of reviving the Company and making it profitable .  
 
 
10. The FACT suffered operational losses during 200 6-07 and 2007-
08.  It is stated that "The key to pulling FACT out  of its troubles lies in 
providing sufficient working capital and adequate n aphtha conversion cost, 
and fixing LNG price at affordable levels at the ea rliest." 
The Committee understands that the working capital problems faced by 
FACT are far from being resolved despite getting a grant- in- aid of Rs. 
200.00 crore in March, 2008, to improve the situati on. The Company has 
now again sought a cash grant to meet working capit al 
requirements.  Further, FACT has also submitted a p roposal for financial 
restructuring to the Government of India. On the ad vice of its administrative 
department, Department of Fertilizers, FACT engaged  a consultant to 
prepare a detailed restructuring and revival packag e.  The proposal prepared 
by the consultant is before the FACT Board and is e xpected to be forwarded 
to the Department of Fertilizer after due approval.  
 
 
11. Meanwhile, the Committee takes note that with a n intention to 
improve the overall performance of the Company, FAC T has identified 
certain forward looking projects and schemes which are under various stages 
of consideration and approval/implementation. The p roject which are 
already underway are Feed Stock conversion of Ammon ia Plant to LNG, 
Automation of Mixing centre at Cochin Division and setting up of Sulphuric 
Acid storage facility at Willington Island.  The pr oposed initiatives include 
the construction of a new Urea plant at Udyogamanda l, setting up an 
additional one 1000 TDP NP plant at Cochin Division , revamp of 
Phosphoric acid plant also at Cochin Division and m arketing of 
SSP.  Further, FACT has also entered into a MoU wit h the Container 
Corporation of India (CONCOR) and Central Warehouse  Corporation 
(CWC) for setting up Containers Freight stations by  the side of the new 
highway to the Vallarpadam terminal which passes th rough FACT 
premises.  These projects are expected to help FACT  meet many 

deficiencies faced by the company. However ,the Com mittee is given to 
understand that the Container Freight Stations have  not been constructed as 
the land required for this purpose has not been subleased by the FACT to the 
CWC on the ground that the Government of Kerala own s the land and, 
therefore, the FACT cannot sublease it. As a result  it has not been built so 
far even though the MOU has been signed since 2008.  The Committee 
strongly recommends that necessary steps must be ta ken by the 
Government of Kerala to give land to the CWC for th e construction of 
the Container Freight Stations which will help the FACT to restore its 
financial and commercial viability and make it a pr ofit making 
company.   
 
12. Certain other impediments faced by the Company were also brought 
before the Committee. These have to be tackled effe ctively for ensuring 
success of FACT arising out of the initiatives deta iled above. The most 
nagging of these problems is the volatility in the price of raw material 
including the petroleum product Naphtha.  The high price of Naphtha 
compared to natural gas only adds to the agony.  As  stated earlier the 
crippling inadequacy of working capital and high in terest rate at which 
funds  offered by banks to FACT contributes to its woes.  Further, 
Government’s policy shift toward Nutrient Based Sub sidy (NBS) and 
provision of giving subsidy directly to farmers ins tead of giving it to 
manufactures has added to the woes of FACT. 
 
13 The Committee finds it encouraging that FACT has  made profit 
during the year 2011-12 after a lurch of years in n egative net profit.  This is 
a sliver lining in the dark clouds despite many adv ersities faced by the 
Company that without doubt may be classified as bey ond its control. It is 
believed  that availability of LNG by  will help FA CT to considerably lower 
production cost and sustain profitability in the ye ars to come.  In fact the 
switch over of FACT from Naptha to LNG would herald  for it a new era of 
growth marked by clean and energy efficient product ion of fertiliser. The 
Committee understands from media reports that all m easures for the 
conversion of FACT from naphtha to LNG based Compan y have been taken 
and it is almost ready to use LNG. Till the LNG is made available for 
production of fertilizer the FACT would require nap htha and the 
Committee recommends that the request of the Compan y for naphtha 
subsidy may be acceded to.   
 
14. The Committee strongly recommends for the reviv al of the 

Company so that the plan envisaged by the new CMD f or a turnover of 
Rs.7,000 crore and a profit after tax of Rs.300 cro re by 2020, can be 
achieved by raising fertilizer production to 20 lak h tonnes and fertilizer 
trading to 10 lakh tonnes.
