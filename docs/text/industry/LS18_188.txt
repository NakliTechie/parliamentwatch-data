1.0.      Introduction
1.1       The Agro and Rural Industries Sector which falls within the mandate of the Ministry of Agro and Rural
Industries has been envisaged as an important avenue for  employment in the rural non-farm sector for the balanced
economic growth of the country.  The Agro and Rural Industries Sector seeks to establish village industries at low cost
based on the local raw materials, skills and technology for providing employment opportunities to the local population
and artisans who have been traditionally engaged in village level economic activities. The main contribution of ARI
sector to the national economy has been in terms of production and employment generation through setting up of village
industries in the rural and semi-urban areas. The Ministry of ARI has informed the Committee  that  over the past five
decades, there has been a big growth in Village Industries (VI) Sector in terms of production and employment. While in
the year 1955-56, the production in terms of value under village industries was at the level of Rs.10.93 crore, the same
has gone up to Rs.10458.89 crore in 2004-05 and Rs.8778.96 crore in 2005-06 (up to January 2006). Similarly,

employment level in VI sector has also increased from 3.07 lakh persons in 1955-56 to 71.47  lakh persons up to
January 2006.
           
 1.2      The Government launched the “Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP)” throughout the country on
01.04.1995 to generate additional employment opportunities in rural areas. In the 10
th plan, a target of 2 million jobs
under REGP was fixed. This target was enhanced to 2.5 million jobs in 2003-04. Accordingly, the employment targets
were revised and fixed for 2003-04, 2004-05, 2005-06 and 2006-07 are 5 lakh, 5.25 lakh, 5.50 lakh and 5.90 lakh
respectively. During 10 th Plan period (upto January 2005), 16.96 lakh job opportunities have already been generated
under this programme. In order to meet the enhanced employment target, the budgetary allocation for REGP was also
enhanced from Rs.326 crore in 2004-05 to Rs.362 crore in 2005-06 which has been further enhanced to Rs.372 crore in
BE 2006-07.
 
1.3       The Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) is another major scheme implemented by this Ministry.  The
programme aims to create self-employment opportunities for educated unemployed youth belonging to weaker sections
of the society through the setting up of tiny units in all economically viable activities. The scheme seeks to create
employment opportunities for 1.65 million persons over the 10
th Plan period. In the first three years of 10 th Plan period,
9.81 lakh employment has been created under this Yojana.   In 2005-06, the employment target was fixed at 2.50 lakh
units  (3.75 lakh persons) under this Yojana against which 193960 cases have been sanctioned up to January 2006.
 
1.4       The coir industry provides employment to more than 6 lakh people mostly in rural sector.  Women constitute 80
per cent of the workforce. The industry generates additional employment in rural areas through coir processing activities
with minimum investment and arrests migration of rural youths to urban areas. The contribution of this industry to the
GDP was estimated roughly at Rs. 1900 crore during 2004-2005.  Out of this, the export earnings of the industry were to
the tune of   Rs. 473.40 crore.  During 2005-2006, the contribution of this industry in terms of export earnings is
expected to be over Rs.500 crore.
 
1.5       The Committee understands that the Agro and Rural Industries need  further reinforcing  and strengthening 
through streamlining  the ARI sector and  by ensuring timely policy interventions. The committee is of the considered
view that the contribution of this sector can be further consolidated and strengthened by ensuring timely and adequate
delivery of credit, technology upgradation, marketing support in the form of financial support for exhibitions, design
intervention and packaging, etc. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of ARI should make strive in
this regard. The committee foresees that   amendment in the Khadi and Village Industries Commission Act, 1956 would
lead  further to streamlining  and strengthening the activities in the KVI sector.
 
1.6       The Committee expresses its concern on the employment opportunity provided by the Village Industries ( VI )
Sector  which remains stagnant in percentile terms in the last fifty years as it is still less than one percent of the
population of the country. It is an indication that VI  sector did not generate employment  in commensurate with the rate
growth  of population.
 
1.7       The Committee feels that growth in this sector as proclaimed by the Government does not lead to similar growth
in the employment generation, hence the contention of the Government is not tenable . The Committee, therefore calls
for immediate  and special attention of the Government and desires that the Government should come forward with a
special Package for VI  Sector  to harness the immense job –opportunities  in this sector .
 
 
2.0       Charter for the Ministry of ARI
2.1       The Ministry acts as a nodal agency for promotion and development of agro and rural industries in small towns
and rural areas. The Ministry has a multi-pronged role to play in the quality implementation of policies, programmes
and schemes. In the role of a policy maker, the Ministry collects, collates and derives inferences, which act as critical
inputs to further policy formulation/amendments. In the role of an advocate, the Ministry advocates and puts up ‘felt
needs’ of the stakeholders involved in the sector as a whole. In the role of a facilitator, the Ministry facilitates,
coordinates and supports its field agencies in implementing the programmes and schemes both quantitatively and
qualitatively.  The Charter for the Ministry is as under:

4.0       Monitoring mechanism
 
4.1       The fortnightly performances are reviewed at the level of Secretary in the Ministry whereas KVIC and Coir
Board review monthly performances. As per the Management Information System approved by the Cabinet Secretariat
for Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries, implementation of PMRY and REGP, coir exports and the declarations made
in National Common Minimum Programme (NCMP) are reviewed.  The activities of KVIC, PMRY and Coir Schemes
are also reviewed by Secretary on a monthly basis and a Brief Note is sent to the PMO and Cabinet Secretary. On the
basis of monthly and quarterly performances of progress in various sectors of development, appropriate instructions are
issued to KVIC, Coir Board and State Governments. This is being done on a regular basis.
 
4.2       The Committee finds that the monitoring mechanism at the Ministry level includes both structured as well as
informal but regular monitoring. Secretary (ARI) periodically monitors implementation of PMRY and REGP along with
other schemes being implemented by the Ministry.  Secretary (ARI) also monitors the implementation of the National
Common Minimum Programme. The Committee believes that the ministry of ARI would achieve the targets set in the
Action Plan for the year 2006-07 without any hindrances.

6.0       General Policy behind the budget proposals
 
6.1       There has been no policy shift in 2006-07 compared to the previous year. However, the emphasis on
employment generation is even greater with a higher allocation for REGP and PMRY.  Further, as per the declaration in
the National Common Minimum Programme (NCMP), regarding regeneration of traditional industries like coir,
handloom, handicrafts, sericulture, leather, pottery and other cottage industries, the Finance Minister in his Budget
Speech of July 2004 had announced the decision to set up a Fund with an initial allocation of Rs. 100 crore. 
Accordingly, the Government has approved “Scheme of Funds for Regeneration of Traditional Industries” (SFURTI)
for the integrated development of traditional clusters of khadi, coir and village industries, including leather and pottery.
It is proposed to take up development of hundred clusters (25 khadi, 50 village industries and 25 coir) over the next five
years beginning 2005-06. For implementation of the scheme, a detailed office memorandum has been issued on
03.10.2005. The Nodal Agencies (KVIC and Coir Board) have been requested to submit proposals to the Ministry for
development of clusters under SFURTI. So, far 50 cluster development proposals (khadi 25, village industries 9 and coir
16) have been received. These proposals are being considered by the Ministry.
 
6.2       The Committee notes that the policy behind the budget proposals for the year 2006-07, like the previous year,
remains the employment – generation with greater emphasis on REGP, PMRY and development of clusters under
SFURTI. The Committee is of the view that employment –generation would lead to increment in the purchasing power
which in turn would boost the domestic market.
 
7.0       Economic philosophy behind the budget proposals
 
7.1       The provision made in the budget proposal of Ministry of A&RI for the year 2006-07 is an instrument of
directing the national economic policy.Poverty alleviation and employment generation are the keystone of the economic
policy framework of the Government.  Thus, the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals 2006-07 is to
empower the people, especially the poor. In line with the above economic philosophy, the Budget proposal for the
Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries for the year 2006-07 has received further support for creation of more
employment on a sustainable basis in the rural areas and small towns.  Thus, the Budget allocation for the generating
more employment opportunities under REGP has been increased from 366.60 crore in year 2005-06 (at RE stage) to
Rs.372.63 crore in 2006-07.  Further, for self-employment generation under PMRY budget allocation has been
increased from Rs. 218.90 crore in the year 2004-05 to Rs.273.46 crore in 2005-06 (at RE stage) and the same has been
further increased to Rs.324.98 crore in 2006-07.
 
7.2       The Committee is in agreement with the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Ministry of
ARI for the year  2006-07 i.e. the Poverty alleviation and employment generation as  the keystone of the economic
policy. The Committee is of the view that fast growing India must invest in society, therefore, the purpose and policy of
budgeting should be aimed at widening the social safety net, as higher the economic development of a nation the greater
the need for a safety net.
 
7.3 Replying to the Committee’ query, the Ministry of ARI stated that the economic philosophy behind the Budget
proposals (2006-07) pertaining to Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries conform to the logistic of on-going
liberalization and globalization process. The liberalisation of economy has enhanced the competitive nature of the whole
industrial set up in the country. Open market mechanisms have come in place of regulated and protected nature of pre-
liberalised economy. The challenge posed by globalised economy has enhanced and enabled nurturing of the inherent
competitive and resilient nature of the agro and rural industries sector. This sector mainly consists of khadi, village
industries, cottage industries (handicrafts), and coir, tiny and micro enterprises. The products of this sector by their very
nature are traditional, location specific and exclusively indigenous. This adds to the specialized and exclusive nature of

188th Report of Committee On Industry

the sector as such and gives it a dynamic and resilient character. For example, herbal products, organic manure, organic
food, honey production, traditional crafts items, etc are so exclusive by nature that it offers immense export potential
which is almost non-competitive in the world economy. The emphasis would be to highlight the exclusivity of ARI
products and its suitability to specific local area both for production as well as marketing as it addresses the felt local
needs and to develop quality diversification and aggressively enter the world market.
 
7.4 The Committee is pleased to note that the economic philosophy behind the Budget proposals (2006-07) pertaining to
Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries conform to the logistic of on-going liberalization and globalization process. The
Committee feels that in the euphoria of the rapid globalization humane economic consideration and approach remains at
the backburner, but, in the ideal situation as the growth gathers momentum, sooner or later, enough of it must trickle
down the poor. The Committee hopes that implementation of the budget proposals for the year would lead to enabling
of the poor to access to minimum standard of life.
 
8.0       Welfare measures and meeting  the social obligation
 
8.1       The economic philosophy of the budget proposals of the Ministry of ARI for the year 2006-07 incorporates the
welfare measures. The measures envisages to meet the social obligation as well. In continuation of the economic
philosophy for the budget 2006-07, welfare of the khadi artisans have also been taken care of by way of enhancing
allocation under Khadi grant head from Rs.120 crore (at RE stage) in 2005-06 to Rs.138.30 crore in 2006-07 to meet the
enhanced expenditure on insurance cover under the Group Insurance scheme of the KVIC. Further, for regeneration of
traditional industries such as coir, handloom, powerloom, garment, rubber, cashew, handicraft food processing,
sericulture, wool development, leather, pottery and other cottage industries a scheme, titled the Scheme of Fund for
Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) has been approved by the Government. For implementation of the
scheme, a detailed office memorandum has been issued on 03.10.2005. The Nodal Agencies (KVIC and Coir Board)
have been requested to submit proposals to the Ministry for development of clusters under SFURTI. So, far 50 cluster
development proposals (khadi 25, village industries 9 and coir 16) have been received. These proposals are being
considered by the Ministry. Further during 2006-07, it has been proposed replaced obsolete charkhas and looms with
new high productive implements.
 
8.2       These will immensely help rural artisans in traditional sectors who have been involved in their vocation for ages,
but for want of adequate support were not able to upgrade the existing technology.  This in turn will help in more
production in the traditional sector, thus leading to higher income generation and better prosperity to the rural artisans.
 
8.3       The Committee takes note of the economic philosophy of budgetary provision for this year aim at empowering
the people specially the poor. The Ministry have not  yet indicated what special measure they are likely to take to give a
boost to their philosophy.  The increase in allocation for 2006-07 does not however give any indication to this effect as
it remains, more or less the  same and it was in 2004-05 & 2005-06.  The Committee desires that the Ministry  may
explain about this to the Committee. 
 
Gender sensitisation programme
 
9.1       The Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries (ARI) is implementing two national employment generation
programmes, namely, Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) and Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana
(PMRY).  Both these programmes are for employment generation in the unorganised sector.  Though there is no specific
reservation for women entrepreneurs, there has been substantial participation of women in these two programmes. The
participation of women in REGP is 26 per cent and that under PMRY is 13.96 per cent in terms of number of projects
set up. In addition, the Coir Board is implementing the Mahila Coir Yojana which is a women oriented self-employment
programme.  Under this programme women are provided training and ratts for carrying out spinning activity and
increasing their earnings. Increased budget allocation under REGP and PMRY is expected to further strengthen the
women participations under these programmes.
 
9.2       The Committee wishes to pin-point that the Tenth Plan reinforced the commitment to gender budgeting to
establish its gender-differential impact and to translate gender commitments into budgetary commitment. It is a well-
known fact that the gender budgeting is perceived as a powerful tool not only for tracking allocation of resources but

188th Report of Committee On Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/188threport.htm[04-07-2016 13:01:56]
also ensures implementation of issues and outcomes. The Union Expenditure Budget 2005-06 has also taken care of the
fact and has instituonalised Gender –budgeting in the budgeting in the budget document through Statement 20,
Expenditure Budget, volume –1 and at present, as per the source of the Economic Survey 2005-06, Gender budget cells
have been set up in 35 department of the government of the Government of India.  The committee would like to know
the response of the Ministry of ARI to this cherished national  policy and whether the Gender Budget cell has been set
up in the Ministry of ARI.
 
9.3       The Committee believes that the proclaimed policy of the Government for empowerment of women cannot
happen unless they are provided with adequate income generating activities, through wage and self-employment. The
Committee desires that the Ministry of ARI may ensure gender commitment into budgetary commitment by
emphasizing Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) and Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) the
same way.

13.7     Status of recovery of loans under PMRY
 
13.7.1  As per the reports received from RBI, the overall status of recovery under PMRY is around 35 per cent i.e. 35.2
per cent, 34.96 per cent and 35.32 per cent for the half year ending March 2003, 2004 and 2005 respectively. The RBI
and Govt. of India have initiatedvarious  steps for improving the recovery of loan overdues under the Scheme . 
Emphasis has been laid on improving the success rate under the Scheme by ensuring backward and forward linkages for
the units set up under the Scheme. Higher success rate of the units under the Scheme is necessary to ensure regular
repayment of loan overdues under the Scheme. The revised Training curriculum  for training of PMRY beneficiaries 
has been  prepared by the Ministry of ARI through Rural Development and Self Employment Training
Institute(RUDSETI), Karnataka and circulated to all States/UTs. Further, banks have been advised to conduct joint
recovery drives with concerned Government Departments viz. District Industries Centres (DICs). Government of India
has  advised also to all State Government to notify PMRY loan overdues as revenues dues under their respective
Revenue Recovery Acts.  The State/UTs also been advised to improve recovery of loan overdues while communicating
annual targets to them. All implementing banks have been advised to file criminal complaints against the borrowers who
misutilise loans sanctioned under the Scheme.
 
13.7.2  A meeting of RBI, implementing banks and State Directorate of Industries was held on 10.2.2004 and the latest
meeting was held on 16.9.2005, in which the States having low recovery of loan under the Scheme were urged upon to
adopt the pattern followed in the State of Andhra Pradesh for better recovery of PMRY loans. States have been advised
again to improve the recovery performance under PMRY. The Reserve Bank of India has informed that they maintain
state-wise data with respect to demand, recovery overdues under the PMRY. The data on total amount of loan under
PMRY enlisted as bad debt is not available.
 
13.7.3  The Ministry have already conceded that rate of recovery of loans under PMRY has been around 35% in the last
three years.  The committee is deeply concerned about this lower rate of recovery.   From the information provided by
the Ministry, the Committee gathers the  impression that steps taken by Government and RBI have not been adequate
enough to enhance the rate of recovery.  The Committee calls for information, State wise for the last three years clearly
indicating the progress made owing to steps taken by Government in this regard.

15.10.  Marketing strategy of KVI
 
15.10.1            The marketing strategy of KVIC is to focus on its eco friendly and natural product range. Khadi &
Village Industries sector produces goods and services both. In village industries service based units basically cover local
area needs. With regard to non-consumer goods normally units established have good market as most of such products
are intermediary products used by other industries. Main difficulty is in consumer goods and khadi. For both these
products areas of intervention involves a detailed marketing strategy for publicity, branding and brand promotion,
design input, better packaging etc. Accessing private marketing channel, modernization of existing channels, organizing
large scale exhibitions are important aspects. Design / packaging development scheme and renovation of marketing
outlets scheme has been launched.
 
15.10.2            For the consumer goods, KVIC has developed “Sarvodaya Brand” consisting of FMCG products like
Toilet Soap, Pickles, Honey, etc., “Khadi” for exclusive up-market and export products like Essential Oils, Herbal
Products, Designer Garments etc.  and “Desi Aahaar” for  Organic foods like Cereals, Spices Powder, Pulses, etc. and
the same have been launched at Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata etc. in  select Outlets.  Based on the experience it is felt that a
professional Marketing Agency / Federation is required, so that KVIC does not get involved in Departmental Trading
Activities, since institutions and marketing professionals can play a better and effective role in marketing these
products. Accordingly  a Marketing Confederation has been constituted out of existing Khadi & V.I. Federations and
has also been accorded provisional recognition by KVIC.  The Confederation is expected to take up aggressive
marketing for KVI products with active support of KVIC. Regarding the institutional sale, KVIC is having a rate
contract with        DGS & D for 32 items of Khadi and rate contract for cotton gauze bandage.  KVIC is also having a
contract with CSD (Military Canteen) for supply of Honey.  Bulk orders have been received from Border Security
Forces for Huck back towels and the strategy has been to increase the product range to various V.I. products for rate
contract with DGS & D so that an captive market is created for KVI products like Hand-made Paper, Shoes, Soap Steel
and Wooden Furniture, etc.
 
15.10.3            The Committee hopes that KVIC’S marketing strategy of institutional  and marketing professionals
approach would lead to enhancement of its market share.  The Committee would like to know as to how much the
design / packaging development scheme and renovation of marketing outlets scheme led to enhancement of sales of
KVI products.

16.6     Potential of generating woman employment  in coir Industries
 
16.6.1  Coir Industry is one of the small scale industries in India which can provide employment to maximum number of
women in coir producing states.  This is because the amount of investment required is the lowest and the technology and
skill requirement are also nominal.  The opportunity offered by this industry is phenomenal when the fact is considered
that the utilization of raw material at the current level is only 37%.  The growing sense of responsibility to the nature
world over also offers an unprecedented opportunity to this industry producing eco-friendly products.  The nature of
work required by this industry is also suitable for women workers as they can do the work at their spare time at their
own residence. About 80% of the work-force in this industry are women from the economically and socially backward
classes of the Society. The developments of skills of artisans through training at the National Coir Training Centre and
Regional Offices of Coir Board have separate allocations for SC/ST categories.  In the training programmes, 30% of the
seats are reserved for SC/ST categories.  Therefore 30% of the total budget outlay under training is earmarked for SC
and ST. The Mahila Coir Yojana Scheme of the Board has been  formulated in such a way that 25% of the total outlay
of the scheme goes to SC category and 5% to the ST category.
 
 16.6.2The Committee finds that coir industry  has the scope for women employment, therefore, the committee
recommends that the government should enhance the budgetary allocation for  Mahila Coir Yojana Scheme so that the
scheme may be popularized not only in the coir producing states but also in the non- traditional zones. 
 
16.7     Export promotion
 
16.7.1 Till the removal of the Minimum Export Price applicable on export of coir and coir products and removal of the
Minimum Purchase Price applicable  for the goods manufactured for export, the coir industry was operating in a
protected environment. 
 
16.7.2  With the advent of globalisation and liberalisation and resultant removal of Minimum Export Price and Purchase
Price the industry is facing severe price competition from among the exporters and manufacturers, resultant
deterioration in quality. This is besides the competition from synthetic and other natural products in markets abroad. 
Coir faces competition from products like Chinese Grass Mats, Rubber, Sisal etc.  In the domestic market also liberal
imports have created competition from substitutes.  It is export which helps to adjust the imbalance in the domestic
market.

16.9     R&D activities
 
16.9.1  `Coir Board has been provided with Plan funds for organising S&T and R&D Programmes for the development
of the coir industry. The Plan S&T Programmes have been broadly classified into four sub-heads as Modernisation of
extraction and processing coir fibre ; Development of coir processing machinery  and Product development &
diversification Testing and services. The research & development programmes of Coir board are being implemented
through the two premier research institutes, Central Coir research Institute, Kalavoor in Kerala and Central Institute of
Coir Technology, Bangalore in Karnataka. As the in-house facilities in the research & development programmes are
inadequate in all the departments of CCRI to meet the requirement of the industry, collaborative projects are also
undertaken with reputed R&D institutions engaged in organising research & development programmes of similar nature.
 
16.9.2  Under the extension service programmes, Coir Board has established physical testing laboratories for the testing
of coir, coir fibre, coir yarn, dyes and chemicals, rubberised coir etc.  The testing facilities are extended to the trade on
realisation of service charges.  All major rubberised coir manufacturing units are availing the facilities of the physical
testing lab for testing their products as per BIS standards.  The exporters engaged in the coir industry are utilising the
services of chemical testing laboratory for the testing of dyed materials for different fastness ratings such as rubbing
fastness, light fastness and wash fastness.  The outcome of the R&D programmes undertaken by the Board were
popularised in the industry especially  for the development and growth of the industry both in export and domestic
market. The export of coir pith has increased manifold during the last five years and this was one of the major
achievement in the coir sector for the increase in the export of coirpith which was considered as a waste material. 
Around 36 units are engaged in the export of coir pith from India. 
 
16.9.3  With a view to transfer technologies developed by the Research Institutes of the Coir Board, a Transfer of
Technology Meet was organized at Alleppey in Kerala State in January 2006. A number of technologies developed by
the twin Research Institutes of the Coir Board (CCRI & CICT) and through collaborative research programmes, were
demonstrated before the entrepreneurs and industrialists numbering 250.

16.10               Co-operativisation Scheme
 
16.10.1            The Committee was informed that the Grants to State Governments relates to  Cooperativisation of Coir
Industry, a Centrally Sponsored Scheme, was introduced during the year 1982. The Government provides for grants
through its budgetary support under this Sub-head to the States for implementation of this scheme.
 
16.10.2            Replying to the Committee’ query regarding  whether the target of Central  assistance under co-
operativisation scheme has been achieved, the ministry of ARI answered in the negative arguing that The scheme  was
launched in 1982 to help thousands of workers from small families, mostly women, engaged in the  primary production
of coir fibre and coir yarn.  In terms of using all the different components of the scheme. Kerala ranks first  which
utilized about 83% of the assistance. However,Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh where the other major users
and  beneficiaries. The objectives of the scheme are yet to be achieved due to a multiplicity of the reasons. The institute
of Rural Management which  conducted and  evaluation of the scheme,  opined that the scheme of co-operativisation in
coir industry as it  envisaged and implemented need to go drastic revision to  meet the emerging challenges.  They have
recommended that the functioning of co-operative societies must be changed from the federal manner to the unitary
manner so that the production  processing and marketing of products can be undertaken effectively .
 
16.10.3            The Committee wanted to know as to  steps taken  to make the workers in the coir sector organized and
also  the various measures undertaken by the Central Government for formation of viable coir cooperatives to improve
the quality and quantity of production of  coir and coir products. The ministry of ARI stated that under the Centrally
sponsored scheme of Co-operativisation in coir industry, the Government had been extending assistance under various
components to coir workers. Coir co-operative societies have been set up in most of the coconut producing States. 
These societies are functioning under the overall supervision of State Governments concerned. The Co-operativisation
scheme aimed at formation of viable co-operative societies and revitalization of potentially viable/dormant societies and
bringing the workers and small scale manufacturers under the co-operative field so that they can be saved from the
clutches of middlemen and negotiate for better returns.
 
16.10.4            The Committee was further apprised that the Scheme had four components viz. Share Capital Assistance,
Managerial Subsidy, Equipment Assistance and Integrated Coir Development Project. The Institute of Rural
Management, Anand, Gujarat which had been authorized by the Ministry for conducting an evaluation study on the
implementation of the scheme, proposed certain changes in the implementation.  Since this is a Centrally Sponsored
Scheme and the co-operatives are functioning under the State Governments, the Board forwarded the report of the study
to the coir producing State Governments requesting to submit proposals on the basis of the recommendations of the
Institute of Rural Management.  Proposals from State Governments are still awaited.
 
16.10.5            The Committee finds that Co-operativisation Scheme of the coir Board is proving a non- starter.  The
Ministry has been releasing funds to the State Governments under the Integrated Coir Development Project, the
modernization component of the Co-operativisation Scheme,  but there has been  no response from the State
Governments under this scheme which is a pathetic situation. The committee is of the view that either the Government
should encourage the Co-operativisation Scheme in the coir sector with result- oriented approach or may think of
winding up of the scheme altogether.

16.11               Working of "Production Oriented Welfare Schemes for Coir Workers"
 
16.11.1            After careful observation of the evaluation report of  IRMA on Model Coir Village Programme, the
Ministry in consultation with the Internal Finance Wing  directed to prepare a new Comprehensive Scheme for welfare
of coir workers for operation during the 10
th Plan in place of Model Coir Village Scheme.   The Ministry suggested that
the new scheme should have a large coverage for the benefit of coir workers and their families engaged in coir industry
and should generate further employment opportunities. The production Enhancement Linked coir workers Welfare
Scheme  intends  to mitigate the hardship of coir workers and to ameliorate their living and working conditions through
provision of production Oriented Welfare Infrastructure
    
16.11.2            The benefits of Production Enhancement Linked welfare Infrastructure is to be provided mainly to
primary sector workers like traditional workers, coir yarn spinners and cottage level weavers who can bear 10% of the
cost of the project either by themselves or organizations who support them like SHGs, NGOs, Local bodies, Co-
operative  Societies, etc.    Coir Board will be the executive agency of the scheme and the NGOs, Local bodies SHGs,
Co.op. societies etc. who are selected by the Coir Board are the implementing agencies. The activities envisaged under
the development of production Enhancement linked welfare scheme will cover programmes linked to production of coir
and coir products like Provision of  Common Facility Centres  ;  Workshed at traditional coir fibre extraction site ;
drinking water and toilet at work site  Crèche  attached to work cite ; Construction of footbridge across the canal at
worksite critical for development of industry in the village ; Community hall/rest room. Such  provision for production
linked welfare infrastructure would  provide a healthy working environment, more number of working days, especially
during monsoon thereby enhancing employment opportunity and also the manual  white fiber extraction in unhygienic
and hazardous working condition will be eliminated which attract younger generation to the  industry. 
 
16.11.3            The Committee hopes that the successful  working of "Production Oriented Welfare Schemes for Coir
Workers" would motivate the workers and the coir production would increase in the years to come.
 
16.12.  Role of NGOs/voluntary organizations in making the Mahila Coir Yojana a success
 
16.12.1            The Mahila Coir Yojana is a scheme intended for providing self employment opportunities to the trained
women workers in the rural areas having power connection in their households. Under the scheme the trained women
workers are eligible to get 75% of the cost of motorised ratts purchased by them from the machinery manufacturers
registered with the Board. The Board proposes to popularise the motorised ratts in all the major coir producing centres
but it does not able to carry out the training activities in a  massive scale due to lack of trainees within the Board. To
over come this, the Board has involved NGOs/Voluntary organisations in organising field training units in all the coir
producing villages. The NGOs will provide worksheds  for organising the training, power required for operating the ratt
and raw material ie. coir fibre required for spinning. The Board will provide stipendiary benefits to the workers @
Rs.500/- per trainee per month and will also reimburse honorarium paid to the trainers @ Rs.3,000/- per month. The
NGOs and VOs have been providing very good service in making Mahila Coir Yojana as successful scheme.
 
16.12.2            The Committee desires that the role of NGOs/voluntary organizations may be encouraged to   make the
Mahila Coir Yojana a success.
 
16.13.  Suitable legislation at the National level for maximization for husk collection is required : Formation of a Hard
Fibre Board
 
16.13.1            Commenting  on the proposal of merger of Coconut Development Board with the Coir Board for better
co-ordination and Integrated planning for maximization of husk collection, the Ministry of ARI stated that The Coconut
Development Board is concentrated on production of coconut.  The Coir Board is concentrated on coir fibre and allied
products.  The raw-material for coir industry is the coconut husk, a byproduct of coconut industry.  The focus of the
above Boards are different.  The coir industry by and large provides employment to 5,00,000 people and it is an export
oriented industry and earn foreign exchange to the tune of Rs.500 Crore annually.
 
16.13.2            Technical know-how for coir industry is lacking in the non-traditional coconut growing areas.  It is

necessary to provide the requisite technical guidance through a professional body like Coir Board. The Coir Board as a
separate commodity Board could do only so much focusing employment generation in rural areas, development of new
products, skill development in non-traditional areas, transfer of technology, etc. The merger of Coir Board with Coconut
Development Board will lose its focus for exclusive development of coir industry and its positive results being
achieved.
 
16.13.3            There are different types of natural fibres like banana fibre, pineapple fibre, sisal, bimal, etc. available in

16.13.4            The Committee,  keeping in view the argument of the Ministry of ARI that the merger of Coir Board

17.1.1  Interest Subsidy (Khadi) is subsidy in lieu of interest that is provided to meet the difference between the cost of
interest charged by Government to KVIC on the khadi loan and 4 per cent which is the rate at which KVIC on-lends the
amount to registered khadi institutions.  This amount is a book transfer as it is adjusted against the khadi loan interest
dues of KVIC.
 
17.1.2  The Khadi and Village Industries Commission functions under the rural and small towns to provide employment
opportunities at low cost to local artisans by utilising the local raw materials. To support such traditional industries,
Government has been giving loans for Khadi and Village Industries to be set up in rural areas through the KVIC. The
KVIC in turn lent this money to Khadi and VI institutions for meeting their working capital fund requirements.  As the
Khadi institutions were to pay zero per cent interest on this loan till 2003 and 4 per cent thereafter, KVIC have hardly
been able to pay any interest or return the loan advanced by Government to KVIC. The amount of interest accrued to
KVIC on loan has been subsidized by the Government through payment of subsidy in lieu of interest over the years. 
Thus, this subsidy in lieu of interest is not actually released to the KVIC but is provided for in the budgetary provision
both Plan and Non-Plan for book adjustments, against the interest payable by KVIC to Government.  Therefore, the
budgetary provision for interest subsidy as a book adjustment is arising on account of the fact that the sanction of Khadi
and VI loan at different points of time over the years has borne varying rates of interest, varying between 11.8 per cent

collateral security is another constraint in raising bank finance by the khadi institutions.

23.2.1  The agro and rural industries, as well as other industries, in the country are facing greater market competition as

Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) for employment generation through the setting up of village
industries.
Rural Industries Service Centre (RISC) Scheme for setting up of common facility centres (CFCs) to provide
infrastructural support and services to such industries.
Product Development, Design Intervention and Packaging (PRODIP) Scheme for improved design and packaging of
village industry products.
Financial assistance to village industry units for participation in national and international level exhibitions for market

23.3                 ARI sector and  balanced regional growth and overall national economic growth and development
 
23.3.1  Rural industries provide the critical forum needed for a balanced regional growth due to its  unique resilient and
vibrant qualities as a sector which spans the Nation as a whole. With the sector being a combination of traditional skills
coupled with modern technology provides a very strong and potential weapon to combat the negative effects of
liberalization. It is an accepted fact that in our country, with regional imbalances as they exist, liberalization without the
presence of this sector would have made the existing imbalances extreme and a potent threat to the objectives of rural
industrialization and poverty alleviation. It is in this scenario that this independent Ministry, focusing totally on this
sector, formulating policies to withstand the effects of liberalization, assumes its critical role in shaping the national
economic scenario. The measures taken so far by the Ministry, as explained earlier, should effectively enable the
achievement of balanced regional growth.
 
23.3.2  The Committee is of the considered view that Government should undertake effective measures for Agro &
Rural Industries for striking  an equilibrium for balanced regional growth and overall national economic growth and
development.

23.4.1  Under the Khadi and Village industries sector so far approximately  80.16 lakh persons have been provided

persons. In coir sector more than 6 lakh coir workers are being gainfully employed .  The disguised employment in this
sector is primarily of the khadi artisans whereas in other avenues it is not so pronounced.  However, the Government has
not undertaken any survey so far in this regard. 
 
 23.4.2The Committee is of the view that the Government should undertake survey in Agro & Rural Industries to
ascertain whether there exist the case of disguised employment in the sector and furnish the committee  a  status note in
this regard.
 
23.5     Unemployment and poverty
 
23.5.1  Commenting   in the context of the Finance Minister's concern and efforts as expressed in General Budget 2005-
06 and 2006-07 to tackle the twin problem of unemployment and poverty with focused and co-ordinated approach
towards boosting the Agro-rural industries, the ministry of ARI has stated that   the Ministry of ARI is implementing
two major schemes, the REGP and the PMRY, throughout the country for generation of employment opportunities
through setting up of Tiny and Village industries in rural areas and through self-employment ventures. Although the
poverty alleviation is not the main objective of the schemes implemented by the Ministry, it certainly is a ‘ consequent’
to the programmes of the Ministry. Hence, schemes which basically aims at poverty - alleviation or other related facets
but which may result in employment generation are substantially different from the schemes implemented by the
Ministry of ARI and it is submitted that there is coordination at the Government of India level as well as at the level of

24.0     "New Deal" for rural India
 
24.1     The Prime Minister in his address to the Nation laid stress on overall growth of the national economy. 
Employment generation has been identified as a tool to banish poverty, hunger and homelessness and improve the
standard of living of the mass of our people.  The Prime Minister also focused on expanding opportunities for gainful
employment in agriculture and in non-farm rural activities. 
 
24.2     Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries through its various employment generation programme is already
focusing on setting up of industries in the rural areas in the non-farm sector.  The Rural Employment Generation
Programme (REGP) being implemented by this Ministry through KVIC since 1995 has successfully generated
employment opportunities to 31.38 lakh persons up to January 2006.  Similarly, under Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana
(PMRY) since 1993, 33.54 lakh educated persons have been provided employment up to January 2006.
 
24.3     Since the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries is functioning in the rural sector, the interaction between farmer
and agro industries has greatly picked up.  To improve interaction between farmers and agro industries, KVIC has taken
several measures to strengthen activities in the rural areas like bee-keeping, food processing industries, handmade paper
industries etc. These programmes along with other activities of the Ministry in the agro and rural sector will continue to
receive focus in the Ministry in line with the message enshrined in PM's speech with a view to empower disadvantaged
sections of the society.
 
24.4     The Committee believes that progress in regard to  "New Deal" for rural India in respect to rural development
and welfare and for achieving sustained overall annual growth of generating employment during the year 2004-05
would automatically lead to growth and development of ARI sector. The committee, therefore, recommends that the
Ministry of ARI should make an assessment of   the progress made in this regard and furnish a detailed  status note on
benefits accrued to the Agro rural industries out of  the "New Deal " for rural India stating  further the steps envisaged to
be undertaken to boost the growth of agro based industries and diversification and development agro processed
products.

banks. In order to overcome the problems faced in implementation of REGP, bankwise/districtwise targets must be

targets should be  fixed for conducting village/taluka level awareness camps, district level workshops, state/national
