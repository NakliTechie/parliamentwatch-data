REPORT 
1.1       Small Scale Industry (SSI) sector is one of the most vibrant and dynamic sectors of the Indian 
Economy in terms of low investment, high potential for employment generation and wider dispersal o f 
industries in rural and semi-urban areas preventing migra tion to big cities. This sector offers and ensures a
more equitable distribution of national income and faci litates effective mobilization of resources of capital 
and skill, which might otherwise remain unutilised. It further stimulates the growth of industrial 
entrepreneurship and promotes and diffuses the pattern  of ownership and location. Over 40% of the gross 
turnover in manufacturing activities as also around one-third of our exports are contributed by SSI sector. In 
the area of employment generation, significant contribution is made by SSI sector both modern and 
traditional units which account for employment of over 192.23 lakhs workers and production in rupee terms 
is projected to reach Rs. 7,42,021 crore during financial year (2002-03). 
1.2       The Small Scale Industries sector presents  a wide spectrum consisting of tiny, cottage and village 
industries on the one end and modern small scale industries on the other end. Units engaged in 
manufacturing, processing and preservation of goods with investment in plant and machinery not exceeding 
Rs. 1.00 crore (original cost) are considered SSI units.  Tiny units/enterprises are those whose investment in 
plant and machinery is upto Rs. 25.00 lakh irrespective of location of unit.
 
1.3       Industry related service and business ente rprises with investment upto Rs. 10 lakh in fixed assets 
excluding land and building are also considered for benefi ts of small scale sector. These type of units are 
known as Small Scale Service and Business Enterprises (SSSBEs) e.g. x-ray clinic photographic lab, 
teleprinter/fax stalls and auto repair etc. 
1.4       Indirect finance to Small Scale Industries sector viz. State Financial Corporation (SFC) and SIDC, 
handloom cooperatives, KVIC units, agencies involved in assisting supply of inputs and marketing o f 
outputs of artisans village and cottage industries, subs cription to bonds of SIDBI,  SFS, SIDC, NSIC etc. 
also come under SSI. 
1.5       With the establishment of WTO in 1995, th e SSI sector is beset with the challenges due to (i) 
removal of Quantitative Restriction (QR) on import; (ii)  gradual lowering of tariff; (iii) phasing out of non-
tariff barriers; (iv) withdrawal of preference in  Government purchase policy; and (v) patenting o f 
manufactured products. The removal of last phase of quantitative restriction from April, 2001 is expected to 
pose new challenges to SSI both in terms of meeting international quality standard and valuation. The 
gravity of the situation gets further aggravated by the fact that the items reserved for SSI within India can 
now be freely imported and this has rendered the con cept of reservation less relevant. Heavy inflow o f 
foreign goods pose a great threat in the changed environment for SSI. The issues which need to be 
addressed are innovation and technological upgradation, greater market reach of products adequate state o f 
art infrastructure to carry out activities with certainity  and continuity, access to right type of information, 
improving factor productivity, imbibing self discipline, adhering to time schedule and maintaining 
international quality standards, internationally accepted labour norms, intellectual property rights etc. 
1.6       If SSI has to compete on international scale, reasonable economies of scale and continuous  
investment in technology upgradation become a necessity, besides modification in definition of Small Scale 
Industrial units. However, post-WTO regime also offe rs opportunity for development as well as export o f 
quality goods. 
1.7       Liberalisation process started in 1991 br ought in its wake several changes and posed challenges 
before SSI sector, the important being gradual de-reservation of the items earlier reserved fo r 
production/processing by SSI sector, e.g. as of now 748 items are under reserved category and 64 items 
have been dereserved in recent past. Entry of MNCs and their subsidiary with state-of-art technology is 
another challenge with growing share of entry by MNCs. However, the Government has taken steps to

strengthen and promote SSI sector. The establishment of a separate Ministry of SSI on 14.10.1999 and the 
new policy named "Mission for the Millennium" is a definite roadmap aimed at creating new investment 
avenues strengthening credit delivery system. Thereby promoting modernization and technology 
upgradation, providing support to enhance internati onal competitiveness etc. The Union Budget in recent 
years also contained measures to promote SSI sector  and enhancement in eligibility criteria from Rs. 50 
lakh to Rs. 1 crore in 1999 now to Rs. 25 lakh, increas ing threshold limit from Rs. 2 crore in 1999 to Rs. 5 
crore for computation of working capital limits based on 20% of projected annual turnover, increasing 
project cost limit under the National Equity Fund Sc heme from Rs. 25 lakh to Rs. 50 lakh, granting Rs. 
75000 to each unit for obtaining ISO 9000 certification from 2002 till Tenth Plan. Introduction of Capital 
Subsidy Scheme with 12% backended capital subsidy for investment in technology upgradation in select 
sectors etc. are some of the pointer of the promotional/developmental measures.
 
1.8       The SSI sector has been receiving due a ttention from the Government as well as other extension 
agencies through various policies by way of financia l and fiscal incentives, infrastructural facilities, 
technology, marketing and other simila r support measures. For credit dispensation, SSI sector has been 
accorded status of "priority sector" by banks. Ho wever, with the onset of economic reforms and 
globalisation, a number of challenges and opportunitie s have emerged for the sector. While, on the one 
hand, the process of liberalisation has expanded the sc ope of cross border transactions, on the other, the 
new order has led to different perceptions about the protection and safeguards that the SSI sector has 
hitherto enjoyed. The changed environment has necessitated technology upgradation, quality 
standardization and strengthening of marketing facilities for various products, more particularly, the SSI 
products, to tap the expanded horizons.
 
1.9       During the course of examination of Demand for Grants 2003-04 pertaining to the Ministry of Small 
Scale Industries, the Department-related Parlia mentary Standing Committee on Industry took up the issue 
of credit flow to SSI sector in details. The Committee was informed that the SSI sector, producing a wide 
range of products, accounts for around 95 per cent of the industrial units in the country, contributing to 40 
per cent of the manufacturing sector output and appr oximately one-third of the Nation's exports. As on 
March, 2002, there were 34.42 lakh SSI units providi ng direct employment to around 192.33 lakh persons. 
As per the estimates of the office of the Development Commissioner (SSI), the output of SSI sector at 
current prices aggregated Rs. 6,90,316 crore during th e year 2001-02 and exports at the level of Rs. 69.797 
crore during the 2000-01. During the financial year 2002- 03, the production of SSI sector is projected to 
reach at Rs. 7,42,021 crore. The sector also acts as a nursery for promoting entrepreneurial talents and as a 
catalyst in the industrial growth.
 
1.10     The Ministry has apprised the Committee that the total advance to SSI from the Public Sector Banks 
has gone up in absolute terms from Rs. 48,445 crores in March, 2001 to Rs. 49,743 crores in March, 2002. 
The percentage of SSI advances to Net Bank Credit (NBC) however, has declined from 14.2% as at the end 
of March, 2001 to 12.5% at the end of March, 20 02. The RBI's study of the specialized SSI/general 
branches has revealed that adequate credit is being made available to all viable projects.
 
1.11     Considering the concerns over the trend of  declining share of SSI credit, the Planning Commission 
convened a meeting on 10th December, 2002 under the Chairmanship of its Deputy Chairman to discuss 
various issues relating to credit flow to SSI sector. As a follow up, the meeting of the Reserve Bank of India 
(RBI's) Standing Advisory Committee on Flow of Ins titutional Credit to SSI sector was held on 15th 
January, 2003. Based on the decisions taken in this meeting, the RBI has advised that the banks may set the 
interest rate on advances to SSI sector keeping in  view the prevailing general southward movement in 
interest rates. In order to further increase the flow  of credit, the Union Finance Minister has announced the 
following measures :- 
                (a)  The limit for composite loans has been increased from Rs. 10 lakhs to Rs. 25 lakhs.
 
                (b)  The limit under Credit Guarantee Sc heme for collateral security free loans has been raised to

NOIDA
 
3.4       Noida Small Industries Associations
 
3.4.1    The President of the Association informed that the SSI sector is heavily dependent upon external 
financing from the State level financial Institutions a nd the Commercial Banks. Most of the units in this 
sector are run by first generation entrepreneurs w ho are endowed with the know-how and entrepreneurial 
zeal, generally have limited financial resources. Most of the problems faced by the SSI Sector stem from 
this fact. The major problems faced by the SSI sector are briefly recounted below :
 
I.          At the start-up stage, the first problem ar ises from the approach of the lender, who is expected to 
have more financial expertise than the SSI borrower , and who should, therefore, follow a venture capital 
approach of : 
                 (i)  Correctly appraising the needs of the unit for both in term loans and working capital;
 
                (ii)  Keeping the documentation requirements simple and transparent, and to render assistance 
with the documentation as required; 
               (iii)  Ensuring timely disbursement so that the project implementation is not delayed; 
               (iv)  Monitoring the fund utilization process to ensure the implementation is on track and to 
recommend timely corrective action; 
                (v)  Being a mentor and guide to the entrepreneur in financial management, and ensuring that 
fund utilization is in order. 
            Instead of an enlightened approach, what  the SSI entrepreneur often finds is a rigid and mechanistic 
project appraisal system, which often fails to make realistic assessments of need based capital requirements, 
thereby sowing the seeds of sickness at the start-up stage itself. 
II.         Another hurdle faced by SSI units is that  fund disbursement has been made contingent on obtaining 
environmental clearance from the concerned Pollution Control Board. This process is sometimes very 
Page 9 of 25129th Report of Committee on Industry
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/indus/129threport.htm

protracted, with the result that project implementation ge ts impeded, and cost escalation affects the project 
viability. While meeting environmental concerns, it should be possible to simplify the process, and to 
remove this requirement in the case of non-polluting industries. 
III.       There is a tendency at the stage of pr oject appraisal to squeeze the sanctioned amount. Many SSI 
entrepreneurs, lacking financial and business experience, agree to terms which cause them to start project 
implementation and then find that resources are inadequate to implement the project completely. This 
creates a state of incipient sickness right from the initi al stages of the project, from which the average SSI 
entrepreneur finds it difficult to extricate himself.
 
IV.       There should be a preferential rate of intere st for the SSI sector as was the case earlier. This is 
essential to enable them to compete. It must be remembered that we are now operating in an increasingly 
liberalized and globalised economy, and as such the SSI sector needs to be globally competitive.
 
V.        There are procedural problems in post start-up dealings with the Banks. For example, many SSI
units find it very difficult to obtain Bank guarantees. Such guarantees are required for, inter alia : 
                (a)  Imports
 
                (b)  Central Excise 
                (c)  Trade Tax 
                (d)  Bid bonds for tenders 
                (e)  performance guarantees 
            The procedure for issue of Bank guarantees is very complex, and usually requires high-level 
sanction. The result is that (a) processing of the guarantee takes long time, which sometimes costs the unit 
heavily, for example in terms of not being able to participate in a tender, or through bearing heavy 
demurrage charges on imported consignments, etc., and (b ) heavy collateral security is demanded, which is 
in many cases beyond the capacity of the promoter to provide.
 
VI.       Many of the specialized SSI branches of Banks do not have an arrangement for foreign exchange 
dealings. In an increasingly globalised economy, this  is often a problem. While some of the Banks may 
have the problem of being understaffed for the pur pose, or the volume of Forex dealings may be 
uneconomical, the procedural delays in bill discounting and other transactions should be minimized.
 
VII.      Another problem arises when a unit is sa nctioned term loan and working capital loans by state level 
financial institutions and commercial banks respectively. In such cases, charge registration often becomes a 
problem. There should be a provision in the loan agreements entered into with the state level financial 
institutions for automatic creation of a charge with the bank providing working capital. 
3.5       Mahila Udyami Sangathan
 
3.5.1    The President of the Association informed about the problems being faced by SSI units as a whole 
in running their business. In view of the Liberalizati on Policy unleashed by the Government of India, the 
Small Scale Industries in the countr y have to face a very tough competition from the international market. 
The main points that make the working of SSI's difficult are mentioned below : 
Banking Sector
 
            1.  The Small Scale Industries who do most of their business against Government tender etc. have to 
provide Bank guarantees. The Banks are charging very heavy interest on issuing Ban k 
Guarantees, besides large sum of hard cash ranging between 25% to 40% are required to be 
deposited as Margin Money for issuing such guarantees. A Small Scale Industry can not afford to 
block its funds for these unproductive aspects of  business. These Bank Guarantees are issued 
mainly for the purpose of giving (i) Bid Security or  Earnest Money; (ii) Co ntract Security; (iii) 
Performance security, which is being issued for longer period involving blocking of cash to a

7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/indus/129threport.htm

large extent and for a longer period. 
            2.  Small Scale units, the name itself means small units where the funds are not available in 
abundance. The Banks have common approach in de aling with large scale and small scale units. 
Sanctioning of loans involve almost common for all the sectors. A Small Scale unit if applying 
for a business loan has to offer an equal collateral security for the loan amount sanctioned by the 
Bank. The country is already facing a serious problem of unemployment. The Government is 
encouraging self-employment i.e. opening of small scale units by the unemployed. But the Banks
are in no way helping to encourage self-employment by providing attractive schemes fo
r 
deserving projects in Small Scale Sector. A person who is educated, but unemployed obviously 
may not be having enough funds or securities to offer to the Bank. Therefore, the very purpose o
f

of the Banking Sector towards Small Scale Industry. 
            3.  The Loans sanctioned by the Banks to SSI units are renewed every year thus a lot of paper wor k 
and furnishing of documents has to be repeated. In a way the borrower has to undergo the same 
harassment every year. Therefore, such loans extended by the banks should be renewed/reviewed 
only after every 2 years.
 
            4.  The stamp duty is charged at very high rates whenever a loan is sanctioned or renewed. Besides

would be solved by a Rs. 100/- N.J. Stamp Paper. Therefore, the stamp paper duty needs to be
made uniform in every state.
 
Sales Tax
 
            Certain raw materials or machinery have to be im ported for better results in production, quality and 
cost reduction but in Noida, a heavy entry tax has to  be paid for import of machinery and critical raw 
materials. The Small Scale Industry has to be much more technically advanced and should be encouraged to 
take help of latest advanced plant and machinery best and cheap available raw materials to compete and 
survive the vicious competition of the day. The Entry Tax should be totally dropped as it adds to the cost, 
which makes the project and the product costlier as compared to big players, which is prevalent in UP only.
 
Central Excise
 
            The Central Excise Department though having some Concessional Tariffs for small scale units; yet 
there is scope for giving more concession to SSI for its  survival and revival. The Government of India has 
given facility for availing and utilizing Modvat Cred it which is accumulated on buying of raw materials 
after payment of Excise Duty. This Modvat Credit is  then utilized by the SSI manufacturer for removal o f 
goods at the time of sale from the unit. The Excise Department, however, insists that duty for removal o f 
goods be paid from PLA/cash. On this it should be left  to the SSI Units to see that it pays Excise duty fo r 
removal of goods from the available Modvat Credit at first and then by paying from PLA/Cash.

Their fate is decided by the behaviour of the bank manager who decides the amount and the rate of interest 
on the loan being granted to them. He also stated the small scale industries are suffering because of delayed 
payment and non-implementation of Delayed Payment Act, 1993. Has this act been properly implemented
this would have given a great boost to the development of small scale industry. As  far as question of credit 
flow most of the banks are not following the guidelines  of RBI regarding the collateral security exemption 
upto Rs. 25.00 lakhs. The representatives also informed that even large units were to close down in the face 
of cut throat competition from multi-national companies and cheap import from China, Korea etc., the state 
of SSI sector is even more serious. This has led to many units incurring heavy losses resulting in thei r 
closure and the consequent loss of employment. Th e enforcement of pollution control laws had furthe r 
aggravated the situation. The small scale sector, which needs some immediate relief to counter various 
threats and to restructure itself. It has been stated that many innovative projects remain only on pape r 
because banks insist on collateral securities. This needs to be relaxed. While the organized sector units have 
options available viz. raising consortium loans, rehabilitation by BIFR etc. Such facilities are not available 
for the SSI units. In case the technology needs upgradation or the small entrepreneur wishes change in his 
line of activity, which is finance viable, then the ba nks should not hesitate to a further exposure of 40-50% 
over and above the existing credit.

RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
5.1       The Committee is of the opinion that the SSIs should be encouraged to have deposits from relatives 
and friends and such investors should be given tax ex emption under section 80 etc. of I.T. Act to enable 
them to have their own financial base. For studying viability of SSI, independent agencies should be 
authorized. Credit rating by an independent agency s hould also be considered. Finance should be available 
at international rates of interest from 7 to 9% to strengthen the competitive strength of SSI's present rates 
which vary from 13 to 16%. The Committee also recommends that 20% of the banks financing ability 
should be utilized for SSI's as per the Government of India's definition i.e. over Rs. 2 lakhs capital 
investment. Delayed Payment Act should be given teeth to control and punish organizations holding and 
delaying payments to SSI. Under the present act  the facilitation committee should be a sub-committee 
nominated by Divisional Udyog Bandhu having full powers to  arbitrate under the provisions of arbitration 
act.
