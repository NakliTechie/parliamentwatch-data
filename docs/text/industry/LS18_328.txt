CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) is a credit-linked 
subsidy scheme launched in August 2008 by merging the Prime Minister’s Rojgar Yojana (PMRY) 
and Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP).  
1.2 Legal and Policy Framework 
1.2.1 PMEGP operates under guidelines issued by the Ministry of MSME. The scheme aligns with 
national policies aimed at promoting entrepreneurship, reducing unemployment, and fostering 
inclusive economic growth. 
1.3 The Parliamentary Standing Committee, as per its mandate, aimed to assess the effectiveness 
of the PMEGP scheme in fostering growth in the MSME sector. As part of the process, the 
Committee- 
i. Reviewed background data and annual reports of the ministry, its official website to identify 
challenges or areas for improvement 
ii. Interacted with officials from the Ministry, key stakeholders like KVIC, public sector banks, 
and beneficiaries 
iii. Circulated detailed questions. 
iv. Conducted study visits to Udaipur (Rajasthan), Mumbai (Maharashtra), Goa, 
Vishakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh), Kolkata (West Bengal), Srinagar (Jammu &Kashmir), 
Varanasi (Uttar Pradesh), Kochi (Kerala), Chennai (Tamil Nadu), & Chandigarh (Haryana- 
Punjab) ,and 
v. Field visits to identify challenges and areas for improvement. 
vi. Desktop research on Global Data related to MSMEs Sector. 
1.4 Objectives of the Scheme:- 
 
Generate employment opportunities in rural as well as urban areas of the country through setting up of new self-employment ventures/projects/micro enterprisesBring together widely dispersed traditional artisans/ rural and urban unemployed youth and give them self-employment opportunities to the extent possible, at their place;Provide continuous andsustainable employmentto traditional andprospective artisans, aswell as rural and urbanunemployed youth. Thiswill help arrestmigration of rural youthto urban areasIncrease the wage-earning capacity of artisans and contribute to an increase in the growth rate of rural and urban employment.

1.5.1 The Union Budget 2025-26 emphasises strengthening the MSME sector, recognizing it as the 
second engine of India’s economic growth alongside agriculture, investment, and exports. Notably, it 
revises the MSME classification by increasing the investment and turnover limits:

1.5.2 The Committee observes that the revised MSME classification under the Union Budget 
2025-26 has modified the Investment and Turnover limits for MSMEs. The new revised investment 
and turnover limits could potentially undermine the purpose of MSMEs by elevating micro-
enterprises beyond their original scope and further noting the increase in margin money 
disbursement, the number of assisted units has declined due to higher per-unit costs.  
1.5.3 The Committee, in its 327th Demand for Grant Report (2025-26) for the Ministry of 
MSME, has recommended the creation of a "nano" category within the MSME framework, 
with a financial limit of Rs. 10 lakh. The Committee recommends the Ministry to provide 
additional benefits and focused support under the PEMGP Scheme. This initiative aims to 
enhance access to credit and subsidies for micro-entrepreneurs, rural artisans, and street 
vendors, fostering financial inclusion in rural and semi-urban areas.

2.4 Eligibility Criteria:- 
2.4.1 For PMEGP new enterprises (Units):- 
i. Any individual, above 18 years of age 
ii. There will be no income ceiling for assistance. 
iii. Beneficiaries must have at least VIII standard education for projects costing over 
Rs.10lakh (Manufacturing) or Rs.5 lakh (Business/Service). Assistance under the 
scheme is available only for new projects. 
iv. Self Help Groups (SHGs) : including those belonging to BPL if they have not availed 
benefits under any other scheme, Institutions registered under the Societies 
Registration Act,1860, 
v. Production Co-operative Societies registered under relevant cooperative acts and 
Charitable Trusts. 
vi. There will be no income ceiling to assist in setting up projects under PMEGP. 
vii. Existing units or those with prior government (Central or State) subsidy schemes are 
ineligible. 
 
Note:-Only one person from one family is eligible to obtain financial assistance for 
setting up projects under PMEGP. The 'family’ includes self and spouse. 
2.4.2 For up-gradation of existing PMEGP units: 
i. Margin Money (subsidy) claimed under PMEGP must be successfully adjusted after 
the completion of the 3-year lock-in period. 
ii. The First loan under PMEGP/REGP/MUDRA must be repaid within the stipulated 
time. 
iii. The unit is profit-making, and the potential for further growth with 
modernization/upgrading the technology.

2.7 The Committee acknowledges PMEGP as a skill-based program preserving traditional 
artisanship and it recommends the ministry that it must remove the VIII standard education 
requirement under PMEGP, recognizing that many artisans acquire skills through family 
traditions and mentorship rather than formal education.  
2.8 The Committee recommends the Ministry/KVIC to accept the application forms both 
online and offline in all 22 scheduled languages and further expanding awareness through 
multilingual training, particularly in rural and semi-urban areas. It suggests creating and 
distributing concise video clips in regional languages to promote PMEGP in remote areas.  
2.9 The Committee recommends that the Ministry must set up a single-window clearance 
system to facilitate faster approvals and reduce procedural delays, improving the overall 
effectiveness of the scheme. 
2.10 The Committee observes that delays and lack of real-time communication in the 
PMEGP application process leave applicants unaware of their status of application. It 
recommends that the Ministry must implement a mobile-based notification system to provide 
real-time updates on PMEGP applications in that single window clearance system. This system 
must inform applicants at each stage, notify them of any delays, apprise about the reasons of 
rejection of their applications both by banks and nodal agencies, and offer updates via SMS 
and additionally a mobile app to give feedback mechanism.

CHAPTER 4 
 DEVELOPMENTS–INNOVATIONS & MODIFICATIONS 
4.1 Various changes and modifications have been incorporated, and activities are being carried 
out for the effective implementation of the PMEGP Scheme: - 
i. COVID years exempted for second loan eligibility under PMEGP. 
Expanded list of permitted activities. 
ii. Implementing Agencies can process applications in all areas (rural/urban). 
iii. Weekly webinars for youth and entrepreneurs. 
iv. The list of activities permitted under the scheme has been expanded. 
v. 1.3lakh units geo-tagged for verification and real-time data. 
vi. Technical, Marketing, and retired bank officials have been hired at state offices to support 
PMEGP beneficiaries. Additionally, 1,056 model Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) for 
various industries are available on the PMEGP portal. 
vii. KVIC introduced a call centre linked to the EDP portal. 
viii. Awareness camps, workshops, and exhibitions held to promote PMEGP. 
ix. Rejection rate dropped by 10% from 2020 to 2023. 
x. Bank sanction time improved, with 50% of applications approved within 30 days in FY 
2022-23 from 28% in FY2019-20. 
xi. Development of a new PMEGP Portal for integration with banks, training partners, and 
agencies for physical verification, credit rating, GST, Udyam, etc. 
xii. An ongoing evaluation study with 30,000 sample PMEGP units will assess the scheme’s 
impact, credit adequacy, and challenges, with interim findings by December2024. 
xiii. India Post was engaged in conducting physical verification of PMEGP units. 
xiv. Applicants from aspirational districts and transgenders are included in Special Category for 
higher subsidy. 
xv. Modified Negative List to allow more activities under Animal Husbandry. 
xvi. All Implementing Agencies can receive and process applications in rural and urban areas. 
xvii. New PMEGP units must be registered under the Udyam Portal before physical verification. 
xviii. District Level Task Force Committee has been discontinued. 
 
4.2 The Committee observes that the current loan application process under PMEGP involves 
extensive scrutiny, leading to delays, especially for small-value loans. The Committee 
recommends Ministry must implement a self-certification mechanism for loan applications up 
to loan amount of Rs 10 Lakh to streamline the process and reduce delays. Applicants would 
submit a self-certified declaration of compliance with eligibility and documentation, with 
random sample testing conducted periodically for transparency.  
4.3 The Committee appreciates the Ministry's involvement of technical/marketing experts and 
ex-bankers in guiding applicants. It recommends establishing a dedicated help desk to provide 
real-time assistance and offer continuous hand-holding support throughout the application 
process till the final loan disbursement.  

11 
 
4.4 To improve outreach, the Committee urges the Ministry and banks to enhance publicity 
through media, workshops, awareness camps, and social media, with a special focus on youth 
and women artisans for employment generation. Additionally, the Ministry is encouraged to 
collaborate with RBI and Rural Self-Employment Training Institutes (RSETI) to educate 
entrepreneurs and strengthen PMEGP promotion. 
4.5 The Committee appreciates the inclusion of aspirational districts and transgender 
applicants. However, it emphasizes the importance of upskilling, reskilling and unlearning. It 
recommends developing training modules on digital literacy, financial management, and 
entrepreneurship in collaboration with the Ministry of Skill Development and 
Entrepreneurship.  
4.6 The Committee further recommends monitoring the impact of increased project costs and 
relaxing the negative list for animal husbandry activities. The Ministry should implement a 
quarterly review system to assess the uptake of these changes and ensure that they effectively 
address entrepreneurs' capital needs. 
4.7 The Committee notes the discontinuation of the District Level Task Force Committees 
(DLFTC) due to the online portal. It recommends that state and district-level bodies continue 
monitoring to maximize PMEGP’s impact. These bodies should address applicant issues, 
improve processes, and encourage local participation. They should conduct regular reviews, 
guide applicants, and track progress. A feedback loop with central offices should inform policy 
adjustments and improve the scheme’s effectiveness. 
4.8 Steps taken by Banks to promote the PMEGP scheme in rural and semi-urban areas and 
reduce application rejections include: 
i. Time to time publicity via Local Newspapers, Print and Electronic Media Broadcasting, 
banner display at post offices, publicity via social media; 
ii. KVIC has initiated a two-day free online Entrepreneurship Development Programme 
(EDP) for prospective entrepreneurs. 
iii. KVIC facilitates marketing support, organizes exhibitions where the institutions and 
entrepreneurs can sell and display their products. 
iv. KVIC has started online selling all its products through www.ekhadiindia.com and 
www.khadiindia.gov.in 
v. Short film of successful entrepreneurs has been processed and showcased during 
workshops, awareness camps, etc. 
vi. Radio jingles in regional languages have been made to create awareness of the scheme; 
and 
vii. Targeted publicity of PMEGP through rural post offices in identified districts with fewer 
applications. 
 

12 
 
4.9 Steps taken by the Department of Financial Services (DFS) 
4.10 To regulate PMEGP implementation by banks include: 
i. Banks organize credit camps and outreach programs to raise awareness about government 
schemes. 
ii. Specialized Loan Factories and MSE branches cater exclusively to MSE customers for 
handholding. 
iii. The RBI conducts a special capacity-building program called NAMCABS to improve 
bankers' attitudes towards MSE entrepreneurs. 
iv. NAMCABS familiarizes bankers with credit-related issues in the MSE sector and 
enhances their entrepreneurial sensitivity. 
Further, Annexure VI gives the measures taken by RBI to improve the flow of credit to the MSME 
sector. 
4.11. To address problems faced by MSEs as given under: 
(i) DFS vide letter dated December14,2022, instructed IBA and SLBC Conveners to 
reduce high rejection rates, especially due to service area, document submission 
deadlines, and target achievement. They were asked to monitor PMEGP progress in 
SLBC meetings. 
(ii) DFS, in a letter dated May 23, 2022, directed banks to clearly specify rejection 
reasons and set time limits for decisions. They emphasized informing women and 
youth about PMEGP benefits and providing helpdesks in local languages. 
(iii) DFS requested that IBA address the high rejection rates of proposals from micro-
enterprises and raise the issue with banks. 
(iv) DFS, in a letter dated November 03, 2023,asked IBA to create a draft checklist for 
screening PMEGP applications at the KVIC level, aiming to reduce rejections and 
save time and resources for banks and applicants. 
4.12 The Committee noted that banks charge interest rates ranging from 9.5% to 12.5% on 
PMEGP loans, while the guidelines suggest a normal interest rate and a repayment schedule of 3 to 7 
years. Furthermore, during its interaction with entrepreneurs with PMEGP loans, the Committee 
came across several beneficiaries who were required to make collateral bonds for loans of even less 
than Rs. 10 lakhs.  
4.13 The Committee notes that high interest rates and inconsistencies across banks are 
challenging for PMEGP loan applicants, particularly given their lack of regular income. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry, in collaboration with the RBI, must establish a 
uniform and streamlined interest/margin rate for MSMEs across all banks. Furthermore, the 
Ministry should focus on increasing allocations and enhancing outreach efforts to ensure the 
scheme reaches its full potential. 

13 
 
4.14 The Committee observes that while PMEGP includes capacity-building measures such as 
Entrepreneurship Development Programmes (EDPs) to equip beneficiaries with the necessary skills 
and knowledge to establish and manage their businesses, there is a need to improve the quality of 
training, extend outreach in rural areas, and provide sector-specific skills to align with evolving 
market demands. 
4.15 The Committee recommends that the Ministry must strengthen capacity-building 
initiatives by updating training modules, increasing collaboration with industry experts, and 
expanding outreach in rural areas. It also suggests introducing digital skills programmes and 
providing continuous post-training support to enhance project sustainability and 
entrepreneurial success. 
4.16 The Committee further observes that Artificial Intelligence (AI) can significantly improve 
the efficiency and accessibility of the scheme. AI-driven analytics could facilitate real-time project 
monitoring, identify emerging business trends, and support evidence-based policy decisions. 
However, the Committee notes that inadequate digital infrastructure and limited technological 
awareness, particularly in rural and semi-urban areas, may hinder the effective implementation of AI 
solutions. 
4.17 In view of the impact of AI in the Scheme, the Committee recommends that the 
Ministry integrate AI technology across PMEGP operations and portal to improve service 
delivery and promote financial inclusion. This includes adopting AI tools for faster application 
processing, automated project monitoring feedback, address the gaps continuously and 
predictive analytics to identify sector-specific opportunities. Additionally, the Committee 
advises the Ministry to collaborate with technology experts and research institutions to develop 
AI solutions tailored for MSMEs.

ON-GROUND ASSESSMENT AND COLLABORATIVE INSIGHTS 
6.1 The  top sectors under PMEGP are Weaving, Pottery, Embroidery, Tailoring and preparation 
of Readymade Garments, Shoe-making, Bee- keeping, Manufacture of Silk Sarees, Cement 
Blocking, Brick Bhatta, Video & Photo studio, Public Transport Service / Rural Transport Service, 
Xerox Centre, Mandap Decoration, Handicraft, Handloom, Carpet, Horticulture, Wool, Sericulture, 
Silk Industry, Tourism, Food Processing, Information Technology, Agriculture & Allied Agriculture 
activities and Food Industry. 
6.2 Steps taken by Banks to improve the PMEGP loan process: 
i. Many Banks dedicate one day a week to focus on Government-sponsored schemes, including 
PMEGP and Mudra Loans. 
ii. Banks have a system where rejections are reviewed by a higher authority or committee, 
separating sanctioning and rejection powers. 
iii. A dedicated desk is set up at the central office or branches to monitor the progress of 
PMEGP applications. 
iv. Nodal officers are appointed at zonal offices to follow up and monitor application progress. 
v. Banks conduct periodic reviews with field staff to ensure adherence to Turn Around Time 
(TAT) and reduce application pendency. 
vi. Zone-wise/Region-wise performance and pendency are shared with field functionaries to 
expedite sanctions. 
vii. In-house training programs are held for field functionaries to promote the PMEGP scheme. 
6.3 Initiatives by respective State Governments- 
1. Strengthening industrial mechanisms by setting up MSE facilitation centers at the district 
level to help entrepreneurs with statutory compliances. 
2. Providing marketing support through organizing fairs, melas, and expos at the state and 
district levels. 
3. Running special drives to encourage MSEs in the unorganized sector to register under 
Udyam. 
4. Educating customers about the PMEGP Scheme during financial literacy camps organized by 
Banks. 
5. Offering inputs about the scheme in meetings conducted by SHGs and the State 
Governments. 
6. Conducting various campaigns and outreach programs in collaboration with NGOs to 
promote Government schemes, including PMEGP. 
7. Holding town hall meetings in coordination with RBI, Rural Self Employment Training 
Institutes (RSETI), etc. 
8. Some Banks have established social banking verticals at corporate offices to closely monitor 
the progress of Government-sponsored schemes, including PMEGP.

6.4 The Committee notes that while margin money disbursement has increased, the

CHAPTER 7 
 CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, the Committee recognises that the PMEGP scheme, designed to promote 
entrepreneurship and employment generation among MSMEs, has demonstrated its capacity to drive 
economic growth and empower a diverse range of beneficiaries across the country. By providing 
financial support and fostering a conducive environment for innovation, the scheme has successfully 
reached multiple sectors and regions, thereby contributing to the broader development agenda of the 
Ministry of MSME. 
7.1 At the same time, the Committee’s in-depth analysis has revealed persistent challenges 
related to policy structures, administrative bottlenecks, and gaps in outreach, particularly in rural 
areas. The integrated recommendations underscore the need for digital enhancements, streamlined 
processes, and better synergy with other government initiatives. Addressing these issues will not 
only reinforce the strengths of PMEGP but also pave the way for a more inclusive and sustainable 
economic future.

 RECOMMENDATIONS AT GLANCE 
1. The Committee, in its 327th Demand for Grant Report (2025-26) for the Ministry of 
MSME, has recommended the creation of a "nano" category within the MSME framework, 
with a financial limit of Rs. 10 lakh. The Committee recommends the Ministry to provide 
additional benefits and focused support under the PEMGP Scheme. This initiative aims to 
enhance access to credit and subsidies for micro-entrepreneurs, rural artisans, and street 
vendors, fostering financial inclusion in rural and semi-urban areas. 
(para-1.5.3) 
2. The Committee acknowledges PMEGP as a skill-based program preserving traditional 
artisanship and It recommends the ministry that it must remove the VIII standard education 
requirement under PMEGP, recognizing that many artisans acquire skills through family 
traditions and mentorship rather than formal education.  
(para-2.7) 
3. The Committee recommends the Ministry/KVIC to accept the application forms both 
online and offline in all 22 scheduled languages and further expanding awareness through 
multilingual training, particularly in rural and semi-urban areas. It suggests creating and 
distributing concise video clips in regional languages to promote PMEGP in remote areas.  
(para-2.8) 
4. The Committee recommends that the Ministry must set up a single-window clearance 
system to facilitate faster approvals and reduce procedural delays, improving the overall 
effectiveness of the scheme. 
(para-2.9) 
5. The Committee observes that delays and lack of real-time communication in the 
PMEGP application process leave applicants unaware of their status of application. It 
recommends that the Ministry must implement a mobile-based notification system to provide 
real-time updates on PMEGP applications in that single window clearance system. This system 
must inform applicants at each stage, notify them of any delays, apprise about the reasons of 
rejection of their applications both by banks and nodal agencies, and offer updates via SMS 
and additionally a mobile app to give feedback mechanism.  
(para-2.10) 
6. The Committee recommends that instead of the existing input based EDP training the 
Ministry must implement an output-based training and eligibility system under the PMEGP 
scheme. The system is to link the approval of loans or subsidies to measurable outcomes, such 

23 
 
as business readiness, skill proficiency, and a viable business plan. This approach would 
enhance accountability, support capable applicants, and improve the scheme’s effectiveness.  
(para-2.11) 
7.  The Committee commends the Ministry for exceeding PMEGP targets since FY2021-
22 and extending the scheme until 2025-26 with increased funding. However, it reiterates that 
the scheme’s success should be measured by employment generated, not by Margin Money 
utilized (as stated in the Committee's 251st Report). The Committee recommends to proper 
fund usage and job creation, expecting the increased budget to increase youth employment 
opportunities. 
(para-3.7) 
8. The Committee appreciates the Ministry's efforts to fully utilize the PMEGP budget 
and seek additional funds. It recommends that the Ministry and DFS focus on generating self-
employment through micro-enterprises. The Committee urges IAs and banks to adopt the best 
practices and suggestions from the meetings. It also suggests holding conferences and seminars 
to share experiences and best practices, helping achieve targets. 
(para-3.8) 
9. The Committee observes that the current loan application process under PMEGP 
involves extensive scrutiny, leading to delays, especially for small-value loans. The Committee 
recommends Ministry must implement a self-certification mechanism for loan applications up 
to loan amount of Rs 10 Lakh to streamline the process and reduce delays. Applicants would 
submit a self-certified declaration of compliance with eligibility and documentation, with 
random sample testing conducted periodically for transparency.  
(para-4.2)

(para-4.3)

through media, workshops, awareness camps, and social media, with a special focus on youth

entrepreneurs and strengthen PMEGP promotion.

ANNEXURE VI 
MEASURES TAKEN BY RBI FOR IMPROVING FLOW OF CREDIT TO MSME SECTOR 
i). Priority Sector Lending Guidelines: In terms of Master Direction on ‘Priority Sector 
Lending (PSL) – Targets and Classification’ dated September 4, 2020, all bank loans to 
MSMEs conforming to the conditions prescribed therein qualify for classification under 
priority sector lending. A target of 7.5 percent of ANBC or Credit Equivalent Amount of 
Off- Balance Sheet Exposure, whichever is higher, has been prescribed for SCBs for lending 
to Micro Enterprises. Further, loans sanctioned by banks to NBFC-MFIs and other MFIs 
(Societies, trusts, etc.) which are members of RBI recognized SRO for the sector for on-
lending to MSME sector, loans to registered NBFCs (other than MFIs) for on-lending to 
Micro & Small Enterprises and bank finance to start-ups up to ₹50 crore, as per definition of 
Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Govt. of India will be considered part of priority sector 
lending. 
ii). Collateral requirements of MSME units: SCBs have been mandated not to accept 
collateral security in the case of loans up to ₹10 lakh extended to units in the MSE sector. 
Banks have also been advised to strongly encourage their branch level functionaries to avail 
themselves of the Credit Guarantee cover, including making performance in this regard a 
criterion in the evaluation of their field staff.  
iii). Working Capital Computation: Computation of working capital requirements of MSE 
units to be done by banks on the basis of simplified method of minimum 20% of the 
projected annual turnover of the unit for borrowal limits up to ₹5 crore. 
iv). Streamlining flow of credit to Micro and Small Enterprises for facilitating timely and 
adequate credit flow during their ‘Life Cycle’: In terms of our circular FIDD.MSME & 
NFS.BC.No.60/06.02.31/2015-16 dated August 27, 2015 on ‘Streamlining flow of credit to 
Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) for facilitating timely and adequate credit flow during 
their ‘Life Cycle’, banks have been advised to tune their policies for lending to MSME sector 
by incorporating therein the provisions for sanctioning of, inter alia, Additional Working 
Capital Limits, Mid Term Review of Regular Working Capital Limits, Timelines for Credit 
Decisions, so as to facilitate timely and adequate availability of credit to viable MSE 
borrowers especially during the need of funds in unforeseen circumstances. 
v). Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS):In order to solve the problem of delayed 
payment to MSMEs, RBI has issued guidelines for setting up and operating the TReDS.  The 
scheme facilitates the financing of trade receivables of MSMEs from corporate and other 
buyers, including government departments and public sector undertakings (PSUs) through 
multiple financiers electronically. Three entities viz. Receivables Exchange of India Limited 
(RXIL), Mynd Solutions Private Limited and A. TREDS have started operating TReDS. 
vi). Priority Sector Lending status for Factoring Transactions: In terms of our circular 
FIDD.CO.Plan.BC.10/04.09.01/2016-17 dated August 11, 2016 ‘with recourse’ factoring 
transactions by banks which carry out the business of factoring departmentally wherever the 
‘assignor’ is a Micro, Small or Medium Enterprise are eligible for priority sector 

37 
 
classification under MSME category.  The factoring transactions taking place through 
TReDS are also eligible for classification under priority sector. 
vii). Interest rates on loans to MSMEs: With a view to improve monetary policy transmission, 
banks have been advised to link loans to Micro and Small Enterprises to an external 
benchmark from October 01, 2019 in terms of circular DBR.DIR.BC.No.14/13.03.00/2019-
20 dated September 04, 2019.To further improve the transmission of monetary policy rates, 
the Reserve Bank vide circular dated February 26, 2020, stipulated that w.e.f April 01, 2020 
loans to Medium Enterprises shall also be linked to external benchmark. The reset clause for 
loans has also been reduced to three months under the external benchmark system. Further, to 
make the benefit of the external benchmark-based interest regime available to the existing 
borrowers, banks have been advised to provide a switchover option as per mutually agreed 
terms. 
viii). Regulatory Retail Portfolio: The Regulatory Retail Portfolio threshold to a single 
counterparty was increased from ₹5 crore to ₹7.5 crore in respect of all fresh as well as 
incremental qualifying exposures vide circular DOR.No.BP.BC.23/21.06.201/2020-21 dated 
October 12, 2020. Accordingly, banks may assign a lower risk weight of 75% to exposure to 
MSME entities with counterparty exposure up to ₹7.50 crore as against ₹5 crore earlier. 
ix). Co-Lending by Banks and NBFCs to Priority Sector: The “Co-Lending Model” (CLM) 
under ‘Priority Sector Lending’ envisages to better leverage respective comparative 
advantages of the banks and NBFCs in a collaborative effort and to improve the flow of 
credit to the unserved and underserved sectors of the economy (including the MSME 
Enterprises). The scheme is applicable to all registered NBFCs (including Housing Finance 
Companies). In terms of the CLM, banks are permitted to co-lend with all registered NBFCs 
(including HFCs) based on a prior agreement. The co-lending banks will take their share of 
the individual loans on a back-to-back basis in their books. However, NBFCs shall be 
required to retain a minimum of 20 per cent share of the individual loans on their books. 
x). Lending by Small Finance Banks (SFBs) to NBFC-MFIs, for the purpose of on-lending 
to priority sectors: With effect from May 5, 2021, fresh credit extended by SFBs to 
registered NBFC-MFIs and other MFIs (Societies, Trusts etc.) which are members of RBI 
recognized ‘Self-Regulatory Organization’ of the sector, and which have a ‘gross loan 
portfolio’ of up to ₹500 crore as on March 31 of the previous financial year, for the purpose 
of on-lending to individuals, is permitted to be classified as Priority Sector Lending. To 
ensure continuation of the synergies that have been developed between banks and NBFCs in 
delivering credit to the specified priority sectors, it has been decided to allow the above 
facility on an ongoing basis. 
xi). Regulatory Sandbox (RS): The Reserve Bank had opened the Third Cohort under RS for 
MSME Lending and eight entities were shortlisted for the Test Phase. Products of the entities 
which completed the ‘Test Phase’ were evaluated and five products have been found viable. 
This cohort is expected to spur innovations that can fill the lending gap for MSMEs through 
the use of technology and data analytics. 
xii). Account Aggregator (AA) Framework: As a regulator, the Reserve Bank has created an 
enabling environment for growth of digital technology for new financial products and 

38 
 
services. RBI has facilitated the AA framework under which information related to financial 
assets of a customer is collected from the holders of such information (Financial Information 
Providers) and is presented to the customers or specified users (Financial Information Users). 
Further, it has been decided to include GSTN on board the AA ecosystem as an FIP for 
MSME lending. The approach is to make sharing and aggregation of financial data possible 
in a secure, transparent, and efficient manner by setting up an intermediary which will also 
be responsible for the customers' consent management.
