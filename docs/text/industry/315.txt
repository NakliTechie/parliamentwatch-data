REPORT 
 
 India‟s Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Sector has emerged as a 
vibrant, dynamic and progressive sector of the Indian economy over the last five decades. 
MSME Sector not  only play crucial role in providing large -scale employment 
opportunities at comparatively lower capital cost than large industries but also help 
industrialization of rural and backward areas of the country, thereby, reducing regional 
imbalances, assuring more equitable distribution of national income and resources.  
MSME Sector has  strong linkages to large indu stries as ancillary units and contributes 
significantly in the inclusive industrial development of the country . MSME Sector is also 
nurturing a culture of entrepreneurship among the people of the country, especially the 
youth thereby mitigating the problem of unemployment effectively. 
 
2. MSMEs are widely spread across the country in almost all the sectors. MSMEs are 
now widening their domain across various sectors of the economy, producing diverse 
range of products and services to meet demands of domestic as well as Global markets 
and also proving to be a boon for aspiring entrepreneurs in India.  As informed by the       
M/o of MSME, the MSME Sector is providing livelihood to around 11.10 crore people of 
the country. There are a round 6.34 crore MSMEs , which contributed 27 per cent to the 
Gross Domestic Product in the year Financial Year 2020 -21.The MSME Sector has the 
huge potential to generate employment and livelihood in the rural hinterlands  of the 
country. It also has the capacity to earn foreign exchange for the exchequer through larger 
exports. 
 
3. COVID-19 pandemic had an enormous adverse impact on almost all sectors of the 
Indian economy including the MSME Sector, due to factors such as lack of funds, supply 
chain disruptions, lower productivity, non -availability of workforce etc. However, t he 
Government has adopted a multi -pronged approach covering the six pillars such as (i) 
New Enterprise Development and Employment Generation (ii) Credit Assistance and 
Capital Infusion (iii) Technological U pgradation (iv) Infrastructure & Capacity Building 
(v) Skill Development & Entrepreneurship Development T raining and (vi) Procurement 
and Marketing Support, in order to help the MSMEs to revive and regain their               
pre-pandemic growth trajectory as well as improve their economic growth. 

2 
 
Ministry of MSME  
 
4. The Ministry of MSME was formed on 9 th May, 2007 merging the erstwhile 
Ministry of Small Scale Industries and the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries.  The 
Ministry designs policies, promotes/facilitates programmes/projects/schemes and 
monitors their implementation, with a view to assisting MSMEs and helping them to scale 
up.  
 
The major Schemes of the Ministry of MSME are mentioned as under:- 
 Prime Minister‟s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP); 
 Modified Market Development Assistance (MMDA); 
 Interest Subsidy and Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme; 
 Strengthening of Infrastructure of Existing Weak Khadi Institutions and Assistance 
for Marketing Infrastructure; 
 Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE); 
 A Scheme for Promoting Innovation and Rural Entrepreneurship (ASPIRE); 
 Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI); 
 Micro and Small Enterprises–Cluster Development Programme (MSE-CDP); 
 Science & Technology (S&T); 
 Honey Mission;  
 Kumbhar Shashaktikaran Programme; 
 The National Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe Hub;  
 Scheme for Promotion of MSMEs in NE Region and Sikkim; and 
 Guaranteed Emergency Credit Line (GECL). 
 
New Definition of MSMEs 
 
5. The Committee was informed that the revision in the definition of MSMEs  which 
was brought in w.e.f. 1stJuly, 2020 as part of the AtmaNirbhar Bharat package introduced 
a composite  criteria of investment and annual turnover and identical limits for 
manufacturing and services sector ( table below). The resulting economies of scale can 
enhance productivity without the MSMEs losing out on several Government incentives 
including market support, export promotion, preferential procurement in the public sector 
and incentives through the Micro Small  Enterprises-Cluster Development Programme 
(MSE-CDP), Prime Minister Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) and Scheme 
of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) and enabling of IT 

3 
 
ecosystems. This enabling environment will promote compet ition among MSMEs .The 
new definition of MSMEs is as under:-

6. The Committee understands that the modified definition of MSMEs will 
facilitate expansion and growth of MSME enterprises , increasing the investment 
limit under the new definition allows MSMEs to invest in plant an d machinery for 
growth without the fear of losing the status.  Further, the increased limit also enables 
MSMEs to invest in new technologies to enhance their productivity.  It also removes 
the distinction between MSMEs in the manufacturing as well as services sector and 
increases the country’s exports through MSMEs. The Committee appreciates the 
Government’s decision to modify the definition of M icro, Small and Medium 
Enterprises as detailed above at para no.  5, as per the long -standing demand of 
Industry Associations.  The Committee also welcomes the decision to include retail 
and wholesale trade under the purview of MSME, thereby enabling them to get 
priority sector lending.  
 
Demands for Grants (2022-23) 
 
7. Regarding the details of the Action Plan set for the Ministry for the year2022 -23, 
the Committee was informed by the Ministry that an amount of Rs. 21,422crore has been 
allocated to the Ministry of MSME for the year 2022 -23. The Action Plan of the Ministry 
focuses on strengthening the overall ecosyst em of MSMEs by providing infrastructural 
support, marketing avenues, proper access to finance,  technical support and capacity 
building etc. This action plan is broadly reflected in the Output -Outcome Monitoring 
Framework Document of the Ministry for FY 2022-23.  
9. During the course of the presentation in connection with DFG (2022 -23), the 
Ministry of MSME apprised the Committee about the main highlights of the Union 
Budget 2022-23 for MSME Sector, which is as under :- 
 
 Udyam, e-shram, NCS and ASEEM portals to be interlinked; 
 ECLGS to be extended up to March 2023; 

4 
 
 Guarantee cover under ECLGS to be expanded by Rs. 50,000 crores , total cover of 
Rs. 5 lakh crores for hospitality and related activities; 
 Rs. 2 lakh crores additional credit for Micro and Small Enterprises facilitated under 
the Credit Guarantee Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE); and 
 Raising and Accelerating MSME performance (RAMP) programme with outlay of 
Rs. 6,000 crores to be rolled out.

9. The Committee notes that for the Financial Year 2022-23, the M/o MSME has 
been allocated Rs. 21422 crore as BE against the projection of Rs. 24979.6 crore 
which translates to 85.76%.  Therefore, there is a shortfall of Rs. 3557.6 crore  in BE 
allocation.  The SME division has been allocated Rs. 200 crore against the demanded 
amount of Rs. 208 crore , which comes to 96.15%.  However, the Committee notes 
that allocation to both DC -MSME and ARI Division of the Ministry has been 
curtailed substantially as DC-MSME has got 88.99% whereas A RI Division got only 
72.73% of the projected amount.  Both ARI Division and O/o DC -MSME are the 
backbone of the Ministry of MSME and are therefore crucial for development and 
growth of MSME sector.  The Committee understands that O/o DC -MSME 
implements poli cies and various programmes/schemes for providing infrastructure 
and support services to MSMEs whereas ARI Division looks after the administration 
of KVIC, COIR Board, MGIRI, besides supervising flagship schemes of the Ministry 
such as PMEGP, SFURUTI, ASPIRE, etc.  Therefore, inadequate allocation of funds 

5 
 
would jeopardise the smooth functioning of the Ministry  and successful 
implementation of its schemes/projects.  The Committee , therefore,  recommends 
that the M/o MSME should impress upon the Ministry of Finance to get enhanced 
budget allocation for both ARI Division and O/o DC-MSME at RE stage.

Views/Suggestions of the Stakeholders 
 
 
16. As part of examination of the DFG (2022 -23), t he Committee had sought the 
views/suggestions of the Associations (FICCI, FISME, CII and ASSOCHAM) on the 
Government‟s target of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises contributing $2 trillion to 
the country‟s target of becoming $5 trillion economy by 2024 . The Associations apprised 
the Committee that although the Covid pandemic had adversely affected the MSME 
Sector but they are very optimistic that MSME Sector will revive quickly but it is very 
important that the blueprint laid down by the Government materializes in a time -bound 
manner. The important suggestions put forth by the Associations in this regard along with 
the recommendations of the Committee are as under:- 
 
SEPARATE POLICY FOR MEDIUM ENTERPRISES 
17. Issues of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises are different from each other and  
unified policy would not be able to treat them well as per their actual requirements.  The 
schemes need to be segregated and modified as per each of the category so that  every 
category has specific solutions. It will help enterprises to identify schemes, which are 
relevant for them. Medium sector enterprises have the potential to grow to become large 
enterprises and subsequently, these enterprises could support many other Micro & Small 
enterprises in cascading effect. If only 10% of medium enterprises could be  transformed 
into large enterprises, there would be a significant cascading effect for Micro and Small 
enterprises. Therefore, separate policy should be formulated  for Medium enterprises. At 
present, no significant scheme is  available for Medium Enterprises and given that new 

11 
 
definition is into effect, announcing special schemes for medium -sized enterprises would 
benefit numerous enterprises who have joined MSME spectre due to new definition. 
 
18. The Committee recommends that based on the divergent needs of the Medium 
Enterprises vis-à-vis Small and Micro Enterprises, it is in interest of both the 
categories to have separate and dedicated policies for better targeted development 
and growth. 
 
SETTING UP OF SME FINANCE COMPANIES 
 
19. At present, no SME Finance Companies exist in the country. Therefore, as in the 
case of infrastructure finance companies or micro-finance companies that are not engaged 
in real economic activity  but in the financing of it, SME finance  companies may  be 
established in the country. 
 
20. The Committee suggest s that SME finance companies may be established in 
the country on the lines of infrastructure finance companies or micro -finance 
companies that are not engaged in real economic activity but in financing of it. 
 
CONSULTATION WITH STAKEHOLDERS 
 
21. There is an absence of a structured need assessment  exercise before proposing and 
designing any of the major schemes. Due to weak stakeholder discussions on proposed 
schemes, the design flaws come to light too late and  also discourage potential 
implementing agencies. There is a special need for the involvement of stakeholders at the 
design stage of the  scheme to make the schemes demand driven, especially the schemes 
that are based  on SPV/consortium approach. Process of structured need assessment will 
help generate „content‟ having clarity on objectives, beneficiaries; scope and coverage of  
schemes; role and function of implementing agency. 
22. The Committee is of the view that Emphasis should be made on involvement 
of stakeholders at design stage of schemes, especially the schemes that are based on 
SPV/Consortium approach. 
GST REGIME 
 
23. The GST regime was meant to be a digitized system; however, department 
authorities still insist that separate hard copies are also provided to claim refund. This 
defeats the  purpose of digitizing process. The time involved by the MSMEs in finally 

12 
 
getting the  refund sanctioned causes a tremendous strain on the cash flows of the 
MSMEs, which in turn restricts the possibility of increasing exports. 
 
24. The Committee recommends that the GST regime should be a digitized 
system, without the requirement o f separate hard copies to be provided the 
concerned Dep artment to claim refund. This not only defeats the purpose of 
digitizing process, bu t also eases inordinate delay in getting the refund sanctioned , 
thereby resulting in tremendous strain on cash flows of the MSMEs. 
 
EMERGENCY CREDIT LINE GUARANTEE SCHEME (ECLGS) 
 
25. The Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme mandated for providing collateral 
free loans to MSMEs has been a very effective financial measure to restore their liquidity 
and financial health. The said emergency credit line has greatly helped MSMEs to restart 
operations by giving them additional working capital support. It is a welcome step of the 
Government in the Budget 2022 -23 to increase the corpus of ECLGS to Rs. 5 lakh crore 
by adding  Rs. 50 thousand crore for hospitality & related enterprises and extend the 
validity of scheme till 31st March, 2023. However, some more concerns have been raised 
by MSME, which are not able to avail the scheme. Repayment period of 3 -4 years 
including moratorium period under the scheme is very little time for MSMEs which are 
struggling to survive through second wave of Covid -19. As the cash  flow has dried, it 
would take a longer period to come back to normal liquidity and hence it is suggested that  
repayment period should be extended up to 7 – 8 years with at least 2 years of moratorium 
on principal amount. With respect to the interest, moratorium for interest from 01.03.2020 
onwards should be announced by the RBI. This accumulative interest  should be made part 
of term loan/principal amount. No interest on interest should be charged from MSMEs 
while amalgamating the interest in principal amount. 
 
26. The Committee finds that under Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme, 
repayment period of 3 -4 years including moratorium period is a very short period 
for MSMEs, which are struggling to survive from the second wave of Covid -19. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the repayment period should be extended 
upto 7 -8 years with at least 2 years of moratorium on principal amount. With 

13 
 
respect to the interest, moratorium for interest from 01.03.2020 onwards should be 
announced by RBI. 
 
 
 
CREDIT GUARANTEE FUND TRUST FOR MICRO AND SMALL 
ENTERPRISES (CGTMSE) 
 
27. During the budget 2022-23, it was announced by the Hon‟ble Finance Minister that 
the CGTMSE Scheme shall be revamped and Rs. 2 lakh crore will also be infused in the  
scheme. It is indeed a welcome step in the direction to make this scheme more viable  and 
convenient for its stakeholders including MSEs. As the scheme is to be revamped,  it has 
been requested that the limit of collateral free loans under this scheme, either term loan or 
working capital loan, should be increased from existing Rs. 2 crore to upto Rs. 5 crores, 
irrespective of the constitution (Private Ltd ., LLP, Partnership and/or proprietorship) of 
applicant MSEs provided all other criteria are fulfilled. 
 
28. The Committee is in consonance with the suggestion of Associations and 
recommends that while revamping the CGTMSE scheme, the limit of collateral free 
loans under this scheme, either term loan or working capital loan should be 
increased from the existing R s. 2 crore upto Rs. 5 crores, irrespective of the 
Constitution (Private Ltd., LLP, Partnership and/or Proprietorship) of applicant 
MSE’s provided all other criteria are fulfilled. 
 
AATMANIRBHAR BHARAT 
 
29. Government is focusing upon Make in India, which is entirely appreciable effort as 
it helps MSMEs to grow. It is a fact that India is importing MSME oriented products in  
huge quantity, which can be manufactured in India. One of the reasons behind this  could 
be explained as lack of awareness amongs t MSMEs about the products that are  being 
imported in the country. Feedback has been received from the industry that  Government 
should put out a list of such products along with their production  specifications so that 
enterprises become excited about getting into the production of such products. Such 
imported products should also carry the information about their  market potential in the 
country. This would be beneficial for a large number of potential entrepreneurs who wish 
to set up new manufacturing enterprises. A Central Market  Intelligence Center for import 

14 
 
related products and components would be of great  help to MSMEs to achieve the goal of 
Aatmanirbhar Bharat. Soft loans at minimal  interest rate of 3 -4% with a minimum tenure 
of 9-10 years for repayment period, Easy land acquisition, less compliances could be key 
factors in developing domestic manufacturing with MSMEs. Till the domestic 
manufacturing is strengthened, high  import restrictions should be avoided and only, if 
necessary, it should be applied in phased manner. 
 
30. The Committee understands that a number of MSME oriented products are 
presently being imported , which can be manufactured in India. The Committee ,

electronic, print and social media to attract interested entrepreneurs to set up new 
manufacturing enterprises in MSME Sector, thereby fulfilling the Government ’s 
vision of Aatmanirbhar Bharat.

Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme 
 
52. The Committee was informed by the Ministry that the Interest Subsidy Eligibility 
Certificate (ISEC) Scheme is one of the components of Khadi  Vikas Yojana. It was 
introduced to mobilize the funds from banking institutions in order to cater to the 
additional fund requirement of Khadi and Village Industries Institutions. For this, an 
Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate is issued to Khadi Institutio ns (KIs). The off take 
of the ISEC depends upon the production and sales capacity of Khadi Institutions and the 
nature of production i.e., Silk, Cotton, Woolen Khadi and Polyvastra. It strengthens the 
KIs to avail the bank finance for the shortfall in working capital. Under the Scheme 4% of 
the interest is to be borne by the KIs and the interest differential between the actual 
lending rate and 4% is provided by the Government of India  through Khadi  & Village 
Industries Commission (KVIC) to the lending Banks. The funds for this purpose are 
provided to KVIC under the Khadi Vikas Yojana Grant.
