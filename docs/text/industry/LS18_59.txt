CHAPTER-I
 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE GOVERNMENT
 
DEVELOPMENT OF AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRY
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
The Committee understands that the cess of 0.05%, which is being charged at present, needs to be increased to 0.25% for making necessary investment in
testing facilities. The committee agrees with the view expressed by the Secretary that the allocation of Rs.10 crores, that has been made, is insufficient as
R&D and testing facilities have to be enhanced. Therefore, Ministry of Finance may consider increasing  at RE stage.
 
COMMENTS
 
It is submitted that the department has taken the issue with Ministry of Finance in accordance with the recommendations of the committee.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
The Committee also agrees with the views expressed by the Secretary that the need of the hour is electric vehicles for having pollution free transportation
in the cities, particularly, in the metropolis.  The Government may consider exemption of custom duty surcharge on items imported for use in manufacture
of Electric Vehicles and reduction of excise duty on Electric Vehicles. The  Ministry of Non-Conventional Sources of Energy may also consider providing
subsidies to the extent of 50% to three wheelers and the Department of Heavy Industry may pursue the matter in this regard.
 
COMMENTS
 
It is submitted that the department has taken up the issue with Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) in accordance with the recommendations of
the Committee.
 
The issue of giving subsidy to electric powered 3 wheeler has also been taken by this Department with the Department of Non-Conventional Sources of
Energy (DNCSE), who have agreed to provide a subsidy of Rs.1 lakh per three wheeler vehicle with a total limit of 200 vehicles as a part of a pilot
demonstration project.

PLAN INVESTMENT IN THE PUBLIC SECTOR
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
The Committee is happy to note that the Plan allocation was raised from Rs. 138 crore in BE 2000-2001 to Rs. 354.8 crore in RE but for the year 2001-
2002 out of total projected requirement of Rs. 231.55 crore only, Rs. 178 crore has been allocated.
 
COMMENTS
 
This Deptt. had proposed a total requirement of Rs. 231.56 crore to the Planning Commission for Annual Plan 2001-2002, Rs. 123.18 crore for the capital
investment programmes and Rs. 108.37 crore for ex-gratia component of VRS. After discussions with Plg. Commission, this requirement was scaled down
to Rs.178 cr. The reduction has been mainly in the ex-gratia component (Rs.38 cr.) of VRS and crucial balancing investments (Rs.13 cr.)  The budget
provision 2001-02 is considered sufficient for plan programmes. However, in case of any additional requirement for packages approved by BIFR/Govt.,
same shall be sought at RE stage.
 
RECOMMENDATION 
 
In view of the present revival schemes in various public sector undertakings and implementation of VRS, the amount allocated is not sufficient and may be
considered for increase as per the requirement at RE stage.  The Committee also hopes that the PSEs would be able to raise IEBR to the tune of Rs.371.17
crore as projected for 2001-02.
 
COMMENTS
           
The capital investments under the revival plan of the PSUs already approved by the Govt./BIFR have been provided for in the plan budget 2001-02. 
Investments for revival plan approved during the course  of the year shall be taken  care of either at RE stage or provided out of crucial balancing
investments head.  IEBR projected in the plan (2001-02) is expected to be realised during the  year.  Out of total requirement of Rs.172.97 crore projected
by PSUs for VRS, Rs.134.60 crore were provided in the budget (Rs.70.00 crore in plan budget towards ex-gratia payment and Rs.64.60 crore in non-plan
budget for other elements of VRS).  Releases are being made based on applications received and requirements given by PSUs from time to time.  In the
event of any further requirement, the same will be provided at RE stage.

PSEs UNDER THE DEPARTMENT OF HEAVY INDUSTRY
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            Even after the persistent efforts of the Ministry, the performance of the PSEs has been below the projected levels.  The Committee feels that the
PSEs are required to put in more efforts to usher in a prosperous future.
 
COMMENTS
 
PSEs are under tremendous pressure to perform in an increasingly competitive business/environment. PSEs have been  continuously readjusting
themselves to the changing environment and have been initiating various cost-cutting measures and also upgrading their technology to the requirements of
the customers.  Excess manpower is being reduced to bring down costs.  Business/organizational restructuring is also being continuously undertaken to
optimise utilization of assets. 
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee desires that the  PSUs should take more efforts in :-
 
(i)         Cost cutting ;
(ii)        Upgradation of technology ;
(iii)       Manpower reduction,  and
(iv)       Business/organisational  restructuring.
 
The Committee recommends that DHI should monitor the above aspects company-wise and submit a status report to the Committee.
 
The  PSUs under DHI should evolve  a proper workable mechanism to meet the deadlines for the supply of orders placed on them and fix accountability
for non-adherence to the delivery schedules.

HINDUSTAN PHOTO FILMS LTD.
 
The Sub-committee-III had interacted with Secretary (HI&PE) and CMD, HPF on the question of the strategies for the revival of HPF on 26.10.01.
 
The Committee observes that HPF is a unique company and the only one of its kind in India.  It caters to strategic needs of the country whether it be
defence or mass health care.  Therefore, a country like India with one billion population cannot be solely dependent on import of photo goods by closing
down the state of the art facilities, which have been recently created with mammoth investment.
 
Present policies of the Government encourage trading/conversion (with value addition of less than 10 per cent).  HPF is an integrated manufacturer with
investment of more than Rs.400 crores in plant and machinery and has necessary skills to manufacture quality products, being an ISO 9002 company.  The
photo goods manufacturing technology is a closely guarded preserve among only a few (6 in all) in the whole world.  The domestic photo goods market is
increasing at around 10 percent compounded annual growth rate and the Government should not lose the hard developed manufacturing expertise of the
last three decades.  In order to encourage integrated manufacturing, it is felt that adequate incentives need to be given to HPF which will culminate in
attracting a prospective JV partner.  In order to facilitate this, the Committee felt that the company’s balance sheet must be cleaned along with reduction in
excess manpower.  It is learnt from the Administrative Ministry that a comprehensive revival plan has been formulated for consideration of the Union

Cabinet envisaging the above parameters.  The infusion of funds requested by the company and the administrative ministry is mainly towards:
 
(i)         One time grant;
Employees related dues;

The Committee feels that though the total infusion of funds is to the tune of around Rs.560 crores, the bulk of the same (around Rs.470 crores) is towards

            In order to encourage the only integrated manufacturer of photo films in the country, Government should impose following tariff and non-tariff
barriers:
 
(i)         Tariff:
 
Increase n customs duty on imports of semi-finished and finished unexposed photo goods to WTO bound rates by converters and traders, who do not have
integrated manufacturing facility of over Rs.400 crores in plant and machinery and technical know-how.
 
Exemption  on customs duty and excise duty (incl. CVD and SAD) for imports of raw materials by HPF, the integrated manufacturer including colour 
semi-finished goods).
 
Exemption of customs duty on capital goods imports by HPF towards technical upgradation.
 
(ii)        Non-tariff:
 
Designating a single sea port in the country for import of all semi-finished and finished UNEXPOSED photo goods falling under chapter 37 of customs
tariff.
Subjecting all the photo goods imports for random sampling and testing to avoid unethical practices.
 
Imports of all photo goods (semi-finished unexposed) in SINGLE UNIT sq. mtrs instead of currently in different units like kgs, boxes, sizes, packets, rolls,
meters, etc. to prevent loss of revenue to the Government of India.
 
Attractive (1992 wage level basis) VRS to be approved for downsizing manpower and to affect 1992 wage revision to existing/retained employees.
 
Export incentive of Rs.50 per mtr. to be given to HPF towards brand promotion and also ti increase the capacity utilization.
 
Reservation upto 50% of the annual procurements of photo goods by all government establishments and defence organisation for HPF.
 
The Committee recommends earliest implementation of the above proposals  with a view to:
 
(i)         revive the only photo goods company in the country;
(ii)        promote socio-economic development of the region – a backward district in Tamil Nadu and
(iii)       Attract a JV partner who can bring contemporary technology including digital.
 
The Committee further observes that in the event of  closure/winding of this company, the only one of its kind in the country and one amongst the six only
in the whole world, then India with a population of one billion will have to perennially depend on imports for ever and that situation is undesirable,
especially so when the manufacturing technology of photo goods is highly secretive and sophisticated and is available in the country.
 
RESEARCH AND DEVELOPMENT OF AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRY

used for other areas of product and process related research with a view to give a fillip to India’s automobile industries. It is also necessary to enable the
automobile industry to survive the imports surge after WTO regime became fully applicable. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should take
stock of what is being done and what further is to be done R&D has to be undertaken for product and process related research also.

automobile industry to survive in the WTO regime.  In this regard, it is submitted utilisation of cess funds and the implementation of cess funded R&D
projects is an ongoing process. The projects are reviewed regularly by the Development Council (DCAAI)’s  Cess Committee and recommendations are
made keeping in view the requirement of the industry. The above recommendations of the Committee will be kept in view in the deliberations of the Cess
Committee while reviewing the cess funded projects.

RECOMMENDATION
 
The Committee recommends that there should be proper evaluation and monitoring by the ministry on the restructuring plans.
 
COMMENTS

BIFR with regard to revival plans under implementation and performance evaluation undertaken vis-à-vis the targets projected in the package.  In case of
restructuring plans approved by the Government on its own, reviews are undertaken at the Board level as well as in the administrative Ministry for
initiating necessary corrective actions.  As a result of such reviews by BIFR/Govt/company’s Board, fresh restructuring plans are under preparation in case

COMMITTEE’S OBSERVATIONS

undertaking joint venture formation.  The Committee understands that no prospective buyer would come forward to take over these sick companies unless
they are restructured by clearing their balance sheets, giving VRS, fiscal incentive, cash infusion by grants, loans, counter-guarantee, etc. and made
potentially viable.  The Committee therefore recommends that these two companies may be restructured before joint venture formation.  The Committee
also feels that JVP may also be afforded some facilities in the form of Price Preference etc. so as to enable them to come above the initial difficulties.

The Sub-committee-III had interacted with Secretary (HI&PE) and CMD, HPF on the question of the strategies for the revival of HPF on 26.10.01.
 
The Committee observed that HPF is a unique company and the only one of its kind in India.  It caters to strategic needs of the country whether it be
defence or mass health care.  Therefore, a country like India with one billion population cannot be solely dependent on import of photo goods by closing
down the state of the art facilities, which have been recently created with mammoth investment.
 
Present policies of the Government encourage trading/conversion (with value addition of less than 10 per cent).  HPF is an integrated manufacturer with
investment of more than Rs.400 crores in plant and machinery and has necessary skills to manufacture quality products, being an ISO 9002 company.  The
photo goods manufacturing technology is a closely guarded preserve among only a few (6 in all) in the whole world.  The domestic photo goods market is
increasing at around 10 percent compounded annual growth rate and the Government should not lose the hard developed manufacturing expertise of the
last three decades.  In order to encourage integrated manufacturing, it is felt that adequate incentives need to be given to HPF which will culminate in
attracting a prospective JV partner.  In order to facilitate this, the Committee felt that the company’s balance sheet must be cleaned along with reduction in
excess manpower.  It is learnt from the Administrative Ministry that a comprehensive revival plan has been formulated for consideration of the Union
Cabinet envisaging the above parameters.  The infusion of funds requested by the company and the administrative ministry is mainly towards:
 
(i)         One time grant;
Employees related dues;
Attractive VRS towards downsizing manpower;
(iv)       Interest free loan:
(v)        towards working capital; and
(vi)       repayment of old debts of the company (secured and unsecured) mainly to the nationalised banks/ FIs and Government-woned ICDs.
 
The Committee feels that though the total infusion of funds is to the tune of around Rs.560 crores, the bulk of the same (around Rs.470 crores) is towards
repayment of the bank loans.  For revival of the company, the realistic fund infusion is only towards working capital.  The sacrifice on the part of the
creditors comprises 68% of the total dues payable to them by the company.  The thrust of the revival is towards granting specific incentives to an
integrated manufacturer viz-a-viz trading/conversion activity and thereby paves way for a possible joint venture. 
 
            In order to encourage the only integrated manufacturer of photo films in the country, Government should impose following tariff and non-tariff
barriers:
 
(i)         Tariff:
 
Increase n customs duty on imports of semi-finished and finished unexposed photo goods to WTO bound rates by converters and traders, who do not have
integrated manufacturing facility of over Rs.400 crores in plant and machinery and technical know-how.
 
Exemption  on customs duty and excise duty (incl. CVD and SAD) for imports of raw materials by HPF, the integrated manufacturer including colour 
semi-finished goods).
 
Exemption of customs duty on capital goods imports by HPF towards technical upgradation.
 
(ii)        Non-tariff:
 
Designating a single sea port in the country for import of all semi-finished and finished UNEXPOSED photo goods falling under chapter 37 of customs
tariff.
Subjecting all the photo goods imports for random sampling and testing to avoid unethical practices.
 
Imports of all photo goods (semi-finished unexposed) in SINGLE UNIT sq. mtrs instead of currently in different units like kgs, boxes, sizes, packets, rolls,
meters, etc. to prevent loss of revenue to the Government of India.
 
Attractive (1992 wage level basis) VRS to be approved for downsizing manpower and to affect 1992 wage revision to existing/retained employees.
Export incentive of Rs.50 per mtr. to be given to HPF towards brand promotion and also ti increase the capacity utilization.
 
Reservation upto 50% of the annual procurements of photo goods by all government establishments and defence organisation for HPF.

            The Committee notes that the recovery of BHEL’s outstanding dues from SEBs as on 30.9.1996 would continue @ 15% of CPA till the entire dues

(10.4 )
            The Committee notes that the matter has been taken up by the Minister of HI & PE with the Ministers of Power and Finance.  The Committee

undertaken by the Ministry of Power and Ministry of Finance based on the recommendations of Montek Singh Ahluwalia Committee to allay the element
of discrimination.
 
            Keeping in view the special role played by BHEL to the economy of the country and to enable it to face the global competition from foreign
companies, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Finance should take early decision in respect of rescindment of Customs Notification No.
85/99/CUS dated 6.7.99  which allows import at zero duty.  This would enable domestic industry to avail 15%, as was available till July 1999, as per
World Bank guidelines, which allows this advantage to developing economies to face competition.
 
            DGFT should also issue notification for refund of terminal excise duty for projects ordered/bid under International Competitive Bidding prior to
1.4.2000 when such a refund was allowed under the extant EXIM policy but withdrawn subsequently.  With the withdrawal of this refund, the 16% ED
being quite substantial, cannot be expected to be absorbed by the manufacturer.  Further, since the customers have frozen their project cost and the
financing structure based on the settled price and obtained approvals of competent/statutory authorities, they are also not prepared to re-open their books
and accept this additional duty burden.  In such a situation, where customers are neither agreeable to re-negotiate the prices to compensate the
manufacturers, nor are they ready to pay the applicable excise duty, the refund of terminal excise duty for at least these projects which were ordered/LOI
placed before 1.4.2000 under ICB must be allowed under a special notification.

(15.6)
 
The Committee observes that it is not only SEBs who are not paying dues to the CPSU but there are other State Government agencies which are also not
paying dues to the CPSUs.  Moreover, Government decision to recover overdues of CPSUs payable by the SEBs through adjustment of release of the
Central Plan Assistance have been taken to help the big companies in the power, coal, railway and atomic energy sector while the relatively small CPSUs
have been left to fend for themselves.  Thus, there is an element of discrimination in the decision.  Non-payment of dues by the SEBs to such shall CPSUs
is a deadly blow to the latter in the liberalised era where Government patronage to Government companies has been brought down.
           
In the circumstances the committee recommends that Government should not only recover the over dues of CPSUs payable by SEBs but also recover dues
of other State Government agencies.  The Committee further recommends that Government should give this facility to other CPSUs other than power, coal,
railway and atomic energy sector.
