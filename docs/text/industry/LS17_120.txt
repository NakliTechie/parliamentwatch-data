REPORT
 
 
1.         The Department of Heavy Industry (Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises) is responsible for promoting
engineering industry in the country and development and growth of capital goods.  The industries covered by this Department
provide goods and services for almost all sectors of the economy, including power, rail and road transport. They also cater to the
requirements of equipment for basic industries such as steel, non-ferrous metals, fertilizers, refineries, petrochemicals, shipping,
paper, cement, sugar etc. The Department is responsible for the development of  wide range of intermediate engineering
products like castings, forgings, diesel engines, industrial gears and gearboxes.  There are 49  Public  Sector Enterprises (PSEs)
under the administrative charge of the Department.
1.1       The PSEs under the administrative control of the Department are engaged in manufacturing of engineering/capital goods,
consultancy and contracting activities. The total investment (Gross Block) in 49 Public Sector Enterprises under the Department
of Heavy Industry was about Rs.8234.00 crores till 31st March, 2002,  employing about 1.18  lakh persons.
1.2       The Department holds interactions with various Industry Associations and evolves plans for the growth of the industry.
The Department also assists industry through policy initiatives, resolution of problems relating to tariffs and trade, technological
collaboration, up- gradation and Research & Development, etc.

RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT OF AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRIES
3.         A provision of Rs.25.00 crores has been made as plan outlay for Research and Development of automotive industries in

B.E. 2003-04. An allocation of Rs.25.00 crores under non-plan budget has also been made in B.E. 2003-04 for the purpose.
3.1       The allocated funds are to be utilized for funding significant projects on automotive safety and emission testing, on
creating infrastructure and validating performance of the components and  vehicles in terms of global emission, safety, tolerance
norms being prescribed by Economic Commission for Europe etc. Introduction of higher safety and emission norms is not
possible in the country without commensurate testing facilities being available. It is in this context that this allocation becomes
really meaningful and important.
3.2       Following delicensing of automotive industry in 1993, this sector has made great advances and has opened up to FDI.
The investment  in the industry is Rs.50,000 crores up to the end of March, 2000. The research activities in the automotive
sector have also comparatively intensified. All auto companies are having their own R&D laboratories to meet their limited
requirements. The infrastructure at Automotive Research Association of India, Pune, (ARAI), has also been considerably
upgraded to meet common requirements of all auto manufacturers. However, the total expenditure by auto industry at present on
its R&D activities is paltry 0.2 per cent. Considering the quality of automobiles in India as compared with the automobiles
manufactured in advanced countries, this expenditure on R&D is rather meager and needs to be increased considerably so that
the automobiles manufactured in India are of the same standard and quality as in developed countries. The improved quality of
automobiles will not only benefit the domestic consumers but will also enable the industry to export in larger numbers and
compete effectively.
3.3       The Government also has to provide financial assistance in this sector. The Committee notes that R&D has not received
the same importance it deserves in so far as allocation of funds is concerned. Although, tax holiday has been extended to R&D
companies up to March 31, 2004, but this measure is perhaps not adequate. What is required is enhanced allocation of funds 
collected as the cess on auto vehicles in order to intensify testing and research activities in the auto sector. The allocation to the
Ministry for R&D falls short of the projected demand i.e. Rs.1500 crores in the coming five to seven years. The Ministry was
allocated Rs.70 crores only during 2002-03. This amount has been reduced to Rs.50 crores for the year 2003-04 (rs.25 crores as
plan and Rs.25 crores as non-plan).
3.4       The Committee observes that with the introduction of new and stricter emission norms as well as safety standards,
upgradation of automotive testing facilities is critically needed for further growth of automotive industry in the country. There is
also a need to look at other areas of product and process related research with a view to propelling India’s automotive industry
into a higher league and for surviving the import surge under WTO regime.  The required level of R&D  can be achieved only
by enhanced funding.  It is a fact that the Government is collecting automotive cess at the rate of 0.125%  per cent over and
above excise duty on vehicles. But since inception in 1984-85,  allocation of funds has been meager and much less than the
actual collection. Now  a comprehensive project on upgrading the testing infrastructure in the country is proposed to be
implemented at a cost of Rs.1500 crore. The Committee, therefore,  recommends that the Government should enhance allocation
under cess to the extent of full amount collected. The Government should also allocate in two installments the entire unallocated

sum of Rs 347.38 crore. The Ministry should strive to attain the international level in R&D so that 100% indigenisation in
manufacturing of components takes place and import bill on auto components is reduced.
3.5       The Committee also notes that  at present the cess for  R&D is levied @ 1/8% i.e. 0.125% over  and above the excise
duty.  Department of Heavy Industry has, however, taken up the matter with the Ministry of Finance for enhancement in the rate
of levy of cess from 0.05% to 0.25%. The Committee strongly supports the view point of Department of Heavy Industry and
hopes Ministry of Finance would respond to the demand. The Committee would also like to be apprised of the progress made in
regard to the enhancement of rate of levy of cess from 0.05% to
0.25%. 
3.6       The Committee is of the view that while Government will no doubt provide funds for R&D activities within the given
availability of funds, at the same time there is an urgent need for the industry also to contribute towards R&D activities in auto
sector particularly in view of the fact that the entire auto sector is in the private sector. Hitherto, the contribution by the industry
for the R&D activities is negligible.

6.3       In contrast to the performance last  year, the use –based classification of  industries  reveals good  performance  of all 
the sectors viz.  basic goods, capital goods, intermediate goods and the consumer goods.  Basic goods, capital goods, 
intermediate goods and consumer goods registered growth rates of 4.7 per cent, 9.9 per cent , 2.6   percent and 7.3 percent  
respectively during  April-November, 2002-03 as against  2.0 percent, 4.9  percent, 2.1  percent and 5.8 percent respectively 
registered for the corresponding period in the previous  year,  whereas in the field of consumer goods, the consumer durables 
registered  a negative  growth of 6 percent  during April-November , 2002-03 as against 12.9 percent in April-November 2001-
02.  On the  other hand, consumer non-durables posted a growth of 12.7  percent during April-November 2002-03 as against 3.2
percent  in April-November 2001-02.  In the industrial sector, a very good growth in  post-liberalization period from 1994-1996
has been registered.  But  from November, 1997, the industrial growth has  slackened, because  the capital goods sector has not
grown at all.  There was a  negative   growth in 2000-01  .  It is only in this year that the capital goods sector has revived.  This
growth is only in  the automotive  and road sector.

CEMENT CORPORATION  OF INDIA LTD.
19.       Cement Corporation of India Limited  (CCI) was established in January, 1965 with the principal objective of setting up
cement factories in public sector to  help achieve self sufficiency in cement production and to .remove regional imbalance in
pursuance of the National policy.  CCI has as on 30.9.2002, authorized  capital  of Rs. 700 crores and paid up capital of
Rs.428.28 crores. It is a multi unit organisation which has  at present ten units spread over in eight States viz.  Mandhar,
Akaltara (Chhattisgarh), Nayagaon (MP), Kurkunta (Karnataka), Bokajan (Assam), Rajaban (H.P), Adilabad and Tandur ( AP),
Charkhi Dadri ( Haryana) and  Delhi Grinding Unit ( Delhi) besides Bhatinda Grinding Unit (Punjab, uninstalled), with total
installed capacity of 38 .48 lakh tonne per annum. The company had been making lasses for many years.  Its net worth has 
become negative and, it was declared sick by BIFR on 8.8.1996
19.1     During the year 2001-2002, the cement production was 6.08 lakh MT as against the target of 7.71 lakh MT (RE). The
sales turnover during  the year was Rs. 131.45 crore as against the target of Rs. 169.63 crore(RE). The company made a net
loss of Rs.215.33 crore during  the 2001-2002.
19.2     In the current  year 2002-03 upto September, 2002 the Corporation  achieved 2.08 Lakh MT of cement production and
sales turnover of Rs. 47.25 crores. The production at seven units viz. Mandhar, Akaltara & Charkhi Dadri, Kurkunta, Adilabad,
Nayagaon and DGU continued to remain suspended during the year Since CCI is a sick company and the BIFR has already
issued directions for sale of its plants individually or collectively.  In its last hearing held on 31.10.2002, BIFR directed to
complete the process of sale of all the units of CCI within six months time w.e.f 15.12.2002.
19.3 The Committee was also apprised that the interest burden of the Corporation has been growing . It has not been able to pay
back the loan which was taken for VRS. Technology is old and power consumption is high. CMD, CCI apprised that salaries
had not been paid to employees for the last nine months.
19.4    The Committee, therefore, recommends that Government should expedite the sale process of Cement Corporation of
India and release salary to employees of the Cement Corporation of India considering the humanitarian aspect of it.
