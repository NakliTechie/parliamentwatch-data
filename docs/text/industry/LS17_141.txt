CHAPTER-I
 
STATUS OF COIR INDUSTRIES IN WEST BENGAL
 
            West Bengal holds fifth position in the country in respect of production of coconuts.  There is a potential
for development of coir industries in the state.  The annual production of coconut in West Bengal is of the order
of 324.3 million nuts with an area of 24,200 hectors for coconut cultivation.  Annual coconut production of five
prominent states of the country is given in the table below:

The area under coconut cultivation in West Bengal during 2002-2003 was placed at 28300 hectares and the
production of coconut is estimated at 389.20 million nuts.  The production of coir fibre in the State is estimated at 186
M.T. per annum.  Coconut is cultivated in 17 districts of West Bengal in which Murshidabad District was the largest
producer of coconut followed by South 24 Pargana, Midnapur (West and East), North 24 Parganas and Howrah.  These
five districts together constitute 79% of total production in the State.  About 15% of total production of coconut is
consumed for domestic uses.  60 to 65% of coconut produced in the State are plucked at the tender stage only due to
increasing demand for tender coconut in the State.  There are about 327 coir units registered with District Industries
Centres.  These units are engaged in the manufacture of bristle/mechanized fibre, coir rope, coir products including
rubberized coir.  Besides, there are 264 Janatha mattress making units.  The turnover of registered and unregistered units
amounted to Rs. 710 lakhs.  The coir industry in West Bengal provides employment to 3140 people, out of which 88%
of workers is in cottage  units.  About 1200 women are engaged in coir activities.  There are two coir cooperative
societies,  of which one is running well.  The State Government is running two coir training centers, one at Uluberia in
Howrah district and the other at Dhanchiberia in South 24 Parganas District.

Scope for development of coir industry in West Bengal
 
            The Sub-Committee observed that the major constraint in West Bengal for the growth and development of Coir
Industries was about 60 to 65% of coconuts produced in the State are plucked in tender stage.  Only 20% of the total
production has been considered as available for extraction of fibre  which works out to 77.84 million coconuts’ husk. 
All the above available husk of 77.84 million nuts cannot be used for fibre extraction because of the following reasons:
 
(i)
                  There is no system for collection of coconut husks from consumers of coconuts in the State.  Only coconut
traders and goladars (stockists) are the sources of husks as they collect coconuts from primary agricultural
markets of different districts, remove coconut for onward disposal to their clients and keep the husks for
selling to the processors.
(ii)
                In most of the districts there is no existence of such traders (stockists) and so there is no adequate
machinery to git husks for processing though husks are available in these districts and are mostly wasted by
using as fuel.  With the development of coir industries in the State in future and with consequent increase of
the demand of husks, the system of collection of husks may develop and supply of husk may increase for
processing purpose and accordingly may also increase the supply of husk for processing purpose.
(iii)
               Only in the three districts of the State namely, Howrah, South 24 Parganas and Midnapur, there are traders
and Goladars who are in a position to supply coconut husks for coir industry.  Annual assured supply from
these districts comes 383.8 million.

200 M.T. per annum.
 
(i)

Problems for development of Coir Industries in the State

            During the course of interaction with the representatives of the State Government the Sub-Committee came
across that the following major problems are being faced by the Coir Industries in West Bengal:-
 
(i)
                 In West Bengal most of the available coconut husk are used for non-remunerative purpose such as
fuel.  About 352 families residing in the district of Howrah and south 24-Parganas are engaged in
crushing of husk by conventional method of wet and hammering.  Thus, they are manufacturing low
cost mattress fibre which could be used for manufacture of value-added coir products.  Only very
nominal portion of the available husk are defibered mechanically for use in the coir industries.
(ii)                Non-existence of any traditional artisans having traditional skill in making coir yarn and other downstream
coir products.
(iii)               Lack of infrastructure for providing training for creating of large number of artisans making yarn and
downstream products.  Existing Training-cum-Servicing Centers of the state Government can provide
training only to a very small numbers of trainees.  Besides, the existing centers are not equipped with modern
machines and technical men like those of Coir Board.
(iv)
              Husk available in West Bengal are unsuitable for extraction by easy process of hand crushing.  Fibre can be
extracted only by mechanical process which involve capital investment.  As a result there are very few
mechanized defibering units and the production of fibre in this State is negligible.  The individual units are to
depend on the supply of fibre from the other states at higher price.
(v)                Lack of product diversification and absence of improved dying process are other factors.  We are still
producing only coir yarn and door mattress of limited variety.
(vi)              Absences of natural soaking places like that of Kerala, non-existence of any collection system of husk,
increasing rate of plucking of tender coconuts etc. also responsible for unsatisfactory development of coir
industries in the state.
(vii)
             Lack of awareness among the people about the use of coconut husk and its value in respect of employment
and income generation, lack of knowledgeable persons, technical institution on coir etc. are also responsible.
 
The Sub-Committee understands that although the state of West Bengal has the potential  growth of the Coir
Industies but at the same time scores of problems are confronting the policy maker for generating proliferation of
coir Industries in the state.  It is fact that there is higher consumption of tender coconut (60 to 65%) and only 20% of
the coconut husk can be made available for coir industry. There is absence of an organized system for collection of
coconut husk from consumers of coconut.  Only coconut traders and Goladars (Stockists) are the sources of husk. 
Traders are available only  in 3 districts viz. Howrah, South 24-Parganas and Midnapur.  Moreover, The coir
processing equipments and machineries available in the State are very old, outdated and obsolete. Poor quality of
raw-material, lack of awareness on the latest development and absence modernized product diversification, further
aggravate the problem. Besides scarcity of skilled manpower and low skill of the existing coir workers, Low
productivity, low management; low investment; lack of entrepreneurs and  no separate wing/officer to look after the
coir development under the State Government are the hurdles in the growth and development of coir industry.

CHAPTER-II
 
Action Plan For Development Of Coir Industry In West Bengal
 
As per the instruction given by the Committee, the Chairman, Coir Board held discussions on 6.6.2003 with the
Principal Secretary (Industries) and other senior officials of the Govt. of West Bengal regarding development of Coir
Industry in the State On the basis of the discussion, an action plan for development of coir industry in West Bengal was
prepared by the Coir Board along with Calendar of Activities and sent to the Government of West Bengal.  The total
outlay of the action plan projects works out to Rs. 384.60 lakhs of which the Board’s contribution works out to Rs.
93.10 lakhs. Implementation of the action plan is expected to generate 1000 employment in coir sector in the rural areas
of West Bengal.
 
            West Bengal is one of the coconut producing States in India.  The development of coir industry in the
State is still in the offspring stage.  The Chairman, Coir Board during his visit to Kolkata, had a detailed
discussion the Principal Secretary (Industries) and other senior officials of the Govt. of West Bengal, on the
development of coir industry in the State.  The Principal Secretary sought assistance of the Coir Board for
developing coir industry in the State.  The Principal Secretary (Industries)  offered all support to Coir Board
from State Government level for developing coir industry in the State.  This action plan is certainly the outcome
of the discussion of the Sub –Committee  held by Chairman, Coir Board with the Principal Secretary
(Industries), Govt. of West Bengal, representatives of Banks and NGOs during its visit to Kolkata.
     
Action Plan
 
1.
                  An officer of the Coir Board will be deputed to visit the existing coir training centers in West Bengal

CHAPTER-V
Observations/Conclusions and Recommendation of the Committee
            Steps to explore the potential of coir industries in non-traditional States like West Bengal, Orissa, Bihar
and Jharkhand.
            The Committee in its 91st report on Performance of Coir Board stated  that adequate steps must be
undertaken to explore the potential of coir industries in non-traditional States like West Bengal, Orissa, Bihar
and Jharkhand as by giving impetus to the Coir Industry to a non traditional states would enhance the
productions of Coir Industry as well as open new avenues for sustainable employment generation and poverty
alleviation.  The Committee appreciates the Government of West Bengal and the Coir Board for implementing
the suggestion of the Committee so suitably in the State of West Bengal and Union Territory of Andaman and
Nicobar.
ROLE OF COIR BOARD
            The Committee in its 91
st report on ‘Coir Board—A Performance Review’ has made a detailed study of
performance of Coir Board.  However, the Committee appreciates the role of coir board and the West Bengal
Government in chalking out action plan for the promotion and proliferation of coir industries in West Bengal and
Andaman & Nicobar on the suggestion and guidelines of the sub-Committee of Department-related Parliamentary
Standing Committee on Industry for the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries during its study visits to Kolkata and Port
Blair.  The Committee desires that the Coir Board should emphasize on the participation of the Private Sector and
NGOs in the development of the Coir industry for optimum utilization of resources, exploitation of untapped export
market, national level project for creation of awareness about Coir Products, up-gradation of technology, brand
endowment, institutionalization and co-operatization of Coir Sector, development of  Coir Industry, adequate stress on
R&D activities, sketching of appropriate marketing strategy for development of  Coir Industry as a whole. 
Resilient Monitoring-mechanism
The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries and the must guide the Coir Board  in the
execution of action plan meant for West Bengal and Andman & Nicobar.  A resilient monitoring mechanism is required
for timely implementation of developmental programmes and for achieving the cherished objectives and mission
underlining each and every scheme.  The Committee recommends that the Coir Board should be guided by the principle
of management by objectives (MBO) and not merely management by activities.  The Committee is of the view that

Khadi and Village Industries as well as the coir industries can facilitate agro and rural base of economic deelopment of
West Bengal.  Therefore, the State Department of industries has to play the pivotal role in guiding the developmental
pattern within a determined economic policy.  By having statistical information at the grass root level, that is at the

root level is desirable.
Potential of Employment generation by coir manufacturing units
            The Committee knows that since Coir Industry is an employment intensive  industry efforts should be
made for searching avenues for generating sustainable and regular employment in the Coir Sector.  
Employment of large number of women in the Coir Sector would fulfill the dreams of empowerment of women
and due share to the economically and socially  backward classes (SCs/STs/OBCs) would enhance the standard of
living of people of weaker section of society.

Collection of Coconut Husk
 
The Committee observes that the Coir Industry gravely suffers from shortage of fiber both white and brown
which sometime has to be imported.  The shortage is mainly because of absence of adequate machinery for collection of
coconut husk from consumers of coconuts in the States in general andWest Bengal and Andaman and Nicobar in
particular.  In the State of West Bengal only the coconut traders or Goldars who collect the husk from the primary
coconut agriculturist market.  The Committee feels that existing arrangements are able to collect only 180 tonnes brown
fiber annually.  With the available husks there is scope of at least 10 modern markets brown fiber manufacturing units to
enhance the production to the level 200 mt per annum.  The Committee, therefore, impress upon the Coir Board and the
Government of West Bengal to make serious efforts for the intensive collection of husk and the system needs to be
institutionalized.
 
            The Committee further impress upon the Government to take a holistic view of the problems being faced
in the Car Nicobar area.  A serious planning is the need of the hour to provide logistic support to the Car
Nicobar Islands.  In order to solve the problem the husk collection, the Committee suggest if it is not possible at
the local level the outside agencies may be brought in to do the job even on commercial line. 
 
            The Committee further recommends that the Government also facilitate the Governments of West Bengal
and  Andaman and Nicobar the logistic support for import of brown fibre from the neighbouring country like Sri
Lanka and Philipines in order to meet the shortage of brown fibre. 
 
Coir Industrial Infrastructure
 
            The Committee recommends that in all major coconut growing areas in the state of West Bengal and the
Union Territory of Andaman & Nicobar, more  clusters may be identified where coir industrial activities may be
established.  In these clusters coir industrial infrastructure should be set up preferably in the private sector. 
Basic techno-economic feasibility/consultancy can be given by the Coir Board for setting up such production
infrastructure.   The Committee further recommends that suitable state level project may be evolved for creation
of awareness about coir products, development of market, production of new and diversified goods of mass
consumption and quality assurance.  A Regional Coir Trade Information Centre may be opened for collection
analyisis its dissemination of information in the state of West Bengal, Andaman & Nicobar, Orissa, Jharkhand
and Bihar.  It should become one of the principal activities of the Coir Board in the region.  Coir sector can be
developed in West Bengal and Andaman & Nicobar through flow of information especially marketing and
technology, which is possible through IT assisted programmes.
 
Technology Up-gradation
 
            The Committee reiterates its observation as made in its 91
st Report that Technological upgradation is a
sina qua non  for the development of the coir industry.  Eco-friendly process and production technologies,
development of cost-effective and efficient machinery with higher productivity, which reduces drudgery will have
to be developed.  Progress in the development of coir machinery has been very tardy.  The Committee
understands that once the potential of the coir industries in West Bengal  and Andaman & Nicobar are being
assessed the next step should be to strengthen  the  R & D projects.  In this regard, the Committee recommends
that special efforts have to be made to identify the potential coir machinery manufacturers who can be supported
with appropriate technology transfer from any available areas.  This activity may be taken up as joint venture
programmes with greater emphasis on private sector participation ensuring faster commercialization. 
 
            The Committee feels that the  State Government of West Bengal and UT  Government of Andaman &
Nicobar in consultation with the Coir Board  should aim at rapid modernization for the overall sustainable
development of the coir industry, focusing on research and development, quality improvement, human resource
development, better marketing of products and welfare support to the needy, particularly the less-privileged
group like women coir workers who constitute more than 80 per cent  of the work-force.  The Committee stresses
the need for greater emphasis on the involvement of the private sector as well as the concerned state
