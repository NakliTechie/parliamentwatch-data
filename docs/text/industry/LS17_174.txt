Prime Minister Rozgar Yojna (PMRY)
 
1.1     The Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) scheme was launched on 2nd October, 1993 in the country. The objective of the scheme is to
assist educated unemployed youth from the weaker section of the society in setting up micro enterprises in all economically viable activities as per
the parameters of the scheme .The scheme is implemented in both urban & rural areas of the country from 1994-95 onwards.
 
1.2       The objective of the Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) is to mitigate the problem of unemployment amongst the
educated unemployed youth belonging to weaker sections of the society through setting up of self employed tiny units.
The annual target of employment generation under the PMRY is as per the target envisaged for the Scheme in the Xth
Plan document of the Planning Commission which envisages to create 50 million person years of gainful employment
opportunities over the Xth Plan. Accordingly, a target of 1.65 million person years over the Xth Plan or an annual target of
employment of 3.3 lakh persons or 2.20 lakh units per annum has been envisaged under the PMRY.

in the recently held review meeting, the State/UTs and banks have been requested to monthly monitor the achievement of
targets set under the Scheme.

3.2       The performance of PMRY in the States of Jharkhand, Bihar, West Bengal and Maharashtra, although slight     improvement has there during
2004-05 as compared to the last year for the corresponding period. The performance of PMRY in these States has been further accelerated after the
visit of the Committee.  It may be seen that the achievement (disbursements as reported by the RBI) in the States of Jharkhand, Bihar, West Bengal
and Maharashtra has gone up from 10, 105, 662 and 3143 Nos. at the time of visit of the Committee to 2130, 4510, 2013 and 5771 Nos. respectively
after the visit of the committee.
 
3.3       The Committee is not at all satisfied with the performance of PMRY in the States of Jharkhand, Bihar, West Bengal and
Maharashtra, although slight     improvement has been  there during 2004-05 as compared to the last year for the corresponding period. 
The Committee observes that it is desirable that Ministry of ARI  in tandem with the Ministry of  Finance, banks and state Government 
should keep monitoring over timely and effectively implementation of Schemes under its purview and accordingly apprise the Committee.
 
  
3.4     The analysis of the performance under PMRY during the last three years based on the reports received from RBI shows that  besides
other reasons, performance under the Scheme in States is positively correlated with the recovery of loan over dues under the Scheme. Other
reasons for failure of Scheme are competition from Central/State Govt. Schemes offering higher subsidy, difficulty in getting the desired
target group due to higher level of per capital income, non viable projects, reluctance on the part of banks to sanction and disburse loan,
militancy, etc.  The Committee, therefore, is of view that the Ministry of ARI in tandem with the banking division, Nationalized Banks and
the State Governments of Bihar, Jharkhand , West Bengal and Maharashtra should take suitable steps ensuring compliance with under -
mentioned yardsticks for better functioning of PMRY in these states:-
(a)
   Minimise the rejection ratio  by banks against no. of application received.
(b)   Avoid the stoppage/delay of disbursement  in  PMRY loans on flimsy   grounds
(c)    Quick disposal of application on the part of DICs  and forwarding the same  to banks
(d)   Coverage  of SC/STs under the scheme as per the  norms  stipulated by Government of India.

4.0       Working of Khadi Industries in West Bengal, Maharashtra, Jharkhand and Bihar
4.1       The financial assistance provided under khadi sales rebate and Interest Subsidy has largely helped the khadi sector
to enhance its performance over the previous year.  While the Interest Subsidy propelled the flow of working capital from
banks, the rebate element helped khadi institutions to boost sales and to get relief from the  blockage of working capital to
a certain extend. The Rebate and Interest subsidy are two important components used for the furtherance of the khadi
programme. The rebate is a promotional expenditure provided on  khadi sales for neutralizing the cost due to higher wage
component thereby to boost its sales. Similarly, the quantum of Interest Subsidy provided on funds availed from
Commercial Banks against ISEC issued by KVIC helps to enhance credit flow to khadi programme.
 
4.2     KVIC has launched an Insurance Scheme for the welfare of khadi workers which brings a sense of social security
among the khadi workers.  The portion of khadi grant is paid to Insurance Company as KVIC’s share of the premium
under this scheme. Similarly, KVIC has launched a Product Development, Design Intervention and Packaging (PRODIP)
for the betterment of khadi sector.  Certain part of khadi grant is utilized for this purpose also which in turn helps for the
furtherance of khadi sector.
 
4.3    During its  study visit to Ranchi, Patna, Kolkata, Mumbai, the Committee took stock of the ground realities of the working of Khadi and Village
Industries in these select States.   The Committee got an opportunity to interact with the representative of NGOs/Industrial Associations of the
respective States and the representative of the respective state Governments.

4.6       West Bengal
 
4.6.1    The Committee was informed that the West Bengal has established its status under Khadi programme for its unique art and craftsmanship.
The climatic condition and abundant local resources have widened the scope for the development of Khadi programme in the State. The districts of
Malda, Murshidabad, Nadia, Birbhum, Bankura, Hooghly, Purulia have the extensive coverage of the Khadi programmes, especially for silk &
Muslin Khadi.
 
 
 
 
4.6.2    The Committee was informed that Mulberry cocoon cultivation in the districts of  Malda, Murshidabad and parts of Birbhum had been
increased adequately and as a result of which, silk reeling and weaving unit have been concentrated in those localities. Further, a good number of
skilled artisans are also available in those area. As such, the scope of silk Khadi activities in those areas have been expanded considerably. Jiagunj,
Raghunathganj, Piarapur area of Murshidabad district is famous for the Garad varieties, while the Bishnupur in Bankura district is famous for the
exclusive Baluachari design. Besides Mulberry silk, Matka Silk, Tassar Silk are produced in good quality and in huge quantity also. In production of
silk Khadi the state contributes 36.37 per cent  of total silk Khadi production in KVI sector and ranks in 1st position in overall performance under
KVIC.
 
4.6.3    The Committee was further informed that another prestigious product of West Bengal is Muslin Khadi. Muslin Khadi has reached to its
fineness to the extent of 500 metric count and there is no competitor of this industry. The main characteristic of this industry is large-scale
employment of women artisans. Around 8,000 women artisans in the state are engaged in this industry. Muslin Khadi Industry has grown in the
districts of Murshidabad, Nadia, Burdwan and Malda.  In production of Muslin Khadi, the state ranks at the top contributing 55.25 per cent of the
total production of Muslin Khadi in KVI sector.

4.10     Bihar
 
4.10.1  Consequent to the   division of Bihar in the year 2000  almost the entire Industrial and rich mineral base along with many of the power
projects of the erstwhile state of Bihar  went to the newly  created   State  of Jharkhand, leaving Bihar an Industrially backward state.  The fact that
Bihar remained largely an agricultural  state   necessitated the  formulation   of a new Indusial Policy, 2003 for Bihar in order to give a boost and
impetus  to industrial growth.  However, with a large agricultural base and available huge manpower resources, Bihar, with a rich historical and 
cultural heritage, has good potential for agro processing, bio technology, tea processing,   software technology, tourism  and other allied industries 
along with handloom, sericulture and handicrafts. Insufficient infrastructure   development, inadequate supply of power, frequent floods, low flow of
capital investment  from outside, adverse C.D. Ratio are some of the major impediments in the industrial growth of the State.  Competition from
well-established products and services from other States and through imports such as through Nepal pose a threat to the viability of the local
industry.  But the State Government is alive to the situation and is trying hard to remove these impediments in conjunction with the Government of
India for creating a more conducive environment for industry and investment.
 
4.10.2  The Committee was apprised that the State Government have taken a series of steps for improving the investment and industrial climate in the
State, which  mainly  included the announcement of the Industrial Policy, 2003; announcement of the Stores Purchase Preference Policy, 2002;
reconstitution of Industrial Area Development Authority and establishment of the amalgamated Bihar Industrial Area Development Authority; and
restructuring of Public Sector Undertakings in the State.
 
4.10.3  Some of the recent developments that have taken place in the State on the industrial side include setting- up  of new units, and opening of
closed industrial  units   such as  Katihar Jute Mill, Rameshwar Jute Mill etc.  The State Government have also set up a Software  Technology   Park
in Patna which has stated  functioning.   On   the  food  processing side the Agri Export Zone has been notified and exports of Litchi has increased  
nearly eleven fold from a level of 35 MT in the year  2000-01 to 396 MT in the year 002-03.  It has also being decided to include Honey and
Vegetables in the Agri Export Zone.  A separate Agri processing side, adequate training ,  EDP courses etc.   are being  organized   to promote the
development of  food processing industries.  Similarly, having regard to the good potential for Tea in Bihar, especially in Purnea,   Kishanganj and

Araria districts, a special   package has been  announced  by the State Government for  promoting the establishment of  Tea  processing unit in the
State.  The first such  tea  processing units  has already commenced production in the State.   Two cluster development programmes have been taken
up under the Government of India  scheme, one in Patna districts for brass and E.P.N.S. items and other in Munger for gun making.
 
4.10.4  The State Government  have given priority to promotion of infrastructure for industry.  Some of the major initiatives include setting - up of

4.10.5  The Committee has been apprised that Bihar has about  1.32 lakhs weavers and nearly 34,000 Handlooms and Power looms Programmes for
development of  clusters of  weavers, establishment of  work-shed, provision of looms and equipments and other measures have been undertaken,   A
comprehensive handloom development project involving a total cost of Rs.8709.80  lakhs  has been prepared and sent to the Government of India for
approval.  Similarly, on the Sericulture side, schemes are being implemented for promotion and development of Tasar, Mulberry and Eri both by the
State  Government and through the Central Silk Board (CSB).  These  include    a composite Tasar and  Eri Development project for  Banka and
Jamui districts  by the CSB at   a  total cost of Rs.1434.00 lakhs and a mulberry project in Purnea and   Kishanganj districts at a cost of Rs.140.00
lakhs.  A Special composite project for sericulture development in the State   for Rs.3709.00 lakhs  has been sent to  the Central Government for
approval.
 
           
4.10.6  The Committee has been apprised that there is a large sugar industry in the State.   The total area under sugarcane in the State during the year
2002-03 was  R.2.54 lakh  hectares with sugarcane  production of   nearly 119 lakh tonnes.  There are  10 sugar mills functioning in the State, all of
them  in the private  sector,  with a combined capacity of nearly   37,700 TCD.  The production of sugar during  the year 2002-03 in   the State was 
4.08 lakhs tonnes.
 
4.10.7  The State Government is thus putting in all efforts for industrial development and promotion   of investment   in the State both in the
manufacturing and the services sector.  The Industrial Policy  2003 provides  the policy framework for the various schemes  that are being
implemented  with this objective.   The  special  package announced in  1997  for industries in the North-East by the Union Government   and for
Utranchal and Himachal Pradesh recently, needs to be provided for Bihar at the earliest.
 
4.10.8  The Committee understand that in post partition phase (since the year 2000),  Bihar  remained  an industrially  backward  state with
insufficient and poor infrastructure development , inadequate supply  of power, low capital flow, adverse C.D. ratio.  The announcement of 
new industrial  policy has not made any observable  headway.  The Committee, therefore, is of the view  that  the Central Government  in
tandem  with the state government has to start  from the stretch  for launching effective   industrial  growth and development  of the  state. 
Since the state  has   largely been remained  an agricultural state.  A   new beginning    can be  made in the areas of Agro -rural  industries
by initiating following course of action:-
 
(i)
                 Due  impetus to anciliarisation  of Jute industry in Purnea, Katihar, Araria, and Kishanganj to extract  benefits of opening up of
Katihar Jute Mills;
(ii)               De-centralisation of setting up of software technology  park in each district of the state;
(iii)             Promotion and proliferation of  tea processing units in Kishanganj, Purnea and Araria;
(iv)             Institutional and logistic  support to Agro -Rural Industries, specially  Sugar Mills and its  backward linkages;
(v)               co-ordination with Central Silk Board (CSB) for development of TAsar, Mulberry, Eri development in Bhagalpur , Banka,
Jamui, Purnea and Kishanganj;
(vi)             Promotion of Handloom and powerloms programmes for development  of clusters of weavers, establishment of work shed   at
Panchayat level of each districts.  Government may approve the composite schemes  for  development   of Handloom and
sericulture in the state; and
(vii)           Having regard to the fact that Bihar is an industrially backward  state, the  special package announced in  1997  for industries in
the North-East by the Union Government  and for Utranchal and Himachal Pradesh recently, needs to be provided for Bihar at
the earliest.

4.10.11            The Committee is of view that Bihar, after the partition and separation of Jharkhand, remained basically an agricultural
state, that too not very organised, and the state being bereft of any large scale or even medium scale industries, the only option that remains
with the state  is the accelerated promotion and proliferation of agro - rural industries and Small Scale and Medium Enterprises which have
the benefit of requiring  low investment and providing vast potential for employment generation.  Therefore, the Committee  strongly

4.18     Working of REGP in West Bengal, Jharkhand, Maharashtra and Bihar
 
4.18.1  As per the recommendations of High Power Committee, KVIC introduced the Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) for
financing viable. Village Industries Projects.  Under this scheme, Margin Money @ 25% of the project cost up to Rs. 10 lakhs and 10% of the
difference of project cost up to  Rs. 25 lakhs over and above Rs. 10 lakhs is provided for setting up viable Village Industries projects.   For certain
categories of entrepreneurs such as women, physically handicapped, ex-servicemen and those belonging to SC/ST/OBC, minority communities,
North Easter Region, including Sikkim, hill border and tribal areas, Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshdweep etc. margin money is @ 30% for
projects up to Rs. 10 lakhs.  The Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) is implemented with the active involvement of 27 nationalized
scheduled commercial banks, their Regional Rural Banks.  Now this programme has deeply percolated  its roots in the countryside and got unique
identity.

effort for effective implementation of REGP in West Bengal , Jharkhand, Maharashtra and Bihar. Further from 2002-03, the payment of margin

Organizational overhauling and  institutional framework for better implementation of REGP especially in Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal
and Maharashtra
 
 
            The Committee was apprised that Small Projects upto five lakh rupees  are being encouraged, however, the Committee desires that 
the  bank-wise/District-wise targets should be allocated and Sector (Group) wise implementation of REGP be encouraged. Further Social
Sector-wise distribution of target, Coverage of new areas uniform coverage of regions/industries, Organizing  more exhibitions, Introduction
of PRODIP Scheme, Organizing Buyer’s Seller’s meet, involvement of Self-Help Groups in implementation of the scheme must be
encouraged. The Committee desires that  the role of Ministry of ARI must be  more effective in implementation of the scheme in terms of
monitoring, coordinating, providing necessary guidance and liaisoning with other Ministry and organizations especially in Bihar,
Jharkhand, West Bengal and Maharashtra, where aggressive  popularization of the REGP is required.
 
(Para4.24)  
 
Formulization of  Rural Entrepreneur  Development Programmes
 
 
            Since the State KVI Boards are major agencies involved in the implementation of REGP, the Committee recommends that the
concentrated efforts should be made for  strengthening of infrastructure/organizational structure because the same often prove hinders in
the expeditious implementation of the scheme.  The Committee desires that the Government should make efforts for coordinating with the 
KVIBs and KVIBs  in Bihar, Jharkhand, Orissa and Maharashtra must be revitalized by their respective State Governments.
 
(Para4.26)  
              The Committee is also of the view that efforts should be made for formulation of Rural Entrepreneur  Development Programmes
especially in Bihar, Jharkhand  and West Bengal where large chunk of population dwell in the rural areas and where the KVIC's
enterprenuer promotional schemes and training programmes have not seen the light of the day. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
effective Action Plan should be chalked out and result- oriented implementation of the programmes must be monitored.   The Committee
further recommends that an all out efforts must be made for changing the mind-set of financial institutions to boost REGP and setting up of
monitoring cells at Regional /Zonal offices to monitor the progress of REGP.
