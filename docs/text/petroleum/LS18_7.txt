PART I 
Introductory                     
India’s petroleum, and natural gas industry stands as the backbone of its energy 
sector, fueling the country’s rapid economic growth and urbanization. As the third-
largest consumer of energy in the world, India’s requirement for hydrocarbons continues 
to surge. Its total primary energy demand is expected to almost double, increasing from 
around 22 million barrels of oil equivalent per day in 2024 to about 43.6 million barrels 
of oil equivalent per day by 2050.  As per International Energy Agency (IEA), India will 
contribute over 40% of the global increase in oil demand and about 8% of the growth in 
natural gas demand between 2024 and 2025.  
 As the country’s energy demand rises faster than that of any other major 
economy, guaranteeing secure, affordable and reliable oil and gas supplies is vital. 
However, India’s energy journey is unfolding at a time of unprecedented flux in global 
markets where geopolitical tensions, supply disruptions and climate imperatives coexist 
and interact in complex ways.   
The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, at the helm of policy formulation and 
sectoral oversight, has introduced various transformative initiatives to boost investment, 
enhance domestic production and promote equitable access to clean fuels.  As per the 
Ministry, India holds proven oil reserves of around 4.5 billion barrels and natural gas 
reserves of nearly 1.3 trillion cubic meters. Despite these resources, the nation remains 
reliant on imports for over 85% of its crude oil needs, underscoring the imperative for 
both increased domestic exploration and strategic diversification of supply sources. 
 Against this backdrop, the allocation for the Ministry during the financial year 
2026-27 has been examined in the Report. The Ministry presented their Demands for 
Grants presented to the Parliament on 10th February, 2026 under Demand No. 76.

IV. Direct Benefit Transfer for LPG (DBTL) 
1.10 DBTL - Pahal Scheme was launched on 15th November 2014 with the approval of 
Cabinet vide note dated 18.10.2014, initially in 54 districts and subsequently extended 
to 622 districts from 01.01.2015. This initiative of the Government was aimed at 
rationalizing subsidies based on an approach to cut subsidy leakages, but not subsidies 
per se. As on 01.01.2026 PSU OMCs (IOCL, BPCL and HPCL) together have 33.21 
crore active LPG customers in the domestic category who are being served by 25,481 
LPG distributors. PSU OMCs enrolled 112.5 lakh new domestic customers in FY 2023-
24. More than 30.61 Crore LPG consumers have joined the Scheme as on 01.01.2026.  
 
1.11 When the Committee desired to know about the extant subsidy on Petrol, LPG 
and Kerosene and details of the subsidy being provided presently per cylinder of LPG 
and per litre of PDS Kerosene, the Ministry submitted the following information:

Marketing Companies (OMCs) take appropriate decision on pricing of petrol and 
diesel in line with their international product prices, exchange rate, tax structure,

subsidy on petrol.

V. LPG Connection to Poor Household (Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana)  
1.16 Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY) was launched in May, 2016 with an 
objective to provide deposit free LPG connection to adult women from poor households 
across the country. The target to release 8 crore connections under PMUY was 
achieved in September 2019. To cover the remaining poor households, Ujjwala 2.0 was 
launched in August 2021 with a target to release 1 crore additional PMUY connections, 
which was achieved in January 2022. Subsequently, the Government decided to 
release 60 lakh more LPG connections under Ujjwala 2.0 and the target of 1.60 crore 
Ujjwala 2.0 connections was also achieved during December 2022. Further, 
Government approved release of additional 75 lakh connections under PMUY Scheme 
for the period FY 2023-24 to 2025-26 which has already been achieved during July 
2024. Government has recently approved the release of 25 lakh additional LPG 
connections under PMUY during Financial Year 2025-26 to achieve saturation of LPG 
access in the country. To further improve access, the eligibility criteria have been 
simplified to “Adult women from poor households based on submission of a Deprivation 
declaration”. As on 01.01.2026, there were 10.43 crore PMUY connections across the 
country.  

10 
 
1.17 On being asked about the number of connections that have been released to 
households under this scheme during the current financial year vis-a-vis the target for 
FY 2026-27, the Ministry submitted the following information:  
  
“PMUY Scheme was initiated in May, 2016 and as on 01.01.2026, a total no. 
of 10.43 Crore connections have been released. For the current financial year 
2025-26, Government approved the release of 25 lakhs additional connections 
under PMUY in Sep, 2025, out of which OMCs have released 10.35 lakh 
connections as on 01.01.2026.”

PART-II 
OBSERVATIONS / RECOMMENDATIONS 
 
1. Need for Prudent Budgeting 
 
The Committee note that during the FY 2023-24, against a total Budget 
Estimate (BE) of Rs.41,007.72 crore, the Revised Estimate (RE) was reduced to 
Rs.14,757.01 crore and the Actual Expenditure (AE) stood at Rs.14,330.21 crore, 
which is only about 34.9 per cent of BE but 97.1 per cent of RE. The Committee 
observe that in 2024-25, the situation somewhat improved with total BE of 
Rs.15,930.26 crore, RE of Rs.20,305.61 crore and AE of Rs.19,887.49 crore, 
resulting in AE standing at about 124.84 per cent of BE and 97.94 per cent of RE.  
 
Further, in 2025-26, with an enhanced BE of Rs.26,702.93 crore and a higher 
RE of Rs.32,934.47 crore, AE was Rs.17,074.19 crore, which corresponds to about 
63.94 per cent of BE and 51.84 per cent of RE.  The under-utilisation is also seen 
in the capital segment where AE of Rs.281.89 crore is 2.28 per cent of the capital 
BE of Rs.12,323.13 crore and 10.83 per cent of capital RE of Rs.2,601.26 crore. 
 
For FY 2026-27, the Ministry has proposed a total BE of Rs.37,653.22 crore, 
with Revenue BE at Rs.37,414.77 crore and a reduced Capital BE of Rs.238.45 
crore, indicating a significant increase in revenue allocation and a marked 
reduction in capital provision compared to previous years. The Committee are of 
the view that the ministry may adopt sound budgeting and forecasting models in 
order to minimize the wide variations in fund allocations and help in reducing 
last-minute surrenders and curtailment or topping-up of provisions at the RE 
stage. 
 
 
2. Enhanced Budgetary Support for Capital Expenditure 
 
The Committee note that the Ministry’s pattern of capital expenditure has 
been marked by volatility and significant under-utilisation of budgeted 

34 
 
provisions. In 2023–24, against a Budget Estimate of Rs.35,508.98 crore under the 
Capital Section, the Actual Expenditure was only Rs.40 crore (0.11 percent of BE), 
primarily because major heads such as ‘Capital support to OMC’ (BE Rs.30,000 
crore) and ‘Payment to ISPRL for Crude Oil Reserve’ (BE Rs.5,000 crore) recorded 
zero Actual Expenditure.  A similar pattern recurs in 2025–26 where the Capital 
BE of Rs.12,323.13 crore translated into an Actual Expenditure of only Rs.281.89 
crore (2.28 percent of BE), with key strategic heads like ISPRL crude oil reserves 
(BE Rs.5,597 crore) and transfers to the Oil Industry Development Fund (BE 
Rs.5,697 crore) again remaining largely unspent. There is reduction in the Capital 
BE to Rs.238.45 crore in 2026–27 against a Revenue BE of Rs.37,414.77 crore. 
Given the fact that Capital expenditure should be on the higher side as it 
creates long-lived public assets and delivers strong returns in terms of economic 
expansion and employment generation, the Committee recommend that the 
Ministry may endeavour to provide government budgetary support for capital 
expenditure in order to fructify the Union Government’s strategy of using 
infrastructure-led investment as the main driver of medium and long term growth.    
 
 
3. Direct Benefit Transfer for LPG 
 
The Committee note that expenditure under the Direct Benefit Transfer for 
LPG (DBTL) Scheme depends on the actual claims submitted by Oil Marketing 
Companies (OMCs) towards net under-recoveries which in turn reflect 
international prices and net ocean freight. In 2024-25, extreme volatility in net 
ocean freight led to some negative bills from OMCs, resulting in a much lower net 
claim than in the previous year, so that against a BE of Rs. 1,500 crore, reduced 
to Rs. 500 crore at RE, actual expenditure was only Rs. 375.27 crore.  For 2025-26, 
an outlay of Rs. 1,500 crore at BE and Rs. 1,000 crore at RE has been provided 
but actual expenditure up to 31.12.2025 is nil because the Scheme is under 
appraisal by the Department of Expenditure (DoE) as it transitions to the next 
Finance Commission cycle. The Ministry has submitted a comprehensive 
proposal on 01.10.2025 for fresh appraisal and continuation of the DBTL Scheme, 
covering both existing commitments and future requirements, and has 
temporarily withheld settlement of part of the OMCs’ bills pending finalisation of 

35 
 
this appraisal, even though DBT transfers to beneficiaries have already taken 
place. Taking into account the accumulated but unsettled past claims of OMCs as 
well as expected future claims, an allocation of Rs. 1,500 crore has been provided 
in BE 2026-27 by the Ministry. 
 
The Committee, therefore, urge the Ministry to coordinate with DoE to 
secure early approval so that the pending claims can be cleared in a timely 
manner and the expenditure position for 2025-26 accurately reflects the 
committed liabilities and the 2026-27 BE allocation is justified with expeditious 
settlements thereby safeguarding OMC liquidity and ensuring uninterrupted 
delivery of subsidised LPG to consumers.  The Ministry has stated that 
corresponding DBT transfers have already been executed and credited to 
beneficiaries’ bank accounts.  The Committee may be furnished the details in this 
regard.  
 
The Committee also opine that in order to cushion LPG subsidies from 
extreme volatility in net ocean freight, the Ministry may explore the feasibility of 
developing a freight management framework that narrows year-to-year swings in 
net ocean freight, thereby smoothing DBTL subsidy claims, enabling more 
realistic BE and RE formulation and avoiding large negative adjustments or 
abrupt underspending that hinder fiscal planning. The Committee further suggest 
that the Ministry, in consultation with Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell 
(PPAC), OMCs and the Department of Expenditure, devise this framework through 
suitable contractual guidelines and monitoring arrangements. 
 
4.  LPG Connection to Poor Households (Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana) 
 
The Committee note that Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY), launched 
in May 2016 to provide deposit-free LPG connections to poor women, has largely 
achieved saturation of LPG access, with 10.43 crore connections issued by 
01.01.2026 and another 25 lakh sanctioned for 2025-26, of which 10.35 lakh are 
already released. Budget utilisation has strengthened, with 100% utilisation of 
Revised Estimates in 2023-24 and 2024-25 and expected full utilisation in 2025-26 
through settlement of pending claims of Rs. 4,958 crore.  The Committee also 

36 
 
note that to improve budgeting and accounting, three separate heads have been 
created viz., (i) new PMUY connections, (ii) targeted subsidies to PMUY 
beneficiaries and (iii) PME/IEC ( Publicity, Monitoring & Evaluation/Information, 
Education and Communication) Campaign expenses.  The Committee find this a 
welcome step towards greater transparency.  While appreciating the 
achievements of the Ministry on PMUY, the Committee wish to underline that the 
main challenge now is sustaining LPG usage, especially in rural areas where over 
80% of PMUY connections are located. 
 
According to the Ministry, average per-connection consumption has 
increased from about 3.6–3.7 refills in 2021-22/2022-23 to roughly 4.8 refills 
(pro-rated) in 2025-26, with most States showing a similar upward trend, 
suggesting that the Rs 200, increased to Rs 300 later on, per-cylinder subsidy (up 
to nine refills) and programme outreach are having a positive impact.  However, 
the Committee are of the view that average usage of only 4–5 refills a year 
indicates that many households still do not rely on LPG as their primary cooking 
fuel throughout the year and inter-State differences—with some States at 5 or 
more refills and others at 3 to 4—show persistent pockets of low usage despite 
near-universal connections.  
 
The Ministry has stated that consumption of domestic LPG by households 
depends on several factors like food habits, household size, cooking habits, 
preferences, price, availability of alternate fules etc.  However, the Committee are 
aware that there is widespread evidence from independent evaluations that high 
effective refill cost, cash-flow constraints at the time of purchase, continued 
availability of solid fuels and service-delivery issues including lack of doorstep 
delivery and transaction costs may be acting as major impediments to sustained 
LPG use among low-income households. The Committee are of the view that 
unless these issues of affordability and refill behaviour are addressed 
systematically, PMUY may not become an effective instrument for durable 
transition to clean cooking fuels. The Committee, therefore recommend that:  
  

37 
 
 
(i) Considering the lower number of refills, the committee recommend that the 
Ministry may explore the feasibility of introduction of a targeted expansion of the 
LPG subsidy components, wherein regions which have lowest refill rates could 
be given a higher-than-average subsidy amount; 
(ii) sustained and affordable LPG availability among existing beneficiaries, 
particularly in rural and low-income areas may be ensured. The Ministry may use 
granular refill-behaviour data for the 10.43 crore PMUY consumers to identify the 
households that, despite holding a connection, are unable to use LPG regularly 
due to refill costs, liquidity constraints or local supply-chain issues.  The Ministry 
may, therefore, explore to design targeted interventions to ensure durable and 
meaningful access to clean cooking; 
(iii)  The Ministry may strengthen their monitoring framework for PMUY; and  
(iv)  The service-delivery aspects of PMUY may be substantially strengthened 
with particular emphasis on rural areas.

7.  Indradhanush Gas Grid Limited

ONGC, a Maharatna Schedule “A” CPSE under the administrative 
control of MoPNG, is a global energy major with a fully integrated 
presence across the entire energy value chain. As India’s largest 
Exploration & Production (E&P) company, ONGC has played a 
pivotal role in the nation’s energy security-having discovered 
seven of the country’s eight producing basins, accounting for 
nearly 83% of India’s hydrocarbon reserves and contributing 
approximately 63% of domestic hydrocarbon production. 
  
Through its subsidiaries and JVs, ONGC has built a diversified 
and robust footprint across upstream, midstream, downstream, 
power and emerging energy segments. Its wholly owned 
subsidiary, ONGC Videsh Limited (OVL), a Navratna Schedule 
“A” CPSE, serves as its international arm, driving global E&P 
activities. ONGC also maintains a strong downstream presence 
through its refinery and petrochemical subsidiaries, including 
Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (HPCL), Mangalore 
Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL), and ONGC Petro 
Additions Limited (OPAL). In line with its commitment to 
sustainability and energy transition, ONGC has further expanded 
into the green energy domain through its wholly owned subsidiary, 
ONGC Green Limited (OGL). 
 
ONGC has been able to maintain stable Crude oil & Natural gas 
production during FY’25. The standalone Crude production for 
FY’25 stood at 19.558 MMT, reflecting a 0.9% increase over the 
previous year. The standalone Natural gas production for FY’25 
stood at 19.654 BCM. 
2 ONGC Videsh

development, and production of oil and gas outside India. It holds 
participating interests in 32 assets across 15 countries and is 
India’s second-largest petroleum company after parent ONGC 
with respect to both reserves and production. 
  
During FY2024-25, ONGC Videsh produced 10.278 Million 
Metric Tons of Oil Equivalent (MMtoe) of oil and gas, which is 
equivalent to about 15.9% of India’s domestic oil and gas 
production.

General of 
Hydrocarbons 
(DGH) 
The objective of DGH is to promote sound management of the 
Indian petroleum and natural gas resources having a balanced 
regard for the environment, safety, technological and economic 
aspects of the petroleum activity. DGH has been entrusted with 
multifaceted functions and responsibilities, which, inter-alia, 
include: 
 
a) Providing technical expertise and advice to the Ministry of 
Petroleum and Natural Gas on pertinent issues related to the 
exploration and optimal exploitation of hydrocarbons in India and 
abroad by national oil companies. 
b) Scrutinizing and assessing the exploration programs of 
companies holding Petroleum Exploration Licenses (PELs) under 
the oilfields (Regulation and Development) Act, 1948 and the 
Petroleum and Natural Gas Rules, 1959, with a view to advising 
the Government on the adequacy of these programs.  
c) Re-evaluating the estimated and discovered hydrocarbon 
reserves in coordination with the operating companies.  
d) Advising the Government on the allocation of exploration 
acreages to companies and matters concerning the relinquishment 
of such acreages.  
e) Reviewing the development plans proposed by operating 
companies for commercial discoveries of hydrocarbon reserves, 
assessing the feasibility of these plans and the proposed 
exploitation rates, and making recommendations to the 
Government.  
f) Concurrently reviewing and monitoring the management of 
petroleum reservoirs by operating companies and advising on any 
corrective actions required to ensure optimal exploitation of 
reserves and conservation of petroleum resources.  
g) Regulating the storage, maintenance and preservation of data 
and samples related to petroleum exploration, drilling, and 
production, and preparing data packages for the allocation of 
exploration acreages to companies.  
h) Undertaking other incidental functions and any other 
responsibilities as assigned by the Government from time to time. 
19 Petroleum and 
Natural Gas 
Regulatory Board 
(PNGRB) 
PNGRB was constituted under the PNGRB Act, 2006. The Act 
provides for the  establishment of PNGRB to protect the interests 
of consumers and  entities engaged in specified activities relating 
to  petroleum, petroleum products and natural gas and to promote 
competitive markets and for matters connected therewith or 
incidental thereto. The Board under the Act has to regulate the 
refining, processing, storage, transportation, distribution, 
marketing and sale of petroleum, petroleum products and natural 
gas excluding production of crude oil and natural  gas so as to 
ensure uninterrupted and adequate supply of petroleum,  petroleum 
products and natural gas in all parts of the country. 
20 Indian Strategic 
Petroleum 
Reserves Limited 
India is heavily dependent on import of crude oil to support the 
economic activities and growth and to meet the energy needs of its 
citizens, these SPRs would serve as buffer to deal with any 

59 
 
(ISPRL) situation of supply chain disruptions, especially due to external 
reasons. Such emergency reserves would enable the country to 
keep its refineries fed with crude oil even in the face of disruption 
of crude oil imports and ensure uninterrupted supplies of 
petroleum products across the country. In exceptional 
circumstances, the buffer stock of crude oil could be used to 
partially absorb an abnormal spike, emergencies such as global 
supply shortages or geopolitical crises in the global oil prices. 
 
Government of India (GoI) in the interest of meeting the strategic 
objective of country’s energy security, decided on 7th January 
2004 to construct Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPRs) facilities. 
To implement the decision of Govt. of India, under phase-I, to 
construct SPR facilities, a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) Indian 
Strategic Petroleum Reserve Limited (ISPRL) was established on 
16th June 2004 as a subsidiary of Indian Oil Corporation Limited. 
Subsequently in January 2006, ISPRL became a wholly owned 
subsidiary of Oil Industry Development Board (OIDB).
