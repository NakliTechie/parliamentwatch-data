I.  INTRODUCTORY 
 
1.     In the context of technology’s growing role in economic development, skilled 
manpower is essential to drive progress. The Ministry of Skill Development and 
Entrepreneurship (MSDE) collaborates with Central Government Ministries, State 
Governments, industry, non-profits, and academia to synergize and accelerate 
skilling efforts. 
 
2.   This year marks MSDE’s eleventh successful year since its inception in 2014. 
Initially established as the Department of Skill Development under the Ministry of 
Sports & Youth Affairs on 31st July 2014, it was later notified as the Ministry of 
Skill Development & Entrepreneurship on 9th November 2014. Annually, more 
than one crore youth benefit from the Skill India program—a mission aimed at 
equipping youth with skills for better livelihoods. 
 
3.  Over time, MSDE has taken on additional roles through various organizations 
and skilling schemes, including: 
 
Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) 
Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Kendra (PMKK) 
Jan Shikshan Sansthan (JSS) 
Directorate General of Training (DGT) 
National Institute for Entrepreneurship & Small Business Development 
(NIESBUD) 
Indian Institute of Entrepreneurship (IIE) 
National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) 
National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET) 
 
4.    India, as one of the world’s fastest-growing economies, recognizes the need to 
expand opportunities within service sectors and integrate training courses with 
apprenticeship programs. In December 2014, a significant reform of the 
Apprenticeship Act paved the way for the introduction of the National 
Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS) in August 2016. NAPS strategically 
incentivizes employers to engage more apprentices in their workforce, resulting in 
remarkable advancements in apprenticeship enrollment and active participation 
in skill development over the past ten years. 
 
5.     While skill development is crucial, its impact remains limited unless 
accompanied by employment generation. By fostering the growth of small and 
medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), the government plays a pivotal role in creating 
additional employment opportunities and engaging the entire workforce. 
 
6.     MSDE’s mandate includes integrating entrepreneurship orientation modules 
into the Employability, Entrepreneurship, and Life Skills component of PMKVY 
courses. Additionally, ITI courses already incorporate entrepreneurship modules 
within employability skills. To promote a culture of entrepreneurship among 
youth, the Ministry instituted the National Entrepreneurship Awards (NEA) in 
2016, recognizing and honoring outstanding entrepreneurs and ecosystem 

 
2 
 
builders. These awards serve as models of excellence for others to emulate and 
improve upon.   
7.   An analysis of the financial and physical performance of the Ministry of Skill 
Development & Entrepreneurship and its Attached and Subordinate Organisation 
in the process of implementation of certain Schemes/Projects has been made in 
the Report.

“In FY. 2025-26, the general demand under Demand No. 92 of Ministry of

Ministry of Finance in FY. 2023-24, the scheme “Skill India Programme’ has 
to be financed through the ‘Madhyamik and Uchchtar Shiksha Kosh

financed through proceeds from ‘Health and Education Cess’. This reserve 
fund is operated towards financing the specific schemes under School 
Education, Literacy and Higher Education of Ministry of Education. As skill 
development constitutes an integral component of human development, it 
has been decided by the Ministry of Finance to also utilize proceeds from 
‘Health and Education Cess’ for financing Schemes under Ministry of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship from FY. 2023-24. In order to enable 
this financing of ‘Skill India Programme’ through ‘MUSK’, Rs. 2200.00 Cr 
has been marked as transfer entry in the Demand for Grants resulting into 
gross demand of Rs. 8300.10 Cr for FY.2025-26. However, to finance ‘Skill 
India Programme’ i.e.  Rs. 2200.00 Cr would be reversed showing the 
reverse entry as ‘Amount met from MUSK’ resulting in net demand of Rs. 
6100.10 Cr for FY. 2025-26.  The demand of Rs. 6100.10 Cr also includes 
Rs. 3000.00 Cr for the ‘New ITI Upgradation Scheme’. This scheme with 
total outlay of Rs. 60,000 Cr for five years has been announced in the 
regular budget of FY. 2024-25.”

13. After pointing out that the budget estimates for the year 2024–25 was Rs. 
4520.00 crore, which was reduced to Rs. 3300.83 crore at RE stage, while the 
Ministry was able to spend only Rs. 1340.04 crore (as on 15.01.2025), which is 
29.64% of BE and 40.59% of RE, the Committee desired to know how the 
Ministry propose to utilize the remaining amount of Rs. 1,960.79 crore in the last 
two months of the current fiscal, the areas where savings are anticipated by the 
end of 31st March, 2025 and the precise reasons thereof. In response, the 
Ministry submitted as under:-  

 
7 
 
“The Cabinet's approval of the "Skill India Programme" on 07.02.2025 has 
resolved a major spending issue. Following the scheme's approval, Rs.830 Cr has 
been released in February 2025 under this scheme. Consequently, as of 
18.02.2025, the Ministry's expenditure stands at Rs.2226.13 Cr, which is 49.25% 
of BE and 67.44% of RE. Thus, Rs.1074.69 Cr remains for utilization in the last 
two months with respect to RE-2024-25. During the remaining period of this FY, 
another around Rs. 598 Cr is expected to be released in ‘Skill India Programme’ 
alone, followed by around Rs. 180 Cr in SANKALP scheme, apart from other 
expenditure.   
The ‘National Scheme for ITIs Upgradation’ has also been recommended by 
the EFC in its meeting held on 9/13.01.2025. The draft Cabinet Note has received 
the approval of Finance Minister on 19.02.2025. Looking to the limited time 
available in the financial year, the original allocations of the scheme i.e Rs. 
1000.00 Cr has been revised to Rs. 294.00 Cr at the RE stage. Further savings 
may also happen in the ‘National Scheme for ITIs Upgradation’ depending upon 
when the Cabinet approval is received. Pending Cabinet approval, the Department 
of Expenditure (DoE) has permitted the release of up to Rs.10.00 Cr only for 
preparatory activities related to the scheme.  
The Ministry expects to incur significant expenditure by the end of this 
Financial Year. Therefore, the Ministry has requested the Ministry of Finance to 
exempt it from the prescribed ceilings of expenditure of 33% in the last Qtr and 
15% the last month.”   
 
14. The BE for Skill India Programme is Rs. 2685.64 crore, which has been 
reduced to Rs. 2318.000 crore at RE stage and the actual expenditure as on 
15.01.2025 is Rs. 895.21 crore, which is 33.33% of BE and 38.61% of RE. 
Observing that the Cabinet has recently granted its approval for ‘Skill India 
Programme’, the Committee enquired as how do the Ministry propose to utilize 
the balance amount of funds amounting to Rs. 1790.43 crore  during the 
remaining part of 2024-25, the Ministry stated as under:-  
 
“The 'Skill India Programme' has been approved by the Cabinet on 
07.02.2025. Following approval, the Ministry has made further releases under the 
scheme. As of 18.02.2025, against the RE of Rs. 2318.00 Cr, the expenditure 
under the scheme is Rs. 1719.57 Cr, which represents 64% of the BE and 74% of 
the RE, of the scheme. There are approximately Rs. 350 Cr of committed liabilities 
remaining under the scheme, which are likely to accumulate up to Rs. 880 Cr by 
31.03.2025, based on ongoing training batches. Therefore, the entire amount of 
the RE under the scheme is expected to be utilized by the end of this financial 
year.” 
15.  It was noted that there was persistent increase in the BE during the last 
three years viz. 17.28% in 2023-24, 28.50% in 2024-25 and 34.95% in 2025-26. 
However since 2022-23, every year BE was reduced at RE stage, which was also 
not fully utilised.  When asked about the reasons  for the consistent reduction in 
RE as well as shortfall in Actual Expenditure during the last three fiscals,  the 
Ministry stated as follows:-     

 
8 
 
“The Budget Estimates (BE) are proposed based on the approved outlays of 
the Schemes, as well as the anticipated expenditure. During the pre-budget 
exercise, if it is determined that the expenditure under any scheme or unit has 
not progressed as planned, the Ministry proposes to reduce the allocations at the 
Revised Estimates (RE) stage. Additionally, the Ministry of Finance conducts its 
own assessment and sets ceilings according to their evaluation. Over the past 
three years, the variations in BE, RE, and Expenditure within this Ministry have 
primarily been due to the non-approval of its major scheme 'Skill India 
Programme'. 
  The 'Skill India Programme' is the Ministry's largest scheme, comprising 60-
65% of its total budget. The EFC recommended it on 27.07.2022, and the draft 
Cabinet Note was moved on 23.08.2022. Anticipating approval, projection for RE 
FY 2022-23 and BE 2023-24 was made and approved by the Ministry of Finance. 
Pending Cabinet approval, looking to the crucial need for continuing with skilling 
activities, the Ministry requested permission from DoE to release funds on 
22.11.2022, which was granted on 29.11.2022. Expenditure began in December 
2022, but due to the limited available time in the FY led to low spending. The 
Ministry could spent only Rs. 1387.85 Cr  in FY 2022-23, against a budget 
estimate of Rs. 2999.00 Cr and a revised estimate of Rs. 1901.71 Cr. 
  
In an effort to improve and accurately depict the expenditure, the Ministry 
rationalized its Schemes structure starting from FY 2023-24. As a result, in FY 
2023-24, the Ministry's expenditure has reached Rs. 2146.92 Cr, showing a 
significant improvement from the previous year. However, the expenditure did not 
meet expectations because the biggest scheme ‘Skill India Programme’ still could 
not receive Cabinet approval.The Ministry had to request the DoE from time to 
time for fund releases pending scheme approval. The non-approval of the Scheme 
and the staggered permission for fund releases led to lower than expected 
expenditure in FY 2023-24 also.’ 
 
In FY 2024-25, the DoE permitted only the first-quarter release for the 'Skill 
India Programme' amounting to Rs. 895.21 crore. Thereafter, the releases were 
frozen pending Cabinet approval. Consequently, by 15.01.2025, the Ministry could 
spend only Rs. 1,340.04 crore against a BE of Rs 4520 crore. Following the 
Cabinet's approval of the Scheme on 07.02.2025, the Ministry promptly released 
additional funds under the scheme. Consequently, as of 18.02.2025, the 
expenditure of the Ministry has reached Rs. 2,226.13 crore, surpassing last year’s 
total. The Ministry has asked for relaxation of the prescribed ceilings of 
expenditure of 33% in the last Qtr and 15% the last month of the financial year 
and on approval anticipates spending upto its RE ceiling.”

A. PRADHAN MANTRI KAUSHAL VIKAS YOJANA (PMKVY4.0)  
28.  MSDE is implementing its flagship scheme Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas 
Yojana (PMKVY), since 2015, for imparting skill development training through 
Short-Term Training (STT) and up-skilling and re-skilling through Recognition of 
Prior Learning (RPL) to youth across the country. It aims to stimulate and 
advance skill development across the country by delivering high-quality, short-
duration training programs in job roles as per the industry requirements. Since 
its inception, three iterations of PMKVY have been successfully implemented, with 
the current phase, PMKVY 4.0, underway. As on 09.01.2025, more than 1.60 
crore candidates have been trained under the PMKVY.  
 
29.  PMKVY 4.0 (2022-26) has been designed with various new elements 
including On-the-job training (OJT), Integration and mainstreaming of vocational 
education with general education as per ‘National Education Policy’ (NEP) 2020, 
offering training in futuristic and new age courses for Industrial Revolution 4.0, 
expanding the delivery network for skill development through cross utilization of 
resources by setting Skill India Centres (SICs) in Institutes of National Repute, 
Schools, Colleges, Universities, Technical Institutions, etc. Under PMKVY 4.0, 
training is being imparted in Short-Term Training (STT), Recognition of Prior 
Learning (RPL) and Special Projects (SP). 
 
The broad design principles of PMKVY 4.0 are: 
 
i. Customized courses with high employability and duration up-to 210 hours. 
ii. Short-Term Training (STT) are by educational institutions (Colleges, 
Schools and Government Institutions), including ITIs to synergize 
vocational and educational streams and increase network of quality 
training providers by increasing partnership of educational and vocational 
institutions. 
iii. Skill courses are selected as per Industry demand. 
iv. Special Projects for identified areas and groups in agriculture, handicraft 
and other traditional sectors. 
v. Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) platform has been launched for capturing the 
entire training lifecycle. 
vi. Enhanced industry-connect through greater reliance on On-the-Job 
Training in short-term training.  

 
16 
 
vii. Emphasis on re-skilling, and up-skilling under Recognition of Prior 
Learning (RPL) through innovation in assessment and better monitoring. 
viii. Simplified procedures for reducing compliance burden by enlisting multiple 
Assessment Agencies and Awarding Bodies, re-engineering IT systems 
based on requirement of stakeholders, etc. 
ix. Creation of a national pool of trainers and assessors to ensure their 
availability and efficient implementation of training and faster assessment. 
x. Flexibility in course curriculum by introducing courses in partnership with 
industry, other Ministries/Departments, etc. 
xi. Focus on online/digital/blended skilling for effective learning, convenience, 
and outreach. 
xii. Emphasis on community engagement through training in job roles 
addressing the skill needs of the informal sector or basic self-employed. 
xiii. Focus on New Age Skills like Industry 4.0, Web 3.0, AI/ML, AR/VR, 
Climate Change, Circular Economy, Green Economy, and Energy 
Transition.

During these phases, there were various challenges affecting placements, including 
as follows: 
 Candidate Choice and Willingness: The decision to accept job opportunities

 Non-Placement Linked Initiatives under PMKVY 3.0: About 43% of trainings

taken to improve their functioning. In reply, the Ministry stated as follow:-      
              
“A robust monitoring framework has been put in place under the Pradhan 
Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) 4.0 scheme to ensure strict monitoring. To 
monitor the effectiveness of the skill training provided under PMKVY, the Ministry 
has launched Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH), a unified platform that integrates 
skilling, education, employment, and entrepreneurship ecosystems to provide a 
life-long array of services targeting a wide range of stakeholders.  
 
Further, the following steps are undertaken to ensure quality standards 
across all training centres, including PMKKs, while imparting training under 
PMKVY 4.0:- 
  
i. The registration process on SIDH ensures e-KYC compliance, Aadhaar based 
authentication of candidate credentials, and mobile number authentication. 
ii. Mandatory Aadhar Enabled Biometric Attendance System (AEBAS). 
iii. Virtual verification of operational training centres using a mobile app, monitor 
remote Training Centres (TCs) locations virtually at any given point of time. 
iv. Call validations are carried out on regular basis for taking feedback of 
candidates on their experiences and support investigation process in case of 
grievances received against Training Partners/Centres. 
v. Physical visits and inspection of the training centre by officials from MSDE and 
State Engagement Officers / other representatives from NSDC are regularly 
undertaken. 
vi. Virtual monitoring through audio and video calls is also conducted by a 
Kaushal Samiksha Kendra set up in MSDE. 
  
Over the last 3 years,a total of 637 Physical Inspections (PIs) have been 
undertaken. The key deficiencies observed during these inspections include non-
conduct of training during visits, PMKKs being closed on working days, non-
compliance with equipment guidelines, candidates being unavailable during visits, 
batch merging, low attendance (below 50%), mismatches between physical 
attendance and AEBAS records, and in some cases, PMKKs not being found at the 
registered address on SIDH.  
          
PMKKs with severe discrepancies are issued Show Cause Notices and 
undergo a Consequence Management process, with penal actions such as 
suspension, financial penalties, and batch cancellations imposed on non-
compliant centres. In order to avoid further occurrence of such discrepancies, 
workshops are also conducted to orient PMKKs on quality delivery of training, 
retention of candidates post mobilization, maintenance of attendance etc.”

B.  JAN SHIKSHAN SANSTHAN (JSS)  
53. The scheme of Jan Shikshan Sansthan  (JSS), initially launched in 1967 as 
Shramik Vidyapeeth (SVP), aims to provide skill development training in a non-

 
31 
 
formal mode at the doorsteps of the beneficiary through registered Societies 
(NGOs) with 100% grant from the Government of India. The objective of the 
scheme is to increase household income by promoting self/wage employment 
through skill development training.  The skill training is low-cost, flexible, and 
highly accessible to Women, Scheduled Caste (SC), Scheduled Tribe (ST), Other 
Backward Classes (OBCs), minorities, and any other disadvantaged groups of 
society. 
 
54. The target beneficiaries of the scheme are non-literate, neo-literate and the 
person having a rudimentary level of education (70%), school dropouts up to class 
12th (20%), and graduates (10%) in the age group of 15-45 years. Age relaxation 
is given to Divyang and other deserving cases, especially women. The priority 
groups are Women, SC, ST, OBCs and minorities in rural areas and urban low-
income areas.  
 
55 The scheme is implemented through JSSs, functioning under the aegis of 
respective parent bodies. The parent body should register the JSS as a separate 
NGO under the Societies Registration Act, 1860. These JSSs receive financial 
assistance in the form of Grants-in-Aid (GIA) from the Government of India for 
imparting skill training to the assigned target.

65.   When asked to provide the details of specific vocational training and skill 
development initiatives that are being implemented under JSS Scheme and the 
manner in which these initiatives address the needs of marginalized 
communities, the Ministry submitted as follows:-  
“The Jan Shikshan Sansthan (JSS) Scheme, initially launched as Shramik 
Vidyapeeth (SVP) in 1967, aims to provide skill training in a non-formal mode at the 
doorsteps of the beneficiary through registered Societies (NGOs) with 100% grant 
from the Government of India. The objective of the scheme is to increase the 
household income by promoting self/wage employment through skill development 
training. The skill training provided through JSSs is, flexible, affordable and highly 
accessible to women, Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), Other 
Backward Classes (OBCs), minorities, and any other disadvantaged groups of 
society. The target beneficiaries of the scheme are non-literates, neo-literates and 
persons having rudimentary level of education, school dropouts up to class 12th in 
the age group of 15-45 years. Age relaxation is given to Divyangjan and other 
deserving cases, especially women.  
  The following vocational training and skill development initiatives are being 
implemented under the JSS Scheme to address the needs of JSS beneficiaries, 
including marginalized communities: 
Livelihood Cell: Each JSS has a livelihood cell that connects JSS beneficiaries to 
employment, develops entrepreneurship skills, facilitates SHG formation, provides 
credit linkages, etc. 
Creation of Model JSS:Training infrastructure of 30 JSS centers has been 
upgraded for 3 identified job roles Assistant Computer Operator, Assistant Dress 

 
43 
 
Maker, and Beauty Care Assistant under SANKALP project of MSDE. Further, the 
upgradation of 70 more JSSs as Model JSSs is under process. 
Onboarding to SIDH Portal: JSS data has been migrated to the Skill India Digital 
Hub (SIDH) portal, ensuring mobile OTP-based candidate enrollment and 
Aadhaar-based e-KYC for transparency in candidate onboarding and training 
within the JSS ecosystem. 
 
NCVET certification: As the JSS Scheme has been onboarded to SIDH portal, 
from FY 2024-25 onwards, the JSS beneficiaries are now being issued NCVET 
certificate, which consists of QR Code, Credits earned, Beneficiary photo, NSQF 
level, etc. This has enhanced the credibility of JSS certification. 
Courses: 13 new-age NSQF-aligned courses (Level 2/3) have been adopted in the 
FY 2024-25, after approval from the National Council for Vocational Education 
and Training (NCVET). 
Convergence:  JSS scheme has been converged with schemes such as PM 
Vishwakarma Scheme, ULLAS scheme of MoE, Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi 
Nyaya Maha Abhiyan (PM JANMAN) , Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Unnat Gram 
Abhiyan (PM JUGA) etc 
Capacity Building: Ministry has organized zonal workshops at various locations 
and various capacity-building activities with stakeholders such as the National 
Institute for Entrepreneurship and Small Business Development (NIESBUD), the 
Institute of Rural Management, Anand (IRMA), etc, for capacity building of the 
JSSs in the areas such as Linkage with Entrepreneurship, Social Media presence 
and advocacy, Strengthening of Livelihood cells, etc. 
 
Simplifying the Board of Management creation in the JSSs: The affairs of each 
Jan ShikshanSansthan (JSS) are to be managed by a Board of Management that 
represents the interest of the target beneficiaries. JSS has its BoM which acts as 
an administrative body to supervise the functioning of the JSS.  
In order to strengthen the Board of the Management of the JSSs, the 
Ministry has undertaken the following initiatives: - 
- Providing Designation based Government nominations for the 
representatives in the BoM. 
- RDSDE as nominee of the Joint Secretary in the Board of Management of 
the JSSs 
Monitoring: To ensure effective on-ground implementation, the JSS scheme is 
monitored through the SIDH portal and field visits by MSDE and RDSDE officials. 
Udhyam Kart: As a step toward providing a platform to showcase and sell 
products made by JSS beneficiaries and to empower budding entrepreneurs under 
JSS Scheme, a dedicated section for JSS products has been successfully created 
on the UdhyamKart portal, developed by NIESBUD.”

C.  NATIONAL APPRENTICESHIP PROMOTION SCHEME (NAPS) - New Version 
 
68. National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS) aims to promote 
apprenticeship training in the country, by providing partial stipend support to the 
apprentices engaged under the Apprentices Act, 1961 and rules thereby. National 
Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS) launched in August 2016 and 
continues as NAPS-2 since 2022-23. Starting August 2023, Direct Benefit 
Transfer (DBT) was launched under NAPS-2. Partial stipend support by GoI 
under NAPS-2 limited to 25% of stipend paid, up to a maximum of Rs.1,500 per 
month, per apprentice is provided during the training period. The stipend support 
by GoI is paid through Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) to the bank account of 
apprentices.

D.  NATIONAL SCHEME FOR ITIs UPGRADATION  
93. Announced in budget 2024-25, Scheme for upgradation of ITIs aims to 
enhance the quality and outcomes of skilling across India by upgrading 1,000 ITIs 
in a hub-and-spoke arrangement over five years. It will be a new Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme, with a total outlay of Rs.60,000 crore, with contributions 
from the Government of India (Rs.30,000 crore), State Governments (Rs. 20,000 
crore), and the industry, including CSR funding (Rs.10,000 crore). 
 
94. It will involve upgradation of 200 hubs and 800 spoke ITIs, all working 
closely with industry partners to redesign existing courses, introduce new 
courses, along with specialized short-term courses. Additionally, the programme 
includes capacity augmentation of five national institutes for training trainers. 
With these initiatives, it is expected to benefit 20 lakh students over the course of 
five years. A demand of  Rs.5010 crore was placed for FY.2025-26. However 
considering that the scheme is yet at the initial stage, the Ministry of Finance 
agreed for an allocation of Rs.3000.00 crore, for FY.2025-26. 
 
95. It was noticed that for the FY 2025-26, the Gross Demand for Grants for the 
Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship is Rs. 8300.10 Cr and the 
net demand is Rs.6100.10 Cr. The demand of Rs. 6100.10 Cr also includes Rs. 
3000.00 Cr for the ‘New ITI Upgradation Scheme’, which is yet to be approved by 
the Cabinet.  The Committee desired to know about the present status of the 
implementation of the Scheme and how the funds earmarked are proposed to be 
utilized during the current fiscal. In response, the Ministry apprised as under:-    
 
“The ‘National Scheme for ITIs Upgradation’ has been recommended by the 
EFC in its meeting held on 9/13.01.2025. The draft Cabinet Note has received the 
approval of Finance Minister on 19.02.2025.  The MSDE is in process of obtaining 
approval of Cabinet Note. Since the approval of the scheme is yet to be obtained, 
the funds could not be released in the FY.2024-25. It is expected that the scheme 
would receive approval in the current FY. Exact amount of release under the 
scheme will depend upon the time available in this FY after the Cabinet approval. 
The allocations in this scheme have been reduced to Rs. 294.00 Cr  at the RE 
stage. Meanwhile, pending the Cabinet approval, the Ministry has been permitted 
to spend only Rs. 10 Cr on the preparatory activities of the scheme.”

15.01.2025 and BE for 2025-26 is Rs. 3000 crore although the BE proposed by

“The New ITI Upgradation Scheme was announced in Union Budget 2024-
25. The initial BE of Rs. 1,000 crore was announced as part of the Budget

delay in the approval of the scheme by the Cabinet. The Committee would

The RE for Rs. 294 crore has been proposed to be utilized for initiating

“The new scheme is being designed on the principles of Investment (need-

undertaken wide stakeholder consultation involving industries, State/UT 
governments, and inter-ministerial consultation, along with Workshops for 
Technical Knowledge Exchange with four countries (China, Germany, Singapore, 
and South Korea) to arrive at the design of the scheme. The scheme is proposed to 
be launched in March 2025.”

the final stage of it. We have taken the approval of the Department of Expenditure 
as well as the approval of the Finance Minister. The new scheme which has been 
announced in this budget, which is about the five centres of excellence, efforts are 
being made to link them with this scheme. When we link both, our scheme will be 
approved. We have progressed a lot in that. Our guidelines are also being 
prepared. We have also talked to the state government and the industry so that we 
can give momentum to this scheme. We have structured this scheme keeping in

under the control of the state with the cooperation of the industry. We can de-

have edited this scheme keeping all these things in mind.”  
 
101.   The Scheme involves upgradation of 1000 ITIs in hub-and spoke 
arrangement i.e. 200 hubs and 800 spoke ITIs.  When asked about the criteria for 
selecting ITIs for upgradation, especially in terms of infrastructure, course 
contents, alignment with industry needs etc. the following information was 
furnished by the Ministry:-   
 
“The ITIs will be identified by State Governments in consultation with the 
industries and as per the guidelines issued by MSDE. It is informed that MSDE is 
in the process of preparing the scheme guidelines.” 
 
102.  The Committee were apprised that the key role of States/UTs will be 
identification of clusters.  It was also informed that the governance of the Scheme 
will be  Industry managed.  When asked to clarify as to how the Ministry will 
coordinate and monitor these aspects, the Ministry in their subsequent written 
reply stated as follows:-  (Post Evi.First Batch LoP 19)  
“The ITI falls under the administrative control of the State/UT government. 
Therefore, States/UTs serve as the primary drivers of the scheme, including the 
responsibility for cluster identification. Similarly, as announced in the Union 
Budget 2024 regarding the upgradation of 1,000 ITIs under a hub-and-spoke 
model with an outcome-oriented approach, the course content and design will be 

 
63 
 
aligned with industry skill needs. Consequently, an industry-led governance 
structure has been proposed.  
Regarding the role of the Central Government, the scheme will be 
monitored by the National Steering Committee, headed by the Secretary, MSDE. 
This committee will play a key role in finalizing operational guidelines, monitoring 
progress, and recommending necessary course corrections. Furthermore, the final 
approval of the Strategic Investment Plan, as recommended by the States, will be 
granted by the Centre. The Central Government will also monitor Key Performance 
Indicators (KPIs) through third-party evaluations and conduct regular meetings to 
track implementation progress.”                                             
103. When asked as to what extent the new Scheme is expected to supplement 
the infrastructure deficiencies in the skilling of manpower in the country and 
cater to the needs of emerging sectors like electronics, renewable energy and 
automation to meet the skills demand in both the national and global job 
markets, the Ministry responded as under:-  
 
“The scheme proposes a hub and spoke model for upgradation of ITIs. A 
hub ITI will have, on an average, four (4) spoke ITIs. Upgraded ITIs are envisioned 
to be equipped with state-of-the-art infrastructure, machinery, and equipment, 
with proper space and facility planning for procurement and placement of required 
lab resources as per the course and curriculum plan. It will ensure that the 
students receive high-quality learning through modern facilities and up-to-date 
equipment, all of which making the ITIs aspirational. On an average, four (4) new 
courses would be introduced, and ten (10) existing courses would be upgraded at 
the hub ITIs. On an average, two (2) new courses would be introduced, and eight 
(8) existing courses would be upgraded in spoke ITIs. The newly introduced trades 
will cater to the emerging sectors like electronics, renewable energy and 
automation to meet the skills demand in both the national and global job 
markets.” 
 
104.  On being asked to provide details of the proposed mechanism to monitor 
and assess the likely impact of the proposed New ITIs Upgradation Scheme on the 
skilling and employment outcomes of the youth in the country, the Ministry 
submitted as under:-  
“Yes, there will be a detailed monitoring framework for the scheme, which 
will be tracked. The scheme will focus on results in two complementary ways. 
First, it will provide a monitoring framework that will include ambitious targets 
and cover inputs, outputs, outcomes, timelines, measurement parameters, 
mechanisms for measurement, and validation. It will focus strengthening 
governance, industry linkages, infrastructure, and training to ensure alignment 
with evolving industry needs.    
To ensure accountability, each hub-and-spoke cluster will be required to 
submit a comprehensive Strategic Investment Plan (SIP) upfront, adhering to the 
provided template. These plans will include measurable parameters for inputs, 
outputs, and outcomes, along with timelines. Progress will be closely monitored, 
through regular reporting and reviewed against these predefined metrics.  
Second, the funding under the scheme will be provided on a performance 
basis, i.e., when certain key performance indicators are achieved. These key 

 
64 
 
performance indicators will be Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) specified in 
results-based loans from the World Bank and ADB and will cascade down from 
the Centre to States to ITIs. 
Moreover, the World Bank and ADB will bring expertise in supporting the 
Ministry to carry out an impact evaluation of the scheme. The combination of this 
impact evaluation and the independent verification of the achievement of DLIs will 
provide robust evidence of the success of the scheme. 
To support implementation and tracking, a five-year investment plan and a 
set of performance indicators for measuring success will be included as part of 
scheme guidelines.”

The Project will focus on comprehensive entrepreneurship training/ capacity-
building combining theory-based training with practical exposure through 
experiential learning.

need-based skill trainings. The NSQF aligned and approved qualifications are 

 
81 
 
reflected in the National Qualification Register (NQR) portal, which is a centralized

qualifications are approved with a validity period of 3 years, unless otherwise 
specified by the NSQC. Upon completion of this period, the qualifications are 
revised by incorporating necessary technological and other changes.  
Additionally, to ensure that only relevant and industry driven qualifications 
remain active in the skilling ecosystem, NCVET follows the prescribed process of 
periodical archival of qualifications which fall under the below mentioned criteria: 
a) The validity of the qualification has expired and the awarding bodies 
concerned have not taken up the same for revision, hence all such qualifications 
are deemed to be not required in the skill ecosystem. 
b) Any entity which had got the qualifications NSQF aligned either during the

body (AB) of NCVET.

inactive for more than a year. 
d) Qualifications which have not been rationalized as per the National Credit 
Framework (NCrF) and revised National Skills Qualification Framework (NSQF).  
e) The NCVET recognized awarding body has requested deactivation/archiving 
of their qualifications.  
Accordingly, the NSQC in its meetings, based on the criteria above, 
undertakes regular archival of qualifications. Based on this, as on date (till 41st

122.   It has been noticed that there were 15,012 ITIs in 2023-24 viz-a-viz 14,619 
in 2024-25 i.e. there is reduction of 393 ITIs. Similarly, there is reduction in the 
number of seats from 26 lakhs in 2023-24 to 23.17 lakhs in 2024-25. When the 
Committee asked the reasons for reduction in the number of ITIs as well as the 
number of seats, the Ministry in their subsequent written reply stated as under:-               
                                                     
“The Directorate General of Training has introduced an objective 
methodology for grading of Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) from 2023.  This 
Data Driven Grading Methodology (DDGM) is based on several parameters 
available in the DGT-MIS portal, on the basis of which ITIs are awarded 
numerical grading.  
While implementing this methodology, discrepancies have been found in 
the data, especially regarding the number of seats in ITIs which were found to be 
lying vacant for several years. This was affecting the grading of an ITI, as 
‘admission percentage’ which is an important criterion in the DDGM gets 
wrongly reflected. Further, sanitization and accuracy of data is also essential for 
preparation of the correct seat matrix for admission and furnishing of 
information to various important forums. 
As per DGT letter no. DGT-12/01/2018-TC (Part) dated 20.12.2018, any 
trade or unit with zero admissions for two consecutive years will be deactivated. 
DGT granted a one-time relaxation in 2022 for ITIs with 100% vacant seats for 
two consecutive years due to the Covid-19 pandemic. 
To optimize seating capacity, DGT conducted a review to identify seats 
continuously vacant for five years. After considering representations from ITIs 
and State Directorates, a total of 4,49,636 seats in 5358 ITIs were de-affiliated 
starting from 17.01.2025. This reduced seating capacity to 23.17 lakh and the 
number of ITIs to 14619 in 2024-25.”

approval of its major Scheme viz. 'Skill India Programme', which comprises 
60-65% of its total budget for which the Department of Economic Affairs 
was requested from time to time for fund releases.

29.    Announced in budget 2024-25, the National Scheme for Upgradation of

upgrading 1,000 ITIs in a hub-and-spoke arrangement over five years. The 
BE for 2024-25 is Rs. 3000.00 crore, which has been reduced to Rs.294 
crore as the Scheme is yet to be approved by the Cabinet. For 2025-26, 
against the demand of  Rs.5010 crore, the Ministry of Finance agreed for an 
allocation of Rs.3000.00 crore. 
 
The Committee have been apprised that the Scheme has been 
recommended by the EFC in its meetings held on 09.01.2025 and

VIII  PM-VISHWAKARMA  
38. The Committee note that PM Vishwakarma Scheme was launched on 
17th September, 2023, which aims at upskilling the Vishwakarma’s with the 
knowledge of modern tools and techniques coupled with credit support for 
expanding their business. The Scheme is being implemented by three 
Ministries i.e., Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME), 
Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), and 
Department of Financial Services (DFS), Ministry of Finance and MSDE is 
the nodal ministry for implementation of Skilling Component of the 
Scheme. A total of 18 trades are covered under the Scheme to promote 
onboarding of training centres under the scheme.  
    The Committee note that as on 15.02.2025, PMV id is available in 679 
districts, however, training has been initiated in 636 districts, the training 
is yet to be initiated in 43 districts. Further, 26,96,358 PMV ID are available 
and as on 15.02.2025, 18,99,385 candidates have been trained and assessed 
against the target of 16 lakh candidates for 2023-24 and 2024-25.  
The Committee observe that the PM Vishwakarma Yojana is currently 
available in 34 out of 36 States/ Union Territories except Tamil Nadu and 
West Bengal. The Ministry have stated that the State Monitoring Committee 
and District Implementation Committees have not yet been constituted by 
these two States. The Committee recommend the Ministry to take up the 

 
122 
 
matter with the two State Governments without any further delay and 
resolve the issue so that products and services of artisans of the two States 
also have their benevolent share in domestic and global value chains. The 
Committee would like to be apprised of the progress made in this regard. 
39. The Ministry have further stated that the concerned State 
Governments and Regional Directorates of Skill Development and 
Entrepreneurship will work together to identify suitable training centers to 
initiate training in the remaining 43 districts. The Committee desire the 
Ministry to strengthen their coordination and monitoring mechanism so as 
to ensure identification and operationalisation of Training Centres in 43 
disticts on urgent basis so that the deserving artisans peddle their products 
and services into domestic and global value chains.

the Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) have been achieved except for 

 
123 
 
DLI7, which is under verification by the Independent Verification Agency 
and is expected to be completed by March 2025. The Committee exhort the
