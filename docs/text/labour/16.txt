INTRODUCTORY  
 
 India is the 6th largest exporter of Textiles and Apparel in the world in 2024. 
The share of textile and apparel, including handicrafts, in India’s total exports 
stands at a significant 8.63% in 2024-25. India accounts for 4.1% of global trade in 
textiles and apparel. The USA and EU are the major export destinations, together 
contributing around 49.2% of India’s total textile and apparel exports. The sector is 
also significant from an employment perspective. It provides direct employment to 
around 45 million people and source of livelihood for over 100 million people 
indirectly, including a large number of women and rural population. The sector 
aligns perfectly with Government initiatives such as Make in India, Skill India, 
Women Empowerment and Rural Youth Employment. 
 
1.2 The Ministry of Textiles, Government of India, is the apex body responsible for 
the development, promotion, and regulation of the textile industry in the country. 
The Ministry’s vision is to strengthen India’s textile heritage while fostering 
innovation and sustainability, the Ministry oversees a broad spectrum of activities 
ranging from traditional handlooms and handicrafts to modern textile 
manufacturing. It formulates policies and implements initiatives to enhance 
production, generate employment, and boost exports. Through various schemes and 
institutions, the Ministry supports skill development, technology upgradation, and 
infrastructure development across the sector. By promoting global competitiveness 
and encouraging eco friendly practices, the Ministry aims to position India as a 
global leader in textiles. Committed to inclusive growth, it works closely with 
stakeholders to ensure the holistic and sustainable advancement of the textile 
ecosystem. 
 
1.3 During the course of evidence, the Secretary, Ministry of Textiles apprised the 
Committee regarding the Textiles sector during 2025-26 as follows: 
 
   “This year in particular, Sir, has been marked by policy reforms that were 
basically targeted to enhance the competitiveness, sustainability and global 
positioning of Indian textiles. These were on removal of duties for an interim period 
for cotton, removal of the quality control orders on the raw materials, correction of 
inverted duty structures in the GST reforms, announcement of the Labour Code 
notification of that has marked a great deal of energy in the field and also 
operationalisation of the FTA and the announcement of signing of two very critical 
FTAs of UK and EU.  That would provide great market access to the Indian textiles. 
The schemes and the programmes were basically targeted towards the artisans, 
weavers and MSMEs while also driving industrial modernisation through PM Mitra 

2 
 
interventions. We have been able to start the PM Mitra parks and there is a great deal 
of energy in those areas now. The production linked incentives scheme was also 
reformed. ”

“The main reasons of shortfall during the last three fiscal years were (a) 
shifting of fund flow mechanism to TSA Hybrid Modal (prescribed by Ministry of 
Finance), under National Technical Textiles Mission (NTTM), which impacted the 
funds release, (b) PM MITRA was relatively a new scheme, and hence only 
establishment related expenses were required, However, efforts were made by the 
respective programme divisions.

resulting in expenditure of Rs. 2,998.42 crore against actual releases of Rs. 3,235.90

budgetary resources during 2024-25. In every Senior Officers Meeting, the Competent 
Authority directed programme divisions to ensure maximum utilization/ release of 
allocated funds and instructed to strictly follow the Monthly/Quarterly Expenditure 
Plan.”

and RE 2025-26 and underutilization of the allocated funds, the Ministry of Textiles 
submitted in their reply as under: 
 “The main hike is due to requirement of additional funds for Cotton 
Corporation of India for the disbursal of subsidies to farmers to ensure payment 
of Minimum Support Price. Under Support to PSU:  Advance amount sanctioned 
for BIC in compliance to Hon’ble Supreme Court order out of contingency funds 
of India for  pending salaries , wages, retirement benefits and statutory dues to 
employees.”

2.11 The Committee observed that the Ministry proposed an amount of ₹ 13739.63 
crore in their Annual Plan 2026-27, out of which the Ministry of Finance allocated 
only ₹ 5279.01 crore. When asked to justify proposing a huge hike in their Annual 
Plan 2026-27, the Ministry commented as under: 
“The Ministry has proposed an allocation of Rs. 13739.63 crore for the financial 
year 2026-27. This allocation has been proposed in view of the Ministry working 
on launch of new schemes/mission. Most of the schemes under the 15th Finance 
Committee are likely to be completed by 31 March 2026. The Ministry proposed 
Rs. 6615.00 crore under Cotton Corporation India for disbursal of subsidy to 
farmers for MSP for 2026-27. 
 
The new schemes are aimed at achieving the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047 in the 
long run and vision of 2030 in the short run. The Ministry has come up with 
several new schemes for the 16th Finance Commission Cycle. In fact, the new 
schemes have found place in the budget announcement of the year 2026-27.”

Ministry of Textiles, the Committee raised a query whether there was any cutting 
down of earmarked funds by the Ministry of Finance for the year 2025-26, the 
Secretary submitted in her deposition as under: 
“No, Sir. We received an additional Rs. 500 crore. For RE, we are uncertain about the 
final RE for this year. For 2026-27, the allocation is slightly higher than the previous 
year. From BE to BE, there have been no cuts. We have not received any reductions 
in funds, and the support for MSP will be determined after audits and account 
submissions. There probably we will be looking for enhancement at the RE stage.” 
2.13 When the Committee further enquired about the actual expenditure till date

“Sir, currently it is ₹ 3,233 crores. ₹ 1,800 crores will come from the scheme from

4.1 Cotton is one of the most important cash crops of the country and is the major 
raw material for the domestic textile industry. It provides sustenance to millions of 
farmers and workers involved in the cotton industry, right from processing to trading 
of cotton. 
 
4.2 In India, cotton is cultivated in three diverse agro-ecological zones, namely the 
Northern zone comprising the States of Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan; the Central 
zone comprising the States of Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Maharashtra; and the 
Southern zone comprising the States of Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and 
Tamil Nadu. Besides these, cotton is also grown in Odisha in the eastern part of 
India. Cotton is also cultivated in small areas of non-traditional states like West 
Bengal, etc. India has brought about a quantitative and qualitative transformation in 
the production of cotton since independence. India has become one of the largest 
producers, consumers and exporters of cotton in the world. The details of production

4.5 The Cotton Corporation of India Ltd. (CCI) has been nominated by the 
Government of India for undertaking MSP operations, in the event kapas prices of 
seed cotton (kapas) fall below the MSP level, for procuring kapas offered by the 
cotton farmers in various APMC market yards at MSP rates without any quantitative 
limit. Before the commencement of the Cotton Year (Oct. to Sept.), every year, the 
Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) recommend the MSP based on 
the formula of 1.5 times the cost of production (A2+FL) to ensure reasonably fair 
remuneration, i.e., at least 50% over the cost of production, to farmers. Accordingly, 
taking into consideration the recommendation of CACP, the Ministry of Agriculture 
declares MSP for two basic varieties of Fair cotton, viz. medium staple length (staple 
length of 24.5 mm to 25.5 mm and micronaire value of 4.3 to 5.1) and long staple 
length (staple length of 29.5 to 30.5 mm and micronaire of 3.5 to 4.3). For Cotton 
Season 2025-26, MoA & FW has fixed MSPs for fair-grade cotton at ₹ 7710/- per 
quintal for medium staple and ₹ 8110/- per quintal for long staple cotton.  
4.6 Based on the Support Price of these two basic varieties of Seed cotton (Kapas) 
and considering the quality differential, normal price differential and other relevant 
factors, the MSP for other classes of Fair Seed cotton (Kapas) is fixed by the Textile 
Commissioner of India. 
4.7 In India, out of an estimated 6 million cotton farmers, about 85% are marginal 
and small farmers with less than 2 hectares. Factors such as crop damage due to 
adverse agro-climatic conditions (e.g., insufficient or untimely rains, floods, 
hailstorms) and natural calamities reduce yields and increase production costs for 
cotton farmers. 
To overcome the above situation and to provide an alternate channel to sell cotton at 
a predetermined reasonable price, the Government of India declares MSP of cotton 
every year based on the formula of 1.5 times the cost of production (A2+FL), to 
ensure reasonably fair remuneration, i.e. at least 50% over the cost of production to 

23 
 
farmers. The main objective of MSP is to prevent distress sales by cotton farmers by 
providing an alternative price at which cotton can be sold. The MSP system ensures 
a minimum price for cotton farmers and protects them from distress sales when 
cotton prices fall below the MSP. 
4.8 According to the Ministry of Textiles, major MSP operations for cotton were 
conducted in 2014-15, 2019-20, 2020-21, and 2024-25 by CCI, with volumes 
ranging from 92 lakh bales to 125 lakh bales and values ranging from around  
₹ 19,500 crore to ₹ 37,437 crore. The volume of MSP operations for cotton largely 
depends on the demand and supply conditions in domestic and international 
markets. The quantum of procurement and the number of beneficiaries tend to 
change each year, depending on market conditions. 
4.9 During the current cotton season 2025-26, as on 31.12.2025, CCI has 
procured 63.57 lakh bales, valuing ₹ 25,555 crore under MSP operations, through 
13.77 lakh transactions of cotton farmers in all cotton-growing States.

V. HANDLOOM SECTOR 
 
5.1 The Handloom Sector is one of the largest unorganized economic activities of 
India and it constitutes an integral part of the rural and semi- rural livelihood 
engaging over 35 lakh persons. The sector engages over 25 lakh female weavers and 
allied workers which makes it an important source of economic empowerment of 
women. Handloom weaving constitutes one of the richest and most vibrant aspects 
of the Indian cultural heritage. The sector has advantage of being less capital 
intensive, minimal use of power, being eco-friendly, flexibility of small production, 
openness to innovations and adaptability to market requirements. Because of the 
uniqueness and exclusivity of designs, capability to produce small batch sizes and 
being eco-friendly fabric, handloom products are in high demand in the 
international and the domestic market and retailers with discerning clientele looking 
for reliable source of authentic handloom products on regular basis. 
 
5.2 One of the largest in terms of employment potential, Handloom industry, with 
28.23 lakh handlooms, plays a very important role in the country’s economy, 
producing both for domestic as well as international consumption. 
 
5.3 The major handloom export centers are Karur, Panipat, Varanasi & Kannur 
where handloom products like Bed linen, Table linen, Kitchen linen, Toilet linen, 
Floor coverings, embroidered textile materials, curtains etc. are produced for export 
markets.  
 
5.4 The Handloom industry mainly exports fabrics, bed linen, table linen, toilet 
and kitchen linen, towels, curtains, cushions and pads, tapestries and upholstery’s, 
carpets, floor coverings, etc. The major importing countries of Handloom products 
from India are USA, UK, Germany, Italy, France, Japan, Saudi Arabia, Australia, 
Netherland and UAE. 
 
5.5 During the course of evidence, the Ministry deposed before the Committee 
elaborating the Handloom Sector as under:  
“…this is one of the largest sectors. There are more than 35 lakh handloom workers, 
72 per cent of whom are women, and 67 per cent belong to socially backward classes. 
In the last five years of implementation, nearly 18 lakh handloom weavers have 
benefited from marketing support. There have been 106 GI registrations, and 35 lakh 
Weaver Pehchan Cards have been issued.”

(a) National  Handloom Development Programme (NHDP) 
 
5.17 National Handloom Development Programme (NHDP) includes following 
Schemes/Programmes: 
 
(i) Small Cluster Development Programme (SCDP): Financial assistance upto 
₹ 2.00 crore per SCDP for various interventions such as Hathkargha 

36 
 
Samvardhan Sahayata, product development, construction of workshed, 
project management cost, design development etc. is provided. The 
proposals are recommended by the State Government. 
 
(ii) Handloom Marketing Assistance (HMA) is one of the components of 
National Handloom Development Programme.  In order to provide 
marketing platform to the handloom agencies/weavers to sell their 
products directly to the consumers, financial assistance is provided to the 
States/eligible handloom agencies for organizing marketing events in 
domestic as well as overseas markets.   
 
(iii) Special Infrastructure Projects: Objective of the scheme is to set up 
project/s for product development/diversification, improving the 
productivity/ quality of handloom products, value addition of handloom 
products, marketing etc. to meet challenges of the dynamic market. 
 
(iv) Mega Cluster Development Programme: Mega Handloom Clusters in 
various parts of the country will be taken up for their holistic 
development, drawing comprehensive development plans. Each Mega 
Handloom Cluster will cover atleast 10,000 handlooms with GoI 
contribution upto ₹ 30.00 crore per mega cluster. Nature and level of 
assistance to each Mega Cluster will be need based. 
 
(v) Concessional Credit/Weavers’ MUDRA Loan: Under the Scheme, margin 
money assistance for individual weaver and Handloom Organizations; 
interest subvention and credit guarantee fees on loans for a period of 
three years are provided to handloom organizations only. MUDRA Portal 
has been developed in association with Punjab National Bank to cut down 
delay in disbursement of funds for margin money, interest subvention 
and credit guarantee fee. 
 
(vi) Handloom Weavers’ Welfare: Handloom Weavers’ Welfare is providing life, 
accidental and disability insurance coverage under the components 
Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY), Pradhan Mantri 
Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY) and Converged Mahatma Gandhi Bunkar 
Bima Yojana (MGBBY).This scheme also provides scholarship for higher 
education to handloom weavers/workers’ children (upto 2 children) and 
financial support for Awardee weavers above 60 years of age in indigent 
circumstances. 
 
(vii) Hathkargha Samvardhan Sahayata (HSS): HSS was introduced on  
01 December 2016 with an objective to provide looms/accessories to the 
weavers to enhance their earnings through improved productivity and 
quality of the handloom products. Under the scheme, 90% of the cost of 
loom/accessory is borne by the Government of India while remaining 10% 
is borne by the beneficiary. The Government of India’s share is released to 
the supplier through Central Nodal Agency. 

37 
 
 
(viii) “India Handloom” Brand- During the celebration of 7th August 2015 as 
National Handloom Day, ‘India Handloom’ Brand was launched for 
branding of high-quality handloom products to promote production of 
niche handloom products with high quality with zero defect and zero 
effect on environment.  Since the launch of “India Handloom” Brand, 
2,191 registrations have been issued under 184 product categories. 
Initiatives with various leading brands has been undertaken to bring out 
a separate range of handloom garments in their brand. 
 
 
(ix) Urban Haats are set up in the big towns/metropolitan cities to provide 
adequate direct marketing facilities to the craft persons/weavers and 
eliminate middle agencies.  37 such Urban Haats have been sanctioned 
across the country so far, of which 35 Urban Haats are functional and 2 
are under process.  
 
(x) Producer Companies (PCs): Govt. of India is supporting in formation of 
PCs across the country in the handloom sector as a thrust area with the 
objective to extend the benefits of various handloom schemes to the 
weavers/workers, in particular to those who are either working 
independently or in the fold of Self-Help Groups/Producer Groups. 
Suitable handloom pockets have been identified and being facilitated for 
formation of Producer Companies for catering to the needs of the weavers 
and craftsmen there. So far, 163 PCs have been registered across the 
country.

VI. HANDICRAFTS SECTOR 
 
6.1 The handicrafts sector plays a significant role in the country’s economy. It 
provides employment to a vast segment of craft persons in rural & semi urban areas 
and serves as a pivotal driver of substantial foreign exchange earnings for the 
country, while concurrently preserving our rich cultural heritage. Handicrafts have 
great potential, as they hold the key for sustaining not only the livelihoods of 
millions of artisans spread over the length and breadth of the country, but also for 
the burgeoning number of newcomers entering in the crafts activity. 
 
6.2 Presently, handicrafts sector contributes substantially to employment 
generation and exports earnings. Despite its significant contribution to the economy, 
the sector faces myriad challenges stemming from its predominantly unorganized 
structure. Key constraints such as lack of education, inadequate capital resources, 

43 
 
insufficient exposure to modern technologies, a dearth of market intelligence, and 
the absence of a robust institutional framework hampered its growth potential. 
 
6.3 Total 33.55 lakh artisans have been mobilized under “Pahchan” initiative by 
the office of Development Commissioner (Handicrafts), Ministry of Textiles as on 
30.11.2025. 
 
6.4 The Office of Development Commissioner (Handicrafts) oversees the 
implementation of two pivotal schemes for promotion and development of 
handicrafts sector namely “National Handicraft Development Program” (NHDP) and 
Comprehensive Handicrafts Cluster Development Scheme (CHCDS) which has an 
integrated approach for development of handicraft clusters in a holistic manner.

b. Skill Development in Handicrafts Sector  
c. Ambedkar Hastshilp Vikas Yojana  
d. Direct Benefit to Artisans (Welfare)

VIII. SERICULTURE SECTOR 
 
8.1      Silk is an insect fibre, with lustre, drape and strength. Because of these 
unique features, silk is known as the “Queen of Textiles” the world over. India has 
been the land of ancient civilisation and has contributed many things to the world, 
silk being one of them. India is the second largest producer of silk in the world and 
also the largest consumer. Nevertheless, India is the only country, which is 
producing all the four commercial varieties of silk, namely Mulberry, Tropical & Oak 
Tasar, Muga and Eri. Indian sericulture industry has the unique distinction of high 
employment potential, low capital requirement and provides remunerative income to 
silk growers. India with the production of 38,913 MTs of silk is the second largest 
producer of silk in the world after China.  
      Among the four varieties of silk produced, Mulberry accounted for 76.82% 
(29,892 MT), Tasar 4.08% (1,586 MT), Eri 18.46% (7,183 MT) and Muga 0.65% (252 
MT). The bivoltine raw silk production has increased by 8.66% to 9,675 MT during 
2023-24 from 8,904 MT during 2022-23. Further, vanya silk, which includes Tasar, 
Eri and Muga silks, have increased by 1.04% during 2023-24 over 2022-23.

8.5  On being asked by the Committee as to what extent the Sericulture Industry in 
India is at par with its counterparts across the world, the Ministry submitted in their 
reply as under: 
 
   “India’s sericulture industry is broadly at par with leading global counterparts and 
holds a strategically strong position in the global silk economy. India is the second-
largest producer of raw silk in the world, next only to China, contributing about 42% 
(41,121 MT in 2024–25) of the global raw silk production (97,351 MT in 2024). 
Together, China and India account for nearly 95% of total global silk production, 
while production growth in other silk-producing countries remains limited. 
Significantly, India is the only country in the world that produces all four commercial 
varieties of silk—Mulberry, Tasar, Eri and Muga, reflecting its unique biodiversity and 
diversified production base.

education, training and input support systems, supported by indigenously developed 
advanced technologies across the silk value chain—from seed to fabric. With robust 
infrastructure, policy support and skilled manpower, India has substantial potential 
to further enhance domestic silk production, improve productivity and strengthen its 
global competitiveness. Additionally, India collaborates with technologically advanced 
sericulture nations to promote innovation, technology exchange and sustainable 
growth of the Indian silk industry.”

11.1 Ministry of Textiles had launched PM Mega Integrated Textile Regions and 
Apparel Parks (MITRAs) Scheme in October 2021 to strengthen the Indian textile 
industry by way of enabling scale of operations, reduce logistics cost by housing 
entire value chain at one location, attract investment, generate employment and 
augment export potential. The scheme is intended to develop integrated large scale 
and modern industrial infrastructure facility for total value-chain of the textile 
industry, for example, spinning, weaving, processing, garmenting, textile 
manufacturing, processing & printing machinery industry. These parks were 
envisaged to be located at sites which have inherent strengths for textile industry to 
flourish and have necessary linkages to succeed. The scheme envisaged leveraging 
Public Private Partnership model for fast paced implementation in a time-bound 
manner.

Textile Region and Apparel (PM MITRA) Parks in Greenfield/Brownfield sites with 
world class infrastructure including plug and play facility with an outlay of Rs. 4445 
cr for a period of seven years upto 2027-28. 
 
The Government has finalised 7 sites viz. Tamil Nadu (Virudhnagar), Telangana 
(Warangal), Gujarat (Navsari), Karnataka (Kalaburagi), Madhya Pradesh (Dhar),

Hybrid model by the Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance. The model was

prerequisite to onboarding to TSA Hybrid Model, Central Nodal Agency (CNA) i.e. 
NIFT, Delhi had to refund the unspent amount of previously sanctioned grant to 
Consolidated Fund of India along with interest. The deposit of unspent amount by 
CNA took considerable time. The implementation of TSA Hybrid model took a 
considerable time due to which assignment of budget and its utilization by respective 
institutions got delayed. All R&D Projects involve procurement of instruments and 
machinery for which Capital Budget is allocated. During various review meetings the 
Research Institutions have pointed out that the procurement process as per GFR

(iii) For F.Y. 2025-26: Under the National Technical Textiles Mission (NTTM), out

GENERAL OVERVIEW  
1. PM Mega Integrated Textile Region & Apparel (PM-MITRA) Parks is the 
flagship programme of the Union Government and the Prime Minister to 
encourage development of Textiles sector which is the highest employment 
generation arena in the country. Hence, these Parks have been envisaged by 
the Government by investing a huge amount. These parks also boost the 
exports and economy of the nation. However, the Committee feel that the 
intent of the Union Government can be converted into a reality only with the 
coordination of all the State Governments, which is not forthcoming. 
Therefore, Union Government is urged to take concrete and concerted steps to 
effectively and successfully implement the above ambitious plan of the Prime 
Minister.

SERICULTURE SECTOR 
18. India is the second largest producer of silk globally next to China. The total 
raw silk production in the country has registered an increase of 21.76% from 
33,770 MT in 2020-21 to 41,121 MT in 2024-25. During 2025-26, production 
of raw silk has reached 32,017 MT (up to December) and is expected to achieve 
42,300 MT by the end of the financial year. The value of Indian silk exports 
has also grown from ₹ 1773.38 crore during 2022-23 to ₹ 2582.29 crore in 
2025-26 (April to December-Provisional) while imports stood at ₹ 1456.18 crore 
during the same period. Though the volume of raw silk production and the 
value of silk exports has increased over the past years, India is far behind 
China which accounts for roughly 75% of the global raw silk production and 
hold dominance over the export market. India, however, occupies a unique 
position in the world as the only country to produce all the four commercial 
types namely, Mulberry, Eri, Tasar and Muga. The Indian sericulture industry 
is also an employment-oriented sector generating estimated employment to 
97.30 lakh persons during 2024-25.  
Given the diverse range of jobs and high employment potential offered by this 
sector in rural areas, especially for small and marginal farmers, women and 
marginalized communities and in view of the low capital requirement, the 
Committee call for a multi-pronged approach for growth and development of 
the sericulture sector. The Committee, therefore, recommend that a roadmap 
for the next five years be prepared for boosting the production of high quality 
silk and silk products through research in genetic improvement and breeding 

103 
 
of silkworms, technological upgradation in processing, product diversification, 
skill development of weavers and artisans, strengthening of international 
marketing for silk products, boosting exports and reducing reliance on imports 
of raw silk, etc.  Sericulture sector being vital for rural economic development, 
the Committee further recommend that the Government should leverage its 
unique position as the only country to produce all the four commercial types 
namely, Mulberry, Eri, Tasar and Muga to boost exports and an outcome-
oriented action plan be worked out annually commencing from FY 2026-27  
and the progress in this regard be intimated to the Committee. 
JUTE SECTOR 
19. India is the largest producer of raw jute in the world, producing 80.36 lakh 
bales to 91 lakh bales in 2025-26 as per First Production Estimates of Ministry 
of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare. The Jute industry is one of the major 
industries in the eastern region, providing direct employment to 0.37 million 
workers in organized mills and in diversified units including tertiary sector and 
allied activities and supports the livelihood of 4.0 million farm families. In 
addition, a large number of persons are engaged in the trade of jute. The 
Committee note that RE was reduced to ₹ 107.10 crore against BE of ₹ 128.00 
crore during 2025-26, yet the AE was ₹ 86.71 crore (as on 31.12.2025). The 
Committee are aware that the Jute Sector plays a significant role in the 
country’s sustainable economic development by providing employment, 
supporting rural livelihoods and contributing to exports. They are, therefore, of 
the considered view that the funds allocated for the growth and development 

104 
 
of this sector should not be surrendered as savings at the end of the FY. The 
Committee recommend that the Ministry should  develop a robust mechanism 
to ensure timely submission of claim applications by scheme beneficiaries so 
that funds can be released and utilized in a more balanced manner throughout 
the financial year rather than being concentrated in the last quarter. For this 
purpose, a strong monitoring and review mechanism should be developed to 
track the progress of financial and physical performance of the schemes on a 
quarterly basis and timely corrective actions taken, wherever necessary. In 
view of the delay in workforce recruitment in the Jute Corporation of India 
(JCI), the Committee recommend that the Ministry should expedite 
recruitment and strengthen institutional capacity to ensure smooth 
implementation of schemes and efficient utilization of allocated funds. 
20. Scrutiny of the figures related to physical and financial targets and 
achievements under the Capital Subsidy for Acquisition of Plant & Machinery 
(CSAPM) for Jute Diversified Products under National Jute Development 
Program (NJDP), to facilitate modernisation/upgradation of the existing Jute 
mills and MSME JDP Units reveals non-achievement of both the targets in the 
last three years as well as the current FY i.e. 2025-26. The Committee, 
therefore, urge the Ministry to conduct a detailed assessment to identify the 
key constraints faced by mills and MSME units in availing the subsidy, 
including financial constraints, procedural delays, or lack of technical 
awareness, and take corrective measures accordingly. The Committee 
recommend that the Ministry should undertake intensive awareness and 
outreach programmes among jute mills and MSME Jute Diversified Product 

105 
 
(JDP) units to ensure that potential beneficiaries are well informed about the 
benefits and procedures of the scheme. The Ministry should also work to 
simplify the application, documentation, and approval procedures under the 
scheme so as to encourage greater participation from eligible units, 
particularly MSMEs. The Committee desire that the Ministry should provide 
technical guidance and handholding support to MSME JDP units for project 
preparation, machinery selection, and submission of proposals to improve 
participation under the scheme.   
TEXTILES SECTOR 
PM MEGA INTEGRATED TEXTILE REGION & APPAREL (PM-MITRA) PARKS 
 
21. The Committee note that Ministry of Textiles have launched PM Mega 
Integrated Textile Regions and Apparel (PM MITRA) Parks Scheme in October 
2021 to strengthen the Indian textile industry by way of enabling scale of 
operations, reduce logistics cost by housing entire value chain at one location, 
attract investment, generate employment and augment export potential. The 
scheme will develop integrated large scale and modern industrial infrastructure 
facility for total value-chain of the textile industry, for example, spinning, 
weaving, processing, garmenting, textile manufacturing, processing & printing 
machinery industry. The Scheme has an outlay of ₹ 4445 crore for a period of 
seven years upto 2027-28.  
Scrutiny of details of financial outlay reveals that despite BE allocation 
of ₹ 300 crore each in the FYs 2024-25 and 2025-26, RE has been drastically 
cut down to ₹ 44.00 crore in 2024-25 and ₹ 200.00 crore in 2025-26. AE in 

106 
 
2024-25 was reported as ₹ 24.51 crore and in 2025-26, it was to the tune of  
₹ 162.66 crore (up to 31.12.2025). The Committee are concerned to note that 
fund utilisation was constrained as funds could only be released after the 
incorporation of Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) and the appointment of 
Master Developers. While SPVs have now been incorporated in all five 
Greenfield States, the Committee recommend that the Ministry should ensure 
strict timelines for operationalisation of SPVs in all PM MITRA Parks so that 
administrative delays do not hinder timely utilisation of allocated funds in 
future. As the appointment of Master Developers is a crucial prerequisite for 
implementation of the scheme, the Committee emphasize the need for 
establishing a time-bound and transparent mechanism for selection of Master 
Developers, with clearly defined milestones and accountability, to avoid delays 
in project execution and fund utilisation. The Committee also call for 
strengthening and maintaining close coordination between the Centre, States 
involved and the Implementing Agencies. Taking note of the fact that ₹164.17 
crore has been released and that steps are being taken to utilise the entire 
amount provided in the Revised Estimates during the current financial year, 
the Committee urge the Ministry to closely monitor the utilisation of RE 
allocation to ensure that the budgetary provision do not remain underutilized 
in the current FY.  
22. The Committee note that the Government has finalized 7 (seven) sites for 
PM MITRA Parks viz. Tamil Nadu (Virudhnagar), Telangana (Warangal), Gujarat 
(Navsari), Karnataka (Kalaburgi), Madhya Pradesh (Dhar), Uttar Pradesh 
(Lucknow) and Maharashtra (Amravati). However, all the 7 parks are still not 

107 
 
fully functional and some of them are still at the construction stage. In respect 
of Karnataka, the project is still at a preliminary stage, with processes such as 
finalisation of the Detailed Project Report, operationalisation of the Special 
Purpose Vehicle, and appointment of the Master Developer yet to translate into 
substantive progress on the ground. The Committee believe that the PM MITRA 
Parks proposed including in Kalaburgi, Karnataka would play a significant role 
in promoting integrated textile manufacturing and generating employment in 
the region, and are of the considered view that delays in the commencement of 
physical infrastructure development may adversely affect the timely 
achievement of the objectives envisaged under the PM MITRA Scheme. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Textiles should take 
proactive measures, in close coordination with the State Governments 
concerned and the implementing agencies to expedite the pending procedural 
and implementation processes so as to ensure early commencement and time-
bound completion of the PM MITRA Parks including the proposed park at 
Kalaburgi. The Committee further recommend that the Ministry should pursue 
the DoE, Ministry of Finance to allocate enhanced budget provision for the 
early completion of the stuck-up projects of PM MITRA Parks as they are very 
ambitious programme of the Government of India where sustainable growth 
linked to exports is a key factor.  
PRODUCTION LINKED INCENTIVE (PLI) SCHEME FOR TEXTILES 
23. The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Textiles, with an 
approved outlay of ₹ 10,683 crore over a five-year period aims to promote 

108 
 
production of MMF Apparel, MMF Fabrics and Products of Technical Textiles in 
the country to enable Textile sector to achieve size and scale and become 
competitive. The Committee are concerned to note the extremely low 
utilisation of funds under the scheme during FY 2025–26, with only ₹ 16.58 
crore utilised against the Revised Estimate of ₹ 400.00 crore. The Ministry 
have apprised the Committee that disbursement of incentives is contingent 
upon eligible companies submitting claims after achieving the prescribed 
thresholds of investment, turnover, incremental turnover and value addition, 
and that such claims are to be filed by 31st December of the subsequent 
financial year after due statutory audit and verification.  The Committee are 
not satisfied with the slow pace of the progress made under PLI Scheme. The 
Committee understand that the scheme is performance-linked in nature and 
that claims are processed only after fulfilment of the stipulated criteria. 
However, they are of the considered view that such a substantial gap between 
the allocated funds and the actual utilisation points to slow pace in achieving 
the prescribed performance benchmarks by participating companies. The 
Committee, therefore, urge the Ministry to strengthen its monitoring and 
facilitation mechanisms for monitoring the progress of participating 
companies to ensure that the intended objectives of the scheme are realised 
within the envisaged timeframe. The Committee also desire that the Ministry 
should undertake periodic reviews with participating companies to identify 
implementation bottlenecks and extend necessary guidance so as to ensure 
optimal utilisation of the budgetary allocations in the current fiscal and 

109 
 
ensuing financial years. The Committee desire to be apprised of the progress 
made in this regard.  
  Scheme for Capacity Building in Textile Sector - SAMARTH 
24.  The Committee note that the Scheme for Capacity Building in Textile 
Sector - SAMARTH, managed by the Ministry of Textiles, is a demand-driven, 
placement-oriented, and implemented primarily by industry or industry 
associations. Under the Scheme, over six lakh persons have been trained, out 
of which 4.52 lakh have been placed and around 88 per cent of them are 
women. SAMARTH Scheme was formed under the broad skilling framework 
adopted by Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship and 
operationalized by a robust digital platform linked with Aadhaar Enabled 
Biometric Attendance System (AEBAS) for ease of implementation and 
monitoring of training programme under the scheme. The skilling programme 
under the scheme is implemented through Implementing Partners (IPs) 
comprising Textile Industry/ Industry Associations, Central/State Government 
agencies and Sectoral Organizations of Ministry of Textiles.  
The Committee note that as on 31.12.2025, an amount of ₹ 207.47 crore 
had been utilised against the Revised Estimate of ₹ 260.00 crore which 
constitutes approximately 79.6% and is broadly aligned with the quarterly 
implementation progress. Albeit the progress reported by the Ministry, the 
Committee emphasize that sustained monitoring is essential to ensure that 
the pace of implementation does not slow down during the later stages of the 
financial year. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of 

110 
 
Textiles closely monitor the achievement of training and placement targets 
under the scheme and ensure timely release and utilisation of funds linked to 
the prescribed milestones. The Ministry should also strengthen coordination 
with implementing partners to avoid any delays in training delivery and 
reporting of outcomes so that the allocated funds are fully utilised within the 
financial year and the objectives of the scheme are achieved. 
INTEGRATIVE TEXTILES PROGRAMME (ITP) 
25. As announced in the Budget Speech, a new Scheme of Integrative Textiles 
Programme (ITP) was launched with an allocation of ₹ 1500 crore as BE  
2026-27. The Scheme encompass five programmes which includes, the 
National Fibre Programme, Textile Expansion and Employment Programme, 
Text Econ for Sustainability, and the Integrated Handmade Programme, which 
is an integration of the Handloom and Handicraft Schemes in a more concerted 
manner. The Committee note that the Integrated Textile Programme has been 
designed to promote the holistic development of the textile sector by 
supporting infrastructure creation, capacity building, and technological 
upgradation across various segments of the textile value chain. While the 
scheme has the potential to significantly enhance the competitiveness and 
sustainability of the textile industry, the Committee are concerned that the 
allocation of funds is not commensurate with the scope of the Scheme. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry should make efforts to 
seek more funds over and above ₹ 1500 crore allocation so far, from the 
Ministry of Finance for this programme to take off as it is a very ambitious 

111 
 
programme, encompassing the growth of the entire textile sector, right from 
farm to fashion.   
NATIONAL TECHNICAL TEXTILE MISSION (NTTM) 
26. The National Technical Textiles Mission is a mission-mode programme 
started to improve India’s standing in technical textiles, which is the fastest 
growing segment globally. Out of the ₹ 1,480 crore outlay for five years,  
₹ 1,000 crore has been earmarked for research and development, and the 
remaining amount is for trading and other components. The Committee note 
that the RE has been revised downwards to ₹ 300 crore against the BE of ₹ 370 
crore during 2025-26. The Ministry have utilized a good chunk of the RE i.e. 
around ₹ 283 crore by February 2026 which roughly constitutes 94 per cent.  
While the Committee appreciate the Ministry’s efforts in achieving a high level 
of utilisation of the revised allocation, they are concerned that the downward 
revision of funds at the RE stage may impact the overall pace of 
implementation and the achievement of the intended objectives of the 
programme. The Committee are of the considered opinion that accurate 
assessment of fund requirements at the budgeting stage is essential to ensure 
stability in programme implementation. The Committee, therefore, 
recommend that the Ministry of Textiles should undertake realistic planning 
and projection of fund requirements at the Budget Estimate stage so as to 
minimise substantial revisions at the RE stage. Concerted efforts should also 
be made to ensure timely implementation and efficient utilisation of allocated 

112 
 
funds so that the objectives of the mission are achieved in a planned and 
sustained manner.
