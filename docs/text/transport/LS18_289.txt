INTRODUCTION 
    The shared c ultural heritage of any nation is what binds it together as a whole and 
assigns a unique identity to its people . India has been  home to a unique blend of diverse 
ethnic groups with rich and varied cultural heritage since ancient times, which reflects in its 
tangible as well as intangible heritage. The richness of the Indian culture , besides its 
significant contribution to the Indian identity across the globe, captures the imagination of 
both domestic as well as forei gn tourists and thus contributes to the economic growth of the 
country. 
2. The primary mandate of Ministry of Culture is preservation and conservation of 
ancient cultural heritage and promotion of tangible and intangible art and culture. Headed by 
Secretary, the administrative set up consists of 5 Bureaus and Divisions. The Minis try has 
two attached offices, six subordinate offices and thirty five autonomous organisations 
including seven Zonal Cultural Centres. There are also four missions namely, the National 
Mission for Manuscripts, National Mission on Monument and Antiquities, National Mission 
on Libraries, and Gandhi Heritage Sites Mission. 
3. Broadly, this Ministry is working on the protection, development and promotion of 
the country’s tangible heritage, intangible heritage and knowledge heritage. In addition, the 
Ministry also harbours the responsibility of nurturing Gandhian heritage and commemoration 
of important historical events and centenaries of great personalities. The Ministry manages all 
the centrally protected monuments of national importance, through the Archaeologica l 
Survey of India. It supports museum movement in the country and majority of the museums 
are under its administrative control. The Ministry also promotes regional museums through 
grant-in-aids. In the field of intangible heritage, the Ministry extends fin ancial support to 
individuals, group of individuals and cultural organizations involved with the performing, 
visual and literary arts. Similarly, through its organizations,  the Ministry  is engaged in 
recognizing excellence in the field of art and culture b y way of awards given by Sahitya 
Akademi and Sangeet Natak Akademi. The National School of Drama is involved in 
promoting a vibrant theatre movement of contemporary relevance in the context of our 
traditions and cultural diversity. 

2 
4. The Ministry is the cust odian of all the major libraries in the country. It frames 
policies and provides grants -in-aid for library development. Through National Archives of 
India, Ministry is responsible for maintenance of all archival records of the country. The 
Ministry protects and promotes Buddhist and Tibetan Culture through  its institutions located 
at Sarnath, Varanasi and Leh. Capacity building programme in the field of Indian art and 
culture are promoted through School of Archaeology, School of Archives, National Museum 
Institute of History of Art, Conservation and Museology, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Institute 
of Asian Studies. The National School of Drama and Kalakshetra Foundation also  offers 
some courses on the capacity building programmes.  
5. On the international front, th e Ministry is responsible for implementation of various 
UNESCO conventions and for entering into Cultural Exchange Agreements with partnering 
countries.

7. The Ministry, in its written reply, informed the Committee that it had projected a 
demand o f ₹3843.68 crore for 2021 -22 against which , it has been allocated an amount of 
₹2687.99 crore only. This comprises of ₹2609.23 crore under Revenue Head and ₹78.76 
crore under Capital Head. The  Ministry conceded that  reduced allocation will impact t he 
activities under the umbrella schemes such as Kala Sanskriti Vikas Yojana and Development 
of Museum etc. and organizations such as Archaeological Survey of India, National Archives 
of India, National Museum, NGMA and other Autonomous Bodies being provid ed support 
under Library, Museum and Akademies. Ministry of Culture has earmarked budgetary 
provisions among various rationalized/umbrella schemes, keeping in view the 
requirements/priorities of various constituents units and overall budgetary allocation. 
8. The Committee is concerned to note that allocation of ₹2687.99 crore in BE 2021-
22 is 14.66% lower than the allocation of ₹ 3149.85 crore  made in BE 2020 -21 which 
witnessed a severe cut of 29.77% at the RE 2020 -21 stage. What is equally disturbing is 
that there is a substantial shortfall in budgetary provisions for FY 202 1-22 vis-à-vis the 
projected demand of ₹ 3843.68 crore  by the Ministry. The Committee observes that 

5 
Culture is not a marginal sector but integral to all development because it not only 
promotes economic growth providing employment to a large section of population and 
spurs other economic sectors like tourism thereby contributing to the GDP of the 
country, but it also creates an environment for sustenance of our rich and diverse 
cultural herita ge. Since the Ministry of Culture has the onerous responsibility of the 
preservation, development and promotion of the country's tangible and intangible 
heritage as well as knowledge heritage, the Committee is convinced that the BE  
allocation of ₹ 2687.99 c rore for 2021 -22 is  not commensurate with the fund 
requirements of the Ministry. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry 
should be provided with a bigger financial outlay at the RE 2021 -22 stage so that the 
Ministry can achieve remarkable improvement in preservation and conservation of our 
cultural heritage and promotion of art and culture. 
9. The Committee observes that the Ministry should have a core set of priorities so 
that the budgeted allocations are not divided  in numerous  areas and become less 
impactful. The Committee accordingly recommends the Ministry to review its priorities 
and concentrate on schem es/programmes which are absolutely essential so that scarce 
resources can be optimally utilized for achieving the desired results in focused areas. 
10. The Committee simultaneously recommends that the Ministry may ensure that 
conservation works that may have been postponed in FY 2020-21 may be completed this 
year, so as to avoid permanent damages to our precious cultural heritage.  The 
Committee further recommends that the Ministry may request for higher allocation at 
the R.E. stage in FY 2021 -22 as per its requirements, so as to keep all the ongoing 
projects/ activities of the Ministry on track.

12. The Committee is constrained to note the consistent trend of decreasing

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA 
23. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) , established in 1861 , is an attached office 
of the Ministry of Culture.   The organization is headed by the Director General.   ASI is 
engaged in protection, preservation and conservation of 3692 centrally protected 
monuments/sites of national importance including 38 World Heritage Monuments and 50 site 

10 
museums, spread all over India  and the excavations of historical sites in the country in terms 
of Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958.    ASI is also 
administering the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act,  1972 and Indian Treasure Trove Act, 
1878. 
24. ASI has 36 Circles which are headed by Superintending Archaeologist, besides 1 
Mini Circles (Leh) located in the various States and Union Territories and 5 Regional 
Directorates (located at Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai,  Bangalore, and Bhopal) through which ASI 
carries out its activities.  Besides, there are 6 Excavation Branches, 2 Temple Survey 
Projects, 1 Building Survey Project, 1 Prehistory Branch, 1 Science Branch, 2 Epigraphy 
Branches (one for Sanskrit and Dravidia n at Mysore and the other for Arabic and Persian at 
Nagpur), and 1 Horticulture Branch having 4 Divisions at   Agra, Delhi, Mysore and 
Bhubaneswar and Pt. Deendayal Upadhyay  Institute of Archaeology to impact training in 
Archaeology stationed at Knowledge Park, Greater Noida, Uttar Pradesh.

NATIONAL MONUMENTS AUTHORITY 
78. The National Monuments Authority (NMA) came into existence in 2011 as a statutory 
body constituted under the provisions of Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites 
and Remains (Amendment & Validation) Act, 2010. The basic objectives for setting  up 
of NMA is implementation of the statutory provisions of prohibited and regulated areas 
of Centrally Protected Monuments for the purpose of construction, repair/renovation of 
buildings and public projects. In addition, the NMA frames monuments specific heritage

NATIONAL CULTURE FUND 
85. The mandate of National Culture Fund is a departure from earlier role of providing 
administrative and financial wherewithal for culture related endeavours in the 
country.  NCF accepts private institutions and individuals as equal partners of the 
government in the managem ent of the cultural heritage of India.  It enables the 
government to mobilize extra budgetary resources by allowing direct contributions to 
the NCF account maintained outside the Consolidated Fund of India.  All 
contributions to NCF are given 100% tax exem ption under Section 80 G (2) of the 
Income Tax Act of 1961. The nature of funding has been made very flexible with the 
donor having an option to either execute the selected project directly wherein the 
donors can appoint and monitor the executive agencies to carry out the project work 
subject to the predefined technical/aesthetic conditions laid down by the ASI.  
Alternatively, the donor can get the selected project executed through the ASI, which 
needless to say, has acquired an expertise to execute such p rojects. The Project 
Implementation Committee is set up with experts and representatives of the concerned 
institutions to monitor and implement the project.  The Project Advisory Committee 
consisting of nominees of donors, NCF and representatives of other interest groups 
oversee the project.  NCF facilitates all necessary clearances and permissions for the 
project.

modern age, especially to reach out to the young generation of the country. It is 
therefore a high time th at the Ministry makes sincere efforts to publish engaging 
content regularly through its various social media accounts, so as to generate the 
interest of the people in the rich cultural heritage of our country. This will not only help 
in increasing the foot fall at the various monuments and museums across the country, 
but also instill a sense of responsibility and pride in people who visit these places which 
will help with the maintenance and upkeep of the monuments.  
(Para 120) 
The Committee further desires the Ministry to do a thorough analysis of the websites 
and apps of the Ministry and its various organizations and carry out improvements and 
updates, where needed, since the websites and apps often become outdated.  
(Para 121) 
Lowering the fees charged for filming at Centrally Protected Monuments and Museums 
The Committee feels that the prices charged by the Ministry for filming at 
archaeological sites/monuments and museums, outside the period notified in the ASI 
notification mentioned above, are very high.  Films and videos featuring the 
archaeological monuments and museums showcase the richness of our cultural heritage 
to both domestic and foreign viewers alike and act as a free tool for publicity of the 
monuments and museums, thus propelling their tourism prospects. In light of this, the 
Committee recommends the Ministry to opt for a pay -as-you-wish payment model, 
wherein the filming company seeking the permission can pay the amount that it can 
afford to pay. The Ministry may also ensure that there are no d elays in grant of 
permission for filming at the monuments while putting in place enough safeguards to 
ensure that the filming activities do not cause any harm to the monuments and/or the 
archaeological artefacts.  

59 
(Para 124) 
Conservation of Regional Cultures and Traditions  
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should take steps to protect and preserve 
culture of regional communities and tribes such as the Dogras inhabiting the Jammu 
region, the Malayaraya  tribal group residing in the hilly areas of Kerala, and other 
similar local communities and tribes in other parts of the country, so that the unique 
richness of these cultures can be passed on to the future generations. Efforts may also 
be made to preserv e their historical sites and structures and maintain the memorials 
which symbolize the history of these regional communities, with the cooperation of the 
concerned community and the State Department. The Ministry may allocate funds to 
organize events and festivals to celebrate such regional cultures.  
(Para 125) 
Archaeological Research 
The Committee desires that the Ministry may carry out extensive marine archaeological 
research in the lost city of Dwarka, Gujarat. Likewise, there may be several 
undiscovered archaeological sites in the country, wherein use of the latest available 
technologies can be made to carry out archaeological research and excavation, since the 
same might possibly lead to major discoveries of artefacts of archaeological and 
historical significance.  
(Para 126)
