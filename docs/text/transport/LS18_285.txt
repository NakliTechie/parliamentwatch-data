ROAD DEVELOPMENT 
Recommendation/Observation 
4.  The Committee is of the view that the efficiency of Highway alignment in Greenfield 
projects is much higher than Brownfield projects. The Committee recommends that as per 
the policy of MoRTH, linear road construction and Greenfield projects should be 
accorded utmost priority for construction of road projects. 
(Para 41) 
Reply of the Ministry 
4.1     Majority of the existing Road projects have been developed in the past with 
connectivity of enroute towns as the key consideration. This has led to sub -optimal 
alignments, involving longer routes to be covered between major destinations. Longer 
the route, h igher is the cost and time involved in traversing the length of the Highway, 
which also entails associated environmental costs. There is a feasibility of completely 
new alignments, following crow -flight concept to the extent feasible, which reduces the 
distance between two major connectivity (Origin and Destination) points. The Ministry 
has issued guidelines dated 26.02.2018 for the DPR Consultants in this regard.  
4.2    Under Phase -I of Bharatmala Pariyojana, about 5,871 km length of Greenfield 
corridors have been identified for development. Out of these , projects in about 1,645 km 
have been awarded. Balance lengths are targeted to be awarded by FY 2021 -22.   
 
Recommendation/Observation 
5.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry should ensure the inclusion of provision 
for service roads on both sides across the National Highways network. The Committee 
feels that important roads with significant traffic load, such as Hyderabad -Kurnool 
Tollgate to Bengaluru Route Tollgate and other such toll gates, s hould be given priority. 
The Committee feels that this would allow smooth passage of city traffic as well as slow 
speed vehicles and lower the number of road accidents happening on the National 
Highways. 
(Para 42) 
Reply of the Ministry 
5.1     The service roads are provided only at urban/ semi -urban locations on NHs where 
local traffic is substantial and as such service roads are provided on a small length of 
NHs. 
5.2   However, as per the extant policy, for NH stretches developed to 6 -lane NH 
standards, th e service roads are provided in continuous length on both sides; unless 
otherwise specified for a project, the continuity is broken at locations of major bridges, 
at locations of toll plazas in a length of one km, in areas where no cross traffic or access 
traffic is expected provided the length of such section is at least one km. However, 
service roads may not be provided where there is no settlement or road side 
development abutting the Project Highway or the connecting roads are spaced more than 
2 km. 

4 
 
5.3   Service Road in a length of 40.838 Km has already been provided on NH -44 in 
Thondupally to Kurnool city section. Further, NHAI has also invited bids for 
construction of service road on both side from km.204.800 to 205.800 in Kurnool city 
for ensuring sa fety to local road users.  
 
MAINTENANCE AND REPAIR OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS 
Recommendation/Observation 
6.   The Committee strongly rec ommends that  a mechanism should be es tablished to 
periodically monitor the quality of Highways. A policy fra mework should be set in place 
to ensure that  repair works and maintenan ce of Highways are carried out within  a 
prescribed tim eframe so  as to ens ure certain minimum  standards of pliability on t he 
Highways. 
(Para 46) 
Reply of the Ministry 
6.1    Regional Officers of the Ministry in consultation with concerned State PWDs 
continuously monitor the condition of National Highways and works on National 
Highways requiring maintenance and repair are taken up for execution as and when 
required to keep the highways in traffic worthy condition.  
6.2      Ministry has issued a guideline making the uses of Network Survey Vehicles 
(NSVs) mandatory. These vehicle s, besides collecting data related to road inventory will 
also help in capturing relevant data related  to the quality and serviceability condition of 
the NHs. 
6.3    A mechanism to measure the health condition of roads has been developed & the 
provisions of equipment -based monitoring, using NSVs, Falling Weight Deflectometer 
(FWD), Mobile Bridge Inspection  Unit (MBIU) etc. at specified intervals, have been 
incorporated in the consultancy agreements for all projects under implementation and 
Operations & Maintenance.   
6.4    Policy decisions were taken in August, 2018 to increase the concurrent defects 
liability cum maintenance periods for projects implemented on EPC mode to 5 years & 
10 years for flexible (bituminous) pavements and rigid (cement concrete) pavements 
respectively. Thus, EPC contractor is made responsible for concurrent maintenance and 
rectification of defects during the above -mentioned enhanced periods of 5/ 10 years as 
are relevant. This is expected to make Maintenance & Repair (M&R) of NHs more 
systematic and organized.  
6.5    As already mentioned, the Ministry has recently taken policy decisi on to allow 
booking / reimbursement of expenditures for ordinary / petty repairs carried out in a 
Financial Year (FY) as a spill over commitment on subsequent FY. This is expected to 
improve efficiency of booking of expenditure.  
6.6    The concerns of the Committee have been noted and the Ministry shall take all 
necessary actions towards ensuring proper M&R of NHs and better pliability of NHs.

CHARDHAM PARIYOJANA 
The Committee takes note of the reasons that have led to the delay in the timely 
completion of the Chardham Pariyojana. The Committee feels that this Chardham 
project is of great significance to bolster domestic as well as foreign tourist influx in 
the regions it connects. The Commit tee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry 
may communicate with the MoEF&CC / HPC requesting them to expedite the final 
report in this matter so as to minimize further delays on this important project.  
(Para 24.5) 
 
BHARATMALA PARIYOJANA PHASE-I 
The Comm ittee notes that the Ministry has not furnished the reasons behind the 
delays in Bharatmala Pariyojana Phase -I, which is a flagship project to serve the 
infrastructural requirements of the country. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry assess in detai l, the reasons behind the delays and its impact on the 
budgetary requirements for this Phase. The Ministry may then develop a revised 
timeline, work out the revised budgetary requirements for completion of rest of the 
BM Phase-I and make sincere efforts to abide by the same.  
(Para 25.3) 
 
PROJECTS LINKED TO COASTAL ROADS AND PORT CONNECTIVITY 
The Committee is distressed to note that less than 10% of the 2000 km length of 
coastal and port connectivity roads identified for development under Bharatmala 
Phase-I, has been awarded till now, even when the targeted completion for 
Bharatmala Phase -I is 2021 -22. The Committee desires that the Ministry may 
urgently look into the reasons behind this inordinate delay. The timely completion of 
the 2000 km length of  coastal and port connectivity roads identified for BM Phase -I 
is directly linked with the performance of the Shipping sector of the country and 
thus, all efforts must be made by the Ministry to resolve the bottlenecks causing 
delay in this project.  
(Para 26.4) 
 
ECONOMIC CORRIDORS AND STRATEGIC HIGHWAYS 
The Committee notes the progress made by NHAI and NHIDCL in this regard. The 
Committee is of the view that a large portion of the length targeted under 
Bharatmala Pariyojana Phase-I has not even been awarde d yet, and thus, it is likely 
to miss the completion target set for 2021 -22. The strategic importance of Border

and International connectivity roads cannot be overstated. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry should make all necessary efforts to keep the 
completion of development of the Border and International connectivity roads on 
track.  
(Para 27.2) 
 
DELAYS IN ROAD PROJECTS 
The Committee takes note of the various efforts being made by the Ministry to 
minimize delays in its road projects. However, it  is clear from the major delays 
noticed in majority of road projects that the efforts of the Ministry are not yielding 
the required results. Delays in road projects not only cause significant inconvenience 
to the road users but create avoidable stress on t he financial health of the Ministry, 
as well as the sectors dependent on road connectivity in a particular region. In light 
of this, the Committee reiterates the recommendation made in Para 178 of its 278 th 
Report on Demands for Grants (2020 -21) of Ministr y of Road Transport and 
Highways seeking establishment of a Special Delay Mitigation Cell dedicated to 
address delays in ongoing road projects of the Ministry.  
(Para 28.9) 
 
Noting that no action seems to have been taken on creation of a dedicated body wit h 
the sole objective of minimizing and mitigating delays in the various projects of the 
Ministry, the Committee reiterates the recommendation made in Para 28.9 in 
Chapter III of this Report.  
(Para 29.2) 
 
CO-ORDINATION WITH STATE GOVERNMENTS 
The Committee appreciates that the existing schemes are being implemented by the 
Ministry in co -ordination with State Governments to bolster their public transport 
system. The Committee further desires that the Ministry may work out a scheme to 
provide special financial  as well as technical support, wherever possible, to public 
transportation systems for the States which tackle issues related to high pollution 
every year.  
(Para 30.4) 
 
STAFFING IN NHAI 
The Committee notes with concern that NHAI is still grappling with large number of 
vacant posts. For overcoming the problem, NHAI has initiated certain measures 
which include revision of Recruitment Rules, filling vacancies on direct and

deputation basis, etc. The Committee apprehends that these measures might not be 
enough to tide over the issue of shortage of manpower in the short term. The 
Committee would therefore recommend that NHAI may explore the avenue of 
engaging skilled manpower on contract basis to address the problem of shortage of 
skilled manpower.  
(Para 31.4)

light of the balance in the Escrow Account being insufficient for recovery of the 
damages. The Committee may also be apprised of the action taken by the Ministry / 
NHAI to ensure that such cases do not recur in the future.  
(Para 32.2) 
 
Public Project Monitoring Facility 
The Committee appreciates the initiative taken by NHAI for publication of 
information about its projects on its website ( https://nhai.gov.in/project-website.htm). 
The Committee, however, notes that several key columns are yet to be updated in 
respect of the projects displayed on this webpage. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that information regarding concessionaires working on the projects 
and details of officer(s) responsible for ensuring desired quality for a particular 
project, may also be published on the project details page.  
(Para 33.5) 
 
Promotion of Electric Vehicles in the country 
The Committee is of the view that electric vehicles are going to be ubiquitous in the 
near future, given their economic as well as environmental benefits. The Committee 
would like to know the reasons for deferring the proposal for exemption of 
registration fees for battery operated/ electric vehicles. The Committee feels that 
exempting registration fees for electric vehicles will give a boost to the demand for 
such vehicles in the country. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, in close 
co-ordination with other Ministries concerned, should strive for setting up the 
necessary infrastructure for this inevitable transition. The demand thus created, will 
also push domestic vehicle manufacturers to start giving more importance to this up 
and coming segment.  The Committee notes that countries like Norway, besides 
granting exemption from vehicle acquisition tax and several other incentives, even 
went to the extent of exempting electric vehicles from toll taxes. As a result of such 
incentives, more than 50% of new car sales in Norway are now electric vehicles. The 

90 
 
Committee recommends that the Ministry may examine the electric vehicles policies 
being adopted worldwide as well as Delhi Electric Vehicles Policy, 2020 and come 
out with a lucrative electric vehicle policy with a view to promote/ incentivize the 
adoption of electric vehicles in the country.  
(Para 34.2) 
 
Coordination with Local Police Departments

National Highways except in certain situations mentioned in their reply, is not 
actually implemented on ground since vehicles being stopped for checking of 
documents is a common sight, even on National Highway s. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry may write to all the State Governments to 
intimate/ remind them about the relevant policy in this regard.  
(Para 35.3)
