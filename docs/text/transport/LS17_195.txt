REPORT
The Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and
programmes for the development and promotion of tourism in the country. In the process, the
Ministry consults and collaborates with other stakeholders in the sector including various Central
Ministries/agencies, the State Governments/Union Territory Administrations and the representatives
of the private sector.
2. The administrative head of the Ministry is the Secretary (Tourism). The Secretary also acts
as the Director General (DG) Tourism. Directorate General of Tourism has a field formation of 20
offices within the country and 14 offices abroad and one sub-ordinate office/project i.e. Indian
Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM)/Gulmarg Winter Sports Project. The overseas offices
are primarily responsible for tourism promotion and marketing in their respective areas and the field
offices in India are responsible for providing information service to tourists and to monitor the
progress of field projects.
3. Tourism is promoted within India primarily by the Ministry of Tourism with the help of 5
Regional Offices and 15 Sub-Offices under them, with the involvement of the State Governments
and with the help of modern and conventional tools of promotion.
4. India Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) is the only public sector undertaking under
the Ministry of Tourism.
5. The Ministry also has the following autonomous institutions:
(i) Indian Institutes of Tourism and Travel Management (IITTM).
(ii) National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology (NCHMCT), and the
21 Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs).
6. Major Programmes/Schemes of the Ministry of Tourism are:-
1. Product/Infrastructure Development for Destinations and Circuits
2. Overseas Promotion and Publicity including Market Development Assistance
3. Assistance to IHMs/FCIs/IITTM/NIWS/NCHMCT etc.
4. Domestic Promotion and Publicity including Hospitality
5. Incentives to Accommodation Infrastructure
6. Capacity Building for Service Providers
7. Computerization and Information Technology
8. Assistance for Large Revenue Generating Projects
9. Externally Aided Projects including Buddhist Centres
10. Market Research Professional Services including 20 years Perspective Plans
11. Creation of Land Bank for Hotels
12. Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism Infrastructure Development
13. Construction of Building for IISM at Gulmarg, Kashmir

10. The Committee notes that during Eleventh Plan period, the Ministry of Tourism
could spend Rs.4896 crore out of the Rs.4953 crore allocated at RE, which is almost 98%
of the total allocations. The Committee also notes that the Ministry of Tourism has
sanctioned 1226 tourism projects for an amount of Rs.4090 crore during the Eleventh Five
Year Plan. But the Committee was not informed about the completion of these projects or
the current status of these projects. It hopes that after sanctioning the projects and
releasing the money, the Ministry of Tourism is monitoring its implementation by the State

4
Governments. It is learnt that the State Governments are diverting these funds according
to their own priority. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should actively pursue
each of these projects sanctioned to ensure that the sanctioned amount is utilized in the
prescribed manner for the approved project only.
11. The Committee observes that under these schemes, Union territories appear to have
been neglected as the data shows that very few projects were sanctioned to them. For
example, Lakshadweep–1, Daman and Diu–1, Dadra and Nagarhaveli–3, and nothing for
Andaman and Nicobar islands. The exceptions were Puducherry-20 and Chandigarh-11. If
non-receipt of the proposal has been the reason, the Committee recommends that the
Ministry of Tourism should guide and encourage these union territories to submit viable
projects so that they may also get the fund allocations under these very important schemes.
These areas have ample potential for tourism development.
12. The Committee also appreciates the NE-States on their eagerness in implementing
tourism projects during the Eleventh Plan period. All the NE States have got good amount
of allocations and that Committee hopes that all these projects are being implemented
earnestly and timely. This will increase the tourist visits in this region which will improve
their over all economic condition in the coming years.
13. The Committee also observes that bigger States having immense potential for
tourism development, particularly pilgrimage, wild life and niche tourism, have not had
commensurate projects/funds under these schemes. The Committee recommends that more
attention should be given during Twelfth Plan to those States which were yet to exploit their
potential for tourism development.

15. The Ministry of Tourism has informed that against Rs.1773.79 crore sought as Plan
allocation for the year 2013-14, Planning Commission allocated only Rs.1282.00 crore. The
Working Group on Tourism recommended various strategies to be taken for development and
promotion of tourism during Twelfth Plan period. Hence, substantial increases for major plan/
schemes were proposed by the Ministry of Tourism to achieve the desired targets. In view of the
reduced budget outlay it will be difficult to achieve the desired targets under all major schemes
such as product/infrastructure Development for Destination and Circuits, overseas promotion and
publicity, Assistance to IHMs/FCIs, domestic promotion and publicity etc.
16. During the course of evidence the Committee asked for the reasons behind the reduced
allocations at RE stage and the under-utilization of funds which was a deviation from the normal
practice of the Ministry of Tourism during the last many years. Secretary, Tourism explained that
due to a change in the interpretation in the norms for fund disbursement, the Ministry could not
spend last year Rs.360 crores.

6
17. The Secretary continued that due to a change in the interpretation of the rules regarding
utilization certificates by the Ministry of Finance, funds could not be allocated to various schemes
implemented by the Ministry. For instance, the Product Infrastructure Development for destination
and Circuits (PIDDC) were given Rs.575 crores but this year it has been reduced to 400 crores.
It is a drop of almost 40 per cent because the Ministry knows that with the new interpretation
of the rules they may not able to spend the allocation. The Secretary explained that during the year
2012-13, the Ministry of Finance insisted that utilization certificates of all the money allocated up
to the year 2010-11 should be obtained before releasing any money to the States. Even if, a single
scheme is lagging behind and the utilization certificates are not submitted by the concerned States,
the entire fund allocation to all the schemes of that particular State should be stopped.
18. Earlier the Ministry of Tourism was releasing 80 per cent of the allocation as first installment
and the utilization certificates were insisted only after a period of 18 months. The utilization
certificates were delayed due to delay in planning, acquisition of land, tendering, getting the DPR
done, allotment of work, etc. These preliminary things may take 14 to 15 months and during this
entire period they could not spend much of the allocation as the project will take off only after
completing all the above preliminary works. Therefore, all those schemes for which funds were
allocated were lagging behind and they could not submit the utilization certificate in time.
19. Since the Finance Ministry has insisted for the utilization certificates, the Ministry of
Tourism has changed the pattern of allocation and now the Ministry is allocating 20 per cent as
the first installment which is essential for completing all the preliminary works. Thereafter, they are
allocating 60 per cent for executing the work and finally 20 per cent as the last installment. In
fact, the Ministry of Tourism has pleaded with the Finance Ministry to permit them to adjust the
unspent balance with that particular State from one project to another, but that was also not agreed
to. Earlier the practice was that whatever the fund was available with the particular State was
readjusted against the active schemes within that State.
20. This Committee has always been arguing the case of Ministry of Tourism for
enhanced allocations every year during the examination of its Demands for Grants. The
Planning Commission, however, appears to have been reluctant to do so for reasons best
known to it. Normally, it does not give any reasons for this to a Ministry. During current
year also, only Rs.1282.00 crore has been allocated to the Ministry against a projected
demand of Rs.1773.79 crore. The Committee, keeping in view the various targets fixed for
development and promotion of tourism infrastructure, creating skilled and trained
manpower, exploring new tourism projects etc., reiterates its recommendation for providing
additional funds to the Ministry of Tourism so that the ideal of linking tourism with
employment generation and poverty alleviation could be achieved in reality.
21. The Committee notes that during the Eleventh Plan period, right from the opening
years, the spending had remained more than 98%. However, in the opening year (2012-13)
of the Twelfth Plan, the spending pattern had not been so. The BE 2012-13 stood at Rs.1210
crores which was reduced at the RE to Rs.950 crore. But only Rs.724.34 crore could be spent
upto February, 2013. If this spending is analyzed on the basis of the BE allocation of
Rs.1210 crore during the period, it forms only 60% of it. The Committee feels that there
are failures on the part of the Ministry in dealing with plan schemes whatever be the
adverse policy changes. The Ministry should have taken up the matter at the appropriate
Governmental levels, rather than blaming the system. The Committee recommends that
Ministry of Tourism needs to be more proactive and work as facilitator for implementation
of its schemes.
22. The Committee notes that the Ministry has not projected their achievement in
measurable terms by providing required information in quantified and comparable format to

7
the Committee. That would have helped the Committee to understand and appreciate the
real problems before the Ministry and concretize its opinion accordingly. Next time the
Committee would like to have information in user friendly format for its purpose.
23. The Committee also notes that some of the schemes such as incentives to
accommodation, assistance to large revenue generating projects, creation of land banks, etc.
are languishing for many years. The Committee recommends that these schemes may be
reviewed so that it may accomplish its targets.
24. The Committee observes that the strict guidelines on adherence to the utilization
certificate of each projects and further allocation of funds strictly thereafter has been able
to bring some accountability and discipline in the spending pattern of the Ministry. However,
the Committee is of the opinion that such measures should be based on practical
consideration as the infrastructure projects are required to get various sanctions/clearances
from so many agencies under different Ministries. Usually, the projects, involving land
acquisition and environment clearance are the most time consuming. Therefore, keeping all
these matters in mind, if the interpretation of rules/guidelines on fund disbursement
scheme has posed any problem to the ministries, it should be taken up at appropriate levels
and resolved as soon as possible.
25. The Committee notes that the Twelfth Plan has given emphasis to rural tourism and
projected tourism as a tool to reduce poverty. The Committee, however, finds no special
schemes or efforts to promote the rural tourism or spread the idea of tourism as a mass
activity. The Committee notes in the Budget 2013-14, just routine programmes which are
going on for the last so many years.
26. The Committee is also of the view that the plans and programmes of the Ministry
of Tourism should be aimed at positioning tourism as a major contributor of economic
growth and harness its direct and multiplier effects for employment and poverty eradication
in an environmentally sustainable manner. The schemes taken up by the Ministry should
be aimed at enhancing employment in the activities relating to tourism sector as well as at
fostering economic integration through developing linkages with other sectors.
27. The Committee further recommends that with the aim to achieve socio-economic
development in rural areas which have core competence in arts, crafts, heritage etc., the
Ministry of Tourism should provide assistance for development of tourism infrastructure and
capacity building of rural inhabitants in the States. The Ministry need to pursue with the
State Governments for completion of rural tourism projects sanctioned in a particular
financial year within a fixed time-frame.
PRODUCT/INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT FOR DESTINATIONS AND CIRCUITS
28. The Plan Allocation during 2013-14 is Rs.400.00 crore under Product/Infrastructure
Development for Destinations and Circuits (PIDDC). The allocation for 2012-13 was Rs.575.00
crore in BE which was reduced to Rs.480.00 crore in RE. The expenditure upto February, 2013
is given to be Rs.343.71 crore. The actual expenditure under PIDDC during 2011-12 was
Rs.599.20 crore.
29. Ministry of Tourism has informed that under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Product/
Infrastructure Development for Destinations and Circuits, they extend Central Financial Assistance
to the State Governments/Union territory Administrations for tourism projects, identified in
consultation with them, for the improvement of existing tourism products and also for developing
new ones. 100% Central Assistance is given under the scheme for development of destinations/
circuits including mega projects, and also for rural tourism infrastructure development.

8
30. The Ministry of Tourism provides financial assistance upto Rs.5.00 crore for the
development of destinations and Rs.8.00 crore for circuits. The upper limits of financial assistance
for development of mega destinations and circuits are Rs.25 crore and Rs.50 crore respectively.
For rural tourism infrastructure development, the thrust is on development of tourism infrastructure
at the identified rural tourism sites so that socio-economic benefits of tourism reach to the rural
community. A maximum of Rs.50.00 lakh is sanctioned for each project.
31. The Ministry has launched a scheme for development of nationally and internationally
important destinations and circuits through Mega Projects. So far, 54 mega projects have been
identified, out of which 40 projects have already been sanctioned. The mega projects are a
judicious mix of culture, heritage, spiritual and eco-tourism and aim to give tourists a holistic
perspective.
32. To achieve a higher level of coordination between various Ministries on issues of tourism
development, an Inter-Ministerial Coordination Committee for Tourism Sector (IMCCTS) was set
up in 2011-12 under the Chairmanship of Principal Secretary to the Prime Minister.
33. The scheme of Rural Tourism was started by the Ministry in 2002-03 with the objective
of showcasing rural life, art, culture and heritage at rural locations and in villages, which have core
competence in art & craft, handloom, and textiles as also an asset base in the natural environment.
It aimed to benefit the local community economically and socially, as well as to enable interaction
between tourists and local population for a mutually enriching experience.
34. Under this Scheme, the development/strengthening of physical infrastructure at the identified
rural site is supplemented by the capacity building of the local population to develop the identified
site as a rural tourism product. Rural tourism projects at 186 villages in 29 States/Union territories
have been sanctioned by the Ministry of Tourism since the inception of the scheme. 56 of these
sites are located in the North–Eastern region. A total amount of Rs 55.40 crores was sanctioned
for Rural Tourism projects during the Eleventh Five Year Plan. Projects at 90 sites have been
completed by December, 2012.
35. During the Twelfth Five Year Plan, the present scheme of Rural Tourism is being replaced
with the scheme of Rural Tourism Cluster. The modified scheme aims at developing a cluster of
villages a tourism product.
36. The Secretary, Tourism during the meeting informed about change in the funding pattern
for the tourism projects from the earlier pattern of 80% and the 20% to 20%-60%-20% for which
18 months time has been allowed for making expenditure at each phase.
37. The Committee notes that allocations for PIDDC scheme are getting reduced as
compared to allocations made during 2011-12 and 2012-13. The Committee also notes that
current year allocation stands further reduced at Rs.400 crore. The Committee thus finds
a regular reduction in allocation from the 2011-12 onwards. A re-interpretation of the
guidelines for funds disbursal in this regard was given as the reason. The Ministry of
Finance regulations provides that spending pattern of the first quarter is criteria for getting
eligible for subsequent allocations on various schemes of the States. The Committee
observes that these guidelines may have adverse impact on other projects also and in such
a case no scheme could be properly implemented by the States. The Committee recommends
that the utilization certificates may be obtained scheme/project-wise. If a particular scheme/
project is lagging behind in its execution, it should not be a reason to deny fund to the
other schemes/projects of the State. The Committee recommends that the guidelines on the
utilization certificates may be reviewed by the Ministry of Finance so that the proposed
projects may not be affected by the bottlenecks in the fund allocation norms.

9
38. The Committee observes that eventhough the rural tourism scheme was started in
the year 2002-03, the scheme has not made much progress so far. Only 186 projects have
been sanctioned since the inception of the scheme. The completion status is less than 50%
as only 90 projects out of 186 have been completed during a decade, i.e., 2002-2012. Now
the scheme has been replaced with Rural Tourism Cluster scheme. The Committee observes
that undue delay in implementing the scheme is not acceptable. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry of Tourism may set the guidelines in such a way that the
scheme may yield results at the earliest.
39. The Committee recommends that Ministry of Tourism should ensure convergence of
resources and expertise through coordinated efforts with State/UT Governments and
stakeholders for an integrated and intensive development of tourism infrastructure in the
country through careful planning and execution while giving importance for improvement in
accessibility, environmental and other aspects.
40. The Committee notes that some projects identified in the years 2008-09, 2009-10 or
2010-11 are yet to take off like Aurangabad destinations of Maharashtra, Eco and Adventure
circuit, Arunachal Pradesh, Mubarak Mandi Heritage, Jammu and Kashmir etc. as no fund
allocations have been made for these so far. The Committee recommends that the
concerned State Governments may be encouraged to take the follow-up actions in these
regards and the funds may be granted in each case at the earliest.
DOMESTIC CAMPAIGN
41. The Secretary, Tourism submitted that as far as domestic campaign to promote tourism is
concerned, it is the social awareness campaign that the Ministry undertakes, and does not promote
individual destinations, with the exception of the North-East and Jammu and Kashmir. Ministry of
Tourism just had a very successful International Tourism Mart for the North-East, at Guwahati.
Such a Mart is organized only for the North-East and not for any other Indian State.
42. Ministry of Tourism also provides financial assistance to tourism service providers approved
by the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India or by the State Tourism Department in the case
of North Eastern States and Jammu and Kashmir for participation in travel marts, annual
conventions of Indian Association of Tour Operators/Travel Agents Association of India/Association
of Domestic Tour Operators of India/Adventure Tour Operators Association/Federation of Hotels
and Restaurants Association of India/Hotels Association of India or any other National level Travel/
Tour Association approved/sponsored/recognized by MOT.
43. The various domestic campaign initiatives undertaken by the Ministry of Tourism during the
year 2012-13 included the following:
✜ Social Awareness Campaigns were released on Doordarshan in July, 2012 and in
September–October, 2012. Campaigns on Social Awareness with the themes of
Atithidevo Bhava, Campaign Clean India and Hunar Se Rozgar were released on Radio
Channels in July, 2012.
✜ The Ministry released campaigns on Doordarshan to promote tourism in the North East
Region and the State of Jammu and Kashmir in September–October, 2012.
✜ An outdoor campaign was undertaken in the T3 Terminal of the Delhi Airport and in
the arrival hall of Mumbai International Airport to showcase the tourism destinations
and products of the country.

10
✜ Campaign as part of Campaign Clean India was undertaken on FM Radio Stations
across the country as well as on Railway Tickets to promote awareness about the need
for Cleanliness and Hygiene.
✜ A Social Awareness Campaign is also being undertaken on Digital Cinema Screens
across the country.
44. The total expenditure up to February, 2013 under the head Domestic Promotion and
Publicity including Hospitality (DPPH) out of the RE amount of Rs.65.00 crore during 2012-13 was
Rs.55.62 crore. During the year 2013-14 the amount sanctioned is Rs.120.00 crore.

93. The details of some of the main taxes levied on tourism industry as well as the impact as
furnished by the Ministry of Tourism are as follows:
• Luxury Tax: The State Governments/UTs have the power to levy luxury tax on hotel
tariff. The taxes range from 4% to 20% from State to State. In many States, the levy
of luxury tax is on printed tariff as against the actual tariff paid by the visitor. The
actual tax rates shoot of up to 30% as the tariff charged can be substantially less than
printed tariff, particularly in the lean season.
• Taxes on Food and Beverages : V AT on food items ranges from 5% to 16.84% in
various states. Similarly, V AT on liquor varies from 13% to 58%. In addition, from
budget of 2011-12, a service tax of 10% (with an abatement of 70%) on air
conditioned restaurants has been imposed.
• Passenger and Road Transport Taxes varies from state to state. The taxes can be
levied per seat basis or per k.m. basis. According to an estimates made by Indian
Tourist Transport Association (ITTA), for a 3 day package between Delhi, Agra and
Jaipur, the total rate and passenger tax paid is 23% of the package cost.
• Taxes on Air Travel: The cost of Air travel in India is also considered on a higher side
compared to the competing destinations. This has not only deterred the domestic travel
but also made the packages for international tourists costly.
94. The Committee observes that the luxury tax on the hotel tariff ranges from 4% to
20% which may shoot up to 30% in the off season. Also the V AT on food items ranges from
5% to 16.84%. Tourist used to get discounts on the printed rates of room rent etc during
the off season but the tax should be paid on the original rate. The Committee recommends
that the tax concern of the tourism industry may favourably be considered.

ENVIRONMENT AND TOURISM
96. The Ministry of Tourism has given that as per the Supreme Court directions, the Ministry
of Environment and Forests has issued guidelines for tourism activities in tiger reserves in the
country. As per this guideline, no tourism activity is permitted in the core areas of Tiger reserves.
The Ministry of Tourism is in favour of regulated tourism in protected areas of the country. The
Ministry of Tourism does not favour a total ban in protected Areas including Tiger Reserves.
International Practices/best National practices should be taken into consideration by the Ministry of
Environment and Forests in finalizing eco-tourism guidelines for Protected Areas. Regulated and
controlled tourism in protected Areas is found to be generally helpful globally in conservation
efforts for wildlife in different countries.
97. As per these guidelines, hotels at the project stage itself are required to incorporate
various eco-friendly measures like sewage treatment plant (STP), rain water harvesting system,
waste management system etc. Once the hotel is operational, it can apply for classification under
a Star category to the Hotel and Restaurant Approval Classification Committee (HRACC) of the
Ministry. During the physical inspection of the hotel by the HRACC Committee, it is ensured that
in addition to the aforementioned measures, other measures like pollution control, introduction of
non-CFC equipment for refrigeration and air conditioning, measures for energy and water
conservation are also undertaken by the hotel. Under the guidelines for project level and
classification/re-classification of operational hotels it has been prescribed that the architecture of
the hotel buildings in hilly and ecologically fragile areas should keep in mind sustainability and
energy efficiency and as far as possible be in conformity with the local ethos and use local
materials.
98. The guidelines for tourism in and around Tiger reserves titled ‘National Tiger Conservation
Authority (Normative Standards for Tourism activities and Project Tiger) Guidelines, 2012' would
promote regulated tourism benefiting wildlife conservation and local communities.
99. As per these guidelines, all the States/UT Administrations have been asked to notify the
State-level eco-tourism strategy within a year from the date of notification of these guidelines. The
guidelines have recommended identification and monitoring of ecologically sensitive areas
surrounding the tiger reserves to ensure ecological integrity of corridor/buffer areas which will
prevent encroachment.
100. The Committee recommends that tourism activities should be based on scientific
carrying capacity and sustainability principles. As such, the normal peaceful living of the
wild life should not be disturbed by the tourism activities. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the ‘National Tiger Conservation Authority (Normative Standards for
Tourism activities and Project Tiger) Guidelines, 2012' should strictly be adhered to, for an
eco-friendly tourism development in the country.
101. The hotel projects also need to follow the guidelines as regards conservation of
natural resources, increased use of recycled products, solar energy, water conservation like
rain water harvesting etc. The Committee recommends that Ministry of Tourism should
strictly monitor other measures like pollution control, introduction of non-CFC equipment

21
for refrigeration and airconditioning, measures for energy and water conservation which are
to be undertaken by the hotels.
102. The Committee recommends that tourism activities at the protected areas should be
regulated as per the environment protection guidelines. No development is sustainable if it
is acted against the existence of nature. The Committee recommends that there should be
sincere efforts in environment protection while enhancing tourism facilities.

ANALYSIS OF BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS 2012-13 AND 2013-14
This Committee has always been arguing the case of Ministry of Tourism for
enhanced allocations every year during the examination of its Demands for Grants. The
Planning Commission, however, appears to have been reluctant to do so for reasons best
known to it. Normally, it does not give any reasons for this to a Ministry. During current
year also, only Rs.1282.00 crore has been allocated to the  Ministry against a projected
demand of Rs.1773.79 crore. The Committee, keeping in view the various targets fixed for
development and promotion of tourism infrastructure, creating skilled and trained
manpower, exploring new tourism projects etc, reiterates its recommendation for providing
additional funds to the Ministry of Tourism so that the ideal of linking tourism with
employment generation and poverty alleviation could be achieved in reality. (Para 20)

28
The Committee notes that during the Eleventh Plan period, right from the opening
years, the spending had remained more than 98%. However, in the opening year (2012-13)
of the Twelfth Plan, the spending pattern had not been so. The BE 2012-13 stood at Rs.1210
crores which was reduced at the RE to Rs.9S0 crore. But only Rs.724.34 crore could be spent
upto February, 2013. If this spending is analyzed on the basis of the BE allocation of
Rs.1210 crore during the period, it forms only 60% of it. The Committee feels that there

adverse policy changes. The Ministry should have taken up the matter at the appropriate
Governmental levels, rather than blaming the system. The Committee recommends that
Ministry of Tourism needs to be more proactive and work as facilitator for implementation
of its schemes. (Para 21)
The Committee notes that the Ministry has not projected their achievement in
measurable terms by providing required information in quantified and comparable format to
the Committee. That would have helped the Committee to understand and appreciate the
real problems before the Ministry and concretize its opinion accordingly. Next time the
Committee would like to have information in user friendly format for its purpose.
(Para 22)
The Committee also notes that some of the schemes such as incentives to
accommodation, assistance to large revenue generating projects, creation of land banks, etc.
are languishing for many years. The Committee recommends that these schemes may be
reviewed so that it may accomplish its targets. (Para 23)
The Committee observes that the strict guidelines on adherence to the utilization
certificate of each projects and further allocation of funds strictly thereafter has been able
to bring some accountability and discipline in the spending pattern of the Ministry. However,
the Committee is of the opinion that such measures should be based on practical
consideration as the infrastructure projects are required to get various sanctions/clearances

these matters in mind, if the interpretation of rules/guidelines on fund disbursement
scheme has posed any problem to the ministries, it should be taken up at appropriate levels

The Committee notes that the Twelfth Plan has given emphasis to rural tourism and
projected tourism as a tool to reduce poverty. The Committee, however, finds no special
schemes or efforts to promote the rural tourism or spread the idea of tourism as a mass
activity. The Committee notes in the Budget 2013-14, just routine programmes which are
going on for the last so many years. (Para 25)
The Committee is also of the view that the plans and programmes of the Ministry
of Tourism should be aimed at positioning tourism as a major contributor of economic
growth and harness its direct and multiplier effects for employment and poverty eradication
in an environmentally sustainable manner. The schemes taken up by the Ministry should
be aimed at enhancing employment in the activities relating to tourism sector as well as at
fostering economic integration through developing linkages with other sectors. (Para 26)
The Committee further recommends that with the aim to achieve socio-economic
development in rural areas which have core competence in arts, crafts, heritage etc., the

capacity building of rural inhabitants in the States. The Ministry need to pursue with the

that the tax concern of the tourism industry may favourably be considered. (Para 94)
The Committee observes that one of the major bottlenecks of tourism sector today
is the absence of uniform and rational Road Transport Tax structure in States and
multiplicity of taxes at inter-state borders. This is acting as an impediment to seamless
movement of tourist vehicles across State borders. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that tourist vehicles with all India permit may be taxed uniformly so that the bottle-necks
in the tax structures and the difficulties faced by tourists in crossing the state borders could
be addressed properly. (Para 95)
EVIRONMENT AND TOURISM
The Committee recommends that tourism activities should be based on scientific
carrying capacity and sustainability principles. As such, the normal peaceful living of the
wild life should not be disturbed by the tourism activities. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the ‘National Tiger Conservation Authority (Normative Standards for
Tourism activities and Project Tiger) Guidelines, 2012’ should strictly be adhered to, for an
eco-friendly tourism development in the country. (Para 100)
The hotel projects also need to follow the guidelines as regards conservation of
natural resources, increased use of recycled products, solar energy, water conservation like
rain water harvesting etc. The Committee recommends that Ministry of Tourism should

33
strictly monitor other measures like pollution control, introduction of non-CFC equipment
for refrigeration and air-conditioning, measures for energy and water conservation which
are to be undertaken by the hotels. (Para 101)
The Committee recommends that tourism activities at the protected areas should be
regulated as per the environment protection guidelines. No development is sustainable if it
is acted against the existence of nature. The Committee recommends that there should be
sincere efforts in environment protection while enhancing tourism facilities. (Para 102)
ITDC
The Committee was given to understand that occupancy in ITDC hotels has remained
40-50 percent even in the peak seasons. The Committee feels this is on low side and does
not know the reasons for this. ITDC has to function and compete with the professionally-
managed hotels in private sector. Therefore, it has to be competitive as regards services
offered by them. (Para 107)
The Committee noticed that the web sites of the Ministry of Tourism or the
Incredible India has no advertisement of ITDC hotels. The Committee feels that proper
links and advertisements about ITDC Hotels may be given in all the web sites under the
Ministry of Tourism. The Committee feels that ITDC hotels should also be marketed as part
of the overseas campaign. (Para 108)
The Committee notes that the financial performance of ITDC has improved during
the last two years. The Committee is hopeful that ITDC would keep its recovery path intact
in the coming years. The Committee appreciates ITDC on performing its social commitment
by extending its help organizing training of youth and contributing to the Clean India
Campaign. (Para 109)
The Committee recommends that light and sound shows have to be introduced in
other tourist spots of the country so that the famous tourist places may be made more
attractive to the visitors. Simulator based light and sound shows as available in the
Chardham Temple, Gangtok can be experimented in other parts of the country with
different themes. ITDC may take the initiative in this regard. (Para 110)
VISA ON ARRIV AL
The Committee notes the recent development in the visa-on Arrival Policy.  The
Committee is surprised to hear that because of non-availability of foreign language experts
of Japanese, German etc. the Ministry is seeking help from locals for the visa related
issues in the foreign countries. (Para 118)
The Committee recommends that our visa forms should be bilingual incorporating
the local language of the country. This will facilitate the tourists and increase their
confidence in visiting our country. The Tourism promotion offices should playa major role
in this regard. The Committee has reason to believe that many of our Tourism promotion
offices are filled up with deputationists who have no idea about tourism of India or the local
languages of the countries they are posted. (Para 119)
The Committee recommends that there should be clarity in issuance of inner-line
permits for the North-East. The Ministry of Tourism should take up the issue with the
Ministry of Home Affairs to clear all confusions in this regard. The Committee recommends
for immediate corrective measures in this regard. (Para 120)

34
NICHE TOURISM PRODUCTS
The Committee recommends for more cruise tourism projects at the viable states on
the lines of the initiative taken in Cochin Port. All the Major Ports, especially the
Mormugao Port has immense potential of cruise tourism which needs to be explored by the
Ministry of Tourism for development. (Para 123)
Adventure Tourism
The Committee notes the various measures taken in the Adventure tourism.  The
Committee also recommends that adequate trained people should be deputed in all the
adventure spots to monitor the participants and for rescue operations if required.
(Para 125)
The Committee feels that there is lot of potential in all these new tourism products.
The Ministry of Tourism should work on a detailed guide map on all these areas.
(Para 127)
SAFETY AND SECURITY
The Committee observes that safety and security is equally important, if not more,
for the growth of tourism sector. The Committee is concerned about the recent incidents
of attacks on tourists at different parts of the country. In addition to this, the Committee
noticed that even though safety guidelines are in place, these are less enforced and the
authorities are negligent towards these aspects until there are some incidents. All the State
Governments therefore may be persuaded/helped to set up tourism Police and prevent touts
and goons from various tourist destinations, airports, railway stations, etc. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the safety measures should be strictly implemented and
monitored at the different levels. The 24x7 infoline in foreign languages is a welcome step
up to attract the tourists. However, the Ministry should ensure that the people who are
manning it are capable and the service is available at ease. It should assist the tourists,
whenever they want. (Para 130)
GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The Committee observes that the Tourism Ministry is mainly a fund-disbursing
agency. More than 90 per cent of its Plan allocation every year goes to four schemes
relating to infrastructure development, training assistance to institutes and publicity -
overseas and domestic. Once these funds are released, it seems it is left with the State
Governments and Union territories to utilise them. Nowhere is it reflected how the Ministry
is monitoring the utilisation of these funds and how they get feedback on whether the
States and Union territories are actually utilising these funds for the purpose for these
funds have been allocated. The Committee desires to know whether there is any robust
mechanism at different levels to coordinate these various aspects. (Para 131)
The Committee observes that sky rocketing of airfares and lack of government
control over it has recently affected the domestic tourist movement in the country. The
Committee also observes that higher user fee and ATF surcharges have eventually made the
air traffic journey un-affordable to many. The DGCA has taken some steps in this regard.
The Committee however, recommends that a more regular and strict watch on predatory
airfares being resorted by the airlines needs to done to prevent unreasonable airfares
especially during the peak seasons. (Para 132)

35
The Committee also observes that the rail travellers are facing problems due to shabby
coaches which are usually unclean with rodents roaming freely. The toilets are very dirty and
are seldom cleansed during the journey. Beggar menace and under quality food served are
other areas to be immediately attended. All these problems have made the train journey a
nightmare for most of the travellers and the tourists in particular. The Committee
recommends that Ministry of Tourism may take up the matter with Railway Board so that the
train journey may be made more attractive and comfortable to the tourists. (Para 133)
The Committee observes that we are not utilizing the full potential of tourism in
India. We always feel very sad about the fact that, probably, there is no country like ours
that has snow, hills to seas and beaches, adventure tourism, wildlife tourism, medical
tourism, etc., within the country. India has such a wide variety and destination. In spite of
that, the confidence does not seem to come that we are able to actually reach anyway close
to using our potential. (Para 134)
