INTRODUCTION

 development and regulation of Civil Aviation and for devising and implementing schemes for orderly growth and

 services and carriage of passengers and goods by air.  The Ministry is also administratively responsible for the
 Commission of Railway Safety, a statutory body set up under the Railway Act.  The Ministry has under its

AIR INDIA
10.     Air India was established in 1953 under the Air Corporations Act, 1953 to provide safe, efficient, adequate,
 economical and properly coordinated international air transport services.  The undertaking of Air India was transferred
 to and vested in Air India Limited, a public limited company registered under the Companies Act, 1956 with effect
 from 1.3.94, after the Air Corporations (Transfer of Undertakings and Repeal) Act, 1994 came into effect.  As on
 31.09.2001, the Company has a total staff of 16,144 employees in India and abroad.  It has a fleet of 27 aircraft
 including four aircraft on dry lease.
11.     The Annual Plan Outlay of Air India for the year 2001-2002 was Rs.445.44 crores, which mainly comprised of
 Aircraft Loan Repayments of Rs.285.44 crores and Capital Expenditure of Rs.150.00 crores.  A token provision for

 expenditure was to be incurred out of the Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources of Air India Limited.  However, the
 actual expenditure is estimated at Rs.384.23 crores resulting in a net shortfall of Rs.61.21 crores, which is mainly on
 account of deferment of expenditure on non-aircraft projects due to financial constraints faced by the company.

14.     The Committee brought to the notice of Ministry of Civil Aviation that during the 9
th Plan, Air India had planned
 to standardize its fleet to three aircraft type.   The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation as to what
 extent Air India has been able to achieve standardization of its fleet and what are the reasons for failure?  In response,
 the Ministry of Civil Aviation informed that Air India had planned to standardize its fleet to two/three types by phasing
 out the B747-200, B747-300 Combi and A300B4 aircraft types.  Although Air India reduced its B747-200 fleet to four
 aircraft, it could not sell the balance of the B747-200 aircraft and the B747-300 Combi in view of poor resale values
 and the non-induction of a suitable replacement long-range aircraft.  The B747-200 aircraft are presently being
 maintained in the fleet to cater to VVIP operations and short haul operations of Gulf.  Efforts are also on to sell three
 A300 B4 aircraft.  It was stated that Air India has been able to standardize its fleet to some extent by inducting on dry
 lease five A310-300 aircraft to support the operations by extending the route network.  After the disposal of B747-200
 and A300B4, Air India will be left with a three-type fleet viz B747-400/300 and the A310s.  The Committee was
 further informed that an expert Committee has already been constituted in order to assess Air India's long-term fleet
 requirements and the report is expected to be submitted shortly.  This would by and large determine the type of the
 fleet, which would be acquired during the Tenth Five Year Plan period.
15.     The Committee, in its 42
nd Report on Demands for Grants 2000-2001 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation, had
 expressed hope that the rationalization of fleet with less types of aircraft will help reduce the demand for investment
 and spares, maintenance facilities and simulators and will also result in better utilization of aircraft infrastructural
 facilities and technical manpower of pilots and maintenance engineers.  The Committee, however, notes that while an
 expert committee has been set up to assess Air India's long term fleet requirements but no time-frame has been given to
 the Committee within which it should submit its recommendations.  The aircraft are required to be acquired during the
 Tenth five Year Plan Period, that is, 2002-2007.  In fact, a decision on this should have been completed by now so that
 by the time the Tenth Plan commenced, the Air India had started acquisition of the type of aircraft it proposed to
 acquire during this period. The Committee, therefore, reiterates the recommendation made in its earlier report that the
 Air India decide in time bound manner and keep the Committee fully apprised as to what kind of fleet it should go in
 for so that necessary steps may be taken immediately to rationalise their fleet.

18.     The Committee observed that the projected operational performance of Air India for the year 2002-2003 was on
 the lower side in comparison to anticipated performance for 2001-2002.  The Committee enquired about the reasons for
 the same and whether any steps had been planned to improve the projected performance during the year 2002-2003?
19.     In reply, the Ministry of Civil Aviation clarified that the projected operational performance for the year 2002-03
 was an extract from the business plan of Air India. The business plan has undergone a change since its formulation, as
 dry lease of capacity was not built into the plan. Air India has dry leased five aircraft between December 2000 and
 January 2002, adding approximately 18% to its capacity. This has not been reflected in the ATKMs projected for 2002-
03 (business plan). The projected operational performance for 2002-03 would be based on the pattern of operations,
 acquisition of aircraft on dry lease during the remainder of 2002-03. In addition to the 5 A310-300 aircraft taken on dry
 lease, Air India is in the process of acquiring 3 more aircraft on dry lease, which are expected to join the fleet by
 December 2002. Apart from this, Air India is in the process of phasing out two B747-200 and three A300-B4 fleet. This
 is likely to add to Air India's operational efficiency as well as improvement in financial performance.
 
20.     As regards the steps taken by Air India to improve its operational performance, it was informed that several steps
 had been taken by Air India in the recent past to improve the operational efficiency. These measures have resulted in an
 improvement in financial performance of Air India in so far as from a loss of Rs.296.94 crores in 1996-97, Air India
 has curtailed its losses to Rs.44 crores in 2000-01 and is expected to make a marginal profit in 2001-02.  The passenger
 load factor also improved from 1996-97 from 65.70% to 73.00% in 2001-02.
21.     The Committee appreciates the steps taken and success achieved by Air India to improve its operational
 performance and the fact that Air India has not only been able to curtails its losses in the year 2000-2001 but is also
 expected to make a marginal profit in 2001-2002.
22.     During the meeting, it was observed by some Members of the Committee that a lot of people from Kerala, mostly
 belonging to the lower/middle strata of the society, have to go to Gulf countries frequently and that Air India tends to
 increase the fares on its Gulf flights from Kerala routes quite often, which also encourages other airlines to increase
 fares on this sector.  Some of the Members also expressed the apprehension that since the summer vacation was
 approaching, Air India would again increase the charges.  It was also brought out fares on this sector were unreasonably
 high when compared to Air India's fares on other routes. The Committee sought the views of the Secretary, (Civil
 Aviation) on the issue raised by the Members.  The Secretary replied as under:-
"Sir, I will look into this. As you know, Sir, I also from Kerala. I get these complaints everyday. I will look into the fare
 structure. If any overcharging is being done, we will look into that."
23.     The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding the fare structure of national carriers from
 Kerala to Gulf Countries. Ministry of Civil Aviation have informed that Published fares on Internal routes are fixed
 through IATA's machinery and subsequently put up to the respective Governments for approval. However, the actual
 fares quoted in the market are usually much lower than IATA fares.  In competitive markets, market forces play a
 dominant role in determining prices. Market fares thus depend largely upon the demand and supply situation. Project
 features like frequency timings, direct/indirect operation, the nature of traffic and seasonality also influence fares. The
 current Indian Airlines market fares between India and Gulf are approx. 40% lower than the IATA approved fares, and
 are in line with market fares offered by other airlines on the Kerala/Gulf routes.
24.     The Committee is not satisfied with the information furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation and would like to
 seek replies as promised by the Secretary during the meeting.  The Committee would like to be apprised of the hike in
 fare of Air India/Indian Airlines flights from Kerala to Gulf countries, during the last three years, alongwith brief
 reasons therefor.

31.       The Committee observed that as against the Revised Estimated loss of Rs.250.65 crores for the year 2001-02,
 Indian Airlines had projected a loss of Rs.98.90 crores for the year 2002-2003 and enquired as to on what basis lower

 that the improvement in financial performance is expected to be on account of improved utilization of capacity in terms
 of Load and Seat Factors. Also, in order to partly neutralize the effect of increase in various input costs, the company
 has assumed improvement in yield, which would result in additional revenue of Rs.140.00 crores during the year 2002-
2003.

AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA
43.     The Airports Authority of India (AAI) aims at providing world class Airport services and facilities as are
 necessary for the sale and efficient operation of air transport services and to make available amenities for passengers
 and other users of the airports. 
44.     During the evidence, the attention of the Secretary (Civil Aviation) was drawn to a note internally circulated by
 the Airports Authority of India wherein it had been stated that the long-term leasing of the airports would not require
 any amendment of the AAI Act.  In this connection, the Committee sought the comments of the Ministry. In their reply,
 The Ministry of Civil Aviation have stated as under:-
"As per the legal advice furnished by Airports Authority of India's legal consultant (M/s Amarchand Mangaldas &
 Suresh Shorff, appointed in connection with the leasing of the airports), which was duly supported with the legal
 opinion given by Justice Bhagwati, Ex-Chief Justice of India, the proposed long term leasing could be accomplished
 within the provisions of the existing statute viz. the Airports Authority of India Act, 1994 read with the Rules under the
 Aircraft Rules 1937.  Accordingly, in the background paper furnished earlier, it was indicated that it would not require
 any amendment of Airports Authority of India Act, which was shown as one of the advantages of opting for the route of
 'Long term leasing'.
However, subsequently, the Ministry of Law to whom the legal opinion was forwarded for vetting has advised that
 amendment by way of amplification of Section 12 of the Airports Authority of India Act for providing an enabling
 provision is a must. Hence the amendment Bill.
It may be seen that other routes of privatization like corporatisation, outright sale, etc. where the ownership of airports
 will also shift to the successful bidder, will involve large scale amendments, unlike the route of long term lease for
 which simple amendment by way of amplification of Section 12 of the Airports Authority of India Act is only
 required."

AIR INDIA
The Committee, in its 42
nd Report on Demands for Grants 2000-2001 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation, had expressed
 hope that the rationalization of fleet with less types of aircraft will help reduce the demand for investment and spares,
 maintenance facilities and simulators and will also result in better utilization of aircraft infrastructural facilities and
 technical manpower of pilots and maintenance engineers.  The Committee, however, notes that while an expert
 committee has been set up to assess Air India's long term fleet requirements but no time-frame has been given to the
 Committee within which it should submit its recommendations.  The aircraft are required to be acquired during the
 Tenth five Year Plan Period, that is, 2002-2007.  In fact, a decision on this should have been completed by now so that
 by the time the Tenth Plan commenced, the Air India had started acquisition of the type of aircraft it proposed to
 acquire during this period. The Committee, therefore, reiterates the recommendation made in its earlier report that the
 Air India decide in time bound manner and keep the Committee fully apprised as to what kind of fleet it should go in
 for so that necessary steps may be taken immediately to rationalise their fleet.
The Committee appreciates the steps taken and success achieved by Air India to improve its operational performance
 and the fact that Air India has not only been able to curtails its losses in the year 2000-2001 but is also expected to
 make a marginal profit in 2001-2002.
The Committee is not satisfied with the information furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation and would like to seek
 replies as promised by the Secretary during the meeting.  The Committee would like to be apprised of the hike in fare
 of Air India/Indian Airlines flights from Kerala to Gulf countries, during the last three years, alongwith brief reasons
 therefor.

INDIAN AIRLINES LIMITED
It is evident from the statements given by the Secretary (Civil Aviation) that the Government's decision not to procure
 any new aircraft for Indian Airlines/Air India pending disinvestment of the two national carriers has adversely affected
 the two companies financially as well as commercially. An atmosphere of uncertainty was allowed to be prevailed by
 the Government.   For the last so many years, there had been a number of proposals etc. for procurement of aircraft
 which, however, did not materialize for one reason or the other.   This had affected the market share, route network as
 well as the product upgradation programme of Air India and Indian Airlines to some extent.   Now that the Government
 has decided to acquire the aircraft, the Committee appreciates the decision of the Government.
The Committee, therefore, calls upon the Government to apprise the Committee of the specific programme of the
 acquisition of aircraft, source of funding, lead period for procurement alongwith specific provisions of financial outlays
 (year-wise).  The Committee would also like to be informed about the slippages in the programme/schedule, as and
 when they occur.
The Committee observes that both Air India and Indian Airlines have a very low equity base.  Indian Airlines has a paid
 up capital of Rs.105.19 crores against an authorized capital of Rs.500 crores.  Similarly, Air India has a paid up capital
 of Rs.153.84 crores and an authorized capital of Rs.500 crores.  This low equity base has resulted in curtailing their
 borrowing ability.  It is of immediate necessity to enhance the equity base of both the airlines to enable them to achieve
 a healthy debt-equity ratio, and also enable them to go in for further borrowings.  The Committee has been informed
 that a commitment has already been made by the Ministry of Finance to contribute to the equity of Indian Airlines
 linking it to the acquisition of aircraft.  The Committee recommends that similar dispensation would be necessary for
 Air India as well.  The Committee appreciates the decision of the Government to strengthen the airlines instead of
 going in for disinvestment as stated by the Secretary in his deposition before the Committee.  For this purpose, the
 Committee feels that the Ministry should also come up with the proposal of financial restructuring, if necessary, for
 both the airlines.
(Para 39, 40 and 41)
DISCONTINUATION OF SERVICES ON CERTAIN SECTORS

 been withdrawn together with the detailed reasons and consider possibility of resuming operations on Chennai-
Coimbatore sector within a time-bound manner.

AIRPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA
The Committee is dismayed to note that the Ministry of Civil Aviation has given direction to keep in abeyance the
 ongoing projects in the four metro airports which includes the International Terminal Building at Netaji Subhash
 Chandra Bose International Airport, Kolkata and the airports at Mumbai and Delhi.  Keeping in view the adverse
 impact the Air India and the Indian Airlines had to suffer on account of the disinvestment process, the ongoing work at
 these airports should not be allowed to suffer.  The Committee strongly recommends that the ongoing and
 developmental activities at the four metro airports should not be kept in abeyance and should be allowed to proceed as
 per the earlier time schedule.
The Committee is aghast to note that the Ministry of Civil Aviation proceeded with the leasing process merely on the
 advice of a legal consultant engaged by the Airports Authority of India.   The Ministry did not take the advice of the
 Ministry of Law in the matter when the Cabinet approved in January 2000, the long term lease of Airports Authority of
 India airports as and when found suitable.  Subsequently, on the advice of the Ministry of Law, the Government had,
 on 30
th November 2000, introduced the Airports Authority of India (Amendment) Bill, 2000 in the Parliament to
 amend the Airports Authority of India Act, 1994 in order to undertake the leasing of the airports.  The Bill was later
 referred to this Committee for examination and report.  Again, without waiting for the final approval of the Parliament
 and before the amended law came into existence, the Government is going ahead with the process of leasing of four
 metro airports as stated by Secretary (Civil Aviation):
"…..We are also planning to lease out four metro airports.  That process has been on for some time. The Finance
 Minister had also announced it in his Budget speech. …."
The Committee would like to be apprised of the circumstances and reasons for (i) not seeking the advice of the Ministry
 of Law before initiating the process of leasing of the airports and (ii) going ahead with the process of leasing of four
 metro airports without a proper law to this effect.
