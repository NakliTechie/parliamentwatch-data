6.5 Landlord Model Port Development - After the enactment of the MPA Act, 2021 in 
February, 2021. The Major Port Trusts were converted into the Major Port Authorities with more 
freedom in decision making through their Boards.   Further, for facilitating the private 
investment, some very conducive interventions were made by the Ministry in recent years like 
the Model Concession Agreement of 2018 was also amended in 2021 to include more facilitating 
features like compensation payment at the pre-COD stage, provision for extension of the existing 
Concession Agreement through contemporary market price and bidding, provision for change of 
cargo in case of change of law etc. Alternat e dispute resolution mechanism was introduced by 
forming a Conciliation and Settlement Committee. Guidelines for reviving the stressed projects 
formulated. Mechanism for dispute resolution through „Society for Affordable Resolution of 
Disputes –Ports‟ (SAROD) was set-up. 
       
6.6 The above interventions have been received well by the investors and market. With these 
interventions, sufficient numbers of bids are now being received in PPP Projects and the royalty 
quotes have also shown upward trends. JNPA has now become a Port being fully operated by the

Assistance to Ship Building, Research and Development 
Recommendation/Observation 
13. The Committee further notes that the shipbuilding industry has the same impact as 
infrastructure sectors due to higher multiplier effects on investment and turnover and high 
employment potential. The shipbuilding industry is a strategically important industry. To ensure 
the safety of our vast coastline, naval requirement of so phisticated and modern vessels is 
growing rapidly. Maritime Vision 2030 envisions Indian shipbuilding to become competitive with 
reaching the threshold on volumes by 2025 and then building the momentum in high volumes to 
reach “Make in India Make for World ” levels and be one of the top 10 shipbuilding nations in 
the world. The Financial Assistance to Ship Building was one of the initiatives made by the 
government in furtherance of this objective. However, the funds under this head have never been 
fully utilised. The Committee desires that the Ministry should look into the reasons why sufficient 
proposals for Assistance to Ship Building, Research and Development were not received and 
take remedial steps. 
 (Para 125) 
Reply of the Ministry 
13.1 Reasons for low off-take of the allocated budget under the Policy:- 
(i) Presently the shipyards that are registered under the policy (except Cochin Shipyard), 
are medium or small-scale shipyards; are building small ships / boats only. Hence the 
subsidy provided under the policy is relatively less. 
(ii) Private shipyard like, ABG Shipyard, Bharti Shipyard and Reliance Naval and 
Engineering Limited, Pipavav, which existed during the starting period of Policy has 
gone under NCLT and still remains non -operational. On the other  hand, the bigger 
government-owned shipyards like Hindustan Shipyard Limited  (Visakhapatnam), 
Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders Limited  (Mumbai), Garden Reach Shipbuilders & 
Engineers (Kolkata) and Goa Shipyard Ltd . (Goa) are predominantly engaged with 
naval projects only, which is not eligible for subsidy under SBFA Policy. The newest 
bigger facility like L&T Shipbuilding, Kattupalli (Chennai) is also engaged with the 
construction of defence orders only. It is to be noted that while the Policy was being 

26 
 
conceptualised during 2014 -2015, the output capacity from these bigger shipyards 
had been factored in. 
(iii) The above facts along with the limitation to the number of bigger shipyards to 
construct bigger size vessels is one of the main reasons, as to why none of th e Indian 
Shipyards have secured shipbuilding orders from main fleet commercial vessel 
owners for shipbuilding contracts for large ships. 
(iv) Further, in view of the low off -take under the policy; a periodic review of the 
Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Policy (SBFAP) shall be carried out, every two 
years, in line with the Guidelines of the Policy.  SBFAP is being reviewed in 
consultation with DGS and stakeholders. 
 
13.2 The DGS is implementing the „Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Policy 2016 -2026‟ 
which is rolled out by Ministry of Shipping, as per the guideline issued vide file no. Sy -
16023/6/2015-SBR dated 27.10.2017, as amended in April, 2022 and further amended in 
November, 2020. 
 
13.3 Under the SBFA policy, the Ministry is providing Financial Assist ance of 20% of 
contract price, actual receipts, fair price (whichever is least) to Indian shipyards for contracts 
signed between April 1, 2016 to March 31, 2026, including the said dates, to promote the Indian 
shipbuilding industry. Under the policy, the f inancial Assistance extended would be reduced by 
3% every three years . Detailed guidelines of the policy are available on the website of the 
Ministry and that of the Directorate General of Shipping. 
 
13.4 The entire process, starting from one -time registra tion of shipyard, making application 
for in -principle approval by shipyards, application for release of Financial Assistance, 
submission of documents and processing the approval by Competent Authority etc., are done 
online on the web portal developed for t his purpose (www.shipbuilding.nic.in). This web -portal 
eliminates physical transactions related to application, submission of documents, scrutiny, 
approval and sanction of the financial assistance.

Shipping Corporation of India  
Recommendation/Observation 
6. The Committee feels that a strategic asset like Shipping Corporation of India ( SCI) is 
critical not only to its blue economy policy but also to its maritime security. SCI is not only 
India‟s largest merchant cargo carrier, it has also diversified into the Indian offshore marine 
business and has gained expertise in manning and operating h ighly specialised vessels. The 
Committee feels that the policy of disinvesting profitable and Core sector PSUs of the 
Government is irrational. There is justification in disinvestments of unviable PSUs. But PSUs 
performing well especially in core sectors should not be sacrificed for short term financial gains. 
While acknowledging that disinvestments enable the Government to check fiscal deficits, the 
Committee feels that a balance should be maintained to protect national strategic interests. The 
Committee r ecommends that the Government may rethink its policy of disinvesting profitable 
PSUs especially in core sectors. 
 (Para 83) 
Reply of the Ministry 
6.1 Going forward, the strategic disinvestment policy will be guided by the New Public 
Sector Enterprise (“PSE”) Policy for Atmanirbhar Bharat, issued with the approval of Cabinet, 
which has been notified on 4 th February, 2021. As per the policy , public sector commercial 
enterprises have been classified as strategic and non-strategic sectors The policy mandates that in 
broad strategic sectors, there will be only bare minimum presence of the public sector enterprises 
with the remaining Central Public Sector Enterprises ( CPSEs) to be either pri vatized or merged 
or subsidiarized with other CPSEs or closed. In non - strategic sectors, CPSEs will be privatized, 
or closed. The policy, however, does not apply to certain entities which are non -profit in nature; 
or which deal with vulnerable sections or have development/promotional role. 
 
6.2 The objectives of the strate gic disinvestment policy have been articulated by the Finance 
Minister in the Budget speech 2021-22 which are as under:- 
a. Minimizing presence of Central Government Public Sector Enterprises including 
financial institutions and creating new investment space for private sector. 
b. Post disinvestment, economic growth of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs)/ 
financial institutions will be through infusion of private capital, technology and best 
management practices will contribute to economic growth and new jobs. 
c. Disinvestment proceeds to finance various social sector and developmental programs of 
the Government.

Policy” including minimizing the presence of t he Central Government PSEs in strategic 
sectors to create new investment space for private sectors and reiterate its recommendation 
that the Government may consider retaining profitable and well performing PSUs, 
especially in core sector instead of disinvesting for short term financial gains.

Recommendation/Observation 
12. The Committee however observes that the International Container Transhipment

Reply of the Ministry 
12.1 Cochin Port was chosen by the Government of India to be the transshipment port for 
India as Cochin was the Indian Major Port closest to the international trunk routes. The ICTT 
was to be in its final phase, a 3.0 Million TEU terminal with berth length of 1800m and draft of 
14.5m capable of handling 8000 TEU ships. In the first phase, it was to be, a 1.0 Million TEU 
terminal with 14.5m draft. The Government of India has invested about ₹1,700 Crore in the 
infrastructure for ICTT (NH & Rail connectivity, and Capital Dredging), while the BOT operator 
(DP World) has invested about ₹1100 Crore. The ICTT commissioned in February 2011 had the 
throughput of 7.36 Lakh TEUs of containers in 2021 -22 of which 1.56 Lakh TEUs were 
Transshipment containers. 
 
12.2 The ICTT was planned to attract transshipment back to India away from foreign 
terminals like Colombo; the competition was therefore, as much in infrastructure provision and 
offering competitive user charges at ports, as it was in providing trade -friendly procedures.  
Cochin Port allows up to 90% rebate on notified Vessel Rela ted Charges (VRC) to the Mainline 
Container Vessel Services and rebates up to 50% on the notified VRC to Feeder Container 
Vessel Services to be competitive in port charges with respect to Colombo and the neighbouring 
Indian Ports, towards promoting ICTT as  a Transshipment Hub. The Mainline Services have the 
potential to reduce the dependence of Indian ports on Colombo for transshipment and thereby 

53 
 
reduce logistics cost for the Indian containers, contributing to the economic growth of the 
country. 
 
12.3 However, still there are several deterrents for ICTT when it comes to competing with 
Colombo. Lighthouse Dues, now, designated as Marine Aids to Navigation Dues, recovered in 
India by the Customs on foreign ships is one of them, dissuading Mainline and Transsh ipment 
container vessels from considering a call at ICTT. The prevailing dues at Indian Ports for 
container ships is ₹85 per TEU plus GST of 18%, as revised in August, 2022. Colombo also 
levies Light Dues on ships at $4 per 100 GT or part thereof, which co mes to only about 30% of 
that applicable at Indian Ports.  Cochin Port Authority has now submitted an expansion plan for 
ICTT for deepening the channel to 16.0m. 
 
12.4 As regards the transshipment capacity at ICTT, Cochin, DP World has already lined up 
an expansion project (capacity to increase to 2 million TEUs) in view of the deepening of the 
channel to 16m. As per the traffic projection given by them, it is expected that by 2030 the total 
transshipment from ICTT Cochin would be 287000 TEUs and 725,000 TEUs by 2043. 
 
Further Recommendation/ Observation 
12.5 The Committee while examining  the functioning of Major Ports  had recommended 
that the Ministry may find ways and means to reduce Lighthouse Dues (LHD) to lower the 
logistics costs. Vessel Related Charges  (VRC) can be reduced only by reducing the 
operating expense which is practically not feasible in most Major Ports. The Committee 
reiterates its recommendation and urges the Ministry to act on it.

been informed at the time of its interaction with the Ministry and IWAI on the subject of 
development of National Waterways that the physical progress for the project s of Ship 
Repair facility at Pandu and alternate road to Pandu Port is only 2% and 18%, 
respectively, as on 30 th June, 2023. Both of these important infrastructure projects have 
been inordinately delayed. Further, the construction of cargo cum passenger te rminal at

fast track these crucial projects without any further delay. The Committee also 
recommends the Ministry to chalk out a roadmap to ensure proper utilization of  funds 
allocated for the B.E. 2023-24, for completion of these delayed projects.

budget head and recommends the Ministry to make all out efforts to ensure maximum 
utilization of funds in FY 2023-24 as well.

minimize the instances of unjustifiable savings.
