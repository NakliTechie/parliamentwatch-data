REPORT
Indian culture, one of the most ancient cultures of the world, is a treasure trove of
innumerable tangible and intangible cultural heritage. The role of the Ministry of Culture,
Government of India is extremely important as they shoulder the responsibility to preserve and
protect the country’s ancient, vibrant, continuous and vast cultural resources. Promoting country’s
culture while preserving its unique diversity becomes all the more a daunting task. The vast
functional spectrum of the Ministry also includes generating cultural awareness at the grassroots
level to promoting cultural exchanges at international level. In order to fulfill its mandate the
Ministry of Culture undertakes the following activities:
➢ Managing ancient sites, monuments, museums, libraries, archives, cultural
organizations;
➢ Literary, visual, performing arts, institutional and individual initiatives;
➢ Commemoration and celebration of important events and personalities.
2. The above mentioned activities spanning from conservation, preservation and promotion of
Indian heritage both tangible and intangible, are carried out through the two attached offices, i.e.,
Archaeological Survey of India and National Archives of India; six subordinate offices and thirty-
four autonomous institutions working under the Ministry.

9. The Committee was informed that the Ministry has to adopt during the Twelfth Five Year
Plan, a “new approach and appropriate policies which are less dependent on Government financing
and catalyze local partnerships”. The Programmes of the Ministry must be integrated into strategies
for sustainable development at every level and take into account the needs and aspirations of the
community where cultural assets are found. Sustainable heritage conservation depends upon the
commitment and involvement of local communities. Conservation policies, to be successful, need
to promote local community stewardship of the heritage as well as to provide socio-economic
benefits for local communities. Therefore, a direct link must be established between safeguarding
the heritage and socio-economic development. This calls for the “deliberate recasting of heritage
conservation as a development activity that brings economic opportunities, creates jobs and
generates income based on traditional technologies and know-how”. This new programme is aimed
at complementing and extending those efforts by moving heritage conservation beyond the exclusive

3
sphere of high technology and elite specialization and to become the concern and responsibility of
every citizen and transform heritage conservation into grassroots movement, which will return the
heritage to the communities that created it and who rely on it as the foundation for their future
development.
10. Due to the increased awareness about the need to protect monuments, the ASI is under
great strain so as to not only protect their own monuments but also to take over new monuments.
The issue of huge developmental scope in Archaeology is the main priority area for the Ministry

monuments for want of funds, central intervention is imperative for maintenance of large number
of such monuments. As such, archaeological sites protected by the State Governments/UTs are
scattered all over the country.
11. During the Twelfth Plan period, the Ministry has laid stress on strengthening and upgrading
State Museums and modernizing and upgrading central museums as well for bringing them at par
with the international standards. The Ministry plans to undertake digitization, networking, access,
etc. of our Libraries, Archives and Museums. Conservation of monuments, manuscripts, and other
art objects too can scale new heights by the application of modern technology.

17. As regards the strategies for the Twelfth Five Year Plan, the Committee is of the
firm view that these are more of vision than strategies, as these are vague and do not
provide a clear road map for the organizations working under the aegis of the Ministry. The
Committee has strong reservation about the Government’s commitment to privatise
heritage conservation activities. Moreover, the Committee also fails to understand the
meaning of the “new approach and appropriate policies which are less dependent on
Government financing.” The Committee feels that these could be high on ideal but low on
implementation on ground and therefore, less practicable. The Twelfth Plan strategies
reflect a paradigm shift in the heritage conservation activities, but the Committee did not
find specific initiatives/intervention in keeping with the said “new approach and appropriate
policies”. Instead, these are vague and high sounding ideals only which do not inspire
required confidence. It turns out to be an attempt to obfuscate the real issues and practical
problems faced by the Ministry and different stakeholders. The Committee finds a critical
disconnect in the approach of the Ministry towards heritage conservation vis-à-vis the people
and community. The view that conservation of our ancient cultural heritage would become
the concern and responsibility of every citizen appears to be too idealistic. The Committee
understands that excavation, recording of excavation findings, chemical and physical
conservation of our monuments require a certain level of knowledge, expertise and deft
handling. The Committee agrees in principle with the need for involving community in this
exercise but it appears to be better said than done. The Committee is surprised to note that
the linkage between culture and economic growth has not been properly reflected in the
strategy outlined by the Planning Commission and feels that because of this, the Ministry
may not be able to harness the Cultural resources for economic development of the
Country.
18. The Committee notes with satisfaction that there has been about two fold increase
in the allocation to the Ministry right from the Eighth Plan onwards, the Committee also
appreciates the fact that Ministry was able to spend about 90% of all its allocation
throughout the Eleventh Five year Plan and the first year of the Twelfth Plan.
19. The Committee also appreciates the constraints under which the Ministry has to
function and notes that the actual Plan allocation for the Twelfth Plan is about 38.97% of
the projected outlay. The Committee observes that although there has been a constant
increase in allocations to the Ministry of Culture right from the Eighth Plan onwards and
every year thereafter, it is of the firm view that even these increased allocations are highly
inadequate in comparison to the ambitious task the Ministry has to undertake during the
Twelfth Plan. Resultantly, protection and promotion of our culture and heritage which are
highly significant to the identity and existence of India, will suffer. The Committee
sincerely feels that apart from protecting our existing heritage, adequate cultural
infrastructure also needs to be created for promoting and projecting our heritage and
culture, for that we need appropriate plan/programme and adequate funds. In the absence
of adequate allocation, the country would not be able to protect and reap the benefits of
such vast cultural resources.
20. The Committee feels that inadequate budgetary provision will certainly hamper the
progress of works underway as well as new programmes to be undertaken by the Ministry.
Creation of infrastructural facilities, modernization of Museums, Archives and Libraries are
long overdue. Heritage conservation/protection is becoming capital intensive. Akademis also

7
need adequate funds to carry forward their work. The Committee sincerely feels that much
more things are required to be done in the core area other than making arrangements for
peripheral activities to keep things going.
21. Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the overall allocation for the
Twelfth Plan period should be increased and the Ministry of Culture may take it up with
the Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance. Since, the Ministry has been consistently
utilizing its allocations above 90%, the Committee feels it is a good case for incentivizing
the Ministry by increasing their allocation.
22. The Committee would like to point out the under-utilization of funds under the heads
– Archaeology, Archives and Archival Libraries, Museums, Public Libraries and International
Cultural relations. The Committee finds a dichotomy  between the Ministry’s claim of
spending more than 90% during the last many years and reporting under utilization in the
areas which constitute a large share in the Ministry’s allocation. The Committee would like
to have a clarification about this.
23. The Committee feels that almost all proposed schemes of the Ministry may be

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA (ASI)
(a) General Functioning
25. Archeological Survey of India, an Attached Office of the Ministry of Culture, was
established with the primary object of surveying antiquarian remains in this country and their study.
The prime task of the ASI is conservation, preservation and maintenance of the centrally protected
monuments. Presently ASI which has jurisdiction over the whole country has 24 circles,
6 Excavation Branches, 2 Temple Survey Projects, 1 Building Survey Project, 1 Science Branch
(with many sub-offices located in different parts of the country), 1 Epigraphy Branch (two offices
located at Mysore and Nagpur) and 1 Horticulture Branch, with its divisional offices located at
strategic points in the country.
26. Plan allocation was increased in BE 2012-13, basically to strengthen the conservation and
development of site museums activities and also to implement new schemes proposed during 12th
Five Year Plan including one Centrally Sponsored Scheme. The provision has now again been
increased in 2013-14 for undertaking various activities/programmes of ASI, including conservation
of ancient monuments, archaeological exploration and excavation, development and maintenance of
Central Archaeological Museums, National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities, Setting up of
Competent Authorities for operation of Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Monuments Sites
and Remains (Amendment and Validation Act, 2010), Implementation of Centrally Sponsored
Scheme and new schemes proposed in the Twelfth Plan period, etc.
27. The Committee specifically wanted to know from the Ministry about the policy of the
Government on restoration and conservation of monuments. The Ministry informed that

8
Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) protected 3678 protects monuments of national importance.
The conservation and preservation work of these monuments is attended to as per the
archaeological norms and principles laid out in John Marshall’s Conservation Manual of 1923 and
International Charters recognized by the UNESCO. The Ministry informed that keeping in view the
new developments including that in technology, a new draft National Policy on Conservation has
been prepared which is being finalized for adoption.
28. The Committee notes that though the budgetary support to ASI has been consistently
increasing since the last three years, the present Budgetary allocation of Rs. 250.00 crore
is insufficient in view of the increasing cost of conservation and preservation and huge
responsibilities to be discharged by the ASI in terms of safety, security, protection and
upkeep of the monuments.
29. The Committee also feels that number of protected monuments under the ASI at
present is far too less and lakhs of our ancient monuments are languishing. As a result,
large numbers of them have either vanished or encroached or illegally occupied. The
Committee emphasizes the urgent need to bring most of them under protected category.
Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that number of protected monuments
should be increased on priority and in a phased manner for which we should have a
roadmap and realistic allocation of funds required.
30. For taking our monuments scattered all over the country under archaeological
protection, we will have to take State on board, who it appears are not able to pay required
attention to this aspect. The Committee is of the view that our National Policy must provide
adequate emphasis on this aspect to make conservation and protection as a joint effort with
adequate financial and other support for the culture.
31. With changing times and technology as well as increasing threat perceptions to our
monuments, works concerning ASI are acquiring newer dimensions and they have to keep
up with them. Therefore, our strengths and weaknesses need a detail examination and the
Committee hopes that the Ministry will keep such aspects in mind while adopting National
Conservation Policy.
32. The Committee also notes that ASI cannot undertake renovation work during
monsoon months which restricts time as well as its fund utilisation capacity. Due to this,
the RE allocation is reduced as it is made on the basis of the BE utilisation and as a result,
their fund lapses. The Committee realises that this is the major reason for their inability
to complete capital work. The Committee strongly recommends that the ASI may be
exempted from the BE utilisation restriction while deciding RE. The Ministry of Finance is
requested to give a favorable consideration to this so that ASI activities do not suffer and
their funds do not lapse. The Committee reiterates its recommendation made in its (Para
30 and 31) 175
th Report for creation of a non-lapsable fund for ASI activities.

(d) Encroachment of Monuments
38. As per the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remain (Amendment and
Validation) Act, 2010 no new construction is allowed within prohibited area (100 mtrs. Zone) of
monument/site and new construction is permitted in regulated areas (200 mtrs. Zone further beyond
regulated area) only with the approval of Competent Authority on recommendation of the National
Monuments Authority. Any new construction raised in prohibited area is illegal construction and any
new construction in regulated area, raised without permission from Competent Authority is
unauthorized. Any construction or installation within the protected area or protected limit of a
monument of national importance is encroachment.
39. On report of unauthorized construction within prohibited or regulated areas of protected
monuments, complaints with the Police are filed and notices are issued to defaulters for removal
of the said illegal or unauthorized construction. If no action is taken, demolition order is issued
under Rule 38(2) of the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remain Rules, 1959 with
a request to the Collector to cause to remove the said illegal/unauthorized construction. Recourse
is taken to courts of law as a last resort.
40. Following the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remain (Amendments and
Validation) Act in 2010, about 780 notices/demolition orders have been issued under Rule 38(1) and
38(2) of the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remain Rules, 1959. No demolition
has been carried out so far. However, Archaeological Survey of India has been persistent in its
efforts to tackle encroachment and has succeeded in removing encroachment from 24 monuments.
41. The Committee is surprised to note that not a single encroachment could be
demolished on the archaeological sites out of 780 demolition notices/orders issued in this

10
regard. The Committee is also aware of the apathy of the civic authorities, local Police and
other agencies in the matter. ASI has no power to ensure compliance for such orders from
them. Even court orders were also ignored at times. The Committee is aware that
encroachment, unauthorized occupation and illegal construction near our monuments is a
problem about which we have not been able to do much so far. The Committee feels that
possibility of a nexus between the vested interests, private parties and civic and police
authorities cannot be ruled out in this context. Defacing of our national monuments is
another serious problem. Therefore, strong action should be taken against not only the

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
As regards the strategies for the Twelfth Five Year Plan, the Committee is of the
firm view that these are more of vision than strategies, as these are vague and do not
provide a clear road map for the organizations working under the aegis of the Ministry. The
Committee has strong reservation about the Government’s commitment to privatise
heritage conservation activities. Moreover, the Committee also fails to understand the
meaning of the “new approach and appropriate policies which are less dependent on
Government financing.” The Committee feels that these could be high on ideal but low on
implementation on ground and therefore, less practicable. The Twelfth Plan strategies
reflect a paradigm shift in the heritage conservation activities, but the Committee did not
find specific initiatives/intervention in keeping with the said “new approach and appropriate
policies”. Instead, these are vague and high sounding ideals only which do not inspire
required confidence. It turns out to be an attempt to obfuscate the real issues and practical
problems faced by the Ministry and different stakeholders. The Committee finds a critical
disconnect in the approach of the Ministry towards heritage conservation vis-à-vis the people
and community. The view that conservation of our ancient cultural heritage would become
the concern and responsibility of every citizen appears to be too idealistic. The Committee
understands that excavation, recording of excavation findings, chemical and physical
conservation of our monuments require a certain level of knowledge, expertise and deft
handling. The Committee agrees in principle with the need for involving community in this
exercise but it appears to be better said than done. The Committee is surprised to note that
the linkage between culture and economic growth has not been properly reflected in the
strategy outlined by the Planning Commission and feels that because of this, the Ministry
may not be able to harness the Cultural resources for economic development of the
Country. (Para 17)
The Committee notes with satisfaction that there has been about two fold increase
in the allocation to the Ministry right from the Eighth Plan onwards, the Committee also
appreciates the fact that Ministry was able to spend about 90% of all its allocation
throughout the Eleventh Five year Plan and the first year of the Twelfth Plan. (Para 18)
The Committee also appreciates the constraints under which the Ministry has to
function and notes that the actual Plan allocation for the Twelfth Plan is about 38.97% of
the projected outlay. The Committee observes that although there has been a constant
increase in allocations to the Ministry of Culture right from the Eighth Plan onwards and
every year thereafter, it is of the firm view that even these increased allocations are highly
inadequate in comparison to the ambitious task the Ministry has to undertake during the
Twelfth Plan. Resultantly, protection and promotion of our culture and heritage which are
highly significant to the identity and existence of India, will suffer. The Committee
sincerely feels that apart from protecting our existing heritage, adequate cultural
infrastructure also needs to be created for promoting and projecting our heritage and
culture, for that we need appropriate plan/programme and adequate funds. In the absence
of adequate allocation, the country would not be able to protect and reap the benefits of
such vast cultural resources. (Para 19)
The Committee feels that inadequate budgetary provision will certainly hamper the
progress of works underway as well as new programmes to be undertaken by the Ministry.

22
Creation of infrastructural facilities, modernization of Museums, Archives and Libraries are
long overdue. Heritage conservation/protection is becoming capital intensive. Akademis also
need adequate funds to carry forward their work. The Committee sincerely feels that much
more things are required to be done in the core area other than making arrangements for
peripheral activities to keep things going. (Para 20)
Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that the overall allocation for the
Twelfth Plan period should be increased and the Ministry of Culture may take it up with
the Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance. Since, the Ministry has been consistently
utilizing its allocations above 90%, the Committee feels it is a good case for incentivizing
the Ministry by increasing their allocation. (Para 21)
The Committee would like to point out the under-utilization of funds under the heads
– Archaeology, Archives and Archival Libraries, Museums, Public Libraries and International
Cultural relations. The Committee finds a dichotomy between the Ministry’s claim of
spending more than 90% during the last many years and reporting under utilization in the
areas which constitute a large share in the Ministry’s allocation. The Committee would like
to have a clarification about this. (Para 22)
The Committee feels that almost all proposed schemes of the Ministry may be
adversely affected due to inadequate funds. A total of 26 planned schemes, including
ongoing/new schemes, will be implemented from 2013-14 by the Ministry. The Archaeological
Survey of India will now implement one Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) from the year
2013-14 as two proposed CSSs have also been merged. Accordingly, the Committee

ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA (ASI)
The Committee notes that though the budgetary support to ASI has been consistently
increasing since the last three years, the present Budgetary allocation of Rs. 250.00 crore
is insufficient in view of the increasing cost of conservation and preservation and huge
responsibilities to be discharged by the ASI in terms of safety, security, protection and
upkeep of the monuments. (Para 28)
The Committee also feels that number of protected monuments under the ASI at
present is far too less and lakhs of our ancient monuments are languishing. As a result,
large numbers of them have either vanished or encroached or illegally occupied. The
Committee emphasizes the urgent need to bring most of them under protected category.
Therefore, the Committee strongly recommends that number of protected monuments
should be increased on priority and in a phased manner for which we should have a
roadmap and realistic allocation of funds required. (Para 29)
For taking our monuments scattered all over the country under archaeological
protection, we will have to take State on board, who it appears are not able to pay required
attention to this aspect. The Committee is of the view that our National Policy must provide
adequate emphasis on this aspect to make conservation and protection as a joint effort with
adequate financial and other support for the culture. (Para 30)
