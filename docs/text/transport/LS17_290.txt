Report 
 India has a long coastline of about 7517 km, spread on the western and eastern shelves 
of the mainland and also along the islands. Hence, Shipping plays a pivotal role in the 
economic development of the country. India has 13 Major Ports and about 200 Minor Ports. 
Approximately 95% of the country’s trade by volume and 68% by value is moved through 
maritime transport. Therefore, development of Major & Minor Ports and harbours, hu man 
resource and finance, maritime safety issues, training and skill development need to be 
strengthened. The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways has been entrusted with the 
responsibility to formulate various policies and programmes on these issues and implement 
them.

5. The Committee observes that allocation made to the Ministry for FY 2021 -22 is 
45 percent less than the projected demand and the shortfall between the projected 
demand of Rs. 3101.34 crore and the Budget Estimates of Rs. 1702.5 for the Ministry of 
Shipping is to the tune of Rs. 1398.95 crore, which is massive by any standards and is 
certain to have an adverse bearing on the implementation of various modernization 
projects of the Ministry. Major reduction has been effected under Sagarmala Schemes 
and Projects and other schemes under Sagarmala in the Revenue section. The same is 
the case with Grants to Inland Water Transport Authority of India, D.G. Shipping and 
Kolkata Port Trust. This being the case, the Committee is convinced that it would be an 
insurmountable task for the Ministry to efficiently run a flagship scheme like Sagarmala 
and other schemes under Sagarmala with this limited allocation and achieve goals 
envisaged under Sagarmala Scheme. The Committee further notes a steep cut in 
allocation to IWAI an d D.G. Shipping, the two important offices of the Ministry.  The 
Committee apprehends that the working of these two offices may get hampered due to 
less allocation.  The Committee further notes  that on the capital expenditure of DG 
Shipping, against a sum of Rs. 108.4 crore sought , only Rs. 25 crore has been allocated.  
The Ministry submitted that due to this inade quate allocation, the priority p roject E -
Governance will suffer.  In this era of digital technology, where the Government has 

7 
 
been emphasizing fo r moving towards digitization, less allocation to e -Governance 
project is not acceptable.  The Committee observes that India has been emerging as a 
modern economy and successive Governments have targeted to increase the economic 
growth at a high rate in a sustained manner. However, the COVID-19 pandemic has had 
a disruptive impact on the Indian economy and almost all the sectors have been 
adversely affected. The Committee is of the view that post -COVID, the economic growth 
of the country will come through I ndia’s P ort and shipping sector because Maritime 
Transport is a critical infrastructure for the economic growth as 95% of the country’s 
trade by volume and 68% by value is moved through Maritime Transport and the ports 
and shipping sector is, thus, a key d river of trade for India. Keeping all the above facts 
in view, the Committee is of the considered opinion that the outlay of Rs. 1702.35 crore 
would not be sufficient to meet the fund requirements of the Ministry  during 2021 -
22.The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should once again assess its 
financial requirements realistically and thereafter, move the Ministry of Finance for 
augmentation of financial resources for it for 2021 -22 at RE stage.  The Committee also 
recommends that the Ministr y of Finance should have a re -look at the allocation made 
for the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways and revise it upwardly at the RE 
2021-22 stage. 
6. The Committee understands that many of the programmes/ schemes relating to  
Port modernization, Po rt infrastructure development, ship building, ship repairing and 
inland waterways transport are lagging behind because of the drastic cuts effected and 
would ultimately lead to both time and cost overruns. The Committee is of the view that 
if the Ministry wants adequate/ substantial budgetary support to complete its projects, 
the pace of implementation of the projects will have to be spruced up and the allocated 
funds be optimally utilized. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry 
should initi ate and implement measures for better financial discipline and ensure that 
the scarce resources do not remain locked up and surrendered later on.

ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2020-21 
14. The Ministry informed the Committee that B .E. of Rs. 1 800.00 crores got reduced to 
Rs. 1433.65 crores at R .E. stage during 2020 -21. The s ector-wise details of expenditure 
statement of Plan fund 2020-21, are given in Annexure I. 
15. The Committee after taking note of the variations between Budget Estimates, 
Revised Estimates and Actual Expenditure had, in its 279th Report on Demands for 
Grants (2020 -21) of Ministry of Shipping , desired the Ministry to be realistic in its 
projection of fund requirements and exercise due diligence in its Budget formulation 
after proper assessment and planning.  The Committee, is therefore, constrained to note 
the failure of the Ministry to fully utilize the budgeted funds under various heads during 
2020-21. For instance, Rs 211.00 crores were allocated at the stage of B E 2020-21 under 
the head “Sagarmala Schemes and Projects” which got reduced to Rs 96.69 crores at the 
RE stage whereas the actual expenditure incurred till 05.02.2021 is only Rs 74.92 crores.
  Similarly, s hip building which is  an essent ial arm of the Shipping sector 
witnessed reduction in allocation under the head “Assistance to Ship Building, Research 
and Development” from BE of Rs 151.00 crores to Rs.129.30 crores at RE 2020-21 stage 
and the actual expenditure till 5 th February, 2021 under the head is only Rs 103.62 
crores which accounts for only 75% of allocated sum  and roughly 25% has remained 
unspent. Under the head “Grants to Inland Water Transport Authority of India”, a sum 

11 
 
of Rs 301.80 crores was proposed in BE 2020 -21, which got reduced to Rs 203.20 crores 
at the RE stage. However, the Ministry was able to s pend only Rs 146.45 crores till 
05.02.2021. All t his clearly reflects the poor financial  management on the p art of the 
Ministry. The Committee  apprehends the same trend this year  also which will hamper 
the progress of some of the ongoing projects of ship building sector.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should regularly track and monitor the 
progress of all the projects under various heads and address the bottlenecks arising 
during the implementation stage to ensure optimum utilization of the allocated funds. 
The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should put in place a r igorous 
monitoring mechanism for the ongoing projects and ensure that the available resources 
for 2021-22 are utilized more efficiently and execution of the projects is done within the 
approved costs and designated timeline. The Committee also implores upon the Ministry 
to undertake a thorough analysis of the persistent underutilization of the funds vis-à-vis 
the allocated funds and delineate the steps taken so far and to be taken in future to plug 
the gaps so as to minimize the variations between BE, RE and AE.

74. The Directorate General of Shipping (DGS), an attached office of the Ministry of 
Ports, Shipping and Waterways, Government of India was established in 1949 as the Maritime 
Administration of India. It deals with implementation of shipping policy and legislation, to 
ensure (i) safety of life and ships at sea; (ii) prevention of marine pollution; and (iii) 
mandatory regulations of the International Maritime Organizations for effective 
implementation including promotion of maritime education and training, examination & 
certification of seafarers and supervision of other subordinate offices for their effective 
functioning etc.   
75. The functions of the DGS are given below:  
Passengers Ship Survey - All passenger ships are subjected to survey of hull, 
Machinery, equipment etc. during construction and there after annually.   
Cargo Ship Safety Construction (CSSC) Surveys - Surveys of various types of 
Cargo Ships under construction and periodical and annual surveys thereafter.  
International Oil Pollution Prevention (IOPP) Surveys-The Classification Societies 
(ROs) are also responsible for survey and certification for Oil Poll ution under the various 
Annexes of MARPOL 73/38 Convention.   
Issuance of Continuous Discharge Certificates - Indian Seafarers are required to 
possess a certificate called Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) to be able to work on an 
Indian Flag Ships. Entire process of issuance of CDC has been made online since June, 2015

It is at the highest level at SMP Kolkata, Deen Dayal and JMPT . Suitable measures may

SHIPBUILDING INDUSTRY 
The Committee welcomes the steps taken by the Ministry to promote 
shipbuilding industry. Rising global trade and strong replacement demand boosted the 
prospects of the Indian shipbuilding industry in the recent past. It is a matter of 
satisfaction that from an industry dependent on government orders till late 90s, the 
Indian shipbuilding industry ha s turned export oriented focusing on the niche offshore 
segment. India is emerging as a major destination for the construction of offshore and oil 
industry ships, such as offshore supply vessels and anchor handling tugs. Though our 
country is able to build ships upto the capacity of 1 lakh 20 thousand tons, when 
compared with the global level, where the capacity is in the range of two and a half to 
three lakh tons, our capacity is far behind. This being the case, the Committee now feels 
that the Ministry should focus on building large ships and chalk out policies for it, for we 
have a natural advantage in terms of availability of low cost labour and skilled people.  
Moreover, India also enjoys a long coastline of around 8,000 km long with severa l deep 
water Ports serving as good locations for setting up shipyards. The Committee notes that 
one of the major challenges faced by the Indian Ship Owners/shipyards as intimated by 
the Ministry, is the availability of long -term finance at competitive rate s.  The 
Committee feels that this hampers the growth of Shipbuilding Industry which, of late, 
has been getting good export orders. The Committee, therefore, desires that this issue 
may be taken up with the Ministry of Finance on a urgent basis which will h elp in 
boosting our ‘Atmanirbhar Bharat Programme’.  
(Para 70) 
The Ministry should also introduce schemes/ programmes for training, research 
and development and introduction of modern technologies in the Ship building sector, 
where the country is lacking.  
(Para 71)

contracts are pending under the shipbuilding subsidy scheme.  A sum of Rs. 153 crores 
for a total number of 51 vessels are to be released in the financial year s 2019-20, 2020-21 
and 2021-22 for which guidelines have been formulated. No details regarding amount of
