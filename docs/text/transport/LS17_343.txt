REPORT  
 INTRODUCTION 
2023 is the Incredible India! Visit India year and also the year of India‟s presidency of G20, 
presenting the perfect opportunity to establish India as a key tourism destination. This year 
therefore offers the brightest outlook for Indian tourism since the beginning of the pandemic in 
early 2020.   One of the largest Service Sectors in India, Tourism has always played a key role in 
socio-economic progress through employment generation, creation of infrastructure and revenue 
earnings.  Tourism also has the potential to contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals, 
particularly, inclusive and sustainable economic growth, sustainable consumption and production 
and sustainable use of oceans and marine resources.  The huge potential of this key sector is set 
to be tapped further this year on Mission Mode with the active participation of states, the 
convergence of government programmes and public private partnerships. 
2. The Nodal Agency for this Mission is the Ministry of Tourism which f ormulates national 
policies and programmes for the development and promotion of Tourism in the country. In the 
process, the Ministry consults and collaborates with other stakeholders in the sector including 
various Central Ministries/ Agencies, State Gover nments/ Union Territory Administrations and 
the representatives of the private sector.

6. As is evident from the above Table, against the projected demand of Rs. 2675.01 
crores, the net allocation made for the Ministry of Tourism under Demand is 2400.00 
crores. The shortfall is Rs. 275.01 crores. 
7. The shortfall is mainly in the major schemes of the Ministry, the Flagship Scheme, 
Swadesh Darshan and PRASHAD and in  promotion and publicity, both  domestic and 
overseas.  The Ministry has launched the Swadesh Darshan 2.0 Scheme and identified 55 
destinations under the same and budgetary cuts at this stage would delay the 
implementation of the scheme.   However, the   Ministry has submitted that the shortfall in 
allocation is not likely to impact any Scheme as the Ministry can ask for additional funds at 
RE stage, if required. Further, the major Schemes of the Ministry are on -going in nature 
and will continue during the financial year 2024 -2025 also. Accordingly, any pending 

Page 6 of 136 
 
liability on any Scheme can be met from the budget of 2024 -2025.  The Committee 
recommends that if the Ministry requires additional funds at the R.E. stage the Ministry of 
Finance should allocate the same.  
8. The reduction under the promotion and publicity budgetary Head is also 
inexplicable given that tourism is to be promoted on a Mission Mode this year and 2023 is 
being promoted as the Visit India Year.  The Ministry has not given any reason for the 
reduction in funds under the Domestic Promotion Head.  The Committee recommends that 
the Ministry of Finance may allocate additional funds under this Head at the R.E. stage.

11. The Ministry submitted that the allocation of Rs. 2400.00 Crores for 2022 -23 was 
reduced to Rs. 1343.13 Crores at RE stage. The actual expenditure till 31st  January 2023 was Rs. 
464.24 Crores.  
12. The Ministry of Finance had changed the procedure for release of funds in respect of 
various Schemes. The Schemes of the Ministry are implemented by the State Governments and 
other State/Central implementing Agencies. There were problems with the State Governments 
and the Implementing Agencies in migration to revised procedure. 
13. Considerable time was lo st in preparation for implementation of the new procedure by 
the State Governments and the Implementing Agencies including mapping of the Bank accounts 
of all agencies with PFMS. The bottlenecks have been cl eared and the release of funds have now 
become se amless. The Ministry is also monitoring the implementation of the projects under 


Page 11 of 136 
 
various Schemes in a variety of ways like Review Meetings at the highest level with State 
Governments, online monitoring of projects, site visits by officers from the  Ministry, etc. With  
these measures in place it is expec ted that the allocation of funds given  to the Ministry for the 
financial year 2023-2024 would be fully utilized. 
SURRENDER OF SAVINGS  
14. The amount of surrender during the current financial year including th e cut imposed by  
Ministry of Finance at RE stage is Rs. 1409.57 Crores. The final allocation of funds  under RE 
was received by the Ministry on 5th January 2023. On the basis of the RE  allocation, the revised 
requirement of funds for Scheme and non -Scheme components was worked out in consultation 
with the Programme Division, India Tourism Offices in  India and abroad, IISM Gulmarg, etc. 
the final savings were arrived at and surrendered to Ministry of Finance on 08.02.2023. 
 
15. The Committee observes that in the last three years, the allocation at the  Revised 
Estimates stage is half of the Budgetary Estimates and the Actual Expenditure is even less.  
The utilisation percentage of the Ministry for the last two years i.e.  2020-21 & 2021 -22 is 
only 89.85 & 82.89  respectively and for the FY 2022 -23 it is only 34.56% till 31 st January 
2023.While expenditure in 2020 may be understandably low due to the pandemic, the 
status in respect of 2021 and 2022 is no better.  The utilization percentage of only 34.6% in 
2022-23 is unacceptable.  The Committee does not accept the reply of the Ministry that the 
change in procedure of release of funds was a factor in low utilisation of funds. The change 
in procedure was for all Ministries and other Ministries have managed to utilis e most of 
their funds by December, 2022.   
16. The Committee finds that the reduction in Revised Estimates in the past three years 
is across all Central Sector Schemes. The Flagship Scheme of the Ministry, Swadesh

18. Much of the budgetary outlay is for development of infrastructure. Swadesh

infrastructure. Infrastructure for all tourist amenities like creation of tourist information 
centre, reception centre, convention centres, cafeterias, beautification of tourist attractions 
are covered under this scheme.  The Committee does not find it rational that though 
Capital assets are being created unde r the schemes of the Minist ry, the entire expenditure 
is classified under Revenue.    Revenue expenditure is short -term, recurring expenditure 
mostly pertaining to salaries, operating expenses, administrative costs etc.  Due to its very 
nature, revenue expenditure cannot be reduced as the expenses such as salaries and wages 
are incurred irrespective of the activities carried out.  The Committee finds however that 
for the last five years, the budgetary allocation of the Ministry has been halved at the 
Revised Estimates stage and even this budget has remained unutilised.  The Actual 
expenditure for the past five years has been much less than the budget allocated in Revised 
Estimates. 
19. The Committee notes the submissions of the Ministry that though at the macro level, 
the budget is un der Revenue Head, yet within the Schemes, there would be Object Heads 
pertaining to “Creation of Capital assets.” Under the two Major schemes, Swadesh  
Darshan and PRASHAD, the re is no recurring expenditure on salary etc.  These schemes 
are mainly meant for  asset creation and only 5% funds are given for O&M (Operations 
and Maintenance).  However, though only assets are being created under these schemes, 
the outgo of funds can be reduced depending on the progress of the projects and receipt of 
Utilisation Certificates.   
20. The Committee observes that creation of capital assets leads to value creation and 
generates future cash flows for the economy.  Further, the multiplier effect of Capital 

Page 13 of 136 
 
expenditure is much more than that of the Revenue expenditure. In In dia, the multiplier 
effect of Capital expenditure made by the Central Government is estimated at 2.45 and for 
State Governments, it is around 2 while the multiplier effect of Revenue expenditure is only 
0.99 (RBI  Bulletin, December, 2 020) .  Wrong classif ication therefore may not give  the 
correct picture on total investment in infrastructure and may impact both the Index of 
Industrial Production and the GDP.  The Committee notes that the classification as 
Revenue is arbitrary without following any valid criteria in case of various projects of 
Ministry of Tourism. The Committee recommends that the Ministry may take up the 
matter with the Ministry of Finance and submit a detailed explanation to the Committee.

42. Based on the detailed review, the Ministry has now revamped the scheme as Swadesh 
Darshan 2.0 (SD2.0) with the mantra of „vocal for local‟, and with the objective to develop 
sustainable and responsible destinations following a tourist & destination centric approach.  
Swadesh Darshan 2.0 is not an incremental change but a generational shift to evolve Swadesh  
Darshan Scheme as a holistic mission to develop sustainable and responsible to urism 
destinations covering tourism and allied infrastructure, tourism services, human capital 
development, destination management and promotion backed by policy and institutional reforms.  
The aim is to develop fewer locations but with a more visible and impactful development. The 
guidelines of the scheme include an illustrative list of admissible components and covers 
development of toilets, wayside amenities etc. at destinations. 
The key principles to be followed under Swadesh Darshan Scheme 2.0 are  
 (i) Develop benchmarks and standards for major tourism themes 
(ii) Sustainable and responsible tourism 
(iii) Destination and tourist-centric approach 
(iv) Integrated development of tourism destination 
(v) Focus on Domestic Tourism 
(vi) Policy and Institutional Reforms

recommends that the Ministry may take steps to conduct frequent market surveys and 
collect data to understand travel trends. The Committee observes that data and market 
intelligence surveys are vital for creating a road map for the travel industry as future 
travel is going to be very different.

2. The Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee and the witnesses who were 
present. The Chairman informed that the agenda of the meeting was to examine the Demands 

139 
 
for Grants (2023 -24) of the Ministry of Tourism. The Chairman noted that the budgetary 
allocation for Tourism sector was Rs. 2400 crores, i.e. the same as the allocation in 2022 -
23.  He further noted that the budgetary allocations in the past few years had been much 
reduced at the R.E. stage and that the utilisation percentage in the past two years was 89.85% 
and 82.89% respectively.   Out of the R.E. 2022 -23 allocation of Rs. 1343.13 crores, the 
Ministry has utilised only 34.56% till 31 st January 2023.  The Chairman wished to know the 
reasons why the Ministry was unable to utilize its allocated budget.  
 
3. The Chairman also desired to know the strategies formulated by the Ministry to 
harness India‟s presidency of the G20 to promote Indian tourism on Mission Mode and plans 
to utilise the funds allocated to Swadesh Darshan , the Ministry‟s flagship scheme.   The 
Chairman then highlighted some inaccuracies in statistics in Tourism Statistics for the years 
2021 and 2022 published by the Ministry and desired to know the methodology employed in 
measuring tourist footfalls in Indian states and monuments.  
 
4. The Secretary, the Ministry of Tourism acknowledged that issues regarding data 
collection were being faced as the data from state sources was not reliable.   To resolve this, 
the Ministry organises training in data collection to g round-level staff in states.   The 
Secretary stated that the issue would be looked into and training given to the staff in the 
concerned states. The Secretary then gave an overview of the Indian Tourism scenario – 
 International Tourists Arrivals (ITA) in India in 2021 were 7.01 million as compared to 
6.34 million in 2020, an increase of 10.6%.   
 Foreign Tourist Arrivals (FTA) numbered 61, 91,399 registering a growth of 305.4% 
over 15, 27,144 in 2021.  
 Foreign Exchange Earnings have also risen steadily from R s. 65,070 crore in 2021 to 
Rs. 1,34,543 crores in 2022, an increase of 106.8% over the previous year. Domestic 
tourism grew by 11.05% over the previous year 
 Tourism contribution to GDP in 2020 -21was 1.06% and to share of jobs in overall 
employment was 12.91% 
 
5. The Secretary briefed the Committee about the various offices/organisations under the 
administrative control of the Ministry and informed them that overseas tourism offices are 
being wound up and alternative methods would be used to promote tourism overseas. 
 
6. The Secretary then informed about the budgetary allocations under various Major 
Heads of the Ministry.   Elaborating on the major schemes of the Ministry, the Secretary 
informed that - 
 76 projects had been sanctioned for Rs. 5320.11 crores under its flagship scheme, 
Swadesh Darshan 1.0 out of which 59 have been completed. Progress in 14 projects is 
between 75% -99% and below 75% in the remaining 3 projects.   The scheme was 
implemented from 2014 -15 till 2018 -19. After review at the highest levels, i t was 
revamped in late 2022 as Swadesh Darshan 2.0 to focus on responsible and sustainable 
tourism by following destination centric approach instead of the earlier theme -based 
circuits. 48 destinations have been identified for development under Swadesh Dar shan 
2.0 through a consultative process with the State Governments.    No new projects had 
been undertaken while revamping the scheme and that was one of the reasons for the 
reduced allocations under Revised Estimates along with implementation -related 

140 
 
challenges. The Scheme has the largest budgetary allocation of Rs. 1412 crores this 
year.  
 PRASHAD Scheme aims at rejuvenating pilgrimage, heritage and spiritual experiences 
through well -developed infrastructure. Out of 45 approved projects in 25 states 
amounting to Rs. 1586.10 crores, 20 projects have been completed and progress in 9 
more projects is more than 60%. 250 crores have been allocated to this scheme this 
year. 
 Under the Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism Infrastructure Development 
Scheme, a total of 60 projects have been sanctioned for an amount of Rs. 841.08 crores 
and 22 projects have been completed till date. 80 crores have been allocated to this 
scheme this year. 
 Under the „Adopt a Heritage Scheme”, 27 MoUs had been awarded to 15 Monument 
Mitras who would take up the responsibility of developing tourist amenities at sites but 
the Scheme has now been transferred to M/o Culture/ASI.  
 Budgetary allocation of Rs. 75 crores has been made for Domestic Promotion and 
Publicity. Overseas Promotion and Publicity Scheme is under review.  
 Allocation of Rs. 70 crores has been done under the Central Financial Assistance for 
the 21 Institutes of Hospitality Management and Sciences and other institutes under the 
Ministry 
 A total of 1,04,371 persons have bee n trained since 2018 -19 till 30/11/2022 under the 
Scheme of Capacity Building for Service Providers (CBSP) through Programmes such 
as Hunar Se Rozgar Tak, Skill Testing & Certification, Entrepreneurship Programme, 
Linguistic Tourist Facilitator, Adventure Tourism Courses, Destination Based Skilled 
Development Programme, etc. 
 An online digital platform to provide training and certification to tourist facilitators was 
launched on 1/1/2020.   IITTM is the implementing agency for this scheme and 4438 
candidates have completed the IITF Basic exam. 
 The Champion Services Sector Scheme has an allocation of Rs. 225 crores for 
extending incentives for improving Air Connectivity. In principle   approval has been 
accorded for 59 Air Tourism routes and 51 have been operati onalized. 140 crores have 
been allocated for the creation of tented accommodation in the Buddhist circuit and 90 
crores for Capacity Building in and around Iconic sites, 75 crores for Incentives to Tour 
Operators in Emerging Markets and 5.40 crores for the Promotion of MICE tourism.  
 The Iconic Destinations Scheme was not approved but some of the concepts have been 
incorporated in the Swadesh Darshan 2.0 Scheme.  
  The Incredible India website with 24 x 7 Live Chat support  interface and Mobile 
application co mprising of features like Maps,  Popular destinations, tourist attractions 
and experiences to assist tourists have been developed with content available in 
multiple foreign languages.  
 
7. A Member commented that none of the Members of the Committee were aware  that 
the Swadesh Darshan Scheme was being revamped and that 57 destinations had been selected 
after the revision of the Scheme. He enquired whether there had been any process of public 
participation or  consultation with Members of Parliament while prepar ing the guidelines for 
the revised Scheme. The representatives of the Ministry replied that State Governments were 
supposed to have public consultations before they submitted their proposals to the Ministry. 

141 
 
Members commented that the Schemes of the Ministry did not have any new components and 
appeared to be a rehash of the past Schemes.    
 
8. The Chairman commented that the Actual expenditure figure for 2022 -23 was very 
low at only 34.6% of the R.E. amount. He then enquired about the reason the entire budget 
allocation of the Ministry was shown in Revenue Expenditure only while infrastructure w as 
being created under some Schemes of the Ministry. He also enquired how the budget for 
Revenue Expenditure which is of recurring nature could be reduced.  The Secretary explained 
that while the overall expenditure is under revenue, the Schemes and projec ts of the Ministry 
are meant for asset creation only.   Further, as per the new policy of the Government, funds 
could be released only after 75% of the earlier funds had been exhausted.   As such the 
Ministry could reduce funds if Utilisation Certificates fo r the projects executed by the State 
Governments were not received.   The Chairman pointed out that the classification of 
budgetary allocations under Revenue and Capital has major impact on the Gross Domestic 
Product (GDP) and the Index of Industrial Produc tion (IIP). The representatives of the 
Ministry stated that the anomaly would be taken up with the Ministry of Finance.  
 
9.  A member enquired whether there had been any method to categorize the Consultants 
who were appointed for earlier Swadesh Darshan Sche me and whether some of them had 
been re -appointed. The Ministry affirmed that about ten Consultants with very good track
