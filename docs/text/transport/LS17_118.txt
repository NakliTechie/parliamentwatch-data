The Department of Road Transport and Highways under the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and
 Highways has been entrusted primarily with the functions of construction and maintenance of National Highways
 (NH), administration of Motor Vehicles Act and Central Motor Vehicles Rules, formulation of broad policies relating
 to road transport, environmental issues, automotive norms, etc., besides making arrangements for movements of
 vehicular traffic with neighbouring countries. There are two Wings in the Department, namely, the Road Transport
 Wing and the Roads Wing.
 
2.         The Road Transport Wing of the Department is concerned with the formulation of broad policies related to road
 transport besides making provisions for the movement of vehicular traffic with the neighbouring countries.   The
 following Acts/Rules which embody the policy relating to motor vehicles and State Transport Corporations are being
 administered in the Road Transport Wing of the Ministry:-
 
·
        The Motor Vehicles Act 1988
·        The Central Motor Vehicles Rules 1989
·        The Road Transport Corporations Act 1950
·        The Carriers Act, 1865
 
3.         The development and maintenance of National highways are being implemented on agency basis.  Out of the
 total length of 65,569 km of National Highways, 44,217 km is with the State Public Works Departments (State PWD),
 15,312 km with the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) and 5,090 km with Border Roads Organisation
 (BRO).  Balance length of 950 km is yet to be entrusted to executing agencies. 
 
4.         Our country having one of the largest road networks of 3.32 million km, consists of expressways, National
 Highways, State Highways, Major District Roads, Other District Roads and Village Roads with following length
 distribution:
 
   
National Highways/Expressways : 66590 km
State Highways : 128000 km
Major and other District Roads : 470000 km
Rural Roads : 2650000 km
 
5.         The National Highways have further been classified depending upon the width formation. Generally, a lane has a
 width of 3.75 metres in case of single lane and 3.5 metres in case of multi lane National Highways.  The break-up of

 entrusted to various agencies, such as State PWD, NHAI and BRO. When the Committee enquired about the reasons
 for the shortfall in achieving the targets, the Department replied that land acquisition, Environmental/forest clearances,

 targets, the Secretary responded that:
" ..... It is true that the same reason generally applies for delay, which is land acquisition and poorly performing
 contractors. But, certainly, we are expected to address these issues more expediously and with greater
 resolution, which we will do to out best".
 
16.       The Department was asked about the impact of budget 2007-08 on the road, transport and highways sector and
 the Department was asked whether the demands made in the BE 07-08 are adequate.  The Department in its reply to the
 Committee has stated that during the year 2007-08, excise duty on petrol and diesel has been reduced from 8% to 6%
 which will stimulate the growth of road transport sector in the country.  The road infrastructure has to keep pace with
 growing requirement of the transport industry.  Further, due to introduction of service tax on work contracts at the rate
 of 2%, perhaps the cost of work would increase.   Keeping in view above two facts, necessity to increase the budgetary
 estimates may be considered at the RE stage.  It is also noteworthy that none of the incentives provided for the Road
 Sector has been withdrawn.

NATIONAL HIGHWAY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (NHDP)  
23.       The Committee finds that one of the most ambitious projects launched in road sector in independent India is the
 National Highway Development Programme (NHDP) comprising the 5,846-km Golden Quadrilateral (GQ); the 7,300-
km North-South, East-West (NS-EW) corridors; four-laning of about 1,000 km of road network that includes port
 connectivity of 400 km and other projects of 600 km.  NHDP is mainly being executed by the National Highways
 Authority of India, which was established under NHAI Act, 1988.  The Central Government from time to time, by
 Notification in the Official Gazette, has entrusted to the Authority stretches of National Highways. The Programme is
 being implemented in phases.  Under Phase I & Phase II of this project, 13,146 km of National Highways are proposed
 to be four or six laned at an estimated cost of Rs.54,000 crore (at 1999 prices).
 
NHDP Phase I
24.  The Committee was informed that National Highways Development Project comprises of Golden Quadrilateral
 (GQ), North-South and East-West (NSEW) Corridors and Port connectivity and other projects. The total length in
 Phase-I is 7498 km i.e.5846 of GQ,981 km of NSEW Corridors and 671 km of Port connectivity and other projects. Up
 to 31 January, 2007, the GQ has been completed to the extent of 5521 km (94%). 964 km of NSEW Corridors have
 been completed as on 31 January 2007. In respect of  Port connectivity and other projects, 427 km have been completed
 as on 31 January 2007. The total completed length of NHDP Phase-I as on 31 January 2007 is 6912 km.
 
NHDP Phase II
25.      The Committee was further informed that the National Highway Development Project Phase II mainly comprises
 6240 km of NSEW Corridors and 496 km of other National Highway projects. Up to 31 January 2007, 4527 km of
 NSEW Corridors have been awarded. It is expected that all the works except the award of 201 km of East- West
 Corridor in West Bengal and 318 km in North-South Corridor in Jammu & Kashmir will be delayed because of the re-
alignment of East West Corridor in West Bengal and difficulties of terrain and technical complexities in Jammu &
 Kashmir.

34        The Committee sought the comments of the Department on the progress of the Golden Quadrilateral Project and
 the likely date of completion.   The Department in its reply stated that 5540 km of the GQ has been completed as on
 28.2.07, which is 94.77% of the total length 5767 km (98.65%) is targeted to be completed by March 2008. The
 following steps have been taken by the Department to ensure early completion of the projects:
a)      The contracts are regularly monitored at various levels such as by Supervision    Consultant, Project Directors,
 senior officers of NHAI.  Progress reviews are also held at the level of Chairman, NHAI, Secretary,
 Department of Road Transport & Highways and Minister, Shipping Road Transport & Highways.
b)  State Governments have appointed senior officers as nodal officers for resolving problems associated with
 implementation of the NHDP such as land acquisition, removal of utilities, forest / pollution / environment
 clearances etc.  These nodal officers hold periodic meetings to review the projects and take action to resolve
 the problems.
c)      A Committee of Secretaries has been constituted under Cabinet Secretary to address inter-ministerial and
 Centre - State issues such as land acquisition, utility shifting, environment approvals, clearance of ROBs.
d)  The procedure of issue of land acquisition notifications has now been simplified.   Earlier all the notifications
 under NH Act were vetted by the Ministry of Law.  Recently, an amendment has been made in the Allocation
 of Business Rules by which these notifications are not required to be sent to the Ministry of Law.  The
 Ministry of Law has approved the standard formats of various notifications keeping in view the similar nature
 of the notifications of land acquisition.
e)
      To expedite the construction of ROBs, an officer of the Railways has been posted to NHAI to coordinate with
 Ministry of Railways.  MOU has also been signed with M/s. IRCON for construction of some of the ROBs.
f)        Action has been taken against non-performing contractors and they are not allowed to bid for future projects,
 unless they improve the performance in existing contracts.
g)      Steps have been taken to improve cash flow problems of contractors by granting interest bearing discretionary
 advance at the request of contractor, release of retention money against bank guarantee of equal amount,
 deferment of recovery of advances (on interest basis) and relaxation in minimum Intermediate Payment
 Certificate (IPC) amount.

52.       The encroachments on the National Highways particularly near urban localities is a common phenomenon and
 also the removal of encroachments is a continuous process and State Government concerned is taking necessary action
 for removal of encroachments whenever these are noticed. As per the Policy, no financial assistance is provided to the

118th Report of Committee On Transport, Tourism & Culture

 State Government for rehabilitation of the encroachers. However, the guidelines of World Bank and Asian
 Development Bank provide for extending assistance to ‘vulnerable encroachers’, which includes SCs, STs,
 handicapped and people falling below poverty line.  The Committee enquired about the steps taken by the Department
 of Road Transport & Highways to curtail encroachments of the land under the National Highways.  The Department
 replied that efforts are made to remove the encroachments on the lands pertaining to National Highways from time to
 time with the help of district authorities. Further, the Government has enacted the Control of National Highways (Land
 & Traffic) Act, 2002, to remove encroachments on NHs.  This Act has come into force with effect from 27.1.2005. 
 Under the provisions of the Act, Government has set up 192 National Highway Administrations at various locations in
 the country. One National Highway Tribunal (NHT) each at Chandigarh, Lucknow, Jabalpur, Kolkata, Guwahati,
 Mumbai, Chennai and Bangalore has also been notified.   Appointments of Presiding Officers have been made for the
 NHT at Lucknow and Chandigarh and proposals for filling up remaining posts of Presiding Officers are under
 consideration. The Committee was also informed that National Highway Administration (an Officer of Central / State
 Government as notified in the Gazette) has been empowered to remove encroachments on NHs under its jurisdiction.
 The power of appeal vests with National Highway Tribunal, which is the Appellate Authority on all such matters. The
 district courts do not have any jurisdiction in these matters.

 National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002 has come into effect from 27.01.2005.  In order to implement the

 Highways Tribunals have been appointed.  Action has been initiated to fill the remaining posts of Presiding Officers. 
 In his connection, a meeting of the Selection Committee constituted for the purpose has already been held on
 20.12.2006, which has recommended a few candidates for appointment to the post of Presiding Officers in National
 Highway Tribunals.  The names recommended by the Selection Committee are being processed further for seeking the
 approval of the Government. As per the proposal approved by the Government in connection with the enactment of the
 Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act, 2002, there is a provision for setting up of 28 National Highway
 Tribunals.  At present, there is not much work-load in the National Highway Tribunals.   So, far, cases have been filed
 only in National Highway Tribunal, Chandigarh.  The present work-load does not warrant setting up of additional
 National Highway Tribunals at this stage.  As and when work-load of the National Highway Tribunals increases, the
 proposal for setting up of additional National Highway Tribunals could be considered in future, based on increased
 work - load.

TOLL TAX ON BRIDGES AND NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
 
101.     During the course of the evidence, the Committee enquired about policy of the Government with regard to
 collection of fee/toll tax on bridges and National Highways.  The Committee was informed by the Ministry of Road
 Transport and Highways that as per National Highways rules, user fee on bridges is being charged since 1976.  As per
 National Highways (Fee for the use of National Highway Section and permanent Bridge-Public Funded Projects) Rules,
 1997 & Amendment Rules 2001, user fee shall be levied as below:-
(a)
                For converting existing 2 lane National Highway Section into 4 laning facilities.
(b)               All projects costing more than Rs.100 lakhs each, completed and open to traffic after 1 st May 1992 but
 before 4th December 2001 and all projects costing more than Rs.500 lakhs each, completed on or after
 4th December 2001.
(c)                All road improvement projects/bridges and bypasses taken up on BOT basis.
 
102.     It has been further stated that the rate of fee as above shall be linked to the wholesale price index of a year and
 determined on yearly basis and be rounded to the nearest rupee by the Central  Government over the rates notified.  The
 rate of fee may be reviewed after every five years based on the wholesale price index and fixed in multiple of rupees
 five by the Central Government over the rates notified.  All fee levied under rules shall be collected by the executing
 agencies concerned, departmentally or through private contractors on the basis of competitive bidding on behalf of
 Central Government.   It further states that the fee shall be collected in perpetuity by the executive agency.   The fee
 collected for the bridges are allocated to the respective States for development of National Highways.
 
103.     The Committee took note of the fact that the toll for bridges and roads was to be collected in perpetuity and
 brought to the notice of the Secretary, Department of Road Transport and Highways that the Government was already
 levying a cess on petrol and diesel for the purpose.  In response, the Secretary clarified that the cess on petrol and diesel
 is for development whereas the toll is for maintenance of roads.
 
104.     The Committee is not convinced with the reasons advanced by the Department of Road Transport and Highways
 for collecting toll on roads and bridges in perpetuity.   The Committee feels that the Government is not justified in
 burdening the common users of highways with the development as well as the maintenance of the roads in the country.

THE TENTH PLAN PROVISIONS AND ANNUAL PLAN 2006-07
The Committee notes that trend of fund utilization pattern of the Department has been fluctuating as is evident
 from the above that Department utilized 98.16% of allocated fund whereas it could utilized only 52.08% of fund during
 2004-05.  This in turn has bearing on the performance of the Department of Road Transport and Highways.  The
 Committee hopes that the Department will take corrective measures to utilise the funds to the maximum extent possible
 during the Eleventh Five Year Plan.   The Committee also strongly recommends that the Department should evolve a
 mechanism to   monitor the utilization of funds periodically so that any shortcoming could be noticed and solved
 immediately.   The Department may also set up a coordination unit with all its Units and also with the State
 Governments, so that the funds earmarked for a particular year are spent timely.
(Para 9)
The Committee observes that the budgetary allocation of the Department of Road Transport and Highways was
 substantially increased from 2005-06(Revised Estimates) of Rs.13293.25 to Rs.14995.21 crore during 2006-07 and for
 2007-08(Budget estimates) the allocation has again been increased to Rs.16193.70 crore.  The Committee, therefore,
 reiterates its earlier recommendation that the Department should devise strategies in such a way that they are able to
 fully utilize the funds placed at their disposal.  They should also evolve some mechanism to identify bottlenecks
 causing delays and work towards resolving all such hindrances in coordination with the all concerned agencies.   This
 would help them to avoid time overrun and consequential cost overrun of the projects to be executed by them.   The
 Committee desires that the State Governments may be requested to send their plan for road projects well in time in
 order to spend the allocated outlay within the particular year.  The Committee also feels that since a large number of
 roads are being identified for up- gradation or inclusion in the NHDP, the Department should ensure that funds are
 utilized well in time for better road connectivity and also to generate good revenue by completing the projects in time.
(Para 22)
NATIONAL HIGHWAY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (NHDP)  
The Committee expresses concern over the fact that there are 7 stretches of Golden Quadrilateral still pending
 out of these 4 stretches are of more than 50 kms. length.   It indicates a sorry state of affairs that Golden Quadrilateral
 project which was scheduled to be completed by the end of 2003 have extended over four more years and now stretched
 upto 2007-08.  Still the Department has not chalked out strategies to deal with contract defaulters or legal wrangles. 
 The Committee also notes that 5 road stretches of N-S corridor and E-W corridor are also pending.  The Committee
 strongly recommends that all these works should be completed at the earliest.
The Committee notes that 94.77% of the Golden Quadrilateral Project has been completed by February, 2007. 
 The Committee expresses its concern over the slow progress of this project and rescheduling of target of completion of
 the project more than 5 years. Though the Department of Road Transport and Highways has enumerated a number of
 steps taken to ensure early completion of the NHDP, like monitoring of contracts at various level, appointing of nodal
 officers by State Governments, constitution of Committee of Secretaries, simplification of procedure of land
 acquisition, action against non-performing contractors and improving cash flow problems of contractors. However,
 when it comes to the implementation of the projects, it appears that things have not improved.  The construction of
 flyover at Delhi - Badarpur    Border can be sighted as an example as how the procedural hurdle delaying in projects
 leading to cost overrun and time escalation.   The project was approved by the Department of Road Transport and
 Highways on 31
st March, 2005, however the project is yet to be finalised for want of a number of clearances.   This
 project forms part of Golden Triangle of Agra, Delhi and Jaipur where lakh of domestic and foreign tourists visit on it. 
 The project could have been taken on priority basis particularly taking into account in the Commonwealth Games,
 2010.  The Committee recommends that the steps enumerated by the Department for the early completion of the project
 should be implemented in letter and spirit so that there is not cost overrun and time overrun.  The Committee hopes that
 the Department would take necessary precautions in future while finalizing such type of projects which require huge
 amount of investment.   In this context the Committee reiterates its recommendation made in the 102

            The Roads of Inter State connectivity and economic importance as evident by its projects are very crucial for

QUALITY CONTROL
The Committee desires that there is a need for indigenization of norms regarding designing of roads and bridges
 taking into account the local site conditions.  The Committee hopes that there should be uniform norms/guidelines in

 are reports that Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi has made third party checks mandatory by a panel of
 institutes i.e., Delhi College of Engineering,  CRRI and IIT Delhi for all road works like strengthening and resurfacing. 
 The Committee desires that the Department may also involve similar mechanism to check the quality of repair work
 carried out on the National Highways and this independent audit should be made part of the contract while awarding it
 which will also ensure that works carried out are at par with international standards or norms.(Para 51)
ENCROACHMENT
The Committee feels that the encroachment of land under National Highways is a serious issue as it involves
 huge loss of public property, hampers the smooth development of highways and is also an irritant in the timely
 completion of various projects.   The Committee recommends that suitable action should be taken to prevent
 encroachments.   The Committee feels that strict enforcement of Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act,
 2002 will play a major role in removing encroachments upon national highways.  The Committee hopes that the
 Department will fill up the vacancies on priority in National Highways Tribunals to ensure that the law regarding
 removal of encroachments on national highways is implemented in both letter and spirit.(Para 55)

accrual of previous fund allocations from the CRF in view of high cost implementation of NHDP and also the inclusion
 of a large number of new roads which are being included in this project. The Committee desires that the Department

 monitor the projects under taken by State Governments.
