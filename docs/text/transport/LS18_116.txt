                                                            REPORT
 
The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation of national policies and programmes for the
 development and regulation of civil aviation in the country and for devising and implementing schemes for orderly
 growth and expansion of civil air transport.  Its functions also extend to overseeing the provision of airport facilities, air
 traffic services, carriage of passengers and goods by air, safeguarding civil aviation operations, regulation of air
 transport services, licensing of aerodromes, air carriers, pilots and aircraft maintenance engineers. In addition to
 framing policies, the Ministry provides guidance to the organisations in the implementation of policy guidelines. It
 supervises implementation by the organisations of special programmes of the Government, particularly those intended
 for weaker sections.  The Ministry also administratively controls the Commission of Railway Safety, a statutory body

16.       The Committee was informed by the Ministry that Air India had earned a Net Profit of Rs.14.94 crore during the
 year 2005-06 as against a profit of Rs.96.36 crore for the year 2004-05.  The Ministry in its reply to the query on the

17.       The Ministry has also informed that the passenger load factor has declined from 69.8% to 66.9% even though
 the number of passengers carried increased by 10.4% i.e. from 4.40 million in the year 2004-05 to 4.85 million in the
 year 2005-06.
 
18.       The Committee desired to know the details of the steps taken to increase the profit/passenger load factor of the
 Air India as the passenger load factor has a direct impact on the profit or loss of the company.   The Ministry in its reply
 stated that the phased induction of new aircraft in the ageing fleet of Air India will have a very positive impact in its
 operations. The Ministry has further stated that new marketing strategies introduced by Air India such as whole sale
 travel discount, aircraft cabin upgradation, companion free scheme, auction of Air India tickets through Internet will
 yield a positive result in its profits and an increase in the passenger load factor in the ensuing years.

 the reasons of appreciation in value of US dollar and increasing fuel price.  The Committee hopes that acquisition of

 that Air India should adopt an aggressive marketing strategy to face the stiff competition at global level. The
 Committee is also of the opinion that more passenger friendly amenities be provided in its aircraft so that more and
 more passengers turn their interest towards the national carrier.   The Committee desires that the number of   bilateral
 agreements which were signed during the last year should be materialised to the maximum possible extent.

22.       The Committee asked the Ministry about the variations in the expenditure to the tune of Rs.356 crore under the
 Annual Plan 2006-07.  The Ministry in its reply stated that the main reason for variation is on account of the fact that
 the Annual Plan was made with the projection that 6 aircraft (out of the 43 aircraft) will be received in 2006-07,
 however, as per the revised delivery schedule, only one new aircraft was scheduled to join in 2006-07, and the same
 (A-319) has already joined in the fleet in October'06.  The difference on this account is Rs.347.71 crore.  The Ministry
 has also stated the remaining difference of Rs.8.29 crore is on account of taking up only essential and operationally
 necessary projects during the year, exchange rate variation and payments made to IGRUA.
23.       The Committee while considering the Demands for Grants in respect of Indian Airlines enquired about the
 acquisition of new aircraft for Indian Airlines.  The Ministry replied that the Government had approved a proposal for
 acquisition of 43 Airbus aircraft, fitted with CFM engines, for Indian Airlines, at an estimated net project cost of
 Rs.9888 crore.  An agreement was executed with Airbus Industries and CFM International on 20.02.2006 in the
 presence of the Prime Minister and the President of France.  These aircraft are scheduled to be delivered between
 October, 2006 to March 2010.  The first A-319 was delivered on 19.10.2006 and the remaining aircraft would be
 delivered at the rate of one aircraft per month from June, 2007 to June, 2009 (except September, 2007).  The aircraft
 delivery during the period of July 2009 to March, 2010 would take place at the rate of two aircraft per month.  On
 induction of new aircraft, the old fleet of A-300 and B-737-200 would be replaced in a phased manner. 
24.       The Committee was informed that the Annual Plan of Indian Airlines for the year 2007-08 has been approved
 with an outlay of Rs.2507.70 crore.  The increase is primarily due to the actual payments to be made to the
 aircraft/engine manufacturers during 2007-08. This would only meet a part of the complete acquisition cost of Indian
 Airlines.  The total acquisition cost is 10,169 crore and the balance payments for the new aircraft would be made in
 subsequent years.
25.       The Ministry informed the Committee that Indian Airlines has earned a Net Profit of Rs.49.50 crore during
 2005-06 as against the Net Profit of Rs.65.61 crore during 2004-05.  The reasons stated for the decline in the profit are
 increase in ATF price, fall in passengers yields owing to intense competition in the domestic aviation market and due to
 exercise capacity and entry of new players in the market.   However, the overall load factor has seen a substantial
 increased from 69.01% in the year 2004-05 to 71.6% in the year 2005-06. 
26.       The Committee notes that even though there is a substantial increase in the passenger load factor for the period
 from April to September, 2006, the profit of the IA seems to be declining. The physical performance of the Indian
 Airlines is showing good signs of improvement but it is not resulting in a good profit to the company. 
27.       The Committee hopes that induction of new aircraft will help Indian Airlines to improve its profitability and the
 merger   of two national carriers will have a positive impact on the performance of both the Airlines.  The Committee
 recommends that Indian Airlines should revamp its marketing strategies to get its due share in the domestic as well as
 in the international market and also to match with the services offered by the private operators.

SCHEMES UNDERTAKEN BY AAI IN NORTH EASTERN REGION
36.       On enquiry from the Committee regarding the status of airports in the North-eastern region the Ministry stated
 that Airports Authority of India (AAI) has taken up several schemes pertaining to development of airports in the North
 Eastern Region. The schemes in the North Eastern Region are not economically viable and operation at these airports
 does not generate adequate revenues even to meet the operational/recurring expenditure.   In view of this, airport
 projects in the North Eastern Region, which are being undertaken for socio-economic considerations, are being funded
 jointly by the North Eastern Council and Ministry of Civil Aviation.
37.       Two separate MOUs one for schemes approved by NEC and other for schemes sanctioned under Prime
 Minister’s initiative have been signed by AAI with North Eastern Council for development of airports under financial
 assistance from the North Eastern Council.   In accordance with the financing pattern of North Eastern Council, the
 expenditure to the extent of 60% of the project cost is met by the Council and the balance 40% is being provided as
 budgetary support by Ministry of Civil Aviation in the normal debt equity ratio of 1:1.

MERGER OF AIR INDIA LIMITED AND INDIAN AIRLINES LIMITED
46.       The Ministry was specifically asked about the merger of Air India and Indian Airlines Ltd.  In its response, the
 Ministry has stated that the Government has decided to merge the two airlines into a new company, which would
 further strengthen the public sector airlines.  The new airline would be the largest airline in the country comparable to
 other airlines in Asia and will provide an integrated international and national footprint.   The merger would enable
 these two airlines to pool their resources, achieve synergies, face competition and establish fresh benchmarks for
 efficiency and reliability.   The merger of the two airlines would be completed in a phased manner over the next two
 years.
47.       The Ministry has also stated that the merger is in keeping with the industry trend of moving towards
 consolidation and mergers enabling the airlines to achieve synergies and reduce costs.  The merged airline would be
 able to offer an integrated schedule from interior points in the country(currently served by Indian) to various
 international destinations (currently served by Indian and/or Air India) and vice-versa, offering seamless connectivity to
 passengers and also extending the connectivity of interior points to various international destinations via the major hub
 airports in India. 
48.       A bigger and stronger public sector national carrier will also help in expanding regional connectivity to hitherto
 under-serviced and un-serviced destinations.   While taking the merger decision, employees interests have been
 accorded prime importance.   The Government is committed to ensure that all legitimate interests of employee,
 including their current compensations, status, etc. are protected.   There will be no retrenchment and all employees of
 both the airlines will continue in the merged company.  The exercise of integrating manpower would be completed in a
 phased manner, on the basis of transparent and objective criteria.  A well-defined Grievance Redressal Machinery will
 also be put in place.
49.       The Committee raised the question on the issue of extra expenditure to be incurred on completing the process on
 which the Secretary, stated as under:
"Sir, there would be cost of the integration merger.   But the consultants have given a roadmap which indicates that on
 balance, there would a saving because there would be savings in terms of synergies, savings in terms of avoidance of
 duplication, etc.  So, at the end of the third year, there would be a saving of Rs.400 crore, taking into account the extra
 costs involved in the higher pay scales, etc".
50.       The Committee also expressed its concern over the issue of positioning of employees of both the carriers in the
 new entity for which the Secretary stated as under:
"Sir, I would be less than honest if I say that there will be no problems.  The HR issues in any merger cause problems,
 and when you have two large entities with 18,000 plus employees and 15,000 plus employees, it certainly not going to
 be an easy task.  But apart from the fact that there will be no retrenchment and nobody would lose his job and so which
 is the most basis thing, what the employees have been assured is that there will be a three-tier grievance redressal
 machinery which will be put in place..."

UNIFORM TAXATION OF ATF     
66.       During the course of the oral evidence of the representatives of Ministry of Civil Aviation, the Committee was
 informed that the domestic air transport industry of the nation has been facing serious problems due to high costs of
 operations. One of the major reasons for this is the heavy cost of Air Turbine Fuel (ATF).  Even though the pricing of
 ATF is now on import parity basis, the rates applicable for domestic operations continue to be significantly higher than
 that of international operations.  The cost of ATF goes up even further due to high rates of sales tax levied by the State
 Governments/Union Territory Administration varying from 25 per cent to 30 per cent which affects the viability of
 domestic airline operations.
67.       The Secretary, while tendering his evidence also raised this issue  before the Committee  and stated as under:
".....Sir, as you aware, is a very important and a major component of the variable cost of operations.   Sir, the cost in
 India, the way it is priced and the way there is excise duty and , then there is Sales Tax in the States, is much higher, at
 least 25-30 per cent higher than what it is overseas.  We have been requesting that at least it be notified as a declared
 good under section 14 of the Central Sales Tax Act for all types of aircraft; so that there is a cap on the quantum of levy
 which can be imposed on this.  Sir, presently it is four per cent; ...... we have got some relief in relation to smaller
 aircraft, but it is not yet available for the aviation sector and all the operators, as a whole".
 
68.       The Ministry was asked about the budget proposal to amend the Central Sales Tax to treat aviation turbine fuel
 as a declare goods for all small aircraft with a maximum take of less than 40,000 kg operated by scheduled carriers and
 also the impact of this amendment on the aviation sector for which the ministry replied that until now, this facility was
 available only to turbo-prop aircraft.   The present proposal to extend the concession to all small aircraft including jet
 aircraft operated by scheduled airlines would encourage and facilitate connectivity to non-metro and other smaller cities
 by more efficient small jet aircraft.
 
69.       The Cost of fuel is an important component of cost structure and has direct bearing on the profitability of any
 airline. High cost of ATF in the country increases the cost of operation of carriers which results in escalation in the
 airfares.  The Committee recommends that Ministry of Finance should sympathetically consider and give approval to
 the proposal of Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding notifying ATF as 'Declared Good' under Section 14 of Central
 Sales Tax Act on priority basis to all the aircraft  so that sales tax on ATF could be contained with the maximum rate of
 4%. The Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Finance may also discuss the issue with the State
 Governments and try to pursuade them to reduce   the sales tax on ATF.  The Committee feels that restructuring the
 excise duty on ATF will have a positive impact on the development of civil aviation sector of the nation.
UTILISATION OF CAT III SYSTEM AT DELHI AIRPORT
70.       During the winter season, the air passengers are put into great hardship by cancellation/diversion of flights due
 to fog at Delhi airport.  The Committee sought the remedial actions proposed to avoid such a situation during the next
 year. The Ministry in its reply stated that the airlines are encouraged to upgrade their aircraft and train their pilots for
 operations in low visibility conditions.   Their flight schedules are approved on the basis of number of pilots trained by
 the operators for low visibility operations and the suitability of the planes for such operations.   If the operator cannot
 provide data for their capability to fly under low visibility, they are not allowed to schedule their flights during normal
 fog hours.  The Ministry also stated that airlines have been directed not to roster their CAT III trained pilots on
 international routes during the fog season.  DGCA also requires that the airlines should train their pilots for ILS CAT
 II/III operations.   Pilots who are CAT II/III qualified are approved as Instructor/Examiner so as to ensure that more
 trained pilots are available for CAT II/III operations during low visibility conditions. 
71.       The Committee is constrained to note that a huge investment has been made in installation of ILS CAT-III
 facilities at IGI airport.  However there is a lot of delay or cancellation of flights due to fog as there are not enough
 pilots trained to operate the flights during fog. It appears that steps taken by the Government in this regard have proved
 inadequate. The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry that Airlines have been directed not to roster their CAT III
 trained pilots on international routes during fog season and also recommended that  training of pilots to fly on low
 visibility conditions be extended to a maximum number of pilots in both the national carriers. The Committee
 recommends that strict penal provisions should be imposed on the airlines which are failing to give proper training to

The Committee finds that the PHHL is without a regular CMD for quite some time and a Joint Secretary of the Ministry
 is holding additional charge of CMD of PHHL which is affecting the functioning of PHHL.  The Committee desires
 that in the larger interest of PHHL a regular CMD preferably from the PHHL establishment should be appointed on
 priority basis. (Paras 44, 45)
MERGER OF AIR INDIA LIMITED AND INDIAN AIRLINES LIMITED
            The Committee feels that the merger of these two national carriers would definitely result in proper
 synergisation of resources and enhance both financial and physical performance of both these carriers.  The Committee
 also feels that this process will help both the national carriers in long run in consolidating their position in domestic and
 international market.   The Committee hopes that the synergisation process would help to improve the operational
 efficiency of both the carriers. However, the Committee would like the Government to take care of the  interests of the
 employees in the new merged entity as merger of such large institutions always results in resentment amongst the
 employees as they are likely to lose their existing work position in the new merged entity.    The Committee notes the
 assurance given by the Government that there would be a three tier grievance cell to take care of the grievances of the
 employees of both the carriers after the merger. The Committee hopes that the mechanism of grievance redressal
 proposed by Government will be able to clear the doubts in the minds of employees. (Para 51)
NATIONAL CIVIL AVIATION POLICY
            The Committee notes that the present civil aviation sector needs a well balanced and market oriented policy to
 address all the issues relating to the boom in passenger and cargo traffic.  The quantum of increase in passenger traffic
 needs strict enforcement of safety and security related issues.  The Committee, however, is constrained to note that
 Ministry of Civil Aviation has not come out with a Civil Aviation Policy till date. Now the Government plans to have a
 policy with a vision for 2020.  The Committee hopes that the policy will be in place soon and take care of all the issues
 relating to civil aviation sector of current as well as future era.  (Para 54)
RESTRUCTURING OF DELHI AND MUMBAI AIRPORTS
            While scrutinizing the outcome budget of the Ministry for the year 2006-07 the Committee finds that a
 substantial amount of money has been spent by the AAI for repair and maintenance work at international airports at
 Delhi and Mumbai even after these two airports were handed over to the JVCs in May, 2006.  The Committee desires
 to know the reasons for this and also wants to know the expenditure incurred by the JVCs on the respective airports for
 maintenance and up keep of airports till date.
            A Tripartite Committee with the representatives of Ministry of Civil Aviation, Airports Authority of India and
 AAI Employees Joint Forum has been constituted by the Ministry of Civil Aviation on     22
nd February, 2006 to look
 into the issues and proposals of modernizing airports by Airports Authority of India and employees related issues
 including job security.  The Committee desires to know the details of meetings which have been taken so far with the
 decisions arrived therein.  The Committee also reiterates its recommendation contained in its 106
th Report that the
 Tripartite Committee should hold regular meetings and finalise its Report in a time bound manner in view of the
 importance of the issues relating to the Committee.

 problem.                    (Paras 56, 57, 58)       
CHENNAI AND KOLKATA AIRPORTS
 
            The Committee is constrained to note that the indecisiveness is preventing with regard to modernisation of
 Kolkata and Chennai Airport as Government is yet to give its approval for modernisation plans of these two Airports.
 The Committee recommends that the modernisation of Chennai and Kolkata airports should be completed on priority so
 as to ease the traffic over Delhi and Mumbai airports.  The Committee also desires that both these airports may be
 developed as international hubs to cater the needs of the people in the eastern and southern part of the country.  The
 Committee hopes that the Airports Authority of India will complete the all modernisation and developmental works of
 Kolkata and Chennai airports within a specified time frame.(Para 62)
SHORTAGE AND TRAINING OF PILOTS
            The Committee hopes that modernization of IGRUA and establishment of new pilot training institute in Gondia
 will help to meet the shortage of pilots. The Committee recommends that more training institute like that of the
 proposed one of the Gondia may also be established at conspicuous places in the Northern, Eastern and Southern parts
 of the country to meet the shortage of Pilots. (Para 65)
UNIFORM TAXATION OF ATF
The Cost of fuel is an important component of cost structure and has direct bearing on the profitability of any airline.
 High cost of ATF in the country increases the cost of operation of carriers which results in escalation in the airfares. 
 The Committee recommends that Ministry of Finance should sympathetically consider and give approval to the
 proposal of Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding notifying ATF as 'Declared Good' under Section 14 of Central Sales
 Tax Act on priority basis to all the aircraft   so that sales tax on ATF could be contained with the maximum rate of 4%.
 The Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Finance may also discuss the issue with the State
 Governments and try to pursuade them to reduce   the sales tax on ATF.  The Committee feels that restructuring the
 excise duty on ATF will have a positive impact on the development of civil aviation sector of the nation. (Para 69)
UTILISATION OF CAT III SYSTEM AT DELHI AIRPORT
            The Committee is constrained to note that a huge investment has been made in installation of ILS CAT-III
 facilities at IGI airport.  However there is a lot of delay or cancellation of flights due to fog as there are not enough
 pilots trained to operate the flights during fog. It appears that steps taken by the Government in this regard have proved
 inadequate. The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry that Airlines have been directed not to roster their CAT III
 trained pilots on international routes during fog season and also recommended that  training of pilots to fly on low
 visibility conditions be extended to a maximum number of pilots in both the national carriers. The Committee
 recommends that strict penal provisions should be imposed on the airlines which are failing to give proper training to
 their pilots in the upgraded instrument landing systems.  The Committee hopes that incidents of cancellation of flights
 during the winter season in 2007 will be minimum.(Para 71)
