REPORT 
The Ministry of Shipping encompasses within its fold the port and shipping 
sectors which include major ports, shipbuilding and ship -repair, national water -ways 
and inland water transport. The Ministry  has been entrusted with the responsibility to 
formulate policies and programmes on these subjects and their implementation. 
2. The following subordinate/attached offices, autonomous organizations, 
societies/associations and public sector undertakings are functioning under the 
administrative control of the Ministry of Shipping. 
 Directorate   General   of   Shipping,   Mumbai   (including   Minor   Ports   
Survey  Organization, Mumbai) 
 Andaman & Lakshadweep Harbour Works, Port Blair.  
 Directorate General of Lighthouses & Lightships, NOIDA  
 Port Trusts at Kolkata, Kochi (Cochin Port Trust), Kandla, Chennai, Mormugao, 
Mumbai, Nhava Sheva (Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust), Paradip, Tuticorin(V.O. 
Chidambaranar Port Trust), Visakhapatnam and New Mangalore.  
 Dock Labour Board at Kolkata  
 Inland Waterways Authority of India, Noida.  
 Tariff Authority for Major Ports, Mumbai.  
 Seamen‟s Provident Fund Organization, Mumbai.  
 Indian Maritime University.

7. The Committee notes from this table two trends – (i) there has been only 
marginal increments in the GBS to the Ministry every year and (ii) this GBS has 
been invariably reduced at RE stage every year.  Taking a realistic view, if 
expenditure is taken in terms of RE allocation, its percentage has been satisfactory 
and as per the financial norms.  It means that Ministry has shown  its capability to 
spend the finds given to it every ye ar.  The Committee, however, fails to 
understand,  how the Ministry of Finance could with such a good expenditure, 
allocate funds to Ministry of Shipping with a marginal increase every year.  In 
other words,  may be, if it  is given more  funds which shipp ing sector needs badly, 
the Ministry could have fruitfully used it.  The Committee in view of the potential 
and capabilities of Shipping sector in our economy in days to come, recommends 
that Ministry of Finance must increase GBS to Ministry of Shipping every year 
substantially. 
2

8. The Committee further observes that the non -plan expenditure is also has 
been good showing above 95% spending during the last 4 years except during 
2013-14 which was 85.86% of the RE.   
9. The Committee further observes that the IEBR gross expenditure stands 
only at 59.35% which also steadily decreased in the first three years.  As in the 
case of Plan allocation IEBR has also shown improvement during 2015 -16.  But, 
still more needs to be done. 
10. In the case of IEBR Committee not es that it has registered consistent 
decline from 2013 -14 on words.  Similarly, IEBR were reduced each year, except 
in 2012 -13, at RE stage; reduction being about 35 to 40%.  Needless to add, 
expenditure of IEBR also had declined and its percentage was not  as good as in 
the case of GBS.  Such a negative variation in case of IEBR shows that the 
performance of our ports and shipping sector need to be examined to find out real 
reasons for decline in generation of internal resources/revenue.  While good 
expenditure of GBS may indicates good performance, declining IEBR generation 
tells opposite story.  The Committee would like to know reasons for this and 
recommends the Ministry of Shipping make all out efforts for correcting this 
situation as shipping sector is going to face tremendous challenge in coming days.

18. The Committee observes that compared to 2015 -16, the allocation in 2016 -
17 for the Major Ports have been highly insufficient.  As per the projection made 
by the Ministry, they demanded Rs.467.48 crore whereas the allocation is a very 
meager amount of Rs.29.01 crore, which wil l adversely affect the 
modernization/capacity enhancement projects of the Tuticorin, Chennai and 
Mormugao Ports.  The Committee recommends that additional allocation for the 
ports may be made at RE Stage. 
19. The Committee further observes that the IWT Sec tor is a very important 
and emerging Sector which will be able to generate sufficient cargo and passenger 
movement in a very economical manner compared to other modes of transport.  In 
such a scenario, allocating only Rs.350.00 crore where demand stands at  
Rs.2384.00 crore is not helping the IWT Sector in a big way.  The Committee also 
understands that the Jal Marg Vikas project will be affected due to insufficient 
allocation.  The Committee recommends for more allocation for the IWT Sector. 
20. The Committee understands that the Ministry of Shipping has completed 
preparatory project development work related to port modernization; coastal 
shipping, coastal berths, IWT and ALHW, which needs additional funds for 
completion of the projects.  The Committee recom mends that the Ministry of 
Finance may look into the matter in view of the importance of the infrastructure 
development in the Port and Shipping Sectors; and sufficient funds may be 
allocated for these projects this year. 
21. The Committee also recommends for granting additional funds for the 
IMU in order to meet the committed liabilities of the approved projects.

25. The Ministry of Shipping during the deliberation informed that allocation of 
only Rs. 29 crore has been made in BE 2016 -17 under Plan for Major Ports. This 
allocation is inadequate t o meet the committed liabilities and additional requirement of 
Rs.388.61crore is needed in 2016-17 detailed as under: 
I. Mormugao Port Trust – Port connectivity project:  The Work of Port 
Connectivity is executed by the State Government of Goa. MormugaoPort ha s 
to provide its share of Rs.171 crore out of which Rs. 75 crore has already been 
released to the port in 2014 -15. In BE 2016 -17 an amount of Rs. 30 crore was 
proposed against which allocation of only Rs. 5 crore has been allocated. 
Additional requirement of Rs.25 crore is needed in 2016-17. 
II. Chennai Port Trust – Coastal road protection work:  Out of total cost of the 
project of Rs.63 crore, Rs.25 crore was released to the Chennai Port Trust upto 
2015-16. Till March, 2016 more than Rs. 35 crore has been spent  and the work 
is likely to be completed by December, 2016. Allocation of Rs. 5 crore in BE 
2016-17 is inadequate to make payment even with respect to the expenditure 
incurred upto 31.03.2016. As work is going on in full swing, non -availability of 
remaining funds would affect the completion of work in time. Additional 
requirement of Rs. 33crore is needed in 2016-17. 
III. V.O. Chidambaranar Port Trust (VOCPT) –Project relating to capital dredging 
in front of North Cargo Berth (NCB) II:  50% of the cost of the project which is 
about Rs. 224 crore is to be borne by the Government of India. Out of 
Government‟s committed liability, Rs. 89 crore has already been reimbursed to 
the port and the balance amount of Rs.135 crore is yet to be reimbursed. 
Additional requirement of Rs.120 crore is needed in 2016-17. 
IV. Cochin Port – Road connectivity to ICTT at Vallarpadam Construction of 
subway: The cost of the project is Rs. 30 crore out which Rs.10 crore has 
already been released to the port. In BE 2016 -17 only Rs. 4 crore has be en 
allocated. Considerable progress has been made and the work is likely to be 
completed by 31.08.2016. In case of delay in payment to the contractor, the 
work may come to a standstill. Further being a road project, the public will be 
put to lot of inconve nience. Additional requirement of Rs.16 crore is needed in 
2016-17. 
V. Cochin Port – Rail connectivity to ICTT Vallarpadam : The original cost of the 
project approved by CCEA in March 2007 was Rs. 245.67 crore. Rs. 36.61crore 
was proposed in the BE 2016-17 against which no allocation has been provided. 
The work has been awarded and is scheduled to be completed in September, 
2016. The work is to be funded through GBS as Cochin Port Trust‟s financial 
position doesn‟t permit it to pay the contractor for this work out of its own 
resources. Work may come to standstill if the bills are not cleared. Additional 
requirement of Rs. 36.61crore is needed in 2016-17. 
VI. Setting up of Colachel Satellite Port, along with setting up of Dahanu Port 
submitted to Cabinet for seeking in principle approval:  Feasibility studies have 
been entrusted with regard to the setting up of the port. A some of`  Rs. 96 crore 
was proposed in BE 2016 -17. No allocation has however been provided.  
Additional requirement of Rs.96 crore is needed in 2016-17. 
8

VII. VPT – Financial assistance to VPT for restoration of damages suffered in Hud - 
hud cyclone: Niti Aayog advised that Ministry of Shipping could provide for 
50% of the damages with balance 50% being contributed by the port from its 
internal resources. Rs . 62crore which represents 50% of the damages suffered 
by the Port was proposed in BE 2016 -17. However, no allocation has been 
provided in BE 2016 -17. Additional requirement of Rs. 62crore is needed in 
2016-17. 
26. The Committee takes a serious view of the  fact that the amount allocated 
for the Major Ports are very insignificant compared to the  role and importance of 
the ports in earning revenue for the country and generating employment on large 
scale.  Many of the Major Ports have not got any allocation t his year and some 
ports have got very negligible amount. Many important projects of the Major 
Ports as mentioned by the Ministry of Shipping have been kept aside due to non -
allocation of funds. The Committee notes that the Ports Sector has been allocated 
only Rs. 29 crore wherein various Major Ports require additional fund of Rs. 
388.61 core for meeting the commitments made by them during 2016 -17.  The 
Committee recommends that sufficient fund allocations may be made to the Major 
Ports at the RE stage so th at they can complete the ongoing projects and  the 
projects to be undertaken during 2016-17.

SAGARMALA 
39. The Ministry of Shipping informed that  Sagarmala, a Coastal & Port -led 
Development Programme, is an initiative approved by the Union Cabinet on 25 th 
March, 2015.  It intends to achieve the broad objectives of enhancing the capacity of 
major and non -major ports and modernizing them to make them efficient, thereby 
enabling them to become drivers of port-led economic development, optimizing the use 
of existing and future transport assets and developing new lines/linkages for transport 
(including roads, rail, inland waterways and coastal routes), setting up of logistics hubs, 
and establishment of industries and manufacturing centres to be served by the ports i n 
EXIM and domestic trade.  In addition to strengthening the port and evacuation 
infrastructure, it also aims at simplifying procedures used at ports for cargo movement 
and promotes usage of electronic channels for information exchange leading to quick, 
efficient, hassle -free and seamless cargo movement.  Skill Development of coastal 
communities is also a critical part of Sagarmala initiative.

Mormugao Ports.  The Committee recommends that additional allocation for the 
ports may be made at RE Stage.                                                                   (Para:18) 
 The Committee further observes that the IWT Sector is a very important 
and emerging Sector which will be able to generate sufficient cargo and passenger 
movement in a very economical manner compared to other modes of transport.  In 
such a scenario, allocating only Rs.350.00 crore where demand stands at 
Rs.2384.00 crore is not helping the IWT Sector in a big way.  The Committee also 
understands that the Jal Marg Vikas project will be affected due to insufficient 
allocation.  Th e Committee recommends for more allocation for the IWT Sector.
                                            (Para:19) 
 The Committee understands that the Ministry of Shipping has completed 
preparatory project development work related to port modernization; coastal 
shipping, coastal berths, IWT and ALHW, which needs additional funds for 
completion of the projects.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of 
Finance may look into the matter in view of the importance of the infrastructure 
development in the Port and Shipping Sectors; and suf ficient funds may be 
allocated for these projects this year.

 The Committee also recommends for granting additional funds for the

PORT SECTOR 
 The Committee takes a serious view of the fact that the amount allocated 
for the Major Ports are very insignificant compared to the  role and importance of 
the ports in earning revenue for the country and generating employment on large 
scale.  Man y of the Major Ports have not got any allocation this year and some 
ports have got very negligible amount. Many important projects of the Major 
Ports as mentioned by the Ministry of Shipping have been kept aside due to non -
allocation of funds. The Committe e notes that the Ports Sector has been allocated 
only Rs. 29 crore wherein various Major Ports require additional fund of Rs. 
388.61 core for meeting the commitments made by them during 2016 -17.  The 
Committee recommends that sufficient fund allocations may be made to the Major 
Ports at the RE stage so that they can complete the ongoing projects and  the 
projects to be undertaken during 2016-17.                                                     (Para:26)

integrated development of the In dian Ports and Shipping Sectors. The Committee 
hopes that the project would be able to enhance the capacity of major and non -

to become drivers of port-led economic development, optimizing the use of existing 
and future transport assets and developing new lines/linkages for  all modes of 
transport, setting up of logistics hubs, and establishment of industries and 
manufacturing centres to be served by the ports in EXIM and domestic trade.                                                                                             
(Para:44)

under Sagarmala, the Ministry requires Rs.1000 crore this year. However , the

additional funds at the RE stage.
