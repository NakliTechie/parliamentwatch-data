REPORT
Tourism is basically a people oriented, multi dimensional and labour intensive activity. It
is dependent on varied activities which are separate but interdependent. Such a broad spectrum
of economic activities involved in tourism, therefore, make it very vital for overall economic
growth. Furthermore, tourism is highly dependent upon natural capital such as forest, wildlife
and culture.
2. The Ministry of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and
programmes and for the coordination of activities of various Central Government agencies, State
Governments/Union Territories and the Private Sector for the development and promotion of
tourism in the country.
3. Demand No.93 containing the details of the budgetary provisions of Ministry of Tourism for
the financial year 2012-13, was scrutinized by the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
Committee on Transport, Tourism and Culture at its meeting held on the 12
th April, 2012. The
Secretary, Ministry of Tourism alongwith the senior officials explained to the Committee on various
aspects of the Demands for Grants.

6. The Committee notes that the total allocation made for the Eleventh Plan was
Rs. 4997 crore and the Ministry was able to spend Rs. 4690.57 crore upto Dec 2011. The
Secretary while deposing before the Committee stated that:
…..Sir, that  we are one of the few Ministries in the Government of India which has  done
nearly 100 per cent. So, Sir, we hope that tourism will now go to a different orbit, if not
a different zone.
7. The Committee also notes that as per year-wise utilization detail (Table-I) submitted
by the Ministry, the total utilization during the Eleventh Plan had been around 98% on an
average. The Committee appreciates that Ministry of Tourism was able to spend the
allocation during the Eleventh Plan and hope that the tourism sector must have got
benefited by the funds that have been put into the sector through various schemes.
8. The detailed analysis of the fund distribution and performance of each scheme during the
Eleventh Plan as per Table I and II and other papers furnished by the Ministry of Tourism and
observations and recommendations of the Committee are given in the succeeding paragraphs.
Product/infrastructure Development for Destinations and Circuits
9. For the Eleventh Plan the total outlay under Product/infrastructure Development for
Destinations and Circuits was Rs.2381 crores. However the expenditure stands at Rs.2621.53 crores
whereas the total sanctions were Rs.2514.12 crores.  During the deliberations, the Committee was
informed that Rs.3872.67 crore was expended till 31 st December, 2011for sanctioning 1163 projects.
The Committee also notes that the Government of Odisha was sanctioned Rs.740.67 lakhs for the
“Integrated Development of Buddhist Circuit at Lalitagiri, Ratnagiri, Udyagiri and Langudi” during
2004-05, in the 10 th Plan Period.
10. It was brought to the notice of the Committee that Jabalpur has been identified in 2010-11
by the State Government of MP as the Mega circuit for development of tourism infrastructure and
has submitted the proposal to the Ministry of Tourism for development. However, the Central
Government is yet to include Jabalpur as a mega tourist destination for development by providing
funds.
11. The Committee is of the opinion that even though the Ministry was able to incur the
surplus expenditure under the scheme, it is equally important to know  reasons for excess
expenditure as the desired targets have not been achieved in terms of creation of
infrastructural facilities  and the resultant tourist arrivals. The Committee also emphasizes
the need for a thorough review of the physical achievements made on the infrastructure
projects of the Ministry during the Eleventh Plan period.
12. The Committee notes that the work of the “Integrated Development of Buddhist
Circuit at Lalitagiri, Ratnagiri, Udyagiri and Langudi”  in Odisha was sanctioned during the
Tenth Plan period and is still going on. In the past also, the Committee has drawn the
attention of the Ministry of Tourism to a number of projects which were taken up and
crores of rupees were invested therein but could not be completed and later on, were
dropped. This results in wastage of taxpayers money and country’s scarce resources. The
Committee also desire that information regarding the status of projects sanctioned before
the Eleventh plan period which are still continuing may be submitted to the Committee. The
Committee also  recommends that the projects involving large amount of investment should
be monitored carefully during the implementation stage itself  to ensure that work is
completed in time and investment made therein do not go waste.

4
13. The Committee recommends that the proposal of the State Government of Madhya
Pradesh for developing Jabalpur as a mega circuit for development of tourist infrastructure
may be taken up by the Ministry of Tourism at the earliest.
Externally Aided Projects
14. The Committee notes that for the externally aided projects out of the total outlay of Rs.37
crore, the total allocations for the entire Plan period was Rs.25.65 crores.
15. The Committee was informed that a loan agreement for an amount equivalent to 7331
million Japanese Yen (Rs. 299 crore approx.) was signed between the Government of India and
Japan International Cooperation Agency on 31
st March, 2003 for Ajanta Ellora Conservation and
Tourism Development Project - Phase II. The loan became effective from 31 st July, 2003. JICA has
extended the validity of loan period from July, 2011 to July, 2013 in September, 2011.
16. The Committee notes that out of the allocation of Rs. 25.65 crores, the Ministry
could spend only Rs. 20.78 crores. The Committee also notes that the Ajanta-Ellora project
has not yet completed as some major work on the part of ADI, MTDC and MJP are pending.
It is expected to be completed by July, 2013. The Committee emphasizes the need for proper
monitoring and coordination be ensured with all agencies concerned to complete the project
on time.
Assistance to IHMs/FCIs/IITM/NIWS
17. The Committee was informed that in XI Plan 17 new IHMs and 17 FCIs were sanctioned.
18. The Committee noted that Assistance to IHMs/FCIs/IITM etc. was Rs. 495.00 crores
as per Planning Commission outlays but the actual allocation was Rs. 387.70 crores only but
actual expenditure being Rs. 393.43 crores. Thus, there is over spending. The Committee,
however, is not convinced with the achievement in ratio of the expenditure. There is need
for more focussed approach and opening of more institutes for this purpose.
Capacity Building for Service Providers
19. Ministry of Tourism informed that skill testing and certification facilities at 14 IHMs were
provided and from 2009 to 2012, 21,483 service providers were certified and under ‘ Hunar se
Rozgar” Yojana 23,540 youths were trained .
20. The Committee notes the cuts made in the outlay for capacity building for service
providers scheme wherein the outlay stands at Rs.95.00 crores and the allocations are
Rs.89.00 crores. The actual expenditure of Rs. 67.79 crores shows that total allocations could
not be utilized by the end of the Plan period. This is disappointing mainly because Ministry
of Tourism is underutilizing the fund meant for skill development at a time when India is
faced with huge shortage of trained and skilled people in the tourism and hospitality
industry. We must have functional linkages with need of the industry and academic
institutions specially IHMS, IITM, CCI, NIWS, etc.
Overseas and Domestic Promotion
21. The Committee notes that extra allocation of Rs.145 crore was made for the
Overseas Promotion and Publicity including Market Development Assistance as the plan
outlay was only Rs.1010 crores but the total allocations stands at Rs.1155 crores. Compared
to other projects, the expenditure pace is also very prompt in this case as Rs.1024.00 crore
has been incurred upto 31
st December 2011, whereas in the case of Domestic Promotion and

5
Publicity including Hospitality there was a cut in allocations amounting to Rs.81 crores as
the allocation of Rs.345 crore was made against an outlay of Rs.426 crore. The expenditure
stands at Rs. 315.85 crore. The Committee notes that the expenditure on Domestic
Promotion has no such momentum in comparison to the expenditure pattern of the
Overseas promotion. The Ministry has admitted that 3/4
th of the economy in the tourism
sector is contributed by the domestic tourists and domestic tourists has witnessed a growth
of more than 10%.
22. The Committee, while appreciating efforts to tap tourism market overseas, the
growing domestic market should not get ignored. The Committee feels that there is a need
for being little careful in utilizing funds for this purpose as on overseas promotion an over
expenditure of Rs.145 crores in 2011-12, has been noticed. The Committee would like to
have a clarification in this regard.
Incentives to Accommodation Infrastructure
23. In the case of Incentives to Accommodation Infrastructure, the Committee noted
that  the total allocations have been considerably reduced, mere Rs.66 crore was allocated
out of Rs.235 crore outlay. The committee feels that during Eleventh Plan period the project
was not properly implemented by the Ministry as per the expenditure pattern and this late
hour wake up for more allocations could have been avoided. The Government has identified
that there is a shortage of 2,26,040 hotel rooms by 2016. In such a scenario underutilizing
the fund meant for creating accommodation infrastructure reflects that there is something
wrong. The Committee, therefore, recommends to revisit this programme during the Twelfth
Plan period so that more funds are allocated in the coming years and utilized it effectively.
Computerization and Information Technology
24. The spending pattern on the computerization and Information technology is also very
poor as the total expenditure stands at Rs.57.30 crores out of outlay of Rs 121.00 crores
and allocation of Rs 83.50 crores. In this case also the Committee noticed a reduction of
about 1/3 on the allocations in comparison to the total outlay. The Committee notes that
lack of fund is not a hindrance to technological advancement. What is lacking is the
required willpower to implement the programmes and utilize the money effectively. The
Committee recommends that the detailed review may be made to ascertain, in the age of
technological advancement where most of the tourist promotion are dependant on websites
why Ministry of Tourism is unable to tap the potential.
Market Research including 20 Years Perspective Plan
25. The Ministry has also informed that a number of research studies and surveys were
conducted as a part to develop more tourist destinations and circuits in various States to develop
tourism during the Eleventh Plan period. During phase-I, Punjab, Delhi, Jharkhand, Maharashtra and
Andhra Pradesh were targeted. Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Sikkim and
Chhattisgarh are currently being surveyed.
26. The total spending for Market Research including 20 years Perspective Plan
stands at Rs.14.49 crores, whereas the expenditure is Rs.19.50 crores. The Committee
notes that States like Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, West Bengal, Odisha,
J&K and Rajasthan which are having greater tourism potential were not covered. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the remaining States need to be included in the
next phase.

Assistance for Large Revenue Generating Projects
27. The total outlay under this head was Rs.200 crores and allocation was of Rs. 89.20 crores,
whereas the actual spending has been at Rs.26.57 crores only. The Committee noted that
Assistance to Large Revenue Generating Projects received a lukewarm response from private
investors. The Ministry has submitted that now it has  proposed to include projects such as hotels,
amusement parks, theme parks etc. within the ambit of the scheme.  In view of poor response
received for the Assistance to Large Revenue generating Projects as accepted by the
Ministry itself, the Committee recommends that the scheme be reviewed and redesign and
the new guidelines may be framed to attract more entrepreneurs to utilize the scheme.
Creation of Land bank for Hotels
28. The Ministry of Tourism has submitted that Creation of Land bank also could not be
implemented as no land was made available to Ministry of Tourism by various land owning
agencies.
29. The Committee notes that creation of Land Bank for Hotels could not pick up during
the Eleventh Plan period as the amount of Rs.12.02 crores actually allocated for the purpose
remained fully unutilized at the end of the Eleventh Plan. The Committee recommends that
this project may be reviewed so that amount, though symbolic, of Rs.1.00 crore for the year
2012-13 earmarked for the purpose is utilized.
Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism infrastructure development
30. The Ministry has informed that Assistance to central  agencies such as ASI,CPWD, ITDC,
Port Trusts etc. to undertake improvement of tourism infrastructure also has received very few
proposals.
31. The Committee observes that Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism
Infrastructure Development was provided extra allocations of Rs.7.01 crore over and above
the total outlay of Rs.25 crores, i.e. Rs.32.01 crore whereas the spending stands only at
Rs.20.51 crores. The Committee feels that this is the result of poor planning and
implementation of the project. The Committee also recommends that if there is any
bottlenecks at the administrative level for the scheme, it should be properly revisited and
necessary corrective measures need to be taken.

37. The Committee appreciates the optimism reflected in the Ministry’s higher targets
specifically for Foreign Tourist Arrival, Domestic Tourist Arrival, Foreign Exchange earnings
and employment generation from tourism during the 12
th Plan period. The Committee
recommends that Planning Commission may be persuaded not to reduce the allocation so
that the objectives of XII Plan are achieved.
38. As regards the imparting ‘pro-poor approach’ during 12 th Plan aimed at increasing net
benefit to the poor from tourism and poverty reduction, the Committee finds it better said
than done. It may be theoretically unquestionable but it is practically difficult to achieve.
We should always aim at something that is in the realm of possibilities and closer to ground
realities.
39. As for employment figures in tourism given above, the Committee would like to
know what kind of employment and for how long and with what income. We should avoid
making vague claims in respect of employment in tourism sector. The Committee would like
to have full facts about this data with necessary details.
40. The Committee is not aware of the mode of collection of data on employment in
tourism.  Regarding employment whether it is remunerative employment or otherwise is not
known to the Committee. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the method of
collection of data and on employment in tourism sector and the organisations involved in
it may be furnished to the Committee. The Committee may also be intimated about the
details of the sector-wise employment in tourism sector.

Cruise Tourism Potential
131. The Ministry of tourism informed that  cruise tourism is in its infancy in India. With the
Indian economy developing at a steady pace, middle class growing in number and possessing
increasing disposable incomes, Indians could also take on cruise tourism in a big way. India’s long
coast line and strong port positioning imparts a natural advantage to the country to attract
international cruise liners. As per research conducted by leading cruise industry associations, cruise
passengers are high spenders with the international average working out to $ 94 per cruise tourist
per port.
132. The Steering Committee of Ministry of Shipping, consisting of a representative of Ministry
of Tourism, constituted for addressing various issues pertaining to cruise shipping has identified
five ports to be developed-mormugao-Goa, Cochin, Mumbai Chennai and Mangalore. The need of
the hour is to start with a ‘hub-a home port’. Important considerations for a successful home port
include, suitable geographical location vis-à-vis other ports of visit, accessibility by air, a sizeable
local market and adequate port facilities and services. Mumbai is considered to be most apt for this
purpose as it has all the necessary features.
133. W aterways based tourism is well developed in various States like Kerala, Goa, Assam and
West Bengal. The important river cruises are in the Brahmaputra in Assam and in the Ganges on
the routes Kolkata-Varanasi and Kolkata Sundarbans. India has tourism destinations in its interiors,
many of which are connected by rivers. Developing river cruises that connect these destinations
would not only create the access that is so important for cruise tourism, but would also enable
India to strongly position its tourism destinations and its exotic heritage.
134. Ministry of Tourism provides funds to Major Ports for development of cruise tourism.
However, so far only Mormugao Port Trust has submitted project for financial assistance. The
Committee is of the view that the scheme need to be discussed with all the Ports and
appropriate initiative may be taken to implement the cruise tourism projects in all the Ports.
135. The Committee was also informed of the high taxation rates for Yatch, boats, ships which
are registered in India. This leads to people preferring to go to other countries. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the tax structure should be rationalized to attract more tourists
from abroad.
136. The Committee had the occasion to visit cruise terminals at some of the ports. The
Committee noted the absence of basic facilities and poor maintenance of the terminals. The
Committee recommends that to make cruise tourism success in India there is need to make
the terminals more accessible and attractive.

investors. The Ministry has submitted that now it has  proposed to include projects such as hotels,
amusement parks, theme parks etc. within the ambit of the scheme. In view of poor response
received for the Assistance to Large Revenue generating Projects as accepted by the
Ministry itself, the Committee recommends that the scheme be reviewed and redesign and
the new guidelines may be framed to attract more entrepreneurs to utilize the scheme.
(Para 27)
Creation of Land bank for Hotels

this project may be reviewed so that amount, though symbolic, of Rs.1.00 crore for the year
2012-13  earmarked for the purpose is utilized. (Para 29)
Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism infrastructure development
The Committee observes that Assistance to Central Agencies for Tourism
Infrastructure Development was provided extra allocations of Rs.7.01 crore over and above
the total outlay of Rs.25 crores, i.e. Rs.32.01 crore whereas the spending stands only at
Rs.20.51 crores. The Committee feels that this is the result of poor planning and

33
implementation of the project. The Committee also recommends that if there is any

Salient Features of the Twelfth Five Year Plan
The Committee appreciates the optimism reflected in the Ministry’s higher targets

 Plan period. The Committee
recommends that Planning Commission may be persuaded not to reduce the allocation so

As for employment figures in tourism given above, the Committee would like to

Budgetary Allocations for 2012-13

progressed as proposed. (Para 46)
Product/Infrastructure Development for Destination and Circuits
The Committee notes that RE allocation of Rs.582.99 crore for Product/Infrastructure
Development for Destination and Circuits was made in 2011-12. The allocation was optimally
utilized to Rs.567.23 crore upto February 2012 with the likelihood of remaining allocation
to be utilised by March, 2012. The Committee, however, is of the view that spending the
entire allocation should not be the only criteria of success of the scheme, it should also be
measured in terms of actual infrastructure it has established. (Para 57)
The Committee appreciates that State Level Monitoring Committees (SLMC) have
been set up to regularly review the progress of tourism infrastructure projects. The

34

For identifying tourist circuits and destinations, there is need to make the States

evenly throughout the country. (Para 59)
The Committee notes that the scheme covers a vast array of infrastructure for which
funds could be utilized such as cruise terminal, adventure facilities, waste/sewage

The Committee notes that religious tourism and pilgrimage are inherent part of

Development for Destination and Circuits. (Para 61)
The Committee came to know that the Durga Hatkati Temple situated at the Shimla

The Committee is of the view that the development of tourism clusters in rural areas
is a welcome initiative. The Indian traditional arts and crafts are now a days on the verge

Tourism Promotion and Publicity
The Committee notes there is a regular growth in the domestic as well as foreign

The Committee notes that the expenditure incurred upto February 2012 is Rs.194.26

commensurate with the return in the form of number of Foreign tourist arrivals i.e. 5.78
million in 2010. The Committee is surprised that with so many attractions-religious, tourist,
health etc. why we have not been able to attract enough tourists to India. The Ministry’s
effort should have to be to tap this potential. Here again, the Committee have doubts about
the overseas promotion campaigns. The Committee recommends that the Government must
have re-look into the necessity of various tourism promotion offices abroad and the efficacy
of various officials under Overseas Promotion and Publicity. (Para 79)
The Committee notes that one of the reasons for low tourists arrival could be lack

maintained. With focussed approach for cleanliness once our tourist destinations/
infrastructural facilities reaches the world standard more and more tourists will come.

The Committee comments the Government of Goa for the initiative to take up major

The Committee also recommends that alongwith aggressive marketing in this age of
technological advancement the our websites should be more attractive and interactive and
secure so that tourist can book from the site itself. This will gradually reduce the
expenditure on marketing and promotion. The Committee also recommends that redressal
mechanism should be made available in the website itself and reply should be given to the
persons concerned which will also help in the form of feedback from the visitors/tourists for
making tourism destinations in India more responsive. (Para 82)
The Committee notes that the number of Indians going abroad for tourism has been

The Committee notes that there are 33 Institutes of Hotel Management and 7 FCIs
which are conducting various courses of National Council for Hotel Management and

requirement of 47.26 lakhs employment  in 2012-13  is of the view that the IHMs and FCIs
may not be able to handle the demand.  The estimates provided by the Ministry of Tourism
states that the training capacity at the end of Eleventh Plan stands at 8342 intake for UG/

36

priority while selecting the places for opening such institutes. The Committee also
recommends that private sector participation wherever unavoidable, need to be regulated
and controlled suitably if they fail to achieve the requisite standard. (Para 95)
The Committee notes that for training manpower the Ministry of Tourism has huge

Committee therefore recommends that the Government should take steps to provide

The Committee notes that the grants are being utilized by very few universities. The

that wide publicity should be given to the scheme so that more Universities are sanctioned
the grant. (Para 97)
Accommodation Infrastructure
The Committee notes the step taken by the Ministry to encourage Budget hotels.
The Committee is of the view that to reduce this shortage some incentives have to be given

availability of hotel accommodation in the country. In this exercise, the State Governments/

Tax Structure
In the Working Group Report pertaining to Ministry of Tourism it was stated  that in

destinations are major tourism attractions for medium and low income tourists. However it is more

affordable. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the tax incentives should be as
pragmatic as to generate maximum income. At the same time, there should not be any

The Committee observes that the investment linked tax incentive for new hotels of

The Committee notes the steps taken for seamless travel which was initiated during

registration for tourist vehicles and one time/annual payment of taxes would facilitate smooth
movement of tourist traffic as it would do away with the requirement to stop for payment of taxes
at State borders. Ministry of Tourism recommended the implementation of a separate “TV” series
registration for tourist vehicles in the long run. The Committee also supports the view of the
Ministry to provide “TV” series registration for tourist vehicles and one time/annual
payment of taxes to be implemented by all the concerned agencies without any delay.
(Para 120)
Environmental Issues and Eco Tourism
The Committee recommends that the Committee constituted for the purpose should
finalize the report in time bound manner and make stringent laws to protect the wildlife
and protected areas. (Para 123)
The Committee also notes that a comprehensive waste management system is lacking in
most of the tourist destinations. This will become a serious problem in the coming years because
of the increase in the number of tourists. The Committee also notes that there is  scarcity of water
in almost all tourist places and specially in Hilly areas. This problem is very acute in places like
North Eastern Region, Coastal Region and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. The Committee during
its recent visits also got many complaints. The Committee recommends that the Ministry
should take up the matter at various levels with State and Municipalities to remove the
problem of  shortage of water and waste management. Eco-friendly toilets are the new
concept which may be explored. (Para 125)
The pollution caused by the plastic products need to be taken as a serious threat
to the environment and the locals and tourists should be made aware of the hazards it
may cause in the region. The Committee recommends that for tourists, carry-bags from

(Para 127)
Isolation of Island Region in Tourism Development
The Committee notes with dismay that the tourism potential in the Islands in India
is, in fact  more than the popular tourist destinations like Thailand, Singapore, Hawai etc.
However, the insufficient facilities have marred the scope of these amazing Islands. So far,
there is no such guide map prepared for benefiting the scenic beauty of these areas as a
major tourist attraction. The Committee, therefore, recommends that a special tourism
focus should be given to the Lakshadweep and Andaman and Nicobar Islands for tourism
promotion. (Para 129)
The Committee is, however, of the view that tourism activities should not be allowed
to be detrimental to the environmental balance. The Committee is of the view that there
should be strict adherence to the environmental regulations while promoting tourism. Our
environmentally fragile areas could not be blindly opened for access to the tourists; however
the regulations should not be applied uniformly in a country like India where the diversity
of geographical distribution is maximum. (Para 130)

The Committee was also informed of the high taxation rates for Yatch, boats, ships which

The Committee had the occasion to visit cruise terminals at some of the ports. The

The Committee also recommends that appropriate Rules and Regulations may be

equipments like life jackets, trained manpower etc. (Para 139)
The Committee during its visit also noted that there is lack of well maintained boats
and small cruises in A & N Islands. The Committee recommends that the boats should be

The Committee recommends that air connectivity to major small tourist places

The Committee also suggests that Ministry of Tourism should take initiatives with
