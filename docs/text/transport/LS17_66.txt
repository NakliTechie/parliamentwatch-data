REPORT
 
The Department of Tourism is the nodal agency for the formulation of national policies and programmes on
 Tourism and for the coordination of activities of various Central Government agencies, State Governments/UTs and the
 Private Sector for the development and promotion of tourism in the country.  The Department of Tourism plays a
 crucial role in coordinating and supplementing the efforts of the State/Union Territory Governments catalyzing
 investment, strengthening promotional and marketing efforts and private in providing training manpower resources.
 The main activities of the Department are: -
 
i)
                  Policy formulation and planning
ii)                Co-ordination
iii)               Regulation
iv)              Infrastructure and Product development
v)                Human Resource Development
vi)              Publicity, Marketing and Facilitation
vii)             Research, Analysis, Monitoring and Evaluation
viii)           International Co-operation and External Assistance
ix)              Parliament work
x)                Management of Public Sector Undertakings and Autonomous bodies
 
2.         The Department of Tourism is headed by a Secretary.  The post of Director General (Tourism) has been merged
 with the post of Secretary (T) who is assisted by the Joint Secretary and the Additional Director General. In addition to
 the Headquarters Organisation at New Delhi, the Directorate General of Tourism has 21 India tourism Offices in India
 (including 5 Regional Offices), situated at Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Agra, Bhubaneshwar, Aurangabad,
 Bangalore, Cochin, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Imphal, Jaipur, Khajuraho, Naharlagun, Panaji (Goa), Patna, Port Blair,
 Shillong, Varanasi and Thiruvananthapuram.  There are 13 India tourism Offices Overseas, which include 5 Regional
 offices at Sydney, New York, Tokyo, Frankfurt and Dubai.  The other India tourism Offices are located at Los Angeles,
 London, Toronto, Paris, Amsterdam, Milan, Singapore and Johannesburg.

9.         The Committee notes that for the financial year 2002-03, the expenditure upto January 2003 has been only 55%
 i.e. Rs.124.876 crores out of the total plan allocation of Rs.225 crores.  Though, the Department of Tourism have
 assured that the total allocation would be spent by the end of the financial year i.e. 31st March 2003 the Committee
 however, is unable to understand as to how the Department would be able to spend the remaining 45% of the plan
 allocation within a short span of just 2 months of the financial year.  The Committee therefore, disapproves the
 tendency of spending funds desperately towards the end of the financial year. The Committee recommends that the
 Department should evolve some mechanism of uniform utilization pattern through out the year so as to avoid incurring
 expenditure at the fag end of the financial year.
 
10.       The Department of Tourism informed that the annual plan 2003-04 aims to enhance the employment potential
 within the tourism sector as well as to foster economic integration through developing linkages with other sectors.  The
 thrust in the annual plan 2003-04 is to implement the policies outlined in the Tenth Plan, with the aim to position
 tourism as a major engine of economic growth and to harness its direct and multiplying effects for employment
 generation and poverty eradication in an environment- friendly sustainable manner.

 on tourism, the Department of Tourism in consultation with the Planning Commission has done zero based budgeting
 exercise and restructured following new schemes for implementation during the Tenth plan in the country viz.: -

3. Large revenue generating projects

INTEGRATED DEVELOPMENT OF TOURIST CIRCUITS

13.       A tourist circuit is defined as a route on which at least three major tourists destinations are located in such a
 manner that none of these are in the same town, village or city.  At the same time they are not ­­­­separated by a large
 distance  so that a tourist would not like to cover them in a sequence.  The scheme is basically a merger of all the
 ongoing schemes for infrastructure development and will be implemented further on six identified tourist circuit (North,
 South, East, West, Central and North East) each year and convergence of all resources and expertise for the
 development of these circuits as international standard destination.  The Department of Tourism would bear 100% of
 the project cost (capital cost only) based on the project plan and estimates. During the year 2002-03, the six circuits
 have been taken up for development as follows: -
 
a)
      Eastern Region – (Buddhist Circuit), Bodhgaya-Rajgir-Nalanda-Varanasi.
b)      Northern Region – (Himalayan Circuit), Route I – Chandigarh-Bilaspur-Kullu-Manali-Rohtang La-Keylong-
Sarchu-Upshi-Leh.
c)      Route II – Shimla-Sangla-Kaza-Chatru-Keylong-Sarhu-Yashi-Leh.
d)      Central Circuit – (Heritage, Nature & Wildlife Circuit), Gwalior-Shivpuri-Chanderi-Orcha-Khajuraho-Jhansi-
Bhopal-Sanchi and surrounding Buddhist areas-Bhimbetka-Panch marhi-Kanha Jabalpur (Bhedaghat).
e)      Western Circuit – (Konkan Riveria Circuit), Mumbai-Alibagh (Mandva) – Muradjanjira-Ganapatipule-
Vijayadurg-Mithibad-Kunkeshwar-Mochetmad-Sindhudurg-Tarkarli
-Shiroda-Savantwadi-Amboli-Goa-Coastal Karnataka-Bekal.
f)
        Southern Circuit – (Backwater and Beach Circuit), Cochin-Kumarakom – (Backwaters) – Kottayam-Quilon-
Thiruvananthapuram (Kovalam).
g)      North-East Circuit – (Eco-Tourism Circuit).
h)      Shillong-Guwahati-Kaziranga-Tezpur-Bhalakpung-Tawang (Arunachal Pradesh) – Majuli-Sibsagar-Kohima.
 
14.       The Secretary also informed that these tourist circuits have been identified by the Department of Tourism in
 consultation with the State Governments with the aim to provide all infrastructural facilities required by the tourists
 within these circuits and for convergence of resources and expertise through coordinated action with States/UT and
 private sector.  The master planning as well as implementation monitoring and reviewing of work is being directly
 carried out by the Department of Tourism.  The work on these circuits had started in the first year of the 10
th Plan and
 an amount of Rs.41.5 crores was allocated in the BE/RE 2002-03 for integrated development of tourists circuits.  It was
 proposed that all these work would spill over to the annual plan 2003-04.  Since the amount of Rs.8 crores was not
 sufficient for one circuit, an amount of Rs.60 crores has been allocated for integrated development of tourism circuits in
 B.E. 2003-04.  Besides more circuits, region-wise would be identified in consultation with the State Governments. 
 
15.       The Committee is happy to note that Department of Tourism is taking a holistic approach for development of
 these six tourist circuits in the country on each year and providing necessary infrastructural facilities within these
 circuits and convergence of resources in coordination with States/Private sector.  The Committee also notes that
 Department of Tourism has decided that in future funds will not be provided to small isolated projects, which are not of
 much importance for the development of tourism in the country and concentrate on the development of important travel
 circuits alone.  The Committee recommends that more tourist circuits region wise covering more than a State/UT may
 be selected and developed on the basis of the tourism potential of the places according to international standards and
 aggressive marketing efforts may be taken up in order to attract more and more tourists to these places.

ASSISTANCE FOR LARGE REVENUE GENERATING PROJECTS
 
25.       An amount of Rs.18 crores has been allocated under this scheme for the annual plan 2003-04.  The Committee
 enquired from the representatives of the Department of Tourism about the details of the scheme and whether any
 proposal from State Governments/UTs has been received.  The Secretary, Tourism informed the Committee that under
 the new Tourism Policy active participation of public sector and private sector has been sought for sustainable
 development and management of tourism product. A restructured scheme is being introduced, wherein support in the
 form of grant to act as a catalyst will be provided to large revenue generating projects through the State Tourism
 Development Corporations, Tourism Finance Corporation of India or the State Investment and Industrial Development
 Corporations and the State Financial Corporations.  This assistance in turn can be utilized by these corporations as
 equity in projects to be promoted by them or jointly with private sector.  Projects like convention centers, luxury trains,
 cruise terminals, ropeways, cruise vessels etc. could be taken up under this scheme.  The projects shall be identified in
 consultation with the State.  The assistance for Department of Tourism shall be limited to 25% of the equity.  The
 Secretary informed that proposals are awaited under the scheme.
 
26.
                          The Committee notes that sufficient funds have been earmarked and support in the form of grants to act as a
 catalyst has also been provided to large revenue generating projects like convention centers, luxury trains, cruise
 terminals, ropeways, cruise vessels. The Committee however, couldn’t understand as to how the scheme has been
 finalized when the proposals under the scheme are yet to be received by the department of tourism. It shows that the

file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/66threport.htm[28-08-2018 13:01:22]
 Department is lacking pro-active approach in the matter, which could result in non-utilisation of plan outlay earmarked
 for the scheme. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department of Tourism should take expeditious steps to
 activate the State Governments so that the States send their proposals under the scheme quickly to ensure that plan
 outlay allocated be utilized by the State Tourism Development Corporation or any such public body or institution as
 equity to projects to be promoted by them or in private sector projects.
 
CAPACITY BUILDING FOR SERVICE PROVIDERS
 
27.       The Department of Tourism has informed that the existing Food Craft Institute, Institute of Hotel Management
 and the institutes in the private sector cater mainly to the organized sector. The Department of Tourism, Government of
 India plans to train the persons in an unorganized sector consisting of small hotels, dhabas, restaurants and other eatable
 joints spread all over the country.  Sensitising Government staffs, which have interaction with tourists and training of
 tourist guides, have been accorded priority.  Setting up of mobile training facilities has been taken up to increase the
 reach of the training Institutes.
 
28.       The Planning Commission in August 2002 approved the Scheme of Capacity Building for Service Providers. 
 The Department has already formulated the Guidelines for the scheme and the same was circulated to the Institute of
 Hotel Managements/Food Craft Institute/Indian Institute of Tourism & Travel Management/Indiatourism offices in the
 country and National Institute of Water Sports in January 2003.  As a first step, the Department has released funds
 under the scheme to the IHMs to enable the Institutes to implement the scheme to train persons in the unorganized
 tourism service provider sector as per the guidelines formulated by the Department of Tourism as an ongoing activity.
 
29.       The Committee appreciates the scheme launched by the Department of Tourism for training the persons in an
 unorganized sector consisting of small hotel, dhabas and restaurant and other eatable joints spread all over the country
 including tourists guides. The Committee is of the opinion since tourism industry plays a nodal role in displaying the
 rich cultural heritage to the world at large, and possesses high employment potential, personnel engaged in this sector
 are different from those required by personnel engaged in other sectors.  The Committee hopes that this would
 adequately cater to the training needs of the persons engaged in the unorganized sector and helps them is upgrading
 their quality.  The Committee desires that sufficient funds should be allocated under this head and efforts should be
 made to make the scheme more effective by expanding its coverage to the important tourist circuits/tourist destinations.
 
OVERSEAS PROMOTION AND MARKETING
 
30.       An amount of Rs.56 crores has been allocated for Overseas Promotion including Market Development
 Assistance for the financial year 2003-04.  The Committee desired to know from the Department about the new
 campaigns and publicity plans, which are proposed to be included during the year with this, increased funds.
 
31.       The Department of Tourism has informed the Committee that the scheme for overseas promotion and publicity
 has been restructured taking into account the changing global scenario and the fierce competition in the tourism
 generating markets.  In the Annual Plan 2003-2004, new initiative for promoting and marketing India overseas have
 been initiated which focused more on cultivating and facilitating various components like electronic (and print) media,
 joint sales efforts with the international airlines operating to India and direct co-operative marketing with the tour
 operators and wholesalers overseas.  There is also a plan to attach greater focus in our efforts to the region of China,
 North East Asia and South East Asia.  A new initiative has been taken to provide marketing development assistance
 support to service providers in the travel, transport and hospitality industry with a view to get larger number of overseas
 visitors through holding of Road Shows, etc.  Apart from the activities in the past, the overseas marketing would also
 focus on IT based marketing and dissemination of information, marketing partnerships, gathering intelligence,
 consumer oriented advertising and joint regional promotion etc. Attention of the foreign tourists would also be drawn to
 the improvements being carried out in new destinations. The plan also envisages a major shift from transitional
 marketing to destination branding with consistent and integrated marketing communication strategy and to create strong
 global brand (Incredible India) with worldwide recognition.

The Committee is happy to note that the plan allocation of Rs.225.00 crores of the Department of Tourism was not

 unable to understand as to how the Department would be able to spend the remaining 45% of the plan allocation within
 a short span of just 2 months of the financial year.  The Committee therefore, disapproves the tendency of spending
 funds desperately towards the end of the financial year. The Committee recommends that the Department should evolve
 some mechanism of uniform utilization pattern through out the year so as to avoid incurring expenditure at the fag end
 of the financial year.

RURAL TOURISM
 
The Rural Tourism is a new market concept to tap tourist traffic as it can form the perfect vehicle for achievement of the
 national objective of achieving partnerships between local communities, government and industry.  Besides, it would
 help generate employment at local level, dispensing with the need of migrations to distant places for earning livelihood
 thereby leading to economic betterment. The Rural Tourism can also provide a solution to the increasing trends of
 industrialization and help in countering the stress of urban life styles and other urbanization syndromes. The Committee
 feels that global tourism growth potential can be harnessed as a strategy for Rural Development and therefore,
 recommends that the Department of Tourism should develop a strong platform around the concept of Rural Tourism for
 achieving the national objectives in better perspective.
(Para 24)
 
ASSISTANCE FOR LARGE REVENUE GENERATING PROJECTS
 
The Committee notes that sufficient funds have been earmarked and support in the form of grants to act as a catalyst has
 also been provided to large revenue generating projects like convention centers, luxury trains, cruise terminals,
 ropeways, cruise vessels. The Committee however, couldn’t understand as to how the scheme has been finalized when
 the proposals under the scheme are yet to be received by the department of tourism. It shows that the Department is
 lacking pro-active  approach in the matter, which could result in non-utilisation of plan outlay earmarked for the
 scheme. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department of Tourism should take expeditious steps to
 activate the State Governments so that the States send their proposals under the scheme quickly to ensure that plan
 outlay allocated be utilized by the State Tourism Development Corporation or any such public body or institution as
 equity to projects to be promoted by them or in private sector projects.

TOURISM AND ITS IMPACT ON INDIA’S GROSS DOMESTIC PRODUCT
 
The Committee is of the opinion that domestic tourism has to act as a pillar of our future tourism policy besides as a
 major engine for economic growth of the country and there is a need to provide strong support to this sector in the form
 of large infrastructure.  The Committee is not satisfied with the efforts of the Department of Tourism due to which
 India’s share of global international tourism remained as relatively small in volume i.e. 0.38% throughout for the last
 five years.  The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should actively coordinate efforts and initiate
 steps in raising funds, liaising with other Ministries/Department of the Govt. of India and other concern International
 Organisation/Agencies for the growth of global international tourism in future, as tourism plays a major role in
 promoting large-scale employment opportunity than any other sector for every rupee invested.
(Para 40)
 
COORDINATION BETWEEN DEPARTMENT OF TOURISM AND OTHER AGENCIES
 
(a)        Coordination Between Department of Tourism and Ministry of Civil Aviation
 
The Committee is happy to note that Ministry of Civil Aviation and Department of Tourism are working in close
 coordination with each other for the development of tourists sector in the country as per the recommendation of the
 Committee.  However, the Committee strongly feels that there exists a dire necessity to increase air capacity to India
 during peak travel months ranging from October to March and especially from major tourism originating destination
 like North America, Western Europe and South East Asia.   The additional seat capacity from the major destinations
 would provide impetus to tourism and have a positive impact on economic development of India. 
(Para 45)
(b)        Coordination Between Department of Tourism and Ministry of Railways
 
The Committee is of the opinion that the potential of domestic tourism has grown substantially during the last few years
 due to increase in income level and emergence of dynamic urban middle class.  The importance and magnitude of
 domestic tourism could better be addressed by promoting amenities and facilities for budget tourists.  The Committee
 recommends that the Department of Tourism should take up with Ministry of Railways for equipping the Railway
 service to meet the growing travel requirements of domestic and foreign tourists. Besides, better hotel accommodation
 should be provided nearer to railway stations, airports etc. such as to cater to the requirements of the NRI’s as the
 existing ones are not up to the standard of NRI’s/foreign tourists.  The Ministry of Railway may be persuaded to
 introduce ‘Special Tourist Trains’ on the pattern of Rajdhani/Shatabdi trains with fewer compartments to run on
 experimental basis on the Buddhist Circuit, which is proposed to be developed soon. For this purpose possibility of
 getting foreign assistance and Private Sector participation should also be fully explored.  Simultaneously, the
 Department of Tourism must be proactive in their approach for providing the sanitation and public convenience
 facilities at the important tourist destinations.  This may be done with the involvement of State Government/Local
 bodies/Municipal Corporation. The Committee notes the reply of the Department of Tourism on the recommendation of
 the Committee contained at Para 60 of the 55
th report which says that: -
 
"The recommendation of the Standing Committee and the provision of luggage carrying trolleys at the

 station, which are of tourism importance has been noted by the Ministry of Railways, Railway Board."
 
The Committee is not satisfied with the lackadaisical attitude of the Department of Tourism in taking up the matter with
 Ministry of Railways.   The Committee should be informed about the actual action taken by the Ministry of Railways in
 this regard and in this connection Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation contained in 55th Report, which is
 reproduced below;
 
 “The committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should take up with the Ministry of Railways
 for providing some alternative method of carrying baggage. One of the solutions could be by providing
 trolleys of the kind that are available at the airports. These trolleys could be made available to the tourists
 against payment as in any case the tourists will have to pay the porterage charges to the porters.”
(Paras 50 and 51)
 
(c)        Coordination Between Department of Tourism and State Governments 
           
The Committee notes with dismay that during the 9th Five Year Plan, the Department of Tourism sanctioned 1563
 projects involving an amount of Rs.372.43 crores to the State Government/UTs for tourism projects out of which only
 571 projects have been completed and 992 projects remained incomplete. Non-completion of projects not only led to
 cost over runs of the project but also the tourists potential in the country could not be exploited fully in the absence of
 the facilities which could have been made available otherwise.  The Committee is unable to understand the reason put
 forth by the Department of Tourism for delay i.e. lack of coordination between the State Government and different
 other Ministries in the implementation of projects, besides low level of monitoring and review of progress.  The
 Committee is of the opinion that involvement of Members of Parliament in identification, development and monitoring
 of tourist destinations in their respective state, can avoid delay and cost overrun in execution of tourism projects. In this
 connection, the committee reiterates its earlier recommendation made in 55th Report, which is reproduced below;
 
“The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should involve the Members of Parliament in the
 identification and development of places of tourist interest in their respective states.  The Committee is of the view that,
 as elected representatives of the people, Members of Parliament can also help the Department in identifying tourism
 sports in their states and inform the Department of Tourism which can take steps to develop such tourism areas.  The
 Committee also recommends that the Department of Tourism should, by way of taking the initiative, request the
 Members of Parliament to identify places, which can be developed as tourist spots.  The Members of Parliament should
 also be kept informed of the status of the tourism projects in their respective states.”
 
The Committee therefore recommends that Department of Tourism should directly involve the Member of Parliament in
 identification, development and monitoring process of tourists destinations/circuits in their respective states and have a
 task force which will coordinate between different Ministries as well as State Governments and respective Members of
 Parliament in that state for monitoring the progress of physical targets every quarter or so.
(Paras 55 and 56)
 
TOURISM DEVELOPMENT IN NORTH EASTERN REGION
 
The Committee observes that Department of Tourism couldn’t utilise the funds allocated for the North-East region
 during 2002-03 for the development and promotion of tourism in the region. The Committee notes that a budgetary
 allocation of Rs.32.5 crores has been made during 2003-04 for various projects to be undertaken in the North Eastern
 Region including Sikkim. Despite incurring a lot of expenditure for development of tourism in North Eastern Region no
 perceptible improvement has been observed on the ground and a lot more has to be done to fully explore the tourism
 potential of this region. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the tourism related developmental work undertaken
 in the North East region should be given the highest priority and recommends that the funds allocated for the North
 East region should be optimally utilised in that region only. 
(Para 59)
