R E P O R T
            The Ministry of Civil Aviation is responsible for the formulation and implementation of national policies and
 programmes in the civil aviation sector.  The Ministry oversees the development and regulation of civil aviation in the country
 including, interalia, establishment of new aerodromes, maintenance and upgradation of the existing aerodromes, regulation of

 railway accidents, also constitutes one of the allied functions of the Ministry.
 
2.         The civil aviation sector is monitored and regulated by two separate organisations under the Ministry of Civil Aviation
 i.e., Directorate General of Civil Aviation and Bureau of Civil Aviation Security.  The functions pertaining to Railway Safety
 are entrusted to a Commission.  The Ministry of Civil Aviation has the following Public Sector
 Undertakings/Companies/Autonomous Bodies under its administrative control:-

9.         According to the 10 th Five-Year Plan, the civil aviation sector has to play an important role in India's economy.  This
 sector will play a more significant role in integrating the Indian economy with the rest of the world.  But the importance
 attached to the civil aviation sector is not reflected in the outlays approved for the first two years of the 10 th Plan period of the
 Ministry of Civil Aviation.   The annual plan outlay of the Ministry of Civil Aviation was reduced from Rs.1779.18 crore for
 the year 2003-2004 to Rs.1469.77 crore at RE stage.  The reduction had been both in the Internal and Extra Budgetary
 Resources (IEBR) component as well as the Gross Budgetary support.  Now an outlay of Rs.1620.98 crore has been approved
 for the year 2004-05.  Thus an amount of Rs.3090.75 crore has been approved for the first two years of the 10 th Plan period. 
 Hence, only 23.9 per cent of the allocation has been approved for the first two years of the 10 th Plan which is fairly short of the
 proportionate 40% allocation which becomes due for this period.  The Committee recommends that keeping in view the
 importance of the civil aviation sector in the contemporary era, the Government should increase the outlay of Ministry of Civil
 Aviation and, if need be, supplement the allocation by substantially increasing the Gross Budgetary Support to the Ministry.

18.        The Committee observed that the fleet expansion programme of Indian Airlines and Air India had been pending for
 quite a long time.  The Committee, therefore, enquired about the present status of the programme of acquisition of aircraft by
 Air India/Indian Airlines.
 
19.        In reply, the Committee was informed that Air India had acquired the last aircraft in the year 1996.  Since then no
 proposal for acquisition of aircraft was made to the Ministry by Air India and the fleet was augmented by way of leasing only. 
 Only in January 2004, Air India   submitted a Project Report for acquisition of 10 A340-300 aircraft and 18 B737-800 aircraft
 to the Ministry of Civil Aviation.  The proposal is under examination of Government in accordance with the investment
 procedures for PSUs.  As regards Indian Airlines, the Committee was informed that a proposal was received from Indian
 Airlines for acquisition of 43 aircraft comprising A319, A320, and A321 from M/s. Airbus Industries during the period 2003-04
 to 2007-08. The tentative cost of the project approximately is Rs. 10,089 crore. The new aircraft were envisaged to replace the
 entire fleet of A-300 and     B-737 aircraft, in a phased manner. The final decision on the proposal could not be taken as Indian
 Airlines was on disinvestment list till April 2003. 
 
20.        The Committee has, during the last 3-4 years, repeatedly emphasized in its reports on Demands for Grants of the
 Ministry of Civil Aviation that there is a need for expansion of the fleet and replacement of the aged aircraft of Indian Airlines. 
 The Committee feels that the fleet acquisition programme of the national carriers i.e., both Indian Airlines and Air India, has
 been ignored for long, as a result of which the two companies have suffered both financially as well as commercially.    Delay
 in fleet acquisition forced Air India to sell the bilateral agreements to foreign airlines as a result of which Air India lost its
 market share which has gone down to 20% which was at one time at 30%. The Committee is of the opinion that atmosphere of
 uncertainty prevailed due to confusion in the Government.   The fleet acquisition programme of the two airlines could not
 materialize due to the prevailing indecisiveness with regard to the management of the companies.  Firstly, the two companies
 were placed in the list of PSUs proposed to be disinvested and then later, their names were deleted from the said list.  The
 Committee noted the fact that now the Government has decided not to go in for their disinvestment.  The Committee is of the
 view that the fleet acquisition was the basic requirement for running the two companies commercially.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that the proposals of the Indian Airlines and Air India for acquisition of aircraft should be approved
 without any further delay.  In this regard, the Committee would also like to refer to its recommendations contained in 58
th
 Report (Para 39) and 69 th Report (Para 31) of this Committee on the Demands for Grants regarding fleet acquisition by Air
 India and Indian Airlines.
INFUSION OF EQUITY IN INDIAN AIRLINES AND AIR INDIA
 
21.        Indian Airlines has a paid up capital of Rs.105.19 crore against an authorized capital of Rs.500 crore.  Similarly, Air
 India has a paid up capital of Rs.153.84 crore and an authorized capital of Rs.500 crore.  This low equity base has resulted in

ON NAMAH SHIVAY

 curtailing their borrowing ability.  The Committee enquired from the Ministry about the plan of infusion of equity in Indian
 Airlines and Air India.  The Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation, during the course of oral evidence, deposed before the
 Committee that in order to bring our national carriers to a financially viable position there is a strong need for equity infusion.  
 The Committee wanted to know that if, at any stage, was there any attempt on their part to approach the Ministry of Finance
 requesting it for capital infusion for overcoming the adverse debt-equity ratio.  The Committee was informed that as far as Air
 India is concerned, Ministry of Civil Aviation did not make any proposal in the last few years.  However, in case of Indian
 Airlines, the Ministry has been pursuing the matter with the Ministry of Finance.   Further the Committee was informed that the
 Disinvestment Commission and the Kelkar Committee have recommended that the Government should consider Rs.1,000 crore
 capital infusion and increase the paid up capital both for Air India and the Indian Airlines.  Since the Air India had acquired its
 fleet long ago, and it did not come forward for expansion, the matter of Air India's equity increase was not pursued by the
 Ministry of Civil Aviation.  But, at the same time, in the case of Indian Airlines, the recommendation of the Kelkar Committee
 was two fold.  One is to increase the equity and to compensate for grounding of A-320s.  The Kelkar Committee has
 recommended for Rs.485 crore to be given to Indian Airlines.  The Planning Commission subsequently examined this and the
 amount has been pruned to Rs.325 crore.  But, at that time, the Cabinet decided that Rs.325 crore would come as additional
 equity but only when the Indian Airlines go in for acquisition of new aircraft.  After that, Indian Airlines's acquisition was
 deferred mainly because of various factors like disinvestment proposal of this airline in the pipeline.  However, it was cleared in
 the year 2002 for acquisition of new aircraft.  Accordingly, the Indian Airlines has submitted a proposal alongwith a request for
 infusion of capital. 
 
22.        The Committee notes that the share, which should have come from the Government, is not coming forth but only a
 small part of it has been infused as equity.  The Committee is sorry to note that there was no proposal from the Ministry for
 equity infusion in Air India and whatever efforts were made for equity infusion in Indian Airlines did not yield any result.  The
 Committee also notes that the Government has made a commitment to contribute to the equity of Indian Airlines.  The
 Committee is of the opinion that a similar dispensation would be necessary for Air India as well.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the equity base of both Air India and Indian Airlines should be enhanced at the earliest to enable them to
 achieve a healthy debt-equity ratio and also enable them to go in for borrowings to meet its requirement of funds. 
 
HIGH COST OF AIR TURBINE FUEL (ATF)
 
23.        During the course of the evidence of the representatives of Ministry of Civil Aviation, the Committee was informed that
 the domestic air transport industry in India has been facing serious problems due to high cost of operations. One of the major
 reasons for this is the heavy cost of Air Turbine Fuel (ATF).  Even though the pricing of ATF is now on import parity basis, the
 rates applicable for domestic operations continue to be significantly higher than that of international operations.  The cost of
 ATF goes up even further due to high rates of sales tax levied by the State Governments/Union Territory administration varying
 from 20 per cent to 36 per cent which affects the viability of domestic airline operations. 
 
24.        It was brought to the notice of the Committee that in March 2000, the cost of ATF was Rs.15,000 per Kl. and in August
 2004, the cost of ATF has gone upto Rs.27,000 per Kl.  Besides Central Excise, Base Price and Sales Tax on ATF is also paid. 
 It was also brought out that in developing countries and even in neighbouring South-East Asian countries and Gulf, the aviation
 fuel accounts for nearly 15% of the operating cost whereas in our country, it was costing nearly 31% of the total operating cost. 
 The representatives of Air India submitted that as per an international agreement, aircraft belonging to a foreign airline
 operating international service to, from and through India are exempted from payment of all custom duties, local duties, taxes,
 etc. on ATF and other items.  However, similar concessions were not available to Air India in India.  Hence, they were at a
 disadvantageous position vis-a-vis other foreign airlines operating to/from India. 
25.        The Committee had also taken up the issue relating to rationalization of sales tax on ATF in its 69
th Report on Demands
 for Grants 2003-04 of the Ministry of Civil Aviation and had recommended that  Ministry of Civil Aviation should impress
 upon the State Governments to reduce the sales tax on ATF to the minimum level.  In response the Committee was informed
 that the then Minister of Civil Aviation had written to the Chief Minister of State Governments requesting to consider
 appropriate reduction in the rate of sales tax on ATF in their respective states.  However, no favourable response has been
 received.  Some state governments have indicated that they can consider reduction provided they are compensated by the
 Central Government for the loss.
 
26.        The Committee feels that high cost of ATF for air transport in the country increases the cost of operation and makes it
 unviable even in areas where it has comparative advantage over other modes of transport.  The high cost of ATF has been
 adversely affecting the cost structure and profitability of our national carriers.  The issue relating to rationalization of sales tax
 on ATF has been lingering on for quite a long time.  Earlier during the course of examination of the Aircraft (Exemption from
 Taxes and Duties on Fuel and Lubricants) Bill, 2000, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Civil Aviation in

ON NAMAH SHIVAY
file:///C/Users/user/Desktop/pri/Committee%20on%20Transport,%20Tourism%20and%20Culture/78threport.htm[28-08-2018 13:07:30]
 November 2001 that a separate proposal was being considered to exempt Indian carriers going abroad from exemption of sales
 tax by declaring upliftment of ATF as deemed exports.  So far there has been no positive outcome of the proposal of the
 Government to exempt Indian carriers going abroad from exemption of sales tax. The Committee reiterates its recommendation
 contained in its 53 rd Report on the Bill and recommends that the proposal be implemented at the earliest.  The Committee
 further recommends that the Government should take immediate steps to exempt the Indian carriers from payment of taxes on
 ATF levied by the Central/State Governments.  The Committee strongly feels that this would certainly solve one of the long
 pending demands of the Indian Airlines and Air India and help them to overcome the adverse effects of the high cost of ATF on
 their cost structure and profitability.
 
INDIRA GANDHI RASHTRIYA URAN AKADEMI (IGRUA)
 
27.        The Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi (IGRUA) is an autonomous body under the control of the Ministry of Civil
 Aviation.  The objective of the Akademi is to conduct airline oriented flying training courses to the level of contemporary
 international standards.  The Governing Council is the supreme body of the organisation and the Secretary, Ministry of Civil
 Aviation is its Chairman.  The Akademi is headed by a Director who is assisted by Departmental Heads, such as, Chief Flying
 Instructor, Chief Ground Instructor, Chief Engineer and Chief Administrative Officer.  The Akademi is equipped with the most
 modern and sophisticated trainer aircraft, up-to-date audiovisual training aids and other facilities for effective ground training. 
 It also has highly qualified flying and ground instructors, with long experience in the field of aviation and flying training.  The
 aim is to reduce the workload of pilot to make him an effective systems manager.

ADVERSE IMPACT OF THE UNION BUDGET 2004-05 ON THE CIVIL AVIATION SECTOR
 
62.        The Committee enquired from the Ministry of Civil Aviation regarding the impact of Union Budget 2004-05 on the civil
 aviation sector in the country.  In reply, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Civil Aviation that the budget
 announcement to do away with the power to exempt the rentals for lease of aircraft and other equipment from withholding tax
 will result in additional outgo of funds from the PSUs to the lessors.   If the PSUs cannot get exemption from payment of
 withholding tax either the lessors will increase the lease rentals so as to compensate for the tax component or insist upon the
 lessee carriers to absorbing the tax component.  Both the PSUs (Indian Airlines and Air India), many existing private domestic
 scheduled operators and those who have filed applications for starting scheduled operations have to import aircraft by way of
 leasing since the purchase option takes a long time.  It is particularly so in the case of  Indian Airlines and Air India  because,
 the process of approval also has to go through various stages of examination and approval by the Government.  It is assessed
 that the additional outgo on account of lease rental could be anywhere between 10.5% to 48% of the lease cost. 
 Representations have been made to the Ministry of Finance by both the scheduled operators and the Ministry of Civil Aviation
 to restore the power to exempt withholding tax on the lease rentals.
 
63.        The Committee agrees with the view expressed by the Ministry that the provision in Union Budget 2004-2005 to do
 away with the power to exempt the rentals for lease of aircraft and other equipment from withholding tax will have an adverse
 impact on the civil aviation sector in the country particularly when the government intends to increase private sector
 participation in the provision of infrastructure facilities as well as air services.  The Committee feels that this will also have
 uneven impact on our national carriers vis-a-vis other private airlines as it would affect all other airlines which are planning
 acquisition on dry leasing.  However, the airlines, which have completed the phase of its acquisition through this route, will not
 be affected much.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that this power of the Ministry to exempt withholding tax
 on lease rentals should be restored.

AT A GLANCE
 
OUTLAY FOR 2004-2005 VIS-A-VIS TENTH PLAN ALLOCATION
 
According to the 10
th Five-Year Plan, the civil aviation sector has to play an important role in India's economy.  This
 sector will play a more significant role in integrating the Indian economy with the rest of the world.  But the importance
 attached to the civil aviation sector is not reflected in the outlays approved for the first two years of the 10 th Plan period of the
 Ministry of Civil Aviation.   The annual plan outlay of the Ministry of Civil Aviation was reduced from Rs.1779.18 crore for
 the year 2003-2004 to Rs.1469.77 crore at RE stage.  The reduction had been both in the Internal and Extra Budgetary
 Resources (IEBR) component as well as the Gross Budgetary support.  Now an outlay of Rs.1620.98 crore has been approved
 for the year 2004-05.  Thus an amount of Rs.3090.75 crore has been approved for the first two years of the 10
th Plan period. 
 Hence, only 23.9 per cent of the allocation has been approved for the first two years of the 10 th Plan which is fairly short of the
 proportionate 40% allocation which becomes due for this period.  The Committee recommends that keeping in view the
 importance of the civil aviation sector in the contemporary era, the Government should increase the outlay of Ministry of Civil
 Aviation and, if need be, supplement the allocation by substantially increasing the Gross Budgetary Support to the Ministry.

HIGH COST OF AIR TURBINE FUEL (ATF)
 
The Committee feels that high cost of ATF for air transport in the country increases the cost of operation and makes it
 unviable even in areas where it has comparative advantage over other modes of transport.  The high cost of ATF has been
 adversely affecting the cost structure and profitability of our national carriers.  The issue relating to rationalization of sales tax
 on ATF has been lingering on for quite a long time.  Earlier during the course of examination of the Aircraft (Exemption from
 Taxes and Duties on Fuel and Lubricants) Bill, 2000, the Committee was informed by the Ministry of Civil Aviation in
 November 2001 that a separate proposal was being considered to exempt Indian carriers going abroad from exemption of sales
 tax by declaring upliftment of ATF as deemed exports.  So far there has been no positive outcome of the proposal of the
 Government to exempt Indian carriers going abroad from exemption of sales tax. The Committee reiterates its recommendation
 contained in its 53
rd Report on the Bill and recommends that the proposal be implemented at the earliest.  The Committee
 further recommends that the Government should take immediate steps to exempt the Indian carriers from payment of taxes on
 ATF levied by the Central/State Governments.  The Committee strongly feels that this would certainly solve one of the long
 pending demands of the Indian Airlines and Air India and help them to overcome the adverse effects of the high cost of ATF on
 their cost structure and profitability.

PAWAN HANS HELICOPTERS LIMITED
 
The Committee is satisfied with the reasons furnished by the Ministry of Civil Aviation for delay in the execution of the
 project relating to Creation of Maintenance Centre and the alternate plan of the Ministry of Civil Aviation for construction of
 the building with hanger on a reduced scale for shifting the existing workshop facilities.  The Committee recommends that after

 plan allocations made for the project in the Budget Estimate 2004-2005 are not underutilized.
(Para 53)
 
GROWTH OF CIVIL AVIATION SECTOR IN THE NORTH-EAST REGION
 
The Committee feels that like Indian Airlines, private airlines should also fulfil their social commitments and should not
 prefer to operate mostly on lucrative routes and complete the formality of fulfilling their social obligation by flying to the
 prescribed minimum flights to the North-East region under the Route Dispersal Guidelines.     The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Ministry of Civil Aviation should evolve some mechanism to ensure that private airlines in the country
 fulfil their social obligation by operating more number of flights in the North-East region.  This would help solve the problems
 of air connectivity faced by the people of North-East region.  The Committee notices that Route Dispersal Guidelines have been
 manipulated by the private airlines to the disadvantage of the backward regions.   The Committee is, therefore, strongly of the
 view that the Route Dispersal Guidelines should be reviewed with a view to making them full proof so that no private airline is
 able to violate them and any such violation should entail heavy disincentive to that airline.
             (Para 61)
 
ADVERSE IMPACT OF THE UNION BUDGET 2004-05 ON THE CIVIL AVIATION SECTOR
 
The Committee agrees with the view expressed by the Ministry that the provision in Union Budget 2004-2005 to do
 away with the power to exempt the rentals for lease of aircraft and other equipment from withholding tax will have an adverse
 impact on the civil aviation sector in the country particularly when the government intends to increase private sector
 participation in the provision of infrastructure facilities as well as air services.  The Committee feels that this will also have
 uneven impact on our national carriers vis-a-vis other private airlines as it would affect all other airlines which are planning
 acquisition on dry leasing.  However, the airlines, which have completed the phase of its acquisition through this route, will not
 be affected much.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that this power of the Ministry to exempt withholding tax
 on lease rentals should be restored. 
(Para 63)

The Committee notes that the 10th Five-Year Plan stresses the need to have a regulatory framework in place considering
 the present size of the market, the need for monitoring quality of services and the provisions of air services for meeting social
 obligations.  However, it seems that the Government has not initiated any steps in this direction.  The Committee is of the view
 that the burden of service tax levied by the government is likely to be passed on to the users by the different airlines, which may
 be in the form of hike in passenger fares.  Presently, there is no agency to monitor as to whether the additional liability imposed
 on the airlines will be proportionately passed on to the passengers or the airlines would increase the charges at their own
 discretion in the name of levying the service tax.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be some monitoring
 body to regulate the fare structure and to ensure that the fare hike passed on to the passengers due to imposition of service tax is
 just and fair.
(Para 65)
 
The Committee wanted to know from the Ministry whether the Airport Tax has been abolished by the Government and
 if there is any plan of abolishing the Airport Tax and also what would be its impact on the civil aviation sector, particularly the
 Airports Authority of India?  The reply to this query is still awaited from the Ministry.  The Committee would like the Ministry
 to furnish a detailed note on the impact of abolition of Airport Tax on the civil aviation sector.
(Para 66)
