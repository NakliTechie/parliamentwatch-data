Recommendation/Observation
5. The Committee observes that the 12th Five Year Plan outlay for tourism development in the country
was ` 22800 crore. Out of it, the major chunk of allocation of ` 9450 crore was for development of
destinations and circuits. From the data provided by the Ministry of Tourism, the total allocations made all
these five years was only ` 5479.03 crore out of which the spending up to 31 st January, 2017 is ` 4644.89
crore. This comes to 20.37% of the 12th Plan outlay, whereas the total allocation itself was very insufficient
as compared to the outlay, which was only 24%. The Committee is very disappointed to note these figures
and desires to know the details of the reasons why such huge difference occurred in allocations to the tourism
sector during 12th Five Year Plan in the country.

9. The achievements of the Ministry of Tourism vis-à-vis the targets set for the tourism sector in the

I. Foreign Tourist Arrivals/Domestic Tourism/Foreign Exchange Earnings from Tourism/
Employment Generation
(a) The share of India’s Foreign Tourist Arrivals (FTAs) in International Tourist Arrivals in the
world in 2016 was 0.7%. However, the International Tourist Arrivals comprises of 2 components
namely FTAs and Arrivals of Nationals Residing Abroad. Recently, Ministry of Tourism has
started collecting the data of Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) Arrivals. The number of International
Tourist Arrivals (FTAs + NRI Arrivals) during 2016 was 14.57 million. Accordingly, share of
India in International Tourist Arrivals of the world has also improved to 1.18% in 2016.
(b) The Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of the number of Domestic Tourist Visits (DTVs)
to different States/Union Territories (UTs) in India from 2011 to 2016 was 13.29% which is
above the stipulated target of Growth Rate of 12.16%.
(c) The Foreign Tourist Arrivals in 2016 were 8.80 million as against targeted 11.24 million. Foreign
Tourist Visits in 2016 were 24.71 million as against targeted 35.96 million.
(d) The Domestic Tourist Visits in 2016 were 1613.6 million against the targeted 1451.46 million.
(e) The Foreign Exchange Earnings from Tourism in India during 2016 were `154146 Crore against
targeted 134383 Crore. In US $ terms the FEEs were 22.92 billion against the target of US $

30.3 billion. The Increase in the levels of FEEs in 2016 over FEEs in 2010 was 89257 Crore. In
US $ term the increase in levels was 8.73 billion.
(f) The figure of employment in tourism is available till 2012-13 only. As per the 2 nd Tourism
Satellite Account of India, the employment in tourism sector during 2010-11 was 58 million and
during 2012 -13 was 67 million (direct plus indirect). Additional employment in 2012-13 over
2010 -11 was 9.0 million.

(a) The Ministry of Tourism has launched the Swadesh Darshan and the National Mission on
Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Augmentation Drive (PRASAD) Schemes for the
development of tourism related infrastructure and facilities to promote tourism in the country.
As part of the Swadesh Darshan Scheme, theme based circuits are being developed spanning
the entire country, for integrated development of tourism infrastructure. The thirteen themes
identified for development under this Scheme are the North-East India Circuit, Buddhist Circuit,
Himalayan Circuit, Coastal Circuit, Krishna Circuit, Desert Circuit, Tribal Circuit, Eco Circuit,
Wildlife Circuit, Rural Circuit, Spiritual Circuit, Ramayana Circuit and Heritage Circuit. In so far

(b) Development of theme/amusement/aquamarine parks is an admissible component under the
Swadesh Darshan scheme and funds have been sanctioned for development of theme/amusement

(c) Construction of craft haats/bazaars/souvenir shop is an admissible component under the Swadesh
Darshan scheme and funds have been sanctioned for development of above components under
various projects park under the scheme.
(d) Ministry of Tourism in collaboration with Ministry of Railways has identified 26 railway stations
for joint development of tourist amenities based on the popularity of stations from the tourism
point of view and has sanctioned an amount of `63.80 Crore for the Joint Development of

mooted the idea of having glass top coaches on picturesque routes for an enhanced experience

top coaches at the cost of `4.00 Crore per coach. One of the coaches has been deployed on the
Vishakpatanam–ArakuV alley Route.

(iii) Guidelines for promotion of Polo Tourism released

V. Hygiene and Sanitation
(a) During the 12 th Five Year Pan Period, Ministry has released Social Awareness Campaign “Ätithi
Devo Bhava” on TV/Radio and Outdoor Medium.
(b) Ministry of Tourism undertakes Swachhta Pakhwadas at various tourist destinations across the
country with the active involvement of the Ministry’s Offices located in various States and other
States/UTs Govts. Major cleanliness drives were carried out at India Gate, Jantar Mantar,
Nizamuddin Dargah, Red Fort, Qutab Minar, Mehrauli Archaeological Park, Sai Mandir, Purana
Quila, Lotus Temple and Laxmi Narayan Mandir during 2016-17.
(c) With the objective of promoting a sense of competition among the State and UT Governments to
achieve high standards of cleanliness in the tourist destinations falling under their jurisdiction,
Ministry of Tourism has decided to institute a National Swachhta Award from the Year 2016.
(d) Ministry of Tourism has prepared a detailed Swachhta Action Plan (SAP) for 2017-18 & 2018-
19 with a budget outlay of `43.20 Crores. The programmes under the SAP consists of
construction of toilets of world class standard, facilities for garbage collection system, solid
waste management system, etc. at selected tourist destinations. SAP also provides creating
awareness regarding cleanliness and training programs for various stakeholders, students, cadets
of NCC, NSS etc.
(e) Ministry of Tourism has launched a Mobile Application -”Swachh Paryatan Mobile App.”, on
pilot basis at 25 locations of Adarsh monuments protected by Archaeological Survey of India
(ASI). A Nodal Officer at each of the 25 monuments has to ensure clearing of the garbage
reported and send an SMS to the complainant that his/her complaint has been attended to
successfully.
(f) Development of wayside amenities is an admissible component under the Swadesh Darshan and
PRASAD Schemes and the Ministry is focusing on its development in the identified circuits.
The wayside facilities are developed based on use of green technology including biodegradable
toilets and on principals of universal access.

VII. Safe and Honourable Tourism
(a) Ministry of Tourism has taken several measures to ensure the safety and security of tourists
including foreign tourists:
(i) The Ministry of Tourism has launched the 24x7 Toll Free Multi-Lingual Tourist Info-
Helpline on the toll free number 1800111363 or on a short code 1363 in 12 Languages
including 10 international languages and in Hindi & English in February 2016, for domestic
and foreign tourists to provide support service in terms of information relating to Travel in
India and also offers appropriate guidance to tourists in distress while travelling in India.
(ii) Ministry of Tourism has launched an initiative of presenting pre-loaded SIM Cards at the
Indira Gandhi International Airport, New Delhi and Cochin Airport to foreign tourists
arriving in India on e-Visa with the objective of providing instant connectivity.
(iii) W elcome card is handed over to foreign tourists on arrival to provide them useful tips.
(iv) Ministry of Tourism has launched code of conduct to be adopted by the Indian travel and
tourism industry for Safe and Honourable Tourism with the objectives to encourage tourism
activities to be undertaken with respect to basic rights like dignity, safety and freedom
from exploitation of both tourists and local residents, in particular women and children.
(v) Ministry of Tourism has advised all the State Governments/Union Territory Administrations
to deploy Tourist Police in the States/Union Territories. At present, a total number of 14
States have deployed the tourist police namely Delhi, Goa, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu &
Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh,
Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in one form or the other.
(vi) Advisories are issued from time to time to various State Governments and UT Administration
to ensure safety and security of the tourists.
VIII. Development and Promotion of Niche Tourism Products
(a) Adventure Tourism: Ministry of Tourism has formed a Task Force on Adventure Tourism to
draw up short term and long terms goals and strategies for the promotion of Adventure Tourism.
Ministry of Tourism had launched a two-year campaign “777 Days of the Himalayas” during
2013-2015 to centre stage the Indian Himalayas on the World Tourism Map. As part of the
campaign a TVC on Indian Himalayas and a Map of Indian Himalayas was produced. Ministry
of Tourism subsidized 50% of the Peak Fee charged by Indian Mountaineering Foundation from
climbing expeditions during the campaign.

8
(b) MICE: Ministry of Tourism actively supports the India Conventions Promotion Bureau in its
efforts for the promotion of MICE industry. Ministry of Tourism is marketing India’s MICE
capabilities by participating in trade fairs exclusively related to MICE like IMEX in Frankfurt and
Las Vegas.
(c) Cruise Tourism: Ministry of Tourism in collaboration with Ministry of Shipping has set up a
Task Force for promoting cruise tourism. The Task Force formulated Standard Operating
Procedure (SOP) and has appointed a consultant for preparation of an Action Plan for development
of Cruise Tourism in India. Ministry of Tourism has provided financial assistance to the ports of
Mumbai, Marmugao, Cochin and Chennai for developing dedicated terminals and facilities for
Cruise operations.
(d) Medical Tourism: Ministry of Tourism has constituted a National Medical & Wellness and Tourism
Board to provide dedicated institutional framework to take forward the cause of promotion of
Medical Tourism, Wellness Tourism and Yoga, Ayurveda Tourism and other forms of Indian
system of medicine. Medical Visa rules have been streamlined and e-Visa facility can be availed
by visitors arriving for medical purpose.
(e) Wellness Tourism: India is promoted as a Wellness Tourism destination through Incredible India
campaigns. The National Medical and Wellness Tourism Board encourages accreditation of
wellness centres and the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Services
have released accreditation standards for AYUSH hospitals, Panchkarma clinics and wellness
centres.
(f) Golf Tourism: Ministry has released guidelines for promotion of Golf Tourism which includes
extending support to domestic and international Golf Tournaments to highlight India as a Golf
destination. Setting up of Golf Courses are eligible for financial assistance under the LRG Scheme.
Ministry also participates in Golf Tourism Trade Shows for promoting India as a Golf Tourism
Destination.
(g) Polo Tourism: Ministry of Tourism has released guidelines for support of International Polo
Tournaments held under the aegis of the Indian Polo Association.
(h) Spiritual/ Pilgrimage Tourism: The ‘National Mission on Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual
Augmentation Drive’ (PRASAD) has been launched by the Ministry of Tourism with the objective
of holistic development of identified pilgrimage destinations. 25 destinations have been identified
for development under the PRASAD scheme.
(i) Film Tourism: A MoU was signed between Ministry of Tourism and Ministry of I&B for promotion
of Film Tourism where in Ministry of Tourism would support the Film Bazaar at IFFI in Goa
and also support participation at the Film Market in major festivals like Cannes. Ministry has
released guidelines for financial support to state governments for facilitating film shoots. Ministry
had also promoted the Oscar awarded winning movie “Life of Pi” which was shot in India.

XI. Market Research and Tourism Statistics
(a) The 2nd Tourism Satellite Account of India (TSAI) has been prepared for the reference year

prepared for the same reference year. The process of preparation of 3rd TSAI and 2nd R-TSAs
has also been initiated.
(b) A study was conducted in the markets of Turkey, South Korea, South Africa, Germany, Japan
& Russia to gauge the perception of tourists (past as well as prospective) about tourism in India

10
which could help in undertaking targeted marketing activities in the potential source markets.
The target respondents of the study included travellers (past and intending travellers to India and
other countries) and various service providers. According to the findings from the study, the
Indian cities most frequently visited by the travellers were Delhi, Mumbai, Goa and Agra. The
main reasons for choosing India as a tourist destination by past travellers were India’s culture,
Heritage and Scenic Tourist Spots. Among the intending travellers, primary attractions of India/
motivation for visiting India were found to be Scenic Tourist Spots, Monuments, Forts &
palaces, Museums and Variety of Food on offer.
(c) The Methodology for conducting survey in States/UTs for determining the number of Domestic
Tourist Visits (DTVs) and Foreign Tourist Visits (FTVs) to various States/UTs has been prepared
and circulated to all States/UTs. Tourism surveys were conducted in 19 States/ UTs during the
period of the 12th plan and demonstrational workshops on the methodology of conducting the
survey were organized for the officials of the concerned State/UT Tourism Departments.
Presently, the methodology is being firmed up by a Technical Advisory Group on the basis of
learnings from the surveys in the Phases conducted so far. After being firmed up, the methodology
would be re-circulated to all States/ UTs. Ministry of Tourism has also provisioned to provide
Central Financial Assistance (CFA) under the MRPS Scheme to State Governments/UT
Administrations for conducting Studies/Surveys related to tourism and conduct capacity building
and skill enhancement workshops for research officials of the State/UT Tourism Departments.
