REPORT 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Ministry of Tourism, Government of India, is the nodal agency for the 
formulation of national policies and programs and for co-ordination of activities 
with various Central Government Agencies, State Governments/UTs and the 
Private Sector for the development and promotion of tourism in the country. It 
plays a crucial role in coordinating and supplementing the efforts of the 
State/Union Territory Governments, catalysing private investment, strengthening 
promotional and marketing efforts and in providing trained manpower resources.  
 
2. The major activities of the Ministry of Tourism can be classified under the 
following heads: - (I) Development of Tourism Infrastructure; (II) Promotion and 
Publicity; (III) Manpower Development; (IV) Regulation of Service Standards for 
Tour Operators & Hotels; (V) Other Initiatives/Programmes. India Tourism 
Development Corporation (ITDC) is the only Public Sector Undertaking under the 
charge of the Ministry of Tourism and the following autonomous institutes under 
its jurisdiction-  
⮚ Indian Institute of Tourism and Travel Management (IITTM) and 
National Institute of Water Sports (NIWS) 
⮚ National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology 
(NCHMCT) and the Institutes of Hotel Management. 
 
It has an attached office namely the Directorate General of Tourism (DGT). 
 
GOALS AND OBJECTIVES  
3. The Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework marks a shift from tracking 
financial and physical progress to an outcome-based governance model. It defines 

2 
 
measurable indicators to assess scheme objectives, ensuring greater development 
impact and public accountability. By actively monitoring progress against set 
targets, the framework enhances transparency and ensures more effective use of 
public funds in India. 
 
4. The Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF) 2025-2026, 
published by the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India, outlines key financial 
allocations, expected project outputs, and anticipated outcomes for various 
tourism-related schemes. The government has prioritised the enhancement of 
tourism infrastructure, skill development, and promotional activities to boost 
domestic and heritage tourism through the following objectives- 
i. Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual, Heritage Augmentation Drive 
(PRASHAD) scheme (allocated budget ₹240 crore): The PRASHAD 
scheme aims to rejuvenate religious and heritage sites, with 39 projects to be 
completed and 273 sub-components developed, with the end result being 
creation of  390 direct jobs. 
ii. Domestic Tourism Promotion (allocated budget ₹131.90 crore): A target 
of 35 events and 15 digital campaigns has been set to boost domestic tourism 
by 10%, with 40 additional events to be supported. 
iii. Hospitality and Tourism Education (allocated budget ₹35 crore): Three 
new projects will be initiated for IHMS/FCIs/IITTM institutes, with a 3% 
increase in hospitality job placements. 
iv. Capacity Building for Service Providers initiative (allocated budget ₹25 
crore): It will aim to train 40,000 new service providers and re-skill 30,000 
existing workers, with the goal of a 2% employment rate among trainees, 
while5% are anticipated to become self-employed. 
v. Assistance to Central AgenciesScheme (allocated budget ₹90 crore): 
Four major projects will be launched under central assistance, which in turn 
will see a tourist footfall of 25,000 at ticketed monuments.

5. The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, 
Tourism and Culture considered Demand No. 99 containing the details of budget 
allocations of the Ministry ofTourism, for the financial year 2025-26, in its 
meeting held on the 3rdMarch, 2025.For the financial year 2025-26, the Ministry of 
Tourism has identified key focus areas for the development and promotion of 
tourism across India. These include: 
Development of infrastructure-Infrastructure development is a primary 
objective, with efforts directed towards completing projects sanctioned 
under Swadesh Darshan, PRASHAD (Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual 
Heritage Augmentation Drive), Assistance to Central Agencies, and Special 
Assistance to States for Capital Investment (SASCI) schemes. Additionally, 
new projects will be approved under Swadesh Darshan 2.0 and Challenge-
Based Destination Development Schemes to enhance tourist destinations 
nationwide. 
Promotion and marketing-To strengthen promotion and marketing, the 
ministry will organise and support various promotional events, trade fairs, 
and exhibitions both within India and overseas. A significant focus will be 
placed on developing promotional films, digital brochures, and media 
campaigns under the Incredible India brand. Furthermore, marketing 
campaigns will be directed at domestic audiences, and Familiarisation 
(FAM) trips will be arranged to highlight India's tourism potential. 
Skill development-In the realm of skills development, the ministry aims to 
provide financial assistance to institutions such as IHMs, FCIs, IITTM, and 
ICI to enhance professional training in the tourism and hospitality sector. It 
will also conduct capacity-building programmes for tourism service 
providers and expand the Incredible India Tourist Facilitators Programme, 
designed to create a pool of well-trained and professional tourist guides. 
Additionally, specialist training programmes will be introduced under the

Paryatan Mitra & Paryatan Didi initiatives to equip local communities with 
tourism-related skills.

Similar patterns of underutilization plague other key initiatives including

inefficiencies in project execution and monitoring systems that require immediate 
redressal to translate financial allocations into tangible tourism infrastructure

10. To address the chronic underutilization of funds, the Committee 
recommends implementing a three-tier monitoring mechanism for Swadesh 
Darshan and PRASHAD schemes, comprising central oversight, state-level 
coordination committees, and destination-specific implementation teams with 
defined quarterly milestones and real-time digital tracking systems. 
Furthermore, the Ministry should strategically leverage the Public-Private 
Partnership (PPP) model to enhance operational efficiency and attract 
additional investment for tourism infrastructure development. Specific 
tourism circuits could benefit from private sector involvement in not just 
construction but also in management and innovation of visitor experiences, 
bringing entrepreneurial dynamism to heritage and spiritual tourism 
destinations. The Committee also recommends establishing a dedicated 
Project Management Unit within the Ministry with tourism infrastructure 
specialists and financial experts to conduct pre-feasibility assessments and 
ensure project viability before fund disbursement. 
 
11. The Committee expresses serious concern over the sharp decline in 
allocation for the Restructured Overseas Promotion and Publicity Scheme, 
which has been reduced to an inadequate ₹3.07 crore in 2025-26. This drastic 
reduction threatens India's competitiveness in the global tourism market 

9 
 
where competing destinations are substantially increasing their marketing 
investments. The Committee recommends establishing an Integrated Global 
Tourism Marketing Framework that leverages digital platforms and artificial 
intelligence to deliver personalized, targeted international campaigns. The 
Incredible India Digital Platform (IIDP) should be enhanced with AI-driven 
personalization capabilities, virtual tourism experiences, and seamless 
booking interfaces to provide potential international visitors with immersive 
pre-visit experiences of India's diverse destinations. 
 
12. The Committee strongly recommends allocation specifically for a 
comprehensive digital marketing campaign targeting key international source 
markets. This initiative should incorporate advanced data analytics to identify 
high-potential visitor segments, create personalized digital experiences, and 
measure campaign effectiveness with precision. The Ministry should 
collaborate with leading technology partners to develop virtual reality tours of 
India's iconic destinations, interactive cultural experiences, and AI-powered 
trip planners that can be accessed through mobile applications and the 
Incredible India website. Implementing a 360-degree digital presence strategy 
across social media platforms, travel forums, and international digital 
publications would ensure maximum visibility with efficient resource 
utilization. 
 
13. While the allocation for domestic tourism promotion remains relatively 
consistent at ₹136.90 crore for 2025-26, the Committee recommends a more 
balanced approach that strengthens both domestic and international tourism 
sectors. The Ministry should develop a Dual Growth Strategy that identifies 
complementary objectives for both sectors, with domestic tourism focusing on 
developing emerging destinations and international tourism emphasizing 
unique cultural and experiential offerings. To stimulate domestic tourism 

10 
 
innovation, the Committee recommends allocating ₹50 crore specifically for 
developing and promoting new tourism concepts such as Caravan Tourism 
across states with appropriate infrastructure and regulatory frameworks. 
This initiative would diversify tourism offerings while creating new 
entrepreneurial opportunities in emerging tourism segments. 
 
14. The Committee views with grave concern the reduction in allocations 
and negative recoveries recorded under the 'Safe Tourist Destinations for 
Women' initiative. This regression contradicts the fundamental principle of 
inclusive tourism development and may significantly impact women travelers' 
confidence in Indian destinations. The Committee recommends establishing 
an Inter-Ministerial Women's Tourism Safety Task Force comprising 
representatives from the Ministries of Tourism, Women & Child 
Development, Home Affairs, and state tourism departments to develop 
comprehensive safety protocols, monitoring mechanisms, and emergency 
response systems specifically designed for women tourists. Additionally, the 
Ministry should allocate funds for implementing technology-driven safety 
measures, including GPS-enabled transportation monitoring, CCTV 
surveillance at tourist hotspots, and a centralized tourism safety application 
with real-time assistance features and emergency response capabilities. 
 
15. The Committee acknowledges that environmental sustainability must 
transition from an auxiliary consideration to a core principle in tourism 
development. To institutionalize this approach, the Committee recommends 
developing the National Sustainable Tourism Certification System with 
clearly defined parameters for ecological impact, resource efficiency, and 
community engagement. This system would incentivize tourism businesses to 
adopt green innovations and responsible practices through preferential 
marketing and access to specialized funding mechanisms. Furthermore, the 

11 
 
Ministry should establish five Regional Centers for Sustainable Tourism 
Excellence that would function as knowledge hubs for research, training, and 
practical implementation of sustainable tourism principles, focusing on 
developing models for responsible destination management in different 
geographical contexts across India. 
 
16. Recognizing the critical importance of skilled human resources in 
delivering quality tourism experiences, the Committee recommends 
substantially increasing allocations for tourism education institutions 
including IHMS, FCI, IITTM, and NCHMCT. The Ministry should develop 
an integrated Tourism Skills Enhancement Program with modernized 
curricula incorporating digital competencies, sustainable tourism 
management, experiential tourism design, and entrepreneurial skills 
development. Additionally, the Committee recommends establishing 
specialized training centers in partnership with industry leaders to develop 
expertise in emerging areas such as wellness tourism, adventure tourism 
management, and cultural interpretation. These institutions should establish 
international exchange programs with leading global tourism education 
centers to ensure alignment with evolving global standards and innovative 
practices in tourism management.

19. The Committee notes with serious concern the chronic pattern of fund 
underutilization by the Ministry of Tourism, which represents not merely an 
administrative challenge but a fundamental impediment to the growth of 
India's tourism potential. The alarming statistics of only 33.4% utilization of 
allocated funds in 2023-24 (₹801.81 crore out of ₹2400 crore) and the 
continuation of this trend in 2024-25 (only ₹396.82 crore utilized thus far out 
of ₹2479.62 crore) indicates deep-rooted systemic inefficiencies that require 
comprehensive structural reforms. While the Ministry has attributed this 
underperformance to administrative bottlenecks, procedural delays, and 
coordination challenges with implementing agencies, the Committee finds 
these explanations inadequate and reflective of entrenched operational 
weaknesses that demand immediate redressal. 
 
20. The Committee recommends implementing a comprehensive Integrated 
Digital Project Management System (IDPMS) modeled after successful 
implementations like Tamil Nadu's tourism data collection and analytical 
method for tracking visitor footfalls. This centralized platform would 
facilitate end-to-end project lifecycle management, from proposal submission 
to fund disbursal and utilization certificate processing with embedded 
automatic alerts and escalation protocols for pending actions. The system 
should incorporate real-time dashboards accessible to all stakeholders 
including the Ministry officials, state tourism departments, and implementing 
agencies, providing transparency on project status, fund utilization, and 
procedural bottlenecks requiring immediate intervention. This digital 
infrastructure would enable predictive analytics to identify potential 
implementation challenges before they create delays and would maintain a 
comprehensive digital repository of all project documentation, eliminating 
physical document submission requirements that frequently cause delays. 

15 
 
 
20.1 The Committee recommends the development of a Tourism 
Implementation Rating System (TIRS) to evaluate and rank states based on 
their efficiency in implementing tourism projects, timely submission of 
required documentation, and overall fund utilization patterns. This 
performance-based framework would create healthy competition among 
states while providing objective metrics for allocating additional resources to 
high-performing regions. States consistently demonstrating superior 
implementation capabilities should receive incentives including priority 
funding for new projects, relaxed procedural requirements, and recognition 
through annual tourism implementation excellence awards. Conversely, states 
with persistently poor implementation records should receive targeted 
capacity-building interventions and simplified pilot projects to build 
implementation expertise before receiving allocations for more complex 
initiatives. This rating system would create accountability while providing 
valuable data on implementation challenges specific to different regions. 
 
20.2 The Committee recommends establishing a specialized Fund Utilization 
Task Force (FUTF) comprising financial experts, project management 
specialists, and representatives from high-performing state tourism 
departments. This dedicated team would conduct monthly reviews of fund 
utilization patterns, identify procedural bottlenecks, and implement rapid 
interventions to accelerate fund absorption. The FUTF should develop 
standardized templates for all documentation required throughout the project 
lifecycle—including detailed project reports, utilization certificates, and 
closure documents—thereby eliminating inconsistencies that frequently delay 
approvals. Additionally, the task force would provide hands-on 
implementation support to underperforming states through expert 
deployment, targeted training programs, and developing simplified process 

16 
 
maps for complex procedures. The FUTF would also conduct quarterly 
implementation audits to identify systemic issues requiring policy 
interventions and would maintain a comprehensive knowledge repository of 
implementation challenges and successful resolution strategies. 
 
20.3 The Committee recommends a fundamental restructuring of the 
PRASHAD Scheme implementation framework to address the specific 
challenges of brownfield projects, which have proven particularly problematic 
due to their complexity and the presence of existing infrastructure. The 
Ministry should develop a specialized "Brownfield Project Implementation 
Protocol" with modified timelines, distinct approval processes, and specialized 
technical assistance. Furthermore, the scheme should incorporate a phased 
implementation approach for complex projects, breaking them into smaller, 
more manageable components with separate funding streams and 
implementation timelines. The Ministry should establish a PRASHAD 
Excellence Cell staffed with heritage conservation experts, religious tourism 
specialists, and infrastructure development professionals who can provide 
specialized technical assistance to implementing agencies. These targeted 
interventions would address the specific challenges that have hindered the 
PRASHAD Scheme's implementation while ensuring that its critical 
objectives of enhancing spiritual tourism infrastructure are achieved. 
 
20.4 The Committee recommends an urgent revival of the RSOPPMDA 
scheme with a comprehensive restructuring focused on market-specific 
promotion strategies and leveraging digital platforms for cost-effective global 
outreach. The Ministry should establish a dedicated International Tourism 
Promotion Cell with marketing specialists and digital experts to expedite the 
development and implementation of promotion campaigns. Furthermore, the 
Ministry should implement a rolling approval mechanism for marketing 

17 
 
initiatives that eliminates the need for annual scheme restructuring while 
maintaining necessary oversight. The Committee also recommends developing 
strategic partnerships with Indian diplomatic missions abroad, leveraging 
their presence and local knowledge for more effective tourism promotion. 
Additionally, a comprehensive digital marketing strategy should be 
implemented with country-specific campaign approaches, recognizing that 
different international markets require tailored messaging and promotion 
channels. 
 
20.5 The Committee strongly recommends transitioning to a performance-
based budgeting approach wherein future allocations are directly linked to 
demonstrated implementation capacity and past utilization efficiency. This 
fundamental shift from the current input-focused budgeting to an outcome-
oriented approach would create powerful incentives for improved fund 
utilization while ensuring that resources are allocated where they can be most 
effectively deployed. The budgeting process should incorporate a two-tier 
allocation strategy: a base allocation determined by strategic priorities and 
tourism potential, and a performance-linked component based on past

36. Analysis of Foreign Tourist Arrivals and India's Global Tourism 
Position 
The Committee has conducted a comprehensive assessment of India's tourism 
performance metrics, including foreign tourist arrivals (FTAs), international 
tourist arrivals (ITAs), foreign exchange earnings (FEEs), and the overall 
contribution of tourism to GDP. While acknowledging the Ministry's various 
promotional initiatives such as participation in international trade fairs, 
roadshows, and the innovative Chalo India Global Diaspora Campaign, the 
Committee observes that despite post-pandemic recovery, significant 
challenges persist in establishing India as a premier global tourism 
destination. Current data indicates that India ranks 39th in the global travel 
and tourism competitiveness index despite possessing extraordinary potential 
through its rich cultural heritage, diverse landscapes, and emerging global 
influence. This positioning represents a substantial gap between India's 
tourism capabilities and its actual market performance, necessitating 
transformative strategies to enhance competitiveness and sustain long-term 
growth in the international tourism sector. 
36.1 Implementing a Multi-Dimensional Competitiveness Enhancement 
Strategy 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry develop a comprehensive 
Tourism Competitiveness Enhancement Framework based on a thorough 
analysis of India's strengths and weaknesses in the global tourism ecosystem. 
This framework should leverage India's significant advantages in natural 
resources (scoring 5.88 out of 7), cultural resources (5.62), and non-leisure 
resources (5.05), while systematically addressing areas of underperformance. 
The Ministry should establish a dedicated Tourism Competitiveness Task 
Force comprising industry leaders, academic experts, and government 
representatives to identify specific barriers to India's advancement in global 
rankings and develop targeted interventions. Furthermore, the framework 

34 
 
should incorporate innovative benchmarking mechanisms that regularly 
compare India's performance against rapidly advancing tourism destinations, 
particularly in emerging markets with similar tourism profiles. Beyond 
conventional tourism metrics, the framework should incorporate 
sustainability parameters, digital readiness indicators, and visitor satisfaction 
indices to ensure a holistic approach to competitiveness enhancement. This 
structured approach would transform India's competitiveness strategy from 
reactive to proactive, positioning the country to advance significantly from its 
current 39th position toward the top 20 global destinations by 2030. 
36.2 Revolutionizing Tourism Marketing Through Advanced Digital 
Strategies 
The Committee recommends a fundamental reimagining of India's tourism 
marketing approach through the implementation of an AI-driven 
Personalized Tourism Marketing Ecosystem. This would leverage the 
revamped Incredible India Digital Platform (IIDP) to create highly targeted, 
personalized marketing campaigns for different international markets based 
on sophisticated data analytics and AI algorithms. The system would analyze 
potential visitors' digital footprints, travel preferences, and cultural interests 
to deliver customized tourism propositions rather than generic promotional 
content. Furthermore, the Ministry should develop market-specific digital 
personas representing key tourist segments from major source countries, 
enabling the creation of tailored content strategies that resonate with specific 
cultural contexts and travel motivations. The Committee also recommends 
establishing a Global Digital Influencer Network dedicated exclusively to 
promoting Indian tourism experiences, systematically engaging with content 
creators who have significant followings in key international markets. This 
revolutionary approach would transform tourism marketing from mass 
communication to precision engagement, substantially improving conversion 
rates and expanding India's share in the global tourism market. 

35 
 
36.3 Transforming the Chalo India Initiative into a Strategic Diaspora 
Engagement Program 
The Committee recommends a significant enhancement of the Chalo India 
Global Diaspora Campaign, transforming it from primarily an incentive 
program into a strategic diaspora engagement framework that systematically 
cultivates the Indian diaspora as tourism ambassadors. The Ministry should 
implement a tiered Diaspora Tourism Ambassador certification program, 
providing specialized training, promotional materials, and digital tools to 
formally recognized diaspora members who actively promote Indian tourism. 
Furthermore, a sophisticated tracking and attribution system should be 
developed to measure the direct impact of diaspora-generated tourism, 
enabling performance-based incentives and recognition for top-performing 
ambassadors. The initiative should incorporate regular virtual forums 
connecting diaspora communities with tourism stakeholders in India, creating 
channels for continuous feedback on international visitor experiences and 
emerging preferences. Additionally, the Committee recommends establishing 
Diaspora Tourism Innovation Grants to support creative diaspora-led 
projects that enhance India's tourism appeal in their respective countries. 
This strategic approach would transform the diaspora from passive 
supporters to active, measurable contributors to India's tourism growth, with 
clearly defined performance indicators ensuring accountability and 
effectiveness. 
37. Implementing Next-Generation Visa Facilitation Programs 
Recognizing visa complexity as a significant barrier to tourism growth, the 
Committee recommends implementing a Progressive Visa Liberalization 
Roadmap that systematically expands visa-free access based on strategic 
tourism priorities rather than conventional diplomatic reciprocity. The 
Ministry, in coordination with the Ministry of External Affairs, should 
develop data-driven criteria for identifying and prioritizing high-potential 

36 
 
source markets for visa liberalization, considering factors beyond current 
arrival numbers to include average spending patterns, length of stay, and 
potential for market growth. Furthermore, the Committee recommends 
implementing a Digital Nomad Visa Program specifically designed to attract 
long-term international visitors who can work remotely while experiencing 
India's diverse destinations, effectively creating a new category of extended-
stay tourism that generates sustained economic impact. For markets where 
visa-free access is not immediately feasible, the Ministry should implement an 
Advanced Traveler Authorization System enabling simplified entry for pre-
approved visitors from key tourism markets. These innovations would 
significantly reduce barriers to entry while maintaining necessary security 
protocols, positioning India as a leader in intelligent border facilitation for 
tourism growth. 
38. Developing Destination-Specific Transformation Initiatives 
The Committee recommends that beyond the general development of 50 
tourist destinations announced in the 2025-26 budget, the Ministry should 
implement a Strategic Destination Transformation Program that selects 10

tourism excellence centers. Each selected destination would receive integrated 
support including infrastructure enhancement, digital transformation, 
capacity building, and targeted international promotion, creating showcase 
models of world-class tourism management. The program should incorporate 
Destination Experience Laboratories where innovative tourism concepts, 
technologies, and service models can be tested and refined before broader 
implementation. Furthermore, the Committee recommends establishing 
Destination Management Organizations (DMOs) with public-private 
governance structures for each selected destination, ensuring professional 
management and sustainable growth through dedicated expert oversight. 
These initiatives would transform India's approach to destination 

37 
 
development from incremental improvement to comprehensive excellence, 
creating globally competitive tourism products that significantly enhance 
India's tourism appeal and international rankings.

47. The Committee has reviewed the Ministry of Tourism’s initiative to provide 
financial assistance for the expansion and upgradation of Central Institutes of Hotel 
Management (CIHMs), Indian Institutes of Tourism and Travel Management 
(IITTM), the National Council for Hotel Management and Catering Technology 
(NCHMCT), and the establishment of new Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs) 
and Food Craft Institutes (FCIs). These institutions are crucial to meeting the rising 
demand for skilled professionals in India’s tourism and hospitality sector. 
48. Strategic Expansion of Hospitality Education Infrastructure 
 
The Committee, while acknowledging the expansion efforts in hospitality 
training institutes, identifies critical geographical disparities that severely 
limit access to quality tourism education in several high-potential tourism 
regions. This education infrastructure gap creates persistent workforce 
challenges and hinders the development of local tourism ecosystems. The 
Committee recommends implementing a Tiered Hospitality Education 
Framework that strategically distributes training facilities based on tourism 
potential and workforce needs rather than conventional administrative 
considerations. This framework would establish three distinct institutional 
models: Comprehensive Institutes of Hotel Management (IHMs) in regional 
hubs, specialized Focused Culinary Institutes (FCIs) aligned with distinct 
culinary heritage zones, and flexible Community Tourism Skill Centers in 
emerging tourism destinations. The Ministry should prioritize establishing at 
least one specialized hospitality training institute in each Northeastern state 
by 2027, with particular focus on developing centers of excellence that align 

43 
 
with each region's unique tourism attributes—adventure tourism in 
Arunachal Pradesh, cultural tourism in Assam, and ecological tourism in 
Meghalaya. Additionally, the Committee recommends introducing the 
Tourism Satellite Campus model, wherein established IHMs develop 
extension facilities in underserved regions, leveraging existing administrative 
infrastructure while expanding geographical reach cost-effectively. This 
transformative approach would systematically address geographical 
disparities while ensuring that educational infrastructure aligns precisely with 
each region's specific tourism development trajectory. 
49. Transforming Hospitality Education Through Industry-Integrated 
Learning 
 
The Committee emphasizes that traditional curriculum development 
processes in hospitality education have become inadequate for preparing 
professionals in the rapidly evolving tourism landscape where technological 
proficiency, sustainability expertise, and experiential delivery capabilities are 
increasingly essential. The Committee recommends establishing a National 
Tourism Education Innovation Council comprising industry leaders, 
academic experts, digital technology specialists, and international hospitality 
education partners to fundamentally reimagine hospitality curriculum beyond 
incremental updates. This collaborative body would develop core curriculum 
frameworks incorporating emerging competencies including digital guest 
experience management, sustainable tourism operations, cultural 
interpretation techniques, and tourism entrepreneurship. Furthermore, the 
Committee recommends implementing a Mandatory Industry Immersion 
Program requiring all hospitality faculty to complete annual industry 
placements of at least four weeks, ensuring instructors maintain current 
practical knowledge alongside theoretical expertise. Additionally, the Ministry 
should establish a Hospitality Education Digital Transformation Initiative 

44 
 
that provides all IHMs and FCIs with integrated learning management 
systems, virtual reality training environments for practical skills development, 
and AI-powered personalized learning pathways that adapt to individual 
student learning patterns. These innovations would transform hospitality 
education from institution-centered to learner-centered while ensuring 
graduates possess the precise competencies required for India's tourism 
growth. 
50. Pioneering Sustainable Funding Models for Tourism Education 
 
The Committee recognizes that the current allocation of ₹35 crore for FY 
2025-26 represents a significant constraint on the necessary expansion and 
modernization of hospitality education infrastructure. Beyond conventional 
funding approaches, the Committee recommends establishing a Tourism 
Education Investment Fund through strategic partnerships with major 
tourism industry stakeholders, international development agencies, and 
corporate CSR initiatives. This dedicated fund would provide sustainable 
financing for infrastructure development, technology acquisition, and faculty 
excellence programs across hospitality education institutions. The Committee 
also recommends developing a Tourism Education Revenue Diversification 
Framework that enables institutes to generate supplementary income through 
establishing commercial training restaurants, student-operated boutique 
hotels, specialized consulting services, and executive education programs for 
industry professionals. Furthermore, the Ministry should implement the 
Destination Adoption Program wherein tourism-intensive destinations 
contribute to hospitality education infrastructure in exchange for dedicated 
workforce development programs tailored to their specific requirements. 
These innovative funding mechanisms would transform hospitality education 
financing from government-dependent to multi-stakeholder supported, 
substantially expanding available resources while creating greater alignment 

45 
 
between educational institutions and industry needs. 
51. Elevating Specialized Tourism Training Through Centers of Excellence 
 
The Committee acknowledges the critical importance of specialized tourism 
training exemplified by the National Institute of Water Sports (NIWS), while 
noting significant limitations in both geographical reach and integration with 
mainstream tourism education. The Committee recommends transforming 
NIWS into the National Center for Adventure Tourism Excellence with 
expanded scope encompassing all adventure tourism segments including 
mountaineering, trekking, wildlife tourism, and desert expeditions, creating a 
comprehensive ecosystem for adventure tourism human resource 
development. The Center should establish Regional Adventure Tourism 
Training Hubs in five strategic locations—Uttarakhand (mountaineering), 
Goa (water sports), Rajasthan (desert tourism), Madhya Pradesh (wildlife 
tourism), and Sikkim (eco-adventure tourism)—ensuring specialized training 
reaches diverse geographical areas with distinct adventure tourism potential. 
Furthermore, the Committee recommends implementing an Integrated 
Adventure Tourism Certification System aligned with international standards 
that creates clearly defined career pathways for professionals across different 
adventure tourism segments. Additionally, the Ministry should develop the 
Adventure Tourism Digital Knowledge Repository, a comprehensive online 
platform providing training resources, safety protocols, and best practices 
accessible to adventure tourism operators nationwide. These transformative 
initiatives would elevate specialized tourism training from isolated programs 
to a comprehensive national framework that develops world-class human 
resources across the adventure tourism ecosystem.

CAPACITY BUILDING FOR SERVICE PROVIDERS: 
Ministry of Tourism under the scheme of “Capacity Building for Service 
Providers” (CBSP) aims to provide education, training and certification to the 
tourism service providers at every level with its main objectives being to train and 
up-grade manpower at every strata of service in order to i) capitalize on the vast 
tourism potential of the country to the fullest, and ii) provide professional expertise 
to the local populace as well as create fresh opportunities for employment 
generation in the tourism sector both in urban as well as rural areas. The Scheme is 
implemented by the Ministry of Tourism through Institutes of Hotel Management 
and Food Craft Institutes including such institutes approved by the All India 
Council for Technical Education (AICTE), Indian Institute of Tourism & Travel 
Management (IITTM), National Council of Hotel Management & Catering 
Technology (NCHMCT).  Training is also given through State/UT/Central 
Training/ Academic Institutions, India Tourism Development Corporation (ITDC) 
and the specialized academic Training Institutes in the private sector engaged in 
giving training in the hospitality sector.The following programmes are being run 
under the scheme of ‘CBSP’: 
a) Hunar Se Rozgar Tak 
b) Skill Testing & Certification Programme 
c) Entrepreneurship Programme 
d) Tourism Adventure Courses 
e) Linguistic Tourist Facilitators (LTF) 
f) Destination Based Skill Development 
g) Other Programs: Tourism Awareness/Sensitization programs for existing 
service providers are also conducted under this scheme.   
A total of ₹  25 cr has been set aside in this year budget for this head. 
 
52. The Committee acknowledges the importance of skill development in 
improving India’s tourism competitiveness but notes that the CBSP scheme 
has not yet achieved uniform accessibility. Many rural and emerging tourism 

47 
 
hubs lack structured training facilities, limiting opportunities for communities 
that would benefit most from employment in the sector. It therefore urges the 
Ministry to imbibe a more balanced regional approach by increasing training 
coverage in underserved areas. 
 
53. The Committee highlights that the budgetary allocation of ₹25 crore, 
though a step in the right direction, may be insufficient to expand the scheme 
at the scale required. It therefore urges the Ministry to explore additional 
funding sources, including public-private partnerships, corporate 
sponsorships, and collaboration with State Governments, to enhance training 
infrastructure and broaden access to professional development opportunities.

KEY HIGHLIGHTS OF THE COMMITTEE'S 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS 
 
Budget Utilization and Monitoring 
1.   Addressing Underutilization of Funds 
To tackle chronic underutilization, the Committee recommends a three-tier 
monitoring mechanism for Swadesh Darshan and PRASHAD schemes, 
involving central oversight, state-level coordination committees, and 
destination-specific implementation teams with quarterly milestones and real-
time digital tracking systems. Public-Private Partnerships (PPP) should be 
leveraged to enhance operational efficiency and attract investments in tourism 
infrastructure. A dedicated Project Management Unit with tourism 
infrastructure specialists should be established to conduct pre-feasibility 
assessments and ensure project viability before fund disbursement. 
2.  Integrated Digital Project Management System (IDPMS) 
The Ministry should implement a centralized digital platform modeled on 
Tamil Nadu’s tourism data system to manage the project lifecycle, track fund 
utilization, and provide real-time dashboards for stakeholders. Predictive 
analytics should be incorporated to identify potential delays, ensuring timely 
interventions. 
3.  Tourism Implementation Rating System (TIRS) 
A performance-based framework to evaluate states on project implementation 
efficiency, fund utilization, and timely documentation is recommended. High-
performing states should receive incentives such as priority funding, while 
underperforming states should receive targeted capacity-building support. 
 

49 
 
4.  Fund Utilization Task Force (FUTF) 
Establish a specialized task force comprising financial experts and project 
managers to address procedural bottlenecks, accelerate fund absorption, and 
provide hands-on support to underperforming states. Standardized templates 
for documentation should also be developed. 
Tourism Infrastructure Development 
5.  Revamping PRASHAD Scheme 
A comprehensive Digital Monitoring Framework should be introduced for 
PRASHAD projects to track progress and visitor metrics in real-time. 
Implementation Credibility Ratings for states/agencies based on past 
performance should guide future funding priorities. Specialized guidelines for 
brownfield projects are recommended to address unique challenges at high-
footfall pilgrimage sites. 
6.  Enhancing SACI Scheme Participation 
Transform the challenge-based selection process into a phased approach with 
quarterly submission windows and fast-track reviews. A DPR Technical 
Support Cell should assist states in proposal development, while Regional 
Knowledge Exchange Forums should foster peer learning . 
7.  Accelerating Central Agency Projects 
Introduce a Multi-tier Performance Monitoring System with quarterly reviews 
and establish a Project Acceleration Task Force to resolve barriers in stalled 
projects. Recognize high-performing agencies through a Tourism 
Infrastructure Excellence Program. 
 
 

50 
 
8.  Digital Transformation Across Schemes 
Develop a Unified Tourism Infrastructure Interface for real-time visibility into 
project status across schemes, supported by AI-driven predictive analytics to 
preempt delays. 
Promotion and Marketing

Establish an Integrated Global Tourism Marketing Framework leveraging AI-
driven personalization, virtual tourism experiences, and seamless booking 
interfaces under the Incredible India Digital Platform (IIDP). Collaborate with 
Indian Missions abroad for targeted campaigns. 
10.  Balanced Domestic and International Promotion 
Develop a Dual Growth Strategy focusing on emerging domestic destinations 
while promoting unique cultural experiences internationally. Allocate ₹50 
crore for innovative concepts like Caravan Tourism. 
11.   Revival of RSOPPMDA Scheme 
Restructure the Overseas Promotion scheme with market-specific strategies 
and rolling approval mechanisms for global outreach campaigns. Strategic 
partnerships with Indian Missions abroad are essential for effective 
promotion. 
12.   Tourism Hubs Along Highways 
Develop 100 tourism hubs along national highways showcasing regional 
heritage, cuisine, art, and culture as immersive visitor experiences. 
Competitiveness Enhancement

i. Oral evidence of Secretary, Ministry of Tourism 
2.  The Chairman, extended a warm welcome to all Members of the Committee and senior 
officials of the Ministry of Tourism. 
3. The Chair man underlined the fundamental role of tourism in promoting economic 
growth, employment generation, and cultural preservation. He urged the Ministry to elaborate on 
its expenditure patterns, the obstacles encountered in project implementation, and the strategic 
roadmap envisaged for the upcoming fiscal year. 
4.  The Secretary, Ministry of Tourism commenced her presentation with a detailed account 
of its budgetary allocations and expenditure trends. It was noted that for the 2023 -24 fiscal year, 
an initial allocation of ₹2,400 crore was subsequently revised to ₹1,692 crore, y et actual 
expenditure stood at only ₹801 crore. Similarly, for 2024 -25, the budget initially set at ₹2,479 
crore was later reduced to ₹850 crore, with the Ministry reporting an expenditure of just 51% of 
the revised amount. The Ministry attributed this to delays in project approvals, the restructuring 
of tourism schemes, procedural inefficiencies arising from the transition from Swadesh Darshan 
1.0 to 2.0., and challenges in transitioning to the Treasury Single Account (TSA) system, which 
temporarily disrup ted fund disbursement. Moreover, the Ministry cited COVID -19-induced 
disruptions, which had a lasting impact on infrastructure development and capital expenditure in 
the tourism sector. The Ministry also cited to the long gestation period of infrastructure  projects, 
which naturally led to slower financial outflows. 
5. Alongside financial matters, the Ministry elaborated on the current state of the tourism 
sector, highlighting significant growth in both domestic and international tourism post-pandemic. 
In 20 23, international tourist arrivals reached 0.95 crore, reflecting a 47.9% increase over the 

76 
 
previous year. Domestic tourism also experienced an upward trend a 45% increase over the 
previous year. Despite these encouraging trends, officials acknowledged tha t the country still 
lags behind leading global tourism destinations, necessitating more aggressive international 
marketing efforts, improvements in ease of travel, and substantial infrastructural enhancements. 
The Committee was also briefed on the status o f key tourism infrastructure projects, including 
Swadesh Darshan 2.0, the PRASHAD Scheme, and the Challenge -Based Destination 
Development initiative, which collectively aimed to improve destination -specific tourism 
infrastructure. However, concerns were raised about the overlapping objectives of these schemes, 
prompting a call for greater synergy between them to enhance their effectiveness. 
6.  During the course of the deliberations, Members  raised several concerns and sought 
clarifications on the following issues: 
 Members expressed serious concerns over persistent underutilisation of allocated funds , 
with some projects seeing only 20% of the sanctioned budget spent.  
 Members sought an impact assessment study  on past investments  and questioned the 
reasons be hind the frequent downward revision of budgets  and whether the Treasury 
Single Account (TSA) system was causing financial delays. 
 Members also expressed dissatisfaction over persistent precedural delays  in finalising 
projects under Swadesh Darshan 2.0 and PRASHAD. 
 Members highlighted poor upkeep of government -built tourism infrastructure , with 
abandoned or underutilised tourism facilities  in several states  There were also calls for 
prioritising maintenance of existing sites over developing new destinations. 
 Members sought data on the impact of promotional campaigns , questioning whether they 
translated into higher foreign tourist arrivals.  
 Concerns were also raised over exorbitant hotel prices  discouraging tourists and 
suggested better regulation of accommodation costs.  
 Members also called for a structured feedback mechanism to improve tourist experiences 
and service quality. 
 Members inquired about inter-ministerial coordination with the Ministries of Railways 
and Civil Aviation to improve connectivity to remote destinations.  
 Concerns were also raised over the lack of private sector participation in tourism 
infrastructure and asked the Ministry to identify investment barriers and enhance public -
private partnerships. 
 Members raised concerns over the duplication of efforts between Swadesh Darshan and 
PRASHAD schemes, urging greater synergy and fund optimisation. 
 There were concerns over the slow progress in developing tourism circuits with 
integrated transport linkages, affecting the overall tourist experience. 
 Members also raised queries on eco-tourism policies, questioning what measures were in 
place to ensure sustainability while expanding tourism footprints.

7.  To address these concerns effectively, the Committee made the following actionable 
recommendations: 
 Implementation of  quarterly reviews and a real -time tracking system to ensure timely 
expenditure. 
 Reducing procedural delays and finalization of scheme guidelines promptly. 
 Prioritising existing tourism sites  by allocating more funds for the upkeep and 
maintenance of existing tourism sites instead of launching new destinations. 
 Encouraging public-private partnerships for tourism infrastructure and maintenance. 
 Improveming global marketing efforts and streamline visa policies to attract more foreign 
tourists. 
 Improving the last-mile connectivity by coordinating with the Ministries of Railways and 
Civil Aviation, so as to ensure seamless travel to key destinations. 
 Establishing a struct ured feedback mechanism for tourists, regulate accommodation 
pricing, and strengthen safety measures such as deploying dedicated tourist police in 
high-footfall areas.
