INTRODUCTION
         I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism & Culture,
 having been authorised by the Committee to present on its behalf, do hereby present this Seventy-second Report of the
 Committee on the Action Taken by the Government on the recommendations/observations contained in its Sixtieth
 Report on Development of Tourism in the Country.
2.      The Sixtieth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Transport, Tourism and
 Culture was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 21
st November 2002.
 
3.      Action Taken Replies on the recommendations of the Committee contained in its Sixtieth Report were received
 from the Department of Tourism on 5th March 2003.
 
4.      The Committee considered the subject and adopted the Report in its meeting held on 29th October 2003.

R E P O R T
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Government on the observations/
 recommendations as contained in its Sixtieth Report on Development of Tourism in the Country, which was presented,
 to the Parliament on 21st November 2002.
2.         Action Taken Replies have been received from the Government on 5 th March 2003 in respect of all the
 observations/recommendations contained in the Report, which have been categorised as follows:-

3.         The Committee desires that final replies in respect of the recommendations, which have been categorized
 as interim replies by the Committee should be furnished to it at the earliest.
4.         On scrutiny of the said Action Taken Replies, the Committee  has made observations on each item of Action
 taken/proposed to be taken by the Department.  The recommendations of the Committee contained in the Sixtieth
 Report, action taken thereon by the Government, and recommendations/observations of the Committee on each item of
 action taken has been given in the succeeding paragraphs.
 
SINGLE WINDOW CLEARANCE SYSTEM
RECOMMENDATION (Para 8)

5.         The Committee had, in its 48 th Report on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Department of Tourism,
 expressed the view that the Government, through its own limited resources, has not been able to provide the requisite
 funds for the development of tourist infrastructure in the country.  Accordingly, the Committee had recommended for
 early implementation of the Single Window Clearance System.  The Committee is of the opinion that the participation
 of the private sector can go a long way in supplementing the efforts of the Government in developing the tourist
 infrastructure in the country and the private sector takes into consideration long-term perspective in facilities required
 therefor.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Central/State/UT Governments must try to remove
 the obstacles faced by the private investors and ensure early implementation of the Single Window Clearance System
 for tourism projects. 
 
Reply of the Government
 
6.         The Department of Tourism is committed towards the development of tourism infrastructure in the country and
 encourages participation of the private sector in supplementing the efforts of the Government in their endeavour for
 developing tourist infrastructure of international standards.  From time to time, the Department of Tourism has taken up
 the issue with the State/UT Governments to facilitate the private investor by the way of reducing/removing the hassles
 in obtaining various clearances/NOCs by implementing a Single Window Clearance System.  Accordingly, the
 Department had sent a communication in March 2001 to all the State/UTs regarding the Single Window Clearance
 facility for tourism projects.  In response the Government of West Bengal has stated that such arrangement has not been
 provided in their State.  Again in April and November, 2002 all the State Governments/UTs have been reminded to
 create Single Window System for clearance of the projects pending with the State instead of multi-agencies.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
7.         The Committee recommends that the Department of Tourism should take up the matter with all the
 States/UTs for implementation of the Single window Clearance System for tourism projects so as to remove the
 hurdles faced by the private investors in development of the tourist infrastructure in the Country.  The
 Committee notes the efforts made by the Department of Tourism towards development of tourism in the country
 and participation of the Private Sector in Development of Tourism infrastructure of international standards.
 
LACK OF VISA-ON-ARRIVAL SCHEME
RECOMMENDATION (Para 11)
 
8.         The Committee agrees that the security considerations of the country cannot be compromised.  However, it must
 also be admitted that granting visa-on-arrival to the foreign tourists at the airports can certainly give a boost to the
 foreign tourist arrivals in the country.  The Committee is of the view that a step in this direction can be taken by
 initially introducing visa-on-arrival for tourists from friendly countries, countries which do not pose a security threat to
 our country and are tourism generating markets.  Later, if the scheme is successful and helps in foreign tourist influx to
 the country, it can be extended to other countries too on a case-to-case basis.
 
Reply of the Government
 
9.         The Ministry of Home Affairs have informed that the scheme of visa-on-Arrival cannot be accepted due to
 current security scenario prevailing in the country.
 
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
10.       The Committee notes the reply of the Government.

RECOMMENDATION (Para 13)
 
11.       The Committee feels that the issue relating to the safety and security of the tourists, especially foreign tourists is
 very important and needs to be looked into seriously.  The Committee is of the view that incidents of harassment of
 foreign tourists bring a bad name to the country and have an adverse effect on the foreign tourist arrival.  Deployment
 of Special Tourism Police Force can be an effective tool for ensuring safety and security of the tourists.  The
 Committee appreciates the creation of the Special Tourism Police Force by some of the states.  The Committee also
 suggests that the police personnel of the States should also be provided special 'tourist-oriented' training.  Such form of
 official protection to international and domestic tourists would certainly instil confidence amongst the tourists visiting
 these territories.  A successful implementation of such measures will definitely encourage the other State Governments
 in implementing similar schemes for the promotion of tourism in their respective States.
 
Reply of the Government
 
12.       The Governments of Kerala, J&K, Rajasthan, Goa, Himachal Pradesh, Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh have
 set-up Tourist Police Force for the safety and security of the foreign tourists.
 
13.       Government of Delhi, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Chandigarh are in the process of setting
 up of Tourist Police Force.
 
14.       Some of the States/UTs e.g. Mizoram, Daman & Diu, Assam, Sikkim, Pondicherry, Lakshadweep have not felt
 the necessity of the Tourist Police Force and have instructed the regular police force to look into safety and security of
 the foreign tourists.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
15.       The Committee notes the reply of the Government.
 
RATIONALISATION OF TAXES ON THE TOURISM INDUSTRY
RECOMMENDATION (Para 19)  
 
16.       The Committee feels that the hotel industry is the backbone of tourism.  The major share of tourism revenue to
 the government is contributed by this sector.  However, there are a lot of taxes and sometimes duplication of taxes at
 both Central and State levels on the hotel sector.  The multiple taxes have imposed a heavy financial burden on the
 hotel industry.  The Committee observes that taxes on hotel rooms and food levied by both the Centre and the States
 add upto over 45 percent, making the industry grossly uncompetitive.  The Committee is of the view that there ought to
 be coordination and synergy between the Centre and the State Governments on the tax front and that there is an urgent
 need to rationalize both Central and State taxes on tourism.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Central
 Government/State Governments must come forward with rationalisation of taxes and provide concessions for the hotel
 industry to give a fillip to the arrival of the tourists in the States.  Further there should be uniformity in the rate of
 various taxes levied by the State Governments on the hotel industry.
 
Reply of the Government
 
17.       The Department of Tourism has been taking steps for the rationalization of taxes levied by the Central
 Government and the State Governments. 
 
18.       Several taxes on the hotel industry like Luxury Tax and Sales Tax are levied by the State Governments.  These
 taxes coupled with the Expenditure Tax levied by the Central Govt. impose a heavy burden on the hotel industry, which
 is passed on to the occupants, thereby increasing the cost of accommodation.
 
19.       In the meeting of Chief Ministers and Tourism Ministers of all States held on 30.10.2001, the State Governments
 were requested to rationalise these taxes.  Again in a meeting held on 5.9.2002 of all the Tourism Ministers and the
 tourism Secretaries, this issue was again taken up with the State Governments.

20.       As regards the levies of the Central Governments, the issue was taken up with the Ministry of Finance in the
 Department of Tourism’s Pre-budget Memorandum for the year 2002-03.  As a result following measures were
 announced in the Budget 2002-03:-
 
Expenditure Tax:

 on room charges as well as other services. Threshold of Rs.2000 raised to Rs.3000 for levy of

 where the entire bill is for catering services, no service tax would be charged.
 
80HHC and 80 HHD:
Disparities between 80HHC AND 80 HHD removed.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
21.       The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Department of Tourism towards rationalisation of
 taxes levied by the Central Government and State Government.  The Committee also appreciates the measures
 announced by the Government in the Budget 2002-03 though State Governments have not yet taken any decision
 on rationalisation of taxes.  The Committee recommends that Department of Tourism should pursue the State
 Governments for rationalisation of different taxes and provide incentives for the hotel Industry so as to boost
 this Industry at large and to generate tourist arrivals.
 
RATIONALISATION OF TAXES ON THE TOURISM INDUSTRY
RECOMMENDATION (Para 20)
 
22.       The Committee observes that there is a lack of uniformity in taxes, laws and regulation across the States.  While
 the State Governments are taking steps to develop tourism in their respective states, the lack of coordination among
 them and the higher rates of road tax/disparity in the taxes in the states, especially southern States have had negative
 effect on the growth of tourism in these states.  These factors also result in hardships for the tourists as also increase the
 cost of travel for the tourists.  The Committee therefore, recommends that there should be a composite transport permit
 for tourist vehicles.  The Committee also recommends that the Central and the State Governments should take
 immediate steps to create uniformity between the local vehicles and the visiting vehicles.  Steps should also be taken to
 facilitate easy travel and free inter-State movement for tourist vehicles.
 
Reply of the Government
 
23.       As regards the taxes on the travel sector, these taxes are levied by the respective State Governments.  Not only
 are the rates of these levies are very high, but even the mode of levy varies from State to State.
 
24.       The Department of tourism took up this matter with the State Government. in the Tourism Minister’s
 Conference.
 
25.       The matter was also taken up with the Ministry of Highways and Road Transport.  They had included this
 subject as an agenda in the Transport Development Council attended by the Transport Ministers and the Transport
 Secretaries of the State Governments.  Though this matter was discussed a consensus could not be arrived at to resolve
 the issue.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE

TOURISM DEVELOPMENT IN ANDAMAN & NICOBAR AND LAKSHADWEEP ISLANDS
 
RECOMMENDATION (Para 24)
 
27.       The Committee feels that Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands have the potential of growing into top
 destinations for beach tourism and water sports.  But for the constraint of transportation of tourists from the mainland,
 these islands would have attracted a still larger number of tourists, earning revenue and generating employment for the
 local islanders.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the work on the extension of runway at Port Blair may be
 completed at the earliest and the frequency of flights may also be increased so that more and more tourists are able to
 land there.  The Committee welcomes the decision of DGCA to permit the foreign tourist charter flights to land at Port
 Blair and recommends that this fact may be given wide publicity amongst national as well as international tour
 operators.  Similarly, round the year air services, well connected with other important centres like Kochi,
 Thiruvananthapuram and Goa may be introduced to give a boost to the development and promotion of tourism in the
 Lakshadweep Islands. Further, clearance to the proposals for construction of airfields at Kavarati, Minicoy, Andrott,
 Kadmat and Kiltan Islands should be expedited.  The Committee also feels that creating eco-friendly tourist
 infrastructure and water sports facility involves huge expenditure.  Due to the limited resources available to these two
 Union Territories, it may not be possible for the administration of these islands to create infrastructure and other
 facilities in the islands.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the administration of these islands should create
 conducive atmosphere for private investment and, thereafter, invite private sector participation in developing the
 necessary infrastructure, accommodation and high quality eco-friendly tourists/resorts.
 
Reply of the Government
 
28.       The Administrator of A&N Islands and Lakshadweep Islands/DGCA have been requested to develop
 infrastructure for the promotion of tourism and for creation of conducive atmosphere for private investment and invite
 private sector participation in developing the necessary infrastructure, accommodation and high quality eco-friendly
 tourist resorts but no reply has been received from the above mentioned departments.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
29.       The Committee notes the reply and recommends that the Department of Tourism should keep pursuing
 the matter vigorously with the administration of A&N Islands and Lakshadweep Islands.
 
PERMIT SYSTEM FOR THE TOURISTS
RECOMMENDATION (Paras 26 & 28)
 
30.       The Committee is of the view that foreign tourists constitute an important component of tourists who wish to
 visit the Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands.  The restrictions on foreigners to stay beyond permitted period
 in the Islands and visiting only selected islands has discouraged the foreigners from visiting these islands.  The
 Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to optimally exploit the potential of tourism in these islands, the
 restrictions on the foreigners on visiting the islands may be removed and they may be permitted to stay/enjoy the
 beauty of the islands for longer periods.  The Committee also feels that opening up new islands for foreigners also
 needs to be given priority and recommends that the Central Department of Tourism should take up the proposal
 regarding opening up of more islands for the foreign tourists with the Ministry of Home Affairs.
 
31.       The Committee appreciates the Government for having opened some areas of Nagaland for foreign tourists and

CONSTRAINT OF COASTAL REGULATION ZONE ACT
RECOMMENDATION (Para 30)
 
34.       The Committee is of the view that the impact of tourism on the environment and ecology requires serious
 consideration.  There is no doubt that unplanned tourism not only degrades the environment physically and otherwise
 but also carries the seeds of its own destruction.  However, the Committee feels that the provisions of CRZ Act do not
 take into account the unique features of the coastal lines of various States in the country, particularly of Andaman &
 Nicobar and Lakshadweep Islands and Kerala.  India has many beautiful beaches, which can be developed from
 tourism point of view.  The Committee recommends that the Government should, therefore, seriously consider relaxing
 the provisions of the Costal Regulation Zone Act.  The Ministry of Environment and Forests should also take a liberal
 view and consider positively the proposals seeking relaxation of CRZ restrictions for tourism projects.
 
Reply of the Government
 
35.       Department of Tourism, Government of India has taken up the issue with the Ministry of Environment & Forests
 to relax CRZ Regulations as it poses serious impediment in the development of beach tourism and have suggested that
 the Notification may be modified to enable State Governments to undertake tourism activities.  A recommendation has
 also been made that certain specified areas may be treated as Special Tourism economic Zones.
 
36.       In the case of Andaman & Nicobar Islands the Department of Tourism has proposed to constitute a High
 Powered Committee under the Chairmanship of Minister of Tourism & Culture comprising of the following members
 to discuss, finalise and approve all tourism projects relating to Andaman & Nicobar Islands:

LACK OF CONNECTIVITY
RECOMMENDATION (Para 32)
 
38.       The Committee feels that there exists a fairly large domestic tourist movement in the country.  However, one
 major stumbling block before the inter-state tourism is poor connectivity.  Domestic Tourism cannot be promoted
 unless the air, rail and road links across the various states and places of tourist interest in the country are improved.  In
 absence of a comfortable access to the places of tourist interest, the tourist cannot be encouraged to visit such places. 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends, that the tourist destinations in the country should be well connected by rail,
 road and air with the urban centres/cities of the region to ensure easy accessibility.  Also the movement of the trains
 should be regularised and speeded up.  The Committee also feels that tourism is closely interlinked with the Civil
 Aviation Sector.  Air linking of the various tourist places in the country as well as places abroad from where tourist
 arrival is expected is essential for development of tourism in the country.  The Committee also recommends that efforts
 should be made to explore the possibility of upgrading the Veer Sarvarkar Airport at Port Blair and Bagdogra Airport
 into international airports so that these places could find a place on the international tourist map.  Also, The Ministries
 of Civil Aviation and Railways should take steps for providing the necessary infrastructure and improve air and rail
 connectivity to the various states in the country.
 
Reply of the Government
 
39.       The matter has been taken up with the Director General Civil Aviation, Ministry of Railway for raising/boosting
 the connectivity in all the tourist destinations.  The Committee has recommended the need of upgrading the Veer
 Sarvarkar Airport at Port Blair and Bagdogra Airport into international airports so that these places could find a place
 on the International Tourists Map and this has also been taken up with the DGCA.  As and when the reply is received
 from the above authorities, the Standing Committee will be apprised of the action taken by the Ministry of Railways/the
 Director General Civil Aviation for increasing tourism connectivity to the tourist destinations as well as upgrading the
 domestic airports to international airports.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION/OBSERVATION OF THE COMMITTEE
 
40.       The Committee notes the reply and recommends the Department of Tourism to vigorously pursue the
 matter with the Ministry of Railways and the Director General Civil Aviation for increasing tourism
 connectivity to the tourist destinations as well as upgrading of domestic airports to international airports.
