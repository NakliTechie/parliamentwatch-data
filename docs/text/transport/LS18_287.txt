REPORT 
INTRODUCTION 
    Road Transport plays a critical role in the economic growth of any country. The 
necessity for the continued need for strengthening the road network in the country is 
underscored by the fact that the transport sector contributed abo ut 4.6 percent in the Gross 
Value Added (GVA) for F inancial Year 2018-19, of which the share of road transport was 
about 67 percent (Economic Survey 2020 -21, Volume -2). A robust, safe, well -maintained 
road network would be key for our country to overcome t he damaging impact of the 
Coronavirus pandemic on the national economy. 
2. The tasks and responsibilities of the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
encompass, inter alia , construction and maintenance of National Highways (NHs); 
administration of the Nati onal Highways Act,  1956; National Highways Authority of India 
Act, 1988; National Highways Fee (Determination of Rates and Collection) Rules, 2008; 
Motor Vehicles Act 1988; and Central Motor Vehicles Rules 1989, as also formulation of 
broad policies relating to road transport and automotive norms, besides making arrangements 
for movement of vehicular traffic with neighbouring countries. 
3. India has the second largest road network in the world of about 62.16 lakh km. This 
comprises National Highways, Expresswa ys, State Highways, Major District Roads, Other 
District Roads and Village Roads as under: 
National Highways 1,36,440 km 
State Highways 1,76,818 km 
Other Roads 59,02,539 km 
Total 62,15,797 km 
 
4. The associated offices under the Ministry of Road Transport  and Highways are as 
follows:  

2 
 
i. National Highways Authority of India (NHAI):   
Set up through an Act of Parliament in 1988, NHAI is responsible for the 
development, maintenance and management of the National Highways entrusted to it 
and for matters related or incidental there to. The NHAI became operational in 
February, 1995. 
ii. National Highways & Infrastructure Development Corporation Ltd. (NHIDCL) : 
NHIDCL is a corporate entity under the Ministry, responsible for the construction/ up-
grading/widening of Natio nal Highways in parts of the country which share 
international boundaries with neighbouring countries in order to promote regional 
connectivity on a sustainable basis. 
iii. Indian Academy of Highway Engineers (IAHE):  
The Indian Academy of Highway Engineers (IA HE) is a registered Society under the 
administrative control of the Ministry. It is a collaborative body which was set up in 
the year 1983 with the objective of fulfilling the long felt need for training of 
Highway Engineers in the country, both at the ent ry level and during their service 
period. 
iv. Indian Road Congress (IRC):  
The IRC was formally registered as a Society on 24 th September 1937 under the 
Societies Registration Act of 1860 for periodical holding of road conferences to 
discuss issues related to road construction, maintenance & development.

7. The Ministry, in its Annual Report (2020-21), has informed that the year 2020-21 was 
a year for consolidating the gains that have accrued f rom major policy decisions taken in the 
previous five years, a time for monitoring of ongoing projects, tackling road - blocks and 
adding to the already impressive pace of work achieved last year. The Ministry took a 
decision to complete all ongoing projects that had been awarded upto 2015-16, and placed the 
highest ever target of construction of at least 11,000 kms of National Highways, while 
aspiring to upscale the construction of about 12,000 kms of National Highways as against 
10,237 kms achieved during  2019-20. Overall road projects exceeding 55,000 km in length, 
costing more than Rs. 6.26 lakh crore, are in progress. National Highways of 7,767 km length 
have been completed in the first nine months of FY2020 -21, as against 6,940 km for the 
corresponding period during the last financial year. The Ministry has scaled new heights in 
expanding the Highway infrastructure throughout the country, despite nation -wide lockdown 
due to Pandemic COVID-19. 
8. The Committee is happy to note that significant increase in a llocation has been 
made to the Ministry at the Revised Estimates (R.E.) stage in 2020 -21 and commends 
the Ministry for managing a good  pace of utilization under major heads such as NH(O) 
and Capital Outlay for NE Region.

ROAD DEVELOPMENT 
25. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has been entrusted with the responsibility 
of development of Road Transport and Highways in general and construction & 
maintenance of National Highways (NHs) in particular. All roads other than National 
Highways in the states fall within the jurisdiction of respective State Governments. In 
order to assist the State Governments in the development of state roads, Central 
Government also provides financial assistance out of the Central Ro ad Fund (CRF) and 
Inter State Connectivity and Economic Importance (ISC & EI) scheme. Besides National 
Highways development Project (NHDP) & National Highway Inter -connectivity 
Improvement programme (NHIIP), Ministry is implementing SARDP -NE & LWE 
schemes which includes National Highways and State roads. The Ministry is also 
responsible for evolving standards and specifications for roads and bridges in the country 
besides acting as a repository of technical information on roads and bridges.

National Highways of 34,800 kms length over a period of 5 years (2017-18 to 2021-22) at

gaps through effective interventions like development of Economic Corridors, Inter 
Corridors and Feeder  Routes, National Corridor Efficiency Improvement, Border and 
International Connectivity roads, Coastal and Port Connectivity roads and Green -field 
expressways. Multi-modal integration is also built into this program. Special attention has 
been paid to ful filling the connectivity needs of backward and tribal areas, areas of 
economic activity, places of religious and tourist interest, border areas, coastal areas and 
trade routes with neighbouring countries.

Quality Inspection 
60. Quality of the construction activities are being checked at site timely as per specifications 
of MoRTH by the Authority Engineer and by concerned Officers posted at Site and at the 
Regional Offices. Poor quality  work found (if any) is immediately rectified by the EPC 
contractor. NHAI has implemented a system for Quality inspection of NHAI projects 
during construction by engaging Retired Officials from State PWD, MoRTH, BRO etc. 
Suitable actions are also initiated  against defaulting Contractors/Consultants.In addition 
to that, a panel of Independent Special Project Monitors (SPMs) have been set up by 
NHIDCL and the SPMs have been engaged from time to time to check the quality of 
work at Site. 
61. A Quality Control Zone has also been created in the Ministry in September, 2020 headed 
by Chief Engineer with an objective that Quality Control Zone will work towards the 

24 
 
preparation of policy guidelines as well as examine and issue directions/suggestions for 
system improvement on Quality Assurance/Control for National Highway works as well 
as Centrally Sponsored Schemes among executive agencies of this Ministry. 
62.  Accordingly, Quality zone has issued detailed guidelines and Standard Operating 
Procedure for Quality Control/Assurance in construction of National Highways and other 
centrally sponsored projects vide circular dated 01.10.2020 in which it is delegated that 
Quality Control Zone may select any project of the Ministry/NHAI/NHIDCL/BRO on 
random basis to inspect the records/documents related to quality control/assurance & may 
also carry out certain tests in the presence of stakeholders to check the quality of the 
National Highways. Based on such inspection of Quality Control Zone, 
instructions/suggestions will be issued to th e PIUs/ PWD Divisions/ RO Offices for 
necessary compliance. 
63. In compliance to the above, Quality Control Zone has inspected 17 nos. of NHAI/PWD

64. The Committee takes note of  the various initiatives taken by the Ministry/NHAI to

stringent quality check mechanisms to ensure high quality construction of roads 
across the country , with specific reference to levelings and smoothness, and also to

117. The Ministry, in its written reply, informed the Committee that road accidents are 
occurring due to multiple ca uses such as over speeding, drunken driving/consumption of 
alcohol, driving on wrong side/lane indiscipline, fault of the driver of motor vehicle, use 
of mobile phones during driving, mechanical defect/fault of the vehicle defect in road 
condition, weather condition and lack of knowledge on road signage etc. These various 
factors can broadly be categorized into human error, road condition/environment and 
vehicular condition that lead to accidents on roads  
118. The Ministry has formulated a multi -pronged strateg y to address the issue of Road 
Safety based on Education, Engineering (both of roads and vehicles), Enforcement and 
Emergency Care. Accordingly, various initiatives have been taken by the Ministry as 
detailed below:-  
(i) Education:  
A.  The Ministry implemen ts a scheme for undertaking publicity measures and 
awareness campaigns on road safety to create awareness among road users 
through the electronic media, print media, NGOs etc.  
B. Observance of National Road Safety Week/Road Safety Month every year for 
spreading awareness and strengthening road safety.  
C. A Certification Course for Road Safety Auditors has been commenced in 
Indian Academy of Highway Engineers (IAHE).  
(ii) Engineering (both of Roads and vehicles)  
A.  Road engineering  
a) High priority to identificati on and rectification of black spots (accident 

42 
 
prone spots) on national highways.  
b) Road safety has been made an integral part of road design at planning 
stage.  
c) The threshold for four laning of National Highways has been reduced 
from 15,000 Passenger Car Units (PCUs) to 10,000 PCUs.  
d) Ministry has delegated powers to Regional Officers of MORTH for 
technical approval to the detailed estimates for rectification of identified 
Road Accident black spots.  
e) Guidelines for pedestrian facilities on National Highways f or persons 
with disabilities have also been issued to all States / UTs.  
B. Vehicle engineering:  
a) Safety standards for automobiles have been improved.  
b) Ministry has notified fitment of Speed Limiting devices on all transport 
vehicles  
c) Scheme for setting up on e model Inspection & Certification Centre in 
each State/UT with Central assistance for testing the fitness of vehicles 
through an automated system  
(iii) Enforcement  
A. The recently passed motor vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 provides for strict

rules.  
B. Rules for protection of Good Samaritans as per MV (Amendment) Act, 2019 
have been published.  
(iv) Emergency care:  
A. The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 provides for a scheme for the

Road Safety 
The Committee takes note of the various initiatives taken/being taken by the Ministry to 
address the issue of road safety in the country. The statistics give a snapshot regarding 
road accidents in the country to suggest that although there is a consistent increase in 
the number of persons getting killed in road accidents, the number of road accidents 
taking place is witnessing a decrease. The Committee is apprehensive whether the trend 
indicates a decrease in the reporting of road accidents across the country or the severity 
of the road accidents occurring in the country has increased over time. The Committee 

78 
 
finds that a  significant percentage of these deaths belongs to working age individuals 
which might be pushing the livelihood of many fa milies at stake, causing serious 
financial trouble/ poverty. The Committee recommends the Ministry to target zero 
accidents on account of faults in road design and construction. The Ministry should 
take up identification and rectification of black spots on  a war footing, across the 
country. The Committee further recommends the Ministry to conduct research and 
analyze into why these black spots existed in the road projects in the first place and how 
the same can be avoided from the DPR preparation stage itself in the upcoming projects 
of the Ministry. 
(Para 121) 
The Committee takes note of the 2021 World Bank Report titled “Traffic Crash 
Injuries and Disabilities: The Burden on Indian Society”  which states that India tops 
the world in road crash deaths, contributing to 11% of all road accident deaths 
worldwide, despite accounting for only 1% of the world’s veh icles. The 2019 World 
Bank Report “Guide for Road Safety Opportunities and Challenges: Low - and Middle-
Income Countries Country Profiles” estimates the road crash and serious injury cost 
estimate at 7.5% of India’s GDP. The Committee is of the considered v iew that the 
observations made in World Bank Report should serve as a stark reminder of the 
gravity of the situation and a sense of urgency with which the Ministry should flag Road 
Safety as top priority, right from the DPR stage of a NH project. 
(Para 122) 
World Bank Report further highlights that as high as 44% of the households in rural 
areas reported at least one death after a road crash, compared to 11.6% of the 
households in urban areas. Similarly, Low Income Households reported twice the 
number of de aths post -crash vis-à-vis High Income Households. The Committee feels 
that this striking difference might be because of the difference in the quality of medical 
care available to the victims of road accidents. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry sho uld work in close coordination with the Ministry of Health and Family 
Welfare, to ensure that free quality medical help is provided to road accident victims 
belonging to Low -Income Households. The Committee recommends that this may be 
partially funded by the penalties collected for the violation of traffic Rules.

III. Oral Evidence of Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
(The Committee re-assembled after lunch break) 
13. At the outset, the Chairman welcomed the Secretary, Ministry of Road Transport and 
Highways and other senior officials to the meeting. The Chairman, inter alia, requested the 
Secretary to make a presentation on the Demands for Grants (2021 -22) with respect to the 
performance of the Ministry in 2020-21 and the budgetary allocations made to the Ministry in 
Budget Estimates 2021-22. 
14. The Secretary made a detailed presentation on the salient features of the Demands for 
Grants (2021-22). He informed the Committee that the Ministry had projected a demand of ₹ 
1,40,234/- crores, whereas, the Ministry of Finance has allocated only ₹ 1,18,101/ - crores at 
the B.E. stage in 2021 -22. He further apprised the Committee that the Ministry has spent ₹ 
74,286/- crores (till 31 st January, 2021) against the allocated Revised Estimates of ₹ 
1,01,823/- crores for the year 2020-21. He also highlighted that National Highways Authority 
of India (NHAI) has raised a total of ₹ 41,435/- crores through Internal & External Budgetary 
(IEBR) sources in the Financial Year 2020 -21, till 31 st January, 2021 and that NHAI would 
be authorized to raise ₹ 65,000/- crores for 2021-22.  
15. Elaborating the physical achievements, the Secretary stated that the length of National 
Highways (NHs) has been increased from 91,287 km (as on April, 2014) to 1,36,521 km at 
present whereas a total length of 1,76,797 km and 59,02,539 km has been constructed on 
State Highways and Other Roads respectively, as on date. He informed the Committee that 
the fund allocation for development of NHs has increased by a rate of about 27% since F.Y. 
2014-15 and stands at ₹ 1,73,000 crores approximately (including IEBR), for FY 2021-22. 
16. The Secretary elucidated upon the fact that during 2020 -21 (as of January,  2021) 
under the ‘Bharatmala Pariyojna’, a length of 2,517 km highways has been awarded, out of 
which, 2,273 km highways have been constructed. He also narrated that for the National

investments, wherein NHAI has revised the Model Concession Agreement (MCA) for Build-
