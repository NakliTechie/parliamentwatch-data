R E P O R T
The Department of Road Transport and Highways under the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways has been entrusted primarily
 with the functions of construction and maintenance of National Highways (NH), administration of Motor Vehicles Act and Central Motor Vehicles
 Rules, formulation of broad policies relating to road transport, environmental issues, automotive norms, etc., besides making arrangements for
 movements of vehicular traffic with neighboring countries. There are two Wings in the department, namely, the Road Transport Wing and the Roads
 Wing.
2.         The Road Transport Wing of the Ministry is concerned with the formulation of broad policies related to road transport besides making
 arrangements for the movement of vehicular traffic with the neighbouring countries.  The following Acts/Rules which embody the policy relating to
 motor vehicles and State Transport Corporations are being administered in the Road Transport Wing of the Ministry:-
 
·
        The Motor Vehicles Act 1988
·        The Central Motor Vehicles Rules 1989
·        The Road Transport Corporations Act 1950
·        The Carriers Act, 1865
3.         Given the staggering number of vehicles of the roads, there has been growing concern about environmental fallouts and safety issues.  Stringent
 emission norms have been codified and are being enforced.
4.         About 65 per cent of freight and 80 per cent passenger traffic is carried by the roads.  Although National Highways constitute only about 2 per
 cent of the road network, it carries      40 per cent of the total road traffic.   The number of vehicles has been growing at an average pace of 10.16 per
 cent per annum over the last five years (1998-1999 to 2002-03). Whereas the share of road in total traffic has grown from 13.8 per cent of freight traffic
 and 15.4 per cent of passenger traffic in 1950-51 to an estimated 65 per cent of freight traffic and 80 per cent of passenger traffic by the end of 2003-
04. The rapid expansion and strengthening of the road network, therefore, is imperative, to provide for both present and future traffic and for improved
 accessibility to the hinterland.  In addition, road transport needs to be regulated for better energy efficiency, lesser pollution and enhanced road safety. 
 While the Central Sector programmes pertain mainly to the National Highways, the responsibility for development of other categories of roads vests
 with the Sates/Union Territories. 
5.         The Roads Wing, headed by the Director General (Road Development) & Special Secretary, is concerned mainly with matters relating to (i)
 advising Government of all general policy matters relating to National Highways (ii) development and maintenance of roads declared as National
 Highways (iii) development and maintenance of roads other than National Highways in Union Territories (iv) administration of Central Road Fund
 (CRF) pertaining to State roads other than rural roads (v) evaluation and specification of standards for roads and bridges (vi) research and development
 in the road sector.
6.         The Central Government is responsible for development and maintenance of National Highways.  The development and maintenance of
 National highways are being implemented on agency basis.  Out of the total length of 65,569 km of National Highways, 44,217 km is with the State
 Public Works Departments (State PWD), 15,312 km with the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) and 5,090 km with Border Roads
 Organisation (BRO).  Balance length of 950 km is yet to be entrusted to executing agencies.  Gradually, more stretches will be entrusted to NHAI as
 additional work of National Highways, when approved projects are taken up.  Roads Wing is also administering the following Acts:
 
1.
      The National Highways Act, 1956.
2.      The National Highways Authority of India Act, 1988.
3.      The Central Road Fund Act, 2000.
4.      The Control of National Highways (Land & Traffic) Act, 2002.

19.       The Committee observed that during 2005-06, the Plan allocation of Rs.12130.36 crore at BE stage in respect of Department of Road Transport
 and Highways was reduced to Rs.11318.00 crore at RE stage.  The Committee desired to know the reasons therefor and the impact of this reduction on
 the projects that were proposed to be executed during 2005-06.  The Department of Road Transport and Highways reported to the Committee that:- 
            “The major areas of shortfall are:
(a)
     NHDP Phase III:  For this programme, the BE provision was Rs.1400 crore and at the RE stage it was reduced to Rs.700 crore. While
 the BE projection was made in the month of December, 2004, the approval for the programme was given only in March, 2005.  This
 apart, for this BOT driven programme the Government support is by way of land acquisition, utility shifting and most importantly
 viability gap funding.  While part of the funds could be utilized for land acquisition and utility shifting, the funds earmarked for
 viability gap funding could not be used as most of the entrepreneurs had not achieved financial closure, which is the essential
 requirement before any fund could be released to them. Further, even in respect of projects which were awarded, the outgo on account
 of viability gap funding was less than what was projected because of  lower than 40% or negative grant being quoted.
(b)
     Externally Aided Project of the Ministry:   The Ministry was implementing the ADB assisted programme for developing NH-34 in the
 State of West Bengal.  This project was initially planned for development as 2-lane project with paved shoulders.  Subsequently, after
 discussions with Government of West Bengal and keeping in view the changed traffic profile, it was decided to develop it as a 4-lane
 facility.  Accordingly, the entire project is being redrawn with the result the provisions of Rs.200 crore for this project in BE 2005-06
 was scaled down to Rs.4.00 crore at the RE stage.”
20.       As regards the utilization of funds allocated to the Department, the Committee observed that out of the RE (2005-06) Plan allocation of Rs.

 reasons for the variation in Budgetary allocation, the Department has intimated as under:-

 expenditure will continue and the projected BE allocation will be fully utilized.”

23.       The Committee finds that despite its earlier recommendations on the aspect of funds utilization and the need for constant monitoring of projects,
 the Department of Road Transport and Highways has done precious little to improve the situation.   This is evident from the reply of the Department
 with regard to non-utilisation of funds that the allocation for road sector is based on anticipated implementation of projects every year.  The trend from
 the year 2002-03 onwards would reveal that the actual utilization has been more or less steady and consistent.  The slippages occur when major
 projects do not get implemented for the years they are projected for implementation.  For instance, allocations for programmes of NHDP Phase III and
 SARDP NE did not materialize fully because the approvals were not available in the time-frame projected.  The Committee desires to know as to why
 approvals were not available in time and whether any responsibility has been fixed in the matter.   The Committee observes that the budgetary
 allocation of the Department of Road Transport and Highways was substantially increased from 2004-05 (Revised Estimates) of Rs.8566.00 crore to
 Rs.13293.75 crore during 2005-06 and for   2006-07 (Budget Estimates) the allocation has again been increased to Rs.15307.31 crore. The Committee,
 therefore, re-emphasises that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should devise strategies, in such a way so that they are able to fully
 utilize the funds placed at their disposal.  They should also evolve some mechanism to identify bottlenecks causing delays and work towards resolving
 all such hindrances in coordination with all the concerned agencies.  This would also help them to avoid time overrun and consequential cost overrun
 of the projects to be executed by them.
24.       The Committee notes that in the Mid-Term Appraisal of the 10 th Five Year Plan, it is recommended that the Ministry of Shipping, Road
 Transport and Highways should prepare a detailed programme for the next two years keeping in view the level of budgetary support available and the
 need for leveraging to the maximum extent by adopting a proper mix of engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) and Build Operate and
 Transfer (BOT) projects.   In view of the repeated under-utilization of Budget allocation year after year, the Committee feels that the Department
 should not feel aggrieved by the fact that the Budget provision for 2006-07 has been enhanced by only 10% from the Budget provision for the year
 2005-06 despite enhancement of 11.2% in the cost of construction during 2005 following increase in price indices of the basic ingredients of the road
 sector – steel, cement and bitumen. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should devise
 effective institutional mechanisms to constantly monitor the progress of the projects under implementation and to ensure optimum utilization of funds.
 This mechanism should also involve means to fix responsibility at Project Head Level for the delay in completion of projects thereby acting as a
 deterrent for cost and time over-runs.  The Committee recommends that the Department should also explore the possibility of opening of more
 Budget/Accounting Offices keeping in view the increasing number of projects under implementation.
NATIONAL HIGHWAY DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (NHDP)  
25.       One of the most ambitious project launched in road sector in independent India is the National Highway Development Programme (NHDP)
 comprising the 5,846-km Golden Quadrilateral (GQ); the 7,300-km North-South, East-West (NS-EW) corridors; four-laning of about 1,000 km of road
 network that includes port connectivity of 400 km and other projects of 600 km.  NHDP is mainly being executed by the National Highways Authority
 of India, which was established under NHAI Act, 1988.  The Central Government from time to time, by Notification in the Official Gazette, has
 entrusted to the Authority stretches of National Highways. The Programme is being implemented in phases.  Under Phase I & Phase II of this project,
 13,146 km of National Highways are proposed to be four or six laned at an estimated cost of Rs.54,000 crore (at 1999 prices).
26.       NHDP Phase-I comprises of Golden Quadrilateral (GQ), North-South and East-West (NSEW) Corridors and Port connectivity and other
 projects.  The total length in Phase-I is 7498 km i.e. 5846 km of GQ, 981 km of NSEW Corridors and 671 km of Port connectivity and other projects. 
 Up to 31
st  December 2005, the GQ has been completed to the extent of 5154 km (88.2%).  It is expected that 96 % of the GQ will be completed by
 June 2006.  Only those works, which were awarded late due to delay in finalization of alignment i.e. Allahabad bypass (one contract awarded in June
 2004 and the other in November 2004) and the work on terminated contracts, will remain incomplete beyond June 2006.  797 km of NSEW Corridors
 (81%) have been completed as on 31
st  December 2005.  In respect of Port connectivity and other projects, 386 km (56%) have been completed as on
 31st  December 2005.  The total completed length of NHDP Phase-I as on 31st  December 2005 is 6337 km (84.5%).
27.       NHDP Phase II comprises 7300-km North-South and East-West corridors (NSEW), which connects Srinagar in the North to Kanyakumari in the
 South (including the Kochi-Salem spur) and Silchar in the East to Porbandar in the West. The work on 557 kms has already been completed and the
 remaining length of the NSEW corridors is targeted to be awarded by next year. The deadline for the completion of the NSEW is December, 2007. In
 addition, the NHAI is also implementing the four laning of 363 km to provide connectivity to 10 major ports. It is expected that all the works except
 the award of 201 km of East West Corridor in West Bengal and 318 km in North South Corridor in Jammu & Kashmir will be delayed because of the
 re-alignment of East West Corridor in West Bengal and difficulties of terrain and technical complexities in Jammu & Kashmir.
28.       NHDP Phase III:Under this Programme, 10,000 kms of National Highways is being    4/6 laned on BOT basis.  This programme is being taken
 up in two phases.  In Phase III (A) 4000 kms of National Highways will be taken up for improvement and in Phase III ( B), the balance 6000 kms will
 be taken up.  The total estimated cost of the project for 10,000 kms is Rs.55,000 crore out of which Rs.25,000 crore will be Budgetary support which
 includes Rs.5,000 crore of Land acquisition and other pre project activities.  In Phase III (A), the estimated cost for 4/6 laning is Rs.22,000 crore out of
 which viability gap funding is estimated to be Rs.8000 crore, the expenditure on land acquisition and other pre project activities is estimated to be
 Rs.2000 crore.  The project is expected to be completed in 7 years commencing from 2004-05.  The current year’s allocation for this programme and 6
 laning / By passes / expressways  is Rs.110 crore.
29.       The NHAI is implementing several projects with foreign assistance in the form of loans from the World Bank, the Asian Development Bank
 (ADB) and the Japan Bank of International Cooperation (JBIC). The loan amounts agreed upon is $1,345 million (about Rs.5918 crore), $985 million
 (about Rs.4334 crore) and yen 32,060 million (about Rs.1335.29 crore) respectively.
30.       Funding of NHAI Projects: NHAI depends on Government support for implementing most of its projects, whether it is by way of Capital

ENCROACHMENT
58.       As per the information available with the Committee, there are encroachments on most of the National Highways particularly near urban
 localities. Removal of encroachments is a continuous process and State Government concerned is taking necessary action for removal of
 encroachments whenever these are noticed. As per thePolicy, no financial assistance is provided to the State Government for rehabilitation of
 theencroachers. However, the guidelines of World Bank and Asian Development Bank provide for extending assistance to ‘vulnerable encroachers’,
 which includes SCs, STs, handicapped and people falling below poverty line .  The Committee enquired about the steps taken by the Department of
 Road Transport & Highways to curtail encroachment of the land under the National Highways.  The Department has replied that efforts are made to
 remove the encroachments on the lands pertaining to National Highways from time to time with the help of district authorities. Further, the
 Government has enacted the Control of National Highways (Land & Traffic)  Act, 2002, to remove encroachments on NHs.  This Act has come into
 force with effect from 27.1.2005.  Under the provisions of the Act, Government has set up 192 National Highway Administrations at various locations
 in the country. One National Highway Tribunal (NHT) each at Chandigarh, Lucknow, Jabalpur, Kolkata, Guwahati, Mumbai, Chennai and Bangalore
 has also been notified.  Appointments of Presiding Officers have been made for the NHT at Lucknow and Chandigarh and proposals for filling up
 remaining posts of Presiding Officers are under consideration. The Committee was also informed that National Highway Administration (an Officer of
 Central / State Government as notified in the Gazette) has been empowered to remove encroachments on NHs under its jurisdiction. The power of
 appeal vests with National Highway Tribunal, which is the Appellate Authority on all such matters. The district courts do not have any jurisdiction in
 these matters. 
59.       The Committee feels that the encroachment of land under National Highways is a serious issue as it hampers the smooth development of
 highways and is also an irritant in the timely completion of various projects.  The Committee recommends that suitable action should be taken to
 prevent encroachment in the first place.  The Committee is surprised to note that there was considerable delay in enforcing the Control of National
 Highways (Land & Traffic) Act, 2002.  Further, the Presiding Officers for only two of eight National Highway Tribunals have been appointed so far. 
 The Committee notes that the National Highway Administrations have been empowered to remove encroachments on National Highways under its
 jurisdiction and the power of appeal vests with National Highway Tribunal, which is the Appellate Authority on all such matters.  The Committee is of
 the view that the number of National Highway Tribunals (NHT) is disproportionate to the number of National Highway Administrations set up so far. 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the number of National Highway Tribunals (NHT) should be increased and preferably one in each State
 should be opened.  The Committee also recommends that the existing vacancies of Presiding Officers should be filled up at the earliest.

 development of rural roads implemented by Ministry of Rural Development.  The balance amount of 50% of cess on HSD oil and the entire cess
 collected on petrol is allocated as follows:-
I)
                   An amount equal to fifty seven and half percent of such sum for the development and maintenance of NHs;
II)                 An amount equal to twelve and half percent for the construction of roads  either under or over the railway lines by means of a bridge and
 erection of safety works at unmanned rail-road crossings; and
III)              The balance thirty percent on development and maintenance of roads other than national highways and out of this amount, ten percent i.e.
 three per cent of the total share of State roads, is kept as reserve by the Central Government for allocation to States for implementation of
 State road schemes (a) of Inter-State Connectivity and (b) of roads of Economic Importance.

OVERLOADING
139.     Recently, the Supreme Court has ruled that the States cannot allow overloaded trucks to ply on roads even if States issue notifications indirectly
 permitting it. It was reported by the All India Transporters Welfare Association (AITWA) that the load bearing capacity of the trucks has not been
 revised since independence, whereas, the condition of the roads as well as power of the vehicles have increased multifold.  The Committee desired to
 know that if the Registered Laden Weight (RLW) is increased by 25% by amending the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, there would be an increase in
 Government revenue.  In this context the Committee understands that players in the road transport industry have apprised the Department of Road
 Transport & Highways about the lack of level playing field with Indian Railways for carriage of freight.   Taking into account the upgradation of
 railway tracks, Indian Railways have increased permissible wagon load from 60 tonnes to 70 tonnes, i.e. an increase of 16.66%.  On the other hand,
 despite improvement in the quality and network of roads and technical upgradation of vehicles, the axle weight / RLW has not been increased, which is
 restricting the ton per km capacity.  In this scenario,  the comments of the Department was invited whether there is any proposal under consideration of
 the Department to increase the RLW for goods carriers.
140.     In response thereto, the Department replied as follows:-
The Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW) permissible for registration had been last revised and notified in the year 1996 and 2000.  The GVW
 of a vehicle depends on the number of axles of the vehicle and the maximum safe axle weight per axle.  Since road design parameters and load
 bearing capacity limit the safe axle weight, the Government has been encouraging use of multi axle vehicles.  The existing rules permit GVW
 up to 25 tons for a three axle rigid vehicle and 49 tons for tractor-trailer combination of a 3 axle tractor and 3 axle trailer.  Therefore, the GVW
 of vehicles has been legislated keeping in view the design parameters and the load bearing capacity of the roads.  It is pertinent to mention that
 design parameters of the roads has not changed since then.  In fact, any change in the design parameters of the road to take care of higher axle
 load, has serious implications not only for existing roads being strengthened / widened but also for the new roads that are being built under
 various programmes both Government of India and State Governments. 
141.     It is true that overloading is taking place illegally, but an across-the –board increase in GVW by 25% will have the following implications:-
 
Road tax is exclusively in the domain of the State Govt. The revenue which is stated to accrue would accrue to the State Govts and the quantum
 of the likely increase in revenue is not possible to assess.  Central Govt does not get any part thereof. 
Increase in the axle load of vehicles without corresponding strengthening of the roads, in terms of design, would result in damage of road. Such
 damage is known to be governed by the 4th Power Law i.e. increasing the GVW by 25% is likely to give rise to damage of the road to the extent
 of 2.44 times.  Certainly, the loss caused on account of such deterioration of road would be much more than the revenue that could be gained by
 the State Governments.  In any case such revenue has no connection with the construction and maintenance of the roads. 
The question of level playing field with Indian Railways is also to be seen against the backdrop that over the years, Railways has been losing
 share of traffic.  While in 1951, Indian Railways carried 80% of the freight traffic of the country, today, it carries 35% of the traffic.  The road
 sector has been steadily gaining at the expense of railways.  Further, while railways pay for the maintenance of their track infrastructure out of
 their revenues. It is only recently that the concept of toll rates has started which partially makes road users pay for such maintenance. 
142.     The Committee understands that the Indian Railways has adopted an aggressive marketing strategy for increasing its share of freight traffic.  The
 Committee is at a loss to understand as to why there is no initiative to upgrade the design parameters of the roads commensurating with the
 technological advancement in the load carriers enabling them to bear higher load capacities.  The Committee is of the view that this has resulted in the
 flouting of provisions related to overloading.   The Committee notes that the Department has held that the revenue likely to accrue on account of
 increase in Gross Vehicle Weight will be accrued to the State Governments and Central Government will not get any part thereof.  Further, on the one
 hand it is submitted by them that the likely increase in revenue is not possible to assess, on the other hand it is claimed that the loss caused  on account
 of the deterioration of roads would be much more than the revenue that would be gained by the State Governments.  The Committee also feels that
 non-accrual of any revenue to the Central Government by way of road tax consequent on the increase in the permissible Gross Vehicle Weight should
 not be taken as an excuse to defer the decision on the suitable amendments to the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.  The Committee expects that the
 Department will either ensure the strict compliance of provisions of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 or consider the amendment in the Act to help the
 carriage sector.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
THE TENTH PLAN PROVISIONS AND ANNUAL PLAN 2006-07
            The Committee finds that despite its earlier recommendations on the aspect of funds utilization and the need for constant monitoring of projects,
 the Department of Road Transport and Highways has done precious little to improve the situation.   This is evident from the reply of the Department
 with regard to non-utilisation of funds that the allocation for road sector is based on anticipated implementation of projects every year.  The trend from
 the year 2002-03 onwards would reveal that the actual utilization has been more or less steady and consistent.  The slippages occur when major
 projects do not get implemented for the years they are projected for implementation.  For instance, allocations for programmes of NHDP Phase III and
 SARDP NE did not materialize fully because the approvals were not available in the time-frame projected.  The Committee desires to know as to why
 approvals were not available in time and whether any responsibility has been fixed in the matter.   The Committee observes that the budgetary
 allocation of the Department of Road Transport and Highways was substantially increased from 2004-05 (Revised Estimates) of Rs.8566.00 crore to
 Rs.13293.75 crore during 2005-06 and for   2006-07 (Budget Estimates) the allocation has again been increased to Rs.15307.31 crore. The Committee,
 therefore, re-emphasises that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should devise strategies, in such a way so that they are able to fully
 utilize the funds placed at their disposal.  They should also evolve some mechanism to identify bottlenecks causing delays and work towards resolving
 all such hindrances in coordination with all the concerned agencies.  This would also help them to avoid time overrun and consequential cost overrun
 of the projects to be executed by them.
            The Committee notes that in the Mid-Term Appraisal of the 10
th Five Year Plan, it is recommended that the Ministry of Shipping, Road
 Transport and Highways should prepare a detailed programme for the next two years keeping in view the level of budgetary support available and the
 need for leveraging to the maximum extent by adopting a proper mix of engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) and Build Operate and
 Transfer (BOT) projects.   In view of the repeated under-utilization of Budget allocation year after year, the Committee feels that the Department
 should not feel aggrieved by the fact that the Budget provision for 2006-07 has been enhanced by only 10% from the Budget provision for the year
 2005-06 despite enhancement of 11.2% in the cost of construction during 2005 following increase in price indices of the basic ingredients of the road
 sector – steel, cement and bitumen. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should devise
 effective institutional mechanisms to constantly monitor the progress of the projects under implementation and to ensure optimum utilization of funds.
 This mechanism should also involve means to fix responsibility at Project Head Level for the delay in completion of projects thereby acting as a
 deterrent for cost and time over-runs.  The Committee recommends that the Department should also explore the possibility of opening of more
 Budget/Accounting Offices keeping in view the increasing number of projects under implementation. (Paras 23 & 24)

 i.e., March 2007.  (Para 51)
EXPRESSWAYS
          The Committee recommends that the Department should formulate specific time frame for each stage of the six Expressways projects and take
 adequate steps to complete the projects well within the targeted date for completion.  The Committee notes that as per the proposed phasing, these
 corridors are targeted for completion by December, 2015.  However, this is yet to be approved by the Government.  The Committee expects that the
 target date for the completion of the proposed Expressways will be finalised  at the earliest.  (Para 54)
QUALITY CONTROL
          The Committee feels that the instances of collapse of newly built or under construction National Highways is a cause of concern and it also
 leads to the delay in the timely completion of project, which in turn disturb the budgetary estimates and planning.   The Committee recommends that
 the recommendations contained in the reports of the Inquiry Committees should be utilized for avoiding faulty constructions in other projects.   Besides
 exemplary action should be taken against the contractor, concessionaire and supervision consultants. The Committee also finds confusion with regards
 to the norms/guidelines for designing of roads in the country.  The Committee also takes exception to the fact that despite having built thousands of
 kilometers of Highways, no norms for designs of roads and bridges have been framed and international norms are being followed.  The Committee
 feels that there exists a need for indignisation of norms regarding designing of roads and bridges taking into account the local site conditions.  The
 Committee also expects that the designs of roads and bridges executed by NHAI and other agencies should be strictly according to the local site
 conditions. (Para 57)
ENCROACHMENT
          The Committee feels that the encroachment of land under National Highways is a serious issue as it hampers the smooth development of
 highways and is also an irritant in the timely completion of various projects.  The Committee recommends that suitable action should be taken to
 prevent encroachment in the first place.  The Committee is surprised to note that there was considerable delay in enforcing the Control of National
 Highways (Land & Traffic) Act, 2002.  Further, the Presiding Officers for only two of eight National Highway Tribunals have been appointed so far. 
 The Committee notes that the National Highway Administrations have been empowered to remove encroachments on National Highways under its
 jurisdiction and the power of appeal vests with National Highway Tribunal, which is the Appellate Authority on all such matters.  The Committee is of
 the view that the number of National Highway Tribunals (NHT) is disproportionate to the number of National Highway Administrations set up so far. 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the number of National Highway Tribunals (NHT) should be increased and preferably one in each State
 should be opened.  The Committee also recommends that the existing vacancies of Presiding Officers should be filled up at the earliest. (Para 59)
CENTRAL ROAD FUND
The Committee notes that the issue of release of previous fund accruals to the Central Road Funds due to additional cess since 1.4.2003 still remains
 unresolved.  However, the Committee is happy to note that the allocation of cess has been increased from Rs.3269.74 crore in 2005-06 to Rs.6407.45
 crore in 2006-07.  The Committee also notes that the amount of additional cess is expected to be approximately half of the budget allocation for the
 year 2006-07.  Therefore, the importance of the allocation to the Department from the Central Road Fund cannot be avoided.  The Committee
 recommends that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should take up the matter immediately with the Ministry of Finance to resolve the
 impasse in the non-accrual of previous fund allocations from CRF in view of the high cost of implementation of National Highway Development
 Programme.  The Committee notes that the pattern of distribution of accruals from CRF is not equitable and despite the enhanced allocation received
 during 2006-07, the share granted to State Roads and Centrally Sponsored Schemes have not been increased in comparison to 2005-06 and in case of
 Rural Roads, it has been decreased from Rs. 3809.50 crore in 2005-06 to Rs. 3725.62 crore in 2006-07. The Committee strongly feels that this will
 have a negative impact on the rural economy and the majority who lives in rural pockets will suffer.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
 allocations should be made strictly in accordance with the percentages fixed in the Central Road Fund Act, 2000 wherein it is mentioned that 50% of
 the cess on High Speed Diesel oil is for the development of rural roads implemented by Ministry of Rural Development.  (Para 63)
DEVELOPMENT OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS IN NORTH-EASTERN REGION 
            The Committee expects that taking into account the strategic importance and socio- economic condition of North Eastern region the Department
 will give special attention for the timely completion of the projects undertaken under the different phases of SARDP-NE. In the context of the plans of
 the Department to attract private investment in the North Eastern region, the Committee reiterates its earlier recommendations/observations made in its
 84
th Report which is reproduced below:-
            “The Committee is apprehensive whether the Government will be able to ensure private sector participation in the North-Eastern region of the
 country to the extent required by them.  The Committee recommends that, in such a scenario, keeping in view the socio-economic conditions of
 the North-Eastern region, the Government must provide resources for the projects on its own and the developmental activities in road sector for
 North-Eastern region should not be allowed to be a casualty.”
            The Committee notes that the Department has now accepted the viability of roads in the North Eastern region through private participation is
 generally low.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that  a suitable decision on the issue of viability gap funding of the private sector projects in the
 North Eastern region should be taken at the earliest so that the development of roads in that region do not suffer.  (Paras 68 & 69)
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS (ORIGINAL) WORKS
            The Committee regrets to note that the outlay for Non-NHDP Projects has been decreased from Rs. 2277.00 crore in 2005-06 to Rs. 1550.30
 crore in 2006-07 despite the fact that the utilization under this head during 2005-06 was 99.83% of the Revised Estimates provision.  While it is
 understandable that there is a need for giving specific attention to NHDP works, the Committee feels that the Department should not ignore non-NHDP
 works, which contribute to the all round improvement of road infrastructure in the country.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the allocation
 to Non-NHDP Projects should be increased at Revised Estimates  (2006-07) stage and the Department of Road Transport and Highways must explore
 the possibility of allocation of more funds to non-NHDP works in future.  With reference to the restoration of roads hit by Tsunami and natural
 calamities the unprecedented rains in some parts of the country, the Committee recommends that suitable financial assistance should be given to the
 affected States during the current financial year also for early completion of the work. (Para 74)
STRATEGIC ROADS
            The Committee notes that the allocation for strategic roads during 2005-06 was Rs.66.00 crore excluding Rs.5.00 crore for North Eastern
 States.  As against this, the allocation in BE 2006-07 is Rs.63.46 crore excluding Rs.10.71 crore for the North Eastern States, which is  a reduction in
 comparison to 2005-06.  The Committee feels that the allocations specifically made for North Eastern States should be treated separately and should
 not be used to misinform the Committee.  The Committee desires that the details of the utilization of the funds during 2005-06 for all the identified
 strategic roads alongwith the proposed distribution of expenditure during 2006-07 should be furnished by the Department. (Para 76)

EXTERNALLY AIDED PROJECTS
          The Committee notes that the works on the projects in respect of which loan agreement were signed in the years 2000 and 2001 are still
 pending.  The Committee expresses its concern over the lackadaisical attitude of the Department over the implementation of the Externally Aided
 Projects.  The Committee is also unhappy to note that the first instalment of the ADB loan was paid nine days beyond the due date not because of
 shortage of funds but due to lapse on the part of the concerned officers, which exemplifies the non professional manner of functioning.  The Committee
 feels that the default should be viewed seriously because country’s prestige is involved in international transactions and recommends that the
 Department should take suitable steps to prevent the repetition of such instances in future.    (Para 79)
MAINTENANCE AND REPAIR OF NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
            The Committee notes that against the total BE for 2006-07 of Rs.15307.31 crore for the Department of Road Transport and Highways, only an
 amount of Rs.814.38 crore has been earmarked for the maintenance and repair of National Highways.  The Committee feels that the proportion of the
 outlay for maintenance and repair of National Highways to the total outlay of the Department of Road Transport and Highways is not upto the desired
 level in view of the lengths of National Highways completed so far and proposed to be added in the current financial year.  The Committee
 recommends that in view of the bad conditions of various National Highways, which carry heavy traffic load, the Department should draw up a
 comprehensive list of high-density traffic National Highways which will enable it to utilize the available funds in a meaningful manner.           (Para
 83)
ROAD TRANSPORT
            The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the budget allocation of Rs.16.00 crore during 2005-06 for the National Highway Accident Relief
 Service Scheme no fresh expenditure could be made and an expenditure of Rs.6.03 crore was made on account of spill-over from the previous year. 
 The Committee also notes that the allocation for this scheme during 2005-06 was almost half of the total allocation for road safety of Rs.35.30 crore. 
 The Committee is appalled at the lack of foresight shown in making early alternate arrangements ahead of the expiry of the DGS&D contract.  The
 Committee feels that the procurement of cranes in a routine manner without assessing the local requirements of the States also indicates the lack of
 farsightedness in implementing the scheme.  The Committee notes that even though no fresh expenditure could be made in 2005-06 out of the budget
 provision of Rs.16.00 crore, the budget allocation in 2006-07 has been decreased to Rs.14.80 crore.  The Committee feels that the budget provision
 should have been increased for the current financial year.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that  suitable enhancement should be made in the
 budget allocation for National Highway Accident Relief Service Scheme so that the pending requests of States/UTs pertaining to the year 2005-06 and
 also the fresh requests for cranes/ambulances could be considered favourably. (Para 91)
            The Committee is of the view that the steps being taken to constitute a Safety Directorate/Commissionerate for Safety, rather belatedly, are in
 the right direction.  The Committee recommends that the preparatory steps in the directions should be completed within a stipulated time frame in view
 of the magnitude of the problem of high incidence of deaths on highways and the urgent need to develop the necessary infrastructure to tackle the
 same. (Para 96)
            The Committee is not satisfied with the response of the Department of Road Transport and Highways that issuance of licenses to untrained and
 incompetent persons is more of a problem of compliance with the provisions of Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989
 rather than the provisions themselves.  The Committee is of the opinion that there is a need to revisit the relevant provisions of the Motor Vehicles Act,
 1988 and Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 to rectify the driving license issuance system by prescribing stringent test conditions and rules and
 regulations.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be a comprehensive review of the existing rules and regulations pertaining to
 driving license issuance system to mitigate the problem of road accidents due to unsafe driving practices. (Para 98)
The Committee is surprised to note the lack of reliable data on road traffic and road accidents.  The Committee is at a loss to understand the
 manner in which the Department is implementing its road safety programmes in such a scenario.  The Committee feels that this factor alone calls for
 concerted efforts to set up the Safety Directorate/Commissionerate for Safety at the earliest.  The Committee is also of the opinion that NGOs/private
 sector can play an active role in the crucial sector of road safety.  The Committee recommends that suitable incentives should be offered to private
 sector to enhance their role in this field. (Para 102)
            The Committee finds that adequate pedestrian path has not been provided in the Delhi-Gurgaon Express Corridor Project.   The Committee
 recommends that the norms relating to pedestrian safety should be strictly followed in all the road projects and that suitable modifications should be
 made in the above project to ensure safe movement of pedestrians.  On the aspect of coordinating with the State PWDs on the issue of compliance of
 engineering standards related to road safety, the Committee is surprised at the apathy shown by the Department in monitoring and rectifying the
 alterations made on the roads by State PWDs endangering road safety.  The Committee notes that although the Department agrees that the raising of
 height of road dividers to 45 cms will be an accident hazard and therefore may lead to more accidents, it has shied away from its responsibility in
 ensuring the compliance of standards prescribed for road construction for promoting road safety.    The Committee, therefore, recommends that a
 suitable mechanism should be put in place to co-ordinate with the State PWDs for the rectification of faulty constructions on roads that endanger road
 safety. (Para 105)
            The Committee agrees that the maintenance of law and order is a State subject.   However the fact that about 40% of the total road traffic is
 carried by the National Highways with the help of drivers/crew of truck etc. The Committee is therefore of the opinion that the Department should
 continue to explore suitable alternatives for preventing the incidents of highway robberies, hijacking of trucks, plundering of loads of vehicles,
 kidnapping and murder of drivers / crew of trucks etc.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department/National Highways Authority of
 India should make efforts to set up a dedicated task force to provide security on National Highways/sections of National Highways entrusted to it.(Para
 107)
            The Committee feels that the easy and quick accessibility of trauma centers/hospitals from National Highways will go a long way in reducing
 the fatalities connected with road accidents.  The Committee, however, agrees that the provision of immediate help to accident victims depends on the
 better coordination between various agencies.  The Committee recommends that in addition to the Ministry of Health, the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways should also make efforts to update the list of hospitals located in the vicinity of National Highways so that the patrol
 vans/ambulances can provide timely assistance to the victims of road accidents.  The Committee further recommends that the Department should
 pursue the matter with the Ministry of Health for enhancing the financial assistance provided to hospitals/trauma centers located on the National
 Highways to upgrade their facilities. (Para 110)
 
NATIONAL DATA BASE NETWORK
            The Committee notes that the National Data Base Network is a sponsored project of the Department to bring about uniformity in Driving
 License and Vehicle Registration documents issued by RTOs across the country.  The Committee feels that the Department should closely monitor the
 implementation of the project in all the States and recommends that the Department should adequately sensitise the States about the necessity of having
 uniform policy all over the country in acquiring Driving License and Vehicle Registration documents as the first measure to check road accident-
related deaths.  The Committee further recommends that the Department should fix a target for each State so that the project is implemented during the
 Tenth Five Year Plan period itself.   (Para 113)
 DATA COLLECTION
          The Committee is constrained to note that the entire amount allocated for data collection has remained unspent during 2005-06.  The Committee
 is convinced that “importance of accurate data and well-researched insights to aid informed policy-making can not be overstated” accepted by the
 Department as a mere rhetoric and not supported by concrete action.  The Committee is of the view that the unorganised sector of road transport has
 remained a neglected area particularly due to lack of critical data relating to this Sector.  The Committee recommends that suitable action should be
 taken to evolve a permanent mechanism to ensure collection and analysis of data relating to road transport sector which will  be useful in research and
 development activities also. (Para 118)
ROAD SAFETY EQUIPMENT & POLLUTION TESTING AND CONTROL
            The Committee notes that Bharat Stage-II standards for vehicular emission has been made applicable through out the country w.e.f. 1.4.2005
 and Bharat Stage III emission norms for four wheeled vehicles have come into force from 1.4.2005 in 11 mega cities.  It is matter of serious concern

            The Committee notes that the Tenth Five Year Plan envisages the setting up of at least one Model Drivers’ Training School in each of the five

RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT
            The Committee is constrained to note that out of the allocation of Rs.4.85 crore for R&D in 2005-06, only Rs. 0.01 crore has been incurred upto
 December 2005.  The Committee is of the view that the R&D activities are imperative to enhance the sustainability of road projects being implemented
 in the country and to make them user friendly.  The Committee feels that a non-utilisation of the budget provision during 2005-06 exemplifies the lack
 of efforts to bolster the R&D activities.  The Committee also feels that there is a need to access the benefits derived from the R&D applications.  The
 Committee expects that suitable institutional mechanisms will be developed to study the impact of R&D initiatives on specific projects so that the
 beneficial results can be replicated in similar conditions in future.  The Committee also expects that adequate steps will be taken for the progressive
 installation of Advance Traffic Management Systems and Intelligent Traffic Systems on the National Highways.  The Committee further recommends
 that the GIS based National Highways Information Systems should be completed and implemented in a time bound manner.  (Para 134)
PUBLIC TRANSPORT   
            The Committee is alarmed at the steep decline in the share of public transport vehicles in the total vehicles population from 11.1 % in 1951 to
 1.1 % in 2003.  The Committee is not aware of any concerted policy measure formulated to arrest this decline.  In this context the Committee reiterates
 its observations/recommendations made in its 84
th Report, which is reproduced below:-
“The Committee expresses its concern that in the absence of user-friendly public transports, the people in the country are resorting to excessive
 use of private vehicles and this has put the roads under undue pressure.  The Committee observes that most of the investment on roads is being
 extracted from the public itself but the returns accruing to the users are not equitable. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the efforts of
 the Government should focus on developing public transport, which discourage excessive use of private vehicles.  The Government should
 adopt a methodology, whereby, in the more densely populated states, the rich and the affluent are asked to pay more for keeping more than one
 vehicle.”  (Para 138)
OVERLOADING
            The Committee understands that the Indian Railways has adopted an aggressive marketing strategy for increasing its share of freight traffic.  The
 Committee is at a loss to understand as to why there is no initiative to upgrade the design parameters of the roads commensurating with the
 technological advancement in the load carriers enabling them to bear higher load capacities.  The Committee is of the view that this has resulted in the
 flouting of provisions related to overloading.   The Committee notes that the Department has held that the revenue likely to accrue on account of
 increase in Gross Vehicle Weight will be accrued to the State Governments and Central Government will not get any part thereof.  Further, on the one
 hand it is submitted by them that the likely increase in revenue is not possible to assess, on the other hand it is claimed that the loss caused  on account
 of the deterioration of roads would be much more than the revenue that would be gained by the State Governments.  The Committee also feels that
 non-accrual of any revenue to the Central Government by way of road tax consequent on the increase in the permissible Gross Vehicle Weight should
 not be taken as an excuse to defer the decision on the suitable amendments to the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.  The Committee expects that the
 Department will either ensure the strict compliance of provisions of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 or consider the amendment in the Act to help the
 carriage sector.  (Para 142)
NATIONAL ROAD TRANSPORT POLICY
            The Committee is surprised to note that even though the Integrated Transport Policy was formulated in the year 2001, the National Road
 Transport Policy has not been finalised so far.  The Committee further notes that the discussions on the draft National Road Transport Policy are still in
 preliminary stage and meetings with stakeholders are still to be held.  Further, the agency to be entrusted the task of submitting a revised draft has not
 been finalised.  In this scenario, the Committee is apprehensive that the National Road Transport Policy document will not be available by December,
 2006 as envisaged.  The Committee recommends that concerted efforts should be made to finalise the National Road Transport Policy expeditiously. 
 In this context, the Committee had observed in its 84
th Report that the absence of the right kind of modal-mix in the country is hampering optimum
 utilisation of all the resources.  The Committee therefore reiterates its recommendations made in the same Report that the Government must come
 forward with an integrated transport policy to make optimum utilisation of the various transport facilities available in the country.
            The Committee was informed that recently the Ministry of Finance has issued instructions vide F.No.7/16/2004-IFU(B&A) dated the 8 th
 December 2004 wherein it has been impressed upon to all Ministry/Department that 67 per cent of the budgetary allocations should be spent upto
 December and rest of the 33 per cent of the budgetary allocations could spent in the last quarter ending March.  However, ceiling of the budgeted
 expenditure cannot be observed in the Department of Road Transport and Highways since most of the construction work starts after the monsoon
 season.  The Committee notes that the problem faced by the Department is very genuine as the most of the construction work starts after the monsoon
 and thus it is not possible for them to spent 67 per cent of the budgetary allocations for the month ending December as per instructions of the Ministry
 of Finance.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that keeping in view of the climatic conditions of the country, the Department of Road Transport
 and Highways should be exempted from complying with the ceiling limits as prescribed by the Ministry of Finance above and the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways may be allowed to utilize their budget provision without any restriction throughout the year. (Paras 147 & 148)
