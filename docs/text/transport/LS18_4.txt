NOTE OF DISSENT

SHRJ SHIVAJI PATNAIK, M.P., SHRI SUKHENDU KHAN, M.P. AND SHRI 
MD. M.P. 
We were opposed to the introduction of ·the Air Corporations (Transfer of Undertakings 
and Repeal) Bill, which was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 4th May, 1992. 
Our opposit ion to the Bill is in pursuance to our' total opposition to the Industria l Policy 
Statement of 24th July, 1991, whach in keeping with the new economic policy as dictated by 
the International Monetary fund. seeks to subvert the self-reliant economy of the count ry , 
dasmember the Public Secto r and em bark upon the path of unbriadled privatisation. 
The Air Corporations Act of 1953. which is being sought to be repealed by the present 
Ball, was enacted in keeping with the Industrial Policy Statement of 1948 and got funher 
impetus by the fndustrial Policy Resolution of 1956, which called for giving a commandeer · 
ing role to the Public Sector by identifying several core sectors of industries to be rC$erved 
for Public Sector for the growth and development of a self-reliant national economy. The 
Air Corporations A~t of !953 served as a correct precursor to this policy by reserving the air 
traffic for the Publi c Sector . This is so vital not only for the growth of national economy but 
aho for the security and defence of the country. 
Over the years the t wo Corpora tions, viz. the Indian Airlines and Air India did register 
impressive growth und contributed to the national economy and safety of the country . For 
the growth of a self-reliant economy and safety and defence of the coun try, it is necessary 
that Bud getary support h;as to be provided to the two Corporations. 
We are tlterefore totally opposed to the Bill, in its entirety, which seeks to repeal the Air 
Corporations Act and thereby clear the path for privatisation of the air traffic, including by 
foreign air liners, enua ngering the defence of the country, its soverei gnty and exposing it to 
imperi;alist machinations.
