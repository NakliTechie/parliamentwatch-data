R E P O R T
In June 2001, the erstwhile Ministry of Surface Transport was bifurcated into two Ministries viz. Ministry of

 Road Transport & Highways and Ministry of Shipping.  These two Ministries have been reconstituted into one Ministry,
 viz. Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways in September 2004 with two Departments i.e. Department of
 Road Transport and Highways and Department of Shipping.
 2.         The Department of Road Transport and Highways is responsible for the formulation and implementation of
 policies relating to Road Transport and National Highways, Central Sector Road Schemes, administration of Central
 Road Fund and sponsoring of research pertaining to road works.  The two major divisions of the Department are the
 Transport Division and the Road Division.
 
3.         The Road Transport Division of the Department is concerned with the formulation of broad policies related to
 road transport besides, making arrangements for the movement of vehicular traffic with the neighboring countries. 
 
4.         The Road Division is concerned mainly with matters relating to:-
(i)                    Advising Government on all general policy matters relating to Highways
(ii)                  Development and maintenance of roads declared as National Highways
(iii)                 Development and maintenance of roads other than National Highways in   Union Territories.
(iv)                Administration of Central Road Fund (CRF) pertaining to state roads other than rural roads.
(v)                  Evaluation and specification of Standards for roads and bridges
(vi)        Research on roads. 
 5.         The following autonomous bodies function under the administrative control of the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways:-
(a)                National Highways Authority of India
(b)               National Institute of Training of Highway Engineers (NITHE).

 so as to reduce the resource gap in this sector to some extent.  In their Action Taken Replies furnished to the Committee,
 the Department of Road Transport and Highways replied that the suggestion of the Committee has been noted for
 compliance and Planning Commission shall be approached for allocations during the remaining years of the Tenth Plan.

20.       The Committee regrets to note that BE Plan Provision of Rs.7912 crore of Department of Road Transport and
 Highways was brought down to Rs.6766 crore on account of slow implementation of Externally Aided Projects by
 NHAI and the Department.  Further, there was delay in finalisation of the loans and obtaining approvals for the
 procurement for the externally aided component of the Corridor Project of NHDP (Phase-II) being financed by the
 Asian Development Bank (ADB).  Also, there was delay in award of works to the contractors and consultants for
 development of NH-34 in West Bengal.  These instances clearly bring out that on the one hand, the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways emphasizes that funds for development and maintenance of National Highways are inadequate
 while, on the other hand, the Department is not able to utilise the higher allocation made to it at BE stage.   The
 Department has failed to make optimal utilization of the funds at its disposal. The reasons for shortfall mentioned are
 delay in land acquisition, poor performance of the contractors, law and order problem etc. are very general in nature and
 are repeated every year. As a result of these, the budgetary allocations of the Department were reduced at RE stage.  The
 Committee is of the view that there is a lack of monitoring system in the Department of Road Transport and Highways. 
 The lack of a proper monitoring system in the Department is exemplified by the delay and non-completion of the
 construction of flyover at Murthal in Haryana and the failure of the Department of Road Transport and Highways in
 verifying the credentials of the contractors who had submitted forged bank guarantees.  The Committee observes that
 the budget allocations of the Department of Road Transport and Highways have been substantially increased during
 2005-06. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should devise
 strategies, whereby they are able to fully utilize the funds at their disposal.  They should also evolve some mechanism to
 identify bottlenecks causing delays and work towards resolving all such hindrances in coordination with all the
 concerned agencies.  This would also help them to avoid time overrun and consequential cost overrun of the projects to
 be executed by them.
 
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS
21.       India has a vast road network of 3.32 million kilometers of which the National Highways and the State Highways
 together account for 1,95,000 km.  As on 31
st March 2004, the length of National Highways network is only 65,569 km
 constituting about 2% of the total road network in the country and carrying about 40% of the total traffic across the
 length and breadth of the country.
 
22.       The Department of Road Transport & Highways is primarily responsible for the construction and maintenance of
 National Highways (NH). All roads, other than National Highway in States, fall within the jurisdiction of respective
 state governments.  The development and maintenance of National Highways are being done on agency basis.  The
 National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), State Public Works Departments (PWDs) and the Border Road
 Organisation (BRO) are the main agencies. Out of the total National Highway length of 65,569 km, about 14,279 km
 have been entrusted to National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) for improvement under National Highway
 Development Project (NHDP) Phase I & II, Port Connectivity Schemes and other schemes.  The balance lengths of
 about 46,586 km and 4,704 km of non-NHDP sections of National Highways are mainly entrusted to State PWDs and
 Border Roads Organisation (BRO) respectively, as agencies of the Ministry. 
 
NATIONAL HIGHWAYS DEVELOPMENT PROJECT (NHDP)
23.       The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways informed the Committee that the Government has embarked upon a
 massive programme to develop highways through the National Highways Development Project (NHDP) in the country. 
 The NHDP is the largest highway project ever undertaken in the country.  This is being implemented by National
 Highways Authority of India.  The NHDP consists of Golden Quadrilateral, North-South and East-West Corridors, Port
 Connectivity and other Projects.  The total length under NHDP is about 14,279 km.  The details relating to NHDP are as
 under:-
(i)                  Golden Quadrilateral (GQ) comprising National Highways connecting four metro cities viz., Delhi,
 Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata.  Total length of GQ is 5846 km.  As on 28

24.       The Committee noted that the Golden Quadrilateral project was originally targeted for completion by December
 2003.  As per revised schedule, 90 per cent of the project was proposed to be completed by December 2004.  The
 Committee sought to know as to whether the proposed target was achieved by the end of December 2004 or whether

25.       In response, the Committee was informed by the Department of Road Transport and Highways that progress of 4-

 expenditure on Golden Quadrilateral will be within the estimated cost, which also allows for escalation. Since the
 project is being implemented through a number of contractors, the exact quantum of escalation can be assessed only
 after the completion of the contracts.   The Committee noted that the revised target date of completion of Golden
 Quadrilateral is December 2005.
 
26.       The Committee further enquired whether there had been instances of delay in completion of jobs due to the
 defaulting contractors and, if so, what action had been taken against such contractors?  The Committee was informed by
 the Department of Road Transport and Highways that a review of performance of various contractors implementing the
 on-going contract packages in NHAI was made. It was found that the performance of 17 contractors (8 foreign and 9
 Indian) was very poor. These 17 contractors have been informed that they will not be pre-qualified for NHAI work, till
 their performance improves.
 
27.       The Committee was also informed by the Department of Road Transport and Highways that following steps had
 been taken for early completion of Golden Quadrilateral:-
(a)          On the request of NHAI, State Governments have nominated nodal officers of the rank of Principal
 Secretary /Secretary to coordinate with other Departments to resolve the issues related to land
 acquisition, shifting of utilities, tree cutting etc.  Regular meetings are being held to resolve these
 problems.
(b)         The progress of projects is being monitored on regular basis by independent supervision consultant,
 NHAI officials and the Department of Road Transport and Highways.
(c)          To expedite the construction of Railway Over Bridges (ROBs), monthly co-ordination meetings are
 being held with Zonal Chief Bridge Engineers of Railways with the officers of the NHAI.  It is also
 being pursued regularly with the Railway Board.
(d)         Forest and environment related matters are being taken up with State Governments as well as
 Ministry of Environment and Forest.
 
28.       The Committee was further informed that NHAI has appointed independent supervision consultants for
 monitoring the quality of construction. The defaulting contractors are penalized on the recommendations of the
 supervision consultants.  The NHAI has also appointed an independent agency M/s Engineers India Limited for quality
 audit and surveillance for 77 on-going contracts of which 61 contracts pertains to Golden Quadrilateral.  NHAI has also
 constituted a cell, headed by a General Manager, to make random check, among other things, of the quality of works
 being implemented under NHDP.

NATIONAL HIGHWAYS NOT COVERED UNDER NHDP
30.       The Committee had discussed the status of National Highways not covered under NHDP in its 82
nd Report on
 Demands for Grants 2004-05 of Department of Road Transport and Highways.  The Committee had noted that out of
 43,833 kilometer of non-NHDP National Highways, only 1.95% are four or more laned whereas 62.55% of such
 National Highways are two-laned and 35.49% of such National Highways are only single/intermediate laned.  The
 Committee felt that there was a mismatch between NHDP and non-NHDP Highways in terms of lanes, surface quality
 and funds.  The Committee had recommended the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways to give equitable
 weightage to the non-NHDP National Highways and provide adequate budgetary allocation required for their
 development.
 
31.       In their Action Taken Replies furnished to the Committee the Department of Road Transport and Highways
 informed that out of the total National Highways network of about 65,569 km, 14279 km have been have been taken up
 under NHDP Phase I & II, comprising the Golden Quadrilateral, North-South & East-West corridors and other projects,
 including connectivity to major ports. The implementation of NHDP Phase I & II is targeted for completion by
 December 2007. The balance length not covered under NHDP (Phase-I & Phase-II) is about 51,000 kms of National
 Highways.  The development of National Highways which are not covered under NHDP are being taken up according to
 the availability of fund, condition, inter-se priority and traffic.  The fund available is not sufficient to take up large
 development works.  Therefore, the Ministry is considering other programmes, using public private partnership, cess
 fund from petrol and diesel for development of National Highways which are not on NHDP.  The development of these
 balance length are being considered under NHDP Phase-III and NHDP Phase-IV and Special Accelerated North East
 Road Development Programme.
 
32.       The rehabilitation and upgradation of about 10,000 km length of existing National Highways to 4 lane, dual
 carriageway on Build, Operate and Transfer (BOT) basis has been proposed to be taken up as NHDP Phase III (A&B).  
 The programme generally comprises stretches of National Highways carrying high volume of traffic, connecting state
 Capitals with NHDP Phase I & II network and providing connectivity to places of economic, commercial and tourist
 importance.  The Government has approved the proposal of NHDP Phase IIIA (4,000 km) at an estimated cost of
 Rs.22,000 crore (2004 prices) and has directed for initiating advance action in the form of preparation of Detailed
 Project Reports  for Phase IIIB (6,000 km). The implementation of NHDP Phase IIIA and IIIB is targeted for
 completion by December 2009 and December 2012 respectively.  For this purpose Rs.1400 crore has been provided in
 the year 2005-06. 
 
33.       Considering the status of development of the roads in North Eastern region, an Accelerated North-East Road
 Development Project for a length of 3251 km of National Highways is under consideration which will mainly provide
 connectivity to all the State Capitals and District Headquarters in the North-East Region including 4-laning of 315 kms
 of National Highways between Nagaon and Dibrugarh and 2/4 laning of 288 kms of National Highways in Meghalaya,
 Nagaland and Sikkim. The proposal would also include up-gradation of other stretches of National Highways and State
 Highways considered critical for economic development of the North-East region.  An amount of Rs.450 crore has been
 provided for the purpose during 2005-06. 
 
34.       Out of the balance length of 38,000 kms, the proposal for approval of upgradation of the length of about 20,000
 kms of National Highways with two-lane paved shoulders is in formulation stage under NHDP Phase IV.

64.       During the meeting of the Committee held on 31
st March 2005, the Committee impressed upon the Department
 of Road Transport and Highways the need to have an integrated transport policy in the country.  The Committee pointed
 out the availability of various types of transport systems in the country and that passenger traffic was not
 proportionately distributed.  The Committee sought the opinion of the representatives of Department of Road Transport
 and Highways in the matter.  In response, while agreeing to the need to have an integrated transport policy, the
 Secretary of the Department stated as under:-
“There is definitely a need to have an integrated transport policy.  This kind of policy one has to see.  We deal
 with roads; the Shipping deals with shipping; the Civil Aviation deals with it.  One has to see it either the
 Planning Commission can do it or we can come up with an integrated transport policy for the road sector.   But,
 we are in touch with the Planning Commission on this issue.  Whatever decision is taken by the Government as a
 whole, we will abide by that.”
 65.       The Committee observes that one of the objectives for the Tenth Plan for the road sector is ‘Development of
 roads to be considered an integral part of the total transport system supplementing other modes, integrating the
 development plans with railways and other modes of transport’.   This objective of the Tenth Plan reinforces the need to
 have an integrated transport policy in the country.  The Committee is of the view that the modal-mix in terms of
 transport is very adverse in the country inspite of the fact that we have long inland waterways, a fairly large railway
 network and the recent upsurge in the aviation and road transport sector.  However, in absence of the right kind of
 modal-mix the country is not making optimum utilization of all those resources.  There is, therefore, a need to have a
 more balanced and equitable distribution of passenger and freight traffic in the country.  The Committee, therefore,
 recommends that the Government must come forward with an integrated transport policy to make optimum utilization
 of the various transport facilities available in the country.

DAMAGE IN TSUMANI HIT AREAS
66.       The Tsunami waves hit the coastal regions of the country in December 2004 and caused a major loss of life and
 property there. The Committee desired to know from the Department of Road Transport and Highways about the
 damage caused to the road network and the relief measures undertaken by the Department in the southern parts of the
 country and Andaman & Nicobar Islands. In response, the Department of Road Transport and Highways informed the
 Committee that the Tsumani waves that hit the coastal regions of south India in December 2004 caused huge loss of life
 and property and extensive damage to road network and bridges.  Five teams of officers from the Department inspected
 the affected areas to assess the damages to road infrastructure.  About 242 km of National Highways and 520 km of
 state roads, 14 bridges and 78 culverts have been damaged in the states of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and
 Union Territories of Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Pondicherry.   The total cost of repairs to damaged National
 Highways and state roads has been assessed at about Rs.200 crore (Rs.31.5 crore for short term and Rs.168.5 crore for
 long term measures).  The following relief measures were undertaken by the Department:-
A Control Room was set up by the Department which functioned from         28 th December 2004 to 28th January
 2005.  It was done with the help of all the concerned field agencies as well as other Ministries.
 
The Arasalar Bridge on NH-45A near Karaikal in the Union Territory of Pondicherry was damaged. A bailey
 bridge has been provided for immediate restoration of traffic.  A bridge by the side of the existing bridge is to be
 constructed as a permanent measure.
 
There have been extensive damages to the road network and bridges in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands also. 
 The Andaman and Nicobar Public Works Department (PWD) had requested for the supply of six power saws,
 eight generators, 20 water pumps, 6000 metre G.I. Pipes, 10 plate compactors, 100 metric tonne cement, 100
 pieces RCC Hume Pipes, 10 hand rammers, 500 metre flexible hose, 1000 metre ERW steel pipes, etc. for
 restoration works.  These items were sent by air/ship.
 
67.       The Committee was further informed that the superstructure of a 160 metre  long bridge over the Pazhyar river in
 Tamil Nadu was washed away by the tsunami waves.  The Department of Road Transport and Highways provided relief
 measures by operationalising a ferry service pending installation of a temporary steel bridge and installing a temporary
 steel bridge within 6 weeks for immediate restoration of traffic.  In addition, a permanent bridge was also proposed to be
 constructed within one and a half years.
 
68.       The Department further stated that 23 proposals amounting to Rs.80 crore had been received from the states of
 Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and Union Territories of Pondicherry for restoration of the above damages.  All
 these proposals are still under examination.
 
69.       The Committee observes that the Tsunami waves caused extensive damage to the road network and bridges in the
 Southern Parts of the country.  The Committee appreciates the promptness with which the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways responded to the situation and carried out the relief measures in the tsunami hit areas.  The
 Committee strongly recommends that the Department should consider the proposals submitted by the State
 Governments for restoration of the damages on priority basis so that construction/repair works could be carried out at

POLLUTION TESING AND CONTROL
81.       In order to combat the problem of vehicular pollution, a scheme to provide central assistance in the shape of
 reimbursement of actual cost of the pollution testing equipment purchased by the State Governments had been made
 effective from 1.4.1993. Funds have been released under this scheme to various State Governments restricting the
 purchase of pollution testing equipment to two equipment per RTO.  The scheme was later on modified with effect from
 01.06.97 to provide the facility of reimbursement of only 75% of the cost of equipment to the States as well as to
 accommodate NGOs within the purview of this scheme.   Since no proper proposal was received from any NGO during
 the financial year 1997-98, the scheme was again modified w.e.f. 1.6.1998 to restrict the facility only to the State
 Governments.

 the BE amount under the scheme of Pollution Testing Equipment was reduced from Rs.2.00 crore to Rs.1.00 crore at RE

84.       Vehicular pollution has been gaining alarming proportions in the country.  The Committee enquired from the
 Department of Road Transport and Highways regarding the steps taken by them for controlling vehicular pollution and
 monitoring pollution levels.  The Department of Road Transport and Highways apprised the Committee of some of the
 important steps taken by them to control vehicular pollution which are as under:-
·        From 1.4.2000, unleaded petrol has been made available in the country.
·        Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) and Liquified Petroleum Gas (LPG) have been  permitted to be used as
 alternate auto fuels.
·        India 2000 (India stage I) which are akin to Euro I norms have been made effective from 1.4.2000 throughout
 the country.
·        Bharat Stage II emission norms  which are akin to Euro II norms have already been introduced in the eleven
 mega cities and are envisaged to come into force all over India from 1.4.2005. 
·        Bharat Stage III emission standards which are akin to Euro III norms, have been notified  to come into force in
 11 mega cities from 1.4.2005.
·        The emission norms for tractors were first notified in the year 1999 and the next stage emission norms  for
 these vehicles have also come into force from 1.6.2003.  Further, even tighter emission norms for new tractors 
 are coming into force w.e.f. 1.10.2005.
·        Stricter Pollution Under Control norms have been introduced for on-road motor vehicles from 1.10.2004. 
 Apart from tighter norms for Carbon Mono Oxide (CO), the measurement of Hydro Carbon (HC) in respect of
 petrol driven vehicles have also been introduced.  Further, the testing protocol in respect of diesel vehicles has
 also been defined. States have also been empowered to prescribe tighter norms in specific areas, if there is such
 a need and the infrastructure available enables doing so.  
 
85.       The Department of Road Transport and Highways further stated that monitoring and enforcement of these norms
 for on-road vehicles comes under the purview of respective State Governments.
 
86.       The Committee is of the view that vehicular emission has become one of the major sources of environmental
 pollution and the share of the transport sector in total emissions is a matter of serious concern.  The Committee
 appreciates the steps taken by the Department of Road Transport and Highways in controlling vehicular pollution.  The
 Committee recommends that the Department of Road Transport and Highways should impress upon the State
 Governments the need for effective monitoring and enforcement of these norms for on-road vehicles so as to minimize
 its ill effects.

87.       The Committee had, in its 82
nd Report on Demands for Grants 2004-05 of the Ministry of Road Transport and
 Highways observed that the automobile population in the country had been growing disproportionately with respect to
 the infrastructure created and the roads were under undue pressure.  In view, the Committee was of the opinion that
 there was a need to adopt a policy which discourages owning of multiple vehicles.  Accordingly, the Committee had
 recommended that acquiring more than one vehicle in a family should invite financial disincentives. The Committee had
 expressed hope that this would discourage owning of multiple vehicles and  also help release the pressure on the surface
 of the roads and also help positively to reduce vehicular pollution.

95.       The Committee understands that Automotive Research Association of India, Pune has been set up by the Indian

 etc. may be certified by an agency which has vehicle manufacturers as its members and on which the Department of
 Road Transport & Highways does not have any administrative control.  Therefore, the Committee recommends that
 either the Department of Road Transport & Highways should have control on the Automotive Research Association of
 India, Pune or the Association should be divested of the power of fixing emission norms as apex certification agency.  It
 is a direct case of the manufacturers themselves being the regulators, which promotes the scope of corruption in the
 functioning of the Institute.  The Committee desires that a proper enquiry may be instituted in the functioning of this
 institute.

 Rs.7.00 lakh for Research & Development out of the total allocation of Rs.7.9 crore during 2004-05, which makes
 evident as to how much priority the Department of Road Transport & Highways attaches to the Research &
 Development activities.  The Committee is dissatisfied that the Department of Road Transport & Highways has no
 Research & Development mechanism of its own and the Department has to depend upon other organisations for its
 Research & Development activities.  The Committee recommends that Department of Road Transport and Highways
 should develop an institutional mechanism to undertake research and development activities.  The Department should
 also create a regulator of its own with the requisite technical wherewithal for fixing emission norms, product design and
 development etc.  The Committee also recommends that the Research & Development activities should also be used for
 developing technology for creating cost-effective high quality road network such as existing in Malaysia and some other
 countries.
97.       The Committee observes that development of road data bank and computerized project monitoring system and
 promotion of use of information technology in the highway sector is one of the objectives of the Government in the
 Tenth Plan.  However, it appears that the Government has not achieved much progress in this sector especially the use
 of Information Technology in online  project monitoring system.  The Committee is of the view that Information
 Technology can also be used as an effective tool for monitoring the implementation of the projects undertaken by the
 Department.    They should also make efforts to integrate research and development activities with their monitoring
 system so that proper monitoring could be kept on the projects undertaken by them, which will also help them to keep
 tab on time overrun and cost overrun of such projects.   The Committee also recommends that efforts cited by the
 Department of Road Transport & Highways like creation of Ministry's chair in the area of development of highway
 system at Indian Institutes of Technology and creation of independent testing facilities for bridges on National
 Highways at Central Road Research Institute, New Delhi may be pursued more vigorously and in case of any difficulty,
 parallel institutions may be identified for Research & Development activities.

ANALYSIS OF THE TENTH PLAN PROVISIONS AND ANNUAL PLAN   2005-06
            The Committee regrets to note that BE Plan Provision of Rs.7912 crore of Department of Road Transport and

 NHAI and the Department.  Further, there was delay in finalisation of the loans and obtaining approvals for the

 Asian Development Bank (ADB).  Also, there was delay in award of works to the contractors and consultants for
 development of NH-34 in West Bengal.  These instances clearly bring out that on the one hand, the Department of Road
 Transport and Highways emphasizes that funds for development and maintenance of National Highways are inadequate
 while, on the other hand, the Department is not able to utilise the higher allocation made to it at BE stage.   The
 Department has failed to make optimal utilization of the funds at its disposal. The reasons for shortfall mentioned are
 delay in land acquisition, poor performance of the contractors, law and order problem etc. are very general in nature and
 are repeated every year. As a result of these, the budgetary allocations of the Department were reduced at RE stage.  The
 Committee is of the view that there is a lack of monitoring system in the Department of Road Transport and Highways.

 evident as to how much priority the Department of Road Transport & Highways attaches to the Research &
 Development activities.  The Committee is dissatisfied that the Department of Road Transport & Highways has no
 Research & Development mechanism of its own and the Department has to depend upon other organisations for its
 Research & Development activities.  The Committee recommends that Department of Road Transport and Highways
 should develop an institutional mechanism to undertake research and development activities.  The Department should

 countries.
            The Committee observes that development of road data bank and computerized project monitoring system and
 promotion of use of information technology in the highway sector is one of the objectives of the Government in the
 Tenth Plan.  However, it appears that the Government has not achieved much progress in this sector especially the use
 of Information Technology in online  project monitoring system.  The Committee is of the view that Information
 Technology can also be used as an effective tool for monitoring the implementation of the projects undertaken by the
 Department.    They should also make efforts to integrate research and development activities with their monitoring

 tab on time overrun and cost overrun of such projects.   The Committee also recommends that efforts cited by the
 Department of Road Transport & Highways like creation of Ministry's chair in the area of development of highway

 2.         At the outset, BRO made a brief presentation about their achievements and problems in the North Eastern
 Region.  The Committee was informed that over the last few years, a number of important National Highways have
 been handed over to the BRO.  Such entrustments include NH-52, linking the entire length of the North Bank of
 Bramhaputra River, which has been handed over to the BRO after the start of 10
th Plan.  The representatives of BRO
 stated that the major problem they were facing was paucity of funds.  They said that the budget outlays for BRO during
 the 10
th Plan period have not matched the requirements of funds for development and maintenance of the National
 Highway network under the charge of the BRO.  The approved outlay for 10 th Plan for National Highway work for
 BRO is Rs.950 crores against the projected outlay of Rs.1863 crores.  The projected requirements itself needs to be
 increased due to entrustment of 616 kms. of additional NHs (NH–52 and NH–109) subsequent to the projection of 10
th
 plan outlays.  The Committee noted their problem and asked the representatives of the Department of Road Transport
 and Highways, Government of India to offer their comments in the matter.  The Committee was then assured by the
 representatives of Department of Road Transport & Highways that fund would not be a problem for the BRO in the
 coming financial year.  Keeping in mind the scope of work and the requirement of funds, an additional Rs.110 crore
 have been allotted at the RE stage for the financial year 2004-05 (Feb’05), making up the total allotment of Rs.320
 crore.  The Ministry was trying to get higher allocation of funds for BRO in 2005-06 to match with final requirement of
 BRO.
3.         The BRO then drew attention to the absence of security at the work sites.  The problem of insurgency and
 providing of dedicated security cover had been discussed at all levels.  The State Government was not in a position to
 provide dedicated security.  After the recent incidents of abduction of vehicles and six personnel of (P) Pushpak, works
 had been stopped completely.  DGBR had also discussed this issue with HE, the Governor of Manipur, Hon’ble Chief
 Minister and Chief Secretary, Government of Manipur.  It had been decided that the Army would clear insurgents by
 carrying out combing operations and thereafter, dedicated Para Military Force would be deployed.  But, so far nothing
 had materialized on ground.  The matter had been discussed by the Hon’ble Minister MOS(RT&H) and subsequently, in
 a meeting chaired by the Special Secretary (IS), MHA on 24
th January, 2005.  Regarding insurgency problem the
 Committee was further informed that the issue of insurgency falls within the purview of Ministry of Home Affairs. 
 However, the construction of roads connecting the remote places of North Eastern Region are proposed in the above
 mentioned “Special Accelerated Road Development Programme (SARDP) in the North Eastern Region”, which will
 help in development of road infrastructure and consequently removing the insurgency in such areas.  Further, the
 Ministry was making all out efforts to provide dedicated security to Border Roads Organisation, who are involved in
 road construction activities in such areas of North Eastern Region, with the help of Ministry of Home Affairs and
 Ministry of Defence. 
4.         The Committee then specifically enquired about the flood control measures to which the Government

 representative (Department of Road Transport and Highways) replied that flood control measures and overall river
 training works fall within the purview of the Flood Control Departments of the respective State Governments.

 expected to be completed by December 2007.  The DPR for NH-52A has been divided into three packages viz. Itanagar
 Helem, Helem-Tejpur and Tejpur-Baihata Chariaili, which is on North-South Corridor.  The work has been awarded to
 3 consultants.  The present target for completion of DPR is June 2005 for first package and November, 2005 for other
 two packages.  The DPR for NH-54 from Silchar to Aizawl has been divided into 2 packages viz. Silchar-Kolasib and
 Kolasib-Aizawl (Silchar is on North South Corridor) and awarded to 2 consultants.  The present target for completion of
 DPR is June 2005.  The civil works under NHDP Phase III will be taken up after its approval from Government.
6.         The Committee enquired that if the responsibilities of construction and maintenance of all the roads of North
 Eastern Region, if given to Border Roads Organisation, can it be handled with the present set up?  To this query, the
 Committee was apprised that the total work load of the BRO in the North Eastern Region currently amounts to
 approximately Rs.700 crore annually.   The BRO had been additionally tasked to take on the Special Accelerated Road
 Development Programme (SARDP) work from the next financial year i.e. 2005-06.  However, with the current
 organisation and commitments, the BRO is only capable of taking on the SARDP work on the National Highways and
 other roads already with the organisation.  Even with this, the total work load translates to approximately Rs.4500 crore
 over the next 7-8 years, or about Rs.700 crore annually.  This would amount to doubling the current level of the work
 load.  As such the BRO would be stretched to its maximum, even with the additional provisions of permitting the BRO
 to outsource consultancy work etc. and hiring of additional equipment.  This is especially true due to the fact that
 outsourcing/availability of contractors is highly restricted in the concerned areas due to insurgency and other problems. 
 In view of this, it will not be possible for the BRO to take over any additional work.
