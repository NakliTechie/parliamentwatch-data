 following factors-:
(i)         Review and pruning of the outlays taking into account the decision to hand over some projects to the private
 sector; reduction in outlays for ongoing as also new schemes based on more realistic estimates.

 the reasons put forth by the Ministry of Shipping and is of the view that funds could have been utilised if timely action

 demanding for more allocation and on the other hand it has failed to utilize the available approved outlay.  The
 Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry should evolve some institutional mechanism to ensure better
 implementation control and monitoring of projects so that the plan outlay is utilized in higher proportions and the
 accountability be fixed for such lapses, if any, in this regard.

DEVELOPMENT OF INTERMEDIATE AND MINOR PORTS
 
20.       There are 184 minor/intermediate ports located along the coastline of India.  Most of these are located in
 Maharashtra, Gujarat, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. The Minor Ports are under the administrative domain
 of the State Governments who are also responsible for their development and creating capacities at their ports. 
 However, only 53 minor ports are well developed and provide all weather berthing facilities for cargo handling.  The
 remaining ports cater to fishing boats, passenger boats etc.  The cargo handled by these ports comprises mainly fishery
 products, food grains, fertilisers, building materials, coal, cement, petroleum crude and petro-products and edible oil. 
21.       The traffic handled by minor ports is continuously on the rise, increasing from 27.83 mt in 1996-97 to 86.58 mt
 in 2000-01. Currently, nearly 25 per cent of the total traffic is being handled by the intermediate/minor ports. 
22.       On a question of increasing the capacity of the minor ports in the country, Ministry of Shipping informed that
 the major ports fall within the ambit of the Central Government, while the responsibility for the development of non-
major ports lie with respective State Governments.  However, the Ministry of Shipping has decided to get a study
 conducted for development of coastal shipping and minor ports.  The study has been given to a consultant who will
 submit a report within this calendar year.  After receipt of the Report, Government will finalise its stand on the
 recommendations of the study.  The Ministry of Shipping also has a scheme for grant of Central Assistance to the
 Maritime States for conducting feasibility/environmental/hydrographic studies etc. for development of minor ports. 
 The total capacity of all ports in the country is sufficient to handle the present cargo.
 
23.       The Committee notes that the minor ports serve not only as trans-shipment points for trade but also as catalysts
 for economic activities in their respective regions. The role of minor ports is becoming increasingly important owing to
 the development of coastal shipping and they should be viewed as an alternative to congested major ports.  The
 Committee recommends that Ministry of Shipping should impress upon the concerned States to provide adequate funds
 for the development of minor ports so that they could effectively cater to coastal vessels and assist in development of
 the hinterland.  The Committee also recommends that keeping in view the contribution and importance of minor ports
 in cargo handling, Government of India may also consider to provide central grants to the Maritime States for
 development of Minor Ports.
 
CALCUTTA DOCK LABOUR BOARD
 
24.       The Committee draw attention of the Ministry of shipping on the representation received from the Calcutta Dock
 Labour Board Pensioner’s Association regarding the delay in payment of pensionary benefits of the employees of
 Calcutta Dock Labour Board, who retired prior to 1.1.98 and could not be given the required pensionary benefits so
 far.

25.       The Ministry of Shipping informed the Committee that Calcutta Dock Labour Board introduced a pension
 scheme for the employees of the Calcutta Dock Labour Board (other than the Registered Workers) with effect from
 1.4.1972. Subsequently pension was also allowed to the Registered and Listed Workers of the Dock Labour Board with
 effect from 1.1.1979.
 
26.       For the last few years, the Calcutta Dock Labour Board has been passing through stringent financial conditions
 and it is saddled with the problems of surplus manpower, huge number of pensioners on roll and inadequate revenue
 earning, etc. As a result, the Board is unable to make regular payment of salary and wages to its 1683 employees and
 workers and pension to 6668 pensioners on its roll.
27.       Further, by virtue of the Wage Settlement dated 2.8.2000 for the Port & Dockworkers, the quantum of pension
 payable to the pensioners was due to be revised notionally from 1.1.1997 and effectively from 1.1.1998. So far, of the
 6668 pensioners (including family pensioners) on roll, only 1711 pensioners are getting pension at revised rates and for
 the remaining pensioners, it has not been possible to pay pension at the revised rates due to financial constraints.
 Further, no pensioner so far has been paid the arrears arising out of revision of their pension. Apart from this, by virtue
 of the amendment of the Gratuity Act, 1972 with effect from 24.9.1997, gratuity at the enhanced rate i.e. Rs. 3.5 lakhs
 maximum, is payable to 540 retirees. The arrear payment for commutation pension payable at enhanced rate i.e. 40
 percent has also not been made so far.  For the employees and workers retired from 1.1.2002, it has not been possible to
 make payment on account of encashment of earned leave on superannuation.
 
28.       To overcome the situation various steps have already been taken by the Board to reduce the expenditure and to
 generate revenue. The Board has also undertaken cost cutting measures to reduce expenditure to the extent possible.
 Board is also identifying assets that are lying idle for disposal to augment its financial position. The Board has also
 considered necessary to introduce a special VRS for about 790 employees and workers to downsize the strength to a
 manageable figure.  For this purpose a proposal for grant of Rs.52.00. crores has been received from the Board and is
 under examination in consultation with the Ministry of Finance.  Further, Chairman, CDLB has also requested
 Government to consider providing one time grant of Rs.43.00 crores to the Board for the purpose of payment of
 outstanding dues to pensioners of the Board. The proposal has been examined in consultation with the Finance Wing of
 the Ministry and the same has been recommended to Ministry of Finance for consideration.
 
29.       The Committee takes strong exception to the fact that CDLB has not implemented revision of pension and
 liberlisation of post retirement benefits in terms of wage settlement and the pensioners/retired employees have not been
 paid pension and other post retirement benefits including gratuity, PF settlement etc. for the past so many months. The
 Committee feels that pensioners after their retirement fully depends on their retirement benefits and if the same are not
 disbursed to them in time, it will lead to harassment and it is also against the law.  The Committee does not approve the
 lackadaisical approach of the Ministry of Shipping in sharing the responsibility of pensioners of CDLB.  The
 Committee recommends that Ministry of Shipping should take responsibility in the matter and ensure that payment of
 pensionary benefits to the employee of CDLB who retired prior to 1.1.98 and revision on pension and payment of
 arrears etc. be implemented at the earliest.

 undertakes various infrastructure development works on National Waterways.  It also carries out feasibility studies and
 prepares proposals for declaration of other waterways as National Waterways.  It also advises Central Government in
 matters related to IWT and assists State Governments in development and regulation of this mode.

 Last year, foreign trade stood at 96 billion dollars.  Even if we take only 75 percent of that value as that
 passing through ports, we are looking at 7.2 billion dollars. It comes to about Rs. 35,500 crores.  If we were
 like other developed countries, our freight charges would come down to Rs. 27,600 crores, a fall of nearly Rs.
 8000 crores.  For this reason, it is very important to have a large fleet.  It is important for other reasons also.  It
 will give a boost to businesses like ship booking, insurance, maritime law, chandlering, ship management,
 manning agencies, etc.  Also, this will provide employment opportunities.  In the U.K., tonnage tax was
 introduced in the year 1998.  Invisibles increased in the U.K. economy by two billion pounds Two billion
 pounds are equal to approximately Rs.14,000 crores, which is a huge amount.  This was the amount of benefit
 that economy was able to get.  We believe that the same thing should happen here.  If reforms could be
 brought about, a lot of things could happen to the Indian fleet.  Not only invisibles will go up, but employment
 will also go up.”

CONSTRUCTION OF SINGLE BUOY MOORING (SBM)
 
77.       Kochi Refinery Ltd. (KRL) had a proposal for augmenting their capacity from 7.5 MTPA to 13.5 MTPA and
 they had ­­­mooted a proposal for installing a Single Buoy Mooring (SBM) in the open sea outside the port limit for
 handling the crude oil without utilizing the Cochin Port Facilities.  However, Cochin Port Trust has opposed the
 proposal on the ground that in the event of setting up of the SBM by the KRL in the open sea, there would be non-
utilisation of the existing infrastructure created by the port exclusively for the Kochi Refineries Ltd. (KRL) for handling
 POL products for their Refineries. 
 
78.       The Committee had in its Fifty-second and Fifty-seventh Reports on Demand for Grants 2001-2002 and 2002-
2003 respectively of the Ministry of Shipping recommended that Ministries of Shipping and Petroleum and Natural Gas
 to evolve an amicable solution to the problem. Kochi Refinery Ltd. should consider, in the national interest, the
 alternative proposals suggested by the Cochin Port Trust before taking up the construction of the Single Buoy Mooring.
 
79.            Commenting upon the subject, the Secretary in his deposition before the Committee stated as under: -
“The Cochin port should be an area in which SBM of the Cochin Refineries should be established.  On the
 recommendation of the Honourable Committee, we took it up with the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.  We
 have now been able to get an agreement for setting up the SBM within the Cochin port limits.  A MoU will be signed
 very shortly.”
 
80.       The Committee appreciates that the Ministry of shipping and Petroleum and Natural Gas have been able to reach
 an amicable solution for the setting up of the SBM and there has been an agreement for setting up the SBM within the
 Cochin Port Limits.
OBSERVATION/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
ANALYSIS OF ANNUAL PLAN 2003-04
The Committee observes that the Budget Estimates of Ministry of Shipping include allocation of Rs.10.06 crores for
 execution of civil works through CPWD for DG (Shipping).  The amount earmarked is also reflected in the Demands
 for Grants of Ministry of Urban Development and is spent by CPWD on the projects of the Ministry of Shipping.  The
 Committee is of the view that the practice of showing the same amount in Demands for Grants of two Ministries creates
 confusion and therefore recommends that this practice should be reviewed and the amount for the civil works should be
 reflected in Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Shipping only and placed at the disposal of Ministry of Shipping
 instead of Ministry of Urban Development in order to ensure better implementation, control and monitoring of
 projects.

PORT SECTOR
The Committee notes with concern that there has been a heavy shortfall in utilization of fund as compared to the
 approved outlay. During 2002-03 an outlay of Rs.1162.00 crores was provided to the Ministry of Shipping for Port
 Sector and the Ministry could utilize only Rs.439.81 crores (upto February 2003).  The Committee is not satisfied with
 the reasons put forth by the Ministry of Shipping and is of the view that funds could have been utilised if timely action
 to address the problem was initiated by the Ministry.  The Committee feels that on the one hand the Ministry is
 demanding for more allocation and on the other hand it has failed to utilize the available approved outlay.  The
 Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry should evolve some institutional mechanism to ensure better
 implementation control and monitoring of projects so that the plan outlay is utilized in higher proportions and the
 accountability be fixed for such lapses, if any, in this regard.                        
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry of Shipping in improving the efficiency and capacity of
 Indian Ports.  The Committee notes that during 10
th Plan the Ministry of Shipping will be able to add a capacity of
 127.10 million tonnes bringing the total capacity to 470.6 million tonnes as against the traffic of 415 million tones by
 the Working Group on Port Sector.  The Committee hopes that with the modernisation of Ports and enhancement of
 service quality, the Ports will be able to utilise the additional capacity vis-a vis traffic comfortably. 
                                                                        (Paras 14 &19)
 
DEVELOPMENT OF INTERMEDIATE AND MINOR PORTS
The Committee notes that the minor ports serve not only as trans-shipment points for trade but also as catalysts for
 economic activities in their respective regions. The role of minor ports is becoming increasingly important owing to the
 development of coastal shipping and they should be viewed as an alternative to congested major ports.  The Committee
 recommends that Ministry of Shipping should impress upon the concerned States to provide adequate funds for the
 development of minor ports so that they could effectively cater to coastal vessels and assist in development of the
 hinterland.  The Committee also recommends that keeping in view the contribution and importance of minor ports in
 cargo handling, Government of India may also consider to provide central grants to the Maritime States for
 development of Minor Ports.    
                                                (Para 23)
CALCUTTA DOCK LABOUR BOARD
The Committee takes strong exception to the fact that CDLB has not implemented revision of pension and liberlisation
 of post retirement benefits in terms of wage settlement and the pensioner/retired employees has not been paid pension
 and other post retirement benefits including gratuity, PF settlement etc. for the past so many months. The Committee
 feels that pensioners after their retirement fully depends on their retirement benefits and if the same are not disbursed to
 them in time, it will lead to harassment and also against the law.  The Committee does not approve the lackadaisical
 approach of the Ministry of Shipping in sharing the responsibility of pensioners of CDLB.  The Committee
 recommends that Ministry of Shipping should take responsibility in the matter and ensure that payment of pensionary
 benefits to the employee of CDLB who retired prior to 1.1.98 and revision on pension and payment of arrears etc. be
 implemented at the earliest.
                                                                                                              (Para 29)
INLAND WATER TRANSPORT
The Committee notes with concern that in India, IWT sector has not been developed with desired pace and thus it
 cannot compete with the developed modes like road and rail.  The Committee observes that though, measures have
 been put in place during the 9
th Plan to move towards giving a push for accelerated and all-around development of IWT
 sector in the 10 th Plan period.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that only an amount of Rs.152 crores
 was utilized against the approved outlays of Rs.308 crores during 9 th Plan period.  The Committee recommends the
 Ministry should gear up its machinery to ensure that 10 th Plan outlay is fully utilized to exploit the potential of this
 important mode of transport and approach the Government of India for higher budgetary allocation at R.E. stage so that
 the work on declaration and development of four Waterways as National Waterways could be taken up on priority.

 Institutes, the unions of the employees be consulted and it should be ensured that the terms and conditions of service of

 safeguarded.
