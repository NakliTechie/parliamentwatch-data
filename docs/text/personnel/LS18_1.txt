OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY

Establishment Wing) and Ministry of Finan ce (Establishment Division), with Union Public Service Commission playing an important  advisory role. The Establishmen t
Officer to the Government of India, functioning under the Cabinet Secretary, had an important role to play in the senior level appointments under the Government of India.
All this made for improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified central direction. 
  
2.         To plug this organisational loophole, the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha, in its 93
rd Report (April 1966) had observed that “the ever expanding role o f 
the Government in a welfare State with its natural concomitant of a large civil service calls for effective personnel control t hrough a single agency”. The Administrative 
Reforms Commission (ARC) too urged upon the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel with a full fledged Secretary-in-charge, to be placed directly under 
the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal agency for the personnel functions of the Government. 
  
3.         The Union Government accepted this recommendation, and as a consequence, led to the reorganization of the Ministry o f Home Affairs as it then stood, resulting
eventually in the formation of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, as it stands today.  This process was initiated on June 26, 1970 when the Department o
f 
Personnel was notified as a department in the Cabinet Secretar iat.  The process was taken further on August 1, 1970 with the no tification of the items of work to be dealt with
by the Department.  Eventually, on March 15, 1985, a full fledged ‘Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions’ was notified. 
4.0       The Ministry is the coordinating agency of the Cent ral Government in personnel matters, specially the issues concerni ng recruitment, promotion and morale o f
services, training, vigilance and discipline, career development and service conditions of the middle and senior level manageme nt, grievances and staff welfare, as well as the
process of responsive people–oriented modern administration.  The Ministry comprises three Departments, namely: - 
  
(i)      Department of Personnel and Training; 
(ii)        Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare; and 
(iii)       Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. 
  
4.1
       The Ministry has under its administrative control four attached offices, namely: - 
             
(i)         Staff Selection Commission (SSC); 
(ii)        Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM);  
(iii)       Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA); and 
            (iv)       The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). 
  
4.2       Pursuant to amendment in the Government of Indi a (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, effected on 30 January 2003, t he Central Bureau of Investigation was
delinked from  the Ministry and was put under the administrative c ontrol of Cabinet Secretariat. However, amendment to the Gove rnment of India (Allocation of Business)
Rules, 1961, effected on 7 June, 2004 brought back the organisation under the administrative control of the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pension. 
  
4.3        The other offices under the administrative control of the Ministry are: - 
  
(i)         Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), a constitutional body;
 
(ii)        Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), a statutory body; 
(iii)       Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), a statutory quasi-judicial body; and
 
(iv)       Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a body set up by a resolution of Government. 
  
5.         The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Pr ime Minister, assisted by a Minister of State.  All the three Depa rtments are functioning under the charge of the 
Secretary (Personnel), assisted by one Special Secretary, three Additional Secretaries and three Joint Secretaries and other supporting officers and staff. 
  
Department of Personnel and Training  
  
6.0       The role of the Department of Personnel and Training can be conceptually divided into two parts.  In its larger nodal  role, it acts as the formulator of policy and the 
watchdog of Government, ensuring that certain accepted standards and norms, as laid down by it, are followed by all Ministries/ Departments in the recruitment, regulation o
f
service conditions and deputation of personnel, as well as other related issues.  Towards this end, guidelines are issued by it  for the benefit of all the Ministries/Departments
and it monitors implementation of these guidelines.  It also a dvises all organisations of the Central Government on issues of p ersonnel management.  At a more immediate 
level, the Department has the direct responsibility of being th e cadre controlling authority for the Indian Administrative Serv ice (IAS) and the Central Secretariat Service
(CSS).  The Department also operates the Central Staffing Scheme under which suitable officers from All India Services and Group ‘A’ Central Services are selected and then 
placed in posts at the level of Deputy Secretary, Director and Joint Secretary, on the basis of tenure deputation.  The Department also deals with cases of appointment to posts 
of Chairmen, Managing Directors, full-time functional Directors/Members of the Board of Management of various public sector und ertakings/enterprises, corporations, banks
and financial institutions.  It also deals with the assignment of Indian experts to various developing countries. 
  
  
Recruitment Agencies 
  
6.1       The two organisations through which the Department en sures recruitment of personnel for Government are the Union Publ ic Service Commission (UPSC) and the
Staff Selection Commission (SSC). The former is constituted under a provision of the Constitution and is responsible for conduc ting examinations for appointment to the
higher civil services and civil posts under the Union Government, including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a m andatory provision for consulting the
Commission on all matters relating to methods of  recruitment, principles to be followed in making promotions and transfers fro m one service to another and all the
disciplinary matters. The SSC is responsible for making recruitment for Group ‘B’ non-gazetted posts of Assistants, Stenographers, etc. 
  
  
Training Institutions 
  
6.2       Two major training institutions, directly under the administrative control of the Department, are the Lal Bahadur Sha stri National Academy of Administration
(LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the Institute of Secretariat Training and Mana gement (ISTM), New Delhi. The former   is   mainly   resp onsible   for   providing   induction   
training   to  recruits   to  the Indian Administrative Serv ice and other All India Services and Central Services. The Institut e of Secretariat Training and Management provides
induction as well as in-service training to members of the Central Secretariat Service. 
  
Senior Appointments under the Government of India 
  
6.3       All proposals for senior appointments under the Governme nt of India, which require the approval of Appointments Commi ttee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the
Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary to the ACC.  These include board level appointments 
to Central Public Sector Undertakings and appointments of Join t Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the Ministries /Departments. In addition, all appointments by
promotion, which require approval of the ACC, are also processed through the Establishment Officer. 
  
Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) 
  
6.4       The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making recommendations for appo intment to the full-time Board level posts o
f 
Chief Executive and functional Director and part-time Chairman in the Central Public Undertakings.

6.5       The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for maintenance of integrity of the public serv ices and eradication of corruption, an d
coordinates the activities of various Ministries/Departments in that area. 
  
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) 
  
6.6       Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Ce ntral Vigilance Commission. It has jurisdiction and powers in re spect of all matters to which the executive powe r
of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted with the supervision of the Central Bureau of Investigation ( CBI). The Commission enjoys the same
measure of independence and autonomy, as the Union Public Service Commission. 
  
  
Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) 
  
6.7       The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is the premier investigating police agency in the country, created through a resolution of Ministry of Home Affairs date
d
April 1, 1963 administering Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946, its jurisdiction covering all the Departments of the Government of India, Union Territories,
which could be extended to the States also with the consent of the State Governments concerned. In due course, with the setting  up of a large number of Public Secto r
Undertakings, the employees of those undertakings were also brought under the purview of CBI. Similarly, with the nationalizati on of the Banks in 1969, the Public Sector  
Banks and their employees also came within the ambit of the CBI .  By now, the CBI has become multi-disciplinary investigative a gency, with its role expanding from 
corruption cases to other cases viz- cyber crimes, terrorist crimes, wildlife crimes, narcotics, arms trafficking, arts and antiquities cases, counterfeiting of currencies, etc.  
  
Joint Consultative Machinery 
  
6.8       The Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM) is a three- tier machinery, consisting of the National Council, the Departmenta l Council and the Regional/Office Council.
Service matters pertaining to interests of the generality of employees or specific groups of them are dealt with by this machinery. 
  
  
Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) 
  
6.9       In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to  employees aggrieved by the Government decisions, Government had  set up the Central Administrative Tribunal  
(CAT) in 1985, which deals with all the cases relating to service matters of Central Govt. employees.   
  
  
Staff Welfare 
  
6.10     The Department of Personnel and Training is intimately involved in various staff welfare and other role led activities .  Mention in this connection can be made of the 
institution of Grih Kalyan Kendra (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860), the Kendriya Bhandar (a registered cooperative society set up in 1963) an
d
the Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860). 
  
6.11     The Department also supports various programmes, which are provided in various Ministries/Departments, Central Governm ent Employees Welfare Coordination
Committee in Central Government offices located outside Delhi, Residents’ Welfare Associations/Area Welfare Officers; Benevolent Fund and Departmental Canteens.   
  
  
Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare 
  
7.0       The Department of Pensions a nd Pensioners’ Welfare is the nodal agency of the Government of India for formulation of general policy on pension and othe
r
retirement benefits, as also for redressal of grievances relating to retirement benefits.  The Department was set up in 1985 as   part of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances 
and Pensions and has been rendering service for the benefit of pe nsioners since then.  The Department has taken a number of ste ps over the years for streamlining the pension
administration system, for greater convenience and welfare of the pensioners. 
  
7.1       This Department, does not deal directly with pensione rs but is the focal point for dealing with issues concerning pen sioners, and for issue of instructions an
d
guidelines regarding matters pertaining to pensioners.  
  
7.2       The main responsibilities entrusted to this Department are:  
  
(i)         Formulation of policies and coordination of matters relating to retirement benefits of Central Govt. employees, including Defence and Railways; 
 
(ii)        Administration of : 
  
(a)        Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972; 
(b)        Central Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981; 
(c)        Central Civil Services (Extraordinary Pensions) Rules 1939; 
(d)        General Provident Fund (Civil Services) Rules, 1960; and 
(e)        Contributory Provident Fund (India) Rules, 1962; 
(iii)       Pension policy structure; 
(iv)       Examination & extending the scope of new facilities for fringe benefits to the Central Government Pensioners; 
(v)        Matters relating to amendments to or relaxation of Pension Rules or any other rules concerning retirement benefits; and  
(vi)       Policy and coordination relating to welfare of Central Government Pensioners/Family Pensioners.  
  
Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances 
  
8.         The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of the Government of India for ad ministrative reforms, as well as for redressal
of public grievances relating to the State in general and grievan ces pertaining to Central Government agencies in particular. T he Department disseminates information on
important activities of Government relating to administrative re forms and public grievances redressal through Management Public ations and Documentation.  This 
Department also acts as the nodal agency in regard to Organisati on and Methods activities and offers advice to the Ministries/D epartments. It also undertakes activities in the
field of international co-operation and exchanges, to promote public service reforms. 
  
8.1       This Department has, in the past few years, taken se veral initiatives with a view to bring about improvement in the f unctioning and effectiveness of Governmen
t
Departments, important ones being the following: 
  
(i)         drawing up an agenda for reforms to be carried out in the Central Government as well as in States; and 
(ii)        undertaking, through a Commission, a review of administrative laws which have become obsolete and no longer need to be in the statute book. 
  
8.2     The Department also initiated an exercise on the part of Departments having public interface to draw up their individua l Citizen's Charter to indicate the commitments
of that organization for quality and the time-frame in which services performed by that organization would be completed for members of the public.

Citizens' Charter and Information & Facilitation Counters 
  
18.                   As per the action plan for effective and responsive administration adopted at the Conference of Chief Ministries held in May, 1 997, the Central Governmen t
and the State Governments,   which have a large public inter -face, have been formulating Citizens' Charters wh ich publicise the services to members of the public,
commitments of the organization, the expected standard of service delivery, time-frame, and grievances redressal mechanism.    
  
18.1           The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances  coordinates the efforts to formulate and operationalise Citizens ' Charter by the Central
Government Ministries/Departments  
and their PSUs/autonomous/statutory bodies.  As stated in the Annual Report (2003-2004) of the Ministry, 25 Ministries/Departme nts had brought out 97 Citizens' Charters
and 24 State Governments/UT Administrations have brought out 616 Citizens' Charters as on 31.3.2004.       
  
18.2                 The Charter is conceptualised as a document of trust  between the Organisation and the citizens, as follows:- 
  
(i)         Trust on the part of the citizens that the organisation will deliver the services according to the commitments made in the Citizens' Charter; 
(ii)        Trust on the part of the organisation that the citizens' expectations will be realistic and that they shall fulfill their own obligations to avail the services.  
  
18.3                 The Citizens' Charter of an organisation is expected to contain the following:- 
  
            •          Vision and Mission of the Organisation;  
            •          Details of business transacted by the Organisation; 
            •          Details of citizens/clients; 
•          Details of services including time norms and quality provided to each citizen/client group; 
            •          How to access the services;  
•          Details of Public Grievance Redress Mechanism and procedures for access;  
            •          obligations of the citizen/client. 
  
18.4                 The Department is in the process of developing a scheme for the award of the ‘Charter Mark’. Charter Mark is a quality standard, which would encourage an d 
reward improvements in public service, with reference to the commitments and standards notified in the Charter.   These agencies, that are implementing their Charte r 
exemplarily and are providing an excellent standard of serv ice, will receive certification/recognition in the form of a ‘Charter Mark’ after an independent assessment. The 
Charter Mark would be given after assessing results, rather than just the processes, depending upon the quality of service that the users actually receive.  
  
18.5                 The Department entered into a contract with M/s Tata Consulta ncy Servies to develop Charter Mark Scheme on 11 March, 2004. The Department o f
Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances has accessed World Bank funds for this project.  
  
19.0                 Information and Facilitaion counter (IFC) is the visible face/phys ical manifestation of Citizens' Charter formulated by an orga nization.  IFCs are set-up outside 
the security zone of each office, with a view to facilitating the dissemination of information to the citizen/user at one place , without the hassle of getting an entry pass.
Information is disseminated through printouts, brochures, book-lets, display boards, and sometimes telephonically too. 
  
19.1          Computerised Information and Facilitation counters have been set up in various Ministries/ Departments/Organizations, etc.  of the Government of India, to provide 
information and assistance to the public regarding the schemes a nd procedures of the organization concerned and also to acknowl edge and to apprise status report o f
individual applications and grievances.

Public Grievance Redressal 
  
20.0                 Public Grievances are redressed in the Government of India th rough a decentralized system, which functions in every Ministry/De partment.  The grievances of 
the public are received at various points in the Government of India.   The following designated nodal agencies exit in the Government of India for handling public
grievances:- 
(i)                  The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions; and 
(ii)                Directorate of Public Grievances, Cabinet Secretariat. 
  
20.1                 The role of the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances comprises primarily to undertake such citizen -centric initiatives in the field o f 
administrative reforms and public grievances in the Government, so  as to enable the Government machinery to deliver quality pub lic services to the citizen in a hassle -free 
manner and eliminate the causes of grievance. 
  
20.2                 Besides external grievance mechanism, Internal Grievance Redress Machinery exist within  each Ministry/Department.    An officer of the rank of Joint 
Secretary is  designated as Director of Grievances, in-charge of the grievances redressal machinery in each Central Government Ministry/Department,  with the powers to call 
for files/papers relating to public grievances which are pending for over three months, and to take a decision with the approva l of the Secretary/Head of the Departmen t
concerned.   
  
20.3                 The grievances received in the Department of AR & PG are sent to the concerned Ministries/Departments and other organisations o f Government for redressal
under intimation to the petitioner.     
  
20.4                 Regular meetings are held by AS (AR&PG) with Directors of Grie vances of Ministries/Departments/Organisations with large pendency of  cases, with  a view 
to expedite action on pending grievances. 
  
20.5                 The Committee was informed that Director -General, Doordarshan has issued instructions to publicise nodal agencies of Public Grievance Redressal to all
Doordarshan Kendras.  Modalities to extend publicity through AIR are also being worked out. 
  
20.6                 Among various other measures taken by Government to  strengthen the grievance redressal mechanism, a   uniform software called Public Grievance Redress 
and Monitoring System, has been installed in 22 Mi nistries/Departments and 13 other organizations.   The internet version of this system was launched on 31.05.2002 
(http://DARPG.grievance/nic.in).  A citizen can launch grievance on this website and can monitor the action taken thereon by accessing the website.   A mechanism of "Fas t 
Track" for taking up grievances of grave nature relating to Government agencies, with large public interface, has also been introduced. 
  
20.7                 The Ministry, in its Action Taken Replies on the recommendations of the Committee on Home Affairs contained in its Ninety -ninth Report, stated that the 
Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, as the nodal agency in the Government for matters relating to publi c grievances, reviews periodically the
performance of Central Government Ministries/Departmen ts with respect to redressal of public grievances.   The Department takes up about 500 grievances every year fo r 
intensive monitoring till their disposal, with the concerned Cent ral Government Ministry/Department/State Government/UT Adminis tration, to monitor the effectiveness o f
the grievances redressal machinery of the concerned Government agency.

Lokpal 
         
 
 
23.0      The proposal to establish Lokpal at the Centre and L ok Ayuktas in States, for redressal of public grievances emanates  from one of the Reports of ARC as mentione d
at para 16.3 of the Report. The concept of institution of Lokpal is quite old in the Indian context, having its genesis in the speech of Dr. L.M. Singhvi MP  in the Lok Sabh a 
 on Demands for Grants of Ministry of Law and Justice on 3 April, 1963.  The institution, from its conceptual stage, has traver sed a long way in the form of a number o f
legislative proposals of the Government within the last four decades.  But the institution has not seen the light of the day yet. 
  
  
23.1                 No doubt there is constant endeavor on the part of the Government to set up Lokpal, which is amply evident  from their attempts to introduce a series o
f
legislative proposals* in Parliament, one bringing improvements upon the other. Equally im portant were the attempts by the Members of Parliament to i ntroduce such a
legislation in the form of Private Members’ Bills**.   
  
  
23.2   Over the years there has been a paradigm shift in the ce ntral focus of the Government Bills from the issue of redressal of public grievances to investigation into
corruption  cases against public functionaries (present and past).   The office of Prime Minister was included in the definition  of public functionaries in the Government Bills
of 1989, 1996,1998 and 2001. 
  
  
23.3   The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs also examined the issue thrice and presented three Reports ♣
. 
  
23.4    The Ministry had finalised certain official amendments to the Bill of 2001, which had lapsed with the dissolution of Th irteenth Lok Sabha. The Government has kept
the issue alive by showing keen interest for establishment of the institution of Lokpal, as budgetary provision for the same was/ is made in the interim as well as in the current
BE (2004-05). 
  
23.5   It may not be out of place to mention that almost fifteen States have already established such an institution in their respective States during the last four decades.   
  
23.6     The Government has announced in the National Common Minimum Programme that the Lokpal Bill will be enacted into law. T he Committee urges upon the
Government  to take appropriate measures for establishing Lokpal without delay.

Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy Of Administration 
  
26.0     The Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA),  located at Mussoori in Uttranchal, is primarily an institu tion for training of entrants to
Government service through the annual Civil Service Examination conducted by the UPSC.   The responsibility is discharged by the Academy by means of a Foundational 
Course conducted for the bulk of the different services covere d by the Civil Services Examination, besides imparting intensive and in-depth training to entrants to the Indian 
Administrative Service and members of the Royal Bhutan Service.   The Academy also conducts programmes for in -service training of IAS officers, including officers 
appointed to the IAS from State Civil Services, as  well as workshops and seminars on policy issues.    The training institutions for other services like the IPS, the Indian 
Revenue Service as well as the different Railway Services, are the National Police Academy at Hyderabad, the National Academy for Direct Taxes at Nagpur and the Railway
Staff College at Vadodara. 
  
26.1     In so far as the Foundational Course for the All India and the Central Services is concerned, the objectives of this course is given below: 
  
(a)                to promote esprit de corps  among the probationers of different services, emphasizing the inter -dependence of various services as part of the higher civil 
services; 
(b)               to motivate probationers towards developing certain basic professional, administrative and human values; and 
(c)                to impart a basic understanding of the constitutional, political, social, economic, legal, historical, cultural and administrat ive framework, within which the
services function. 
26.2     In addition to the induction and professional courses exclusively for IAS officers, the Academy also runs a large number of sho rt duration courses also, ranging from
2-3 days to two weeks duration.

that the course content should be made releva nt to their posting and include components on self -awareness and human values.   It had further recommended that the training 
should be broad based to cater to the maximum number of officers.

to conduct programmes for officers joining in secretariat positions.   With regard to the Committee's recommendations to counter the declining seriousness of the participants
in the training programmes, the Ministry informed that the input s of the Committee will be reviewed, in consultation with the S tanding Syllabus Review Committee.   The 
Committee, was also informed that the Academy can run more courses on the module on "Ethical Issues in Administration", if slot ted by Department of Personnel an d
Training (DOPT), subject to infrastructure and faculty availability. 
  
26.4                 The Committee expects that the premier training institution of the country ought to be adequately equipped to conduct programme  for officers joining in

Central Bureau Of Investigation (CBI) 
  
27.0     The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), the premier investigating police agency in the country, was created to admi nister DPSE Act, 1946. In due course, with the
setting up of a large number of Public Sector Undertakings, the employees of those undertakings were also brought under the pur view of CBI.  Similarly, with the 
nationalization of Banks in 1969, the Public Sector Banks and thei r employees also came within the ambit of the CBI.  By now, t he CBI has become multi-disciplinary 
investigative agency, its role expanding from the corruption cases to other cases viz – cyber crime, terrorist crime, wildlife crime, narcotics, arms trafficking, arts an d 
antiquities cases, counterfeiting of currencies, etc.  
  
27.1     In 1994 the  structure of CBI was organised into foll owing Divisions: (i) Anti Corruption Division (ACD) (ii) Special Crime Division (SCD) (iii) Economic Offences
Division (EOD), which are Investigating Divisions.   
  
27.2     Anti Corruption Division investigates cases of corruptio n and frauds committed by public servants of all the Central G overnment Departments, Central Public Secto
r
Undertakings and Financial Institutions.  The Special Crime Divi sion investigates into cases of terrorism, bomb blast, sensatio nal homicide, kidnapping, murder and crimes
committed by the mafia/underworld. Economic Offences Division investigates economic crimes, including bank frauds, cheating, fi nancial frauds, Import Export and Foreign
Exchange violations, large scale smuggling of narcotics, antiques and other contraband items.   
  
27.3     Following the Supreme Court Judgment of 18 December, 1997 in the Vineet Narain   case, the Directorate of   Prosecution was set up in CBI in September, 2001. This 
Directorate is responsible for conducting prosecution of CBI cases in courts and  giving legal advice to CBI officers. 
  
27.4     The CBI, through its Policy and Coordination Division, coordinates investigation for the members of INTERPOLφ 
  
27.5     The CBI has also set up a Cyber Crime Investigation and Research Cell which besides doing research in this field caters to the long felt need of the investigating
agencies also. It has also set up Criminal Intelligence Cell.  The organizational structure of the CBI as on date is given below:-

Tribunals for adjudication of disputes and complaints with rega rd to recruitment and conditions of service of persons appointed  to public services and posts ,in connection
with the affairs of the Union and the States. 
  
28.1     Administrative adjudication, which is quasi -judicial in nature, is the main function of Administrative Tribunals.   The basic objective of enacting the Administrative 
Tribunals Act was: 
(i)                  to relieve congestion in the ordinary courts; and 
(ii)                to provide for speedy disposal of disputes relating to service matters.

28.2     The Central Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 provides that the Tribunal shall not be bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 bu t
shall be guided by the principles of natural justice, and the ot her provisions of Administrative Tribunals Act and rules made b y the Central Government.   The Tribunal shall 
have power to regulate its own procedure, including the fixing of places and times of enquiry.   As a result of the judgement of the Supreme Court in the case of L. Chandr a 
Kumar dated 18 March, 1997, the appeals against the orders of an Administrative Tribunals lie before the Division Bench of the concerned High Court. 
  
28.3     Administrative Tribunals are distinguishable from the ordinary  courts with regard to their jurisdiction and procedure.   They are free from the shackles of many of the 
technicalities of the ordinary courts.   The litigant(s) submit an application in proper book form, prescr ibed by rule 4 of Central Administrative Tribunals (Procedure)  Rules,
1985.  It means that every application should be accompanied by the following:- 
(i)                  Certified copy of the order against which the application has been filed; 
(ii)                All documents relied upon by the applicants and mentioned in the application; 
(iii)               Details of the crossed Demand Draft or crossed Indian Postal Order, representing the application fee of Rs. 50/-; and 
(iv)              Index of documents. 
  
The aggrieved person can appear before the Court personally.  Government can also present its case through its Departmental officers or legal practitioners. 
  
28.4     A Tribunal consists of a sanctioned strength of one Chairman, 16 Vice -Chairmen and 49 Members.   The Members are drawn from both judicial as well as 
administrative streams, so as to give the Tribunal the benefit of expertise, both in legal and administrative spheres.

number of cases are being brought forward each year.   It had, therefore, recommended that the Government should classify cases age -wise and give priority in clearing 
pendency of cases.  The Ministry, in its Action Taken Replies had  informed that the Government does not interfere in the judicial functioning of the CAT and had furnished a
statement showing age-wise break-up of the cases pending in all the benches of the CAT, as follows:

28.6     The Committee notes that the pendency of cases in some Benches of CAT (Principal Bench at New Delhi, Allahabad Bench, Lucknow Bench, Kolkata Bench, Cuttack
Bench and Patna Bench) are prominently and disturbingly high.   Although the Ministry had claimed that the Government  does not interfere in the judicial functioning o f

Central Vigilance Commission 
  
29.0                 The Central Vigilance Commission, an apex body exercising general superintendence and control over vigilance matters in adminis tration o f
Ministries/Department/Organisations of Central Government was conceptualized under Government of India Resolution dated 11 February, 1964.  The Commission is headed 
by the Central Vigilance Commissioner, who is assisted by a Secret ary who is of the rank of Addi tional Secretary to the Governme nt of India, two Additional Secretaries o f
the rank of Joint Secretary to the Government of India and other supporting Officers and staff.   There are thirteen Commissioners of Departmental Inquiries (CDI), who 
conduct oral inquiries relating to major penalty proceedings on behalf of the disciplinary authorities, in serious and important disciplinary cases. 
  
29.1     The CVC assumed statutory status by the CVC Act, 2003 on 12 September 2003.   The Department of Personnel and Training is responsible for laying Annual Repor t 
of the CVC before each House of Parliament, alongw ith Explanatory Memorandum on the reasons for non -acceptance of the Commission's advice.   Annual Report for the 
year 2001, alongwith Explanatory Memorandum was laid on the Table of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha on 16 and 23 December, 2003, respectively.

Table of both the Houses.  Since preparation of Memorandum gets  delayed due to delay in obtaining information from the concerne d Ministries/Departments, thereby
resulting in delay in submission of Annual Report to Parliament .  After enactment of the CVC Act, 2003, the CVC is required to submit its Annual Report to the Presiden t
within six months of the close of the year under report.

Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) 
  
31.0     In order to evolve a sound managerial personnel policy fo r the Public Sector Enterprises, and in particular, to advise  Government on appointments to the top
management posts, the Government of India constituted a Publ ic Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a high powered body, by a Re solution dated 30th
 August, 1974.  The 
Public Sector Enterprises Board (PSEB) is under the administrative control of the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions. Consequent upon the shifting of the
Secretariat of the PESB from the Department of Public En terprises to the Department of Personnel & Training from 9th  July, 1986, a comprehensive review of the policy 
relating to the functions, membership, methodology for selection a nd infrastructure of the PESB was made by Resolution of Minis try of Personnel, Public Grievances &
Pensions dated 3rd March 1987.  Amendments have been made to the Resolution from time to time, the latest being made on 19th April, 2000. 
  
31.1     The PESB functions as a professional body, with a large measure of autonomy.  Its function are: 
(i)                  to be responsible for the selection and placement of personnel in  the posts of Chairman, Managing Director or Chairman-cum-Mana ging Director (Level I), and 
Functional Director (Level II) in PSEs as well as in posts at any other level, as may be specified by the Government; 
(ii)                to advise the Government on matters relating to appointment, conf irmation or extension of tenure and termination of the service s of personnel of the above
mentioned levels; 
(iii)               to advise the Government on the desired structure at the Board level, and for senior management personnel, for each PSE or group of PSEs; 
(iv)              to advise the Government on a suitable performance appraisal system for both the PSEs and the managerial personnel in such enterprises; 
(v)                to build a data bank containing data relating to the performance of PSEs and its officers; 
(vi)              to advise the Government on formulation and enforcement of a Code of Conduct and Ethics for managerial personnel in PSEs; and 
(vii)             to advise the Government on evolving suitable training and development programmes for management personnel in PSEs. 
  
31.2     The PESB is headed by a full time Chairman, with two Me mbers.  The Chairperson and Members shall be persons who have h ad a long and distinguished career in
management of public or private corporations or public administration and have a proven record of achievements, preferably in t he field of personnel, finance, production o r
marketing.  A Member of the PESB shall be: 
(i)                  A distinguished serving or former Chief Executive of a Public Sector or Private Sector or Joint Sector Enterprise; 
(ii)                A distinguished person with experience in, selection of top management personnel; 
(iii)               A distinguished serving or former civil servant with experience in management of PSEs or in areas of finance, industry or economic affairs. 
  
31.3     A Committee comprising of Cabinet Secretary, Secretary (Personnel), Secretary (Finance) and Secretary (Public Enterprises) recommends a panel of names in order of
preference, for appointment to the posts of Chairperson and Members of PESB. The recommendations of the Committee are submitted to the Appointments Committee of the
Cabinet (ACC). 
  
31.4            The Committee on Home Affairs, while examining the Annual Re port (2002-03) of the Ministry, had expressed its unhappy over th e high vacancy position in PSUs,
for want of recommendations of PESB.  It had urged upon the PESB and the DoPT, to take prompt action,  so that delays do not ta ke place in filling up the board level 
vacancies in PSUs. 
  
31.5           The Ministry, in its reply had stated that out of the 16 vacan t posts (4 posts of Chief Execu tives and 12 posts of Functional Directors), selection had been held up in
one case and the remaining 14 vacancies were either unforeseen or were newly created posts.  The Ministry had stated that the v acancies are not filled up on the 
recommendations of the PESB in a time bound manner mainly becaus e incomplete proposals are received from the administrative Min istries/ Departments, resulting in bac k
references and delayed vigilance clearance from CVC.  The Ministry  had further stated that to minimise  the back references, ac tion has been taken to revise the check-list, 
issued in 1995, and put it on the official website of the Department, which will be accessible to all Ministries/Departments and facilitate formulation of more sound proposals. 
  
31.6           A perusal of the Annual Report (2003-04) of the Ministry reflects 28 vacant posts (7 posts of Chief Executives and 21 posts of  Functional Directors), for which the
recommendations of the PESB are yet to be sent which is far higher than that of the previous year. 
  
31.7           The PESB, in consultation with major PSEs and the DoPT, had taken the initiative to improve the selection process, by initiati ng  process of action one year in 
advance of the date of vacancy, instead of six months as in the past in order to facilitate early selection and timely appointm ents to Board level posts.  This had facilitated the 
process of Board level appointments.
