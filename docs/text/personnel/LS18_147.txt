INTRODUCTION 
 
  I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice, having been authorized by the Committee to

Law and Justice).

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
 
Functions of the Department 
 
1.4 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, functions of the 
department are as under: 
 
i. Appointment, resignation and removal of the Chief Justice of India and Judges 
of the Supreme Court of India; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of 
absence (including leave allowance), pensions and travelling allowances; 
ii. Appointment, resignation and removal etc. of Chief Justice and Judges of 
High Courts in States; their salaries, rights in respect of leave of absence 
(including leave allowances), pensions and travelling allowances; 
iii. Appointment of Judicial Commissioners and Judicial Officers in Union 
Territories; 
iv. Constitution and organization (excluding jurisdiction and powers) of the 
Supreme Court (but including contempt of such Court) and the fees taken 
therein; 
v. Constitution and organization of the High Courts and the Courts of Judicial 
Commissioners except provisions as to officers and servants of these courts; 
vi. Administration of justice and constitution and organization of courts in the 
Union Territories and fees taken in such courts; 
vii. Courts fees and Stamp duties in the Union Territories; 
viii. Creation of All India Judicial Service; 
ix. Conditions of service of District Judges and other Members of Higher Judicial 
Service of Union Territories; 
x. Extension of the Jurisdiction of a High Court to a Union Territory or exclusion 
of a Union Territory from the Jurisdiction of a High Court; 
xi. Legal Aid to the poor; 
xii. Administration of Justice; and 
xiii. Access to Justice, Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms. 
 
1.5 The Department of Justice is the administrative Department for the following 
institutions/autonomous bodies/policy initiatives: 
a) National Legal Services Authority (NALSA):- The National Legal Services 
Authority has been constituted under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. The 
Chief Justice of India (CJI) is the patron-in-chief and the second senior-most judge of 
the Supreme Court is the Executive Chairman of the Authority. NALSA monitors and 
evaluates implementation of legal aid programmes, and lays down policies, principles, 
guidelines and frames effective and economical schemes for the State Legal Services 
Authorities (SLSA) to implement the legal services programmes throughout the 
country. The State Legal Services Authorities, District Legal Services Authorities 
(DLSA), Taluk Legal Services Committees (TLSC), etc. provide free legal services to 
underprivileged litigants and organize Lok Adalats for speedy resolution of disputes. 

 11 
The prime objective of NALSA is speedy disposal of cases and reducing the burden 
of the judiciary. 
b) Family Courts:- The Family Courts Act, 1984 was enacted with a view to providing 
for establishment of Family Courts by the State Governments in consultation with the 
High Courts to promote conciliation and secure speedy settlement of disputes relating 
to marriage, family affairs and related matters. In accordance with the provisions of 
Section 3 of the Act, the State Government has to mandatorily establish Family Court 
for every area in the State comprising a city or a town population of which exceeds 
ten lakh. In other areas of the States, the Family Courts may be set up if the State 
Governments deem it necessary. 
c) Special Courts for trial of criminal cases involving elected MPs/MLAs:- In 
compliance to Hon’ble Supreme Court Judgment in Ashwini Kumar Vs UoI (WP (C) 
699 of 2016)10 Special courts (02 Special Courts in Delhi, and 01 each in the State of 
Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, 
Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal) are functional for expeditious trial and disposal of 
criminal cases involving elected MPs/MLAs. 
d) Fast Track Special Courts:- In pursuance of the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 
2018, and direction of Hon’ble Supreme Court in Suo Motu Writ (Criminal) No. 1/2019 
dated 25th July, 2019, Union of India started a Centrally Sponsored Scheme in October 
2019 for setting up of 1023 Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs) including 389 
exclusive POCSO (e-POCSO) courts across the country for expeditious trial and disposal 
of pending c ases related to rape and Protection of Children from Sexual Offences 
(POCSO) Act, 2012.  
e) National Judicial Academy (NJA):- The National Judicial Academy (NJA), Bhopal 
is an autonomous body established in 1993 under the Societies Registration Act, 
1860. This independent body functions with its office at the Supreme Court of India 
and its campus at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh. This is an Apex body which imparts 
judicial training to Judges/Judicial Officers of the country and provides facilities for 
training of ministerial officers working in the Supreme Court, study of court 
management and administration of justice in the State/Union Territories, organizing 
conferences, seminars, lectures and research in matters relating to court management 
and administration. The core objectives of the said Academy have been to foster 
development of national judiciary in the country and strengthen administration of 
justice, judicial education, research and policy formulation. 
f) National Mission for Justice Delivery and Legal Reforms: The Mission was set up 
with twin objectives of increasing judicial access by reducing delays and arrears in the 
system and enhancing accountability at all levels through structural changes and 
setting performance standards and facilitating enhancement of capacities for 
achieving such performance standards. The schemes/initiatives under the Mission are 
Action Research and Studies on Judicial Reforms, Access to Justice for all, E-Courts 
Mission mode, Infrastructure development of Subordinate Judiciary, judicial 
performance evaluation, etc. 
 

 12 
g) Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) for Development of Infrastructure Facilities 
for the Judiciary: The government has been implementing a Centrally Sponsored 
Scheme (CSS) for development of infrastructure facilities for judiciary since 1993-94. 
The scheme covers construction of court buildings and residential accommodations 
for Judicial Officers/Judges of District and Subordinate Courts. The fund sharing 
pattern of the Scheme has been revised to 60:40 (Centre: State) and 90:10 (Centre: 
State) for the North-Eastern and two Himalayan States, and 100% for Union 
Territories. The Union Cabinet, in its meeting on 14th July 2021, approved the 
continuation of the scheme from 2021-22 to 2025-26, with a financial outlay of Rs. 
9,000 Crore, including a central share of Rs. 5,357 Crore. In the current financial year 
(2024-25), Rs. 909.29 crore has been released to the States, bringing the total funds 
disbursed under the scheme to Rs. 11,838.30 crore. 
h) Computerization of District and Subordinate Courts: E-Courts Mission Mode 
Project (MMP) Phases – I, II & III: - This Project is being implemented by the 
Department under the guidance of e-Committee, Supreme Court of India primarily for 
universal computerization of all District and Subordinate Court complexes. Initiated 
in 2010, the Phase-I of the Project concluded at a total cost of Rs 639.14 crore. The 
Phase II of the Project with an outlay of Rs 1670 crore, of which Rs. 1,668.43 Crore 
has been utilized.  Phase III of the e-Courts Project, with a total financial outlay of Rs. 
7,210 Crore spread over four years, was approved by the Cabined on 13.09.2023. The 
Project aims to enhance the use of technology in the judiciary to improve efficiency, 
transparency and accessibility of justice. Building upon the success of Phase II (2015-
23), which focused on the ICT enablement of district and subordinate courts, Phase III 
is designed to strengthen digital case management, enhance virtual court 
functionalities and promote AI-driven legal research tools. Under Phase II, 3452 court 
complexes and 18,735 courtrooms were computerized, WAN connectivity was 
established across courts and digital case management tools such as the National 
Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) were implemented. Under the eCourts Project Phase-III, 
Rs. 1200 crore has been allocated under RE 2024-25 and the actual expenditure is 
mere Rs. 317.43 Crore as on date. A sum of Rs. 2151.57 crore is projected for BE 
2025-26, as per the requirement submitted by eCommittee, Supreme Court of India. 
i) Establishment of Gram Nyayalayas under the Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 :- The 
Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 has been enacted by the Parliament to provide for the 
establishment of the Gram Nyayalayas at the grass root level for the purpose of 
providing speedy and inexpensive access to justice to the citizens at their door steps. 
The Central funding for Gram Nyayalayas includes one-time (non-recurring) 
assistance of Rs. 18 lakh per Gram Nyayalaya. Additionally Government meets fifty 
percent of the recurring assistance incurred in the first three years, subject to a ceiling 
of Rs. 3.20 lakh per Gram Nyayalaya per annum. 
j) Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice In India (DISHA):- 
Department of Justice has created a new scheme Designing Innovative Solutions and 
Holistic Access to Justice - DISHA for a period of five year 2021 -2026 .To prioritize 
citizen-centric justice delivery system by integrating access to justice with 
technology, DISHA has the objective - : 

 13 
 To strengthen pre -litigation mechanism through Tele -Law; 
 To develop an effective dispensation of pro bono legal services through 
Nyaya Bandhu programme; and 
 To empower citizens through pan India Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness 
programme . 
SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 
1.6 The Supreme Court of India is at the apex of a pyramidal, unified and integrated 
judicial system. Though the political system of the country is federal in nature, the judicial 
system is unitary. The Supreme Court came into existence on 28th January, 1950 and it 
comprises the Chief Justice of India and 33 Judges. The strength of the apex court was 
enhanced over the years from 8 in 1950 to 11 in 1956, 14 in 1960, 18 in 1977, 26 in 1986, 31 
in 2009 and 34 in 2019, with the increase of case load on it. The Constitution of India 
provides for the Supreme Court to be a court of record, guardian of liberties & rights and also 
as the final arbiter in most disputes. It is an appellate court, can review its own judgement and 
in its advisory capacity may answer references by the President on any question of law of 
public importance. The Supreme Court is designated as the custodian of fundamental rights 
and the 'rule of law'. It has power of judicial review and the law declared by the Supreme 
Court is binding on all courts. 
1.7 The administrative wing of the Supreme Court is called 'Registry'. The Registry is 
divided into two wings - (i) Administrative Wing; and (ii) Judicial Wing. These wings are 
further divided into various Divisions, Branches, Sections and Cells. The Chief Justice of 
India is the Administrative head of the Supreme Court. The highest Administrative Officer of 
the Supreme Court is the Secretary General, the post manned by a judicial officer of the rank 
of a District & Sessions Judge and is of the rank of Secretary to the Government of India. The 
Secretary General is assisted by Registrars, Officers on Special Duty, Additional Registrars, 
Deputy Registrars, officers of other lower ranks and subordinate staff. 
1.8 The Department of Justice is the nodal Department for the Supreme Court of India. 
There is a separate demand, Demand No. 67 pertaining to the Supreme Court of India, which 
is entirely charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be submitted to the vote 
of Parliament. However, Article 113 of the Constitution provides for those estimates to be 
discussed in either House of Parliament.

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
Vacancies in the Department of Justice 
 
3.0 The Department of Justice was separated from the Ministry of Home Affairs and set 
up as an independent Department under the Ministry of Law and Justice and was made 
operational w.e.f. 01.01.2010.  In the beginning it was decided that the manpower to be 
provided to the Department of Justice will be held on the common cadre with the Ministry of 
Home Affairs. In this joint cadre, all posts are first allotted to Home Affairs and thereafter as 
per the assessment of need of the Department of Justice they allot the staff.  Under this 
arrangement, in the Department of Justice as against the sanctioned strength of 102, only 60 
officials are working resulting in vacancies to the tune of 41%.   
 
3.1 The Department of Justice has apprised this Committee that at different times, the 
matter of posting sufficient strength of officials in the Department of Justice has been taken 
up with the Ministry of Home Affairs and other cadre controlling departments like DoPT.  
However, there continues to be an acute shortage of manpower in the Department. After 
becoming an independent Department, the matter of sanctioning additional posts was taken 
up with the Ministry of Finance and additional posts have also been sanctioned by the said 
Ministry. However, these posts continued to remain vacant as adequate staff has not been 
posted by the Ministry of Home Affairs. This manpower shortage is having an impact on 
functioning of the Department, which is inter alia performing important functions like justice 
delivery and access to justice.  The Department of Justice  has also informed the Committee 
that to cope up with the extreme shortage of staff, besides requesting MHA and DoPT to fill 
the vacancies, this Department has engaged retired Government servants as Consultants and 
also hired outsourced staff i.e. DEOs/MTSs.  
 
3.2 The Committee notes that even after 15 years of its separation from the Ministry 
of Home Affairs and becoming an independent Department, the Department of Justice 
is still dependent on the MHA for all its man power requirements. The vacancies in the 
Department are particularly staggering at the middle and operating level of SO(32% 
Vacancy), ASO (50% vacancy), UDC (100% vacancy), PA (100% vacancy). The 
Committee feels that Department of Justice (DoJ) should implement the 
recommendation made in this regard by this Commitee in its 113th Report on 
establishing a separate cadre unit. This should be done in collaboration with the DoPT 
and MHA, with a clear-cut timeline.   Also, the Committee desires that DoJ may submit 
a detailed proposal for the creation of a separate cadre to DoPT and MHA for approval, 
without any further delay.

Government supplements the resources of State Governments by providing financial 
assistance based on a prescribed fund-sharing pattern: 60:40 (Centre: State), 90:10 for the 
North-Eastern and two Himalayan States, and 100% for Union Territories. However, central 
funding is subject to budgetary allocations. In the current financial year (2024-25), Rs. 
909.29 crore has been released to the States, bringing the total funds disbursed under the 
scheme to Rs. 11,837.30 crore. 
3.14 As of 31.12.2024, there are 21,974 court halls/rooms available in District and 
Subordinate Courts, with 3,166 under construction. Additionally, 19,682 residential units 
have been completed, while 2,638 are under construction. The Union Cabinet, in its meeting 
on 14th July 2021, approved the continuation of the scheme from 2021-22 to 2025-26, with a 
financial outlay of Rs. 9,000 Crore, including a central share of Rs. 5,357 Crore, of which Rs. 
50 crore is earmarked for Gram Nyayalayas. Beyond the construction of court halls and 
residential units for judges and judicial officers, the scheme now also covers the construction 
of Lawyers' Halls, Toilet Complexes, and Digital Computer Rooms in Subordinate Courts.

3.19 The Department in their written submission has informed that  as per data available,

have found out following challenges in operationalization and implementing of Gram 
Nyayalayas in implementing States: 
 It is not mandatory on the part of State Governments to set up Gram Nyayalayas. 
 The Gram Nyayalayas in most of the States do only 2 days sitting (Example MP 
and Kerala) in a week, which need to be done on regular basis on every working 
day. 
 There is a need to provide basic infrastructure, ICT facilities and CIS system for 
efficient conduct of Mobile Courts for the Gram Nyayalayas. 
 The creation of Nyayadhikari post and filling up of vacancies of independent

 Mobile courts are not functional in all the States where Gram Nyayalayas are 
functioning. 
 Non-availability of public prosecutors, notaries and other facilities and lack of 
interest shown by advocates for working in the Gram Nyayalayas is another 
challenge. 
 Training of the Gram Nyayadhikari is needed to make him aware of legal 
procedural requirements. 
 There is need to give wide publicity about the date and place of holding of mobile 
courts and adequate security for Mobile Courts is also needed. 
 Overlapping jurisdiction of Gram Nyayalayas with regular courts.  
 Shortage of First Class Judicial Magistrates to man the Gram Nyayalayas;  
 Reluctance of police and other state functionaries to appear before them.   
 
3.20 The Committee notes that out of 488 entities notified across various states, only 
319 are functional. It is observed that states like Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and UTs 
like Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh have notified entities but none are functional. The 
Committee feels that these states/UTs should prioritize operationalization of the notified 
entities through development of their infrastructural facilities and deployment of 

 32 
required workforce. The Committee also opines that that states with partially functional 
entities (e.g., Odisha, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Punjab, Haryana) should address their 
challenges such as funding allocation, administrative bottlenecks, and workforce 
shortages.   
3.21 The Committee feels that Gram Nyayalayas should operate every working day so 
that the backlog of cases can be handled more effectively instead of just two days a 
week. The current limited sitting schedule  of just two days a week significantly reduces 
the capacity of these courts to deal with the volume of cases. The Committee opines that 
prioritizing the recruitment of Nyayadhikaris (Judicial Officers) and ensuring the 
timely filling of vacancies may help in providing uninterrupted functioning of Gram 
Nyayalayas. 
3.22 The Committee suggests that an extensive public awareness campaign may be 
launched about Gram Nyayalayas which will  include information on the schedule of 
mobile courts, the process for filing cases, and the availability of legal support. The 
Committee further recommends the jurisdiction of Gram Nyayalayas may be clearly 
defined to avoid overlap with regular courts.

SCHEMES/PROGRAMMES OF NALSA 
 
3.34 Article 39A of the Indian Constitution provides free legal aid to ensure that 
opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other 
disabilities.  Articles 14 and 22 (1) mandate the state to ensure equality before law.  The 
Parliament enacted the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 on 9th November 1995 to 
establish a nationwide uniform network for providing free and competent legal services to the 
weaker sections of society based on equal opportunity. The principal objective of NALSA is 
to provide free and competent legal services to the weaker sections of society to ensure that 
opportunities for securing justice are not denied to any citizen by reason of economic or other 
disabilities, and to organize Lok Adalats for amicable settlement of disputes.

MANPOWER REQUIREMENT AT NALSA 
3.51   National Legal Services Authority was established in the year 1995 to facilitate Access 
to Justice to the poor and marginalized sections of the country with the sanctioned staff 
strength of 27 posts. Since its establishment, in about 30 years, only 6 additional posts have 
been created. However, the functions, activities and even the mandate of NALSA has 
increased manifold during the period leaving a huge gap between the required and sanctioned 
staff strength which ultimately impacts NALSA’S working.   
3.52 In their written submission to the Committee NALSA  has informed that they are 
facing difficulties in executing and monitoring its schemes and programs due to shortage of 
staff, especially at the lower levels. This staffing shortage has also affected their ability to 
maintain accurate data, proper accounts, and other essential records. To address these 
challenges, NALSA initially submitted a proposal to the Department in February 2022 for 
creation of 40 additional posts. Following the advice of the Department, NALSA revised the 
proposal and in April 2024, submitted an updated request for creation of 46 posts. In January 
2025, NALSA sent a proposal for revival of 12 posts, which were abolished earlier. 
 3.53 The Committee recommends that the Department should prioritize the approval 
of NALSA's proposals for creation of 46 additional posts and revival of 12 posts. The 
Committee feels that this will help bridge the significant gap between the current and 
required staff strength, enabling NALSA to efficiently execute and monitor its 
programs and schemes. 
National Judicial Academy, Bhopal 
3.54 The National Judicial Academy (NJA) is entrusted with the judicial education and 
capacity building of Judicial Officers across the country and Hon’ble Judges of the High 
Courts. In line with the Resolution adopted during the Chief Justices' Conference, 2016 
(Agenda Item No. 9), the National Judicial Academic Council (NJAC) was established under 
the aegis of the Hon’ble Chief Justice of India (CJI). The NJAC comprises: 
 Two Senior Puisne Judges of the Supreme Court of India 
 Chairpersons of State Judicial Academies 
 Judges-in-Charge (Judicial Education) of High Courts 
 Secretary, Department of Justice 
 Director, National Judicial Academy 
 
The NJAC is chaired by the Hon’ble Chief Justice of India and is responsible for: 
 Advancing the cause of judicial education  
 Formulating the academic calendar of NJA 
 Approving and prescribing academic calendars for State Judicial Academies(SJAs) 
 Enhancing resources for judicial training and education 
 Coordinating and monitoring NJA & SJA activities

ASSESSMENT OF BUDGET OF DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
 
1. I t is observed that the allocation to the Department has increased since 2023-24.

appreciates the Department for its proper utilization of funds under different heads 
during the financial year 2024-25 and hopes that the department will continue to utilize 
the funds on the same pattern in future. 
(Para 2.5) 
2. The Committee observes that there is a significant increase in BE for 2025-26 
when compared to the RE 2024-25.   A detailed review of expenditure trends may be 
conducted to ensure that budget estimates may accurately measure the actual usage of 
funds with respect to expenditures.       (Para 2.6) 
Assessment of Budget Allocated to National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) 
3. The department has provided Rs. 400 Crore as grant-in-aid to NALSA, 
considering its activities against its projection of Rs. 730.73 Crore for F.Y. 2025-26 
which may still be insufficient. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its 
recommendations made in its 116th and 128th Reports to increase the Grant-in-aid for 
NALSA, to enable them to conduct all activities on time. The Committee feels that 
without adequate budgetary provisions, both ongoing and newly implemented schemes 
and activities of NALSA will not take off. The Department of Justice and NALSA 
should once again reach out to the Ministry of Finance and press the matter with 
greater emphasis. 
(Para 2.10) 
Assessment of Budget Allocated to National Judicial Academy (NJA) 
 
4. The financial data on grant-in-aid for the National Judicial Academy (NJA) 
indicates fluctuations in budget estimates (BE), revised estimates (RE), and actual 
utilization over the years. While the BE for 2023-24 was ₹20 crore, it was later revised 
to ₹24.5 crore, with full utilization. However, in 2024-25, the BE remained at ₹20 crore, 
and the RE increased slightly to ₹20.78 crore. The actual expenditure as of January 31, 

 48 
2025, stands at ₹20 crore, suggesting that the allocated funds are being utilized 
efficiently. For 2025-26, the projected requirement was ₹26 crore, but the Ministry of 
Finance has allocated only ₹21.66 crore, leading to a shortfall of ₹4.34 crore. This 
reduction in allocation may impact key training and capacity-building programs 
undertaken by the NJA. Additionally, the variation of ₹1.66 crore between BE 2024-25 
and BE 2025-26, and ₹0.88 crore between BE 2024-25 and RE 2025-26, suggests a 
pattern of conservative budgeting, potentially limiting the Academy’s ability to expand 
its initiatives. To ensure smooth functioning of  NJA, it is recommended that the budget 
allocation aligns more closely with actual requirements. The shortfall of ₹4.34 crore 
between projections and allocation for 2025-26 should be reviewed, and funds may be 
provided as and when required. 
(Para 2.12)

need for improved expenditure planning and timely fund disbursement.

POLICIES, PROGRAMMES AND SCHEMES 
Vacancies in the Department of Justice 
 
6. The Committee notes that even after 15 years of its separation from the Ministry 
of Home Affairs and becoming an independent Department, the Department of Justice 
is still dependent on the MHA for all its man power requirements. The vacancies in the 
Department are particularly staggering at the middle and operating level of SO(32% 
Vacancy), ASO (50% vacancy), UDC (100% vacancy), PA (100% vacancy). The 
Committee feels that Department of Justice (DoJ) should implement the 
recommendation made in this regard by this Commitee in its 113th Report on 
establishing a separate cadre unit. This should be done in collaboration with the DoPT 
and MHA, with a clear-cut timeline.   Also, the Committee desires that DoJ may submit 
a detailed proposal for the creation of a separate cadre to DoPT and MHA for approval, 
without any further delay. 
                                                                                                                       (Para 3.2) 

 49 
Vacancy of Judges and Pendency of Cases in Higher Judiciary 
 
 
7. The Committee observes that the process of judicial appointment and pendency  
of cases are critical issues that require urgent attention. There must be synergy between 
the Judiciary and the Government to ensure timely processing of recommendations and 
appointments. The Committee feels that a coordination mechanism should be developed 
between the Judiciary and the Ministry of Law and Justice to track the status of 
appointments delays. 
 (Para 3.8) 
8. The Committee feels that an institutional mechanism may be developed wherein 
the process of the retirement of a judge and the appointment of his/her successor is 
aligned which would  ensure that the vacancy is filled immediately after the retirement.. 
The High Courts with the most significant backlog (Allahabad, Bombay, and 
Rajasthan) should be prioritized for judicial appointments.  The Committee feels that 
judicial workforce may be augmented by hiring more administrative staff, junior 
judicial officers, and court assistants to ensure smooth and efficient operations.  
(Para 3.9) 
9. During its recent study visit to the Kerala High Court, the Committee observed 
the successful deployment of Mobile Legal Aid Units (Mobile E-sewa Kendras) which 
get stationed periodically in the districts in remote areas. These units have been actively 
providing legal services to people in far-flung locations, particularly in large tribal 
population, where they have played a crucial role in delivering justice. Based on this 
effective model, the Committee recommends the expansion of this services to other 
remote areas to conduct awareness sessions and assist people in accessing legal services, 
in the Country. 
(Para 3.10)

Gram Nyayalayas 
10. The Committee notes that out of 488 entities notified across various states, only 
319 are functional. It is observed that states like Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and UTs 
like Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh have notified entities but none are functional. The 
Committee feels that these states/UTs should prioritize operationalization of the notified 
entities through development of their infrastructural facilities and deployment of 
required workforce. The Committee also opines that that states with partially functional 
entities (e.g., Odisha, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Punjab, Haryana) should address their 
challenges such as funding allocation, administrative bottlenecks, and workforce 
shortages.
