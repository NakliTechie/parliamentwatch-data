CHAPTER–I
OVERVIEW OF THE MINISTRY
The management of personnel policies in the Government of India, to begin with, was the
joint responsibility of the Ministry of Home Affairs (Services and Establishment Wing) and Ministry
of Finance (Establishment Division), with Union Public Service Commission playing an important
advisory role. The Establishment Officer to the Government of India, functioning under the Cabinet
Secretary, had an important role to play in the senior level appointments under the Government of
India. All this made for improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified central direction.
1.1 To plug this organisational loophole, the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha, in
its 93
rd Report (April 1966) had observed that “the ever expanding role of the Government in a
welfare State with its natural concomitant of a large civil service calls for effective personnel
control through a single agency”. The Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) too urged upon
the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel with a full fledged Secretary-in-
charge, to be placed directly under the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal agency for the personnel
functions of the Government.
1.2 The Union Government accepted this recommendation, and as a consequence, led to the
reorganization of the Ministry of Home Affairs as it then stood, resulting eventually in the formation
of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, as it stands today.  This process was
initiated on June 26, 1970 when the Department of Personnel was notified as a department in the
Cabinet Secretariat.  The process was taken further on August 1, 1970 with the notification of the
items of work to be dealt with by the Department.  Eventually, on March 15, 1985, a full fledged
‘Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions’ was notified.
1.3 The Ministry is the coordinating agency of the Central Government in personnel matters,
especially the issues concerning recruitment, promotion and morale of services, training, vigilance
and discipline, career development and service conditions of the middle and senior level
management, grievances and staff welfare, as well as the process of responsive people–oriented
modern administration.  The Ministry comprises three Departments, namely: -
(i) Department of Personnel and Training;
(ii) Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances; and
(iii) Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare.
1.4 The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister, assisted by a Minister of
State. The Department of Personnel and Training is functioning under the charge of an independent
Secretary while the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances and Department
of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare functions under the charge of a Secretary.

2
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING
2. The role of the Department of Personnel and Training can be conceptually divided into two
parts.  In its larger nodal role, it acts as the formulator of policy and the watchdog of Government,
ensuring that certain accepted standards and norms as laid down by it, are followed by all
Ministries/Departments in the recruitment, regulation of service conditions and deputation of
personnel, as well as other related issues. Towards this end, guidelines are issued by it for the
benefit of all the Ministries/Departments and it monitors implementation of these guidelines.  It also
advises all organisations of the Central Government on issues of personnel management.  At a more
immediate level, the Department has the direct responsibility of being the cadre controlling authority
for the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) and the Central Secretariat Service (CSS). The
Department also operates the Central Staffing Scheme under which suitable officers from All India
Services and Group ‘A’ Central Services are selected and then placed in posts at the level of
Deputy Secretary, Director and Joint Secretary, on the basis of tenure deputation. The Department
also deals with cases of appointment to posts of Chairmen, Managing Directors, full-time functional
Directors/Members of the Board of Management of various public sector undertakings/enterprises,
corporations, banks and financial institutions.  It also deals with the assignment of Indian experts
to various developing countries.
Recruitment Agencies
3. The two organisations through which the Department ensures recruitment of personnel for
Government are the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and the Staff Selection Commission
(SSC). The former is constituted under a provision of the Constitution and is responsible for
conducting examinations for appointment to the higher civil services and civil posts under the Union
Government, including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a mandatory provision for
consulting the Commission on all matters relating to methods of  recruitment, principles to be
followed in making promotions and transfers from one service to another and all the disciplinary
matters. The SSC is responsible for making recruitment for Group ‘B’ non-gazetted posts of
Assistants, Stenographers, etc.
Training Institutions
4. Two major training institutions, directly under the administrative control of the Department,
are the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the
Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM), New Delhi. The former is mainly
responsible for providing induction training to recruits to the Indian Administrative Service and
other All India Services and Central Services. The Institute of Secretariat Training and Management
provides induction as well as in-service training to members of the Central Secretariat Service.
Senior Appointments under the Government of India
5. All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the
approval of Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the Government of India
(Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961 are processed through the Establishment Officer as
Secretary to the ACC.  These include board level appointments to Central Public Sector
Undertakings and appointments of Joint Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the

3
Ministries/Departments. In addition, all appointments by promotion, which require approval of the
ACC, are also processed through the Establishment Officer.
Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB)
6. The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making
recommendations for appointment to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executive and
functional Director and part-time Chairman in the Central Public Undertakings.
Administrative Vigilance
7. The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for maintenance
of integrity of the public services and eradication of corruption, and coordinates the activities of
various Ministries/Departments in that area.
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC): Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Central
Vigilance Commission. It has jurisdiction and powers in respect of all matters to which the
executive power of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted with the
supervision of the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI).
The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013: The Act has been brought into force with effect from
16th January, 2014. The Department is entrusted with the administration of the Act.
Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
8. The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is the premier investigating police agency in the
country, created through a resolution of Ministry of Home Affairs dated April 1, 1963 administering
Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946, its jurisdiction covering all the Departments
of the Government of India, Union Territories, which could be extended to the States also with
the consent of the State Governments concerned. In due course, with the setting up of a large
number of Public Sector Undertakings, the employees of those undertakings were also brought
under the purview of CBI. Similarly, with the nationalization of the Banks in 1969, the Public
Sector Banks and their employees also came within the ambit of the CBI.  By now, the CBI has
become multi-disciplinary investigative agency, with its role expanding from corruption cases to
other cases viz- cyber crimes, terrorist crimes, wildlife crimes, narcotics, arms trafficking, arts and
antiquities cases, counterfeiting of currencies etc.
Joint Consultative Machinery
9. A well structured three-tier machinery is in place for joint consultation between the Central
Government and its employees for promoting harmonious relations and securing the greatest
measure of cooperation between the Central Government and the general body of employees in
matters of common concern. The objective is to increase the efficiency of public services along
with the well-being of the employees. The three tiers of JCM are:
(i) National Council- at the apex level, functioning under the Department of Personnel
and Training;
(ii) Department Councils- functioning at Ministries’/Departments’ level; and
(iii) Office Councils- functioning at offices/ organisations under various Ministries/
Departments.

4
Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
10. In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to employees aggrieved by the
Government decisions, Government had set up the Central Administrative Tribunal (CA T) in 1985,
which deals with all the cases relating to service matters of Central Government employees.
Right to Information
11. The Department is entrusted with the administration of the Right to Information Act, 2005
and Rules including amendment thereof. The Department also overlooks the administration of the
Central Information Commission including selection of Chief Information Commissioner and
Information Commissioners.
Staff Welfare
12. The Department of Personnel and Training is intimately involved in various staff welfare and
other role led activities.  Mention in this connection can be made of the institution of Grih Kalyan
Kendra (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860), the Kendriya Bhandar (a
registered cooperative society set up in 1963) and the Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports
Board (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860).
12.1. The Department also supports various programmes, which are provided in various
Ministries/Departments, Central Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in Central
Government offices located outside Delhi, Residents’ Welfare Associations/Area Welfare Officers,
Benevolent Fund and Departmental Canteens.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEV ANCES
13. The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of
the Government of India for administrative reforms, as well as for redressal of public grievances
relating to the State in general and grievances pertaining to Central Government agencies in
particular. The Department disseminates information on important activities of Government relating
to administrative reforms and public grievances redressal through Management Publications and
Documentation. This Department also acts as the nodal agency in regard to Organisation and
Methods activities and offers advice to the Ministries/Departments. It also undertakes activities in
the field of international co-operation and exchanges to promote public service reforms.
13.1. This Department has, in the past few years, taken several initiatives with a view to bring
about improvement in the functioning and effectiveness of Government Departments, important
ones being the following:
(i) drawing up an agenda for reforms to be carried out in the Central Government as
well as in States; and
(ii) undertaking, through a Commission, a review of administrative laws which have
become obsolete and no longer need to be in the statute book.
13.2.  The Department also initiated an exercise on the part of Departments having public interface
to draw up their individual Citizen’s Charter to indicate the commitments of that organization for

5
quality and the time-frame in which services performed by that organization would be completed
for members of the public.
DEPARTMENT OF PENSIONS AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE
14. The Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare is the nodal agency of the
Government of India for formulation of general policy on pension and other retirement benefits, as
also for redressal of grievances relating to retirement benefits.  The Department was set up in 1985
as  part of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions and has been rendering service
for the benefit of pensioners since then.  The Department has taken a number of steps over the
years for streamlining the pension administration system, for greater convenience and welfare of
the pensioners.
14.1 This Department, does not deal directly with pensioners but is the focal point for dealing
with issues concerning pensioners, and for issue of instructions and guidelines regarding matters
pertaining to pensioners.
14.2 The main responsibilities entrusted to this Department are:
(i) Formulation of policies and coordination of matters relating to retirement benefits of
Central Government employees, including Defence and Railways;
(ii) Administration of :
(a) Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972;
(b) Central Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981;
(c) Central Civil Services (Extraordinary Pensions) Rules 1939;
(d) General Provident Fund (Civil Services) Rules, 1960; and
(e) Contributory Provident Fund (India) Rules, 1962;
(iii) Pension policy structure;
(iv) Examination and extending the scope of new facilities for fringe benefits to the
Central Government Pensioners;
(v) Matters relating to amendments to or relaxation of Pension Rules or any other rules
concerning retirement benefits; and
(vi) Policy and coordination relating to welfare of Central Government Pensioners/Family
Pensioners.

DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING
(i) Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
31. The foremost investigative police agency in the country and involved in major criminal
probes and is the interpolagency, the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has Plan schemes such
as ‘ Modernisation of Training Centre ’ of CBI intended for improvement of training facilities in CBI
Academy and its Regional Training Centres and modernization in Information Technology, strategic
options, re-engineering of process, up-gradation of training system, logistic and support facilities,
scientific and forensic support services and human resource management; ‘ e-governance ’ to equip
the field formations with modern equipments in order to fasten the enquiries; construction of a
state-of-the-art building is on at CGO complex, New Delhi to house the CBI Head Quarters; and
purchase of land from the MMRDA, Mumbai for accommodating the CBI Mumbai branch.
30.1 The Plan Outlay for the CBI in BE(2016-17) has been ` 75.00 crore as against ` 85.07
crore in BE (2015-16) which was reduced to ` 75.25 crore at the RE stage.
30.2 During the examination of the Demands for Grants (2016-17), the Director of CBI had
informed the Committee that during the last one year, the CBI has been taking steps to improve
its impact in the fight against corruption and investigation of crimes. The focus in the recent past
has been on modernizing and re-shaping its organization, capacity enhancement and upgradation of
skills of its manpower to meet the challenges of the 21 st century. With the recommendation of this
Committee in it’s 76 th Report to not compromise on funds projected for the plan scheme for
modernization of the CBI, the allocation for the Plan schemes of the CBI has remained the same.
However, the funds earmarked are inadequate and therefore, requested for additional funds.
30.3 The Committee strongly recommends for making adequate funds available to the
CBI to enable it to implement its Plan schemes to the ideal stipulation. The Committee is
however unable to understand why the allocation  of ````` 85.07 BE (2015-16) was reduced   to
````` 75.25 crore at the RE stage and then to ````` 75.00 crore as BE this year. The Committee
however surmises that fund reductions could be for the reason that the spending units, CBI
in this case, were unable to utilise 50% of the funds within the first six months of the
financial year, hence funds got reduced at next year’s BE. The Committee apprehends that
such a reduction may affect the pace of implementation of schemes. The Ministry in their
action taken replies should clarify the same, explain the reasons for such reductions and
17

18
also suggest how it can be avoided. Meanwhile, the Committee recommends to the CBI to
beef up the pace of the implementation of its proposed schemes.
30.4 The Committee noted from the  written replies to the questionnaire on Demands for Grants
(2016-17) submitted to the Committee the alarming number of vacancies across the various areas
of posts in the CBI, viz., 21% Executive Officers, 28% Legal Officers, 58% Technical Officers,
17% Ministerial Staff and 37 Canteen Staffs which are after the personnel engaged on contract
basis included in the Actual Strength in position. In response to the query on the pendency of
cases, the Committee was informed that as on 29.02.2016, CBI has a total number of 1200 cases
pending under investigation. Out of these, 31 cases are pending for more than three years. During
the interaction of the Committee with the Director, CBI, a Member also pointed out the 62 Foreign
Investigations pending with the CBI.
30.5 While deposing before the Committee, the Director, CBI proposed to provide longer tenure
to the officers on deputation in the CBI from the State and other Central forces in addition to the
other steps taken to manage the vacancy position. He further apprised the Committee that the
capacity of the CBI for investigation was around 700 cases a year which has now almost doubled
and as such more officers to supervise the investigation is needed. He also assigned the influx of
numerous cases to CBI partially due to the many cases referred by the States as they are over-
burdened with large number of cases instituted with them and also by orders of the Supreme Court
and High Courts. He therefore, expressed that unless the State Governments provide the manpower
by way of sending more of their personnel on deputation to the CBI, a time could come that the
CBI would collapse and fail. He further stressed the need for capacity building of their counterpart
State Police forces which he felt would go a long way in checkingthe inflow of cases to CBI.
30.6 On the query relating to officers of gazetted rank of the Government of India and public
sector undertakings suspected to be indulging in corruption vis-a-vis the time taken to complete
investigation, the Director informed that 2411 such officers have been identified which is an
increase of 87% over the last year. As regards the time taken for investigations, the duration range
from six months in small cases which can go upto one year in bigger cases. There are also some
very complex cases which may necessitate the investigation to go beyond a year. Since the
prosecution in the Courts depends on the evidence presented, there is no scope to either delete a
process to unduly hasten the process which could adversely effect the investigation.
30.7 The Committee expresses deep concern on the state of affairs relating to the vacancy
position in the CBI. It therefore impresses upon the DoPT to complete the exercise of cadre
review by June, 30, 2016 as per the direction of the Supreme Court to deal with the vacancy
position of the establishment. The Committee is apprehensive that unless the present
malady of massive vacancy is not corrected immediately, the very purpose of establishment
of the CBI would be eroded and its credibilitytarnished.  At this juncture when the nation
envisage the premier investigating agency of the country to be at par with their
international counterparts,  performance and result oriented and equipped to combat the
challenges of international pressures of transnational terrorism, cyber-crimes and
corruption, the Committee is disheartened that the CBI is crippled with a very basic
requirement, shortage of manpower.

19
30.7.1 With cases being referred by States and Courts to the CBI for investigation, the
Committee acknowledges the burden of load bestowed upon it. However, it is of the
concerted views that, the CBI turn to the State or other Central Forces to plug the vacancy
in the establishment. It therefore, desires that the CBI manage its cadre more efficiently
and the DoPT should take holistic steps to hasten the part of recruitment in consultation
with the UPSC. For that matter, the Committee strongly recommends the DoPT to revisit
the 24
th Report of the Committee on ‘The Working of the Central Bureau of Investigation
(CBI)’ wherein, after an exhaustive examination and intensive study of the issues, had
recommended workable ways to strengthen the CBI which included an enactment for the
CBI.
30.7.2 The Committee endorses the need for capacity enhancement and skill development
of the CBI as well as its counterpart State Police Forces. It is of the firm belief that,
augmenting the prowess and competence of the State Police Forces parallel to the CBI will
go a long way in abating cases referred to it by the States and thereby relieving the work-
load of the agency. The Committee commends the role played by the International Centre
for Excellence and Investigation, Ghaziabad in the field of capacity building and encourages
more exposure in the international arena by way of holding more interactions with their
international counterparts through the Centre.
30.7.3 Keeping in view the high pendency of cases with the CBI and especially those of
Foreign Investigations which have a direct bearing on the country’s credibility, the
Committee recommends that the activities of the Centre should be expanded to provide a
continuous in-service training to the CBI personnel in order to hone the skill and expertise
of those engaged in the field of crime investigation. The Committee further recommends
that, if need be, more funds for the purpose may be made available to the CBI.
(ii) Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA)
31. The Plan Budgetary allocation for Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration
(LBSNAA) of ` 46.73 crore at BE (2015-16) was reduced to ` 45.43 crore at RE stage and out
of which the Actual expenditure was ` 38.65 crore which accrues a mere 85% utilization of the
available funds. Accordingly, the projection in BE 2016-17 has been pegged at ` 53.98 crore. The
Committee was informed by the Financial Adviser of the Ministry that the shortfall in utilization
was due to certain environmental clearances to the Academy.
31.1 While deposing before the Committee in connection with the Demands for Grants (2016-17)
the Director, LBSNAA submitted that the basic problem of the Academy is on the side of the
trainers because it needs the support of both the Centre and the States Governments who have to
relieve their officers. Currently, 50% of the posts of Deputy Directors, who are key to running
the Academy is vacant.
31.2 The Committee expects that the premier training institution of the country ought to
be adequately equipped not only in physical terms but also in terms of its manpower.  Due
to the fact that the whole system of governance is now changing and the international
pressure and expectations are now high, flexibility has to be brought into the system. In
that context, all training programmes confined to one location may not be the single answer

20
to the existing problem of shortage of manpower. Accordingly, other feasible options of
handling the situation may be explored. At the same time, the DoPT should evolve a policy
which can address this constraint of the LBSNAA.
31.3 As regards the reduction of funds of the LBSNAA, while acknowledging the
justification of the Ministry, the Committee is of the view that every spending unit should
make a prudent exercise of the avenues of actually carrying out plan schemes it proposes
to undertake before budgetary allocations are sought. Furthermore, strategies should be
drawn for the utilization of funds made available to spending units and should be closely
monitored.
(iii) Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
32. The Central Vigilance Commission, an apex body exercising general superintendence and
control over vigilance matters in administration of Ministries/Department/Organisations of Central
Government was conceptualized under Government of India Resolution dated 11th February, 1964.
The CVC assumed statutory status by the CVC Act, 2003 on 12th September, 2003.
32.1.  In BE 2015-16 ` 27.68 crore has been projected for Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
independently under Demand No. 65 which was the same as BE 2015-16 and reduced to ` 24.26
crore at RE 2015-16. The Actual 2015-16 was pegged at ` 24.05 crore which account 99.13%.
33.2. The Secretary, CVC deposed before the Committee on the Demands for Grants (2016-17)
and informed that the Commission has taken up two new initiatives since 2015, viz., ‘Focus on
Preventive Vigilance’ wherein outreach to 1,67,495 youth in 1575 colleges and 2293 schools in 101
cities for inculcating ethical behaviour; and ‘Training and Capacity Building’ whereby training policy
was formulated for personnel engaged in corruption work and customized training modules and
programmes to address the changing corruption scenario.
32.3. To the query on the time taken to give vigilance clearance for Board level appointments, the
Secretary informed that clearance is given within two months. She clarified further that it takes
this much time because when the information is received from the cadre the officer belongs to,
it has to be vetted with the data base of the CVC and also referred to CBI.
32.4. The Committee fails to comprehend the status quo  in respect of the budgetary

many of the cases in service matters can be settled by mediation within the system itself.
The Committee is of the opinion that in the administration, the senior officer be made
duty-bound to resolve the grievances of the subordinate officers. He is expected to sit along
with the aggrieved and decide the matter, get the concurrence of the supreme authority and
complete the  entire  process.  The tribunals/ Courts are need to be resorted to  only in
the rarest matters. Thus, the Committee calls for a drastic change in the attitude of the
administration.

however surmises that fund reductions could be for the reason that the spending units, CBI
in this case, were unable to utilise 50% of the funds within the first six months of the
financial year, hence funds got reduced at next year’s BE. The Committee apprehends that
such a reduction may affect the pace of implementation of schemes. The Ministry in their
action taken replies should clarify the same, explain the reasons for such reductions and
also suggest how it can be avoided. Meanwhile, the Committee recommends to the CBI to
beef up the pace of the implementation of its proposed schemes. (Para 30.3)
The Committee expresses deep concern on the state of affairs relating to the vacancy
position in the CBI. It therefore impresses upon the DoPT to complete the exercise of cadre
review by June, 30, 2016 as per the direction of the Supreme Court to deal with the vacancy
position of the establishment. The Committee is apprehensive that unless the present
malady of massive vacancy is not corrected immediately, the very purpose of establishment
32

33
of the CBI would be eroded and its credibility tarnished.  At this juncture when the nation
envisage the premier investigating agency of the country to be at par with their
international counterparts,  performance and result oriented and equipped to combat the
challenges of international pressures of transnational terrorism, cyber-crimes and
corruption, the Committee is disheartened that the CBI is crippled with a very basic
requirement, shortage of manpower. (Para 30.7)
With cases being referred by States and Courts to the CBI for investigation, the
Committee acknowledges the burden of load bestowed upon it. However, it is of the
concerted views that, the CBI turn to the State or other Central Forces to plug the vacancy
in the establishment. It therefore, desires that the CBI manage its cadre more efficiently
and the DoPT should take holistic steps to hasten the part of recruitment in consultation
with the UPSC. For that matter, the Committee strongly recommends the DoPT to revisit
the 24 th Report of the Committee on ‘The Working of the Central Bureau of Investigation
(CBI)’ wherein, after an exhaustive examination and intensive study of the issues, had
recommended workable ways to strengthen the CBI which included an enactment for the
CBI. (Para 30.7.1)
The Committee endorses the need for capacity enhancement and skill development
of the CBI as well as its counterpart State Police Forces. It is of the firm belief that,
augmenting the prowess and competence of the State Police Forces parallel to the CBI will
go a long way in abating cases referred to it by the States and thereby relieving the work-
load of the agency. The Committee commends the role played by the International Centre
for Excellence and Investigation, Ghaziabad in the field of capacity building and encourages
more exposure in the international arena by way of holding more interactions with their
international counterparts through the Centre. (Para 30.7.2)
Keeping in view the high pendency of cases with the CBI and especially those of
Foreign Investigations which have a direct bearing on the country’s credibility, the
Committee recommends that the activities of the Centre should be expanded to provide a
continuous in-service training to the CBI personnel in order to hone the skill and expertise
of those engaged in the field of crime investigation. The Committee further recommends that,
if need be, more funds for the purpose may be made available to the CBI. (Para 30.7.3)
The Committee expects that the premier training institution of the country ought to
be adequately equipped not only in physical terms but also in terms of its manpower.  Due
to the fact that the whole system of governance is now changing and the international
pressure and expectations are now high, flexibility has to be brought into the system. In
that context, all training programmes confined to one location may not be the single answer
to the existing problem of shortage of manpower. Accordingly, other feasible options of
handling the situation may be explored. At the same time, the DoPT should evolve a policy
which can address this constraint of the LBSNAA. (Para 31.2)
As regards the reduction of funds of the LBSNAA, while acknowledging the
justification of the Ministry, the Committee is of the view that every spending unit should
make a prudent exercise of the avenues of actually carrying out plan schemes it proposes

34
to undertake before budgetary allocations are sought. Furthermore, strategies should be
drawn for the utilization of funds made available to spending units and should be closely
monitored. (Para 31.3)
The Committee fails to comprehend the status quo  in respect of the budgetary
projection for CVC inspite of its earlier recommendation for making the Lokpal
inclus ive. (Para 32.4)
As regards the time taken by CVC to give vigilance clearance for Board level
appointments, the Committee is of the view that the two months time should be reduced
by way of digitalising the data base of these officers which may be maintained by each cadre
controlling authorities and synchronised with that of the CVC and the CBI. Such procedural
delays should be done away with in this age of information technology. (Para 32.5)
The Committee feels that CAT needs to be strengthened in terms of infrastructure
and also the human resource. Therefore, the Committee suggests the Ministry to resolve
the issues related therewith. The Committee also feels that the CAT should function as
mediator rather than a court or an appellate authority. Effort should be to avoid further
appeal against CAT decision   to the High Court or the Supreme Court.  The Committee,
however, suggests that even where it is felt that the law has not been properly applied,
review petition should be encouraged and the CAT should mediate till the case is
settled. (Para 33.3)
The Committee is of the concerted opinion that employees frustrated with the service
condition approach Courts, including CAT where they end up waiting for years for the
judgement and thereby the entire system is adversely affected. The Committee feels that
many of the cases in service matters can be settled by mediation within the system itself.
The Committee is of the opinion that in the administration, the senior officer be made
duty-bound to resolve the grievances of the subordinate officers. He is expected to sit along
with the aggrieved and decide the matter, get the concurrence of the supreme authority and
complete the  entire  process.  The tribunals/ Courts are need to be resorted to  only in
the rarest matters. Thus, the Committee calls for a drastic change in the attitude of the
administration. (Para 33.4)
The Committee appreciates that the UPSC has devised ways to shorten the duration
of time taken from the time of publication of advertisement to time of actual recruitment
and also with regard to DPCs for promotions and deputations. However, it is constrained to
point out that the earlier recommendations of the Committee to explore the feasibility of
adopting a computerized GMAT kind of system having validity of the score for at least three
years in the preliminary stage of examinations has not so far been made applicable by the
UPSC. The Committee therefore reiterates its earlier recommendation to explore a modality
akin to the GMAT system in order to save on the expenditure on stationery as well as
processing time of the examinations at the earliest time possible. (Para 34.3)
The Committee is necessitated to refer to the submissions of the various subordinate
offices within its jurisdiction, such as, the CBI, CIC, CVC, LBSNAA, ISTM, etc. wherein
they have highlighted the gross vacancies of sanctioned posts are lying unfilled-up. The

35
Committee, therefore, recommends that the DoPT in sync with the UPSC assess the extent
of shortfall of staffs in all Ministries/Departments and attached bodies of the Central
Government. The DoPT and the UPSC may thereto work out the manner in which these
vacancies may be filled up at the earliest. (Para 34.4)
The Committee appreciates the initiatives of the SSC in adopting the Computer
based mode of conducting its examinations which has reduced the time taken from filling
up of applications by the candidates to declaration of results. The Committee hopes that
more efforts for better management may be invested by both the Commission  which would
further reduce the time. It is of the firm belief that the time of the young applicants is
very valuable and therefore, the Commission should make all out efforts to not waste their
time by making long lapses in declaring result of examinations. (Para 35.3)
In view of the high increase in number of applicants, the Committee recommends
that commensurate enhancement in the budgetary allocation should also be made by the
DoPT. As the SSC adopted the Computer base mode of conducting examinations, the
Committee desires that all caution should be taken to ensure that examinations are
inaccessible to candidates from far flung and rural areas were commodities such as
electricity, computers are scarce. The Committee further recommends that the SSC should
ensure to authenticate institutions in rural areas to train the youths in those rural areas
with regard to the computer system. (Para 35.4)
The Committee takes note of the upcoming building for the CIC which on
completion,it hopes, will boost the morale of the CIC and in turn will result in the greater
efficiency of the Commission. With regard to the pendency of cases with the Commission,
the Committee recommends that the CIC should take holistic approach to ensure that the
cases pending with it are disposed off in a time-bound manner. (Para 36.3)
In this context, the Committee is of the view that the purpose of the institution of
RTI and thereby the CIC is to ensure transparency in the working of the Government and
not to institute another platform of justice dispensation. Therefore, the CIC should function
more as a mediator between the information seeker and the information giver. On the
other hand, all department and Ministries should be encouraged for voluntary disclosure of
information as per Section 2(4) of the RTI Act. All information providers, such as the
Departments and Ministries should be in a position to give remedy to the ordinary people
to inform how the tax-payers money is spent. (Para 36.4)
In this context the Committee views that the DoPT should impress upon the State
Governments to strictly comply with the service rules relating to tenure of the high
ranking Officers in service so that the Officers are able to discharge their duty without
fear. (Para 37.3)
The Committee desires that posts reserved for PwDs which could not be filled up
should be carried forward and not transferred to another category. The Department should
take all out effort to fill up vacancies under reserved categories and not take the plea of
not finding any candidate eligible.  The Committee further recommends that the eligibility
criteria for these reserved posts be reviewed, where eligible candidates are scarce and skill
development approach should be applied to absorb them into the system. (Para 38.4)

36
The Committee is disappointed that in only one year short of three  decades the
Department has been unable to complete this Plan scheme. Keeping in view the
advancements in IT and communication all over the world, the Committee urges upon the
Government to modernize the entire administrative apparatus, which should come up to the
levels of excellence in administration in the present era of globalization within a set time-
frame. In this regard, the Committee recommends that, if the need be, higher budgetary
projections may be made at the RE stage under this component so that the completion of
the Scheme is not further procrastinated.  This will enable the Department to focus on new
initiatives. (Para 40.2)
While taking note of the steps taken by the Department of Administrative Reforms
with regard to modernization of Government Offices, the Committee is of the view that the
Department should not merely play the role of a funding agency for the schemes, but
should take upon itself the task of monitoring the effective execution of the schemes in the
offices undergoing modernization. While the Committee appreciate the advocacy of Good
Governance and Best Practices in public administration through informative booklets,
pamphlets, CDs, etc. by the Department, it advocates that implementation of these schemes
should not only confine to printing of information material, but  actual achievements of
principles and targets envisaged in the schemes and policies should be accomplished through
other innovative means and continuous training and skill development. (Para 41.3)
The Committee appreciates the initiative of the Department in involving
International Experts to analyze and study the e-governance methods, processes and
technology employed by Government institutions which will be indicative of the country’s
actual standing in the international standards. The move for a Knowledge Repository is also
welcome. It hopes the endeavor of the Department to take the country at par with the e-
governance practices of other advanced countries will be realized very soon. (Para 41.5)
The Committee takes note of the colossal increase in the number of grievances
received lately and the corresponding disposal of the Grievances by the concerned
Departments.  It is of the conviction that the essence of good governance is defeated in the
absence of efficient and timely redressal of grievance. Therefore, the DARPG should keep
close vigil over monitoring of grievance redressal in every Ministry/Depart ment. (Para 42.4)
The C ommittee welcomes the initiative taken by the Department to assess and
analyze the quality of grievance redressal in the Ministries/Departments of the
Government of India. It hopes that the Department will continue the exercise with other
Ministries/Departments as well. The Committee further recommends that the data thus
collected from the Ministries/Departments should be uploaded in its website and given
wide publicity to procreate the zeal for holistic address of the issue in the Ministries/
Departments. (Para 42.5)
“Good Governance” is reflected by the peoples’ satisfaction on the working of,
accessibility to, delivery of services by the system whereas a large number of grievance
petitions show a Department in a bad light.  The Committee is of the view that the spirit
of Public Grievances Redressal Mechanism is primarily to resolve the grievances to the
satisfaction of the petitioner. The Committee strongly feels that the relief and remedial

37
mechanism should also fix the accountability on the decision making authorities. The
Committee notes that the data provided to the Committee does not reflect the satisfaction
of the petitioners in respect of the redressal of their grievances. The Committee is
apprehensive that in the event of dissatisfaction of petitioners on the redressal of their
grievances, the petitioners are bound to approach courts for redressal of their grievances.
Hence, the Committee desires that the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public
Grievances should conduct a study to find out the number of complainants who had gone
to courts/tribunals and how many cases the courts/tribunals ruled in favour of the
petitioners. It further desires that while submitting the ATRs, the findings of the study may
be furnished for the consideration of the Committee. (Para 42.6)
The DARPG should realise the crucial role of the Department in this regard and
having so many high cadre officers should take a pro active stand to see that these
recommendations are implemented in war footing mode to give “Good Governance” in real
time basis. (Para 42.7)
The Committee desires that this Department should play a role as an apex level
coordination entity for addressing the grievances of pensioners. The Committee is of the
firm belief that pension is a right and not merely a privilege. It welcomes the initiatives
‘Bhavishya’, ‘Sankalp’ and ‘Anubhav’ and hopes that maximum pensioners will be benefited
by these initiatives. The Committee, at the same time is very much concerned about their
welfare and accordingly recommends that nodal officers should be appointed in every
Department including PSUs in their respective establishments to attend to the welfare of
pensioners and represent them if required to the concerned authorities. (Para 43.3)
