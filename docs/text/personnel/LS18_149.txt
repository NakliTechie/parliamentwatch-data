CHAPTER - I 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
1.0 The Ministry of Law and Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by 
the British Parliament and it is one of the oldest limbs of the Government of India. In 
accordance with the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Ministry 
comprises three Departments under the overall charge of the Law and Justice Minister 
assisted by a Minister of State. Each department is administratively headed by an independent 
Secretary. 
 
1.1.  The key mandate of the Ministry includes providing policy support in administration 
of justice; giving legal advice to Government of India and defend Government of India in 
various Courts and Tribunals and draft Supreme and Subordinate form of legislations for 
Government of India, etc. 
1.2. In this report, the demands allocated to Legislative Department (Demand No. 65) and 
Election Commission of India (Demand No. 66) have been analyzed and recommendations of 
the Committee have been made at the end of each heading/sub-headings. 
 
1.3. Legislative Department  acts as a service  provider to the Ministries/Departments in 
drafting of principal legislation and vetting of subordinate legislation. The Department is also 
involved in Drafting of Ordinances promulgated by the President, Presidential Acts for the 
States and the President' s Rule, Presidential Regulations to be made by the President under 
Article 240 for Union Territories, Constitution Orders etc.  
 
1.4. This Department also deals with the matters related to Elections to Parliament, the 
Legislatures of States and Union Territories and Offices of the President and Vice -President.. 
This Department also drafts the Finance Bill to give effect to the financial proposals of the 
Central Government every year, which is undertaken in the Legislative Department on the 
budget proposals being brought before it by the Ministry of Finance.  The Department is also 

 
9 
involved in the Publication of Hindi translation of selected judgments of the Supreme Court 
and High Courts on cases pertaining to constitutional, civil and criminal laws in the for m of 
law Journals (Patrikas).  
 
1.5. Further, Ordinance promulgated by the President of India, Regulation made by 
President of India for Union Territories, Electoral Laws and Electoral reforms, maintenance 
of the updated Central Legislation under Bharat Sanhita (India Code) and authoritative 
translation of Central Legislations and Constitution of India in languages specified in the 
Eight Schedule to the Constitution under the Authoritative Text (Central Laws) Act 1973, 
publication and preparation of standard legal terminology, delimitation of Parliamentary and 
Legislative Assembly Constituencies, etc. are also assigned to the Department under 
Allocation of Business Rules, 1961. The Department also imparts training in legislative 
drafting to Officers of the Union/State Government and UT administration through the 
Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research (ILDR). 
 
1.6. It inter-alia administers Personal laws, Electoral laws, the Indian Contract Act, 1872, 
the Specific Relief Act, 1963, the Evidence Act, 1872, the Indian Trust Act, 1882, the Code 
of Civil Procedure, 1908, the Limitation Act, 1963, the Election Commission (Conditions of 
Service of Election Commissioners and Transaction of Business) Act, 1991, the 
Representation of People Act, 1950 and the Representation of People Act, 1951. It also 
facilitates procurement of Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) for the Election Commission of 
India, Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) Machines and reimbursement of election 
expenditure to State Governments and UT administrations. 
1.7. Legislative Department does not have any statutory or autonomous body under its 
control. It has two oth er wings under it, namely, the Official Languages Wing and Vidhi 
Sahitya Prakashan, which are responsible  for propagation of Hindi and other Official 
Languages in the field of law. 
(i) Official Languages Wing (OLW): The Official Languages Wing is a 
successor organisation of the Official Languages (Legislative) Commission 
under the Legislative Department and is responsible for preparing and 
publishing standard legal terminology and also for translation of Hindi, all the 
Bills to be introduced in Parliament, all Central Acts, Ordinances, Subordinate 
legislations, etc. as required under the Official Languages Act, 1963. The 
Official Languages Wing is also responsible for arranging translation of the 
Central Acts, Ordinances, etc., into the Official Languages as specified in the 
Eighth Schedule to the Constitution as required under the Authoritative Texts 

 
10 
(Central Laws) Act, 1973. The Official Languages Wing also grants-in-aid to 
various registered voluntary organisations engaged in promotion and 
propagation of Hindi and other regional languages and those organisations, 
which are directly engaged in the publication of legal literature and 
propagation of Hindi and other Languages in the field of law.  
       
 (ii) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan (VSP): VSP is a wing under the Legislative 
Department, Ministry of Law and Justice. This wing was set up under the 
recommendations of the Parliamentary Committee on Official Languages in 
the year 1968 for promotion and propagation of Hindi in the legal field and for 
this purpose a monthly Hindi law journal for publication of reportable 
judgements of Supreme Court was started under the name and style of 
Uchchttam Nyayalay Nirnay Patrika. Thereafter, in the year 1969, another 
Hindi law journal was started under the name and style of Uchch Nyayalay 
Nirnay Patrika for publication of Hindi version of the judgements passed by 
various High Courts. In the year, 1987, Uchch Nyayalay Nirnay Patrika was 
divided into two Hindi law journals i.e. civil nirnay partika and dandik nirnay 
patrika. In this way VSP is publishing three journals for promotion and 
propagation of Hindi in the legal field. Apart from these three law journals, 
VSP is also publishing standard law text books in Hindi written by eminent 
jurists. This Prakashan is also responsible for sale of official diglot edition of 
Central Acts, Constitution of India, Election Manual, Legal Glossary etc. This 
Prakashan also organizes exhibition and sale of official diglot edition of 
Central Acts, Constitution of India, Election Manual, Legal Glossary, law 
journals etc. and also conducts seminars and workshops for promotion and 
propagation of Hindi all over India. 
 
1.8. The organisational set -up of the Legislative Department includes the Secretary, 
Additional Secretary, Joint Secretary & Legislat ive Counsel, Additional Legislative 
Counsel, Deputy Legislative Counsel, Assistant Legislative Counsel and other 
supporting staff. The work relating to legislative drafting, scrutiny and vetting of 
subordinate legislation have been distributed among variou s Legislative Groups, and 
each group is headed by a Joint Secretary & Legislative Counsel or Additional 
Secretary. The Secretary of the Legislative Department also acts as the Chief 

 
11 
Parliamentary Counsel and the Additional Secretary is in -charge of all sub ordinate 
legislation. 
ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA 
1.9. The Legislative Department is the administrative Department for the Election 
Commission of India, an autonomous constitutional authority created under Article 324 of 
Constitution for superintendence, direction, control of elections to Parliament, State 
legislatures, and to the offices of the President and the Vice-President of India.  The 
functions, responsibilities, structure and power of ECI are enlisted in the Articles 324 to 329 
of Part XV of the Constitution of India. Article 324 of the Constitution of India bestows 
electoral powers, duties and functions on the ECI, while Section 14 of the Representation of 
the Peoples Act, 1951 provides for conduct of the elections to constitute a new Lok Sabha 
before the expiry of its current term. 
1.10. The Election Commission of India is a multimember body (since 1993) headed by the 
Chief Election Commissioner, who acts as Chairman of the Commission. The Chief Election 
Commissioner and Election Commissioners a re appointed by the President of India and have 
tenure of six years, or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. The Commission is 
assisted by Deputy Election Commissioners, Chief Electoral Officers (CEO), District 
Election Officers (DEO) and Electoral Registration Officers (ERO), appointed by the 
Election Commission under Representation of People Act, 1951.  
1.11. It registers political parties and allots symbols to them and ensures inner party 
democracy in their functioning by insisting organizational elections at periodic intervals. 
Political parties registered with the Commission are granted recognition at the State and 
National levels on the basis of their poll performance at general elections according to criteria 
specified by it. It implements Model Code of Conduct (MCC) for smooth conduct of free and 
fair elections. It also observes 25th January as National Voters' Day to encourage young 
electors to take part in election process.  
1.12. The electoral expenses on account of conduct of elections  to the House of the People 
and the State Legislative Assembly are borne by the Central Government and the concerned 
State Government, and case of simultaneous elections, the expenditure is to be shared on 
50:50 basis between Central and the concerned Stat e Government. The initial expenditure is 
borne by the respective State Governments, and on submission of the audited report, the 
Central Government’s share is reimbursed through the Legislative Department.

1.13. The Election Commission has its independent  Secretariat for the work relating to the 
smooth conduct of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, and t here is an exclusive 
demand (Demand No. 66) i.e. voted expenditure under which budgetary allocation is sought 
for running the activities of Election Commission of India.

Repeal of Obsolete Laws  
 
2.37  The Department in its written submiss ion has stated that the Government, as a part 
of its resolve to bring reforms in the legal system had constituted a two Member Committee 
in 2014 to undertake comprehensive review of laws with a view to remove obsolete and 
redundant laws. On the basis of th e recommendations of the said Two Member Committee 
and the Law Commission, the Legislative Department had identified 1824 redundant and 
obsolete Acts. After due examination and consultations with concerned Ministries and 
Departments, a total of 1562 Central Acts have since been repealed. Presently, a total number 
of 76 obsolete and redundant laws have been repealed in 2023 through the Repeal and 
Amending Bill, 2023 (Act No. 37 of 2023) by the Government of India. Total 1486 obsolete 
laws were repealed since  2014 till the last Demands for Grants . Further, the Twenty -third 
Law Commission of India has been constituted with a mandate inter alia to examine 
review/repeal of obsolete laws. 
 
2.38  As far as repealing of Obsolete laws is concerned, the Committee appreciates 
the work done in this regard  and that 1824 reduntant and obsolete laws have been 
identified to be repealed. The Committee also feels that the laws prevalent in a coun try 
should be representative of the political and social structure  existing at that time. The 
Committee hopes that the department will undertake such exercise of identifying and 
repealing the obsolete laws on regular and continuous basis in future also.

SOME GENERAL RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS 
3.11      The Committee is given to understand that whenever the elections are 
announced in states, the Model Code of Conduct comes into place immediately on 
announcement and trade gets hit. Though, Digital transactions have made a huge 
impact, still a large portion of the country’s economy is cash economy. In the pretext of 
implementation of the Model Code of Conduct, even the genuine transactions of the 
traders are seized which impacts the trader and the economy. The Committee desires 
that ECI should have a mechanism to verify the purpose for which the money is being 
transacted. On verification, if it is found to be for legal purpose, then the entire money 
should be released immediately.

OVERVIEW OF THE BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS AND POLICY ISSUES 
PERTAINING TO LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT 
OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS 
1. The Committee observes that the Department has been able to spend about 80% 
of the RE (2024-25) upto 31st January, 2025. The Committee appreciates the Department 
for incurring the expenditure uniformly as a whole till 31 st January, 2025 keeping in 
view the instructions issued by the Ministry of Finance (MoF), wherein the expenditure 
in the  last quarter has to be restricted to 33% ceiling and in last month (March) 
expenditure has to be restricted to 15% of ceiling. The Committee hopes that the 
Department shall strive to utilize the budget uniformly in the remaining part of FY 
2024-25 and in forthcoming financial year 2025-26.(Para 2.20) 
 
 2.      The Committee observes that huge reductions in allocations in BE (2025 -26) over 
the projections made to the Ministry of Finance for the year 2025 -26 under various 
heads have been done, namely Legislative Department (about 36%), Official Language 
Wing(OLW) (about 61%) and Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan (about 27%) on the revenue 
Side and EVMs for Election Commission (about 94%), Official Language Wing(OLW) 
(about 158%) , Legislative Department (about 386%) and Vidhi Sahitya 
Prakashan(about 272%) on the Capital Side . The Committee is not aware about the 
reasons for such major reductions under various heads. The Committee feels that the 
Department is failing on its part to seek realistic financial projection targ ets from the 
Ministry of Finance which may be due to its inaccurate projection or inflation of funds. 
The Committee is therefore of the view the department should improve its financial 
forecasting methods using past expenditure trends to prevent such large  discrepancies 
between its projections and actual allocations.  Further, the department should engage 
with the Ministry of Finance (MoF) regularly to avoid budget cuts.  
 (Para 2.21) 
 
3. The Committee observes that the department has spent only about 10%  upto 31st 
January, 2025, of the allocated Rs. 8.17 crore for EVMs in RE 2024-25 . The Committee 
therefore, suggests the Department to improve its spending pattern on EVMs and 
further, hopes the department to improve its procurement planning. (Para 2.22)

POLICY ISSUES PERTAINING TO THE DEPARTMENT 
 
Staff Strength in the Department 
 
5. The Committee observes that the number of vacancies in the Legislative 
Department has increased over a period of time. Despite the recommendations of the 
Committee in its earli er reports regarding the filling of vacancies, it seems that the 
Department is not giving a thought to fill up the vacancies as it has been furnishing 
vague and routine replies. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the department 
should coordinate with the recruitment agencies continuously to fill up all the required 
vacancies so that the work of it is not hampered in terms of the assigned mandate. 
Further, the Department may consider hiring through outsourcing until permanent 
staff is recruited and als o, expedite the amendments in RRs to remove outdated criteria 
hindering recruitment. (Para 2.27) 
 
Translation Of Constitution Of India/Statutes In Scheduled Languages 
 
6. The Committee in its previous reports, 101 st, 107 th, 114 th and 130 th  reports on 
Demands for Grants suggested the Department  to take the work related to translation 
of the Constitution in scheduled languages on priority. The Committee also observes 
that translation work have been done upto 106 th Amendment in Hindi, Maithili, 
Malayalam, Marathi, Punjabi, Sanskrit and Urdu this year, as the updation work upto 
105th amendment was done till the last Demands for Grants. However, so far 106 
Amendments have taken place.(Para 2.35) 
 
 
7. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that the work related to translation 
and updation of the Constitution of India in all the Scheduled languages of the 
Constitution be expedited. The Committee, further, recommends that a policy 
framework or a legal mandate  may be introduced by the Department to ensure that 
every constitutional amendment is translated within a fixed timeframe in all scheduled 
languages. Further, the feasibility of  outsourcing a part of the translation work to 
certified language experts or universities specializing in regional languages may be 
explored.(Para 2.36)
