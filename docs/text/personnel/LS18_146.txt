DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND  
PUBLIC GRIEVANCES (DARPG) 
Evolution of the Department 
1.3 In March, 1954, an Organisation and Method (O & M) Division was set up in Cabinet 
Secretariat as a follow up to one of the recommendations made by Paul H. Appleby. In 
March, 1964, a Department of Administrative Reforms was set up within the Ministry of 
Home Affairs and Organisation and Method (O & M) Division was transferred to its charge 
from the Cabinet Secretariat. On 7th February, 1973, the work relating to the Department of 
Administrative Reforms was transferred to Department of Personnel created under Cabinet 
Secretariat on 1st August, 1970 and was re-designated as Department of Personnel and 
Administrative Reforms. In April, 1977, the Department of Personnel and Administrative 
Reforms was shifted from Cabinet Secretariat to the Ministry of Home Affairs. In March 
1985, the Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms was elevated to a full fledged 
Ministry of Personnel and Training, Administrative Reforms, Public Grievances and 
Pensions. On 10th December, 1985 Ministry of Personnel and Training, Administrative 
Reforms, Public Grievances and Pensions was re-designated as the Ministry of Personnel, 
Public Grievances and Pensions with three Departments namely, Department of Personnel 
and Training (DOPT), Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances and 
Department of Pension and Pensioners Welfare.  The Ministry was placed under the overall 
charge of the Prime Minister assisted by a Minister of State. 
About the Department 
1.4 The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal 
agency of the Government of India for administrative reforms as well as for redress of public 
grievances relating to the States in general and grievances pertaining to Central Government 
agencies in particular. The Department disseminates information on important activities of 
the Government relating to administrative reforms and public grievance redressal through 
publications and documentation. The Department also undertakes activities in the field of 
international exchange and cooperation to promote public service reforms. There are 8 
Divisions in the Department namely Administrative Division, Administrative Reforms (AR) 
Division, Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) Division, Documentation And 
Dissemination (D&D) Division, e-Governance (e-Gov) Division, International Exchange & 
Cooperation (IE&C) Division,  Organization & Method (O&M) Division, Public Grievances 
(PG) Division. The Department comes under the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances 
and Pensions and is headed by a Secretary.

MAJOR ACTIVITIES / INITIATIVES OF THE DEPARTMENT 
Public Grievances 
1.6 An online system for grievance redress, called the ‘Centralized Public Grievance 
Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS) has evolved since 2007. The Department 
extensively uses this System to forward public grievances to the Central Government 
Ministries/Departments/Organisations as well as to the State Governments concerned. 
 1.7 Public grievance mechanism of PMO, the President’s Secretariat, the Directorate of 
Public Grievances (Cabinet Secretariat), Department of Administrative Reforms & Public 
Grievances (DARPG) and that of Department of  Pension & Pensioners’ Welfare portal are 
integrated through CPGARMS thus enabling grievances lodged to any of these entities to be 
transferred to the Central Ministries/Departments and to the State / UT Governments online 
through CPGRAMS.  
Introduction of an Appeal Mechanism/functionality in CPGRAMS 
1.8 Centralized Public Grievances Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS) is one 
of the most widely used IT platforms, provides a direct access to the citizens to the 
government authorities. Citizens can file and track their grievances in CPGRAMS 24x7. All 
Ministries/ Departments of GOI and State Governments are part of this system.  The Appeal 
provision is made for redressal of dissatisfied grievances identified through a mandatory 
feedback rating to be given by the Citizen on disposal of the grievance by the Nodal 
Grievance Officers.

Citizens' Charter 
1.9 A Citizens' Charter represents the commitment of the Organisation towards standard, 
quality and time frame of service delivery, grievance redress mechanism, transparency and 
accountability. Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances, in the Ministry 
of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, Government of India, in its efforts to provide 
more responsive and citizen-friendly governance coordinates the efforts to formulate and 
operationalise Citizens' Charters. Various Central Government Ministries/ Departments/ 
Organisations have brought out their Citizens' Charters. With a view to ensure effective 
implementation of Citizens' Charter, Nodal Officers have been appointed in the concerned 
Central Government Ministries/ Departments/ Organisations.   
Sevottam 
1.10 Sevottam is a generic framework for achieving excellence in public service delivery. 
It comprises of 3 modules namely, Citizen’s Charter, Grievance Redressal Mechanism and 
Capability Building for Service Delivery. As part of the Strategic plan for Capability 
Building towards implementing Sevottam Quality Management System, a Scheme for 
Strengthening of Administrative Training Institutes has been implemented during the  
12th Five Year Plan 2012-2017 in 10 States.  This Scheme was extended for a further period 
of 3 years till 31.03.2020 and for another period of a year upto 31.03.2021.  The Scheme is 
presently under review in the Department.   
e-Office - Mission Mode Project  
1.11 e-Office is one of the Mission Mode Projects approved by the Cabinet under e-
Governance Plan (NeGP). The project is aimed at significantly improving the operational 
efficiency of Central Government Ministries/Departments through improving the workflow 
mechanism and associated office procedure manuals. The Department of Administrative 
Reforms & Public Grievances is the nodal agency to monitor and facilitate implementation of 
the project. With constant monitoring and support, use of e-office has registered further 
increase with 74 Ministries have migrated to latest version of e-office 7.0. E receipt has 
grown from 32% in 2021 to 94.19% in Dec 2024. Over 80% work of Central Ministries are 
now on e-office.   
Central Secretariat Manual of Office Procedure (CSMOP)  
1.12 DARPG is responsible to bring out manual for office procedure in Central Secretariat. 
This is an important reference document provide much needed guidance to Central 
Secretariat system which saves time and efforts in figuring out processes, reduces possibility 
of mistakes and improves efficiency of employees. The Sixteenth edition of the Central 
Secretariat Manual of Office Procedure CSMOP-2022 was released on 5th August 2022. The 
new edition reflects the changes that have been brought in as a result of exercise of increasing 
efficiency in decision making and special Campaign 2.0. DARPG also brought out the first 
Hindi edition of CSMOP on 31st October 2022 to facilitate Secretariat functioning in the 
Official Language.

Increasing efficiency in Decision Making  
 
1.13 Process re-engineering for increasing efficiency in decision making in the government 
is one of critical reforms undertaken by the Government to make the governance responsive, 
accountable and efficient. Delayering. Delegation of powers to lower formulations, 
Digitization and Desk officer system have been identified as key reforms towards increasing 
efficiency in decision making. DARPG is following up implementation and impact of these 
measures in the Central Ministries/ Department. DARPG published the Impact Analysis 
Report based on 3rd party evaluation conducted by Quality Council of India. The study 
revealed that reforms of delayering, delegation, digitization and desk officer system have led 
to defined work allocation, reduced channels of submission and timely decision making, 
embodying the principle of "Minimum Government, Maximum Governance."  
 
Special Campaign on Swachata and reducing pendency in Government offices 
  
1.14 In order to reduce pendency in the government Departments and to institutionalize 
Swachhata, the Honb'le Prime Minister Called for annual campaign for taking up these two 
objectives in mission mode. DARPG is nodal Department to curate and steer the Special 
Campaign across all institutions of the Central Government. DARPG structured the 
Campaign outline, issued the guidelines and coordinated the Campaign.  
Prime Minister’s Award for Excellence in Public Administration and Civil Services Day 
1.15 Government of India observes 21st of April every year as "Civil Services Day 
(CSD)". This is an occasion for civil servants of the country to rededicate themselves to 
citizens and renew their commitment to public service and excellence in work. The function 
is normally organized over two days. The 16th Civil Services Day was organised in Vigyan 
Bhawan, New Delhi on 20th-21st April, 2023, on the theme “VIKSIT BHARAT-
Empowering Citizens & Reaching the Last Mile”.  The Civil Services Day could not be held 
in the year 2024 due to the General Elections. The Prime Minister‘s Awards for the year 2023 
and 2024 is proposed to be conferred on the occasion of the 17th Civil Services Day on 21 
April, 2025.               
National Conference on e-Governance (Annual event)  
1.16 Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances, in association with 
Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology and one of the State/UT Governments, 
has been organizing the National Conference on e-Governance (NCeG) every year since 
1997. This Conference provides a platform to the senior officers of the Government including 
IT Secretaries of State Governments, IT Managers of the Central Government, Industry, 
Experts and academia to discuss, exchange views and experiences of various e-Governance 
initiatives and road ahead.

Good Governance Webinar Series  
 
1.18 To disseminate and encourage replication of good governance practices, DARPG is 
conducting National Good Governance Webinar series on a monthly basis from April 2022. 
The themes selected for the Webinars cover various sectors like Health, Education, 
Environment, Sports- Khelo India, Innovation Category, Jal/water management, Public 
grievance redressal, Service delivery etc. Purpose is to highlight the best practices adopted by 
the award-winning initiatives for replication by other States/Districts. District Collectors, 
Secretaries in-charge of Administrative Reforms in the States/Union Territories, trainees 
from national and state level administrative training institutes and senior officials from the 
concerned line department in the States/ UTs/ Districts are invited to attend the webinars. 6 
Webinars have been held till now under the National Good Governance Webinar Series 
2024-25. The Webinar has drawn good response and will be scaled up in the next year.  
 
Good Governance Index (GGI)  
1.19 Good Governance Index (GGI) is a comprehensive and implementable framework to 
assess the state of governance across the States and UTs on identified governance parameters. 
Key objective is to create a tool which can be applied uniformly across the States/UTs to 
assess impact of various interventions made by the Central and State Governments. DARPG 
releases Good Governance Index (GGI) on biennial basis. The first GGI was released in 2019 
and the second GGI was released in 2021. The GGI 2021 evaluated performance of States/ 
UTs on 58 Indicators spread across 10 Sectors. The ten sectors covered are (1) Agriculture 
and Allied Sectors, (2) Commerce & Industries, (3) Human Resource Development, (4) 
Public Health, (5) Public Infrastructure & Utilities, (6) Economic Governance, (7) Social 
Welfare & Development, (8) Judicial & Public Security, (9) Environment, and (10) Citizen- 
Centric Governance. GGI 2025 is proposed to be taken up during the next financial year 
2025-26.

National e-governance Service Delivery Assessment (NeSDA):  
 
1.20 The Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances (DARPG) had 
constituted the National e-governance Service Delivery Assessment (NeSDA) in 2019 as part

excellence. The study assesses States, Union Territories (UTs), and focus Central Ministries

replication of PM awarded and National e-Governance awarded initiatives.

NATIONAL CENTRE FOR GOOD GOVERNANCE (NCGG) 
1.22 National Centre for Good Governance was established as an autonomous institute 
under the aegis of the Department of Personnel and Training by upgrading the erstwhile 
National Institution of Administrative Research, Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of 
Administration, Mussoorie. DARPG has been made the Administrative authority for NCGG 
since 08.11.2017.  
1.23 The Centre is envisaged to be the apex think-tank that would guide the Government 
and help in implementing good Governance reforms. It seeks to be a high-level institute for 
research and capacity building to deal with the entire gamut of governance issues from the 
National to State to the local level and in sectors across the Government.  
1.24 NCGG is governed by a Governing Body, under the Chairmanship of the Cabinet 
Secretary, GoI and a Management Committee with the Secretary of the Department as the 
Chairperson.  
Objectives of NCGG 
1.25 Following are the objectives of the NCGG:  
 Conducting Capacity Building Training Programme at both National and 
International level on Public Policy & Governance; 
 To function as a national repository on information on best practices, initiatives 
and methodologies that promote Good Governance, E-Governance, innovation 
& change management within the government; 
 To initiate and participate in action research and capacity building on various 
aspects of regulatory and development administration, public policy, governance 
and public management at National/State & local levels; 
 To advise on key issues in governance and development synergy across various 
Ministries/ Departments of GoI, and State Governments; 
 To promote sharing and replication of innovative ideas and best practices in 
Governance; 
 To interact with national and international organizations, in and outside 
government, engaged in research and capacity building in the above spheres.  
 
Mandate of NCGG 
 
1.26 The mandate of the NCGG is to promote Good Governance by:  
 Carrying out Action Research/ Studies on issues relating to governance. 
 Capacity Building on Public Policy and Governance at both National and 
International level. 
 Organizing consultations, workshops, seminars/webinars & talks to promote 
improved governance. 
 Undertaking initiatives for citizen centric governance.

DEPARTMENT OF PENSION AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE 
(DoPPW) 
About the Department 
1.27 The Department of Pension & Pensioners' Welfare was set up in 1985 as part of the 
Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions to formulate policy and coordination 
of matters relating to retirement benefits of Central Government employees. The Department 
started functioning independently with effect from April 1, 2007 with the mission to 
formulate pension policy for central government employees. The sanction process and 
implementation of the pension policy is done by the respective Central Ministry/Department. 
This department further, from time to time, simplifies the rules/guidelines and procedure to 
facilitate welfare of pensioners. All the Ministries/Departments falling under the jurisdiction 
of the Controller General of Accounts (CGA) are governed by the pension policy framed by 
this Department. However, Defence, Railways, Post and Telecom have their own processing 
and sanctioning mechanism for pension. It is noteworthy that the expenditure incurred on 
pension is directly dealt with by the Controller General of Accounts, M/O Finance for Central 
Government employees other than Defence, Railway, Post and Telecom. The Department 
also receives a large number of references from Ministries/Departments and renders advice 
on interpretation of Pension Rules which govern Central Civil service.   
Functions of the Department 
1.28 The Allocation of Business Rules broadly allocates the following areas of work to the 
Department:- 
(i) Formulation of policy and co-ordination of matters relating to retirement 
benefits to Central Government employees (Civil, Defence and Railway 
Pensioners). 
(ii) Administration of – 
(a) The Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972; the Central Civil 
Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981; the Central Civil Services 
(Extraordinary Pension) Rules, 1939; the All India Services (Death-cum-
retirement benefits) Rules, 1958, and 
(b) any other scheme relating to Central Government pensioners, entrusted to 
the Department.  
(iii) Pension structure and relief to pensioners. 
(iv) New facilities of fringe benefits to the Central Government pensioners. 
(v) Matters relating to amendment to, or relaxation of, Pension rules or any other 
rule concerning retirement benefits. 
(vi) Policy and co-ordination relating to welfare of Central Government 
Pensioners.

MAJOR ACTIVITIES / INITIATIVES OF THE DEPARTMENT 
Pensioners’ Portal 
1.29 The scheme, ‘Pensioners Portal’ was started with an objective to serve as a one stop 
information source for the pensioners of Government of India and also facilitate redressal of 
their grievances online. With the passage of time, new activities were added under the 
‘Pensioners' Portal’ which now includes CPENGRAM, Bhavishya, Anubhav and Sankalp etc. 
with an objective to create a single window system for civil pensioners.  
1.30 Pensioners’ Portal through its website provides pensioners with updated information 
on pension rules, personalized pension road map, online calculator for calculation of 
pension/family pension and redressal of Pensioners’ grievances through CPENGRAM.  
Pensioners’ Grievance Redressal Machinery (CPENGRAM) 
 
1.31 The Department, being the nodal Department for formulation of policies relating to 
pension also provides a platform for timely and structured redressal of grievances, pertaining 
to pensioners through on-line Centralised Pension Grievance Redress and Monitoring System 
(CPENGRAMS). All Ministries/Departments with their subordinate units are linked in 
CPENGRAMS with the objective to provide online resolution of the pensioners’ grievances.  
1.32 On this portal the pensioners can register their pension related grievances pertaining to 
any of the Central Government Departments/Ministries, which are then forwarded to the 
concerned Ministry/Department for redressal.  
1.33  Uniqueness of the Portal: On CPENGRAMS Portal, apart from pensioners from Civil 
Ministries/Departments, the Pensioners from Ministry of Defense, Ministry of Railways, 
Department of Posts and Department of Telecommunication can also file their grievances. For 
this, only basic information is required and log in is not required. Hence, about 64.88 lakh 
central government pensioners can file their grievances on the portal. Moreover, it is the only 
portal which is exclusively for redressal of the grievances of Central Government Pensioners. 
 
1.34 Grievances can be registered online on the portal (https://pgportal.gov.in/pension/)  
or through the Post, Email (care.dppw@nic.in) or Department’s Toll-free number:  
1800-11-1960. The last three modes are handled by Integrated Grievance Cell and Call Centre 
(IGCCC), which has been in operation since July, 2019. All such grievances of Central 
Government pensioners are registered into the CPENGRAMS and forwarded to the concerned 
Ministries or Departments promptly.

Pension Adalat 
 
1.36 These were introduced to tackle ongoing issues within the current policy, foster 
collaboration among stakeholders such as the Ministry/Department, Pay & Accounts Office, 
Central Pension Accounts Office, Bank, and Pensioner. This alternative dispute resolution 
method aims to alleviate pension-related grievances, reducing the need for legal recourse like 
courts or the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT). The streamlined process not only 
addresses concerns more efficiently but also cuts government expenditure on legal matters, 
saving valuable time and resources.  The first Pension Adalat of the Department was held on 
September 20, 2017 in New Delhi and 11th Pension Adalat was held on August 28, 2024. 
Pension Adalat provides an additional forum for redressal of pension related grievances 
obviating the need to approach Court/ CAT.  Therefore, this would reduce time and resources 
of the Government in handling court matters. Thematic pension Adalat especially for family 
pensioners and super aged (above 80 years age) have also been extremely effective for 
redressal of grievances through pension Adalats.  
 
The ‘Bhavishya’ System 
 
1.37 To overcome the problems of delay & clerical errors in processing the pension cases, 
duplication as well as financial loss and harassment to the pensioners, the DOPPW 
introduced, on a pilot basis, a unique innovative centralized pension processing software 
called ‘Bhavishya’ for all central government Ministries/Departments.  
1.38 After a successful pilot, Bhavishya’ was made mandatory for all central civil 
Ministries and Departments w.e.f 01.01.2017. At present, 99 Ministries/ Departments, 1033 
Offices and 9168 DDOs are on Board. The ‘Bhavishya’ software was indigenously made by 
the Department of Pension & Pensioners’ Welfare and was meant to be a common platform 
for processing of pension cases by all civil Ministries/Departments of Government of India.  
Digital Empowerment of Pensioners’ - Digital Life Certificate (DLC) Campaign 3.0 
1.39 Every year in the month of November a pensioner is required to produce life 
certificate for further continuation of his pension. In the traditional mode, pensioners could 
submit life certificate by physically presenting themselves before the pension disbursing 
authority such as banks or send a certificate issued by a gazetted officer or other designated 
authorities. Due to infirmity or other ailments and old age, this manual system was a cause of 
extreme discomfort and inconvenience, as pensioners had to wait outside the bank branches 
in long queues for submission of Life certificate Keeping in view the concerns of senior 

 20 
pensioners and discomfort faced by them in the manual process, it was felt necessary to 
streamline the process to make it digital and initiate Door Step Service for submission of Life 
Certificate. 
 
1.40 In November 2014, the Aadhaar-based Jeevan Pramaan Scheme was launched to 
allow pensioners to submit life certificates online. In 2018, the DoPPW initiated the DLC 
from Home pilot program with Pensioners’ Associations, successfully creating 2,500 Digital 
Life Certificates for elderly and infirm pensioners across seven cities. 
 
1.41 To expand doorstep services for submitting life certificates, the DoPPW partnered with 
India Post Payments Bank (IPPB) in November 2020. Leveraging the network of 2,06,200 
postmen and Gramin Dak Sevaks, the DLC from Home initiative reached all corners of the 
country, especially benefiting pensioners in rural areas.. 
 
1.42 In coordination with Public Sector Banks Alliance (PSB), the DoPPW introduced 
doorstep banking services for pensioners to submit their Life Certificates by calling banking 
agents at home. To address difficulties faced by senior pensioners in submitting digital Life 
Certificates due to non-capturing of biometrics, DoPPW directed Pension Disbursing Banks 
to adopt Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP), enabling a smoother process. 
Most banks have successfully implemented V-CIP for their pensioners, enhancing 
convenience and accessibility.   
 
1.43 Face Authentication Technology has been launched on 29th November, 2021 with a 
view to enhance ease of submission of Digital Life Certificate by pensioners. This technology 
allows pensioners to submit their DLC from home using an Android mobile, eliminating the 
need for external biometric devices. By capturing and submitting a live photo, pensioners' 
identities are authenticated through the UIDAI server, generating the DLC. This innovation 
has made the submission process more accessible and affordable, particularly for senior 
citizens. The technology has been widely promoted through social media platforms like 
YouTube, Facebook, Twitter, and print media, enhancing awareness and ease for pensioners.

CHAPTER - III POLICIES, PROGRAMMES AND SCHEMES  DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS & PUBLIC GRIEVANCES (DARPG) Public Grievances  Centralized Public Grievances Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS)  3.0 CPGRAMS is an online system for grievance redress to facilitate citizens to lodge their grievances, for redress from anywhere and anytime (24x7) to Ministries/Departments/ Organizations/State Governments/UTs who scrutinize and take action for speedy and favorable redress of these grievances. CPGRAMS also facilitates tracking grievances through a system generated unique registration number. The grievances received manually are also digitized and uploaded on the system. The system enables Ministries/Departments to take appropriate action and upload the Action Taken Report (ATR) on the system which can be viewed by the citizens online with the help of the unique registration number.  3.1 Public grievance mechanism of PMO, the President’s Secretariat, the Directorate of Public Grievances (Cabinet Secretariat), Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances (DARPG) and that of Department of  Pensioners’ portal have been duly integrated through CPGRMS thus enabling grievances lodged to any of these entities to be transferred to the Central Ministries/Departments and State Governments online through CPGRAMS.   CPGRAMS Reforms 3.2 In 2024-25 a 10-step CPGRAMS reforms process was adopted by DARPG for improving quality of disposal and reducing the time lines. The 10-step reforms include: 10 Step CPGRAMS Reform    


 28 
3.3 The 10 Step Reforms are outlined as follows:  
 
(i) Universalization of CPGRAMS 7.0 -Auto-routing of grievances to the last mile  
(ii) Technological Enhancements -Automatic flagging of urgent grievances 
leveraging AI/ML  
(iii) Language Translation –CPGRAMS Portal in 22 scheduled languages along with 
English  
(iv) Grievance Redressal Index -Ranking of Ministries / Departments on their 
Performance  
(v) Feedback Call Centre -50-seater call centre to collect feedback directly from 
every citizen whose grievance is redressed  
(vi) One Nation One Portal -Integration of State Portal and other GoI portals with 
CPGRAMS  
(vii)Inclusivity and Outreach -Empowering the remotest citizen to file grievances 
through CSC‘s  
(viii)Training and Capacity Building -Conducted by ISTM and State ATIs under

(ix) Monitoring Progress -Monthly reports for both the Central 
Ministries/Departments and States/UTs  
(x) Data Strategy Unit -Established at DARPG for insightful data analytics  
 
 
3.4 Impact of these Reforms: This 10-Step CPGRAMS reforms process adoption has 
resulted in a remarkable decrease in the average time of disposals of Public Grievances. In 
2023, 1.25 lac PG cases in January, 1.22 lac PG cases in February and 1.43 lakh PG cases in 
March, 1.06 PG lakh cases in April, 1.16 lakh PG cases in May, were disposed by 
Ministries/Departments. May 2023 was the 10th time in a row, since inception of CPGRAMS 
that PG case redressal has crossed 1 lac cases/month. The disposal in State PG cases on 
CPGRAMS portal has crossed 50,000 cases/month since September 2022. Average disposal 
time of Central Ministries/Departments has improved from 32 days in 2021 to 27 days in 
2022 to 18 days in 2023, to just 13 day in 2024. A significant 1,13,131 grievances were 
disposed in the month, making December the 30th month in a row where disposal crossed 1 
lakh cases in the Central Secretariat, showcasing the system's efficiency, while 58,138 
grievances remain pending, as on 31st December, 2024.  
3.5 The Committee appreciates the 10-step CPGRAMS reforms process that was 
adopted by DARPG for improving the quality of grievance disposal and reducing the 
time lines. The Committee commends the Department for faster rate of disposal of 
grievances by reducing the average disposal time from 32 days in 2021 to 16 days in 
May 2023.  To further improve the disposal rate, the Committee recommends the 
Department to integrate state portals with CPGRAMS, standardize naming 
conventions, and establish a feedback mechanism to monitor and enhance the quality of 
grievance redressal.

II. PENSION ADALATS 
3.39 This Department had started the unique experiment of holding Pension Adalats to 
resolve chronic grievances of Central Govt. Pensioners, falling within the four walls of extant 
policy. These Adalats, as the term might sound, do not have any judicial or quasi-judicial 
powers but have proved a highly successful mode of grievance resolution. All the stake-
holders of a particular grievance are invited on a single table viz. the concerned 
Ministry/Department, the Pay & Accounts Officer of the concerned Department, the CPAO 
(Central Pension Accounts Officer, Ministry of Finance), the concerned Bank and the 
Pensioner or his/her representative and they resolve the case across the table. 
3.40 Under this system, all stakeholders for a particular grievance - viz. concerned 
Ministry/Department,  Pay & Accounts Office, Central Pension Accounts Office, the 
concerned Bank and the Pensioner or his/her representative - are invited on a common 
platform. The issue is discussed among all the stakeholders and resolved across the table. In 
cases where additional information is required, a fixed time is given to decide the matter and 
the unresolved grievances are monitored by DoPPW on regular basis. On a given date, 
DoPPW arranges Pension adalat for chronic grievances in the CPENGRAMS, covering 
various Ministries/Departments. Other Ministries / Departments are advised to hold pension 
adalats simultaneously for their pensioners, in different parts of the country. Till date Eleven 
Pension Adalats have been conducted by DoP&PW and out of  25,224  cases taken up in the 
Pension Adalats, 18,005 grievances were resolved (71.38%) by the different 
Ministries/Departments/ Organisation which participated in this initiative.

V. BHAVISHYA 
3.47 Implementation of Bhavishya in all Ministries/Departments; governed by 
CCS(Pension) Rules, 1972/CCS(Pension) Rules, 2021-DOPPW introduced and fine-tuned 
an online pension processing software called ‘Bhavishya’ for all central government Civil 
Ministries/Departments. This software brought on a common platform, all the stakeholders 
who were till date decentralized. The involved integration of PFMS (Public Finance 
Management System) of Controller General of Accounts and PARAS (Pension 
Authorization, Retrieval and Accounting System) of Central Pension Accounts Office under 
the Ministry of Finance. The system laid down a common methodology incorporating all the 
rules and regulations of CCS Pension Rules, 1972/ CCS Pension Rules, 2021 and automatic 
calculation of pension. This system does automatic pension processing of dues and therefore 
eliminates manual intervention ensuring transparency in the system. The system on its own 
keeps throwing up alerts to the pensioner as well as to the stakeholder for meeting the 
deadlines prescribed for various stages of Pension processing. At present, 99 
Ministries/Departments, 1033 Offices and 9168 DDOs are on Board and 268290 e-PPOs 
have been issued till 24.01.2025.

disposed over the years. In 2021-22, 66,736 cases were received with a 95.26% disposal 
rate, which declined to 91.8% in 2022-23 despite a lower number of cases received 
(58,964). In 2023-24, cases received surged to 94,108, but the disposal rate slightly 
dropped to 90.88%. The trend continued in 2024-25 (till 27.01.25) with a sharp rise in 
cases (1,05,058) and a further dip in disposal rate (87.1%), indicating a growing backlog 
and potential challenges in handling the increased workload efficiently.  Therefore, the 
Committee recommends the Department to improve its efficiency, by enhancing its staff 
strength and adopting automation, and streamline workflows. The Committee also feels 
that coordination and leveraging digital tools will ensure timely resolution and prevent 
backlog amidst rising volumes of cases. 
(Para 3.32)

15. To alleviate the burden on pensioners regarding the submission of annual life 
certificates, the Committee recommends the Department to promote the use of the 
Aadhaar-based Digital Life Certificate (DLC) system, which will enable online 
submissions without their physical presence. Furthermore, life certificates attested by 
authorized officials of Indian Embassies, High Commissions, or Consulates may be 
allowed for submission for pensioners, residing abroad eliminating their need for 
travelling to India solely for this purpose. 
(Para 3.34) 
 
16. The Committee observes that rate of grievances have increased drastically, 
particularly in the Ministry of Defence (MoD) [72%], which remains a major concern 
for pensioners of PCDA, Pension Allahabad and Department of Ex-Servicemen 
Welfare, indicating persistent issues in pension processing. Further the trends in other 
ministries like Railways, Financial Services, and Posts shows a decline, which means 
that MOD requires targeted intervention rather than general reforms.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends the Department to establish a specialized grievance cell with 
representatives from MOD, PCDA, and Ex-Servicemen Welfare to identify key 
bottlenecks and expedite timely resolution of grievances.    
(Para 3.36) 
 
17. The Committee appreciates the Department for holding Pension Adalats every 
year since 2017. The Committee is of the view that, while the Nationwide Pension 
Adalats have played a crucial role in grievance redressal, the resolution rate of 71.38% 
indicates that nearly 29% of cases remain unresolved, which raises concerns about 
efficiency. The Committee therefore, desires that efforts may be made to address the 
unresolved cases by constantly pursuing them with the concerned Departments. 
(Para 3.42) 
 
 
SOME GENERAL RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS:- 
18. Sankalp – A number of pensioners feels the loss of a daily routine as well as of 
feeling if not being wanted.  To alleviate this condition and keep them busy, physically, 
mentally and to remain relevant in society it will be a great help that the Department 
opens CSOI branches in NCR to start with immediately and till such a time this facility 
comes up the Department may take up with Haryana and UP Govt. to allow 
membership of their existing clubs to the pensioners who do not hold CSOI membership 
as on date.  This facility will go a long way in improving the quality of life to remain 
physically and mentally agile. 
(Para 3.53) 
 
  

 48 
19. CGHS – A large number of Pensioners are availing CGHS facilities, as a welfare 
measure the pensioners may be allowed to avail private hospitals facilities without the 
necessity of referral slip from the dispensaries.  The Committee feels this will reduce a 
lot of mental and physical pressure on pensioners and the matter may be taken up with 
the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare to issue necessary instructions in this regard. 
(Para 3.54) 
 
20. Pension – The Committee observes that many a times the disbursing banks take 
away money unilaterally in the name of over disbursal, without even informing the 
pensioners and that too in one go.  Since pension is meant for sustenance such a money 
lender behaviour or a coercive measure of taking funds on its own by the bank goes to 
show a complete disrespect to the system.  The Committee feels that the banks should be 
sensitized about it and this sort of exercise should be stopped immediately. 
(Para 3.55) 
 
 
21. The Committee appreciates the Department of Pension and Pensioners Welfare 
for its overall performance and hopes that it will continue good work to bring about 
'ease of living' for pensioners, especially for super senior citizens and family pensioners.
