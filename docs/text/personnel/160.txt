Wings of Department of Personnel and Training: 
1.4 Various wings of DoPT are as under: 
(i) Establishment Officer's (EO) Wing - The Establishment Officer's Wing deals 
with matters relating to senior appointments under the Government of India 
requiring approval of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC). 
(ii) Services & Vigilance (S&V) Wing - All matters relating to Administrative 
Vigilance, including the administration of the Central Vigilance Commission 
(CVC), and issues concerning the All India Services are handled by the 
Services & Vigilance Wing. The Wing deals with cadre management of IAS 
officers, service allocation of candidates recommended by the UPSC through 
the Civil Services Examination, and the regulation and administration of the 
All India Services Rules. 
(iii) Personnel Policy Wing - The Personnel Policy Wing handles all matters 
relating to personnel policies concerning Central Government servants other 
than All India Service officers. These include issues relating to service 
conditions and rules, cadre review, recruitment rules, reservation in services, 
pay, leave and allowances, and the Joint Consultative Machinery for Central 
Government employees. The Wing also deals with matters relating to UPSC, 
SSC and PESB, States Reorganisation, retraining and redeployment of 
surplus staff, and lateral entry in Government. 
(iv) International Cooperation & Central Secretariat Wing - The International 
Cooperation & Central Secretariat Wing deals with matters relating to cadre 
management of the Central Secretariat Service (CSS), Central Secretariat 
Stenographers’ Service (CSSS) and the Central Secretariat Clerical Service 
(CSCS). 
(v) Administrative Tribunals & Administration Wing -This Wing deals with 
service matters of employees posted in the Department, general 
administration and internal coordination within the Department. It also 
handles matters relating to the Citizens’ and Client Charter, budget matters of 
the Ministry and its attached and subordinate offices, parliamentary matters, 
and issues relating to Administrative Tribunals. In addition, the Wing deals 
with matters relating to the Right to Information Act, 2005 and the Central 
Information Commission, welfare of Central Government employees and 
initiatives such as Mission Recruitment. 

 
3 
(vi) Training Wing - The Training Wing deals with matters relating to the 
formulation and implementation of the National Training Policy and 
coordination of training policy for the All India and Central Services. It also 
handles matters relating to the National Programme for Civil Services 
Capacity Building (Mission Karmayogi), which was approved by the Cabinet 
on 2 September 2020. The Wing formulates Cadre Training Plans for officers 
of the Central Secretariat Service (CSS) and Central Secretariat 
Stenographers Service (CSSS) and manages programmes such as the 
Assistant Secretary Programme. The online training platform i-GOT 
(Integrated Government Online Training) was launched on 20 December 
2018 to support capacity building initiatives. 
(vii) e-HRMS Wing - The e-HRMS Wing manages the eHRMS 2.0 portal under 
Mission Karmayogi to streamline HR operations, ensure compliance and 
optimise resource allocation. The portal functions as a service delivery 
platform providing employee services such as leave management, TA/DA, 
bill reimbursements, LTC, tour approvals, IPR filing, transfer and posting 
requests, pay slips, GPF services, NOCs for higher studies, requests for 
promotions, deputations and extensions, as well as access to Government 
orders and a comprehensive e-Service Record. The system is integrated with 
platforms such as iGOT, SUPREMO, PFMS, SPARROW, Bhavishya and 
CGHS to enable an end-to-end Human Resource Management System. The 
portal also generates a functional organogram mapping Central Government 
institutions and personnel across ministries, departments, statutory bodies, 
PSUs and other entities.

Major responsibilities/functions of the Department of Personnel and Training: 
1.6 The following are major responsibilities/functions of DoPT: 
 
Personnel Policies  
1.7 The Department is responsible for framing rules and regulations governing service 
conditions including recruitment rules, promotions and seniority, Flexible Complementing 
Scheme, Leave Travel Concession, Deputation and Leave of employees. Personnel are 
recruited for the Central Government by the Union Public Service Commission through 
competitive examinations conducted by them for appointments to higher civil services and 
through the Staff Selection Commission for non-gazetted staff in Group ‘B’ & ‘C’ categories. 
 
Reservation 
1.8 In order to achieve the objective of upliftment and welfare of the Scheduled Castes 
and Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes and Persons with Disabilities and 
Economically Weaker Sections (EWS); the Department frames policies to provide 
reservation to these groups in various Central Government services and for monitoring its 
implementation.  
 
Cadre Management 
1.9  This Department is responsible for the management of the cadres of All India 
Services (AIS) (IAS, IPS, and IFoS) and all three Secretariat Services namely Central 
Secretariat Services (CSS), Central Secretariat Stenographers’ Services (CSSS) and Central 
Secretariat Clerical Services (CSCS). In addition, this Department frames and revises Rules 
and Regulations regarding conditions of the All India Services, such as Indian Police Service 
(IPS) and Indian Forest Service (IFoS), in consultation with the Ministry of Home Affairs 
and Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change respectively. 
 
Senior Appointments under the Government of India 
1.10  The Department deals with appointments at senior level and personnel policies of the 
Government of India. All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, 
which require the approval of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC), are 
processed by the Department. These include board-level appointments to Central Public 
Sector Undertakings and appointments under the Central Staffing Scheme for posts of Joint 
Secretary, Director, and Deputy Secretary in Ministries /Departments. In addition, all 
appointments by promotion, which require the approval of the ACC, are also processed by 
the Department.  

 
11 
Training Policy and Programmes 
1.11  The Department is the nodal authority to impart training to the government 
functionaries. The Training Wing of the Department formulates policies and implements its 
training programmes by identifying areas of training, designing training programmes, 
development of trainers and training capabilities and administering policies in training. Major 
training activities undertaken during the year are (i) In-service training of AIS/CSS/CSSS 
officers (ii) Mid-Career Training of IAS Officers (iii) Post Graduate Programmes in Public 
Policy/Sustainable Development/Management (iv) State Category Training Programme 
(SCTP); (v) Trainer Development Programme (TDP); (vi) Comprehensive Online Modified 
Modules for Induction Training (COMMIT); (vii) Augmentation of the Capacity of Training 
Institutions (ACTI); (viii) Integrated Government Online Training Programme (i-GOT); and 
(ix) National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building (NPCSCB)– Mission 
Karmayogi. Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), 
Mussourie, Uttarakhand and Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM), New 
Delhi are two premier training institutions, attached to this Department.  
 
Mission Karmayogi & e-HRMS 
1.12 National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building (NPCSCB) – Mission 
Karmayogi is being implemented with the goal of systematically imparting behavioral and 
functional competencies needed by a government official to best discharge their assigned 
roles and aims to effect a signal shift in competency driven training and Human Resource 
(HR) management of officials, by transitioning from a ‘rule-based’ system to a ‘role-based’ 
system of capacity building.  
1.13  Through the adoption of modern technological tools such as a digital platform - the 
integrated Government online Training (i-GoT) portal - https://igotkarmayogi.gov.in/ and 
with effective harnessing of artificial Intelligence, machine learning and data analytics, 
Mission Karmayogi endeavors to break silos in capacity development and democratize 
knowledge on an equitable basis across civil services, by bringing easy access to world class 
continuous learning opportunities for all government officials. The NPCSCB facilitates data-
driven decisions for training and personnel management through the electronic Human 
Resources Management System (e-HRMS), which has now been revamped as e-HRMS2.0 
and also integrated with the i-GOT portal.  
 
Administrative Vigilance Division 
1.14  The Department is the nodal agency responsible for the formulation and 
implementation of vigilance and anti-corruption policies of the Government. Administrative 

 
12 
Vigilance Wing in the Department oversees government programme for maintenance of 
discipline and eradication of corruption from public service. The Central Vigilance 
Commission (CVC) advises the Central Government on all vigilance matters. The jurisdiction 
of the Commission extends to all the organisations to which the executive powers of the 
Union of India extend. To deal with international cooperation on these aspects an 
International Cooperation Cell was created in this Department.  
 
Joint Consultative Machinery 
1.15 The Government provides for a Joint Consultative Machinery for joint consultation at 
three levels between the Central Government and its employees for promoting harmonious 
relations and securing the optimum level of co-operation between the Central Government 
and its employees in matters of common concern. The objective is to increase the efficiency 
of public services along with the well-being of the employees. The three tiers are:  
(a)  National Council – at the apex level, functioning under the Department of Personnel 
and Training.  
(b) Department Councils – functioning at the level of the Ministries / Departments.  
(c) Office Councils – functioning at offices/organisations under various Ministries / 
Departments.  
 
Administrative Tribunal 
1.16  In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice delivery system to the employees 
who feel aggrieved by Government decisions, the Government set up the Central 
Administrative Tribunal (CAT) in 1985, which now deals with all cases relating to service 
matters. The CAT has 19 regular benches, 17 of which operate at the Principal seats of High 
Courts and the remaining two are at Jaipur and Lucknow. 
 
Staff Welfare 
1.17 The Central Government being the largest single employer in the country discharges its 
responsibility for looking after the welfare of employees through various welfare measures. 
The Department also extends support to various staff welfare measures. The DoPT is the 
nodal Department for registered societies set up for the welfare of the Government employees 
and their families. In addition, the Department lays down policies for Departmental Canteens 
and supports the Resident Welfare Associations.

mechanism where the Central Information Commission, being the apex body at the Centre, 
facilitates its citizenry in accessing information in a time-bound, hassle-free and affordable 
manner.

2.19 The case of the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) warrants specific attention. 
Despite receiving additional funds at the Revised Estimates 2025-26 stage (from ₹515.15 
crore (BE) to ₹548.50 crore (RE)), only 50.45 per cent of the funds have been utilized as 
on 31 January 2026, leaving an unspent balance of ₹271.79 crore. Furthermore, for 
2026-27, SSC projected a substantially higher outlay of ₹719.13 crore, whereas the 
approved Budget Estimate stands at ₹525.20 crore (73.04 per cent of projection).  
2.20 The Committee also notes significant compression of Budget Estimates in 2026-27 in 
certain other organizations (Table X). For instance, the Lokpal received only 20.24 per cent 
of its projected outlay, while the Institute of Secretariat Training and Management and 
LBSNAA received 48.47 per cent and 52.68 per cent respectively of their projected 
requirements.

2.21 The Committee believes that projections must be firmly aligned with the 
expenditure trends and the ability of the organization to utilize the funds within the 
financial year. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should 
strengthen quarterly expenditure monitoring system and ensure strict adherence to the 
Ministry of Finance guidelines regarding even pacing of expenditure. As reflected in 
Table IX, the average utilization across organizations stands at 73.45 per cent as on 31 
January 2026, with certain organizations such as the Staff Selection Commission (50.45 
per cent) and the Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (56.00 per cent) 
exhibiting particularly low expenditure levels. At the same time, at the Budget 
Estimates stage for 2026-27, organizations such as Lokpal received only 20.24 per cent 
of their projected outlay, while ISTM and LBSNAA received 48.47 per cent and 52.68 
per cent respectively (Table X). The Committee is of the view that closer alignment 
between projections, Revised Estimates and actual utilization is essential. Upward 
revisions at the Revised Estimates stage without proportionate expenditure, as seen in 
the case of SSC, may weaken fiscal credibility and adversely impact future allocation 
decisions.

3.2 The Committee notes that at the national level, nearly one-fifth of the sanctioned IAS 
posts remain vacant, which is a matter of serious concern. An examination of the cadre-wise 
distribution of vacancies reveals that the shortage is uneven and disproportionately severe in 
certain States. While the national average shortage is 18.90 per cent, several cadres are 
functioning well above this level. Nagaland is facing the most acute shortage at 43.62 per 
cent, followed by Kerala (32.03 per cent) and Manipur (30.43 per cent). Tripura (27.45 per 
cent), Odisha (25.40 per cent), AGMUT (25.09 per cent) and Sikkim (25.00 per cent) are also 
operating with one-fourth or more of their sanctioned strength vacant. 
3.3 The Committee is particularly concerned that a number of North-Eastern and smaller 
cadres are experiencing disproportionately high shortages in percentage terms. Given their 
strategic location, developmental priorities and administrative sensitivities, such persistent 
vacancies may adversely affect governance delivery, supervision of field administration and 
effective implementation of Central and State schemes in these areas. 
3.4 Moreover, even larger and administratively significant States are not insulated from 
this challenge. West Bengal (19.84 per cent), Rajasthan (19.28 per cent), Haryana (20.00 per 
cent) and Jharkhand (20.98 per cent) are functioning with nearly one-fifth of their sanctioned 
strength vacant. The AGMUT cadre, servicing multiple Union Territories including the 
National Capital Territory of Delhi, has a deficit exceeding 25 per cent, which raises concerns 
regarding administrative capacity in centrally governed regions. The Committee further notes 
that no cadre is currently operating at full sanctioned strength, indicating that the issue of 
shortage is systemic rather than isolated. 
3.5 The Committee is of the view that persistent shortage continues to adversely 
affect administrative capacity at the Centre and in the States, particularly at the field-
level positions where timely decision-making and policy implementation are critical. 

 
46 
3.6 The Committee, therefore, urges the Department to immediately prioritize filling 
the 25% vacancies in the AGMUT cadre, considering its unique administrative spread 
across multiple Union Territories and the National Capital Territory. Moreover, the 
Committee feels that there is a need to formulate a special filling strategy for North-
Eastern and smaller cadres such as Nagaland, Manipur, Tripura and Sikkim, where 
percentage shortages are disproportionately high.   
3.7 While the Department has stated that retirement, resignation and deputation are 
ongoing dynamic processes being dealt with as per extant rules and guidelines, the 
Committee is of the view that cadre planning cannot be treated as a purely reactive exercise 
and requires forward-looking manpower forecasting. The Committee also notes that Cadre 
Review of IAS cadres is undertaken under Rule 4(2) of the IAS (Cadre) Rules, 1954, which 
inter-alia states: 
The Central Government shall, ordinarily at the interval of every five years, re-examine 
the strength and composition of each such Cadre in consultation with the state 
Government or the State Governments concerned and may make such alternations 
therein as it deems fit. 
3.8 However, the persistence of significant shortages across cadres indicates that periodic 
review alone may not be sufficient unless accompanied by timely implementation, vacancy 
forecasting, and intake adjustments. 
3.9 It is pertinent to highlight that this Committee has been considering the serious issue 
of shortage of IAS officers for a long time now. The Committee recalls that in its 126th 
Report (Para 3.3), it had specifically recommended an increase in the annual intake of Direct 
Recruit IAS officers based on the assessment of cadre strength requirements. Further, in its 
145th Report (para 3.3), the Committee had reiterated its concern regarding the significant 
shortage and had urged the Department to study and implement the recommendations of the 
Committee chaired by Shri C. Chandramouli at the earliest. 
3.10 Against this backdrop, the Committee notes with concern that despite repeated 
assurances that the Chandramouli Committee’s report is under consideration in the 
Department, no concrete decision, roadmap, or timeline has been communicated to the 
Committee so far. The absence of any time-bound goals with measurable progress in 
this regard reflects a lack of urgency in addressing a structural issue that has direct 
implications for governance, service delivery, and institutional effectiveness.

3.11 The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendations made in the

Empanelment of Joint Secretaries 
3.12 The Committee notes that empanelment of Indian Administrative Service (IAS) 
officers for appointment at the level of Joint Secretary in the Government of India is 
undertaken through a structured process conducted by the Department of Personnel & 
Training. The process involves evaluation of officers based on their service records, Annual 
Performance Appraisal Reports (APARs), vigilance status and overall career profile. 
3.13 The Committee appreciates that over the past several years, the empanelment process 
for IAS officers has incorporated a 360-degree assessment mechanism, under which multi-
source feedback is obtained from senior officers, peers and other stakeholders who have 
worked with the officer concerned. This process is intended to supplement formal 
performance records with qualitative inputs regarding leadership qualities, domain expertise, 
integrity, decision-making capacity and overall suitability for senior policy-making roles in 
the Government of India. 
3.14 The Committee is of the view that the principles underlying the 360-degree 
assessment mechanism, namely holistic evaluation, multi-source feedback and qualitative 
assessment of leadership attributes, are relevant not only for IAS officers but also for officers 
from other services considered for empanelment as Joint Secretaries and other higher posts in 
the Government of India. 
3.15 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department examine the 
feasibility of institutionalising a structured 360-degree review mechanism, for 
empanelment of Joint Secretary and other higher posts drawn from services other than 
the IAS. Such an approach would promote parity in evaluation standards across 
services, strengthen merit-based selection at senior levels, and enhance confidence in the 
objectivity and robustness of the empanelment process.

Personnel Well-being and Mental Health 
3.16 The Committee notes that civil servants today operate in an increasingly complex 
administrative environment marked by expanding welfare schemes, technological transitions, 
heightened public scrutiny, judicial oversight and continuous crisis-response responsibilities. 
As none of the cadres are operating at their full sanctioned strength, officers are frequently 
entrusted with additional charge responsibilities, extended working hours and compressed 
tenures. As such, sustained administrative overload may adversely affect officer well-being, 
decision-making quality and institutional continuity. 
3.17 The Committee is of the view that personnel management must extend beyond 
recruitment and cadre strength to encompass the holistic well-being and sustainability 
of the administrative workforce. The Committee recommends that the Department of 
Personnel & Training, in consultation with cadre controlling authorities and State 
Governments, develop a structured framework for officer well-being, including 
institutionalized mental health/counseling support mechanisms, periodic monitoring of 
prolonged additional charge assignments, reasonable tenure stability as against 
frequent transfers, integration of stress-management modules under Mission 
Karmayogi, and consider annual well-being surveys to identify systemic stress factors. 
The Committee believes that proactive attention to officer well-being will enhance 
administrative effectiveness, reduce burnout risks and contribute to better governance 
outcomes. 
Use of Artificial Intelligence in Public Administration 
3.18 The Committee notes with appreciation the various digital governance initiatives 
undertaken by the Department, particularly the rollout of e-HRMS 2.0 as a comprehensive 
lifecycle management platform for Government employees, integration with iGOT-
Karmayogi, Bhavishya and PFMS. Further, the Committee is pleased to see the deployment 
of AI-enabled dashboards, AI-based deputation alerts, AI-assisted recruitment rule 
generation, and the introduction of AI-enabled tools at LBSNAA and other training 
institutions.  
3.19 The Committee holds the view that these initiatives represent an important shift 
towards data-driven and technology-enabled personnel administration. However, given the 
scale and sensitivity of Government of India operations, there remains significant scope to 
institutionalise and mainstream the use of Artificial Intelligence in day-today functioning of 
the Government, albeit in a structured and accountable manner. 

 
49 
3.20 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department, in coordination 
with relevant Ministries and Departments, take the lead in developing a comprehensive 
Government-wide framework for the ethical, secure and accountable use of Artificial 
Intelligence in public administration. Such a framework should clearly define 
safeguards for data protection and confidentiality, ensure proper record-keeping of AI-
assisted actions, and retain final decision-making authority with designated human 
officers. This is particularly important as AI tools across Ministries may handle varied 
and sensitive categories of data, including personnel, financial, regulatory and citizen-
related information.  
3.21 The Committee further recommends that the use of AI tools for official purposes 
be governed through centrally approved enterprise-level agreements with service 
providers, including Large Language Model (LLM) platforms, so as to ensure 
confidentiality of Government data, compliance with national data protection and 
security standards, and clear contractual safeguards regarding data storage, processing 
and access.  
3.22 The Committee further recommends that AI-enabled systems adopted by 
Ministries adhere to robust information security protocols and confidentiality 
safeguards, particularly in handling service records, vigilance matters, performance 
data, financial transactions and other sensitive government information. 
3.23 The Committee also emphasizes the need to expand AI literacy and responsible-
use training across all levels of government, particularly for dealing hands and lower-
level staff who routinely process official data, so that technological adoption is 
accompanied by awareness of ethical boundaries, confidentiality obligations and cyber-
security safeguards.

Chapter 4 
RECRUITMENT INSTITUTIONS 
 
A. Union Public Service Commission 
4.0 Union Public Service Commission makes recruitment for All India Services, Group 
‘A’ Central Civil Services/posts, and Group ‘B’ Gazetted posts in Ministries/Departments of 
the Central Government. The Commission also conducts examination for recruitment of 
Commissioned officers in the Defense forces. Some Union Territories (UTs) also avail the 
services of the Union Public Service Commission for recruitment to the posts under the UT. 
4.1 The functions of the Commission are specified in Article 320 of the Constitution. By 
the exercise of powers conferred by the proviso to Article 320 (3) of the Constitution, the 
President has made the UPSC (Exemption from Consultation) Regulations, 1958 as amended 
from time to time, in respect of the All India Services and also in respect ofother services and 
posts in connection with the affairs of the Union specifying the matters in which it shall not 
be necessary for the UPSC to be consulted. 
Civil Services Examination Reforms 
4.2 The Committee, in its 126th and 145th Reports had recommended that UPSC may 
explore and take steps to release the answer key immediately after the preliminary 
examination and provide candidates with an opportunity to raise objections before proceeding 
further. The Commission had informed this Committee that the matter of releasing answer 
keys after completion of Preliminary Examinations, i.e., before the final result of the 
respective Examinations is presently sub-judice in W.P.(C)No.118/2024 filed by Shri 
Himanshu Kumar & Ors Vs UPSC &Ors, pertaining to Civil Services Examination, before 
the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India.  
4.3 It has been further informed that pursuant to the affidavit filed by the Union Public 
Service Commission before the Hon’ble Supreme Court, the matter was disposed of on 
14.10.2025 with a direction to formulate necessary guidelines for implementation of the 
decision regarding publication of provisional answer keys by UPSC. The Committee 
appreciates that UPSC has since framed the requisite guidelines and has stated that disclosure 
of the provisional answer key shall be implemented from the Civil Services (Preliminary) 
Examination, 2026 onwards. 
  

 
51 
4.4 The Committee welcomes this development as a step towards enhancing 
transparency, fairness and candidate confidence in the examination process. However, 
the Committee desires to be apprised of the detailed modalities framed by the 
Commission, pertaining to the timeline for publication of the provisional answer key 
after the Preliminary Examination; the mechanism and time window for submission of 
objections by candidates; and the process for examination and disposal of such 
objections. The Committee may also be informed whether the final answer key along 
with a reasoned clarification on accepted/rejected objections will also be placed in the 
public domain.  
4.5 During the course of the deliberations, the Committee also discussed the structure of 
the Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination, particularly the Civil Services Aptitude Test 
(CSAT) paper. The Committee noted that the quantitative and analytical orientation of the 
CSAT paper may pose challenges for candidates from non-science and non-quantitative 
academic backgrounds, especially those from remote and under-served regions with limited 
access to specialized preparatory support. The Committee believes that the objective of the 
Civil Services Examination has historically been to attract talent from diverse academic 
disciplines, and feels that the design and level of the CSAT paper may warrant review to 
ensure a level-playing field.  
4.6 The Committee recognizes the importance of assessing analytical ability, 
comprehension and decision-making skills in prospective civil servants. At the same 
time, the Committee is of the view that the Preliminary Examination should ensure a 
level-playing field for candidates from varied academic streams. Although the CSAT 
paper is qualifying in nature, the Committee recommends that the Union Public Service 
Commission undertake a comprehensive review to rationalize the CSAT component, 
including its syllabus and level of difficulty, so as to assess its impact across different 
academic backgrounds. The review may be supported by empirical analysis of 
candidate performance patterns to ensure that the examination continues to uphold 
principles of fairness, inclusivity and equal opportunity. 
4.7 The Committee also discussed issues relating to the alleged misuse of disability 
(PwBD/Divyang) certificates in recruitment processes. The Committee also noted that similar 
concerns have also been raised regarding the authenticity of certificates issued under the 
Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) category. The Committee is of the view that if 
fraudulent certificates are used to secure appointments, it not only affects the credibility of 

 
52 
the recruitment system but also results in denial of opportunities to genuinely eligible 
candidates. 
4.8 The Committee considers that any misuse of PwBD or EWS certificates in 
recruitment is a matter of serious concern, as it undermines the principles of equity, 
transparency and fairness that underpin public employment. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Department of Personnel and Training, in consultation with 
UPSC and other recruiting agencies, strengthen verification mechanisms for PwBD and 
EWS certificates at appropriate stages of the recruitment process. The Committee 
further recommends that technology-enabled verification systems, including advanced 
digital scrutiny tools where feasible, be explored to detect forged or manipulated 
certificates in a timely manner. Such measures may include digital validation, 
systematic cross-verification with issuing authorities, and close coordination with the 
State Governments and competent certification bodies. 
Annual Reports of the Union Public Service Commission 
4.9 The Committee had sought information regarding the present status and timeline for 
publication of the Annual Reports of the Union Public Service Commission, which are 
presently available in the public domain only up to the year 2022-23. In response thereto, the 
Commission has stated that its Annual Reports, along with the Explanatory Memoranda 
explaining cases where its advice was not accepted and the reasons therefor, are required to 
be laid before each House of Parliament in terms of Article 323(1) of the Constitution of 
India prior to being placed in the public domain. It has further been informed that the Annual 
Reports for the years 2023-24 and 2024-25 are presently under various stages of 
finalisation/approval and will be laid before Parliament upon completion of the requisite 
procedures. 
4.10 The Committee notes the position explained by the Commission. While 
recognising the constitutional requirement of laying the Reports before Parliament, the 
Committee emphasises that timely finalisation and presentation of Annual Reports is 
essential for ensuring transparency and accountability. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Commission adhere to a definite timeline for preparation and 
laying of its Annual Reports and avoid undue delays in future.

Impact Assessment  
4.11 The Committee, in its 145th Report, had suggested that the recommendations of the 
Baswan Committee on Civil Services Examination reforms be examined and implemented in 
a time-bound manner, with a clear roadmap indicating short-term and long-term measures. In 
its 159th Report (Action Taken Report) also, the Committee had reiterated its concern 
regarding delay in implementation and had emphasized the need for a structured 
implementation plan with defined timelines. 
4.12 In its response to the 159th Report (Action Taken Report), the Commission has stated

and with wide ramification on the scheme of examination for Civil Services and it does not

4.13 Further, in relation to the Baswan Committee’s recommendations, this Committee

which is presently under examination in the Department. 
4.14 The Committee notes that the matter remains under examination and that no 
specific timeline has yet been indicated for implementation of the reforms. While 
recognizing that the recommendations have broad policy implications, the Committee 
opines that prolonged consideration without indicative timelines may delay necessary 
reforms in a critical national recruitment process. The Committee, therefore, reiterates 
its earlier recommendation that a structured and phased implementation roadmap be 
prepared, identifying reforms that may be taken up in the short term and those 
requiring wider consultation, and that the Committee be apprised of the progress made 
in this regard.

New Initiatives 
4.15 PRATIBHA SETU, earlier known as the Public Disclosure Scheme (PDS), is an 
initiative of the Union Public Service Commission to facilitate employment opportunities for 
willing, non-recommended candidates of UPSC examinations by connecting them with 
registered organisations. The Commission has informed that 292 organizations are currently 
registered on the portal (as against 159 in the previous year), and data of over 13,000 willing 
candidates is available. In recent years, organizations such as ESIC, MECL and MY Bharat 
have selected candidates through the portal. The Scheme aims to leverage the pool of 
screened candidates and enhance their placement opportunities beyond the final 
recommended list of UPSC. 
4.16 The Committee is pleased to note that 292 organizations have registered on the 
PRATIBHA SETU Portal. However, it also observes that employers are not regularly 
updating the status of selected candidates on the portal. While welcoming the outreach 
efforts undertaken by UPSC, the Committee recommends that a structured monitoring 
mechanism be put in place to ensure regular updating by registered organizations and 
to assess the actual placement outcomes under the Scheme. The Committee further 
recommends that periodic feedback be obtained from participating organizations as 
well as candidates to evaluate the effectiveness and impact of the initiative. An annual 
performance review of the Portal, including data on registrations, placements and 
feedback received, may also be incorporated in the Commission’s Annual Report. 
B. Staff Selection Commission 
4.17 Staff Selection Commission is one of the largest recruiting agencies in India in terms 
of number of candidates who apply for various posts in the Central Government. Itis 
mandated to make recruitment to Group ‘B’ (Non-Gazetted) and Group ‘C’ (Non-Technical) 
Posts in the Government of India.  
4.18 The Commission had expressed that one of the key operational challenges faced by 
them is the non-utilization of all reported vacancies, as some selected candidates do not 
report for document verification or subsequent joining. This results in avoidable carry-
forward of vacancies to subsequent recruitment cycles, leading to delays in filling sanctioned 
posts and affecting manpower availability in Ministries/Departments. The Committee notes 
that the Commission is contemplating introduction of a sliding mechanism/counselling 
system to address this issue. 

 
55 
4.19 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Staff Selection Commission 
expeditiously finalize and implement a structured sliding mechanism /counseling system 
with clearly defined timelines so that reported vacancies are optimally utilized within 
the same recruitment cycle. Moreover, the Committee recommends that SSC examine 
the feasibility of maintaining a reserve/waiting list on the lines of the Union Public 
Service Commission (UPSC), so that vacancies arising due to non-joining or withdrawal 
can be promptly filled without waiting for the next examination cycle. 
4.20 Furthermore, the Committee was informed that recruitment through the Staff 
Selection Commission is not geographically balanced. A disproportionately high number of 
selected candidates appear to originate from certain regions, particularly the NCR and 
Northern States, while participation and selection from other regions, especially the North-
Eastern States, Aspirational Districts and certain parts of Southern India, appear 
comparatively limited. 
4.21 The Committee feels that SSC being a premier national recruiting agency, must 
ensure that its recruitment processes do not, even inadvertently, lead to regional 
concentration in recruitment outcomes. The Commission must conduct an analytical 
study to examine whether structural factors, such as awareness levels, access to 
coaching, digital infrastructure, language barriers, or examination centre distribution, 
are contributing to regional disparities. The Committee further recommends to 
formulate a targeted outreach strategy for underrepresented regions, particularly the 
North-East, tribal districts, rural areas and Aspirational Districts, including awareness 
campaigns in local languages, and increasing presence of examination centres in remote 
areas. 
C. National Recruitment Agency 
4.22 National Recruitment Agency (NRA) was set up as an autonomous body in 2020 to 
conduct a Common Eligibility Test (CET) to shortlist candidates for vacancies of Group ‘B’ 
Non-Gazetted posts, Group ‘B’ Gazetted posts, which are exempted from consultation with 
the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC); Group ‘C’ posts in the Government and 
equivalent posts (where no such classifications exist) through a computer-based Tier-I 
examination.  
4.23 NRA will conduct a separate CET for the three levels viz., graduate, higher secondary 
(12th pass) and the matriculate (10th pass) candidates for those non-technical posts to which 
recruitment is carried out by the Staff Selection Commission (SSC), the Railway Recruitment 

 
56 
Boards (RRBs) and by the Institute of Banking Personnel Selection (IBPS). The score 
obtained by candidates in CET will be shared with the SSC, RRB & IBPS for their next level 
(Tier-II onwards) examinations for selection to various Government jobs. 
4.24 The Committee is pleased to see that the Unified One-Time Registration (UOTR) 
Portal is being developed by NIC under the guidance of NRA and is expected to be 
operational within three months, subject to completion of API integration with SSC, RRBs 
and IBPS and onboarding with UIDAI. The Committee further notes that, in the first phase, 
only three major Public Examination Authorities (PEAs), namely, SSC, RRBs and IBPS are 
being onboarded. 
4.25 The Committee, therefore, recommends that NRA adhere to the proposed three-
month timeline and furnish a clear implementation schedule indicating key milestones. 
Once SSC, RRBs and IBPS are on boarded, other Central Public Examination 
Authorities should be brought onto the UOTR platform in a time-bound and phased 
manner to ensure uniform adoption. The Committee also emphasizes that robust data 
security and audit safeguards must be ensured prior to full-scale rollout. 
4.26 In response to a query on the challenges faced by NRA in expanding the scope of the 
Common Eligibility Test (CET), the Committee has been informed that NRA has estimated 
that nearly 5 crore candidates may appear in a single cycle of the CET, with 1.5-2 crore

A. Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) 
6.0 The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is the premier investigating agency of the 
country entrusted with investigating cases of corruption, major economic offences, banking 
and financial frauds, and other serious conventional crimes. The Bureau also undertakes 
investigation of certain specialized and sensitive matters, including terrorist offences and 
cases of national importance. Cases are referred to the CBI by the Central Government, with 
the concurrence of the concerned State Governments where required, as well as by the 
Hon’ble High Courts and the Hon’ble Supreme Court. 
Human Resource Management 
6.1 The Committee notes that against a sanctioned strength of 7,300 posts, the in-position 
strength of CBI is 6,212, leaving a shortfall of 1,088 posts (14.90%). The rank-wise details of 
sanctioned strength, in-position strength and vacancies are placed at Annexure–I. While the 
Committee appreciates that the vacancy position has reduced from 20.57% in 2024 to 14.90% 
in 2025 and is projected to decline further, the Committee believes that a vacancy level of 
nearly fifteen per cent in a premier investigative agency remains a matter of serious concern.  
6.2 As per the information provided by the Department, the major reasons for vacancies 
are: 
i. CBI is not receiving sufficient & suitable nominations of officers from State Police 
which has traditionally been a major source of induction, particularly in the rank of 
Inspector & SPs; 
ii. Some candidates recommended by UPSC and SSC against the direct recruitment 
quota do not ultimately join the organization because of that there always remain 
some vacancies under Direct Recruitment Quota; 
iii. Delay in sending the relevant documents by lending departments after identifying the 
personnel by CBI for deputation; 
iv. Non identifying suitable / skilled personnel by the lending departments which results 
in non-selection of the personnel for induction even after scrutiny; 
v. There are vacancies in promotion quota because of non-availability of eligible 
officers. 

 
66 
6.3 The Committee notes that one of the major reasons for vacancies under the 
Direct Recruitment quota is that certain candidates recommended by UPSC and SSC 
do not ultimately join the organisation, resulting in persistent shortfalls. The Committee 
is of the view that such avoidable vacancies should not be allowed to spill over and affect 
operational efficiency. The Committee, therefore, recommends that a system of 
maintaining a reserve panel/waitlist, in consultation with the recruiting agencies, may 
be institutionalised so that vacancies arising due to this phenomenon can be filled in a 
time-bound manner without initiating a fresh recruitment cycle. 
6.4 The Committee further notes that as informed by the Department, there are 77 
vacancies out of 500 posts in the Sub-Inspector grade as on 31.12.2025. A high attrition rate 
has also been indicated in this cadre. Considering that Sub-Inspector is the feeder grade for 
supervisory positions and forms the backbone of investigation work, the Committee views 
this as a matter of serious concern. While the Committee acknowledges the earlier reforms in 
Recruitment Rules and introduction of the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination 
(LDCE) to improve career progression, it notes that some structural challenges in cadre 
management continue to persist. 
6.5 The Committee recommends that a detailed root cause analysis be undertaken to 
examine the reasons for attrition in the SI cadre, including issues relating to workload, 
career progression, working conditions, transfers, training, and inter-organisational 
mobility. Based on such analysis, appropriate corrective measures may be introduced to 
enhance retention and morale. 
6.6 The Committee emphasizes that sustained and coordinated efforts are necessary to 
reduce vacancies to minimal levels so that the operational efficiency, investigative capacity 
and institutional credibility of the CBI are not compromised.

CBI Courts 
6.10 During the deliberations, the Committee was apprised of the current practice about the 
posting of senior-level judges in CBI-designated Courts,which results in limited tenures due 
to their superannuation. Such short tenures, particularly at the senior level, may result in 
disruption of judicial continuity, loss of familiarity with voluminous records and technical 
evidence, and consequent delays in trial proceedings, particularly in complex corruption and 
economic offence matters. 
6.11 The Committee is of the view that specialized courts dealing with CBI cases 
require stability and judicial continuity to ensure timely adjudication and effective 
prosecution. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department, in 
consultation with the concerned High Courts, explore the feasibility of ensuring a 
reasonable and fixed tenure for judicial officers posted to CBI-designated Courts. The 
Committee further recommends that junior and mid-level judges may be posted to such 
Courts for an adequate duration so as to ensure continuity and development of domain 
expertise. 
Cybercrime 
6.12 The Committee notes with serious concern the growing menace of cybercrime, 
particularly cases where fraudsters impersonate officers of premier investigative agencies, 

 
68 
including the CBI and intimidate victims through threats of legal action, arrest or financial 
penalties, thereby coercing them to transfer money. The Committee observes that senior 
citizens are especially vulnerable to such tactics due to limited digital literacy, lack of 
familiarity with cyber fraud methods and heightened susceptibility to fear-based 
manipulation. Such impersonation not only causes financial and emotional distress to victims 
but also risks undermining public trust in investigative institutions. 
6.13 The Committee recommends that the CBI, in coordination with the Ministry of 
Home Affairs and State Governments, undertake targeted public awareness campaigns, 
particularly in regional languages and through platforms accessible to senior citizens, 
clarifying that the Bureau does not demand money or conduct investigations through

response mechanisms in coordination with banks and digital payment platforms to 
enable immediate freezing of suspected fraudulent transactions. Moreover, the 
Committee underscores the need for enhancing inter-agency coordination to dismantle 
organized cyber fraud networks impersonating public officials, and continuously 
upgrading the capacity of investigating officers in emerging cybercrime domains, 
including digital forensics and crypto currency-related offences.

Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) 
6.25 The Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) was established in 1985 to provide 
speedy and cost-effective redressal to government employees aggrieved by decisions relating 
to service matters. The Tribunal adjudicates service-related disputes that were earlier dealt 
with by courts up to and including the High Courts. At present, CAT functions through 19 
regular Benches across the country, including its Principal Bench at Delhi. 
Pendency of Cases Pending for More than Ten Years 
6.26 The Committee acknowledges that the number of cases pending for more than ten 
years has shown a gradual decline, decreasing from 1,394 cases as on 31.12.2023 to 1,321 
cases as on 31.12.2024 and further to 1,238 cases as on 31.12.2025. However, the number of 
such cases remains significant. The Tribunal has informed that the key constraints in disposal 
of such long-pending matters include the following: 
cases adjourned sine-die due to related matters being sub-judice before the Hon’ble 
Supreme Court or High Courts; cases restored or remanded after long intervals while 
retaining their original institution dates; matters initially dismissed in default and

1. The Committee notes that during 2023-24 and 2024-25, Revised Estimates

52. The Committee is of the view that specialized courts dealing with CBI cases 
require stability and judicial continuity to ensure timely adjudication and 
effective prosecution. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the

organization‑wise thrust areas for the next financial year and in understanding financial and 
other constraints, including logistics and manpower shortages, under the present Budget 
allocations. 
 
3. The Chairman then welcomed Shri Sriram Taranikanti, Director, LBSNAA; Dr. Alka 
Mittal, Member (Admn.), Capacity Building Commission; Shri Rajiv Manjhi, Director, 
ISTM; Shri Surendrakumar Bagde, Director General, IIPA and other officers to the meeting 
of the Committee and invited their views on the subject. 
  
4. The Chairman then requested the Director, LBSNAA to briefly introduce his team 
and proceed with the presentation, to be followed by the Member (Admn.), CBC, Director, 
ISTM and Director General, IIPA. Shri Sriram Taranikanti, Director, LBSNAA, thanked the 
Committee for the opportunity and, with the permission of the Chairman, a short video film 
depicting the Academy and its activities was shown to the Members. 
 
5. Thereafter, the Director gave an overview of the Academy’s faculty strength, 
infrastructure and academic activities. He informed that out of 34 sanctioned faculty posts, 21 
were presently filled and that the faculty comprised officers drawn from the IAS and other 
services. He also apprised the Committee about the Academy’s training infrastructure, 
academic centres and budgetary position, stating that the total allocation was about ₹130 

 
106 
crore and that mid-year expenditure appeared lower due to seasonal training cycles, with 
utilisation increasing in the later months of the financial year. 
 
6.    Following the presentation by LBSNAA, Dr. Alka Mittal, Member (Admn.) and Shri 
Jagdeep Gupta, Secretary, Capacity Building Commission made a presentation before the 
Committee. The Committee was apprised of the role of the Commission in steering 
capacity‑building reforms under Mission Karmayogi, developing frameworks and guidelines 
to support the Ministries and Departments in preparing their capacity‑building plans. 
Secretary, CBC also referred in broad terms to the Commission's Budget allocation, 
utilization of funds and key initiatives and focus areas for the coming year. 
 
7.    Thereafter, Director, ISTM, made a presentation on the mandate and functioning of 
ISTM. He informed that ISTM was a premier training institution for Central Government 
employees, conducting programmes on Secretariat procedures, office management, rules and 
emerging areas identified in capacity‑building efforts. He briefly apprised the Committee of 
its faculty structure, infrastructure, Budget allocation and major training activities during the 
year and indicated broad thrust areas for 2026‑27. 
 
8.       Lastly, Director General, IIPA, made a presentation before the Committee on the 
activities of the Institute. He stated that IIPA was engaged in training, research and 
consultancy in public administration and governance and provided programmes for civil 
servants and other stakeholders. He gave a brief account of its academic and research work, 
training programmes, institutional infrastructure and financial position and mentioned priority 
areas and planned activities for the next financial year. 
 
9. Ms. Chhavi Bhardwaj, Joint Secretary (Training) supplemented the presentations by 
providing an overall perspective on training and capacity‑building under the Department. She 
briefly mentioned the linkage of these organizations with Mission Karmayogi, the overall 
Budgetary picture for training‑related activities and coordination and monitoring 
arrangements.

witnesses noted these concerns and assured to furnish additional information in their written 
replies.
