CHAPTER - I 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
1.0 The Ministry of Law and Justice owes its origin to the Charter Act, 1833 enacted by 
the British Parliament and it is one of the oldest limbs of the Government of India. In 
accordance with the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Ministry 
comprises three Departments under the overall charge of the Law and Justice Minister 
assisted by a Minister of State. Each department is administratively headed by an independent 
Secretary. 
 
1.1.  The key mandate of the Ministry includes providing policy support in administration 
of justice; giving legal advice to Government of India and defend Government of India in 
various Courts and Tribunals and draft Supreme and Subordinate form of legislations for 
Government of India, etc. 
1.2. In this report, the demands allocated to Legislative Department (Demand No. 65) and 
Election Commission of India (Demand No. 66) has been analyzed and recommendations of 
the Committee have been made at the end of each heading/sub-headings. 
1.3. Legislative Department acts mainly as a service provider in so far as the legislative 
business of the Union Government is concerned. It ensures smooth and speedy processing of 
legislative proposals of various administrative Departments and Ministries.  
 
1.4. This Department not only performs functions as a servicing Department for drafting 
Bills on behalf of Ministries and Departments of the Government of India, but also initiates 
legislative proposals in respect of the matters with which it is administrati vely concerned. 
This Department also drafts the Finance Bill to give effect to the financial proposals of the 
Central Government every year, which is undertaken in the Legislative Department on the 
budget proposals being brought before it by the Ministry o f Finance. For the purposes of

1.5. Further, Ordinance promulgated by the President of India, Regulation made by 
President of India for Union Territories, Electoral Laws and Electoral reforms, maintenance 
of the updated Central Legislation under Bharat Sanhita (India Code) and authoritative 
translation of Central Legislations and Constitution of India in languages specified in the 
Eight Schedule to the Constitution under the Authoritative Text (Central Laws) Act 1973, 
publication and preparation of standard legal terminology, delimitation of Parliamentary and 
Legislative Assembly Constituencies, etc. are also assigned to the Department under 
Allocation of Business Rules, 1961. The Department also imparts training in legislative 
drafting to Officers of the Union/State Government and UT administration through the 
Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research (ILDR). 
 
1.6. It inter-alia administers Personal laws, Electoral laws, the Indian Contract Act, 1872, 
the Specific Relief Act, 1963, the Evidence Act, 1872, the Indian Trust Act, 1882, the Code 
of Civil Procedure, 1908, the Limitation Act, 1963, the Election Commission (Conditions of 
Service of Election Commissioners and Transaction of Business) Act, 1991, the 
Representation of People Act, 1950 and the Representation of People Act, 1951. It also 
facilitates procurement of Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) for the Election Commission of 
India, Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) Machines and reimbursement of election 
expenditure to State Governments and UT administrations. 
1.7. Legislative Department does not have any statutory or autonomous body under its 
control. It has two other wings under it, namely, the Official Languages Wing and Vidhi 
Sahitya Prakashan, which are responsible  for the progressive use of Hindi and other 
Scheduled languages:- 
(i) Official Languages Wing (OLW): The Official Languages Wing has been entrusted 
with the preparation and publication of standard legal terminologies and for translating into 
Hindi language of Central Acts and Ordinances and Subordinate legislations, etc. under the 

 
10 
Official Languages Act, 1963. This Wing is also responsible for translation of the Central 
Acts, ordinances, etc., into the Official Languages as specified in the 8th Schedule to the 
Constitution. 
(ii) Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan (VSP): The Vidhi Sahitya Prakashan is mainly concerned 
with bringing out authoritative Hindi version of select reportable judgements of the Supreme 
Court and the High Courts, with the objective of promoting the progressive use of Hindi in 
the legal field. It brings out various publications of legal literature in Hindi and also holds 
exhibitions for giving wide publicity to legal literatures available in Hindi and to promote 
their sales. 
1.8. The organisational set -up of the Legislative Department includes the Secretary, 
Additional Secretary, Joint Secretary &  Legislative Counsel, Additional Legislative Counsel, 
Deputy Legislative Counsel and Assistant Legislative Counsel and other supporting staff. The 
work relating to legislative drafting, scrutiny and vetting of subordinate legislation have been 
distributed among various Legislative Groups, and each group is headed by a Joint Secretary 
& Legislative Counsel or Additional Secretary. The Secretary of the Legislative Department 
also acts as the Chief Parliamentary Counsel and the Additional Secretary is in -charge of all 
subordinate legislation. 
ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA 
1.9. The Legislative Department is the administrative Department for the Election 
Commission of India, an autonomous constitutional authority created under Article 324 of 
Constitution for superintendence, direction, control of elections to Parliament, State 
legislatures, and to the offices of the President and the Vice-President of India.  
1.10. The Election Commission of India is a multimember body (since 1993) headed by the 
Chief Election Commissioner, who acts as Chairman of the Commission. The Chief Election 
Commissioner and Election Commissioners are appointed by the President of India and have 
tenure of six years, or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. The Commission is 
assisted by Deputy Election Commissioners, Chief Electoral Officers (CEO), District 
Election Officers (DEO) and Electoral Registration Officers (ERO), appointed by the 
Election Commission under Representation of People Act, 1951.  
1.11. It registers political parties and allots symbols to them and ensures inner party 
democracy in their functioning by insisting organizational elections at periodic intervals. 
Political parties registered with the Commission are granted recognition at the State and 

 
11 
National levels on the basis of their poll performance at general elections according to criteria 
specified by it. It implements Model Code of Conduct (MCC) for smooth conduct of free and 
fair elections. It also observes 25th January as National Voters' Day to encourage young 
electors to take part in election process.  
1.12. The electoral expenses on account of conduct of elections to the House of the People 
and the State Legislative Assembly are borne by the Central Government and the concerned 
State Government, and case of simultaneous elections, the expenditure is to be sha red on 
50:50 basis between Central and the concerned State Government. The initial expenditure is 
borne by the respective State Governments, and on submission of the audited report, the 
Central Government’s share is reimbursed through the Legislative Department.  
1.13. The Election Commission has its independent Secretariat for the work relating to the 
smooth conduct of elections to Parliament and State Legislatures, and t here is an exclusive 
demand (Demand No. 66) under which budgetary allocation is sought for running the 
activities of Election Commission of India.

DEMAND NO. 66 
2.13. The Election Commission of India has a separate Demand No. 66, under which it has 
been allocated Rs. 260.00 crore in BE (2022-23), and the entire expenditure incurred by the 
Election Commission of India is voted.  
 
2.14. In BE 2022-23, the ECI had projected Rs. 388.17 crore to the Ministry of Finance 
which included Rs 65.21 crore in salary, Rs 315.96 crore in Non-salary Revenue 
{Establishment (Other than IT)-Rs. 60.06 crore, Information Technology-Rs. 101.69 crore, 
SVEEP-Rs. 122.60 crore, Training-Rs.31.61 crore} and Rs 7.00 crore for Capital Section, 

 
17 
against which the Commission has been allocated only Rs. 260.00 crore. 
2.15. The Committee notes that as against the projection of Rs. 388.17 crore, the 
Commission has been allocated only Rs. 260 crore (67 percent approx.) in BE (2022-23), 
leading to a shortfall of Rs. 128.17 crore. The major shortfall is in Information Technology 
(Rs. 45.05 crore), Voters’ Awareness (Rs. 62.80 crore), and Training (Rs. 6.55 crore). Out of 
the total allocations in BE (2022-23), 24.12 percent is proposed to be incurred on the salary 
and allowances, 21.79 percent on Information Technology, 23 percent on Voters’ Awareness, 
9.64 percent on training and 2.69 percent on capital head. 
2.16. As observed, out of the total allocation of Rs. 260 crore in BE (2022-23), Rs. 253 is 
for revenue expenditure and Rs 7 crore has been allocated for capital expenditure. The 
Commission in its written submission has informed that the Demand under the Capital 
Section was based on the basic minimum requirement keeping in view of the ongoing Covid-
19 pandemic and additional requirement under the Capital Section may arise when plan for 
construction of building for the ECI at Dwarka is received from the NBCC. 
2.17. The Committee observes that as against an allocation of Rs. 253.00 crore in the 
current Financial Year, the expenditure of the ECI till 31st January, 2022 is only Rs. 
158.74 crore (63 percent approx.). The Committee observes that out of the total 
allocation of Rs. 7.00 crore for capital expenditure in the current financial year; the ECI 
has not been able to spend a single rupee till 31st January, 2022. The Committee 
understands that expenditure under the capital head is directly linked with the external 
agencies like CPWD and NBCC for construction of building for ECI at Dwarka, 
however, the Committee  fails to understand why only Rs. 158.74 has been spent under 
the Revenue head which is well within the control of ECI, and accordingly, the 
Committee recommends ECI to furnish cogent reasons for under-utilisation of funds 
allocated to it under the Revenue head. 
2.18. The Committee also observes allocations have been reduced under Revenue 
heads- Information Technology, Voters’ Awareness, and Training of ECI, which the 
Committee believes are important components of electoral processes. The Committee 
feels that reduction in allocations under the Revenue Expenditure for the forthcoming 
financial year vis-à-vis projection to the Ministry of Finance may be due to the inability 
of the ECI to spend the allocations made for various heads in the current financial year.

2.19. The Committee, accordingly, recommends the ECI to improve its spending 
capacity on various activities, as non- expenditure of amount allocated reflects under-

due to financial constraints and recommends the Department/ECI to approach the

Legislative Impact Assessment 
3.13. Legislative Impact Assessment involves making a comprehensive and systematic 
analysis of the actual impact of the legislation relative to the objects identified at the time of 
its passage. Legislative Impact Assessment broadly covers two stages, namely, ex ante 
assessment and ex post assessment. Ex ante assessment involves making an assessment of the 
anticipated outcomes of the proposed legislation while ex post assessment, also known as 
Post Legislative Scrutiny is the assessment of the actual impact of th e legislation relative to

the objects and purposes identified at the time of passage after a certain period and the 
assessment may be continuous. The objective of the entire LIA exercise is to determine 
whether the law achieved the intended outcomes and if  not, to understand the reason and

3.14. The Department in its written replies to the Committee has submitted that it is merely 
a service provider department catering its drafting services to the Ministries/Departme nts of 
the Government of India and pre -legislative consultations, legislative impact analysis or post 
legislative consultation are within the domain of the concerned Ministries/Departments, and 
therefore, Legislative Impact Assessment would have to be done  by that concerned 
Ministry/Department which initiates the legislation. 
 
3.15. The Committee observes that this Department is involved in examining new 
legislations for different Ministries/Departments and also for repealing of obsolete laws. 
The Committee  in its 101 st Report on Demands for Grants (2020 -21) had made 
observations with regard to drafting errors and ambiguity in law/statutes and desired 
for pre-legislative consultation and periodic review of legislations.  The Committee is of 
the view that legislation enacted are not able to keep pace with changing socio -economic 
developments leading to excessive litigations, financial loss to exchequer, disconnect 
with social milieu and so forth.

drafting is the most suitable Department under the Government of India for the 
purpose. The Committee, accordingly, recommends the Department to prepare a white 
paper on the Legislative Impact Assessment in the country by taking learning 
experiences of other developed countries.

Common Electoral Roll 
3.25. As per the  Article 324(1) of the Constitution of India, the electoral rolls for 
Parliament and State Legislatures is prepared by the Election Commission of India,  whereas 
Articles 243K and 243ZA empowers State Election Commissions (SECs) to prepare electoral 
rolls for panchayats/local urban body elections. 
3.26. The ECI has informed this Com mittee that c urrently, 32 States/UTs are using ECI's 
electoral roll, though in different forms i.e., Maharashtra and Goa use the same roll, while 
others publish draft and receive claims and objections and then prepare their own final roll. 
The ECI has also been facilitating the SECs by sharing electoral database with them, as and 
when they make a request to the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) of the concerned state. 
3.27. The Election Commission of India (ECI) in its communication to the Government in the 
year 1999 and again in 2004 made reference for creation of Common Electoral Roll. The Law 
Commission of India in its 255 th Report on Electoral Reforms’ (2015) also endorsed the idea 
of Common Electoral Roll. This Committee in its earlier reports, including  the recent 107 th 
and 109th, had also dealt with the issue of Common Electoral Roll. 
3.28. There is a Common Electoral Roll for both Lok Sabha and State Legislative 
Assembly elections as per the Section 13D of the Representation of People Act, 1950, 
according to which the electoral roll for every parliamentary constituency shall consist of the 
electoral rolls for all the Assembly constituencies within it and it shall not be necessary to 
separately prepare or revise the electoral roll for any such parliamentary constituency.  
3.29. The Election Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021 amended the Representation of the 
People Act, 1950 and the Representation of the People Act, 1951. Section 14(b) of the 
Representation of the People Act, 1950 has been amended and now four qu alifying dates- the 
1st day of January, the 1st day of April, the 1st day of July and the 1st day of October have 
been inserted for preparation and revision of electoral roll. Under Section 23 of the 
Representation of the People Act, 1951, new sub -clauses (4), (5) & (6) have been added for 
linking/authenticating Aadhar with the electoral roll. 
3.30. The Committee notes that around majority of States/UTs are already using the 
Electoral Roll prepared by Election Commission of India for conducting elections to  their 
Local Bodies elections which are conducted by the State Election Commissions.

for preparation and revision of electoral roll, thereby, encouraging the States to adopt 
the Common Electo ral Rolls. The Committee is of the view that since the relevant 
statutes have already been amended, the Department/ECI may pursue with the 
remaining State Governments/UTs to implement the common electoral roll.

Overview of the Budgetary Allocations 
 1.     The Committee observes that allocation to the Official Language Wing (OLW) has 
marginally increased from the Financial Year (2020 -21) to (2021 -22), except in the

2. At the outset, the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee 
and the officials of the Department of Justice, NALSA, Supreme Court of India 
and National Judicial Academy attending the meeting on Demands for Grants 
(2022-23). He then asked them to give a brief overview of the functioning and 
budgetary allocations made to them. 
3. The Secretary, Department of Justice in his opening remarks informed the 
Committee about the functioning of the Department and a presentation was 
made highlighting the issues related to the budgetary allocations, projections to 
Ministry of Finance, functioning and recent initiative s/ achievements of the 
Department. He apprised the committee of the status of infrastructural 
development of judiciary and vacancy positions of judges in higher judiciary. 
4. Thereafter, the Member -Secretary, NALSA made a presentation before 
the Committee informing about the organization and its budgetary allocations, 
NALSA mobile app, Lok Adalats and other initiatives taken by the NALSA in 
the preceding year.

5. After that, the Registrar, Supreme Court of India apprised the Committee

6. Thereafter, the Director, National Judicial Academy, informed the 
Committee about the training programmes conducted by them during 202 1-22, 
including training of foreign trainees and the budgetary allocations made to the 
Academy. 
7. After the presentations, some queries were raised by the Chairman and 
Members relating to budgetary allocation of the Departments/Organization's 
actions, time-line of live streaming of cases, break -up and pendency of cases in

infrastructural development of courts, data on judges of reserved categories, 
backlog of vacancies in Courts and ITAT, judicial accountability, operationality 
of Gram Nyayalayas and Nyaya Mitras, roadmap to establishmend of legal aid 
clinics, confinement of virtual courts and e -Sewa Kendras to some  States only, 
revision of fees of  panel lawyers of State Legal Serv ices Authority. The 
Chairman of the Committee asked them to furnish the detailed note/replies in 
writing within three working days.

Institute and Chairman, Bar Council of India and other officers related to these 
Departments.

work done in the preceding financial year. Thereafter, the President, ITAT,
