Recommendation (Para No. 2.15) 
  
1.5. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“From a thorough examination of the Demands for Grants (DFG) over the past 
three years, the Committee observe a persistent trend of underutilization of 
funds across key schemes such as ADIP, SIPDA, DDRS, and Scholarships, with 
actual expenditures consistently falling short of Revised Estimates (RE) and 
Budget Estimates (BE). The BE allocations were invariably reduced at the RE 
stage in 2022-23, 2023-24, and 2024-25, yet the Department was unable to fully 
utilize even the reduced funds. The Department utilized 97.38 per cent of RE in 
2022-23 and 93.39 per cent in 2023-24. However, as of 03.03.2025, only 
₹892.68 crore of ₹1,167.27 crore (76.48 per cent) has been spent. This shortfall 
was primarily due to disruptions from the introduction of the CNA and TSA 
models. Delays in receiving Utilization Certificates (UCs) from State Governments 
under SIPDA and a lack of adequate proposals from North-Eastern States further 
hindered funds utilization. While the Committee were assured by the 
Department’s representatives that the remaining 2024-25 funds would be fully 
utilized by 31.03.2025, the Committee strongly urge the Department to adopt 
targeted remedial measures and strategic interventions to ensure efficient fund 
utilization in 2025-26. Proactive engagement with State Governments, NGOs, 

3 
 
and implementing agencies is essential to streamline proposal submissions and 
disbursement process, particularly for skill training programs and capital 
expenditure. Furthermore, given the challenges in meeting fixed targets for 
schemes like SIPDA and DDRS, the Department should reassess target-setting 
methodologies to ensure that they are realistic, flexible, and aligned with 
performance trends. The Committee recommend decisive action to improve fund 
utilization and program execution in the remaining year of the 2024-26 financial 
cycle, ensuring that allocated resources effectively advance the welfare of 
persons with disabilities and that the Department successfully meets its 
commitments under various schemes.” 
  
1.6. The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Empowerment 
of Persons with Disabilities), in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as under:-  
 “The suggestions of the Committee has been noted. 
In FY 2024-25, the Department has spent ₹1,112.61 crore against the RE of 
₹1,167.27 crore i.e. 95.32 % of RE allocation. Further, it is also mentioned that 
approximately ₹47 crore has been returned to CFI from TSA account as various 
State Governments/Implementing agencies/NGOs/organizations etc have not 
withdrawn funds by 31.03.2025.  The Scheme wise details are given below: 
  
ADIP Scheme: For FY 2024-25, against the RE of ₹350.00 crores, amount of 
₹348.81 crores has been spent. The balance amount of ₹1.19 crores was 
surrendered under the media head due to non-withdrawal of funds by 
Directorate of Advertisement and Visual Publicity (DAVP) for expenditure incurred 
by them under ADIP Scheme. Under the ADIP Scheme, BE for FY 2025-26 is        
₹316.70 crores that will be utilized in various activities under ADIP Scheme. For 
FY 2025-26, sufficient demand for funds has been raised by ALIMCO which is the 
major Implementing Agency of ADIP Scheme. Apart from this, demand for funds 
has also been raised by National Institutes for pilot projects on teaching/learning 
materials (TLM) Kits.

SIPDA Scheme: Under SIPDA Scheme, during 2024-25, an amount of ₹44.16 
crore has been spent out of RE of ₹111.00 crore, Further, due to implementation 
of TSA module from 2024-25, some States could not onboard on TSA module 
and make expenditure of sanctioned amount of about ₹ 40 crore. 
In order to address the shortfall in fund utilization and to facilitate full 
appropriate utilization of funds, the Department is actively pursuing several 
redressal mechanisms, such as: 
Screening Committee: 
  
A committee has been constituted of Joint Secretary (SIPDA), Director/Deputy 
Secretary (SIPDA), and two experts in the field of disability to duly examine the 
proposals for release of Grant-in-Aid (GIA) under the Barrier Free and Accessible 
India component. 
  
Strengthening Coordination with States and Implementing Agencies: 
The Department engages proactively with State Governments, particularly 
focusing on improving the timely submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs) and 
correct proposals. Periodic virtual and in-person review meetings are being held 
to track progress under SIPDA. 
  
Process Optimization Post-CNA and TSA Introduction: 
  
Recognizing the transitional disruptions caused by the adoption of the Central 
Nodal Agency (CNA) and Treasury Single Account (TSA) systems, the 
Department has streamlined fund release protocols and updated internal SOPs to 
minimize delays in disbursement and expenditure. 
  
DDRS Scheme: Under DDRS Scheme, an amount of ₹139.39 crore has been spent 
during the financial year 2024–25 as against the revised estimate of ₹139.00 crore. 

5 
 
Under the DDRS scheme, the following measures have been taken by this 
Department for smooth utilization of funds as under: 
a. Model projects have been conceptualized to cover all 21 disabilities as per RPwD 
Act, 2016. 
b. Maximum age limit of the ID beneficiaries has been increased from 18 to 23 
years. 
c. Grant-in-aid to the Implementing Agencies is being released on Advance-cum-
reimbursement basis. 
d. With effect from 2023-24, the implementing agencies are required to submit 
UDID cards of all the eligible beneficiaries. The data regarding UDID cards is 
being directly fetched through API from UDID Portal (Swavlamban Portal). 
e. With effect from 01.10.2024 the following procedure has been rolled out for 
expediting fund release: If the proposal submitted by the PIAs/NGOs on e-
anudaan portal is pending at District level for one month without any action, 
then it will automatically be forwarded to the State Government. Further, if the 
same proposal is pending with the State Government for one month without any 
action, then it will automatically be forwarded (without recommendation of State 
Government) to the Department. However, this will not be applicable in case of 
supplementary grant is being given by the State Government to the PIAs/NGOs. 
f. CPMU consultants and young professionals of the Department are conducting 
video inspections to monitor the smooth functioning of the organizations and the 
timely submission of grants-in-aid proposals. 
  
Scholarship Scheme: Under Scholarship Scheme, an amount of ₹89.71 crore has 
been spent during 2024-25 against the RE allocation of ₹80.00 crore. The 
Department is actively organizing meetings with State Nodal Officers (SNOs) to 
ensure effective implementation of the schemes. The Department is also taking 
necessary steps to sensitize the States/UTs to clear the pendency at various levels 
of the State Nodal Officers and other Stakeholders. The Department has published 
advertisements in leading Newspapers across the country twice to increase 

6 
 
awareness of these schemes amongst students and other stakeholders. All other 
types of media and social media platforms are also used for spreading awareness. 
The Chief Secretaries/Principal Secretaries of the States are also addressed to 
spread awareness of the schemes through various mediums of communication in 
their respective States/UTs. 
  
The Department reiterates its commitment to the efficient and impactful 
implementation of all its flagship schemes and assures the Committee of sustained 
efforts to maximize fund utilization, enhance delivery mechanisms, and fulfill its 
mandate for the welfare of persons with disabilities.” 
 
1.7. In their original recommendation, the Committee had flagged the 
persistent underutilization of funds across key schemes—namely ADIP, 
SIPDA, DDRS, and Scholarships—despite fixed allocations and physical 
targets under the EFC framework. The Committee had called for targeted 
interventions, better coordination with States, and reassessment of planning 
and implementation processes. However, the Committee are not satisfied 
with the Government’s reply. While the Department has claimed improved 
fund utilization in 2024–25, actual figures—especially under the SIPDA 
scheme—continue to reflect substantial shortfalls, with less than 40% of RE 
spent and significant funds returned due to procedural delays. This is a 
recurring pattern which is observed over the past three years. The 
Committee note that procedural reforms have been introduced, such as 
automated proposal forwarding and stakeholder outreach. However, these 
do not adequately address structural issues like weak inter-agency 
coordination, delayed approvals, and lack of region-specific planning. The 
Committee reiterate that a more data-driven, accountable, and flexible 
implementation framework is needed to ensure that available resources are 
fully leveraged in the remaining years of the financial cycle.

Recommendation (Para No. 3.20) 
1.8. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee recognize that the Assistance to Persons with Disabilities for 
Purchase/Fitting of Aids/Appliances (ADIP) Scheme is an important initiative 
aimed at enhancing mobility, independence, and economic empowerment of 
Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). The Committee note that fund allocation and 
utilization under ADIP have shown a steady increase, with actual expenditure 
surpassing initial Budget Estimates (BE) in multiple years. In 2022-23, the BE 
was ₹235.00 crore, which was revised to ₹230.00 crore, yet actual expenditure 
exceeded expectations at ₹242.29 crore. Similarly, in 2023-24, the BE of ₹245.00 
crore was significantly revised to ₹305.00 crore, with actual spending at ₹290.60 
crore. In 2024-25, the BE was further enhanced to ₹315.00 crore, and the 
Revised Estimate (RE) increased to ₹350.00 crore, with ₹346.27 crore utilized as 
of 03.03.2025. The Committee also note that increased financial allocations has 
been ably matched by the physical performance/targets set under the Scheme. 
In 2022-23 and 2023-24, the target was exceeded, benefiting 2.89 lakh and 3.15 
lakh beneficiaries, respectively. However, in 2024-25, only 2.10 lakh beneficiaries 
were assisted as of 14.02.2025, accounting for 67 per cent of the target of 3.15 
lakh beneficiaries. While the Department has cited the imposition of the Model 
Code of Conduct during General Elections and the transition to the TSA Model-I 
as reasons for the slower pace of the expenditure, the Committee urge the 
Department to learn from these experiences and establish contingency plans to 
ensure uninterrupted service delivery in the future. The Committee however 
express concern over the reduction of the physical targets for 2025-26 to 2.05 
lakh beneficiaries and recommend that the Department continuously assess 
demand trends and explore possibilities for expanding beneficiary coverage.” 
 
 

8 
 
1.9. The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Empowerment 
of Persons with Disabilities), in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as under:- 
  
“It is to apprise the committee that the process of onboarding on TSA module 
has been streamlined and Grants-in-Aid to Implementing Agencies under ADIP 
Scheme is being released through TSA module smoothly for financial year 2025-
26. However, the physical targets for beneficiaries was fixed considering the 
budgetary allocation of ₹316.70 cr under the Scheme for financial year 2025-26 
vis-à-vis RE of ₹350 cr. in 2024-25. Another major reason for decrease in 
number of beneficiaries is that demand for high end devices including motorised 
and lithium battery operated tricycles is low in comparison to other low-cost 
devices. 
  
As regard to beneficiary coverage, regular camps are being organised on basis of 
requests received from VIPs/other dignitaries. Also, sufficient demand for aids 
and appliances are being raised at PMDKs.  Apart from this, the Department is 
also encouraging Implementing Agencies for distribution of aids and appliances 
through Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR).”  
 
1.10. In their original recommendation, the Committee had acknowledged 
the positive financial and physical performance of the ADIP Scheme over the 
years but had expressed concern on the significant drop in beneficiary 
coverage during 2024–25, alongside the downward revision of physical 
targets for 2025–26. The Committee had urged the Department to draw plan 
of action based on their experiences gained from recent administrative and 
operational challenges and ensure that service delivery remains consistent 
and demand-driven. 
 
In this context, the Committee find the reply of the Department to be 
somewhat limited in its scope. While the reasons cited—such as reduced 

9 
 
budgetary allocation and variation in demand for higher-end assistive 
devices—may partly explain the decline, the reply does not sufficiently 
elaborate on steps being taken to assess unmet needs or proactively expand 
outreach. The Committee believe that a more detailed and data-backed 
explanation would have added clarity, especially given the scheme’s 
significance in empowering persons with disabilities. The Committee, 
therefore, reiterate that the Department must strengthen its planning and 
monitoring mechanisms to ensure targets remain aligned with field-level 
demand. They recommend that future beneficiary projections be based on 
more granular assessments of regional needs and device preferences. 
Additionally, greater synergy with CSR initiatives and increased involvement 
of local stakeholders may help broaden the scheme’s reach and improve 
delivery outcomes. 
 
Recommendation (Para No. 4.18) 
1.11. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee note that physical targets under DDRS have witnessed a 
downward revision since 2024-25, with the target reduced from 40,000 in 2023-
24 to 32,000 in 2024-25 and 2025-26. The Department has attributed this to the 
number of projects supported remaining largely the same over the years, with 
new proposals being considered only after the release of funds to organizations. 
The Committee emphasize that rehabilitation services for PwDs should not be 
constrained due to procedural delays in fund disbursal and recommend that the 
Department take proactive steps to expedite approvals for new projects. The 
Committee urge the Department to explore expanding the scope of DDRS to 
cover more PwDs by identifying underserved areas where rehabilitation services 
are limited or absent.”

The Committee appreciate the Department’s efforts in introducing 
monitoring mechanisms, including video inspections of NGOs and time-bound 
forwarding of proposals under the revised DDRS. The provision for automatic 
transmission of pending proposals from the district levels to the State 
Government and further to the DEPwD if no action is taken within a month of 
submission is a commendable measure aimed to streamline the approval

recommend strict enforcement of these provisions to ensure that no proposal

support the decision that organizations running both DDRS and National 
Trust schemes should choose only one to avoid duplication of services, 
ensuring that funds reach the maximum number of beneficiaries. 
 
 Recommendation (Para No. 5.15) 
1.14. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
“The Committee also find that persistent challenges such as delays in fund 
disbursement, pending utilization certificates, and incomplete proposals have 
impacted the effective implementation of SIPDA. Given that SIPDA is the flagship 
scheme for PwDs, the Committee strongly recommend that the Department 
strengthen its monitoring and coordination mechanisms by ensuring regular 
follow-ups with State Governments and implementing agencies. The Central 
Project Monitoring Unit (CPMU) should be further empowered to enable realtime 
tracking of fund utilization, proposal approvals, and physical progress under 
various sub-schemes. Furthermore, timely clearance of compliance documents 
such as Utilization Certificates (UCs) and pending proposals should be ensured to 
prevent underutilization and last-minute fund surrender.”

1.16. The Committee, in their earlier recommendation, had drawn attention 
to persistent issues in both financial and physical performance under the 
SIPDA scheme. Over the last three years, the Department has witnessed 
wide fluctuations between Budget Estimates, Revised Estimates, and actual 
expenditure—indicating inconsistencies in financial planning and 
inefficiencies in fund absorption.  
Despite the Department’s reply outlining multiple redressal measures—
such as constitution of a Screening Committee, improved coordination with 
States, field reviews, and capacity-building initiatives—the Committee find 
that these responses remain largely procedural and fail to convey how they 
are addressing the core issues of delayed approvals, underutilization of funds 
and lapses in monitoring. 
Given that SIPDA is a flagship initiative for advancing accessibility and 
empowerment of persons with disabilities, the Committee reiterate the 
urgent need for the Department to overhaul its approach by instituting a 
robust, real-time project tracking system through the Central Project 
Monitoring Unit (CPMU), ensuring timely submission of Utilization 
Certificates, and reducing delays in proposal processing. Financial planning 
must be made more realistic and performance-driven to avoid unnecessary 
mid-year revisions and surrender of funds. The Committee expect that the 
Department will act on the recommendation with greater urgency and 
accountability to ensure that SIPDA achieves its stated goals in the 
remaining years of the current financial cycle.

Recommendation (Para No. 5.23) 
1.17. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee note with appreciation the initiatives undertaken under the 
Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan) to enhance accessibility in 
built environments, transportation, and information & communication technology 
(ICT).However, with the campaign now subsumed under the “Creation of Barrier-
Free Environment (CBFE)” sub-scheme under SIPDA, the Committee express 
concern over the absence of a dedicated budgetary allocation for CBFE and the 
continued financial support required for sustained accessibility initiatives. The 
Committee strongly recommend that the Department create a structured 
financial framework within SIPDA, ensuring that accessibility projects receive 
adequate funding without dependence on overall SIPDA allocations. Additionally, 
a long-term accessibility roadmap should be developed to maintain momentum 
and expand initiatives to underserved regions.” 
 
1.18. The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Empowerment  
of Persons with Disabilities), in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as under:- 
“The Department is engaging with allied Ministries (e.g., Urban Affairs, Railways, 
and Education) to integrate accessibility components within their infrastructure 
development programs and explore co-funding opportunities for large-scale 
accessibility interventions. Demi-Official letters have been sent to Secretaries of 
different ministries from Secretary, DEPwD for the same. 
SIPDA scheme has several sub-components. Notional allocations are made to 
each scheme sub- component which are reviewed periodically for making 
adjustments as per requirement.” 
 
 
 
 
 

15 
 
 
1.19. The Committee appreciate the Department’s continued engagement 
with other Ministries to integrate accessibility into their infrastructure 
development programs and acknowledge the use of notional allocations 
under the SIPDA scheme. However, the Committee remain concerned that 
the current arrangement—relying on internal adjustments and periodic 
reviews—may not offer the consistency and strategic prioritization needed to 
scale up and sustain accessibility initiatives, especially following the 
subsuming of the Accessible India Campaign under the “Creation of Barrier-
Free Environment (CBFE)” sub-scheme. The Committee reiterate the need for 
a clearly delineated financial framework within SIPDA for CBFE, with specific, 
trackable allocations that do not remain contingent on broader SIPDA 
funding dynamics. The Committee further recommend that the Department 
finalize a long-term, outcome-oriented roadmap for accessibility, with a 
focus on underserved regions and measurable targets, to ensure that the 
objectives of the Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan continue to be advanced in a 
systematic and sustained manner.

Recommendation (Para No. 1.15) 
The Committee appreciate the proactive initiatives taken by various State Governments

(Recommendation Para No.2.15) 
From a thorough examination of the Demands for Grants (DFG) over the past three 
years, the Committee observe a persistent trend of underutilization of funds across key 
schemes such as ADIP, SIPDA, DDRS, and Scholarships, with actual expenditures 
consistently falling short of Revised Estimates (RE) and Budget Estimates (BE). The BE 
allocations were invariably reduced at the RE stage in 2022-23, 2023- 24, and 2024-25, 
yet the Department was unable to fully utilize even the reduced funds. The Department 
utilized 97.38per cent of RE in 2022-23 and 93.39per cent in 2023-24. However, as of 
03.03.2025, only ₹892.68 crore of ₹ 1167.27 crore (76.48per cent) has been spent. 
This shortfall was primarily due to disruptions from the introduction of the CNA and TSA 
models. Delays in receiving Utilization Certificates (UCs) from State Governments under 
SIPDA and a lack of adequate proposals from North-Eastern States further hindered 
funds utilization. While the Committee were assured by the Department’s 
representatives that the remaining 2024-25 funds would be fully utilized by 31.03.2025, 
the Committee strongly urge the Department to adopt targeted remedial measures and 
strategic interventions to ensure efficient fund utilization in 2025-26. Proactive 
engagement with State Governments, NGOs, and implementing agencies is essential to 
streamline proposal submissions and disbursement process, particularly for skill training 
programs and capital expenditure. Furthermore, given the challenges in meeting fixed 
targets for schemes like SIPDA and DDRS, the Department should reassess target-
setting methodologies to ensure that they are realistic, flexible, and aligned with 
performance trends. The Committee recommend decisive action to improve fund 
utilization and program execution in the remaining year of the 2024-26 financial cycle, 
ensuring that allocated resources effectively advance the welfare of persons with 
disabilities and that the Department successfully meets its commitments under various 
schemes.
