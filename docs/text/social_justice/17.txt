INTRODUCTION 
 
  I, the Chairperson, Standing Committee on Social Justice and 
Empowerment (2025-26) having been authorized by the Committee do 
present on their behalf this Seventeenth Report on 'Demands for Grants for 
the year 2026-27' pertaining to the Ministry of Social Justice and 
Empowerment (Department of Social Justice and Empowerment). 
2. The Committee considered the ‘Demands for Grants (2026-27)’ of the 
Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Social Justice 
and Empowerment) which were laid on the Table of the House on 10 
February, 2026. After obtaining the Budget Documents, Explanatory Notes, 
etc., the Committee took evidence of the Ministry of Social Justice and 
Empowerment (Department of Social Justice and Empowerment) on 24 
February, 2026. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at the 
sitting held on 10.03.2026. 
3. The Committee wish to express their thanks to the Officers of the 
Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Social Justice 
and Empowerment) for tendering oral evidence and placing before them the 
detailed written notes and post evidence information as desired by the 
Committee in connection with the examination of the ‘Demands for Grants 
(2026-27)’. 
4. For ease of reference observations/ recommendations/ comments of 
the Committee have been printed in bold in the body of the Report.

INTRODUCTORY 
 
The Department of Social Justice and Empowerment envisages to empower the 
socially, educationally and economically marginalized sections of the society including 
Scheduled Castes, Other Backward Classes, De-notified and Nomadic Tribes (DNTs), 
Economically Weaker Sections, Senior Citizens, Persons engaged in act of begging, 
Transgenders, Victims of Alcoholism and Substance abuse. The vision of the Department 
is to build an inclusive society wherein members of the target groups can lead productive, 
safe and dignified lives with adequate support for their growth and development. 
1.2 The Programmes and Schemes of the Department of Social Justice and 
Empowerment are categorized into Central Sector Schemes and the Centrally Sponsored 
Schemes. The Central Sector and Centrally Sponsored Schemes are as follows:-

2.8 In view of the surrender of funds during the Year 2024-25, the Committee asked, whether 
any assessment has been done with regard to the allocation of budget on each scheme. The 
Department submitted vide their written reply that the Department of Social Justice and 
Empowerment undertakes assessment and review of budgetary allocation under its schemes to 
ensure that implementation does not suffer for want of funds.  
 
(i) Under the Pre-Matric Scholarship for SCs and Others and the Post Matric Scholarship 
Scheme for SC students, which are implemented through DBT mode, States/UTs submit realistic 
targets in their Annual Action Plans. These proposals are appraised every year by the Project 
Appraisal Committee (PAC) under the Chairmanship of Secretary, Department of Social Justice 
and Empowerment, generally in April/May. This appraisal process ensures that allocations are 
based on actual requirement and realistic beneficiary projections.  
(ii) Under SHREYAS-SCs, no separate assessment with regard to allocation of budget on 
each component has been undertaken; however, allocations are made keeping in view past 
expenditure trends and ongoing commitments to ensure smooth implementation during FY 
2025–26.  
 
(iii) Under PM-AJAY, the total outlay for the period 2021–22 to 2025–26 has been approved 
by the Expenditure Finance Committee. Allocation among components is governed by approved 
scheme guidelines and clearly defined criteria. As per the guidelines, up to 50% of the total 
allocation is earmarked for the Adarsh Gram component based on actual requirement, up to 5% 

14  
for administration, monitoring and evaluation, and up to 2% for construction of hostels in Central 
institutions, with the remaining funds allocated to States/UTs under the Grants-in-Aid component. 
Savings under any component are redistributed to other components or States/UTs against 
viable proposals, subject to scheme guidelines. These provisions ensure balanced and need-
based allocation so that the scheme does not suffer due to lack of funds. 
 
(iv) Under SRESHTA, no separate assessment exercise has been undertaken for allocation 
of budget; however, allocations are made in accordance with approved scheme guidelines and 
projected admissions to ensure continuity of support.  
 
(v) Under AVYAY, the Department regularly reviews scheme performance and coverage to 
ensure adequate budgetary provisioning. Over the last five years, the number of Senior Citizen 
Homes supported under the Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens has increased from 607 
to 705, reflecting enhanced coverage. Under Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana, the number of assistive 
aids and devices has been expanded from 8 to 17, increasing beneficiary outreach. Periodic 
National Review Conferences with States/UTs and independent evaluation by NITI Aayog 
through the Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO) also guide budget 
projections and strengthen implementation to prevent any shortfall in allocation; and 
 
(vi) Under NAMASTE, the budget is proposed based on yearly provisions approved under 
the SFC/EFC process, and regular reviews are conducted to monitor expenditure across 
components to ensure adequate provisioning. 
 
2.9 On being enquired with regard to the measures being taken to ensure that the funds 
allocated do not remain idle so that the targeted sections do not suffer due to procedural lapses 
at the level of States/UT Governments, the Committee were informed by the Department of 
Social Justice and Empowerment vide their written reply that has stated that: 
 
“Yes, the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment has evolved systems to 
ensure that funds allocated under its schemes do not remain idle and that the targeted 
sections do not suffer due to procedural lapses at the level of States/UT Governments. The 
mechanisms introduced in recent years have yielded positive results in improving 
transparency, accountability and timely utilisation of funds.” 
 
(i) Under the Pre-Matric Scholarship for SCs and Others and the Post Matric 
Scholarship for SC students, the schemes are open-ended and demand-
driven for all eligible students of recognised institutions across India. 
Applications are invited through a designated online portal and the Central 
share is released through DBT directly into Aadhaar-seeded bank 
accounts of beneficiaries. States/UTs are responsible for inviting 
applications and verification through their established mechanisms. The 
DBT framework and portal-based processing minimise the possibility of 
funds remaining idle and ensure that eligible students receive scholarships 
directly.  

15  
 
(ii) Under SHREYAS-SCs, being a Central Sector Scheme, there is no 
State/UT-wise allocation of funds. Funds are released centrally based on 
approved proposals and ongoing commitments, thereby reducing the risk 
of idle balances at the State level and ensuring timely support to 
beneficiaries.  
 
(iii) Under PM-AJAY, since FY 2021–22, ₹ 1712.56 crore has been released 
under the Adarsh Gram component and 13,947 villages have been 
declared as Adarsh Gram. Under the Grant-in-Aid component, ₹1527.43 
crore has been provided to 2,48,570 beneficiaries, and ₹ 177.63 crore has 
been sanctioned for 90 hostels covering 10,649 beneficiaries. The 
introduction of the SNA SPARSH system, PFMS-based monitoring and 
regular review meetings has helped reduce idle funds and improve 
transparency. The scheme has been onboarded on SNA-SPARSH with 
effect from 01 July 2025, enabling just-in-time releases and preventing 
accumulation of unspent balances. Releases are linked to utilisation and 
compliance, thereby safeguarding beneficiary interests.  
 
(iv) Under SRESHTA, funds are released directly to selected schools/NGOs 
under the Central Sector framework. Releases are linked to compliance 
with scheme guidelines, inspections and submission of Utilization 
Certificates, which minimises the risk of funds remaining idle and ensures 
that students continue to receive support without disruption.  
 
 
(v) Under AVYAY, the introduction of the SNA-SPARSH fund flow system, 
mandatory submission of Utilization Certificates, inspection-based 
releases and periodic review meetings with States/UTs have significantly 
improved fund utilisation. Releases to States/UTs and NGOs are linked to 
implementation progress, compliance verification and physical 
inspections. In cases of delay at the State/UT level, the Department 
undertakes continuous follow-up and provides handholding support so that 
benefits to senior citizens are not adversely affected.  
 
(vi) Under NAMASTE, centralised procurement of PPE kits for sanitation 
workers and safety devices for Emergency Response Sanitation Units is 
undertaken and supplies are delivered to States/UTs for onward 
distribution. This centralised model reduces delays and ensures that 
beneficiaries receive equipment in a timely manner. The mechanisms 
introduced in recent years have yielded positive results in improving 
transparency, accountability and timely utilisation of funds.

2.10 On being asked about the States/ UT that have expressed inability in complying to the 
prescribed procedure for allocation/ disbursement of funds to the beneficiaries, the Committee 
were inter-alia informed vide written reply that: 
 
“Release of Central share is contingent upon States/UTs sharing their paid data on the 
designated Scholarship Portal. No specific report has been received from States/UTs indicating 
inability to comply with the prescribed procedure, however, timely sharing of verified data 
remains essential for smooth release of funds. Under PM-AJAY, which is implemented through

States/UTs have reported procedural constraints such as delays in on boarding under SNA-
SPARSH, returning balances to the Consolidated Fund of India and closure of earlier SNA 
accounts. These procedural issues have contributed to delays in fund utilisation during FY 
2025–26. The Department has been regularly conducting review meetings, providing 
handholding support and issuing necessary guidance to resolve these issues and ensure 
compliance.”

2.13 The Committee appreciate that the BE has been continuously increased over the 
last few years. From ₹ 12847 cr in 2023-24 it has now been increased to ₹ 13687.59 cr for 
the year 2026-27.The Committee however are not pleased to note that the Department of 
Social Justice and Empowerment has not been able to spend the allocated amount over 
all  these years. Even in the year 2025-26, ₹7317.95 crore only has been spent out of the 
Revised Estimates of ₹11402.74 crore. The Department still has a balance of almost 30 
percent of the Revised Estimates, even though the financial year 2025–26 is about to end. 
The Committee are also concerned to note that the Budget Estimates is invariably reduced 
by almost 25 to 30 percent every year at the time of the revision of estimates. The 
Committee do not find this to be a good indicator with regard to the performance of the 
Department. The hurdles in implementing Schemes, due to which the estimates are to be 
revised downwards or the funds remain unspent, should not be a recurring feature. These 
issues need to be resolved within a specific time period. The Committee, therefore, expect 
that the factors such as onboarding of States/UTs on SNA-SPARSH, submission of 
Utilization Certificates, release of matching shares by the State Governments, etc., will be 
sorted out in this financial year so that there is no more hinderance in the implementation 
of the Schemes. The Committee are optimistic with regard to the changes proposed to 
Expenditure Finance Committee of the Ministry of Finance in the Schemes of the 
Department of Social justice and expect that once approved for next financial cycle of 
2026-27 to 2030-31 will not only benefit but also boost the morale of the deprived sections 
of society.

POST MATRIC SCHOLARSHIP FOR SCHEDULED CASTES 
 
The Post-Matric Scholarship Scheme for Scheduled Caste (SC) students is a Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme, implemented by the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment 
(DoSJE) through State Governments/Union Territory Administrations. This Scheme is currently 
in the final year of the existing Finance Commission cycle (2020-21 to 2025-26) till 2025-26. In 
view of the completion of the current cycle, the process of revising the Scheme guidelines for the 
next cycle (2026-27 to 2030-31) has been initiated. The Post-Matric Scholarship are awarded by 
the Government of the State/UT to which the applicant actually belongs i.e. permanently settled 
or domiciled. Parent/Guardian's income should not exceed ₹ 2.5 lakh per annum. The objective 
of the scheme is to provide financial assistance to the Scheduled Caste students and to 
appreciably increase the Gross Enrollment Ratio of SC students in higher education with a focus 
on those from the poorest households. It is the endeavor of the Government to raise the GER of 
SCs in higher education from 23.0 % to the national average.

₹204.04 crore against the Budget Estimate of ₹212.00 crore at the time of examination. The 
Committee find that the target of 2000 fellowships fixed at the inception of the scheme in 
2005-06 has remained the same even in subsequent years. The Committee does not find 
any justification for not increasing the target for more than 20 years and thereby restricting 
students from availing the benefits of the scheme to undertake advanced studies and 

42  
research leading to PhD degrees. The Committee believe that increasing the number of 
fellowships under this scheme would provide greater opportunities to under-privileged 
candidates in addition to those qualifying the cut-off marks notified by UGC-CSIR for NET-
JRF. Even after the several evaluations of the scheme conducted over the year, the 
Committee are surprised that no recommendation for enhancement of fellowship numbers 
has been made till date. The Committee are of the view that the department should analyze 
the number of applications received under the scheme in the last three years. If the number 
of applications received each year is increasing then it justifies the need to increase the 
number of fellowships so that a larger number of applicants get an opportunity to avail the 
fellowship. It is also a fact that several students do not pursue advanced studies due to lack 
of financial assistance. Keeping in view the educational backwardness of a large section of 
the SC population, the Committee strongly believe that the number of fellowships need to 
be enhanced so that the scheme accommodates more aspiring students. The Committee 
would like to be informed of the action taken in this regard. 
 
4.39 The Committee are happy to note that the beneficiaries of the PM- CARES Children 
Scheme have been included in the free coaching scheme for SCs and OBCs from 2024-25. 
All beneficiaries of the PM- CARES Children Scheme are eligible to avail the benefits of the 
scheme irrespective of income whereas the ceiling of the total family income for SC and 
OBC students is ₹8 lakh per annum. The Committee find that the target fixed for 2023-24 
and 2024-25 could not be achieved even after the inclusion of PM-CARES Children Scheme. 
The requirement for coaching support is well recognized particularly among students 
belonging to the educationally disadvantaged sections of Scheduled Castes and Other 
Backward Classes. It is also well known that the coaching is an expensive affair and 
everybody cannot avail coaching due to its high cost, hence the candidates from Scheduled 
Castes and Other Backward Classes aspiring for coaching cannot avail this facility even if 
the annual income of family is more than ₹ 8 Lakh. The Committee strongly feel that the 
total family income criterion fixed for SC and OBC students may be one of the major hurdles 
preventing candidates from these categories from availing the benefits. The Committee also 
does not understand the reason for fixing a target of 7000, which is double the previous 
target, particularly when the target fixed earlier is not being achieved. The Committee desire 
that the family income ceiling should be re-examined so that a large number of candidates 
can avail the facility and scheme becomes more effective.

but subject to the availability of budget. The Committee feel that non-fixing of target become 
meaningless if the budget is not appropriately enhanced as the budget available for 2026-
2027 is ₹120 crore whereas actual expenditure incurred in 2024-2025 was ₹103.26 crore. The 
Committee are of the view, that the Scheme will give much benefit to the candidates if the 
Budget is  enhanced to accommodate all applicants getting admission in selected 
Institutions. Hence, the changes proposed, if implemented, may prove superfluous unless 
the budget is substantially enhanced, as the available budget can only accommodate a 
limited number of students and not all eligible candidates. The Committee also find that the 
family income ceiling of ₹8 lakh fixed in 2020-21 may also be one of the constraints for 
students intending to avail scholarship under the scheme. The Committee would also like 
to draw the attention to the fact that the Department is spending the allocated budget 
despite receiving less applications than the target fixed for previous years. This sufficiently 
indicates that the income eligibility criteria need to be revised to attract more applicants, as 
it has been more than five years since the ceiling was revised from ₹6 lakh to ₹8 lakh. The 
students with the income eligibility limit of ₹8 lakh may still not be in a position to bear the 
burden of the high fees charged by such institutions. The Committee are sure that the 
Department will understand and take substantive measures to enhance the income ceiling 
and annual budget to extend the benefits of the scheme to the maximum number of needy 
candidates securing admission in to premier selected Institutions.  
 
4.41 The Committee are happy to note that the slots of National Overseas Scholarships

facility of direct transfer of scholarship amounts to students’ bank accounts and there is no

Nomadic Tribes, Landless Agricultural Laborers and Traditional Artisans have remained 
unutilised over the years due to financial constraints. The Committee recommend that the

7.5 The objective of Pre-Matric Scholarship for OBC, EBC and DNT students (who are not 
covered under SC, ST or OBC) is welfare and motivation to the children of these categories by 
providing financial assistance at pre-matriculation or secondary stage. Scholarship under the 
Scheme will be available for studies in India only. The Salient Features of the scheme are as 
follows: 
 
(i) The scheme is implemented through respective State Governments/ UTs. 
(ii) The scholarship will be provided for students studying in class IXth and Xth on a full-
time basis in Government School only. 
(iii) Parents /guardians income from all sources does not exceed Rs.2,50,000/- per 
annum.  
(iv) The students shall be given a consolidated academic allowance of Rs. 4000/- per

CONSTRUCTION OF HOSTELS FOR OBC BOYS AND GIRLS 
7.22 The scheme aims at providing hostel facilities to students belonging to socially and 
educationally backward classes, especially from rural areas to enable them to pursue secondary 
and higher education in the Govt. schools, universities, institutions and institutes located at a 
reasonable distance in order to provide them greater access to high quality education. The 
scheme as such mainly targets the students hailing from remote areas and from poor families 
as well who generally discontinue their studies either because no secondary school and college 

70  
is available near their location or due to non-availability of adequate hostel facilities at a 
reasonable cost at places where such educational institutions are located. The Salient features 
of the Scheme are as follows: 
 
(i) The scheme is implemented through respective States/UTs in respect of State 
Universities. For Central University, the grant is given directly to the institution. 
(ii) Agencies eligible for financial assistance under the revised Scheme will be as follows:- 
a) State Government and UT Administrations; 
b) The institution of repute ranked in the latest NIRF rankings of Ministry of 
Education subject to following conditions:- 
- The institution shall provide free of cost land within the campus.  
- The institution shall undertake to complete construction within 24 
months.  
- The institution shall maintain the hostel after construction as per 
standards prescribed.  
- The institution shall agree to provide as many hostel seats to the new 
OBC students as per the sanction order of the hostel. However, this 
additional accommodation shall be regulated in other hostels as well 
to avoid the hostel being designated as 'OBC hostel' exclusively i.e. if 
a hostel with 100 capacity has been sanctioned under this scheme, 
the institution shall provide 100 additional seats to the OBC students, 
but it can be spread over in all other hostels. 
(iii) Agencies eligible for assistance under the Scheme should have clear title and 
possession over the land where the hostel is to be constructed, and will submit 
proposals in the prescribed formats. 
(iv) The cost of construction of hostels for boys has to be shared between the Centre 
and the State in 60:40 ratio (in case of Girls Hostels 90%   of cost will be borne by 
the Central Govt). In case of Union Territories, the Central Assistance shall be 100% 
and for North Eastern States and 02 Himalayan States (Himachal Pradesh and 
Uttarakhand), it shall be 90%.  
(v) For the Institution of repute ranked in the latest NIRF rankings of Ministry of 
Education, Govt. of India, the Central Assistance shall be 100% irrespective of 
regional States.   
(vi) The construction work of the Hostel has to be completed within eighteen months 
from award of work order or two years from the release of 1st installment of Central 
assistance, whichever is earlier. In no case the time will be extended beyond 2 
years.   
(vii)  The cost per hostel seat in different areas are as follows:-   
- North Eastern Region/Himalayan Regions  -   ₹3.50 lakh per seat 
- Rest of country  -    ₹3.00 lakh per seat, or as per the schedule of the rates 

71  
for the concerned State Government, whichever is lower.  
(ix) The amount of grant will be released in 3 installments in 50:45:5 ratio, of which 5% 
grant will be released after completion of the work and occupation of the rooms by 
the OBC Boys and Girls.  
(x) There will be a one-time non-recurring grant of ₹2500/- per seat for providing 
furniture/equipment to the hostels constructed under the Scheme.

7.25 On being asked, the purpose of having separate hostel for OBC students the Department 
of Social Justice and Empowerment submitted vide written reply that: 
 
“The provision of separate hostels for OBC students is primarily intended as a 
supportive measure to improve access to education for students from socially and 
educationally disadvantaged backgrounds, particularly those coming from remote or 
economically weaker families. If seats reserved for OBC remains vacant, it would be 
filled up in following preference:-  
 
(a) De-notified Tribes (DNT) students 
(b) Scheduled Tribes (ST) Students 
(c) Scheduled Caste (SC) Students 
(d) Economically Weaker Section (EWS) Students 
 
The objective is not segregation, but facilitation of safe, affordable accommodation 
where such facilities are otherwise inadequate. Care is taken to ensure that these 
hostels function as part of the larger campus ecosystem, with students participating 
fully in academic, cultural and extracurricular activities alongside their peers. 
Institutions are advised to promote inclusivity and equal opportunity so that no sense 

73  
of isolation or stigma arises. The overall approach is developmental and welfare-
oriented, aimed at strengthening educational participation without compromising 
social integration.”

ATAL VAYO ABHYUDAY YOJANA (AVYAY) 
The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (MoSJE) is the nodal Ministry for the 
welfare of senior citizens. In alignment with the principles of active and healthy ageing and to 
ensure dignified living for the elderly, the Ministry is implementing the umbrella scheme Atal 
Vayo Abhyudaya Yojana (AVYAY). AVYAY seeks to provide comprehensive and integrated 
services through the convergence of various interventions targeted at different aspects of elderly 
welfare ranging from shelter and medical care to assistive devices, helpline services and 
caregiver training. The components under AVYAY are as follows: - 
 
INTEGRATED PROGRAMME FOR SENIOR CITIZENS (IPSRC) 
 
8.2 The objective of the IPSrC is to enhance the quality of life of senior citizens, particularly 
indigent elderly persons, by providing essential amenities such as shelter, food, medical care 
and recreational facilities, while promoting productive and active ageing. Under the scheme, 
financial assistance in the form of grant-in-aid is provided to eligible implementing agencies, 
including NGOs and voluntary organizations, for the operation and maintenance of various 
facilities, including Senior Citizens’ Homes for 25 beneficiaries, Senior Citizens’ Homes for 50 
beneficiaries (male only or combined male and female), Senior Citizens’ Homes exclusively for 
50 elderly women, Continuous Care Homes and homes for senior citizens suffering from 
Alzheimer’s disease or dementia for a minimum of 20 beneficiaries, Regional Resource and 
Training Centers, and Mobile Medicare Units (MMUs) and Physiotherapy Clinics for senior 
citizens. 
 
STATE ACTION PLAN FOR SENIOR CITIZENS (SAPSRC) 
 
8.3 The State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC) is a component of the Atal Vayo 
Abhudaya Yojana (AVYAY) under which financial assistance is provided to States and Union 
Territories to implement state-specific initiatives for the welfare of senior citizens, with a primary 
focus on cataract surgeries for senior citizens, training of geriatric caregivers, awareness 
generation about the scheme, and any other activities considered appropriate by the States/UTs 
to improve the well-being of senior citizens. 
 
RASHTRIYA VAYOSHRI YOJANA (RVY) 
 
8.4 Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (RVY) was first announced in the 2015-16 Union Budget 
speech by the finance minister and subsequently launched on 01st April, 2017. The objective of 
the scheme is to provide eligible senior citizens suffering from any of the age-related disability/ 
infirmity, with assisted living devices which can restore near normalcy in their bodily functions, 

81  
overcoming the disability/ infirmity manifested such as low vision, hearing impairment, loss of 
teeth and loco-motor disabilities. 
  
ELDERLINE: NATIONAL HELPLINE FOR SENIOR CITIZENS (NHSC) 
 
8.5 National Helpline for Senior Citizens was dedicated to the Nation on 01st October, 2021 
on the occasion of the International Day of Older Persons, with the toll-free number 14567. 
Elderline operates for 12 hours a day (8:00 AM to 8:00 PM) and provides free information, 
guidance, emotional support and field intervention in cases of abuse, neglect and rescue. The 
National Institute of Social Defence, an autonomous body under MoSJE, is the nodal agency for 
implementation of Elderline across the country. 
 
 
SENIOR CARE AGEING GROWTH ENGINE (SAGE) 
 
8.6 SAGE portal was dedicated to the Nation on 01 October 2021. The scheme aims to 
encourage entrepreneurs and start-ups to develop innovative solutions to address challenges 
faced by the elderly. Under the scheme, Government of India support is provided to start-ups 
through equity participation of up to 49%, with assistance up to ₹1 crore, to promote elder-care 
innovations. 
 
SYSTEM FOR PROVISION OF ELDERCARE IN-HOUSE AND ASSISTED LIVING (PM 
SPECIAL)/ TRAINING OF GERIATRIC CARE GIVERS 
 
8.7 The main objective of the PM Special (Training of Geriatric Care Givers) scheme is to 
bridge the gap in supply and increasing demand in the field of geriatric caregivers so as to 
provide more professional services to the senior citizens and also to create a cadre of 
professional caregivers in the field of geriatrics. The Scheme will ensure sufficient availability of 
dedicated, professionally trained manpower of geriatric caregivers who can attend to diverse 
and dynamic needs of elderly population relating to recreation and holistic well-being. So far, 
35,480 trainees have been imparted training under the scheme.

NATIONAL ACTION FOR MECHANIZED SANITATION ECOSYSTEM (NAMASTE) 
 
NAMASTE, a joint initiative of the MoSJE and the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs 
(MoHUA), is being implemented since FY 2023-24. The scheme envisages safety and dignity of 
sanitation workers by creating an enabling ecosystem that recognizes sanitation workers as one 
of the key contributors in operations and maintenance of sanitation infrastructure thereby 
enhancing their occupational safety through capacity building and improved access to safety 
gear and machines. Components of erstwhile Self Employment Scheme for Rehabilitation of 
Manual Scavengers (SRMS) have been subsumed in NAMASTE scheme from 2023-24. Further, 
waste pickers have been added as target group under NAMASTE in FY 2024-25. The Key 
Interventions of the scheme are as follows:- 
 
A. Sewer and Septic tank Workers (SSWs) 
Interventions  
(i) Profiling of Sewer/ Septic Tank workers. 
(ii) Distribution of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) kits to identified 
SSWs 
(iii) Distribution of safety devices to ERSUs 
(iv) Imparting occupational safety training to SSWs 
(v) Health insurance coverage of SSWs and manual scavengers and their 
dependents under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY (after profiling of SSWs by 
ULBs) 
(vi) Upfront capital subsidy upto ₹7.50 Lakh for individual projects costing upto 
₹ 15.00 lakh for sanitation related projects and capital subsidy of ₹25.00 
Lakh for a group project of five sanitation workers including manual 
scavengers for a project costing ₹ 50.00 Lakh. 
(vii)Appointment of Responsible Sanitation Authority (RSA) in every District. 
(viii)Constitution of Emergency Response Sanitation Unit (ERSU) in larger 
Urban Local Bodies (ULB) of Districts to cover Urban and Rural areas.  
(ix) Operationalization of Helpline number (14420). 
(x) IEC Campaign: Targeting Safai Mitras, Operators/ Contractors and 
Citizensthrough RWAs etc. through print and digital media, innovative 
media interventions and by organizing workshops/webinars 
  

91  
B.  Waste pickers  
Waste Pickers have been included as an additional target group along with sewer 
septic tank workers under NAMASTE scheme. The identified waste pickers would 
later on be provided occupational/ up gradation training for waste value addition. The 
scheme has various components for welfare of waste pickers namely profiling, Health 
Insurance under AB-PMJAY, Social Security Benefits, Occupational Safety Training, 
PPE Kits, Capital Subsidy for Dry Waste Collection Centres (DWCCs) to procure 
Waste Collection Vehicles and other welfare measures. The waste pickers 
component of the Scheme intends to integrate waste pickers in the solid waste 
management by addressing challenges, difficulties faced by them by mainstreaming 
them in the society, recognizing and strengthening their contribution in waste 
collection, recovery & recycling, providing safe, dignified and sustainable livelihood 
by empowering them through various interventions. Department of Social Justice & 
Empowerment, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, National Health Authority, 
National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation, Ayushman 
Bharat Empanelled Hospitals and Waste Pickers are the major stakeholders. The 
core objective of the Scheme is to ensure that waste pickers live dignified lives and 
work in a safe environment by enabling their enumeration, recognition and integration 
into the solid waste management value chain, while ensuring access to social security 
and welfare schemes based on their occupational identity.

10.9 The Committee note that the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment is 
the custodian of civic rights and is responsible for the prevention of atrocities against 
Scheduled Castes. Funds are allocated to the Department for strengthening of machinery 
for enforcement of protection of Civil Rights Act,1955 and Prevention of Atrocities Act 
,1989. The Department has accordingly taken measures over the years for the functioning 
and strengthening of Protection Cells and Special Police Stations as envisaged under 
these Acts. The Committee however find that though almost all States/UTs have set up 
Protection Cells but only 181 Special Police Stations have been established in only 7 
States/UTs. The Committee are shocked to know that even after more than three decades 
of enactment of PoA Act only 7 States/UTs have such police stations. The Committee are 
of the opinion that the setting up of Special Police Stations by the States/UTs is essential 
as victims first approach police station for protection at the time of atrocity and it is 
assumed these police stations would be able to address such situations more effectively.  
The Committee do not find any information regarding setting up of Special Courts also. 
The regular meetings with the State government over the years have also failed to yield 
the desired result. 
The Committee note that the Department has submitted a proposal before the 
Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) to enhance the relief amounts and provide 
financial support to States for setting up Special Police Stations and Special Courts. The 
Committee hope that the Department should have incorporated the expected increase in 
funds for the year 2026-27 so that there is no shortage of funds to meet the expenditure 
likely to increase with the approval of the proposal. The Committee accordingly desire 
that the Department should take necessary steps to ensure that the proposal is approved 
at the earliest so that it is implemented from 2026-27 itself and the machinery is 
adequately strengthened.
