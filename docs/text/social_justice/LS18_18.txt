CHAPTER – I 
INTRODUCTORY 
The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (DEPwD) was carved out 
of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment on 12 May, 2012 with the objective 
of giving focused attention to policy issues and providing a meaningful thrust to 
activities aimed at the welfare and empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). 
Subsequently, on 8 December, 2014, the Department of Disability Affairs was renamed 
as the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities. 
 
1.2 The Department acts as the nodal agency for matters relating to disability and 
persons with disabilities and is responsible for coordinating with various stakeholders 
including Central Ministries, State Governments, Union Territory Administrations and 
voluntary organizations working in the disability sector. The Department is primarily 
entrusted with the task of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities. The subjects, 
allocated to the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (DEPwD) as 
per Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules are:- 
i) The following subjects which fall within List I-Union List of the Seventh Schedule 
to the Constitution: 
ii) Indo-US, Indo-UK, Indo-German, Indo-Swiss and Indo-Swedish Agreements for 
Duty-free import of donated relief goods/supplies and matters connected with 
the distribution of such supplies. 
iii)  The following subjects which fall within List-III - Concurrent List of the Seventh 
Schedule to the Constitution (as regards Legislation only): 
iv)  "Social Security and Social Insurance, save to the extent, allotted to any other 
Department"  
v) For the Union territories, the following subjects which fall in List II -State List or 
List III Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution, in so far as 
they exist in regard to such territories:  

2 
 
vi)  "Relief of the Disabled and the unemployable; Social Security and Social 
Insurance, save to the extent allotted to any other Department". 
vii)  To act as the nodal Department for matters pertaining to Disability and Persons 
with Disabilities.  
1.3 The Department also administers important legislations governing different 
aspects of disability for the welfare and empowerment of persons with disabilities. 
These include the following: 
i. The Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992 
ii. The National Trust for the Welfare of Persons with Autism, Cerebral Palsy, 
Mental Retardation and Multiple Disabilities Act, 1999 
iii. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 
1.4 To discharge its mandate effectively, the Department operates through a network 
of Statutory Bodies, National Institutes, and Public Sector Undertakings: 
Statutory Bodies 
 Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI): Established to regulate and oversee training 
programs for professionals in rehabilitation and special education, maintaining 
the Central Rehabilitation Register (CRR). 
 The Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities (CCPD): Set up under 
Section 74(1) of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, to safeguard 
the rights of PwDs and look into complaints regarding the deprivation of such 
rights. 
 The National Trust: An autonomous body discharging legal duties through Local 
Level Committees (LLC) and welfare duties through various schemes for persons 
with Autism, Cerebral Palsy, Mental Retardation, and Multiple Disabilities.  
 
 

3

The Department oversees several specialized National Institutes located across the 
country, namely: 
i. Ali Yavar Jung National Institute of Speech and Hearing Disabilities (Divyangjan) 
(AYNISHD), Mumbai 
ii. National Institute for the Empowerment of Persons with Intellectual Disabilities 
(Divyangjan) (NIEPID), Secunderabad 
iii. National Institute for the Empowerment of Persons with Visual Disabilities 
(Divyangjan) (NIEPVD), Dehradun 
iv. National Institute of Mental Health Rehabilitation (NIMHR), Sehore, Madhya 
Pradesh 
v. Swami Vivekanand National Institute of Rehabilitation Training and Research 
(SVNIRTAR), Cuttack

vii. Pt. Deendayal Upadhyaya National Institute for Persons with Physical Disabilities 
(Divyangjan) (PDUNIPPD), New Delhi 
viii. National Institute for Empowerment of Persons with Multiple Disabilities 
(Divyangjan) (NIEPMD), Chennai 
ix. Atal Bihari Vajpayee Center for Disability Sports (CDS), Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh 
x. Indian Sign Language Research and Training Centre (ISLRTC), New Delhi. 
 
1.5 In addition to its statutory and research frameworks, the Department exercises 
administrative control over the following Central Public Sector Undertakings (CPSUs), 
which are tasked with providing financial assistance and manufacturing high-quality 
rehabilitative aids: 
i. National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation (NDFDC) 
ii. Artificial Limbs Manufacturing Corporation of India (ALIMCO), Kanpur

1.7 The Committee sought to ascertain from the Department whether there exists an 
updated and reliable estimate of the population of persons with disabilities in the 
country, particularly in view of the fact that the currently available figures are based on 
the Census of 2011 which is estimated at 2.68 crores and that the scope of recognized 
disabilities has since been expanded under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities 

5 
 
(RPwD) Act, 2016. The Department, during the course of evidence before the 
Committee, deposed as under: 
“When we talk about the exact population, the most common data to be harped 
upon is the 2011 census data. At that point in time, the 2011 census data it was 
2.68 cr. But now there are lot many caveats regarding these figures. Firstly, when 
the 2011 census was done, we had PWD Act 1995 in vogue. Our present RPWD 
Act came in vogue in 2016. In 2011, it was PWD Act, 1995 which was 
operational, and that Act mentioned only seven disabilities. Whereas right now, 
since 2016, we are legally recognizing 21 disabilities. That is the first point, there 
are 21 number of disabilities. ******** Now we have again requested the 
Registrar General of India to take into account 21 types of disabilities and to 
consult us whenever they feel like, so that the forthcoming census, whenever the 
results are out, we can rely upon those figures”.  
1.8 The Committee sought to know the mechanism adopted by the Department for 
preparing Budget Estimates in the absence of updated or latest data on the population 
of persons with disabilities. The Committee also desired to know whether the 
Government has any interim mechanism to estimate and track the changing 
demographics of persons with disabilities in the absence of updated census data. In 
reply, the Department submitted as under: 
"The Central Sector schemes being implemented by this Department namely 
SIPDA, DDRS, ADIP and Scholarship are demand driven and funds are disbursed 
based on the proposals received from States/UTs/Institutions/NGOs and 
individuals. The budget estimates are therefore prepared on the basis of previous 
years’ performance and expected demands rather than the population of persons 
with disabilities."  
 
1.9 The Committee note that the presently available estimate of persons 
with disabilities in the country is based on the Census of 2011, which 

6 
 
recorded the number at 2.68 crore. The Committee further note the 
submission made by the representative of the Department during the course 
of evidence that while around 1.35 crore Unique Disability Identity (UDID) 
cards have been issued so far, the Department has also requested the 
Registrar General of India to take into account all the 21 categories of 
disabilities recognized under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) 
Act, 2016 while conducting the forthcoming Census. In this context, the 
Committee recommend that the Department may impress upon the Registrar 
General of India the need to ensure accurate and comprehensive 
enumeration of all categories of disabilities, particularly those disabilities 
which may not visible or have physical symptoms. The Committee are of the 
view that a robust and reliable database, supplemented by the UDID 
framework, would greatly assist in evidence-based policy formulation and 
more effective targeting of welfare schemes for persons with disabilities.

2.3 The Committee sought to know the details of the Budget Estimates proposed by 
the Department for the year 2026–27. In reply, the Department submitted as under:  
“The Department had proposed a Budget Estimate (BE) of ₹1773.74 crore for the 
financial year 2026–27. However, the Ministry of Finance has approved an 
allocation of ₹1669.72 crore for BE 2026–27.” 
2.4 On being asked how the Department proposes to meet the gap between the 
estimates, proposed and allocated, the DEPwD submitted as below:-  
“Although the allocation is lower than the proposal submitted by the 
Department, it represents an overall increase of 30.95% over the BE of 2025–26. 
Therefore, in comparison with the BE 2025–26, the Department has received an 
enhanced budget allocation for FY 2026–27. 
The reduction in the proposed BE may be attributed to overall fiscal 
prioritization and resource constraints. The scheme-wise allocations are 
provisional at this stage, as the Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) proposals 
for 16th Finance Commission Cycle in respect of the schemes of the Department 
are currently under process. The final scheme-wise allocation will be determined 
after the completion of the EFC appraisal and approval process. 
The Department proposes to meet any shortfall between the proposed 
and approved estimates through the phased implementation of activities, 
wherever feasible; and submission of proposal to the Ministry of Finance at the 
Revised Estimates (RE) stage for seeking additional funds, if required, based on 
the pace of implementation and actual fund requirement”.  
2.5 The Committee further sought to know whether the enhanced Budget Estimates 
(BE) for the year 2026–27 adequately meet the financial requirements for the effective 
implementation of various schemes of the Department. In their written replies the 
Department has submitted that: 

12 
 
“The Budget Estimate (BE) 2026–27 has been enhanced by 30.95% as compared 
to the previous year, keeping in view the expanding scope of activities, 
introduction of new schemes, and the need for strengthening implementation of 
ongoing schemes. Further the enhanced BE has been formulated after Review of 
past expenditure trends (including BE/RE 2025–26), Assessment of committed 
liabilities and spill-over components, Projection of physical and financial targets 
under each scheme, Provision for newly announced schemes and expansion of 
coverage. The enhanced allocation is assessed to be adequate for effective 
implementation of the Department’s programmes during 2026–27”.  
2.6 On being enquired as to what specific steps are proposed by the Department to 
improve the lives of persons with disability during the year 2026–27. In response, the 
Department submitted as under: 
a) Strengthened Financial Planning & Monitoring: Preparation of Quarterly 
Expenditure Plans (QEPs) and Monthly Expenditure Plans (MEPs). 
b) Regular review meetings with States/UTs/Organizations to monitor 
physical and financial progress. 
c) Close monitoring of pending utilization certificates (UCs) to ensure timely 
release of subsequent installments. 
d) Timely sanction and release of funds to implementing agencies. 
e) Digital Monitoring & Transparency through UDID portal, NSP Portal, 
ARJUN MIS Portal etc. 
f) Focused monitoring of implementation of schemes in North-Eastern 
Region (NER) and aspirational districts.  
2.7 Regarding the thrust areas of the Annual Plan for the year 2026–27 and the 
roadmap for the implementation of the said Annual Plan, the Department submitted the 
following details: 
“The thrust areas of the Annual Plan 2026–27 of the Department of 
Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities are focused on strengthening 

13 
 
inclusion, accessibility, rehabilitation, skill development and social 
empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). The major priority 
areas include continuation and expansion of ongoing flagship programmes 
and infrastructure development initiatives. 
The Department is implementing various Central Sector schemes such as 
SIPDA, DDRS, ADIP and Scholarship Schemes, which are demand-driven 
in nature. Funds are released to implementing agencies based on 
proposals received from States/UTs, institutions, NGOs and individual 
beneficiaries. The roadmap includes: 
a) Strengthening coordination with States/UTs/ implementing 
agencies/NGOs/other bodies for timely submission and approval of 
proposals; 
b) Regular review meetings with States/UTs and stakeholders to monitor 
progress and resolve bottlenecks; 
c) Capacity building and awareness programmes for effective outreach; and 
d) Periodic evaluation and outcome-based monitoring of schemes”.  
 
2.8 In response to a query regarding the criteria adopted by the Department for 
allocation of funds across various schemes under its purview and the methodology 
followed for fixing physical targets for implementation of these schemes so as to ensure 
effective utilization of resources and achievement of intended objectives, the 
Department submitted as under: 
“The Budget Estimates in respect of the Central Sector Schemes of the 
Department namely ADIP, SIPDA, DDRS and Scholarship have been broadly 
recommended by the Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) upto 31.03.2026. All 
the four schemes are appraised and approved with financial allocation and 
physical targets for the entire period of the ongoing 15th Finance Commission’s 
recommendations. Along with EFC recommendation, past expenditure trends are 

14 
 
also taken into consideration while allocating budget outlays under these 
schemes. 
Based on the previous year’s actual expenditure, achievement of physical targets 
and EFC approved allocations, the physical targets are fixed for individual 
schemes. However, in case of additional demands under schemes, the targets 
are revised on the basis of demand for aids and assistive devices, camp requests 
of VIPs, number of scholarship claims received on NSP Portal and proposals from 
Statement Government/ training partners”.  
2.9 On observing that the Actual Expenditure in the past several years fell short of

undertaken by the Department to ensure more effective utilization of funds and 
improved outcomes under its schemes. In reply, the Department submitted as under:  
The key shortcomings identified in previous years include: 
a) Under-utilization of funds in initial quarters leading to expenditure 
concentration in the last quarter. 
b) Delay in submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs) by 
States/UTs/implementing agencies, affecting timely release of subsequent 
instalments. 
c) Late receipt of proposals from States/UTs/other implementing agencies 
d) Procedural delays in release of funds due to issues concerning onboarding 
of States/UTs/ organization to TSA-Hybrid Model. 
2.10 The Department has informed that the following the ameliorative steps or new 
initiatives are being taken by the Department to improve the utilization of fund 
allocations and program execution during the year 2026–27. In reply, the Department 
submitted as under: 

15 
 
(a)   The department regularly reviews the schemes guidelines to simply the

(b)   Regular Review Meetings with States/UTs/ implementing agencies 
(c)   All the Schemes of the Department onboarded on TSA-Hybrid Model”. 
2.11 The Committee find that in the past few years the Actual Expenditure 
of the Department has consistently fallen short of the Revised Estimates. In 
2023–24, against the Revised Estimates of ₹1225.01 crore, the Actual 
Expenditure stood at ₹1143.89 crore (about 93.37 per cent). Similarly, in 
2024–25, against the Revised Estimates of ₹1167.27 crore, the Actual 
Expenditure was ₹1112.61 crore (about 95.32 per cent). In the current 
financial year 2025–26, the Actual Expenditure as on 26.02.2026 is ₹1008.49 
crore against the Revised Estimates of ₹1291.60 crore, which is only about 
78 per cent of the Revised Estimates. The Committee are of the view that 
such gaps between allocations and actual expenditure point to structural 
issues in planning, timely receipt of proposals, and coordination with 
implementing agencies. While the Department has indicated certain 
administrative constraints such as delay in submission of Utilization 
Certificates and late receipt of proposals from States/UTs, the Committee 
feel that these issues require a more systematic and proactive approach.   
 The Committee therefore recommend that the Department strengthen 
its expenditure management framework by instituting a more rigorous 
system of quarterly expenditure targets, real-time monitoring of fund 
utilisation, and advance identification of implementable proposals from 
States/UTs and implementing agencies. The Committee also urge the 
Department to establish strict timelines for submission of Utilization 
Certificates and proposals, and to introduce digital tracking mechanisms and 
periodic review meetings with States/UTs to ensure that procedural delays 
do not hinder timely release and utilisation of funds. The Committee are of 

16 
 
the firm view that such measures would help prevent concentration of 
expenditure in the last quarter of the financial year and ensure that the 
funds allocated translate into tangible and timely benefits for persons with 
disabilities. 
2.12 The Committee note that the major share of the Department’s scheme 
expenditure is concentrated in a few key demand-driven schemes such as 
ADIP, SIPDA, DDRS and the Scholarship Schemes, which together accounted 
for ₹741.80 crore in BE 2025–26 and have been allocated ₹1117.00 crore in 
BE 2026–27. The Committee observe that the effectiveness of these schemes 
depends significantly on timely submission of proposals, completion of 
documentation and submission of Utilization Certificates by States/UTs and 
implementing agencies. However, delays in these processes have been cited 
by the Department itself as major reasons for under-utilisation of funds in 
previous years The Committee therefore recommend that the Department 
put in place a structured mechanism for advance planning of proposals, 
periodic engagement with State Governments and implementing agencies, 
and a streamlined process for verification of documentation so that funds 
earmarked under these major schemes are released and utilised in a timely 
manner. The Committee further recommend that the Department may 
strengthen the monitoring system for tracking proposals, approvals and fund 
releases under each scheme, thereby ensuring greater transparency and 
efficiency in implementation. 
2.13 The Committee note that the Budget Estimates for the year 2026–27 
have been significantly enhanced to ₹1669.72 crore, reflecting an increase of 
about 30.96 per cent over the Budget Estimates of ₹1275.00 crore for the 
year 2025–26. While welcoming the enhanced allocation, the Committee 
emphasise that the increased outlay must lead to measurable improvements

ASSISTANCE TO PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES FOR PURCHASE/FITTING  
OF AIDS/APPLIANCES (ADIP) 
 
 The Scheme of Assistance to Persons with Disabilities for Purchase/Fitting of Aids 
/Appliances (ADIP Scheme), launched in 1981 by the Government of India, aims to 
empower persons with disabilities (PwDs) by supplying scientifically manufactured, 
durable, modern, and cost effective aids and assistive devices that promote their 
physical, social and psychological rehabilitation, reduce the effects of disabilities, and 
enhance beneficiaries’ educational and economic potential. The Scheme was last revised 
w.e.f. 26.09.2024 and the major modifications undertaken are as under: 
a) “Since, ADIP is 100% central Sector Scheme, the requirement of State 
Government recommendations has been removed for receiving proposals. 
b) Data of assessment of beneficiaries and distribution of aids & assistive 
devices has been made mandatory through ARJUN Portal. Also beneficiaries 
can register themselves on the ARJUN Portal for requisite aids and appliance. 
ARJUN Portal is developed and maintained by ALIMCO, a CPSE under this 
Department ensures proper implementation and monitoring of the Scheme. 
c) The Department has taken a major step of opening Pradhan Mantri Divyasha 
Kendra (PMDK) of ALIMCO. These PMDKs are the walk-in centres where 
eligible beneficiary can directly avail required aids and appliances free of cost 
by visiting these centers.  
d) Eligibility of high-end Prosthesis has been reduced from 80% disability to 
40% disability. 
e) Under ADIP SSA eligibility for Motorized Tricycle has been relaxed 80% 
disability to 40% disability. 

19 
 
f) Benefits of ADIP SSA has also been extended to students studying through 
home Schooling and Special Schools. 
g) Age of Children criteria for availing aids and assistive devices every one year 
has been increased from 12 years to 18 years for customized items. 
h) Provision for self-certification of income by beneficiaries themselves has been 
introduced. 
i) In case of amputees without any disability certificate may also be provided 
with the benefit of Artificial Limbs only after the recommendation of P&O 
officials of ALIMCO/NIs/CRCs and enrolment for UDID Card. 
j) Inclusion of Motorized Tricycle with Li-Iron-Phosphate batteries in ADIP 
scheme with subsidy up to Rs 50,000.00 in line with recent advancement and 
towards green environment.  
k) Inclusion of Folding Joystick wheelchair in ADIP scheme with subsidy up to 
₹50,000/- 
l) Inclusion of Active Folding Wheelchair in ADIP scheme for high activity 
Divyangjan.”

CHAPTER-IV 
DEENDAYAL DIVYANGJAN REHABILITATION SCHEME (DDRS) 
 
  The Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme is a central sector scheme of 
the Department to provide grant-in-aid to non-governmental organizations (NGOs) for 
projects relating to rehabilitation of persons with disabilities aimed at enabling them to 
reach and maintain their optimal, physical, sensory, intellectual, psychiatric or socio-
functional levels.  
4.2  The Grant-in-aid is provided to Implementing agencies (IAs) which are registered 
with NGO-Darpan Portal of NITI Aayog and have obtained unique ID of NITI Aayog. 
The Grant-in-Aid (GIA) is provided through e-Anudaan portal of the Department. All 
financial assistance is provided to the Implementing agencies through Public Financial 
Management System (PFMS).  
There are eight model projects under the DDRS Scheme.  
i. Cross Disability Pre-Schools and Early Intervention with option for Home 
Based Rehabilitation & Community-Based Rehabilitation Project  
ii. Special School for the Children with Hearing Disability with option for 
Home Based Rehabilitation & Community-Based Rehabilitation Project  
iii. Special School for the children with Visual Disability (including Deaf 
blindness) with option for Home Based Rehabilitation and Community 
Based Rehabilitation Project and Low Vision Centre Project. 
iv. Special School for the children with other disabilities (ID/CP/ASD/MD/ 
Muscular Dystrophy, Deaf blindness etc.) with option for Home Based 
Rehabilitation and Community-Based Rehabilitation Project. 
v. Rehabilitation of Leprosy Cured Persons with option for Home-Based 
Rehabilitation & Community-Based Rehabilitation Project. 

31 
 
vi. Half Way Home for Psycho-Social Rehabilitation of Treated and Controlled 
individuals with Mental Illness with option for Home-Based Rehabilitation 
& Community-Based Rehabilitation Project vii. Preparatory / Remediation 
Centre for Children with Specific Learning Disabilities to continue Inclusive 
Education Project. 
vii. Cross-Disability Therapy and Counseling Centre Project. 
Activities/components admissible for grant under DDRS include:  
Honorarium to staff, Transportation of beneficiaries, Stipend for 
beneficiaries/hostel maintenance Cost of raw materials, and Contingencies to 
meet office expenses, electricity & water charges, rent, furniture, equipment etc.  
The eligibility conditions for Grants under DDRS are as under:  
Organizations registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 (XXI of 1860) 
or any relevant Act of the State/Union Territory; or A Trust registered under the 
Indian Trust Act, 1882 or any other similar Act for the time being in force; or, A 
Not-for-Profit company registered under Companies Act, 2013 or any relevant Act 
for the time being in force. 
Registration should have been in force for at least 2 years at the time of applying 
for grant under this scheme.  
a. Must be registered in the NITI Aayog Portal (NGO-Darpan) Portal and 
obtain Unique ID of NGO-Darpan before applying for Grant under the 
scheme.  
b. Must be registered either under erstwhile Persons with Disabilities Act, 
1995 or Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.

4.4  District Disability Rehabilitation Centre (DDRC)

NATIONAL ACTION PLAN FOR SKILL TRAINING UNDER SIPDA 
 
The National Action Plan for Skill Development of Persons with Disabilities (NAP-
SDP) is a component under the Scheme for Implementation of Rights of Persons with 
Disabilities Act 2016 (SIPDA) and was launched in March 2015 with the objective to 
enhance skills of Persons with Disabilities, enable them to have gainful employment/ 
become self-employed and to make them self-reliant and independent members of 
society. Training under NAP-SDP is being provided through various Government and 
Non-Government Organizations. The common norms prescribed by the Ministry of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE) for skill training are being followed. PwDs 
with at least 40% disability in the age group of 15-59 years are eligible to avail skill 
training programme. The scheme is being implemented through PM-DAKSH-DEPwD 
portal.

placement and is released on 70% verified employment (continuous employment of 3 
months-desk and verified placement as per standard norms), with at least 50% 
minimum wage employment of the certified trainees.

The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (DEPwD) is 
implementing the Central Sector Umbrella Scheme of Scholarship for Students with 
Disabilities.  The main objectives of these scholarship schemes are to 
empower students with disabilities to study further in order to prepare themselves for 
earning their livelihood and  find a dignified place for themselves in the society, as they 
face several barriers-physical, financial, psychological in pursuing studies and living a 
dignified life. 
7.2  The scheme consists of the following six components :- 
a) Pre-matric (for classes IX and X). 
b)  Post-matric (for classes XI to postgraduate degree/diploma). 
c) Higher education (for undergraduate and postgraduate degree/diploma in 
notified institutions of excellence in education). 
d) National Fellowship (for M.Phil and Ph.D. in Indian universities). 
e) National Overseas Scholarship (for postgraduate degree/doctorate from foreign 
universities). 
f) Free coaching (for government recruitment examinations and entrance 
examinations to technical and professional courses).

disability i.e. 40% or more disability as defined in ‘Rights of Persons with 
Disabilities Act, 2016

7.10  The Committee find that the financial allocation under the Scholarship 
Scheme for Students with Disabilities has remained largely stable during the 
period under review, with the Budget Estimates ranging between ₹142.68 
crore and ₹155.00 crore during 2023–24 to 2026–27. However, the 
Committee observe that the physical achievement under the Scheme has 
consistently fallen short of the prescribed targets. Against a target of 43,520 
beneficiaries, only 29,374 beneficiaries were covered in 2023–24 (about 67.5 
per cent) and 23,085 beneficiaries in 2024–25 (about 53 per cent). Similarly, 
17,378 beneficiaries have been covered so far in 2025–26, indicating a 
continuing gap between the intended outreach and actual coverage. The 
Committee note the Department’s explanation that stricter verification 

65 
 
through e-KYC authentication, UDID linkage and the National Scholarship 
Portal has led to elimination of duplicate or ineligible applications. While 
appreciating the steps taken to improve transparency in the system, the 
Committee recommend that the Department intensify awareness campaigns, 
coordination with States/UTs and monitoring of pendency at the State Nodal 
Officer level, so that a larger number of eligible students with disabilities are 
able to access the benefits of the Scheme. 
7.11  The Committee note the Department’s proposal to introduce a uniform 
lump-sum scholarship structure for certain components of the Scheme from 
the next EFC cycle (2026–27 onwards) with the objective of simplifying 
calculations and expediting disbursement. The Committee also note that 
delays in verification and processing at various stages, particularly due to 
pendency of applications and prolonged scrutiny at the level of State Nodal 
Officers, have adversely affected the timely release of scholarships in several 
cases. The Committee are of the view that scholarships constitute a critical 
support mechanism enabling students with disabilities to pursue education 
without financial hardship. The Committee therefore recommend that while 
finalising the revised framework, the Department ensure that the scholarship 
amounts remain adequate to meet actual educational expenses and that 
appropriate mechanisms are put in place to minimise processing delays at 
the State level, so that the simplified disbursement system results in faster 
and more predictable release of funds to eligible beneficiaries.

NEW SCHEMES INTRODUCED IN THE YEAR 2026–27 
 
The Department has introduced two new schemes during the year 2026–27, namely 
Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana and Divyangjan Sahara Yojana, with allocations of ₹200 
crore and ₹100 crore respectively.  
Objectives of the Schemes 
The Department in their written have stated that Divyangjan Sahara Yojana is a newly 
introduced scheme during FY 2026-27 aimed at strengthening the assistive device 
ecosystem for Persons with Disabilities (Divyangjan) through enhanced provisioning of 
high-quality assistive aids and appliances, including modern and advanced assistive 
technologies. The Scheme will be implemented through ALIMCO a CPSE under this 
Department. The proposal is under consultation stage with Stakeholders However, the 
objective proposed by ALIMCO is as under: 
(i) Scaling up ALIMCO Manufacturing and R&D Capacity: With the 
increased demand for high-end assistive devices under the scheme, 
scaling up ALIMCO’s manufacturing capacity is essential to ensure timely 
production and supply. 
(ii) Strengthening and Expansion of Pradhan Mantri Divyasha–Vayoshri 
Kendras (PMD-VKs): With an endeavour to enhance outreach and to 
provide assistive devices to Divyangjan and Senior Citizens nearer to their 
doorstep with minimum waiting time, PMD-VKs are being opened across 
the country. As on date, 100 PMD-VKs have already been established. 
(iii) Support for Establishment of Assistive Technology Marts (AT-Marts): 
ALIMCO is in the process of establishing Retail Assistive Technology Marts 
(AT-Marts) to provide high-end, good quality assistive devices to 
Divyangjan and Senior Citizens.

CHAPTER-XI 
 
MONITORING MECHANISM 
The Department implements a number of schemes involving substantial financial 
outlays for the welfare and empowerment of persons with disabilities. In this context, 
effective monitoring and oversight mechanisms assume importance to ensure that funds 
released under various schemes are properly utilized and that the intended objectives of 
the schemes are achieved. 
11.2  In this regard, the Committee sought to know the monitoring mechanism put in 
place by the Department to prevent misuse of funds and to ensure optimal utilization of 
financial resources under its various schemes. The Committee also sought to know how 
effective these mechanisms have been in actual practice. In reply, the Department 
submitted as under: 
 The monitoring mechanism in the Department to prevent misuse of funds and to 
ensure optimal utilization of financial resources to achieve the intended objectives of 
various schemes  
is given below: 
a. Monitoring of the Scheme is undertaken regularly at the level of the Secretary 
(DEPwD). 
b. The organizations have to furnish an audited utilization certificate in time in 
respect of the previous grant (s).   
c. Implementing agencies are required to maintain an updated official website and 
upload complete details of beneficiaries, including particulars of aids and assistive 
devices distributed, on their website as well as on the ARJUN Portal. 
d. Proposals of NGOs are being received and processed online on e-Anudaan Portal. 
e. Mandatory Registration of NGOs on NITI Aayog Portal (NGO Darpan). 

81 
 
f. ‘Arjun Portal facilitates near real-time Monitoring of beneficiary data and also 
prevent & check duplication of beneficiaries. The Implementing Agencies have to 
mandatorily upload the data of beneficiaries on ARJUN Portal. Further, duplicacy is 
also checked through UDID card which has been mandatory for beneficiary. 
g. Sample checking of beneficiaries against the utilisation of Grant-in-Aid released 
to Implementing Agencies by nearest NI/CRC/ALIMCO or by the Department of 
Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities if required. Sample Checking covers at 
least 2% of aids & assistive devices provided to the beneficiaries. 
i. Periodic field inspections and sample checks by the Central Monitoring Unit(CPMU) 
team are conducted to verify distribution of aids and appliances and beneficiary 
coverage. 
j. The regular follow ups are made with States/UTs for submission of pending 
utilization certificates (UCs) by writing letters, Video Conferencing (VCs) and 
telephonic correspondences so that any proposal with complete documents might 
not be kept on hold for want of pending UCs. Due to these efforts, the pending UCs 
have been reduced. 
k. Under Pre Matric, Post Matric & Top class Education, the Department monitors the 
times lines of verification of applications, application status and through the NSP 
portal on regular basis for proactive intervention if bottlenecks are identified. 
l. National Fellowship for PwDs in implemented through DEPwD and disbursement 
through Canara Bank on DBT Mode. NDFDC has been nominated as Implementing 
Agency and CNA for smooth implementation of NFPwD Scheme. Further, 
m. National Overseas Scholarship, the scheme is implemented by DEPwD and 
disbursement through MEA and the Department ensures that scholarship funds are 
disbursed in a timely manner and effectively utilized, enabling PwD students to 
pursue their education abroad without financial barriers or delays.

 11.3  Replying to the queries on the challenges faced in the execution and monitoring 
of the schemes implemented through State Governments/UT Administrations, Non-
Governmental Organizations and other agencies, and the measures being adopted to 
overcome these constraints the Department has furnished the following details: 
ADIP Scheme: Under the ADIP Scheme, certain operational challenges are 
experienced due to its implementation through multiple agencies such as 
NGOs/National Institutes/CRCs and ALIMCO. These include delays in 
submission of proposal for grants in aid, Utilization Certificates, difficulty in 
organizing distribution camps in remote areas/hilly areas, and ensuring 
timely and accurate uploading of beneficiary data due to high-speed 
internet facility. 
To address these issues, the Department has strengthened digital 
monitoring through UDID card, the ARJUN and e-Anudaan portals, 
mandated NGO Darpan registration, enforced submission of audited UCs 
before release of further grants, and undertaken periodic reviews and 
sample verifications through NIs/CRCs/ALIMCO. These measures have 
improved transparency, streamlined fund flow, and enhanced accountability 
in execution of the Scheme. 
SIPDA Scheme: Owing to the demand driven nature of the Creation of 
Barrier Free Environment (CBFE) component, fund disbursement is 
constrained by the actual demand from State Governments, the timely 
submission of appropriate proposals and documentation, and the receipt of 
Utilization Certificates from implementing agencies. This demand-driven 
nature presents a distinctive challenge in target-setting and budgetary 
projections, as allocations cannot be pre-determined independent of 
demonstrated state engagement and proposal quality. Estimates and 
targets for the CBFE/AIC component shall be recalibrated at the beginning 
of each financial year based on historical expenditure trends and 

83 
 
demonstrated state absorptive capacity recognizing the inherent constraints 
of demand-driven disbursement. The Department recognizes that rigid 
allocation and fixed target-setting methodologies have created persistent 
mismatches between budgetary projections and actual utilization; 
consequently, Revised Estimates shall be arrived based on the 
implementable commitments within the financial year, ensuring alignment 
between allocations and achievable outcomes given the demand-driven 
nature of the scheme. The Department commits to demonstrating sustained 
improvement in fund utilization through reduced proposal-to-sanction 
timelines, timely UC submissions, accelerated project completion rates, and 
enhanced state participation, with measurable progress being periodically 
reviewed to ensure accountability in advancing accessibility objectives 
across all government buildings in all states and UTs under the increasingly 
integrated and data-driven implementation framework. 
Scholarships: The Department is not facing any challenges/constraints in 
execution and monitoring of the Schemes. However, the scholarship scheme 
is a Central Sector Scheme and implemented pan India basis and is a 
demand-driven scheme. To cover all area across the country, the 
Department publishes advertisements in leading Newspapers across the 
country twice to increase awareness of these schemes amongst students 
and other stakeholders All other types of media and social media platforms 
are also used for spreading awareness. The Chief Secretaries/Principal 
Secretaries of the States are also addressed to spread awareness of the 
schemes through various mediums of communication in their respective 
States/UTs. Also, the department is actively organizing meetings with State 
Nodal Officers (SNOs) to ensure effective implementation of the schemes.

11.4 The Committee find that the Department has put in place several 
monitoring mechanisms such as ARJUN Portal, e-Anudaan, UDID integration, 
NGO-Darpan registration, field inspections and periodic review at the level of 
Secretary (DEPwD) to ensure transparency and proper utilisation of funds. 
However, the Committee also note that implementation of several schemes 
continue to face operational challenges such as delays in submission of 
proposals and utilisation certificates by implementing agencies, gaps in 
timely uploading of beneficiary data and logistical constraints in conducting 
camps and outreach activities in remote and hilly areas. The Committee are 
of the view that such procedural bottlenecks weaken the efficiency of 
otherwise well-designed monitoring systems. The Committee therefore 
recommend that the Department institutionalise a time-bound compliance 
framework for implementing agencies, strengthen digital integration of 
monitoring platforms such as ARJUN, UDID and e-Anudaan, and establish a 
structured follow-up mechanism with States/UTs and implementing agencies 
so that delays in documentation, reporting and fund utilisation are minimized 
and scheme benefits reach beneficiaries in a timely manner. 
11.5  The Committee note that several schemes of the Department, 
particularly ADIP and SIPDA, are implemented through a large network of 
States/UTs, NGOs, National Institutes, CRCs and other agencies, which poses 
inherent challenges in ensuring uniform standards of implementation and 
monitoring. The Committee further observe that issues such as delayed 
proposals, weak absorptive capacity at the State level, pending utilisation 
certificates and infrastructure or connectivity constraints in certain regions 
continue to affect the pace of scheme implementation. The Committee are of 
the view that addressing these structural constraints requires stronger 
institutional coordination and capacity building. The Committee therefore 
recommend that the Department develop a comprehensive monitoring and 
capacity-building framework for implementing agencies, including regular 

85 
 
performance reviews, targeted training for State and NGO partners, and a 
robust data-driven dashboard for real-time tracking of scheme 
implementation. Such measures would help improve accountability, 
strengthen implementation capacity across States/UTs and ensure that 
public funds allocated for the welfare of persons with disabilities are utilised 
efficiently and deliver measurable outcomes.

organisations, to promote establishment of DDRS 
projects and DDRCs in such underserved areas. The 
peoples representatives should also be involved to 
generate awareness and better implementation of the 
scheme. Focused outreach and facilitation in these 
regions would help ensure more equitable availability 
of rehabilitation services across the country and 
enable a larger number of Divyangjan to benefit from 
the Scheme. 
12. 5.7 The Committee note that the budgetary allocation and 
expenditure under the Scheme for Implementation of 
the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 
(SIPDA) have shown significant volatility during the 
last 3 financial years While the Budget Estimates of 
₹150.00 crore in 2023–24 were reduced sharply to 
₹67.00 crore at the Revised Estimates stage, in 2025–
26 the allocation was enhanced from ₹115.10 crore to 
₹200.00 crore, indicating considerable fluctuation in 
financial planning. The Committee further note that in 
2024–25 the Actual Expenditure stood at ₹44.16 crore 
against the Revised Estimates of ₹111.00 crore, 
reflecting utilisation of less than 40 per cent of the 
available allocation. The Committee are of the view 
that such instability and under-utilisation under an 
umbrella scheme meant for implementing statutory 
obligations under the RPwD Act, 2016 may adversely 

98 
 
affect the continuity of key interventions relating to 
accessibility, skill development and UDID 
implementation. The Committee therefore recommend 
that the Department adopt more realistic budgeting 
and stronger advance appraisal of proposals, along 
with closer monitoring of utilisation certificates and 
project readiness at the State/UT level, so that 
allocations under SIPDA are utilised in a timely and 
effective manner. 
13. 5.8 The Committee observe that actual expenditure under 
SIPDA has frequently fallen short of the allocated 
funds, with the Department attributing these shortfalls 
to transitions in the Central Nodal Agency (CNA) and 
Treasury Single Account (TSA) models, as well as 
delays in the submission of Utilization Certificates 
(UCs) by States/UTs. While appreciating the corrective 
measures taken by the Department, including revised 
proformas and digital processing of proposals, the 
Committee recommend that the Department 
strengthen its coordination with States/UTs and 
implementing agencies through regular reviews and 
advance identification of projects. This should also 
include providing dedicated technical support to States 
and implementing agencies to ensure their financial 
systems are fully integrated with the TSA/CNA 
framework. This would help ensure timely submission 
of proposals and more effective utilisation of funds for 
improving accessibility, rehabilitation services and 
overall empowerment of persons with disabilities. 
14. 6.6 The Committee observe with concern that the physical 
achievements under the National Action Plan for Skill 
Development of Persons with Disabilities (NAP-SDP) 
have remained significantly below the set targets over 
the last three years The performance data reveals a 
widening gap: against a target of 17,000 in 2023–24, 
the Department trained 9,333 beneficiaries; this fell 
sharply to 836 trainees against a target of 15,000 in 
2024–25, and further to only 2,264 beneficiaries 

99 
 
against a target of 10,000 in 2025–26 (as of January 
2026). While the Committee acknowledge the 
administrative challenges posed by the transition to 
the PM-DAKSH-DEPwD portal and the Hybrid Treasury 
Single Account (TSA) model, the continued shortfall in 
achieving even 25% of the set targets is a matter of 
serious concern. With the newly announced 
Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana, the Committee 
recommend that the Department adopt a more robust, 
industry-linked approach to bridge the gap between 
training and gainful employment. It is essential that 
the Department prioritize the placement of certified 
trainees by strengthening partnerships with private 
employers and ensuring that training modules remain 
aligned with current market demands. The Committee 
further suggest that the targets for the 2026–27 fiscal 
year be monitored quarterly to ensure that the 
transition to the new Yojana results in a measurable 
improvement in both the number of beneficiaries 
trained and their subsequent placement in sustainable 
employment. 
15. 7.11 The Committee find that the financial allocation under 
the Scholarship Scheme for Students with Disabilities 
has remained largely stable during the period under 
review, with the Budget Estimates ranging between 
₹142.68 crore and ₹155.00 crore during 2023–24 to 
2026–27. However, the Committee observe that the 
physical achievement under the Scheme has 
consistently fallen short of the prescribed targets. 
Against a target of 43,520 beneficiaries, only 29,374 
beneficiaries were covered in 2023–24 (about 67.5 per 
cent) and 23,085 beneficiaries in 2024–25 (about 53 
per cent). Similarly, 17,378 beneficiaries have been 
covered so far in 2025–26, indicating a continuing gap 
between the intended outreach and actual coverage. 
The Committee note the Department’s explanation 
that stricter verification through e-KYC authentication, 
UDID linkage and the National Scholarship Portal has 

100 
 
led to elimination of duplicate or ineligible 
applications. While appreciating the steps taken to 
improve transparency in the system, the Committee 
recommend that the Department intensify awareness 
campaigns, coordination with States/UTs and 
monitoring of pendency at the State Nodal Officer 
level, so that a larger number of eligible students with 
disabilities are able to access the benefits of the 
Scheme.

7.12 The Committee note the Department’s proposal to 
introduce a uniform lump-sum scholarship structure 
for certain components of the Scheme from the next 
EFC cycle (2026–27 onwards) with the objective of 
simplifying calculations and expediting disbursement. 
The Committee also note that delays in verification and 
processing at various stages, particularly due to

hardship. The Committee therefore recommend that 
while finalising the revised framework, the 
Department ensure that the scholarship amounts 
remain adequate to meet actual educational expenses 
and that appropriate mechanisms are put in place to 
minimise processing delays at the State level, so that 
the simplified disbursement system results in faster 
and more predictable release of funds to eligible 
beneficiaries. 
17. 8.5 The Committee express their displeasure with the

has been able to utilize only 50% of the funds 
allocated in the year 2023-24 & 2024-25. The 
Committee note that the Centre for Disability Sports

18. 9.9 The Committee note that the National Institutes and 
Composite Regional Centres (CRCs) play a crucial role 
in human resource development, research and 
rehabilitation services in the disability sector. 
However, the Committee observe that a significant gap 
exists between the sanctioned strength and the 
present staff strength in many of the Institutes and 
CRCs. For instance, institutions such as SVNIRTAR, 
Cuttack (115 against 197 sanctioned posts) and 
AYJNISHD, Mumbai (121 against 173 sanctioned 
posts) are functioning with substantial vacancies. 
Similarly, several CRCs are operating with limited 
manpower and in some cases posts remain entirely 
vacant. The Committee are of the view that such 
shortages may affect the quality of training, research 
and rehabilitation services being provided by these 
institutions. The Committee therefore recommend that 
the Department pursue the matter with the Ministry of 
Finance for early approval of creation and revival of 
posts and simultaneously take steps to expedite 
recruitment to existing vacancies, so that the National 
Institutes and CRCs are adequately staffed to 
effectively discharge their expanding mandate under 

102 
 
the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. 
19. 9.10 The Committee appreciate that the National Institutes 
are undertaking several important research initiatives 
in the disability sector, including the development of 
tools such as the NIEPID Indian Test of Intelligence 
(NITI), assistive technologies and innovations in 
rehabilitation sciences. The Committee also note that 
financial support for research and development is 
being extended through the SIPDA component on 
priority areas of disability. The Committee are of the 
view that the National Institutes should increasingly 
function as centres of excellence in research, 
innovation and assistive technology development. The 
Committee therefore recommend that the Department 
encourage greater collaboration of the National 
Institutes with premier scientific institutions, 
universities and technology agencies, so that 
indigenous assistive technologies, rehabilitation tools 
and evidence-based practices are developed and 
widely disseminated for the benefit of persons with 
disabilities. 
20. 10.5 The Committee welcome the introduction of the two 
new schemes during 2026–27, namely Divyangjan 
Kaushal Yojana and Divyangjan Sahara Yojana, with 
allocations of ₹200 crore and ₹100 crore respectively, 
aimed at strengthening skill development and the 
assistive device ecosystem for persons with 
disabilities. However, the Committee note that the 
detailed modalities of implementation, including 
operational guidelines and physical targets, are still 
under formulation. The Committee are of the view that 
early finalisation of the scheme framework is essential 
to ensure that the budgetary allocations translate into 
tangible outcomes. The Committee therefore 
recommend that the Department expedite the 
finalisation of guidelines and put in place a clear 
implementation framework with measurable targets 

103 
 
relating to skill training, employment opportunities 
and assistive device distribution, so that the schemes 
are operationalised effectively and deliver meaningful 
benefits to Divyangjan. 
21. 11.4 The Committee find that the Department has put in 
place several monitoring mechanisms such as ARJUN 
Portal, e-Anudaan, UDID integration, NGO-Darpan 
registration, field inspections and periodic review at 
the level of Secretary (DEPwD) to ensure transparency 
and proper utilisation of funds. However, the 
Committee also note that implementation of several 
schemes continue to face operational challenges such 
as delays in submission of proposals and utilisation 
certificates by implementing agencies, gaps in timely 
uploading of beneficiary data and logistical constraints 
in conducting camps and outreach activities in remote 
and hilly areas. The Committee are of the view that 
such procedural bottlenecks weaken the efficiency of 
otherwise well-designed monitoring systems. The 
Committee therefore recommend that the Department 
institutionalise a time-bound compliance framework 
for implementing agencies, strengthen digital 
integration of monitoring platforms such as ARJUN, 
UDID and e-Anudaan, and establish a structured 
follow-up mechanism with States/UTs and 
implementing agencies so that delays in 
documentation, reporting and fund utilisation are 
minimized and scheme benefits reach beneficiaries in a 
timely manner.

Department, particularly ADIP and SIPDA, are 
implemented through a large network of States/UTs, 
NGOs, National Institutes, CRCs and other agencies, 
which poses inherent challenges in ensuring uniform

Committee further observe that issues such as delayed 
proposals, weak absorptive capacity at the State level, 

104 
 
pending utilisation certificates and infrastructure or

therefore recommend that the Department develop a 
comprehensive monitoring and capacity-building 
framework for implementing agencies, including 
regular performance reviews, targeted training for 
State and NGO partners, and a robust data-driven

across States/UTs and ensure that public funds 
allocated for the welfare of persons with disabilities 
are utilised efficiently and deliver measurable
