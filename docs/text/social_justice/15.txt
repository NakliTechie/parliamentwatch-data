A. TRIBAL RESEARCH INSTITUTES (TRIs) 
 
Recommendation (Para No. 3.14) 
 
5. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
“The Committee appreciate the initiative of Ministry of Tribal Affairs for 
establishing of ‘Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums’ as they acknowledge the 

2 
 
heroic and patriotic deeds of tribal idols who are otherwise often under-
represented in the mainstream history. The Committee note that the Ministry 
have so far supported 11 Tribal Freedom Fighters’ museums in 10 States to 
celebrate the lives and deeds of tribal heroes and inculcate a spirit of pride in the 
minds of tribal communities. However, the Committee note that out of 11 
sanctioned Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums by the Ministry, only 3, namely, 
‘Bhagwan Birsa Munda Memorial Freedom Fighter Museum’ at Ranchi, 
Jharkhand, ‘Badal Bhoi State Tribal Freedom Fighters Museum, Chhindwara and 
‘Raja Shankar Shah and Kunwar Raghunath Shah Freedom Fighter Museum’, 
Jabalpur, have been inaugurated. The Committee would like to underline the 
slow progress in construction of remaining 8 museums, as they were sanctioned 
far back in 2017-18, 2018-19 and 2019-20, such as, Tribal Freedom Fighter 
Museums in Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Telengana, Gujarat and Mizoram 
which are yet to be completed. The Committee are also concerned for the Tribal 
Freedom Fighters’ Museums sanctioned for Kerala, Manipur and Goa in 2017-
2018, 2018-19 and 2020-21 respectively as they are still at DPR stage even after 
the lapse of several years. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Ministry of Tribal Affairs may evolve a strategy for timely completion of these 
Museums. The Committee would like the Ministry of Tribal Affairs to ensure that 
the four Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums scheduled to be completed by 
November, 2025 and one by May, 2026, are completed by the stipulated date” 
 
 
6. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as 
under:- 
 “The projects for construction of Tribal Freedom Fighter Museums are 
sanctioned by Ministry of Tribal Affairs after the same is received from the 
State. The project is implemented by the State and could include state 
components beyond the Ministry. The identification of the land, floating of 
tender, selection of Project Management consultant and the implementation of 
civil and curation work are the responsibility of the State Govt. The Ministry 
releases the funds to the state taking into account the status on submission of 
utilization certificate and project Progress Report which is a continuous process. 
For early completion of TFFMs, the Ministry of Tribal Affairs is taking various 
steps such as regular visits by nodal officers of the Ministry to the construction 
site of TFFMs to monitor/inspect the progress of construction work, holding of 
frequent review meetings/ Video Conferences at Secretary/Joint Secretary level 
with Tribal Welfare Department/Tribal Research institute(s) of State 
Government in which TFFM is under construction. Apart from this support is 
also sought from experts and institutions” 
 
7.  While appreciating the initiative of the Government to establish eleven  
‘Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums’ in 10 States to acknowledge the heroic and 
patriotic deeds of tribal idols, the Committee in their original Report had noted 
slow progress in construction of 8 museums as they were sanctioned far back in 
2017-18, 2018-19 and 2019-20 to the extent that 3 of the Museums were at DPR 

3 
 
stage. The Committee had, accordingly, recommended the Ministry to evolve a 
strategy for timely completion of these Museums and expected that the four 
Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums scheduled to be completed by November, 
2025 and one by May, 2026, are completed by the stipulated date. The Committee, 
are, however, not satisfied with the action taken reply as they expected that the 
Ministry would at least assure the Committee that they will ensure that the four 
Tribal Freedom Fighters’ Museums scheduled to be completed by November, 
2025 and one by May, 2026, would be completed as stipulated but the Committee 
do not find any such assurance from the Ministry. The Committee would, 
therefore, like to reiterate their earlier recommendation and desire that the 
Ministry should meet the targets and the timelines once fixed for establishing the 
Museums to avoid delay and cost escalation. The Committee would also like the 
Ministry to consider only those proposals where the State Governments are in a 
position to readily identify land, float tenders, select the Project Management 
Consultant etc. at the time of submission of proposal. In this regard, the Ministry 
may consider revising guidelines to fix the time limits for floating of tender, 
selection of Project Management consultant and the implementation of civil and 
curation work so that the State Governments are mandated to complete these 
formalities in the given timeframe.  The Committee would also like to be informed 
about the status of under-construction museums at Final Action Taken stage. 
 
B. EKLAVYA MODEL RESIDENTIAL SCHOOL (EMRS) 
 
    Recommendation (Para No.4.17) 
 
8. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
“The Committee note that 720 Eklavya Model Residential Schools (EMRS) have 
been sanctioned out of 728 targeted to be set up in the country to provide quality 
education to the tribal children from class VI to XII in their own environment in 
remote areas to enable them access to the best opportunities in education and to 
bring them at par with the general population. The Committee also find that an 
allocation of ₹7,088.60 crore has been made for the year 2025-26 for EMRS 
Scheme which is 47 percent of the total budget of the Ministry. The Committee 
find that under the Scheme, out of budgetary allocation of ₹5,943.00 crore and 
₹6,399.00 crore in the years 2023-24 and 2024-25 ₹2,471.81 crore and 
₹4,748.92 crore (till 17.02.2025) were utilized respectively. The Ministry attributed 
the factors for low utilization of funds during the past two years to non-availability 

4 
 
of land for construction of schools, recruitment of staff, capacity building, digital 
learning facilities, etc. The Committee urge that concerted efforts are required to 
be made by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs to overcome these hurdles so that the 
funds allocated for the Scheme are utilized fully and objective of the Scheme to 
provide quality education to Tribal students is achieved.” 
 
9.  The Ministry of Tribal Affairs, in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as 
under:- 
 
 “During F.Y. 2023-24 and F.Y. 2024-25, 99.98 % of the revised budget allocation 
(Rs. 2471.81 Crore) for FY 2023-24 and 99.34% of the revised budget allocation 
(Rs. 4748.92 Crore) for FY 2024-25 was utilized respectively. Some of the steps 
concerning construction, human resources, and students’ learning etc taken to 
ensure maximum utilization of funds are as under: 
 
1. Regular review meetings with construction agencies, state officials, and 
district collectors to address land and forest issues. 
2. Ongoing communication with state and district officials through letters to 
resolve land-related challenges. 
3. Active coordination with state governments on legal, ownership, and forest-
related matters; 67 land issues resolved in 2024–25. 
4. A detailed school-wise tracker was implemented for monitoring project 
completion. 
5. Administrative approvals and DPR processes streamlined, cutting approval 
time by half. 
6. Underperforming agencies replaced by CPWD and state agencies. 
7. Over 200 engineers were oriented through a national-level workshop. 
8. Land and construction issues discussed in Manthan Shivir 2024 and PM 
JANMAN 2025. 
9. Daily labour monitoring started in August 2024, doubling monthly progress 
from 2% to 4% 
10. Payment processes improved via escrow accounts to ensure contractor 
fund flow. 
11. Show cause notices issued to agencies for poor quality and delays. 
12. Quality control measures include: 
 
o Retired civil consultants for oversight 
o Third-Party Quality Assurance (TPQA) 
o CBRI for final-stage structural checks 
o Physical verification by state officials and dedicated district committees 
13. Through a direct recruitment exam, viz. EMRS Staff Selection Exam-2023, 
9878 candidates have been posted in EMRSs across the country. Further 
proposal for the recruitment of staff is under consideration.  
14. Academic programs are being conducted for EMRS Students to enhanced 
digital learning facilities. EMRS DTH channel, namely PM e-Vidya has been 
launched, implementation of smart classrooms with internet connectivity etc 
have been implemented in EMRSs”

Recommendation (Para No. 4.18) 
 
10. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
“Taking the fact that out of 477 EMRS, 341 are functional in their own Buildings, 
the Committee desire that all the EMRS are made functional from their own 
buildings and not from rented or other Government buildings as these buildings 
may be lacking required infrastructure for a school. Since the Ministry have 
sufficient funds to set up their own infrastructure, the Committee also desire that 
a comprehensive action plan should be chalked out to make them functional 
from their own buildings and the construction work of each EMRS is completed 
in a stipulated period of 2-3 years. The Committee expect that the steps taken 
by the Ministry to make all Schools functional would yield the desired results 
only if the timelines are fixed to sort out the bottlenecks and their timelines are 
not compromised”. 
 
11.  The Ministry of Tribal Affairs, in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as 
under:- 
“The Ministry takes note of the Committee's observation and agrees that setting 
clear timelines and ensuring their strict adherence is critical for making all 
schools functional. Efforts are underway to identify bottlenecks and implement 
time-bound action plans to address them effectively. However, it is pertinent to 
mention that many schools have been constructed and made functional in last 
one year and Ministry is committed to make all efforts to achieve target of 
making all approved schools functional. The achievements of EMRSs (From 
June 2024 to April 2025) in this regard is summarized as under:   
 Locations Approved -16 
 EMRSs have been made functional- 73 
 Construction Completed- 70 
Consequently, as on date locations for 722 EMRSs have been approved and 
477 EMRSs are functional across the country.”  
 
12. The Committee find that only 477 Eklavya Model Residential Schools 
(EMRS) have been made functional across the country out of 722  such schools 
approved by the Ministry. Further, out of 477 schools made functional only 341 
are functioning from their own buildings. Acknowledging the hindrances that 
have been crippling the progress of construction, the Committee had 
recommended in their original report that the issues such as non-availability of 
land for construction, recruitment of staff, capacity building, digital learning 
bottlenecks etc, may be concertedly addressed by the Ministry to achieve the 
objectives of the Scheme and also to chalk out a comprehensive action plan with 
specific timeline fixed therein to make all operational EMRS schools functional 

6 
 
from their own building.  The Committee appreciate the steps taken by the 
Ministry concerning construction, human resources, and students’ learning etc. 
to ensure maximum utilization of funds.  However, still, the Committee observe 
that non-availability of land is an important issue which the Ministry need to sort 
out on priority with the State Governments. The Committee, are therefore, of the 
strong view that in future the proposal of setting up of EMRS from State 
Governments should be considered by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs only when 
they ensure beforehand, the availability of land required for the purpose. The 
Committee expect that the Ministry will deliberate on this issue urgently. The 
Committee would also like the Ministry to address the other issues which are 
hampering the proper implementation of this scheme and apprise them of the 
steps taken in this regard at Final Action Taken stage.   
 
C.  UPGRADATION OF OLD EKLAVYA MODEL RESIDENTIAL SCHOOLS 
 (EMRS) 
 
Recommendation (Para No. 4.21) 
 
13. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
 “The Committee find that out of 728 approved EMRS, 288 schools are 
sanctioned/established under Grants under Article 275(1) of the Constitution at 
the cost of ₹5.00 crore each. The “Committee may like to draw the attention of 
the Ministry of Tribal Affairs to their visit of an EMRS in Karnataka, perhaps 
established under old Scheme. The Committee found that the facilities such as 
food, mattress, utensils’ including maintenance of the building were not up to 
the mark. The Committee find that many EMRS have been running without 
basic facilities and required infrastructure such as compound wall, labs, sports 
facility, additional classrooms, staff quarter, hostel blocks, furniture etc. To 
provide these facilities, a survey of such schools was done and 211 EMRS were 
identified for upgradation out of which 167 EMRSs have been approved till date. 
The Committee feel that the EMRS set up under old scheme needs to be 
revamped in a stipulated time period and sufficient funds are sanctioned to 
them in tune with the funds being sanctioned for each new EMRS. Further, the 
day- to- day hindrances faced by Schools should be managed by the schools 
themselves and the management should be suitably decentralized so that day- 
to- day issues are not brought to them and NESTS is able to concentrate on the 
larger responsibilities” 
  
 
 
 

7 
 
 
14.  The Ministry of Tribal Affairs, in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted as 
under:- 
 
“The Ministry acknowledges the concerns of the Committee and concurs that 
EMRSs established under Article 275(1) require systematic revamping. In this 
regard, out of 211 schools, proposals for the upgradation of 192 schools have 
been approved by NESTS. Additionally, the Ministry of Tribal Affairs has 
sanctioned funds to the respective State Governments based on the proposals 
received to bridge infrastructure gaps, such as residential quarters, classrooms, 
and boys’ and girls’ hostels. 
A comprehensive assessment of these schools is currently underway to identify 
areas requiring further improvement. Necessary proposals will be taken to ensure 
that these institutions receive funding on par with newly established EMRSs and 
MoF will be approached. 
Further, in order to empower State Societies and Schools to take certain 
decisions independently, NESTS has been issuing various circulars and 
guidelines, such as streamlining fund release procedure, guidelines for 
admissions, vacations, engagement of outsourced guest staff etc. These 
guidelines/circulars are also uploaded on NESTS website for wider outreach.”  
 
15.  In their Original report, the Committee had desired that 288 schools 
sanctioned/established under old scheme through Grants under Article 275 (1) of 
the Constitution needs to be revamped in a stipulated time period and sufficient 
funds are sanctioned to them in tune with the funds being sanctioned for each 
new EMRS and the day- to- day hindrances faced by Schools should be managed 
by the schools themselves by decentralizing the management. The Committee are 
happy to note that the Ministry in their action taken reply have acknowledged the 
concerns of the Committee and have taken suitable action to address the issues. 
The Ministry have also informed that a comprehensive assessment of these 
schools is currently underway to identify areas requiring further improvement 
and after the completion of this process, necessary proposal would be taken to 
ensure that these institutions receive funding at par with newly established 
EMRSs and, also, Ministry of Finance will be approached. In this regard, the 
Committee, would like to urge the Ministry of Tribal Affairs to set a timeline for 
up-gradation of old schools since without a specific timeframe even the best of 
intentions result in inordinate delays and such delays, once systemic, would 
defeat the very purpose of construction of EMRS. The Committee would like to be 
apprised about the completion of on-going assessment of schools and timeline 
fixed with regard to up-gradation of old EMRS schools at Final Action Taken 
stage by the Ministry.

Recommendation (Para No. 2.10) 
 
The Committee note that the Ministry of Tribal Affairs have been allocated ₹14,925.81 
crore by the Ministry of Finance against the proposed allocation of ₹13,424.24 crore for 
the financial year 2025-26. Since Budgetary allocation for 2025-26 is higher as 
compared to previous year, the Ministry owes a greater responsibility for overall 
development and welfare of the tribal population through various Schemes and expect 
that the Ministry will utilize the funds fully towards all the schemes and programmes that 
are under the purview of the Ministry. The Committee hope that the trend of previous 
years will not be continued as ₹7,287.77 crore could only be spent out of the budgetary 
allocation of ₹8,451.92 crore and ₹7,545.23 crore out of budgetary allocation of 
₹12,461.88 crore during 2022-23 and 2023-24 respectively. The Committee further note 
that the Ministry have spent ₹8,119.51 crore by 6th February, 2025 out of budgetary 
allocation of ₹13,000 crore for the year 2024-25. The Committee understand that the 
reasons for low utilization of funds in previous years and downwards allocation at RE 
stage are due to various reasons such as SNA balances, non-submission of utilization 
certificates, technical issues with PFMS etc. The Committee strongly believe that the 
Ministry of Tribal Affairs need to strictly implement the measures to overcome the 
difficulties so that the funds earmarked for each scheme are spent by States/UTs in 
implementation of the various schemes and are not parked unutilised. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Ministry should ensure that the funds allocated to the 
Ministry for 2024-25 are fully utilized and the enhanced allocation made for 2025-26 is 
not revised downward at RE stage but fruitfully utilized in achieving the objectives of all 
the Schemes. 
 
Reply of the Government 
 
The budgetary allocation of Rs.13000 crores for the year 2024-25 was reduced to Rs. 
10,237.33 crores at RE stage and 99.37% of the RE allocation i.e. Rs. 10,173.21 crores 
has been utilized. To ensure optimum utilization of funds released to the States/UTs 
under the schemes, Ministry will conduct meetings/ conferences with the State/UT 
Governments at regular intervals besides written communications and will strive for 
complete utilization of funds through further follow up actions to avoid reduction at RE 
stage. 
 
Ministry of Tribal Affairs OM No. F.No.16015/03/2025-PC {E file No. 29655} dated 
12.06.2025 
 
Recommendation (Para No. 3.13) 
 
The Committee are happy to note that 28 Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs) set up in 
various States in the country are administrated by the State Governments and 
supported by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs in their infrastructural needs, research and 
documentation activities, training & capacity building programmes, organization of tribal 

9 
 
festivals/ yatras for promotion of unique cultural heritage and in promotion of tourism 
and organization of exchange visits by tribals so that the tribal cultural practices, 
languages and rituals are preserved and disseminated. The Committee find that 
budgeted estimates for 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 were revised downward at RE 
stage from ₹121.00 crore to ₹58.50 crore, ₹118.64 crore to ₹50.00 crore and ₹111.00 
crore to ₹90.00 crore respectively. However, the revised estimate could not be spent 
fully during these years. The Committee believe that mere allocations of funds would 
not ensure effective implementation of TRI Scheme, the Ministry of Tribal Affairs should 
pursue with the State/UT Governments and other Implementing Agencies so that 
annual Action Plan from State/UT Governments are timely received to achieve the 
purpose for which the Scheme came into existence. They would also like the Ministry to 
immediately resolve other issues such as delay in submission of Utilization Certificates 
(UCs), clearance of SNA balances, submission of progress report and DPR etc by the 
State Authorities. Thus, the Committee, recommend that the Ministry should take 
requisite measures to remove technical and procedural bottlenecks, administrative 
delays, capacity building issues, etc. The Committee also desire that regular review 
meetings with State Governments at periodic intervals are held with the objective of 
augmenting the usage of funds. The Committee would like to be informed of the action 
taken by the Ministry in this regard. 
 
Reply of the Government 
 
Ministry of Tribal Affairs extends financial support to 29 State/UT Tribal Research 
lnstitute(s) under the scheme "Support to Tribal Research Institute(s)". Under the 
scheme, funds are provided to the State/UT Tribal Research lnstitutes (TRls) on the 
basis of Annual Action Plan submitted by them, subject to the approval of the Apex 
Committee in the Ministry. With a view to ensure timely approval of the proposals by the 
Apex Committee and thereby enabling State/UT TRls for timely implementation of the 
sanctioned projects, Ministry has asked State/UT Tribal Research Institute(s) to submit 
their Annual Action Plan for Financial Year 2025-26 by the month of May. ln order to 
ensure optimum utilization of funds released to the States/UTs TRI under the scheme, 
the Ministry conducted physical meetings/video conferences with the State/UT 
Governments (State/UT TRIs) at regular intervals besides written instructions in this 
regard and also monitor sanctioned projects/activities from time to time. Moreover, the 
Ministry also follows up with the State/UT Government constantly to reduce their SNA 
balance and to ensure timely submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs) under the 
scheme. 
Ministry of Tribal Affairs OM No. F.No.16015/03/2025-PC {E file No. 29655} dated 
12.06.2025 
 
Recommendation (Para No. 4.19) 
 
The Committee note that an expenditure of ₹1,09,000/- per student per annum was 
specified in the EMRS guidelines,2020. The Committee were informed that this amount 
was not sufficient since revision in dearness allowance, implementation of pay revision, 
revision in labour cost, inflation in food and other commodities have increased 
significantly and could not be met in the amount fixed in the guidelines. The Committee 
were informed that the amount has been revised by EFC to ₹ 1,47,062/- per student per 

10 
 
annum w.e.f. 01.04.2025. The Committee are happy to find that EMRS were to be 
modeled on the basis of Navodaya Vidyalayas as per the approval of Cabinet. The 
Committee, however, note that the revision of recurring expenditure in NVS on mess, 
uniform and other direct expenditure on students is done from time to time and total 
average expenditure for a student in NVS is ₹1,56,000/- for 9 months of functioning of 
NVS. The Committee feel that since the modeling of EMRS is based on NVS, hence, 
the expenditure of EMRS should also be in comparison with the NVS. The Committee 
are also not convinced with the idea of including expenditure on salary head while 
calculating the expenditure per student per annum. They desire that the Ministry of 
Tribal Affairs should examine and calculate average expenditure for each student in 
EMRS by excluding salary head expenditure as the average recurring expenditure 
would be adversely affected as the salary expenditure would keep on increasing 
annually due to increase in dearness allowance and annual Increment. The Committee 
would like the Ministry to consider the above suggestions while calculating average 
expenditure for EMRS student and ensure that at least the average expenditure is not in 
any case less than provided under NVS.  
 
Reply of the Government 
The Ministry had provided a comprehensive comparison of recurring costs per student 
from 2018 to 2025 for NVS and EMRS, with a proposal for revision in the recurring cost 
per student of EMRS to Rs. 1,56,206/- in EFC 3.0 for F.Y. 2025-26, which was in line 
with the NVS recurring cost projected for the same reference period.  However, Ministry 
of Finance vide its OM dated 20.01.2025 in respect of EFC for EMRS 3.0, approved 
inflation adjustment in existing recurring cost of Rs. 1.09 lakh per student for balance 
EFC period i.e., for F.Y. 2024-25 to Rs. 1,40,059/- and, for F.Y. 2025-26 to Rs.

any case less than provided under NVS.

The Committee find that out of 728 approved EMRS, 288 schools are 
sanctioned/established under Grants under Article 275(1) of the Constitution at the cost

including maintenance of the building were not up to the mark. The Committee find that 
many EMRS have been running without basic facilities and required infrastructure such 
as compound wall, labs, sports facility, additional classrooms, staff quarter, hostel 
blocks, furniture etc. To provide these facilities, a survey of such schools was done and 
211 EMRS were identified for upgradation out of which 167 EMRSs have been 
approved till date. The Committee feel that the EMRS set up under old scheme needs 
to be revamped in a stipulated time period and sufficient funds are sanctioned to them

established under Article 275(1) require systematic revamping. In this regard, out of 211

Governments based on the proposals received to bridge infrastructure gaps, such as 
residential quarters, classrooms, and boys’ and girls’ hostels.

requiring further improvement. Necessary proposals will be taken to ensure that these 
institutions receive funding on par with newly established EMRSs and MoF will be 
approached. Further, in order to empower State Societies and Schools to take certain 
decisions independently, NESTS has been issuing various circulars and guidelines, 
such as streamlining fund release procedure, guidelines for admissions, vacations, 
engagement of outsourced guest staff etc. These guidelines/circulars are also uploaded 
on NESTS website for wider outreach.
