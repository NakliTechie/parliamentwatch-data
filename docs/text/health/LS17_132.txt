PREFACE 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health 
and Family Welfare, having been authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its 
behalf, present this One Hundred Thirty -Second Report of the Committee on Action Taken by 
the Government on the Recommendations/ Observations contained in the 127 th Report on on 
Demands for Grants 2021 -22 (Demand No. 45) of the Department of Health Research (DHR), 
Ministry of Health & Family Welfare.  
2. The One Hundred Twenty Seventh Report of the Department -related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Health and Family Welfare was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on 
the Table of Lok Sabha on 8 th March, 2021.  The Action Taken Notes of the Government on t he 
recommendations contained in the Report was received from the Departments on 10 th August, 
2021.   
3.  The Committee made a total of 51 recommendations in the 127 th Report, out of which  23 
recommendations have been accepted by the Ministry and has been categorized under Chapter I. 
There are 10 recommendations, which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the 
Ministry‟s replies that have been categorized under Chapter II. There are 14 recommendations/ 
observations, in respect of which replies of the Ministry have not been accepted by the 
Committee and the Committee has made further recommendations thereon and has been 
categorized under Chapter III while 4 recommendations/observations in respect of which final 
replies of the Ministry have not been received, has been categorized under Chapter IV. 
4. The Committee, in its meeting held on the  1st December, 2021 considered the Draft 
Report and adopted the same.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE 
MINISTRY 
Recommendation 
1.1 The Committee observes that the Covid -19 pandemic has created the need for 
transformation of the allocation of budgetary resources for research and innovation in the health 
sector. The Department of Health Research being the nodal department for medical re search 
needs to reorient its focus in strengthening research capabilities and infrastructure and 
reinvigorating interest in medical research amongst the youth. Availability of up -to-date, 
sophisticated, state of the art research infrastructure will provide  impetus to research and 
innovation. These insights help in formulating group or population -specific policies. 
Technologically backed, swift and accurate testing & diagnostic services will effectively counter 
existing and emerging viral diseases. Incorpora ting niche technologies like ML and AI in 
understanding human behaviour and tendencies can generate qualitative insights into issues like 
how human behaviour impacts the transmission of diseases. The Covid -19 pandemic has made 
us realize that there is an u rgent need for improving infrastructure for enhancing bio -security in 
the country by strengthening testing and research facilities. The Department of Health Research 
needs to ramp up its resources and strategic research priorities. 
(Para 1.6) 
Action Taken 
1.2 Important initiatives taken by the Department of Health Research (DHR) and ICMR for 
improving infrastructure, strengthening bio -security as well as improving innovations are as 
follows: 
 Creating National Network for Clinical Trials utilizing the services of Multi -Disciplinary 
Research Units (MRUs) in Government Medical Colleges and Model Rural Health 
Research Units (MRHRUs).   
 Establishment of 30 MRUs and 11 MRHRUs during the 15th Finance  Commission 
period (2021 -22 to 2025 -26) in addition to 85 MRUs and 28 MRHRUs already 
established upto 31.03.2021. 
 Establishment of 42 more Viral Research & Diagnostic Laboratories (VRDLs) during the 
15th Finance Commission period (2021 -22 to 2025 -26) in ad dition to 124 VRDLs 
already established upto 31.03.2021.  
 Establishment of 15 new „DHR -ICMR Advanced Molecular Oncology Diagnostic 
Services‟ (DiaMonds) Centres in addition to 9 existing Centers under the scheme of 
Human Resource & Capacity Development Scheme.  
 About 2800 Laboratories in medical colleges/ institutes/ private sector for COVID testing 
have been made functional. 
 Need- based custom platforms developed for COVID -19 testing (RT -PCR, True NAT, 
CBNAAT, Antigen testing, mobile vans, home testing). 
 Developed COVID KAWACH Elisa, first indigenous human IgG ELISA kit for SARS -
CoV-2 antibodies diagnosis. 
 Developed fully indigenous COVID -19 vaccine, Covaxin in collaboration with Bharat 
Biotech. Has been rolled out.

 With a view to ramp up and prioritize the research activities, following additional 
initiatives are proposed: 
 Establishment of Regional Research Platform for South East Asian Region Countries for 
collaboration in health research in partnership with WHO and 10 countries of SE Asia to 
combat emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases in SE Asia Region. 
 Establishment of Centre for One Health at Nagpur. 
 Setting up of 4 Zonal Institutes of Virology for North -East, North, South and Central

 Setting up of National Centre for Assistive Health Technology at AIIMs/IITs.  
1.3 Setting up of ICMR -Centres of Excellence at the IITs for boosting innovation and

II. Macro-Analysis of Budgetary Allocation to the DHR 
Recommendation 
1.4 The Secretary, DHR in his deposition before the Committee submitted that ideally, the 
overall public expenditure on health research should be at least 5% of the budget of the  
Department of Health & Family Welfare. The current allocation of 3.74% is thus short by 
1.26%. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should make all -out efforts 
to allocate 5percent of the budget of the Department of Health & Family Welfare to DHR at the 
R.E. stage. 
  
       (Para 2.5) 
Action Taken 
1.5 This aspect will be brought out to the notice of the Ministry of Finance during the

III. ICMR 
Recommendation 
1.6 The Committee appreciates the ICMR’s track record with respect to the utilization of 
funds. Barring the year 2020 -21, the ICMR has a near -perfect track -record of utilizing the 
budgeted funds. The Committee, however, is surprised to note that the grant that ICMR receives 
is less than the actual allocation that the DHR get s in the name of ICMR. It is quite intriguing to 
the Committee that while on the one hand the Ministry of Finance does not allocate the fund as 
per the projected demand and even the allocated fund is not released in totality to the ICMR. The 
Committee opines that the role of ICMR is very crucial considering the current and future health 
challenges; inadequacy of funds to ICMR can undermine the desired outcomes in the health 
sector. The Committee, therefore, recommends that sufficient funds must be released to ICMR 
including additional funds, at the RE stage. 
(Para 3.4)

budgetary allocations for the ICMR would be emphasized at the RE stage.

general and India in particular.

1.24 The Committee is pleased to note that the ICMR is planning to establish the country’s 
first-of-its-kind Centre of One Health on the campus of Maharashtra Animal and Fishery

advanced data management tools for ac curate results. Further, such a model must be scaled up 
across the country. 
(Para 3.35)

Action Taken 
1.25 COVID-19 pandemic has highlighted the risks of zoonoses  i.e. diseases or infections that 
can be transmitted between humans and animals. This has underlined the need to build effective 
mechanisms to avoid as well as to deal with such future pandemics. In pursuant to these 
directions, ICMR has already started th e preparatory activities for establishment of National 
Institute of One Health at Nagpur, which will also house a BSL -IV laboratory. Necessary land 
for establishment of the Centre has already been provided by the Maharashtra Animal and 
Fishery Sciences University (MAFSU), Nagpur.  This proposal to set up the One Health Centre 
forms part of the PM -ASBY package and the implementation of the project will be commenced 
immediately on approval.  
1.26 An International Workshop on One Health was organized on 12 th April, 2021 with all the 
stakeholders and their views/suggestions will be taken into consideration for achieving the best 
outcome of the project.  
Recommendation 
1.27 The Committee further recommends ICMR to establish meaningful research 
collaborations acro ss the world. Moreover, medical, veterinary, paramedical sectors and 
bioscience researchers must be integrated to form a task force to address the emergent issues. 
The Committee recommends that the ICMR should collaborate with health, veterinary, 
agriculture and life science research institutions and universities to broad base its R&D. 
(Para 3.36) 
Action Taken 
1.28 In order to adopt a holistic and integrated approach to solve health issues, a high level

Committee would like to further recommend that the Department must carry out studies

(Para 3.39)

1.30 The proposal for development of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tool for screening and 
detection of TB has been initiated in collaboration with Institute of Plasma Research, Department

Recommendation 
1.31 While appreciating the initiatives of ICMR to combat malnutrition, the problem of 
fluorosis, IDD and menace of Vitamin-A deficiency, the Committee is of the considered view that 
given the mandate of ICMR with regard to medical and health research, a lot mo re needs to be

Committee would like to be updated on the findings of the stu dies and the actions taken by DHR 
thereon.  
1.32 The Committee also recommends the Department to promote such studies in other 
regions of the country and incorporate findings of such studies in the National Health Policy. 
(Para 3.41) 
Action Taken 
1.33 Following actions have been taken by the ICMR to promote studies in this regard: 
i. Prevalence of fluorosis in the community of selected districts of India and development 
of an appropriate intervention model for prevention and control of fluorosis‟ was a Task 
Force Study under the Centre for Promotion of Nutrition Research and Training (CNRT) 
– ICMR HQ. ICMR-NIN, Hyderabad conducted the study in Prakasam district of Andhra 
Pradesh. Dental fluorosis screening was done in 60,000 population from 30 villages and 
the data was shared with ICMR for analysis 
ii. Similarly, Vitamin -A Deficiencies data was collected as part of the ICMR Task Force 
Study led by NCRT, ICMR -HQ. Of the eight states in which the study was being carried 
out, ICMR -NIN led the data collection in Medak district of Telangana.  The data was 
collected and sent to the ICMR in March 2020 and report is awaited 
iii. The ICMR Task Force Study on Assessment of Iodine Status Among Pregnant Women in 
Selected Districts of India” has been initiated in April 2017 in ten di stricts, that is, one 
district from each of the ten states of India. ICMR -NIN is one of the participating centers 
for the longitudinal study and coordinated and implemented the same in Telangana State. 
The data collection (demographic, health, WASH, releva nt pregnancy, child birth and 
lactation related data, diet and anthropometry) and sample collection (blood, urine and 
household salt samples for relevant biomarker estimations) from 200 pregnant women 
recruited from 100 villages from the district of Yadadr i/Bhuvanagiri has been completed 
(including data scrutiny) during all the six study time points (2017 to 2020). The data and 
samples have been shared with the Centre for Promotion of Nutrition Research and 
Training – (CNRT), ICMR, New Delhi and analysis is in progress. 
1.34 ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition agrees with the recommendation of the committee 
and would take up such studies in future on a continuous basis in regular time intervals so as to

IV. Schemes/ Projects of Department of Health Research (DHR) 
Recommendation

on the available resources. The Committee however, appreciates the efforts of DHR  for 
strengthening the network of Viral Research & Diagnostic Laboratories for managing  epidemics 
and national calamities. The Committee notes that the Department managed toset up 118 VRDLs

(Para 4.5)
