I. BRIEF BACKGROUND
1.1 The Department of Indian systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy was created in 1995 and
renamed as Department of Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy (AYUSH)
in 2003 and as Ministry of AYUSH in 2014. The Ministry is responsible for policy formulation, development
and implementation of programmes for the growth, development and propagation of Ayurveda, Yoga and
Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homeopathy (AYUSH) systems of Health care. Sowa Rigpa is the recent
addition to the existing family of AYUSH systems.
1.2 The Ministry has a broad institutional framework with the objective of positioning AYUSH systems
as the preferred systems of living and practice for attaining a healthy India. There are regulatory bodies
like Central Council of Indian Medicine (CCIM) and Central Council of Homoeopathy (CCH); research
councils like Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS), Central Council for Research
in Unani Medicine ( CCRUM), Central Council for Research in Yoga and Naturopathy (CCRYN) and
Central Council for Research in Siddha (CCRS) under the ambit of the Ministry.
1.3 The Ministry has five major Central Sector Schemes like; (i) Strengthening of Ministry of AYUSH
(ii) Support to AYUSH Institutions (iii) Support to statutory institutions (iv) Research and Development
(v) Strengthening of AYUSH delivery system and one Centrally sponsored Scheme i.e. National AYUSH
Mission (NAM).

2.5 The Committee is constrained to observe that physical targets set for the Twelfth Five Year
Plan with respect to the schemes listed above have not been achieved. This is mainly due to
incomplete work in projects due to procedural delays leading to delay in construction activities.
This obviously leads to time and cost overruns in the long run. Time lines for completion of these
procedural processes must be set and efforts need to be made to adhere to them for timely
completion of projects. Other reasons for non-accomplishment of targets with respect to schemes
were non-filling up of vacancies and incomplete research projects and publication of research
papers. Two schemes of Pharmacovigilance Initiative for  ASU drugs and Central Drug Controller
for AYUSH are yet to be approved. With no dearth of funds, the Ministry could have easily
achieved the set targets had it done proper planning and implementation. The Committee,
therefore, recommends the Ministry to be more vigilant about the physical as well as financial
targets set so that they are achieved within the specified time period. A proper mechanism for
monitoring at various levels and addressing pending issues pertaining to conceptualization can
be devised for timely completion of projects, starting off new schemes etc.

2.7 In his oral evidence before the Committee, the Secretary, Ministry of AYUSH informed that the
Ministry had been successful in optimal utilization of allocated funds in 2015-16 and 2016-17 compared
to earlier years. However, against the projected demand of `2478.78 crores for 2017-18, the Ministry
has been given `1428.65 crore. Even though the allocated amount seemed enhanced compared to last
year's allocation, the Secretary was of the view that it was quite less for implementing its major schemes.
Comparing the allocation for the Ministry with the allocation for Ministry of Health and Family Welfare,
he highlighted that allocation for AYUSH was a mere 3.01% of the total budget of the Ministry of Health
and Family Welfare. He was of the view that the major casualty due to the shortfall in allocation for
2017-18 would be the National AYUSH Mission. The Secretary voiced his concern that the Ministry
needed enhanced allocation for achievement of its physical as well as financial targets.
2.8 The Committee observes that there is a shortfall of `````1050.13 crores against the projected
demand of the Ministry of `````2478.78 crore for the financial year 2017-18. The Committee is of
the view that AYUSH has huge potential for effective presentation and therapy that is also safe
and cost effective and hence that the Ministry should not face any fund crunch. Keeping in view
the fact that peoples' out-of-pocket expenditure on health amounts to 64% of the total Health
Expenditure (source: National Health Accounts 2013-14), it would be befitting to allocate a
substantially higher proportion of the Health Budget for the Ministry of AYUSH. Inadequate
allocation for AYUSH is, in the opinion of the Committee, the main reason for not achieving the
objectives of AYUSH. The Committee simultaneously observes that it is imperative that in order
to obtain the best possible health outcomes, allocation of resources is prioritized across competing
ends and the available resources deployed in a more efficient manner. The Committee would,
therefore, like the Ministry to prioritize the schemes and utilize funds accordingly and move the
Ministry of Finance for more funds at RE 2017-18 stage based on the efficient and productive
utilization of BE 2017-18 allocation.

2.12 The Committee is concerned about the trend of under-utilization of funds due to non-filling
up of posts, pending UCs and non-receipt of sufficient and viable number of proposals for
schemes. The Committee is of the view that non-filling of posts and pending UCs are routine
challenges which could have been tackled had the Ministry pursued them in a sustained manner.
The Committee would, therefore, recommend to the Ministry to adopt an aggressive approach
to fill up the vacant posts so that funds are put to productive use. As regards not receiving viable
proposals under the schemes of Development of Common Facilities for AYUSH Industry Clusters
and AYUSH and Public Health, the Committee is of the considered view that concerted efforts
are required to be taken to popularize the scheme and its salient features so as to generate
awareness amongst the concerned stakeholders who would then submit viable proposals. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that appropriate steps be taken to speed up the process of
implementation of these schemes and arrest this trend and take up the issue of underutilization
of funds with the concerned agencies.
2.13 The Committee would also like to express concern about the schemes where there have
been nil allocation at the RE stage. Schemes like National Institute of Medicinal Plants, National
Institute of Sowa Rigpa, Indian Institute of AYUSH Pharmaceutical Sciences, Central Council for
Research in Sowa Rigpa, Pharmacovigilance initiative for  ASU drugs and Central Drug Controller
for AYUSH indicate such a trend. SFC approval for the scheme-Pharmacovigilance initiative for
ASU Drugs is still awaited. Similar is the case with Central Drug Controller for AYUSH as
Recruitment Rules for 12 regulatory posts were yet not approved and required officers yet not
to be recruited. Further, Central Council for Research in Sowa Rigpa, National Institute of Sowa
Rigpa, National Institute of Medicinal Plants, Indian Institute of AYUSH Pharmaceutical Sciences
have not taken off as envisaged by the Ministry for the past three to four years. The Committee
would like to reiterate its earlier recommendations of proper assessment and planning for
launching any new scheme before seeking allocations. All necessary approvals / clearances need
to be obtained beforehand so that there is no inordinate delay in implementation of
schemes.

2.16 The Committee has been given to understand that the Ministry has aggressively taken the approach
to liquidate the long term pending UCs especially for erstwhile Centrally Sponsored Schemes. Regular
periodical reminders were sent to States to take prompt action to liquidate UCs. As for NAM, five
regional meetings were held with States/ UTs at Lucknow, Chandigarh, Varanasi, New Delhi and Bangalore
during 2016 to give due attention for the immediate implementation of the programme and submit the
UCs immediately. The States / UTs were being pursued vigorously to liquidate pending UCs. Efforts were
also being made to clear the long outstanding UCs for Central Sector Scheme of NMPB by nominating
officers State-wise and pursuing the agencies by sending reminders regularly. No grants were released
by the Ministry against whom the UCs were pending. It was also informed that during the year
2015-16, total amount of `111.15 crore had been liquidated and `61.64 crore had been liquidated upto
31st December, 2016.
2.17 The Committee time and again has been impressing upon the Ministry the imperative need
for speedy liquidation of pending UCs. Though some progress has been made, there is still a large
number of pending UCs and it seems that the efforts made by the Ministry to liquidate them
are not enough. The Committee, therefore, believes that a pro-active and aggressive approach
is required on the part of the Ministry for liquidation of pending UCs. The Committee is of the
firm view that in this era of e-governance and with IT resources currently available, submission
of UCs should be possible almost on "Push of a button". The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Ministry should chalk out an action plan for online submission of UCs and also take
up the matter of pendency of UCs with States which have a large number of pending UCs like
Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Himachal Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, Uttarakhand
etc so that all pending UCs are settled within a period of six months.
III. REGULATION AND QUALITY CONTROL OF AYURVEDIC, SIDDHA, UNANI AND
HOMOEOPATHIC (ASU&H) DRUGS
3.1 Ministry of AYUSH has a Drug Control Cell (DCC) to administer regulatory and quality control
provisions for Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy (ASU&H) drugs. The DCC deals with Drugs

9
and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules thereunder and the associated matter pertaining to ASU&H Drugs.
It is the objective of the Ministry to ensure quality control of ASU&H drugs in the interest of public
safety and to achieve uniform administration of Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules thereunder.
3.2 The major achievements / Initiatives of the Ministry with respect to regulation and quality control
of ASU&H drugs in the last year is as follows:-
• Meeting of the State regulators was held on 4th April, 2016 chaired by Secretary AYUSH
to review the quality control and regulatory situation of Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani and
Homoeopathy drugs.
• Meeting of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board (ASUDTAB) held on
14th October, 2016 to deliberate upon proposed regulatory amendments and revision of
GMP provisions.
• Following Action points circulated on 29-04-2016 to States/UTs to streamline the enforcement
of legal provisions of ASU&H drugs:-
• As per rule 162-A of the Drugs & Cosmetics Rules, 1945 adequately qualified personnel
may be appointed as State licensing Authority/Drug Controller for ASU drugs.
• State Licensing Authorities may be appointed in accordance with the qualification provided
in Rule 162A and their details uploaded on the State/UTs Department's Website and sent to
the Ministry of AYUSH.
• Status of regulatory framework and quality control of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and
Homoeopathy drugs during the last three years may be provided by Bihar, Jharkhand,
Chandigarh, Nagaland, Daman & Diu and Delhi.
• List of Patent & Proprietary Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drugs licensed during past three
years may be sent to the Ministry of AYUSH for uploading on the Ministry's website and
the same should be uploaded on the State Department's website.
• Suitable officers under section 8 (1) of the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable
Advertisement) Act 1954 may be appointed urgently to monitor the rampant misleading
advertisements of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drugs and take action against the defaulters.
State/UT's were requested to inform the Actions taken at regular (quarterly) intervals.
• Thorough scrutiny of license applications for Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drugs may be
done in accordance with the provisions of Rule 158 B of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules,
1945.
• State Licensing Authorities/Drug Controllers of AYUSH were requested to ensure that the
labelling provisions are properly complied by the manufacturers.
• State Licensing Authorities/Drug Controllers of AYUSH may appoint adequate number of
Drug Inspectors.

V. NATIONAL MEDICINAL PLANTS BOARD
5.1 As per information given in the Annual Report 2016-17, Indian systems of Medicine use various
raw materials and medicinal plants constitute 90% of it. About 3000 plant species are reported to be used
in the codified Indian systems of medicine. In addition to their use in the preparation of traditional
medicines, the medicinal plants are being used in preparation of various pharmaceuticals and health
products under the modern medicine system. India is home to 7% of the world's biodiversity. Keeping
in view the need for availability of authentic raw drugs, the Government of India has taken the lead by
establishing the National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPS) under the Ministry of AYUSH in 2000. It is an
apex body which coordinates all matters relating to medicinal plants in the country. It implements
programme through 36 State Medicinal Plant Boards, State Forest Departments etc. An online portal and
mobile application 'e-charak' has been launched by the Ministry to facilitate online trade of medicinal
plants. The Ministry also intends to establish National and Regional Raw Drug Repositories in different
zones of the country for reference purposes.
5.2 For assessment of Demand and Supply position of medicinal plants in India as well as international
market, NMPB had commissioned a study to Indian Council of Forestry Research and Education
(ICFRE), Dehradun. Major findings of the study are:
• Total Commercial Demand of Herbal Raw Drugs in the country for the year 2014-15 has
been estimated at 5,12,000 MT.
• Estimated Exports of Herbal Raw Drugs, including Extracts has been estimated 1,34,500
MT in 2014-15 (Value - `3211 crore).

18
• Estimated Consumption by Domestic Herbal Industry has been estimated 1,95,000 MT
2014-15 (V alue - `1950 crore).
• An Estimated 1,67,500 MT of Herbal Raw Drugs are also used by rural households every
year (V alue - `418.75 crore).
• Trade Value of Herbal Raw Drugs in Commercial Demand for the year 2014-15 has been
estimated at `5,500 crore which is 5-times increase over the total trade value of `1069
crore estimated for the year 2005-06.
• About 1178 medicinal plant species recorded in the practices of trade.
• 242 plant species recorded in high annual trade.
5.3 The Secretary, Ministry of AYUSH in his oral evidence before the Committee submitted that many
medicinal plants were distressed and endangered due to reckless and irresponsible harvesting. Also, the
market for medicinal plants is crude and primitive. He was of the view that the entire chain from the
producer to the consumer was fragmented and the biggest challenge in the sector of medicinal plants,
a part from reckless harvesting is of marketing chain. Therefore, there was a need to streamline the
chain. He also informed the Committee that apart from the demand of medicinal purposes, high demand
has been witnessed for them from the cosmetics industry which is large and ever growing industry in
the country. Highlighting the challenges faced in the medicinal plants sector, the Secretary (AYUSH) made
the following submissions:-
"Today, quality is very important because for any item which goes for export —and, especially,
in research—they want the passport data of where that particular herb was grown, where it was
harvested from, at what time it was harvested and the kind of phytochemical properties which
change its time. But, unfortunately, we do not maintain passport data in terms of any of our
produce. This is a major area. In terms of the produce sold in the market, there are no test reports
of its chemical composition or its time of harvest or the place of harvest, which are the three basic
parameters."
5.4 The Secretary, therefore, expressed the need to create sustainable markets for medicinal plants for
the AYUSH sector.
5.5 It is a matter of serious concern that despite India being home to 7% of the world's bio-
diversity which makes it one of the 17 mega bio-diversity rich countries in the world, the market
for medicinal plants is not well developed in India and the country has lagged behind in maintaining
the passport data regarding place, time and phytochemical properties of medicinal plants, which
are the three basic parameters of quality assurance. Since the greatest challenge to widespread
acceptance of AYUSH is faced in quality assurance which largely depends on the source of raw
materials, the Committee recommends that the Ministry take necessary initiative to build a
database that traces the source of raw materials.

II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
The Committee is constrained to note that out of the total outlay of `````10044.00 crores for
the Ministry for the 12th Five Year Plan, the Ministry got only `````5186.00 crore at the BE stage.
This was further reduced to `````3814.72 crores at the RE stage. Out of this, the Ministry has been
able to utilize only `````3247.35 crores. The Committee observes the disappointing trend of utilization
for the entire Five Year Plan. The Committee in its earlier Reports on Demands for Grants has
reiterated the need for the realistic assessment of the requirement of funds and corresponding
utilization of those funds. There has been r ecurrent and persistent under-utilization of the funds
allocated at the stage of BE as well as RE in all the five years of the Twelfth plan. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that lessons need to be learnt so that funds are not kept idly
parked, year after year but are gainfully utilized for other fund-starved programs/schemes.
(para 2.3)
The Committee is constrained to observe that physical targets set for the Twelfth Five Year
Plan with respect to the schemes listed above have not been achieved. This is mainly due to
incomplete work in projects due to procedural delays leading to delay in construction activities.
This obviously leads to time and cost overruns in the long run. Timelines for completion of these
procedural processes must be set and efforts need to be made to adhere to them for timely
completion of projects. Other reasons for non-accomplishment of targets with respect to schemes
were non-filling up of vacancies and incomplete research projects and publication of research
papers. Two schemes of Pharmacovigilance Initiative for  ASU drugs and Central Drug Controller
for AYUSH are yet to be approved. With no dearth of funds, the Ministry could have easily
achieved the set targets had it done proper planning and implementation. The Committee,
therefore, recommends the Ministry to be more vigilant about the physical as well as financial
targets set so that they are achieved within the specified time period. A proper mechanism for
monitoring at various levels and addressing pending issues pertaining to conceptualization can
be devised for timely completion of projects, starting off new schemes etc. (para 2.5)
The Committee observes that there is a shortfall of `````1050.13 crores against the projected
demand of the Ministry of `````2478.78 crore for the financial year 2017-18. The Committee is of
the view that AYUSH has huge potential for effective presentation and therapy that is also safe
and cost effective and hence that the Ministry should not face any fund crunch. Keeping in view
the fact that peoples' out-of-pocket expenditure on health amounts to 64% of the total Health
Expenditure (source: National Health Accounts 2013-14), it would be befitting to allocate a
substantially higher proportion of the Health Budget for the Ministry of AYUSH. AYUSH has
huge potential for effective prevention and therapy that is also safe and cost effective. Inadequate
allocation for AYUSH is, in the opinion of the Committee, the main reason for not achieving the
objectives of AYUSH. The Committee simultaneously observes that it is imperative that in order

III. REGULATION AND QUALITY CONTROL OF AYURVEDIC, SIDDHA, UNANI AND
HOMOEOPATHIC (ASU&H) DRUGS
The Committee observes that as per the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1946, and Rules made
thereunder, the quality control and regulation of AYUSH remedies primarily fall in the domain
of State/UT Governments. However, the State Drug Regulatory Framework is very weak and
State Drug Licensing Authorities are ill-equipped to handle the task of regulation of AYUSH
remedies. During 2016, the Ministry has given ````` 1.86 crore as grant-in-aid to the States for
strengthening their quality enforcement mechanism. The Committee observes that a large part
of the population uses AYUSH remedies as treatment of their choice. The Committee is therefore,
of the opinion that the limit of financial assistance to States needs to be increased significantly.
( para 3.5)
The Committee finds that a large number of samples of ASU&H drugs have failed the
testing procedure in 2013-14, 2014-15 and 2015-16. Even in the year 2016-17, out of 330 samples
which were tested, 36 samples have failed. Interestingly 35 samples were Homoeopathic drugs
and only 1 ASU drug. The Committee would like to point out that the Ministry has not informed
about the action taken with respect to the defaulters as done in previous years. The Committee
feels that testing of samples and consequent action thereupon is one of the most important
components of quality control of ASU&H drugs. The Committee is of the firm view that in case
of samples failing the testing process, strict action needs to be taken. However, merely issuing
show cause notices, suspending/cancelling licenses would not help as the defaulters may start
with a new and different license. The Committee believes that in order to ensure proper and
effective regulation of ASU&H drugs, the defaulters must be prosecuted for trying to play with
the lives of people. The Committee, therefore, recommends that stringent measures should be
in place against the erring manufacturers so as to dissuade them from trying to pass just any
formulation as ASU&H drugs. (para 3.7)
The Committee observes that there are only 29 Drug Laboratories and 46 Pharmacies
available in the public sector in the States for testing and manufacturing of ASU&H drugs. 6
States did not have any manufacturing unit. The Committee is of the view that given the huge
size and needs of AYUSH sector, there is a need to increase the number of Drug Testing
Laboratories and Pharmacies in the country. There should be more than one Drug Testing
Laboratories in big States and at least one each in smaller States. Similarly, there should be
many more ASU&H manufacturing units in the country. Special attention needs to be given to
the 6 States which do not have a single manufacturing unit.  (para 3.9)

36
The Committee is dismayed to note that even after two years since the decision of the
creation of a vertical structure in CDSCO was taken, no real progress has been made so far.
Only 12 regulatory posts have been notified. Recruitment rules for these posts were still in the
process of finalization. The Committee would like to point out that even through CDSCO is
under the Department of Health and Family Welfare of which the Ministry of AYUSH was once
a part of, things are not moving at a swift pace towards finalization of the vertical structure. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry may take up this issue with the Department
of Health and Family Welfare on a priority basis and also fix a timeline for creation of this
vertical structure. The Committee would like to emphasize the fact that regulation and quality
control of ASU&H drugs is one of the most important areas in the Ministry and if the ASU&H
drugs market is regulated efficiently, it will go a long way in building credibility of these drugs
and strengthen the process of mainstreaming of AYUSH. The Committee, therefore, reiterates
its recommendation made in its 95th Report that all inter-ministerial issues may be ironed out
within a designated timeline and the vertical structure created at the earliest. (para 3.11)
The Committee also takes into cognizance the fact that the Central Sector Scheme for
developing Pharmacovigilance system for ASU&H drugs was still being conceptualized. The

pharmacovigilance system for AYUSH so that the scheme is implemented as early as possible and

The Committee observes that in the past the oft-repeated contention of the Ministry has
been that due to the ineffectiveness of the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable
advertisements) Act, 1954, it has not been possible to take stringent action against the misleading
and exaggerated claims of efficacy of the AYUSH remedies and that certain amendments were
needed to be carried out in the said Act. The Department of Health and Family Welfare which
had constituted a Task Force for suggesting amendments to the aforesaid Act, has been requested
to expedite consolidation of the amendment provisions recommended by the Task Force. But the
latest submissions of the Ministry indicate that the amendment to the Drugs and Magic Remedies
Act is far from being materialized. The Committee would like the Ministry to indicate the time-
frame within which the amendment of the Magic Remedies Act would be enacted. (para 3.15)
The Committee welcomes the signing of MoU with the Advertising Standards Council of

brought to book and appropriate action taken against them. The Committee would like to be
apprised of the efficacy of the new monitoring mechanism. (para 3.16)
IV . NATIONAL AYUSH MISSION (NAM)
The Committee is aware that the overall standard of AYUSH education in the country
continues to be extremely poor. The Committee feels that the mandatory requirement of
accreditation of teaching hospitals and surprise inspection teams are efforts in the right direction.
Further, in order to promote AYUSH education, effective curricula reforms will also need to be

37
carried out. The Committee also recommends that practioners of Allopathy should be sensitized
to the strengths of AYUSH system and to the cross system referrals between healthcare providers
of all systems of medicine so that ignorance about AYUSH is dispelled and AYUSH is integrated
in the health delivery system. The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should prepare
a framework for fostering collaborative and inter-disciplinary AYUSH research of contemporary
relevance. Further, NEET and NPGET test for UG and PG students to regulate the entry of
qualified students into these institutions is a much awaited step. The Committee, therefore,
recommends the Ministry that these measures may be implemented at the earliest for visible
improvement in the quality of AYUSH education. (para 4.6)
The Committee is dismayed to note that National AYUSH Mission being one of the most
important schemes of the Ministry, would be facing shortfall of funds in Financial Year 2017-18.
Similar was the case last year i.e 2016-17 when the Secretary, Ministry of AYUSH had submitted
that NAM would be affected due to shortfall in allocation. NAM envisages better access to AYUSH
services through increase in number of AYUSH Hospitals and Dispensaries, ensuring availability
of ASU&H drugs and trained manpower, upgradation of AYUSH educational institutions for
improvement in quality of AYUSH education, increase in number of Pharmacies, setting up Drug
Laboratories in States and sustained availability of herbal raw drugs by promoting medicinal
plants. Keeping in view the vital objectives of NAM, it is imperative that adequate funds are
allocated for this scheme. The Committee also is of the view that the objectives of NAM signal
a significant improvement in the quality of AYUSH healthcare services, expanding its outreach
and making available AYUSH healthcare facilities to the people. The Committee, therefore,
strongly recommends that the Ministry should approach Ministry of Finance for additional funds
with proper justifications for enhanced allocation of funds at RE stage. (para 4.9)
V . NATIONAL MEDICINAL PLANTS BOARD
It is a matter of serious concern that despite India being home to 7% of the world's bio-diversity
which makes it one of the 17 mega bio-diversity rich countries in the world, the market for
medicinal plants is not well developed in India and the country has lagged behind in maintaining
the passport data regarding place, time and phytochemical properties of medicinal plants, which
are the three basic parameters of quality assurance. Since the greatest challenge to widespread
acceptance of AYUSH is faced in quality assurance which largely depends on the source of raw
materials, the Committee recommends that the Ministry take necessary initiative to build a
database that traces the source of raw materials. (para 5.5)
The Committee takes note of the major findings of the study of the Indian Council of Forestry
Research and Education (ICFRE), Dehradun entailing the total commercial demand of herbal raw
drugs, exports of such drugs, consumption by domestic herbal industry as well as rural households.
The Committee believes that our country has a vast bio-diversity of medicinal plants which can
be put to great use by the Indian systems of medicine if there is proper system in place for its
production, procurement and marketing. The Committee would also like the Ministry to establish

38
effective forward and backward linkages to protect, sustain and encourage production of medicinal
plants in a sustainable and non-exploitative manner so that there is no distortion between
demand and supply. (para 5.6)
The Committee takes note of 588 pending UCs amounting to `````81.21 crore with respect to
Central Sector Schemes of NMPB. For the Centrally Sponsored Schemes of NMPB, 28 UCs are
pending amounting `````42.45 crore. These UCs are pending from the year 2001-2002 to 2009-10. The
Committee is of the view that pending UCs need to be liquidated as they block the further
release of funds which in turn disrupts the whole chain of production of medicinal plants, its
procurement and use of such plants to develop medicines for the benefit of the people. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that concerted efforts should be made for liquidation of
pending UCs and the issue needs to be taken up with the concerned State Governments at the
highest level. (para 5.9)
The Committee observes that the Expenditure Finance Committee had agreed in principle
to increase the sanctioned strength in 2014 and the proposal for creation of 65 new posts was
under process. Further, out of 16 sanctioned posts, only 10 posts were filled up while 5 posts were
being managed either on loan or contractual basis. The Committee believes that the temporary
and ad-hoc  measures of filling posts on loan or contractual basis is not a healthy trend though
a stop gap arrangement. The Committee notes the small strength of manpower of the NMPB and
feels that this certainly needs to be increased so that the important mandate of the Board is not
jeopardised due to this. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the creation of 65 new posts
be finalized within a period of three months from the presentation of this Report. (para 5.11)
It is worrisome to note that the SMPBs are weak and dysfunctional bodies which are not
suitable to carry out the mandate envisaged by the NMPBs. Despite grants from NMPB to the
SMPBs, the SMPBs still suffer from staff shortage, and only 8 States SMPBs were functioning
with a CEO. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should impress upon the State/UT
Governments the imperative need to strengthen and turn them into robust structures that the
SMPBs need to. The Committee also recommends that the Ministry should interact with other
States still not having full-fledged body and encourage them to carry out necessary reforms to
have full-fledged SMPBS. (para 5.13)
