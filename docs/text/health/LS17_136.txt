CHAPTER I 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 Ayush systems of medicine include Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy. 
Ayush is an acronym for Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa and 
Homoeopathy. Ayurveda is the oldest system with a documented history of its practice for 
more than 5000 years whereas Homoeopathy is in practice in India for around 100 years. 
These systems are be ing practiced in the country with diverse preferences of people and 
infrastructural facilities. 
 
1.2   The Ministry of Ayush has the mandate to develop Ayush systems viz. Ayurveda, 
Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa -Rigpa and Homoeopathy. The Ministry  works 
with the vision to position Ayush systems as the preferred systems of living and practice for 
attaining a healthy India. Such a vision has gradually emerged through the thought processes 
of experts concerned with health care provisioning in the country. The Ministry has identified 
its Mission in terms of seven broad thematic areas of Ayush activities. The thematic areas are 
as follows: 
 
(i) Effective Human Resource Development 
(ii) Provision of Quality AYUSH Services 
(iii) Information, Education and Communication 
(iv) Quality Research in AYUSH 
(v) Growth of the Medicinal Plants Sector 
(vi) Drug Administration 
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminars/Workshops in Ayush sector 
 
1.3  In executing its mandate, the Ministry works with a multipronged strategy encompassing 
effective hu man resource development, provision of quality Ayush services to the public, 
dissemination activities emphasizing Information, Education and Communication (IEC), 
quality research in Ayush, effective drug administration for ensuring development of Ayush 
drugs, ensuring growth of the medicinal plants sector and expanding AYUSH horizons 
through international exchange programmes/seminars/workshops on Ayush. 
 
1.4    The major activities of the Ministry of Ayush are performed through the following: 
 
• Autonomous Institutions: Activities are performed by various Institutes/Councils 
under direct control of Ministry  
• Central Sector Scheme 
• Centrally Sponsored Scheme: Grants are provided through National AYUSH Mission 
to States/UTs to take up AYUSH activities

New Initiatives taken in the financial year 2021-22 
 
1.9   The Committee was apprised about the new initiatives taken in the year 2021 -22. By 
merging of existing schemes, new schemes like the AYURGYAN, Ayurswasthaya Yojana  
and Ayush Oushadhi Gunvatta Evum Utpadan Samvardhan Yojana (AOGUSY) were

AYUSH systems in public healthcare system. The Committee notes that with a wide 
functional area and proper institutional support of research councils, educational 
institutes, regulatory bodies etc, it is evident that the Ministry is treading towards 
establishing itself as a stron g alternative system of medicine with growing number of 
people reposing their faith in its efficacy. The Committee appreciates that t he National 
Health Policy (NHP) 2017 had strongly advocated mainstreaming the potential of 
AYUSH within a pluralistic syste m of Integrative healthcare and re -emphasizing the 
need for integrating AYUSH in the National Health Mission, research and education. 
The Committee, therefore, believes that the Ministry has a very important mandate to 
strategize and devise ways to galvani ze the AYUSH system with a definite roadmap for 
quality education, quality healthcare delivery system, safety and efficacy of AYUSH 
drugs, promotion to exports etc. The Committee, accordingly, recommends the Ministry 
to lay focus on the implementation of i ts schemes, maintaining high standards of 
education and research and regulation and control over quality and safety of AYUSH 
drugs.

SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY 
NATIONAL AYUSH MISSION (NAM) 
3.1    National AYUSH Mission (NAM) is a Centrally Sponsored flagship scheme of 
Ministry of AYUSH approved and notified on 29.09.2014. It is the only Centrally Sponsored 
Scheme of the Ministry of Ayush. The Union Cabinet had approved continuation of Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme of National AYUSH Mission (NAM) on 15.12.2017 from 01.04.2017 to 
31.03.2020. It has approved on 14.07.2021 for the  continuation of NAM as a Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme with financial implication of Rs. 4607.30 Crores (Rs. 3000.00 Crore as 
Central Share and Rs. 1607.30 Crores as State Share) with effect from 01.04.2021 to 
31.03.2026.  
3.2  The National AYUSH Mission (NA M) envisages better access to AYUSH services 
through increase in number of AYUSH Hospitals and Dispensaries, ensure availability of 
AYUSH drugs and trained manpower. It also aims at improvement in quality of AYUSH 
Education through enhancement in the numbe r of upgraded educational Institutions, 
sustained availability of Quality Raw materials and improved availability of quality 
Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani & Homoeopathy (ASU & H) drugs through increase in number of 
Pharmacies, setting up of Drug Laboratories in the States which are responsible for 
enforcement mechanism of ASU & H drugs. 
3.3  As per information on the official website of NAM, there is a paradigm shift in the 
recent past in the approach from disease management towards achieving wellness. The 
AYUSH systems advocate holistic wellness approach aiming at prevention of diseases and 
promotion of health and wellbeing. A decision was taken on 30 th January, 2019 that at least 
12,500 Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs) under Ayushman Bharat will be upgraded by  
the Ministry of AYUSH. The Union Cabinet during its meeting on 20th March, 2020 has 
approved the proposal to operationalize these 12,500 AYUSH HWCs through States/UTs in 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme mode and under the broad umbrella of National AYUSH 
Mission (NAM) in a phased manner by 2023-24 with a financial outlay of Rs. 3399.35 crore.

3.31 The Government of India, after carefully considering the issues relating to medicinal 
plants constituted a Nationa l Level Body viz. National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB) to 
look after policy formulation, co -ordination with Ministries/ Departments, ensuring 
sustainable availability of Medicinal Plants and to co -ordinate all matters relating to their 
development and sustainable use. National Medicinal Plants Board was set up vide resolution 
notified on 24 th November, 2000 headed by Union Minister of Health &  Family Welfare. At 
present, the Board is headed by Hon‘ble Minister of Ayush with Secretaries of various 
Departments as ex-officio members and nominated members representing various fields. The 
Board was reconstituted on 01st August, 2018 and the first meeting of the reconstituted Board 
was held on 30th August, 2018.

Scheme in ―Conservation, Development and Sustainable Management of Medicinal Plants‖.

traditional Indian systems of medicine with scientific vigor and tap the huge potential of

an affordable and efficacious line of treatment. The growth and popularity of Ayush 
Systems rose tr emendously during the pandemic C ovid-19 with high demand for

system, infrastructure, enhanced budgetary investment, ensuring the accessibility of

need of the hour is strategic long term planning and enhanced investment for Ayush 
sector.
