OVERVIEW OF DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE AND 
FARMERS WELFARE 
 
 
Mandate 
 
The Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DA&FW) serves as a key 
Department in strengthening India’s agricultural sector by boosting productivity, 
ensuring food security, and improving the livelihoods of farmers. Its mandate 
focuses on promoting sustainable agricultural development through modern 
farming techniques and resource-efficient practices. A core objective of the 
Department is to enhance crop productivity by implementing schemes that 
improve soil health, expand irrigation coverage, and increase overall farm 
efficiency. DA&FW is also responsible for framing agricultural policies, including 
crop pricing mechanisms, promotion of organic and natural farming, and the 
development of a unified national agricultural market to safeguard farmers’ 
interests. Through flagship initiatives such as Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman 
Nidhi (PM-KISAN), the Department provides direct income support to small and 
marginal farmers, enabling them to access essential agricultural inputs and 
maintain financial stability.  Additionally, the Department promotes research in 
areas such as advanced farming practices, pest management, and crop 
diversification to encourage sustainable agriculture. It works closely with State 
Governments to implement Central Sector Schemes effectively, monitor 
agricultural data, and strengthen local agricultural initiatives across the country. 
 

2  
1.2  Two new Schemes namely Bharat-VISTAAR and Support for High 
Value Agriculture have been announced in the Union Budget (2026-27). 
Bharat-VISTAAR is a new AI-powered, multilingual digital platform launched to 
provide farmers with real-time advisories, weather updates, market prices, 
scheme details and crop support in a single gateway, improving farm decisions 
and productivity. The Bharat VISTAAR tool integrates data from multiple sources 
like weather, APMC prices, pest alerts and scientific guidance to offer 
personalised support in local languages, including voice access for basic 
phones. The newly launched Scheme, Support for High Value Agriculture will 
focus on enhancing cultivation and processing of crops such as cashew, cocoa, 
sandalwood and other niche, high-return crops with the objective of increasing 
income by strengthening existing post harvesting mechanisms, value chains and 
boosting export competitiveness of high-value produce.

1.12  On being asked to state the specific quantum and percentage share of the 
net budgetary increase/decrease for 2026-27 vis-à-vis BE of 2025-26, that is 
attributed to Recurring Expenditure comprising of committed liabilities such as 
salary grants, existing subsidies and operational costs of ongoing schemes vs 
Non recurring/One off expenditure comprising capital outlays for new 
infrastructure, launches of new pilot schemes and specific project grants, the 
Department has stated: 
“The net budgetary change for the year 2026–27 vis-à-vis BE 2025–26 is primarily on 
account of adjustments in allocations under ongoing schemes, rationalisation of 
provisions, and merging/restructuring of certain schemes. The expenditure of the 
Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare is predominantly recurring in nature, 
comprising committed liabilities such as salary grants, operational expenses, and 
support to ongoing schemes and interventions for farmers. 
Non-recurring or one-time expenditure, such as capital outlays for new infrastructure, 
pilot initiatives, or specific project-based grants, constitutes a relatively smaller share 
of the Department’s overall budget and is provided on a need-based basis. 
Accordingly, the major portion of the net budgetary increase/decrease for 2026–27 
over BE 2025–26 is attributable to recurring expenditure under existing schemes and 
committed obligations, while only a limited share relates to non-recurring or one-off 
components. Exact proportions vary scheme-wise and are determined based on 
annual requirements and implementation priorities.”

the Department has submitted: 
 
“Major reforms in monitoring and evaluation of schemes have been undertaken by 
the Government over the last few years, including bringing the public funded schemes 
and projects under a monitorable Output-Outcome framework. Since 2017-18, in 
addition to the financial outlays of schemes of the Ministries being indicated in the 
Budget document, the expected outputs and outcomes of the schemes are also being 
presented in a consolidated Output Outcome Monitoring Framework, along with the 
Budget. 
Outlay is the amount that is provided for a given scheme or project in the Budget; 
while Output refers to the direct and measurable product of program activities, often 
expressed in physical terms or units. Outcomes are the collective results or qualitative 
improvements brought about in the delivery of these services. This will significantly 
enhance transparency, predictability and ease of understanding of the Government’s 
development agenda. 
Through this exercise conducted jointly by Administrative Department, NITI Aayog 
and Ministry of Finance, the Government aims to nurture an open, accountable, pro-
active and purposeful style of governance by transitioning from mere outlays to result-
oriented outputs and outcomes. This effort enables us to keep track of the scheme 
objectives and work towards the development goals set by them. 
Apart from this, individual schemes have their own monitoring mechanism.”

revenue point of view. 
 The pace of release/utilization of funds, during 2021-22, under the Centrally

(i) States have been impressed upon to ensure full utilization of funds based on

(ii) States are being sensitized from time to time to complete the documentation

manner.

Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK)  
1.23 Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) are pivotal agricultural extension centers 
established to enhance the productivity and sustainability of agriculture. They 
were initiated to serve as a bridge between agricultural research and the farming 
community. KVKs focus on assessing and demonstrating location-specific 
agricultural technologies tailored to local conditions. This involves conducting on-
farm testing (OFT) and frontline demonstrations (FLD) to validate these 
technologies in real-world settings. They aim to develop the skills and knowledge 
of farmers through various training programs, workshops, and educational 
initiatives. This includes both short-term and long-term training courses designed 
for different target groups, including women and youth. KVKs provide farm 
advisory services using information and communication technology (ICT) to 
disseminate knowledge about modern agricultural practices, pest management, 
and crop production techniques. KVKs organize training sessions for farmers, 
extension personnel, and other stakeholders to update their knowledge about 
agricultural innovations. They conduct demonstrations to showcase the potential 
of various crops and farming techniques, helping farmers understand the 

19  
practical applications of research findings. Acting as knowledge hubs, KVKs 
compile and share valuable resources related to agricultural technologies, 
including seeds, planting materials, and bio-agents.

submitted: 
“It is submitted that under Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), digital 
technologies and remote sensing–based assessment tools are already being 
operationalized for crop loss estimation, in line with the emphasis in Union Budget 
2026–27 on leveraging digital technologies and AI-based assessment tools for faster 
and more accurate crop loss estimation. 
Satellite-derived indices such as Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) are 
used for monitoring crop health and vegetation stress, while Land Surface Water 
Index (LSWI) and similar spectral indicators are employed for identification of flood 
and waterlogging–affected areas. 
 Primarily, data from the Sentinel satellite series (Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-
3) are utilized for agricultural analysis. With spatial resolutions of 10–20 metres and 
revisit cycles of about 5 days for Sentinel-2 and 12 days for Sentinel-1, crops are 
monitored consistently throughout the crop cycle, from sowing to harvest, enabling 
timely detection of stress and damage. 
Further, these multi-temporal satellite indicators are integrated with weather 
parameters and historical yield data through Artificial Intelligence and Machine 
Learning (AI/ML) models to objectively classify affected areas and quantify crop 
damage. Such data-driven models support faster turnaround time and improved 
accuracy in loss assessment, particularly during large-scale calamities such as 
droughts and floods, thereby reducing subjectivity associated with manual 
assessments. 
The generated remote sensing outputs are also expected to play a significant role in 
supporting dispute-resolution mechanisms across different forums in future, by 
providing objective and time-stamped evidence of crop conditions. Additionally, the 
study facilitates assessment of cost–benefit aspects of satellite-based monitoring in 
comparison with drone-based approaches. 
AIC is also in discussions with the India Meteorological Department (IMD) to utilize 
various datasets available in the public domain. These datasets will be accessed via 
the IMD API and integrated into the inhouse developed GCMS platform of the 
Company  for various risk management activities.”

traditional knowledge. Natural Farming recognises the interdependence of the natural 
ecosystem amongst soil, water, microbiome, plants, animals, climate and human 
requirements.  
Budget outlay is ₹2481.00 crore, Government of India share is ₹1584.00 crore and 
States’ share is ₹897.00 crore (60:40 Centre and General States), (90:10- Centre and 
North Eastern States) Union Territories- 100% by the Centre.  
The Mission aims to introduce natural farming in 15,000 clusters covering 7.5 lakh 
hectares and to establish 10,000 Bio-Input Resource Centres (BRCs) to support 
18.75 lakh farmers in adopting natural farming and create awareness among 1 crore 
farmers about natural farming. So far, 18,624 clusters have been formed, 17.67 lakh 
farmers have been registered, and 8.71 lakh hectares have been covered under this 
scheme and 6,191 Bio-Input Resource Centres (BRCs) have been established. 30.63 
lakh farmers have been made aware about natural farming.  
Natural Farming Certification System (NFCS) has been initiated as “PGS-INDIA 

51  
NATURAL” for certification of all produces of natural farming in accordance with the 
Natural Farming Standards. It is an online certification system and implemented 
through National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF). More than 12.15 
lakhs farmers have been registered under the natural farming certification system till 
date with more than 4.85 lakh farmers issued NF certificates. Certification will enable 
farmers to get premium price for natural products. 
Against a budget allocation of ₹616.01 crore for the financial year 2025-26, a total of 
₹564.14 crore has been released and out of this ₹321.06 crore have been utilized by 
the State/UTs.”  
 
1.58 On being asked to state the long-term investment plan for water-use 
efficiency, particularly the allocation for micro-irrigation, to ensure the "Per-Drop-
More-Crop (PDMC)" vision is realized nationally before 2047, the Department 
has submitted: 
“PDMC focuses on enhancing water use efficiency at farm level through Micro 
Irrigation namely Drip and Sprinkler Irrigation Systems. The Micro Irrigation helps in 
water saving as well as reduced fertilizer usage through fertigation, labour expenses, 
other input costs and overall income enhancement of farmers. 
PDMC also envisages to establish convergence with other various ongoing 
schemes/programmes such as Watershed Development, MGNREGS, Atal Bhujal 
Yojana, Command Area Development & Water Management, PM-KUSUM etc. for 
better utilization of available resources particularly created water sources for their 
potential use. It also envisages to utilise the source/infrastructure created under 
Modernisation of Command Area Development (MCAD) Programme for Micro 
Irrigation. Emphasis is given to prioritize implementation of Micro Irrigation through 
Cluster approach involving Water User Associations/Groups, Farmer Producer 
Organizations, Cooperative societies, Self-Help Groups, Growers’ Associations etc. 
for coverage of more area under micro irrigation.  
From 2015-16 to so far, Central Assistance of Rs. 25856.83 crore has been provided 
to States for implementation of PDMC and 108 lakh ha has been covered under Micro 
Irrigation in the country through PDMC during this period which is a significant 
achievement. 
With the objective of facilitating the States in mobilizing resources for expanding 
coverage of Micro Irrigation, a dedicated Micro Irrigation Fund of Rs 10000 crore has 
been created with NABARD to emphasize the micro-irrigation coverage in the 
country. States can avail loan from the MIF for taking up special and innovative 

52  
projects for expanding coverage of Micro Irrigation and for incentivizing micro 
irrigation beyond the provisions available under PDMC scheme to encourage 
farmers. Government of India provides interest subvention @ 2% on the loan availed

So far, projects of Rs. 4970 crore has been sanctioned under MIF and loan amount

UT of Jammu and Kashmir.  
Coverage of more area under Micro Irrigation is one of the thrust areas of 
Government, as it has important role in enhancing  farmers’ income which is the 
priority/vision of the Government. Adoption of micro irrigation helps in increasing 
water use efficiency, productivity, reduction in cost etc which ultimately results in 
enhancing farmer’s income. In view of ensuring more coverage of area under micro 
irrigation, continuation of the PDMC scheme is proposed beyond 15th Finance

Government. This scheme is voluntary for States as well as farmers. All willing farmers

a farmer having land cultivation. Further, under AgriStack the sown crop and area is also 
validated to ascertain that the insured crop and insured area remains same and within 
the given extent of sown area, validation of crop sown is being done through Digital Crop 
Survey done as well from Kharif 2025 in 4 States.    Further, remote sensing technology 
and physical assessment on the ground through District Authority and concerned 
Insurance Companies is used for identifying the acreage discrepancies and instances of 
ineligible insured crops & areas under District Level Monitoring Committee (DLMC) 
chaired by the respective District Magistrate of the districts.  Crop Cutting Experiments 
are being co-observed by insurance companies and OTP based validation from such co-
observance is measured to ensure transparency.  GOI  has also mandated 100% CCEs

NEED TO ENHANCE RATE OF GROWTH IN CAPITAL FORMATION IN 
AGRICULTURE 
 
3. The Committee note that Agriculture is the backbone of our economy 
& dependence on Nature enhances the risk quotient of our Agriculture and 
this is precisely where high growth rate of Capital formation in agriculture 

88  
can effectively mitigate the risks arising due to natural calamities.  It is a 
proven fact that the growth of capital formation is a vital prerequisite for 
agricultural development.  Capital Formation in Agriculture refers to 
investment in-(i) creating physical assets such as infrastructure (ii) 
upgradation and honing of skills of human resources; and (iii) institutional 
capacity to boost productivity.  
Sustained growth in agricultural output and farmer incomes depends 
mainly on higher capital formation in agriculture. The rate of growth in 
Capital Formation in Agriculture is essential to enhance productivity as the 
most significant impact of increase in capital formation is the increase in 
yield per hectare. Growth in agricultural capital creates demand for 
industrial goods such as fertilizers, pesticides and so on. Increased 
agricultural output supplies raw materials to industries such as textiles and 
food processing. Strong infrastructure enables farmers in getting higher 
price realization for their agricultural produce. Thus, capital formation in 
agriculture acts as an engine for overall industrial growth too. 
The growth rate in Capital Formation (at current prices) in Agriculture 
and Allied Sector during the year 2019-20 was 5.1% which rose to 22.8% 
during the period 2020-21, then fell down to 15.5% in the year 2021-22, 
increased marginally during 2022-23 to 16.1% and then fell significantly to 

89  
9.6% during the period 2023-24. 
The Committee also note that the budget outlay for Agriculture & 
Farmers Welfare was Rs.1,24,000 Crore in 2022-23 whereas the total Budget 
outlay of the Union Government was Rs.39,44,908.6 Crore for the same 
period. Similarly, the budgetary allocation to Agriculture in 2026-27 BE is 
Rs. 130,561.38 Crore whereas the total budget outlay of the Union 
Government is Rs.53,47,315 Crore. Simply put, from 2022-23 to 2026-27, 
there is an increase of Rs.6561.38 Crore in Budgetary allocation for 
Agriculture and Farmers Welfare whereas the Total Budget outlay of the 
Union Government increased by Rs 14,02,406.34 Crore during the same 
period which indicates that the budgetary allocation for Agriculture did not 
get an increase in the same proportion. The Actual Expenditure in total 
Scheme Budget (Central Sector + Centrally Sponsored Schemes) of 
Agriculture in 2020-21 was Rs.1,08,090.13 Crore, out of which the  Actual 
expenditure incurred on PM-KISAN and Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima 
Yojana(PMFBY) was Rs.75151.38 Crore, which translates into Actual 
Expenditure of Rs.32938.75 Crore on other heads including creation of 
Capital Assets. This meant that around 69.53% of total Scheme Budget of 
Agriculture was spent on payment to farmers and Insurance companies 
during the period 2020-21. Similarly, the Actual Expenditure in Total 

90

 The Committee recommend the Government to enhance investments

NATIONAL MISSION ON NATURAL FARMING(NMNF)/ORGANIC FARMING 
 
8. The Committee are of the view that shifting Indian agriculture, which 
is deeply entrenched in the "Green Revolution" mindset of chemical inputs,

The Committee recommend that to make Natural & Organic Farming  a

making fertilizers available to a "Demand-Pull" model for natural produce.  
Farmers need to be given an option to get cash equivalent to the amount of 
subsidy spent on Chemical fertilizers  in their respective farmlands through 
Direct Benefit Transfer(DBT).  Proper checks and balances including 
checking of samples of Soil Health need to be added to ensure 
transparency and accountability. Institutions such as Food Corporation of 
India(FCI), NAFED etc. needs to be directed to a mandatorily procure a 
certain percentage of Natural and Organic Products from Farmers/FPOs to 

99  
give boost to Natural & Organic Farming. The Department in consultation 
with Department of Food & Consumer Affairs also launch a “Bharat 
Natural” brand with select cereals with distinct identity for Natural and 
Organic Products by offering a premium price to consumers initially. By 
giving farmers an option of replacing subsidy on chemical fertilizers with 
transition grants and premium market access, the government can make 
Natural & Organic Farming not just an ecological choice, but an 
economically superior one for our farmers. The Committee also 
recommend the Department to prioritize  promoting native seeds, which are 
of superior grade — climate resilient and nutrient-rich for sustainable 
farming. These should be made readily available to farmers nationwide

PRADHAN MANTRI FASAL BIMA YOJANA(PMFBY) 
 
 
9. The Committee note that Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) 
was launched in 2016 to provide financial support to farmers in the event 

100  
of crop failure and since then, it has removed the capping on premiums and 
lowered farmer premium rates. The Committee feel that Post-Harvest 
Losses (PHL) component may require further attention under the scheme. 
The flaws in area approach in PMFBY needs to be rectified to protect 
farmers. The data regarding yield of crops is still largely collected through 
manual Crop Cutting Experiments (CCEs), which are slow, labour intensive 
and prone to human error or manipulation. Due to the delay in conducting 
CCEs and submitting data, claim settlements often happen months after 
the damage, sometimes even in the next season. This delays in getting 
instant relief for the farmer. Post-harvest losses is currently restricted only 
to crops kept in "cut and spread" condition in the field, which is a very small 
and narrow window.  The definition in PMFBY must be expanded to include 
losses occurring during pre and post harvest crops damaged due to 
lightning, fire, hailstorms, stray or wild animals, rodent & locust infestation 
irrespective of the percentage. Further, losses incurred during 
transportation, threshing, and temporary storage at the farm level, in gunny 
bags or open courtyards for up to four weeks after harvest may also be 
covered.  A "Crop Losses Reporting App" needs to be mandated  in the 
guidelines where farmers can upload geo-tagged photos of damaged 
harvested produce within 48 hours of the event subsequently followed by 

101  
assessment of damage by drones which will expedite the claim process 
faster. 
The Committee recommend that Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana 
need to become a truly comprehensive one to usher into a latest version of 
itself, by evolving from a "Crop Yield Scheme" to a "Farm Income 
Protection Scheme"  and for this to materialize, a shift from “Yield 
insurance” to “Value Chain insurance” needs to be adopted. and include 
Post-Harvest Losses by using a blend of technology such as drones& 
digital apps for assessment and actuarial reforms to cover the extended 
storage period post harvest. A “Post Harvest Clause” may be inserted into 
PMFBY to include losses from fire, natural calamities, losses occurring 
during transportation, threshing, and temporary storage at the farm level,  
kept in gunny bags or open courtyards for up to four weeks after harvest 
to enlarge the existing safety net. 
 
FARMER PRODUCER ORGANISATIONS 
 
10.  The Committee are of the view that Farmer Producer Organizations 
(FPOs) have massive potential to transform the landscape of Indian 
Agriculture. FPOs are central to the government's strategy to enhance 
farmer income, as they aggregate small and marginal farmers to achieve 
economies of scale. The Committee feel that while the formation of FPOs 

102  
has been rapid, their functionality and sustainability remain major 
challenges. 
Most FPOs are new and hence, have less assets. Banks are hesitant 
to lend to them without collateral, and the available Credit Guarantee 
Fund(CGF) covers only a fraction of the working capital needs. The Central 
Sector Scheme promises equity grants up to ₹15 lakh to match farmer 
contributions, but delays in release severely cause issues  the FPO's initial 
working capital. FPOs are typically managed by farmer-directors who 
possess agricultural knowledge but often lack business management 
skills. FPOs often lack access to aggregation centers, sorting/grading 
units, and cold storage. Without these, they are forced to sell raw produce 
immediately after harvest, missing the opportunity for price arbitrage. 
The  Committee recommend that the focus of the Government  should 
shift to handholding the FPOs during the first 5 years to ensure that  they 
become engines of rural prosperity and the work done  to provide the initial 
push through grants and other support, does not go unrewarded.

EMPHASIS ON SOIL HEALTH 
 
13. The Committee feel that Soil health is the main edifice of agricultural 
productivity and food security.  The Committee feel that the time lag 
between sample collection of soil and delivery of Soil Health Card(Soil 
Health Card) needs to be reduced to less than a week. Presently,  by the 
time the  Soil health card is received, the cropping season may have already 
begun or passed, rendering the advice useless for that cycle. The grid size  
issues also needs to be sorted  out as  a farm within a grid of 10 
hectares(rainfed) or 2.5 hectares(irrigated) may have different nutrient 

105  
profiles. There is a shortage of laboratories, leading to outsourcing to 
private players. The quality of analysis varies between labs due to a lack of 
standardized equipment and quality control protocols. On many occasions, 
the Soil Health Card issued miss details regarding secondary nutrients 
such as Sulphur and micronutrients such as Zinc, Boron, Iron which are 
the  limiting factors in the soil today due to intensive farming. The technical 
jargon on the cards also needs to be simplified to the maximum extent 
possible so that small and marginal farmers can easily decipher the 
recommendations made.  The availability of micronutrients such as Zinc, 
Gypsum  and so on also needs to be made  on regular basis across the 
Cooperative Stores.  
The Committee recommend that dynamic measures need to be taken 
to increase carbon content in the soil across the country and geo tagging 
of soil samples need to be done to create a geospatial database which will 
help in creating precise "Soil Fertility Maps" for districts, moving away from 
generic grid sampling to farm-specific analysis.  Testing for biological 
parameters (organic carbon, microbial biomass) and physical parameters 
(soil structure, water holding capacity) also needs to be included 
mandatorily in Soil Health Card. The Committee also recommend that Near-
Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS) and satellite imagery to map soil properties 

106  
need to be completed across the country at the earliest years as this would 
allow for larger area coverage with higher accuracy. The Soil Health Card 
needs to be simplified in common terms   and over a period of time replace 
it with a Digital App in areas where internet connectivity is good so that real 
time nutrient recommendations based on GPS-tagged soil samples.  
Extension workers also need to be trained to interpret Soil Health Cards 
and counsel farmers while distributing them. Soil health data should be 
integrated with the PM Fasal Bima Yojana as healthy soils reduce crop risk 
and farmers with good soil health scores could be offered lower insurance 
premiums, turning soil conservation for farmers into an additional revenue 
stream. The Committee feel that the Department need to maintain subsidy 
on drip and sprinkler irrigation  as this lowers farmers’ initial investment in 
micro-irrigation systems and encourage faster adoption while allowing 
cultivation in water scarce areas as well as allowing precise and timely 
application of fertilizers thereby providing cost advantage especially to 
small and marginal farmers. 
 
The Committee further recommend the Department to explore ways 
and methods to implement subsurface drainage systems existing in 
Belgavi in Karnataka, in water logging areas & coastal areas  across the 
country  to  effectively combat excess surplus subsoil water for disposal 

107  
into surface drains which will transform underproductive lands, enhance 
soil health and  increase farmer prosperity.

NATIONAL SEEDS CORPORATION 
 
14. The Committee note that National Seeds Corporation (NSC) is the 
apex body for seed production in India and has played a historic role in the 
Green Revolution. It now faces competition from a vibrant private sector 
and hence, it requires a strategic roadmap to transform itself to meet the 
existing and upcoming challenges. The Committee are of the considered 
view that Upper Price Ceiling of Seeds needs to be implemented by the 
Department to protect farmers and their legitimate interests. 
The Committee feel that NSC needs to invest in the state of the art  
machinery for seed production and packaging & establish climate-
controlled warehouses to prevent seed deterioration, implement RFID 
(Radio Frequency Identification) and Blockchain technology for real-time 
tracking of seed lots from production to the retailer, ensuring traceability 
and checking counterfeiting. NSC need to use satellite imagery and drones 
to monitor seed crops in real-time to ensure isolation distances and 
rouging (removal of off-type plants) are done effectively, addressing the 
issue of genetic impurity. NSC also needs to promote native seeds to 
farmers across the country to strengthen crop resilience, maintain 
ecological balance, secure and enrich crop diversity. NSC is heavily tilted 
towards cereals such as wheat, paddy whereas the market is shifting 

109  
towards horticulture, millets, and hybrids. NSC also needs to shore up its 
last mile delivery to boost sales. 
The Committee recommend that National Seeds Corporation (NSC) 
needs to transform its identity from a "Government Supplier" to a " 
futuristic, global-grade seed entity " by combining public sector trust and 
reach with the efficiency and technology of private sector. The Committee 
also recommend that NSC should ensure that Indian farmers have access 
to affordable, high-quality, and climate-resilient seeds, even in areas with 
sub-optimal connectivity, in securing the nation's food future. 
 
NATIONAL HORTICULTURE BOARD(NHB) 
 
15. The Committee note that National Horticulture Board (NHB) plays an 
important role in developing Post-Harvest infrastructure (PHI) and 
promoting high-tech Horticulture. The Committee feel that NHB needs to 
engage itself more proactively and act as a catalyst to transform the 
landscape of Post Harvest Infrastructure and high-tech Horticulture. 
The Committee recommend that NHB needs to completely digitize its 
operations, focus on integrated value chains, and link horticulture directly 
to export markets. For the farmers who are not tech savvy, options to need 
to be given to them to submit their application in physical copies. The 
administrative processes in NHB schemes also needs to be streamlined to 

110  
expedite approvals and 100% of subsidies need to be transferred directly 
to the beneficiary's bank account, removing intermediaries and state-level 
bottlenecks. NHB should mandate the creation of integrated "Pack Houses" 
linked to cold storages and transport. NHB schemes need to be aligned 
with Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF) to provide interest subvention, 
making capital cheaper for entrepreneurs setting up processing units. The 
Committee also recommend that NHB should register Geographical 
Indication (GI) tags for horticultural produce and create an umbrella brand 
for Indian agro produce for international marketing and also promote bio 
fortified varieties. 
 
VIKSIT BHARAT-2047 
 
16.  The Committee recommend that in order to visualize the dream of a 
developed India- Viksit Bharat by 2047, the roadmap requires shifting from 
a "production-centric" approach to an "income-centric" and 
"sustainability-centric" model. Artificial Intelligence needs to be integrated 
with precision farming to optimize input use such as water, fertilizers etc. 
thereby reducing costs & environmental damage and drones need to be 
utilized for crop monitoring purposes.  The Committee also recommend 
that a unified digital ecosystem connecting land records, soil health, 

111  
weather forecasts, and market prices needs to be implemented. This data 
should be available to farmers on a single smartphone app to support real-
time decision-making. Shift from "per acre" irrigation to "per drop" 
irrigation needs to be done and Micro-irrigation (drip/sprinkler) must 
become the norm, covering 100% of irrigated areas. For this purpose, inter-
departmental convergence may be strengthened between the Department 
of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare(DAFW) and the Department of Water 
Resources, River Development & Ganga Rejuvenation under the Ministry 
of Jal Shakti. Steps may also be taken to enhance participation at the 
grassroots level through the involvement of village-level Water Committees 
at the Gram Sabha Level. The Committee further recommend that Indian 
farmers  need to be positioned as providers of "Carbon Credits" thereby 
creating an additional revenue stream for them. The Department should 
also establish specialized training centres for modern techniques like 
hydroponics, aquaponics, and drone piloting at block level in the country. 
A "Green Revolution 2.0" specifically for the Eastern and North-Eastern 
states, needs to be launched to bridge the regional productivity gap, 
focusing on horticulture and pulses. FPOs need to be transformed as 
centres of Global Trade completely transforming the profile of the 
Annadata into a prosperous individual. Most importantly, by 2047, 

112  
agriculture needs to be turned into a sustainable, profitable and resilient 
business thereby catapulting India to lead the Ivy league of nations. 
 
EDUCATIONAL SUPPORT FOR CHILDREN OF SMALL AND SEMI-MEDIUM 
FARMERS  
 
17. The Committee observes that small and semi-medium farmers form 
the backbone of India’s agricultural economy. However, many farming 
households continue to face financial distress due to low and uncertain 
farm incomes, rising input costs and indebtedness.  
In view of this situation, the Committee is of the considered opinion 
that the State must extend stronger social and educational support to the 
families of farmers. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Government of India, through the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare 
in coordination with the Ministry of Education, introduce a National 
Scholarship Scheme for the Children of Small and Semi-Medium Farmers, 
with special provisions for the children of farmers affected by extreme 
financial distress. 
 
18. The Committee recommend that before concluding any bilateral or 
multilateral agreements which affect the farming sector, appropriate 
consultations may be held with the relevant stakeholders.

SUBSIDY ON EV TRACTORS 
 
 
19. The Committee feel that EV (Electric Vehicle) tractors are vital for 
sustainable farming as they cut diesel emissions significantly thereby 
promoting a greener planet with lesser carbon emissions. They also 
promote energy independence via solar charging and align them with 
India's net-zero goals. 
The Committee recommend that proportionate subsidies may be 
given on EV Tractors to that of Diesel Tractors to make them economically 
feasible and boost their acceptance among the farmers especially Small 
and Marginal farmers. The Committee also recommend that infrastructure 
relating to EV charging facilities may also be scaled up in rural areas to 
make EV tractors popular among the farmers.

4 Pg.88-89 CHANGE OF NOMENCLATURE OF THE DEPARTMENT OF 
AGRICULTURE & FARMERS WELFARE TO DEPARTMENT OF 
AGRICULTURE, FARMERS & FARM LABOURERS WELFARE 
 
    The Committee are of the considered view that the nomenclature of 
the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare of the Union 
Government may be changed to Department of Agriculture, Farmers & 
Farm Labourers Welfare. This would not be merely a semantic change 
but would represent and enable a paradigm shift in policy focus. Till now, 
the focus of the Department has mostly been on production and yield of 
agricultural produce but the proposed change in nomenclature will also 
focus on the human resources involved in the production and recognise 
the unsung efforts of "invisible hands" of Indian agriculture, Farm or 
Landless labourers. This inclusion of “Farm Labourers” in the official 
nomenclature will send a strong message that the Government grants 
official recognition to the Farm Labourers and acknowledge their 
immense contribution in the agricultural ecosystem ensuring that they 
do not remain on the margins of policy benefits. 
 
The Committee feel that revising the nomenclature of the Department 
and adding the term “Farm Labourers” to the title, offers several potential 
benefits to this vulnerable section of Farm Labourers, including paving 
the way for their inclusion in specific social security schemes such as 
pension funds, health insurance (under Ayushman Bharat), and 
maternity benefits tailored specifically for agricultural labourers, distinct 
from general labour welfare. 
 
      The Committee recommend that the proposed change of 
nomenclature of the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare of the 
Union Government at present to Department of Agriculture, Farmers & 
Farm Labourers Welfare may be done to send a clear signal that the 
Government accords the same dignity and status to Farm Labourers as 
that of Land-owning farmers and that the sweat and toil of Farm 
Labourers is immensely valued by the nation. This would also broaden 

188  
the vision of the Department which manages crops at present to the one 
which would be responsible for the protection of the entire human 
resources involved in ensuring food security of the country and aligns 
with the broader goals of Social Justice and Equity as enshrined in the 
Constitution. The new change would also ensure that this vulnerable 
group of Farm Labourers, majority of whom belong to SC/ST/OBC, are 
protected during agrarian crises as they are the ones who primarily lose 
their employment during such hardships. This change in nomenclature 
would also enable the creation of specific budgetary support for skill 
development of Farm Labourers helping them to acquire new skill sets 
in operation of latest agricultural machines & gadgets, thereby 
increasing their earning potential. The Committee further recommend 
that the Department should explore ways and methods in which 
schemes like PM-KISAN may be extended to Farm Labourers. This 
would significantly play an important role in curbing distress migration 
from rural to urban areas by Farm labourers, as this workforce can be 
retained in rural areas through employment guarantees and livelihood 
diversification. This will facilitate a broad-based growth across the 
country.” 
 
The Committee were of the view that detailed discussion with all 
relevant stakeholders including different government departments 
including Ministry of Labour may be done and hence, the matter of 
change of nomenclature may be deferred. However, the Committee 
were of the view that the above narration may be replaced with the 
following- 
 
IMPORTANCE OF FARM LABOURER AND THE NEED FOR THEIR 
INCLUSION IN THE MANDATE OF DEPARTMENT OF 
AGRICULTURE AND FARMERS WELFARE  
 
     The Committee are of the view that Farm Labourers are an 
important component of the Agricultural ecosystem in the country. 
Farm Labourers face unique & diverse challenges which are totally 
different than faced by any other class of Labourers including 
industrial labourers. In the current welfare measures being 
implemented by the Ministry right focus is being accorded for 
farmer welfare, but the labour which provides the necessary 
workforce in the agriculture fields is unable to reap the benefit as 
they do not own the land which they till.  The Committee feel that 
the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DA&FW) should 
make concerted efforts for identifying the unique challenges being 
faced by the labourers working on the farms. The Committee 
further note that while the   Ministry of Labour is the nodal Ministry 
to implement the welfare schemes for the labour class in the 
country, however farm labour being a distinct category and are 
different from industrial labour. As such, the agriculture labour are 
not identified as workmen/industrial labour and they are unable to 

189  
reap the benefits being accorded to industrial labourers viz. 
Medical Care, Insurance and social Security. In this backdrop the 
Committee recommend that the Ministry Agriculture & Farmers 
Welfare (DA&FW) should consider to widen the ambit of 
component of Farmer Welfare and should include the welfare of 
Farm Labourers within its scope. The Committee also recommend 
that the Ministry needs to identify programmes, schemes, 
missions etc for their inclusion in specific social security schemes 
such as pension funds, Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana, 
and maternity benefits tailored specifically for agricultural 
labourers.

     The Committee recommend that before concluding any bilateral or 
multilateral agreements which affect the farming sector, appropriate 
consultations may be held with the relevant stakeholders. 
 
      The Committee feel that EV(Electric Vehicle) tractors are vital for 
sustainable farming as they cut diesel emissions significantly thereby 
promoting a greener planet with lesser carbon emissions. They also 
promote energy independence via solar charging and align them with 
India's net-zero goals. 
 
      The Committee recommend that proportionate subsidies may be 
given on EV Tractors to that of Diesel Tractors to make them 
economically feasible and boost their acceptance among the farmers 
especially Small and Marginal farmers. The Committee also recommend 
that infrastructure relating to EV charging facilities may also be scaled 
up in rural areas to make EV tractors popular among the farmers.
