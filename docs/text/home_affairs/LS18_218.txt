INTRODUCTION
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having
been authorized by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Two Hundred
Eighteenth Report on Action Taken by Government on the Recommendations/Observations contained in the
Two Hundred Tenth Report on Demands for Grants (2018-19) of the Ministry of Development of North
Eastern Region (DoNER).
2. The Two Hundred Tenth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Home Affairs was presented to the Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of the Lok Sabha on 4th April,
2018. After presentation of the Report, the copies thereof were sent to the Ministry of DoNER on 4th
April, 2018 requesting them to furnish Action Taken Notes on the recommendations of the Committee
contained in the Report. The Ministry of DoNER furnished the English Version of the Action Taken Notes
on the above mentioned Two Hundred Tenth Report to the Committee on 16th October, 2018 and the
Hindi Version on 5th November, 2018.
3. The Committee has noted that the statement on the status of implementation of the recommendations
contained in the said Report of the Committee has been laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha
on 3rd and 2nd January, 2019 respectively in pursuant to the direction of Chairman, Rajya Sabha dated
24th September 2004.
4. The Committee considered the draft Two Hundred Eighteenth Report and adopted the same at its
meeting held on 5th February, 2019.
5. The recommendations/observations of the Committee are printed in bold letters.

REPORT
The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the Ministry of
Development of North Eastern Region on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Tenth
Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2018-19) of the Ministry. The Two Hundred Tenth
Report was presented to Rajya Sabha /laid on the Table of Lok Sabha, on 4th April, 2018.
2. Action Taken Notes as received from the Ministry of DoNER in respect of the recommendations
contained in the Two Hundred Tenth Report have been categorized as follows:
Chapter I: The Recommendations/Observations, which have been accepted by the Government:
Para Nos. 2.6.4, 2.9.7, 2.10.8, 2.11.10, 2.12.3, 2.14.8, 2.19.6, 2.22.6, 3.11.12, 3.11.13 and 3.15.4
(of Two Hundred Tenth Report)
Total recommendations-11
The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry has accepted its recommendations as
mentioned in Chapter-I of the Report.
Chapter II: The Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue
in view of the Government's replies: Para Nos. 2.2.2, 2.5.5, 2.9.5, 2.25.8, 3.1.6 and 3.10.2, ( ibid)
Total recommendations - 6
The Committee is convinced with the explanations furnished by the Ministry and, therefore,
does not want to pursue the recommendations further.
Chapter III: The Recommendations/Observations in respect of which the Committee is not satisfied
with the replies of the Ministry: Para Nos. 2.9.9, 2.11.11, 2.18.4, 2.18.5, 2.21.6, 2.21.10, 2.23.8,
3.6.3, 3.6.6, 3.6.14, 3.8.3, 3.11.11, 3.12.8, 3.13.3, 3.14.5 and 3.16.4 ( ibid)
Total recommendations - 16
The Committee expresses its concern over the non-implementation of its recommendations
contained in Chapter-III and desires that the Ministry should furnish convincing action taken steps
in respect of those recommendations.
Chapter IV: The Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the Government
have not been received: Para Nos. 2.4.4, 2.8.11, 2.20.5, 2.24.11, 3.6.10, 3.7.16, 3.9.2, 3.11.14
and 3.11.15 ( ibid)
Total Recommendations - 9
The Committee desires that the Ministry should furnish pointed and detailed Action Taken Notes
in respect of the Observations/Recommendations made in all chapters of the Report.
3. The details of the ATNs have been analyzed in the succeeding chapters.

1.2 SPECIAL DEVELOPMENT PROJECTS
Recommendation
1.2.1 The Committee takes note of the submission of the Secretary, NEC that the moment the NEC
transfers the money to the CPWD, it would be assumed that it has been spent. The Committee finds this
response of the NEC to be very unsatisfactory. The Committee observes that if the NEC transfers the
money in the month of February or March, the CPWD will find it difficult to spend and most probably
it will lapse, which is highly objectionable. The Committee also observes that simply transferring the
funds does not mean that it would be spent by the end of the Financial Year for the intended purpose
and recommends that this distinction should be clearly defined and taken into account in future projects.
(Para 2.9.7 ibid)
Action Taken
1.2.2 The recommendations of the Committee are noted for compliance. The matter is related to the
scheme of North Eastern Road Sector Development Scheme (NERSDS). The latest sequence of events
are as follows:-
(i) Continuation of the scheme beyond March, 2017 was recommended by EFC in December,
2017 with an allocation of ` 1000.00 crore, which has been approved by the Cabinet in its
meeting held on 28th March, 2018.
(ii) In the meantime the preparation of the DPRs for the identified projects was assigned to BBJ
and CPWD.
(iii) NEC signed MoU with CPWD on 21st February, 2018 for implementation of the Scheme.
Therefore, fund amounting to ` 138.00 crore was transferred to CPWD.
(iv) However, now with the approval of competent authority in place, the situation shall be
avoided in future.

1.6 NER Livelihood (EAP) Project (NERLP)
Recommendation
1.6.1 The Committee feels that the major impediment in the implementation of NERLP is the absence

the banks. Hence, NERLP is doing its best on this aspect.
1.6.4 The following steps are being taken for bank linkages of the SHGs:-
(i) MoUs have been signed with IDBI (Sikkim), Tripura Grameen Bank (TGB), Tripura State

(ii) Bank Sakhis are already deployed in various bank branches of Sikkim (21 nos.) and Tripura
(90 nos.). Another 21 Bank Sakhis are being trained in Mizoram and deployed in the banks
of Mizoram for credit linkage. In September first week 11 Bank Sakhis were trained again
in Nagaland covering Peren and Tuensang Districts and they will be deployed to the bank
branches of these two districts very soon.
(iii) Two Banking Correspondents (BCs) are identified in Sikkim. They will be provided with the
infrastructures from the project. Accordingly, the BCs will be placed in the unbanked areas
of Sikkim. Efforts are being made to identify more BCs in the unbanked areas of all the
four project states.

CHAPTER-II
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS ON WHICH THE COMMITTEE DOES NOT DESIRE
TO PURSUE THE MATTER IN VIEW OF THE GOVERNMENT'S REPLY
2.1 Utilisation of Funds during the Last Three Years Recommendation
2.1.1 The Committee takes strong exception to the low utilisation of allocation, i.e. 29.3 per cent in
2015-16, 28.1 per cent in 2016-17 and 42.6 per cent in 2017-18 under the Social and Infrastructure
Development Fund (SIDF) in the allocations granted at BE Stage. The Committee had also expressed
its anguish over the dismal utilisation under this Fund in its 202nd Report on Demand for Grants
(2017-18) of the Ministry of DoNER. In its action taken notes on the report, the Ministry had taken the
plea that the poor utilisation during 2016-17 was due to non-submission of the alternative projects of
bigger size by the State Governments after scrapping of the projects identified earlier that were having
small envelope size. However, the Ministry had further stated that the State Governments had revisited
their proposals and had resubmitted projects of higher estimated cost subsequent to which the projects
were identified against the fund available and no balance fund was available to sanction any more
projects under the SIDF. The Committee had noted the said reply of the Ministry with the hope that all
the projects identified would be allocatedfunds under this scheme and would be completed on time.
However, the Committee is disappointed to note that during 2017-18, the allocation under SIDF has been
reduced at the RE stage. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry must furnish
a detailed project-wise utilisation schedule of funds under SIDF and the steps taken to improve the
utilisation. (Para 2.2.2 of 210th Report)
Action Taken
2.1.2 A total of 37 projects have been approved under Social and Infrastructure Development Fund
(SIDF) and no balance fund is available to sanction any new project. Out of total SIDF of ` 586.20
crore, ` 493.69 crore has been released so far. 14 new projects have been sanctioned recently (from
March, 2017 to March, 2018) in Arunachal Pradesh (9), Manipur (4) and Mizoram (1). Out of these,
utilization certificates, worth ` 30.00 crore, are due in 3 projects of Arunachal Pradesh and State
Government has been requested to furnish latest UC and other requisite documents for processing
release. Moreover, release of subsequent installments are pending for want of requisite documents in
2 old projects of Assam for which State Government has been reminded.
2.1.3 Ministry of DoNER periodically reviews/monitors the physical and financial progress of projects
through video conferences and field visits by senior officers. State Governments have also been requested
to increase the pace of utilization of funds and scrupulously adhere to the timelines in completion of the
projects to obviate any time and cost overrun.

2.5 Vision 2020
Recommendation
2.5.1 The Committee takes note of the thrust areas of Vision 2020 Document which inter-alia includes
inclusive growth, poverty eradication, rural sector, infrastructure, human resource development, banking,
industries and tourism. As the year 2020 is approaching, the Committee feels that progress of the projects,
undertaken under Vision 2020 Document, should be reviewed by Ministry of DoNER along with the
Ministries concerned to assess the current status vis-a-vis the set targets and make requisite policy
modification/intervention to ensure their completion within the stipulated time frame to achieve the
objectives of mission of Vision 2020. (Para 3.1.6 ibid)
Action Taken
2.5.2 NER Vision 2020 document, prepared after extensive public consultations held in all the 8 States
of NER and discussions in the 55th NEC Plenary held in December, 2007 was adopted in the following

11
Plenary Session held at Agartala on 13th May, 2008. The Vision 2020 document is a statement of the
felt requirements and needs of the people of the North Eastern Region of India. It included sectoral
strategies for areas such as inclusive growth, poverty eradication, rural sector, infrastructure, human
resource development, banking, industries and tourism. It identified a six-pronged strategy to bring about
inclusive development, help alleviate poverty and ensure reasonable standards of living for every family.
The six components of the strategy can be summarised as under:-
(i) Empowerment of the people to maximise self-governance and participatory development
through grassroots planning.
(ii) Expanding opportunities for a majority of the people living in villages through rural development
initiatives.
(iii) Developing sectors with comparative advantage so as to utilize the resources of the region
productively for the benefit and welfare of the people.
(iv) Human development including health and creating and building capacity of governance and
development institutions both in the private and public sectors.
(v) Creation of an investment climate by providing state-of-the-art infrastructure, especially
connectivity both, within the region and with the rest of the world.
(vi) Generating adequate resources for public investment in infrastructure, implementing a
framework for private participation in augmenting infrastructure
2.5.3 Since the adoption of the Vision 2020, NEC has referred to the document while planning the
various developmental strategies of the NER.
2.5.4 While PRI institutions exist in most North Eastern States and 14th Finance Commission had made
substantial fund devolution for the 3rd Tier of the Government. Provisions contained in parts IX and IXA
of the Constitution providing for Panchayats and Municipalities respectively, exempt certain areas from
the applicability of these parts. Besides, there are excluded areas under State Legislation eg Manipur Hill
areas. The amendment of the Sixth Schedule of Constitution for extending certain features of PRIs is
under consultation by Ministry of Parchayati Raj Institution (MOPRI) and Ministry of Tribal Affairs with
the concerned State Governments and the endeavour is for early alignment of Central and State Laws
with Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act (PESA). Efforts are also on by MOPRI to pursue
action to integrate traditional institutions with the formal system. The Home Ministry has launched
development packages in some Sixth Schedule districts of Assam which are being implemented by M/
o DONER. Thirteenth Finance Commission had made devolutions to local bodies in sixth Schedule and
other excluded areas for subjects assigned to PRIs. While the 14th Finance Commission did not devolve
funds to excluded areas, it had highlighted the need to address the development needs of these areas in
a focused manner. Accordingly, Ministry of Finance had made provisions for these areas. Ministry of
DONER and NEC have 25 per cent allocation for shelf of projects in these areas. Ministry of DoNER
has recently initiated a pilot in remote hilly district, namely, Tamenglong district in Manipur.

Ministry of DoNER should form an inspection team of Officers from the Ministries of DoNER
and Road Transport and Highways to visit the project sites by March, 2019 and review the
construction activities of roads. The Ministry desires to be apprised about the detailed findings
of the team at the earliest. (Para 3.6.3)
STATUS OF MAJOR ROADS
The Committee expresses its anguish at the abysmal physical progress of the work
undertaken at various sections of Dimapur-Kohima road for four laning as not even 50%
progress has been achieved on any of the sections with less than a year left in the scheduled date
of completion. The Committee notes that this slow pace of construction is attributable to delay
in shifting of 132 kv HT line tower of Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (PGCIL) and
delay in removal of encumbrances and submission of estimates of additional land. The Committee,
however, feels that these factors should have been taken into account beforehand and tackled
proactively. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry of DoNER must
constantly monitor the progress of this project and work with the other agencies concerned to
remove all the bottlenecks so that the project is completed within a set time-frame without any
cost escalation. (Para 3.6.7)
STATUS OF MAJOR ROADS
The Committee expresses its displeasure at the manner in which the road projects are
being planned and executed in the North Eastern region. The Committee notes that the damage
of the Agia-Medhipara-Phulbari-Tura (AMPT) Road, which is a crucial road in Meghalaya, is
mainly due to overloaded trucks plying on the road and the number of commercial vehicle plying
on this road is double the number that was considered in the original design. The Committee is
displeased with the short-sighted planning and non-existent monitoring of the traffic plying on
the road. The Committee recommends that the State Government should fix the accountability

46
for this failure and take necessary action. The Committee further recommends that urgent
measures may be initiated to re-lay the badly damaged parts of this road. The Committee also
recommends that the Ministry of DoNER should review its policy pertaining to roads maintenance/
upgradation that being the responsibility of the State Government concerned and also formulate
certain monitoring mechanism to inspect the condition of roads. The proposed inspection committee
may comprise of officials from both the Central Government and the State Government concerned.
(Para 3.6.11)
TELECOM SECTOR
The Committee takes note of the progress made with regard to the 20 per cent of the
Gram panchayat covered under Bharat Net Phase-I and the target set to cover 4938 Gram
Panchayat in phase-II. The Committee also notes the submission of the Ministry that electricity
is not available in more than 90% of Gram Panchayat locations. The Committee is anguished
at the poor state of infrastructure due to which the connectivity woes of the NER are not being
resolved. The Committee also notes that the Ministry has not provided any details of timeframe
of completion of Bharat Net Phase I and II. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Committee may be apprised about the implementation status and completion timeframe of
projects under both the Phases. The Committee also recommends that the Ministry of DoNER
must coordinate with State Governments in NER for removing hindrances faced by the Department
of Telecommunication which inter-alia include site acquisition for Mobile Towers (Assam and
Manipur), electricity Project and Economic Blockade (Manipur) and NOC from DC office (Arunachal
Pradesh, Meghalaya and Mizoram). (Para 3.7.3)
TOURISM
The Committee finds the response of the Ministry of DoNER evasive and unsatisfactory
with regard to the Committee's recommendation for release of the sanctioned amount of
````` 1267.49 crore against which only a sum of ````` 686.19 crore was released under the Swadesh
Darshan Scheme. The Committee strongly feels that the very purpose of creation of the Ministry
of DoNER was to coordinate with other Ministries including the Ministry of Tourism and the
Ministry of Finance regarding various developmental activities of NER. Further recommendation
of the Committee has not been communicated so far rather it is being communicated after so
much time. Therefore, Committee has no option but to reiterate its recommendation.
(Para 3.8.3)
NORTH EASTERN INDUSTRIAL AND INVESTMENT PROMOTION POLICY (NEIIPP)
The Committee notes that Union Cabinet has approved North East Industrial Development
Scheme (NEIDS) 2017, with an outlay of ````` 3,000.00 crore with effect from 1st April, 2017 and
that would remain in force upto 31st March, 2022. The Committee recommends that a strategy
should be chalked out to implement the programme/action plan to boost integrated and balanced
industrialization in NER. The Committee also desires to be apprised in detail about the North
East Industrial Development Scheme (NEIDS) 2017 and a comparative statement of components,

47
scope, and incentives of NEIDS vis-a-vis North East Industrial & Investment Promotion Policy
(NEIIPP), 2007 may be furnished. (Para 3.9.7)
NORTH EASTERN INDUSTRIAL AND INVESTMENT PROMOTION POLICY (NEIIPP)
The Committee further notes that the special scheme for promotion of MSMEs in NER and
Sikkim as approved in 2016, has now been made a component of new Central Sector Scheme
called "Trade and Economic Relations Committee (TERC)". The Committee hopes that necessary

apprised about the same. (Para 3.9.8)
AGRICULTURE AND FOOD PROCESSING SECTOR
The Committee notes the trend of allocation of funds to Mission Organic Value Chain
Development (MOVCD) for NER. Against the total budget allocation of ````` 15886.98 lakh in

of ````` 10,000.00 lakh in 2016-17, a sum of ````` 4868.42 lakh was released and against budget
allocation of ````` 10,000.00 lakh in 2017-18, a sum of ````` 6648.07 lakh was released. The Committee
notes that against the total fund released to the tune of ````` 22595.57 lakh only a sum of ````` 17791.83
lakh was utilized and a sum of ````` 4803.74 lakh remains unspent balance. The Committee,
therefore, recommends the Ministry to review the implementation status of MOVCD in NER and
undertake appropriate measure to achieve the targets. (Para 3.10.4)
ENVIRONMENT AND FORESTS
The Committee notes that NER received a total amount of ````` 52.47 crore for implementation
of National Mission for Green India (GIM). The Committee, however, expresses its displeasure
as no action seems to have been taken for implementation of Committee's recommendation. The
Committee feels that mere allocation and release of funds would not be effective in expanding
the forest cover without robust and effective monitoring, and regular evaluation of the various
components of Green India Mission. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its recommendation
and desires to be apprised about the measures taken in various North Eastern States for providing
training on sustainable agricultural practices, offering alternative livelihood opportunities to the
people living around the forest areas, and intensifying afforestation programmes. The Committee
also desires to be apprised about the various outcomes achieved in different States of the NER
as a result of implementation of Green India Mission. (Para 3.11.4)

in NER. (Para 3.12.13)
