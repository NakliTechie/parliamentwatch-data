REPORT
The North Eastern Region comprises of 8 States of Arunchal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur,
Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura and Sikkim. Excluding Sikkim, the region covers an area
of 255,000 sq. kms representing around 7.8 per cent of the total area of the country. It is a
distinct geographical entity connected with the rest of the country through a narrow corridor in
North Bengal thereby standing out as a classic example of cultural and geographical isolation from
the other composite States of India. The region shares 98 per cent of its borders with other South
East Asian countries viz. Bhutan and China in the North, Myanmar in the East and Bangladesh in
the South and West.
1.2 The region has a total population of 38.5 million as per 2001 census which is around 3.75
per cent of the country’s population.
1.3 The North Eastern States are categorised as Special Category States which entitles them to
receive 90% of the Central plan assistance as grants. However funds are not allocated State-wise.
Instead, Project specific assistance is released for sanctioned projects under the Central Pool. The
release of funds to the State Governments depends on their capacity to formulate sound project
proposals, utilize the funds released and the pace of implementation of the projects on the ground.
These funding sources and quantum of funds broadly take care of the requirement for development
programmes of the North Eastern States and are intended to continue till the Region reaches a
certain minimum level in terms of infrastructure development.
2. The Department of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) was constituted in
September, 2001. An extract of the amendments to the Government of India (Allocation of
Business) Rules, 1961 containing the work allocation to the Department is as follows:
• Matters related to the planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes
and projects of North Eastern region including those in the sectors of power, irrigation,
roads and communications.
• Hill Areas Development Programme and Border Areas Development Programme in
North Eastern region.
• Non-lapsable fund for the North Eastern region.
• North Eastern Council.
• North Eastern Development Finance Institution (NEDFI).
• North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited (NERAMAC).
• Sikkim Mining Corporation Limited (SMC).
• North Eastern Handlooms and Handicrafts Development Corporation (NEHHDC).
• Road works financed in whole or in part by the Central Government in the North
Eastern region.
• Planning of road and inland waterways transport in the North Eastern region.
3. The thrust of the Department in the development of North Eastern region consists of
improving infrastructure, providing greater access to basic social services like education, health,
drinking water and sanitation, etc., expanding economic and social opportunities for all, building

2
capacities aimed at increased efficiency, creating an environment for private participation conducive
to growth, and providing a policy frame to influence in a positive manner, wide ranging economic
activities having employment/income generation potential.

(i) Other Social Services
* Advocacy and Publicity
* Technical Assistance and Capacity Building
(ii) Industrial Financial Institutions
* Loans to North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd.
(iii) Non-ferrous Mining and Metal Industries

7.1 Under ‘Other Social Services’, the Department proposes to promote private sector

through other suitably designed programmes cutting across the Governmental and non-
Governmental sector. It also address the institutional capacity constraints of the region through
Capacity Building and Technical Assistance Programme.
7.2 Under ‘Industrial Financial Institutions’, DoNER extends long term interest free soft loan to
NEDFi. During 2002-03, an amount of Rs.15.40 crore has been disbursed to NEDFi. For
BE 2003-04, a similar amount has been approved.

7.3 As part of assistance to non-ferrous mining and metal industry, Sikkim Mining Corporation,
a joint venture of Governments of India and Sikkim with equity share capital ratio of 51:49

8. When asked about the objectives of DoNER, the Government, in its written reply, has stated
that DoNER attempts to promote development of North Eastern region by creating synergies and
convergence of the various programmes of different Departments of Government of India taking
up activities in the North-East. DoNER, however, has no mechanism or mandate to implement the
projects directly by itself. This apart, for meeting the gaps in the infrastructure sector, DoNER
assists the State Governments and Central agencies. DoNER has also been regularly monitoring the
requirement of 10% expenditure by Central Government Ministries/Departments in the North
Eastern region.
9. Regarding cultural activities the Department has stated that it is taking up cultural and sports
activities in a major way. It has partially funded the Sports Complex in Imphal (Khumlanpak) where
the 1999 National Games were held. In Sikkim, Paljor Stadium has been funded from NLCPR.
Another Sports Hall has been sanctioned this year at Mokokchung in Nagaland. In Bodo
Autonomous Council area in Assam, a mini stadium at Chapaguri and apex stadium at Kokrajhar
have also been sanctioned. In the field of culture, the Shankardev Kalakshetra in Guwahati is also
being assisted by the DoNER. Besides, a large number of schools, colleges and universities have
been assisted under NLCPR. In addition, NEC has also extended support for creation of
infrastructural facility for sports. It has assisted a sports complex each at Aizawal, Silchar and
Agartala. Rs. 34.50 crore has been earmarked for Tenth Plan. The proposed outlay for 2003-04
is Rs. 4 crore. On the cultural side, Fashion Shows, Crafts Exhibition, North Eastern Day and various
other cultural exchange programmes have been sponsored in North Eastern region by the DoNER.
10. On the greater use of Doordarshan for disseminating of information pertaining to the region
the Department has informed that the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting has declared the
year 2003 as the “Year of the North East”. On its part, DoNER has also taken up with Prasar
Bharati the need for screening North Eastern news and programmes on the National hookup of
Doordarshan. Besides, strengthening and upgradation of the low power transmitters in the North
Eastern region has been identified as an area to be attended to by the Ministry of Information and
Broadcasting.
11. As regards, ensuring financial discipline in the States of the region, the Department has
stated that in so far as DoNER’s own projects/schemes are concerned, a strict regime of on-
releases based on utilisation, photographs and quarterly progress reports is followed. The States
have been asked to reflect DoNER’s projects in the Annual Budget at the major and minor head
levels so that actual expenditures as per Accountant-General’s accounts can also be verified. For
the general financial discipline among the States, however, DoNER does not have a direct mandate
or authority. DoNER, however, is represented in the Core Group of Officers constituted by
Ministry of Finance which inter alia is looking into these aspects.
12. Regarding funding of more developmental programmes through Central Government
agencies the Department has stated that the budgetary allocation to DoNER is classified as
‘Assistance to State Plan’. Therefore, like State Plan, the DoNER funds are released as 90% Grant
and 10% Loan which is the liability of the respective State Governments. While DoNER does strive
to give as much projects for execution directly by Central Government agencies as it can, this can
only be done if the State Government agrees to it since it is the State Government which would
be repaying the 10% loan. With the consent of the State Governments, the DoNER has engaged
Central Government agencies like Border Roads Organisation (BRO), Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd.
(BHEL), Power Grid Corporation of India Ltd. (PGCIL), etc. for execution of some of the
projects.

NORTH EASTERN COUNCIL (NEC)
15. The North-Eastern Council was set up in 1972, following the enactment of the North
Eastern Council Act, 1971. For a long time, the North Eastern States had been demanding
restructuring of the Council so as to make it more effective. In pursuance of the demand, the
North-Eastern Council (Amendment) Act, 2002 came into force on 23rd December, 2002. In terms
of the amendment, Sikkim has become the eighth member of the Council. The composition of the
Council has been revised and now consists of the Governors and Chief Ministers of Arunachal
Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura. In addition, three
members are to be nominated by the President of India to the Council. The President of India may,
if deemed necessary, nominate a Union Minister to be a member of the Council. The Chairman of
the Council will be nominated by the President. By practice, the Governor of one of the
Member States is nominated as Chairman. Presently, the Governor of Assam is the Chairman of
the Council.
15.1 The Council’s functions have also been modified and it is now to function as a regional
planning body for the North-Eastern region. While formulating the regional plan, the Council will
give priority to schemes and projects which benefit two or more States, with the exception of
Sikkim for which the Council will formulate State-specific Projects and Schemes. The Council will
also review, from time to time, implementation of projects and schemes, progress of expenditure
and measures taken by the Member States for the maintenance of security and public order and
recommend to the State Governments further measures to be taken in this regard. For
accomplishing the above functions, the Council shall have such powers as may be delegated to it
by the Central Government. In terms of the amendment, the Council is required to meet at least
twice a year. The amendment also provides for participation of a representative of Department of
Development of North Eastern Region in the meetings of the Council.
NEC Secretariat
15.2. The Secretariat of the North Eastern Council is located at Shillong. The Secretariat is
responsible for the formulation of NEC’s Plan Schemes and their implementation. Secretary heads
the Secretariat. The Secretary is assisted by 12 Sectoral Heads viz. Planning Adviser, Financial
Adviser, Adviser (Transport and Communications), Adviser (Power), Director (Manpower), Ex-
Officio Adviser (Health), Director (S&T), Ex-Officio  Adviser (Forests), Ex-Officio  Adviser
(Minerals), Adviser (Horticulture), Adviser (Banking and Industries) and Adviser (Irrigation and
Flood Control).
15.3 From inception in 1972, NEC Secretariat has been under the administrative control of the
Ministry of Home Affairs. Since September, 2001, the administrative control has been transferred
to DoNER.

18. In regard to overlap of jurisdiction between NEC and DoNER, the Department, in its written
reply, has stated that there is clarity in the respective roles of NEC and DoNER. NEC generally
sanctions infrastructure projects and other schemes, which are either inter-State or have a region-
wide impact. On the other hand, DoNER undertakes projects in socio-economic infrastructure
sector which are State specific. NEC also has a mandate to undertake projects in areas other than
infrastructure, for example, manpower development, training and other areas like eco-preservation
and weaning the local public away from Jhooming, etc. NEC also directly runs several institutions
especially in the health sector. There is a close coordination between DoNER and NEC and no
overlap is allowed. DoNER as a Department also focuses on Advocacy, Plan and Budget matters,
Parliament-related affairs and Public Undertakings such as NEDFI, NERAMAC, NEHHDC and SMC
and above all, coordinates and monitors the work of other Departments of the Government of India
in the North Eastern region.
19. In relation to the role of NEC in internal security matters the Department has stated that
the issue was discussed in the 47th Council Meeting held on 14th November, 2002 at Guwahati.
On the recommendation of the Council Members, the Chairman, NEC has taken up security related
issues with the Ministry of Home Affairs.
NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES FOR DEVELOPMENT OF NORTH
EASTERN REGION (NLCPR)
20. The Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) has been constituted out of the
unspent amount from 10% of the budget of various Union Ministries and Departments (except
those specifically exempted) exclusively earmarked for the socio-economic development of the
North East. Budget for the purpose is defined as Gross Budgetary Support to the Plan, which is
inclusive of Externally Aided Projects.
20.1 Until recently, the Planning Commission was handling the Non-lapsable Central Pool of
Resources (NLCPR). The work has since been transferred to DoNER. The broad objective of this
pool is to ensure speedy development of the socio-economic infrastructure in North Eastern region
by increasing the flow of budgetary financing for new infrastructure/projects/schemes in the
region. Both physical and social infrastructure sectors such as irrigation and flood control, power,
roads and bridges, education, health, water supply and sanitation—are considered for providing
support under the Central Pool with projects in physical infrastructure sector receiving priority.
Only project specific assistance is released from the NLCPR and no diversion is permissible.

8
20.2 Funds from the NLCPR can be released for State sector as well as Central sector projects/
schemes. However, the available funds are not meant to supplement the normal plan programmes
either of the State Governments or of the Union Ministries/Departments/Agencies.
Institutional Arrangement to Administer the NLCPR Funds
20.3 The Department of Development of North Eastern Region is the nodal Department for the
Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources. A set of guidelines has been issued on 22.11.2002 for
administering the NLCPR. A Committee has been constituted to administer the NLCPR. The
composition of the Committee is as follows:
(i) Secretary, Department of Development of North Eastern Region—Chairman
(ii) Finance Secretary or his representative not below the rank of Joint Secretary—Member
(iii) Home Secretary or his representative not below the rank of Joint Secretary—Member
(iv) Secretary of the concerned Ministry/Department proposing a Central sector project—
Member
(v) Principal Advisor/Advisor in-charge of North Eastern States and Sikkim in the
Planning Commission—Member
(vi) Finance Advisor, DoNER—Member
(vii) Joint Secretary in-charge of Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources in DoNER—
Member Convenor
20.4 The Committee decides the policy matters, scrutinises and approves the project proposals
for assistance under NLCPR.
20.5 When the Committee desired that the presence of Central Government in NER must be felt
the Department replied that the guidelines of Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NCLPR) have
recently been modified (22nd December, 2002) specifically stipulating that a sign board at each
NLCPR project site shall be placed giving the details like the name of the project, the approved
cost, duration, facilities to be created, name of the contractor, etc. Similar instructions have gone
for all NEC projects. Each Central Government Ministry/Department will also be requested to
ensure similar arrangements for their respective Central Sector/Centrally Sponsored Schemes as
well. North Eastern States have already been requested to widely disseminate the projects and
ensure sign boards on each NLCPR project so that the people in the region can appreciate the role
of Government of India in the development of the region.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES (NLCPR)
The Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) became operational in April,
1998. The pool has come into existence out of the shortfalls in the stipulated expenditure
norm of 10% by each Central Ministry/Department for the North-Eastern region. The total
amount accrued to this pool during 1998-2002 was Rs.5806.07 crore. Thus, on an average a
little over Rs.1400 crore accrued to NLCPR. Consequent upon the exercise undertaken by
DoNER to ascertain the actual size of the pool, Rs.2853.97 crore were calculated to be
available in it as on 1 April, 2002, after taking into account the debits. However, only
Rs.1318.66 crore have been released to the States during 1998-2002. The release of
Rs.1318.66 crore during 1998-2002 is less than 50% of the cost of the sanctioned projects
worth Rs.2728 crore. (Para 25)
As on date, a total of 548 projects have been sanctioned out of NLCPR with an
approved cost of Rs.3097.24 crore. In sharp contrast to that only Rs.1868.66 crore have
actually been released leaving a committed liability of Rs.1228.58 crore. Against this
requirement, a provision of only Rs.550 crore has been made in BE 2003-04. (Para 25.1)
The above figures clearly indicate that the amount being released out of this central
pool of resources is far less than the accruals to it. There are two aspects of the mechanism
evolved to ensure adequate flow of funds to the process of socio-economic development in
the North-Eastern region. One, Central Ministries/Departments (except for a few exempted)
are required to earmark 10% of their gross budgetary support exclusively for North-Eastern
region. However, statistics prove that Central Ministries/Departments have only been, able
to spend in the range of 6 to 7% out of the earmarked 10% of their gross budgetary
support. (Para 25.2)
The second aspect flows from the first one. The inability of the Central Ministries/
Departments to spend the whole of the 10%, paved the way for creation of NLCPR which
is, as stated elsewhere, constituted of the shortfalls in the stipulated expenditure norm of
10%. As stated in the preceding paragraphs, the release of funds out of this pool is less
than 50% of its actual size. (Para 25.2.1)
After having had a look at the two aspects of the mechanism evolved to ensure flow
of funds, it becomes amply clear that neither 10% norm nor NLCPR has been able to
maintain a steady flow of funds. Although, on an average Rs.1400 crore accrue to this pool
every year, the annual release out of it has hovered around Rs.300 crore or so. This proves
that there is a clear mismatch between the accruals and the release of funds. As against
a projected requirement of Rs.1050 crore for 2003-04, the Planning Commission has
approved only Rs.550 crore. The amount sanctioned by Planning Commission is on the lower
side keeping in view the fact that Sikkim has also become part of the North-Eastern region.
In this way, the purpose behind setting up of NLCPR is getting defeated as adequate funds
are not flowing out of it. (Para 25.3)
25.4 In view of the above, the Committee recommends that the Government should not
only release the backlog amount of Rs.1228.58 crore out of NLCPR to enable the DoNER
13

14
to maintain the momentum of developmental activities in the North-Eastern region but it
should also ensure pro rata release of funds out of total accruals to NLCPR henceforth.
NORTH EASTERN COUNCIL (NEC)
During Ninth Five Y ear Plan, an outlay of Rs. 2114 crore was made for NEC. Out of
that Rs.1970.90 crore were allocated to it and Rs.1937.71 crore were actually spent by
it. The annual allocation during the plan period progressively grew from Rs.324 crore in
1997-98 to Rs. 450 crore in the terminal year i.e. 2001-02. Similarly, during the Tenth Plan,
the Planning Commission has made an outlay of Rs.3500 crore for NEC. Out of this
plan outlay, Rs.450 crore were allocated for 2002-03 which has now been enhanced to
Rs.500 crore.
Thus, the pattern of allocations to NEC followed during the Ninth Plan is being
adhered to in the Tenth Plan also. In other words, the flow of funds to NEC continues to
be progressive in nature starting with lower allocation in the first year and then gradually
going up with the progression of the Plan. In contrast to NEC’s projection of Rs. 800 crore
for 2003-04, only Rs. 500 crore have been provided to it and that too when Sikkim has
recently joined as the eighth member of the Council.
Here, the Committee would like to emphasise the point that annual allocations out
of plan outlay should be on the higher side in the initial years of the plan period so that
the projected activities get underway with a head start. The Committee is of the view that
the maximum momentum should be provided in the beginning of the Plan so that its steady
progression is maintained in later years also. Besides, as a Plan progresses, the value of the
allocations in real terms declines with the passage of time. Therefore, by providing a
sizeable chunk of the outlay in the first two years of the Plan, an optimum use of the funds
can be made. In this background, the Committee is of the view that the Government should
enhance NEC’s allocation as per actual requirements projected by it.
In addition, the Government may also consider to place the unutilised funds of the
NLCPR at the disposal of the North-Eastern Council. (Para 26, 26.1, 26.2 and 26.3)
The North-Eastern Council has recently been remodelled as a regional planning body
with its emphasis on projects and schemes involving two or more States. Over the years,
NEC has, primarily, been playing the role of a central funding agency. As a result, it has
not been able to focus its attention on a balanced regional growth. In the absence of a
comprehensive planning for the region aimed at all-round development, the North-Eastern
region continues to lag behind. The Committee, therefore, is of the considered view that the
North-Eastern Council should play a proactive role in the development of the North-Eastern
region while focusing its attention on regional planning. (Para 27)

28. Sikkim Mining Corporation has been incurring losses ever since it came into

IMPROVEMENT IN ROAD INFRASTRUCTURE
Roads are an important engine of growth. For a region as remote as the North-East,
the development of a good network of roads is a sine qua non for an all-round socio-
economic development. There is an urgent need to take necessary steps to bring about
qualitative and visible improvement in the condition of roads. For, poor condition of roads
will prove to be a big hurdle in attracting private investment in various sectors. Therefore,
keeping in view the crucial importance of a good road network, the Committee recommends
that a comprehensive action plan should be formulated by the Government to improve,
expand and maintain a durable and reliable road network in the North-Eastern region.
(Para 29)
TECHNOLOGY MISSIONS
30. North-Eastern region is full of natural resources such as medicinal plants, bamboo,
horticulture, floriculture, agricultural products such as ginger and potato, etc. These natural
products have a potential to catapult the region to the position of being a major base for
agri-business and agri-exports. Another bountiful natural resource is water which can be
harnessed in a big way to generate hydel-electricity. The hydel-electricity potential of the
region is 32000 MW which, if realised optimally, can transform the region as the
powerhouse of the country. Similarly, oil and natural gas is also a major potential area
which could prove to be a big help in import substitution.
30.1. Undoubtedly, the North-Eastern region is a resource-rich area. But the abundant
resources of the region have remained untapped. Consequently, it has lagged behind in the
national process of development. Today, the region is beset with multiple problems such as
unemployment, economic backwardness, low industrial development, poor social and
economic infrastructure etc. In order to overcome these problems, concerted, well-
coordinated, focused and persistent efforts are needed.
30.2 The Committee, therefore, is of the considered opinion that the Government should
launch ‘Technology Missions’ with a view to harness the immense potential of various areas
within a fixed time-frame. (Para 30, 30.1 and 30.2)
PROMOTION OF TOURISM
31. North-Eastern States are not rich just in natural resources but they are also endowed
with pristine natural beauty. This virgin beauty has, however, remained beyond the reach
of people from other parts of the country as well as foreigners due to a variety of reasons
such as poor tourist infrastructure, lack of adequate rail and air services, less exposure to
the outside world, spread of insurgency-related violence in various parts, etc. All these and
many other factors have contributed to the non-realisation of the potential of the tourism
sector.
31.1 As the times are changing and the region is now getting more and more exposed
coupled with a strong urge among the people to attain economic prosperity and self-
sufficiency, the time is opportune to showcase the region as a major tourist destination.
Therefore, the Committee is of the view that a comprehensive plan should be prepared and
implemented to give the much-needed boost to the tourism industry in the North-Eastern
region. The Committee hopes that such a step will prove to be a major turning point in the
economic development of the region. (Para 31 and 31.1)
