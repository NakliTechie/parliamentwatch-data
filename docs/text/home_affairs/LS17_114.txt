REPORT
 
1.0       Partition of the country in 1947 resulted in mass exodus from the dominion of India to Pakistan and vice-versa. 
The following five Acts were enacted to rehabilitate displaced persons from erstwhile West Pakistan to India:
(i)
                  The Displaced Persons Claims Act 1950; 
(ii)                The Administration of Evacuee Property Act, 1950;
(iii)               The Evacuee Interest (Separation) Act, 1951;
(iv)              The Displaced Persons (Claims) Supplementary Act, 1954; and
(v)                The Displaced Persons (Compensation and Rehabilitation) Act, 1954. 
 
The purpose of the above mentioned Acts was inter-alia to make provisions for the registration and verification
of claims of displaced persons in respect of immovable poverty in Pakistan, the administration of evacuee property,
providing for the separation of the interests of evacuees from those of other persons in property in which such other
persons are also interested, the payment of compensation and rehabilitation grant to displaced persons and the disposal
of certain proceedings pending under the Displaced Persons (Claims) Act, 1950. The Displaced Persons Claims and
Other Laws Repeal Bill, 2004 seeks to repeal those five Acts. 
 
2.0       The term “Displaced Persons” is defined in Section 2(b) of The Displaced Persons (Compensation and
Rehabilitation) Act 1954, as any person who, on account of the setting up of the dominions of India and Pakistan, or on
account of civil disturbances or the fear of such disturbances in any area forming part of West Pakistan, had, after
March 1947, left or been displaced from his place of residence and had been subsequently residing in India.  In short,
the people who came to India from West Pakistan  are known as displaced persons.  Section 2(d) of the Administration
of Evacuee Property Act, 1950 defines “Evacuee” as any person who, on account of the setting up of the dominions of
India and Pakistan, or on account of civil disturbances or the fear of such disturbances, leaves or had, on or after 1
st
March 1947, left for any place outside India.  A ‘Compensation Pool’ was constituted for payment of compensation to
Displaced Persons which, inter alia, included Evacuee Property, i.e., properties left behind by Evacuees.  Central
Government was empowered to acquire Evacuee Property for the ‘Compensation Pool’ under Sections 12-14 of the
Displaced Persons (Compensation and Rehabilitation) Act, 1954.
 
3.         As mentioned in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill, the major works of claims regarding
compensation and rehabilitation had been largely completed by the end of 1970.  Subsequently, the erstwhile Ministry
of Labour and Rehabilitation (Department of Rehabilitation) which was responsible for the aforesaid rehabilitation work
also concluded that only a limited number of acquired evacuee urban and agricultural lands or properties had remained
to be disposed of and the expenditure which was being incurred for the purpose was out of proportion to the volume of
work and the receipts from their disposal.  To effect economy in expenditure in the management of the evacuee
properties under the Compensation Pool, the Central Government made administrative and financial arrangements with
the concerned State Governments for the disposal of the residuary assets in the manner consistent with the purposes of
the aforesaid Acts and transferred the surplus evacuee properties to the State Governments in various package deals.

This arrangement virtually put an end to the ownership of the Central Government on the undisposed evacuee properties
which has been upheld by the Supreme Court in Pala Singh Vs Union of India (AIR 1988 SC 873).
 
3.1       Subsequent to the transfer of the ownership of the Central Government on the undisposed evacuee properties to
the State Governments concerned, it was reported by the State Governments that a large number of claims under the
aforesaid Acts are being continued to be filed in the various courts.  It has further been brought to the notice of the
Central Government that a number of bogus claimants  posing as legal heirs of displaced persons are presenting
repeated demands for lands in collusion with unscrupulous  elements.   Therefore, the State Governments of Punjab &
Haryana had categorically suggested for repeal of those Acts. Examinations have revealed that in most of such cases the
claimants  under the temptation to grab more land, have managed to obtain bogus and excess allotments.  It, therefore,
has become difficult for the State Governments to retrieve the Government lands and properties worth crores of rupees
from the hands of unscrupulous persons.
 
3.2       The Acts proposed to be repealed by the Bill under consideration, are concerned with 16 State Governments
including Government of Punjab & Haryana. Feedback from those States was received by the Union Government. An
analysis of those feedbacks revealed that eight States (Punjab, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Maharastra,
Rajasthan, West Bengal, Himachal Pradesh and Delhi) concurred with the proposal of repeal. Another eight States
(Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Orissa, Bihar, Jharkhand and Chattisgarh) have no objection to the
proposal of repeal. After consulting the State Governments, the Central Government constituted a Core Group on the 7
February, 2003 to examine the proposal to repeal the aforesaid Acts.  The said Core Group in March, 2003
recommended repeal of the aforesaid Acts and rules made thereunder.  The Central Government decided to accept the
recommendations of the concerned State Governments and the Core Group to repeal all the five Acts.
 
4.         While examining the Bill, considering its public importance and its effect on general public, the Committee
decided to issue a Press Release inviting written memoranda containing suggestions/views/comments of
individuals/institutions/organisations interested in the subject matter of the Bill. The press release on the Bill appeared
in National and regional newspapers between 17 and 25 February 2005.  In response thereto the Secretariat received 59
memoranda, which were forwarded to the Ministry of Home Affairs for its comments. The Ministry in their comments
informed that 34 Memoranda pertained to Enemy Property and that the five Acts under consideration for repeal did not
pertain to Enemy Property, which is distinct and separate from Evacuee Property. 17 Memoranda required action by
State Governments and 5 contained suggestions on the Bill such as unsatisfied verified claims, court cases, management
of Evacuee Property, fate of Compensation Pool, etc.
 
4.1       The Committee in the meeting held on 30 June 2005 expressed its concern over the fate of the existing claims of
the displaced persons and court cases to which the Ministry replied that  Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897,
inter alia, provides protection to any right, privilege, obligation or liability acquired or accrued under any enactment
repealed.  It, inter alia, also provides that the repeal shall not affect any investigation, legal proceeding or remedy in
respect of any such right, privilege, obligation, liability, penalty, forfeiture or punishment as aforesaid; and any such
investigation, legal proceeding or remedy may be instituted, continued or enforced, and any such penalty, forfeiture or
punishment may be imposed as if the repealing Act or Regulation had not been passed.
 
4.2       In this light, The Committee felt that the possibility of existence of unsatisfied verified claims was very low and
rare and that the Court cases would continue to be taken care of under the provisions of the concerned Acts till final
settlement.             
 
4.3       During the deliberations on the provisions of the Bill, an issue was  raised that though migration from erstwhile
West Pakistan was on a large scale but migration also took place from the erstwhile East Pakistan (now Bangladesh). 
Since all the five Acts proposed to be repealed deal with migrants from West Pakistan the displaced Persons from East
Pakistan were discriminated in the matter of rehabilitation and compensation.
 
4.4       The Ministry, while replying to the issue, stated that the question of displaced persons from East Pakistan now
Bangladesh was considered in the First Five Year Plan document and separate provisions were made in that regard as
the respective conditions of displaced persons from West Pakistan and from East Pakistan were (as of then) perceived
differently, having regard to their respective specificities.   In a  detailed note furnished on relief and rehabilitation

provisions in respect of displaced persons from erstwhile East Pakistan, it was stated that about 52.31 lakh displaced
persons had migrated from erstwhile East Pakistan during the period 1946 to 1971.  Those who arrived between October
1946 and December 1963 were called “Old Migrants” and those who arrived between January 1964 and 25 March 1971
were called “New Migrants”.  Substantial assistance was given to the State-owned organizations, such as West Bengal
State Electricity Board and Calcutta State Transport Corporation for creating jobs for refugee youths under employment
generating measures. The Rehabilitation Industries Corporation Ltd. was established by the Government of India in
Calcutta for the same purpose.  For providing educational facilities more than 1,300 primary schools were opened for
refugee children.  Government of India assisted in the regularization of 931 refugee colonies in different groups in West
Bengal.  The West Bengal Legislature had also passed the West Bengal Act XVI of 1951, i.e., ‘The Rehabilitation of
Displaced Persons and Eviction of Persons in Unauthorized Occupation of Land Act, 1951’, to provide for the
rehabilitation of displaced persons and eviction of persons in unauthorized occupation of land and for certain other
matters connected therewith.
 
5.0       The Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sitting held on 30 June 2005 wherein the
representatives of the Ministry of Home Affairs, Legislative Department and Department of Legal Affairs were present.
