REPORT
CHAPTER-1
Overview
         The North-Eastern Region comprising the States of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram,
Nagaland, Tripura and Sikkim has lagged behind the rest of the country as far as development is concerned. As a result,
it has been the focal point of Special Assistance of the Central Government, particularly during the last few years. In
that genre, a separate Department of Development of North-East Region was created in September, 2001. Later on, a
full-fledged dedicated Ministry designated as Ministry of Development of North-East Region (DoNER) was created
w.e.f. 27th May, 2004.
2.      In the National Common Minimum Programme (CMP) all North-Eastern States are to be given Special Assistance
to upgrade and expand infrastructure. The North­-Eastern Council is to be strengthened and given adequate
professional support. It also talks of a Special Economic Package announced for the North East to be implemented
expeditiously. It accords priority to the development and expansion of physical infrastructure like roads, highways,
power, water supply etc.
3.      To fulfil the above objectives, the Ministry of DoNER has following subjects under its remit:­
(i)     Matters of planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes and projects of North Eastern
Region including those in the sectors of Power, Irrigation, Roads and Communications;
(ii)     Hill Area Development Programme and Border Area Development Programme in North Eastern Region;
(iii)    Non-lapsable Fund for the North Eastern Region;
(iv)    North Eastern Council;
(v)    North Eastern Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi)
(vi)    North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited (NERAMAC);
(vii)   The Sikkim Mining Corporation Limited (SMC);
(viii) North Eastern Handlooms and Handicrafts Development Corporation (NEHHDC), Shillong;
(ix)    Road works financed in whole or in part by the Central Government in the North Eastern Region; and
(x)    Planning of Road and Inland Waterways Transport in the North Eastern Region.
4.      The Ministry is headed by a Cabinet Minister and supported by one Secretary and three Joint Secretaries among
other officers. The Ministry has the following three broad divisions:-
(i)     Policy and Programme Division: It looks after Policy formulation, Cabinet and PMO matters, Coordination
with Central Ministries/Departments and State Governments, International Corporation, North Eastern
Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi), North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation
Limited (NERAMAC), North Eastern Handicrafts and Handloom Development Corporation Limited
(NEHHDC), Inland Waterways, Business Summit, Private Sector Investment and Parliamentary Work. This
Division also looks after Plans and Budget and Administration of the Ministry.
(ii)     NLP Division : It looks after the work of administration of the Non Lapsable Central Pool of Resources
(NLCPR).
(iii)    NEC Division : It looks after North Eastern Council, Schemes of Technical Assistance, Advocacy and
Publicity, Hill Area Development Programme, Border Area Development Programme and Information
Technology. This Division also looks after the work of Sikkim Mining Corporation.
5.      The Ministry has formulated four Central Plan Schemes. They are Advocacy and Publicity, Capacity Building,
Assistance to Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC) and North Eastern Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi). In the
plans of the North -Eastern States, grants from Non Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) and scheme for North
Eastern Council (NEC) are included. The Projects for Infrastructure Development under NLCPR are
identified/prioritized by the State Governments and submitted to the Ministry of DoNER. The NEC provides financial
assistance for regional infrastructure projects like construction of roads, improving air connectivity, assistance to
regional medical colleges etc.

6.0    Prime Ministers' Package - Prime Ministers during their visit to the North Eastern Region from time to time have
announced socio-economic packages for the development of the Region. These packages are implemented by the
concerned Ministries/Departments/Organizations of the Central Government. The Ministry of DoNER implemented
certain schemes/programmes under the following packages:­
(i)     Socio-Economic Package for the North Eastern Region as announced by the then Prime Minister Shri A.B.
Vajpayee in January, 2000 at Shillong.
(ii)     Socio-Economic Package for Sikkim announced by the then Prime Minister Shri A.B. Vajpayee at Gangtok
on 13.4.2003
(iii)    Socio-Economic Package for Nagaland announced by the then Prime Minister Shri A.B. Vajpayee at
Kohima in October, 2003
6.1    The implementation of the Schemes and Programmes in the Agenda for Socio­-Economic Development of the
North Eastern States and Sikkim are being monitored and reviewed by the Prime Minister’s Office and also by the
Ministry for Development of NE Region with the concerned implementing Ministries/Departments/Agencies.
7.0    PROGRAMMES OF THE MINISTRY
7.1    Hill Area Development Programme (HADP)- The main objective of the programme is sustainable development
in the hilly areas by eco-preservation and eco­ restoration with emphasis on preservation of bio-diversity and
rejuvenation of the hill ecology. At present, HADP is being implemented in two Hill Districts of Assam.
7.2    Border Areas Development Programme (BADP)- It is 100 per cent Centrally funded special area programme,
which covers border blocks of the North Eastern States having international borders. This programme is carried out in
the seventeen States of the country including States of the NER. The broad objective of the programme is to meet
special needs of the people living in the remote and inaccessible areas situated near international border.
8.0    SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY
8.1    Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) - The Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR)
became operational in April, 1998 due to the fact that there were short falls in the stipulated expenditure norm of 10 per
cent of the Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) by some Union Ministries/Departments for supporting infrastructure
development projects of the North East. Enhancement of the budgetary provision for new infrastructural
projects/schemes and its speedy implementation are the broad objectives of the NLCPR schemes. Both economic and
social sectors projects like Power, Roads and Bridges, Education, Health, Water Supply are considered for funding
under the Central Pool.
8.2    Advocacy and Publicity ----- The Plan Scheme of Advocacy and Publicity has been formulated to highlight the
inherent potentials and achievements, in social, economic and cultural sectors, of the people of the North Eastern
Region and to expose their best practices to the rest of the country. It also seeks to highlight the role of Government in
catalyzing the development process.
8.3    Capacity Building ----- Capacity Building and Technical Assistance to the North Eastern States is a key mandate
of the Ministry. Under this scheme, the Ministry has formulated a plan focusing on development, in consonance with the
people’s wishes and aspirations. The Capacity Building plan scheme was formulated with a view to filling the gap in the
Technical and Management skills required for the development of the Region. The emphasis is on the participation of
people in the developmental process.
9.0.   ATTACHED BODIES AND PUBLIC SECTOR UNITS OF THE MINISTRY
9.l .0 North Eastern Council (NEC) - The North Eastern Council (NEC) is a statutory body, created under an Act of
Parliament. The Council functions as a Regional Planning Body for securing the balanced development of the North
Eastern Region and for Inter­ State coordination. NEC is mandated to plan, implement and manage regional projects. It
is also entrusted with the task of reviewing the measures taken by the North Eastern States for the maintenance of
security and public order in the region. Under NEC, special emphasis is on the projects/schemes, which will benefit two
or more States. In addition, the NEC will also review the implementation of projects/schemes and recommend measures
for effective coordination in the matter of implementation.
9.1.1 On the Revitalization of NEC, the Committee under the Chairmanship of Shri P.P Srivastava had submitted its
report on 30th July 2004, which inter alia recommended a series of measures for operationalising the new mandate of
the North Eastern Council. These include composition of Council, organizational set up, regional planning for

9.2    North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi) ----- North Eastern Development Finance
Corporation is a financial institution supporting entrepreneurs in the region by sanctioning projects and disbursement of
loans. During the Tenth Plan Rs. 100 crore is being given to NEDFi in the form of a soft loan to increase its liquidity,
which is repayable as bullet repayment at the end of the Tenth Plan.
9.3    North Eastern Handicrafts and Handloom Development Corporation Ltd. (NEHHDC) ----- The main
objective of the NEHHDC is to develop and promote handicrafts, handlooms and allied projects of the North Eastern
Region and the welfare of the indigenous artisans and weavers. NEHHDC was incorporated on 31 March 1977 as a
Central Public Sector Undertaking and is owned by the Government of India. Currently, the corporation is running in
losses and the net loss is 1.90 crore in the current financial year, whereas the turnover the Corporation during April
2004- January 2005 was Rs 6.50 crore.
9.4    North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Ltd. (NERAMAC)  ----- NERAMAC is
expected to build up marketing infrastructure, processing facilities and providing assistance in marketing arrangement
for agro/horticulture products of the region. The corporation has also been associated in programmes of helping the
farmers of the region in making them aware about the competitive prices for their produce and organizing training for
technical know-how on commercial aspects.
9.5    Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC) ----- Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC) was constituted for the purpose of
developing the deposits of copper, lead, zinc and other minerals in Bhotang and other places in Sikkim. This joint
venture between the Government of Sikkim (51%) and Government of India (49%) has been operating at a loss.

12.0  The Committee observes that the policy decision of the Government to stop budgeting of loan component of
Central assistance to States by encouraging them to arrange loan from market is a retrograde step for the special

budgetary provisions may be kept at least at the level of BE 2004-05.

CHAPTER - III
Policies, Programmes and Schemes of the Ministry
Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR)
13.0  The Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) came into existence in      April, 1998 for supporting
infrastructure development projects in the North East. The NLCPR are accrued from the unspent balance of the
mandatory provision of 10 per cent            of Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) made for the North Eastern Region in the
Budget of Central Ministries/Departments.
13.1  The main objective of the NLCPR is to ensure speedy development of infrastructure in NER by increasing the
How of budgetary provision for new infrastructure projects/schemes, both in economic and social sectors such as
Power, Roads and Bridges, Education, Health, Water Supply and Sanitation. Funds from Central Pool are available for
State sector as well as Central sector projects/schemes.
13.2  The Planning and Development Department of the North Eastern States is the nodal Department for NLCPR and is
DoNER’s interface with all other Departments of these States. The NE States through these Departments submit
prioritized list of projects to the interdepartmental NLCPR committee headed by Secretary, Ministry of DoNER. That
Committee selects projects out of the prioritized list for retention. After the approval of the Ministry of DoNER,
Detailed Project Reports are obtained from the respective State Governments for getting examined by Ministry for

13.4  The Committee was informed that Rs. 1020 crore was projected to the Planning Commission as the requirement
for NLCPR projects during 2005-06 but Planning Commission approved the budgetary allocation of Rs. 650 crore
including Rs. 100 crore meant exclusively for Bodo-land Territorial Areas District Council (BTC). Owing to the policy

13.5  The Committee expressed its concern about implications of the Government’s decision for not providing
budgetary support to 10 per cent loan component to States. The Committee has doubt about the capacity of these special

13.6  The Committee expressed its concern with regard to the decision of the Government for not providing ten per cent
budgetary support of loan component and recommends that the Ministry should pursue with the Ministry of Finance for
enhancement of the budgetary allocation by stating the difficulties that might be faced by the NE States in raising loan
from the market.

Border Area Development Programme (BADP)
15.0  The Border Area Development Programme (BADP) for the NER, is a hundred per cent centrally funded
programme, which has been transferred to the Ministry of DoNER from the Ministry of Home Affairs. It covers Border
Blocks of the NE States having international Borders with Bangladesh, Myanmar, China and Bhutan. Funds are
provided on hundred per cent grants basis. The main objective of the programme is to provide special attention to the
needs of the people living in the inaccessible areas situated near the international border.
15.1  It was pointed out by the Committee that many a time the development under the BADP takes place in the areas,
which are very far away from the border points.
15.2  The Committee is happy to note that the BADP has been placed under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of DoNER
and observes that the development projects were confined to nodal points of the border Blocks, and areas which are
situated very near to the international border have not been included in this programme.
15.3  The Committee further opines that for better development in the border areas, which lie along the international
border of the NE States, programmes related to roads, water, medical facility etc., should be given top priority. The
Committee also feels that the budgetary allocation of Rs. 325 crore for the programme in the NE States should be
increased to atleast Rs. 500 crores.
Promotion of Tourism
16.0  The NE region is one of the most beautiful part of the country blessed with natural beauty and diverse culture but
its tourism potential has not been adequately explored neither for the domestic nor for the foreign circuit.
16.1  The Committee, in its One hundred and Tenth Report has recommended that the Ministry should tie-up with the
Ministry of Tourism to formulate perspective plan for development of tourism infrastructure.
16.2  The Committee was informed that the Ministry of Tourism has chalked out a plan for the NER by allocating Rs
52.05 crore for the year 2005-06 which is ten per cent of their GBS. The programme mainly includes publicity and
publication relating to the tremendous potential of the region, focussed marketing, generating more avenues for tourist
besides a special emphasis on Eco-tourism and Ethnic tourism.
16.3  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of DoNER in coordination with the Ministry of Tourism should
make constant efforts to explore various avenues for development of better tourism related infrastructure to attract
tourists, which would provide boost to the sagging economic condition of this region.
16.4  The Committee made a special mention of the Manas Wildlife Sanctuary and feels that this sanctuary alongwith
other numerous places of attraction in the region should be developed and the concepts such as Eco Tourism, Ethnic
Tourism and Buddhist Monasteries have the potentials to bring this region on the tourist map of the world.

17.0  The Committee is of the view that the infrastructure development programmes relating to Tourism would be futile
in absence of providing better connectivity to the region, whether it is within the NE States or with the rest of the
country. Lack of proper road and rail transportation system coupled with its uneven terrain makes air travel as the best
option for communication. Well-equipped airports will provide further encouragement for the transportation in this
region.
17.1. The Committee takes note of the fact that out of the 26 airports of the NER, only 11 are in the working condition.
The Committee has been apprised that a business like approach has been initiated to improve the airports in NER.
Several private investors have been consulted in this regard. The Committee was also informed that NEC, the regional

17.2  The Committee while taking note of the efforts of the Ministry regarding the development of Civil Aviation

Highway is in deplorable condition and needs immediate and utmost repair works. A Member of the Committee pointed
out that inter state roads between Meghalaya and Assam is in worst condition and no maintenance is being done either
by the NEC or by the State PWD.

alongwith respective State Governments. Rs. l4 crore had been sanctioned for maintenance of 6000 km. stretch of road,
which had been constructed during last two years by NEC. The Committee was further apprised that for the inter-state
road between Meghalaya and Assam, Rs. 40 lakh had been sanctioned for maintenance.

of the North East Road Development Project, an externally aided project sanctioned by Asian Development Bank
(ADB) and the progress made in this regard. The Committee opines that more such progremmes are needed in NER and
also recommends that the issue of maintenance and construction of new roads should be taken up with the State PWD
for better implementation of the projects specially the North East Express Highway. It also feels that this project should
be taken up on priority basis and more funds should be allocated for early completion of the entire road network of the
NER.
Development of Autonomous District Council
19.0  The Sixth Schedule of the constitution provides for provision to the administration of tribal Areas in the NE States.
These provisions shall apply to the administration regarding the Scheduled Area and Scheduled Tribes of NE States.
They are identified as Autonomous District Council Area and are entrusted to advise on         matters pertaining to the
welfare and advancement of the Schedule Tribes in the State.
19.1  The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee stated that the Ministry of DoNER had sanctioned 72
projects for Autonomous District Council area in the NE States. He further submitted that most of these projects are
sanctioned for Bodoland Autonomous Council Area whereas 10 projects are sanctioned for Karbi Anglong Autonomous
District Council and 9 projects for North Cachar Hills Autonomous District Council.
19.2  The Committee understands that development of the Autonomous District Council areas is long overdue. These
areas are identified & included in the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution so as to devote special attention to them as they
are lagging behind the rest of the NER both in social & economic development.
19.3  Members of the Committee belonging to the NE States have very strongly raised the issue that the Autonomous
District Council areas are not getting any kind of benefits out of the Ministry of DoNER as well as North Eastern
Council. The Council hardly sanctions any developmental projects for autonomous District Council. Even in NLCPR
projects only Bodo-land Territorial Council Area (BTC) caught the attention of the Ministry and get the budgetary
provision of Rs. 100 crore, which is quite insufficient as the BTC comprises of four districts. And the amount allocated
for each district comes to Rs. 25 crore only, which is too meagre for the developmental work required in these most
backward regions of the NER.
19.4  The Committee recommends that special allocation needs to be made for the overall development of this region.
Planning for these areas should be done keeping in mind the classification of the areas as the Most Backward, Backward
and Developing areas. The geographical factors and the seasonal variation should also be taken into account while

Implementation Mechanism
20.0  During the discussion on Demands for Grants, the issue of insurgency was highlighted by one of the Members of
the Committee. The Secretary, Ministry of DoNER             stated that no project has been reportedly hampered till now
due to the insurgency factor. The Committee was informed that the Ministry is further strengthening the monitoring and
evaluation mechanism. The Ministry has further stated that NEC is facilitating consultation on an ongoing basis on
security related aspect in the implementation of the project. The NEC is planning to develop Link Office System, which
would work as a linkage between security concern and development.
20.1  The Committee is of the view that the implementation of projects is tardy inspite of best efforts made by the
Ministry. The Committee in this regard recommends that the Ministry should constantly endeavour to improve upon the
monitoring and evaluation mechanism. The Committee even suggests that a nodal office of the Ministry of DoNER
might be established in the NER for better monitoring of projects: A strict and regular on-the-spot assessment of the
actual implementation should be done by the concerned agencies in the State to ensure proper execution of all the
projects.
Miscellanous
21.0  The Committee takes serious view on the lowest per capita income of the people of NE States and skeptical about
the benefits for the various developmental programmes reaching to general public. The Committee thinks that the
progress made in NE States should reflect in the increase of income of the people of North Eastern Region.
21.1  In this regard, the Committee was informed that the special category States of North East should not be equated
with the other States of the country. Special concession have to be given in regard to the North East States due to the
fact that this region has very tardy growth in its industrial and service sectors. Lack of infrastructure, peculiar
socio-­political situation prevailing there besides the ability and capacity of the States in implementing the projects is
the main reasons behind this situation.
21.2  The Committee after taking note of the special circumstances prevalent in the NER, recommends that the Ministry
should ensure equitable distribution of funds amongst NE States keeping in mind the existing inter-State and intra-State
regional disparities, Human Development Indices (HDI) including per capita income, poverty level etc. The Committee
further recommends that the entrepreneurs from within the country and outside should be encouraged to invest in the
North Eastern Region to speed up the process of its economic development.
21.3  The Committee further takes note of the ongoing border trade programme in the States, which is presently taking
place from Mureh in Manipur. The Committee suggests that more such centres could be identified so that Border-trade
between NE States and neighbouring countries could be strengthened further. The Committee feels that this exercise
will not only provide much needed boost to the marketing of agricultural products, which are perishable in nature, but
also to the agricultural/horticultural produces, which are in abundance and it will go a long way in providing an
opportunity to the related industries to expand their horizons.

3.      The Secretary made a powerpoint presentation on Demands for Grants (2005-06) of Ministry of DoNER which
inter alia included a comparison of the BEs for 2002-03 to 2005-06; details of expenditure of various Central
Ministries/Departments under mandatory ten percent allocation from 1999-2000 to 2003-04 in NE States; percentage
         utilization of funds in the Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) and NEC for the last three years. He
then elucidated upon the NLCPR projects and presented the sector-wise sanction of NLCPR projects, and utilization of
NLCPR funds by NE States. He also presented the sector- wise outlay and performance of NEC. The need for
enhancement of allocation under NLCPR and NEC budget was also shown in the presentation. The Secretary then made
a brief introduction regarding the programmes going on under two plan schemes viz Advocacy Publicity Scheme and
Capacity Building & Technical Assistant Scheme. He then apprised the Committee about the initiatives taken by the
Ministry of DoNER and progress made in respect of Externally Aided Projects in the NER. Lastly, he drew the attention

4.      The Chairperson while thanking the Secretary for the presentation raised a query regarding the policy decision of

4.1    Responding. to the query, the Secretary of the Ministry also expressed its concern over these issues and requested
the Committee to consider recommendation for allocation of ten per cent of budgetary support as loan component to the
special category NE States.
4.2    Members made a number of suggestions for various developmental projects going on in NER. Some Members
also raised queries regarding reasons for continuous failure implementing the projects in NER particularly in militancy
affected areas; status of the blueprint for the North East Express Highway Project; lack of maintenance or the entire road
stretch in the NER; basis on which funds were allocated to each State of NE; re-­identification of Border Areas so that
the benefits could reach to the affected people; status of Border Trade in NER; non-existence of Agricultural Promotion
Councils; Autonomous District Council Areas; and the economic condition in NER despite special packages for the
region. Members also raised queries regarding functioning of North Eastern Council and were skeptical on overall
development of NER in the absence of a comprehensive road map. Members were also of the view that seasonal
variation and geographical condition of the NER should be taken into account while formulating the developmental
projects for NE States.
4.3    The Secretary, Ministry of DoNER replied to most of the queries of the Members and welcomed their suggestions
with the assurance that the Ministry will give due weightage to those while planning for the development of NER. He
further sought some time to send the written replies to the remaining queries.
5.      The Chairperson thanked the Secretary for his cooperation and suggested that the Ministry may take into
consideration the suggestions of the Members as it would be useful to them in formulating future plans for development
of NER. The Chairperson also thanked the Secretary, NEC and other representatives of Ministries/Departments for
helping the Committee to resolve its queries.
