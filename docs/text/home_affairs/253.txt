OVERVIEW 
 
1.1 Introduction 
 
1.1.1 The North Eastern Region (NER) comprises eight States namely Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, 
Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura, with a total area of almost 8 per cent of 
India. It is characterized by its rich bio -diversity and heavy precipitation a nd has immense potential for 
development. The region shares International Borders with several neighbouring countries - Nepal, 
Bhutan, China, Myanmar and Bangladesh. As per the India State of Forest Report, 2021, the forest 
resources in the North Eastern States account for 23.75% of the total forest cover of the country. As per 
Census 2011, the population of the region is about 4.5 crores, constituting 3.78% of the total population 
of India. The rural population makes up 81.64% of the total population of the  region, while the urban 
population accounts for 18.36%.  
 
1.1.2 The Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) was constituted, first as a 
Department in 2001 and then as a Ministry in 2004, for better coordination between the State 
Governments of North Eastern Region and the Central Ministries/Departments of Government of India. 
It is the only Ministry with a territorial jurisdiction and functions to coordinate the developmental efforts 
in the North Eastern Region (NER). 
 
1.1.3 The Ministry of DoNER aims to transform North Eastern Region through development, in 
sustainable yet accelerated manner, affording comprehensive growth and access to ease of living to all 
its citizens. The following are the main objectives of the Ministry: 
(i) Synergize policies, plans and execution for rapid development of NE  Region through 
Collaborating with Central Ministries, States, Agencies & Expert Stakeholders; 
(ii)  Ensuring full utilization of 10% Gross Budget Support (GBS) and other budgetary resources for 
NER; 
(iii) Promoting investment in the NER, including public, private, corporate & multilateral funding; 
(iv) Developing infrastructure to mitigate connectivity and social sector deficits of the region; and  
(v) Strengthening institutions and networks for livelihood, social services, skills and capacity. 
 
1.1.4 The Ministry has also been implementing various Schemes for development of North East Region 
viz. North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (Roads) -NESIDS (Roads), North East 
Special Infrastructure Development Scheme Other Than Road -NESIDS (OTRI), Schemes of North 
Eastern Council (NEC), Prime Minister’s Development Initiative for North East Region (PM -DevINE) 
scheme and Special Development packages (SDP). Ministry also coordinates with other stakeholders, 
who can be from non -government sector or multilateral sector, to work for the development of NER. 
During 2024-25, MDoNER has taken some major initiatives for faster implementation of projects for the 
development of NER.

1.4
 Coordination with Central Ministries and Departments 
1.4.1
 Coordinating role of the Ministry of DoNER emanates  from monitoring development projects 
for socio -economic development of the NER. The Ministry coordinates with various Central 
Ministries/Departments regarding the development and welfare schemes/programmes they implement 
in the North Eastern Region. Besides, calling the progress of expenditure out of the earmarked outlay 
for NER, a set of Ministries/Departments has been assigned to senior officers of the Ministry for regular 
and continuous interactions with respective Ministries/Departments. Moreover, all non- exempted 
Ministries/Departments have appointed nodal officers in their respective Ministries to coordinate over 
10% GBS with the Ministry of DoNER and to apprise them about the major initiatives undertaken in 
NER.  
1.4.2
 The Ministry formulates thematic reg ional plans for coordinated development and welfare of 
people in NER. It works towards resource mobilization for NER through convergence, expert 
partnerships and Public Private Partnership (PPP).  

4 
1.4.3 In addition to the routine interactions, the Ministry also convenes sectoral review meetings with 
key Ministries such as Railways, Road Transport & Highways, Civil Aviation, Power, Environment, 
Forest & Climate Change, Tourism etc. for effective implementation of schemes and time -bound 
completion of major infrastr ucture projects in NER. Ministry of DoNER is represented in Inter -
Ministerial Groups of major Ministries such as the Ministries of Road Transport and Highways 
(MoRTH), Railways, Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (MSME), Home, etc. and is also consulted 
at policy formation and project implementation stage through EFC/ SFC and Cabinet notes.  
1.4.4
 The Ministry has five Inter-Ministerial Committees (IMC) viz. IMC on Roads, IMC on Health 
& Nutrition, IMC on Medicinal Aromatic Plants, IMC on HADP and IMC on STINER wherein issues 
of importance in these domains are discussed with the Stakeholder Ministries/Departments.  
1.5
 Gross Budgetary Support (GBS): Earmarking of Union Budget by Central Ministries/ 
Departments for North Eastern Region 
1.5.1
 In October 1996, the Central Government announced ‘Some initiatives for North Eastern 
Region (NER)’, one of these measures was the policy decision to earmark at least 10% of Plan Budgets 
of the Central Ministries/Departments for the development of NE States. Accordingly, it was decided 
that Central Ministries/Departments, unless specifically exempted, will set aside 10% of their Plan 
Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the NER to ensure a quantum jump in budgetary resource flows to 
the region and to fill the backlog and gaps in basic minimum service and infrastructure. Subsequently, 
allocation of funds under 10% GBS for the NER introduced in 2014-15 (Statement-23), is being done 
annually on a lump sum basis by the non-exempted Central Ministries/Departments at the BE/RE stage 
under Major Heads 2552 (for revenue expenditure), 4552 (for capital expenditure) and 6552 (for loan 
expenditure) under their respective functional schemes. The details of expenditure of non- exempt 
Ministries/Departments are at Annexure I.

1.6 Monitoring expenditure by Ce ntral Ministries/Departments and Central Resource Pool 
for the North East (NLCPR) 
1.6.1
 The scheme of Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) has been a scheme for gap 
funding for creation of infrastructure in the States of NER. The NLCPR Scheme of the M inistry has 
been re-structured as North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS) to meet the 
gaps in social and physical infrastructure in the region. NESIDS is a 100% Centrally funded Central 
Sector Scheme. Hill Area Development Pilot Schem e for development of backward districts of 
Tamenglong and Noney in the state of Manipur is a sub- scheme of NESIDS, which was introduced 
during 2018-19 and is under implementation. Under NLCPR -Central - a Central Sector Plan scheme, 
projects of Union Ministries/Departments, which exceeds 10% GBS expenditure in a year, pertaining 
to subjects listed in the Union and Concurrent Lists, are being funded. However, no new projects are 
sanctioned under NLCPR - Central and only on -going projects are funded under the Scheme. Union 
Cabinet approved the continuation of Schemes of MDoNER during the remaining period of 15th 
Finance Commission i.e. from 2022-23 to 2025-26.

1.7 Central Pool of Resources for NER 
1.7.1 In 1997-98, when it was found that the actual expenditure of Ministries/Departments for NER 
was less than the mandatory ceiling of 10 per cent, the Government decided to create a "Central 
Resource Pool" into which the unutilized portion of the 10 per cent earmarked GBS would accrue at 
the end of the financial year.  Since 1998 -99, the annual surrendered amounts out of the earmarked 
outlays of all the non- exempted Central Ministries/Departments for NER have been accounted as 
accruals to this Pool and the annual expenditure of the Ministry under the NLCPR scheme is being 
accounted as withdrawal from the Pool. However, this is a notional pool, maintained on a proforma 
basis by the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry of DoNER monitors Ministry -wise expenditure/ 
surrendered amounts for the purpose of ascertaining the accrual to the NLCPR pool in consultation 
with the Ministry of Finance.

1.8
 North Eastern Council (NEC) 
1.8.1 T
he North Eastern Council (NEC) was constituted as a statutory advisory body by an Act of 
Parliament under the NEC Act, 1971 (84 of 1971) marking the beginning of a new chapter of concerted 
and planned endeavor for rapid socio- economic development of the North Easter Region (NER). Over 
the last fifty years, the Council has played a significant role in ushering development into the region at a 
time when most of states were in their formative stages. Road and air connectivity was supported, 
foundations for important regional Institutions were established, and various socio- economic 
infrastructures were created across the length and breadth of the Region, making a difference in the lives 
of the people and thereby earning a lot of goodwill in the local community. Presently, NEC is looking to 
continue its efforts, especially in terms of gap-filling in areas which have escaped the attention of various 
development agencies, and creating convergence at different levels. NEC is also looking at building 
capacities, sharing best practices and introducing cutting edge technologies among the NE states.

1.9 North East Cane and Bamboo Development Council (NECBDC) 
1.9.1 North East Cane and Bamboo Development Council (NECBDC) [erstwhile Cane and Bamboo 
Technology Centre (CBTC)] is an ISO 9001:2008 certified organization and was established in the year 
2000 as part of Cane and Bamboo Technological Up-gradation and Networking Project of United Nations 

7 
Development Programme (UNDP). NECBDC was registered as a Society on 21 st July 2004 with the 
Secretary, NEC as the Chairman and Mission Directors of State Bamboo Mission of the eight North 
Eastern States as Members of the Governing Body of NECBDC. NECBDC has been steadfast in its 
commitment to developing the bamboo and cane sector, particularly for the NER. NECBDC has created 
16 cane and bamboo clusters (02 in each NE state) on different themes. It is also developing cane and 
bamboo clusters and promotion of eco tourism bamboo based housing techniques.

development of Handlooms and Handicrafts Sector in the North Eastern Region. NEHHDC has been 
providing marketing support through procurement of Handicrafts and Handlooms products directly 
from the Artisans, Weavers and Self -Help Groups at clusters and selling the same through its Sales 
Emporia located at Shillong, Guwahati, Kolkata, New Delhi and Gujarat and through Sales Promotion

marketing events all over the country. NEHHDC promotes crafts of all 8 states by providing market 
linkages to the artisans and through its emporiums “Purbashree” & e-commerce platform mobile outlets 
and exhibitions. It also provides skill development in various job roles under various Centra l 
Government Schemes.

incorporated in 1982 as a CPSE. It functions under the administrative control of the Ministry of

1.14  N orth Eastern Region Community Resource Management Society (NERCORMS) 
1.14.1 The North Eastern Region Community Resource Management Society (NERCORMS) is a society

NERCORMP project i. e. (a) NERCORMP -I from 1999 to 2008, jointly funded by Govt. of India and

1.14.2 The NERCORMP-I&II were implemented in 6 districts in 3 states i.e. Karbi  Anglong and Dima

(including Logding) districts of Arunachal Pradesh; Chandel and Churachandpur districts of Manipur. 
During three phases of NERCORMP, the society formed 2,889 Natural Resource Management Groups 
(NaRM-G), a village planning and implementation organisation, 8,403 Self Help Groups (SHGs), 290 
NaRM-G Cluster Associations, 300 SHG Federations covering 1,18,843 households in 2,532 villages. 
Total investment from Government of India for the 3 phases of NERCORMP was ₹ 731.28 Crore 
(Total investment including fund from other agencies, support from financial institutions and 
community contribution was  ₹ 970.94 crore).

3.1.1

.2 Central Pool of Resources for North East and Sikkim/North East Special Infrastructure 
Development Scheme (NESIDS) 
3.2.1. The North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS) is a centrally -funded 
initiative launched in 2017-18 and extended until 2026. It has been restructured into two components: 
NESIDS (Roads), managed by the North Eastern Council, and NESIDS (OTRI), overseen by the 
Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region. NESIDS (OTRI) includes unfinished projects from 
previous schemes such as NLCPR, NESIDS, and HADP. This restructuring aims to streamline 
infrastructure development, ensuring efficient resource utilization and effective project implementation 
in the North Eastern region, thereby promoting holistic development and economic growth. The Non -
Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) scheme, initiated in 1998 and managed by the Ministry 
of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) since 2001, aims to bridge infrastructure gaps in 
the North Eastern Region (NER) of India. The scheme funds projects prioritized by state governments, 
with a 90:10 cost-sharing ratio between the central and state governments. Over the years, NLCPR has 
sanctioned numerous projects across various sectors to enhance the region's development. In 2017, the 
North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS) replaced NLCPR for new projects, 
although ongoing NLCPR projects continue to receive funding.

4.1.2 T
he major Schemes implemented by DA&FW to develop agriculture and farmers’ welfare in 
NER include:  
a) Pr
adhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi
The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM Kisan) is a central sector scheme under which
financial support of ₹ 6,000 per year in three equal instalments is transferred directly into the bank 
accounts of eligible farmers under Direct Benefit Transfer. The scheme became operational in 
December 20 18. Under the scheme, State Government and UT administration identify the farmer 
families which are eligible for financial support as per the scheme guidelines.  
b) Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana
The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) provides insura nce coverage and financial
support to the farmers in the event of failure of any of the notified crops due to natural calamities, pests 
& diseases. The scheme also encourages farmers to adopt innovative and modern agricultural practices 
and stabilizes the income of farmers to ensure their continuance in farming activity. The scheme is 
implemented by a multiagency framework by a few selected insurance companies under the supervision 
of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (MoA&FW), Govt. of India. 
c) Agriculture Infrastructure Fund
The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF) is a central sector scheme to facilitate the financing  
of agricultural infrastructure at the post- harvest stage i.e., farm gates and aggregation points.  The 
scheme was launched in 2020 and has a total outlay of ₹ 1 Lac crore. The scheme provides Interest 
subvention of 3% p.a., limited to ₹ 2  crores per project in one location, though the loan amount can be 
higher. The scheme also has a cap on lending rate, so that benefit of interest subsidy reaches the 
beneficiary and services to farmers remain affordable. 
d) Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North – Eastern Region
The Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North Eastern Region (MOVCDNER) is a 
submission under National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA). It is a central sector scheme 

30 
and aims at the development of certified organic production in a value chain mode to link growers with 
consumers and to support the development of the entire value chain starting from inputs, seeds, and 
certification, to the creation of facilities for collection, aggregation, processing marketing and brand 
building initiative.
