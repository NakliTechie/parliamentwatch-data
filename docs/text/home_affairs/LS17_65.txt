R E P O R T
           
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having been
authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf,  present  this Sixty-fifth Report of the Committee,
relating to the Companies Bill, 1997¨.      
2.         In pursuance of the Rules relating to Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees, the Chairman,
Rajya Sabha  referred** the Companies Bill, 1997,  as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on  14 August 1997 and pending
therein, to the Committee for examination  and report.
3.         The Department of Company Affairs was earlier part of the Ministry of Finance when   the Bill was introduced
in the Rajya Sabha, and as such was referred to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance
for examination and report on 2 September 1997.   Later on, the Department  of Company Affairs was  made  part of 
the Ministry of Law and Justice  (now known as Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs) and, therefore, the Bill
was referred to  this Committee on  3 July 1998.
4.         Meanwhile, the Secretary, Department of Company Affairs during the course of his presentation before the
Committee (1998-99) on 30 June, 1998 relating to the Demands for Grants of his Department, made the following
observations with regard to the Companies Bill, 1997  :-
“………..But in the meantime the Government has not yet made up its mind whether to continue  with this Bill
or not.  In all probability very soon they will take a decision  because they may  like  to make some changes in
the Bill.  But there is the question of procedure whether it can be  referred to a Committee when the Bill is
pending  in Parliament.  As per the Law Ministry’s advice,  it has to be withdrawn first before  it is  referred to
any other Committee.  So, at this  stage, I can only say that the Bill is under   review by the Government   and
very soon a decision  will be taken about the continuance of the Bill…..”
 
5.         The Companies Bill, 1997  having  been  referred  to   the  Committee  and  the       Secretary,
Department of Company Affairs having made the above observations, a reference   was  made to the
Department on 4 August 1998 to let the Committee know the thinking of the Government with regard to the  enactment
of the Bill in its present form, so as to enable the Committee to proceed further in the matter.
6.         The Department of Company Affairs vide
 their communication dated 5 November, 1998 conveyed that the
Cabinet had approved certain amendments in the Companies Act, 1956 and, accordingly, an Ordinance had been
promulgated on 31 October, 1998  amending the Principal Act.  That Department also conveyed the Cabinet’s decision
for taking appropriate  action to get the Companies   Bill, 1997,  pending in Parliament considered  and passed,
incorporating  the provisions in the Ordinance.   Accordingly, the Committee was requested for examination of the
Companies Bill, 1997 and for submission of the report at the earliest.
7.0       The Department of Company Affairs  vide
 their  Office Memorandum dated 8 February, 1999  brought the
following facts to the notice of the Committee:-
(i)                  Certain changes have been made in the Companies Act, 1956 through the Companies (Amendment)
Ordinance, 1998 promulgated  on 31 October 1998.  Some of the provisions brought through this
Ordinance such as buy-back of shares and inter-corporate investments etc. also find a place in the
Companies Bill, 1997.  Subsequently, the Companies (Amendment) Bill, 1998 was introduced in the Lok
Sabha on 22 December   to replace the Ordinance.  However, this Bill could not be passed as the Lok
Sabha was adjourned sine
 die on 23 December, 1998.

11.       After the Budget Session was over, the Committee again  reviewed the position of the Bills pending before it, at

followed by other Bills, in the four rounds of sittings – 29, 30 & 31 May and 1 & 2 June,  in the first round; 12, 13 & 14
June in the second round; 26, 27, 28, 29 & 30 June in the third round;  and 10, 11, 12, 13 & 14 July, 2000 in the fourth

12.       The Companies Bill, 1997 seeks to remove existing lacunae in the Companies Act, 1956 and to streamline some
of the existing provisions for better working and administration of the Companies Act.  Certain incidental and
consequential changes are also sought to be made.  In view of  the extensive changes proposed to be  made in the
Companies Act, 1956, the Bill seeks to repeal and re-enact the said Act.
13.       The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill states that -
“The Companies Act, 1956 has been in operation for the last forty year.  In the last four decades, the
economy has undergone considerable changes and the Companies Act, 1956 was amended as many as seventeen
times.
 
The objectives of liberalisation, deregulation, simplification of laws, rules, procedure and changing
economic, corporate and international corporate market require that the law on Companies –
 
            (i)         be moulded      in tune with the changing economic environment;
(ii)
                be responsive to the changing corporate practices;
(iii)               should incorporate appropriately the concepts developed in the international corporate market;

(iv)              should protect the rights of investors, depositors, employees, creditors and other participants;
(v)                should restore corporate democracy and impose extensive obligations on the Board of Directors;
(vi)              should categorise the companies and provide for different degrees of regulations for the different
categories;
(vii)             should confer rights and impose duties, including obligations of disclosure, upon different categories of
companies;
(viii)           be restructured so as to remove or replace the inadequate and obsolete provisions;
(ix)              should incorporate a strong code of corporate governance.
 
A Working Group comprising of persons with knowledge of law, economics and company affairs was set
up in August, 1996 pursuant to the 1996 Budget speech to rework a new draft of the law on Companies and
make it available for public debate.
           
The Working Group had a series of meeting during which it interacted with, and received responses
from, Reserve Bank of India, Securities and Exchange Board of India, banks and financial institutions, merchant
bankers, capital market players, stock exchange officials, industry associations, professional bodies and others
who significantly contribute to the corporate environment.  The Group prepared a working draft of the
Companies Bill and submitted the same on 2.5.1997 to the Government which  was released for public debate. 
The suggestions and observations on the working draft were made known to Government through various
workshops and seminars organised by chambers of commerce, industry associations, professional institutes, etc. 
Memoranda and detailed suggestions/representations were received by the Government from various
organisations, Members of Parliament, professional bodies and other individual experts having extensive
knowledge and wide experience of corporate affairs.
 
The main objective of re-enacting the Companies Act is to facilitate a healthy growth of the Indian
corporate sector under a liberalised, fast changing and highly competitive environment.  While redrafting the
law, an endeavour has been made to adopt a balanced  approach that recognises an international trend, i.e.
flexibility and greater self regulation by companies subject to better disclosure, more efficient enforcement of
law, and prompt and deterrent punishments to those who violate the law.  The core object of the Companies Bill
is to provide effective protection to the different participants, leaving management free to direct its energies to
the pursuit of the company’s objectives”.
 
14.       The Bill has re-grouped the scattered provisions relating to specific subjects and deleted redundant provisions.
The salient features of the present Bill which seeks to repeal and re-enact the Companies Act, 1956 are as under -
(i)
                  Reducing the size of the present Companies Act, 1956, from 658 sections and fifteen Schedules to 458
sections and three Schedules;
(ii)        Providing for appropriate rules and guidelines to ensure flexibility and clarity;
                 (iii)       Providing three-fold classification of  companies, that is –
 
(a)                Private companies, which are proposed to be largely self-governing but prohibited from inviting
and accepting deposits from the public and not to be deemed as public companies;
(b)               Unlisted public companies, which will be subject to minimum Government regulations;
(c)                Listed public companies, which will be subject to greater regulation including stricter disclosure
norms;
 
(iv)              Permitting issue of shares with differential voting and dividend rights;
(v)                Enabling companies to mobilise funds through new forms of securities, such as hybrids, derivatives,
options, etc.
(vi)              Permitting companies to buy-back their own shares subject to certain rules and safeguards;
(vii)             Permitting issue of Indian Depository Receipts (IDRs) by foreign companies to enable them to tap the
Indian capital market for setting up enterprises in India;
(viii)           Permitting companies to make investments or loans to other bodies corporate without approval of the
Government but with the approval of the shareholders;
(ix)              Prescribing minimum paid up capital of one lakh rupees for private companies and five lakhs rupees for

20.       At its sittings held on  28 & 30 June, 2000 the Committee considered the Bill clause-by-clause.
21.       Before the Committee commenced clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill, the Secretary, Department of
Company Affairs made a detailed statement for its consideration.  The relevant extracts therefrom are reproduced below
:-
“The Companies Bill, 1997 contains 458 clauses.  Apart from definitions, 34 clauses containing new concepts
were inserted in the said bill for the first time.  The other clauses of the Bill contains either minor amendments to
the sections of the Companies Act, 1956 or simply restructures these section according to the subject.
 
Out of these 34 clauses, the Companies Amendment Act, 1999  has already implemented concepts of Buy Back
of  shares (Section 77A), Nomination facility to investors (Section 109A & B), Investor Education and
Protection Fund (Section 205C), National Advisory Committee on Accounting Standards (Section 201A), Issue
of Sweat Equity Shares (Section 79A) and Infrastructure Default Finance Company defined as Public Financial
Institutions – Clause 4A.
 
With the approval of the Companies (Second Amendment) Bill, 1999 we are now left with only some more
important concepts inserted in the Companies Bill for the first time.  I would like to submit the position in regard
to such clauses, and group them into four distinct classes.
 
Firstly, concept which relates to definitions e.g. clause 2(1)’Abridged Prospectus’, clause 2(18) ‘Depository’,
clause 2(19) ‘Derivative’, clause 2(22) ‘Dividend, clause 2(24) ‘Employees Stock Option’. Clause 2(29)
‘’Hybrid, clause 2(30) ‘Information Memorandum’, clause 2(35) ‘Listed Public Companies’, clause 2(45)
‘option in securities’, clause 2(63) ‘Securities’, and clause 2(66) ‘share with differential rights’.  In addition
(clause 43) ‘Indian Depository Receipts’, (clause 51) ‘shelf prospectus’, (clause 52) ‘information memorandum’,
(clause 59) ‘initial offer of securities to be in dematerialised  form’, (clause 103) ‘debenture  trust deed’, (clause
104) ‘duties of debenture trustees’ (clause 105) ‘liability of the company to create security’.  All the provisions
relating to Capital Market were included in the Companies Bill, 1997.
 
The above clauses relate to the activity of the company for raising capital from the market after
introduction of the Companies Bill, 1997.  SEBI  has framed certain regulations on these subjects.

General observations
27.0     The Committee having gone through the process of clause-by-clause consideration of the Companies Bill, 1997,
notes  that  in respect of provisions relating to Accounting Standard, Winding up/Dissolution, Insolvency of  Companies
and constitution of Company Law Tribunal, the Government  is still engaged in consultation process with various
authorities.  After the said process is over and Government formulates   its views on these areas, it may introduce
suitable amendments in the House in respect of the relevant provisions, , as may be deemed necessary.
27.1     With regard to Part XII of the Bill on Company Law Tribunal, discussion took place in the Committee.  Views 
were  expressed  that  it  was  a half -baked concept and that the
Government had not taken a comprehensive view in the matter.  Another view was expressed that  the  Committee  had 
not  been  fully  posted  with all the decisions/development which had
taken place  on the subject.  It was also pointed out that under the various clauses in Part XII of the Bill, it appeared that
almost every aspect of the matter had been relegated to the realm of subordinate legislation. 
27.2.1
    The Committee while expressing its concern on this issue, urges upon the Government to carefully   consider  all
the  points  mentioned  above    before  arriving at a final decision
 
with regard to the Company Law Tribunal, as this is the most important aspect from the point of view of corporate mis-
governance.
28.0     A suggestion of a general nature was received relating to Section 77A of the Companies Act, 1956, pertaining to
buy back shares by companies.  It was suggested that the two years’  restriction should not be there and flexibility
should be provided to Indian companies, which have completed buy back, to retain the said shares as Treasury Stocks
for being issued later. 
28.1     The suggestion was examined by the Department of Company Affairs and it was explained as under –
The Department has considered  both the   option i.e. ‘Cancellation Option’ as well as ‘Treasury Option’. 
However, serious doubts were expressed by experts and commercial and trade bodies and they suggested that

65th Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/65threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:39]
initially we should confine to the Cancellation Option while permitting buy back.  It was apprehended that if
Treasury Option is adopted and companies are permitted to re-issue shares bought back, the directors could be
tempted to manipulate the stock market in a manner that their own relatives and kith & kin would benefit by
selling shares purchased by them at low price to the company at a huge personal profit to them.  In this process
the companies were likely to suffer.  Therefore, after careful consideration Department of Company Affairs
decided that initially we should permit only the Cancellation Option in the scheme of buy-back and the shares
purchased by the company should be physically destroyed.  This will prevent any misuse of the provision.
 
28.2     The Committee, having noted the comments of the Department, feels that there is a need to re-examine the
matter closely.
29.       The Committee has noted that certain provisions were taken out of the Companies Bill, 1997 and incorporated in
the Companies Act, 1956, first through Ordinance and thereafter by  the Companies Amendment Act, 1999, replacing
the Ordinance and the provisions  are already in force.  Certain further provisions have been taken out of the 1997 Bill
and included in the Companies (Second Amendment) Bill, 1999.  Thus, in effect, the Companies Bill, 1997 is virtually
left with certain provisions  which are new concepts being introduced for the first time in the corporate laws of the
country.  The Committee has cleared both the Bills.  It is now for
 
the Government to take appropriate follow up action and adopt a suitable procedure in piloting the Bill (s) in Parliament,
as may be deemed necessary and appropriate.

General observations
g        The Committee having gone through the process of clause-by-clause consideration of the Companies Bill, 1997,
notes  that  in respect of provisions relating to Accounting Standard, Winding up/Dissolution, Insolvency of 
Companies and constitution of Company Law Tribunal, the Government  is still engaged in consultation process
with various authorities.  After the said process is over and Government formulates   its views on these areas, it
may introduce suitable amendments in the House in respect of the relevant provisions, , as may be deemed
necessary.
g
                With regard to Part XII of the Bill on Company Law Tribunal,  discussion took place in the Committee.  Views 
were  expressed  that  it  was  a half -baked concept and that the Government had not taken a comprehensive
view in the matter.  Another view was expressed that  the  Committee  had  not  been  fully  posted  with all the
decisions/development which had taken place  on the subject.  It was also pointed out that under the various
clauses in Part XII of the Bill, it appeared that almost every aspect of the matter had been relegated to the realm
of subordinate legislation. 
g        The Committee while expressing its concern on this issue, urges upon the Government to carefully consider all
the points mentioned above  before arriving at a final decision with regard to the Company Law Tribunal, as this
is the most important aspect from the point of view of corporate mis-governance.
g        A suggestion of a general nature was received relating to Section 77A of the Companies Act, 1956, pertaining to
buy back shares by companies.  It was suggested that the two years’  restriction should not be there and
flexibility should be provided to Indian companies, which have completed buy back, to retain the said shares as
Treasury Stocks for being issued later. 

65th Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/65threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:39]
g        The suggestion was examined by the Department of Company Affairs and it was explained as under –
The Department has considered  both the   option i.e. ‘Cancellation Option’ as well as ‘Treasury
Option’.  However, serious doubts were expressed by experts and commercial and trade bodies and they
suggested that initially we should confine to the Cancellation Option while permitting buy back.  It was
apprehended that if Treasury Option is adopted and companies are permitted to re-issue shares bought
back, the directors could be tempted to manipulate the stock market in a manner that their own relatives
and kith & kin would benefit by selling shares purchased by them at low price to the company at a huge
personal profit to them.  In this process the companies were likely to suffer.  Therefore, after careful
consideration Department of Company Affairs decided that initially we should permit only the
Cancellation Option in the scheme of buy-back and the shares purchased by the company should be
physically destroyed.  This will prevent any misuse of the provision.
 
g        The Committee, having noted the comments of the Department, feels that there is a need to re-examine the
matter closely.
g
                The Committee has noted that certain provisions were taken out of the Companies Bill, 1997 and incorporated
in the Companies Act, 1956, first through Ordinance and thereafter by  the Companies Amendment Act, 1999,
replacing the Ordinance and the provisions  are already in force.  Certain further provisions have been taken out
of the 1997 Bill and included in the Companies (Second Amendment) Bill, 1999.  Thus, in effect, the Companies
Bill, 1997 is virtually left with certain provisions  which are new concepts being introduced for the first time in 
the corporate  laws  of the  country.  The
Committee has cleared both the Bills.  It is now for the Government to take appropriate
follow up action and adopt a suitable procedure in piloting the Bill (s) in Parliament, as may be deemed
necessary and appropriate.
(Para 27.0,  27.1, 27.2, 28.0, 28.1, 28.2 & 29)
