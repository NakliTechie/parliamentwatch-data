OVERVIEW 
 
1.1 Introduction  
 
1.1.1 North Eastern Region (NER) comprises eight States namely Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, 
Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura with a total area of 2,62,179 Sq. 
km. This sums up to 1 20 districts and 7.98% of the total geographical area of the country. The 
region shares International Borders of about 5483 km with several neighboring countries- Nepal, 
Bhutan, China, Myanmar and Bangladesh. As per India State of Forest Report, 2021, the forest 
resources in the North Eastern States account to 23.75% of total forest cover of the country.
 The 
area is characterized by its rich bio-diversity and heavy precipitation and has immense potential 
for development.  As per Census 2011, the population of the region is about 45 million (4.5 
crores), constituting 3.78% of the total population of India. The rural population contributes 
81.64% of the total population of the region, while the urban population is 18.36%. 
 
 
1.1.2 The Department of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) was set up in 2001 to 
give focused attention to address the special needs of the NE Region. It was converted into a full-
fledged Ministry in 2004. It is the only Ministry with territorial jurisdiction.
 The vision of the 
Ministry is to accelerate the pace of socio-economic development of the NER so that it may 
enjoy growth parity with the rest of the country. For this, it coordinates with various 
Ministries/Departments primarily concerned with development activities in NER.
 However, 
respective Ministries/Departments remain responsible for the implementation of programmes in 
their respective fields.

1.4 Coordination with Central Ministries and Departments   
 
1.4.1 In October 1996, the Central Government announced ‗N ew initiatives for the North 
Eastern Region (NER)‘, one of these measures announc ed was the policy decision to earmark 
at least 10% of Plan Budgets of the Central Ministri es/Departments for the development of 
NE States. Accordingly, it was decided that Central  Ministries/Departments, unless 
specifically exempted, will set apart 10% of their Plan Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for 
the NER to ensure a quantum jump in budgetary resour ce flows to the region and to fill the 
backlog and gaps in basic minimum service and infra structure. Subsequently, allocation of 
funds under 10% GBS for the NER, introduced in 2014 -15, is being done annually on lump 
sum basis by the non-exempted Central Ministries/De partments at the BE/RE stage under 
Major Heads 2552 (for revenue expenditure), 4552 (f or capital expenditure) and 6552 (for 
loan expenditure) under their respective functional  schemes.  
 
1.4.2 In the Budget 2017-18, several changes were introdu ced, e.g. dispensing with the 
distinction between Plan and Non-Plan expenditure, adoption of revenue/capital classification, 
merger of Railway Budget with Union Budget, disconti nuation of block grants, etc. In 
addition, the earmarking for NER was reflected thro ugh Statement-11 of Expenditure Profile 
of the Union Budget, as against Statement-23. To mi tigate dislocation, Ministry of DoNER 
has special dispensation until year 2022 to carry o ut their schemes under Budget Head 3601.  
  
1.4.3 As per the Statement-11, a total of ₹ 68440.26 crores was allocated by 54 non-
exempted Central Ministries/Departments for NE Regi on during 2021-2022 (RE), including 
Ministry of DoNER. Similarly, for the Financial Yea r 2022-23, the total budgetary allocation 
for the NER by 54 non-exempted Ministries/Departmen ts is ₹ 76040.07 crores. 
 
1.4.4 All the funds under schemes of Ministry of Develop ment of North Eastern Region are 
released to the State Government through Public Fin ancial Management System (PFMS) 
portal. The State Governments have also been direct ed to implement Expenditure Advance 
Transfer (EAT) module of PFMS, and release funds to  implementing agencies through this 
system only. Now, all the State Governments of Nort h Eastern State have to rollout EAT 
module of PFMS. The mapping of all the implementing agencies and updation of financial 
data on the PFMS portal by the State Governments is  under process.

1.6 Central Pool of Resources for NER 
 
 In 1997-98, when it was found that the actual expenditure of Ministries/Departments for 
NER was less than the mandatory ceiling of 10 percent, the Government took the decision to 
create a "Central Resource Pool" into which the unutilized portion of the 10 percent earmarked  
GBS would accrue at the end of the financial year.  Since 1998-99, the annual surrendered 
amounts out of the earmarked outlays of all the non-exempted Central Ministries/Departments 
for NER have been accounted as accruals to this Pool and the annual expenditure of the Ministry 
under the NLCPR scheme is being accounted as withdrawal from the Pool. However, this is a 
notional pool, maintained on a proforma basis by the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry of 
DoNER monitors Ministry-wise expenditure/ surrendered amounts for the purpose of 
ascertaining the accrual to the NLCPR pool in consultation with the Ministry of Finance.

INSTITUTIONS UNDER MINISTRY OF DONER 
 
1.7 North Eastern Council (NEC) 
 
 The NEC was constituted as a statutory advisory body under the NEC Act 1971 (84 of 
1971) and came into being on 7 th November 1972 at Shillong. It is a nodal agency for rapid 
socio-economic development of the North Eastern Region (NER) under the administrative 
control of the Ministry of DoNER. It acts as a Regional Planning Body, which provides financial 
assistance for a variety of projects including infrastructure projects like construction of roads, 
improving air connectivity, etc., through State Governments.
  
 
1.8 North East Cane and Bamboo Development Council (NECBDC) 
 
 Cane and Bamboo Technology Centre (CBTC) registered as a Society on 21 st July, 2004 
with Secretary NEC as Chairman.  It is now re-named as North East Cane and Bamb oo 
Development Council (NECBDC). It is an ISO 9001:2008 certified organization and as part of 
Cane and Bamboo Technological Upgradation and Networking Programme of United Nations 
Development Programme (UNDP). The Department of Science & Technology (DST), Govt. of 
India and United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO) were the main 
stakeholders. 
 
1.9 North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Limited 
(NEHHDC) 
 
 NEHHDC was set up in 1977 at Shillong, Meghalaya, with the mandate of promotion and 
development of handicrafts and handlooms sectors in the NER of the country.  NEHHDC plays a 
key role in the development and promotion of the handicrafts and handlooms sectors of the 
North Eastern Region. NEHHDC has been providing Marketing support through procurement of 
handicrafts and handloom products directly from the Artisans, Weavers, Self-Help Groups at 
clusters and selling the same through
 its chain of retail outlets by the Brand name ―Purbashree‖,   
located at Shillong, Guwahati, Kolkata, New Delhi and Gujarat and also its e-commerce 
platform  purbashree.com which is a one-stop shop for various handloom and handicraft products 
of North East India.
 
 
1.10 North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC)  
 
 North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC) Limited was 
incorporated in the year 1982 as a Central Public Sector Enterprise under the administrative 
control of the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER). NERAMAC has 
been envisaged to function as a marketing organization dedicated to the agricultural sector of the 
North-eastern region. It is lending a supporting hand to the farmers‘ beginning from the fields 
and up to the markets to the end consumers for their produces. NERAMAC procures produce 
from the farmers at remunerative prices and maintains the supply chain for proper distribution 
and placing of produces in the markets throughout the country.

LIVELIHOOD & ENTREPRENEURSHIP 
 
1.11 North Eastern Region Community Resource Management Project (NERCORMP) 
 
 NEC supports Community Based Sustainable Livelihood Projects i.e., North Eastern 
Region Community Resource Management Project (NERCORMP), which was launched in May, 
1999.
 NERCORMP is running under North Eastern Region Community Resource Management 
Society (NERCRMS) which is a registered Society under North Eastern Council. The project 
operates  in six districts of  three states viz: Assam (Karbi Anglong and North Cachar Hills), 
Manipur (Ukhrul and Senapati) and Meghalaya (West Garo Hills and West Khasi Hills).
 The 
project adopts a holistic approach of development with two broad focus areas: (i) Social 
mobilization, organization and capacity building to tap and realize the latent potential of the 
communities by employing their time tested traditional value systems and culture; and (ii) 
Intervene with the economic and social activities and infrastructure with predominant thrust on 
income generating activities to achieve economic transformation. 
 
1.12 North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Limited (NEDFi) 
 
 The North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Limited (NEDFi) is a public 
limited company registered under the Companies Act, 1956, in 1995. It is notified as a Public 
Financial Institution under Section 4A of the said Act and is registered as an NBFC (Non-
Banking Financial Company) with RBI. The core business of the Corporation is lending 
activities undertaken through the project finance schemes & microfinance.
 NEDFi plays an 
important role in facilitating the setting up of new industrial and service sector projects in the 
North Eastern States. The Corporation also provides fee based Advisory & Consultancy Services 
to the Central and State Governments. Ministry of DoNER provides interest-free loans to NEDFi 
to catalyze industrial growth and nurture entrepreneurship in the North East Region. 
 
 
1.13 North East Venture Fund (NEVF) 
 
 NEDFi in collaboration with the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region 
(MDoNER) has set up the North East Venture Fund (NEVF), a dedicated venture capital fund for 
the region, to encourage startup ventures in the NER. The capital commitment to the fund is 
₹100 crores (MDoNER ₹45.00, NEDFi ₹30.00 crores & SIDBI ₹25.00 crores).
 The fund targets 
to invest in Start-Ups and unique business opportunities to provide resources for new 
entrepreneurships.
 The main focus of NEVF is in the enterprises involved in Food Processing, 
Healthcare, Tourism, IT, etc.  
 
1.14 Science & Technology Intervention North Eastern Region (STINER) 
 
 The new initiative for Science & Technology Intervention in North Eastern Region 
(STINER) was envisaged by M/o DoNER in close collaboration with the Office of Principal 
Scientific adviser (PSA) to Govt. of India with an aim to improve the livelihood of vulnerable 
groups especially that of women, unemployed youth and the poor farmers and artisans of the 
North Eastern Region of the country in a sustainable manner.
 The STINER project was initiated 

7 
 
in Hub and Spoke model, where STINER Hub is established at CSIR-NEIST, Jorhat which is its 
main center, designated as STINER-TFC Hub (STINER Technology Facility Centre Hub), and 
Spoke will be established at each states of North East India. Already, Nagaland spoke has been 
established at School for Agricultural Science & Rural Development (SASRD), Nagaland 
University, Mezdiphema and activities have been initiated. In addition to the technology mall, 
the hub will also have a quality testing laboratory for food and other products to support the 
growing demand of food processing industries, food processing self help groups, NGOs working 
on food and allied sector to promote regional and export market of food in the region.

2.2.7 On being asked about the reasons for non utilization of funds, the Ministry has replied 
that the main bottlenecks faced is  late receipt of Utilization Certificates (UCs), delay in 
awarding the work, delay in release of funds by State Governments to implementing agencies, 
slow pace of implementation of projects due to difficult terrain and reduced working season.  
 
2.2.8 The Ministry has informed that these problems have been addressed by frequent review 
meetings with the senior officers of the State Governments, and close coordination and 
communication between Ministry of DoNER and NE State Governments for project 
implementation. Ministry of DoNER has appointed State-wise Chief Nodal Officers/Nodal 
Officers for effective implementation/monitoring of projects funded under various schemes of 
this Ministry.  In order to avoid delay in release of funds by State Government to Implementing 
Agencies, all the funds are released by the Ministry to the State Government through Public 
Finance Management System (PFMS) portal. Further, the State Government has also been 
directed to implement EAT module of PFMS, and release funds to implementing agencies 
through this system only. Other steps taken to strengthen the monitoring mechanism include 
online portal for specific schemes/projects, Geo-tagging of project sites, monitoring of physical 
progress through geo-tagged photographs, ‗just in time‘ release of funds (linked to physical and 
financial progress) and inspection of major projects by Technical Wing of the Ministry, on 
sample basis. 
 
2.2.9 Further, status of the ongoing projects under schemes of MDoNER is also reviewed 
during the meeting of Inter-Ministerial Committee, which is Chaired by the Secretary, MDoNER 
and co-chaired by the Chief Secretary of the concerned States. During the months of January and 
February, 2022 a series of meetings with the senior officers of the State Government and their 
implementing agencies were held under the Chairmanship of Secretary, MDoNER during which 
all ongoing projects older than three years were reviewed along with all the concerned State 
officials and implementing agencies. It was impressed upon the State Governments that further 
release for projects would be based upon progress of old projects in the States. As a result of 
these meetings, the State Governments have identified projects to be completed within a fixed 
timeline and the actions to be taken for achieving the same. The Ministry is coordinating closely 
with the State Government for monitoring the same

CENTRAL SECTOR SCHEMES 
 
3.1 Schemes of North East Council  
 
3.1.1 The North Eastern Council, which was constituted as a statutory advisory body by an Act 
of Parliament under the North Eastern Council Act, 1971 (Act No. 84 of 1971) works under the 
administrative control of Ministry of DoNER. It provides financial assistance for a variety of 
projects including infrastructure projects like construction of roads, improving air connectivity, 
etc., through State Governments. 
 The ―NEC General Guidelines‖ were first prepared in 2010 
and laid down the importance of preparation of perspective plans with regional approach; 
procedures to be followed for obtaining priority list from the NE States; selection, appraisal, 
sanction, monitoring and reporting, evaluation of projects; composition and functions of various 
committees and other details for NEC to follow.  
 
3.1.2 The Committee was informed that the "Schemes of the NEC" is a Central Sector Scheme 
with 100% Grant by the Government of India.
 The projects under the scheme are implemented in 
focused areas through designated agencies of the States/Central Government with a stipulation 
that in case of State agencies, the funds will be transferred to implementing agencies.  
 
3.1.3 The Committee was further informed that the Union Cabinet, in its meeting held in 
January 2020, approved earmarking of 30 percent of North Eastern Council's (NEC's) allocation 
for new projects under the existing "Schemes of North Eastern Council" for focused 
development of deprived areas; deprived/neglected sections of society and emerging priority 
sectors in the North Eastern States. The balance allocation shall be bifurcated in the existing two 
components viz. State component-60 percent and Central component-40 percent. 
 
3.1.4 For identification of new projects for the earmarked State component of NEC allocations, 
States may submit projects under the sectors covered under NEC's mandate upto two times the 
normative allocation with the approval of SLEC chaired by Chief Secretary, along with Concept 
Notes, indicating the tentative project cost etc. Projects under Central component and NEC 
component will be identified by NEC in consultation with State Government/other stakeholders 
along with Concept Note and tentative project costs.

3.3.18 The guidelines to administer NLCPR were formulated in December, 2001 and were 
revised from time to time on the basis of past experience. In pursuance of the Government 
initiative of Maximum Governance with Minimum Government, Scheme Guidelines were last 

25 
 
revised in 2016 to make the process of retention and sanction of projects more transparent and 
participative. The new provisions in the Guidelines empowered State Governments, put a cap on 
retention of projects, discouraged parking of fund and encouraged timely completion of projects. 
The salient features of Revised Guidelines 2016 are as under:- 
  
(i) Constitution of State Level Empowered Committee (SLEC) in each State to 
prioritize and recommend projects for retention/sanction. 
 
(ii) Delegation of power of vetting the DPRs, techno- economically, to State Level 
Empowered Committee (SLEC). 
 
(iii) Value of Projects to be retained not to exceed three times normative allocation 
minus value of retained project. 
 
(iv) Number of instalments for release of funds for sanctioned projects reduced to two. 
Now, the funds to be released in two instalments of 40% and 60%. 
 
(v) To ensure just-on-time release of funds, initially a token amount of ₹10 lakh to be 
released and balance amount of first instalment to be released after receipt of letter of 
award of the contract.

3.5 North East Road Sector Development Scheme (NERSDS) 
 
3.5.1 The North East Road Sector Development Scheme (NERSDS) was conceived by the 
Ministry of DoNER during the financial year 2014-15 pursuant to the budget announcement by 
Hon'ble Finance Minister in his budget speech as it was noticed that several strategically 
important inter- State roads mostly constructed by NEC and BRO were not properly maintained 
by either of the States and were treated as "Neglected Roads". The NERSDS mainly focuses on 
rehabilitation, construction and up gradation of the following type of roads. 
i. Inter-State roads previously built by NEC and other agencies which are of vital 
connectivity for one State, but of little importance for other State and hence remained 
neglected, but with available formation width of roads. 
ii. Road in socio-politically neglected pocket of NER. 
iii. Roads required from security or strategic viewpoint not covered in other programmes, 
and 
iv. Road necessary from the viewpoint of market access for agriculture produce and roads of 
economic importance on gap-filling approach.

4.1 Smart City Project in NER 
 
4.1.1 The main objective of the Smart Cities Mission, which  was launched in 2015,provide 
core infrastructure, clean and sustainable environment and a decent quality of life to the citizens 
through the ap plication of ‗smart solutions‘. The Mission aims to drive economic growth and 
improve quality of life through comprehensive work on social, economic, physical and 
institutional pillars of the city. The focus is on sustainable and inclusive development by creation 
of replicable models which act as lighthouses to other aspiring cities.

North Eastern Region. The Ministry informed the Committee that Government of India launched

2018. Under SCM, the Central Government shall give financial support to the Mission to the 
extent of ₹ 48,000 crores over five years, on an average of ₹ 500 crores per city. An equal 
amount, on a matching basis, will be contributed by the State/Urban Local Body.

4.10 Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport (KMMTT) project 
4.10.1 The Committee was appraised by the Ministry that Kaladan Multimodal Transit 
Transport (KMMTT) is a strategic connectivity project to connect the eastern ports of India with 
Myanmar by developing a port at Sittwe on the western coast of Myanmar and linking the Sittwe 
port with river channel and by road to Mizoram. The project has two components: (i) Waterway 
component and (ii) Roadway component.  
 
4.10.2 The Waterway component of KMMTT includes construction of a Port and an Inland 
Water Transport (IWT) Terminal at Sittwe; dredging of access channel and river channel at the  
Port, development of 158 km long navigational channel along Kaladan River from Sittwe to 
Paletwa; construction of an IWT Terminal at Paletwa and construction of six 300-ton barges. 
The construction work on waterway component is complete.  A port operator, has been 
appointed jointly by the Government of India and Myanmar in December 2019. The port 
operator has commenced its operation from 01 February 2020. Efforts are being made to 
operationalize the port and IWT terminal. Tender/contract for works required for 
operationalization of port, has been awarded in November 2021, and work has started. 
  
4.10.3 The Road component of KMMTT includes a new greenfield road project of 109.2 km to 
be built from Paletwa in Myanmar to Zorinpui on India-Myanmar border (Mizoram) to double-

62 
 
lane National Highway specifications. M/s. IRCON Infrastructure & Services Limited is the 
Project Management Consultant. 
  
4.10.4 The execution of KMMTT project, especially the road component has faced many 
challenges due to the following reasons :- 
 
i. One of the JV partner of the appointed contractor (M/s C&C Constructions) has gone 
bankrupt and insolvency proceeding has been initiated in National Company Law 
Tribunal since March 2019. 
ii. Prolonged monsoon season in Myanmar (May to November). 
iii. Completely greenfield nature of the project and difficult geographical terrain along the 
project site in Myanmar.  
iv. Due to COVID-19 pandemic, Government of Myanmar has implemented strict 
restrictions on movement of personnel and machines which has affected the access and 
pace of the work.  
  
4.10.5 The Revised Project cost approved by the Cab inet in October 2015 is ₹2904.04 crores 
(Rs 982.99 crores for waterways and Rs 1921.05 crores for road component). Funds released for 
Kaladan Road component till 31.01.2022 are approx. ₹ 63 crores.  Funds spent on developing the 
Kaladan waterways component are approx. ₹ 566 crores.  
 
4.10.6 On being asked about the current physical progress of road component of KMMTT, the 
Ministry informed that the physical progress is 4% as on 31st January 2022. Due to slow progress 
and bankruptcy of lead partner of JV con tractor for road component, ‗Show cause‘ notice has 
been issued to the agency for termination.  The process for appointment of fresh implementation 
agency has thus been initiated and likely to be concluded shortly thereafter. 
 
4.10.7 The Committee notes that one of the JV partner of the appointed contractor has 
gone bankrupt and this has been one of the reasons for slow physical progress of the road

4.18 Environment and Forest 
4.18.1 The Committee enquired about the various steps taken to minimize human-wildlife 
conflict & number of casualties on account of man-animal conflict. in response thereto, it was 
informed that the State Governments/UT Administration take care of conservation and 
protection of forests and wildlife in the States/UT‘s. The Central Government supplements their 
efforts by providing necessary technical support and financial assistance under various Schemes 
to protect, conserve and manage forest and wildlife. Central Government provides financial 
assistance to States/UT‘s for habitat enrichment, water augmentation, construction/erection of  
physical barriers such as solar powered electric fence, bio-fencing using cactus, boundary wall, 
animal proof trenches around forests, etc. In a recent advisory issued by Ministry of 
Environment, Forests & Climate Change (MoEF&CC) for handling human wildlife conflict, 
there is provision for constitution of coordination committees at the State/ UT and district levels. 
 
4.18.2 A guideline for Management of Human Elephant Conflict has been issued by the 
Ministry on 6 th October, 2017 and States have been requested for implementation of the same. 
Critical elephant habitats are notified as ‗Elephant Reserve‘ for focus and synergy in elepha nt 
conservation and to reduce conflict. The notification is carried out with the approval of Steering 
Committee formed in the Ministry.  Periodic awareness campaigns are organized to sensitize, 
guide and advise the general public on man-animal conflict, including dissemination of 
information through various forms of media. So far, 31 Elephant Reserves have been established 
in 14 major elephant States. 
 
4.18.3 Government of India, through the National Tiger Conservation Authority, has advocated 
a three pronged strategy to manage human-tiger negative interactions as follows: -  
 
(i)        Material and logistical support:  Funding support through the ongoing Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme of Project Tiger is provided to tiger reserves for acquiring 
capacity in terms of infrastructure and material, to deal with tigers dispersing out of 
source areas.   
 
(ii) Restricting habitat interventions:  Based on the carrying capacity of tigers in a 
tiger reserve, habitat interventions are restricted through an overarching TCP.  In 
case tiger numbers are at carrying capacity levels, it is advised that habitat 
interventions should be limited so that there is no excessive spill over of wildlife 
including tigers thereby minimizing man-animal conflict.   
 

77 
 
(iii) Standard Operating Procedure (SOPs):  The National Tiger Conservation 
Authority has issued three SOPs to deal with human-animal conflict, which are 
available in public domain. 
 
4.18.4 SOPs inter alia include the issue of managing dispersing tigers, managing livestock kills, 
so as to reduce conflict as well as relocating tigers from source areas to areas where density of 
tiger is low, so that conflict in rich source areas does not occur. As per Tiger Conservation Plan, 
need based and site-specific management interventions are done for improving quality of wildlife 
habitat, for which, funding is provided under the ongoing Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Project 
Tiger. There has been 2 casualties on account of man-animal conflict in North-East Region. The 
number of people injured or permanently incapacitated in human wildlife conflicts are not 
collated by the Ministry but compensated as per rules by State/UT Governments.

4.22 Disaster Management 
4.22.1 The Committee sought the information regarding earthquake prone areas in NER and 
institutional mechanism which are in place for early detection and warning in case of earthquake 
and other natural disasters such as floods, excessive rainfall, etc. In its reply, Ministry informed 
the Committee that entire North Eastern Region lies in seismic Zone -V as per IS 2893 : 2016. 
The area is susceptible to severe intensity of earthquake ground shaking of MSK (Medvedev –
Sponheuer–Karnik i.e. macroseismic intensity scale) Intensity VII. 
 
4.22.2 National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) issued revised National Disaster 
Management Plan in November, 2019. This plan, inter-alia provides a framework and direction 
to the government agencies for all phases of disaster management i.e. Prevention, Mitigation, 
Preparedness, Response, Rehabilitation and Reconstruction. The plan also contains roles and 
responsibilities of Central and State Agencies for disaster risk reduction, under different thematic 
area like understanding risks, inter-agency coordination, investing in disaster risk reduction – 
structural and non-structural measures, capacity development and climate change risk 
management. The plan has been shared with Central Ministries / Departments, all States / UTs 
and other stakeholders to enable the States / UTs to develop their plans and strategies for  DRR. 
 
4.22.3 The details of various initiatives undertaken for disaster preparedness and management 
are as follows:-  
(i)       NDMA has issued various guidelines on various disasters which includes the aspects 
of disaster mitigation, preparedness and response activities. NDMA facilitates the 
conduct of State and Multi State Level Mock Exercises on various disasters in close 
coordination with States and UTs and various other stakeholders like First 
Responders, Early Warning Agencies, CAPFs, NDRF and Armed Forces etc. 
Integrated Mock Exercise provides an optimal, cost effective means for generating 
awareness about the Incident Response System – Incident Response Team construct 
and for building capacity of States / UTs to respond effectively to any disaster 
situation. 
(ii) NDMA has undertaken a project for the Development of Urban Flood Early Warning 
System (FEWS) for Guwahati City. The FEWS was developed in collaboration with 
Indian Meteorological Department (IMD), North Eastern Space Application Centre 
(NESAC) and Assam State Disaster Management Authority (ASDMA) and it is 

80 
 
implemented by The Energy and Resources Institute (TERI). The fully automated 
web platform provides early warnings related to heavy rainfall and subsequent flood 
with 72 hrs lead time to the concerned authorities to take necessary actions for flood 
management in the city of Guwahati. 
(iii) NDMA has also undertaken the flood risk assessment for all the flood prone districts 
in India in consultation with the State. NDMA has undertaken the study to prepare 
district level flood hazard atlas. The preparation of flood hazard Atlas was entrusted 
to National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad. NRSC has prepared the 
Atlas on the basis of data provided by the States, CWC, IMD and by the use of 
Satellite imageries over the period of time.  Under this the district wise flood hazard 
Atlas of Assam State has been prepared and released. 
(iv) NDMA has been implementing two Schemes namely i) Implementation of the Sendai 
Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction and (ii) Strengthening of District Disaster 
Management Authorities of Hazard Prone Districts out of the 115 identified backward 
districts in all the States of NER, inter-alia, for strengthening disaster risk 
management. 
(v) NDMA has also started a Pilot Project to improve Earthquake Resilience of masonry 
Life Line Structures and upcoming Constructions. 
 
4.22.4 The Committee notes that the entire North Eastern region lies under seismic Zone- V

 The Committee is deeply concerned regarding man-animal conflict and su bsequent
