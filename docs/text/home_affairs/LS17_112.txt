INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairperson of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs having been
authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this  One-Hundred and Twelfth
Report on the Demands for Grants (2005-2006) relating to the Ministry of 
Home Affairs.
2.         The Committee in its previous Reports on Demands for Grants had dealt with almost all the activities which
come under the purview of the Ministry of Home Affairs. However, this time besides considering the Demands for
Grants for 2005-06, the Committee has decided to give special focus to “Disaster Management” in this Report as this
subject has been transferred to the Ministry of Home Affairs from the Ministry of Agriculture in 2002 and also keeping
in view the havoc and devastation caused by Tsunami not only in India but in many of its neighbouring Asian countries
on 26th December, 2004. The Tsunami tragedy was followed by unprecedented snowfall in Jammu & Kashmir in early
2005 causing extreme difficulties to the residents of the State and other people. The Committee has therefore devoted an
exclusive chapter (Chapter-IV) to this subject so as to highlight the significance and importance of Disaster
Management The Committee, besides having the presentation of Home Secretary on the subject also heard experts viz.
Dr. A.S. Arya, National Seismic Advisor; Dr. K. Radhakrishnan, Director, Department of Ocean Development; Shri S.K
Subramanian, Deputy Director General, Indian Meteorological Department and Prof. Santosh Kumar from National
Institute of Disaster Management in its sitting held on 28th March, 2005. The Committee also heard the Home Secretary
on Demands for Grants (2005-06) of Ministry of Home Affairs on 29th March, 2005 on the same day the Committee
held in-house discussion on “Disaster Management” and “Demands for Grants”. The Committee considered and
adopted this report in its sitting held on 15th April, 2005.

REPORT
CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
            Maintenance of internal security in the country is the primary responsibility of the Ministry of Home Affairs. In
addition, the Ministry also shoulders the following broad responsibilities:
            (i)         Issue of Notification in respect of Appointment of President/Vice-President, Prime Minister and
Members of the Union Council of Ministers,
            (ii)        Appointment of Governors and Lt. Governors;
            (iii)       Regulation of Pay and Allowances of the Ministers;
            (iv)       Carrying out Census in India once every ten years,
            (v)        Management of the International Borders;
            (vi)       Management and Deployment of the Central Police Forces;
            (vii)      Centre-States Relations/Inter-State Relations;
            (viii)      Administration of Union Territories;
            (ix)       Implementation of the Provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963;
            (x)        Maintenance of Communal harmony and Promotion of National Integration;
            (xi)       Co-ordination of Policy Relating to Human Rights;
            (xii)      Providing Relief and Rehabilitation to Refugees/Displaced Persons;
            (xiii)      Regulation of Entry of Foreigners into India;
            (xiv)     Grant of Pension and Other Benefits to Freedom Fighters;
            (xv)      Prevention and combat of drug abuse and drug trafficking;
            (xvi)     Formulation and implementation of National Disaster Management Programme; and
            (xvii)     Border Area Development Programme.
2.         To discharge the above responsibilities, the Ministry has been divided into following six constituent
Departments:
            (i)         Department of Internal Security;
            (ii)        Department of States;
            (iii)       Department of Official Language;
            (iv)       Department of Home;
            (v)        Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs; and
            (vi)       Department of Border Management.
2.1       The Department of Internal Security deals with Police, Law and Order and Rehabilitation, Department of States
deals with Centre-State relations, inter-State relations, Union Territories and Freedom Fighters’ Pension; Department of
Official Language is concerned with the implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating to Official
Language and provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963; Department of Home deals with notification of
assumption of office by the President and Vice-President, notification of appointment of the Prime Minister and other
Ministers, etc; Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs deals with all constitutional provisions with respect to the
State of Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters relating to the State of Jammu and Kashmir excluding those with

international borders including coastal borders.
2.2       The Ministry is under the overall charge of Home Minister assisted by three Ministers of State. The Department
of Official Language has a separate Secretary and functions independently. All Departments of the Ministry except the
Department of Official Language are interlinked and are headed by the Home Secretary who is assisted by Secretary
(Border Management), one Special Secretary, three Additional Secretaries and seventeen Joint Secretaries. The Ministry
is further divided into seventeen divisions for administrative purposes and are concerned with the tasks mentioned
below for each division:----
            (i)         Centre State Division
                        The Division deals with Central-State relations, including working of the Constitutional provisions
governing such relations; appointment of Governors; creation of new States; nominations to Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha;
Inter-State Boundary disputes; over-seeing the crime situation in States, etc.
            (ii)        Internal Security Division
                        The Division deals with matters relating to internal security, including anti-national and subversive
activities of various groups/extremist organizations.
            (iii)       Police Division
                                    The Division functions as the cadre controlling authority of IPS officers and also deals with all
matters relating to Central Police Forces, including their deployment.
            (iv)       Union Territories Division
                        The Division deals with all legislative and Constitutional matters relating to Union Territories including
Delhi and Pondicherry. It also functions as the cadre controlling authority of Arunachal, Goa, Mizoram Union
Territories (AGMUT) cadre of IPS/IAS as also Delhi Andaman Nicobar Islands Civil Service (DANICS)/ Delhi
Andaman Nicobar Islands Police Service (DAMPS). It is also responsible for overseeing the crime situation in UTs.
            (v)        North-Eastern Division
                        The Division deals with the law and order situation in North-Eastern States, including matters relating to
insurgency and talks with various extremist groups operating in that region.
            (vi)       Jammu & Kashmir Division      
                        The Division deals with the Constitutional matters including Article 370 and general policy matters in
respect of J&K and terrorism/militancy in that State; it is also responsible for implementation of the PM’s Package for
J&K.
            (vii)      Foreigners Division
                        The Division deals with all matters relating to Foreigners Act and Passport (Entry into India) Act,
Registration of Foreigners Act, the Citizenship Act, and the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act. It also controls
Bureau of Immigration.
            (viii)      Freedom Fighters andRehabilitation Division
                        The Division frames and implements the Freedom Fighters Pension Scheme and the schemes for
rehabilitation of migrants from former West Pakistan/East Pakistan and provision of relief to Srilankan and Tibetan
refugees.
            (ix)       Human Rights Division
                        The Division deals with matters relating to the Protection of Human Rights Act and also matters relating
to national integration and Ayodhya Affairs.
            (x)        Disaster Management Division
                        The newly created Division is responsible for coordination of relief measures in the event of natural
calamities and man-made disasters (except drought and epidemics).
            (xi)       Border Management Division
                        This Division deals with all matters relating to management of international borders.
            (xii)      Police Modernization Division
                        The Division handles all items of work relating to modernisation of State Police Forces,
provisioning/procurement of various items for modernisation of Central Police Forces, police reforms, police training
and VlP, Installation Security.
            (xiii)      Policy Planning Division
                        It deals with matters relating to policy formulation in respect of counter-terrorism, international
covenants, bilateral assistance treaties and related items of work.
            (xiv)     Finance Division

            (xv)      Security Division

DEMAND NUMBERS 95, 96, 97, 98 AND 99 - UNION TERRITORIES (WITHOUT LEGISLATURE)
6.7       Though the budget of the Union Territories without legislature forms part of Ministry of Home Affairs, being the
nodal Ministry, it, however, does not dilute the responsibilities of the other Ministries of the Central Government from
scrutinising and sanctioning the schemes under their purview. The factual position regarding budgetary procedure in this
regard is that the entire budget requirement for Plan and Non-Plan, revised estimates for current year and Non-Plan

SCHEMES, POLICIES AND PROGRAMMES OF THE MINISTRY
Rehabilitation of J&K Migrants and Security related Expenditure (SRE)
8.0       The targeted attacks by the militants against civilians in the initial phases of the terrorist violence in J&K forced
a large number of Kashmiri Pandits and a sizeable number of Sikhs and other Hindus and a few Muslims-to migrate
from the Valley in 1990s. There were 56487 migrant families as per official records. The Central Government initiated a
policy to provide relief and rehabilitation to such families/people. The thrust of the policy was to minimise the
difficulties and hardships of the migrants and provide a reasonable amount of sustenance and support. Various State
Governments/UT Administrations were reimbursed by the Central Government for providing relief to such Migrants.
8.1.0    On being asked about the reasons for shortfall in the performance of the scheme of rehabilitation of J&K
Migrants and a saving of Rs. 184 crore, the Ministry clarified that the provision of funds were made under “Security
Related Expenditure” and “Relief and Rehabilitation” for J&K for the financial year 2004-05 taking into consideration
the expenditure incurred during the preceding year, as well as the anticipated expenditure during the year. The Ministry
also informed that a Committee was set up to look into the condition of the migrants who were till now living in one
room tenements. It was further stated that since the families living in relief camps have grown and one room tenement is
highly inadequate for them for proper living there is need for at least two room tenements. The Ministry informed that it
has started making efforts to convert one-room tenements into two rooms by merging two tenements.

Police strengthening on Indo-Nepal Border
9.0       The stretch of 1751 Kms. of Indo-Nepal border covering 5 States and 20 districts is now being guarded by the
Shasastra Seema Bal (SSB). Its charter of duty was amended on the recommendation of the Group of Ministers under
the policy of ‘One border One force’. Earlier the Indo-Nepal border was guarded by BSF and ITBP. Besides that, the
respective State Police also take care of the security aspects in the area for which the Ministry of Home Affairs has a
special scheme of reimbursement of expenditure on Police infrastructure and intelligence network.
9.1       On being asked about the reasons for shortfall in the ‘Police strengthening on Indo-Nepal border’ the Ministry
has stated that pursuant to the acceptance of recommendations of Group of Ministers on Reforming the National
Security System by Government of India, the State Governments having border with Nepal were asked to send proposal
for strengthening of policing infrastructure and intelligence network in their respective areas. A provision of Rs.21 crore
was kept in the BE 2004-2005 for reimbursing the expenditure to be incurred by the respective State Governments on
police strengthening and intelligence network etc.
9.2       The Ministry further stated that while reviewing the scheme it was found that there was a lot of overlapping
between the proposed scheme and the scheme of Modernization of Police Forces (MPF). The MPF scheme also
provides for similar assistance to the State Governments for modernizing their police forces. It was, therefore, felt that it
would be administratively prudent to link this scheme with the existing scheme of Modernization of Police Forces by
providing a special component under the MPF scheme for giving assistance to the States having border with Nepal to
strengthen their policing and intelligence network. Due to this change in policy necessitated by administrative and
financial propriety, the funds kept for this purpose in BE 2004-2005 could not be utilized under the specific scheme.
However, the same were reappropriated at the RE stage and utilized in some other schemes.
9.3.0    The Committee feels that the scheme of Modernisation of Police Forces is taking proper care for strengthening
infrastructure and intelligence network in the States bordering Nepal. However, in light of the recent spurt in Maoist
violence in Nepal and its percolation to bordering States affected by Naxalism, the Ministry should take special
measures to strengthen Policing on the Indo-Nepal Border.
9.3.1    The Committee also recommends for empowering and strengthening of SSB, the border guarding force on the
Indo-Nepal border so as to curb not only the ISI activities through this route but also the Maoist groups in assisting the
MCC. It also feels that the Ministry should explore the possibility of amending the Indo-Nepal Treaty so as to make it
compulsory for everybody crossing the border to have his identity proof.

Modernisation of Police Forces
11.0     “Police” being a State Subject, its modernisation is the primary responsibility of the State Governments.
However, the Ministry of Home Affairs has been implementing a Non-plan Scheme for Modernization of State Police
Forces since 1969-70 to supplement the efforts of the State Governments in modernizing their police forces. The
Scheme provided assistance to the State Governments on 50% “Grants-In-Aid” and 50% loan basis. In view of the
changing situation of internal security in the country, the annual Central assistance under the scheme was pegged at
Rs.1000 crore in 2000-01 which was further enhanced to Rs. 1400 crore from 2003-04. While enhancing the annual
Central allocation during 2003-04, it was also decided to give the Central assistance only as “Grants-in-Aid”. The cost-
sharing basis was also changed by grouping the States into three categories A, B1 and B2 depending on the level of
insurgency/terrorism including cross-border terrorism being faced by the State and the funding pattern was decided as
100%, 75% and 60% respectively to these three categories of States. The Ministry is further considering the
recommendation of the Committee to group all the States in two categories (A&B) only.
11.1     On being asked about the effectiveness of the scheme of Modernisation of Police Forces of the States, the
Ministry has stated that the Scheme has made perceptible impact, as proper buildings for police stations/outposts taken
up under the Scheme has provided safe and secure environment, construction of houses for police personnel and
provision of modern weaponry have boosted their morale. From the reports made available by the States and as per the
interim impact assessment report submitted by the Bureau of Police Research & Development (BPR&D), the scheme
has performed well during the last three years and it has made perceptible changes in police mobility, communication,
weapons, housing satisfaction level and Police Station buildings. Besides that, according to BPR&D, the forensic
science laboratories in the States are getting modernized also; for instance, the State Forensic Science Laboratory of
Andhra Pradesh got ISO-9002 Certificate also. Forensic Science Laboratory in Gujarat could add Brain-mapping and
Narco-analysis. State Government of Andhra Pradesh could launch an ambitious computerization project named E-cops.
Gujarat, Haryana and West Bengal have established Wide Area Networks. Training infrastructure in most States
particularly Assam and Haryana have been upgraded. State police are now being provided bullet­ proof vehicles, mine
protected vehicles, bomb detection and disposal equipment. Modern control rooms have been set at major cities. Reports
received from the States also indicate that enhanced mobility and provision of modern communication equipment have
brought down response time of the police force.

Police Grant No. 54
12.0     There has been an incremental growth since 2003 in Grant No. 54:Police. The increase is 19.78 percent in the

12.1     In view of this increased allocation for police under Grant No. 54, the issue of the image of the police in public

station for seeking their assistance in redressal of grievances for booking the culprits who commit crimes of various
types and for protection of lives and properties.
12.2     The Home Secretary while deposing before the Committee stated that this aspect has been considered by
different Commissions, which have made valuable recommendations. Though the process of implementation of those
recommendations is ongoing, a lot reforms need to be carried out for improving the image of the Police.

67 per cent of the total budget of the Ministry of Home Affairs, besides on an average Rs. 800 crore per year is being
spent for modernisation of Police Forces of States during the last three years. While endorsing the increased outlay on
the Police, the Committee feels that despite the Parliament voting such a large outlay on Police, there is no perceptible
improvement in the behavioral and attitudinal aspects of the Police personnel, which needs proper training at all levels.
12.4     The Committee, therefore strongly recommends that the Ministry should initiate sincere efforts towards
improving the police-people relationship. It also recommends that an impact analysis of the performance of the Police in
terms of crime rate, police-population ratio, satisfaction rate of people with regard to performance of police should be
undertaken and all out efforts should be made to improve and enhance the image of the Police.

western belt of Mizoram covering Bru settlement areas is being contemplated. So far twelve rounds of talks have been
held between representatives of Government of Mizoram and BNLF. The Government of Mizoram has since prepared a
draft agreement to be signed with BNLF leaders. It is expected that the Government of Mizoram would be signing this
agreement with BNLF leaders very soon.

counter insurgency operations in Mizoram, grants-in-aid of Rs. 3.11 crore for ex-gratia payment to legal heirs of persons
killed during insurgency in Mizoram and Rs. 8 crore as first instalment of grant-in-aid to Government of Mizoram for
rehabilitation and resettlement of about 195 BNLF returnees and approximately 5000 families of Bru (Reang) refugees
The rehabilitation and resettlement of BNLF returnees/Bru refugees has not yet started, as the repatriation of BNLF

CHAPTER-IV
DISASTER MANAGEMENT
Introductory
17.0     Disasters are those catastrophes, mishaps, calamities, accidents which the community itself is unable to cope
with. The occurrence of a natural hazard of sufficient magnitude capable of causing substantive loss of property/life is
known as natural disaster. Floods, droughts, cyclones, earthquakes, Tsunami, landslides, avalanches and fire hazards are
mainly the cause of natural disasters. The factors leading to a natural disaster can be meteorological, geological,
ecological, environmental, technological, etc. Besides that, in the era of growing global terrorism, there are manmade
disasters which could be caused through nuclear weapons, radioactive material chemical agents or toxic industrial
chemical and biological agents. These disasters cause loss of lives, properties, damage to livelihood, economic
hardships, environmental damage, sociological & psychological after-effects, malnutrition, distress sale of people’s
assets, etc. Thus disaster can be broadly classified under two heads, viz. natural and man-made or human induced.

India's Vulnerability
17.1     India has been traditionally vulnerable to natural disasters on account of its unique geo-climatic conditions.
Floods, droughts, cyclones, earthquakes and landslides, etc. have been recurrent phenomena. About 60 per cent of the
landmass of India is prone to earthquakes of various intensities; over 40 million hectares is prone to floods; about 8 per
cent of the total area (coastal States particularly in the East coast and Gujarat) is prone to cyclones; 68 per cent of the
area is susceptible to drought; 55 per cent of total area is in seismic Zones III - IV and is vulnerable to earthquakes and
sub Himalayan/Western Ghat is vulnerable to landslides/hailstorms/cloudbursts. In the decade 1990-2000, on an average
about 4344 people lost their lives and about 30 million people were affected by disasters every year. Considering these
geo-climatic conditions disaster management occupies an important place in the country’s policy framework. The
following Ministries have been designated Nodal Ministry for various categories of disasters as shown in the table
below:—

18.0     While making a presentation on the subject, the Home Secretary informed the Committee about the major
natural disasters occurring in the country from 1990-2005, which included four major cyclones, five earthquakes of 6
plus magnitude on the Richter Scale, Tsunami on 26 December 2004, Avalanche in February 2005 and severe floods
and droughts every year or alternate years.
18.1     He further informed the Committee that even though the primary responsibility for undertaking rescue, relief and
rehabilitation measures in the event of a disaster is that of the concerned State/UT Government, the Central Government
supplements the efforts of the State Governments by providing financial and logistic support in case of major calamities.
The existing system for disaster management in some of the major countries like USA, Australia and Japan also have
the similar pattern where federal Governments are responsible for providing financial/legislative assistance to State
units.
19.0     Despite the vulnerability, till a few years back there was neither any proper policy framework nor any
institutional mechanism in the country to deal with such type of calamities. The approach to the disaster management
hitherto was primarily relief-centric. Very little attention was paid to mitigation, preparedness and measures to reduce
the impact of disasters. It was only after the major calamities such as the super cyclone in Orissa in October, 1999 and
the Bhuj earthquake in Gujarat in January, 2001 that the Government thought of a multi-dimensional, multi-disciplinary
and multi-sectoral approach involving diverse scientific, engineering, social and financial processes. With the
emergence of terrorism as a major threat to internal security during the last two decades, the need of being prepared for
dealing with manmade disasters as well in an effective and professional manner was felt imminent. Since the man-made
disaster poses threat to national security, Ministry of Home Affairs also shares responsibility for its management. The
other Ministries/ Departments/’ Organisations involved in the management of disasters are :—

History of disaster management policy
19.2     The history of the Disaster Management Policy framework can be traced when a High Powered Committee was
constituted on Disaster Management under the Chairmanship of the then Secretary Agriculture, Government of India on
20 August 1999 to review existing arrangements for preparedness and mitigation of natural disasters. The Committee
recommended measures for strengthening the organizational structures and to formulate a comprehensive model plan
for Natural and Manmade Disaster Management at the National, State and District levels.
19.3     In the wake of the Bhuj Earthquake, an All Party National Committee on Disaster Management (NCDM) was
constituted under the Chairmanship of the then Prime Minister on 13 February 2001 to suggest short-term, medium-term
and long-term steps for relief, rehabilitation and reconstruction in the earthquake affected areas of Gujarat; deliberate on
the necessary institutional and legislative measures needed for an effective and long-term strategy to deal with natural
calamities in the future; and to look into the parameters that should define a natural calamity. On the recommendation of
the NCDM, in February 2002, the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 were amended to transfer
the work relating to Disaster Management, except drought, from the Ministry of Agriculture to the Ministry of Home
Affairs which actually took place in June 2002.      
19.4     The recent steps being taken by the Government in disaster management emanate from the approach as outlined
above. This approach has been translated into a National Disaster Management Framework. Keeping this objective in
view, the Government propose to enact a law on disaster management which will provide requisite institutional and
coordinated mechanism and powers for undertaking prevention and mitigation measures for ensuring preparedness and
capacity building to handle disasters. The proposed law will provide coordinated mechanism by constitution of a
National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) at National level; State Disaster Management Authority at State
level; District Disaster Management Authority at district level; and the local authority at local level. The local authority
includes Corporations and Municipalities in urban areas and Zilla Parishads/Gram Panachyats in rural areas to enable
the elected representatives to discharge a key role in disaster management.
19.5     Replying to a query, the Ministry has stated that the new approach lays emphasis on (i) awareness generation,
(ii) formulation of disaster management plans, (iii) standard operating procedures (SOPs) for hazard-specific response,
(iv) strengthening of Control Rooms, (v) training of Disaster Management Teams at all levels, (vi) organizing mock
drills and (vii) establishment of search and rescue teams following a multi-pronged approach.  
19.6     Tsunami disaster, being the first of its type in India, had an element of surprise. While local administration got
into action immediately, State Governments organized rescue and relief assistance in the affected districts quickly.
Mobilization of national resources was also swift in view of the magnitude of the disaster. The flow of information,
which is extremely crucial for decision making from local level to the national level, through various Control Rooms,
was very smooth. In the post-Tsunami phase, mobilization of resources for rescue, relief and rehabilitation was
facilitated by the India Disaster Resource Network, (IDRN) an electronic web based inventory of Disaster Response
Resources.
19.7     The role of the State Government, which is primarily responsible for disaster management in the State, is to
undertake various activities in the areas of prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, relief and rehabilitation. The
Ministry has stated that it: being the nodal Ministry for Disaster Management, supplements the efforts of State/UT
Governments and coordinates Central Government’s action on all the aspects of disaster management in consultation
with the nodal Department for Disaster Management in the State Governments/UT Administrations. At the State level,
the Department of Relief and Rehabilitation, now being renamed as Department of Disaster Management is an
indication of the change in approach of being pro-active rather than reactive. The Ministry of Home Affairs is in regular
touch with the State Departments of Disaster Management and it facilitates action by various
Ministries/Departments/Agencies of the Government of India. Central Government is providing technical and financial
assistance to the State Governments for prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, relief and rehabilitation. This
well-coordinated mechanism between the States and the Central Government ensures that there are no overlaps.

Three tier policy-Mapping, Coordination, Implementation system
21.0     The Committee was informed that at the apex level, there are two Cabinet Committees, one on Natural
Calamities and the other on Security. Major issues relating to natural disasters are placed before the Cabinet Committee
on Natural Calamities for decisions. In case of calamities which impinge on internal security or which may be caused
due to use of nuclear, biological and chemical weapons/materials, the matters are placed before the Cabinet Committee
on Security.
21.1     The Cabinet Committee on Disaster Management (CCDM) is headed by the Prime Minister. The National Crisis
Management Committee (NCMC) is headed by the Cabinet Secretary. While the Crisis Management Group (CMG) is in
the Ministry of Home Affairs, which is the nodal Ministry for response and relief in the event of natural and manmade
disasters, the Central Relief Commissioner in the Ministry of Home Affairs is the Chairperson of CMG. All the three
levels are part of the disaster management mechanism and are activated depending upon the severity of the disaster and
the national efforts required for providing necessary support to the States. There is a regular and coordinated interaction,
in this institutional mechanism, during a period of crisis.
Funding pattern for disaster management
22.0     Replying to a query of a Member, the Home Secretary mentioned about the funding mechanism for relief
through Calamity Relief Fund (CRF) which is based on the recommendation of Eleventh Finance Commission for 2000-
2005 and Twelfth Finance Commission for 2005-2010 which is available to States for immediate relief and
rehabilitation. But if the disaster is of a very high magnitude then relief is provided from National Calamity
Contingency Fund (NCCF) on the recommendation of the Inter Ministerial Group headed by Agriculture Minister.

share in the CRF.

an adversary.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
 
REHABILITATION OF J&K MIGRANTS AND SECURITY RELATED EXPENDITURE (SRE)
            The Committee expresses its deep concern over the living conditions in rehabilitation camps of Kashmiri
migrants in Jammu and Delhi and Government’s dilemma and lack of clear vision on the future of these migrants. The
Committee is of the considered view that the migrants should be sent back to the Valley only when adequate security of
their lives and properties is ensured. Keeping in view the living conditions in the camps, the Committee feels that
Government instead of projecting a saving Rs. 174 crore under Relief and Rehabilitation, should have utilised the full
amount allocated under the said head for improving the living condition in these camps. The Committee is also of the
view that instead of conducting studies on migrants some concrete steps to improve their living conditions should be
taken urgently.(Para 8.4)
POLICE STRENGTHENING ON INDO-NEPAL BORDER
            The Committee feels that the scheme of Modernisation of Police Forces is taking proper care for strengthening
infrastructure and intelligence network in the States bordering Nepal. However, in light of the recent spurt in Maoist
violence in Nepal and its percolation to bordering States affected by Naxalism, the Ministry should take special
measures to strengthen Policing on the Indo-Nepal Border.
                        (Para 9.3.0)
            The Committee also recommends for empowering and strengthening of SSB, the border guarding force on the
Indo-Nepal border so as to curb not only the ISI activities through this route but also the Maoist groups in assisting the
MCC. It also feels that the Ministry should explore the possibility of amending the Indo-Nepal Treaty so as to make it
compulsory for everybody crossing the border to have his identity proof.           (Para 9.3.1)
MULTI-PURPOSE NATIONAL IDENTITY CARD SCHEME
            The Committee is of the view that the shortfall of expenditure on such an important project having far-reaching
ramifications for the maintenance of internal security of the country and useful in many other areas is unacceptable and
that the Ministry should take appropriate action to complete the pilot project at the earliest so that the same may be
implemented at the national level.    (Para 10.3)

            The Committee has taken note of the information furnished by the Ministry with regard to the implementation of

people of North East and serves as a viable tool in the hands of undesirable elements to carry on a negative campaign
against the Government.            (Para 15.4)

            The Committee expresses its serious concern that under the present set up it may not be feasible for the Union

such circumstances insufficient budgetary provision may create serious financial crunch for the UTs. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that keeping in view the financial constraints of the Union Territories, the Ministry should
urgently set up enabling institutional mechanism in the Union Territories for facilitating borrowing from open
market.        (Para 16.2)
NATIONAL POLICY ON DISASTER MANAGEMENT
            The Committee, while appreciating the recent efforts of the Government in putting in place a disaster
management mechanism at the national level, notes that a lot of exercise has already been done at the governmental
level during the last three years starting from the constitution of the High Powered Committee to the setting up of a
Disaster Management Authority. As mentioned by the Ministry, the National Policy on Disaster Management has to
pass through several stages before coming to the Cabinet for final approval. The Committee feels that the Ministry
should speed up the processes leading to finalisation of the National Policy. Keeping in view the geographical
vulnerability and recurrent natural disasters in India, the Committee urges the Ministry to lay the Policy in Parliament at
the earliest so that the Disaster Management Authority could immediately implement the same.           (Para 20.1)
