of the Commirtee relating to the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Bill, 1997. • 
2. ln pursuance of the rules relating to the Department -related Parliamentary Standing 
Comminees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha, referred** lhe Marriage Laws (Amendment) Bill, 
1997, as introdu ced in the Rajya Sabha on the 13th August, 19'!7 and pending therein , to the 
Comminee for examination and repon. 
3. While considering the bill, the Committee took note of the following documents/ 
information placed before it: 
(i) Background note on the Bill; and 
(ii) Note on the curative aspects of epilepsy furnished by the Ministry of Health & 
Family Welfare. 
4. The Ministry of Law, Justice .ami Company Affairs (Legislative Department) 
informed the CommiHee that the Law Commission of India in its 59th Report on "Hindu 
Marriage Act, 1955, and the Special Marriage Act, 1954," had recommended for amendment 
of Section 5 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, and Section S(b) of the Special Marriage Act, 
1954, so as to define the words "idiot and lunat ic" which inter-alia also suggested 
incorporating there in based on the English Law that at the time of marriage the party should 
not also be subject to recurrent attacks of insanity or epilepsy. However, the Law 
Commission in its Report had not given any reason for inclusion of the word 'epilepsy' in 
the relevant provision along with 'insanity'. They had recommended that a provision may be 
included on the lines of the English Law. As English Law provided for epilepsy a.lso, the 
same had been incorporated in the relevant provisions of our laws as well. The Acts were 
accordingly amended vide the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 1976, which made the 
position with regard to the difficulties of interpretation for reckoning different degrees of 
lunacy, clear. 
4.1 Ever since the amendment had been made in 1976, the Government had been 
receiving representations from Neuro -surgeons, Members of Parliament and other prominent 
persons of the society and also from Women's Organisations, against the said amendment 
stating that the disease 'epilepsy' should not onder any circumstances, be equated with 
"insanity" and it would do great injustice to several patients suffering from epilepsy since the 
disease was completely controllable. It was, therefore , urged upon the Government to 
undertake necessary legislation to remove references to epilepsy from the matrimonial laws 
on the ground that epilepsy had become fully controllable by proper medical care and 
atte ntion. The National Commission for Women and the Women's Organisatio ns have also 
recommended for undertaking necessary legislation in this regard. In view thereof the 
Government considered it appropriate to undertake the necessary legislation to remove 
references to 'epilepsy' in the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, and the Special Marriage Act, 
1954, as a ground for obtaining a decree for nullity of marriage.

of Law, Justice and Company Affairs (Legislative Department) thereon in its meeting held

that in a majority of cases (about 80 per cent) epilepsy could be well controlled with modern 
anti-epileptic drugs and as such pleaded that the reference to 'epilepsy' should be removed 
from the matrimonial laws of the country. ln this context it was pointed out by the medical 
experts that the words 'epilepsy is completely curable' were wrongly used tn the Statement 
of Objects and Reasons of the Bill and should be deleted therefrom. It was clarified that 
even though a lot of scicmiric information was available on a particular dtsease, yet the 
c:xpression 'complc1ely cur!lble' was not used. What the medical fraternity ~aid was that the 
majority of patients could be fairly well controlled to the extent of 80 per cent of the cases. 
It was, therefore , suggested that the words objected to may be substituted by the words 
'fairly well controllable in a majority of cases who can lead a normal life'. 
5.2 The Committee agrees in principle with the point raised by the Doctors with regard 
to use of expression 'fully curable' in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill. 
However, as mentioned in the Practice and Procedure of Parliament by Kaul and Shakdher 
(4th Edition, 1991, page 485), 'the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill can be 
amended by the Member·in·charge of the Bill with the permission of the Presiding Officer at 
the tinle of introduction of the nm. Any lacuna in the Statement of Objects and Reasons 
must be pointed out at tbe time of introduction of the Bill; a point ntised in this regard at 
the consideration stage of the Bill is not valid'. 
5.3 ln view of the position stated above the Comminee feels that the best course 
available is that the Minister-m-charge of the J3ill may clarify the posttion m this regard in 
the House while moving the motion for consideration of the Bill. 
6. With regard to the recommendation of the Law Commission of India as contained in 
their 59th Report on 'The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 and the Special Marriage Act, 1954', 
for incorporating therein based on the English Luw that at the time of marriage the party 
should not be subject to recurrent attacks of im,anity or epilepsy, and the subsequent 
:tmcndments carried out vide the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 1976, the <;ommittee is 
constrained to observe that the Law Commission and the Government ought to have gone 
into the rationale for inclusion of the word 'epilepsy' in the relevant provisions of the Law 
along with insanity. The Law Commission having not given any reason in support of their 
recommendation and the Government having accepted the same, justice was not done to the 
epileptic persons.

their fifty-ninth Report on "Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 and Special Marriage Act,

Marr iage Act, 1954 were amended vide the M"arriage Laws (Amendment) /\ct. 1971i 
so as to remove the doubts about the degrees of lunacy. Ever since the aforesaid 
amendment came into force, the Central Government have been receiving 
representations from eminent medical experts, jurists <md Members of Parliament for 
omission of reference to "epilepsy" conta ined in the aforesaid Acts of 1955 and 1954 
on the ground that medical experts have found that epilepsy is fully curable and, 
therefore, equating "epilepsy" to "insanity'' would do great IDJUSrice to pat1ents 
suffering from epilepsy in matrimonial matters. The grounds for annulling a marriage 
by a decree of nullity as contained in section 12 of the Hindu Marr iage Act, 1955 
refers to section S(ii) thereof. Similarly, the grounds and declaring a marriage as null 
and void by a decree of nullity as contained m section 24 of the Special Marnage 
Act, 1954 refers to ;ection 4(b) thereof. Hence, the removal of references to 
·:epilepsy" in section 5(ii) of the H indu Marriage Act , 1955 and section 4(b)(iii) of 
the Speciol Marriage Act, 1954 has the effect of non-availability of the ground of 
epilepsy for getting a decree of nullity of the marriage as well. ln order to mitigate 
the hardship> of the persons suffering from epilepsy on account of the aforesaid 
provisions, necessary amendments tO remove references to "epilepsy" in the 1955 and 
J 954 Acts are proposed.
