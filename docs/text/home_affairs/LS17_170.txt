CHAPTER-I
Overview
1.1 INTRODUCTION
1.1.1 According to the Outcome Budget 2013-14, Ministry of Development of North Eastern
Region was formed as a ‘Department’ in September 2001 under the Ministry of Home Affairs and
subsequently upgraded to status of a full-fledged Ministry in May, 2004. The Ministry implements
the following work mandated in the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961:–
• Matters relating to the planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes
and projects of North Eastern Region (NEC) including those in the sectors of Power,
Irrigation, Roads and Communications.
• Hill Area Development Programme and Border Areas Development Programme (BADP)
in North Eastern Region.
• Non-lapsable Fund for the North Eastern Region.
• North Eastern Council
• North Eastern Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi)
• North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited (NERAMAC).
• North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation (NEHHDC)
• Road works financed in whole or in part by the Central Government in the North
Eastern Region.
• Planning of Road and Inland Waterways Transport in the North Eastern Region.
1.1.2 Goals of the Ministry
The Outcome Budget 2013-14 mentions the following goals of the Ministry of Development
of North Eastern Region:–
(i) Accelerating the pace of socio-economic development in the North Eastern Region.
(ii) Synergizing the efforts of Central/State Governments/Other Stakeholders for balanced
development of the Region.
(iii) Integrated and holistic planning for the Region, taking into account the heightened
consciousness of the people of the Region, their aspirations, needs and sensitivities.
(iv) Evolving a common approach for all the agencies working for the development of the
Region.
(v) Formulating appropriate policies for the rapid development of the Region.
1.1.3 The North Eastern Council (NEC) is an organization set up under the Ministry which
functions as a Regional Planning Body for the region. The NEC also implements various Plan

2
Schemes in the region. It was constituted under the NEC Act, 1971 for securing the balanced
development of the North East. The Minister in charge of the Ministry of Development of North
Eastern Region is the ex-officio Chairman of NEC. Its members are the Governors and the Chief
Ministers of the eight Member States of the region and other three Members nominated by the
President of India.
1.2 PLAN SCHEMES:
1.2.1 Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) would be implementing
following Plan Schemes during the year in 2013-14:
(a) Central Plan Schemes :
(i) Advertising and Publicity
(ii) T echnical Assistance and Capacity Building
(iii) Assistance to North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi)
(iv) Asian Development Bank assisted North Eastern States Road Investment Programme
(Externally Aided Project)
(v) North East Road Project Management Unit
(vi) North Eastern Region Livelihood Project (NERLP) with World Bank assistance
(Externally Aided Project)
(vii) Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR)-Central
(b) Additional Central Assistance to State Plans:
(i) Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR);
(ii) Plan Schemes of the North Eastern Council (NEC); and
(iii) In frastructure development projects under the special package for Bodoland Territorial
Council (BTC)
(c) Schemes for creation and upgradation of infrastructure facilities, specially in Arunachal
Pradesh and other border areas in North Eastern Region, under Social and Infrastructure
Development Fund (SIDF).
1.3 NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES FOR NORTH EASTERN REGION
(NLCPR)
1.3.1 All the States in the NER are Special Category States, whose development plans are
centrally financed on the basis of 90% Grant and 10% Loan. Further, the Special Category States
are allowed to use up to 20% of the central assistance for Non-plan expenditure. In October 1996,
the then Prime Minister announced “New Initiatives for North Eastern Region” and stipulated that
at least 10% of the Budget(s) of the Central Ministries/Departments will be earmarked for the
development of North Eastern States. A preliminary exercise undertaken by the Planning Commission
revealed that the expenditure on the North East by some Union Ministries during 1997-98 fell short
of the stipulated 10% of the Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for that year. Planning Commission
thereafter set up a Central Pool of Resources for the North East out of the unspent amount of
stipulated 10% of GBS to support infrastructure development projects in the North East. Since
1998-99, all non-exempted Ministries/Departments of Government of India are required to earmark
at least 10% of their GBS for the development of North Eastern States for creation of both
economic and social infrastructure such as power, roads and bridges, education, health, water
supply and sanitation, etc. in the region.

3
1.3.2 The unutilized portion of the 10% Plan allocations of the concerned Ministries/Departments
is credited at the end of each financial year in a reserve fund known as the Non-Lapsable Central
Pool of Resources (NLCPR). The funds from this Pool are used for financing new infrastructure
projects/schemes in the region to ensure speedy development of infrastructure in the North Eastern
Region by increasing the flow of budgetary financing for such projects/schemes. Both physical and
social infrastructure sectors are considered for providing support under the Central Pool and
projects in physical infrastructure sector receive priority. For this purpose, projects are sanctioned
for State as well as Central sectors. However, the funds available under the Central Pool are not
meant to supplement the normal Plan programmes either of the State Governments or Union
Ministries/Departments/Agencies.
1.4 NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES FOR NORTH EASTERN REGION
– CENTRAL (NLCPR-CENTRAL)
1.4.1 The Union Cabinet, on 11th August 2011, approved for introduction, of a new Central
Sector Plan Scheme titled “Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources–Central (NLCPR-Central)”.
This plan scheme has been formulated with the objective of enhancing effective and optimal
utilization of funds in the NLCPR Pool towards development of the North Eastern States. The
scheme is not meant to supplement the existing schemes, but to fill critical gaps in the existing
developmental programmes or schemes. As a Central sector plan scheme, projects of Union
Ministries/Departments pertaining to subjects listed in the Union and Concurrent Lists of
constitution of India, can be proposed for funding under the scheme. Expenditure incurred under
the NLCPR-Central scheme will be debited to the NLCPR Pool. The guidelines for the scheme have
been circulated to concerned Union Ministries/Departments, inviting them to propose appropriate
projects. The Governments of North Eastern States and North Eastern Council have also been kept
informed about the procedure by which they may propose projects under the scheme.
1.4.2 The Ministry also supervises work of Public Sector Enterprises, North Eastern Handicrafts
and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd. (NEHHDC) and North Eastern Regional Agricultural
Marketing Corporation Ltd. (NERAMAC). The North Eastern Development Finance Corporation
Ltd. (NEDFi), is also anchored in the Ministry. The Government provides Interest-free loan to the
North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi), a financial institution which, inter
alia provides credit and other facilities to industrial enterprises, infrastructure projects, agro-
horticulture units etc. in the region for promotion, expansion and modernisation.

2.3.6 The Committee expresses its disappointment over the dismal achievement rate of
certain Plan schemes of the Ministry viz. capacity building and technical assistance NERLP,
NESRP and others where financial performance is below 50%. This situation calls for drastic
measure to improve the efficiency levels in order to justify proposed estimates which are,
more often than not, cut down because of lack of absorption capacity. The Committee would
like to highlight that the under utilization below 50% of allocated budget, would have
affected the achievement of physical targets and such situation would further negate the
fulfillment of projected demand for higher allocation in forthcoming financial year. The
Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry of DoNER must prevail upon
line departments/Ministries/Agencies to enhance their capacity to utilize allocated amount
scrupulously on the undertaken plans/projects/programs for their timely completion and
make a strong case with all supportive factors for higher allocation from the next financial
year onwards.

2.4 NORTH-EASTERN COUNCIL
2.4.1 The North Eastern Council (NEC) was set up under the NEC Act 1971, to foster balanced
and integrated economic development of seven constituent member States. By the amendment of
NEC Act in 2002, Sikkim has been added as its eighth member. It functions as a regional planning
body for its constituent States. It is also mandated to maintain security and public order. The
Council plays an advisory role in areas of interest that are common to all its constituent States such
as economic and social planning, transport, communications, power and flood control. Besides
ensuring effective coordination, it is also mandated to put forward proposals for developmental
schemes and projects submitted by the States to the Centre.

material as well as limited capacity of contractors and executing agencies. The Committee
feels that the Government should come forward to address those deficiencies affecting
execution of the projects in NER by augmenting supply of raw material and assistance to
the executing agencies by providing soft loans to them.

2.8 NORTH EASTERN DEVELOPMENT FINANCE CORPORATION LIMITED (NEDFi)
2.8.1 NEDFi is the premier development finance institution of the NER which functions under the
administrative control of the Ministry of DONER. With the passage of the time, NEDFi has
positioned itself as a lending institution besides providing other support services to the new
entrepreneurs of the Region, to acquire competitive edge. The Corporation has taken several skill
development initiatives for regional development in addition to its core business of providing term
finance to industry and infrastructure. The Corporation has been able to extend financial assistance
to projects spread all over the eight North Eastern States in consonance with the market demand,
in a wide spectrum of sectors, e.g., power, hospitality sector, infrastructure, etc.
2.8.2 On enquiring about the projected demand of the Ministry for this Head in Budget 2013-14,
the Ministry replied that  NEDFi has forwarded its Business Plan for the 12th Plan Period to the
Ministry of DoNER. The fund requirement during the 12th Plan Period (2012-17), to keep up with
the projected sanctions of Rs. 6410 crore and disbursement of Rs. 4690 crore, is Rs. 5184 crore.
Against this, the Corporation requested the Ministry of DoNER for interest free loan of Rs. 1000 crore
for the period 2012-17. However, during the FY 2012-13, the Corporation received Rs. 60 crore
from the Ministry of DoNER as interest free loan. Further, the projected demand of the Ministry
of DoNER for this Head in Budget 2013-14 is Rs. 60 crore.
2.8.3 W ith the implementation of North Eastern Industrial and Investment Promotion Policy
(NEIIPP), 2007, there is a buoyancy in the market and new projects in infrastructure sectors,
power, cement, steel, oil and gas, tea, healthcare, tourism and hospitality, higher education and
technical institutes, etc. are coming up with large capital requirements. Being in the fore-front of
financing in the Region, the Corporation has been receiving ever increasing demand for financial
assistance for new projects. Although NEDFi has an ambitious growth plan to expand its business
by at least 25-30 per cent on year-on-year basis, the same will be constrained by its limited
resources. The revised disbursement target of NEDFi for the FY 2013-14 is Rs.450 crore. The
Corporation is projecting internal generation of around Rs. 300 crore, market borrowing of Rs. 90
Crore and soft loan of Rs. 60 Crore from the Ministry of DoNER.
2.8.4 The Ministry also informed that in the financial year 2011-12, NEDFi has sanctioned an
amount of Rs.432.23 crore covering 253 projects and during financial year 2012-13, the sanctioned
amount is expected to touch Rs.450 crore spread over all the eight States of North-East India. The
total disbursement for the year 2011-12 was Rs.334.56 Crore whereas during FY 2012-13 as on
date, the amount disbursed is Rs.255.00 crore which is expected to touch Rs.350 crore at the end
of the financial year.
2.8.5 During the last two years, under its microfinance scheme, NEDFi has sanctioned and
disbursed an amount of Rs.127.29 crore. and Rs.118.94 crore respectively. The cumulative
sanction and disbursement are Rs.229 crore and Rs.214 crore, respectively. NEDFi has identified
the Micro and Small Enterprises (MSE) Sector, especially to help the first generation
entrepreneurs, as its priority sector for assistance. The Corporation has designed several need
based schemes (specific to certain sectors and certain categories) like NEDFi Opportunity
Scheme for Small Enterprise (NOSSE), North East Entrepreneurs Development Scheme
(NEEDS), Women Enterprise Development Scheme (WEDS), Scheme for Northeast Handloom
and Handicrafts (SNEHH), Jute Enterprise Development Scheme (JEDS), Initiative for
Development of Entrepreneurs in Agriculture (IDEA), etc. The Corporation is also giving top
priority to micro finance to bring micro-credit to the grass root level in the rural areas as well
as the unserved and underserved hill areas. In terms of Industries, several industries like
infrastructure, power, hospitality, healthcare, food processing, higher education, etc. has been
identified as the priority sectors for assistance.

up business ventures, the corporation has developed a scheme, Women Enterprise Development
Scheme (WEDS). Under the scheme, assistance up to 75% of the project cost is funded at the
subsidized interest rate of 8 per cent p.a. for new/expansion projects with a maximum project cost
of Rs. 15 lakh for income generating activities like handloom and handicrafts, dairy farming, poultry,
grocery shops, small restaurants and tea shops, etc. Under this scheme, so far NEDFi has assisted
991 women entrepreneurs all over North-Eastern Region, which is more than one-third of the total
project assisted by the Corporation so far. The cumulative amount sanctioned and disbursed under

3.1 WORKING/MANDATE OF THE MINISTRY OF DoNER
3.1.1 The Committee has been informed that the primary role of the Ministry of DoNER is limited
to advocacy to stimulate planning and facilitate and accelerate projects/scheme implementation in
NER, but the main problem faced by the Ministry is its weaknesses inherent in any advocacy and
coordination role. The Committee has also been informed that it faces problems of inadequate
technical support, inadequate overall support, inadequate space and lack of field offices.
3.1.2 The Committee fails to understand that a Ministry, which was carved out to focus
and expedite development in the North east region, after more than a decade of its
existence has realized that it lacks sufficient power and necessary infrastructure to carry
out its mandate, and is completely dependent on various Line Ministries/Departments for
execution of projects. The Committee takes a serious view of the situation and feels that
a holistic view at the functioning and powers of the Ministry should be taken at this stage.
The Committee is not at all impressed with the functioning of the Ministry and gets an
impression that the line ministries are not supporting or paying heed to the suggestions/
requests of the ministry. The Committee intends to discuss this issue separately at a later

indicating about the bottlenecks and shortcomings and the desired solutions. The Committee
also proposes to discuss the matter with the Cabinet Secretary and Secretaries of all the

3.2 INFRASTRUCTURAL PROJECTS
3.2.1 The Committee was given to understand that cost of construction in NER projects is
invariably high in comparison to similar projects in rest of the country and delays in execution of
infrastructural projects in NER are also common. Limitations and challenges for implementation of
infrastructure projects in NER are many, viz.  limited working season, difficulty in getting
environment and forest clearances, especially in view of Forest Rights Act, 2006, non-availability
of experienced contractors, deficiency in capacities of Government executing agencies, law &
order problems, non-availability of building materials in the Region etc.
3.2.2 The Ministry has informed that it has taken several initiatives with Ministries/Departments
concerned like holding regular coordination meetings on major infrastructure projects, capacity
building of State Governments’ executing agencies, follow up action with MoEF for forest
clearances etc. The issue of land acquisition for infrastructure projects is challenging, especially in
view of provisions of Forest Rights Act, 2006. All environment and forest clearances for these
projects require ‘No Objection’ from concerned authorities and evidence of consent of the Gram
Panchayats.

33
3.2.3 The Ministry has informed that it takes up the review with the Project Implementation
Authorities, State Governments, Central Ministries/Departments of all NER infrastructure projects.
Regular coordination meetings of stakeholders are held to facilitate identification of problem areas
and steps are taken to resolve them. NE States mostly do not have their digitized land records and
in many States, the cadastral survey of their land has not been done. Under such situation, the
village councils consent is sought as per provisions of Forest Rights Act, 2006. As these are
statutory provisions applicable for almost all projects, delays become unavoidable. Hence, Ministry
of DoNER is developing standard procedures to be followed in such cases and strengthening them.
3.2.4 The Committee is extremely concerned about abnormally long delays taking place in
execution of infrastructure projects in the Region. The Committee observes that the
Ministry comes up with similar alibis every time whenever they are asked about the delays.
The Committee, while acknowledging the fact that the region suffers from peculiar
problems, feels that a way out has to be found out, because neither the climate of the
region is going to change nor the region can be allowed to lag behind on the pretext of
adverse circumstances. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should look for
various technological interventions such as pre-fabricated structures, other innovative
methods and digitization of land records among others to speed up the process of project
execution.
3.3 POTENTIAL OF HYDRO-ELECTRIC (HE) PRODUCTION IN NE REGION
3.3.1 The Committee is aware of the fact that NE Region has enormous hydro-electricity
generation potential. Based on the studies for re-assessment of hydro-electric potential of the
country by Central Electricity Authority in 1987, the identified potential of 58356 MW in NE Region
constitutes about 40.1% of the total identified hydro power potential of 145320 MW in the country.
The Ministry has informed that a capacity of 1200 MW has so far been harnessed in NE region
(2.06% of the identified capacity) while 6 HE projects with aggregate capacity of 2852 MW
(4.89%) are under construction in NER.
3.3.2 The Committee was also informed that a number of policy measures have been taken by
the Government in the past to give the thrust to the development of the hydro electric potential
in the region. These include:
(a) Policy Liberalisation for encouraging Private Sector Participation
(b) Policy on Hydro Power Development, 2008
(c) National Water Policy, 2002
(d) Electricity Act, 2003
(e) National Electricity Policy  announced by Government in Feb.’2005
(f) National Policy on Rehabilitation and Resettlement, 2007 in order to protect the
interests of affected persons
3.3.3 The Committee was apprised by the Ministry that to expedite development of balance hydro
electric projects in the country, a large number of projects have been allotted/identified by various
States for implementation in Private, Central and State Sector during Twelfth Plan period and
beyond. This included 138 projects (above 25 MW capacity) aggregating to 56063 MW of capacity
for implementation in NE Region. Out of these, 98 schemes with aggregate capacity of 34434.5 MW
have been allotted to private sector while 11 with an aggregate capacity of 8247 MW have been
allotted to CPSUs. The remaining schemes are either to be implemented in State Sector or are yet
to be allotted.

3.8 INLAND WATER CONNECTIVITY
3.8.1 Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAT) is improving the Inland Waterways to provide
alternate connectivity within the NER and between the NER with the rest of the country. The
Ministry has provided details of progress in development of inland waterways in NE-Region as
given below:
A. National Waterway-2.
To develop Inland Water transport in North East, River Brahmaputra from Bangladesh
Border (near Dhubri) to Sadiya (891 km.) was declared as National Waterway No.2 in 1988.
Inland Waterways Authority of India, a statutory Body of Ministry of Shipping, is carrying
out various developmental works like navigable fairway, day and night navigation aids and
terminals etc. since then as briefed below:
(i) Fairway development works/ River conservancy works:
A navigable fairway of 45 meter width and 2.5 meter Least Available Depth (LAD) from
Bangladesh Border (Dhubri) to Neamati (630 km.), 2.0 meter from Neamati to Dibrugarh
(139 km.) and with 1.5 meter from Dibrugarh to Oriumghat (92 km.) is being developed
and maintained by undertaking annual river conservancy works like dredging. Till 2009-10,
IWAI was developing the navigational channel with 2.0 meter LAD in the entire waterway
which has been increased to 2.5 meter LAD in about 630 km. stretch now providing
increased viability for transportation by bigger cargo vessels. The dredging is carried out
with three departmental dredgers. Two more dredgers are planned to be deployed on this
waterway.
(ii) T erminals:
(a) Pandu IWT terminal with multi modal transit system : Pandu is the most important
location on NW-2 for development of river port. An amount of Rs. 95 crore has been
invested for construction of a low level jetty, a high level jetty (nearing completion) and
Railway BG siding. The port is being developed as a multi-modal modern Inland Water
Transport Terminal equipped with cargo handling facilities, transit sheds, appropriate open
space facilities/hard stands and rail and road connectivity. An alternate approach road for
movement of Heavy/Container Traffic connecting NH-31 at a cost of Rs. 11 crore has also
been approved and the work has been awarded to State PWD (NH Division). However,
construction could not be commenced as the land through which the alignment passes, is
yet to be handed over by NF Railway.
(b) Roll on-Roll off terminals: At Dhubri and Hatsingimari (opposite bank of Brahmaputra
at Dhubri), permanent terminals with Roll on-Roll off (Ro-Ro) facilities are proposed to be
established for which projects have been approved at an estimated cost of Rs. 31.80 crore
and Rs. 35.57 crore respectively by IWAI based on the detailed project reports prepared
through M/s L&T- ECC Chennai. Land has been acquired at both the locations and work
for construction of these terminals has been awarded to CPWD on 18.01.2013. Once these

44
terminals are constructed, there will be a direct link between Assam and Meghalaya across
the Brahmaputra through IWT avoiding circuitous route through Jogighopa. The work at
Dhubri will be commenced shortly. At Hatsingimari, severe erosion has been noticed and
work will commence after implementation of anti-erosion works by Brahmaputra Board.
B. Central Sector Plan Scheme for development of IWT infrastructure by the States of
NER:
Under a new Central Sector Scheme for NER for IWT sector (made operational from
August 2008), 100% grant is being provided by the Ministry of Shipping for development of Inland
Water transport projects in North Eastern States of the Country. Presently, following two projects
are under implementation under this scheme;
1. Mizoram: Development of Inland Water Transport in Tlawng River at a cost of
Rs.527.93 lakhs. (Completed recently)
2. Assam: Construction of 17M long floating terminals at 20 ghat on the river
Brahmaputra at a cost of Rs. 450.08 lakhs.
3.8.2 The Ministry has informed that keeping in view the poor response of NE States for
development of IWT projects, IWAI took the initiative for identification of IWT projects having
IWT potential in NE States and conducted a study through M/s RITES which identified 22 rivers
having navigation potential in the NE States. IWAI held a meeting on 25-11-2011 with the

3.8.3 The Committee understands the importance of Inland Waterways as an alternative

3.10 INDUSTRIAL SECTOR
3.10.1 The Committee believes that the main strength of the North Eastern Region for industrial
development emanates from its natural resource base. The huge hydro-electric potential has been
assessed. Minerals like oil, gas and limestone have long been used to feed various industries and
there is scope for further use of such resources for industrial use. Forest and bio-diversity can
be the resource base for developing industries in the region in future. Besides, the tea industry
continues to be a major strand in the region’s industrial set up. Processing of rubber and high value
horticulture crops can also emerge as important industrial activity once the agriculture sector of
the region gets commercialised and diversified.
3.10.2 The Committee further understands that traditional handicrafts skills of the people of the
region is yet another resource with potential for supporting considerable industrial employment once
the highly acclaimed tourism potential of the region is harnessed. However, in spite of being
endowed with vast natural resources in terms of forests, biological diversity, hydro-electricity, the
region has remained largely underdeveloped. A key constraint to the growth has been poor
infrastructure and limited connectivity, both within the region as well as with the rest of the
country.
3.10.3 The Committee has been apprised of the SWOT analysis of the industrial situation in NER
as under:
Strength
• Natural Resources.
• High level of literacy.

47
• Comparatively low cost of real estate.
• Availability of Inland water transportation.
3.10.4 Against the advantage of natural resource base, the major hindrances for industrial
development of the region appear to be:
Weaknesses
• Low productivity.
• Lack of skilled manpower.
• Lack of entrepreneurship.
• Low level of savings and investments.
• High freight cost.
• Isolation and inadequate linkage with the national economy.
• Dependence on other parts of the country for day to day consumption goods.
• Inadequate marketing initiatives.
Opportunities
• Setting up of processing units and industries based on locally available resources.
• Setting up of downstream units for oil industries.
• Cross-border trade with neighbouring countries.
• Possibilities of plantation and processing of aromatic/medicinal plants.
• Developing SMEs for producing items currently procured from other parts of the
country.
• Abundant possibilities for taking up tourism activities in the region.
• Government initiatives for development of NER.
Threat
• Law and order situation.
• Natural Calamities.
3.10.5 The Committee takes into account the SWOT analysis of  the industrial situation
in NER and views that the Ministry of DoNER should make a detailed analysis of the same
to take up the issue of the industrial development in NER with the concerned line
Ministries/Departments. The Committee recommends that the following prospects of
industrial development in NER must be harnessed for ensuring balanced regional
development:–
• The crude oil production and its refining as well as tea plantation and bamboo/
cane industries are the few resource-based industries in the region.
• Exploitation of the large hydro potential in the NER could be used for exporting
to the power deficit northern and western regions of the country.

48
• The Region’s comparative advantages in producing fruits, vegetables and other
horticulture products like spices can be tapped by setting up small-scale
processing units for the local market which would also boost rural employment.
• The North-East is an ideal destination for adventure, leisure and eco-tourism.
The mineral-rich north-eastern States can also explore and promote geo-tourism.
• The education sector in the North-East has emerged as a major destination for
private firms. To explore the opportunities available, several private players are
now targeting the region to set up educational institutions which would serve the
cross-country market and cater to the needs of neighbouring countries.
• The North-Eastern Region is an ideal destination for Information Technology,
specifically for ITES sector. IT enabled service coupled with BPO business are
other areas which have enormous scope to flourish in the North–Eastern Region
because it has an advantage in its English speaking population and proficiency/
efficiency acknowledged by captains of IT industries outside the NER. This
should be explored to the full potential.
• Prospects for investors from outside the Region for setting up of industrial units
taking advantage of the incentives provided under NEIIPP–2007 by the Ministry
of Commerce and Industry.
• Since North-East is located at a crossroads between three major economies –
East Asia, South Asia and Southeast Asia, with the implementation of the ‘Look
East policy’ and opening up of trade routes, processing industries manufacturing
quality products, can be offered in international markets at competitive prices.
3.10.6 The Committee believes that the spill-over benefits of industrial development for the
region would be the development of infrastructure such as roads, communications, and
electricity supply to remote hilly areas, resulting in better quality of life. Natural gas based
industries are also feasible. The Committee also believes that NER could be an excellent
centre for horticulture, spices, and hub for educational and IT sector.
3.10.7 Since  the region is expected to be declared as SEZ, there is every reason for the
Committee  to conclude that once the region is declared a SEZ by the Government of India,
there  would automatically come about a wide range of infrastructural development and a
rapid change in the industrial sector, opening scope for a rapid change in the socio-economic
scenario in the NE States. The Committee believes that industrial development can be made
in the NE States at par with the development in other regions of the country if all the steps
are taken in right ernest. The Committee recommends that the Government should make
concerted efforts in this direction.

plagued by myriad problems which inter-alia  include high level of sickness in this sector,
inadequate infrastructure, poor connectivity and inaccessibility due to difficult terrain, inadequate
power supply, law and order, natural calamities – Floods, landslides, etc., lack of entrepreneurship,
low level of public sector investment, low productivity, lack of skilled manpower,  low level of
savings and investments, high freight cost, isolation and inadequate linkage with the national
economy, dependence on other parts of the country for day to day consumption goods and
Inadequate marketing initiatives.

Ministry of Finance should also understand this problem and pave the way for providing
sufficient funds. (Para 2.4.4)
The Committee, while noting that the success of NEC efforts  depends heavily on the

structure for better performance so that performing States could be motivated to do better
and non-performing States may be made to feel that they may loose on account of non-
performance. (Para 2.4.8)
The Committee desires that the Council must also evolve mechanisms to complete
the projects on time despite very short working season in the North-East Region as one
cannot alter the geographical cycles. The Committee recommends that NEC may conduct a

conditions suffering from similar handicaps.  The Committee also notes unavailability of raw
material as well as limited capacity of contractors and executing agencies. The Committee
feels that the Government should come forward to address those deficiencies affecting

POTENTIAL OF HYDRO-ELECTRIC (HE) PRODUCTION IN NE REGION
The Committee is at a loss to understand that despite such elaborate planning and
huge administrative mechanism, the realization of the potential remains abysmally low. The
Committee finds that even though Inter-Ministerial Groups (IMG), Task Force, Advisory
Groups and Special Monitoring Groups (SMG) were set up by the Government at one point
of time or other to sort out the problems afflicting the projects, yet perceptible outcome is
still a distant dream. (Para 3.3.7)
The Committee finds that while the identified potential in NE Region constitutes
about 40.1% of the total identified hydro power potential (145320 MW) in the country, but
only 1200 MW has so far been harnessed in NE region which is 2.06% of the identified
capacity and only 6 HE projects with aggregate capacity of 2852 MW (4.89%) are under
construction in NER. Even if the under construction projects in the region are completed on
time, the potential likely to be realized will only be close to 7%. This leaves a huge gap
between potential capacity and potential realized. In an energy deficit country like ours,
this is totally unacceptable. The Committee feels that the huge bureaucratic mechanism put
in place for the purpose and the policy guidelines adopted by themselves are not enough and

a spirited effort is required to make things happen on the ground. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that a very practical approach with quantifiable progress indicators
should be adopted for progress on ground. (Para 3.3.8)
The Committee feels that the Ministry of DoNER must play pro-active coordinating
role and act in tandem with Ministry of Water Resources, Power, Forest and Environment
and concerned State Governments in NER to harness Hydro power potential optimally.
(Para 3.3.9)
The Committee is extremely disappointed to note that Dimapur Power Project has
been abandoned after spending an amount of Rs. 90 crore. The Committee observes that
money spent on the project and subsequently abandoned, reflects lack of proper planning
and is a criminal wastage of fund. The Committee is of the considered opinion that the
project should have been more carefully planned and executed avoiding huge waste of
precious resources. (Para 3.3.11)
The Committee is astonished to find that on Lower Subansiri Project the estimated
completion cost has gone upto Rs. 11,000 crore and Government is losing Rs. 60 crore  for
every month of delay due to the concerns of the people over the Dam Safety. The

Safety should attempt to win the confidence of the affected people by proper counseling so
that  the project takes off at the earliest. The Committee also hopes that once the project
gets started, the Ministry would ensure that the project is completed without further delay
and cost escalation. (Para 3.3.14)

AIR CONNECTIVITY
Good air connectivity to the North-East, like rest of the country, is a national

strategic importance of the region. The Committee welcomes changes in the Route
Dispersal Guidelines of the Ministry of Civil Aviation that have made the public and private
airlines to fly in the North-East and thus facilitating not only people of the region to other
parts of the country but also people of other parts of the country travelling to NER.

The Committee notes that night landing facilities are proposed to be extended to the

The Committee understands the importance of Inland Waterways as an alternative

land. Waterways are the cheapest mode of transport anywhere in the world. In Western
European countries, about 40 per cent of the cargo movement is by waterways. The world’s
average is about 8 per cent, whereas in India it is less than 1 per cent. The Committee is

on the list of priority. The Committee, therefore, recommends that navigation in NER

INDUSTRIAL SECTOR
The Committee takes into account the SWOT analysis of  the industrial situation in
NER and views that the Ministry of DoNER should make a detailed analysis of the same to
take up the issue of the industrial development in NER with the concerned line Ministries/
Departments. The Committee recommends that the following prospects of industrial
development in NER must be harnessed for ensuring balanced regional development:-
• The crude oil production and its refining as well as tea plantation and bamboo/
cane industries are the few resource-based industries in the region.
• Exploitation of the large hydro potential in the NER could be used for exporting
to the power deficit northern and western regions of the country.
• The Region’s comparative advantages in producing fruits, vegetables and other
horticulture products like spices can be tapped by setting up small-scale
processing units for the local market which would also boost rural employment.

59
• The North-East is an ideal destination for adventure, leisure and eco-tourism. The
mineral-rich North-Eastern States can also explore and promote geo-tourism.
• The education sector in the North-East has emerged as a major destination for
private firms. To explore the opportunities available, several private players are
now targeting the region to set up educational institutions which would serve the
cross-country market and cater to the needs of neighbouring countries.
• The North-Eastern Region is an ideal destination for Information Technology,
specifically for ITES sector. IT enabled service coupled with BPO business are
other areas which have enormous scope to flourish in the North–Eastern Region
because it has an advantage in its English speaking population and proficiency/
efficiency acknowledged by captains of IT industries outside the NER. This
should be explored to the full potential.
• Prospects for investors from outside the Region for setting up of industrial units
taking advantage of the incentives provided under NEIIPP–2007 by the Ministry
of Commerce and Industry.
• Since North-East is located at a crossroads between three major economies –
East Asia, South Asia and Southeast Asia, with the implementation of the ‘Look
East policy’ and opening up of trade routes, processing industries manufacturing
quality products, can be offered in international markets at competitive prices.
(Para 3.10.5)
The Committee believes that the spill-over benefits of industrial development for the
region would be the development of infrastructure such as roads, communications and
electricity supply to remote hilly areas, resulting in better quality of life. Natural gas based
industries are also feasible. The Committee also believes that NER could be an excellent
centre for horticulture, spices, and hub for educational and IT sector. (Para 3.10.6)
Since the region is expected to be declared as SEZ, there is every reason for the
Committee  to conclude that once the region is declared a SEZ by the Government of India,
there  would automatically come about a wide range of infrastructural development and a
rapid change in the industrial sector, opening scope for a rapid change in the socio-economic
scenario in the NE States. The Committee believes that industrial development can be made
in the NE States at par with the development in other regions of the country if all the steps
are taken in right ernest. The Committee recommends that the Government should make
concerted efforts in this direction. (Para 3.10.7)
The Committee is of the firm opinion that simultaneous growth of Micro, Small and
Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) along with big Business Enterprises can create an overall
growth story. Unfortunately the development pattern of the MSMEs of the Region is far from
encouraging. The Committee is of the firm view that the growth of MSMEs touches more
directly, the lives of common people and can raise their income levels much more drastically.
The Committee therefore, strongly recommends that Government must address the problems
being faced by the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprise (MSMEs). (Para 3.10.9)
EDUCATIONAL OPPORTUNITIES IN THE REGION
The Committee notes that in the absence of availability of adequate educational

of the country. The Committee urges the Ministry of HRD to explore ways and means to
provide such opportunities in the NE Region establishing more institutes and provide
adequate number of faculty positions. (Para 3.11.1)

3. The Chairman, then, asked the Secretary to focus on both the budgetary aspects and the
issues concerning the major policies, programmes and projects taken up under the Ministry while
making the presentation. He also asked him to focus on the ongoing Infrastructure projects and

66
economic development of the North-Eastern Region, including the focus on programmes/projects
where the targets could not be achieved or where the schemes were delayed.
4. He further asked him to update the Committee on targets set and achievements made under
Vision 2020 document, achievements of the physical targets during the Eleventh Five Year Plan and
targets set for Twelfth  Five Year Plan for various projects, schemes and programmes and also on
subjects like human resource development, investment and trade promotion, tourism prospects and
countering insurgency through development initiatives, rail projects linking all the capital cities of
NE States and other Measures to improve connectivity in the region including air connectivity.
5. He then asked the Secretary to respond to the following issues:–
• The primary role of the Ministry of DoNER is advocacy to stimulate planning and
facilitate and accelerate projects/scheme implementation in NER, but the main problem
faced by the Ministry is weakness inherent in any advocacy and coordination role.
What was proposed to be done to combat such weakness?
• The Ministry faces problems like inadequate technical support; inadequate overall
support; inadequate space and lack of field offices. Has the Ministry taken any steps
to overcome these problems?
• The projected demand by the Ministry of DoNER for 2013-14 for various plan
schemes was to the tune of Rs. 3,614.91 crores but approved budget estimate was
only Rs. 2,006.00 crores. What is the likely impact of the reduced allocation?
• Projected budget estimate for NER Livelihood Project for 2012-13 was Rs. 94.00
crores but the approved budget estimate was Rs. 35.00 crores which was further
reduced to Rs. 9.00 crores and actual expenditure till 31st December, 2012 was merely
Rs. 0.01 lakh. What are the reasons for such drastic reduction and miserable actual
expenditure?
• Again for financial year 2013-14, against projected budget estimate of Rs. 94.30
crores, approved budget estimate for the NER livelihood project was to the tune of
Rs. 30.00 crores. What was the original planning of the Ministry and what would be
the effect because of reduced budget estimate?
• Projected budget estimate for 2012-13 for North-East States Road Investment
Programme was Rs. 100.00 crores but Rs. 45.00 crores were allocated at BE stage
which was further reduced to Rs. 20.00 crores; however, actual expenditure up to
31
st December, 2012 was only Rs. 62 lakh. What are the reasons for such under
utilization and what was the consequential impact on the project?
• The delay and ineffectiveness of the implementing departments/agencies is a major
concern of NEC and this creates impediments in achieving the desired results and
targets. What strategic steps the Ministry is contemplating to overcome these problems
of NEC?
6. The Chairman further observed that with regard to utilizations, the expenditure part of it has
been an average of 25 per cent. He wondered that if this is the pattern of average expenditure
incurred, then, how will the Ministry go to the Finance Ministry and ask for higher allocations. He
further observed that even with regard to completion of projects, the total projects in different
States are to the tune of 1,408 out of which completed are only 49.64 per cent. He then asked
them to prepare a realistic note, based on the last 12-13 years experience, about the bottlenecks,
problems, shortcomings, solutions etc. Then the Chairman felt that the Committee should take up
the matter with the Cabinet Secretary, if necessary, to have a re-Iook at the functioning of the

67
Ministry and also discuss with all the Chief Secretaries and representatives of Planning
Commission.
7. The Committee, then, took into account that a Ministry, which was carved out to focus
and expedite development in the North-east region, after more than a decade of its existence has
realized that it lacks sufficient power and necessary infrastructure to carry out its mandate, and
is completely dependent on various Line Ministries/Departments for execution of projects. The
Committee took a serious view of the situation and felt that a holistic view at the functioning and
powers of the Ministry should be taken at this stage. The Committee was not at all impressed with
the functioning of the Ministry and got an impression that the line Ministries were not supporting
or paying heed to the suggestions/requests of the ministry. The Committee, then, decided to discuss
this issue separately at a later stage with all stakeholders and authorities. The Committee directed
the Ministry to furnish a detailed note, based on the last 12-13 years of experience indicating about
the bottlenecks and shortcomings and the desired solutions. The Committee also proposed to
discuss the matter with the Cabinet Secretary and Secretaries of all the line Ministries and the Chief
Secretaries of the State Governments so as to enable the Ministry to effectively tackle the issues
faced by it. The Committee also decided to discuss with the Planning Commission about the need
for reviewing all the schemes being implemented in the North-East to have a holistic view in the
matter and also to find solutions to the problems faced by the Ministry.
