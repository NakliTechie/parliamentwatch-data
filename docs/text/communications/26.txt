I. INTRODUCTION 
 
The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting is one among the early Ministries set 
up after India’s Independence. It is a vital Ministry  that represents the face of the 
Government of India in reaching out to the masses. It is also the focal point regarding 
policy matters relating to private broadcasting sector, administering of the public 
broadcasting service (Prasar Bharati), multimedia advertising and publicity of the 
policies and programmes of the Government of India, film promotion and certification, 
and regulation of print and digital media.  
 
2. Ministry of Information and Broadcasting is entrusted with the task of 
disseminating information about Government policies, schemes and programmes 
through the different types/medium of mass communication covering radio, television, 
press, social media, printed publicity like booklets, posters, outdoor publicity including 
through traditional modes of communication such as dance, drama, folk recitals, puppet 
shows etc. The Ministry is functionally divided into three sectors:- 
“(a)Information Sector : The Information Sector is responsible for information 
dissemination and awareness creation of the policies and activities of the 
Government of India through print, electronic and digital media, framing of 
policy guidelines for rate fixation of Government advertisements on print, 
electronic, and online platform, and administering the Press and Press and 
Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023, and the Press Council Act, 1978. 
(b) Broadcasting Sector: The Broadcasting Sector aids the Ministry in the far -
reaching dissemination of Government schemes and initiatives via All India 
Radio and Doordarshan. The Sector oversees these public broadcasters by 
administrating the Prasar Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) Act, 1990. 
It also regulates private TV channels and the network of multi -system operators 
and local cable operators through the Cable Television Networks (Regulation) 
Act, 1995 and policy guidelines issued from time to time. It gives license to 
DTH/HITS operators for their respective operations. The private FM radio 
network is regulated by the Ministry through auctioning of FM channels, 
operationalization of Community Radio Stations in rural and remote areas. 
(c)Film Sector: The Films Sector is responsible for production, promotion and 
preservation of films, and filmic content. It also includes documentaries, 
organisation of international film festivals and promotion of good cinema by 
institution of awards. It administers the Cinematograph Act, 1952, which looks 
into certification of films for public exhibition, and handles other matters relating 
to film industry, including developmental and promotional activities.”

15. On being asked about the initiatives being taken to avoid rush of expenditure 
during the last few months of the financial year, the Ministry submitted as under:- 
“To avoid rush of expenditure in the last months of the financial year, the 
Ministry of I&B has adopted quarterly and monthly expenditure planning, 
ensuring realistic budget estimation, and conducting periodic reviews of 
spending by the Concerned Additional/Joint Secretaries at the Wing level and 
by Media heads at the level of the Media Units. Also, the Ministry has directed 
all the spending units to strictly adhere to the quarter expenditure ceilings 
prescribed by the Ministry of Finance and to surrender any anticipated 
savings in a timely manner, so as to ensure financial discipline and prevent 
avoidable-year end spending pressures”.

V. BROADCASTING SECTOR (INCLUDING PRASAR BHARATI) 
 
32. The Broadcasting Sector is broadly categorised into two categories viz. 'Content' 
and ‘Carriage’ services. This Ministry regulates the content of private satellite channels 
and networks of Multi -System Operators (MSOs) and Local Cable Operators (LCO) 
through the Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995, and policy guidelines 
issued from time -to-time. Broadcasting carriage services include MSOs, LCOs, Direct -
to-Home (DTH) operators, Headend -In-The-Sky (HITS) operators and Internet Protocol 
Television (IPTV) service providers. This Ministry gives license/permission to DTH/HITS 
operators for their respective operations. In the Broadcasting Sector, there are two 
Schemes. The first is the Broadcast Infrastructure and Network Development (BIND) 
scheme implemented through Prasar Bharati. The other Scheme is Supporting 
Community Radio Movement (CRS). 
33. Prasar Bharati was constituted as a statutory corporation under the Prasar 
Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) Act, 1990 w.e.f. 23 rd November, 1997. 

23 
 
Prasar Bharati, with Akashvani (erstwhile All India Radio) and Doordarshan as its two 
constituents, is mandated to organize and conduct public broadcasting service to 
inform, educate and entertain the public and to ensure a balanced development of 
broadcasting on radio and television. Prasar Bharati with changing times has also 
transcended into the progressive and transformational mega wave of ‘media 
convergence’ of a digitally oriented platform. For the upwardly mobile and digital savvy 
population, Prasar Bharati provides access to its variety content by way of digital 
platforms like OTT, YouTube and mobile App ‘NewsOnAIR’ for audience globally. 
Prasar Bharati also uses a number of social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter etc. 
to disseminate updated information about its content and other relevant details about 
events and news. Balancing these twin challenges has been the focus of policies and 
initiatives of Prasar Bharati. Prasar Bharati currently reaches 90% by way of area and 
98% by way of population of the whole Country through its Radio network and almost 
100% through satellite mode of TV network. Prasar Bharati is currently having 
591Akashvani Broadcasting installations, and 66 Doordarshan Kendras to provide 
various Akashvani and DD services across the Country.

“DD Free Dish is a Free -to-Air (FTA) and an alternative affordable platform for

India, was launched in December 2004 by Prasar Bharati. 
DD Free Dish is an affordable platform reaching millions of people in remote, 
inaccessible and border areas, becoming a tool for empowerment of the 
masses particularly marginalized and weaker sections of the society, for 
dissemination of quality information free of cost. News and views of outside 
world, as well as quality entertainment, which is available to affluent class at a

Doordarshan, 11 HD DD Channels and 95 popular Private TV channels, 320 
DD Co-Branded Educational channels and 64 Radio channels 
E Auction Methodology for allotting vacant slots on DD Free Dish to private 
satellite TV Channels was introduced in 2011 and has evolved since then as 
per learnings from past experiences and market forces. 
E Auction Methodology is driven by the approved Guiding Principles by MoIB 
which are as follows: 
• Carriage of Private Channels on DD Free Dish will be based on e -
auctions but with differential pricing based on Genre and / Or Language. 
• Channel Providers desirous of placing their channels on DD Free Dish 
shall be allowed to bid only in e -auctions specific to the Genre and 
Language their channels belong to. Broadcasters are required to declare 
Genre and Language of their channel to Prasar Bharati while applying for 
e-auction bidding process to be eligible to participate in the e-auction. 
• Differential pricing for Genre will be based on the principle of higher 
Reserve Price for Genre with greater commercial potential. 
• E-auction will be held such as to maximize revenue potential from e -
auctions and to ensure a stable & inclusive portfolio of channels from a 
viewer standpoint. 
• Bidding in the e-auction will be conducted in multiple rounds till all vacant 
unreserved slots are filled up. 
• The payment terms for successful bidders upon award of a slot will be 
such as to encourage maximum participation of bidders in e-auction”.

43. On being asked about the reasons for not increasing the slot of DD  Free Dish 
and the roadmap to promote reach of DD Free Dish, the Ministry replied as under:- 
“Recently several factors like surfacing of various Digital platforms, OTTs, 
financial stress in market & decreasing popularity of linear channels has 
challenged revenue across all DTH services. DD Free Dish, in spite of 
increasing its viewership over the years, could end up earning only around 
814 Cr this year i.e. 2025 -26. Since revenue through DD Free Dish is earned 
through e Auction of vacant slots on DD Free Dish to private TV channels and 
the yield through e -Auction depends on supply and demand. Hence, 
increasing the slots on DD Free Dish, especially MPEG -2 slots carrying 
higher reserve price, could hamper revenue through DD Free Dish further. 
Also, MPEG-2 compression is inefficient compression as compared to MPEG-
4 compression. Hence, two more transponders allotted to Prasar Bharati are 
under process of getting adequately equipped so that MPEG -4 slots on DD 
Free Dish, carrying a much lesser reserve price, are increased further. 
Roadmap: - Un represented / under represented regional language channels 
are promoted by reserving slots adequately for them at a very nominal / 
promotional / invitational reserve price on MPEG -4 slots of DD Free Dish 
which may attract more popular regional language channels to come on DD 
Free Dish thereby increasing penetration of DD Free Dish Set Top Boxes in 
those areas. In the financial year 2026 -27, the number of MPEG -4 slots have 
been enhanced adequately for annual e -auction to onboard a greater number 
of regional language channels”.

(ii)  SUPPORTING COMMUNITY RADIO MOVEMENT IN INDIA 
72. Community Radio (CR) is an important third tier in Radio Broadcasting, distinct 
from Public Service Radio broadcasting and Commercial Radio. Community Radio 
Stations (CRSs) are low -power Radio Stations, which are meant to be set up and 
operated by local communities. In December 2002, the Government of India approved a 
policy for the grant of license, for setting up of CRS, to well established educational 
institutions. The CR provides a platform for communities to air local voices on issues 
concerning Health , Nutrition, Education, Agriculture, etc. Since the Community Radio 
broadcast is in local languages and dialects, people are able to relate to it instantly. To 
support the Community Radio Movement, a Central Sector Scheme namely “Supporting 
Community Radio Movement in India” had been approved with an outlay of Rs. 50 
crores for a period of 5 years i.e. from 2021- 2026. The Scheme aims to strengthen new 
and existing CRSs with resources, capacity and technology in order to upgrade the 
standard of the stations and increase reach and visibility to the interior regions of India. 
This would help promote growth of CRS in remote and rural areas thereby aiding socio -

44 
 
economic and cultural development of communities by using CRS as a powerful 
medium of social mobilization. The scheme therefore aims to revitalize new and existing 
CRSs by providing required funds for resources, capacity and technology thereby 
increasing effectiveness of operational community radio stations.

102. When asked for a plan of action for bringing the Prasar Bharati Amendment Bill 
along with the details, the Ministry, provided details of the steps taken for amendment of 
the Act as recommended by the Group of Ministers (GoM) and stated the following:- 
“(i) GoM in 2010 recommended a comprehensive amendment in the Prasar 
Bharati (BCI) Act. A comprehensive draft amendment Bill was circulated 
through a draft Cabinet Note in 2013, which was withdrawn in 2014 for further 
assessment and analysis. 
(ii) After assessment and analysis a draft comprehensive amendment 
proposal was again submitted, however, it was decided in the Ministry to 
process those amendments which are of imminent necessity. Accordingly, 
another draft amendment bill was circulated through a draft Cabinet Note 
dated 15.11.2018. With the approval of HMIB, the bill was formally withdrawn 
on 20.11.2019. 
(iii) After the approval of HMIB, this Ministry vide O.M. dated 08.07.2023 had 
circulated a draft Cabinet Note (DCN) for IMC with different Ministries. The 
proposed amendments aims at bringing the definition of “Broadcasting” in line 
with the modern technological developments, remove the anomalies in the 
term of office of whole -time Members and streamline and improve the 
operational efficiency of the Corporation by removing certain procedural 
impediments. 
(iv) Comments on the instant matter were received from Department of 
Financial Services, Department of Expenditure, Department of Legal Affairs, 
Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeiTY) and Department 
of Telecommunications. However, Prasar Bharati in the meanwhile informed 
that it had hired an agency for consultancy services for its HR Transformation, 
thus the Prasar Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) Act, 1990 [PB 
(BCI) Act, 1990] could require some more amendments as per the findings of 
study being conducted by the agency hired in that regard. However, this 
Ministry vide O.M. dated 24.07.2024 had withdrawn the draft cabinet note 
circulated on 08.07.2023 for further assessment and analysis. HR 
Transformation of Prasar Bharati is still ongoing. Therefore, the process of 
effecting required amendments to the Act will be taken after this exercise 
reaches its finality.” 
(v) In view of the above, there is no requirement of amendment to the Act till 
finality of HR Transformation of PB”.

DD Free Dish  
4. The Committee observe that DD Free Dish is a Free -to-Air (FTA) DTH 
Service and an alternative affordable platform for people who do not have means 
to pay high subscription fee of private platforms.  DD Free Dish platform, an 
approved scheme of the Government of India, was launched in December 2004 by 
Prasar Bharati. Since then it is reaching millions of people in remote, inaccessible 

66 
 
and border areas, becoming a tool for empowerment of the masses particularly 
marginalized and weaker sections of the society. Total Bouquet of DD Free Dish 
consist of 482 TV channels including 35 Doordarshan  channels, 11 HD DD

channels and 64 Radio channels. The Committee note that DD Free Dish signal is 
available pan India, except Andaman & Nicobar Islands and can be viewed with a 
very modest one-time investment of around Rs. 2500/- towards small Dish, MPEG-
4 Free-to-Air Set Top Box and other ancillary peripherals. DD Free Dish Set -Top 
Boxes are free to air (FTA) and available in open market and Prasar Bharati has 
no direct control over these Set Top Boxes which are available over the shelf in 
open market for general public, hence there is no accurate data available 
regarding viewership of DD Free Dish. As per market estimates and FICCI & E&Y 
report published in March 2025, more than 49 million households are viewing DD 
Free Dish. The  Committee understand that there is no monthly subscription fees 
required to tune in to DD Free Dish channels, unlike other DTH services. With 
respect to challenges, the Committee note that DD Free Dish viewership is facing 
challenges in southern and eastern part of India and to increase penetration of 
DD Free Dish set Top Boxes in these regions promotional measures are  being 
taken from time to time. As a latest measure, 17 numbers of popular 
unrepresented / under represented regional private TV channels were onboarded 
on MPEG-4 slots of DD Free dish Free of cost for the period from  01.01.2026 to 
31.03.2026, on a pilot basis. 
The Committee perceive that several factors like surfacing of various 
Digital platforms, OTTs, financial stress in market & decreasing popularity of 
linear channels ha ve challenged revenue across all DTH services and it could 
earn only around Rs. 814 crore during FY 2025 -26. On the issue of increasing the 
slots, the Ministry have submitted that since revenue of DD Free Dish is earned 
through e Auction of vacant slots on DD Free Dish to private TV channels and the 
yield through e -Auction depends on supply an d demand , h ence, increasing the 
slots on DD Free Dish, especially MPEG -2 slots carrying higher reserve price, 
could hamper revenue of DD Free Dish further. When asked about the road map 
with respect to DD Free Dish, the Ministry ha ve informed that in the financial year 
2026-27, the number of MPEG -4 slots have been enhanced adequately for annual 
e-auction to onboard a greater number of regional language channels. Besides, 
unrepresented/under represented regional language channels are promoted by 
reserving slots adequately for them at a very nominal / promotional / invitational 

67 
 
reserve price on MPEG -4 slots of DD Free Dish which may attract more popular

Remote, Tribal and LWE areas (under BIND) has undergone a change and is now 
being implemented in Direct Benefit Transfer mode wherein Ministry of 
Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) in conjunction with the Bureau of

SUPPORTING COMMUNITY RADIO MOVEMENT IN INDIA 
8. The Committee note that the Community Radio is an important third -tier 
Radio in Radio Broadcasting, distinct from Public Service Radio broadcasting 
and Commercial Radio. Community Radio Stations (CRSs) are low -power Radio 
Stations, which are meant to be set up and operated by local communities. In 
December 2002, the Government of India approved a policy for the grant of 
license, for setting up of CRS, to well established educational institutions. The CR 
provides a platform for communities to air local voices on issues concerning 
health, nutrition, education, agriculture etc. The Scheme (Supporting Community 
Radio Movement in India) aims to strengthen new and existing CRSs with 
resources, capacity and technology so as to upgrade the standard of the stations 
and increase its reach and visibility to the interior regions of India. The 
Committee are given to understand that the Scheme has been revised ‘wherein it 
is proposed to extend for the period from 2026-27 to 2030 -31 with a total 
proposed allocation of Rs. 50 crore. The thrust areas under the Scheme are (i) to 
increase the number of stations in CR dark areas and (ii) to have at least one 
Community Radio Station in each district of the Country. At present, 560 CRS are 
operational in India (till January, 2026). With respect to budgetary allocations and 
expenditure, the Committee note that Rs. 8.00 crore has been allocated for the FY 
2026-27 (at BE stage). During the FY 2025-26, the BE and RE allocations were Rs. 
17.40 crore and Rs. 8.00 crore , respectively, and the actual expenditure till 8 th 
January, 2026 was Rs. 4.52 crore which is 56.50 % of RE allocations.   
 
          Initiatives taken under the Scheme ‘Supporting Community Radio 
Movement in India’ include organization of National and Regional Sammelans, 
awareness workshops in CR dark areas, etc. However, the Committee find that 
fund utilization has been uneven over the years, and Demand for Grants remains 
limited due to lack of awareness among potential beneficiaries. The Committee , 
therefore, urge the Ministry to streamline grant disbursement, strengthen 
capacity-building programs for CRS staff and provide technical support to 

73 
 
enhance operational efficiency. The Committee, also desire to be apprised of the 
action taken in respect of the following:- (i) Ensure optimum utilistion of allocated 
funds during the FY 2026-27; (ii) Prepare a definite timeline/roadmap and intensify 
efforts for ensuring at least one Community Radio Station in each district, 
particularly CR dark areas,  as per the vision under the Scheme; (iii) Explore the 
feasibility of having one dedicated training cell for CRS personnel in each 
State/Zone to improve technical skills, content creation and community 
engagement; and (iv) Involve local Government, educational institutions, NGOs, 
and community organizations to promote awareness and local participation.

INFORMATION SECTOR  
 
Press Information Bureau (PIB) 
10. The Committee note that the Press Information Bureau (PIB) is the nodal 
agency of the Government of India to disseminate information through print, 
electronic and social media on Government policies  and programmes, initiatives 
and achievements. It functions as an interface between the Government and the 
media and also provides feedback to the Government on people’s reaction as 
reflected in the media. It also advises the Government on communication 
strategies best suited to meet the needs of the media.  Unde r PIB, a Fact Check 
Unit (FCU) was set up in November, 2019, in order to monitor and check the 
circulation and dissemination of factually incorrect/doctored news and 
information over different modes of news media and social media platforms, 

77 
 
which often mislead people on various issues. The mandate of the PIB Fact 
Check Unit is to provide the official/authentic version of any news doing rounds 
on various media platforms by making available the factually correct information 
in the public domain. The PIB -FCU receives communication/messages from the 
public at large through WhatsApp, email, Twitter and PIB’s website. These fact -
checks are available at the website of PIB. The fact -checked content by PIB -FCU 
is taken up with the concerned social media platforms for appropriate action 
under rule 3(1)(b) of the IT Rules, 2021. Briefing about the Fact Check Unit of PIB, 
the Ministry informed that during Operation Sindoor, the Fact Check Unit of PIB 
worked round the clock to curb fake news, quickly detected and issued swift 
rebuttals to edited videos, misinformation, and propaganda and around 1,400 
URLs were blocked. The Committee are informed that the central Fact -Check Unit 
which falls under the guidelines of the Information Technology (IT) Act, is under 
judicial review. However, a Fact Check Unit under the Press Information Bureau 
(PIB) is currently functional.  
While Clarifying about the mandate of MIB, the Ministry submitted that 
under the IT Act and the Intermediary Guidelines they deal with IT content and 
media content connected with news and media outlets and to that extent, they 
make use of the relevant provisions for removing content under certain 
considerations. The Ministry further admitted the existence of regulatory gaps in 
respect of online media and OTT platforms.  On a related issue, the Ministry 
stated that during the last year, on the basis of Section 294  of the ‘Bharatiya 
Nyaya Sanhita’ Act and Section 4 of the ‘Indecent Representation of Women Act’ , 
25 OTT platforms were blocked initially, and subsequently another 18 platforms 
were blocked. At present, blocking orders are continuously being issued against 
OTT apps and content circulating in online media. In the light of the role and 
mandate of PIB-FCU, the Committee are of the view that the increasing spread of 
misinformation and manipulated content across various media platforms 
necessitates strengthenin g of institutional mechanisms for timely verification , 
quick detection and blocking of fake news, harmful content  and dissemination of 
authentic information. Accordingly, the Committee seek that the following areas 
may also be explored and apprise d them of the outcome: - (i) To enhance the 
outreach and accessibility of the Fact Check Unit under the  PIB through wider 
public awareness campaigns and improved coordination with social media 
platforms; (ii) To enhance technological capabilities, including the use of artificial 
intelligence tools for real -time monitoring of misinformation trends, improved 

78 
 
coordination with social media platforms to facilitate faster identification and 
removal of misleading content;   (iii) Expand the Fact Check Unit’s capacity   with 
quantifiable target for the next FY including trained personnel for regional 
languages; (iv) Publish quarterly transparency reports  on misinformation 
identified, corrective action taken, and engagement with media platforms;  (v) 
Formulate a comprehensive regulatory framework to effectively address issues 
relating to misinformation, harmful content and public grievances related to it , 
coordinate with other Ministries and MeitY  to close regulatory gaps;  and (vi) 
Develop a structured grievance redressal mechanism for online and OTT content, 
integrating Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act and existing civil redress provisions. 
 
NORTH EASTERN REGION (NER) 
11. The Committee note that the budgetary allocations for the FY 2026-27, has 
been fixed at Rs. 111.21 crore at BE stage. The budgetary allocations for NER 
during the financial year 2025 -26 at BE and RE stage, were Rs. 111.85 crore and 
Rs. 117.84 crore respectively. Whereas the actual expenditure, till 31 st December, 
2025, was Rs. 30.71 crore which is just about 26.16% of RE allocations. Under the 
Schemes/Projects/Organisations of the Ministry of Information Broadcasting in 
North Eastern Region, a trend of under -utilisation of funds has been observed 
during the financial years 2022 -23, 2024-25 and 2025 -26.  Regarding the reasons 
for low expenditure during the FY 2025 -26, the Ministry ha ve informed that it 
happened due to multiple factors including (a) On 12.09.2025 withdrawal of BE 
allocations for FY 2025 -26 for the components ‘Continuing Projects of three year 
BIND scheme (2017 -20) to be completed’ in Project Mode and it was later 
reinstated on 16.10.2025; (b) under DCFDC, receipt of limited number of 
proposals from the North East Districts. Also, as per the directives, NFDC was to 
limit its investment to 40% of the production cost. Hence, in regions like NER, for 
producers to invest the remaining 60% or more was challenging; (c) Under CRS, 
there was a very low level of awareness about CRS in the region, consequently 
the numbers of operational  community radio  stations in the region were very 
less.   
With respect to initiatives planned for better performance under the 
Schemes/projects being implemented in North -East Region for the FY 2026 -27, 
the Ministry have informed: (a) Under BIND scheme, the frequency of monitoring 
meetings has been increased at all levels, an Apex Committee has been 
constituted to monitor the progress of projects under the Scheme; (b) under 

79 
 
DCDFC scheme, several measures have been taken for promotion and production 
of feature films, documentaries, animation films in various North Eastern 
languages; (c) under CRS, financial assistance are given to Operational 
Community Radio Stations for purchase of equipment to set -up Community 
Radio Stations, Workshops are held since a significant barrier to the expansion of 
Community Radio Stations (CRSs) across India is that eligible organizations don’t 
know about what community radio is or how it works. The purpose of such 
workshops are spreading awareness among  eligible organizations; (d) under 
DCID, Regions/Units-wise Annual and Quarterly targets are set up; etc. 
In the light of above, the Committee are of the view that  under-utilisation 
undermines the very objective of promoting media infrastructure and cultural 
outreach in the North Eastern Region. The Committee , therefore,  urge the 
Ministry to take  positive action with respect to the following and apprise them 
accordingly:- (i) Prepare a time-bound action plan for optimum utilisation of funds 
allocated under all the schemes of the North Eastern Region (NER);  (ii) Prepare a 
road map with timeline for having at least one Community Radio Station (CRS) in 
each district of the North Eastern States as per the vision under supporting 
Community Radio Movement in India and take concrete steps accordingly. Also 
increase awareness among eligible institutions ensuring timely financial 
assistance to establish CRS and to have mechanism for impact assessment;  (iii) 
Upgrade technical infrastructure, including studios, transmitters, and digital 
broadcasting equipment, to improve signal quality and reach in remote and tribal 
areas; and (iv) Intensify outreach efforts, organise more awareness programmes 
and extend necessary facilitation support so that stakeholders/producers from 
the NER are encouraged to actively participate in such initiatives. Also explore 
the possibility of having regional training hubs  in collaboration with IICT and 
other media institutions to strengthen skill development in NER.
