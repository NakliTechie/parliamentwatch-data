A. Introductory 
 
1. As per Article 151 of the Constitution of India, the Reports of the Comptroller and 
Auditor General of India, after being laid in the Parliament stand referred to the 
Public Accounts Committee for their scrutiny. The reports by C&AG highlight 
systemic deficiencies, irregularities and wasteful or avoidable expenditure, etc. in 
Government accounts as well as  in implementation of policies and programmes 
approved by Parliament. The Public Accounts Committee, in its each term, 
selects a number of subjects amongs t the Reports of C&AG for detailed 
examination. As for the remaining subjects which are not selected due to paucity 
of time and voluminous nature of these reports, the Public Accounts Committee 
has evolved a system whereby the Government Departments concer ned are 
required to furnish remedial or corrective Action Taken Notes in respect of all non-
selected paragraphs contained in the Audit Reports to the Committee through the 
Ministry of Finance, Department of Expenditure within four months from the date 
of laying of Audit Reports in the Parliament. This system has been put in place to 
ensure accountability of the Ministr ies/Departments responsible for  taking 
corrective/remedial actions for any irregularities/deficiencies reported upon by the 
C&AG. For the facilitation of this procedure, t he Audit Para Monitoring System  
(APMS) Portal has been developed by the Department of Expenditure, Ministry 
of Finance on the recommendation of the Public Accounts Committee. 
 
2. Sub-Committee VI of Public Accounts Committee (2025-26) on ‘Non-Compliance 
in timely submission of Action Taken Notes on Non -selected audit paragraphs 
and excess expenditure’ was constituted for detailed examination of pendency in 
submission of Action Taken Notes by Ministries/Department. The Sub-Committee 
functioned under the convenorship of Shri Shaktisinh Gohil, Member of PAC and 
Member of Parliament from Rajya Sabha.  
 
3. The Public Accounts Committee has also, in its previous terms, constituted similar 
Sub-Committees for scrutin y of pendency in submission of Action Taken Notes 
on non-selected audit paragraphs and the Committee keeps monitoring corrective 
and remedial action by Ministries in re spect of non-selected audit paragraphs 
regularly.  
 
B. Procedure and prescribed timelines for submission and vetting of Action 
Taken Notes  
 
4. As submitted by Audit, t he prescribed timeline of four months from laying of the 
Report of C&AG in Parliament till furnishing of Final Action Taken Notes 

 
2 
 
forwarded to the Public Accounts has been detailed hereafter. In the first step, 
after laying of the C&AG Report in Parliament, the Report shall be uploaded on 
the Audit Para Monitoring System (APMS) Portal within five days by the Office of 
the C&AG. Thereafter, the concerned Ministry/Department is required to submit 
the first draft Action Taken Notes on the report/paragraphs uploaded against them 
within thirty days. Following this, the Audit carries out and uploads the vetting of 
the first ATN replies within the next thirty days. 
 
5. In the second journey i.e. after the first vetting of the replies by the Audit, the 
Ministry/department is required to furnish their revised Action Taken Notes within 
twenty days, incorporating the observations made by Audit. The Audit shall again 
vet the revised replies within fifteen days. Following this, there is a third journey 
which is generally the final journey, where the Ministry/Department has to provide 
their final replies within ten days, and then the Audit shall complete the vetting of 
the final replies within five days.  At the last stage, after the completion of these 
journeys, the final Action Taken Notes are forwarded to the Public Accounts 
Committee. The entire procedure is carried out on the APMS Portal by the 
Monitoring Cell , functioning under the Controller General of Accounts in 
Department of Expenditure under the Ministry of Finance , which is responsible 
for monitoring and coordination of submission of Action Taken Notes by Ministries 
on Audit Observations. 
 
6. In case of explanatory notes which are to be submitted by the 
Ministries/Department in cases of excess expenditure or saving above Rs 100 
crore and above, a similar timeline is to be followed. By the 30th of June of each 
year, the Monitoring Cell uploads the Explanatory Notes (EN) pending against the 
Ministries on the APMS Portal. The draft EN has to be uploaded by the concerned 
Ministry/Department within twenty-five days. The vetting of the draft EN is carried 
out by the Audit  within further period of twenty-five days. The final Explanatory 
Notes are prepared within ten days of the vetting by the Audit and then is 
submitted by the Monitoring Cell within five days thereafter.  
 
C. Role of the Monitoring Cell (Department of Expenditure, Ministry of 
Finance) 
 
7. The Monitoring Cell coordinates and facilitates submission of ATNs on the C&AG 
Paras, ATRs on PAC paras and Explanatory Notes on excess expenditure or 
savings of Rs 100 crore and above through APMS Portal. The Cell also carries 
out administration of the APMS portal, interaction and training programs for users 
from all the Ministries/Departments, offices of C&AG and their field offices and  
the Lok Sabha Secretariat  (PAC Secretariat)  and coordinates with the Nodal 
Officers in Ministries/Departments as well as the Audit.  
 
 

 
3 
 
D.  The Audit Para Monitoring System (APMS) Portal  
 
8. The Audit Para Monitoring System (APMS) Portal is a web-based computerized 
monitoring system of effective monitoring of submission of Action Taken Notes 
on C&AG audit paragraphs, action taken replies on the PACs observations, 
recommendations and Explanatory Notes on excess expenditure or saving above 
Rs 100 crore . It has been developed and is maintained by the Monitoring Cell 
upon the recommendation of the Public Accounts Committee . The key 
stakeholders in the APMS portal include the Office of the Comptroller and Auditor 
General of India, which uploads its observations contained in the Audit Reports ; 
the Ministries and Departments responsible for taking action and uploading the 
Action Taken Notes; the Monitoring Cell in the office of the CGA, which finalizes 
everything and sends it to the C&AG and the PAC once the paras have been 
vetted and the Public Accounts Committee, which monitors and examines both 
the selected and non -selected paras and the explanatory notes . The Portal 
serves as a centralized system for uploading, vetting, and finalizing responses in 
the monitoring process.

16. In this context, t he representatives of O/o C&AG informed the Committee as

“Three reports of C&AG pertaining to UGFAR for the years 2019, 2020 and 2021

17. With regard to the pending audit paras, the representatives of the Ministry 
submitted as under:

“This Committee has brought to consideration 16 audit paragraphs and four PAC 
paragraphs. Of the 16 audit paragraphs, eight relate to earlier years, that is, the 
years 2016, 2021, 2022, and 2023, while the remaining eight have been received 
recently during this year. The four PAC paragraphs received in 2024, that is last

18. During the course of evidence, the Committee desired to know the status of the 
each of the pending audit paras and the Ministry responded to each. The Ministry 
acknowledged the delays in submission of Action Taken Notes and informed the 
Committee that steps had been taken to improve monitoring. The Ministry 
informed that out of 16 audit paragraphs under consideration, eight related to 
earlier years (2016, 2021, 2022 and 2023), while the remaining eight were 
received during the current year. They also submit ted that  ATNs on several 
paragraphs had already been uploaded and the remaining would be submitted 
shortly. The Ministry also admitted that some delays occurred because replies 
were not uploaded in the prescribed format on the APMS portal, leading to their 
return by the Monitoring Cell. The Ministry stated that a mechanism had been put

Part- II 
Observations/Recommendations of the Committee 
 
1. One of the main functions of the Public Accounts Committee is to scrutinise 
reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India pertaining to the Union 
Government which stand referred to it after being laid in the Parliament under Article 
151 of the Constitution of India. However, due to paucity of time, the Committee are 
not in a position to examine each and every para in these reports. The Committee 
therefore, take up relatively important audit reports in each term for detailed 
examination. To ensure timely corrective action and accountability of the Executive 
to observations of the Supreme Audit Institution, the Committee over time has 
devised a mechanism for submission of Action Taken Notes (ATNs) on all audit 
paragraphs in all C&AG Reports. Through this mechanism, Ministries/Departments 
are required to furnish ATNs in respect of all audit observations within four months 
from the date of laying of the C&AG Report in Parliament. In 2011, on the 
recommendation of the Committee, a digitised platform/ por tal, the ‘Audit Para 
Monitoring System’ came in effect for continuous monitoring of the Action taken 
Notes by the Ministries. The Ministries/Departments are required to furnish Action 
Taken Notes on all audit paragraphs, provide explanatory notes in cases of 
expenditure/savings over Rs 100 crore and submit replies in respect of 
recommendations contained in PAC Reports through this portal which is monitored 
by the Committee.  
 
2. The Sub -Committee of PAC (2025 -26) on ‘Non -compliance in timely 
submission of ATNs on non-selected audit paragraphs and excess expenditure’ was 
constituted with a specific mandate to monitor and reduce pendency in submission 
of ATNs by Ministries/Departments, recommend imp rovements and ensure that 
corrective action is taken on the observations of Audit. The Committee found that 
total 2491 C&AG paras  (including selected and non -selected) were pending for 
submission of ATNs as on 21.10.2025 and of these, 1372 paras were pending beyond 
the prescribed time limit of four months from the date of laying of the Audit Reports 
in Parliament. As on 21.04.2026, there are 2235 C&AG paras (including selected and 
non-selected) pending for submission of ATNs and of these 1046 audit paras were 
added between the period from 21.10.202 5 to 21.04.2026. The Committee observe 

 
21 
 
from the statement of settlement of ATNs on APMS portal  that the settlement of 
ATNs during the term of the Sub -Committee was double when compared to the six 
months period before the Sub-Committee was constituted. 
 
3. The Committee took oral evidence of the representatives of Ministries of Civil 
Aviation, Finance (Departments of Revenue – CBDT and CBIC) and Commerce & 
Industry (Department of Commerce) and Defence. During oral evidences, the 
Committee observed that the main reasons for pendency were submission of 
incomplete replies by Ministries concerned, lack of supporting documentation, non-
adherence to stipulated procedure / timelines, prolonged correspondence between 
the Ministries and Audit authorities and often lack of dedicatedly trained staff in 
Ministries for managing the APMS portal. The Committee are of the view that not 
only is timely submission of ATNs and explanatory notes essential for effective 
parliamentary oversight but also for improving the function ing of the Executive for 
efficient delivery to the citizens of the country. Therefore, after deliberation with 
several Ministries outlined above, the Committee have recorded their observations 
and recommendations on issues that merit consideration, in the succeeding 
paragraphs. 
 
Monitoring Cell, Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance 
4. The Committee desire that the Monitoring Cell may take necessary action and 
issue instructions in accordance with the recommendations being made by the 
Committee in the succeeding paragraphs to all the Ministries / Departments.  
 
Recommendation No.1 - Adherence to stipulated timelines in submission of 
ATNs 
5. The Committee acknowledge the efforts made by the Monitoring Cell, 
Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance in coordinating with and sensitising 
all the Ministries/ Departments for timely submission of ATNs thereby reducing 
pendency. The Committee n ote that as per the timelines prescribed for the 
submission of ATNs, Ministries are required to upload the first draft action taken 
notes within 30 days of the uploading of the C&AG Report on the APMS portal. 
However, the Committee observe there are ofte n delays on this account because 
Ministries wait for completion of corrective action before uploading the first draft 

 
22 
 
ATNs, due to which timelines get bypassed. The Committee are of the opinion that 
the Ministries/Departments should submit their first draft action taken notes within 
prescribed time limit, even if, only preliminary action has been initiated without 
waiting for the completion of the requisite corrective measures. In following the due 
process for settlement of audit paras, it is essential that the Ministry in the first 
instance provide the action initiated towards settlement of the concerned para. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministries/Departments should 
necessarily submit their first draft action taken notes upon initiation of corrective 
action within the prescribed time period, even if the action has not been fully 
completed. 
 
Recommendation No.2-  ATNs to be concise and targeted and relevant 
6. The Committee, during examination of the action taken notes to the non -
selected paragraphs, found that, apart from, instances of non -adherence of 
timelines, in general, the replies being furnished to the Audit, tend to focus more on 
technical aspects of t he observations rather than on actual compliance, either in 
action or in spirit. The Committee are of the view that the Action taken notes are not 
merely a procedural requirement to the audit objections raised but, an important 
opportunity for the Minist ries/Departments, to identify the specific areas of 
deficiencies or operational challenges that may be frequently recurring without 
coming to the notice of the Headquarters i.e. the Ministries/Departments and to take 
necessary corrective measures to ensure  that the public funds are put to effective 
and efficient use. Accordingly, the Committee urge all the Ministries/Departments to 
not treat the submission of Action taken notes solely as a procedural requirement 
and recommend that, the replies furnished in the Action Taken Notes submitted to 
the Audit should be concise, targeted, relevant to the Audit objections raised and 
complete with relevant documentary evidence where ever applicable.  
Recommendation No.3- Dedicated Staff/Cell in each Ministry/Department for 
coordination and submission of ATNs 
7. The Committee observe that the responsibility for monitoring submission of 
ATNs primarily rests with the concerned Ministries and Departments. While the 
Monitoring Cell under the Controller General of Accounts (Department of 
Expenditure) performs an impor tant coordinating role, effective follow -up action 

 
23 
 
must originate within the administrative Ministries in the prescribed time period. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that a structured mechanism for submission and 
coordination of ATNs ensuring adherence to the prescribed timelines may be 
instituted within Ministries/Departments and for the purpose, suggest that the 
feasibility of a se parate dedicated Cell/staff for submission of Action Taken Notes 
may be explored in each Ministry/Department The Committee recommend that 
meetings of Nodal Officers of all Ministries/ Departments may be conducted as and 
when required and at least once in six months to review progress, address 
challenges, and identify measures to reduce pendency and improve system 
efficiency. 
 
Recommendation No. 4. - Training of Officials specifically assigned the work 
relating to submission of ATNs 
8. The Committee also observe that the personnel responsible for uploading and 
submitting of the action taken notes, often lack adequate knowledge of the 
procedures and timelines to be adhered to. The Committee, therefore, recommend 
that the current personnel are adequately updated on the functionality of the APMS 
portal and that Monitoring  Cell may conduct regular training sessions for 
Ministries/Departments and also depute only the personnel trained in work relating 
to the APMS portal may be deployed to attend the works. The Committee desire that 
a six-monthly report on trainings carried out maybe submitted to Committee (PAC) 
clearly indicating the performance status of various Ministries, categorizing them 
based on satisfactory and unsatisfactory progress.  
 
Recommendation no. 5- Regular Conducting of Standing Audit Committee 
(SAC) Meetings in Ministries/Departments 
9. The Committee note that as per Para No. 12 of Report No. 1 of the 16 thLok 
Sabha, all Ministries/Departments were required to hold SAC meetings on a monthly 
basis, under the chairmanship of Joint Secretary (JS) level or above and details of 
such meetings were required to be uploaded on the APMS portal. The Committee, 
however, find that either SAC meetings are not being conducted regularly in some 
Ministries/Departments or the details of such meetings are not being uploaded on 
the APMS portal in a timely m anner. The Committee emphasise the importance of 
roles played by SAC meetings in settlement of Audit paras and recommend that the 

 
24 
 
Ministries/Departments should ensure regular meetings of SAC and timely 
uploading of the minutes of the same on the APMS portal.  
 
Recommendation No.6 -  Replies on PAC Recommendations and Explanatory 
Notes 
10. The Committee note that Action Taken Replies on 224 PAC paragraphs 
pertaining to various Ministries are pending as on 21.10.2025 which came down to 
170 as on 23.04.2026.  The Committee emphasise that once recommendations are 
made in its Reports, it is incumbent upon the concerned Ministries/Departments 
concerned to submit Action Taken Replies within the stipulated time period. The 
Committee, therefore, urge all Ministries/Departments to ensure timely submission 
of Action Taken Replies so that the follow-up action on the recommendations of the 
Committee are not delayed. 
 
11. The Committee also note that 163 explanatory notes relating to savings and 
excess expenditure exceeding ₹100 crore were pending submission as on 
21.10.2025. Of these, 59 explanatory notes were pending with 
Ministries/Departments and 104 were pending with Audit for vetting. The Committee 
observe that such explanatory notes are essential for enabling the Committee to 
understand the reasons for substantial varia tions between budget estimates and 
actual expenditure. The Committee emphasise that Ministries/D epartments should 
accord high priority to preparation and submission of explanatory notes so that 
cases of significant savings or excess expenditure can be examined in a timely 
manner. The Committee desire that Ministry of Finance, Department of Expenditure, 
Monitoring Cell to issue necessary advisory in this regard to all 
Ministries/Departments. 
 
Recommendation No.7-  Review and upgradation of APMS Portal  
12. The Committee further note that in certain cases, delays in submission of 
ATNs have been attributed to technical or procedural issues related to the APMS 
portal. While the Committee appreciate the efforts made by the Monitoring Cell under 
the Controller G eneral of Accounts (Department of Expenditure) to streamline the 
process through the APMS portal, the Committee are of the opinion that further 

 
25 
 
improvements in the system/portal are required to enhance efficiency and to make 
it user-friendly. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Finance 
(Department of Expenditure) to undertake a comprehensive review of the 
functioning of the APMS portal with a view to strengthen its monitoring and 
reporting capabilities and make the interface user friendly and take necessary action 
to upgrade the software /portal. The Committee also desire that the Department of 
Expenditure may explore the possi bility of incorporating the provision of alert 
notifications in the portal to appropriately alert the nodal officers of 
Ministries/Departments about approaching timelines.  
 
Recommendation No.8-  Adequate manpower deployment in Monitoring Cell 
13. The Committee observe that the Monitoring Cell is functioning with minimal 
administrative and technical staff. In view of the volume, complexity, and criticality 
of its functions relating to the Portal, and the requirement of upgradation of the 
portal, the Committee recommend that manpower may be adequately strengthened 
in the Monitoring Cell to enhance efficiency and enable the Cell to contribute 
significantly to improved governance outcomes. 
 
Recommendation No.9- Punitive action against officials found responsible for 
delay in settlement of Audit paras 
14. The Committee, during the course of its examination, find that several audit 
reports have remained pending settlement for a prolong period of time, in one case 
extending to almost more than a decade. The Committee observe that such delays 
in settlement of  audit paras result in dilution of the intended impact of the Audit 
Report for corrective actions to be taken. The Committee emphasise that the 
challenges impeding the settlement of these long pending paragraphs must be 
addressed with utmost priority. The Committee are of the view that persistent delays 
lead to systemic weaknesses within administrative structures resulting in recurring 
audit observations and thereby defeating the purpose of audit and parliamentary 
scrutiny. While reiterating the importance of adhering to the prescribed timelines, 
the Committee recommend that strict action be taken against officials found 
responsible in cases of undue delay in settlement of pending audit paragraphs. The 
Committee further recom mend that the Ministries may i ncorporate provisions for 

 
26 
 
appropriate punitive action against responsible officials as part of their internal 
mechanisms to address delays in submission of Action Taken Notes.  
 
Recommendation no. 10 - Production of records 
15. During the course of examination of the settlement of non-selected paras, 
it was also brought to the notice of the Committee that, in certain cases, the 
Ministries find it difficult to retrieve old documents and are thus incapable of 
providing documentary evidence required by Audit for final settlement of the 
said para. Keeping this in view, the Committee stress the need to preserve 
relevant documents and records. The Committee recommend that the Ministries 
and Departments may ensure provisions in internal office manuals and relevant 
documents pertaining to audit objections till final settlement of audit paragraphs 
and explore the feasibility of digitization of records. 
 
 
Monitoring Cell and O/o C&AG 
Recommendation No. 11 - Uploading of Recommendations and Illustrative 
Cases 
16. The Committee take note of the submissions of Audit and Ministry regarding 
pendency arising due to illustrative cases along with recommendations in 
performance audit and compliance audit reports of the C&AG. The Committee note 
from the submission of the Mi nistry of Finance, Department of Expenditure that all 
the Illustrative Cases and Recommendations made in such C&AG Reports are 
uploaded as a single Audit Para on APMS Portal by the Office of the C&AG which 
makes it difficult for the Ministry to upload indi vidual responses. The Committee 
note that as a consequence, even if response to one illustrative case is left, the entire 
para is treated as unsettled. The Committee also note that responses to Illustrative 
cases require collation of data from field authorities and concerned divisions of both 
Ministries and the Office of the C&AG, and are therefore often delayed, resulting in 
delays in their timely settlement. The Committee are of the opinion that it is 
important that the illustrative cases are responded to  and settled in the prescribed 
time frame. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Monitoring Cell and O/o 
C&AG may deliberate and explore viable solutions to this issue and thereafter, 

 
27 
 
necessary modifications of the portal may be undertaken to ensure that it accurately 
reflects the status of pendency against each Ministry. The Committee would like to 
be apprised of the steps taken in this regard. 
 
Ministry of Commerce 
Recommendation No. 12- Coordination between Department of Commerce and 
Department of Revenue-CBIC 
17. During examination of pendency related to Department of Commerce, the 
Committee found that the C&AG Report 6 of 2008 remains pending t ill date.  After 
more than 18 years since the laying of the C&AG Report, the Committee are alarmed 
to find the settlement of the said C&AG Report still pending. The Committee were 
informed by the Department of Commerce during their deposition that out of 32 
paragraphs contained in the said report for settlement, 30 paras pertaining to 
Department of Commerce have been sett led and only 2 sub -paragraphs (para nos. 
4.7.2.2 and 4.7.2.3) pertaining to the Department of Revenue – CBIC remain pending. 
The Committee were further informed that the records required by the office of the 
C&AG to settle the paras have not been received from the Department of Revenue-
CBIC. The Committee observe the serious lack of coordination between the two 
departments impeding the settlement of the concerned paragraphs and recommend 
that the Departments of Revenue -CBIC and Department of Commerce may 
immediately convene and coordinate with each other as well as with the Office of 
C&AG for expeditious settlement of the pending paragraphs and apprise the 
Committee of the status thereof. 
 
Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue (CBDT) 
Recommendation no. 13-  Expeditious settlement of pending paras in 
coordination with Audit  
18. The Committee note that a large number of audit paragraphs in respect of the 
Department of Revenue-CBDT are pending settlement, some of them going back to 
the year 2013 and several having undergone multiple journeys with the Audit. The 
Committee also obse rve from the submissions of the Audit that a difference in 
settlement of number of ATNs is also being claimed in some cases. The Committee 
find that in many instances, the replies are being given to the Audit without 

 
28 
 
providing any key supporting documents which prolongs the process resulting in 
multiple journeys of the ATNs before the final settlement. The Committee are also 
aware that many of the audit paragraphs found to be pending in case of CBDT, were 
either due to court cases or involve interpretation of tax laws or unavailability of any 
appropriate remedial action due to lapse of time -barring date for the application of 
the law . Considering the entirety of the issue, the Committee recommend the 
Ministry of Finance , Department of Revenue -CBDT and the Office of the C&AG to 
jointly convene and undertake expeditious settlement of all the pending audit paras 
which are more than two years old, and address them on mission mode basis. The 
Committee may be apprised of the actions taken in this regard.

2. At the outset, the Convener, Sub -Committee VI of PAC on Non -Compliance by 
Ministries/Departments in timely submission of Action Taken Notes on non-selected Audit 
Paragraphs & Excess Expenditure - welcomed the Members of the Committee and Audit 
Officers to the sixth sitting of the Sub -Committee, convened to take oral evidence of 

 
50 
 
representatives of Ministry of Defence on “Non-compliance in timely submission of Action 
Taken Notes on non-selected audit paragraphs and excess expenditure”. 
3. The Convener requested Audit officers to brief the Sub -Committee with their 
inputs on Ministry of Defence regarding submission of Action Taken Notes by them. 
Audit Officers briefed the Sub-Committee on status of pendency against Ministry of 
Defence and informed of certain common issues seen by them leading to pendency 
such as not following the prescribed format, lack of requisite approvals, lack of 
necessary revisions and lack of supporting documents. 
4. The witnesses were then called in. The Convener welcomed the Defence 
Secretary and other representatives of the Ministry to the sitting and requested them 
to give the status and reasons for pendency in submission of action taken notes. 
The Ministry apprise d the Committee of the status of pending audit paras, recent 
progress made in reducing pendency, internal mechanism for monitoring audit 
paragraphs and submission of ATNs, including regular review meetings and other 
efforts for minimizing pendency. 
5. Members raised concerns regarding delays in submission of ATNs, non -
inclusion of audit comments in revised ATN, and long -pending audit observations. 
The Committee also discussed the pending audit paras taking them up one by one 
and raised issues relating to delays in pension disbursement, procurement matters, 
project execution, internal accountability, and follow-up action.  
6. The Ministry informed the Committee about creation of a digitized database for 
pensioners, improved monitoring systems, inquiries in specific matters, coordination 
with service headquarters and formations. The Ministry further apprised the 
Committee regarding ongoing investigations relating to Air force project completions 
and other remedial actions taken in view of audit paras and was asked to furnish 
written replies to the information sought.  
7. The Convener then thanked the Defence Secretary and all officers of the 
Ministry of Defence for appearing before the Sub -Committee and for sharing their 
views. The Convener also thanked the Audit officers for briefing the Sub-Committee 
on the said subject. 
The Sub-Committee then adjourned.
