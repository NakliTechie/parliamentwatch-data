A. Teledensity & Rural T elephony
(Paragraph No. 5)
5. In their Thirtieth Report, the Committee, taking note of the
Private Sectors dynamism in the impressive growth of urban telephony,
had desired that the entrepreneurial skill displayed by Private Operators
in urban areas should be extended to rural areas also in view of the
meagre rural teledensity. The Committee also recommended that the
Department of Telecommunications should make elaborate plans and
extend all possible support to the service providers in this regard.
6. The Department in their Action Taken Note have stated that
tenders were floated for extending subsidy support for installation,
operation and maintenance of individual rural household DELs (RDELs)
in 1685 eligible SDCAs out of the total of 2647 SDCAs in the country,
where cost of providing telephone connection is more that the revenue
earned. This covers about 64% of the total geographical area covered
by telecommunications. A total number of 7 Service Providers
participated in the tendering process and there was keen competition
amongst the bidders. As an outcome of the competitive bidding,
agreements were signed in March 2005 with four successful bidders,
namely Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited, Reliance Industries Limited,
Tata Telecommunications Limited and Tata Telecommunications Limited
(Maharashtra) for provision of RDELs after 01.04.2005. The share of
SDCAs amongst Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited, Reliance Industries
Limited, Tata Telecommunications Limited and Tata Telecommunications
Limited (Maharashtra) is 1267, 203, 172 and 43 SDCAs respectively.
About 7,47,151 RDELs have been provided by Bharat Sanchar Nigam
Limited (4,40,286), Reliance Industries Limited (75,510), Tata
Telecommunications Limited (2,02,217) and Tata Telecommunications
Limited (Maharashtra) (29,138) till July, 2006.

3
The Department have further stated that the growth in urban areas
has come primarily through mobile telephones. It is cheaper to provide
mobile connections and the roll out is faster. With this in mind, the
proposal regarding extension of subsidy support for creation of
infrastructure to provide mobile services in rural and remote areas has
been got approved. Subsidy support is proposed to be provided for
shareable components like land, tower, power connection, power back
up, boundary wall etc. and for non-shareable components like outdoor
BTS with antennas and a portion of the backhaul. The infrastructure
shall be created in those specified areas where there is no existing
mobile coverage and the same shall be shared amongst the telecom
service providers. While a bill to amend Indian Telegraph Act has
been introduced in Parliament, the President has promulgated an
ordinance “The Indian Telegraph (Amendment) Ordinance, 2006” to
amend Section 3 of Indian Telegraph Act, 1885 thereby extending
subsidy support for creation of infrastructure to promote mobile services
in rural and remote areas.
7. The Committee are aware that the growth in urban areas has
come primarily through mobile telephones as it is cheaper to provide
mobile connections and the roll out is faster. Keeping this in mind
the Committee, in their Thirty-Fourth Report, had agreed with the
proposal for extending subsidy support from USOF for creation of
infrastructure to promote mobile services in rural and remote areas.
The Committee note with satisfaction that the proposal regarding
extension of subsidy support in terms of shareable components like
land, tower, power connection etc. and non-shareable components
like outdoor BTS with antennas for creation of infrastructure to
provide mobile services in rural and remote areas has already been
got approved by the Government. The Committee feel that the step
taken in this regard is in the right direction and desire the
Government to act faster and extend subsidy support to create
shareable infrastructure for provision and expansion of mobile
services in the hitherto neglected rural and remote areas. The
Committee also recommend that the Department should ensure that
the Service Providers set up the required number of Rural Direct
Exchange Lines (RDELs) in the designated Short Distance Charging
Areas (SDCAs) as per the contractual agreements signed in March,
2005.
B. Lesser Utilisation of funds by BSNL
(Paragraph No. 16)
8. Observing lesser utilization of funds by BSNL during the years
2003-2004 and 2004-2005 involving non-completion of Projects/Schemes

4
and non finalization of the accounts for the year 2005-2006, the
Committee in their earlier Report impressed upon BSNL, to take
corrective measures in order to ensure timely completion of Projects/
Schemes targeted during the year 2006-2007 and thereby optimally
utilizing the outlay proposed for the fiscal.
9. The Department in their Action Taken Note have stated that the
actual capital expenditure incurred during 2005-06 was Rs. 7248 crores
against RE sanctioned for Rs. 15463 crores. This is due to non-
completion of some of the projects in time and therefore, non release
of payments against such projects. BSNL, being a Public Sector
Undertaking, has to follow transparent departmental procedure which
certainly takes more time.
Regarding full utilization of funds during 2006-07, the Department
have stated that all efforts are being made to minimise procedural
delays and thus completing the projects in time. Since major thrust is
on Cellular Mobile, BSNL has set up project coordination team in each
of the circles which is monitoring these projects on daily basis. Also,
BSNL Head Quarter is regularly monitoring the project through weekly
reports and through monthly zonal meetings. BSNL is also pursuing
the matter with WPC for availability of spectrum and with respective
agencies for timely availability of power connections, land and
permission for installation of towers.
10. The Committee are highly dissatisfied to note that the actual
capital expenditure incurred by BSNL during the year 2005-2006 was
Rs. 7248 crore against the RE sanctioned amount of Rs. 15,463 crore
reportedly due to non-completion of some of the Projects in time.
The Department’s statement that BSNL being a Public Sector
Undertaking has to follow transparent Departmental procedure which
certainly takes more time does not convince the Committee in view
of the fact that less than fifty per cent utilization of the approved
amount by the Company in a particular year cannot be condoned,
reasons whatsoever notwithstanding, least of all for following
transparent procedure which is expected in every PSU. The
Committee, therefore, desire that the Department should make all
efforts to remove the procedural delays for optimum utilisation of
funds and ensure timely completion of the projects as per the targets
laid down in this behalf.

D. Outstanding dues of BSNL
(Paragraph No. 21)
14. Being not satisfied with the measures taken by BSNL to recover
an amount of approximately Rs. 4000 crores which was outstanding as
on 31 December, 2005 against the Basic, WLL and cellular subscribers
of the company, the Committee in their earlier report had urged BSNL
to take recourse to additional effective methods for early recovery of
the outstanding dues.
15. The Department in their Action Taken Note have stated that
the outstanding dues of telephone bills as on 31.03.06 were Rs. 3736.04 crore
(Rs. 3299.46 crore for landline services and Rs. 436.58 crore for mobile
services). BSNL was able to reduce Rs. 92.64 crore outstanding of basic
services just in 2 months against issue of bills of Rs. 3169.13 crore during
Feb-06 to Mar-06.
The Department have further submitted that the outstanding dues
do not belong to one particular year but have accumulated over a
number of years. The Telecom market has undergone change over the
past 10 years. Deregulation and increase in number of service providers,
has made it easier than ever before for the customers to switch between
service providers so that disconnection is no longer a threat as it once
was. An incumbent operator like BSNL, who, in a monopoly
environment was able to use threat of disconnection to ensure payment,
suddenly found that its customer base was able to switch over to
other service providers, leaving unpaid bills behind. Also few customers
come forward for reconnection after disconnection, as they are able to
switch over from landline to mobile/land line of other operators.
It has also been stated that if the outstanding dues as on 31.03.2006
are compared with total cumulative billing from 1991-92 to 2005-06,
the percentage of outstanding dues to total billing during the period
are less than 2% (1.62%). However, BSNL is very much aware of its
outstanding dues and is taking all necessary steps as enumerated below
for recovery of the same:—
(a) Instructions have been given for separate monitoring of
Billing & Collections of High Callers who contribute major
revenue for the department.

9
(b) The phone is permanently closed after 3 months from the
date of disconnection. If outstanding can be adjusted against
the deposit, it is adjusted and the balance deposit is
refunded to the customer. If outstanding is more than the
deposit: the balance outstanding amount after adjusting
deposit is transferred to pursuit cell for recovery. Instruction
have been issued to the circles to hold meeting of liquidation
boards and high power committees on time and at regular
intervals and give suitable directions for recovery and write
off of dues, which seems irrecoverable. Recovery and write
off policies are being reviewed and monitored so that pace
of liquidation and recovery is increased.
(c) The State Governments have been requested to amend land
revenue act so that Telephone bill defaulter is also treated
as land revenue defaulters.
(d) BSNL Head Quarters has authorised Heads of circles to
Appoint private Recovery agents etc. on commission basis
to assist BSNL in recovery. State Governments have also
been taken into confidence to assist BSNL in recovery of
outstanding dues.
(e) Instructions have been issued to the circles for payment of
discount ranging between 10% to 20% to subscribers on
payment of outstanding dues of over 2 years.
(f) Existing Write off policy is also being modified for
simplification of write off procedure and delegating more
powers to circles to write off dues expeditiously. The tread
of accumulation of outstanding may not be seen in years to
come as company’s major chunk of revenue shall be realized
in advance through pre-paid services being introduced in
fixed line segments.
(g) Instruction has also been issued to the circles/SSAs to
authorize them to settle the cases of defaulters (including
court cases) through Lok Adalats or through out of court
cases by offering the discount package between 10% to 20%
for 2 years old outstanding dues along with waival of
interest and adjustment of legal expenses with discount.
(h) Pre-paid service has also been introduced to increase
collection efficiency and to minimize instances of default by
subscribers.
16. The Committee note that the outstanding dues of BSNL
telephone bills as on 31 March, 2006 stood at Rs. 2736.04 crore, the

10
breakup being Rs. 3299.46 crore for landline services and Rs. 436.58
crore for mobile services. BSNL’s claim of recovery of Rs. 92.64 crore
in just two months i.e. February & March, 2006 does not satisfy the
Committee in view of the whopping amount, i.e.  more than
Rs. 3,600 crore which is yet to be recovered from the defaulting
subscribers. The Committee are well aware that the outstanding dues
do not belong to one particular year, but have accumulated over a
number of years. That is precisely why the Committee have been
recommending in almost all their Reports on Demands for Grants
that BSNL should take all prudent measures to recover the
outstanding dues from the defaulting subscribers. But it seems that
due to lack of the required seriousness on the part of the Company
to recover the outstanding dues, the same has taken a huge
proportion over the years. So far as the monopoly environment in
which BSNL was able to use threat of disconnection to ensure
payment was concerned it was to end sooner than later due to the
rapid changing telecom scenario and BSNL should have foreseen
such a situation and taken adequate preacutionary measures.
However, now that BSNL is taking all the necessary steps for recovery
of the outstanding dues, the Committee hope that BSNL would
succeed in its endeavour and check the outstanding dues from
compounding further. The Committee would like to be kept informed
of the status of the recovery.

F. Sharing of cell sites/towers
(Paragraph No. 30)
20. Recognising the need and benefit of sharing cell sites/towers
across the country amongst various telecom service providers, the
Committee in their earlier Report impressed upon the Department to
make a study of the extent of sharing of cell sites/towers among the
service providers, maintain a statistics thereof and subsequently promote
and incentivise such sharing.
21. The Department in their Action Taken Note have stated that
they have initiated action to promote sharing of towers in the urban
areas and a scheme to promote creation of shared infrastructure in the
rural areas is likely to be launched shortly.

Recommendation (Paragraph No. 5)
The Committee note that as on 31 December, 2005, the overall
teledensity of the country stood at 11.32 per 100 population, the urban-
rural breakup being 34.77 and 1.79 respectively. Commercial unviability
of providing communication infrastructure in rural areas and lack of
infrastructure coupled with irregular power supply and difficult
topography have been cited as reasons for the unimpressive growth of
rural telephony. Provision of rural fixed line individual connections in
1,685 commercially unviable Short Distance Charging Areas (SDCAs)
with support from Universal Service Obligation fund and provision of
shared infrastructure for mobile services in rural areas are some of the
measures reportedly being taken by the Department to facilitate speedy
penetration of telecom services in rural areas. The Committee recognize
the fact that the private sector, displaying dynamism and
entrepreneurial spirit, has played a significant role in the growth of
telecom services in the country. But that spirit has largely been confined
to the urban areas as a result of which the teledensity in rural areas
has remained abysmally low. The Committee, therefore, desire that the
dynamism and entrepreneurial skill displayed by the Private Telecom
Service Providers in the urban areas should be extended to the rural
areas also in order to give a fillip to the spread of telecommunication
services in such areas. The Department, on their part, should make
elaborate plans and extend all possible support to the service providers
in this regard to tap the immense rural potential so that the growth
of rural telephony keeps pace with the impressive growth in urban
connectivity.

found that its customer base was able to switch over to other service
providers, leaving unpaid bills behind. Also few customers come

over from landline to Mobile/Land line of other operators.

cumulative billing from 1991-92 to 2005-06, the % age of outstanding
