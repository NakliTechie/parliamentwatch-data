 INTRODUCTORY 
 
  In this era of information, telecommunication plays a significant role in the growth and development of the 
various sector s of the economy of a Country. In India, the various telecom reform s have brought  important 
changes beginning with the announcement of National Telecom Policy, 1994 that liberalized the telecom sector. 
This was followed by the National Telecom Policy, 1999 that allowed service providers to migrate from fixed licence 
fee regime to a revenue sharing regime and now with the announcement of the formulation of a comprehensive 
National Telecom Policy, 2011 it is pertinent to see how the Government would fulfill its objective of setting up of a 
world class telecom infrastructure and information which would bring about a rapid socio-economic development 
in the Country. In recent times, India has emerged as one of the fastest growing telecom markets in the world, 
following the unprecedented growth in the mobile telephony. With its latest figure of 806.13 million telephone 
connections, India has become a Nation which has the second largest network in the world after China. 
 
2. The Departm ent of Telecommunications (DoT)  is responsible for formulation of Telecom Policy of the 
Country. It also carries out important functions such as international cooperation, wireless spectrum management, 
Research & Development and grant of licences to operators for providing basic and value added services in various 
cities and telecom circles as per appr oved policy of the Government. It is also responsible for looking after the 
various programmes/schemes carried out by its different wings as well as the various Universal Service Obligation 
(USO) activities carried out with the help of Universal Service Ob ligation Fund (USOF). Another important function 
is to look after the matters relating to its Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), Autonomous and Regulatory Bodies 
and is also responsible for the administration of the Indian Telegraph Act, 1885, Indian Wirel ess Telegraphy Act, 
1933 and Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997.  
 
3. The Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) of the PSUs i.e. BSNL and MTNL fund the development 
and expansion of telecommunication activities. The Gross Budgetary Supp ort (GBS) funds the 
programmes/schemes carried out by Wireless Planning and Coordination (WPC), Wireless Monitoring Organisation 
(WMO), Telecom Engineering Centre (TEC), Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI), Telecom Dispute s 
Settlement and Appellat e Tribunal (TDSAT), Centre for Development of Telematics (C -DoT), and the f ive 
Departmental projects i.e. Setting up of Telecom Testing and Security Certification Centre (TETC), Technology 
Development and Investment Promotion (TDIP), Undersea Cabling betwe en Mainland and Andaman & Nicobar 
(UM&AN), Optical Fibre Cable based network for Defence Services (DS), etc..  
 

2 
 
4. The budgetary support is also provided for executing the Universal Service Support Policy (USSP) through 
the Universal Service Obligation Fund (USOF) for bringing rural telecom connectivity. The resources for meeting the 
same are generated through a Universal Service Levy (USL) which is 5 per cent of the Adjusted Gross Revenue 
(AGR) earned by all the telecom operators except pure value added se rvice providers like internet service provider, 
voice mail etc. As per the information furnished by the Department, up to 2010 -11, USOF schemes/activities were 
part of non -plan expenditure, however, from the year 2011-12 onwards, all expenditure on account  of USOF 
schemes/activities has been made part of Plan expenditure by the Ministry of Finance with the approval of the 
Finance Minister. 
 
5. For the year 20 11-12, Demands for Grants of Rs. 9773.78 crore which includes Rs. 8745.82 crore as 
Revenue Outlay (c omprising Rs. 4490.04 crore as Plan Outlay and Rs. 4255.78 crore as Non -Plan Outlay) and                 
Rs. 1027.96 crore (Plan) as Capital Outlay were laid in Lok Sabha on 16 March, 2011. The Demands for Grants for 
2011-12 were passed on 17th March, 2011  by Parliament after the suspension of Rule 331G of the Rules of 
Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha.  However, as per the  ruling of the Hon’ble Speaker, Lok Sabha, 
the Committee may examine the Demands and make Report thereon.  
6. The Committee  in this Report have inter-alia analysed the position of Outlay s and the expenditure, 
particularly the performance of the Plan schemes which are carried out with the help of Outlays provided under 
IEBR, GBS and USOF.

V. Thrust Areas of the Department 
 
 Network Expansion 
 
19. The Committee have been informed that the target of 600 million connections by 2012  has already been 
achieved by February, 2010.   
Wireline vs. Wireless 
 
Declining Growth of Wireline in the last five years 
 
20. The Committee have been informed that t he percentage share of wireline connectivity vis -à-vis the total 
connectivity during the year 2005 was 42.11 per cent, but it has sharply declined to 5 per cent as on January, 2011. 
The situation is opposite in case of the wireless connectivity. During the year 2005, the perc entage share of 
wireless connectivity vis-à-vis the total connectivity in the country was 57.89 per cent, but it has grown to 95 per 
cent as on January, 2011.   
 
21. Enquired as to why in -spite of the various steps taken by the Department , the wireline con nectivity has 
shown a negative growth of 1.51 per cent from 31st March, 2010 (5.95 per cent) to 31st December, 2010 (4.46 per 
cent), the Department has simply stated that it is a worldwide trend.  
 
The significance of wireline on broadband connectivity 
22. When asked whether any study has been conducted to find out the relation ship between the wireline and 
broadband connectivity, the Department has stated that at present there is no plan  to have any study , however, a 
scheme for creation of National Optical Fibre Network (NOFN) for providing broadband connectivity to the 
Panchayats by extending the existing Optical Fibre Network is being formulated.  
  
23. During evidence, the representative  of the Department while elaborating on the plan for wireline and 
wireless broadband stated as under:- 
“After the auctions have been over, now we are set for the launch of wire line broadband in the 
rural areas.  We are almost ready with the plan, and this wire line broadband is required because 
the reach of wireline in the  rural areas is limited.  There are many areas which ar e not possible to 
cover by wire line.  Hence , we are taking up this wireless broadband, and it is also cost effective 
wherever wire line is not there.” 
 
Public Sector Vs. Private Sector 
 
 
 
, Declining share of Public Sector in the last five years 
 
24. As per the information furnished by the Department, the total share of the public sector in the network 
expansion has declined from 52.95 per cent in 2005 to 15.40 by the end of December, 2010.  In case of private 
sector, the total percentage share has increased from 47.05 per cent to 84.60 per cent during the same period.    

7 
 
Market Share of Public Sector Vs. Private Sector 
25. As per the data furnished by the Department, the market share of public sector both for wireline and 
wireless services showed a decline . The wireline showed a decline by 1.95 per cent from March, 2010 to January, 
2011. During the same period the wireless service s also showed the decline from 12.76 per cent to 12.22 per cent. 
Contrary t o this, the private sector showed a growth of 1.95 per cent in the wireline service and witnessed a 
growth from 87.24 per cent to 87.78 per cent in the wireless service during the aforesaid period. 
26. In reply to a specific query whether the Department has ever reviewed/propose to review the performance 
of its two PSUs i.e. MTNL and BSNL vis-a-vis the performance of other private service providers, the Committee 
have been informed that BSNL/MTNL Management are completely free to decide and finalize its com mercial and 
business polices.  However, BSNL/MTNL has been signing Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the 
Department on yearly basis as per the MoU guidelines of the Department of Public Enterprises.  Besides, there are 
various interactions which take place periodically to assess the overall performance, the quality of service offered 
by them, suggestions for improving the services etc. 
27. With regard to 3G services by BSNL, the Department has stated that it was launched commercially on 27th 
February 2 009 in 12 selected cities.  23,86,348 of 3G service subscribers are working in BSNL network as on 
28.2.2011.  Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited has proposed to provide 3G services in total 760 cities in first phase 
covering all the district headquarters, commer cially important towns and tourist places.    Bharat Sanchar Nigam 
Limited has launched 3G services in 758 cities as on 11.2.2011.       
 
VI. Rural Telephony 
 
Urban Teledensity vs. Rural Teledensity 
 
28. The Committee have been informed that out of 806.13 million telephone subscribers in the country, 268.16 
million are in rural areas (3 2.19 per cent of the total phones) and the remaining 5 37.97 million are in the urban 
areas (67.81 per cent of the total phones) as on January, 2011.  The overall teledensity which was 52.74 per cent as 
on 31 March, 2010 now stands at 6 7.67 per cent as on January, 2011.  The increase in the rural teledensity during 
the same period is stated to be from 24.31 per cent to 3 2.19 per cent, whereas, the urban teledensity has shown 
an increase from 119.45 per cent to 150.20 per cent.  
 
29. In the above context, the Committee  enquired about the major areas that have been identified to improve 
rural telephony especially for the year 2011 -12. In reply it was stated that some of the import ant identified areas 
inter-alia include financial support for provision of wireline, wireless broadband,  broadband kiosks and Optical 
Fibre Cable (OFC) network especially in North East region.

VII. USOF Schemes:  Rural Telephony  
 
Newly Identified VPTs (as per Census 2001) 
 
30. A total of 62443 new villages were identified as per Census 2001 to be covered under the Newly Identified 
VPTs scheme. As per the target, 90 per cent of the VPTs w ere to be covered by August, 2010 and the remaining 10 
per cent by Fe bruary, 2011. However, as on December, 2010 only 45783 i.e. 73.31 per cent VPTs have been 
installed and the deadline to complete the scheme has now been extended upto February, 2012. As on December, 
2010, Rs. 18.53 crore has been utilized out of a total Ou tlay of Rs. 21.53 crore provided for the year 2010 -11. For 
the year 2011 -12, an Outlay of Rs. 194 crore has been pro jected with a physical target to install 14160 VPTs by 
February, 2012.  
 
31. Some of the bottlenecks identified by the Department include villages being located in extreme remote 
areas, tough terrain, naxalite/ insurgency affected areas etc.   
 
Social Audit for the Telecom facilities provided with Government funding 
32. The Committee have been  informed that in order to involve Gram Panchayats  for social audit of telecom 
facilities provided with subsidy support from USO Fund, various Panchayats, are being consulted through the 
Department of Rural Development to ensure that the telecom facilities provided are maintained by the Services 
Providers effectively.  
 
33. In th e above context , the Committee enquired whether the consultation with Panchayats has been 
completed. In response, the Department has stated that the instructions ha ve been issued to all field   units i.e. 
Controller of Communication Accounts (CCAs) to interact with State/ Local Government Bodies to obtain necessary

49. When asked to furnish details about the 500 clandestine / illegal operation of telecom networks that have 
been busted by TERM Cells, the Department has explained to the Committee that Clandestine / illegal operation of 
telecom networks broadly means an illegal activity (not permitted und er Indian Telegraph Act) by persons not 
granted telecom license, causing loss to the ex chequer. For instances, illegal termination of International Long 
Distance (ILD) calls bypassing the authorized National Long Distance (NLD)/ International Long Distance  (ILD) 
operators’ networks. In such case, the offenders use the facility of leased lines, High speed Internet Connections or 
illegal VSAT (Very Small Aperture Terminal) Link with foreign satellite to connect the incoming international calls to 
the recipient Networks, bypassing the ILDOs international gateway and NLDOs. In most of such cases , Caller Line 

12 
 
Identification (CLI) is also tampered. The Department also added that s uch types of cases are also a security threat 
to the Nation, as these type of calls may escape monitoring.

“TERM Cells broadly have following sources/ methods to find out the clandestine activity/ grey 
market operations: 
 Through the complaints booked by public on the telephone no. 1800 -110-420 of 
DOT 
 Through complaint/ information received by any other means 
 Through Observation of unusual traffic in operators networks 
 Through social contacts 
 Through already investigated/ under investigation cases 
 Through Security/Law enforcement Agencies 
 
Whenever such information is re ceived by TERM Cells, they examine/ analyse the 
information and identify the culprits/ location of illegal operation and then a raiding party

some infrastructure in India for interception of Blackberry Messenger (BBM) service in readable format, but the

look into all types of known encrypted communication has been constituted so that the issue can be dealt

necessary, with some other Ministries/Departments.  
, 
120. Elaborating on the issue, the department also informed the Committee that as regards the solution of the

31st January and again to 31 st March, 2011.  The Committee have now been informed by the 
Department regarding the constitution of the Committee to look into all types of encrypted 
communications. The expected date for submission of the Report by the aforementioned 
Committee was 20th April, 2011.  The Committee express unhappiness over the way the extension 
after extension are being given to resolve such an important issue related to security of the 
country.  Constitution of Committee for the purpose is another delaying tactics.  The Committee 
are of the firm opinion that no compromise can be made when it is the question of the security of 
the Nation.  As such, the issue need to be taken strongly with BlackBerry so that the matter is 
resolved.  The Committee would also like to recommend to analyse the position internationally to 
know how the issue has been addressed in other countries.      
 
Mobile Number Portability 
30. The Committee in their Eighth Report had recommended that the Department should 
address the issue raised by the security agenci es that the intercept message should not get 
affected due to the Mobile Number being ported from one operator’s network to other while 
implementing the Mobile Number Portability. Subsequently, the Committee have been informed 
that various rounds of discuss ions were held and views of the Security Agencies/Ministry of 
Home Affairs were sought on the final procedure before issuing the instructions.  The views of the 
Ministry of Home Affairs /Nodal Security Agency have been received and necessary secret 
instructions have been issued to the Service Providers addressing the security concerns in the 
porting process. However, the Committee are disheartened to note that there have been 
numerous instances across the country where the subscribers are making calls on th eir officially 
deactivated SIMS even after availing of Mobile Number Portability raising concerns of abuse and 
possible security risk.  The Committee are dismayed to note that instead of taking instant 
corrective measures to remedify the situation, the Dep artment has simply informed that a close 
watch on the matter is kept by the TERM Cells of the DoT and based on the feedbacks from the 
Telecom Service Providers and the subscribers, various instructions have been issued to resolve 

58 
 
the problem. While deplori ng the state of affairs concerning the Mobile Number Portability and 
security risk, the Committee strongly recommend that the Department should again take up the 
matter with the Ministry of Home Affairs and determine the potential security threats at the 
earliest. The Committee also recommend that the various provisions of the ‘Telecommunications 
Mobile Number Portability Regulations 2009 as amended’ should be followed by the various 
Telecom Service Providers in letter and spirit.  
 
TRAI on Pesky Calls 
31. The Committee note that in order to address the issue of pesky calls, the 
Department/TRAI have taken various steps which include technical upgradation of network to 
filter the unwanted telemarketing calls/SMSs and have brought about a regulation entitled ‘ The 
Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2010’. The Committee 
are, however, surprised to note that as per the Department most of the service providers except 
BSNL and MTNL are ready to implement the Regulation. As per the BSNL , the Regulation cannot 
be implemented as they are of the opinion that the Regulation to be brought out under 
Regulations 20(2)(h) and 20(2)(i) is not technically feasible for the BSNL at this stage. The BSNL 
has also has opined that it would entail a huge  avoidable capital burden and it is totally 
unwarranted for BSNL to implement the Regulation as they have no role with regard to 
unsolicited commercial communications. MTNL also informed the Committee that they have also 
similar problems to BSNL regarding the implementation of the Regulation. However, if at all they 
have to implement the TRAI Regulation, 2010, it will take them about one year time for arranging 
the filtering facility in their network. The Committee in their earlier Report had recommended 
that expeditious action should be taken by the Department so that the subscribers get respite 
from the pesky calls. However, the above observation clearly indicates that the Department has 
not been able to resolve the problems of pesky calls and therefore,  the Committee once again 
recommend that the Department should thrash out all the problems associated with pesky calls 
and at the same time address to the various difficulties expressed by the various service providers 
to meet the requirements of TRAI Regulations 2010.  
Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL) 
32. The Committee are constrained to note that in spite of concerns shown by the Committee 
year after year for improving the deteriorating condition/performance of BSNL, the PSU has been 
plagued by the vari ous problems. The scrutiny of the various documents with regard to the 
performance of BSNL reveal that the persistent problem of non -utilisation of Outlay has 
continued even during 2010 -11. The utilisation status of IEBR with respect to Revised Estimate in  

59 
 
the last four years of the Eleventh Plan upto February 2011 is only 45.07 per cent. The Committee 
further note with regret that the revenue earned by BSNL has declined considerably in the last 
four years. Year after year the revenue has declined and durin g 2009-10 it has incurred a loss of 
Rs. 1823 crore and for the year   2010 -11 the provisional unaudited figure as on 28 th February, 
2011 projects a loss of Rs. 5220 crore. For the year 2011 -12, the PSU has projected a loss of Rs. 
2380 crore as per MoU targ et. Besides, there are many areas of concern which include huge 
outstanding arrears, surrender of telephones, impact of Mobile Number Portability, etc. As per 
the documents furnished by the Department BSNL has an outstanding dues of Rs. 587.95 crore as 
on 31st January, 2011. Circle wise analysis of surrender of BSNL phone indicates that the net 
achievements for wireline during the last four years has been negative which means 
disconnection has always been more than connections. During 2007 -08, it was -2186308 and in 
2008-09, it was -2205865. For the year 2009 -10, the net achievements was -1515871 and for the 
year 2010-11 upto January, 2011, the net achievement is -2353240. The same trend was seen for 
the WLL phone and as on January, 2011, the net achievement  was -762920. Regarding Mobile 
Number Portability, the Committee are disheartened to note that as on 23 rd March, 2011 a total 
of 5,00,123 subscribers have ported-out of BSNL and 1,83,961 subscribers has ported-in into BSNL 
GSM Network. The Committee also n ote that BSNL inspite of commercially launching the 3G 
services on 27 th February, 2009 has not been able to carve a formidable position for itself. From 
the above observations, it clearly indicates that a lot needs to be done to improve the 
performance of BSNL. The Committee feel that BSNL being the Public Sector Undertaking should 
have taken various benefits given by the Government tactfully and should have competed with 
the private sector service providers more forcefully. With the declining growth of the  public 
sector in the network expansion as mentioned in the earlier part of the Report the PSU need to 
review the various aspects such as the quality of service, competitive price, easy reach and 
availability of its various products by its clientele. The C ommittee feel that besides the various 
schemes/projects taken up by BSNL with the help of USOF, it should also explore the possibility of 
capturing the rural market more vigorously. Not only that, BSNL should effectively provide 
broadband connection to the Community Service Centres (CSCs) in coordination with Department 
of Information Technology so as to ensure the various e -Governance programme carried out 
under the National e-Governance Plan are implemented effectively. 
Mahanagar Telephone Nigam Limited (MTNL) 
33. The Committee are disheartened to note that MTNL has not been able to put its house in 
order to ensure that precious funds meant for the development of telecom sector in the country 

60 
 
are optimally and judiciously utilized. The above observation of  the Committee can be 
substantiated by the fact that in the last four years, it has been able to utilize only 61.08 per cent 
of  the Outlays with respect to the Revised Estimates as on February, 2011. Further, the PSU has 
incurred huge financial loss since  the year 2009 -10. During 2009 -10, it had incurred a loss of Rs. 
2610.97 crore and during 2010 -11 it has incurred a loss of Rs. 1727.38 crore upto 31 st December, 
2010. As per MoU target, the PSU has projected a loss of Rs. 1140.11 crore for the year 2011-12. It 
has also a huge outstanding arrears of Rs. 2303.77 crore as on 28 th February, 2011. The 
Committee also note that year after year the surrender of telephones is also increasing especially 
in Mumbai. The surrender of telephone in Mumbai during 2008 -09 and 2009-10 were -66,118 and 
-82,606 respectively. For the year 2010 -11 till February, 2011, the total number of phones that 
have been surrendered in Mumbai sector is reported to be -51,763. In Delhi the gross connection 
both for wireline and wireless has declined over the years. During 2007 -08, the total gross 
connection was 1,48,966, however, declined to 89,559 connections as on February, 2011. The 
Committee also note with dissatisfaction that after the implementation of Mobile Number 
Portability, a total of 34698 subscribers have ported out of MTNL and only 7718 subscribers have 
ported in MTNL GSM Network. From the above observations, it clearly indicates that MTNL has to 
really gear up all its energies to overcome the various lacunae as pointed out by th e Committee 
and take corrective actions immediately. Needless to say that the PSU should crease out all the 
grey areas and set realistic and achievable targets and accordingly project their financial Outlay to 
ensure their effective and optimal utilisation. The Committee hope their concerns would be taken 
into account while initiating various schemes/projects by MTNL during the Twelfth Five Year Plan.  
 
Indian Telephone Industry (ITI) Limited 
 
34. The Committee are disheartened to note that inspite of vario us steps taken by the 
Department to transform ITI from a sick company to a vibrant institution, the revival plan of ITI 
has not borne fruit.  The Committee note that the strength of ITI lies in the strategic area of 
communication for Defence and the same h as been epitomized by the prestigious Army Static 
Switch Communication Network (ASCON) Project. It has also executed satellite project such as 
Carnation for Army, Earth Stations for Airforce and Civilcon for Ministry of Home Affairs. 
However, the Committee  are constrained to note that for the entire Eleventh Plan, the Planning 
Commission has made an allocation of only Rs. 2.01 crore out of the total Outlay of Rs. 105.87 
crore provided for the entire Eleventh Plan. Some of the initiatives taken by the Depart ment 
include ‘Revival Plan 2009’ which led to release of a sum of Rs. 2820 crore for discharging its 
liability to clean up its balance sheet as per the revival package, the policy of Reservation Quota 

61 
 
of 30 per cent of the BSNL/MTNL purchases of the teleco m equipments from ITI, release of Rs. 
180 crore during March, 2011 towards the working capital for ITI. Further, the Committee also 
note that the Department has given various advice to ITI which include chalking out of concrete 
strategic plan for its revival based on a thorough review of its working, operation, establishment, 
marketing strategy, setting long term goals through value proposition by designing a road map 
and initiative to bring out some unique value which ITI could present to its prospective 
customers, technology mapping and benchmarking vis-à-vis the current market scenario. The 
Committee feel that the various suggestions and advice given by the Department to ITI for its 
revival are in the right direction and feel that Department as well as IT I should take the revival 
plan forward so that the objectives of setting up ITI i.e. to develop strategic area of 
communication for Defence is met.

improve the telecom expansion in the North -East areas which inter-alia includes increase in

rings to cover all District Headquart er/Sub District Headquarters and en -route towns of Assam, 
NE-I and NE -II and expansion of broadband service in area using high speed ADSL2+ technology.

January, 2011 is only 61.19 per cent. The Committee also note that dur ing 2010 -11, out of the

Net Switching Capacity Lines (Wired+WLL +CMTS), Wireline and Wireless Connections, VPTs, 
Broadband capacities and Broadband connections. Out of the target of 6,50,366 Net Switching 
Capacity Lines, only 1,63,857 could be achieved as on 31 st December, 2010. During the said

per Census 2001, only 415 VPTs could be achieved out of the stipulated target of 2,477 VPTs.  The 
broadband capacity had made an achievement of only 16,388 ports out of the target of 40,367 
ports, whereas, the broadband connections could be made to only 26,194 out of the target of 
79,185 connections. This clearly indicates that the measures taken by the Department are not

is devoid of various communication infrastructure do not remain cut off from the rest of the 
Country.

Emerging Issue 
 
E-Waste 
 
36. The Committee note that the total quantity of e -Waste generated during 2007 was 
3,32,979 metric tonnes which is expected to increase to ab out 8 lakh metric tonnes by 2012. The 
e-Waste generated by mobile phones during 2007 is stated to be 1655 metric tonnes.  A number 
of Research and Development have been initiated at the various National Institutions in India 
which inter-alia include develo pment of processing technology for recycling and reuse of 
electronic waste, environmentally sound methods for recovery of metals from Polychlorinated 
Biphenyls (PCBs) and recovery and conversion of plastic from Waste Electrical and Electronic 
Equipment (WE EE) to value added products. The Committee also appreciate to note that the 
National Metallurgical Laboratory at Jamshedpur has successfully recycled upto 1 metric tonnes 
of e-Waste on pilot basis which include e -Waste out of mobile phones and is now attem pting to 
take it to possible commercial application. The Committee appreciate the initiatives taken by the 
various organizations to deal with the issue of e -Waste, nevertheless, they would like the 
Department to continue its efforts to overcome the potenti al threats of e -Waste taking into 
account that it contents hazardous constituents that negatively impact the environment and 
human health. The Department in coordination with DIT should also strive to have in place a 
legislation mandating electronic manufa ctures and importers to take back used electronic 

63 
 
products at the End-of-Life (EoL) based on the principle of Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) 
and the Committee informed accordingly.
