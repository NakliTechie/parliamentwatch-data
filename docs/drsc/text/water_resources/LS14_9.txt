CHAPTER-II 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS 
 
 Water is one of the most elementary  and necessary commodity for survival 
of life on earth.  The casual attitude towards use of water and global warming has 
forced people to think how to conserve a nd efficiently use it.  All over the world 
concerns have been raised  for development, conservation and management of the 
limited water resources through proper planning and coordination by all concerned 
towards the goal of sufficient and equitable distribution to all sections of society. 
2.2 The Ministry of Water Resources in th e Government of India is responsible 
for laying down policy guidelines for overall development, conservation and 
management of water as a national resource, evolve an overall national perspective 
and coordination in diverse water uses.  The activities of the Ministry includes 
general policy, technical assistance, resear ch and development,  training, matters 
relating to irrigation, protection and preservation of ground water resources, flood 
control and management, drought proofing, regulation and development of inter-
State river valleys, water quality assessmen t, water laws, legislation, international 
issues on water etc.

 “During the year 2008-09, the Plan allocation for central sector schemes is

regulation, (d) Research and development, and (e) Infrastructure 
development.  Increase in the scheme  for river management activities and

development has also been increased for taking up research in key areas

2.17 When asked as to why these vacancies are persisting in the above 
mentioned organizations and what efforts ha ve been made to f ill up the vacancies, 
the Secretary, during evidence stated as follows :- 
“For the Gazetted posts, we send the request to UPSC and receive 
allotment of officers through the Minist ry of Railways.  We have been 
requesting UPSC to send adequate numbe r of officers, but every year we 
are getting less number of officers.  Last year, we required 29 officers 
while 10 officers were sent.  As for promotions, we send the proposals to 
UPSC and then they take up DPC a nd send the names.  For the non-
Gazetted, we get the personnel from the Ministry of Personnel.  We also 
make some direct recruitment through employment exchanges.  Apart from 
this, there is a norm of the Ministry  of Finance called the ‘optimisation 
scheme.’  Every year, if some vacancy  arises, we can fill up only one-third 
of the vacancies.  Every year, we have  to abolish a number of posts.  That 
is why, the number is going down every year.  This is as per the 
Government policy.”    
 
2.18 Asked how the Ministry proposed to implement / monitor the many of up 
coming projects for which vacancies are required to be filled up particularly in the 
technical side, the Secretary, stated that the Ministry fully shares the concern of the 
Committee.  They have, therefore, already sought exemption from the 
Optimisation Scheme of the Ministry of Fi nance because if posts of officers in a 
technical Ministry like ours are vacant than the work cannot be accomplished.  
2.19 The steps taken to fill up the vacancies by the Ministry are as follows : 
(i) All posts are filled up either by direct recruitment or by promotion 
or by deputation.  Group A pos ts are filled up on the 
recommendation of UPSC.  Requisiti ons have been sent to UPSC 
from time to time for filling up of direct recruitment posts and 
proposals have been sent for filling up the vacancies by promotion 
and by deputation.  The matter is be ing continuously pursued with 
UPSC. 
(ii) The matter regarding direct re cruitment of Group B Gazetted posts 
has also been pursued with UPSC.  DoPT has been requested from 
time to time for providing services  of staff belonging to Central 
Secretariat Services in the secretar iat as well as attached offices.  
DoPT has also been requested to fill up the vacancies under the 
central staffing scheme.  The remain ing posts are being filled up by 
the concerned organizations. 
(iii) There are still some posts which are encadred in the Indian 
Statistical Service, Indian Economic Service, Civil Accounts 
Service, etc.  The respective cadre controlling authorities are 
requested for filling up these posts.

(iii) National Projects 
2.24 During the course of the Study Visits  of the Committee, the Committee has 
come across suggestions from several St ate Governments to consider certain 
projects as ‘National Pr ojects’ fully funded by Central Government.  The 
Committee in their 7 th Report on Demands for Gran ts (2007-08) desired the 
Ministry to formulate a policy for declar ing Major Irrigation Pr ojects as ‘National 
Projects’.  The Ministry in their furt her action taken repl y on the Action Taken 
Report (8 th Report) have stated that the concept of ‘National Projects’ was 
incorporated in the Cabinet Note dated 28.09.2007 which was considered by the 
Union Cabinet in its Meeting on 25.10.2007 and it was decided to refer the 
Cabinet Note to a Group of Ministers (GoM ).  The GoM in its first Meeting while 
discussing the proposal directed that a sepa rate note for ‘National Projects’ should 
be formulated and brought up before the next Meeting of the GoM.  The GoM also 
finalized criteria for selection of ‘N ational Projects’.  The GoM approved the 
revised note with some amendments.  The decision of the GoM are now to be 
brought up before the Union Cabinet for its approval.   
2.25 The Finance Minister in the Budg et Speech (2008-2009) stated that 
Government has declared 14 projects as  National Project of which 3 projects 
would require Rs. 7000 crore during the XI  Plan.  When asked, the concept / 
criteria of selection of a project as a National Proj ect and the f unding pattern 
adopted / approved for these projects, the Ministry in a written reply stated : 
“The criteria/concept for selection of  a project as a National Project as 
approved by the Union Cabinet is as under:  
 
(i) International projects where usage of water in India is required by 
a treaty or where planning and earl y completion of the project is 
necessary in the interest of the country. 
 
(ii) Inter-State projects which are dragging on due to non-resolution 
of inter-State issues relating to sharing of costs, rehabilitation, 
aspects of power production etc. , including river interlinking 
projects.  
(iii) Intra-state projects with additional potential of more than 2,00,000 
ha. and with no dispute regardin g sharing of water and where 
hydrology is established

The National Project will be eligible for 90% grant of the project cost”.

3.31 The management of water resources is  a highly complex task that involves 
multidisciplinary domains including da ta acquisition, numerical modeling, 
optimization, data warehousing, and the analysis of socio-economic, 
environmental and legal issues.  In view of water’s vital role in human life, there is 
a need for better design and optimal use of hydro-systems.  In this context, a 
rational analysis must be based on an a pproach that considers all related causes 
and effects and systematically evaluate s the various alternatives.  The water 
resources information system plays a vital role in achieving optimal utilization of 
the resources.  The objective of the scheme  is to provide support to (a) collection 
of the hydrological data/information from network of key station, (b) water quality 
assessment, (c) minor irrigation census for collecting reliable information about 
minor irrigation in the c ountry, (d) monitoring of ma jor & medium projects and 
collecting necessary information, and (e) to develop a water resources system and 
made it fully operational.

projected a grant requirement of Rs. 10,200 crore for the year 2008-09 to the 
Planning Commission against which Rs . 4,300 crore has been provided for 
AIBP for 2008-09. The Ministry would approach the Planning Commission 
for additional allocation at Revised Estimate stage.   The Committee expect 
the Ministry also to pursue the Stat e Governments to come forward with 
matching share of the funds which would enable them to mobilize the 
allocation of Rs. 10,200 crore projected earlier.  The Commi ttee, at the same 
time desire the Ministry to ensure that the funds allocated are released in time 
and are fully utilized by the States. 
7.22 The Committee observe that 23 new projects have been recommended 
by the Ministry of Water Resources to the Ministry of Finance for release of 
funds in 2007-08.  A perusal of the list of 23 projects reveal s that only a few 
States have cornered the major portion of the Central assistance.  While the 
Committee are happy to note that fe w States are taking initiative and 
submitting good proposals for getting Central Assistance under AIBP, many 
others have not been able to take ad vantage of the vast sums being provided 
as Central Assistance year after year.   They, however, desire the Ministry to 
encourage and assist other States to come forward with the viable proposals 
for more equitable share of funds among the States for AIBP so that the 
benefit of completed irrigation projects is available to the farming community 
evenly all over the country.

5. 2.51 The Committee observe that efficiency in water use is as important 
as creation of irrigati on potential and its fuller utilization.  The 
Committee are given to understand that  irrigation efficiency in most 
of the irrigation systems in th e country ranges between 25% and 
35% and in exceptional cases the usage is between 40% and 45%.  
The water for crop consumption per hectare of the gross irrigated 
area (4.8 feet) is much higher as co mpared to water use in USA (3 
feet).  The Working Group of  the National Commission on 
Integrated Water Resources Development Plan (NCIWRDP) has

irrigation purposes from surface water.  The Commission was of the

efficiency in ground water will be a fair approximation.  The

the assessment studies initiated by CWC completed in a time bound

to evolve and initiate measur es aimed at improving Water Use
