1.
 
Background
 
 
The Chit Funds Act, 1982 was enacted to provide for the regulation of chit funds
 
which are indigenous business in India and have conventionally satisfied the financial
 
needs of the
 
low
-
income households. The chit is a mechanism which combines credit 
and
 
savings in a scheme, in which a group of individuals come together for a pre
-
determined
 
duration and subscribe a certain sum of money by way of periodical 
instalments
 
and
 
each
 
such s
ubscriber, in his turn as determined by lot or by aucti
on or 
by tender or any other 
specified manner, gets the collected sum. In this way, people 
who are in need of funds and
 
those who want to save are able to meet their 
requirements simultaneously.
 
1.2
 
In
 
past, concerns had been expressed by various stakeholders regarding 
challenges
 
being faced by the chit business. Therefore, the Central Government 
constituted
 
a
 
Key
 
Advisory Group on Chit Funds to review the existing legal, regulatory 
and
 
institutional
 
fr
amework for Chit Funds and its efficacy and to suggest legal and 
regulatory initiatives
 
required for orderly growth of the said sector. The Key Advisory 
Group
 
submitted its
 
recommendations relating to improvements in the institutional and 
legal structure t
o further
 
develop the chit business in order to reduce the regulatory
 
burden of the chit business
 
and
 
to
 
protect the interest of the subscribers of the chits.
 
 
1.3
 
The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance (Sixteenth Lok Sabha), in its
 
twenty
-
first 
Report on Efficacy of Regulation of Collective Investment Schemes (CIS), 
Chit
 
Funds, etc.,
 
presented to the Parliament on 3
rd
 
December, 2015
 
had also 
recommended to finalise the legislative and administrative proposals
 
for strengthening 
and streamlining of
 
the registered Chit Fund sector. Further, the
 
Committee, in its thirty
-
fifth Report on the Action Taken by the Government on the
 
Recommendations contained in the twenty
-
first Report, had recommended the need to 
quickly
 
firm up the legislative and administ
rative proposals for the Chit Funds sector.
 
1.4
 
In view of the above,
 
the Government decided to 
amend the Chit Funds Act, 
1982.
 
The Chit Funds (Amendment) Bill, 2018 
which was referred to this Committee on 
27th April, 2018 
seeks to reduce the regulatory or compliance burden of the Chit Funds 
industry on the one hand, and also protect the interest of the subscribers of Chit Funds 

on the other hand. The amendments to the Chit Funds Act, 1982 have been proposed 
to facilitate or
derly growth of the Chit Funds sector, to remove bottlenecks being faced 
by the Chit Fund industry, and to enable greater financial access to people. The main 
beneficiary of the amendment would be the subscribers to Chit Funds as well as the 
registered Chi
t Funds industry.
 
 
Salient features of the
 
Chit Funds (Amendment) Bill, 2018
.
 
1.5
 
The Chit Funds (Amendment) Bill, 2018 proposes :
 
(a)
 
the use of "fraternity fund" also for chits by amending sections 2(b) and 11 
of the Chit Funds Act, 1982;
 
(b)
 
to allow the 
mandatory presence of two subscribers, as required under 
section 16(2) of the Act, either in person or through video conferencing duly 
recorded by the foreman;
 
(c)
 
that where the presence of the mandatory two subscribers was through 
video conferencing, the min
utes of proceedings should be signed by them 
within two days;
 
(d)
 
the increase of ceiling of foreman's commission from five percent to seven 
percent under section 21 of the Act;
 
(e)
 
to enable the foreman to have a right 
to
 
lien for the dues from subscribers, 
so th
at set
-
off is allowed by the chit fund for subscribers who have already 
drawn funds, so as to discourage default by them;
 
(f)
 
to amend section 85(b) of the Act, so as to confer power upon the State 
Government to specify the amount, by notification, upto which 
any chit fund 
shall be exempted under the said section.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
 
1.
 
Mobilising short
-
term funds to meet various personal needs including 
emergencies has been a chronic problem faced by the general public in a 
developing country like ours. They have to 
often resort to high
-
interest loans 
from money lenders and the informal sector for this purpose, which creates a 
huge burden for them. The banking sector has not yet been able to fill these gaps 
and meet the financial needs of the people to the desired ext
ent. In such a 
scenario, the Committee find that the Chit Funds (Amendment) Bill, 2018 
regulating the registered chit funds industry in the country is a welcome 
legislative proposal from the Government. The Committee believe that the chit 
funds sector has 
the potential to bridge the gap in the financial sector with proper 
regulation and safeguards. Accordingly, the Committee has examined the 
Amendment Bill in all its ramifications and would make the following 
recommendations for the Government's considerati
on, which will help this sector 
to modernise and grow with adequate safeguards for the public
.
 
2.
 
 
 
i).
 
The All India Association of Chit Funds has in their memorandum 
submitted to the Committee requested for allowing a chit fund company 
to mention under their
 
name as "A ROSCA Institution" (Rotating Savings 
& Credit Association) which, according to them will help in distinguishing 
their business from other unconnected business. The Bill already seeks 
to amend Sections 2(b) and 11 of the Chit Funds Act, 1982, by
 
incorporating the name "fraternity fund
" instead of the 
commonly known
 
"
chit fund
"
. While endorsing the prop
osed amendment of "fraternity 

fund", the Committee would also recommend that the Government may 
bring in further amendment to incorporate the above
-
mentioned 
suggestion of the chit funds industry
 
by adding the words "A ROSCA 
Institution" to the nomenclature "fraternity fund" as already proposed in 
the 
B
ill
. These changes may help them in their image make
-
over and 
brand building.
 
ii).
 
The All India Associa

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
The Chit Funds Act, 1982 was enacted to provide for the regulation of chit funds
which are indigenous business in India and have conventionally satisfied the financial
needs of the low-income households. The chit is a mechanism which combines credit and
savings in a scheme, in which a group of individuals come together for a pre-determined
duration and subscribe a certain sum of money by way of periodical instalments and each
such subscriber, in his turn as determined by lot or by auction or by tender or any other
specified manner, gets the collected sum. In this way, people who are in need of funds and
those who want to save are able to meet their requirements simultaneously.
2. In past, concerns had been expressed by various stakeholders regarding challenges
being faced by the chit business. Therefore, the Central Government constituted a Key
Advisory Group on Chit Funds to review the existing legal, regulatory and institutional
framework for Chit Funds and its efficacy and to suggest legal and regulatory initiatives
required for orderly growth of the said sector. The Key Advisory Group submitted its
recommendations relating to improvements in the institutional and legal structure to further
develop the chit business in order to reduce the regulatory burden of the chit business and
to protect the interest of the subscribers of the chits.
3. The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance (Sixteenth Lok Sabha), in its
twenty-first Report on Efficacy of Regulation of Collective Investment Schemes (CIS), Chit
Funds, etc., had also recommended to finalise the legislative and administrative proposals
for strengthening and streamlining of the registered Chit Fund sector. Further, the said
Committee, in its thirty-fifth Report on the Action Taken by the Government on the
Recommendations contained in the twenty-first Report, had recommended the need to quickly
firm up the legislative and administrative proposals for the Chit Funds sector.
4. In view of the above, it has been decided to amend the Chit Funds Act, 1982.
Accordingly, the Chit Funds (Amendment) Bill, 2018, inter alia, proposes—
(a) the use of “fraternity fund” also for chits by amending sections 2(b) and 11;
(b) to allow the mandatory presence of two subscribers, as required under section
16(2), either in person or through video  conferencing duly recorded by the foreman;
(c) that where the presence of the mandatory two subscribers was through video-
conferencing, the minutes of proceedings should be signed by them within two days;
(d) the increase of ceiling of foreman’s commission from five per cent. to seven per
cent. under section 21;
(e) to enable the foreman to have a right to lien for the dues from subscribers, so that
set-off is allowed by the chit fund for subscribers who have already drawn funds, so as to
discourage default by them;
(f) to amend section 85(b), so as to confer power upon the State Government to
specify the amount, by notification, upto which any chit fund shall be exempted under the
said section.
5. The Bill seeks to achieve the above objects.
