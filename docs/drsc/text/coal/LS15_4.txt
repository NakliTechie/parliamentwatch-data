CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTORY
India has a long history of commercial coal mining covering nearly
220 years starting from Raniganj Coalfield along the Western bank of
river Damodar and today India is one of the major coal producers in the
world with an annual production of over 400 million tones.
Coal has been recognized as the most important source of energy
for electricity generation in India. About 75% of the coal in India is
consumed in the power sector. In addition, other industries like steel,
cement, fertilizers, chemicals, paper and thousands of medium and small-
scale industries are also dependent on coal for their process and energy
requirements. In the transport sector, though direct consumption of coal
by the Railways is almost negligible on account of phasing out of steam
locomotives, the energy requirement for electric traction is still dependent
on coal converted into electric power. On account of the growing needs
of the economy, a thrust has to be given on systematic exploitation of
coking coal reserves in the country.
1.2 The main subjects allocated to the Ministry under the
Government of India (allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 as amended
from time to time, are exploration and development of coking coal and
non-coking coal and lignite deposits in India; all matters pertaining to
production, supply, distribution and pricing of coal; development and
operation of coal washeries; low temperature carbonization of coal and
production of synthetic oil from coal; Administration of Coal Mines
(Conservation and Development) Act, 1974, Coal Mines Provident Fund
and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1948, Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition
and Development) Act, 1957, the Coal Mines Provident Fund Organization;
and administration of Public Sector Enterprises dealing with coal and
lignite.
1.3 The Ministry of Coal has, under its direct administrative control,
the following two Public Sector Undertakings;
(i) Coal India Ltd. (CIL), and
(ii)Neyveli Lignite Corporation Ltd. (NLC)

1.4 The Singareni Collieries Company Limited (SCCL) incorporated
as a public limited company in 1920, became a Government Company
in 1956 with headquarters at Kothagudem in Andhra Pradesh. The
company is a joint undertaking of Government of Andhra Pradesh and
Government of India. The share capital of this Company is held by the
Government of Andhra Pradesh and Government of India in the ratio
of 51:49 respectively. This company is engaged in the exploitation of coal
reserves in the State of Andhra Pradesh.
Coal Controller’s Organisation, Kolkata
1.5 The statutory functions performed by Coal Controller’s
Organisation, a subordinate office, are checking of quality of coal including
settlement of complaints vis-à-vis grading and quality of coal despatches,
adjudicating claims on grade; regulatory authority to grant permission
for opening and reopening of seams and mines; distribution of coking
as well as non-coking coal; disposal of objections received under the Coal
Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development) Act, 1957 and collection
and publication of statistical information on coal and lignite in pursuance
to Statistics Act, 1955.
Coal Mines Provident Fund Organisation (CMPFO), Dhanbad
1.6 Coal Mines Provident Fund Organisation (CMPFO) is an
autonomous body set up under the Coal Mines Provident Fund and
2

Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1948. CMPFO administers the Coal Mines
Provident Fund Scheme , 1948, the Coal Mines Deposit Linked Insurance
Scheme, 1976 and Coal Mines Pension Scheme, 1998. All these schemes
have been formulated under the provision of the Act of 1948.
1.7 The Ministry in Outcome budget (2010-11), while detailing the
Budget Estimates and the Plan Outlay of the Ministry of Coal and Coal
PSUs also brings out the quantifiable deliverables and physical outputs
along with the timelines for both the Plan and Non-Plan schemes operated
in the Ministry of Coal. The new initiatives and policy measures on the
new coal distribution policy, fresh E-auction scheme, revision in the coal
Royalty Rate, the formation of Coal Ventures International (Special Purpose
Vehicle of the Central Public Sector Undertakings including Coal India
Limited) etc. are also brought out in the Outcome Budget 2010-11. In
addition, the Outcome Budget also addresses the issues relating to
measures to increase coal production through Emergency Coal Production
Plan and Captive Coal Block allocation.
1.8 The detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Coal were
presented to Lok Sabha on 10 March, 2010. The Committee in the present
Report have examined various issues related to implementation of various
schemes/plans/programmes under the preview of the Ministry of Coal. In
the context of Demands for Grants 2010-11, the detailed analysis alongwith
observations/recommendations of the Committee on various issues have
been given in the succeeding chapters of the Report.

2.5 On being asked about the reasons for the drastic increase in
the budgetary allocations for the year 2010-11, the Secretary, Ministry
of Coal deposed before the Committee as under:—
“In BE of 2010-11, Rs. 9800 crores which you are saying as three
times of what it was, out of that, Rs. 3800 crores is actually the
capital outlay for the year and we have earmarked Rs. 6000 crores
for the purpose of asset acquisition abroad on which certain
developments have taken place. We expect that because of
these developments, it may be possible for certain sanctions to
come in this area and we may require to invest abroad. You have
asked about the efforts that we have been making for projects
abroad.
First of all, in the competitive bidding process, we have been
able to get two blocks in Mozambique. These blocks are unexplored.
We have created subsidiary companies there called Coal India
Africana Limited which has been registered in Mozambique. We
have got the exploration licence in favour of that company
just now. There the expenditure will be limited. It will be only for
exploration purposes. It will take about two years to complete the
exploration and then we will come to a stage when we will start
the mining process. But another initiative we have taken, that is
floating of global expression of interest throughout four countries,

CHAPTER V
MEASURES TO ADDRESS ENVIRONMENTAL CONCERNS
The operation of coalfields in India has added tremendously
to the environmental pollution. The heaps of fly ash generated during
these operations in the coalfields area created environmental hazard in
air, water and land, causing serious diseases to the working class and
the inhabitants. The samples analysis from the ground water sources
show that various physical and chemical parameters are not with in the
limits of Indian Drinking Water Standard. Air quality-monitoring results
of industrial (Mining) and residential (village) areas adjacent to Coal
Fields, confirm high degree of Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM) in
mining areas and residential areas.
The reserve forest and protected forest in the buffer zone of coalfield
areas are degraded. Occurrence of dense forest is vary rare. The effluents
from coal mining site and coal utilization site not only run over the
agricultural field but also get discharged into various tributaries of Rivers.
These effluents slow down the seed germination and initial plant growth
process of rice and wheat. Therefore the need for undertaking measures
for environmental protection and preservation becomes is of paramount
importance to tackle climate change challenges.
5.2 Giving details of the measures taken by the Ministry of Coal
and Coal PSUs, the Committee has been informed by the Ministry as
under:—
“A Coal-mining project is approved only after obtaining the
Environment Impact Assessment/Environment Management Plan
clearance from MoEF. Strict compliance of Environmental mitigation
measures as laid down in the EMP is a regular activity during and

CHAPTER VI
REHABILITATION AND RESETTLEMENT
6.1 A major challenge confronting any investor in India, where
project implementation involves involuntary displacement of people in
the equitable and effective resolution of Rehabilitation and Resettlement
issues. Inevitably, these issues are acute in a country like India where
70% of the population subsist on agriculture and 36% are below the
poverty line. These factors are experienced most severely in mining areas
located in isolated and less developed region of the country predominantly
inhabited by scheduled castes and scheduled tribes, and by other
economically weak and disadvantageous section of the society.
6.2 With regard to Resettlement and Rehabilitation Policy, the
Ministry have informed the Committee as under:
“We had a Resettlement and Rehabilitation Policy in 2000. It
was not yielding results. After that the Government announced
NRRP in 2007. After taking clue from that, we took it up and we
discussed it with the Parliamentary Standing Committee in 2008-
09 and hon. Members’ points were taken into account. It was
discussed with the MPs of that area and that is how the policy was
made. This policy liberalized employment. The earlier policy was
clear that employment would be given only those who are losing
at least 2 acres. We have now made the policy that for every 2 acres
of land, we give one employment which means that for 100 acres,
I am committing 50 employment.”
6.3 On being asked that CIL face a lot of litigation in Kolkata High
Court for the simple reason that they have not provided employment to
people although they have taken their land, the Ministry in their reply
stated as under:—
“The problem is different. We have a clearly laid down system that
it has to be linear dependent. Secondly, there could be a dispute
on the title of the land.”
What happens is that in a State like Jharkhand or West Bengal
where there are small plots of land and if 100 acres is taken, you
will hardly find five or six people losing more than two acres.
35

According to the policy only five or six were eligible for
employment. We made a liberalization of that in 2008 and we said
that for every 100 acre I take, I will give employment to 50 people.
It is far better than the NRB of 2007 because it says that employment
will be given to nuclear families subject to availability and vacancy.
In our case the availability vacancy will be our problem, we will
find out. But we will increase that number of employment. As a
result, if I give 50 employment for 100 acres, the last fellow who
gets employment is naturally having less than two acres of land.
Somebody will have more than two acres of land and somebody
will have less than two acres of land and even with less than two
acres of land, he will be entitled to employment.
One round of special dispensation was given by the Ministry to
all subsidiaries where these agreements are conferring employment
rights in violation with the agreed policy. As a one time dispensation,
in every coal company, an assessment was made as to how many
such cases were there and one on-time basis a dispensation was
given by the Ministry. It has been accepted in the Coal India Board
and it was given to the subsidiary companies. But that is only one
time.
There are some disputes because the time has passed so long. There
are linear dependents not available or the title is not very clear and
so many other issues.”

CHAPTER VII
CLEAN COAL AND WASHERY CAPACITY
Coal washing is an important area from economic and environment
point of view. A number of studies carried out earlier have clearly
highlighted benefits of using washed coal in improving the economic of
power generation and also reduction of emissions. The directive of
Ministry of Environment and Forests restricts the use of coal containing
more than 34% ash content in power stations located 1000 km away from
pit heads. With this as a driver, a number of power utilities have shown
inclination to use washed coal for power generation and also coal washing
is one of the clean coal technologies prior to combustion of coal. Coal
India is heading in a big way for Coal Beneficiation of all types of coals.
The present installed capacity of washery for thermal coal of about
110 Mt per annum is envisaged to reach about 250 Mt per annum in the
next five years time.
7.2 To meet the demand supply gap of washed coal, guidelines
for setting up of coal washeries on Public Sector Coal Company’s
land have been issued by Coal Ministry in September’ 05. Accordingly,
subsidiary coal companies of CIL are extending necessary assistance
to facilitate setting up coal washeries on their land to the private
operators.
7.3 CIL has also decided in principle to wash all inferior grade
coal linked to non-pit head power stations by setting up washeries
with the state-of-the art technology on Build-Operate-Maintain (BOM)
concept where CIL will provide the capital funding and other
infrastructure facilities to the NOM operator. Further, it has been decided
that all new opencast projects of 2.5 Mt and above capacity, which are
not linked to pithead power stations should be designed with integrated
washery.
7.4 CIL has already decided to set up 20 washeries by the end of
XI five years plan in its various subsidiaries with total installed capacity
of 111 Mty, out of which 2 washeries of total 6 Mty are proposed under
37

“Turn-key” execution and the rest 18 are on BOM concept under
which the capital funding and other infrastructural facilities will
be arranged by CIL/subsidiary company. Tenders for seven washeries –
four of BCCL, two of MCL and one of CCL, have already been floated.
Offers for six washeries have been received and their evaluation is in
progress.

CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPNOSIBILITY (CSR) MEASURES
Mining of coal has profound impact on the communities living in
the areas where the mines are established. The obvious impact of the
introduction of any industrial activities in such areas is change in the
traditional lifestyle of the original inhabitants and indigenous communities
and also change in the socio-economic profile of the area. In the above
context, Coal India strongly believes that the people living in mining
areas are an important stakeholder in the process of mine development
and for sustainable develoment they have to be given share of the benefits
of development of the mining projects.
8.2 In view of the above as a part of Corporate Social Responsibility
Coal India and its Subsidiary companies are undertaking different walfare
activities in and around the coalfield areas for the benefit of inhabitants.

and a comprehensive CSR policy.
