INTRODUCTION 
 
 I, the Chairperson, Standing Committee on Coal, Mines and Steel (2025-26) having been 
authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on their behalf, present this Twenty Eight 
Report (Eighteenth Lok Sabha) on Action Taken by the Government on the 
Observations/Recommendations contained in the Twentieth Report of the Committee on 
Demands for Grants (2026-27) of the Ministry of Steel.

Government to all the Observations/Recommendations contained in the Report were received 
on 23.06.2026.

03.08.2026. The Minutes of the sitting of the Committee has been given at Annexure -I to the 
Report.

Recommendations contained in the Twentieth Report of the Committee is given at Annexure-II.

Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme For Specialty Steel In India 
 
(Recommendation No. 3) 
 
5. The Committee in their Report had observed/recommended as under: 
 
“The Committee note that the PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel is a flagship initiative of 
the Ministry aimed at promoting manufacturing of high-value specialty steel grades within the 
country, which represents the future of India's steel sector. The Committee observe from 
details of PLI 1.0 that the committed investments are Rs. 27,106 crore and achieved 
investment are Rs. 22,655 crores while the figures for PLI 1.1 are Rs. 17000 crores and Rs. 
968 crores respectively. Similarly, committed production so far vis a vis the actual production 
in Metric Tonnes(MTs) till December, 2025 under the scheme for PLI 1 are 7,940,000MT and 
2,472,000MT whereas the same for PLI 1.1 are 6,400,000MT and 15,000MT respectively. 
The Committee note that there are various parameters like market dynamics, product specific 
technical challenges, supply chain constraints, and global economic conditions which have 
impacted the achievement of committed targets. PLI scheme is a very important Central 
Sector scheme of the Ministry for speciality steel which is the future of Steel in the country. 
The Committee appreciate the visible efforts and proactive approach of the Ministry in making 
the scheme a success, including the progressive refinements introduced in PLI 1.1 and PLI 
1.2 to address bottlenecks such as reduced thresholds for brownfield investments, flexibility 
in production commitments, and updated price indices. The Committee recommend that the 
Ministry continue its consultative approach with industry stakeholders to identify and address 
implementation challenges in a time-bound manner, and work towards full utilization of the

apprised of the year-wise progress and outcomes achieved under the scheme, including 
investment realized, production achieved, employment generated, and import substitution 
attained”.

Green Steel and Decarbonisation Initiatives 
(Recommendation No. 16) 
8. The Committee in their Report had observed/recommended as under: 
 
“The Committee appreciate the pioneering steps taken by the Ministry of Steel in 
promoting green steel and decarbonization in the steel sector. The Committee note with 
satisfaction that India is the first nation globally to release the Taxonomy for Green Steel, 
which provides clear standards for defining and categorizing low-emission steel. As of 
31.01.2026, 78 steel units have been awarded Green Steel certification with star ratings, 
accounting for 11.18 MT of certified green steel production, which is a commendable 
achievement. The Committee further note that the Ministry has released a comprehensive 
report titled "Greening the Steel Sector in India: Roadmap and Action Plan", which has been 
developed based on the recommendations of 14 Task Forces constituted for this purpose and 
provides a detailed roadmap towards achieving the net-zero target by 2070. The Committee 
are also pleased to note that the Ministry has awarded four pilot projects for the use of 
hydrogen in the steel sector under the National Green Hydrogen Mission launched by the 
Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. The Committee believe that these initiatives position 
India as a global leader in sustainable steel production. The Committee recommend that the 
Ministry continue to accord high priority to these endeavours, facilitate technology 
development and adoption, provide appropriate policy incentives for green steel production, 
promote participation in international green steel markets, and work towards making India a 
global hub for green steel manufacturing and carbon emissions reduction. The Commi ttee 
would like to be apprised of the progress on pilot projects, outcomes of the green steel 
certification scheme, industry response to the green steel taxonomy and the roadmap for 
scaling up hydrogen-based steel production.” 
 
9. In its Action Taken Reply furnished to the Committee, the Ministry of Steel has stated 
as under: 

5  
“As part of the National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM), the Ministry of New 
and Renewable Energy (MNRE) has allocated INR 455 crore for pilot projects 
focused on the use of hydrogen in steel production. Metallurgical & Engineering 
Consultants (India) Limited (MECON) has been selected as the Scheme 
Implementing Agency (SIA). Currently, four projects have been awarded under 
this scheme which are currently in the design and commissioning phase. This 
scheme aims to identify and demonstrate advanced technologies for the 
utilization of green hydrogen in steelmaking through pilot projects over a five-
year period up to 2029–30. The pilot projects awarded in steel sector under this 
scheme are aimed for assessing economic, technological and operational 
aspects. Globally, hydrogen-based steel production technology is at a nascent 
stage and success or failure of these pilot projects will give insight for 
commercial viability/scaling-up of the use of hydrogen in the certain 
technologies used for steel manufacturing. 
Further, under the Green Steel Taxonomy, as on 31.03.2026, 89 steel units 
have been awarded Green rating Certificates, covering a total green rated steel 
production of 12.34 million tonnes. The introduction of the certification 
framework has encouraged steel units to participate and obtain certification for 
achieving emission intensity levels below the threshold value of 2.2 tCO₂e/tfs. 
This recognition of sustainability and reduced emissions in steel production is 
expected to catalyze sector-wide transformation, strengthen India’s transition 
towards a climate-resilient steel industry, and contribute to the country’s 
broader climate goals.” 
 
10. The Committee in their original report were of the view that the Ministry continue 
to accord high priority to the promotion of green steel and decarbonization in the steel 
sector, facilitate technology development and adoption, provide appropriate policy 
incentives for green steel production, promote participation in international green steel 
markets and work towards making India a global hub for green steel manufacturing 
and carbon emissions reduction and had desired to be apprised of the progress on 
pilot projects outcomes of the green steel certification scheme, industry response to 
the green steel taxonomy and the roadmap for scaling up hydrogen-based steel 
production. The Ministry, in its reply, has stated that in collaboration with the Ministry 
of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE), Rs.455 crore have been allocated for pilot 
projects which focuses on the use of hydrogen in steel production. Furthermore, the 
Ministry has informed that these pilot projects also aim to identify and demonstrate 
advanced technologies for the utilization of green hydrogen in steelmaking. While 
appreciating the efforts taken by the Ministry in

6  
introducing hydrogen-based steel production technology and certification framework 
to help reduce emissions and strengthen India’s transition towards a climate-resilient 
steel industry, the Committee also desire to be apprised on the outcomes of the Green 
Steel certification scheme and industry response to the green steel taxonomy.  
 
Technology Upgradation and Digitalization 
(Recommendation No. 18) 
11. The Committee in their Report had observed/recommended as under: 
 
“The Committee note that the global steel industry is witnessing rapid technological 
advancements including automation, artificial intelligence-driven process optimization, 
advanced analytics, digital twins, Internet of Things (IoT) enabled monitoring, and Industry 
4.0 applications. The Committee believe that adoption of digital technologies and advanced 
process control systems can significantly enhance productivity, energy efficiency, quality 
consistency, and cost competitiveness of Indian steel plants. The Committee recommend that 
the Ministry, in consultation with Steel CPSEs and private sector steel companies, formulate 
a comprehensive strategy for technology upgradation and digitalization in the steel sector. 
This should include promoting collaborative research and development, facilitating technology 
partnerships with global leaders, supporting pilot projects for emerging technologies, and 
creating a favourable ecosystem for technology adoption, especially for small and medium 
steel enterprises. The Committee would like to be apprised of the initiatives being taken in 
this regard and the outcomes achieved.” 
 
 
 
12. In its Action Taken Reply furnished to the Committee, the Ministry of Steel has stated 
as under: 
 
“Ministry of Steel is providing financial assistance for Research & Development 
(R&D) by supporting collaborative projects with academic institutions, research 
laboratories, and steel companies to address technological challenges in the 
iron and steel sector. An annual budget of ₹5–10 crore has been earmarked 
under the R&D Scheme to promote innovation in the steel sector. The thrust 
areas for providing financial assistance under the R&D Scheme, are 
development of new alternate processes & technologies to address the burning 

7 
 
issues faced by the Iron & Steel Sector such as climate change (low 
emission steel production, H2 based iron & steel production, CCUS etc.), 
waste utilization, energy & resource efficiency etc.”. 
 
 
13. The Committee in their original report had noted that global steel industry is 
experiencing rapid technological advancements including automation, artificial 
intelligence  driven process optimization, advanced analytics, digital twins, Internet 
of Things (IoT) enabled monitoring and Industry 4.0 applications. The Committee 
also recommended the Ministry to formulate a comprehensive strategy for 
technology upgradation and digitalization in the steel sector, in consultation with 
Steel CPSEs and private sector steel companies. The Ministry, in its reply, stated 
that financial assistance is being provided for Research & Development (R&D) by 
supporting collaborative projects with academic institutions, research laboratories 
and steel companies to address technological challenges in the iron and steel 
sector. The Committee while appreciating the Ministry for earmarking an annual 
budget of Rs. 5-10 crore under the R&D scheme to promote innovation in the steel 
sector would like to be apprised regarding formulating a comprehensive strategy for 
technology upgradation and digitalization in the steel sector. 
 
Skill Development and Employment Generation 
(Recommendation No. 19) 
14. The Committee in their Report had observed/recommended as under: 
 
“The Committee recognize that the steel sector, including upstream and 
downstream industries, is a significant source of employment and livelihood for millions of 
people across the country. With ongoing capacity expansion, technological upgradation, 
and diversification into specialty steel products, there is a growing need for skilled 
workforce equipped with contemporary technical skills, safety consciousness, and digital 
literacy. The Committee recommend that the Ministry, in coordination with the Ministry of 
Skill Development and Entrepreneurship and industry stakeholders, strengthen efforts 
towards skill development, vocational training, and capacity building programs tailored to 
the needs of the steel industry. Special attention may be given to training programs in 
emerging areas such as green steel technologies, automation, advanced process control, 
quality management, and safety practices. The Committee also recommend that Steel 
CPSEs actively participate in skill development initiatives, provide apprenticeship 

8 
 
opportunities, and collaborate with Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) and Polytechnics to 
develop industry-ready skilled workforce. The Committee would like to be apprised of the 
initiatives taken in this regard.” 
 
15. In its Action Taken Reply furnished to the Committee, the Ministry of Steel has 
stated as under: 
“Skill development and employment generation in the steel sector are of 
critical importance, and concerted efforts are being undertaken to strengthen 
the skilling ecosystem in line with evolving industry requirements. 
Steel CPSEs have established structured and continuous skill development, 
vocational training, and capacity-building frameworks across technical, 
operational, managerial, and behavioural domains. Regular in-house and 
external training programmes are conducted to ensure that the workforce 
remains aligned with contemporary skills and industry best practices. 
Training initiatives are increasingly focused on emerging areas such as 
green steel technologies, decarbonisation, Industry 4.0, automation, 
advanced process control, quality management systems, and safety 
practices. Institutions such as NISST and BPNSI are also contributing 
through targeted programmes, particularly for the secondary steel sector, 
with emphasis on energy efficiency, sustainability and operational 
excellence. 
Apprenticeship training constitutes a key pillar of the skilling ecosystem. 
Steel CPSEs are actively engaging apprentices under various national 
schemes such as NAPS and NATS across multiple trades and disciplines. 
For instance, SAIL has engaged over 10,000 apprentices in the past five 
years across its integrated steel plants, while other CPSEs are also 
undertaking apprenticeship training in technical trades relevant to mining and 
steel operations. These programmes provide hands-on industry exposure 
and enhance the employability of youth in the steel sector. 
Steel CPSEs have also established collaborations with Industrial Training 
Institutes (ITIs), Polytechnics, sector skill councils, and premier 
technical institutions to align training curricula with industry requirements. In 
particular, SAIL has entered into multiple partnerships with ITIs, technical 
universities, and industry partners for curriculum development, certification, 
and hands-on training. Other CPSEs are also progressively expanding such 
collaborations to enhance workforce employability”. 
 
The Ministry remains committed to further strengthening the skilling 
ecosystem in the steel sector to support its growth and global 
competitiveness.”.

Budget Allocation for FY 2026-27 
 
(Recommendation No. 1) 
  
The Committee note that a sum of Rs 443.18 crores have been allocated under Demand No. 
97 of the Ministry of Steel for the year 2026-27.  Out of this, Rs 439.84 crores is under the 
Revenue Section and Rs. 3.34 crores is under Capital Section of the Ministry.  The Demand 
includes Rs. 393.00 crores for Central Sector Schemes, Rs. 46.78 crores (Revenue Section- 
Rs. 43.44 crores and Capital Section- Rs. 3.34 crores) for Secretariat/Establishment 
Expenditure and Rs. 3.40 crores for Other Central Sector Expenditure. The Committee 
appreciate that the outlay for Central Sector Schemes has increased during 2026-27 to Rs. 
393.00 crores from Rs. 318.00 crores during 2025-26 while the BE for Establishment 
expenditure during the current FY is in line with previous projections. The Committee also 
note that the primary focus in the Budget Allocation for FY 2026-27 for the Steel Sector is 
the Central Sector Schemes, particularly the PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel in India. 
Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL) in BE 2026-27 has a nil allocation, while in 2025-26, a 
larger amount of Rs. 3017 crore was kept on account of equity infusion by the Government 
into RINL.  As the Steel Sector is the backbone of the economy, serving as an important 
infrastructure input under primary driver of industrialisation, the Committee call upon the 
Ministry to make efforts for getting more funds allocated to the sector.  
  
Reply of the Government 
  
The Ministry of Steel has launched three rounds under the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) 
Scheme for Specialty Steel. The latest round, PLI 1.2 (third round), was launched on 4th 
November 2025, with an estimated committed investment of INR 11,887 crore from industry. 
This round is expected to add approximately 8.28 million tonnes of specialty steel capacity by 
FY 2030–31. Production under PLI 1.1 (second round) has commenced in FY 2025–26, with 
incentive disbursements scheduled to begin from FY 2026–27. Production under PLI 1.0 (first 
round) is also expected to increase compared to previous years, following the resolution of 
initial bottlenecks. 

11 
 
  
Till FY 2025-26, about ₹235 crore of incentives have been disbursed. Collectively, all three 
rounds of the PLI Scheme are expected to significantly enhance domestic production of 
specialty steel, resulting in increased incentive disbursement over time. 
  
(Ministry of Steel O.M No. H-11013/2/2026-parl dated  22.06.2026) 
  
 
Actual Expenditure Trends vis-a-vis Budget Estimates (BE) and                            
Revised Estimates (RE) 
 
(Recommendation No. 2) 
  
The Committee observe that the Ministry of Steel’s BE, RE and Actual Expenditure trends 
over the last five years show that Actual Expenditure frequently fell below Budget Estimates 
(BE) and Revised Estimates (RE).   During 2022-23, against RE of  Rs 57.72 crores, the 
actual expenditure was Rs. 46.04 crores which indicated 79.8 % achievement with respect to 
RE.  Also, during 2023-24, against BE of Rs 70.15 crores which was subsequently reduced 
to Rs 54.55 crores at RE stage, the actual expenditure was Rs. 51.87 crores (achievement of 
95.1%). For the year 2024-25, the percentage achievement with regard to RE was 99.7%. In 
the current scenario during 2025-26, the actual expenditure till 11.02.2026 shows 75.25% 
achievement with respect to RE.  The Committee appreciates the progressive improvement 
in expenditure management demonstrated by the Ministry over the last three years. The 
Committee recommend that the Ministry further strengthen its planning and monitoring 
mechanisms to maintain this positive trajectory and ensure optimum utilization of funds within 
the fiscal year, thereby avoiding any surrender of funds at the RE stage. 
  
Reply of the Government 
 
The Ministry of Steel has taken note of the observations of the Hon’ble Committee regarding 
expenditure trends and the need for strengthened planning and monitoring mechanisms. In 
line with these observations, the Ministry has undertaken focused efforts to improve 
expenditure management and ensure optimal utilisation of allocated funds. 
 
These efforts have yielded significant results during FY 2025–26, wherein the Ministry has 
achieved an expenditure of Rs. 3266.08 crores which is 99.8% of RE 2025-26 of Rs. 3272.44 
crores. This demonstrates the Ministry’s continued commitment to efficient financial planning 
and close monitoring, thereby minimizing any surrender of funds at the RE stage. 

12 
 
  
The Ministry remains committed to further strengthening its internal processes to sustain this 
positive trajectory in the coming years. 
 (Ministry of Steel O.M No. H-11013/2/2026-parl dated  22.6.2026)

disbursement of funds under the scheme, facilitate greater participation of Indian-flagged 
vessels, and encourage Steel CPSEs to actively consider Indian shipping options in their 
procurement processes, thereby contributing to the growth of India's merchant shipping fleet 
and reducing dependence on foreign shipping services.

CPSEs under the administrative control of Ministry of Steel are beneficiaries under the 
scheme. The objective of the scheme is to promote greater participation of Indian-flagged 
vessels and encourage Steel CPSEs to actively consider Indian shipping options in their 
procurement processes, thereby contributing to the growth of India's merchant shipping fleet 
and reducing dependence on foreign shipping services.

Monitoring and Evaluation Framework 
 
(Recommendation No. 20) 
  
The Committee note that robust monitoring and evaluation mechanisms are essential for 
ensuring effective implementation of schemes, timely achievement of targets, and optimal 
utilization of public resources. The Committee appreciate that the Ministry has adopted the 
Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF) for tracking performance of schemes based 
on clearly defined output and outcome indicators. The Committee recommend that the 
Ministry further strengthen its monitoring mechanisms by establishing real-time digital 
dashboards for tracking scheme performance, conducting periodic evaluations to assess 
impact and effectiveness, organizing regular review meetings with CPSEs and other 
stakeholders, and publishing periodic progress reports to enhance accountability. The 
Committee would like to be apprised of the progress achieved under various schemes and 
CPSE performance against set targets.
