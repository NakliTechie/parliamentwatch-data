Recommendation (Serial No. 2)
ANNUAL PLAN OF PSUs
5. The Committee were concerned to note that although Rs. 1729.39
crore was earmarked at BE stage during 2014-15 for Public Sector Undertakings
(PSUs) under the Ministry of Mines [through Internal and Extra Budgetary
Resources (IEBR)], the same was reduced to Rs. 629.64 crore at RE stage
and the actual utilization was only Rs. 445.04 crore (upto February, 2015)
which comes to just 64% of the RE. Similarly, for the year 2013-14, the BE of
Rs. 2452.12 crore was drastically reduced to Rs. 952.75 crore at RE stage
and the actual utilisation was only Rs. 821.00 crore. The Committee felt that
under-utilization of funds year after year earmarked under IEBR for various
PSUs reflects serious shortcomings in the implementation of ongoing projects
as well as setting of unrealistic targets. The Committee, therefore, recommended
that besides preparing realistic budgetary proposals, there is an imperative
need to ensure that the expenditure by PSUs is closely monitored by the Ministry
to ensure the better utilization of the earmarked funds.

Recommendation (Serial No. 4)
ONGOING IMPORTANT PROJECTS OF GSI
8. The Committee also noted that so far the GSI have been carrying
out mineral exploration for mineral deposits upto G4 and G3 levels of United
Nations Framework Classification (UNFC). Now with the passing of the Mines
and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2015 allowing
competitive bidding of minerals, the GSI shall take up G2 and G1 stage
exploration of UNFC classification of mineral investigation from
2015-16 onwards. The Committee, however, found that presently, GSI is
poorly equipped to meet this challenge as the fleet of drilling rigs available with
GSI is quite old and the GSI needs to develop its drilling capacity through
bringing in cutting edge technology, procurement of high quality drilling
equipment. Similarly, analytical capacity of the chemical laboratories of GSI
have also to be enhanced. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommended
that all the necessary steps in this regard must be undertaken by the GSI/
Ministry for timely procurement of additional equipment/technology which in
the opinion of the Committee would also yield better utilization of funds during
2015-16. The Committee would like to be informed about the steps taken by

7
the Ministry/GSI in this regard and desired that manpower requirements should
also be reviewed to meet the requirement of additional work being undertaken
by GSI.
9. The Ministry in their Action Taken Reply have informed the
Committee as under:—
“GSI had been carrying out exploration for mineral deposits upto G4
and G3 levels of United Nations Framework Classification (UNFC) till
the FS 2014-15. From FS 2015-16 onwards, GSI will be taking up
mineral exploration items pertaining to G2 and G1 level of UNFC. For
successful execution of these items, the drilling capacity and the chemical
analytical capability of the chemical laboratories of the department have
to be developed. GSI is taking/planning to take the following steps to
ensure development/strengthening of the drilling capacity and analytical
capability of chemical laboratories:—
1. Replacement of old drills/pumps (more than 10 years) and other
accessories like drill rods, casings, barrels etc. by new ones.
2. Procurement of truck mounted hydrostatic drills of 1500 m depth
capacity and procurement of wire line drilling accessories.
3. Extensive use of synthetic diamond core bit (serrated) and also
poly crystalline diamond (PCD) bits are to be adopted for increase
in productivity.
4. All drilling sites engaged in bentonite mud drilling to be equipped
with mud testing facility and adopting extensive use of polymer in
hard/fractured formations which will ensure improved borehole
cleaning and bit performance.
5. Long distance shifting to be done by hiring private transport.
6. Improvement of living condition and provisioning of computers/
laptops in drilling camps.
7. Recruitment and training of Gr-C technical personnel and streamline
career prospects to attract drilling Engineers in Gr-A and B cadre.
8. Periodic training facility/Orientation programme for drilling
personnel for acquiring the latest knowledge and keeping updated
in state-of-the-art drilling technology.

8
Regarding enhancement of the analytical capability of the chemical
laboratories, GSI is procuring state-of-the-art instrument like ICPMS,
XRF and AAS for different regional and State unit laboratories which are
going to increase the capability of the chemical laboratories many fold.
As per the recommendations of the High Powered Committee (HPC),
which has been approved by the Union Cabinet on 25.10.2011, the
sanctioned strength of GSI in all grades has been enhanced by 949 posts
from 11420 to 12369 posts. The total sanctioned strength of geology
stream will be achieved by FY 2019-20, that of geophysics stream will
be achieved by FY 2017-18 and that of chemical stream will be achieved
by FY 2018-19 progressively. Filling up of vacancies of engineering
stream is under process and the total sanctioned strength will be achieved
very shortly. The posts in survey, finance, personnel, stenography and
other streams may be filled up progressively by 2018. Thus, the total
sanctioned strength of GSI may be achieved gradually by FY 2019-20.”
10. The Committee had observed that with the passing of the
Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment
Act, 2015 allowing competitive bidding of minerals, the Geological Survey
of India (GSI) decided to take up G2 and G1 stage exploration of United
Nations Framework Classification (UNFC) classification of mineral
investigation from 2015-16 onwards. The Committee, however, found
that GSI is poorly equipped to meet this challenge as the fleet of drilling
rigs available with GSI is quite old and the GSI needs to develop its
drilling capacity through bringing in cutting edge technology,
procurement of high quality drilling equipment and analytical capacity
of the chemical laboratories of GSI have also to be enhanced. In this
regard, the Committee note from the action taken reply of the Ministry
that GSI is taking/planning to take certain steps to ensure development/
strengthening of the drilling capacity and analytical capability of chemical
laboratories. For enhancement of the analytical capability of the chemical
laboratories, GSI is procuring state-of-the-art instruments like
Inductively Coupled Plazma Mass Spectrometer (ICPMS), X-Ray
Fluorescence (XRF) and Atomic Absorption Spectrometer (AAS) for
different regional and State unit laboratories which are going to increase
the capability of the chemical laboratories many fold. While appreciating
the various corrective steps being undertaken by GSI to ensure
development/strengthening of the drilling capacity and analytical
capability of chemical laboratories, the Committee would like to

9
emphasize upon the need for sustained endeavours on the part of the
Ministry of Mines/GSI to make all efforts for ensuring implementation
of such planned initiatives in a time bound manner.
Recommendation (Serial No. 8)
RESTRUCTURING OF IBM
11. The Committee have noted that IBM has initiated implementation
of 26 non-financial recommendations of Review and Restructuring Committee
constituted for the purpose of Review and Restructuring of IBM. The
Committee felt that in the wake of transfer of 31 minerals as minor minerals to
States after coming into force of MMDR Amendment Act, 2015, the role of
IBM needs to be redefined. Although, the Ministry of Mines have submitted
that role of IBM would be redefined by way of subordinate legislation by
amending Mineral Concession Rules, 1960 and Mineral Conservation and
Development Rules, 1988, the Committee felt that immediate steps were needed
to be taken by the Ministry of Mines to revise the restructuring proposal of
IBM so that it can meet its added responsibilities.
12. The Ministry have submitted in their Action Taken Reply as below:—
“Substantial changes are made in MMDR (Amendment) Act, 2015 to
regulate the Act of MMDR 1957; to regulate the mining sector in fair and
transparent manner. Auction rules prescribes for sale price of the mineral
which shall be based on the prices published by IBM. It is being envisaged
to include IBM in the National Mineral Exploration Trust as member. As
maintained by the Hon’ble Standing Committee, 31 minerals have been
recently declared as minor mineral. Nevertheless the Monitoring
Committee is examining the sphere of redefining the concrete role for
IBM in the context of new rating regime.
Ministry is also examining the scenario if MCDR is extended to all minerals
including minor minerals which may take some more time. After receipt
of such report by the Monitoring Committee, Ministry shall examine
action plan for IBM.”
13. The Committee in their original Report had taken note of
the ongoing review and restructuring of IBM and felt that its role also
needs to be redefined in the wake of transfer of 31 minerals as ‘minor
minerals’ to States after coming into force of MMDR Amendment
Act, 2015. In this regard, the Committee note from the action taken

10
reply of the Ministry that a Monitoring Committee is presently examining
the sphere of redefining the concrete role for IBM. In addition, the
Ministry is also examining the possibility of extending Mineral Concession
Rules to all minerals including minor minerals which may take some
time. The Ministry have submitted that after receipt of the Report of
the Monitoring Committee, the Ministry shall examine action plan for
IBM. The Committee, therefore, desire that the entire process of
examination of various issues pertaining to review, restructuring and
redefining the role of IBM should be completed in a time bound manner
and the Committee be apprised of the status within 3 months of the
presentation of the Report.
Recommendation (Serial No. 17)
NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF MINERS’ HEALTH (NIMH)
14. The Committee had noted that the National Institute of Miners’
Health (NIMH) established in 1990 is the only Institute exclusively devoted to
occupational health issues of mine workers. The Institute had done pioneering
works like conducting large scale studies on exposure to equipment induced
vibration, formulated guidelines for health risk assessment, noise mapping in
mines, detection of high prevalence of silicosis among stone mine workers in
Karauli District of Rajasthan etc. The Committee noted that the Institute is
currently implementing a large scale ‘Multi-centric study on dust related diseases
and developments of sustainable preventive programmes’ sponsored by the
Ministry of Mines and the Ministry of Labour.
While appreciating the pioneering works being undertaken by the Institute,
the Committee desired that the Government should extend all possible support
for ensuring that the Institute is able to carry out its works without any kind of
physical or financial constraints. The Committee further recommended that
utmost attention should be given to implement the suggestions made by the
Institute on issues concerning the health of mine workers.
15. The Ministry have submitted in their Action Taken Reply as below:—
“The Institute in its march towards the objectives, goals and vision of
“Safe Mines and Health Miners” is taking forward strides in its endeavour
to help the mining and mineral based industry in detection, prevention
and control of occupational health problems at workplace through a
focused program of work environment monitoring, health surveillance
and health promotion.

11
At present, the Government is sponsoring the following S&T projects to
the Institute:—

mapping in mines and surrounding community. (total cost of the
project Rs. 78.10 lakh; duration — 3 years; sponsored by —
Ministry of Mines)
 Evaluation of biomarkers for early detection of noise induced hearing

16. The Committee while appreciating the pioneering works like
conducting large scale studies on exposure to equipment induced
vibration, guidelines for health risk assessment, noise mapping in mines,
detection of high prevalence of silicosis among stone mine workers in
Karauli District of Rajasthan etc., by NIRM had recommended that
utmost attention should be given to implement the suggestions made by
the Institute. Although, the Government in their action taken reply
have informed the Committee about the S&T Projects of NIRM sponsored
by them and plan and non-plan grants-in-aid to the Institute, the reply
of the Government is silent on implementation of the recommendations
of NIRM on issues concerning the health of mine workers. Health and
safety of mine workers is sine-quanon and a threshold commitment for
any organization involved in mining projects. The Committee would,
therefore, like to be apprised of the recommendations made by
NIRM on Workers safety and action taken by the Government for
implementation of the same in public and private sector companies
engaged in mining activities.

GEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA (GSI)
Under-utilization of Funds
Recommendation (Serial No. 3)
The Committee note that the Gross Budgetary Support of Rs. 462.58
crore to GSI as per the approved plan allocation for 2015-16 constitutes about
81.07% of the total GBS of the Ministry which stands at Rs. 570.58 crore
indicates the importance of GSI in creation and updation of national geo-
scientific Data and mineral resource assessment besides other important
activities being carried out by it. In this regard, the Committee observe that the
budget allocation of Geological Survey of India (GSI) for the year
2014-15 was substantially reduced from Rs. 483.70 crore at BE stage to
Rs. 267.20 crore at RE stage and the GSI could spend only Rs. 248.54 crore
(upto 23 March, 2015). The Ministry of Mines have attributed this reduction
and under-utilization of funds to the non-implementation of two major projects
of GSI viz. implementation of Online Core Business Integrated System (OCBIS)
and procurement of Geo-technical Vessel (GTV). As regards OCBIS, Rs. 97.17
crore was earmarked for its implementation, but due to delayed sanction for
the project, this amount was withdrawn. For the procurement of GTV, the
sanctioned amount of Rs. 41.00 crore could not be utilized as approval could
not be obtained due to technical reasons. The Committee feel that such delays
in execution of projects not only lead to under-utilization of the planned funds
but also adversely affect the overall functioning of the concerned organization.
According to the Ministry’s own admission, there is every likelihood that some
of the operational activities of the GSI may be adversely affected by the budget
cut in all major missions. The Committee, therefore, recommend the Ministry
to review the progress of projects/fund utilization at regular intervals for
ensuring that the projects are implemented as per planned schedule right from
preparation of feasibility report to their completion. The Committee would like
to be apprised of the steps taken by the Ministry/GSI for ensuring that the
OCBIS and GTV projects as well as all other projected schemes are implemented

13
well in time during 2015-16 as per their scheduled target and no funds remain
unutilized.
Action Taken
The non-receipt/late receipt of timely approval for the major projects
like the OCBIS and GTV were two of the main reasons for reduction in
requirement/under-utilization of funds during the FY 2014-15.
During the FY 2015-16, GSI has been allocated a total plan budget
grant of Rs. 462.58 crore the plan allocation under different schemes for
2015-16 has been increased in various degrees keeping in view the following
factors:—
(i) DTE under Direction and Administration head has been increased
to meet the requirement for new recruits in S&T streams and also
for taking up of about 737 FSP programmes under different
schemes, OE (V) has been increased substantially to accommodate
the HQ/Office expenses on POL and other official expenses which
have increased manifold.
(ii) Maintenance charges for new OGRV , along with the existing coastal
vessels (under Survey and Mapping Scheme—Other Charges) to
Shipping Corporation of India and maintenance/running of airborne
survey systems (Twin Otter and Heliborne Survey System),
outsourcing of analysis of NGCM samples under OC head of Survey
and Mapping.
(iii) Earlier, GSI carried out only G3 and G4 stage of exploration and
did not take up detailed exploration of minerals under the G1 and
G2 stage investigation, but these investigations shall be taken up
from the current year 2015-16 by GSI. It is expected that
expenditure shall increase under mineral exploration head towards
taking up of increased number of items including G1 and G2 level
under UNFC as well as for outsourcing of drilling, vehicles etc.
(iv) Development and upgradation of the existing training centres and
to provide free of cost training to the scientists of the State
Government Geological Bodies; for preparation of IGC 2016 in
South Africa and IGC 2020, India.
(v) Enhancement in IT (OAE) activities for development of
Phase-III Portal (OCBIS), maintenance of existing portal, software,

14
softcopy conversion of report, e-procurement, e-library, map
service in internet, geo-physical repository etc. It is worth
mentioning here that the requisite approval of the competent
authority regarding implementation of OCBIS has been received
during the last quarter of the last FY.
(vi) Additional items under special investigation and research activity
in GSI on National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping, Deccan

ILLEGAL MINING
Recommendation (Serial No. 9)
The Committee note that there is an increase in the cases of theft of
major and minor minerals in different States of the country during the last
three years resulting in considerable loss to the national/State exchequer. The
Committee also note that total FIR cases registered for illegal mining in different
States during 2012-13 were 1970. In 2014-15, these were increased to 3761
and during the year 2014-15 (upto quarter ending December, 2014) these
were 2240. The Committee feel that IBM cannot absolve itself of responsibility
entrusted to it under MMDR Act and, therefore, recommend that IBM/Ministry
should maintain a data of not only of registered FIRs but also the arrest/
outcome of these cases. The Committee also desire that the Ministry should
come out with a foolproof mechanism for detection and prosecution in respect
of cases relating to illegal mining.
Action Taken
IBM observes illegal mining activities during the course of routine
inspections by it. IBM maintains data on registered FIRs etc. through the
quarterly return on illegal mining received from various State Governments.
In this quarterly return, there is column on court cases filed/decided but not

100 MW WIND POWER PLANT OF NALCO
Recommendation (Serial No. 14)
The Committee are happy to note that NALCO had a successful entry in
the renewable energy sector with commissioning of two major wind generating
units and in order to further strengthen its position in this sector, the company
has decided to invest further for a 100 MW plant anywhere in India having a
good potential and a tender in this regard has already been floated. For this
purpose, a sum of Rs. 495.00 crore has been proposed in BE 2015-16. The
Committee find such initiative laudable as it not only promotes the use of non-
conventional energy in the country but also enables the Company to earn
‘green credit’ thereby making it a good investment of money. The Committee
would like to be apprised of the progress made in this regard.
Action Taken
NALCO plans to set up 100 MW of Wind Power Plant at any wind
potential place(s) in India at an estimated investment of Rs. 660.00 crore.
Company’s Board has accorded in-principle approval for initiating tendering
action for selection of wind power developer. Bids have been received
in response to our NIT for selection of wind power developer which
are under evaluation, that include legal due diligence of lands offered by the
bidders, independent wind resource assessment, techno-economic evaluation
of the proposals. It is expected that the tender finalization could take place
by end of July, 2015. Execution of the project would take twelve months
thereafter.

therefore, strongly urge the Government to favourably consider the request

regard.
