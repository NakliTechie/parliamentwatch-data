A. Order Book Position: 
 
Recommendation No.2 
  
6.        The Committee had recommended as under: 
 
 The Committee observe from the information provided by Department of Defence 
Production that there exists substantial variation in Order Book Position amongst nine 
old DPSUs.  The Order Book Position as on 31.12.2025 of old DPSUs viz, HAL, BEL, 
BEML, BDL, MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL stood at Rs.  2,60,960 crore, Rs. 
73,400 crore, Rs. 16,359 crore, Rs. 25962 crore, Rs. 2,440 crore, Rs. 27,415 crore, Rs. 
20205 crore, Rs. 32,400 crore and Rs. 18308 crore.  The Committee understand that 
the above variation in order book position is due to nature of products/equipment and 
DPSUs are mostly competing to get orders.    
 The Committee have also been informed that future order book would depend on 
the requirements of armed forces and other customers and ability of DPSUs to serve 
those needs based on nomination or on competitive bidding basis. The Committee, are 
therefore, hopeful that order book position of old DPSUs would certainly improve in 
future and competition would act as a catalyst to excel.  The Committee urge the 
Department of Defence Production to take appropriate actions through policies and 
incentives to promote these old DPSUs so that they are able to fulfil the requirements of 
Armed Forces and also venture into global markets for further enhancement of their 
order books. The Committee also wish these DPSUs to venture more into foreign 
markets through diversification of their products and new designs to improve their order 
books position. 
Reply of the Government 
7. Department of Defence Production is working with DPSUs to fulfil the increasing 
requirements of the Armed Forces and increasing their global footprint.Recently, 
Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders Limited (MDL) has acquired a controlling 51% stake in 
Colombo Dockyard PLC (CDPLC) for USD 26.8 million, making CDPLC a MDL 
subsidiary and marking MDL’s first international acquisition aligned with India’s Maritime 
Amrit Kaal Vision 2047.  
The Order Book position of Old DPSUs as shown below is quite healthy and 
many of the contracts are in pipeline for finalization which further strengthen their order 
book position.

B.  Defence Export 
Recommendation No. 3 
9. The Committee had recommended as under: 
The Committee are glad to note from the information provided by the Ministry 
that India is exporting to nearly 100 nations and defence exports in the current financial 
year till 31.01.2026 stood at Rs. 27,085 crore.  As per the data, the defence exports for 
the year 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 stood at Rs.  12815 crore, Rs. 15918 
crore, Rs. 21083 crore and Rs. 23622 crore, respectively which indicates a positive and 
sound growth.  
  As regards the export figures for the year 2023-24 for nine old DPSUs viz, HAL, 
BEL, BEML, BDL, MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL the same stoods at Rs. 311 
crore, Rs. 764 crore, Rs. 1066 crore, Rs. 161 crore, Rs. 66.24 crore, Rs. 15.43 crore, 
Rs. 46.90 crore , Rs. 37.49 crore and nil (for HSL), respectively. Similarly, the export 
figure for the year 2024-25 for nine old DPSUs viz, HAL, BEL, BEML, BDL, MIDHANI, 
MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL stood at Rs. 400 crores, Rs. 892 crore, Rs. 299 crore, Rs. 
1270 crore, Rs. 94.19 crore, Rs. 6012 crore, Rs. 73.63 crore, Rs. 82.04 crore and Rs. 
2.96 crores, respectively. The Committee find that the above export figures particularly 
of MDL and HSL are on a lower side as compared to other DPSUs.  However, the 
overall export figure looks satisfactory in terms of its yearly growth.   
The Committee hope that the above positive growth trajectory in defence exports 
would continue as Indian defence products are gaining popularity due to competitive 
pricing and quality.  The Committee are also of the view that Ministry should facilitate 
ease of doing business(EoDB) to promote defence exports. The Committee opine that 
there is a need to explore new foreign markets for selling our defence products along 
with designing newer and advanced competitive products. There are various 
complexities involved in it which the Committee feel Ministry may look into 
appropriately. 
Reply of the Government 
10. The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. Department of 
Defence Production is taking concerted efforts to keep positive growth trajectory in 
Defence exports. It is submitted that the department brought several EoDB reforms in 
recent times. Few of the export related EoDB reforms are, fit for export certification, 
simplified SOP for Export Authorisations (EAs), revamped Defence EXIM portal, 
reduction timelines for issuance of EAs etc. We are constantly trying to improve our 
export performance by finding out new markets. 
Efforts are continuously undertaken by the department to open up new foreign 
Defence markets to the Indian Defence Companies. Regular interactions with the 
potential foreign customers are conducted with the support of Defence wings of the 

6 
 
Indian missions abroad through high level visits, webinars, seminars, bilateral 
engagements, B2B interactions etc. 
Comments of the Committee 
11. The Committee are happy to note the initiatives taken by the Department of 
Defence Production to promote defence exports through various Ease of Doing 
Business (EoDB) reforms and sustained efforts to expand India's footprint in 
international defence markets.  The Committee appreciate the measures such as 
simplified SOP for Export Authorisation(EA) procedures,  fit-for-export 
certification, revamping of the Defence EXIM Portal and reduction of timelines for 
issuance of EAs which are expected to improve export performance by finding 
out new markets.    
The Committee further desire that the Department may continue to review 
the effectiveness of the ease of doing business (EoDB) reforms on a periodic 
basis of all the DPSUs, identify procedural bottlenecks, and take timely corrective 
measures so as to sustain the robust growth in defence exports. 
The Committee  are also glad to  note the Department's efforts to speed up 
new foreign markets through diplomatic outreach, high-level engagements, 
Seminars and Business-to-Business interactions.  However, the Committee are of 
the view that these initiatives should translate into a more balanced growth in 
exports across all the Defence Public Sector Undertakings, particularly those 
DPSUs whose export performance continues to remain relatively modest.   
The Committee observe that Department of Defence Production has made 
concerted efforts to promote defence exports, but the Department’s reply is silent 
with regard to details regarding MoUs, partnerships, collaborations, if any, 
DPSUs wise with foreign countries/organizations.  The Committee would like to 
know the efforts made by the Department/DPSUs to enter into 
partnership/collaboration with foreign companies or institutions to explore 
foreign markets.

R & D Expenditure 
Recommendation No. 4 
12. The Committee had recommended as under: 
The Committee learn from the reply of the Ministry that comparative study i.e. 
comparing products with international manufactures is a routine practice in line with 
market requirements and market is scanned on regular basis by the R&D group to 
develop new technological innovation.  Further, the Committee have been given to 
understand that DPSUs are having world class composite R&D establishments 
engaged in Design and Development of products to meet changing customers 
expectations and R&D activities are undertaken with their own sources.  
  
The Committee, find that the budget earmarked for R&D expenditure in FY 2025-
26 for old DPSUs viz, HAL, BEL, BDL, BEML, MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL 
stood at Rs. 2500 crore, Rs. 1630 crore, Rs.282 crore, Rs. 156 crore, Rs. 26 crore, Rs. 
16 crore, Rs. 35 crore, Rs. 20 crore and Rs. 5 crore, respectively.  Similarly, the 
planned R&D expenditure of DPSUs for 2026-27 for old DPSUs viz HAL, BEL, BDL, 
BEML, MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL stood at Rs. 2538  crore, Rs.; 1900 crore, 
Rs. 348 crore, Rs. 250 crore, Rs. 30 crore, Rs. 16.5 crore, Rs. 45 crore, Rs. 22 crore 
and Rs. 5 crore respectively. 
   
The Committee note from above details that R&D expenditure of some DPSUs 
particularly MIDHANI, MDL, GRSE, GSL and HSL for FY 202526 stood at Rs. 26 crore, 
Rs. 16 crore, Rs. 35 crore, Rs. 20 crore and Rs. 5 crore respectively, and for planned 
expenditure 2026-27 it stood at Rs. 30 crore, Rs. 16.5 crore, Rs. 45 crore, Rs. 22 crore 
and Rs. 5 crore, respectively, which is very meager as compared to other leading 
DPSUs. However, R&D component of  HAL and BEL still seem to be on a satisfactory 
line.  
 The Committee are of the considered view that Investments in R&D are not just 
expenses but strategic assets, directly proportional to longterm profitability and market 
competitiveness. For DPSUs, greater allocation of resources toward R&D is therefore in 
their own profit interest, ensuring sustained innovation, resilience against market 
disruptions, and long-term survival in an increasingly competitive defense landscape. 
The Committee hope that with the release of 5 year R&D road map, the proposed 
combined expenditure of Rs. 33,000 crore (out of which Rs. 11000 crore each is to 
spent by  HAL& BEL and rest by other seven DPSUs) to be spent by all nine DPSUs on 
R&D would be utilized fruitfully to achieve the aim of indigenization and market 
competitiveness. 
 

8 
 
Reply of the Government 
13. Department of Defence Production& DPSUs are thankful to the Committee for its 
insightful observations regarding the strategic imperatives of Research and 
Development (R&D).   
DPSUs are continuously focusing on achieving indigenisation in products and 
enhancing competitive edge technology. R&D allocation funds are expanded into high-
impact projects that foster innovation and reduce dependency on foreign imports for 
securing long-term survival and growth.  
Old DPSUs’ planned R&D expenditure for FY 2025-26 was Rs.4,816 Cr, against 
which  Rs.5159.73 Cr (prov.) has been spent which is in line with the 5-year R&D 
roadmap & Old DPSUs are committed to the effective utilization of the proposed R&D 
expenditure upto FY 2029-30. 
Further,DPSUs are preparing R&D Manual sync with best practices of relevant 
industrial concerns in the world. In this context, HAL has prepared its R&D Manual 
which includes a clear road map forimplementation of R&D to be adopted by HAL in 
sync with the applicable best practices adopted by the Global Aerospace Industry. 
Further, DPSUs also actively collaboratewith private industry, startups, MSMEs and 
academia to foster indigenous innovation, R&D activities etc. 
Comments of the Committee 
 14. The Committee are happy to note that the actual R&D expenditure incurred 
by the erstwhile DPSUs during the financial year 2025-26 has exceeded the 
planned allocation, reflecting the commitment of the Department and the DPSUs 
towards strengthening indigenous research and development capabilities. The 
Committee also appreciate the preparation of R&D Manuals in sync with best 
global practices and the increasing collaboration of DPSUs with private industry, 
start-ups, MSMEs and academic institutions for promoting innovation.  
The Committee, however, reiterate that sustained investment in research 
and development is indispensable for achieving technological self-reliance and 
maintaining the global competitiveness of the Indian defence industry.  While the 
overall increase in R&D expenditure is encouraging, the Committee desire that 
the Department should closely monitor the implementation of the five-year R&D 
Roadmap and ensure that the proposed investment of ₹33,000 crore during the 
five years roadmap results in achieving the aim of indigenization and thereby 
reducing dependency on foreign imports. 

9 
 
The Committee further desire that the Department may establish 
measurable outcome indicators for R&D projects, and periodically review the 
adequacy of R&D allocations across all the old- DPSUs so that DPSUs with 
comparatively lower R&D expenditure are adequately supported in developing 
niche technologies relevant to their respective domains. The Committee may be 
apprised and shared with R&D expenditure details and its utilization by DPSUs at 
the time of furnishing the Action Taken Statement by the Ministry/Department. 
C. Indigenization and Self-Reliance in Defence Sector 
 
Recommendation No. 6 
 
15. The Committee had recommended as under: 
The Committee learnt that indigenization of domestic defence production is 
mainly driven by Aatmanirbhar initiative, a strategic priority aimed at achieving self – 
reliance and reducing import dependency. The Committee observe that the Government 
is focusing on achieving indigenization and self – reliance in defence through various 
initiatives and policies.  These include launching of portal named ‘SRIJAN’ in August, 
2020 to offer defence items to industry for indigenization which gives access to Indian 
Manufacturing industry of the items which have been imported in the past or are likely to 
be imported in future by DPSUs/SHQs and as on December, 2025 more than 47000 
items are available on SRIJAN portal out of which more than 15400 items have already 
been indigenized.   
The Committee have also been made aware that DPSUs are participating in 
Innovation for Defence Excellence (IDEX) launched in April, 2018 with outlay of 5 year 
to foster innovation and technology development in Defence and Aerospace by 
engaging industries including MSMEs, startups by providing grants /funds and other 
support.  The Committee have been further apprised that it has an offshore scheme 
called ADITI Scheme launched on 14th March, 2024 upto 2025-26 with outlay of Rs. 
750 crore for futuristic and critical technologies.   
 The Committee note that initiatives taken for self reliance under Aatmanirbhar 
Bharat are showing desired results.  The defence production in FY 2024-25 of Rs. 
1,54,071 crore and defence exports in FY 2025-26 (upto 31.12.2025) of Rs. 25929 
crore is ample to substantiate the above fact.   
The Committee further desire that concerted and timely measures be taken by 
the Ministry and DPSUs to achieve complete self-reliance in defence production.  For 
this, the DPSUs and defence private sector requires adequate budgetary support, 
investment in R&D, availability of technology, modernization etc. The Committee hope 
that adequate and concrete steps will be taken to meet the above requirements.   

10 
 
Reply of the Government 
16. The Government has taken several policy initiatives and brought in reforms to 
encourage indigenous design, development and manufacture of defence equipment, 
thereby promoting self-reliance in defence manufacturing and technology in the country. 
These initiatives, inter-alia include the following: - 
(a) DAP 2020 prioritizes procurement from domestic sources. 
(b)    SRIJAN portal lists import-substitution items; more than 47,000 items listed, more 
than 15,500 indigenized (as of Mar 2026). 
(C) Five Positive Indigenisation Lists (PILs) cover 5,012 items; 3,190 indigenized 
(d) Srijan DEEP platform connects industry capabilities with defence needs; included 
40,000+ companies, 17,000+ MSMEs, and 2.58 lakh products/services. 
(e) ‘Make’ procedure boosts industry participation: 
 (i) Make-I: Govt-funded (up to 70%); 37 projects approved 
 (ii) Make-II: Industry-funded; 99 projects approved. 
 (iii) Make-III: Manufacturing via ToT from foreign OEMs; 3 projects approved. 
(f) iDEX initiative (2018) supports innovation; Rs. 498.78 Cr allocated (2021-26). 
(g) ADITI scheme: Rs. 750 Cr (2023-26), grants up to Rs. 25 Cr for advanced 
technologies. 
(h) Technology Development Fund (TDF) promotes R&D; funding increased to Rs. 
50 Cr per project. 
(i) Defence Industrial Corridors in UP & Tamil Nadu: Rs. 71,006 Cr proposed, Rs. 
9,458 Cr invested. 
(K) MSME conclaves (13 annually) raise awareness and foster collaboration. 
(j) National Defence Industries Conclave (NDIC) promotes advanced 
manufacturing; 1,500+ participants. 
Comments of the Committee 
17. The Committee note with satisfaction that the Government has taken 
several policy initiatives and brought in reforms to encourage indigenous design, 
development and manufacturing of defence equipments, thereby promoting self-
reliance in defence manufacturing and technology in the country.  The Committee 
in particular appreciate the progress achieved through the SRIJAN Portal, the 

11 
 
Positive Indigenisation Lists, the SRIJAN DEEP platform, the Make procedure, the 
iDEX initiative and ADITI schemes, the Technology Development Fund (TDF), 
establishment of Defence Industrial Corridors, MSME conclaves and National 
Defence Industries Conclave.   
The Committee are, therefore, pleased to note the policy initiatives and 
reforms brought in to promote self-reliance in defence manufacturing and 
technology.  The Committee welcome the organizing of MSMEs conclaves to 
raise awareness and foster collaboration.  In this regard, the Committee would, 
further like to know the details of MSMEs/Start-ups involved/entered into 
MOU/partnerships with various DPSUs.  The Committee may be apprised of the 
same at the time of submitting the  Action Taken Statement by the Ministry.

D. Financial Performance and Profitability of DPSUs 
 
 
Recommendation No. 7 
 
18. The Committee had recommended as under: 
The Committee note from the data provided by the Ministry on the profits figures 
of new DPSUs that the profit after tax (PAT) in FY 2024-25 for new DPSUs viz, AVNL, 
AWEIL, GIL, IOL, MIL, TCL and YIL stood at Rs 335 crore, Rs. 82.74 crore, Rs. 16.67 
crore,  Rs. 294.73 crore, Rs. 839.86 crore, Rs. 132.96 crore, and Rs. 509 crore, 
respectively.  Similarly, the PAT in FY 2023-24 for new DPSUs , viz, AVNL, AWEIL, 
GIL, IOL, MIL, TCL and YIL was Rs. 605 crore, Rs. 20.24 crore, Rs. 10.76 crore, Rs. 
233.03 crore, Rs. 558.78 crore, Rs. -303 crore, and Rs. 425.58 crore, respectively.   
The Committee have also been apprised that  final PAT for the current year i.e. 
2025-26 will be available only after finalization of accounts in FY 2026. The Committee 
observe that PAT has been highest for MIL and second highest for YIL for both the 
above years.  The profit figures for AVNIL in the year 2023-24 was Rs. 605 crore and 
the same has reduced considerably to Rs.335 crore in the year 2024-25.  Similarly the 
PAT for TCL in the year 2023-24 was Rs.303 crore and the same reduced to Rs.132.96 
crore in the year 2024-25.   The Committee further noted that profits for the other new 
DPSUs viz. AWEIL and GIL are comparatively on lower side.   The Committee observe 
that Ministry have given various reasons for loss of AWEIL and GIL stating inter alia that 
reasons for loss were due to account change in according treatment of government

Reply of the Government 
25. At present, there are 20 lakhs authorized NCC vacancies under JD/JW and 
SD/SW categories, including Regular, FSFS, and Expansion Plan. Considering the 
persistent high demand for NCC affiliation across the country, an additional 3 lakh 
vacancies were approved by the Government of India in 2024. Enrolment against these 
vacancies has commenced and is planned to be completed over a three-year period 
from 2025 to 2028. 
  With regard to the concern expressed by the Hon’ble Committee on the number 
of Waitlisted Institutions, it is submitted that liquidating the entire waiting list would 
require sanctioning of approximately 11,48,600 additional NCC vacancies, assuming 
allocation of one Troop (100 cadets) per institution. This would constitute more than 
50% of the existing sanctioned authorized strength of NCC Cadets. 
  Further, to cater to such an accretion in cadet strength, a substantial number of 
existing minor and major NCC Units would require to be standardized. In addition, a 
certain number of new NCC Units would also need to be raised. This would entail 
significant augmentation of manpower, including Service Officers, JCOs, and NCOs, as 
well as the authorized state civilian staff for each NCC Unit. It would also involve 
considerable financial implications and necessitate the availability of adequate 
infrastructure such as office accommodation, storage facilities, parade grounds, firing 
ranges, etc. 

18 
 
  It is also pertinent to highlight that NCC Group Headquarters and Units are 
presently facing an approximate 47% deficiency in state civilian manpower, which 
further impedes the effective functioning of NCC Units. 
  It may also be noted that the demand for NCC vacancies is increasing steadily 
across the country. Over the past six years, a total of 5 lakh additional NCC cadet 
vacancies have been sanctioned (2 lakh under the Fully Self-Financing Scheme (FSFS) 
and 3 lakhs under the expansion plan) to minimize the waiting list of institutions. 
Correspondingly, there has been a significant rise in demand for NCC affiliation from 
educational institutions. In view of the foregoing, while continuous efforts are being 
made to expand NCC coverage, complete liquidation of the waiting list may not be 
feasible in the immediate future.

Reply of the Government

persistent high demand for NCC affiliation across the country, an additional 3 lakh

from 2025 to 2028.

of Waitlisted Institutions, it is submitted that liquidating the entire waiting list would 
require sanctioning of approximately 11,48,600 additional NCC vacancies, assuming 
allocation of one Troop (100 cadets) per institution. This would constitute more than 
50% of the existing sanctioned authorized strength of NCC Cadets. 
 
 3. Further, to cater to such an accretion in cadet strength, a substantial number of 
existing minor and major NCC Units would require to be standardized. In addition, a 
certain number of new NCC Units would also need to be raised. This would entail 
significant augmentation of manpower, including Service Officers, JCOs, and NCOs, as 
well as the authorized state civilian staff for each NCC Unit. It would also involve 
considerable financial implications and necessitate the availability of adequate 
infrastructure such as office accommodation, storage facilities, parade grounds, firing 
ranges, etc.

presently facing an approximate 47% deficiency in state civilian manpower, which 
further impedes the effective functioning of NCC Units.

and 3 lakhs under the expansion plan) to minimize the waiting list of institutions. 
Correspondingly, there has been a significant rise in demand for NCC affiliation from
