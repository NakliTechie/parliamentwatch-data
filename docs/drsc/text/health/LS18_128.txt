CHAPTER I 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The popular nomenclature of the Ministry of AYUSH is the abbreviated form for 
Ayurveda, Yoga, & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa -Rigpa and Homoeopathy. The primary 
objective of the Ministry is to so develop AYUSH systems as to position them as the preferred 
systems of living and practice for attaining a healthy India.  
 
1.2 In order to attain its objectives, the Ministry has identified t he following seven broad 
thematic areas of AYUSH activities. The thematic areas are as follows: 
 
(i) Effective Human Resource Development 
(ii) Provision of Quality AYUSH Services 
(iii) Information, Education and Communication 
(iv) Quality Research in AYUSH 
(v) Growth of the Medicinal Plants Sector 
(vi) Drugs Administration 
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminars/Workshops on AYUSH

1.7 The Committee was given to understand that the AYUSH Ministry's response in Covid -
19 Pandemic included - (i) Health Advisory and Immunity care guidelines through AYUSH 
Systems, (ii) Standardized AYUSH treatment guidelines & National Protocol for management of 
Covid-19 through Ayurveda & Yoga, (iii) Placement of 8,32,445 AYUSH professional on 
covidwarriors.gov.in, (iv) Utilization of AYUSH hospitals and colleges, (v) Constitution of 
AYUSH inter disciplinary R&D Task Force for Covid-19 and Total Research studies-109. 
1.8 The Committee takes cognizance of the myriad functional areas of the Ministry of 
AYUSH, its key initiatives and the emergence of AYUSH in public healthcare system. The 
Committee is appreciative of the work done by the Ministry for management of Covid 19. 
The efforts have not only generated genuine interest but also a lot of trust in the Indian 
Systems of medicine. Renewed interest in AYUSH system of medicine had led the country 
on the path of rediscovery of glorious past. The Committee is of the firm belief that even 
though Covid -19 pandemic has caused havoc world over, it has provided a golden 
opportunity for the AYUSH Systems to emerge as a strong alternative system of medicine.  
1.9 The Committee takes note of the efforts being made by the Ministry to increase the 
presence of AYUSH systems in public health which include s creating AYUSH departments 
in defense and railway hospitals; granting soft loans and subsidies  for the establishment of 
private AYUSH hospitals and clinics; building world -class institutes for both teaching and 
research in AYUSH systems; building digital infrastructure like AYUSH GR ID for better 
access and reach etc by the Ministry to promote and popularize the AYUSH system at 
district level. The Committee believes that it is high time the AYUSH system is encouraged 
by recognizing its importance and allocating adequate funds for its development and 
strengthening to enable it to regain its lost glory.

1.10 To a specific query about the achievement of 'Objectives of Health for All' during the 
year 2020 -21, the Committee has been informed that propagation of AYUSH systems of 
medicines (through Information, Education and Communication) has been intensified by 
increased webinars and digital events on the AYUSH Virtual Convention Centre (AVCC) 
platforms. This has been made possible by partnership wi th the stakeholders like Confederation 
of Indian Industry (CII), Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce & Industry (FICCI), 
Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India (ASSOCHAM) and other chambers of 
Commerce. Some of the activities organised  during the current financial year i.e. 2020 -21 are as 
under: 
i. Upscaling of social media platforms : During the International Day of Yoga (IDY) 2020, 
the paid promotion of 'My Yoga My Life' Contest helped increase the reach and views on 
social media sites to exponential level. 
 
ii. Webinars on virtual platforms : Ministry of AYUSH through various platforms including 
its own AYUSH Virtual Convention Centre (AVCC) held webinars on various topics. A 
total of 6 webinars took place from 14 th Aug 2020. The event was als o streamed live on 
the official Facebook handle of the Ministry of AYUSH with live viewership of 60000 
people. 
 
iii. Launching of AYUSH for Immunity Campaign : With a view to leverage all its resources 
in Information Education Communication (IEC) AYUSH for Immun ity Campaign, 
Research Councils and National Institutes have used their platforms under guidance and 
monitoring from the Ministry.  The Ministry of AYUSH has also made effort to come up 
with many contents and creatives on its social media platforms to conv erge the activities 
undertaken by the Research Councils and National Institutes. Over 1.5 Crore people were 
reached through these activities and 74,000 people attended the online inaugural event. 
 
iv. Contribution from State Governments : The State governments have also been guided to 
utilise the creatives and contents of the Ministry of AYUSH and take forward the 
message among a vast population. 
 
v. Creation of videos: Ministry of AYUSH is also preparing short videos with the support of 
National Film Development Corporation of India (NFDC). A video on All India Institute 
of Ayurveda (AIIA) perspective is ready. For National AYUSH Mission (NAM) related 
videos, BOC have been provided with Rs. 3.06 Crore for 15 videos on Ayush in 
Nutrition. 
 
vi. Community Radio programmes : Ministry of AYUSH has also engaged Commonwealth 
Educational Media Centre for Asia(CEMCA), an Inter -Governmental organisation to 
disseminate information through Community Radio Stations(CRS) among rural 
population. The Ministry engaged CEMCA for developin g, managing, conducting, 
preparing and reporting for 5 minutes programme on 25 CRS (Community Radio 
Stations) for a perid of 2 months during the pandemic period.   The programme started on 
10th December, 2020 and concluded on 10th February, 2021.

1.13 The Committee takes note of the efforts made by Ministry in a chieving its objective 
of “Health for all” during 2020 -21 under NAM and Information, Education and 
Communication Schemes by way of providing cost effective AYUSH services and also to 
strengthen and popularize AYUSH systems at the grass root level. The Comm ittee, 
however, feels much more needs to be done towards making AYUSH healthcare facilities 
accessible to all and making its presence felt in rural and remote areas of the country.  
There have however, been doubts and questions regarding the efficacy of Ind ian systems of 
medicine. Even though the popularity and presence of AYUSH systems is increasing, the 
need of the hour is to lay focus on standardization of academic courses and scientific 
validation of AYUSH system.  The Committee desires the Ministry to fo cus on 
standardisation of quality of AYUSH healthcare services, quality control of AYUSH drugs, 
strengthening of drug testing laboratories, pharmacies and improving the quality of 
AYUSH education in the country so as to position AYUSH as a true alternative  system of 
medicine.  
1.14 The Committee also recommends that the Ministry must devise concrete strategy to 
address the issue of practice of AYUSH system of medicine by quacks which has eroded the 
faith of the public in the system. The Ministry may also st udy and take advantage of the 
Chinese experience of integrating traditional Chinese Medicine with the modern system of 
medicine. The Committee would like the Ministry to prepare a comprehensive and specific 
course of action to re -discover the glorious past  of AYUSH system that reigned and ruled 
the world healthcare delivery system.

2.3 The Committee expresses its serious concern over the sharp shortfall in allocation of 
the Ministry as compared to the projected demand, despite its repeated recommendation 
for increasing the budgetary allocation of the Ministry, keeping in view the scope, reach 
and value AYUSH can add to the health of the people. Given its indigenous/traditional 
element, cost effectiveness and a safe alternative to modern system of medicine, the 
Ministry of AYUSH, through its schemes and strategies, can help  AYUSH system of 
healthcare to emerge as a valuable system of medicine in healthcare delivery. The 
Committee, therefore, is of the view that budgetary constraints should not be allowed to act 
as a stumbling block to achieve the mission objective s to positi on AYUSH system as the 
primary and preferred system of healthcare in India. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that adequate budgetary support be provided to the Ministry as and when 
required by it.

head start and will significantly help in achieving the desired objectives of the scheme. The 
Committee, however, expects the Ministry of Finance to provide adequate fund for Central 
Sector Scheme at R.E. stage.

SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY  
NATIONAL AYUSH MISSION (NAM) 
3.1 The National AYUSH Mission is a flagship Centrally Sponsored Scheme of the Ministry 
which was launched in September, 2014 and was approved originally for continuation till March, 
2021. The Ministry of AYUSH is proposing for continuation of NAM from 1.04.20 1 to 
31.03.2026 for consideration of EFC of Department of Expenditure.  
 
3.2 NAM envisages better access to AYUSH services through AYUSH Hospitals and 
Dispensaries, mainstreaming of AYUSH through co -location of AYUSH facilities at Primary 
Health Centres (P HCs), Community Health Centres (CHCs) and District Hospitals (DHs) and 
ensuring availability of AYUSH drugs and trained manpower. It also aims to improve the quality 
of AYUSH Education through enhancement in the number of upgraded educational Institutions,  
sustained availability of Quality Raw materials and improved availability of quality Ayurveda, 
Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy (ASU &H) drugs through increase in number of Pharmacies 
and setting up of Drug Testing Laboratories in the States.  
 
3.3 The Natio nal Health Policy (NHP) 2017 has advocated mainstreaming the potential of 
AYUSH systems within a pluralistic system of Integrative healthcare. Accordingly, i n 
convergence with National Health Mission (NHM), a total of 27495 AYUSH doctors (11741 
AYUSH docto r under co -located facilities and 15754 AYUSH doctor under Rashtriya Bal 
Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK) have been deployed under NHM as on 30.06.2020.  
 
3.4 A comparative analysis of BE & RE 2020 -21 and projected demand BE allocation for 
2021-22 for NAM shows that an allocation of Rs. 705.00 crore was made in BE 2020 -21 which 
was reduced to Rs. 400.00 crore at the RE stage.  An allocation of Rs. 553.80 crore has been 
made in BE 2021-22 against the projected requirement of Rs.775.00 crores.

respect to institutions drug testing labs, etc. Keeping in view a target of setting up of 3100

requirements realistically and approach Ministry of Finance to enhance the fund at the RE 
stage.

MISLEADING ADVERTISEMENTS DURING PANDEMIC COVID 19 
 
4.10 The Committee notes that during the Covid-19 period, the Ministry of AYUSH registered 
a sharp spike in complaints against manufacturers of Ayurveda and other traditional products for 
misleading consumers. The Committee has been given to understand that the Ministry received 
over 570 complaints between March and December 2020, related to false, ambiguous and 
misleading claims by manufacturers of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani products. The figure is 
twice the 268 complaints that the ministry registered in March 2019-20. 
 
4.11 The Committee believes that misleading and unfounded advertisements particularly 
by ASU drug makers will force people to gradually d esist from consuming such ASU 
formulations. The Committee recommends that provisions of both the Drugs & Cosm etics 
Act Rules & Magical Remedies Act should be made more stringent to tackle the problem of 
misleading and unfounded advertisements. The Committee desires the Ministry to revisit 
both the period of imprisonment and the fine amount under these rules to re gulate and 
control the violation of the provisions of the said Acts. The licensing authorities and drug 
controllers at the State level must also take prompt actions against any such misconduct.  
 
4.12 The Committee has been given to understand that the Ministry of AYUSH established a 
three-tier network of Pharmacovigilance centres - 01-National; 05-Intermediary and  Peripheral 
Pharmacovigilance Centers. This network has the task of surveillance of misleading 
advertisements of ASU &H drugs/systems in print and  TV media for reporting to State 
regulatory authorities.  
 
4.13 The Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry must ensure stringent 
implementation of Pharmacovigilance schemes. For this, the Committee recommends that 
the education-related to Pharmacovigilance should be included in the curriculum itself and 
periodic awareness programmes should be organized for both health care professionals 
and patients. Moreover, monthly reporting of ADRs (Adverse Drug Reactions) reports 
should be made mandatory at all AYUSH wellness centres. The Committee believes that 
maintaining a comprehensive and updated medicine literature database would provide 
credible and latest medical information to health care professionals. Better sync between 
drug regulators would ensure that authorities are well educated on safety issues in 
everyday practice and thus would be relevant to future regulatory action. The Committee 
desires that the submission of Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSUR) must be made 
mandatory for AYUSH drugs.  The Committee, therefore, would like to reiterate that all 
these measu res can only be realized if the Pharmacovigilance network is provided with 
adequate funds and manpower. The Committee, therefore, strongly desires that the 

46 
 
Ministry should seek adequate funds from the Ministry of Finance (MoF) to strengthen the 
pharmacovigilance network both at the central and regional level.

OUTPUT-OUTCOME FRAMEWORK FOR SCHEMES 2021-22 
 The Committee has taken into consideration the output -outcome framework for 
schemes of Ministry of AYUSH which envelops financial outlay, targets sets for outputs 
and outcome during 2021-22. The Committee hopes that the financial outlay earmarked for 
various schemes would create the enabling environment and bring forth catalectic change 
in the basic infrastructure for AYUSH education and research and improve the AYUSH 
healthcare services.  The Committee is of the considered view that the budgetary allocatio n 
to the Centre Sector Schemes and National AYUSH Mission would definitely improve the 
quality of AYUSH health care facilities in the country. The Committee hopes that all the 
projects undertaken during 2021 -22 would be completed without time and cost over run 
especially construction projects at National Institutes. The Committee recommends the 
Ministry of AYUSH to strengthen its monitoring mechanism to oversee the execution of 
research projects and to ensure that physical targets set during the year are ach ieved. The 
Committee also recommends the Ministry to undertake annual performance evaluation of 
each scheme and programme through cost benefit analysis and SWOT analysis. The 
Committee desires that the Ministry should formulate output -outcome framework for  
remaining years of 15th Finance Commission in order to have a holistic overview of the 
schemes under the Ministry. The Committee, while taking into account the financial and 
the physical achievement under various schemes of the Ministry, recommends the following 
organizational interventions for improvements:-  
(i) Advance planning to be made for completion of various formalities so that the 
process of sanctioning and release of funds for the various projects be started 
in the beginning of the next financial year. 
(ii) More workshops and review meetings to be organized for addressing the issues 
in the implementation of the schemes.  
(iii) More field visits to be organized for onsite appraisal of progress of 
implementation of the schemes 
(iv)  Dashboard for online monitoring of schemes and programmes.

PROGRESSIVE LEGISLATION AND INTEGRATED HEALTH MANAGEMENT 
 The Committee believes that all progressive legislation undertaken by the 
Government aims at focusing integrated Health and Well -being and that is the guiding 
philosophy of the Budget 2021 -22. The Committee has already supported the adoption of 
an inclusive and integrated health care policy. The Committee believes that AYUSH and its 
holistic approach to promoting preventive and holistic healt hcare must also be 
strengthened. The Committee, in this regard, again impresses upon the Government to 
expedite the process of piloting the Yoga and the Naturopathy Bill, to give further impetus 
to the integrated and holistic approach to the Health and Well-being.  
(Para 4.29) 
 The Committee observes that the trend of diseases as well as severity of illnesses has 
undergone a substantial change in the modern age. Advancement in modern science has 
brought out a revolution in the way one identifies, diagnoses and treats illnesses now -a-
days. The Committee believes that AYUSH with its traditional/classic knowledge has 
immense potential in devising a holistic approach against the ever changing challenges in 
the health sector and ensuring good health of the masses . However, AYUSH has remained 
neglected and modern system of medicine, with a scientific evidence base, has 
overshadowed AYUSH's popularity and growth. Nevertheless, the Committee observes 
that with the formation of a separate Ministry for AYUSH, various e fforts have been made 
for the revival of AYUSH over the years. The Committee appreciates the Schemes and 
Programmes implemented by the Ministry of AYUSH for mainstreaming of AYUSH 
services and acknowledges its satisfactory trend of budgetary utilization. T he Committee 
also believes that it is high time to make AYUSH more competitive and expand its outreach 
across States as well as in the international market. The Committee believes that for 
strengthening AYUSH research, education institutes, its industry an d the quality of 
AYUSH healthcare, a substantial enhancement in budgetary allocation is the need of the 
hour. AYUSH must also collaborate and coordinate with other scientific disciplines as well 
as the modern system of medicine.  Integration of AYUSH with modern advances will 
enhance its reliability and scientifically validate the ancient AYUSH traditions.  
(Para 4.30)
