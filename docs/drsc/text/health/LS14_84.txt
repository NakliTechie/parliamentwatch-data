Introduction
1. The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy (ISM&H) was established as a
separate Department under Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Government of India in 1995. It was
renamed as Department of Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy (AYUSH) in
November, 2003. The Department of AYUSH has now been made an independent Ministry w.e.f. 09.11.2014.
The Ministry is mandated for policy, planning and program implementation for growth, development and
propagation of AYUSH systems across national and international fronts. Sowa Rigpa is the recent inclusion
to the family of AYUSH systems. The Ministry under its purview has National Institutes, Research Councils,
Regulatory Bodies, Pharmacopoieal Laboratories, Pharmacopoeia Commission, medicines manufacturing
enterprise and National Medicinal Plants Board.
2. The Twelfth Five Year Plan aims at promoting the health goals with appropriate utilization of AYUSH
in the health delivery system based on their strength areas and interventions for health promotion and
management of disease burden.
3. All the existing Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) have been merged into a single umbrella Scheme
namely, National AYUSH Mission (NAM) and introduced w.e.f. 29.09.2014.
4. The scope of Pharmacopoeia Commission of Indian Medicine was expanded to include Homoeopathy
in its purview with necessary fundamental changes in the structure for smooth performance.
5. Approval of National AYUSH Mission, as a unified Centrally Sponsored Scheme, will help to support
implementation of state programs and projects with more flexibility and better funding pattern. Revised
Central Schemes for public health interventions of AYUSH, Extra Mural Research, Development of Industry
Clusters, promotional activities of AYUSH, International co-operation and incentive to the industry are meant
for propelling propagation of AYUSH in tandem with the public aspirations and needs.
6. The proposed budget amounting to Rs.1008 crore for the financial year 2015-16 would be utilized for
comprehensive development of AYUSH sector. Further, the outcomes would go a long way in giving much
needed boost towards providing health care services and global leadership of the country in the field of
traditional medicine.
7. The Ministry under its purview has two Regulatory Councils, five Research Councils, seven National
Institutes, two Pharmacopeia Laboratories, a National Medicinal Plants Board and a public sector undertaking
for manufacture of Ayurveda & Unani medicine.
8. During the Twelfth Plan period, the Ministry seeks to strengthen initiatives taken earlier to expand the
reach of health care through Traditional Systems of Medicine. The Twelfth Plan duly recognized the fact
that the strengths of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy, if suitably used, can help advance the
goals of the Twelfth Plan. AYUSH systems would be main-streamed using their areas of strengths namely in
preventive and promotive health care, diseases and health conditions relating to women and children, older
persons, Non-communicable diseases, preventive health care, mental ailments, stress management, palliative
care, rehabilitation and health promotion.

10. A perusal of the budget allocation made for the six years from April, 2007 to March, 2013, the
Committee notes that total allocation of Rs. 4849 crores was made for the Department of AYUSH and out of
this amount only 76% i.e. Rs. 3709 crores could be spent. The Committee fails to comprehend this state of
consistent under-utilization when a lot of work is still required to be accomplished by AYUSH, namely
efficacy and quality of AYUSH drugs are still to be brought under Controller, education for producing
AYUSH Doctors and allied staff is in a bad shape and the Department has failed in bringing a legislation for
creating a regulatory authority for control and regulation of AYUSH system of Health care, quality of drugs
and education/training for producing adequate manpower.

16. Explaining the persistent under-utilization of allocated funds over the years, the Secretary (AYUSH)
during his evidence before the Committee on 31st March, 2015, attributed the main reasons for less utilization
of funds to structural weaknesses of State AYUSH Institutions, less absorption capacity of the States and
pending Utilization Certificates.
17. The Secretary also enumerated the following steps taken to ensure that the un-utilized balance is
minimized.
(i) The Ministry has comprehensively reviewed the expenditure profile of each major scheme/
programme before preparing the budget estimates.
(ii) Timely release and assimilation of funds by State Governments/ Institutes/Councils and other
organizations, is being emphasized.
(iii) Nodal Officers for individual States have been nominated by Ministry of AYUSH to follow-up
the matter with individual States for liquidation of outstanding UCs.
(iv) Regular meetings with state officials are held at the level of Secretary (AYUSH) to emphasize
and ensure optimal and efficient utilization of funds. In the first-ever meeting of state AYUSH
Ministers Chaired by Hon'ble Minister (IC), AYUSH the issue of pending Utilization Certificates
has also been reviewed.
(v) Scheme-wise Roadmaps for future utilisation of funds have been drawn for efficient and
timely utilization of funds - particularly in the first and second quarter of the next financial year
and identify possible bottlenecks well in advance and address the same. For example, emphasis
is laid on holding of the regular meetings of Mission Directorate, Project Screening Committee
(PSC), Technical Screening Committee (TSC) and Standing Finance Committee (SFC) etc.
for various schemes, well in advance so as to ensure timely release of funds in 2015-16.
(vi) One of the flagship schemes, the National AYUSH Mission (NAM), was approved and notified
on 29.09.2014. NAM focuses on implementation efficiency and improving absorption capacity
of the States through setting up of State AYUSH Societies and strengthening of State Programme
Management Units with adequate staff and infrastructure for implementing and monitoring the
mission activities. One of the criteria for deciding the allocation 'envelop' to the states is their
track record in utilizing funds in the past.
(vii) Action plan from 27 States for Rs. 185.72 already sanctioned. The required budget Head for
NAM has already been created. Out of Rs. 185.72 crore merely Rs. 9.90 crore could be
released so far. Around Rs.120 crore is estimated to be released for already approved NAM
action plans in the first quarter of the next year itself against a total annual allocation of Rs.318
crore.
(viii) The Ministry is also working to support the States to strengthen the institutional framework
and improve capacity for better leverage and efficacy in utilization of funds. For example,

7
under NAM, there is a specific component for strengthening State Programme Management
Units. The 21 States/ UTs have already intimated the constitutions of State AYUSH Societies
and other states/ UTs are also gearing up for the constitution of AYUSH Societies. Similarly
support to State Medicinal Plant Boards has been significantly increased under the revised
Central Sector Scheme Guidelines of the NMPB.
18. The Secretary also drew attention of the Committee to the changeover from the Society route of
funds to the Treasury Route in the financial year 2014-15. He stated that earlier the funds were released to
the State Health Societies directly. As per the new arrangement, funds have to go to the State Budgets. The
State Governments have to contribute their share and draw that amount and pass it on to the Health societies.
The Secretary submitted that in the direct transmission of money from the Central Government to the State
Health Society through the Treasury Route, UC conditionality is not an absolute bar for release of funds. He
also submitted that the problems have now been largely ironed out and utilization rate of funds is expected
to increase in the financial year 2015-16 because all the Centrally Sponsored Schemes are to be funded
through the Treasury Route. Highlighting the weak AYUSH structures in the States, the Secretary informed
the Committee that in the entire country only two States, namely Uttarakhand and Rajasthan have separate
AYUSH Departments. 25 of 36 States/UTs have AYUSH Directorates which are not at par with the full-
fledged Department.

22. The Committee is concerned to find that there were savings of significant magnitude under
several important heads during 2013-14 and 2014-15 as is evident from Table Nos. 2 and 3 given
above. The fact that the savings were not just the result of implementation of economy measures
but occurred due to various reasons like non-filing of posts, non-approval of schemes, delay in
execution work, slow space of construction etc, clearly suggests that there were inadequacies in the
monitoring mechanism, as well as budgetary planning process and budget allocations were proposed
without obtaining all necessary clearances. The Committee is of the firm view that the savings
would have been minimized substantially if proper internal auditing and streamlined monitoring
mechanisms had been in place and periodic review of the trends and progress of expenditure had
been adopted. The Committee notes that the Ministry of AYUSH has initiated, at the behest of this
Committee, a number of steps to ensure optimal and efficient utilization of allotted funds. The
Committee would like the Ministry to scrupulously implement the initiatives taken in this regard
and simultaneously evaluate the impact of new initiatives on utilization of budgeted funds. The
Committee would like to be kept apprised of the impact of the new initiatives taken on utilization
of budgeted funds.
23. The Committee is constrained to note that there has been persistent and substantial
underutilization of Plan funds under the heads - "Hospitals and Dispensaries", "Educational
Institutions", "Research and Development including Medicinal Plants", "Development of AYUSH
Industry", and "National Mission on AYUSH". The allocations made in BE 2013-14 and BE 2014-15
under the above heads, were subsequently revised downwards at the RE stage and the sums which
remained grossly underutilized, were surrendered by the end of the year. The Committee observes
that such huge variations in BE, RE and Actuals are indicative of the fact that principles of fiscal
prudence and discipline were neither observed nor proper assessment of the projects was carried
out. The Committee observes that in a resource-constrained country like India, all care needs to be
taken to ensure that the scarce resources do not remain locked up and surrendered at the fiscal
end. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry chalk out its fiscal roadmap in such
a way that the allocated funds are utilized optimally and more productively under the above Heads
and such a situation does not recur in future.
24. From a scrutiny of information furnished, it is revealed that the entire budgeted plan funds
had to be surrendered due to non-approval of certain Schemes. For example, a sum of Rs. 4.00 crore
and Rs. 25.00 crore had been proposed in BE 2013-14 and BE 2014-15 respectively under the head
"All India Institute of Unani Medicine." The Ministry spent a meagre Rs. 0.20 crore in 2013-14
while the entire budgeted funds for 2014-15 had to be surrendered due to non-approval of the
project. What is alarming is that this is not the only example of surrender of the entire budgeted
funds. Similar is the case with quite a few other projects such as "All India Institute of Yoga", "All
India Institute of Homoeopathy", "Homoeopathic Medicines Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd",
"Pharmacovigilance Initiative for ASU drugs", "National Institute of Medicinal Plants", etc. The
Committee deprecates the Ministry for making unrealistic projection of fund requirements without
obtaining necessary approvals for implementation of the above projects. The Committee, therefore,
strongly recommends that the Ministry should ensure that all pre-approval formalities are completed
before budget allocations are proposed and realistic financial outlays are earmarked for the above
projects/schemes. There is no point in making ritualistic allocations which remain on paper only.
25. The Committee observes that to obtain greater evenness in the budgeted expenditure within
a financial year and reduce rush of expenditure in the last quarter of the financial year, Ministry of

9
Finance has prescribed the expenditure ceiling of 33% for the last quarter of the financial year. As
is evident from Table No. 5, the Ministry of AYUSH has breached the ceiling of 33%, though only
marginally, by incurring expenditure of 36.61% of plan funds in the last quarter. The Committee
would like the Ministry to monitor expenditure of funds more closely and avoid rush of expenditure
in the last quarter.

Updation of Central Registers of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy
47. The updation of Central Registers of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy has been engaging the attention
of the Committee for quite some time. In reply to a question regarding the status of updation of Central
Registers of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy, the Ministry has furnished the following information
(Annexure - 1). On being asked about the time lag between the receipt of information from the State
Governments and its updation into the Central Registers of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy, the Ministry
have furnished the following information:
"As regards time lag of updation in the Central Registers of Indian Medicine, it is informed that the procedure
for entry of information received from the State Boards/Councils to Central Register takes at least four to six
months depending on the number of practitioners registered in the State Register because the procedure
includes, inter alia, that:
(i) State Register is screened keeping in view that all the particulars fulfilling the requirement of
the CCIM of the registered practitioners are furnished in respective columns;
(ii) Further screening of the State Register is done to ensure that the practitioners are possessing
recognized medical qualification of Ayurveda, Unani and Siddha included in the Schedules to
IMCC Act, 1970;
(iii) Translation of Central Register of Indian Medicine from English to Hindi or vice versa;
(iv) Thereafter the Central Register is typed bilingually in English and Hindi and compared to the
State Register in order to ensure the correctness of the same; and
(v) The prepared Central Register in English and Hindi is sent/submitted to the Controller of
Publication, Deptt. of Publication, Govt, of India for its Gazette Notification in the Gazette of
India.
48. Regarding time lag of updation in the Central Registers of Homoeopathy, it is informed that the state
Registers, as and when received, are scrutinized and'as required, clarifications are sought by C.C.H. from
State Boards/ Councils which require some time. However, on an average taking data from State Register to
Central Register consumes three months to 24 months in view of the said reason."
49. The Committee regrets to note that despite its earlier recommendation to timely update the
Central Registers of the Indian systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy, not much headway has
been made in the matter, mainly due to delay in submission of accurate information by the State
Boards and its incorporation in the Central Registers. This is contrary to a provision in the Indian
Medicine Central Council (IMCC) Act 1970 which mandates the State Boards to submit updated
registers of practitioners on the first of April every year. The Committee is also dismayed to note
from the information furnished that the data received from the States is often found to have several
inaccuracies which further delays the updation of the Registers. The Committee is also surprised to
note that the time-lag between the receipt of information from the State Governments and its
updation /incorporation is 6 to 9 months in respect of the Central Register of Indian Medicine and
3 to 24 months in respect of the Central Register of Homoeopathy. Given the IT resources currently
available, the Committee is unable to reconcile itself with this huge time-lag. The Committee
would, therefore, recommend that the Ministry should initiate action, both statutory and
administrative, to facilitate timely maintenance and submission of accurate information and its
incorporation in both the Central Registers of the Indian systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy.
The Committee would like to be apprised of the action initiated in this regard.

Issues concerning regulation of Naturopathy and Yoga
63. On being asked whether there is any mechanism in place for registration of Practitioners of Naturopathy
and Yoga, the Committee has been informed that presently there was no provision for registration of
Naturopathy & Yoga practitioners at Central Government level. However, some of the State Governments
like Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamilnadu, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and West Bengal
have provision for registration of such practitioners for BNYS graduates. The Central Government had
issued guidelines in the year 2006. In 2014 again, the Ministry had circulated a concept note on this subject
to all the State Governments, Universities & Colleges, and other stake holders. Based on the feedback
received in response thereto, the shape and structure of future mechanism for registration is under deliberation.
The then Department of AYUSH had decided in 2006, not to regulate Yoga and Naturopathy at Central level
on the basis of the following considerations:
(a) The CCIM did not agree to the proposed inclusion of Yoga and Naturopathy in their mandate
on the plea that it had nothing common with other systems namely Ayurveda, Siddha and
Unani.
(b) Setting up of separate Central Council for Yoga and Naturopathy was not agreed to on the
grounds that there were only few practitioners in the field of Yoga and Naturopathy and limited
infrastructure, which does not justify a separate Regulatory Body.
64. The Ministry also informed the Committee that the issue of regulation of Yoga & Naturopathy is
under deliberation.
65. The Committee observes that Naturopathy and Yoga are gaining popularity not only amongst
the Indians but all over the world and feels that there is an urgent need to regulate the practice of
Naturopathy and Yoga. Some guidelines/norms should be prescribed in this regard and the people
should be made aware of the benefit of these practices but under the guidance of the trained
manpower.

National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB)
92. The increasing global interest in natural remedies has increased the demand for medicinal plants. The
resource base for AYUSH is largely plants which are largely sourced from the wild areas. This has led to the
emergence of a number of issues like sustainability, conservation, cultivation, quality assurance, protection
of Traditional Knowledge and related issues of access and benefit sharing etc. To coordinate all these
matters, Government of India established the National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB) under the Department
of AYUSH in 2000. NMPB is the apex national body which co-ordinates all matters relating to medicinal
plants in the country and provides financial support to programmes relating to conservation, cultivation and
the all-round development of the medicinal plants sector.

Quality Control of ASU&H Drugs
101. As per information furnished to the Committee, the Ministry of AYUSH has a Drug Regulatory Cell.
The Cell is responsible for handling regulatory and quality control matters of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and
Homoeopathy (ASU and H) drugs. The AYUSH system of Medicine is governed by the Drugs and Cosmetics
Act 1940 and the Rules thereunder. Enforcement of the provisions contained under Chapter IV A of the Act
is the responsibility of the State Governments. In superintending the provisions of Drugs and Cosmetics
Act, 1940 and Rules thereunder and Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954 in
relation to ASU and H drugs, the Drug Control Cell coordinates with the State Licensing Authorities and
Drug Controllers to achieve uniform administration of the legal provisions and for providing regulatory
guidance and clarification to the States. It also undertakes program management and examination of State
Annual Plans for Quality Control under National AYUSH Mission (NAM) through which grant-in-aid is
provided for improving infrastructure and functional capacity of Drug Testing Laboratories, Pharmacies
and Enforcement Mechanism in the States.
102. During the course of examination of Demands for Grants (2013-14) the Ministry of AYUSH (erstwhile
Department of AYUSH) had admitted that due to weak organizational set-ups in the State AYUSH Directorates
and Licensing Authorities, the utilization of grants released under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for
Quality Control has not been up to the mark. The problem of non-submission of Utilization Certificates
deprived the States of availing any more financial assistance for improving the quality control measures. The
compliance to Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) by ASU and H drug manufacturers and its enforcement
by the State regulators is not in the desired perspective. Besides, drug testing facilities in the States are
inadequate to meet the regulatory needs because of the infrastructural bottlenecks in the State Drugs Testing
Laboratories and scarcity of Approved Drug Testing Laboratories and Capacities.
103. On being asked about the allocation for quality control of AYUSH drugs for 2015-16, the Secretary
during his evidence replied that out of Rs. 318.00 Crore allocated for the Centrally Sponsored Schemes
about sixteen crores of rupees will be available for the quality control of AYUSH drugs for all the States
together, which will work out to Rs. 20.00 to Rs. 30.00 Lakhs for each State.

have been approved and the proposal of remaining 7 States, viz. Punjab, Maharashtra, Meghalaya, Jharkhand,

107. During the course of examination of Demands for Grants (2013-14), the Ministry of AYUSH, on
being asked as to why it is not desirable to let CDSCO regulate ASU Drugs, had submitted that CDSCO has
been basically dealing with quality control of allopathic drugs and accordingly, the whole paraphernalia there
does not have the wherewithal to effectively regulate ASU&H drugs. The basic reason is that ASU&H drugs
are distinct from allopathic drugs in terms of philosophy, principles, concepts, standards, quality aspects,
applications, numbers and count of manufacturing units and testing requirements, hence they need to have
different regulations. Inspite of common Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, the regulatory issues of ASU&H
drugs are not being taken care of by the Central Drugs Controller Office due to non-availability of requisite
expertise and huge workload of matters related to allopathic drugs. The idea of having a common Central
Drugs Authority with Additional Drug Controller of AYUSH had not been earlier agreed to. However, the
office of the Drug Controller General (India) was strengthened by sanctioning 175 posts for modern medicine
and was renamed as Central Drugs Standards Control Organisation (CDSCO), but no posts were sanctioned
for the Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy. It was thereafter only that in accordance with the
recommendations of a high powered Committee and ASU Drugs Consultative Committee and considering
the specific regulatory requirements of ASU&H drugs, setting up a separate Central Drugs Controller of
AYUSH was found to be a necessary requirement.
108. The Committee observes that quality control and standardization of ASU&H drugs leaves
much to be desired. It has often been reported in the media that Ayurveda and Homoeopathy drugs,
which were given to patients for various ailments, were laced with steroids and other improper
drugs. Permissible limits of heavy metals, pesticide residue, microbial load etc. are also sometimes
breached and regulatory needs of standardization and quality control of ASU&H drugs get seriously
impaired. On the issue of setting up of a separate Drug Controller of AYUSH, the decision to
develop a vertical structure of AYUSH in the present Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation
(CDSCO) headed by the Drug Controller General of India is not acceptable to the Committee. The
Committee feels that in order to strengthen regulatory mechanism and effectively oversee the
quality control of ASU&H drugs, setting up of a separate Central Drug Controller for ASU&H drugs

27
is an imminent necessity since a wing of AYUSH in the existing CDSCO may not produce the
desired result of effective regulation of ASU&H drugs. The Committee is of the view that a separate
Central Drugs Controller of AYUSH will help synergise the core competencies of the Central and
State Regulatory Authorities by developing dynamic relation between them and also go a long way
in building credibility in ASU&H drugs besides promoting AYUSH in foreign countries. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that action be initiated to set up an independent and separate Central Drug
Controller for ASU&H drugs at the earliest.

Budgetary Allocation
The Committee is concerned to find that there were savings of significant magnitude under
several important heads during 2013-14 and 2014-15 as is evident from Table Nos. 2 and 3 given
above. The fact that the savings were not just the result of implementation of economy measures
but occurred due to various reasons like non-filing of posts, non-approval of schemes, delay in
execution work, slow space of construction etc, clearly suggests that there were inadequacies in the
monitoring mechanism, as well as budgetary planning process and budget allocations were proposed
without obtaining all necessary clearances. The Committee is of the firm view that the savings
would have been minimized substantially if proper internal auditing and streamlined monitoring
mechanisms had been in place and periodic review of the trends and progress of expenditure had
been adopted. The Committee notes that the Ministry of AYUSH has initiated, at the behest of this
Committee, a number of steps to ensure optimal and efficient utilization of allotted funds. The
Committee would like the Ministry to scrupulously implement the initiatives taken in this regard
and simultaneously evaluate the impact of new initiatives on utilization of budgeted funds. The
Committee would like to be kept apprised of the impact of the new initiatives taken on utilization
of budgeted funds. (para 22)
The Committee is constrained to note that there has been persistent and substantial
underutilization of Plan funds under the heads - "Hospitals and Dispensaries", "Educational
Institutions", "Research and Development including Medicinal Plants", "Development of AYUSH
Industry", and "National Mission on AYUSH". The allocations made in BE 2013-14 and BE 2014-15
under the above heads, were subsequently revised downwards at the RE stage and the sums which
remained grossly underutilized, were surrendered by the end of the year. The Committee observes
that such huge variations in BE, RE and Actuals are indicative of the fact that principles of fiscal
prudence and discipline were neither observed nor proper assessment of the projects was carried
out. The Committee observes that in a resource-constrained country like India, all care needs to be
taken to ensure that the scarce resources do not remain locked up and surrendered at the fiscal
end. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry chalk out its fiscal roadmap in such
a way that the allocated funds are utilized optimally and more productively under the above Heads
and such a situation does not recur in future. (para 23)
From a scrutiny of information furnished, it is revealed that the entire budgeted plan funds
had to be surrendered due to non-approval of certain Schemes. For example, a sum of Rs. 4.00 crore
and Rs. 25.00 crore had been proposed in BE 2013-14 and BE 2014-15 respectively under the head
"All India Institute of Unani Medicine." The Ministry spent a meagre Rs. 0.20 crore in 2013-14
while the entire budgeted funds for 2014-15 had to be surrendered due to non-approval of the
project. What is alarming is that this is not the only example of surrender of the entire budgeted
funds. Similar is the case with quite a few other projects such as "All India Institute of Yoga", "All
India Institute of Homoeopathy", "Homoeopathic Medicines Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd",
"Pharmacovigilance Initiative for ASU drugs", "National Institute of Medicinal Plants", etc. The
Committee deprecates the Ministry for making unrealistic projection of fund requirements without
obtaining necessary approvals for implementation of the above projects. The Committee, therefore,

33
strongly recommends that the Ministry should ensure that all pre-approval formalities are completed
before budget allocations are proposed and realistic financial outlays are earmarked for the above
projects/schemes. There is no point in making ritualistic allocations which remain on paper only.
(para 24)
The Committee observes that to obtain greater evenness in the budgeted expenditure within
a financial year and reduce rush of expenditure in the last quarter of the financial year, Ministry of
Finance has prescribed the expenditure ceiling of 33% for the last quarter of the financial year. As
is evident from Table No. 5, the Ministry of AYUSH has breached the ceiling of 33%, though only
marginally, by incurring expenditure of 36.61% of plan funds in the last quarter. The Committee
would like the Ministry to monitor expenditure of funds more closely and avoid rush of expenditure
in the last quarter. (para 25)
The Committee notes with concern that out of the plan allocation of Rs. 1069.00 crore each in
BE 2013-14, and BE 2014-15, as much as Rs. 507.99 Crore and Rs. 573.21 Crore were surrendered in
Financial Y ears 2013-14 and 2014-15 respectively. The Committee takes a serious view of the fact
that the Ministry kept unspent budgetary provisions of Rs. 507.99 Crore in 2013-14 and Rs. 573.24
Crore in 2014-15 till the last day of the financial years despite reduction in allocations at RE stage.
The Committee observes that the unspent budgetary provisions should have been surrendered as
soon as these were foreseen without waiting till the end of the Financial Year so that they could
have been gainfully utilized under some other heads. The Committee would like the Ministry to
improve financial discipline. (para 26)

incorporation in both the Central Registers of the Indian systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy.

Quality Control of ASU&H Drugs
The Committee in its 55th Report on Demands for Grants (2012-13) of erstwhile Department
of AYUSH had inter-alia observed that "the quality and standardization of ASU&H products and
stringent implementation of the provisions of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and the rules

requirements of ASU&H drugs cannot be met without setting up adequate number of quality control
laboratories and strengthening of the enforcement mechanisms of the States." From information
made available to the Committee, it is revealed that the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for supporting
quality control activities has been evaluated by an external agency and revised suitably on the basis

the remaining period of the 12th Five Year Plan. The Committee recommends that the Ministry

The Committee also notes that Indian Ayurvedic drugs were found containing excessive mercury
and other substance and therefore were banned by Eurpoean Union and some other Countries and
these Countries had imposed ban on Ayurvedic drugs and cosmetics which apart from bringing a bad
name to the country also resulted in loss of precious foreign exchange. The Committee recommends
that such incidents need to be avoided. (para 105)
The Committee observes that quality control and standardization of ASU&H drugs leaves
much to be desired. It has often been reported in the media that Ayurveda and Homoeopathy drugs,
which were given to patients for various ailments, were laced with steroids and other improper
drugs. Permissible limits of heavy metals, pesticide residue, microbial load etc. are also sometimes
breached and regulatory needs of standardization and quality control of ASU&H drugs get seriously
impaired. On the issue of setting up of a separate Drug Controller of AYUSH, the decision to
develop a vertical structure of AYUSH in the present Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation
(CDSCO) headed by the Drug Controller General of India is not acceptable to the Committee. The
Committee feels that in order to strengthen regulatory mechanism and effectively oversee the
quality control of ASU&H drugs, setting up of a separate Central Drug Controller for ASU&H drugs
is an imminent necessity since a wing of AYUSH in the existing CDSCO may not produce the
desired result of effective regulation of ASU&H drugs. The Committee is of the view that a separate
Central Drugs Controller of AYUSH will help synergise the core competencies of the Central and
State Regulatory Authorities by developing dynamic relation between them and also go a long way
in building credibility in ASU&H drugs besides promoting AYUSH in foreign countries. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that action be initiated to setup an independent and separate Central Drug
Controller for ASU&H drugs at the earliest. (para 108)
