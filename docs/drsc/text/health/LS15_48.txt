III. INDIAN COUNCIL OF MEDICAL RESEARCH  
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
3.7 The Committee notes that the shortfa ll between the amount allocated and the 
amount projected for ICMR is to the tune  of Rs.630.00 crore which is quite large by 
any standards and is certain to have an adverse bearing on the implementation of 
various research projects of ICMR.  The Committee observes that in view of the 
dynamic international health research envi ronment and the current and future health 
challenges, the role of ICMR is very crucia l and if its projects are plagued with under-
funding, it can have very serious repercu ssions and undermine the desired outcomes 
in the health sector.  The Committee, th erefore, recommends that more funds be 
allocated to ICMR.  Simultaneously, th e Committee impresses upon the ICMR, to 
exercise better fiscal discipline and take cred ible and tangible action to improve the 
financial administration which will ultimatel
y bolster its claims for more funds.                                  
  
 
Action Taken 
 

A request has already been made for a llocation of additional funds to DHR 
including ICMR at the RE stage. ICMR is taking the necessary steps to improve its 
financial administration. Utilisation of funds received by ICMR has been very good.  
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
3.21 The Committee observes that no single system of health care has the capacity 
to solve all of India’s health care needs. It is in this context that the scientific 
research in the area of traditional medicine assumes added significance. The 
Committee notes that the ICMR is funding 48 projects in traditional medicine. The 
Committee is of the view that it is more important to focus on health outcomes 
rather than health outlays. The Committee, therefore, urges upon the Department 
not to remain complacent by merely fundi ng the 48 projects but to reorient its 
attention for the health outcomes accruing from the above research projects. The 
Committee would like to be apprised of the health outcomes of all the research 
projects under reference. 
 
Action Taken 
 
The observations of the Committee have been noted for compliance. Some of 
the salient health outcomes emerging from the projects under reference are mentioned 
below- 
 
• Successful micro-propagation of Bijasal ( Pterocarpus marsupium Roxb.) , an 
endangered medicinal leguminous tree whose bark is used as a potent anti-
diabetic medicine so that enough wood is available for medicinal purpose and 
the species does not become extinct. 
•  Monograph of medicinal plants having various therapeutic values of Jawa 
Ramgarh area of Rajasthan prepared.   
• 3 medicinal plants ( Ammania baccifera, Annona squamosa and Lycopersicon 
esculentum) showed good antioxidant activity and anti-proliferation activity in 
cancer cell lines. 
• Leaves and testa of Anacardium occidentale (cashew) have been studied for anti-
diabetic activities of their phyto-constituents by various in vitro  and in vivo  
parameters. Catechin, a polyphenol whic h can be used as a nutraceutical has 
been successfully obtained.  
• Fruit of plant Withania coagulus (D oda Paneer) had shown glucose lowering 
effect in animals. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
3.22 The Committee also recomme nds that the task of validation of traditional 
AYUSH drugs and development of new drug s for identified diseases should be 
accomplished within the stipul ated time-frame. Similarly,  the project for validating 
the safety and efficacy of therapeutic practi ce should be completed well in time. It 

would also like to be informed of the ac hievements made so far from the “Golden 
Triangle Partnership, and ICMR’s colla boration with the National Innovation 
Foundation” for validating the safety and efficacy of therapeutic practices.

The recommendations of the Committee have been noted for compliance. The 
actions taken so far under the abovementi oned partnership/collaboration are given 
below – 
 
Golden Triangle Partnership 
 
o Integrated protocols for conducting clin ical trials of seven diseases (Benign 
Prostate Hypertrophy, Osteoporosis, Hypertension, Dyslipidemia, HIV/AIDS, 
Anxiety Neurosis, and Oligospermia) finalised. The clinical trials on 
formulations made available by the Department of AYUSH will be undertaken 
on the basis of these protocols.  
 
ICMR-NIF collaboration 
 
o 5 herbal practices have been selected for the validation. 
o Extramural research projects have been sanctioned on the following topics – 
• Anti-hepatotoxic activity of Abelmoschus esculentus and Datura metel used by

III. INDIAN COUNCIL OF MEDICAL RESEARCH 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
 
3.15 The Committee observes that health research projects funded with public 
money should be assessed in terms of tangible health outcomes for the masses and 
there should also be a mechanism for evaluating the health benefits ensuing from such 
research projects. The Committee, therefore, recommends that a system be put in 
place to do audit of heal th research being funded, wholly or partially, with 
government money, so that an assessment could be made as to whether or not the 
health research being done is justified and relevant. 
 
Action Taken 
 
Research audit of ICMR’s intramural pr ojects is being undertaken through a 
three tier system as explained below: - 
A Scientific Advisory Committee (SAC), comp rising subject related experts, assists 
each ICMR Institute/Centre. The SAC over sees the research activities including 
review of ongoing projects and clearing new project. 
A Scientific Advisory Board (SAB) comprising 21 members representing 
different institutions and disciplines, as sp ecified in the Rules and Regulations of the 

ICMR, reviews the reports and recommendations of the Advisory committees/groups 
and offers its advice in regard to the scientific objects of the Council.  
The following are some  additional parameters that are being added to improve the 
quality of the audit - 
• assessment of promising leads; 
• ensuring basic translational research is  being done at the institute level 
so they would assess the feasibility of use by the population of technology 
developed; 
• making technical and financial resources available to achieve this. 
 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
 
 
3.16 The Committee takes special note of the following achievements of ICMR. 
• A Phase I HIV vaccine trial using the pr ime boost approach was initiated in 
NARI, Pune and TRC, Chennai. The enrolment for the trial has been completed 
and the volunteers are now being followed. 
• Draft guidelines on management of duca l mucous cancer and cervical cancer 
are being finalized on the lines of suggestions of experts. 
• A Web Based Management for Acute Co ronary Events (MACE) Registry 
network has been initiated. 
• New proposal on “ICMR- Indian National  Diabetes Study-Phase I,” has been 
initiated at three states and one Union Territory with the aim to understand 
prevalence of diabetes and pre-diabetes  along its association with the genetic 
aspects.

Needs no comments

The progress achieved so far under the programmes/projects in question is 
mentioned below: 
 
ICMR Project on Cardiovascular Disease Surveillance among the Adivasi 
Population of Wayanad District in Kerala: The project has been completed.  From the 
findings of the project it is clearly evident that underweight is still a major problem 
among the tribal population . The project also evaluated the accuracy of CARDIAC 
CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS BY TELE-AUSCULTAT ION. This preliminary experience 
suggests that transmitted heart sounds have  promise in screening for SHD in remote 
locations. 
 
  RF/RHD registry in Wayanad, Kerala  under Jai Vigyan Mission Mode 
project on ‘Community cont rol of RF/RHD in India’ : The registry has successfully 
completed the laid down objectives of th e study. The highest numbers of RHD cases 
reported by the registry were females in the age group of 30-44 years. The prevalence 
of RHD cases in the registry is 0.47 per 1000 population. Only 1 case of RHD was 
confirmed. The low prevalence of RHD in school children may be due to lower 
diagnostic value of murmur as compared to ECHO in community screening. 
 
Screening for Cancers of Cervix, Breast & Oral Cavity in three districts of 
Himachal Pradesh: ICMR-Government of Himachal P radesh collaborative project on 
Cancer Screening through existing health infrastructure was aimed at screening for 

cancers of cervix, breast and oral cavity, in  three districts of Himachal Pradesh. The 
preparatory phase saw the finalization of strat egy including quality control exercises; 
development of manuals; development of he alth education aids; baseline survey on 
knowledge, attitude and practices related to the chosen cancer sites; establishment of a 
population based cancer registry; purchase  of necessary equipments; preparation of 
proforma and forms; establishment of a referral mechanism; and training of 
professionals and para-professionals. The pr oject was subsequently handed over to 
the state government and intervention  was continued by the State health 
infrastructure using the trained manpower & professional for reporting. The project 
has been extended on request to provide mo re ICMR guidance in execution and data 
analysis. 
 
After review of more than 100 leads/no vel information derived from studies 
carried out at ICMR Institutes/centres, 53 ha ve been identified for translation into 
products / processes / system. Action has been initiated to accomplish the goal of 
Translation.

Recommendations/Observations 
 
14.8       The Committee is surprised to note  that our premier Institute in the field of 
medical research has been working with th e minimum staff for all these years. The 
very fact that not a single post has been  created during the last 25 years confirms 
Committee’s concerns in this regard. Not only this, a large number of  posts, that too,

when such a situation has been continuing . The Committee can imagine the kind of 
work stress being faced by our scientists, specially during the time of epidemic 
outbreaks. The Committee recommends that al l the existing vacant posts in ICMR 

should be filled up without any further delay. All 500 posts of scientists and 1000 
matching posts of research fellows and technical posts should be created at the earliest 
and filled up. This exercise needs to be c arried out in a time-bound manner. It should

a protracted exercise of getting them filled up. Similarly, concerted efforts would also 
be required for creation and filling up of 210 posts of scientists and 400 posts of 
technical staff required for the Department  of Health Research. The Department will 
have to play a proactive role so that lack of specialized manpower does not hamper 
the crucial research activities, both ongoing and proposed. 
 
Action Taken 
  
 Needs no comments. 
 
Comments of the Committee 
 
 The Committee had invested the creation of posts of scientists and matching 
posts of research fellows and technical po sts with Parliamentary support and it was 
the obligation of the Department to make earnest efforts to apprise the authorities 
concerned of the Committee’s recommendation in this regard so that necessary 
action could be initiated towards creating and filling up the posts. 
 
 The Committee is therefore baffled by the Department’s use of the phrase 
“needs no comments”.  The phrase employed by the Department lends credence to 
the impression that it does not attach much  importance to the creation of necessary 
posts.  The Committee, therefore, urges upon the Department to attend to the 
matter with an astute sense of sincerity and urgency.

The Committee expresses its displeasure over the tenor of response of the

st Report, the
