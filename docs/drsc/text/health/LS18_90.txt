II.  BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
Recommendation/Observation (para 2.11)
1.1 The Committee is of the opinion that keeping in view the mandate of DHR/ICMR to bring
modern technologies to the people through research and innovations and the fact that Department
has just launched the above mentioned Schemes to fulfill its mandate of creating a strong and
effective eco-system for carrying out health research in the country and for introduction of new
technologies, new methods of treatment and products/processes into the public health system; any
underfunding to the schemes would prove detrimental in achieving the desired objectives that too
at a time when these schemes have just picked up their pace. In view of the fact that with this
funds crunch it would not be possible for the Department to roll out many of its proposed
initiatives under the schemes, the Committee recommends that the Department should vigorously
pursue with the Ministry of Finance for enhanced funds at RE Stage. The Committee,
simultaneously, also recommends to the Department that it should take all out efforts for optimum
and judicious utilization of available resources and funds for implementation of various Schemes.
Funds for new initiatives should be limited to availability of funds.
Action Taken
1.2 As recommended by the Committee, efforts will also be made by the Department for
optimum utilization of the available funds, by taking following actions:
(i) The Department would impress upon the Ministry of Finance for making enhanced
allocations of funds at the RE stage for meeting the targets of the various schemes
in 2015-16.
(ii) While funding of new initiatives, preference will be given to the research projects/
proposals on health issues of national and public health importance, like, Cancer,
Cardio-vascular diseases, Diabetes, Maternal and Child Health, Nutritional
Deficiencies, V ector Borne Diseases, TB, Leprosy and Blindness control.
(iii)  Monthly monitoring to review the physical and financial progress of the schemes
will be done at the level of Joint Secretary to ensure efficient and effective
utilisation of budgetary allocations.
(iv) Similar review and monitoring on periodic basis will be done at the level of
Secretary, DHR.
(v) Conducting field visits to the medical colleges and institutions for reviewing the
progress of implementation of the schemes and making rational assessment of
requirement of funds.

III. INDIAN COUNCIL OF MEDICAL RESEARCH
Recommendation/Observation (para 3.6)
1.3 The Committee puts on record its appreciation for ICMR for near optimum utilization of
allocated funds. Such a trend of utilization by the Institute would strengthen its demand for
additional funds at RE stage during the current year, as per the projections made. However, the
Committee would also like to say that mere utilization of funds is not an indicator of success,
unless the research outcomes are translated into health products or are used in better interest of
people. The Committee would like to be apprised of the steps being taken in speedy translation of
research leads into public utilities. The Committee would also like to be apprised of the number
of leads which were actually put into public use for their betterment.
Action Taken
1.4 The ICMR’s technologies/leads are shared with National Research Development Corporation
(NRDC) and Biotech Consortium India Limited (BCIL) under the Ministry of Science and
Technology, for technology transfer/commercialization.
1.5 The Division of Innovation & Translational Research (ITR) of ICMR is regularly following
up with the companies to review the progress. Through this process, eleven technologies have been
transferred to industries for commercialization.
Recommendation/Observation (para 3.11)
1.6 The Committee is happy to note that at its behest the Department has revisited its priorities
areas for research and drawn three most important priorities areas namely Cancer Research,
Environmental Health and Maternal and Child Health for carrying out research. The Committee is
hopeful that this move of the Department would result in visible and desirable results. The
Committee desires to be apprised of the outcome of the above move.
Action Taken
1.7 ICMR has constituted a core committee to develop cancer management guidelines of 20
specific types of cancers. Guidelines for 4 types of Cancers: Colorectal Cancer, Gall bladder
Cancer, Buccal Mucosa Cancer and Gastric cancer have been published and uploaded on the ICMR
website for public use. Guidelines for another 4 sites of cancers are in process of uploading on
the website.
1.8 ICMR has established the National Institute for Disease Informatics and Research, at
Bangalore for compiling the Cancer registries on national basis.
1.9 On the issues related to Environmental Health, ICMR has established the National Institute
for Research in Environmental Health (NIREH) at Bhopal.
1.10 On health issues related to air pollution, a Centre for Advanced Research has been
established in Chennai. The Centre has created a network of scientists across the country to
conduct research on households air pollution.
1.11 In the Maternal and Child Health (MCH), the priority areas of research are: reduction of
pre-natal and neonatal morbidities and mortalities; safe pregnancy and motherhood practices;

4
discovery of new, safe and acceptable contraception; reduction of respiratory (pneumonia) infection
in infants; polycystic ovarian syndrome diseases and better survival of low birth weight babies.
Recommendation/Observation (para 3.18)
1.12 The Committee appreciates the intervention made by the Department in the area of
environmental pollution and desires that research activities should not be restricted to air pollution
only as the health hazards of water/noise pollution are also affecting a large chunk of population.

1.13 Research activities of the National Institute of Occupational Health of ICMR, Ahmedabad
are not restricted to air pollution only and their thrust areas of research also include other
environmental hazards like, like noise pollution, water pollution thermal pollution and industrial

Recommendation/Observation (para 3.20)

Institutes/Centres spread across the country. On a perusal thereof, the Committee noted that there
are regional imbalances in the presence of ICMR across the country. The Committee noted that a
large area of the Central India which also  happened to be tribal area which is malaria infected and
affected with various other environment-related morbidity factors is not covered under the projects

attention in those areas which are not well served and seek additional resources for Eastern India
and Central India.
Action Taken

the 5 new schemes, viz. (1) Establishment of Multi-Disciplinary Research Units in Government
Medical Colleges, (2) Establishment of Model Rural Health Research Units in the States, (3)
Establishment of a Network of Laboratories for Managing Epidemics and Natural Calamities,
(4) Human Resource Development for Health Research and (5) Grant-in-Aid for Inter-sector
Convergence & Coordination for Promotion & Guidance on Health Research, which will result in
the opening of new centres/laboratories in central and eastern India and unserved areas.

II. BUDGETARY PROVISIONS
Recommendation/Observation (para 2.3)
3.1 The Committee notes that on one hand the Department claims that significant progress has
been made in respect of Schemes while on the other the Committee notes that only a meagre
amount of expenditure of R179.36 crore upto 4.3.2015 could be made against the allocations of
R5213.00 crore made for the Schemes for Twelfth Plan Period. The Committee understands that
all the Schemes could be rolled in the year 2013-14 only and hence the expenditure booked was
very low. The Committee, however, is constrained to comment that this state of affairs indicates
that the Department was consistently over estimating its requirements and has not been able to
make use of scarce financial resources. The Committee, accordingly, impresses upon the
Department to make all out efforts to implement its proposed activities under the schemes in the
remaining two years of the Twelfth Five Year Plan for optimum utilization of allocations and for
achieving the desired objectives.
Action Taken
3.2 The Twelfth Plan allocation of R5213.00 crores for the five newly formulated schemes of
the DHR was based on the proposed estimated cost of each scheme. However, the final cost of
all the five schemes approved by the EFC/Cabinet worked out to be R3055.00 crores (approx.).
Against this, the actual budgetary allocation in the first four years (2012-13 to 2015-16) has been
to the extent of R677.00 crores only.
3.3 Allocation in 2012-13 for the schemes could not be utilised as the schemes could be finally
approved by the EFC/Cabinet in 2013-14. In fact, the two schemes, namely, (1) Human Resource
Development for Health Research and (2) Grant in aid scheme for Inter sectoral Convergence &
Promotion and guidance on Health Research were approved in February, 2014 only.
3.4 The Department has made its estimation meticulously at the time of finalization of Twelfth
Plan allocation for all the five schemes. However, while approving the schemes, the physical targets
were reduced, due to which the financial cost of the schemes came down from the original
proposed cost of R5213 crores to R3055 crores.
3.5 The schemes were rolled out for implementation immediately and the proposal were
processed for technical evaluation and final approval for release of fund in the same year. This was
possible only since pre-project actions had been taken by the Department in respect of all the five
schemes.
3.6 Depending upon the budgetary allocations, all efforts will be made by the Department to
implement the schemes by achieving maximum physical targets, through optimum utilization of
allocated resources.
8

9
Further Recommendation
3.7 The Committee does not find plausible the explanation given by the Department on
the formulation and approved procedure for the five schemes. Either the Department lacks
serious wherewithal to ensure timely formulation or approval of schemes or does not have
the vision to plan and implement schemes. The Committee seriously doubts whether these
schemes would be taken to their logical conclusion at the end of the Twelfth Five Year Plan
keeping in view the physical and financial progress made so far. The Committee, once again
recommends that the Department should identify and overcome the shortcomings in the
mechanism of budgetary planning so that the under-utilization of budgeted funds being
witnessed do not recur in the remaining years of the Twelfth Plan and the desired objectives
are not compromised.
Recommendation/Observation (para 2.6)
3.8 As evident from the above tables there are huge variations between the BE and RE for all
the schemes as well as for ICMR. The reasons cited by the Department for this trend were non-
receipt of adequate number of applications on account of approval of the scheme at fag end of
2013-14, pendency of utilization certificates, receipt of less number of proposals from States and
non-completion of requisite administrative and technical formalities under the Schemes guidelines by
certain States. The Committee, in the circumstances, is constrained to observe that inspite of its
repeated recommendations to the Department to take all out measures, including completion of pre-
project planning and formalities of obtaining necessary approvals for optimum and judicious
utilization of budgeted funds, not much has been done in this regard. The Committee fails to
understand the reasons for the delay in formulation of all the schemes even though the Schemes
first got in- principle approval by the Planning Commission in December 2010. The Committee is
not convinced with the statement of the Department that inspite of its best efforts, the various
actions like the appraisal of the Schemes, responding to various appraisal agencies, clearance of
the Schemes by EFC/CCEA took more than anticipated time which ultimately led to the delay. The
Committee observes that this is a clear case of lack of foresight and faulty planning by the
Department. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should make all possible
efforts with proper and timely planning to utilize the allocated funds at BE Stage. The Committee
would like to be apprised of the steps being taken or proposed to be taken to arrest this trend of
underutilization of budgeted funds which eventually led to massive cuts at RE stage.
Action Taken
3.9 The in-principle  approval of the Planning Commission to the Schemes was received in
December, 2010 i.e. in the midst of Eleventh Five Year Plan. The detailed EFC documents were
prepared and circulated by the Department for the comments of the appraisal agencies, soon
thereafter. However, since 2011-12 was the last year of the Eleventh Five Year Plan, the
Department was advised to formulate comprehensive schemes for the full Twelfth Plan period.
3.10 Hence, revision of EFC documents with modified cost estimates for the Twelfth plan period
and the process of obtaining approvals from the EFC and Cabinet took time and the schemes could
be finally approved in 2013-14 only.

10
3.11 The Department had already taken advance actions for inviting proposals from the States/
Medical Colleges, etc. and it was as a result of this action that it was possible to roll out and
implement all the five schemes during 2013-14 itself, alongwith the release of funds for the various
projects.
3.12 W ith a view to ensure optimum utilisation of allocated funds under the various schemes,
regular monitoring of physical/financial progress of the projects is being made through periodical
progress reports as well as  through onsite visits by the teams from DHR/ICMR. Review meetings
are also held at the level of Joint Secretary and Secretary (DHR) and action plan is formulated for
effective implementation of all the schemes and for utilisation of allocated funds.
Further Recommendation
3.13 The Committee would like to be apprised of the progress made in the actual
implementation of these five schemes and whether the intended objectives of the five
schemes would be achieved by end of the Twelfth Plan.

VI. DEVELOPMENT OF INFRASTRUCTURE FOR PROMOTION OF HEALTH RESEARCH
Recommendation/Observation (para 6.6)
3.18 The Committee observes that deaths caused due to non-communicable diseases are much
more than those occurring due to communicable diseases and any underfunding to the Scheme,
which promotes research in Non-Communicable diseases, will prove detrimental to control lifestyle
diseases. The proposed shortfall in allocation to the Scheme may lead to curtailment in the second
or subsequent installments to the Institutes where the Department has already made a beginning and
would result in seriously hampering of research activities initiated. The Committee, therefore, lends
its support to the demand of the Department for enhancement of allocations at RE Stage for the
Scheme so that investments already made in the medical colleges under the Scheme do not go
down the drain. The Committee finds that life style-related disease like hypertension, blood
pressure, diabetes etc. are on the rise and are the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in the
country and, therefore, it is a cause of serious concern. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the life style-related diseases may be included in the research programmes of the Department.
Action Taken
3.19 ICMR and the Medical Colleges will be requested to include life-style related diseases,
namely, diabetes, obesity and cardiovascular diseases, in their research programmes.
Further Recommendation
3.20 The Committee would like to be apprised of the specific steps taken by ICMR to
include lifestyle diseases in their research programmes.
VII. GRANT-IN-AID FOR INTER-SECT ORAL CONVERGENCE & PROMOTION AND
GUIDANCE ON HEALTH RESEARCH
Recommendation/Observation (para 7.8)
3.21 The Committee observes that some of very important activities are targeted to be
accomplished under the Scheme for Inter-Sectoral Convergence & Promotion and Guidance on
Health Research. The Committee is happy to note that pursuant to its recommendation made in 56th
Report and reiterated in 67th Report for creation of an institutional coordination mechanism for
synergizing the core competencies of the different organizations/agencies a group of Secretaries has
been constituted, consisting of Secretaries from DBT, DRDO, DARE/ICAR, ISRO, Department of
Space, DIT etc. to coordinate and collaborate on Health Research activities among various
