RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS IN RESPECT OF WHICH REPLIES OF
THE GOVERNMENT HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE COMMITTEE
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
Recommendation/Observation
1.1 The Committee observes from Table No. 2 that as against the total Twelfth Plan approved outlays
of ` 10044.00 crore, the total allocation made in RE from 2012-13 to 2015-16 is ` 2786.78 crore and
the expenditure incurred as on 29th February, 2016 is ` 2362.93 crore only. The Committee notes from
Table 1 that the actual expenditure has invariably been considerably lower than the Revised Estimates in
2012-13 and 2013-14 and slightly lower in 2014-15. Despite the fact that a Zero Based Budgeting
exercise which requires that budget request be evaluated thoroughly, starting from the zero base, was
undertaken at the beginning of the Eleventh Plan, a substantial amount of ` 423.85 crore of the budgeted
funds could not be utilised during the first four years of the 12th Plan period, which is a matter of serious
concern. The Committee is constrained to observe that such a substantial gap between the RE allocations
and the actual expenditure is indicative of fiscal indiscipline and unrealistic projection of estimates. Had
the Ministry assessed requirement of funds realistically and monitored their deployment diligently such
a shortfall in utilization of the budgeted funds could have been avoided and the unspent amount utilized
more gainfully somewhere else. The Committee, therefore, recommends that a comprehensive analysis
of the trend of expenditure incurred under various heads of the Ministry during the last four years be
carried out with a view to rectifying and improving upon the existing system of assessing fund requirement.
The Committee would also expect the Ministry to make its estimates only after proper assessment and
planning in future. (Para 2.18)
Action Taken
1.2 As recommended by the Committee, the Ministry has analysed the expenditure for the last four
years which is given below:

Recommendation/Observation
1.3 The Committee notes that the Ministry has been allocated ` 1050.00 crore in BE 2016-17 on the
plan side against the projected demand of ` 1819.77 crore and thus there is a shortfall of ` 769.77 crore.
The Committee takes note of the submission of the Secretary (AYUSH) that "we will be able to achieve
almost hundred per cent utilization" of the funds budgeted for 2015-16 and that the goals of the National
AYUSH Mission will be affected due to the shortfall in allocation for 2016-17. The Committee observes
that health needs of a vast and diverse country like India cannot be met by a single healthcare system
and AYUSH healthcare systems, which besides being curative, are also preventive and promotive healthcare
systems, have a vital role to play in expanding the outreach of healthcare to the people because of their
inherent advantages in terms of diversity, modest cost, low level of technological input, etc. The shortfall
in allocation for 2016-17 will have adverse impact on the key priorities of the National AYUSH Mission
which aims at mainstreaming of AYUSH, improving quality of ASU&H drugs, setting up of drug laboratories,
improvement in AYUSH education etc. The Committee observes that the BE 2016-17 allocation of
` 1050.00 crore is lower than the BE 2013-14 and 2014-15 allocation of ` 1069.00 crore each and is
only marginally higher than the allocation of ` 1008.00 crore in BE 2015-16. The Committee observes
that in a resource-constrained country like India, it is extremely difficult to meet the demands for funds,
however genuine, of the Ministry in full and therefore in order to obtain the best possible health
outcomes, it is imperative that allocation of resources is prioritized across competing ends. The Committee
would, therefore, like the Ministry to work out the bare minimum additional increase that the Ministry
needs in its budget for 2016-17 for carrying out activities/ programmes which are absolutely essential
and cannot be postponed till next year and approach the Ministry of Finance for allocating that amount
through supplementary grants. The Committee desires to be kept apprised of the response of Ministry
of Finance in this regard. (Para 2.19)
Action Taken
1.4 The Ministry appreciates the concern expressed by the Committee on the lower allocation of funds
vis-a-vis the projected requirement during the last 2-3 years and its likely impact on the health needs of
the country and the adverse impact it would have on the key priorities of National AYUSH Mission.
1.5 The Ministry has, as advised by the Committee, made a realistic assessment of the minimum
additional funds required and have communicated the same to the PMO. A response on the request is
awaited.
REGULATION OF NATUROPATHY AND YOGA
Recommendation/Observation
1.6 The Committee observes that Yoga is being practised as part of healthy lifestyle and is popular
world-wide. The therapeutic potentials of yoga in the treatment of many life-style related or psychosomatic
disorders have been demonstrated in several studies. Similarly, Naturopathy has great health promotive
and restorative potential. The Committee feels that at a time when yoga centres and naturopathy clinics

4
are mushrooming, standardization of yogic practices and naturopathic treatments and their regulation are
of vital importance. The Committee, therefore, desires to be kept apprised as to what was the outcome
of the proposal for regulation of Y oga and Naturopathy. (Para 8.2)
Action Taken
1.7 The Task Force constituted by the Ministry under the Chairmanship of Dr. H.R. Nagendra to
deliberate on important issues concerning the Ministry of AYUSH has made many recommendations. One
of the recommendations relates to "National Board for Promotion and Development of Yoga and
Naturopathy" under the Chairmanship of MOS(IC)(AYUSH) and the Ministry has initiated a proposal for
"Constitution of National Board for Promotion and Development of Yoga and Naturopathy" and Hon'ble
Minister has in principle approved constitution of the Board. After constitution, the Board will consider
the issue of framing uniform guidelines for various Degree/Diploma/Certificate and Post Graduate Courses
in Yoga and Naturopathy regarding curriculum, required infrastructure, required human resources of
various capabilities etc.
ALL INDIA INSTITUTE OF AYURVEDA
Recommendation/Observation
1.8 The Committee observes that the Ministry has not been able to utilise the budgetary allocations
for AIIA like other schemes mainly due to slow progress of construction and procurement by M/s HSCC
and delay in obtaining clearances from local bodies. The Committee observes that these impediments
could have been overcome had the Ministry proactively monitored and pursued the matter with the
implementing agency. The Committee expresses its strong displeasure at the tardy progress of
implementation of the Project that has led to cost escalation as well as surrender of the budgeted funds.
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should evolve a strong monitoring mechanism under
which periodic/quarterly meetings should be held to keep a track on the progress of operationalisation
of the Institute. The Committee also desires the Ministry to ensure that there will be no further escalation
of the project cost. (Para 11.6)
Action Taken
1.9 The Ministry is monitoring the progress on weekly basis through Director, AIIA, fortnightly basis
by Joint Secretary(AYUSH) and on monthly basis by Secretary (AYUSH) . The Ministry is taking every
step to ensure that there is no further escalation of the project cost. The original cost of the project is
` 157 crore and there will be no escalation of the project. The construction of the building is complete.

CHAPTER-II
RECOMMENDATOINS/OBSERV ATIONS WHICH THE COMMITTEE DOES NOT DESIRE
TO PURSUE IN VIEW OF THE GOVERNMENT’S REPLIES
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
Recommendation/Observation
2.1 The Committee notes that as compared to the BE 2016-17 allocation of ` 31300.00 crore for the
Department of Health and Family Welfare, the Central expenditure on AYUSH for 2016-17 is ` 1050.00
crore only. State Governments are spending much more on AYUSH than the Union Government. To make
an in-depth analysis of total Government expenditure on AYUSH, the Committee would like the Ministry
to collect and furnish information to the Committee on the total Government expenditure on AYUSH ( i.e.
combined spending on AYUSH by the Centre and all States) as also the Centre-States ratio in total
Government expenditure on AYUSH. The Committee would also like to know whether any policy
pronouncement of raising AYUSH share in total Government expenditure on health had been articulated
in the Twelfth Five Year Plan. (Para 2.20)
Action Taken
2.2 The Information has been sought from State/UT Governments. So far, relevant information has
been received from 25 State/UT Governments only. The information received from State/UT Governments
on the expenditure on AYUSH for 25 States/UTs is at Annexure-I. Relevant Information for remaining
States/UTs Governments is still awaited.
Recommendation/Observation
2.3 The Committee's scrutiny of Table No.5 reveals that for 14 heads of account, budgetary provisions
had been obtained in BE 2015-16 but not a single rupee was spent. Of the 14 schemes, the budgeted
funds of as many as nine schemes could not be utilised due to non-approval of the schemes. For
example, ` 15.00 crore had been allocated at the stage of BE 2015-16 for "All India Institute of Unani
Medicine''' but nothing was spent and the entire allocated amount of ` 15.00 crore was surrendered. The
Committee is of the considered view that this trend is indicative of serious shortcomings in formulating
the Budget Estimates and ineffective monitoring of utilization of the budgeted funds for implementing
schemes. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to take appropriate corrective measures
to arrest this trend of inappropriate projection of fund requirements in formulating budget estimates for
schemes, which are left unutilised owing to lack of approvals, non-receipt of proposals etc. The
Committee would advise the Ministry to propose budget allocations for schemes only after obtaining all
necessary clearances so that the scarce resources do not remain locked up and surrendered later.
(Para 2.21)
Action Taken
2.4 The concern expressed by the Committee on the non-utilization of the budgeted funds has been
duly noted. After, a thorough review, the Ministry has now allocated funds only in respect of those
5

6
schemes, where progress can be envisaged in their operationalization. Accordingly, no allocation has been
made in the current financial year in respect of the following five schemes:
(i) Homoeopathic Medical Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd.
(ii) Survey on Usage and acceptability of AYUSH
(iii) Scheme for Acquisition, Cataloging, Digitisation and Publication of Text Books and
Manuscripts
(iv) Funding of NGOs engaged in local Health traditions/midwifery practices etc. under AYUSH
(v) National AYUSH Library and Archives
However, the recommendations/suggestions of the Committee have been noted.
Recommendation/Observation
2.5 The Committee observes from Table No.4 that the Ministry incurred expenditure of ` 38.83 crore
of the non-plan funds in the second quarter of the Financial Year 2015-16 and has thus breached the
ceiling of 33% as stipulated by the Ministry of Finance. The Committee would expect the Ministry to
show greater financial discipline and responsibility by evenly laying out expenditure in future.
(Para 2.22)
Action Taken
2.6 The Ministry of Finance in their O.M. No.7(2)/E.Coord/2013 dated 18.3.2016 giving detailed
instructions on Expenditure Management Economy Measures and Rationalization of Expenditure have
stipulated the ceiling of 33% only for the last quarter of the financial year. No ceiling as such has been
imposed in respect of the remaining quarters. Nevertheless, it is clarified that the allocation under Non-
Plan is generally utilized to meet expenditure on salaries/pension/pensionary benefits of employees in
autonomous bodies. The reason for higher expenditure during the quarter was due to payment of pension
and pensionary benefits. However, the observations of the Committee have been brought to the notice
of all concerned and they have been advised to regulate the Non-Plan expenditure in such a way that
there is balanced cash out-flow during the year.

Recommendation/Observation
2.7 The Committee has been reviewing the vacancy position at NIA, Jaipur and urging the Ministry
to fdl them up within a short span of time. The Committee is, therefore, deeply concerned to note that
out of 95 Group A posts, as many as 23 are vacant. The Committee observes that such a large number

(Para 4.4)

Action Taken

of the 33 vacancies of teaching posts, 24 have been notified. 8 posts are for promotion, 1 post (Lecturer)

open market or Direct Recruitment or on deputation basis.
NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF HOMOEOPATHY (NIH), KOLKATA
Recommendation/Observation
2.9 The expansion of the Hospital at NIH from 100 to 250 bed has been engaging the attention of the
Committee and has been commented upon in its various reports on Demands for Grants of the Ministry.
The Committee observes that the expansion of the Hospital at NIH was targeted to be completed during
2010-11. During the examination of Demands for Grants (2013-14), the Ministry had informed the
Committee that the original cost of ` 11.59 crore had been revised upward to ` 13.08 crore and that
the project of the expansion of the Hospital Building was likely to be completed by May, 2015. The
Committee is, therefore, astonished to note the submission of the Ministry that "major works had been
completed and the project was expected to be completed by May, 2016." The Committee would,
therefore, like the Ministry to explain as to why the project could not be completed by May, 2015 and
whether the further delay of one year has entailed any cost escalation of the Project. (Para 5.7)
Action Taken
2.10 The delay in completion of the construction of Hospital Building of NIH was due to labour
problems and complaints. Now, the building has been completed. At present the hospital is operational
with 100 beds.
Recommendation/Observation
2.11 The Committee notes that the research activities on Osteoarthritis, Thyroid disorders and Cancer
are being undertaken at NIH. The Committee observes that research projects often witness appreciable
time-lag in translating research leads and outcomes into tangible health products. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Ministry should ensure that the above research projects are taken to their logical
conclusion within the shortest possible span of time and the products generated out of the research
projects reach our healthcare system and are used by the people. (Para 5.9)
Action Taken
2.12 In compliance with the recommendations of the Committee, the Research wing of the Institute
has been tasked to implement outcome of the logical achievement of the Research on certain diseases
(Osteoarthritis, Thyroid disorders and Cancer) undertaken so far.

REGULATION AND QUALITY CONTROL OF AYURVEDIC, SIDDHA, UNANI AND
HOMOEOPATHIC (ASU&H) DRUGS
Recommendation/Observation
3.13 The Committee observes that to enhance the acceptability of ASU&H medicines within the country
and abroad, the quality control and standardization is of prime importance, but the regulatory framework
of certification and quality control of AYUSH remedies is very weak, which raises serious concerns
regarding safety issues. The Committee in its Reports on Demands for Grants of the Ministry of AYUSH
(erstwhile Department of AYUSH) has been commenting on quality assurance and standardization of
Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathic medicines and urging the Ministry to speed up strengthening
and streamlining of regulatory mechanism of ASU&H drugs. The Committee is aware that there is a
separate Chapter-IV A in the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1946 which regulates the manufacture and sale
of ASU drugs. Since the enforcement of provisions of Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules made
thereunder is under the purview of State Drug Licensing and Drug Control Authorities, the State Drug
Regulatory Framework has a crucial role to play in ensuring safety and quality of ASU and H drugs. It
is in this context that the Committee views with seriousness the submission of the Secretary (AYUSH)
that the State Drug Regulatory framework is very weak and State Drug Licensing Authorities are very
ill-equipped both technically and infrastructurally, to handle the task of regulation of AYUSH medicines.
The Committee also takes note of the submission of the Secretary (AYUSH) that it is the allopathic side
which does the regulation of AYUSH medicines and the AYUSH industry regulation gets neglected
because the State regulators are more focused on modern medicine than on AYUSH products as a result
of which regulatory needs of standardization and quality control of ASU&H drugs get seriously impaired.
The Committee also observes that quality control and standardization of ASU&H drugs, which are mainly
herbal/ herbo-mineral preparations, is very complex and different from synthetic molecules of the allopathic

21
system which are produced under controlled laboratory conditions. Taking all these facts into account,
the Committee recommends that the Government should move quickly towards setting up a separate
regulator for AYUSH industry and apprise the Committee of the time-frame within which a separate
regulator for AYUSH industry would be set up. The Committee also desires to be apprised of the
recommendation of the AYUSH Task Force set up by Government under Chairmanship of Prof. H. R.
Nagendra on the issue of regulator for the AYUSH industry. (Para 12.13)

discussion with the concerned entities on the issue of creating a separate regulatory structure for the

of this Report. (Para 12.14)
Action Taken
3.19 Government has considered setting up a structured central regulatory regime for AYUSH drugs.
In this regard, the current proposal is to develop a vertical structure for AYUSH in the Central Drugs
Standard Control Organization (CDSCO) for a unified regulatory mechanism. Accordingly, amendments
required in the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 pertaining to regulation of Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani,
Sowa-rigpa and Homoeopathy drugs were conceptualized and conveyed to the Department of Health and
Family Welfare under whose jurisdiction the proposals of amending Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and
strengthening of CDSCO are being processed. Ministry has notified creation of 12 posts of Deputy
Drugs Controllers, Assistant Drugs Controllers and Inspectors of Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy
on 17.2.2016 and approval of Recruitment Rules for these posts is in process. In the meantime, on the
basis of recommendations of the Group of Ministers (GoM), the amendment Bill, 2013 of Drugs and
Cosmetics Act, 1940 has been withdrawn from the Parliament by the Ministry of Health and Family
Welfare and the process initiated for framing a fresh legislation for regulating the drugs, medical devices
and cosmetics including AYUSH drugs.
Further Recommendation
3.20 The Committee observes that creation of a vertical regulatory structure in the CDSCO has
been pending since March 2015 when the decision to this effect was taken. The Committee, being
deeply concerned about the poor state of regulation of AYUSH medicines, is dismayed at the
snail's pace of progress in the matter. The Committee would, therefore, like the Ministry to set
a time frame within which this regulatory structure is in place so that quality control and
standardization of AYUSH drugs could be ensured.
Recommendation/Observation
3.21 The Committee in its earlier Reports has been commenting upon the misleading advertisements
concerning AYUSH remedies over TV and radio and in the print media and exhorting the Ministry to take
necessary remedial measures in preventing inappropriate advertisements in electronic and print media.
The oft-repeated contention of the Ministry in this regard has been that due to ineffectiveness of the
Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954, it has not been possible to take
stringent action against the misleading and exaggerated claims of efficacy of the AYUSH remedies.
Subsequently, the Ministry constituted on the recommendation of the Committee, a Task Force for the
purpose of suggesting amendments in the aforesaid Act. The Ministry in its Action Taken Notes on the
84th Report of the Committee had inter-alia informed that Department of Health and Family Welfare,
under whose jurisdiction Task Force for suggesting amendments in the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act,
1954 was constituted, has been requested to expedite consolidation of the amendment provisions
recommended by the Task Force." The Committee notes that there has been recurring delays in amending
the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 and Rules framed thereunder.
Though the process of amending the said Act and Rules has been set in motion, its logical culmination

23
is still some distance away. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should vigorously
pursue the matter of amending the said Act and Rules thereunder. The Committee also recommends that
apart from giving sharper teeth to the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, a provision should also be
incorporated in the relevant Rules to ban such practices and penalize offenders. (Para 12.16)
Action Taken
3.22 Ministry of Health and Family Welfare set up a Task Force for suggesting required amendments
in the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, 1954 and its recommendations were circulated for comments/
suggestions from all the Ministries/Departments of the Central Government, State Governments,
stakeholders and general public. During examination of this matter after scrutiny of objections and
suggestions received, it was considered that in view of the certain degree of convergence between Drugs
and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954, a
comprehensive new legislation containing the existing and proposed amendment provisions of the Drugs
and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954
may be attempted and introduced in Parliament in case Drugs and Cosmetics (Amendment) Bill, 2015
does not go through. Now, with the recommendations of the Group of Ministers, a new comprehensive
legislation containing the existing and proposed provisions of both the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940
and the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 is being attempted. In the
meantime, Ministry of AYUSH has separately notified on 4th March 2016 the draft Rules for prohibition
of advertisements of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drugs and the comments/suggestions/objections of the
stakeholders are being examined to finalize the provisions and seek approval of Minister ln-charge and
Law Ministry and then notify them under Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.
Further Recommendation
3.23 The Committee would like to reiterate that the process of amending the Rules and the said
Acts should be done at the earliest so as to check the misleading advertisements concerning
AYUSH remedies and taking strict action against the offenders. The new comprehensive legislation
containing the existing and proposed provisions of both the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and
the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable advertisements) Act, 1954 should be expedited to
put to end such practices. The Committee notes that in the meantime Ministry has separately
notified draft Rules for prohibition of advertisements of Ayurveda, Yoga, Siddha and Unani drugs
under Drugs and Cosmetic Acts, 1945 but they are still under approvals. The Committee,
accordingly, recommends necessary approvals thereof be expedited so that these rules may be
notified at an early dates.
