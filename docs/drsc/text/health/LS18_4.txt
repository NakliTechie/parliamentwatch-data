IV.    SAFDARJUNG HOSPITAL
Recommendations/Observations
4.5    The Committee observes that whereas Non-Plan funds for the hospital allocated in the first two years of Tenth
Plan could be utilized fully, plan allocation remained underutilized. In view of the higher allocation of plan funds in
2004-05 (from Rs. 45.00 crore in 2003-04 to Rs. 60.00 crores in 2004-05), the Department should take all the required
steps for its optimum and judicious utilisation.
Action Taken
         The major reason for under utilization of the plan funds for 2002-03 and 2003-04 was due to savings under the
sub-head Machinery and Equipment. This was due to the fact that the debits against the Letter of Credits were opened
for procurement of imported Machinery and Equipments; but debits against the LOCs could not be received during the
same financial year from foreign suppliers. Funds also remained unutilized due to non-completion of various codal
formalities regarding procurement.
         There is higher allocation of funds in 2004-05 i.e. Rs. 60 crores against Rs.45 crores in 2003-04. The Additional
funds are provided to meet the normal increase in expenditure annually and to procure the major equipment including
the equipments like M.R.I., Spiral C.T. Scan for which action for procurement was initiated during 2003-04. The
expenditure involved in procurement of these equipments and Brachytherapy is to the tune of Rs. 13.24 crores (approx.).
The procurement of these equipments is at an advanced stage.
         The pattern of expenditure is reviewed periodically in the Ministry to ensure that funds are utilized fully.
V.     RAM MANOHAR LOHIA HOSPITAL, NEW DELHI
Recommendations/Observations
5.1    The Plan allocation for this hospital in BE 2003-04 was Rs. 19.00 crores which has been marginally increased to
Rs. 22.00 crores in BE 2004-05. In respect of setting up of a Post Graduate Institute at RML hospital, the Department
has informed that the total Tenth Plan allocation for Dr. RML Hospital is Rs. 150.00 crores which also includes the cost
of establishment of Post Graduate Institute at Dr. RML. Hospital. An EFC for establishment of Post Graduate Institute
at Dr. RML Hospital has already been approved. However, the construction work for the Institute is yet to be started. An
outlay of Rs. 8.00 crores has been made for construction works under capital head for the Hospital during this financial
year i.e. 2004-05. Besides, a provision of Rs. 50.00 lakhs as token amount for the Institute has also been kept in the Plan
budget of the Hospital.
Recommendations/Observations
5.2    The Committee is anguished to note that during the first two years of the Tenth Plan, nothing beyond obtaining
EFC approval for the Post Graduate Institute could be achieved. The construction work is yet to start. The Committee
feels that at this sluggish pace, the proposed teaching institute may not be established during the Tenth Plan. The
Committee feels that a time bound Action Plan for completion of this Project must have been chalked out by the
Department in consultation with the hospital authorities. The Committee strongly feels that the Department should
strictly adhere to this Action Plan so that the proposed Dental Institute is established at the earliest. If setting up of this
PG institute requires more funds, the Committee recommends that the Department may provide the same.
Action Taken
         The foundation stone for the Post Graduate Courses at Dr. RML Hospital has been laid by the Hon’ble Union
Health Minister on 25
th November, 2004. Land has already been acquired and an allocation of Rs.30 crores have been
proposed under the Plan Head (for the year 2005-06) for construction of building of PGIMER. Initiative has been
undertaken to complete the construction work of the PGIMER in a time bound manner. Necessary steps are also being
taken to ensure that the required manpower for teaching is available to run the PG Courses at the Institute so that the
Institute starts functioning at the earliest.

made available to the programme through statement of expenditure and utilization certificates submitted by

XI.    TUBERCULOSIS CONTROL PROGRAMME
Recommendations/Observations
11.2  The Committee notes that the plan allocation of Rs. 108 crores in 2003-04 has been enhanced to Rs. 115 crores in
2004-05. As per the State-wise figures available with the Committee, position regarding fund allocation/release and
utilisation thereof during 2001-02, 2002-03 and 2003-04 does not reveal a satisfactory performance. In 2001-02, against
an allocation of Rs. 133.00 crore, only Rs. 102.88 crore could be utilized. During 2002-03, against an allocation of Rs.
107.00 crore, utilisation figures are Rs. 87.46 crore. However, during 2003-04, utilisation figures (Rs. 113.09 crore)
exceed the funds allocated (Rs. 107.60 crore). The Committee observes that overall details do not reveal the factual
position about the performance of individual States. Expenditure in a large number of states exceeds the allocated funds;
there are some states that could not fully utilize the allocated funds. The Committee fails to comprehend the real
problem areas being indicated in this programme. Whereas there are some States, which failed to utilize the allocated
funds, there are States which exceeded the allocated funds. The Committee, therefore, is of the view that the Department
should pursue the matter of proper utilisation of funds as per the prescribed norms/Guidelines with the concerned State
Governments.
Action Taken
         Under the National TB Control Programme, initial allocation of funds to the States is made in the beginning of the
financial year tentatively on the pro-rata basis of population of the respective States. However, the expenditure of the
States depends on their actual requirement of cash grant and commodity assistance. The cash grant depends on the trend
of utilization of the funds released to them. As the allocation is only tentative and the expenditure is based on the cash
assistance and the commodity assistance actually released to the States, this sometimes results in differences between
allocation and expenditure for some States and in some cases minor variation in expenditure could be from the tentative
allocation.
         For ensuring proper utilization of funds by the States, the following steps have been taken:---

            (a)    State TB Societies Guidelines which include the guidelines for expenditure have been widely circulated.
            (b)    Provision has been made for contractual appointment of Accounts Officers in the State TB Societies for

            (c)    The Accounts Officers are given training in Finance & Accounts matters to acquaint them with the Govt.

            (g)    The Accounts of the Societies are subject to Audit.

XVII. NATIONAL ILLNESS ASSISTANCE FUND
Recommendations/Observations
17.2  The Committee notes that during 2003-04, financial assistance of Rs. 78.54 lakh was granted to patients. Besides
that assistance was also provided to NIMHANS, Bangalore, AIIMS, Delhi, CNCI Kolkata, JIPMER Pondicherry and
Illness assistance Fund Society Lakshadweep for maintaining their revolving fund. The Committee notes that thirteen
States/UTs were released financial assistance under the society. The Committee was given to understand that all the
States are not maintaining such a fund. The Committee feels that the Department should pursue with the States not
covered so far to setup such a fund.
Action Taken
         At present, following States/UTs are maintaining State Illness Fund with a view to provide an assistance up to Rs.
1.5 lakhs in an individual case to the patients Below Poverty Line (BPL), suffering from life threatening diseases and
taking treatment in a Government hospital, viz., (i) Karnataka  (ii) Madhya Pradesh (iii) Tripura (iv) Andhra Pradesh (v)
Tamil Nadu (vi) Himachal Pradesh (vii) Jammu & Kashmir (viii) Maharashtra (ix) West Bengal (x) Kerala (xi)
Mizoram (xii) Rajasthan (xiii) Goa 
(xiv) Gujarat (xv) Sikkim (xvi) Bihar (xvii) Chhattisgarh (xviii) Jharkhand (xix) Uttaranchal (xx) NCT of Delhi and
(xxi) Pondicherry. The State/UT level funds could also seek contributions/donations from private charity/industry for
which income tax exemption is given. The Central Government releases grant in aid to the extent of Rs. 50 per cent of
the contribution made by a State, subject to maximum of  Rs. 5 crores to States with larger number and percentage of
population Below Poverty Line and  Rs. 2 crores to other States/UTs with legislatures, in a year.
         Some States (viz. Assam, Haryana, Manipur, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Orissa, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and
Nagaland) are reluctant to set up such a fund. Despite repeated requests and follow up action, these States have not
responded. A possible reason could be the current sharing pattern under the scheme of l/3
rd Central contribution to the
2/3rd contribution made by the State concerned. A proposal for modifying the scheme so that the sharing of
contributions is 50:50 between Central Government and State Government is, therefore, being moved for consideration.
XIX.SETTING UP OF MEDICAL GRANTS COMMISSION
Recommendations/Observations
19.0  The Committee notes that setting up of Medical Grants Commission has been provided in the National Health
Policy, 2002. Apart from its role in coordination in the field of Education and Health Science, it is also envisaged that
the Medical Grants Commission would fund the upgradation of the infrastructure of the existing Government. Medical
and Dental colleges so as to ensure an improved standard of medical education. The Committee notes that in BE 2004-
05 (plan), only a token provision of Rs. 1.00 crore has been made. The Committee feels that this is a proposal which
needs to be actively supported in the overall interest of health sector. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
adequate provision for this purpose may be made at the RE stage.
Action Taken
         Medical Grants Commission has been proposed to act as a funding agency through which the Government can
provide continuous financial assistance to various medical colleges and its attached hospitals under the Government
sector. Planning Commission has asked the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare to examine the possibility of this
scheme being merged with any other scheme of similar nature.
         In this connection, considering the fact that upgradation of primary health in the backward area of the country
would require strong manpower support which in turn depends on the quality of medical education being imparted by
the Government medical colleges, it has been felt that funding for the Medical Grants Commission could be channelized
through the fund allocation to be made to the Department of Family Welfare under Rural Health Scheme. The details
are in the process of being worked out.
