FAMILY WELFARE
 
I           INTRODUCTION
 
1.1   India, the largest democratic republic in the world possesses 2.4% of the world’s land area and supports 16% of the
world population. In terms of population growth rate, there has been decline during the decade 1991-2001 as compared
to previous decade 1981-1991, due to the prolonged Family Planning Campaign. According to world population
projections by the United Nations, it is estimated that India would have a population of 1533 million by the year 2050
and in that year it will surpass China, currently the most populous country in the world.
1.2       Since 1951, the year India’s Family Planning programme was launched; its demographic and health profiles
have changed radically. Between 1951 and 2002, Crude Birth Rate (CBR) has declined from 40.8 to 25.0 per thousand
population and Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) from an average of 146 to 63 per thousand live births during the same
period. The Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) has also come down from 437 in 1992-93 as per NFHS-I to 407 in 1998
(as per SRS) per 100,000 live births. Significant improvement is also there in the Immunization Coverage.
1.3       Committee observes that there have been significant changes in the demographic and health profile of the
country. The Committee would, however, like to point out that average rate of various health indicators does not
indicate the ground realities in the big and populous states of the country. The Committee notes that
performance level in such states continues to remain much below the average national rate. A few examples as
indicated below are self-revealing:-

1.4       Committee’s attention has been drawn by a recent World Bank study, ‘Country Strategy for India - September,
2004’ which has mentioned that based on the existing data, the Maternal Mortality Rate has worsened. The Committee
notes that the National Family Health Survey-I (NFHS-I), 1991-92 estimated the Maternal Mortality Rate at 424 per
100,000 live births. However, NFHS-II , 1997-1998 estimated the MMR as 540 per 100,000 live births. It has also been
mentioned in the World Bank study, that there are problems with the data for this indicator in some of the largest states
that raise questions regarding data at the national level. The report of the NFHS-II also acknowledges that the difference
in the estimates of MMR of NFHS-I and NFHS-II is not statistically significant due to various reasons such as large
sampling errors etc.
1.5     Committee is not fully convinced by the contention of the Department that SRS, RGI have estimated the MMR
for the country as 407 per 100,000 live births in 1998 which is the same period when NFHS-II have estimated the NMR
as 540 per 100,000 live births. The fact remains that there are some problem areas in data collection. Non-availability of
reliable estimates of maternal mortality has been admitted by the Department also in its Annual Report (2004-05).   The
Committee notes that an expert group has been constituted in the Department which is looking into the modalities of
carrying out a survey for collection of data on Maternal Mortality.  A Pilot survey for this has already been completed.
Similar problems may be there in respect of other health indicators also. The Committee, therefore, is of the view that
the Department should carry out a comparative analysis of findings of NFHS-I and NFHS-II in order to understand the
factors responsible for discrepancies in the two Survey Reports. The Committee would also like to emphasise that the
entire Family Welfare planning is based on the National Surveys being conducted at regular intervals. Therefore, it
should be ensured that accurate data is made the basis of all health surveys. This can be achieved to a large extent by
monitoring of data being obtained specially from the states not performing well by involving Central teams also in the
collection of data, to the extent possible.

IV        NATIONAL RURAL HEALTH MISSION  
4.1       The Committee has been informed that National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) (2005-12) is to be launched
throughout the country in April, 2005 with special emphasis on eighteen  states with weak health infrastructure
including eight EAG States (U.P, M.P, Bihar, Rajasthan, Orissa, Jharkhand, Uttaranchal and Chattisgarh), eight NE
States, Himachal Pradesh, and Jammu & Kashmir. The Mission aims to provide comprehensive integrated primary
healthcare services, including RCH services and National Disease Control Programmes.
4.2       The Committee notes that under NRHM, the gaps in the existing scenario of rural health care are to be addressed
by creation of a cadre of community level functionaries called Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA) to assist the
Angandwadi Worker and ANM at village level. The general norm for selection will be one ASHA per 1000 population
to be selected by the community and the process of selection would involve meeting of Gram Sabha and Gram
Panchayat. Following are some of the other anticipated Mission outcomes:-
Ø
      Raising CHCs to the level of Indian Public Health standard
Ø      Increasing utilization of First Referral units from less than 20% (2002) to more than 75% by 2010.
4.3       The Committee notes that NRHM is the most ambitious Project proposed to be launched in the Health and
Family Welfare Sector. Mission has received the Cabinet approval very recently, i.e. 4 th January, 2005. States are being
now advised to undertake preparatory activities. The Committee has been given to understand that in view of the recent
Cabinet approval, requirement for activities under NRHM could not be included in the proposal sent to the Planning
Commission. A minimum of Rs. 600 crores will be required as additional budgetary support during 2005-06.          
4.4       Objectives of the Mission are laudable and are very much required in the present scenario. The Committee
observes that divergent activities not only of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, but also number of other
Ministries are proposed to be implemented in an integrated manner. Two Departments of the Ministry-Health and
Family Welfare have been integrated for optimum utilization of financial resource and manpower. The Committee,
however, has a word of caution in this regard. Both the Departments as separate entities were already implementing 
large number of schemes and supervising institutions falling under their respective jurisdiction. Not only this, quite a
few programmes under both the Departments were not being administered effectively due to a very weak monitoring
mechanism relying only on state figures. The Committee feels that with the convergence of a number of Health and
Family Welfare  Schemes under NRHM, effective monitoring may not be feasible.  The Committee would, therefore,

V         RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES          
5.1
              Rural Family Welfare Services are meant to provide comprehensive primary health care to the masses at grass-
root level. The primary health care infrastructure has been developed as a three tier system and is based on the following
population norms:-
Center Plain area Hilly/Tribal/Difficult area
Sub-Center 5000 3000
Primary Health Center 30,000 20,000
Community Health Center 1,20,000 80,000
 
5.2       The Sub-Center is the lowest level of the primary health care system which is manned by one Auxiliary Nurse
Mid-wife (ANM) and one Male Health Worker (Multi Purpose Worker-MPW). In addition, one LHV is entrusted with
the supervision of 6 sub-centres.
5.3       Govt. of India bears the salary of ANMs as per state Govt. pay scales and honorarium for voluntary workers.
Rent @ Rs. 3000/- per annum and contingency @ Rs  3200/- per annum is also borne by the Central Govt. Salary of
male worker is provided by the State Govt.
5.4       Primary Health Centres (PHCs) and Community Health Centers (CHCs) are established and managed by the
State Govts. under the Minimum Needs Programme/ Basis Minimum Service Programme. PHC acts as referral unit for
six Sub-Centers and CHC services as a referral center for four PHCs.
5.5
                   The Budgetary allocation of Rs. 1532.71 crores in BE 2004-05 was increased to Rs. 1722.10 crores at RE
level and it has further been increased to Rs. 1869.20 crores in BE 2005-06. The Department has submitted that the
enhanced allocation in BE 2005-06 will be utilized in funding the additional sub-centers whose number has increased
from existing 1,37,311 to 1,42,655 and also to meet the increased unit cost due to enhanced DA and other miscellaneous
expenditures. There is marginal saving of Rs. 4.50 crores in 2004-05 which is due to unspent balance lying with
States/UTs and less infrastructure in NE states.
5.6       Additional Sub-Centres required as per 1991 Census are 8669 and as per 2001 population are 26,478. Against a
Tenth Plan target of opening of 8181 Sub-centres fixed by the Planning Commission, annual target for 2003-04 and
2004-05 is 1941 in each year. The Committee has been given to understand that after Cabinet approval in January,2004,
funds for establishing new Sub-Centres @ Rs. 10,000 each were released on February 2004. The Committee, however,
notes that as per the figures made available to it, 5344 Sub-Centres are supposed to be established in 2003-04 and 2004-
05. Three states are the major beneficiaries, with 1954 Sub-Centres established in Andhra Pradesh, 706 Sub-Centres in
Madhya Pradesh and 2230 Sub-Centres in U. P. The Committee has been given to understand that the target in
establishment of Sub-Centres is given by the Planning Commission for respective States/UTs. The Committee would
like to be apprised of exact number of Sub-Centres established (state-wise) during the first three years of Tenth
Plan.
5.7       As per 2001 population, 4977 more PHCs are needed to be established in the country. The Committee notes that
only  234 PHCs were established during 2003-04 and 2004-05, 104 were established in Andhra Pradesh and 136 PHCs
in Himachal Pradesh. The capital cost of one PHC is Rs. 24.50 lakhs with a recurring liability of Rs. 13 lakhs. This
expenditure is met by the State Govt. under the PMGY programme.

5.8       The Committee is surprised to note that some states have already established PHCs in excess of the prescribed
norms.   There are also states where shortfall in the number of PHCs is evident. Against a population norm of 30,000 per
PHC, average rural population (2001) per PHC is 45,095 in Bihar, 37,169 in Madhya Pradesh, 36348 in Tripura 36,169
in U.P. 49,231 in West Bengal and 1,18,090 in Delhi. The Committee is not convinced by the clarifications of the
Department that the target for establishment of PHCs is given by the Planning Commission to the respective States/UTs
and it is the responsibility of the State/UTs to establish them. The Committee strongly feels that with funds being
provided under PMGY scheme, it is the responsibility of the Department to monitor the establishment of PHCs .
5.9       The Committee finds that the same problem is there with regard to CHCs also. Arunachal Pradesh, U.P,
Mizoram, Maharashtra, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep and Pondicherry have excess infrastructure in terms
of CHCs .Capital cost of one CHCs is Rs. 80.50 lakhs with a recurring liability of Rs. 27.00 lakhs is met by the State
Govts. under the PMGY programme. The Committee is of the opinion  that in view of the non-adherence to  the
prescribed population norms, whether short or excess by majority of the states, it is time that a review of the existing
population norms are looked into by the Department.    
5.10     The Committee’s attention has been drawn by an evaluation study of 1040 Sub-Centres covering 300 districts of
14 major states conducted by   National Council of Applied Economic Research (NCAER). The findings of the
evaluation were submitted on 22
nd November, 2004. Major recommendations/conclusions of NCAER included in its
Report submitted on 22.11.04 are as under:-
(i)                  Lack of permanent Sub-Centres building is an important constraint in the functioning of Sub-Centres. 
Sub-Centres in rented buildings keep on shifting, thus creating information gap among the users.
Permanent SC buildings provide an incentive to furnish it properly. Permanently owned building is a pre-
condition for improving the quality of delivery and utilization of services. It is also desirable that ANM
and Anganwadi Workers both have accommodation in a common compound to avoid duplication of
certain services. For better service delivery, SC building should be located at a central place.
 
(ii)
                Shortage of staff is quite high and almost half the SCs are functioning without Male Health Worker. The
data shows that significantly higher proportion of respondents from the SCs with both ANM and MHW
are “fully satisfied”, therefore appointing MHW alongwith ANM in all the SCs should be done on
priority basis.
 
(iii)       Irregularity in the disbursement of salary to the staff is a serious problem especially in Jharkhand, Bihar,
Orrisa and Utter Pradesh.
 
5.11     The Committee understands that the Report of the NCAER study is at present under the considerations of the
Department. The Committee would like to emphasise that the Department should initiate action at the earliest for
strengthening the functioning of Sub-Centres, PHCs and CHCs, the only service providers to the targeted beneficiaries
in the remote and inaccessible  areas in the country. A beginning can be made with the fourteen states where the sample
study was conducted by NCAER. The Committee would also like to emphasise that a similar study is also required to be
undertaken with regard to functionaries of both PHCs and CHCs which are also the main service providers likes Sub-
Centres. 
5.12     The Committee would like to point out that demographically weak states have higher birth rate and Infant
Mortality Rate. The workload of ANMs in these states is thus higher. The Committee, therefore, feels that in such states,
sanctioning of ANMs should be based on existing norms as per 2001 population. At the same time, the Department
should also closely monitor the filling up of all the sanctioned posts of ANMs.

VII      REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH PROGRAMME (RCH)
 
7.1       RCH is the flagship programme of the Govt. on Reproductive Child and Maternal Health. A paradigm shift is
envisaged in the manner in which the RCH Programme has been conceptualized and implemented based upon key
learning from the first phase of the Programme (1997-2002).   RCH-II is set to start from April, 2005.
7.2       The Committee observes that RCH-II programme is envisaged as umbrella programme which will integrate all
the related and interlinked stand alone schemes into a single composite programme. All the schemes under the Flexible
Pool will cease to be prescriptive and will only provide generic guidelines. The key characteristic of the Flexible Pool is
to enable the states to devise need-based intervention and undertake decentralized planning. The Flexible Pool will be
operated by providing the State with a financial envelope based upon which each state will formulate its own PIP rather
than subscriber to multiple schemes.
            Accordingly the budget heads of the Department are also proposed to be rationalized and compressed into four
omnibus schemes:-
Ø
      Operational support to the states -  committed expenditure such as salary of ANMs, grants to State Training
Institutes, free distribution of contraceptives

Ø      Operational cost of Institution supported by the Department -grants to central institutions like NIHFW,ICMR
etc.
Ø      Activities centrally implemented by the Department - procurement of drugs and equipment, vaccines for routine

Ø      Activities in the State Programme Implementation Plan (PIP) –base flexible pool to support state PIPs.    
7.3     The Committee has also been informed that all the externally aided projects are proposed to be merged with

they would be implemented as envisaged to get the desired results.

VIII     JANANI SURAKSHA YOJNA (NATIONAL MATERNITY BENEFIT SCHEME)
 
8.1       The Committee has been informed that the National Maternity Benefit Scheme is being revised as Janani
Suraksha Yojna (JSY) with the aim of promotion of institutional delivery to bring down the high Maternal Mortality
Rate. Under this scheme, a BPL mother will get Rs. 1000 per girl child and Rs. 400/- per male child, if delivered in a
health institution as against uniform rate of Rs. 500/- per child for the first two live births under the earlier scheme.
Besides, transport assistance upto Rs. 150/- and incentive to Dais @ Rs. 200/150 for female/male child has been
envisaged in lieu of appropriate antenatal and postnatal care and referral for institutional delivery. The scheme is also
aimed to operationalise FRUs at district levels to ensure 24 hrs services for improved delivery.
8.2       The Committee observes that exercise of revision of this scheme has been going on for the last  two-years.   As a
result, funds allocated have remained under-utilized. Against the allocation of Rs. 500 crores in the Tenth Plan, the
expenditure for first two years is only Rs. 87.57 crores and the balance available for the remaining period is Rs. 300.43
crores. The Committee feels that there should be no further delay and finalization of the schemes should be expedited. 
The Committee would also like to emphasize that the Department should not be complacent by just revising the scheme,
rather an effective monitoring mechanism should be evolved for its proper implementation.
8.3       The Committee has observed that under the existing scheme, there is no mechanism to ascertain the exact
number of actual beneficiaries of the scheme. The Department has been estimating the number of beneficiaries on the
basis of amount of grants released to the District Authorities. The Committee feels that under the proposed revised
scheme, there should be an inbuilt mechanism so that there is periodic survey to verify the exact number of
beneficiaries. The Committee also observes that state wise details of estimated number of beneficiaries indicate that
those states where need for population stabilization measures is the most do not seem to be taking benefit of this
scheme. The Committee is of the opinion that the revised scheme should be given due publicity, specially in
backward/tribal areas.
8.4       The Committee has also noticed that under the existing Scheme due to unspent balances, non-receipt/ late receipt
of UCs/Audited accounts/receipt of incomplete/incorrect documents, there was improper and under utilization of
allocated funds. This was evident in respect of all the States in the country. The Committee, would therefore, like to
emphasize that all preventive measures need to be taken to avoid such problems/shortcomings under the revised
scheme.

reliable estimates of maternal mortality has been admitted by the Department also in its Annual Report (2004-05).   The
Committee notes that an expert group has been constituted in the Department which is looking into the modalities of
carrying out a survey for collection of data on Maternal Mortality.  A Pilot survey for this has already been completed.
Similar problems may be there in respect of other health indicators also. The Committee, therefore, is of the view that
the Department should carry out a comparative analysis of findings of NFHS-I and NFHS-II in order to understand the
factors responsible for discrepancies in the two Survey Reports. The Committee would also like to emphasise that the
entire Family Welfare planning is based on the National Surveys being conducted at regular intervals. Therefore, it
should be ensured that accurate data is made the basis of all health surveys. This can be achieved to a large extent by
monitoring of data being obtained specially from the states not performing well by involving Central teams also in the
collection of data, to the extent possible. (Para 1.5)
