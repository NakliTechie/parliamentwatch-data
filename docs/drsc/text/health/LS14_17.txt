I.         INTRODUCTION
1.1       Ever since independence in 1947, there has been state patronage and policy support to the Indian systems of
Medicine but its institutionalized development started when the Department of Indian Systems of Medicines and
Homoeopathy (ISM&H) was set up by the Government of India as an independent department in March, 1995, under
the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.  It was renamed as Department of Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani,
Siddha and Homoeopathy (AYUSH) in November, 2003.   The main objective of this full- fledged Department headed
by a Secretary, is to provide focussed attention for the proper development and propagation of Ayurveda, Siddha,
Unani, Homoeopathy, Yoga and Naturopathy and to fully utilize the potentials of these systems.
1.2       Thrust areas of the Department are: -
i)
                    Improvement and up-gradation of standards of education in AYUSH;
ii)                   Quality Control and Standardization of drugs;
iii)                 Ensuring sustained availability of raw materials, i.e. medicinal plants, metals, minerals and materials of
animal origin etc;
iv)                 Research & Development on the efficacy of the systems;
v)                  Participation of AYUSH in the National Health Care Delivery System & National Health & Family Welfare
Programme;
vi)                 Information, Education and Communication.
 
1.3       Over the years, there has been a steady growth in infrastructure of AYUSH. At present, the number of
practitioners of AYUSH are 7.18 lakhs, out of which 5.17 lakhs are institutionally qualified. The number of non-
institutionally qualified practitioners is 2.01 lakhs. There are 549 teaching institutions which include 450 undergraduate
colleges and 99 post graduate colleges. Number of drug manufacturing units both in Government and private sector in
the country is 9493. The number of GMP complaint manufacturing units is 3973 and the number of Government drug
testing laboratories is 21. There are 1,355 hospitals with total bed strength of 53296 and 22,635 dispensaries, out of
which maximum numbers of hospitals and dispensaries provide Ayurveda system of treatment. Total number of
Ayurveda hospitals and dispensaries in the country are 753 and 15,193 respectively. The population coverage through
AYUSH system is 7  doctors per 10,000 population.
1.4       The Secretary, during the course of his presentation before the Committee, stated that the Department started
playing a vital role in strengthening, upgrading and popularization of the AYUSH systems during the 10
th five year plan
with the introduction of a number of Central Sector and Centrally Sponsored  Schemes aimed at development of
educational institutions, mainstreaming of AYUSH speciality clinics/OPDs in the existing allopathic hospitals, research
and quality control. Achievements made during the year 2005-06 include:-
Ø
      Setting up of AYUSH wings in 139 District Hospitals, opening of 299 AYUSH speciality clinics, 44
OPD/polyclinics and supply of essential AYUSH drugs to 13,273 AYUSH dispensaries in rural and backward
areas throughout the country
Ø      133 AYUSH educational institutions have been assisted under various schemes relating to upgradation of
UG/PG colleges and reorientation training programme or continuing  medical education.
Ø      29 AYUSH colleges (Ayurveda-15, Siddha-1, Unani-5, Homoeopathy-8) including 12 during the year have been
sanctioned financial assistance for development as a model institute on the norms of developing one model
college per system per State. Now this scheme is proposed to be modified to assist more than one college per
system per State in view of the fact that some systems like Siddha and Unani are widely prevalent only in
particular areas and also that some States are very large.

II.        BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
 
2.1       The Committee observes that there has been massive enhancement in the allocation of plan funds for the
Department.   As compared to Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 364.43 crores, funds provided to the Department in the Tenth
Plan were Rs. 1194.60 crores.   Department has   managed optimum utilisation of funds during both the plan periods,
massive enhancement of funds notwithstanding.   The Committee notes that the trend of allocation and utilisation of
funds during the last two years of both Ninth and Tenth Plan indicates interesting similarity.   The Department could not
match the substantive increase  in allocation of plan funds in 2000-01 and 2001-02,  the last two years of Ninth Plan. 
Committee notices the same position in 2005-06 where funds had to be reduced at the RE stage.  The Committee
apprehends the same position during 2006-07 also.
2.2       The Plan Allocation of the Department for the year 2006-07 is Rs. 381.60 crores, showing an increase of Rs.
81.60 crores when compared to RE allocation of  Rs. 300.00 crores in 2005-06.   Expenditure  reported by the end of 
the year is Rs. 292.35 crores.   The Committee has taken note of the fact that plan allocation of Rs. 345.00 crores of the
Department at BE stage of 2005-06 was reduced to Rs. 300.00 crores at RE stage. Reason given is delay in
administrative and financial sanctions in respect of the North-East institute of Ayurveda and Homoeopathy and All
India Institute of Ayurveda, New Delhi.    Clarifying that the situation was beyond their control, the Secretary assured
the Committee that the Department would make earnest efforts to obtain the required sanctions for the two projects and
optimum utilisation of the allocated funds.   
2.3       The Committee is not fully convinced by the justification given by the Department.   The Committee would like
to point out that during 2005-06, expenditure reported upto 15.1.2006 was Rs.223.27 crores which went upto Rs.292.35
crores by the end of the financial year.  This clearly shows that the last quarter of the year reported higher rate of
expenditure as compared to the remaining three quarters of the year. 
2.4       Committee’s attention has been drawn by another disturbing trend in the utilisation of allocated funds.   As
reported in the Performance Budget  (2005-06) , as on 31
st March, 2004 there were as many as 373 pending Utilisation
Certificates amounting to Rs.107.02 crores.   As per the latest information made available to the Committee, number of

While there was nil achievement during 2003-04, performance level during 2004-05 showed noticeable improvement.

needs to be focussed more.

 IV     
HOSPITALS AND DISPENSARIES
4.1       Budget allocation of Rs.90 crores for the two schemes of AYUSH hospitals and dispensaries in 2005-06
was enhanced to Rs. 100.82 crores at the RE stage and the actual expenditure reported is Rs. 119.71 crores.      
The Committee notes that separate allocation  for the two schemes indicates that whereas for AYUSH hospitals,
allocation of Rs. 45 crores was enhanced to Rs.67.65 crores and the final  expenditure was even higher, being
Rs.85.02 crores.  In contrast provision for AYUSH dispensaries was reduced from Rs. 45 crores to Rs.33.17
crores and the actual expenditure was only Rs.32.32 crores.   
4.2       The Committee observes that there are about 21000 AYUSH dispensaries in the country and the scheme
calls for financial assistance @Rs.25000 per dispensary per annum.  The Committee notes that during 2003-04
and 2004-05,  utilisation was optimum and the funds were not found sufficient for the projected requirements of
the existing dispensaries.    The Committee is not aware about the reason for the reduction in the allocated funds
for AYUSH dispensaries during 2005-06.   The Committee would however like to point out that the core
strategy of mainstreaming of AYUSH under NRHM is to make available AYUSH services at the PHC/CHC
level, either by reallocation of AYUSH doctors from existing dispensaries or by hiring of additional doctors on
contract basis for which funds will be provided under NRHM.  The Committee apprehends that  on the plea of
providing  AYUSH doctors at PHC/CHC with  the funds being provided therefor under NRHM, AYUSH
dispensaries  spread across the country may perhaps  not continue to provide the services as being done uptil
now.  Out of the 10000 AYUSH dispensaries and units located in rural and backward areas, only 7936
dispensaries could be covered for improving the supply position of essential AYUSH drugs during the year
2005-06.  The Committee would, therefore, like to emphasize that every effort should be made for effective
functioning of the existing AYUSH dispensaries.

6.2  Central Council of Indian Medicine
6.2.1   The Council is a statutory body set up in 1971 with the main objective to maintain the Central Register of
Indian Medicine and also for maintenance of minimum standard in education of Indian Medicine and issues
related to recognition of new colleges of increase in seats and starting of new courses. 
6.2.2   The Committee notes that   allocation of Rs. 15 lakhs in 2005-06 was brought down to Rs. 10 lakhs and
the actual expenditure reported is only Rs. 1.08 lakhs.   The reason given is delay in the formulation of a new
proposal and availability of Rs. 3.32 lakhs as unspent balances. The Committee finds the position was the same
during 2004-05.  The Committee apprehends that availability of unspent balances might have been the factor
responsible for initialization of allocated funds.
6.2.3    The Committee observes that the status of Central Register of Indian Medicine as on 28.02.2006 clearly
reveals that almost all the concerned states are lagging behind in sending the requisite information of updation of
Central Register.  States like J&K have sent the details which date back to March 1993.  The Committee
understands that at present information relating to the period April 1983-October 2005 is under process by the
Council.  In respect of other States also the Committee observes that although information have been received
from the State Councils for 10-15 years on an average in each case, the same is yet to be incorporated in the
Central Register.  It is not known that since when this information is under compilation by the Central Council. 
Even if has been received the recent past, the Committee would like to emphasize that the process needs to be
expedited.  The plea of the Central Council due to non receipt of inputs from some states, there is delay in
updating the Central Register can not be considered to be very convincing.  The Committee is of the view that in
the event of non-submission of required information by any state council, a mechanism needs to be evolved so as
to ensure that the updation of Central Register does not get unduly delayed.  In the event of the AYUSH
practitioners of a particular state being deprived of the opportunity of getting registered in the state council, the
fault should lie with that council. 
6.2.4    The Committee has observed that the funds duly allocated for conduct of Election for the Council during
the last three years remained underutilized, making the Council not fully constituted. On a specific query in this
regard the Committee has been informed that the Council is fully constituted inspite of delay in election of
Members from some States due to the very fact that the members elected previously are still holding office. 
From the Statewise information furnished by the Department, the Committee finds that elections have been
delayed in Bihar, MP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Punjab, Tamil Nadu and W. Bengal either due to delay in

            The Committee has been observing the progress being made in the construction of the building for the past few

            The hospital set up in 1978 is meant for extending Ayurvedic treatment facilities to CGHS beneficiaries and

from the non-plan side. Not only this there is delay in the matter of creation of posts for the hospital. Year 2005-06 was
also no exception. Provision of Rs. 1.00 crore was brought down to Rs. 32.00 lakhs and expenditure reported upto 7
th
March 2006 was only Rs. 7.43 lakhs. The Committee notes that the proposal of upgradation of 25-bedded hospital to

approach the Ministry of Finance in this regard.

VIII  NATIONAL MEDICAL PLANT BOARD  
8.1       An enhanced allocation of Rs. 38.00 crore has been made for the Board for 2006-07 against an allocation of Rs.
30.00 crore in 2005-06. The Committee observes that there has been optimum utilization of funds allocated for the
Board during the Tenth Plan period.
8.2       The Board was set up in November 2001 with the objective of development of medicinal plants used in AYUSH
medicines for sustained availability. The Committee observes that the Board was set up on the recommendation of a
Task Force constituted by the Planning Commission. Quite a few achievements could be credited in the name of the
Board.  So far, 32 State Medicinal Plant Boards have been set up. 32 medicinal plants have been identified at national

XII.     Use of Animal and Marine Products in Ayurveda, Sidha and Unani Drugs.
 
12.1     The Committee observes that some of these animal products namely Praval (coral), Shankha (conch shell),
Kapardika (cowrie) and Mrigashringa (stag horn and deer horn), tiger nails, musk, discharge of civet cat have been
banned under Wild Life                  ( Protection) Act, 1972. The Committee was informed that the Department has taken
up with the Ministry of Environment and Forest who are exploring the possibility of providing some of these items to
the Ayurveda drug industry without causing and harm to the environment. 
12.2     The Committee strongly feels that the Department has to play a more pro-active rote in this sensitive area by
banning all animal products and wild honey in all preparation. Pig and cow products are likely to take on communal
colour. Other items like Ambrgris and elephant parts are internationally banned. Earthworm, animal teeth, peacock
feathers have no medicinal value and reduce the medicine to a placebo.

The Committee is of the view that in the event of non-submission of required information by any state council, a
mechanism needs to be evolved so as to ensure that the updation of Central Register does not get unduly delayed.  In the
event of the AYUSH practitioners of a particular state being deprived of the opportunity of getting registered in the state
council, the fault should lie with that council.  (Para 6.2.3)
The Committee is not fully satisfied with the justification given by the Department.  The Committee hopes that
the Govt. would be bringing forward the amended Bill relating to the CCIM before Parliament which would take care of
these anomalies. (Para 6.2.4) 
VII.         HOSPITALS AND DISPENSARIES
7.1   All India Institute of Ayurveda, New Delhi
The Committee apprehends that not much progress would be made during 2006 also as the planning for the
project of building continues to be under consideration.  The Committee is not aware of the original estimate approved
for the construction of the building for the hospital.  However, as things stand, it would be definitely on a enhanced
level when completed. (Para 7.1)
7.2       Ayurveda Hospital, New Delhi
The Committee strongly feels that in order to expand the services of the hospital, required number of permanent
posts need to be created. The Department should once again approach the Ministry of Finance in this regard. (Para 7.2)
7.3      Expansion of AYUSH Dispensaries in CGHS
            The Committee does not appreciate this kind of approach. Implementation of SIU Report needs to be given
priority so that dispensaries already set up can become fully functional. The Committee would, accordingly, recommend
the creation of additional posts for AYUSH dispensaries without any further delay. (Para 7.3)
VIII.   NATIONAL MEDICAL PLANT BOARD  
            The Committee is surprised to note that the proposal and projected requirements did not find favour with the
Department of Expenditure and the Planning Commission. Instead it was suggested that 5 posts including 2 posts on
outsourcing basis suggested by the Department of Expenditure would serve the purpose and accordingly there was no
justification for creation of 30 additional posts. (Para 8.4)
The Committee, accordingly, reiterates once again to expedite the decision about the mandate and structure of
the Board acceptable to all concerned. The Committee is afraid that continuance of an air of indecisiveness about this
vital Body would lead to our endangered medicinal plants reaching the brink of extinction. (Par 8.5)
            The Committee observes that the existing monitoring mechanism has perhaps not proved to be effective enough.
Basis for this assessment made by the Committee is the alarming increase in the number of pending Utilization
Certificates under both the schemes. (Para 8.8)
The Committee apprehends that the pendency is likely to arise further in view of sanctioning of as many as 1233
projects worth Rs. 32.10 crore and 233 projects worth Rs. 25.65 crore under the Contractual Farming Scheme and
Promotional Scheme respectively during 2005-06 alone. (Para 8.10)
The Committee observes that both pendency rate of UCs involved therein is very high. During the course of
discussion with the representatives of the Department as well as the National Medicinal Plant Board, it was

categorically stated that amount involved in pending UCs would be negligible. However, the following information
forwarded by the Department proves the contention to be wrong and misleading. The Committee takes strong exception
to this.
            The Committee is not convinced by the justification given by the Department that pending UCs was a common
problem with all Ministries administering Centrally Sponsored Schemes on account of delay in release of CHS grants by
the states to implementing agencies and consequent delay in submission of UCs. The plea of the Department that the

with by farmers is also not acceptable to the Committee. The Committee would like to emphasize that the pendency is
bound to increase keeping in view the level of central assistance released under both the schemes during 2004-05 and
2005-06. (Para 8.11)
The Committee hopes that reports of these studies would provide critical input to the Board to modify and recast
the scheme accordingly. The Committee has a suggestion to make in this regard. Involvement of Forest Department will
not be helpful simply because they do not have any expertise in growing trees. The Committee is of the firm opinion
that agriculture universities like Pant Nagar need to be involved in the implementation of schemes like Promotional and
Contractual Farming. The Committee fails to understand the reason for not initiating the provision of recovery of funds
provided in the terms and conditions of both the schemes. The Committee strongly feels that a review of the functioning
of state Boards also needs to be done. They need to be strengthened and made accountable for the drawbacks being
noticed in the two schemes.   (Para 8.12)
IX.          STRENGTHENING OF PHARMACOPOEIAL LABORATORIES
The Committee is constrained to observe that the post of Director, PLIM, still remains to be filled up.   The
Committee has been given to understand that the post had to be readvertised under the directions of UPSC. The
Committee does not have much hope of further progress in the matter during the last year of the Tenth Plan. (Para 9.2) 
The physical targets and the achievement level during 2005-06 with regard to various activities undertaken by
the lab also indicate a good track record.           (Para 9.3)
The Committee finds that the building of PLIM although completed structurally is yet to be handed over by the
CPWD.   At present, the matter is being pursued vigorously with the CPWD engineers.     Statement of the Department
that the building for HPL is reportedly complete indicates that there may be some formalities to be completed in respect
of this project also.   The Committee is not aware whether the buildings for both the labs were handed over on 10 th
April, 2006 after fulfilling all mandatory requirements as assured by the CPWD.   The Committee would like to be
apprised about the final position.  The Committee would also like to know about the original estimate and the actual
expenditure of both the projects.  (Para 9.4)
X.        DEVELOPMENT OF NORTH-EAST UNDER PLAN SCHEMES OF THE DEPARTMENT
The Committee has been observing the progress of this project for quite some time.  In the beginning of every
year, nominal provision for this project is being religiously made.  The proposed allocation for the entire Tenth plan was
Rs. 5 lakhs and the beginning of last year has started without any progress whatsoever being made.  As per the latest
information furnished by the Department, the matter has been reviewed by the Minister on 4 th October 2005 in the light
of observations made by the Ministry of Finance, Department of Expenditure about the viability of   the project.  The
Committee is surprised to note that a decision has now been made to locate the Institute of Health and Medical Sciences
at Shillong and allotment of land  for the Institute has also been made.   At present, approval of CCEA is awaited.  The
Committee is not very happy with the way the project of Institute  to be set up for the benefit of NE region has been

apprehends that another plan period would perhaps be over in taking a final decision and starting the preparatory
exercise for this much needed institute.  The Committee can only emphasize that the Department should vigorously
pursue this matter with all concerned authorities.   (Para 10.2)
XI.    PERFORMANCE AUDIT OF DEPTT. OF AYUSH FOR THE PERIOD OF 2000-01 TO 2004 – 05 BY
CAG
 
The Committee notes that a number of areas which deserve to be seriously viewed have been raised in the CAG
Report.  The Secretary of the Department admitted before the Committee that most of the observations made by the
CAG were valid and exercise has been started at the Department level to take the corrective measures.  The Committee
would like to be apprised about the full details of follow up action to taken by the Department.   (Para 11.2)
XII.     USE OF ANIMAL AND MARINE PRODUCTS IN AYURVEDA, SIDHA AND UNANI DRUGS.
 
            The Committee strongly feels that the Department has to play a more pro-active rote in this sensitive area by
banning all animal products and wild honey in all preparation. Pig and cow products are likely to take on communal
colour. Other items like Ambrgris and elephant parts are internationally banned. Earthworm, animal teeth, peacock
feathers have no medicinal value and reduce the medicine to a placebo.    (Para 12.2)
XIII.    STANDARDIZATION OF NATUROPATHY COURSES
 
            The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for regulation of education of naturopathy. There are
ten-degree colleges of naturopathy in the country. But in the absence of any council having the power to regulate the
education of naturopathy, such colleges are running courses in a haphazard way, without any Central Standardization or
and any accountability whatsoever. The Committee is of the firm view that the Department should initiate immediate
action so that people are not put to any trouble. The Committee is happy to note that Department is reviewing the status
of both education and treatment aspects of naturopathy and would be coming out with a proper regulatory system in this
regard within a period of three months. The Committee would like to be apprised of the same. (Para 13.4)
XIV.            INTERNATIONAL EXCHANGE PROGRAMME
The Committee would like to have an idea about the contributions of AYUSH made by such Exchange Programme /
Conferences.  (Para 14.3)
 The Committee would like to have full details in this regard, amount of sanction, expenditure (industry-wise) users.
The Committee would also like to understand the criteria fixed for participation of drug industries in international fairs.
(Para 14.4)
 
XV.      ACQUISITION AND PUBLICATION OF TEXT BOOKS AND MANUSCRIPTS 
 
The Committee would like the Department to furnish information regarding the nodal agency involved in this
process. Details like the date of release of grant and the time given to complete the project and the selection of textbooks
and manuscript which are to be acquired should also be given.  (Para 15.2)
           
XVI.    MISCELLANEOUS
              The Committee is of the opinion that the Tibetan system of medicine should be brought within the ambit of

as to regulate and recognize it for the benefit of the people. (Para 16)
