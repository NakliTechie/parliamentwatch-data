I. INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Ministry of AYUSH has the mandate to develop AYUSH systems viz Ayurveda, 
Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa -Rigpa and Homeopathy. In keeping with the 
policy of the Government to lay focused thrust on the India Traditional Systems of Medicine, 
the Department of AYUSH was granted status of M inistry w.e.f. 9 th November, 2014.  The 
Ministry is working with a vision to position AYUSH systems as the preferred systems of 
living and practice for attaining a healthy India. 
1.2 The Ministry has identified its mission in terms of seven board thematic areas of 
AYUSH activities.  The thematic areas are as follows:- 
(i) Effective Human Resource Development 
(ii) Provision of Quality AYUSH Services 
(iii) Information, Education and Communication 
(iv) Quality Research in AYUSH 
(v) Growth of the Medical Plants Sector 
(vi) Drug Administration 
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminars/Workshops on AYUSH

2.2 The Secretary in his deposition before the Committee highlighted the fact that the 
projected requirement of the Ministry was realistic as the Ministry needed the allocation to 
tide over funds constraints.  The major areas that will be affected due to less allocation of 
funds for the year 2020-21as informed by the Ministry are as follows: 
 
(i) National AYUSH Mission (NAM) - NAM is the only Centrally Sponsored flagship 
programme of this Ministry under National Health Mission.  An amount of 
Rs.1000.00 crore was projected against which an allocation of Rs.540.00 is provided 
leaving a shortfall of Rs.460.00 crore.   
(ii) Health and We llness Centres- This Scheme is a part of Ayushman Bharat project 
being implemented through National AYUSH Mission of this Ministry. It is proposed 
to establish 12500 AYUSH Centres across the country.  Though an amount of 
Rs.150.00 crore was made available for the scheme during the year 2019-20, no funds 
could be utilized due to non approval of the Scheme. An amount of Rs.971.34 crore 
was projected for the year 2020 -21 but an amount of Rs.165.00 crores could only be 
provided within the overall resources allo cated to this Ministry leaving a shortfall of 
Rs.806.34 crore.   
(iii) Champion Sector Scheme - This is a new Scheme being implemented under the 
nodal Ministry of Commerce.  The Ministry of AYUSH is also one of the 
implementing Ministries.  Though an amount of Rs .50.00 crore was allocated during 
the year 2019-20, the same could not be utilized due to non -approval of the Scheme. 
An amount of Rs.221.17 crore was projected for the year 2020 -21 but only Rs.21.20 
crore could be provided within the overall resources pro vided to the Ministry leading 
to a shortfall of Rs.199.97 crore. 
(iv) Autonomous Bodies 
 
(a) Construction Projects- This Ministry ha d projected an amount of Rs.673.58 crore 
under GIA Capital for the completion of ongoing construction projects being 
undertaken in various National Institutes and Research Councils, out of which an 
amount of Rs.411.54 crore could only be allocated within th e available allocation of 
this Ministry. To overcome the shortage to some extent, this Ministry had separately 
sought a loan of Rs.1000/ - crore from Higher Education Funding Agency (HEFA) to 
support, in particular, the 8 construction projects of National I nstitutes.  The total 
project cost of these 8 construction projects is Rs.1628.76crore, out of which, an 

3 
 
amount of Rs.281.65 crore has already been spent out of the allocation of this 
Ministry so far.   EFC proposals for taking loan have already been submi tted to the 
Ministry of Finance and the approval is still awaited.  In case, the said amount is 
approved through HEFA as a loan, the shortfall indicated above would be reduced to 
that extent 
(b) GIA – General-In the 17 Autonomous Bodies of this Ministry , the funds allocated 
under GIA -General is utilized for Research, payment of stipends, scholarships, 
medicines, salaries of outsourced staff etc. apart from incurring day to day 
expenditures.  
Under GIA General against a projected demand of Rs.673.58 crore for 2020-21, the 
actual allocation possible within the total resources allocated to this Ministry was 
Rs.411.51 crore leading to a shortfall of Rs.262.07 crore. 
2.3 The Ministry submitted that it has made continuous and persistent efforts for adequate 
budgetary support. The efforts started during the pre -budget discussions with the Ministry of 
Finance held on 08.11.20219 followed by a meeting of Secretary (AYUSH) with Secretary 
(Expenditure) on 02.01.2020 and 30.01.2020. The Secretary (AYUSH) again took up the 
matter with the Secretary (Economic Affairs) through D.O. letter dated 02.01.2020 and even 
by personally meeting him again. Hon‘ble  MoS (IC), Ministry of AYUSH also held a 
meeting with the Finance Minister on 09.01.2020 to apprise  the situation and requested to 
enhance the allocation under RE 2019 -20 as well as BE 2020 -21.  Due to these efforts the 
Ministry of Finance increased the RE allocation from Rs.1756.83 crore to Rs.1857.00 crore.  
However, no additional funds have been provided in BE 2020-21. 
2.4 The Committee is disappointed to note that against the projected demand of Rs. 
5035.27 crore for 2020-21, the Ministry of AYUSH has been allocated Rs. 2122.08 crores 
reflecting huge shortfall of Rs. 2913.19 crores.  The budgetary es timates of 2020 -21 
when compared with RE 2019 -20 indicate a marginal increase of Rs. 265.08 crores only 
for the Ministry. The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry to get 
enhanced allocation but could not succeed . The Committee is constrai ned to note that 
several schemes of the Ministry would be affected due to shortfall of funds. The 
Committee apprehends that the objectives of important schemes like National Ayush 
Mission, Health and Wellness centres etc would not be fulfilled  due to financial 
constraints. Moreover, several research projects, construction work of National 
Institutes and Research Councils would also get affected.  The Committee takes note of 
submission of the Ministry to make up the shortfall of funds by seeking a loan of 
Rs.1000 crore from Higher Education Funding Agency (HEFA) , to support, in 
particular, the 8 construction projects of National Institutes.  The Committee is 
surprised to note that financial constraints have forced the Ministry to raise loan which 
shows that Government is not very serious towards promoting AYUSH and letting it 
emerge as an alternative and economical system of medicine . The Committee, however, 
is of the view that that the Ministry, being a relatively new one requires adequate funds  
and resources  for proving its efficacy and worth . The Committee, in its previous 
Reports, has consistently been emphasizing upon substantial increase in the allocation 

4 
 
to the Ministry for  executing its various schemes/programmes . The Committee, 
therefore, strongly, recommends the Ministry to  approach Ministry of Finance at RE  
stage with the request for additional demand so that implementation of schemes of the 
Ministry are not affected , adversely. It also recommends to the Ministry to  utilize the 
allocated funds optimally  with focus on the priority areas and make best use of the 
available funds. 
2.5 The Committee notes a difference of Rs. 1693.3 crore in the projected demand of 
Rs. 4469.59 crore in BE 2019 -20 and the demand of Rs. 2776.29 crore at the RE stage.  
This, according to the Committee, speaks volumes not only about the paradigms shift in 
its original conceived schemes but also utilization capacity of the Ministry.  The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry must make realistic assessment of 
its fund requirement  keeping in view on -going projects/projects to be undertaken  and 
accordingly project its demand.

2.11 The Committee observes that out of Rs. 1857.00 crore in RE 2019 -20, Rs. 1426.58 
crore have been utilized which is 76.82% only. Scheme s where major under -utilization is 
being witnessed are the Autonomous Bodies and the Centrally Sponsored Scheme.  

6 
 
2.12 Giving explanation for utilization being on a lower side compared to previous years, 
the Secretary, Ministry of AYUSH submitted before the Committee that the slow progress in 
expenditure is due to delay in getting the Statement of Audited Accounts (SAR) in respect of 
few Autonomous Bodies during the year which is a pre -requisite for release of funds beyond 
75% of BE. He further submitted that out of the total in RE allocation, an amount of Rs. 
288.00 crore which is 15.51 % of the RE will be available for utilization only after passing 
the 2 nd Batch Supplementary which is likely to be received in March, 2020. Hence the 
expenditure of 76.82% of the budget as on 12.02.2020 is not a true reflection of the state of 
affairs. The progress of the uti lization is being monitored on daily basis and this Ministry is 
hopeful of utilizing the entire fund allocated in RE 2019-20 by 31st March, 2020. 
2.13 Steps taken for optimum utilization during 2019-20 by the Ministry are as follow:- 
 Review meetings held by Secretary 
 Constant reassessment to locate savings to meet extra requirements 
 Supplementary demands amounts to Rs. 29.95 crore and Rs. 288.95 crore obtained / 
in being obtained in the 1st and 2nd Batch 
 Pace of expenditure and utilization constantly being monitored 
2.14 The Committee finds that the trend of utilization of budgetary allocation of the 
Ministry has been quite satisfactory till the fiscal year 2017 -18 but thereafter the 
Ministry has not been able to make optimum utilization of allocated fund.  However, the 
Committee is anguished to find that the Ministry would receive 15.51% of the RE as 
late as in March, 2020 thereby leaving a very short span of time to spend on the on -
going project/schemes. The Committee , therefore, urges upon  the Ministry to ensure 
full utilization the allocated funds so that its demand fo r additional funds is justified  at 
RE stage or from next financial year. 
 
2.15 Attention to the Committee has been drawn to details given of Budget Allocation, 
Revised Estimate and Expendi ture for the years 2018 -19 and 2019 -20 under different 
schemes including reasons for under utilization of earmarked funds.  The Committee was 
informed that for most of the schemes which witnessed under -utilization, the reason s were  
non-receipt of adequate proposals, delay in allotment of new projects, delay in completion of 
recruitment process, underutilization due to non -filling up of vacant posts and non -
finalization of construction project as reflected in Annexure A and B. 
2.16 The Committee observes that the reasons for under-utilization of allocated funds 
are mainly administrative which could have easily been avoided had there been proper 
prudential monitoring and supervision during execution of the on -going 
projects/schemes.  T he Committee is of the firm view that proper co -ordination and 
financial controlling needs to be spearheaded on all the construction activities 
undertaken so that funds are not left idle due to delays in construction causing time as 
well as cost overruns. Similarly, filing up of vacant posts in all the institutes/bodies 
under the Ministry should be accorded priority.  Also, delay or non -receipt of adequate 
proposals from stakeholders especially States/UTs for any scheme indicates that the 

7 
 
scheme needs to be restructured and incentivized to attract viable proposals for various 
schemes. The Committee would recommend the Ministry to be vigilant and monitor the 
progress of on -going activities for all the schemes so that the physical as well financial 
targets of various schemes as set in Output-Outcome Statement are accomplished.

BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
 The Committee is disappointed to note that against the projected demand of Rs. 
5035.27 crore for 2020-21, the Ministry of AYUSH has been allocated Rs. 2122.08 crores 
reflecting huge shortfall of Rs. 2913.19 crores.  The budgetary estimates of 2020 -21 
when compared with RE 2019-20 indicate a marginal increase of Rs. 265.08 crores only 
for the Ministry. The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry to get 
enhanced allocation but could not succeed. The Committee is constrained to note that 
several schemes of the Ministry would be affected due to shortfall of funds. The 
Committee apprehends that the objectives of important schemes like National Ayush 
Mission, Health and Wellness centres etc would not be fulfilled due to financial 
constraints. Moreov er, several research projects, construction work of National 
Institutes and Research Councils would also get affected.  The Committee takes note of 
submission of the Ministry to make up the shortfall of funds by seeking a loan of 
Rs.1000 crore from Higher Education Funding Agency (HEFA), to support, in 
particular, the 8 construction projects of National Institutes. The Committee is 
surprised to note that financial constraints have forced the Ministry to raise loan which 
shows that Government is not very ser ious towards promoting AYUSH and letting it 
emerge as an alternative and economical system of medicine. The Committee, however, 
is of the view that that the Ministry, being a relatively new one requires adequate funds 
and resources for proving its efficacy  and worth.  The Committee, in its previous 
Reports, has consistently been emphasizing upon substantial increase in the allocation 
to the Ministry for executing its various schemes/programmes . The Committee, 
therefore, strongly, recommends the Ministry to approach Ministry of Finance at RE 
stage with the request for additional demand so that implementation of schemes of the 
Ministry are not affected, adversely. It also recommends to the Minist ry to utilize the 
allocated funds optimally  with focus on the priority areas and make best use of the 
available funds.

and more research activities/projects . The Committee would also like to highlight that 
tangible outcomes of these researches would  incentivize the students at large willing to 
opt for Indian system of medicine. Keeping this in mind, the Committee is of the firm 
view that the allocation of budget to these Councils should be enhanced.  The 
Committee, accordingly, recommends the M inistry to persuade Ministry of Finance for 
the higher allocation of Budgetary provision  at the RE stage. 
(Para 7.7) 
 The Committee notes that the CCRUM, CCRYN, CCRH and CCRS are in the 
process of applying/obtaining accreditation from the National Accredi tation Board for 
Hospitals and healthcare providers (NABH). The Committee has been emphasizing 
upon the adherence to the minimal required parameters and other quality indicators 
which are essential for improving quality of research in particular and qualit y of 
healthcare service in general. The Committee recommends that all the Research 
Councils/Institutes must obtain NABH accreditation without much procrastination. 
(Para 7.9) 
NORTH EASTERN AREAS 
 The Committee has been consistently emphasizing on the need for increasing 
the budgetary outlay of different Institutes and Schemes under AYUSH. However, the 
Committee is dismayed to note that some of the apex AYUSH institutes which are 
envisaged for mainstreaming of AYUSH services have fared poorly in the optimum 
utilisation of its budget. The Committee notes that though the budget allocated to 
various Institutes and Schemes under the NER in FY 2018 -19 & 2019 -20 is quite low, 
the actual expenditure under these Schemes and Institutes is still below the allocated 
budget.  
(Para 8.7) 
 The Committee also observes that the budget outlay of NEIFM, which was 
established to create an interface between traditional/folk medicine practitioners and 
research in the field, has over the years reduced drastic ally. Expenditure of Rs. 0.78 
crore in 2018-19 and Rs. 0.92 crore speaks volume of the neglected state of the Institute. 
The Committee also takes note of the submission of the Ministry that the Institute has 
seen an upward trend in the inflow of the patien ts. The Committee observes that the 

32 
 
Institute has failed to fill all the sanctioned scientific posts. The Committee, therefore, 
strongly, recommends the Ministry to ensure that appointment process of all the 
sanctioned posts are completed in a time frame a nd the Institute is able to effectively 
utilise the allocated budget.  
(Para 8.8) 
 
 The Committee also notes that there has been an increase in the budget 
allocation under NAM (HWC) {Rs.17.00 crores} and NAM (GBS) [Rs.54.00 crores] in 
BE 2020-2021. The Committee strongly recommends that the Budget allocated must be 
optimally utilised and the envisioned aim of co -location of AYUSH facilities at 
PHCs/CHCs/DHs under NAM must be fulfilled. 
(Para 8.9) 
 The Committee believes that North East India has the potenti al to strengthen 
the AYUSH system in the country. Considering its rich biodiversity , the Committee 
calls upon the Ministry to make concerted efforts to explore the rich resource of 
medicinal plants in the region. The Committee also believes that the servic es of the 
AYUSH practitioners can be successfully utilised in improving the delivery of quality 
healthcare services in the North East. The Committee believes that AYUSH can emerge 
as a reliable healthcare treatment and provide an alternative medicine syste m to a 
region that is poorly connected with the mainland India.  
(Para 8.10)
