REPORT
INTRODUCTION
1.1 The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare is instrumental and responsible for
implementation of various programmes at the national level in the areas of Health and Family
Welfare, prevention and control of major communicable diseases and promotion of traditional and
indigenous system of medicines. It also assists the States in preventing and controlling the spread
of seasonal disease outbreaks and epidemics through technical assistance.
1.2 The Ministry incurs expenditure either directly under Central Schemes through its three
Departments i.e. Department of Health and Family Welfare, Department of AYUSH and Department
of Health Research (Swasthya Anusandhan Vibhag), including the attached offices of DGHS and its
various subordinate offices, or by way of grants-in-aids to the autonomous/ statutory bodies and
NGOs. In addition to the fully funded Centrally Sponsored Family Welfare Programmes, the
Ministry is implementing several World Bank assisted programmes for control of AIDS, Malaria,
Tuberculosis, Disease Surveillance etc. in designated areas. Besides, State Health Systems
Development Projects with World Bank assistance are under implementation in various states. The
projects are implemented by the respective State Governments and the Department of Health and
Family Welfare only facilitates the States in availing of external assistance. All these schemes aim
at fulfilling the national commitment to improve access to Primary health care facilities keeping in
view the needs of rural areas and where the incidence of disease is high.
1.3 Explaining the trend of growth of healthcare in the country, the Secretary informed that
Health Sector has witnessed significant changes over the last decade. Health indicators showed a
discernible improvement with the Maternal Mortality Ratio, which was 327 per 1,00,000 live births
in 1999-2001 improving to 301 during 2001-2003 (SRS, 2005). Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)
recorded an improvement from 68 (SRS-2000) to 57 (SRS-2006). Total Fertility Rate showed some
progress, declining from 3.2 (SRS 2000) to 2.9 (SRS 2004).The latest National Family Health
Survey 2005-06 (NFHS-III) and District Level Household Surveys 2003-04 reveal that there is
upward movement in the provision of ante-natal care, institutional deliveries and post-natal care.
The percentage of pregnant women who received antenatal care was 76.9 in 2005-06 as against
65.8% in 1998-99. The NFHS-III survey indicates that 50.7 per cent of the pregnant women
had visited health care institutions for at least three antenatal care visits in 2005-06 as against
44.2 per cent in 1998-99. The State wise picture, however, shows that the developments in the
health indicators do not show a uniform pattern. During 2001-03, MMR was highest in Uttar
Pradesh including Uttaranchal (517) and lowest in Kerala (110). Two out of three maternal deaths
are reported from 8 EAG States and Assam. In comparison with SRS 1990-01, SRS 2001-03
indicates that the MMR has declined in all the States except Assam where it has shown an increase
from 398 to 490 per 1,00,000 live births. However, only 40.8 per cent of deliveries took place in
health facilities during the period.
1.4 The National Common Minimum Programme (NCMP) recognized healthcare as one of the
seven thrust areas for development and mandated the need for enhancing public health expenditure
to atleast 2-3% of GDP. The proposed outlay in the Eleventh Five-year plan for Health sector in
the Central sector is Rs.1,36,147 crores, against an expenditure of Rs.41,262 crores during Tenth
plan, registering an astounding increase of 229%. For the year 2008-09, the total outlay of the

2
Department of Health and Family Welfare increased by 14% to Rs.16,000 crores out of which the
NRHM component is 74.56% at Rs.11,930 crores. The balance outlay of Rs.4070 crores is
towards Health (Rs.3650 crores) and Health Research (Rs.420 crores). Under NRHM, the
major schemes in terms of allocations are Mission Flexipool (Rs.2285 crores), RCH Flexipool
(Rs.2535 crores), Maintenance of Infrastructure (Rs.3088 crores) and Disease Control Programmes
(Rs.1146.25 crores). In the Health Sector, an allocation of Rs.1100 crores has been made for HIV/
AIDS, Rs.373 crores for Human Resources, Rs. 490 crores for Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Suraksha
Yojana and for Redevelopment of Hospitals and Institutions, Rs.550 crores.
1.5 The financial envelop provided under the flexible pool will enable the States to develop the
required capacities in deficit areas and provide funds on the basis of needs rather than a strict
schematic format in terms of programme implementation. This would also provide the States
operational support for institutional strengthening and effectively designing programmes relating to
maternal and child health with clear pro-outcome and pro-poor focus. On the. health side, the
emphasis will be on developing human resources and evolving national programmes for healthcare
of the elderly and prevention and control of diabetes, cardiovascular diseases and stroke etc.
Another important initiative would be the National Urban Health Mission for the benefit of urban
poor families, slum dwellers, migrant workers and extremely vulnerable population who would be
identified by the Urban Local Bodies. Synergies are envisioned with schemes within and outside
health sector for maximizing benefits. A number of initiatives on specific programmes like National
Organ Transplant Programme, Oral Health, Prevention and Control of Fluorosis, National Advisory
Boards for standards, National programme for Blood and Blood Products etc. are proposed to
be taken up during the Eleventh Five Year Plan. Some of these schemes will be initiated during
2008-09.

II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1 The Ministry had projected a demand for Rs.21210.05 crores for the Department of Health
and Family Welfare for the Annual Plan 2008-09 to the Planning Commission. As against the
proposed outlay, Planning Commission approved an outlay of Rs.15580.00 crores for the
Department out of which the NRHM component is Rs.11930.00 crores. For Health Programmes
outside NRHM, the approved outlay is Rs.3650.00 crores, which is about 63% of the projected
outlay requirement of Rs.5761.49 crores. Out of this amount, Rs.1592.00 crores have been
allocated to the Central Sector Schemes and Rs.2058 crores to Centrally Sponsored Programmes.
2.2 The Committee is informed that this reduction in outlay is likely to affect implementation
of a number of ongoing schemes like Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Suraksha Yojana (PMSSY),
Redevelopment of Hospitals and Institutions etc. Some new initiatives like Urban Health Mission and
Health Care for Elderly may also require additional funds during the current year.
2.3 The Committee observes that Oversight Committee has been granted only Rs.100.00
crores against last year’s approved outlay of Rs. 600 crores, which, however, could not be
utilized. In the light of the recent Supreme Court’s judgment and the Government’s
propensity towards implementing it from this year itself, the Committee opines that
the funds would not be sufficient. Similarly against proposal of Rs.453.00 crores, only
Rs.329.00 have been provided to CGHS; against projected requirement of Rs.87.88 crores,
Safdarjung Hospital has received Rs.50.00 crores; Dr. RML Hospital has been allocated
Rs.59.20 crores against projected Plan requirement of Rs. 72.11 crores and Rs.68.95 crores
against Non-Plan requirement of Rs.71.88 crores; AIIMS has been granted Rs.452.00 crores
in Grant-in-Aids against projected requirement of Rs.753.00 crores and against the projected
total requirement of JIPMER for Rs.150.80 crores, it has received only Rs.98.00 crores. The
Committee observes that under provisioning in many of the Institutes is likely to adversely
affect the completion of construction and developmental activities undertaken by them and
in the process of the Institutions.
2.4 The Committee notes that the overall allocation for the health sector (outside
NRHM) has been on the higher side. Still, the Committee is constrained to note that the
Department has failed to project its budgetary requirements in an effective way for the
simple reason that the utilization of funds during the Tenth Plan is far from satisfactory
which is evident from the fact that the sanctioned BE for 2005-06 and 2006-07
was Rs.4253.84 crores and Rs.3564.95 crores, respectively, which was reduced to
Rs.4096.25 crores and Rs.3433.02 crores, respectively at the RE stage. The actual
expenditure figures of Rs.2907.96 crores and Rs.3377.34 crores were even less than the
budgeted allocations at the RE stage. The Committee notes that this trend has continued
during 2007-08 also. This goes to illustrate that the Department’s record in utilization of the
allocated funds is below par. The Committee would, therefore, recommend the Department
to first ensure optimum and judicious utilization of the funds allocated instead of
highlighting the reduced budgetary allocation. The Committee also recommends to the
Department to take substantive steps so that the stumbling blocks in the utilization of
sanctioned funds for 2008-09 for a crucial sector like health, are removed.

2.6 Committee was informed that by the end of financial year 2007-08, Department was able
to utilize 90 per cent of allocated funds under RE 2007-08 and the figure was expected to rise
once utilization certificates are received. The Committee would like to point out that
expenditure reported upto 29th February, 2008 was only Rs.1608.59 crores and within a span

utilization is taking place in the last quarter of the year.
2.7 Committee observes that in spite of its raising concerns over this trend of under
utilization of funds year after year, there does not seem to be any noticeable improvement
in this regard. Time and again the Committee has been advocating for higher allocation of
funds for this sector and this time the Committee is happy to note that the approved outlay

concern and recommends to the Department to play a more pro-active role in identifying
the problems afflicting the utilization of funds in the Health Sector and initiate remedial
measures from the very beginning of the current financial year so that the old story of
revising estimates downwards and subsequent under utilization does not recur.

of slow pace of expenditure on implementation of schemes like Pradhan Mantri Swasthya
Suraksha Yojana (PMSSY), Cancer, Mental Health, Capacity Building Project for Food and
Drugs, non-implementation of the recommendations of the Oversight Committee, New
Initiatives, and also non-finalizations of proposals relating to procurement of various
equipments, non-filling up of vacant posts, non-receipt of proposals from State Government/
Implementing Agencies etc. The Committee recommends the Department to keep the
activities of these schemes/institutions under constant monitoring so as to arrest this trend
of under utilization.
2.9 The pendency level of Utilization Certificates has been engaging the attention of the
Committee for the last few years. According to latest figures as on 1st March, 2008, there
were as many as 1318 Utilization Certificates in respect of Health Sector amounting to
Rs.677.61 crores for the grants released upto 2005-06. On a specific query regarding any
plans of the Department to reach a justifiable level of pendency, the Committee is given
to understand that a communication was sent by the Chief Controller of Accounts (CCA)
to all the States through Resident Commissioners on this issue giving full details of pending
UCs in respect of the Department of Health and Family Welfare. The Committee is
dismayed to note that in the name of efforts; only a letter has been issued. The Committee
feels that spirited efforts, which are apparently lacking, need to be made on priority basis
to clear the UCs which are pending for the last three decades or so. The Committee also

recommends to the Department to take up the case with concerned State Governments at
the highest level so as to assess whether the funds have been utilized properly or not.
2.10 In response to another specific query regarding audit of various Programmes/Bodies, the
Committee is given to understand that a fresh proposal will be submitted to take up the audit of
all centrally sponsored programmes, PSUs and autonomous bodies under the Department. As per
GFR 211 (1), the Grantee Bodies like autonomous bodies, PSU etc. receiving Grant-in-aid are to
be audited by Principal Accounts Office of the Ministry and C and AG and for this purpose a
provision is being incorporated in the Sanction. The Committee has been informed by the
Department that it has taken steps to strengthen its Internal Audit Wing also.
2.11 The Committee welcomes the proposal of the Department to introduce a provision in
the Sanction itself regarding audit of all centrally sponsored programmes, PSUs under the
Department and autonomous bodies which are receiving Grant-in-Aid. The Committee
recommends to the Department to expedite the same and intimate the progress made in
this regard.
2.12 The Committee is given to understand that the Department proposes to undertake the audit
of more centrally sponsored health programmes and PSUs and autonomous bodies during the
Financial Year 2008-09. Besides the names of Bodies/Programmes which would be covered
during 2008-09, the Committee would like to be informed about the centrally sponsored
health programmes, PSUs and autonomous bodies which were audited during 2007-08 along
with the adverse audit findings and the action taken by the Department thereon.
2.13 The Committee’s attention has been drawn by high level of pendency of Audit paras
contained in CAG Report and Internal Audit on matters relating to the Department. The
Committee is surprised to note that there are 62 pending Audit paras dating back from
CAG Report No.2 of 1998 to the latest CAG Report No. 20 of 2007 on the Performance Audit
of procurement of medicines and medical equipment. The Committee fails to understand the
reasons for such high level of pendency. The very fact that such a large number of Audit
paras remain to be reconciled indicates that internal financial management of the Ministry
is weak. The Committee would like to be apprised about the corrective measures taken in
the light of the pending Audit paras.
III. CENTRAL GOVERNMENT HEALTH SCHEME (CGHS)
3.1 CGHS, a welfare scheme of the Central Government, was started in 1954 and takes care
of the medical needs of the Central Government employees (serving and pensioners), sitting and
Ex-MPs, Judges of the Supreme Court/High Court (sitting and retired), Freedom Fighters (free of
cost), Ex-Governors, Ex-Vice Presidents of India, employees of the semi autonomous bodies/semi
Government Organisations, Accredited Journalists, serving and pensioner employees of a few
autonomous organisations in Delhi and the cities where CGHS is in operation. It provides medical
services-both inpatient and outpatient care including diagnostic and investigations at the level of
dispensaries, Central/State Government Hospitals, recognised private hospitals/diagnostic centres,
dispensaries/units under ISM and H, regional cancer centre, polyclinics to its beneficiaries. As per
the latest figures, there are 9.11 lakh CGHS cardholders and 33.01 lakh beneficiaries.
3.2 Plan allocation of Rs.50.00 crores and Non-Plan allocation of Rs.329.00 crores has been
made for CGHS for 2008-09. The Committee notes that Plan allocation for 2008-09 is at an
enhanced level when compared with provision of Rs.40.00 crores made for 2007-08. As per
the Outcome Budget (2008-09), against a Plan allocation of Rs.40.00.crores for 2007-08
under CGHS; expenditure reported upto 29.02.2008 is only Rs.19.08 crores. The Committee
is, however, surprised to note that in response to a specific query made by it, utilization of

6
Rs.32.16 crores was reported upto February, 2008. The Committee is unhappy with the
conflicting figures of utilization of Plan funds. Either the information published in the
Outcome Budget is incorrect or inaccurate status report has been furnished to the
Committee, that too on a specific query made by it. Both the positions compel the
Committee to conclude that there is a need for better management at the Department
level. The Committee apprehends that chances are there that major utilization under CGHS
has taken place during the last quarter of the year. If it is true, this indicates improper
handling of the funds. The trend of utilization of major chunk of funds during the last
quarter is not a healthy sign. The Committee would like to be apprised about utilization of
Plan and Non-Plan funds (quarter-wise) during 2007-08. In the light of last year’s experience
of utilization of funds, the Committee recommends that concerted efforts need to be made
for optimum and judicious utilization of enhanced allocation made for 2008-09.
3.3 The Committee has been given to understand that against a proposal for allocation of
Rs.453 crores submitted under Non-Plan in BE 2008-09, an allocation of Rs. 329 crores has been
made, which is lower when compared to provision of Rs. 368.00 crores in RE 2007-08. The
Committee has been given to understand that additional Non-Plan funds were meant for salary for
vacant posts likely to be filled up during the year and also for payments to the MSDs, local
chemists etc. The Committee, therefore, recommends that allocation of funds under Non-
Plan be enhanced adequately so that the services available to the CGHS beneficiaries are
not affected.
3.4 The computerization of CGHS dispensaries and offices in Delhi was taken up at an
expenditure of Rs.8.74 crores. The Committee was informed that as on 26.3.2008, 83
dispensaries in Delhi have been fully computerized and the process of computerization of
remaining 4 dispensaries was under way. The Committee would like to point out that
computerization process of CGHS dispensaries in Delhi could not be completed as scheduled.
Originally targeted to be completed by 2006-07, revised date of 17th August, 2007 could also
not be adhered to. By the end of financial year, 2007-08, 4 dispensaries in Delhi still
remained to be computerized. Computerization of CGHS dispensaries located in Mumbai,
Kolkata, Chennai, Hyderabad, Nagpur and Pune to be taken up in 2007-08 as scheduled, is
yet to start. The Committee can imagine the status of third phase slated to be completed
in 2008-09. The Committee, keeping in view the past experience in respect of Delhi
dispensaries, can only emphasize that all steps need to be taken to complete the
computerization of dispensaries outside Delhi at the earliest. Corrective measures based on
the problem areas noticed in the computerization of dispensaries in Delhi need to be taken.
3.5 The Committee is given to understand that the feedback received from the officials
regarding computerization is very encouraging while there has been a mixed reaction from the
beneficiaries. The Committee would like to point out that situation is some what different.
Problems are being faced both by the CGHS functionaries and beneficiaries. Technicians,
junior staff manning counters in dispensaries and attending doctors need to be fully trained.
Practical problems like internet and electricity failure have to be tackled by having an
alternative arrangement. These initial teething problems should not be allowed to be
prolonged. At present, beneficiaries which include senior citizens also are being made to
wait for hours at the dispensaries. Their ordeal starts right from the registration counter
and continues upto delivery of medicines. The Committee would appreciate if all the
measures including training, smooth running of computers with full connectivity, backup for
power failure are taken at the earliest and also closely monitored.
3.6 The Committee has been emphasizing for long for introduction of two shift system
in CGHS. The Committee does not appreciate the contention of the Ministry that a large

7
number of private hospitals and diagnostic centres have been empanelled under the CGHS
and in case of emergency, beneficiaries can go there for treatment and claim re-
imbursement later. The Committee would like to draw the attention of the Ministry once
again to the fact that idea behind having two shifts in CGGS dispensaries is the
inconvenience being faced by the CGHS beneficiaries, majority of them office goers in going
to the dispensaries in the morning hours. The Committee is disturbed by the element of
helplessness shown by the Ministry in their candid admission of staff side vehemently
opposing the move. In this era where the service providers like banks etc. are opting for
flexible timings to give their customers maximum possible services, at least the Ministry
could have come forward with a positive and proactive approach. The Committee would
reiterate its recommendation made last year for starting two shifts in a few dispensaries as
a test case. One must not forget that CGHS is a service oriented scheme, supposedly
providing health care mainly to our serving employees and pensioners. It is the
responsibility of the Ministry to make the CGHS employees understand and not to be
pressurized by them.
3.7 The Committee’s attention has also been drawn by the problems being faced by a very large
number of CGHS beneficiaries residing in Dwarka sub-city in Delhi. At present, these beneficiaries
have to travel 10-12 Kms. for medical treatment at the nearest dispensary located in Manglapuri,
Palam. For pathological tests they have to travel even further to Janakpuri Dispensary. The
Committee was informed that approval for opening of CGHS dispensary in Dwarka has been
received and work would start soon. The Committee recommends that construction of
dispensary in Dwarka where land is already available should start at the earliest. All
procedural formalities should be expedited and completion of project strictly monitored.
3.8 The Committee finds that in-principle approval for opening of new CGHS dispensaries
in Sector 82, Noida and Gurgaon was given by the Planning Commission way back on
14th March, 2006.  The Committee is surprised to note that even after two years, suitable
accommodation for housing the dispensary in Sector 82, Noida and Gurgaon could not be
managed. Secretary (Health) Government of Haryana is now being requested to allot a
building for CGHS Dispensary in Gurgaon. Proposal for housing the CGHS Dispensary in
Community Centre, Sector 82, Noida on rent-basis is under consideration. The Committee
strongly feels that the Ministry should pursue the two proposals in hand so that large
number of CGHS beneficiaries residing in these two areas do not continue to remain
deprived of health care any more.
3.9 The Committee notes that CGHS continues to suffer from manpower shortage. Against 84
sanctioned SAG posts, 74 posts are lying vacant. The latest figures supplied by the Department
show that 495 vacancies are there in the grade of GDMO alone. The Committee has been given
to understand that on the basis of CMSE 2006, 125 candidates had been offered appointment and
pre-appointment formalities for 175 candidates were under different stages of process. Based on
CMSE 2007 , dossiers of 176 candidates had been received and were being processed. As regards
vacancies in technical posts, after necessary approval, advertisement has been issued. Requisition
for stenographers and LDCs had been placed with SSC. Action for vacant promotional posts had
been initiated through DPCs. Further after a long but avoidable delay, the Department had finally
started implementing the SIU report. Accordingly, all surplus posts identified in cities outside Delhi
have been abolished and action for creation of posts has been initiated for obtaining approval of
the Ministry of Finance. The Committee recommends to the Department to take the issue on
priority with the Ministry of Finance to complete all the procedural formalities so that
necessary posts are created and subsequently filled up. The Committee would also like to
be apprised about the cadre strength created as a result of implementation of SIU report
and the efforts taken so far to fill up the vacant posts.

8
3.10 The Committee’s attention has also been drawn by a very large number of vacancies in
Group ‘C’ and ‘D’ categories. Out of 3022 category ‘C’ sanctioned posts, 568 posts were vacant,
with Delhi alone having the maximum vacancies of 292. Similarly, there were 550  vacant posts
against 2934 sanctioned Group ‘D’ posts, with Delhi having the maximum vacancies of 325.
Besides that, staff other than medical officers was being out sourced/engaged on contract basis in
Dehradun, Bhubaneswar, Ranchi, Chandigarh, Shillong and Bhopal. The Committee understands
that third party administrators for processing of claims and medical audit are also proposed
to be introduced. The Committee has been given to understand that as recommended by
SIU, Department of Expenditure, vacancies arising in Group ‘D’ posts are to be
progressively outsourced.
3.11 The Committee views with serious concern the growing number of vacancies in
different categories of posts in CGHS dispensaries. The Committee understands that all
surplus posts identified in cities outside Delhi have been abolished. It has also been
informed that the staff strength in the dispensaries is decided as per the workload. The
very fact that at the present juncture, no CGHS city can be stated to have full complement
of the staff strength as per the SID norms establishes that the existing dispensaries are not
functioning as envisaged. The Committee also strongly feels that outsourcing/hiring of staff
on contract-basis on a big scale is a remedy worse than the disease. The concept of public
private partnership cannot be applied across the board specially in a set-up meant for
providing health care. The Committee would like to have a status note on the sanctioned
posts and vacancies, category-wise and region-wise in CGHS dispensaries.
3.12 To a specific query of the Committee as to payment of consultation fees to other agencies
owing to new fulfillment of needs of various indenters by Medical Stores Organization (M.S.O.),
it was informed that M/S HSCC alone was paid Rs.9.03 crores towards consultancy fees. The
Committee recommends that all out efforts be made for reduction in the consultation fees
to 2% for procurement of drugs/medicines for CGHS as assured by the Ministry and the
Committee be informed accordingly.
3.13 The Committee notes that as there is no private hospital empanelled under CGHS for
treatment of cancer patients in Delhi only adhoc arrangements are in place. Accordingly, cancer
patients could get treatment from Apollo Hospital, New Delhi, Batra Hospital, New Delhi,
Dharamshila Cancer Hospital, Delhi and Rajiv Gandhi Cancer and Research Institute, Delhi upto
31st March, 2008. The Committee is happy to note that these  adhoc  arrangements would
continue until further orders. The Committee would, however, like to emphasize that

14.2 In order to provide immediate financial assistance to the extent of Rs.50,000/-per case, to

Family Welfare as the Chairman. During the year 2006-07, financial assistance to the tune of
Rs. 305.35 lakhs was given directly to 234 patients under Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi.

XV. NATIONAL URBAN HEALTH MISSION (NUHM)
15.1 As per 2001 census, 28.6 crores people live in urban areas, out of which 3.26 crores live
in slum areas of the cities/towns having a population of 1 lakh and above, which is expected to
reach 5.01 crores by 2007-08, thus putting greater strain on urban health infrastructure which
already has serious deficiencies. For urban poor, access to Urban Health facilities is severely
restricted due to their being ‘crowded out’ because of the inadequacy of the public health delivery
system; lack of economic resources inhibits/restricts their access to private facilities and lack of
standards and norms for urban health delivery, illegal status, poor environmental condition, over
crowding and environmental pollution makes the urban poor more vulnerable and worse off than
his rural counterpart. During 2008-09, it is proposed to launch the National Urban Health Mission
(NUHM) to address primarily health needs of these segments of population. This will complement
the activities undertaken by NRHM in rural areas. The Programme will be on Public-Private
Partnership mode.
15.2 All Cities with population above 1 lakh and State Capitals would be covered under it. 430
cities with a population of 22 crores with focus on 6.25 crores slum population would benefit.
Duration of the Mission is Eleventh Plan (2008-2012). Urban Poor families living in listed and
unlisted slums, un-authorized colonies and all other vulnerable population such as homeless, rag-
pickers, street children, rickshaw pullers, construction and brick kiln workers, sex workers and
any other temporary migrants would be identified by the Urban Local Body (ULB) concerned or
any other State specific mechanism and shall be issued a photo Family Health Card. Though, no
person would be denied service in the public health facilities, marginalized urban families identified
under the scheme would receive service on concession terms under National Urban Health Mission
(NUHM). Synergies are envisioned with Jawaharlal Nehru Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM),
SJSRY , Integrated Child Development Scheme (ICDS) Swarna Jayanti Sharkari Rozgar Yojana
(SJSRY), etc. and with the ongoing National Health Programmes for maximizing benefits.
15.3 The Committee notes that the programme has been assigned an approved outlay of
Rs. 4495.00 crores during the Eleventh Plan out of which Rs.50.00 crores are earmarked for
2008-09.

XVI. NATIONAL AIDS CONTROL PROGRAMME (NACP)
16.1 The AIDS epidemic has emerged as one of the communicable diseases of immediate
concern. Based on the estimated prevalence rate of 0.36% (0.27% - 0.47%) it is presumed that
there are 2 to 3.1 million HIV infected persons in the country. Adult HIV prevalence is greater than
1 in Manipur (1.68%), Nagaland (1.27%) and Andhra Pradesh (1.06%). Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka,
Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu contribute 63% of the HIV infected persons in the country. Around
39% of the infections are in women and 3.8% in children. Out of the reported AIDS cases in 2007
(Jan-Oct 2007), 87.4% of the infections were transmitted through the sexual route and peri-natal
transmission accounted for 4.7% of infections. 1.8% and 1.7% of infections were acquired through
injecting drug use and contaminated blood and blood products respectively. The HIV prevalence
among high risk groups continues to be nearly 6 to 8 times greater than that among the general
population.
16.2 The National AIDS Control Programme accounts for about 9% of our national health
budget. NACO is scaling up its programmes through Targeted Interventions (TI) for High Risk
Groups, strategizing comprehensive Information, Education and Communication (lEC) packages for
specific segments and scaling up of the service delivery component.
16.3 The Committee was informed that the States of Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra have shown
a decline over a period of time and have recorded less than 1 % HIV prevalence in ANC clinic
attendees. There is decline in the HIV prevalence among ANC clinic attendees in most of the high
burden states, reflecting the impact of interventions. Similar trends are seen in Female Sex Workers
also. However, there are indications of increase in HIV prevalence among ANC population in some
of the low prevalence States. New pockets of HIV infection among ID users have been detected
in States like West Bengal, Punjab, Orissa and Tamil Nadu. The dual mode of transmission has been
observed in North Eastern States. Based on HIV/AIDS prevalence, 156 districts have been classified
in Category ‘A’ and 39 districts in Category ‘B’, which are focus of the programme interventions.
16.4 Currently, there are 571 SACS-funded TIs for FSWs/MSMs/IDUs with a coverage of 42%
and 165 TIs covering bridge populations (migrants/truckers), resulting in a total of 736 TIs. In
addition, by March 2008, it is expected to put in place 284 new TIs. During 2008-09 there will
be a scale up of 53% of core TIs which will ensure 85% coverage as per the present estimates
for core population. The scale up for Non-core TIs will be 58% during 2008-09 which would entail
the setting up of 308 TIs for Commercial sex workers and MSMs, 46 for Injecting Drug Users,
100 for Migrants and 106 for the Truckers totaling 506 interventions.

training of doctors on second line treatment; establishing laboratories for viral load estimation which
is central to facilitate second line therapy; developing technical and operational guidelines for
providing second line treatment and clinical diagnosis and monitoring tools. The TRG further
recommended that above protocols must be in place before the rollout as drug resistance to second
line therapy has no further recourse and therefore great care needs to be taken to ensure that
patients, to the extent possible, are delayed being put on second line therapy. Complete adherence
to the second line is a must in order to prevent progression of the disease. The necessary
preparatory activities for roll out have now been done such as preparation of technical documents,
monitoring tools and handouts, counselling mat erial, job aids and patient education material. A team
of doctors from two centres was sent to Bangkok for hands-on experience on second line ART.
Drugs for second line ART are being supplied by Clinton Foundation which has an agreement with
UNIT AID, an international non-profit organization which raises funds by collecting tax on airlines
for funding HIV/AIDS treatment. As per regulatory mechanism developed, the patient selection for
second line will be done by a special panel of experts constituted by NACO. A SACEP (S tate AIDS
Clinical Expert Panel) has been established at state level. The panel consists of ART centre in
charge, another expert from outside the centre and programme person from the SACs. The panel
will meet periodically to review the records of patients failing on first line and decide on second
line drugs for these patients. The panel shall ensure that a very strict adherence protocol is
maintained for patients on second line ART and shall monitor them closely for adverse effects.
Hence, the second line roll out has been done at two sites initially-GHTM, Chennai and JJ Hospital,
Mumbai on a pilot basis and it shall be expanded later to other centres based on the experience
gained. The patients will be followed up very strictly and home visit shall also be made in case
of default to treatment. At NACO, the data from both centres is being collected about patient
evaluated for second line, tested for viral load and actually initiated on second line at these two
centres.
16.10 The Committee is happy to note that though delayed, the Department has taken the
desired step towards starting the second line of treatment to lessen the sufferings of drug
resistant patients. The Committee would like to recommend to the Department to put in
place an effective mechanism so that no patient in need of second line ART is deprived of
it.

delivery system and also setting up of teaching institutions in the medical as well as para medical

• A three tiered structure consisting of a one year diploma course, a three year B.Sc.

NATIONAL RURAL HEALTH MISSION (NRHM)
AND FAMILY WELFARE PROGRAMMES
1. The National Rural Health Mission was operationalised in April 2005 throughout the country.
The Mission covers the rural areas of the entire country with special focus on 18 States viz.
8 Empowered Action Group States (Bihar, Jharkhand, M.P., Chhattisgarh, U.P., Uttarakhand, Orissa
and Rajasthan) 8 North-East States, Himachal Pradesh and Jammu and Kashmir. The main objective
under the Mission is to provide universal access to equitable, affordable and quality healthcare
delivery, especially to the poor and the vulnerable sections of the society, bridge gaps in healthcare,
facilitate decentralized planning in the health sector, ensure population stabilization, gender balance
and bring about inter-sectoral convergence. The NRHM also provides an overarching umbrella to
the existing programmes of Health and Family Welfare including RCH-II, V ector Borne Disease
Control Programme, TB, Leprosy, NPCB and the Integrated Disease Surveillance Project.
Mainstreaming of AYUSH is also one of the strategies under the Mission.

III. INFRASTRUCTURE STRENTHENING
3.1 NRHM aims at strengthening of Primary Health Infrastructure and improving effective
service delivery across the country. Sub-centers, Primary Healthcare Centers and Community
Health Centres are the three pillars on which rural infrastructure is built upon. As on March. 2007,
there are 1,45,272 Sub-Centers, 22,370 PHCs and 4045 CHCs in the country. There is a shortfall
of 20,855 Sub-Centres, 6491 PHCs and 2525 CHCs in the country based on the 2001 population
norms. The Committee has been constantly drawing the attention of the Department to the uneven
spread of these centres across the country with prominent gaps in the High Focus States. The
Committee finds that there is no marked improvement in this regard.
The Committee has been expressing its serious concern on the continuance of lack of rural
health infrastructure, especially in the High Focus States where the need is the most. On being
asked to identify the problem areas, the Committee has been informed that health infrastructure and
allocation of health workers under NRHM is planned on the population, workload and distance
norms. Shortages on the basis of 2001 population are quantified as the difference between the
required number of facilities/providers as per the norms and those that are functioning or in
position. This method of determining norms and shortage does not take into account the varying
epidemiological, geographical and socio-demographic patterns across the different States!
Communities and between rural and urban areas within States. The actual availability of different
types of providers or the extent of private/voluntary sector involvement in health care delivery at
the community level is also not taken into account. The Committee notes that against the backdrop
of these very visible deficiencies, norms for setting up of the Sub-Centres and PHCs have been
modified by the Steering Committee of the Planning Commission. These norms to be adopted
during the Eleventh Plan have been duly approved by the Planning Commission also. The
Committee has been given to understand that population-centric norms are replaced with
flexible norms comprising habitation-based needs, community based needs and disease-pattern
based needs.
3.2 The Committee welcomes the modification in the criteria for assessing the
requirement of health infrastructure in the country. In a vast country like ours, rigid
norms based on population only and made uniformly applicable were perhaps not proving to
be justified and not based on ground realities. The Committee is also of the view that
change in the norms for health infrastructure proposed by the Planning Commission was
called for. The Committee is, however, not aware whether any preparatory exercise has been
initiated for conducting surveys as per the revised norms. The Committee is afraid that if
this issue is allowed to linger on for long, the very objective of NRHM of bridging gaps in
the health care will continue to be a distant dream. The Committee, accordingly, impresses
upon the Department to initiate action at the earliest.

ASHA
3.11 One of the most important strategic interventions under the National Rural Health Mission
is ASHA—a social health activist. ASHA is envisaged as a trained woman community health
volunteer who will reinforce community action for universal immunization, safe delivery, new born
care, prevention of water borne disease and communicable disease, improved nutrition and
promotion of household toilets. She is required to inform, interact, mobilize and facilitate improved
access to preventive and promotive healthcare and also provide basic curative care through her
drug kits. She will assist in formulation of village health plan by the Village Health and Sanitation
Committee. ASHA will also promote awareness and usage of ISM. The requirement for ASHA is
one per thousand population with relaxation to this norm depending upon the workload and terrain.
ASHA is envisaged to be a primary resident of the village who would be selected by the Gram
Sabha following an intense community mobilization process. She will be fully accountable to the
Panchayats. ASHA has been a major intervention for the ten high focus States namely Uttar
Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Bihar, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Orissa, Rajasthan, Assam
and Jammu and Kashmir.

V. NATIONAL T.B. CONTROL PROGRAMME
5.1 T.B is a major health problem in India. India accounts for 1/5th of the global incidence of
T.B. cases. As per the latest estimates, there are approximately 18 lakh new cases every year in
the country. Two persons die from T.B. in India every three minutes, more than 1000 people every
day, and almost 3,70,000 every year.
5.2 To control this major health problem of the country, Revised National TB Control Programme
is being implemented. Phase-II of the project (period October, 2005 to September, 2011) has
a total outlay of Rs.1,156 crores which includes credit from World Bank of Rs.756 crores and
commodity assistance of anti-TB drugs from DFID through WHO for Rs. 287 crores with balance
of Rs.191 crores being given by GoI. In addition, Rs.385.5 crores through GFATM and USAID
have also been provided. Phase-II of RNTCP is a step towards achieving the TB related Millennium
Development Goal (MDG) targets. DOTS remain the core strategy. The Department has informed
that the total allocation under the Programme during 2007-08 was to the tune of Rs.267 crores
with expenditure of Rs.237.20 crores reported upto 29th February, 2008.
5.3 The Committee notes that around 4,03,917 patients have been put on treatment during 2nd
quarter of 2007 saving around 65,000 lives. More than 1,20,000 patients are being put on DOTS
every month. One of the major initiatives taken is the involvement of 2421 NGOs and 15,900
private practitioners working under the programme under Government approved NGO/ PP
Guidelines. Over 240 medical colleges are participating in the programme. Besides that, all the 16
centrally owned ESI Hospitals, Zonal Railway Hospitals, Coal, Steel and Mines Health Facilities,

The Committee appreciates the successful implementation of RNTCP Phase-I and
ongoing Phase-II. Several initiatives/interventions taken at different levels and variety of
implementing agencies have contributed to the increasing population coverage and 85% cure
rate. However, India still contributes 20% of TB cases reported in the world. Out of 8.8
million cases per annum, 1.8 million cases are from India alone. New complex off shoots of
TB also keep on emerging, the most visible being worsening HIV epidemic increasing the
number of TB cases. The Committee notes that HIV is the most potent risk factor for
progression of TB infection to disease form. HIV-TB patients detected in 2005 were 12,062,
13,614 in 2006 and 18,559 in 2007. The Committee is aware that increase in number of
co-infected (HIV-TB) patients being detected over the last three years is a result of innovative
and co-ordinated measures taken by the Department. But the other dimension of this trend

99
is increasing complexities involved in the treatment of such cases. The Committee hopes that
the National Framework formulated by RNTCP and NACP for joint TB/HIV collaborative
activities will lead to better and effective control of this emerging problem area. (Para 5.4)
The Committee observes that the other major challenge to TB control in the country
is increasing number of MDR- TB cases. The Committee is aware that such cases are bound
to increase the magnitude and severity of TB epidemic and pose a major threat to TB
control. The Committee in its earlier Report has pointed out that 7% defaulters in a year
is quite a significant number of cases remaining untreated or half-treated. With a single
TB patient infecting 10-15 persons in a year, rate of infection is bound to increase. The
Committee would like to point out that formulation of guidelines for management of MDR
TB cases and publication thereof will not prove to be sufficient enough. The Committee had
given a simple suggestion for identifying the defaulter TB patients from the records being

VI. NATIONAL LEPROSY ERADICATION PROGRAMME (NLEP)
The Committee in its 22nd Report, had voiced its serious concern for
under-utilisation of allocated funds in 2006-07 due to unspent balances in majority of
the States. The Committee takes note of detailed analysis of actual expenditure pattern in
2006-07 carried out by the Department. The Committee is surprised to observe that even
this detailed analysis indicates provisional figures of 77.59% of utilisation of allocated funds
by the States. The Committee’s concern is further deepened by the fact that the two major
States Bihar and Jharkhand could utilize only 33.9% and 30% of the allocated funds during
the year 2006-07. Creation of confusion due to merger of the leprosy society with the State
Health Society under NRHM has been cited as the main reason for this position. The
Committee has been given to understand that a number of measures like review of
programme performance and expenditure pattern in the quarterly review meetings of
district Leprosy Officers streamlining of procurement procedure and monitoring at the
central level were initiated during 2007-08. The Committee also notes that more focus was

101
given on implementation of Action Plan during annual meeting and quarterly review
meetings of State Programme Officers. Not only this the States were also instructed to
prepare a month-wise Action Plan of IEC and Training from July, 2007 onwards and
submitting the monthly implementation reports of these activities. The Committee is
however, constrained to observe that these elaborate measures initiated by the Department
failed to make any visible impact as the ground realities continue to remain unchanged.
Drastic reduction of allocated funds had to be made at RE stage in 2007-08 with even that
provision remaining under-utilised upto 29.02.08. (Para 6.2)
The Committee is apprehensive that an enhanced allocation during 2008-09 may also
face similar outcomes. The Committee feels that though the plan of action under the
programme is well prepared, monitoring the timely and proper utilization of funds needs to
be taken on a more serious note. Mere advisories to State Implementing Agencies will not
serve the purpose. It, therefore, recommends that the Department take up the issue with
the State Programme Officers especially of Bihar and Jharkhand in an emphatic manner
during its quarterly review meetings. (Para 6.3)
The Committee welcomes the initiatives taken by the Department to provide

modalities for implementing this new scheme are finalised at the earliest. The Committee
would appreciate if this scheme takes off at the earliest. The Committee would, however,

benefited. Adequate publicity also needs to be given. All the stake-holders both
implementing agencies and targeted beneficiaries, need to be made aware of this new
initiative proposed under the programme. (Para 6.5)
VII. NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR CONTROL OF BLINDNESS
The Committee, is, however, surprised to find that implementation of new initiatives
depends on the approval of EFC Memo by the Ministry of Finance and CCEA. Till then, the
funds allocated for 2008-09 will continue to be utilized as per the existing scheme approved
for the Tenth Plan. The Committee is not happy with this state of affairs. Second year of
Eleventh Plan has already started and by the time approval of the competent authority is
finally received, the year 2008-09 would be nearing its end. The Committee would like to
point out that some of the new initiatives like construction of dedicated Eye Wings/Eye
OTs in District Hospitals, appointment of manpower, development of mobile Ophthalmic
Units in NE States will have to undergo a number of procedural formalities before their
actually taking off. The Committee accordingly recommends that concerted efforts need to
be made for getting the formal approval at the earliest. The Committee would appreciate
if simultaneously preparatory activities are also started. (Para 7.4)
The Committee has been taking note of instances of malfunctioning of Eye Camps
reported in the Media in the different States. The Committee feels that monitoring of Eye
Camps is being exclusively done by the District Blindness Control Societies. Department’s
role starts as and when reports regarding malfunctioning of Eye Camps held in States are
brought to the notice of the Ministry. Corrective measures are accordingly taken by the
Ministry. The Committee, however, feels that the Department needs to be more pro-active
in such cases. One corrective measure which can be initiated by the Department without
any special efforts involved is to review the performance of such Eye Camps in the State
level meetings, with required follow-up action taken on the reported incidents of
malfunctioning of Eye Camps. The Committee is also of the view that follow-up treatment
after Eye Camps should be made mandatory and strictly enforced. District hospital

102
authorities also need to be involved in the conducting of such Eye Camps by introduction
of compulsory, pre-checks in Eye Camps before start of Eye surgery and other treatment.
The Committee would like to add here that the Department should also devise some method
of surprise inspection of such eye camps organized by the NGOs to facilitate cataract
operations. This, the Committee feels, would ensure stricter compliance by all the concerned
entities and help reduce eyes mishaps. The Committee notes that the process for
independent evaluation of NPCB including evaluation of work being done by NGOs has been
initiated. The Committee would appreciate if this exercise is expedited so that corrective
measures are taken at the earliest. (Para 7.5)
The Committee is not convinced that the IEC activities are being carried out as
intensely as is stated by the Department. The Committee has been given to understand that
out of the total allocated budget under National Programme for Control of Blindness,
around 7% has been spent on IEC, which according to the Committee, is quite a sizable
amount. The Committee strongly feels that public awareness about the noble cause of eye
donation for blind persons continues to be very low. The Committee is of the view that a
review of IEC activities on this aspect need to be made in consultation with all the stake
holders, specially reputed Eye Hospitals/NGOs doing commendable work in this area and
also both Government and private ad agencies. The Committee would also like to emphasise
that the functioning of Eye Banks in the country requires to be reviewed in view of reported
lack of logistic support for timely collection of eyes by the Eye Banks. Steps also to need
to be taken for increasing the number of Eye Banks in the country. (Para. 7.7)
The Committee notes with disappointment that the target to bring prevalence of
blindness to 0.8% by 2007 has not been reached. The Department has informed that as per
the report of Rapid Survey on Avoidable Blindness (2007), the prevalence of blindness has
come down to 1% and that Cataract remains the single largest cause of blindness, low
vision and one eye blindness in India. The Committee also infers from the above study that
the prevalence of blindness due to Trachoma also continues to be there. It is a well known
fact that blindness is a bane of nature. The Committee believes that most of the people in
smaller towns and rural areas are not aware of the symptoms of the above ailments, and
the methods to manage eye care, their eye-care service seeking behavior pattern do not
change. The Committee exhorts the Department to take all possible necessary steps to
devise IEC activities in local media and television channels so that awareness among the
people is increased. The Committee believes a lot of cases of blindness can be checked by
such a measure and would in the long run help to achieve the targeted goals under the
programme. (Para 7.9)

ROUTINE IMMUNISATION PROGRAMME
The Committee has been observing that State specific problems specially in the low
performing States continue to persist. Shortage of Health Workers, lack of awareness and
laxity in monitoring and supervision of the programme are some of the problems being
noticed in such States. Reported coverage of different vaccines under the Routine
Immunization Programme confirms the Committee’s apprehensions that in some of the
States like Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, Manipur, position is very unsatisfactory.
The Committee would like to point out that the steps taken for strengthening the Routine
Immunization Programme like recruitment of health workers, regular reviews at all levels
and catch up rounds in poor performing States do not seem to have yielded the desired
results. The Committee can only say that catch-up rounds in poor performance States with
the districts identified before hand is the most important step for effective reduction in the
number of un-immunized and partially immunized children. Entire focus of the
Implementing agencies should be on this alone. (Para 9.15)
The Committee understands that the three pioneer Institutes namely Central
Research Institute (CRI), Kasauli in Himachal Pradesh, Pasteur Institute of India (PTI),
Koonoor, Tamil Nadu and the BCG Vaccine Laboratory (BCGVL) in Chennai, Tamil Nadu
have been directed to stop vaccine production. The Committee strongly feels that these
vaccine production units should not be converted to only testing laboratories. The
Committee accordingly, recommends that the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare should
withdraw the order without delay. (Para 9.16)
