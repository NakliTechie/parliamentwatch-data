PART-A
HEALTH SECTOR
I. INTRODUCTION
1.1 The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare is instrumental and responsible for
implementation of various programmes on the national scale in the areas of Health and Family
Welfare, prevention and control of major communicable diseases and promotion of traditional and
indigenous systems of medicine. Apart from these, the Ministry also assists the States in preventing
and controlling the spread of seasonal diseases’ outbreaks and epidemics through technical
assistance.
1.2 The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare incurs expenditure either directly under Central
Schemes or through its four Departments:–
• Department of Health and Family Welfre
• Department of AYUSH
• Department of Health Research; and
• Department of AIDS Control
1.3 The National Health Policy framed from time to time provides the framework for the
implementation of policies and programmes for health care. The Eleventh Five Year Plan had
focused on the poor and the underprivileged. Accessible, equitable and affordable health care was
a priority concern and therefore emphasis was accorded to reducing disparities in health across
regions and communities by ensuring access to affordable health . The Twelfth Plan envisages
building on the achievements of the Eleventh Plan for extending outreach of public health services
and for moving towards the long term objective of establishing a system of Universal Health
coverage through National Health Mission.
1.4 The Department of Health and Family Welfare comprises of Health Sector and NHM Sector.
The various activities under the Health Sector to name a few include Pradhan Mantri Swasthya
Suraksha Yojana (PMSSY), Central Government managed hospitals and autonomous institutions of
Medical Education under the Department,Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS), Central
Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO), various National Programmes, etc. Similarly, the
National Health Mission is comprised of two sub-copmponents viz. National Rural Health Mission
(NRHM) and National Urban Health Mission (NUHM). The Main activities being covered under both
Health and NHM Sector are described in detail below.

2.2 From a glance of the above Table, the Committee notes that the funds allocated over
the period of Eleventh Plan and Annual Plan 2012-13 has increased steadily. However, the
Committee is constrained to observe that in a time of increasing demand due to
demographic pressures, public expectations and medical advances there was substantial and
persistent under-utilization of the budgeted funds during the Eleventh Plan and Annual Plan
of 2012-13. The Committee desires that the processes of allocation and utilization of funds
should be mutually reinforcing instead of being parallel. That the savings occurred under
the Plan head underlines the fact that the development activities might have been adversely
affected in the health sector. The Committee, accordingly impresses upon the Department
that in future utilization must reflect the allocation policy objectives to the extent possible.
2.3 In the Twelfth Plan the proposed and approved outlays for the Department of Health and
Family Welfare (Health Sector) are Rs. 184788.24 crore and Rs. 75145.29 crore respectively. The
Committee was informed that this reduction in outlay was likely to affect implementation of a
number of ongoing schemes. It was also informed that National Urban Health Mission which was
proposed to be rolled out in the year could be provided only a token provision of Rs. 1.00 crore
during 2013-14. The Committee was further informed that several new programmes proposed in
the Twelfth Plan could not take off due to limited availability of funds. Moreover, new initiatives
like provision of free generic medicines in all public health facilities, strengthening of district
hospitals to provide advanced secondary care, Human Resources for Health, Strengthening
regulations in drugs and food sector, would be impacted negatively, as approved outlay is barely
sufficient to meet on-going health interventions. The focus has been to sustain the current
interventions, so that progress achieved so far is not reversed.
2.4 With this trend of allocation of funds and persistent under utilization, the Committee
is concerned that the Department needs to tackle the problems of shrinking resources, rising
demand and under utilization of allocated funds in a balanced manner. Utilization status of
the Eleventh Plan and for 2012-13 reveals inability of the Department to utilize the allocated
funds efficiently. During 2012-13 only 52.26 per cent of allocated funds have been utilized till
31.12.2012, thus leaving the balance to be utilized in the last quarter of the financial year.
The Committee is disappointed by this evident lack of fiscal discipline and strongly
recommends that the Department should seek target oriented allocation in sync with the
ability to utilize the allocated funds; instead of indicative allocation of funds. The Committee,
further opines that a token provision of Rs.1 crore for National Urban Health Mission during
2013-14 on account of limited availability of funds would further impact the health indicators
of poor people residing in urban slums and recommends that the Department should approach
the Planning Commission for enhanced allocation at RE stage.

2.5 The Ministry had projected a demand for Rs. 49624.72 crore to the Planning Commission
for the Department of Health and Family Welfare (Health Sector) for the Annual Plan 2013-14. As
against the proposed outlay, the Planning Commission approved an outlay of Rs. 29165.00 crore,
indicating a shortfall of 41 per cent.
2.6 The Committee is constrained to note 41% shortfall in allocation of funds vis-à-vis
the proposed outlay for 2013-14 that too at a time when the Government has committed
itself to raising its expenditure on health from roughly 1.29% of the GDP to 2.5% of the
GDP by the end of Twelfth Plan. The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to
overcome the financial constraints the Department should make efforts to achieve
efficiency gain in utilization each year and also to convince the Planning Commission and
the Department of Expenditure for enhancing the Annual Plan allocation for the remaining
years of the Twelfth Plan and also at RE 2013-14 so as to achieve the ultimate target for
expenditure of 2.5% of GDP in health sector.

2.8 From a perusal of the above Tables, the Committee observes that with such level of
underutilization of funds, the financial objectives and target achievement of the Department
would suffer to a large extent. The Department has informed that during the Eleventh Plan
the extent of savings on the Capital Account has been Rs. 2883.25 crore. Most of the
savings were due to slow progress of capital work and slow pace of procurement of
equipments. The Committee observes with deep concern that savings under the Capital
section implies that it will impede the developmental activities. Slow progress in capital
work would critically hamper both the quality of care provided and the ability of the system
as a whole to respond to increased demand in this sector. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department should take concrete steps to address this issue by means
of prudent assessment of the fund requirement and design effective mechanism to make a
balance between spending and savings.
2.9 The Committee desires to be apprised of the hurdles faced by the Department in the
path of utilization of allocated funds and delivering the objectives and the steps initiated and
pursued by the Department to streamline formulation of the budgetary requirements in a
realistic fashion and also to expedite necessary approvals for operationalization of various
formulated schemes/projects.
2.10 Taking all the above factors into consideration, the Committee recommends that the
Department should once again assess its fund requirements realistically and thereafter
move the Planning Commission for augmentation of financial resources for health sector
for 2013-14 at RE stage. The Committee would like to be apprised of the follow-up action
taken in this regard.

PART-B

INTRODUCTION
The National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) was launched in 2005, to provide accessible
affordable and quality healthcare to the population, with focus on people living in rural areas. The
various initiatives under NRHM aim to close the Inter regional and interstate disparities to promote
delivery of health services with focus on equity. The NRHM has been able to show a direction to
provide health services to the poorest household in the remotest areas with focus on strengthening
public health systems, reducing overall morbidity, mortality and establishing a fully functional
community owned decentralized health delivery system with flexibility for need based planning.
1.1 The Department has informed that the Twelfth plan would build on the achievements of the
Eleventh Plan for extending outreach of public health services and for moving towards the long
term objective of establishing a system of Universal Health coverage through National Health
Mission. As per the information put out on the website of NRHM, at the national level, the targets
in the Twelfth
 Five Year Plan would be as under:-
• Reduction of MMR to <100 per 100000 live births
• Reducing IMR to <27 per 1000 live births
• Reduction in NMR to <18 per 1000 live births
• Reducing TFR to 2.1
• Elimination of Filaria – in all 250 districts; Kala-azar in all 514 Blocks and Leprosy in
all districts
• Reduction in TB prevalence and mortality by 50%
• Reduction in Annual Malaria incidence to <1/1000 pop.
• Reduction in JE mortality by 50%
• Sustaining case fatality rate of less than 1% for Dengue
1.2 The Government plans to fill critical gaps in infrastructure and manpower planning in
recognition of fresh challenges being posed due to increasing migration from rural to urban areas.
During the Twelfth
 Plan, the interventions under National Rural Health Mission would be extended
to the urban areas under the National Urban Health Mission which will be launched as a
sub-mission under an overarching National Health Mission, with NRHM being the other
sub-mission. It would meet the long standing need to strengthen the healthcare in urban areas with
special focus on the urban poor. Accordingly, Public health care services are sought to be further
expanded during the 12th Plan. Efforts would be directed towards financial and managerial health
systems redesigning to ensure better outcomes. To encourage the participation of States in
addressing their State specific problems, the flexible pool would be enlarged. This would enable
States to prioritize the interventions there in keeping with their needs.
1.3 Accordingly, flexi pools would comprise of the NRHM-RCH flexi pool, the National Urban
Health Mission Flexi Pool, flexi pool for communicable diseases and flexi pool for non

55
communicable diseases, injury and trauma. All interventions by the Central Government under these
flexi pools would be for interventions at the district and below levels. The NRHM-RCH flexi pool
would include interventions under Reproductive Child Health and Mission flexi pools, routine
immunization, pulse polio immunization and iodine deficiency disorder control programme,
strengthening of district hospitals for providing advanced secondary care and providing free generic
medicines in all public health institutions in the country. The flexi pools for communicable diseases
would inter-alia encompass interventions under National Vector Borne Control Programme, National
T.B. Control Programme, National Leprosy Eradication Programme and Integrated Disease
Surveillance Programme. Interventions under National Programme for control of Blindness, National
Mental Health Programme, Health care for Elderly, National Programme for Prevention and Control
of Deafness, National Tobacco Control Programme, National Oral Health Programme, National
Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke
and Assistance to States for capacity building for Burns, would be included in the flexi pool for
non-communicable Diseases, Injury and Trauma. Assistance would also be given under the National
Health Mission for Infrastructure Maintenance to States.
1.4 The National Health Mission (NHM) will build on the experience gained in implementation
of the National Rural Health Mission and focus inter-alia  on augmentation of Human Resources
by encouraging States to engage health personnel including doctors, nurses and paramedics
strengthening health infrastructure by providing support to the States for new construction/
upgradation/ renovation of healthcare facilities; strengthening First Referral Units and
Operationalisation of more 24x7 facilities, decentralized planning through Village Health Sanitation
and Nutrition Committees and Rogi Kalyan Samitis, preparation of District Health Action Plan
with convergence from all health related sectors, provisioning for health service delivery
especially in un-served and underserved areas through Medical Mobile Units; providing financial
assistance to States for selection and training of Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs) who
act as a link between community and healthcare facilities; establishing Emergency Transport and
Patient Transport System, taking forward the Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK) which
entitles all pregnant women delivering in public health institutions to absolutely free and no
expense delivery, including caesarian section. The initiative stipulates free diagnostics, blood,
drugs and diet, besides free transport from home to institution, between facilities in case of a
referral and drop back home. Similar entitlements have been put in place for all sick newborns
accessing public health institutions for treatment till 30 days after birth; initiatives for tracking
every pregnant women and child to ensure registration of all pregnant mothers and neonates to
monitor the ante-natal and post-natal care of mothers and immunization of children would receive
attention. Apart of this, other areas of importance would be distribution of contraceptives
(condoms and oral pills) through ASHAs to the user homes, supply of sanitary napkins through
ASHAs at subsidized rates to rural adolescent girls; weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation
Programme; the National School Health Programme and appointments of post partum counselors
for RMNCH Services at high case load facilities. Reduction in mortality, morbidity and disability
due to burn injuries during the T welfth Five Year Plan would be focus area. The initiatives taken
by the Government for prevention and control of communicable diseases and non-communicable
diseases would be strengthened.

5.6 As per the information furnished by the Department; Tuberculosis is a major public health
concern in India. The country accounts for nearly one-fifth of the global TB incidence and in 2009,
out of the estimated annual incidence of 9.4 million TB cases, 2.0 million were estimated to have
occurred in India. The Revised National TB Control Programme (RNTCP), based on internationally

result, TB mortality in the country has reduced from over 42/lakh population in 1990 to 23/lakh
population in 2009 as per the WHO global report 2010.The prevalence of TB in the country has

VIII. Pulse Polio Immunization
8.1 The Department has informed that in pursuance of the World Health Assembly resolution
of 1988, the Pulse Polio Immunization (PPI) Programme was started nation-wide from 1995 to
eradicate polio in India covering children in the age group 0-3 years. In order to accelerate the pace
of polio eradication, all children under the age of 5 years were targeted since 1996-97. Till
1998-99, two rounds were organized in the month of December and January each year. From
1999-2000, house to house vaccination of missed children was also introduced to vaccinate
children missed during the fixed booth based vaccination of children. This resulted in increasing
coverage of 2-3 crore additional children. The annual strategy on polio eradication is decided on
the basis of recommendation of India Experts Advisory Group (IEAG). Since the PPI initiative in
1995, significant success has been achieved in reducing the number of polio cases in the country
and total cases declined gradually. Of the 3 types of polio causing viruses, type 2 (WPV-2) has
already been eradicated in 1999. In November, 2009 meeting of the India Expert Advisory Group
(IEAG) had recommended bivalent OPV vaccine effective against both type 1 and type 3 viruses
use in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. The bivalent vaccine (bOPV) was introduced in the country for
the first time in 2010. The last polio case in the country was reported from Howrah district of
West Bengal with date of onset 13
th January, 2011. Thereafter no polio case has been reported in
the country. WHO on 24 th February, 2012 removed India from the list of countries with active
endemic wild polio virus transmission. The National Immunisation Days (NID) rounds covers
approximately 172 million children and Sub-National Immunization Days (SNID) rounds cover
about 86 Million children.

10.6 The following special initiatives are being undertaken under National Rural Health Mission

(i) Promotion of Institutional Delivery through Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) and Janani

assistance. JSSK entitles all pregnant women to absolutely free and zero expense

(ii) Strengthening Facility based newborn care: Reduction of Newborn mortality is one of

are set up at all health facilities where deliveries take place to provide essential

14, 21, 28 and 42
nd days and one additional visit within 24 hours of delivery in case
of home deliveries. Additional visits will be made for babies who are pre-term, low
birth weight or ill.
(iv) Capacity building of health care providers: Various trainings are being conducted under
National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) to build and upgrade the skills of doctors,
nurses and ANM for early diagnosis and case management of common ailments of
children and care of newborn at time of birth. These trainings include Integrated
Management of Neo-natal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI) and Navjaat Shishu Suraksha
Karyakram (NSSK). NSSK has been launched to improve the skills of health care
providers in provision of new born care at birth. A total of 5.8 lakh health care
workers have been trained in IMNCI in 505 districts and 89,962 health workers

XI. MILLENIUM DEVELOPMENT GOALS (MDG) (PERTAINING TO HEALTH AND
FAMILY WELFARE)
11.1 Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) are Eight International Development Goals that all 192
United Nations member states and at least 23 International Organisations have agreed to achieve by
the year 2015. These include, among others, reducing extreme poverty, reducing Child Mortality Rate,
improving Maternal Health, fighting disease epidemics such as AIDS and developing a global
partnership for development. From amongst them, the goal number 4, 5 and 6 relate to Health.

86
11.2 The Department in a written submission has informed that three goals of MDG, i.e.,
MDG-4, MDG-5 and MDG-6 relate to health sector.
MDG-4: Reduce Child mortality
(1) As per MDG-4, the target is to reduce the Under Five Mortality Rate (U5MR) among
children by two-third between 1990 and 2015. In case of India, it translates into a goal
of reducing U5MR from 115 per 1000 live births in 1990 to 38 in 2015.
(2) In 1990, India’s U5MR stood at 115 when Global average was 88. In 2011 (SRS
2011), U5MR in India stood at 55 against a Global average of 57. India has thus
achieved U5MR decline at an accelerated pace to bridge the yawning gap between
Global U5MR and U5MR in India.
(3) Annual rate of decline in U5MR was 2.2% during the period 1990 to 2008. In 2009,
annual rate of decline rose to 7.2% and in 2010 to 7.8%. The annual rate of decline
in 2011 is 6.8%. This points to the success of several initiatives launched under the
NRHM for health system strengthening and reproductive and child health strategies. In
points terms, India has been achieving a consistent decline of 5 points each year
during the period 2008-2010. There has been a further decline of 4 points in 2011. At
this pace, India is expected to reach the MDG target.
MDG-5: Improve Maternal Health
(1) MDG-5 target is to reduce Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) by three quarters between
1990 and 2015. Recent estimates on MMR are not available and data of MMR pertains
to 2007-09 only and puts MMR in India at 212 per 100,000 live births. Further, the
estimates of MMR in India have been reworked by UN Inter-Agency Expert Group for
the year 1990 and is estimated to be 600 instead of 437 as earlier projected. This
translates to reducing the MMR from 600 in 1990 to 150 in 2015.
(2) Decline in MMR between 2004-06 and 2007-09 has been 14 points per year, i.e. 5.5%
annual decline. Assuming that the annual rate of decline observed during 2004-06 and
2007-09 continues, India’s MMR is likely to reduce from 212 during 2007-09 to 143
in 2015. According to this, India would very well achieve the MDG target of 150 by
2015.
MDG-6: Combat HIV/AIDS, Malaria and Other Diseases
The Department has informed that the target is to halt by 2015 and begun to reverse the
spread of HIV/AIDS. In respect of other diseases, the target is to halt by 2015 and begin to reverse
the incidence of malaria and other major diseases. Regarding Malaria, its annual incidence rate
(cases of malaria/1000 population) has come down from 2.57 per thousand in 1990 to 1.10 per
thousand in 2011, achieving about 57% reduction. In the case of Tuberculosis, the prevalence rate
has come down from 586 per 100,000 population in 1990 to 249 in 2009 per 100,000 population,
achieving 57.5% reduction. In case of HIV/AIDS, new annual HIV infections have declined by 56%
during the last decade from 2.7 lakhs in 2000 to 1.2 lakhs in 2009.
11.3 Out of six diseases under NVBDCP, Kala-azar and Lymphatic Filariasis have been targeted

11.5 The Committee feels that the success rate of achievements with regard to Millenium

II. BUDGETARY PROVISIONS
A plain reading of the allocation made vis-a-vis the utilization figures underlines the
fact that the Department of Health and Family Welfare was not able to utilize even the
allocated funds. The Committee feels that had the Government released the full approved
outlay made during the Eleventh Plan, the track record of the Department would have made
a poor reading on the efficiency of the delivery mechanism in physical as well as financial
terms. The Committee is, therefore, of the view that the Department should ensure that
the expenditure incurred should commensurate with the amount allocated so as not to invite
reduction of allocation at RE stage. Huge and persistent mismatch between the Budget
Estimates, Revised Estimates and Actual Expenditure is indicative of unrealistic projection
of fund requirements without application of proper financial yardsticks and reflects poorly
on the process of formulation. of Budget Estimates. The Committee, therefore, implores
upon the Department to undertake a thorough analysis of the persistent underutilization of
the funds vis-a-vis the allocated funds and delineate the steps taken so far and to be taken
in future to plug the gaps, so as to minimize the variations between BE, RE and AE and
also for ensuring achieving the targets visualised. (Para 2.1)
Approved outlay for National Health Mission (NHM) for the Twelfth Plan is
Rs. 193405.71 crores as against the projected outlay of Rs. 219702.45 crores. Further against a
projected demand for Rs.32,520.13 crore for NHM, the Planning Commission has approved an
outlay of Rs.20999.00 crore for 2013-14. The Committee is aware that public spending on
health was targeted to be increased to 2.5% of GDP at the launch of NRHM in 2005.
However, as per figures quoted by the Economic Survey 2012-13, public expenditure on
health is just 1.29% of GDP. Private expenditure on health is 2.9% of GDP. The Committee
observes that even if the level of government expenditure on health is increased to 2.5%
of GDP, there would still be a very substantial health care expenditure which is out-of-
pocket payments made by the households in time of health distress. The cost of health care
services in the private sector is so exorbitant that it is just unaffordable for the poor and
the under-privileged. It is in this context that the reduction in approved outlay of
Rs. 193405.71 crore for NHM for the Twelfth Plan against the projected demand of
Rs. 219702.45 crore is cause for extreme concern to the Committee. Keeping in view the
fact that illness of a family member is the most common reason leading to a financial crisis
among poor households, the Committee lends its full support for greater health outlays. The
Committee, simultaneously, recommends that the Department should strengthen its
monitoring mechanism so as to ensure that the available resources are utilized more
efficiently and responsibility be fixed to ensure timely disbursement and utilization of funds
at all levels. (Para 2.2)
