REPORT
I. Introduction
1.1 The Department of Health Research (DHR) was created as a separate Department within the
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare by an amendment to the Government of India (Allocation of
Business) Rules, 1961 on the 17th September 2007. The Department became functional from November
2008 with the appointment of first Secretary of the Department.
1.2 The aim of the DHR/ICMR is to bring modern health technologies to the people through research
and innovations related to diagnosis, treatment methods and vaccines for prevention; to translate them
into products and processes and, in synergy with concerned organizations introduce these innovations
into public health system.
The following 10 functions (nine new functions, plus the ongoing function of administering the ICMR)
have been allocated to the Department of Health Research:
1. Promotion and co-ordination of basic, applied and clinical research including clinical trials
and operational research in areas related to medical, health, biomedical and medical profession
and education through development of infrastructure, manpower and skills in cutting edge
areas and management of related information thereto.
2. Promote and provide guidance on research governance issues, including ethical issues in
medical and health research.
3. Inter-sectoral coordination and promotion of public - private - partnership in medical,
biomedical and health research related areas.
4. Advance training in research areas concerning medicine and health, including grant of
fellowships for such training in India and abroad.
5. International co-coperation in medical and health research, including work related to
international conferences in related areas in India and abroad.
6. Technical support for dealing with epidemics and natural calamities.
7. Investigation of outbreaks due to new and exotic agents and development of tools for
prevention.
8. Matters relating to scientific societies and associations, charitable and religious endowments
in medicine and health research areas.
9. Coordination between organizations and institutes under the Central and State Governments
in areas related to the subjects entrusted to the Department and for the promotion of special
studies in medicine and health.
10. Administering and monitoring of Indian Council of Medical Research.
1.3 With a view to fulfill its mandate of nine new functions, the DHR had formulated following five
new schemes and all these schemes have since been approved and rolled out in 2013-14.
(i) Establishment of Multidisciplinary Research Units (MRUs) in Govt. Medical Colleges and
Research Institute.

2
(ii) Establishment of Model Rural Health Research Units (MRHRUs) in the States.
(iii) Establishment of Network of Research Laboratories for Managing Epidemics and Natural
Calamities.
(iv) Human Resource Development for Health Research.
(v) Grants in Aid Scheme for inter-sectoral convergence & promotion and guidance on research
governance issues.
1.4 The Department has also been working on bringing suitable legislations aimed at:
(i) Regulating ethical issues pertaining to biomedical and heath research;
(ii) Regulating medical, social, ethical and legal aspects of surrogacy; and
(iii) Assisted reproductive technology services in the country, and also for regulating the processes
for recognition of any proposed alternative systems of medicine.

2.6 As evident from the above tables there are huge variations between the BE and RE for all
the schemes as well as for ICMR. The reasons cited by the Department for this trend were non-
receipt of adequate number of applications on account of approval of the scheme at fag end of
2013-14, pendency of utilization certificates, receipt of less number of proposals from States and
non-completion of requisite administrative and technical formalities under the Schemes guidelines
by certain States. The Committee, in the circumstances, is constrained to observe that inspite
of its repeated recommendations to the Department to take all out measures, including completion
of pre-project planning and formalities of obtaining necessary approvals for optimum and judicious
utilization of budgeted funds, not much has been done in this regard. The Committee fails to
understand the reasons for the delay in formulation of all the schemes even though the Schemes
first got in- principle approval by the Planning Commission in December 2010. The Committee
is not convinced with the statement of the Department that inspite of its best efforts, the various
actions like the appraisal of the Schemes, responding to various appraisal agencies, clearance of
the Schemes by EFC/CCEA took more than anticipated time which ultimately led to the delay.
The Committee observes that this is a clear case of lack of foresight and faulty planning by the
Department. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should make all
possible efforts with proper and timely planning to utilize the allocated funds at BE Stage. The
Committee would like to be apprised of the steps being taken or proposed to be taken to arrest
this trend of underutilization of budgeted funds which eventually led to massive cuts at RE stage.

2.11 The Committee is of the opinion that keeping in view the mandate of DHR/ICMR to bring
modern technologies to the people through research and innovations and the fact that Department
has just launched the above mentioned Schemes to fulfill its mandate of creating a strong and
effective eco-system for carrying out health research in the country and for introduction of new
technologies, new methods of treatment and products/ processes into the public health system;
any under funding to the schemes would prove detrimental in achieving the desired objectives
that too at a time when these schemes have just picked up their pace. In view of the fact that
with this funds crunch it would not be possible for the Department to roll out many of its
proposed initiatives under the schemes, the Committee recommends that the Department should
vigorously pursue with the Ministry of Finance for enhanced funds at RE Stage. The Committee,
simultaneously, also recommends to the Department that it should take all out efforts for
optimum and judicious utilization of available resources and funds for implementation of various
Schemes. Funds for new initiatives should be limited to availability of funds.
III. Indian Council of Medical Research. (ICMR)
3.1 As per the information given in the Annual Report of the Department, ICMR serves as the fulcrum
of the Department of Health Research. It is an apex organization to formulate, conduct and promote
biomedical research. It has been mentioned that the proposed seamless integration between the ICMR
(generation of new knowledge) and the DHR (putting this knowledge to public good) which began in
recent past has been progressing well in the right direction.
3.2 ICMR carries out intramural research through its institutes/centres. Of 32 institutes/centres, 17
deal with communicable diseases and coordinated by Division of Epidemiology and Communicable
Diseases; 7 with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and are coordinated by Division of RCH; 3 deal
with diseases related to nutrition and are coordinated by Division of Nutrition and 3 deal with diseases
related to basic medical sciences (BMS) and are coordinated by Division of BMS.
3.3 Besides, ICMR funds extramural research in medical Colleges, Universities/Institutions. An average
of 1200-1500 projects annually are funded which include both ongoing and new projects. ICMR provides
1200-1500 students fellowships - JRF, SRF, PDF, STS ongoing and new, every year.

Committee noted that the ICMR had shortlisted a large number of thrust areas for carrying out its

having such a large number of priorities was not conducive for more allocations and advised the
Department to have a core set of priorities so that allocations made are not divided in numerous areas.
The Committee impressed upon the Department to review its priorities and concentrate on those areas
which were absolutely essential so that scarce resource could be optimally utilized for achieving the
desired results in the focused areas.

ICMR had prioritized the research activities in the following areas: (i) Environmental Health, (ii) Non-

like emerging and re-emerging viral infections, Zoonatic Diseases, Anti-microbial Resistance, (iv) Stem
cell research and therapy, Genomics including pharmaco-genomics. (v) Operational research in area of
nutrition. (vi) Maternal and Child Health, (vii) Basic medical sciences-, Clinical Pharmacology, Human
Genetics, Social Determinants of Health and Adolescent Health etc. It was further informed that the three
most important priorities of the above were: 1. Cancer research, 2. Environmental Health and 3. Maternal
and Child Health.
3.11 The Committee is happy to note that at its behest the Department has revisited its
priorities areas for research and drawn three most important priorities areas namely Cancer
Research, Environmental Health and Maternal and Child Health for carrying out research. The
Committee is hopeful that this move of the Department would result in visible and desirable
results. The Committee desires to be apprised of the outcome of the above move.

II. Budgetary Allocation
The Committee notes that on one hand the Department claims that significant progress has been
made in respect of Schemes while on the other the Committee notes that only a meagre amount
of expenditure of Rs. 179.36 crore upto 4.3.2015 could be made against the allocations of Rs.

the Department was consistently over estimating its requirements and has not been able to make
use of scarce financial resources. The Committee, accordingly, impresses upon the Department
to make all out efforts to implement its proposed activities under the schemes in the remaining
two years of the 12th Five Year Plan for optimum utilization of allocations and for achieving the
desired objectives. (para 2.3)
As evident from the above tables there are huge variations between the BE and RE for all
the schemes as well as for ICMR. The reasons cited by the Department for this trend were non-

(i) Establishment of Multi-disciplinary Research Units (MRUs) in Govt. Medical Colleges
and the Research Institutes.
The Committee observes that deaths caused due to non-communicable diseases are much
more than those occurring due to communicable diseases and any under-funding to the Scheme,
which promotes research in Non-Communicable diseases, will prove detrimental to control lifestyle
diseases. The proposed shortfall in allocation to the Scheme may lead to curtailment in the
second or subsequent installments to the Institutes where the Department has already made a
beginning and would result in seriously hampering of research activities initiated. The Committee,
therefore, lends its support to the demand of the Department for enhancement of allocations at
RE Stage for the Scheme so that investments already made in the medical colleges under the
Scheme do not go down the drain.
The Committee finds that life style-related disease like hypertension, blood pressure,
diabetes etc. are on the rise and are the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in the country
and, therefore, it is a cause of serious concern. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
life style-related diseases may be included in the research programmes of the Department.
(para 6.6)
(ii) Establishment of Model Rural Health Units (MRHRUs)
The Committee desires that more States may be covered under the Scheme. The Department
may also set up more MRHRUs covering all the States. (para 6.10)
VII. Grant-in-aid for Inter sectoral Convergence and Promotion and guidance on Health
Research:
The Committee observes that some of very important activities are targeted to be
accomplished under the Scheme for Inter Sectoral convergence and Promotion and Guidance on
Health Research. The Committee is happy to note that pursuant to its recommendation made
in 56th Report and reiterated in 67th Report for creation of an institutional co-ordination
mechanism for synergizing the core competencies of the different organizations/agencies a group
of Secretaries has been constituted, consisting of Secretaries from DBT, DRDO, DARE/ICAR,
ISRO, Department of Space, DIT etc. to co-ordinate and collaborate on Health Research activities
among various departments. The Committee hopes that anticipated dividends are being accrued
from this move. (para 7.8)
The Committee apprehends that shortfall in allocations made for the Scheme would hamper
the pace of projects already initiated. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department
to take all measures to offset impact of shortfall of allocations. Simultaneously, the Department
should also present a strong case for seeking more funds at RE Stage. (para 7.9)
VIII. Establishment of Network of Laboratories of managing Epidemics and Natural Calamities:
The Committee fails to see through the reasons for underperformance under the Scheme
of Establishment of Network of Laboratories of managing Epidemics and Natural Calamities.

22
With this pace of performance, the Committee does not understand how the target of establishing
10 Regional Labs, 30 State Level Labs and 120 Medical Colleges Labs, as envisaged during the
12th Plan period would be achieved. Even funds could be released to only 4 Regional Labs, 5 State
Level Labs and 16 Medical Colleges Labs out of the approved 5 Regional Labs, 11 State Level
Labs and 35 Medical College Labs. The Department needs to do some serious introspection to
look into the cause for not meeting the targets set. The Committee impresses upon the Department
to draw an action plan to meet the physical and financial targets set under the Scheme.
(para 8.7)
The Committee is perturbed to find that the component for creation of the Revolving fund
to facilitate a rapid mobilization of outbreak/ disaster response to infectious diseases outbreak
or a natural or man made disaster was not approved in the EFC meeting held under the
Chairmanship of Finance Secretary and Secretary of Expenditure. The Committee observes that
creation of such a fund was very vital for the country owing to recent outbreaks of diseases like
Swine Flu, Chikunguniya etc. As the Department has also expressed its inability to establish a
budget line to cater for the emergency action in case of infectious diseases outbreak, the
Committee recommends that the Department may approach the Ministry of Finance for reviewing
its decision in the matter. (para 8.8)
