REPORT
 
INTRODUCTION
            The Union Ministry of Health and Family Welfare is instrumental and responsible for implementation of various
programmes on a national scale in the areas of Family Welfare, prevention and control of major communicable diseases
and Promotion of traditional and indigenous systems of medicine.
1.2       The Department of Health incurs expenditure either directly or through various institutions and establishments
run by it and by way of grants given to autonomous and voluntary organisations. The Department is implementing
several World Bank Assisted programmes for control of AIDS, Malaria, Leprosy, TB and Blindness in designated
areas.  Besides, State Health Systems Development Projects with World Bank assistance are under implementation in
six States, viz. Karnataka, W. Bengal, Punjab, Orissa, Maharashtra and UP.  These Projects are implemented by the
respective State Govts and the Department.  
II.            BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The Secretary, Department of Health in his presentation before the Committee stated that one of the important
priorities of the National Common Minimum Programme is to raise public spending on health from 0.9 % of GDP at
present to minimum 2-3% of GDP over the next five years.   For increasing the public spending on health to 2% of
GDP, w.e.f. 2004-05, a public health expenditure of Rs. 1,12,639 crore will be required by 2009-2010.  However,
against a proposed outlay of Rs. 14,153 crores for the Tenth Plan period for the Department, the outlay approved was
only Rs. 10,252 crores. 
2.2       The Committee notes that position of outlays approved during the first three years of the Tenth Plan vis-à-vis the
proposed outlays is not very encouraging as indicated below:  -

to Rs. 1403.70 crores and the utilisation of funds stands at Rs. 1321.00 crores.    The Committee is of the view that the
year 2004-05 being the third year of Tenth Plan is the right stage for taking a mid-term appraisal of all the
programmes/institutions of the Department, specially those not performing well and taking remedial steps wherever
required.

III.            CENTRAL GOVERNMENT HEALTH SCHEME
3.1       CGHS provides comprehensive medical care to Central Govt. employees and their family members, covering
about 44 lakh beneficiaries. CGHS facilities are available in 23 cities in the country covering 19 States; with 323
dispensaries, out of which 244 are allopathic dispensaries.  The plan allocation for CGHS in BE 2003-04 was Rs. 14.00
crores which has been marginally increased to Rs. 16.50 crores in BE 2004-05.
 3.2      The Committee notes that the non-Plan allocation of Rs. 276.00crores for CGHS in BE 2003-2004 was
substantially increased to Rs. 392.14 crores at the RE stage only to be drastically reduced to Rs. 187.00 crores in BE
2004-2005.  The Department has clarified  that enhancement in non-Plan allocation in RE-2003-2004 was  mainly to
meet the pending liabilities and the ongoing expenditure for making reimbursement to the CGHS recognized private
hospitals that extend credit facilities to the pensioner cardholders, which amounted to Rs. 66.00 crore.  The Committee
was informed that a decision was taken by the Department to
decentralize the work related to reimbursement of medical claims of pensioners  being handled by the CGHS by
transferring the same  to the  parent departments of the pensioners. 
3.3            Accordingly, vide Department O.M. dated 14.10.03, the pensioner was given an option to seek settlement of
medical claims either from the Department from where he/she retired or from the Department of Health.   In anticipation
of a positive response from majority of the pensioners, allocation in BE-2004-2005, was kept at a much lower rate.   
The Committee, however, notes that in view of lack of positive response from the beneficiaries, the decentralization
scheme has been kept in abeyance since 12
th May, 2004. 
3.4       The Committee fails to understand the non-acceptance of this initiative by majority of the pensioners.  The
Committee feels that the idea of decentralizing the work relating to reimbursement of claims of pensioners to their
respective Departments from where they had retired is a welcome one.  The Committee would like to have the feedback
from the Department regarding percentage of pensioners who had shown reluctance/non acceptance to this reported
move of the Department.
3.5     Last year, the Committee was informed that a Committee under the Chairmanship of Dr. R.K.  Srivastava, Addl.
Director General in the Directorate General of Health Services had been constituted to prepare the combined drugs
formulary of the CGHS and MSO and its report was being examined then.   The Committee was given to understand
that once the new drugs formulary was formally notified, the procurement of drugs for the CGHS would be based on
time.   The Committee is constrained to observe that one year has passed and the recommendations of the Committee on
drugs formulary are still under the consideration of the Department.   It seems that the Department has no time bound
action plan for notification of the drugs formulary.  The Committee feels that notification of the new Drugs formulary
would lead to quality control of drugs being provided through CGHS dispensaries.  The Committee enjoins upon the
Department to get all the formalities completed for notification of drugs formulary.  
3.6       The Committee in its 135
th Report had strongly advocated the need for restoring the earlier two-shift system in
CGHS dispensaries which was more convenient for the beneficiaries.  The Department has justified the single shift
working of CGHS dispensaries due to majority of CGHS employees commuting daily from long distances.  The
Committee was given to understand that double shift system is practically impossible for such employees.  The
Committee expresses its dissatisfaction about the justification given by the

IV. SAFDARJUNG HOSPITAL
4.1       The Plan allocation for Safdarjung Hospital in BE 2003-04 was Rs. 45.00 crores.  Expenditure incurred was only
Rs. 38.83 crores.  Plan allocation for the 2004-05 is Rs. 60 crores, an enhancement of Rs. 15 crores.  The Committee
notes that proposed increase is mainly meant for procurement of a spiral CT Scan, Cobalt Teletherapy machine,
additional requirements of Vardhman Mahabir Medical College, meeting the increased demand and cost escalation of
various consumable items required for patient care.
4.2
              The Department has informed that for the current year there would be an over all increase in the total number of
OPD attendance, indoor admissions, investigations (Laboratory), radiological investigations, (X-ray, ultrasound, CT
Scan), Specialized procedures and surgeries.  The Committee notes that  whereas availability of beds remains
unchanged, i.e. 1531 during the period 2002-2005, bed occupancy rate is anticipated to go up from approximately 98%
to 119%. Safdarjung Hospital is a premier tertiary care hospital of Delhi which provides medical care to millions of
citizens not only from Delhi but also the neighbouring States. As a result, this hospital remains overcrowded resulting in
enormous pressure on the medical, paramedical, administrative staff, Laboratory and other infrastructural facilities.  
The Committee feels that in view of the ever increasing pressure in all respects, the hospital needs urgent expansion.
4.3
              The Committee notes construction of a multi-storeyed building is proposed in the area presently occupied by old
barrack/single/double storey building   (approximately 10% area of the hospital) The Committee was informed that the
matter of enhancement of ground coverage has been taken up with the Ministry of Urban Development.  The Committee
recommends that all the procedural formalities required for expansion of Safdarjung hospital should be finalised in a
fixed time frame and construction work should begin at the earliest.
4.4       The Vardhman Mahavir College with 100 seats and affiliated to the Guru Gobindsingh Indraprashtha University,
Delhi was established in Safdarjung hospital in November, 2001.
The Committee notes that admission for the first batch of MBBS students for academic session      2001-02 was done in
Jan-Feb., 2002, and the classless could commence from 18
th February, 2002 only.  The Committee, however, finds that
teaching faculty and the other required staff is still not in place.   The Committee is constrained to observe that the
college is at present functioning without a Principal.   The Committee is of the view that the Department should take
urgent steps for providing the teaching faculty, and paramedical staff and other infrastructure as per the prescribed
norms to make the college fully functional at the earliest.
4.5       The Committee observes that whereas non-plan funds for the hospital allocated in the first two years of Tenth
Plan could be utilized fully, plan allocation remained underutilized.  In view of the higher allocation of plan funds in
2004-05 ( from Rs. 45.00 crore in 2003-04 to Rs. 60.00 crores in 2004-05),  the Department should take all the required

V.        RAM MANOHAR LOHIA HOSPITAL, NEW DELHI
5.1       The Plan allocation for this hospital in BE 2003-04 was Rs. 19.00 crores which has been marginally increased to
Rs. 22.00 crores in BE 2004-05. In respect of setting up of a Post Graduate Institute at RML hospital, the Department
has informed that the total Tenth Plan allocation for Dr. RML Hospital is Rs. 150.00 crores which also includes the cost
of establishment of Post Graduate Institute at Dr. RML Hospital.  An EFC for establishment of Post Graduate Institute
at Dr. RML Hospital has already been approved.  However, the construction work for the Institute is yet to be started. 
An outlay of Rs.8.00 crores has been made for construction works under capital head for the Hospital during this
financial year i.e. 2004-05.  Besides, a provision of  Rs.50.00 lakhs as token amount for the Institute has also been kept
in the Plan budget of the Hospital.
5.2       The Committee is anguished to note that during the first two years of the Tenth Plan, nothing beyond obtaining
EFC approval for the Post Graduate Institute could be achieved.  The construction work is yet to start.  The Committee
feels that at this sluggish pace, the proposed teaching institute may not be established during the Tenth Plan.    The
Committee feels that a time bound Action Plan for completion of this Project must have been chalked out by the
Department in consultation with the hospital authorities.  The Committee strongly feels that the Department should
strictly adhere to
this Action Plan so that the proposed Dental Institute is established at the earliest.  If setting up of this PG institute
requires more funds, the Committee recommends that the same may be provided by the Department.
VI.       ALL INDIA INSTITUTE OF MEDICAL SCIENCES, (AIIMS) NEW DELHI.
6.1
              Plan allocation for AIIMS, New Delhi was Rs. 105.00 crores during BE 2003-04 which has been increased to
Rs. 119.00 for BE 2004-05.  The Committee notes that the AIIMS proposes to incur additional expenditure during the
current financial year for the three most important schemes, i.e. Rs. 17.65 crores for the Trauma Centre (for construction
alone), Rs. 22 crores for Dental Centre (for construction alone), and Rs. 9.32 crores for (Position Emission Topography
Machine).  Besides, after the revised EFC for AIIMS Trauma Centre is approved, nearly Rs. 47.05 crores would be
required for procurement of equipment. The Department has also informed that more equipment need to be procured in
Renal, Cardiology and Oncology Departments to raise the level of diagnostic and patient care services to a higher level.
6.2       The Committee is deeply anguished to observe that although considerable time has elapsed the Trauma Centre is
yet to be established. The Committee observes that the revised EFC for Trauma centre is yet to be approved.  Rs. 17.65
crores are proposed to be utilized for the construction of Trauma Centre in 2004-05.   Thereafter, approximately Rs.
47.05 crore would be required for procurement of equipment. The Committee apprehends  that due to procedural
formalities, formal approval of revised EFC may take some more time, which would result in  cost escalation in the
initial proposed amount for setting up of Trauma Centre.  The Committee is of the strong view, that the Department in
coordination with the AIIMS should get all the procedural formalities expedited so that the Trauma Centre becomes
functional at the earliest.

VIII                 NATIONAL VECTOR BORNE DISEASE CONTROL PROGRAMME
8.1       In pursuance of the concept of convergence, the National Vector borne Disease Control Programme was
approved in 2003-2004 which includes three ongoing programmes of Malaria, Filaria and Kala-Azar, J.E and
Dengue/DHF have been added. 
8.2       Under the National Anti-Malaria Control Programme, 100 percent funding is provided from Central budget for
seven North-Eastern States and also for the hundred districts covered by eight EMCP states under the World Bank
assisted Programme.  For the remaining States/UTs, cost sharing is on 50:50 basis.
8.3       The Committee was informed that the modified plan of action for control of Malaria has moderated the reported
number of cases at much less than 2.0million from approx.7.0 Million in 1970s, During the Tenth Plan, it has been
proposed to achieve an Annual Blood Examination Rate (ABER) of over 10% and Annual Parasite Incidence (API) at
1.3 or less against ABER and API for 2002 of 9.22 and 1.64 respectively.
8.4       Plan allocation for the National Anti Malaria Programme in BE 2003-2004, was Rs. 173.00 crores, which has
been enhanced to Rs. 187.00 crores in BE 2004-2005. The Committee was informed that the additional allocation was
proposed to be utilized to accelerate anti malaria activities by provisioning of Rapid Diagnostic Kits, and other required
material for difficult and tribal areas. It was also proposed to provide supervisory vehicles to States for improving
monitoring and supervision of the programme.

XIII. NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR CONTROL OF BLINDNESS (NPCB)
13.1     The NPCB was launched throughout the country in 1976 with the aim to reduce blindness from 1.4% to 0.3% by
2000 AD “ As per National Survey on Blindness carried out in 2001-02 in 15 States of the Country, prevalence of
blindness is estimated to be 1.1%.  According to the above survey, cataract (62.6%) continues to be the main cause of
blindness followed by uncorrected refractive error which is responsible for 19.7% of blinders in the country.
13.2
   A World Bank assisted Cataract Blindness Programme was implemented from April, 1994 to June, 2002 in seven
high prevalence states, namely Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, including Chatisgarh, Maharashtra, Orissa, Rajasthan,
Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh including Uttranchal.  Against a target of 11 million cataract operations, 15.35 million
cataract operations were performed during 1994-2002 in the Project states.  During the Project, IOL implantation
increased sharply from 3% in 1993 to 75% in 2002 with the percentage reaching 83% in 2003-04. 307 dedicated eye
units (Operation Theatres and/or wards) were constructed and equipped in Government sector and 30 in Non-
Government sector supported in the Project States.  More than 800 eye surgeons were trained in IOL surgery through 8-
week hands-on training in premier eye care institutions of the country.  The Project which was independently evaluated
for various parameters, was rated “Highly Satisfactory” and “Highly Sustainable” by the World Bank.
13.3
    The States of Gujarat, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Delhi and UTs of Pondicherry and
