REPORT 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1      The Indian Systems of Medicine  and Homoeopathy (ISM& H) were given an 
independent identity in the Mi nistry of Health and Family  Welfare in 1995 by creating a 
separate Department, which was renamed as Department of Ayurveda, Yoga and 
Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoe opathy (AYUSH) in November, 2003.  The 
Department is entrusted with the responsibil ity of developing and propagating officially 
recognized systems, namely, Ayurveda, Yoga, Naturopathy Siddha, Unani and 
Homoeopathy.  The AYUSH system of healthcare has a number of comparative strengths 
including a holistic approac h, personalized system of car e, fewer side effects, 
affordability, accessibility etc. 
  1.2    Encouragement to scientific research and education, laying down pharmacopoeial 
standards to ensure quality drugs, evolving good laboratory practices, following good 
manufacturing practices, regulating educati on standards, supplementing the efforts of 
State Governments in setting up speciality clin ics of AYUSH in allopathic hospitals and 
AYUSH wings in district allopathic hospitals, creating awareness through organization of 
Health Melas and other information, educa tion and communication means are some of 
the measures through  which the Department is  helping in the growth and wider reach of 
AYUSH System of healthcare.  
1.3    The Department has over the years de veloped a broad institutional framework to 
carry out the activities in the field of AYUSH.   The institutional framework consists of 
statutory bodies, apex research bodies, ap ex educational instit utes, Pharmacopoeial 
Committees for different systems of Indian Me dicine.       The Committee was informed 
that over the years the infr astructure under AYUSH sector has grown to 1355 hospitals 
with 53,296 bed capacity, 22,635 dispensaries, 450 undergraduate colleges, 99 colleges 
having Post Graduate Departments, 9,493 licensed manufacturing units and 7.25 lakh 
registered practitioners.  

1.4 The Secretary of the Department in he r presentation before the Committee gave 
an idea about the achievements made during the year in mainstreaming AYUSH at all 
levels, in improving access and quality of AYUSH delivery and in promoting preventive 
health care.

III.  STATUTORY INSTITUTIONS 
 
3.1 Eleventh Plan outlay for the two existi ng statutory  Institu tions, i.e. Central 
Council of Indian Medicine and Central Council of Homoeopathy and the proposed 
Central Pharmacy Council of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy is Rs.2.95 crore.  Major 
allocation of Rs. 2.00 crore was meant for setting up of the Pharmacy Council.  
3.2 The Committee has been observing that setting up of the Pharmacy Council 
has been getting unduly delayed. There is no standardized and uniform education 
system or curriculum available for training  pharmacists in AYUSH.  To facilitate 
regulation of education and practice of pharmacists in this sector, the Indian 
Medicine and Homoeopathy Pharmacy Council Bill, 2005 was introduced in 

Parliament on 12th August, 2005.  The Commit tee submitted its Report on the Bill 
to the Parliament on 28 th July, 2006.   The Committee finds it v ery disturbing that 
inspite of its submitting its Report well in time, the revised Bill is yet to come before 
Parliament.  Mere allocation of funds year after year will not result in setting up of 
the long-awaited Pharmacy Council of Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy. 
3.3 The Committee does not have much hope about the proposed Council 
coming up during the Eleventh Plan period .  Committee’s apprehensions arise from 
the allocation of Rs.1 lakh projected by  the Department for 2010-11 which has been 
finally fixed at Rs.50 lakhs by the Government .  On a specific query in this regard, 
the Committee was informed that the rev ised Bill has got the approval of the 
Minister and now has been sent to the Minist ry of Law for drafting the revised Bill.  
The Committee can only emphasize that every  conceivable effort needs to be made 
at all levels to ensure its passage in  Parliament in the  coming Monsoon Session. 
3.4 The Committee has been regularly urgi ng the Department to take concrete 
specific steps to expedite the process of updation of Central Registers of Indian Medicine 
and Homoeopathy which were far from satis factory.  The Committee notes that review 
meetings with the State Secretaries/ Direct ors (ISM&H) and Registra rs were being held 
for timely execution of the updation of State Re gisters.  The last such meeting had taken 
place in September, 2009.  Registration Commit tee of Central Council of Homoeopathy 
had also visited the Stat e/Board Council of Orissa and Himachal Pradesh in 
January/March, 2009.  The Committee appreciates  the efforts made by both the Councils 
for updation of Central Registers. However, as per the latest feedback given to the 
Committee, considerable backlog still continues to be there.

IV.  HOSPITALS  AND  DISPENSARIES 
ALL  INDIA  INSTITUTE  OF  AYURVEDA,  NEW DELHI 
 
4.1.1   A state-of-the-art All India Institute of  Ayurveda is being set up at New Delhi to 
provide quality patient care services under th e Ayurvedic System of Medicine.  The 
Institute once operational would facilitate quality research ai med at bridging gaps in the 
scientific information about quality, safe ty and efficacy of Ayurveda products and 
developing benchmarks of Ayurvedic education, research and health care. 
4.1.2 The Committee notes that against the Eleventh Plan Outlay of Rs.150.00 crore, 
only Rs.45.00 crore could be releas ed during the first  three y ears of the Plan period and 
the actual expenditure level till 15th March, 2010 is  Rs.11.74 crore.  The Committee also 
notes that the capital cost of the project as approved by the Cabinet is Rs.134.00 crore. 
 
4.1.3 The Committee has been monitoring the progress being made in the setting up of 
the All India Institute of Ayurveda in Ne w Delhi.  This ambitious project although 
conceptualized much before the beginning of the Eleventh Plan continues to remain on 
paper inspite of only two years remaining for the Plan period to end.  Track-record of 
progress made so far has been very discour aging.  After completi on of the preparatory 
exercise, EFC gave its approval on 9 th May, 2007.  Thereafter, it took more than one and 
a half years to get the Cabinet approval in January, 2008.  It took anot her year in getting 
M/s HSCC (India) Ltd. appointed as the Consultant for the project.  The Committee finds 
that the only progress made during  2009-10 is payment of advance of Rs.4.50 crore to 
the contractor through  HSCC.  In reply to a query regarding the present status of the 
Institute, the Department has informed that there is no change in the estimates of the 
project which is expected to be completed within two years from the date of 
commencement of construction viz. May, 2010. 

 
4.1.4 The Committee is happy to note the subm ission of the Department that there 
is no change in the estimates of the projec t and the project is on track and expected 
to be completed within two years from the date of commencement of construction, 
i.e., May 2010.  However, more than tw o years have already elapsed since the 
Cabinet approved the project in Janua ry, 2008 but the designs and plan of 
construction of the project are yet to be approved by the Delhi Development 
Authority (DDA).   Therefore, the constr uction of the building scheduled to 
commence in 2009-10 itself has been rescheduled for May, 2010.  
4.1.5 The Committee observes that it is impera tive on the part of the Department to 
vigorously pursue approval of designs and drawings of the building with the DDA at 
the highest level.   The Committee also  recommends that the progress of the 
construction of the building be monitored  at a sufficiently higher level in the 
Department so that no room is left for time overruns and the resultant cost 
overruns. 
 
CGHS  AYURVEDA  HOSPITAL,  NEW DELHI.

4.2.2     On a specific query about the latest status of upgradation of CGHS

However, the work relating to Operati on Theatre and Panchkarma Therapy

4.2.3   Another area of concern for the Committ ee is the non-filling up of required

the Department to make all efforts so as to ensure the outreach of envisaged health

Thereafter, a Central Sector Scheme for Conservation, Development and Sustainable 
Management of Medicinal Plants was a pproved by  the Cabinet Committee on Economic 
Affairs in June, 2008 with a total outlay of  Rs.321.30 crore during the Eleventh Plan.  
This scheme is a revised version of the Ce ntral Sector Scheme implemented till 2007-08 
and seeks to provide greater focus on the following activities: 
- Survey, Inventorisation and In-sit u conservation by setting up Medicinal 
Plants Conservation Areas. 
- Support to Joint Forest Management Committee (JFMCs)/Panchayats for 
value addition, warehousing and marketing 
- Ex situ Conservation of Prioriti zed species of Conservation concern 
-  Research and Development  
- Training, Education and Capac ity Building of stakeholders 
- Promotional Activity viz. home/school herbal gardens etc.

 IX   QUALITY CONTROL  OF  AYUSH  DRUGS
 
 
9.1 The AYUSH system of medicine is gove rned by Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 
and Rules made there under.  Enforcement provision of Chapter IV A of drugs & 
Cosmetics Act, 1940 is a responsibility of St ate Governments.  Establishment of state 
Drug Testing Laboratories is also a statutory responsibility of States under the Act.  To 
provide financial assistance to States for strengthening enforcement mechanism, the Drug 
Quality control scheme was started in IXth pl an.  Over the years, 29 state drug testing 
laboratories and 46 state pharmacies  have been set up and upgraded @ Rs.1.50 
crore/DTL and Rs. 2.00 crore/pharmacy.  In the Eleventh plan the scheme was revised to 
include three new components of strengthening  of the state   enforcement mechanism, 
upgrading the in-house testing facilities of manufacturing un its and assisting 
manufacturing units desirous of obtaining higher GMP like EU and WHO-GMP. 
9.2   The Committee notes that inspite of ma jor changes in the scheme, fund utilization 
status has been far from satisfactory.  Agains t an Eleventh Plan outlay of Rs.225 crore, 
funds amounting to only Rs. 45.00 crore could be allocated during the first three years of 
the plan period and expenditure reported upto 15th March, 2010 is only Rs.26.52 crore.   
9.3 The Committee finds that out of 29 Drug Tes ting laboratories assisted financially so 
far, 17 labs are established in terms of e quipment and buildings.  Other labs are in 
different stages of construc tion of building and procuremen t of equipment.   Only 46 
ASU&H pharmacies have been covered under the scheme so far. 
9.4   Committee’s attention has also been  drawn by the very low number of GMP 
compliant ASU&H Drug manufacturing units in  the country.  Out of total 9837 such 
units only 4663 units are GMP complying uni ts.  Ratio of GMP compliant /non-GMP 
compliant  units is very unsatisfactory wi th the maximum gap noticed in States like 

Assam, Haryana, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, U.P., Tamil Nadu, Uttarakhand, 
Punjab.  As many as 2336 legal notices have been issued/licenses cancelled so far.   
9.5  The Committee has taken note of a num ber of corrective measures suggested 
during the mid-term appraisal of Eleven th plan.  The Committee would like to 
emphasize that quality control and quality assurance are the major issues with 
reference to ASU drugs.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that corrective 
measures as suggested in the mid-term appraisal may be initiated without delay. 
 
 
9.6 The Committee also takes note of the fact that the rules regarding proper 
labeling on Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathic (ASU & H) drugs are not 
being complied with strictly.  The Commi ttee feels that the absence of proper 
labeling on ASU & H drugs may be a caus e of health hazards.  The Committee 
therefore, recommends that the monito ring mechanism in this regard be 
streamlined to ensure proper labeling on ASU & H drugs.

   Committee’s attention has been dr awn by some reports about successful 
treatment of Cancer patients by Ayurvedic drugs.  On a specific query about the 
veracity of these reports, the Committee was pleasantly surprised to note the 
significant achievements made by the Ayurvedic practitioners as indicated below:- 
- On the recommendation of the Scient ific Advisory Committee (Ayurveda), 
the CCRAS has initiated the study of  ‘CARCTOL’ a proprietary medicine 
of Vaid Nand Lal Tiwari of Jaipur supposed to have anti-cancer properties.  
For verifying the therapeutic claim,  standardization work has been 
completed by the Council.  Safety and toxicity studies have also been 
completed.  As informed by the Ex. Director-General, CCRAS, 
‘CARCTOL’ has also been subjected to in-vitro study at Advanced Cancer  
Research  Centre of TATA, Mumbai against 14 types of human cancer cells 
out of which it was found effective agains t pancreatic and lung cancer.  It 
was placed on record that CARCTOL is being used for treatment of cancer 

patients both in the country and ab road.  After preparation of dossiers, 
multi-centric clinical trials will be conducted. 
 
- Pre-clinical study of ‘Cancer Gaza Kesari’ a proprietary drug of Vaid 
Krishan Gopal of Ajmer has also been conducted by the Council in 
collaboration with Veterinary College,  Chennai.  The study revealed that 
the drug significantly reduced the mammary tumour incidence, latency, 
frequency, rate of growth and malignancy.  It was also subjected to in-vitro 
study against 14 types of human cancer cell line, out of which it has shown 
to be of effect against Pancreatic and Lung Cancer. 
 
-  Pilot project was sanctioned by the Ministry in 1996 to study the effects of 
metal based formulations prepared by  Vaid Balendu Prakash of Dehradun 
in the treatment of Acute pro-myllo lic Leukemia.  The Committee was 
informed that the drug was patented jointly by the Council with Germany.  
The Committee was also given to und erstand the Vaid Balendu Prakash 
has been treating Cancer patients with  the drug.On a specific query it was 
clarified that the Indo-German patent was a process patent.  So far only a 
pilot study of 21 patients has been conducted by the Council. Multi-
Cultural Clinical Trials of the dr ug were to be undertaken which would 
take four years. 
 
- The Committee also notes that ethical approval and release of funds for 
clinical trial of AYUSH QOL-2C at AIIMS has been completed.  
Registration of patent is to be init iated shortly. Simultaneously, 10 patients 
have been enrolled for study at St. Johns  Medical College, Bangalore.  This 
drug is reported to improve quality of life of those receiving 
chemotherapy/radiotherapy. (Para 6.6) 
 
 The Committee places on record its appreciation of commendable work 
being done by the Research Councils on th eir own and also in collaboration with 
well-known institutions in the country a nd abroad.  Work of the CCRAS touches 
both routine ailments as well as chronic ailments where permanent cure is yet to be 
found inspite of giant strides made by th e modern medical scie nces.  In such a 
scenario, for the treatment of the most deadly cancer disease, any significant 
achievement by any system of medicine sh ould be welcome to all.  The Committee 
considers it a strange irony that although large number of patients are getting relief 
by the drugs formulated by individuals a fter years of their single-handed research, 
official recognition still remains elusive to them.  The Committee strongly advocates 
the streamlining and expediting of the official certification process, so that humanity 
at large gets the long-awaited relief.  (Para 6.7)
