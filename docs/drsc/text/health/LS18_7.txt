HEALTH
I           INTRODUCTION
 
1.1
              The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare consists of two departments, namely the Department of Health and
Family Welfare and the Department of AYUSH.  The Central Government has the sole executive responsibility in respect
of subjects included in the Union list such as Port Quarantine including hospitals connected therewith, Seaman’s and
Marine hospitals and Union agencies for professional training and research.  In regard to items included in the State list,
such as Public Health, Hospitals and Dispensaries, the Central Government’s direct responsibility is restricted to the Union
Territories without legislature.  The Central Government regulates standards through legislation and providing enabling
framework for development of infrastructure in respect of matter falling under the Concurrent list e.g. Medical Education,
Prevention of Food Adulteration, Quality Control in the manufacture of drugs etc.  The Central Government evolves broad
policies and plans through the Central Council of Health, set up under the Article 263 of the constitution, arranges for
collection and exchange of information and renders financial and technical assistance to State Governments, Union
Territory Administration and other bodies for the implementation of important health programmes and various Centrally
Sponsored Schemes, including control of epidemics throughout the country.
1.2       Department of Health incurs expenditure either directly or through various institutions and establishments run by it
and by way of grants given to autonomous and voluntary organizations.  Grants to States and Union Territory
Administrations are mainly for Centrally Sponsored Health Schemes being implemented through the State Governments
and Union Territory Administrations.  They include national programmes for prevention, control and eradication of
diseases.
II   BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       Approved outlay for the Department of Health for the Tenth Plan is Rs. 10,252 crores which includes an outlay of
Rs. 999 crores transferred from the Department of Family welfare for the “Pradhan Mantri Swasthaya Suraksha Yojana”.
Total plan allocation for the first three years of Tenth Plan is Rs. 5308 crores, thus leaving a disproportionately high
balance of Rs. 4944 crores for the remaining two years of the Tenth Plan.
2.2       The Committee was informed that the Department had proposed an outlay of Rs. 3239 crores for the fourth year of
the Tenth Plan i.e. 2005-06. The Planning Commission, however, approved an outlay of only Rs. 2908 crores. The
Committee was given to understand that although the allocation for 2005-06 shows an increase of Rs. 700 crores on the
revised BE of 2004-05, it would have an adverse impact on a number of Centrally Sponsored and Central Schemes. Not
only this, projected demand of Non-plan funds of Rs. 1385.94 crores for 2005-06 has been drastically scaled down to Rs.
945.87 crores by the Ministry of Finance. Shortfall of non-plan allocation of Rs. 440.07 crores is likely to adversely affect
the maintenance of medical services.
2.3       The Committee observes that in accordance with the goals as contained in the National Health Policy 2002,
allocation for central health sector should imply a manifold increase in the budget requirement. The Committee is
in agreement with the contention of the Department that past trends of allocation of budgetary outlays and the
overall resource constraint do not indicate a very encouraging scenario. Two suggestions were put forth before the
Committee in this regard by the Department. First was that the external assistance for different schemes being
obtained from various agencies as grants be taken as an additionally over and above the budget. Committee was
given to understand that a major constraint of National AIDS Control Programme has been that of external
assistance which comes through Gross Budgetary Support as a result of which all external assistance has to be

7th Report of Committee on Health & Family Welfare

accommodated within the Annual Plan/ Five year plan outlay ceiling. The Committee is in agreement with the
viewpoint of the Department that external assistance coming in the shape of grant-in-aid  (distinct from loan)
should flow over and above the Gross Budgetary Support. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that the
Department should take up this issue with the Planning Commission.
2.4       The Department has also advocated that on the analogy of 2% Education Cess, a similar cess may also be
introduced in the health sector to enable realization of the goal of raising public spending on health to at least 2% of GDP
over the next five years. The Committee, however, has serious reservations on this proposal, not only due to such a
cess involving taxpayer’s money but also due to the past record of utilization of funds by the Department. While not
denying that approved outlay for 2005-06 will adversely affect a number of schemes, the most affected being the
ambitious ‘Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Suraksha Yojana’ the Committee is more perturbed by the trend of
allocation of non-plan funds. Non-provision of required non-plan funds for premier hospitals like AIIMS,
PGIMER, Chandigarh, NIMHANS, and Bangalore, even affecting the salary component of the staff is a matter of
serious concern.
2.5       The Committee notes that against an initially approved outlay of Rs. 5118.19 crores for Ninth Plan, subsequently
enhanced to 
Rs. 5975.40 crores, expenditure figures are Rs.  4906.40 crores, showing a shortfall of more than Rs. 1000 crores. Tenth
Plan allocation of Rs. 10252 crores is almost the double of Ninth Plan provision. However, utilization figures of the first
three years do not indicate any improvement in the performance level of Ninth Plan. On a specific query, the Secretary
admitted that there was only 71% utilization of enhanced allocation of Rs. 1800 crores in 2004-05. The Committee was
given to understand that existing framework of national health was lacking financial and managerial expertise. However,
with the recruitment of about 300 trained personnel at district, state and central level, funds would be managed in a
professional manner.
 2.6      The Committee is dismayed to note that as a result of declining trend of allocation of non-plan funds,
Department is compelled to divert plan funds to non-plan to meet the obligations of some major health institutions.
The Committee feels that this cannot be considered a healthy development, being against the prescribed norms.
Diversion of Plan funds to non-plan side very well indicates the poor status of schemes of the Department, very vital
for the development of the country as a whole. While on one side, Department is advocating imposition of 2% cess
for generation of additional funds, it is not in a position to utilize the allocated plan funds due to poor management
and incapacity of implementing agencies. The Committee would like to emphasize that the Department should take
remedial measures in the light of findings of mid-term appraisal of various schemes.
 
III  CENTRAL GOVERNMENT HEALTH SCHEME
3.1       The Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS) was started in           the year 1954 with the objective of
providing comprehensive medical care facilities to the Central Government employees and their family members. Now,
besides Central Government employees, the scheme also provides services to M.Ps, Ex- MPs, judges of Supreme Court
and High Court  (Sitting and retired), Freedom Fighters, Central Government Pensioners, Employees of certain

7th Report of Committee on Health & Family Welfare

autonomous bodies/semi Government Organizations, Accredited Journalists, and Ex- Governors and Ex-Vice Presidents of
India. There are 244 allopathic and 86 AYUSH dispensaries for 10.44 lakh card holders and 44.72 lakh beneficiaries of
CGHS.
3.2       The plan allocation of Rs. 16.50 crores for CGHS in BE 2004-05 has been substantially increased to Rs. 26.00
crores in BE 2005-06. The additional money, as informed by the Department, is meant fro expansion activists for CGHS
under which the newly sanctioned dispensaries for Dehradun, Bhubaneshwar, and Ranchi would be made operational. The
Committee also notes that during 2004-05 and 2005-06 10 new Allopathic two Ayurvedic, one Unani, three Homoeopathy
dispensaries in different of the country are proposed to be opened. Besides that, buildings of 14 dispensaries in Delhi are at
present under construction. The Committee would like to have the details about the progress made in this regard.  
3.3       The Committee notes that allocation of Rs. 40 crores for CGHS during the Ninth Plan was subsequently enhanced
to Rs. 62 crores. However, allocated funds remained under-utilized (utilization figures being only Rs. 42.19 crores). There
has not been optimum utilization of Tenth Plan allocation of Rs. 80 crores for CGHS so far. During 2004-05 against an
allocation of Rs. 16.50 crores, expenditure incurred up to 9
th March, 2005 is only Rs. 13.95 crores. The Committee hopes
that enhanced funds amounting to Rs. 26 crores for 2005-06 will be fully utilized for the proposed expansion plan
for the CGHS.
3.4       The Committee has been monitoring for the last two-three years the progress made in the notification for combined
formulary of CGHS/MSO in respect of proprietary medicine based on recommendation of the Report of the Shrivastava
Committee. The Committee finds that not much progress seems to here been made as the process of fixation of rates of
drugs formulary is still not over. The Committee reiterates that the Department should expedite the notification of
over-delayed drugs formulary without any further delay.
3.5       Under the existing system, CGHS beneficiaries can obtain the medicines prescribed by the specialists from the
authorized local chemist. In the absence of authorized local chemist, beneficiaries are permitted to purchase the medicines
from the open market, reimbursable afterwards. The Committee has been observing that the authorized chemists go on
strike for long periods at regular intervals, thereby putting the beneficiaries to great inconvenience. On a specific query
about the factors responsible for such developments, paucity of funds has been citied as the main reason by the
Department. The Committee notes that during the last three years, there has been increase in the budget allocation for
supply of local purchase indent medicines through the authorized local chemists. While appreciating the release of
additional funds for the purpose, the Committee is of the view that there is a need for streamlining the system so
that required funds are available in time for clearing the anticipated bills of authorized local chemists. The
Committee would also like to mention that a review of the existing system of procuring of specialist prescribed
medicines should be made so as to remove the bottlenecks/ constraints, if any.
3.6       The Committee has been emphasizing for the lasts few years for the need to revert to the earlier two shift system of
CGHS dispensaries. The Committee has been informed that as recommended by the SIU Report on the Norms study of
CGHS ( allopathic) dispensaries, 466 additional posts of medical officers and other required para-medical and group ‘D’
staff are required to be created for the purpose. The Department has expressed its inability to do so due to financial
constraints. The Committee is not fully convinced by the Departments, projection of need for such a large number of

posts. The Committee would like to point out that there has been gradual increase in the number of allopathic
dispensaries. Earlier also CGHS dispensaries were working in double shift. The Committee would like to have the
details about the number of dispensaries working in double shift and the staff in position in such dispensaries and
the present sanctioned strength and in position.
            The Committee feels that the present working hours can be easily divided into two shifts as per the earlier
practice. The Committee fails to understand the rationale for projection of the requirement at such a high scale.
The Committee would, therefore, emphasize that the Department should get a review done based on the practical
requirement on the analogy of earlier double shift system.
3.7       Facilities of outpatient care and emergency/ inpatient care, free supply of drugs, labs and radiological
investigations, specialist consultation etc. are being provided to the CGHS beneficiaries through Government recognized
private hospitals/diagnostic centres. At present there are 628 private hospital/ diagnostic centres in 18 cities in the country.
3.8       The Committee finds that referral of CGHS beneficiaries to seven private hospitals and two private diagnostic
centres in Delhi has been stopped w.e.f  23.09.04 based on an inspection carried out by the Standing Committee of DGHS.
Some of the irregularities noticed in the inspection are; -
(i)
                  Patients kept in ICU/ ICCU without justification
(ii)                Planned surgeries shown as emergency admission
(iii)                Unrelated and irrelevant investigations done
(iv)              Gross error in billing the CGHS beneficiaries
3.9       The Committee understands that at present a CBI inquiry is going on a complaint received with regard to
recognition/reimbursement  of hospitals bills. The Committee is of the view that review of the existing system of this
specific facility available to the CGHS beneficiaries should be carried out in the light of the findings of ongoing CBI
inquiry.
3.10     The Committee is unhappy to note that the CGHS network has not been able to achieve its desired objective
of providing efficient medical service to its beneficiaries. The Network of CGHS dispensaries has degenerated to a
stage where patients receive neither treatment nor consultation. In fact, beneficiaries with very basic ailments only
such as cold and cough are attended to in these dispensaries . In Committee’s view, the position today is that
treatment of the beneficiaries is practically taking place at the specialist level only in a few hospitals and in
designated dispensaries in Delhi, and the number of specialists available in Delhi vis-a vis CGHS beneficiaries is
very low. Position of CGHS beneficiaries residing in NCR such as Noida, Gurgaon is more worse as they are given
dates of visit by specialists, sometimes ranging from 45 days to 60 days and the beneficiaries have to wait for the
arrival of specialists for long periods. Therefore, the idea of providing a convenient health care to the Central
Government employees has remained a dream. The Committee strongly recommends that Government should take
immediate steps to ensure that CGHS dispensaries, function in such a manner that the dispensaries, instead of
being a place for referring patients to specialists and disbursement of medicines recommended by them, should
become health centres where its beneficiaries actually receive proper treatment. It is high time that the role and
function of CGHS dispensaries is re-determined.

3.11     For years now, a large number of CGHS dispensaries are operating from rented accommodations, which are not at
all suited for either the doctors/staff or the patients. The Committee is aware that Government is working to house such
dispensaries in either their own building or in some better accommodation. But the progress has been slow and it seems to
have made no tangible difference during the past several years. The Committee strongly recommends that the
Government should take stock of the dispensaries which have not been housed properly so that they move into
preferably their own building or at least in some bigger accommodation for the time being in the interest of the
doctors/staff and the patients. To start with at least those buildings for which land has been acquired / allotted can
be constructed on priority basis     
 
IV    SAFDARJUNG HOSPITAL 
4.1   Plan allocation for Safdarjung hospital for 2005-06 is Rs. 84.30 crores as compared to Rs. 60.00 crores provided in
2004-05. The Committee notes that there has been steady increase in the plan allocation for the hospital keeping in view its
requirements for additional infrastructure. Last year, Committee had commented upon the increasing pressure on the
available health facilities with sanctioned bed strength continuing to remain at 1531 and no increase whatsoever in the
strength of medical/para-medical staff for a considerably long period. The Committee, however, finds that against a Ninth
Plan allocation of Rs. 122.93 crores, utilization figures remained at Rs. 97.12 crores. An enhanced allocation of Rs. 230
crores for the Safdarjung Hospital for Tenth Plan has failed to make any noticeable improvement as during the first three
years, allocated funds remained under-utilized.
4.2       The Committee has been informed that out of the allocation of Rs. 60 crores for 2004-05, Rs. 10.09 crores will not
be utilized due to delay in procurement of MRI, cobalt machine etc. The Committee is perturbed to observe that delay in
completion of procedural formalities for procurement of equipment/machine continues to be the main factor responsible
for under-utilization of funds. While noting that equipment worth Rs. 566.35 lakhs was purchased during 2004-05, the
Committee finds that as many as 46 categories of equipment are at different stages of purchase. What is more surprising is
that the contracts have been signed for eight equipment amounting to Rs. 80.63 lakhs. The Committee fails to
understand the constraints being faced in procuring these equipment for which contracts have been signed and
funds are also available. The Committee strongly feels that there is urgent need for review of entire procurement
process so as to eliminate the bottlenecks being faced.
4.3       The Committee’s attention has been drawn by media reports about two wings of new OPD Blocks at Safdarjung
Hospital, although completed in August and October in 2004, remaining inoperational for want of formal handover by
CPWD. On a specific query in this regard, the Committee was given to understand that Delhi Fire Department had
inspected the building two times in December 2004 and the last inspection was done on the 3
rd March, 2005. The outcome
of the inspection report is, however, awaited. The Committee has been given to understand that the building of the OPD
complex being high rise in nature it cannot be handed over to the hospital without clearance from the Fire Department .
The Committee, however, is not happy with the existing system of getting NOC from the Fire Department. 
Although more than six months have lapsed after completion of the OPD building, it remains inoperational for
want of requisite clearance from the Fire Department. The Committee strongly feels that the present guidelines

/norms of Fire Department need to be reassessed in the present scenario.
4.4       Another disturbing trend noticed by the Committee is the undue delay in getting the building for VMMC
constructed. The Committee notes that the construction of VMMC building could not be taken up due to restrictions
applicable to Safdarjung Hospital in ground coverage of 25% and FAR 100. With the Ministry of Urban Development
agreeing to increase the ground coverage from 25% to  35%  and FAR from 100 to 150 vide notification dated 20 th
September, 2004 construction of the building should have started. However, as per the information made available to the
Committee, time-schedule is being drawn up for completing the construction by March,2006. The Committee hopes that
construction of the VMMC building will be completed within the time-schedule, thereby eliminating any increase in
the original estimate of expenditure.
4.5 According to the information submitted before the Committee, there are 30 sanctioned posts of Central Health Services
in VMMC. Out of these 17 posts are filled and out of the remaining unfilled posts, offer of appointment for 2 posts has
been issued, for another 2 posts pre-appointment formalities are being completed, whereas 4 posts could not be filled as
the matter is sub-judice. For the remaining 5 posts, the matter has been taken up with the UPSC. It has further been
informed that so far as teaching posts of clinical subjects is concerned, the matter relating to grant of teaching designation
to specialists working in the Safdarjung Hospital has been taken up with the concerned University.
            The Committee recommends that the Department should make serious efforts to get the vacant posts filled
up at the earliest. The Committee emphasizes that the Department should get the construction of VMMC expedited
and fulfil the prescribed norms for teaching and non-teaching faculty and the required infrastructure before the
inspection by MCI in March, 2006.
 
V     DR. RAM MANOHAR LOHIA HOSPITAL, NEW DELHI
5.1       Allocation for Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia hospital for Ninth Plan was Rs. 84.47 crores out of which an amount of Rs.
70.26 crores could be utilized. Tenth Plan provision of Rs. 150 crores for the hospital shows a substantial increase.
However, the trend or utilization of funds in the first two years of Tenth Plan does not reveal a very encouraging picture as
allocated funds remained under-utilized, reasons being change in the accounting procedure and non-fulfillment of vacant
posts. Details of actual expenditure about 2004-05 are not known but position may be more or less the same.
5.2       The Committee notes that plan allocation of Rs. 47.30 crores has been proposed for 2005-06 which shows a
substantive increase when compared with allocation of Rs. 22.00 crores for 2004-05. Provision of Rs. 11.81 crores out of
Rs. 47.30 crores has been proposed for the construction of building of Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and
Research. Foundation stone for the institute hasbeen laid on 25
th November, 2004. Project cost of the Institute as approved
by EFC is Rs. 42.96 crores which is expected to be commissioned by August, 2006. The Committee is happy to note
that the hospital possesses the basic infrastructure, faculty, clinical materials and the environment required for
setting up the Institute. The Committee would, however, like to emphasize that the Department should monitor the
construction of the Institute Building in co-ordination with the hospitals authorities and the construction agency, to
ensure the completion of this project within the prescribed time-limit.
5.3       The Committee observed that besides the project of Post Gradate Institute of Medical Education, nine more

VI     ALL INDIA INSTITUTE OF MEDICAL SCIENCES
  6.1     A sum of Rs. 119.00 crores for BE 2004-05 was allocated for AIIMS which was substantially increased to Rs. 170
crores at the RE stage. The plan allocation of 2005-06 stands at Rs. 201.26 crores. The Department has informed that
expenditure has been restricted to Rs. 151.00 crores in 2004-05 to facilitate re-appropriation of Rs. 19 crores to non-plan.
The Committee expresses its serious concern over diversion of funds from plan to non-plan side. What is more
disturbing is that actual expenditure during 2004-05 is only Rs. 86 crores, with allocated funds amounting to Rs. 65
crores remaining unutilized. The reason citied for these savings is that revised proposals for Trauma Center and
Dr. B.R. Ambedker Institute of Rotary Cancer Hospital are yet to be approved by the competent authority. The
Committee finds that not much progress seems to have been made in the Trauma Center project. The project was
initially approved for Rs. 54.14 crores. However, due to change in the scope of work and additional statutory
requirements, revised EFC for Rs. 177.82 crores was submitted on 11
th December, 2003. Since then, the revised
proposal is under the process of being placed before the CCEA. The Committee has been given to understand that
comments of all appraisal agencies Ministry of Finance, Planning Commission etc. have been obtained on the draft
cabinet note and are in the process of finalization. The Committee notes that the date of completion of the Trauma
Center will be six months from the date of approval of CCEA. The Committee apprehends that there would be
further cost escalation if the project fails to get the final clearance without any further delay.
6.2       The Committee has also been monitoring the progress made in the completion of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar Institute of
Rotary Cancer Hospital with an initial cost of Rs. 19.50 crores. The project was to be completed by September, 1996.
However, since then project cost has escalated to Rs. 136.25 crores which may increase further. A Committee constituted
for fixation of responsibility of time and cost over of the project has duly submitted its Report. However, the revised EFC
is still awaiting the comments of the Planning Commission.
6.3       The Commission expresses its serious concern over the very tardy progress made in the completion of two
very important projects meant for saving precious human lives in the premier Institute of the country. The
Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for streamlining the procedure for grant of formal approval
to the proposed projects. Project Proposals keep on moving from one stage to another from one authority to
another with the initial estimated cost increasing manifold. What is more disturbing is that procedural formalities
do not end with the project getting final approval from the competent authority. Once the construction starts,
another set of concerned agencies take over. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Department

should review the present system and take steps for removal of all unnecessary/ repetitive/ formalities arising in the
operationlisation of a proposed project.
6.4       An outlay of Rs. 999 Crores has been transferred from the Department of Family Welfare for the Tenth Plan for a
new scheme, i.e.“Pradhan Mantri Swasthya Surkasha Yojana”. Under this scheme setting up of six AIIMS like institutions
one each in Bihar, Chattisgarh, Himachal Pradesh,Orissa, Rajasthan and Uttarachal and upgradation of six medical
colleges, one each in the states of Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, Jammu & Kashmir, Tamil Nadu, Utter Pradesh, and West
Bangal is envisaged . The Committee is, however, constrained to note that by the third year of the Tenth Plan, the project
has only been cleared by EFC and is now to be placed before the competent authority for approval. Comments of
Department of Expenditure have been received and are under examination in the Ministry. Comments of the Planning
Commission are still awaited. For want of formal approval from CCEA, no major activity could be initiated during 2004-
05. As a result, allocation of Rs. 60 lakhs for the purpose had to be reduced to Rs. 10 lakhs at the RE stage. Pending these
clearances, start-up activities like construction of boundary wall , process for selection of Project Consultant and selection
of architectural  concepts/ designs have been taken up. The Committee does not visualize any major progress in this
ambitious project in 2005-06, keeping in view the past track record in such proposals. The year 2005-06 may perhaps be
utilized only for getting formal approval form CCEA. By then, only one year of Tenth Plan would be left and Rs. 999
crores earmarked for the project would thus remain unspent. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Department should initiate all required steps to get expedited the clearance of the project “Pradhan Mantri
Swasthaya Suraksha Yojana”.

Nicobar Islands in the country has established the urgent requirement of such facilities.  The Committee is, however,
perturbed to note that the project on Self-contained Container based Mobile Hospital was approved in April, 2003.
However, the revised cost estimates of the project could be finally approved by SFC only in February, 2004.  Another year
has passed and the only progress made is the engagement of M/S Hospital Services Consultancy Corporation as the
Procurement Consultant for the project. The Committee notes that 5 to 6 mobile units are proposed to be set up all over the
country and one such unit would be procured during the Tenth Plan itself.  The Committee would impress upon the
Department not   to further delay the Procurement of the proposed Mobile Unit, specially in view of disaster prone
geographical position of the country.

II   BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
            The Committee observes that in accordance with the goals as contained in the National Health Policy 2002,
allocation for central health sector should imply a manifold increase in the budget requirement. The Committee is in
agreement with the contention of the Department that past trends of allocation of budgetary outlays and the overall
resource constraint do not indicate a very encouraging scenario. Two suggestions were put forth before the Committee in
this regard by the Department. First was that the external assistance for different schemes being obtained from various
agencies as grants be taken as an additionally over and above the budget. Committee was given to understand that a major
constraint of National AIDS Control Programme has been that of external assistance which comes through Gross
Budgetary Support as a result of which all external assistance has to be accommodated within the Annual Plan/ Five year
plan outlay ceiling. The Committee is in agreement with the viewpoint of the Department that external assistance coming

2.3)

Mantri Swasthya Suraksha Yojana’ the Committee is more perturbed by the trend of allocation of non-plan funds. Non-
provision of required non-plan funds for premier hospitals like AIIMS, PGIMER, Chandigarh, NIMHANS, and Bangalore,
even affecting the salary component of the staff is a matter of serious concern.  (Para 2.4)
            The Committee is dismayed to note that as a result of declining trend of allocation of non-plan funds, Department

          The Committee expresses its serious concern about the status of medical/para-medical/technical/general staff in

both routine and emergency to ever-increasing number of patients not only from Delhi and NCR but also from adjoining
states. The Committee feels that with such a large number of vacancies, specially of medical posts, nursing posts and
technical posts, these hospitals can never be in a position to provide the required services. The Committee is more worried
about the situation in Safdarjung Hospital where some posts have simply lapsed. In some cases, status of the posts is not
even confirmed, they seem to have lapsed.  With a large number of vacancies of para-medical, technical and those related
to general administration, there can not be proper working conditions for the doctors working specially in Safdarjung
hospital. There seems to be total lack of co-ordination between the Hospital Administration and DGHS. The Committee
feels that position will be similar more or less in other Central Govt. hospitals outside Delhi. The Committee is of the view
that the Department will have to take the initiative in revamping and streamlining the administration of these hospitals.
First step should be the immediate review of all vacant/lapsed posts of all categories. Thereafter, the Department should
initiate the process of filling up the vacant posts at the earliest. The Committee is also of the view that a monitoring/co-
ordination mechanism needs to be evolved which can serve as a link between DGHS and hospital administration.  (Para
