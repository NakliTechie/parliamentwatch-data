CHAPTER – I 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Health Research in India 
 
1.1 Health Research is primarily a guide to suggest improvements in the health care of the 
population of a country. Along with headway in healthcare delivery, well-directed research is 
essential to improve public health. In India, health research topography is highlighted as a 
potential market, fuelled by a large population, proactive government initiatives, rising public 
-private partnership, increased adoption of digital health technologies, recognition of 
importance of clinical trials, etc. At the same time, the challenges and gaps in the area 
include dearth of proper research culture, infrastructure deficiencies, inadequate funding for 
research initiatives, weak intersectoral linkages, misalignment between funded research 
areas and the country’s actual health needs, etc. 
 
Department of Health Research 
 
1.2 Department of Health Research (DHR) was created as a separate Department within the 
Ministry of Health & Family Welfare by an amendment to the Government of India 
(Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 on 17thSeptember, 2007. The Department became 
functional with the aim is to bring modern health technologies to the people through research 
and innovations related to diagnosis, treatment methods and vaccines for prevention and to 
translate such innovations into products and processes in synergy with concerned 
organisations and stakeholders and finally introduce these innovations into public health 
system.

1.3.5 Taking into account the requirement of professionals having good scientific astuteness 
in the emerging field of Health Research, the Committee asked about the plans of the 
Government regarding any programme to incentivize Indian researchers working abroad to 
return and contribute to health research in India and also about the programme to encourage 
young scientists and researchers to pursue careers in health research. In response the 
Department has informed about the program “to encourage research personnel [Non-resident 
Indian (NRI), Persons of Indian Origin (PIO), Overseas Citizen of India (OCI)] serving 
abroad, to come back to India for undertaking research in identified areas”. This Scheme has 
been designed to provide contractual research positions to the Indian scientists and 
researchers settled abroad who are willing to come back to India on a full-time basis or for a 
short duration to pursue medical/health research in India and take up health research projects 
in collaboration with Indian scientists, particularly in areas of national health priority. Each 
selected researcher shall be eligible to receive a consolidated stipend of Rs.1.2 lakhs per 
month for 3 years. Research Grants would be available for research studies/projects not 
exceeding Rs. 20 lakhs/year. The project will be extendable for another 2 years at the 
discretion of the Secretary DHR on the recommendation of the Expert Committee. In case the 
researcher finds a permanent position during the tenure of the position, the remuneration shall 
be discontinued from the day he/she joins in permanent position, but he/she may continue 
with the Research Grant for the project, for the remaining duration of the project.

1.3.6 The Committee takes note of the program envisaged by the Department to 
attract Non-resident Indians (NRIs), Persons of Indian Origin (PIO), Overseas Citizens 
of India (OCI) serving abroad back to India. At the same time, the Committee expresses 
its concerns about continuing Brain Drain, the exodus of trained, proficient and talented 
Indian professionals to developed countries. India's brain drain is caused by a lack of 
higher education options, research funding and facilities, pay & perks that is lower than 
global standards in both the public and private sectors and fewer opportunities in terms 
of career progression, especially in specialized fields. Other facilitative factors like 
environment of equity, transparency, protection of ownership of genuine research 
outcomes, delayed systemic response, etc also add to contributing factors.   
1.3.7 The Committee, even though aware of the funding limitations, is not fully 
convinced of the adequateness of a consolidated stipend of Rs.1.2 lakhs per month for 3 
years for a selected researcher under the Scheme to attract professionals from abroad. 
The Committee feels that the Department should consider keeping the window open to 
enhance the quantum of stipend, at least based on the project proposals presented and 
the remuneration the person is receiving in the foreign country. The same flexible 
approach should be adopted in case of the quantum of grant, the Committee desires.  
Accordingly, to address the intricate issue of brain drain, India must adopt a 
comprehensive strategy that includes enhancing higher education, strengthening 
research infrastructure, and implementing policies to improve the standard of living. 
These measures would encourage skilled Indian professionals to return from abroad 
and undertake domestic health research projects. A unified and long-term approach is 
essential for India to retain and recover its most valuable asset—human capital. 
1.3.8 Further, the Department should look into the possibility of involving private 
sector which can contribute through competitive pay packages, which would be well 
within their realm as promotion of PPP.  
Landmarks in Health Research in India 
 
1.4 Certain landmark outcomes or breakthroughs achieved during the last decade in the 
area of health research in India, funded by the Government of India are as follows: 
A. Communicable Disease 
i. Enabling rapid containment and response strategies to outbreak and pandemic 
investigations for Monkeypox, Zika, and Nipah.  
ii. Development of Covaxin, India’s first fully indigenous COVID-19 vaccine, in an 
exemplary public-private partnership (PPP) with Bharat Biotech.  
iii. National Sero Surveys for COVID-19, tracking the virus's spread and informing 
public health policies.  
iv. Indian SARS-CoV-2 Genomics Consortium (INSACOG) contributed to genome 
sequencing for identifying Variants of Concern, shaping India’s pandemic 
response. 

6 
 
v. Transformative initiatives in tuberculosis (TB) control, through the India TB 
Research Consortium (ITRC), which won the prestigious Kochon Prize. Key 
innovations include National TB Survey, newer drug regimens for DR-TB, rapid 
molecular testing with TruNAT, the C-TB test, and the TIE-TB project, which 
won the BMJ Award for its innovative active case finding model. 
vi. Research on the Triple Drug Regimen (IDA) and DEC-fortified salt, significantly 
reducing Filariasis transmission.  
vii. The Vaishali Model for Kala Azar elimination demonstrated a successful 
intervention strategy in eliminating the disease.  
viii. For Malaria, ICMR’s research has demonstrated elimination models in Madhya 
Pradesh, Odisha, and Punjab, guiding India’s national malaria elimination 
roadmap. 
B. Non- Communicable disease (NCD) 
i. The India Disease Burden Study, conducted in collaboration with the Indian 
Council of Medical Research (ICMR), Public Health Foundation of India (PHFI), 
and Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME), provided crucial insights 
into the shifting burden of diseases in India, highlighting the growing prevalence 
of NCDs.  
ii. The National Cancer Registry Program (NCRP) instrumental in tracking cancer 
incidence, mortality, and trends across India. 
iii. The Indian Diabetes Study (InDIAB) has gathered epidemiological data from 22 
states critical for formulating preventive and management strategies.  
iv. The India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI), implemented across 104 
districts, integrated hypertension screening into primary healthcare, earning global 
recognition with a UN Award for its impact on reducing cardiovascular risk.  
v. The Stroke Network, aimed at improving stroke surveillance, prevention, and 
treatment outcomes, ensuring timely interventions for stroke patients.  
C. Reproductive Health, Child Health & Nutrition 
i. Invention of RISUG, a male contraceptive.  
ii. The subdermal, single-rod contraceptive implant IMPLANON for long-term birth 
control options.  
iii. Formulation of Balamrutham and Balamrutham Plus for Telangana and Andhra 
Pradesh to combat malnutrition.  
iv. Additionally, the Vitamin A Prophylaxis Program critically analysed the need for 
Mega-dose Vitamin A Supplementation (MDVAS), leading to a recommendation 
for targeted supplementation instead of universal coverage. 

7 
 
v. The Dietary guideline for Indians, revised Recommended Dietary Allowances 
(RDA) and the report on "What India Eats" provide scientific evidence on dietary 
patterns.  
vi. The Indian Food Composition Tables offer comprehensive nutritional data, while 
the District Level Nutrition Project and urban nutrition data collection help 
policymakers address regional disparities in malnutrition. 
vii. The Women and Infant Integrated Interventions for Growth (WINGS) study 
demonstrated that integrated interventions in health, nutrition, psychosocial care, 
and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WaSH) during preconception, pregnancy, and 
early childhood substantially reduced low birth weight and stunting at 24 months. 
viii. Development of The Nutrition Atlas 2.0 serves as a one-stop digital platform that 
provides interactive access to national nutrition data.   
ix. The Nutrify India Now 2.0 mobile app, an advanced version with updated 
Estimated Average Requirement (EAR) and Recommended Dietary Allowance 
(RDA), integrates wearable devices for personalized diet tracking, to dynamically 
create recipes, monitor nutritional intake, and maintain a personal diet diary. 
D. Innovative initiatives 
The i-Drone project - using drones to transport vaccines & medical supplies in the 
challenging terrains of the North Eastern States and Himachal Pradesh, blood 
bags in metros, Intra-operational tissue samples for advanced diagnosis in 
Karnataka & TB sputum samples in tribal regions of Telangana. 
1.4.1 While going through the major landmarks in Health Research in India in the 
last decade, the Committee notices that the India has done considerably well in the 
field of Communicable Diseases, commensurate to its own needs as well as to 
contributing the global knowledge pool. The Committee is appreciative of this 
achievement. The India Disease Burden Study, mentioned by the Department as a 
breakthrough initiative, provided crucial insights into the shifting burden of diseases 
in India, highlighting the growing prevalence of Non-Communicable diseases (NCDs). 
Going by the indicators provided by the Study, India’s efforts in NCDs needs further 
impetus. 
1.4.2 Accordingly, the Committee observes that the policies, priorities and funding for 
health research in the country should be optimally aligned with its actual health needs 
rather than with the global research agenda. Hence, India should prioritise research on 
under-studied diseases in the country, such as cardiovascular, respiratory diseases, etc. 
along with research on public health, healthcare infrastructure/systems and 
implementation of clinical medicine. The Committee feels that alignment of health 
research with the National Health Policy & Priorities would be cardinal in this direction 
and hence recommends, accordingly.

Role of public-private partnerships (PPPs) in Health Research Funding 
1.5 Taking into cognizance the successful Public-Private Partnership (PPP) of ICMR 
with Bharat Biotech which led to the development of Covaxin, India’s first fully indigenous 
COVID-19 vaccine, the Committee sought to know about the current scenario regarding the 
private funded health research in India and the measures adopted by the Government to 
encourage PPP for health research in India. In reply, DHR submitted that Public private 
partnerships are pivotal for development of innovations. Despite absence of an institutional 
mechanism for PPP, ICMR has successfully completed several projects under public private 
partnerships.  The Department submitted that ICMR proposes a multi-faceted strategy to 
encourage private funding for health research in India, recognizing the critical role of PPPs, 
Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) contributions, and Venture Capital investments in 
advancing medical innovations. To attract CSR funds, ICMR is engaging with Corporates 
and PSUs to allocate a greater share of their CSR budgets toward priority research areas, 
including vaccines, antimicrobial resistance (AMR), rare diseases, and AI-driven healthcare 
solutions. To facilitate greater industry participation, ICMR also supports technology 
transfer and licensing agreements, allowing private firms to commercialize ICMR-
developed innovations under fair pricing mechanisms.  
1.5.1 The Committee reckons that since the Government is always under compulsion 
to apportion the limited financial resources amongst the varied needs of the country, it 
would be prudent to leverage the financial resources, technological expertise and 
innovation capabilities of the Private sector in furthering the needs of the health 
sector, especially in research. The Committee holds the view that when cutting edge 
research and innovation outcomes, produced in collaboration with the Private sector, 
are dovetailed with policy initiatives and wide access infrastructure of the 
Government, it can change the health care landscape of India. Taking into account the 
fact that promotion of public-private partnership is one of the businesses allocated to 
DHR as per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government of India. The 
Committee recommends that the Department should consider adopting more 
measures to secure the participation of the Private sector in the health sector in the 
Country with tailor made collaborative and mutually beneficial outreach measures. 
1.5.2 The Committee notes that while on the one hand DHR acknowledges the 
importance of Public Private Partnerships (PPPs) for the development of innovations 
and informs that ICMR has successfully completed several projects under PPP mode, 
there is an absence of an institutional mechanism for PPP. In view of the submission of 
DHR and the fact that promotion of PPP is one of the businesses allocated to DHR as 
per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government of India, the Committee 
recommends that DHR should come out with an institutionalised mechanism for 
promoting PPP.  
Emerging Technologies and Ethical Concerns related to Health Research 

9 
 
1.6 In the emerging world of AI-driven healthcare solutions, database is crucial, hence 
the Committee was curious to understand the current ethical challenges in research, such as 
informed patient consent, data privacy and its impact on health research landscape in India. 
The Committee also wanted to know the steps taken by Government to ensure ethical 
compliance without compromising innovation, especially when vulnerable populations are 
involved. In their written submission in this regard, the Department shared the various steps 
taken to ensure ethical compliance and to encourage innovation while protecting the rights 
and safety of all, including the vulnerable.  The ICMR National Ethical Guidelines 2017 
established the ethical framework for health research in India. All biomedical and health 
research conducted in the country must follow these guidelines to address ethical concerns 
that may arise including participant consent and data privacy.  
1.6.1 Further, New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 govern the conduct of Clinical 
Trials, Biomedical and Health Research, Academic Clinical Trials, Bio Availability/Bio 
Equivalence studies, etc., primarily to ensure robust conduct of clinical trials for the 
purpose of approvals to introduce new products in the Indian market with right ethics 
safeguards. The regulatory architecture involved may be summarised as under: 
(i) Registration of Ethics Committees: To ensure ethical compliance and functioning 
of ethics committees, it is mandatory that all ethics committees involved in biomedical 
and health research be registered with the Department of Health Research (Naitik portal) 
and also with Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (SUGAM portal) if 
applicable. This helps to improve transparency and accountability of the ethics review 
process. 
(ii) Ethics Approval: As per the established rules and guidelines, prior approval from 
the relevant ethics committees is mandatory before commencing any biomedical and 
health research. This is to protect the rights, safety, dignity and welfare of research 
participants in any biomedical health research or clinical trials. 
(iii) Protection of Vulnerable: The ICMR National Ethical Guidelines, 2017 provides 
an ethical framework and specifically Chapter 5 of this guidelines outlines the informed 
consent process, while Chapter 6 addresses ethical considerations for research involving 
vulnerable populations, emphasizing measures to safeguard their rights, safety and well-
being. Clinical; and 
(iv) Trials Registry of India: ICMR-CTRI Today, is another initiative to improve 
transparency, accountability, and accessibility of clinical trials in order to re-establish 
public trust in clinical trial data. All clinical trials are required to be prospectively 
registered in a centralized clinical trials registry. 
1.6.2 The Committee recognises the importance of adoption of new technologies, 
innovation and constant endeavour for development of new drugs and treatment 
methods. Clinical trials are an inalienable data generating component of this 
continuing quest. The regulatory architecture now in place in the Country are taken 
note of, however, the Committee would like to emphasise the need to ensure 
‘informed’ consent of the participants of clinical trials which should invariably include 
sharing of information beforehand about how the data generated out of the trial would 

10 
 
be used or shared. It is important to ensure that the participants fully understand the 
risks and benefits of a study, management of sensitive personal information and 
balancing the need for research advancement with individual privacy rights. 
Digitalization and sharing of patient information bears privacy implications that needs 
to be addressed and fixed with appropriate provisions.  
1.6.3 The Committee also wanted to know about the steps taken to leverage new 
technologies like AI in health research in India along with the funds earmarked. The 
Department replied by stating that research is a crucial component for developing India-
specific AI-based solutions for strengthening the healthcare system in the country, with an 
overarching goal of achieving Universal Health Coverage. ICMR has mandated one of its 
institutes (ICMR-NIMS) in Delhi to cater to this objective, and it has been repurposed as 
ICMR-National Institute for Research in Digital Health and Data Science (ICMR-
NIRDHDS) in April 2024. A key vision of the institute is to innovate and develop low-cost 
AI-based technologies which can be translated and integrated into the health system at all 
the levels of healthcare. 
1.6.4 The Committee notes that the Government has taken a key step in setting up 
ICMR-National Institute for Research in Digital Health and Data Science and 
entrusting it with the task of developing India-specific AI-based solutions for 
strengthening the healthcare system with an overarching goal of achieving Universal 
Health Coverage. The Committee finds research in Digital Health and Data Science as 
one of the areas that can be prioritised for collaboration with domain leading private 
sector organisations. The Committee observes that the Department should explore the 
possibility to further augment the efforts in this direction. The Committee in this 
connection recommends that adequate money is allocated to ICMR-NIRDHDS for its 
new responsibility. In this regard, the Committee emphasises the all-important need to 
protect data, personal as well as research based, while developing AI based solutions.

unforeseen circumstances, often requiring modifications to timelines, budgets, 
strategies, or resource allocations. The Committee is pleased to observe that the 
Department has oriented their activities to utilise their enhanced allocation. 
Accordingly, the Committee views the plans made by DHR positively and is optimistic 
about the results coming out in tune with the plans chalked out.  
 
2.3.6 The Committee, however, finds that under some schemes, the funds sought by 
DHR for the year 2025-26 have been curtailed citing the reason that only the 
remaining amount approved by the Standing Finance Committee (SFC) for the 15th 
Finance Commission period has been allocated. The Committee finds the given reason 
untenable as it comes in the way of much needed continuance of the beneficial 
activities of the schemes beyond the 15th Finance Commission period. In the 
Committee's view, such situations exemplify the classic case of 'red tape' causing 
financial constraints that hinder development. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends that situations where genuine need for funds cannot be met due to 
procedural pre-requisites should be avoided in future. Further, the Committee wants 
to be apprised about the results of the recalibrated activities/targets under each 
scheme.

the establishment of Regional Research Platform were also not executed due to non-signing of

compared to the RE funds, which is reasonably appreciative. Utilisation under

Research/contribution/achievement of ICMR to Public Health Programmes/Policies 
4.6 As part of the discussion related to DFG 2025-26 of DHR, ICMR submitted 
instances whereinits research findings are translated into actionable public health 
programmes as well as instances when ICMR's research has influenced Government 
healthcare policies in India in the last decade. 
4.6.1 Translating Research into Actionable Public Health Programmes 
i. Identifying National Health Priorities – Research is aligned with the most 
pressing health challenges in India, including infectious diseases, non-
communicable diseases (NCDs), maternal and child health, and emerging health 
threats. 
ii. Evidence Generation through Multicentric Studies – ICMR conducts large-scale 
epidemiological and clinical studies to generate high-quality evidence that can 
inform policy and programmatic interventions. 
iii. Collaboration with Government and Stakeholders – Research findings are shared 
with policymakers, including the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
(MoHFW), state governments, and regulatory bodies to facilitate evidence-based 
decision-making. 
iv. Guideline Development and Capacity Building – ICMR translates research into 
practical guidance by developing Standard Treatment Workflows (STWs), 
Disease Management Guidelines, and Training Modules for healthcare providers.   
v. Policy Advocacy and Implementation Support – Findings are communicated 

72 
 
through policy briefs, white papers, and direct engagement with government 
agencies to ensure integration into public health programmes. 
vi. Real-World Demonstration Projects – ICMRimplements pilot studies and 
demonstration projects before scaling up interventions nationwide, ensuring 
feasibility and effectiveness in diverse settings. 
vii. Technology Transfer and Industry Partnerships – To accelerate the impact of 
research, ICMR facilitates collaborations with biotech firms, startups, and 
pharmaceutical companies for vaccine development, diagnostics, and medical 
innovations. 
viii. Public Awareness and Community Engagement – Through health communication 
campaigns, ICMR ensures that research findings reach the public, encouraging 
behavioural changes and better health outcomes. 
4.6.2 Research Influenced Government Healthcare Policies  
I. COVID-19 Response and Vaccine Development: – 
(a) ICMR played a pivotal role in India’s pandemic response by conducting 
Serosurveys, developing indigenous diagnostic kits, and supporting the 
development of Covaxin in partnership with Bharat Biotech and conducting bridge 
trials for Covishield in Indian population. 
(b) Research findings on variants, vaccine efficacy, and post-COVID complications 
directly influenced India's vaccination strategy and pandemic management policies. 
II. National TB elimination: – 
a) ICMR conducted a National TB Prevalence Survey in 20 States/group of States to 
assess burden of TB at sub-national levels. Based on the prevalence survey, the 
programme interventions were re-strategized to focus on vulnerable population to 
reduce TB incidence and mortality. ICMR’s studies on drug-resistant tuberculosis 
(DR-TB) led to the inclusion of newer drug regimens & newer diagnostics like 
TrueNAT, C-TB skin test etc in the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP) 
III. Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) Policy: The ICMR AMR Network provided 
critical data on antibiotic resistance patterns, leading to the National Action Plan on 
AMR, which now guides antimicrobial stewardship and infection control 
programmes across India. 
IV. Hypertension Control Initiative: ICMR’s studies on the growing burden of 
hypertension and cardiovascular diseases informed the launch of the India 
Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI), of which ICMR is also a part along with 
MoHFW& WHO, which integrates blood pressure management into primary 
healthcare settings. 
V. Vector-Borne Disease Control: ICMR’s research on VBDs transmission dynamics 
and vector control strategies contributed to different National Centre for Vector 

73 
 
Borne Disease Control (NCVBDC) guidelines like National Strategic Plan for 
Malaria, Manual on Integrated Vector management 2022, Common Protocol for 
Uniform Evaluation of Public Health Pesticides for use in Vector Control etc, 
including improved surveillance and early warning systems. Based on the 
evidence produced by ICMR, replacement of miltefosine 28 days regimen for VL 
with single dose liposomal amphotericin B was made & Combination regimen 
(LAmB + miltefosine): Adopted and recommended for VL-HIV treatment. 
VI. Revised Nutritional Guidelines and AnaemiaMukt Bharat: ICMR studies on 
malnutrition, anaemia prevalence, and dietary patterns influenced the 
AnaemiaMukt Bharat initiative, leading to policy changes in iron and folic acid 
supplementation programmes for children and pregnant women, Mandated Food 
fortification with Vitamin A and many more. 
VII. Cancer Policies: ICMR’s National Cancer Registry data has led to cancer being a 
notifiable disease in 15 states of India.  
VIII. HPV Vaccination: ICMR’s clinical trials and advocacy contributed to the phased 
introduction of the HPV vaccine into India’s Universal Immunization Programme 
(UIP) in 2022-23 to combat cervical cancer. 
IX. National One Health Mission: ICMR’s research on zoonotic diseases and 
antimicrobial resistance has significantly shaped India’s One Health approach, 
influencing policies for integrated human, animal, and environmental health 
surveillance. ICMR is also a crucial part of National One Health Mission, 
strengthening the future outbreak response and preparedness of the nation.  
X. Standard Treatment Workflows: The ICMR, and DHR receive the topics for 
developing STW from NHA and experts all over the country. So far, ICMR has 
released 125 Standard Treatment Workflows in 23 specialties. These workflows 
comprise symptoms, signs, diagnostics, treatment, etc. for concerned diseases. 
NHA is also using the STW as the clinical guidance document to prepare their 
reimbursement package under the Ayushman Bharat Scheme. 
4.6.3 The Committee takes note of the contribution/achievement of ICMR to public 
health programmes/policies and recommends that ICMR should continue to research 
projects of national health priorities and ultimately translating research projects into 
Actionable Public Health Programmes. The Committee recommends DHR to give 
incentives to ICMR so that ICMR undertake large scale epidemiological and clinical 
studies to generate high quality evidence for timely policy and programmatic 
interventions.  Needless to say, ICMR should translate health research into practical 
guidance by developing Standard Treatment Workflows (STW) and formulating 
Disease Management Guidelines and Modern and Advanced Training Modules for 
healthcare providers.

4.8 While discussing some of the major health concerns the country is currently facing, 
the Committee was curious to understand reasons for the rise in cases of sudden deaths due 
to myocardial infarctions post-COVID and the findings of ICMR regarding the subject. In 
reply, the Secretary, DHR, who is also the Director General of ICMR, stated that ICMR 
NIE conducted a study titled “Factors associated with unexplained sudden deaths among 
adults aged 18-45 years in India – A multicentric matched case–control study”. The study 
has been completed and the results of this study have been published in the Indian Journal 
of Medical Research.  
4.8.1 The study was conducted at 47 tertiary care hospitals located across 19 states/UT 
of India during May- August 2023. A multi centric matched case control study was 
conducted. Information was collected regarding data on COVID-19 vaccination /infection, 
post-COVID-19 conditions, family history of sudden death, smoking, recreational drug 
use, alcohol frequency, binge drinking and vigorous-intensity physical activity two days 
before death among the cases / interview among controls.Hence, the study observed that 
COVID-19 vaccination did not increase the risk of unexplained sudden death among 
young adults in India. Past COVID-19 hospitalization, family history of sudden death and 
certain lifestylebehaviours increased the likelihood of unexplained sudden death. 
 
4.8.2 During the Oral evidence of DHR, the Committee posed a question on the matter 
regarding apprehensions in the society that the anti-COVID 19 vaccines were behind some 
of the sudden deaths of young individuals reported across the country and the Secretary, 
DHR while explaining the issue further replied that the Covid vaccines, Covishield and 
Covaxin were found to be protective with respect to sudden death; they were protective 
rather than heightening the risk.

Patent-protection to Innovators  
4.14 During the oral evidence of DHR, the Committee posed a question on the 
possibility of reduction in the duration of patent-protection to innovators for affordable 
mass production of monoclonal antibodies. Replying to the question, the Department 
submitted that as per India Patent Act, 1970, Patents generally grant exclusive rights to 
innovators for a period of 20 years from the filing date. Hence, patent life available for 
exploitation of IP byinnovators including the innovators of monoclonal antibodies, may be 
increased by earlier grant of patent. To request an early grant of a patent under the Indian 
Patent Act, 1970, an applicant can file a request for ‘expedited examination’ which is done 
through Form 18A, allowing for a faster review process and potentially a quicker patent 
grant; this request should be accompanied by a request for early publication in Form 9 
along with the prescribed fee, enabling publication within a shorter timeframe than the 
standard 18 months. Early publication and expedited examination can significantly reduce 
the time taken to receive a patent grant.In India, the following entities can apply for 
expedited examination of a patent Application-Startups, Small entities, Female applicants, 
Government entities, Applicants in certain sectors, Applicants who have designated India 
as an International Solar Alliance (ISA) or International Preliminary Examination 
Authority (IPEA) in Patient Care Technician (PCT) application. 
 
4.14.1 The Committee has been given to understand that reducing the duration of 
patents is one of the options for making healthcare more affordable in India as once 
the duration of patents is over, Indian manufacturers can produce the drugs at a 
lowercost. So,the reduction of Patent duration can reduce the cost of treatment of 
diseases like cancer through cheaper drugs like antibody-based drugs which would 
certainly bring down the out-of-pocket expenditure of patients.  Since the subject 
matter of Patent and IP is under the Ministry of Commerce the Committee, therefore, 
recommends for an inter-ministerial consultation for a viable solution in the matter.  
The Committee desires that ICMR-DHR to look into the matter and have an inter-
ministerial discussion at the earliest. 
 
4.14.2 The Committee notes that as per India Patent Act, 1970, Patents generally 
grant exclusive rights to innovators for a period of 20 years from the filing date. 
Hence, patent life available for exploitation of IP by innovator including innovator of

Impact of packaged foods on human health 
 
5.12 The Committee has been informed that the total market size of the packaged food 
sector was 2.8 billion U.S. dollars in India in 2023. The recent times in the context of the 
growing overweight obesity and diet related non-communicable diseases, (NCDs) there is a 
concern that the prepacked foods consumption might be a contributing factor. However, 
packaged foods can have both positive and negative impacts on human health depending on 
the extent of processing and their contents and how often they are consumed. The 
healthiness of a packaged food depends on various factors including the level of processing 
of the food and the extent of nutrients/ingredients of concern that are present in the food 
like refined flours, fats, added sugars, salt/sodium. Some of the Pre-packaged foods are 
likely to be highly processed and can become High in fats, salt and sugars. Many packaged 
foods, especially snacks, desserts, and ready-to-eat meals, are high in sugar, sodium, and 
unhealthy fats. Regular consumption of these can contribute to obesity, heart disease, 
diabetes, and high blood. Packaging materials, especially single-use plastics, can have 
harmful effects on the environment, indirectly affecting health by contributing to pollution 
and waste. 
 
5.12.2 Ultra-processed foods (UPFs) refer to food and beverage products that have 
undergone extensive industrial processing and contain a high number of additives such as 
preservatives, sweeteners, colourings, flavourings, emulsifiers, and other substances that 
are not commonly used in culinary preparations. Research suggests that a diet high in ultra-
processed foods may be associated with negative health outcomes NIN, 2024, Dietary 
Guidelines for Indians. The lack of fibre and poor micronutrient content make UPFs 
unhealthy.Consumption of UPFs is linked to overweight/ obesity and increased risks of 
coronary heart disease, stroke, and diabetes.  
 

99 
 
5.12.3 According to the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES, 2024) the 
monthly per capita expenditure on beverages and processed food has risen from 8.98 per 
cent and 7.9 per cent (2011-12) to 11.09 per cent and 9.84 per cent (2022-23) in urban and 
rural areas, respectively. A recent report by WHO and ICREAR which covered five 
popular categories of ultra-processed food in India, namely (a) chocolate and sugar 
confectionery, (b) salty snacks, (c) beverages, (d) ready-made and convenience food, and 
(e)breakfast cereals have reported that this sector grew at a compound annual growth rate 
(CAGR) of 13.37% in retail sales value between 2011 to 2021.

packaged foods.  
 
5.13.1 The Committee expresses concern over pre-packed foods/UPFs consumption

grades in DHR have been lying vacant now for an unacceptably long time. Though

contractual appointments to so significant posts of Scientists is totally unacceptable 
and ad-hoc approach and practice of contractual appointment should be 
discontinued. Contractual appointments to such posts that inherently warrant 
continuity as a critical prerequisite for their effectiveness would jeopardise the very 
success of the tasks for which the scientists are engaged.

private sectors and fewer opportunities in terms of career progression, especially in 
specialized fields. Other facilitative factors like environment of equity, transparency, 
protection of ownership of genuine research outcomes, delayed systemic response, etc 
also add to contributing factors.

respiratory diseases, etc. along with research on public health, healthcare

Emerging Technologies and Ethical Concerns related to Health Research 
10. The Committee recognises the importance of adoption of new technologies,

continuing quest. The regulatory architecture now in place in the Country are taken

‘informed’ consent of the participants of clinical trials which should invariably

implications that needs to be addressed and fixed with appropriate provisions.

89 The Committee is in agreement with DHR that ASHAs can be incentivized for 
contributing more towards health research, through activities like house-to-house 
surveys. The extensive grassroots presence makes ASHA ideal for bridging the gap 
between research and public health implementation, the Committee opines. 
Accordingly, the Committee recommends DHR to encourage and incentivise the 
ASHAs and peripheral health institutions to contribute more towards health research 
through activities like house-to-house surveys, disease surveillance, and community-
based data collection. The Committee also recommends that the ASHA workers

the regular remuneration of ASHAs.

Impact of packaged foods on human health & Policy Measures to enhance informed 
food choices 
 
 94 The Committee expresses concern over pre-packed foods/UPFs consumption 
that becomes a contributing factor in the growing overweight obesity and diet related 

124 
 
non-communicable diseases, (NCDs). The Committee takes note the findings of the 
Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES, 2024) concluding that the 
monthly per capita expenditure on beverages and processed food has risen from 8.98 
per cent and 7.9 per cent (2011-12) to 11.09 per cent and 9.84 per cent (2022-23) in 
urban and rural areas, respectively. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends 
the Government to resort to stricter guidelines on targeting marketing and 
advertising of unhealthy foods for children. The Committee takes into account that 
the Food Safety Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) is taking steps to introduce a 
Front of Pack labelling System to indicate the healthiness of the pre-packaged foods. 
The Committee hopes that measures so undertaken will not only help the consumer 
make informed food choices but also compel the food industry actors to reformulate 
their products to achieve better rating.
