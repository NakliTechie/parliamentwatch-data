CHAPTER – I 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Department of Health Research (DHR) was  created as a separate Department 
under the aegis of Ministry of Health & Family Welfare by an amendment to the Government 
of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 on 17 th September, 2007. The Department 
became fully functional from November 2008 with the appointment of fir st Secretary of the 
Department.  The aim of the DHR is to introduce modern health technologies to the people 
through research and innovations related to diagnosis, treatment methods and vaccines for 
preventions; to translate them into products and processe s and, in synergy with concerned 
organizations, introduce these innovations into public health system.  
Vision of DHR 
 
Mandate/Objective of DHR 
 
 
1.2 The Secretary, DHR in his deposition to the Committee on February 22, 2022 
outlined the vision of the DH R and also highlighted ten broad objectives of the Department. 
The objectives of the department mainly include promotion & coordination of basic, applied 
and clinical research, inter-sectoral coordination and promotion of Public Private Partnership 
(PPP), international cooperation, investigation of outbreaks of epidemics and development of 
tools for its prevention and administration of ICMR. He further apprised the Committee that 
to achieve the set objectives, DHR -ICMR are running the following schemes acro ss the 
length and breadth of the country:- 
i. Nationwide Network of Viral Research and Diagnostic Laboratories for Managing 
Epidemics and Natural Calamities (VRDL); 
ii. Development of infrastructure for promotion of Health Research by establishing 
Multi-Disciplinary Research Units (MRUs) in Gov ernment Medical Colleges 
Research Institutes and Model Rural Health Research Units (MR HRUs) in rural 
areas; 
iii. Development of tools/supports to prevent outbreaks of epidemics 


2 
 
iv. Human Resource Development (HRD) for Health Research; 
v. Grants in Aid Scheme (GIA) for inter -sectoral convergence & Co -ordination for 
Promotion and Guidance on Health Research; 
vi. Health Technology Assessment in India (HTAIn); 
vii. Prime Minister‟s Ayushman Bharat Health Infrastructure Mission (PM-ABHIM)  
1.3 The Committee recognizes the vision of the Department of Health Research and 
acknowledges the objectives set by the Department to achieve the set vision. However, 
the Committee is of the strong opinion that with changing times and fickle nature of the 
health emergencies, the Department needs to continuously evaluate its objectives and 
approach and recalibrate it , if the need arises. The Committee also believes that no 
objective can be achieved in its entirety if the Department functions in isolation or in 
association with research based institutes only. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that to realise its vision, DHR must work in synergy with various departments and 
Ministries of the Central and State Governments as well as the leaders in healthcare 
industry in the private sector.  
1.4 The Committee recommends DHR -ICMR to establish and manage Rapid 
Response Teams (RRT) consisting of epidemiologists, immunologists, virologists,  
experts of infectious disease, physicians and other relevant scientific and cli nical staff to 
counter any emergency situations developing due to disease Outbreaks.  
INDIAN COUNCIL OF MEDICAL RESEARCH (ICMR)  
1.5 Established in  1911, the ICMR was initially known as Indian Research Fund 
Association (IRFA). It was renamed as  Indian Council of Medical Research in 1949. Indian 
Council of Medical Research (ICMR) is the apex body in India for the formulation, 
coordination and promotion of biomedical research and is one of the oldest medical research 
bodies in the world.  The Counc il‟s research priorities coincide with the National health 
priorities such as control and management of communicable diseases, fertility control, 
maternal and child health, control of nutritional disorders, developing alternative strategies 
for health care delivery, containment within safety limits of environmental and occupational 
health problems; research on major non -communicable diseases like cancer, cardiovascular 
diseases, blindness, diabetes and other metabolic and haematological disorders; mental he alth 
and drug research (including traditional remedies).  All these efforts are undertaken with a 
view to reduce the total burden of disease and to promote health and well -being of the 
masses. 
MAJOR ACHIEVEMENTS OF ICMR IN THE LAST FEW YEARS 
1.6 In his dep osition to the Committee on February 22, 2022, the Secretary, DHR 
highlighted major achievements of the premier research institute in the recent years. He also 
apprised the Committee about the note -worthy tasks performed by the institute in the Covid -
years i.e. 2019-2021.  
1.7 Below are some of the major achievements of the ICMR as informed by the Secretary 
DHR:- 
i. Conducting path breaking research for covid -19 vaccine-Covaxin and drone delivery 
of vaccines; 
ii. PPE kits and other equipments during covid waves; 
iii. Establishing BSL-3 and 4 labs in different regions of the country; 

3 
 
iv. Containing Zika and Nipah virus outbreak in Kerala; 
v. India Hypertension Management Inititiave; and  
vi. India TB-Constortium. 
1.8 The Committee takes note of the path -breaking efforts of ICMR in the 
development of Covid -19 vaccine Covaxin. The Committee appreciates ICMR for 
undertaking initiatives like India Hypertension Management Initiative, India TB -
Constortium, Mobile Stroke Unit to contain the spread of communicable and non 
communicable diseases in the country. The Committee believes that ICMR can play a 
major role in achieving the objectives  of SDG (Sustainable Development Goals) 3, 
which is to ensure health and well-being for all, including a bold commitment to end the 
epidemics of AIDS, tu berculosis, malaria and other communicable diseases by 2030. It 
also aims to achieve universal health coverage, and provide access to safe and effective 
medicines and vaccines for all. The Committee recommends ICMR to prepare a 
detailed framework consistin g of proper planning, well defined SOPs, smooth 
coordination between different organisations and effective implementation, directed 
towards realising objectives of SDG3.  
1.9 To a query regarding changes if any required in the organizational functional structure 
of the Department of Health Research for effective and efficient discharging of the role and 
responsibilities in times of pandemic and post pandemic phase, the Department informed the 
Committee that DHR and ICMR has worked in synergy with the Minist ry of Health and 
Family Welfare as well as with other  Scientific Departments and State Government which 
has provided a big support during the pandemic and that should be continued in post 
pandemic phase to improve the health indicators. However, in future more skilled manpower 
may be needed in terms of bio -safety and bio -security as well as to deal with diseases 
identified for elimination like TB, Malaria, F ilariasis as also new focus may be required to 
strengthen health infrastructure and renewed efforts o n one health, climate , zoonotic 
infections, telemedicine, Artificial Intelligence and data analytics. 
1.10 Various new initiatives undertaken by the Department of Health Research (DHR) as 
mentioned above and to deal with situations arising out of Covid -19 like pandemics in future, 
would require strengthening of the Department by bringing in more professionalism in its 
working  by having more scientific staff; building the capacity of experts through skill up -
gradation, including that of scientific manpower, fostering  closer integration with 
programmes of the Department of Health & Family Welfare (DoH&FW) & other  Scientific 
Departments, etc.  
1.11 The Committee is contended to note that the Department is well aware of the 
need for more skilled manpower in future  and bringing in more professionalism in its 
working for realizing the objectives of bio -safety and bio-security as well as to deal with 
diseases identified for  elimination like TB, Malaria, F ilariasis within a definite 
timeframe.  However, the Committee, feels that Department should take action to fill 
the posts of scientific professionals so that  crucial r esearch work  is not delayed . T he 
Committee recommends Department to fast -track recruitment processes of skilled and 
scientific professionals. The Committee further recommends DHR to create a work 
culture and environment which attracts highly skilled profes sionals having great 
scientific acumen. The Committee believes that offering lucrative career prospects along 
with a good work -life balance and liberty to carry out world class research would 
definitely attract best scientific brains to ICMR.

2.5 The Secretary further apprised the Committee that the total health research Budget, in 
India, is very small. He told the Committee that the total health research budget of the USA is 
2.2 per cent of the GDP.  In UK, it is 1.7 per cent of the GDP. However, in India, it is 0.02 

5 
 
per cent of the GDP only.  He mentioned that the total budget for health research is very 
small, and expressed the need to increase the same to at least 5% of total Health Budget.  
2.6 The Committee observes that for DHR, BE 2021 -22 allocation was Rs. 2663.00 
crores which was increased to Rs. 3080.00 crores in RE 2021 -22 and the allocation for 
BE 2022 -23 is Rs. 3200.65 crores only.  The Committee furt her notes that while the 
increase in budget from BE 2021-22 to RE 2021-22 is almost 16%, the increase from RE 
2021-22 to BE 2022 -23 is just 3.92% only. The Committee further observes that while 
countries like UK and USA are allocating budget of around 2% o f their GDP to health 
research, India which faces humongous burden of diseases which lead to poverty 
inducing out-of-pocket health expenditure by its population, allocates only 0.02% of its 
GDP for health research.  
2.7 The Committee is of the view that the increase in health research budget, year on 
year is very meagre and is not commensurate with the tasks DHR -ICMR are entrusted 
to deal with. Thus the allocated budget is grossly insufficient to meet the existing and 
future healthcare challenges. The Com mittee feels that higher budgetary allocation for 
health research will result in more research based findings which in turn would help in 
devising more informed affordable and practical plans/methodologies for diagnosis and 
treatment of both communicable and non-communicable diseases. Higher allocation for 
health research would also make the country better prepared for tackling any future 
outbreaks and health exigencies. The Committee, in its 127 th Report on Demand for 
Grants had recommended Government to a llocate 5 per cent of the budget of the 
Department of Health & Family Welfare to DHR. The Committee further reiterates its 
recommendation to allocate 5% of the total health budget for health research in the 
next two years. The Committee also recommends that considering global standards with 
respect to investment in health research, the Gover nment must invest at least 0.1% of 
the GDP in the next five years. Here the Committee also advances its support to DHR 
for seeking higher budgetary allocation from Ministry of Finance.

SCHEMES/PROJECTS OF DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH RESEARCH (DHR) 
 
A. Establishment of Viral Diagnostic & Research Laboratories (VRDL) to manage 
epidemics and national calamities 
3.1 In order to enhance the capacity for early identification and diagnosis of all viral 
infection, the Department of Health Research lau nched the scheme of establishment of 
network of Viral Research & Diagnostic Laboratories (VRDLs).  The scheme provides for 
establishment of a total number of 166 Viral Diagnostics & Research Laboratories (VRDLs) 
upto 2025-26 to serve as an important platfo rm for diagnosis and surveillance of existing as 
well as emerging viral infections.  
 
3.2 The Department has submitted that till date 132 VRDLs have been established across 
25 States and 5 Union Territories. These Viral Research and Diagnostic Laboratories  
(VRDLs) are established in a three -tier mode, viz., Regional, State and Medical College 
Level. Regional level VRDLs are equipped with serology, RT -PCR, virus isolation, 
sequencing and fluorescence microscopy; State Level labs are equipped with serology, R T-
PCR, fluorescence microscopy and sequencing, while Medical College Level are equipped 
with serology and Rt-PCR. These VRDLs are intended to cover the entire country for timely 
diagnosis/identification of viruses during outbreaks of epidemics, generation of data about 
viral diseases for facilitating quick deployment of adequate resources and measures to save 
the human lives during outbreaks. 
 
3.3 To a specific query regarding steps taken by the DHR to increase the spread of 
VRDLs, in the North Eastern Region of the country, the Department informed the Committee 
that due cognizance has been taken to disseminate benefit of the scheme to the various s tates 
in the North Eastern Region of the country. VRDLs have greatly contributed in enhancing the 
country‟s capacity for early identification and diagnosis of all viral infections of public health 
importance. So far, 11 VRDLs have already been sanctioned i n 5 north eastern states i.e. 
Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Tripura, as indicated in the table below. The 
VRDL scheme is meant to provide financial support for setting up VRDLs in government 
medical college/research institutions only, hence VRDLs in Sikkim and Nagaland could not 
be set up due to non -existence of government medical college.  List of State -wise VRDLs

3.4 The Committee observes that since Viral Research and Diagnostic Laboratories 
(VRDLs) are established in a three -tier mode, viz., Regional, State and Medical College 
Level, VRDLs can be established in Sikkim and Nagaland as well at regional and state 
level even if there is no Medical College in these two States. The Committee notes that 
the States of Sikk im and Nagaland had highest fatality rate amongst all the North -
Eastern States, non-availability of testing facility could have been one of the reasons for 
high causality. The Committee, therefore, recommends DHR to work in association with 
the State Governments of or North -Eastern Region and establish VRDLs in any 
government run research centre or any government hospital.   
 
3.5 The Committee has been given to understand that the scheme has been approved for 
another five years (till 2025 -26) to support the VRDL network. Considering the vital role 
played by the VRDLs during  the early phase of Covid -19 pandemic relating to validation of 
diagnostic kits, inter- laboratory Quality Control and testing  of samples, etc and the need to 
further strengthen the bio -safety network in the country to tackle/contain future pandemics/ 
epidemics of the scale of the Covid -19 pandemic, a separate provision has been made under 
the PM -ABHIM Scheme to upgrade 5 existing VRDLs into BSL -3 Labs; Strengthening 
already functional 80 VRDLs and supporting the VRDLs for undertaking research activities, 
etc.  
 
3.6 The Committee is pleased to note that the scheme for establishing Viral Research 
and Diagnostic Laboratories (VRDLs) has been extended for another five years i.e. till 
2026. The Committee observes that out of the 132 VRDLs established across 25 States 
and 5 Union Territories in the country, only 80 VRDLs are functioning. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends DHR to facilitate for trained manpower and work 
on other administrative procedures for swift commencement of operations in all the 
VRDLs. The Committee believes that for catering to the testing needs of a vast country 
like India , the spread/network of testing labs must be expanded. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that, DHR must aim towards -"One VRDL in each District" by 
2026.  The Committee hopes that increasing the spread of VRDLs would help in 
reducing the turnaround time from the present 24-48 hours.

diagnosis often leading to serious repercus sions over result of diagnostic test. The 
Committee is of the strong opinion that lack of adequate number of BSL -3 and BSL -4 
labs would greatly hinder India's chances of defeating viral outbreaks thereby exposing 
India's large population to various deadly diseases. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends ICMR to establish one BSL -IV lab in each of the four zones. The 
Committee also recommends DHR to launch more mobile laboratories as such mobile 
units could be of great help during outbreaks particularly in rural and remote areas.

4.12 Answering to a query regarding challenges faced by ICMR in operationalising clinical 
trials in India during the COVID -19 pandemic, the ICMR maintained that the challenges 
faced in operationalizing clinical trials during COVID-19 pandemic were as under: 
(a) The health care workers were already overloaded with the COVID -19 test and 
treatment. Setting aside time for quality conduct of trials was difficult. 
(b)  The patients, once discharged, were reluctant to revisit the hospital. Any follow-up 
had to be done virtua lly. Collection of samples like convalescent plasma was 
difficult and required motivation and counselling of potential donors. 
(c) All training and monitoring of study sites had to be done virtually due to restricted 
movement.

regarding medical research are some of the other objectives of this network. 
4.14 The Committee takes note of the challenges faced by the ICMR in

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE-AT A GLANCE 
 
VISION OF THE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH RESEARCH 
The Committee recognizes the vision of the Department of Health Research and 
acknowledges the objectives set by the Department to achieve the set vision. However, 
the Committee is of the strong opinion that with changing times and fickle nature of the 
health emergencies, the Department needs to continuously evaluate its objectives and 
approach and recalibrate it, if the need arises. The Committee also believes that no 
objective can be achieved in its entirety if the Department functions in isolation or in 
association with research based institutes only. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that to realise its vision, DHR must work in synergy with various departments and 
Ministries of the Central and State Governments as well as the leaders in healthcare 
industry in the private sector.  
       (Para 1.3)  
The Committee recommends DHR -ICMR to establish and manage Rapid 
Response Teams (RRT) consisting of epidemiologists, immunologists, virologists, 
experts of infectious disease, physicians and other relevant scientific and clinical staff to 
counter any emergency situations developing due to disease Outbreaks. 
       (Para 1.4)  
ACHIEVEMENTS OF ICMR 
The Committee takes note of the path -breaking efforts of ICMR in the 
development of Covid -19 vaccine Covaxin. The  Committee appreciates ICMR for 
undertaking initiatives like India Hypertension Management Initiative, India TB -
Constortium, Mobile Stroke Unit to contain the spread of communicable and non 
communicable diseases in the country. The Committee believes that ICMR can play a 
major role in achieving the objectives  of SDG (Sustainable Development Goals) 3, 
which is to ensure health and well-being for all, including a bold commitment to end the 
epidemics of AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria and other communicable disea ses by 2030. It 
also aims to achieve universal health coverage, and provide access to safe and effective 
medicines and vaccines for all. The Committee recommends ICMR to prepare a 
detailed framework consisting of proper planning, well defined SOPs, smooth 
coordination between different organisations and effective implementation, directed 
towards realising objectives of SDG3.

back to their country of birth and serve their country.  
                 (Para 3.34)  
 
The Committee takes note of the efforts put by DHR for capacity building and 
skill development of the human resource. However, the Committee believes that the 
major problem now-a-days in medical research is its sub -standard quality. Poor quality 
medical research leads to almost no or little impact in clinical practice. The Committee 
takes note that in India, faculties and scientists in medical institutions, are largely 

43 
 
engaged in teaching and clinical care and there is very little number of scientists 
interested in medical research.  
 
                 (Para 3.37)  
The Committee is of the strong view that quality of medical research can only be 
improved by inviting large no. of scientists, who can devote specific time for research 
from medical institutions es pecially in the field of diseases engulfing the country. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends utilising college facilities having specific and 
exclusive time for research activities.  The Committee also recommends that there 
should be added incentives for researchers for conducting high -quality and viable 
medical research that can be translated into action in treatment of life threatening 
diseases. 
                 (Para 3.38)  
Grant in aid Scheme for inter -sectoral convergence for health research & governa nce 
issues including Health technology Assessment  
The Committee observes that over the years there has been gross 
underutilization of the funds allocated and like in the case of Human Resource 
Development for Health Research component,  the reason for under -utilization is same 
for all the years. The Committee notes that during last three years i.e. 2019 -20, 2020-21 
and 2021-22, RE allocation has significantly been reduced, thereby implying that DHR 
did not get enough viable research proj ects to be undertaken and appropriation of 
allocated fund thereon. The Committee recommends DHR to make serious efforts to 
identify research projects which have potential for treatment of diseases and therefore 
rational utilisation of allocated funds could  be made. The Committee, strongly suggests 
DHR to promote research and encourage researchers to undertake research for the 
benefit of public health. The Committee also recommends that in view of the increasing 
biological threats, the DHR should put more em phasis on research related to biological 
weapons to ensure that in the eventuality of biological attacks, the country is 
safeguarded against any biological warfare.  
 
                (Para 3.41)  
 
 
The Committee acknowledges the basic objective of HTAIn i.e. to analyse the 
health technologies viz. medicines, devices and health programmes for its cost -
effectiveness, clinical -effectiveness and equity issues by means of Health Technology 
Assessment (HTA), and in turn helping in decision making for an optimal u tilization of 
allocated health budget. The Committee believes that HTAIn scheme and the scheme of 
establishing Model Rural Health Research Units (MRHRU), if operated in consonance 
& conjunction to each other can greatly aid in improving the overall health 
infrastructure, particularly in rural and semi -urban areas. T he Committee, believes 
that adequate budgetary allocation and subsequent optimal utilization of allocated 
funds under this scheme would also help in minimizing the cost for healthcare by 
reducing the out of pocket expenditure that still remains enormous. The Committee 
notes that out of the 17 Resource Centres of HTAIn only one such centre is in NE region 
and none in the UT of J&K and Ladakh. The Committee, therefore, recommends DHR

to work towards setting up atleast one such centre in J&K/Ladakh and one more in NE

Establishing DHR -ICMR Advanced Molecular Oncology Diagnostic Services 
(DIAMOnDS) 
 The Committee understands that North -Eastern states have the highest incidence 
of Cancer in India, for   example Mizoram has the highest incidence of stomach cancer, 
Meghalaya has big number of esophageal cancer cases and Nagaland reports high 
Nasopharyngeal cancer cases, but due to unavailability of specialized facilities, patients 
often visit other cities of India for diagnosis and treatment. Thus, specialized cancer 
centre for screening, early diagnosis and treatment of cancer within the region needs to 
be established. The Committee, therefore, recommends DHR to fast track setting up of 
oncopathology labs in NE region.

NORTH EASTERN AREAS 
The Committee expresses its displeasure over lower expenditure under „Creation 
of Capital Assets‟ & „Grant -in-Aid General‟ heads of ICMR.  The Committee believes 
that creating assets is key to developing health infrastructure in the North -East region 
as is  granting aid for research projects and capacity building of 
professionals/researchers and local human resource. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends DHR to work towards capacity building of local human resource in health 
and related sectors in a time boun d manner. The Committee, further recommends 
ICMR to study, capture, evaluate and implement good practices of NER for 
improvement in overall health.  
 
                                         (Para 3.50)  
The Committee expresses its satisfaction to note tha t DHR is aware of the „Key 
Areas of Activities‟ to improve the overall health infrastructure and thus public health 
in the North Eastern Region of the country. The Committee recommends DHR to 
prepare a detailed roadmap to implement these measures within a definite timeframe. 
The Committee, also recommends DHR to  seek higher budgetary allocation from 
Ministry of Finance for improvement of health infrastructure in the NER with the hope 
that the fund allocated would be fully utilized for the intended purpose. The Committee 
also recommends DHR to explore using drone delivery services for organ 
transplantation particularly eye transplantation.

BUDGETARY ALLOCATION TO ICMR 
The Committee observes that the BE 2022 -23 allocation to Indian Council of 
Medical Research (ICMR), government's premier health research organization and the 
agency steering the research response to pandemic Covid -19, is Rs. 2,198.00 crores 

45 
 
which is a meagre increase from Rs 2,133.07 crore, as per the revised estimate for 2021 -
22.  
       (Para 4.4)  
           Considering the pivotal role of Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) in the 
field of bio -medical and health research in India, the Committee is  deeply concerned to 
note the large gap in funding of ICMR when compared with health research institutions 
of countries like US, UK, France and China. What worries the Committee more is that 
the allocation of fund to ICMR and subsequent utilization of fund s by ICMR has always 
been less than the projected demand, even in the Covid -years, where the need for 
adequate funds and optimal utilization was call of the hour. The Committee believes that 
the budgetary allocation to ICMR in 2022 -23, is grossly inadequat e keeping in view the 
mandate of the ICMR. However, the Committee is also aware that the utilization trends 
of ICMR over the years don‟t present a justified reason for increase of funds. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends ICMR to improve the absorption c apacity of its 
scheme implementing agencies. The Committee recommends ICMR to provide more 
money for research projects, joint -research projects with national and international 
bodies, establish network of labs for diagnosis of Communicable and Non -
Communicable diseases, extensively fund research projects, provide trainings/ 
international exposure to researchers, develop programmes to encourage youth to take 
up research, scheme to develop inclination towards health -research in students of 
higher-secondary and senior secondary. 
       (Para 4.5)  
 
            The Committee believes that appropriate expenditure as per originally conceived 
plan  by ICMR-DHR would make a good case for seeking higher budgetary allocation at 
the RE stage and BE 2023 -24 as it is wel l established that only through the in -depth 
health research, the country can be saved from the life -threatening non communicable 
and communicable diseases.   
       (Para 4.6)  
BSL-III &IV LABS 
The Committee notes the establishment of 2 new BSL -IV and three new BSL -III 
labs. The Committee commends ICMR for starting a mobile BSL -III lab, recently. The 
Committee understands that the two new BSL -IV labs would cover the east and west 
part of India, h owever, the North and South India still don‟t have BSL -IV labs. The 
Committee believes that in the absence of well equipped and high -tech labs, samples 
have to be transported over long distances to the nearest lab, this causes delay in 
diagnosis often lead ing to serious repercussions over result of diagnostic test. The 
Committee is of the strong opinion that lack of adequate number of BSL -3 and BSL -4 
labs would greatly hinder India's chances of defeating viral outbreaks thereby exposing 
India's large popula tion to various deadly diseases. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends ICMR to establish one BSL -IV lab in each of the four zones. The 
Committee also recommends DHR to launch more mobile laboratories as such mobile 
units could be of great help during outbreaks particularly in rural and remote areas.   
       (Para 4.9)  

46 
 
The Committee is also well aware of the fact that research in BSL -IV labs has its 
own share of fallouts as these facilities are built for researchers to work with the most 
dangerous pathogens . However, the Committee believes that science can enable 
humanity to reap the benefits of research if it meets ethical, considerate and smart 
policymaking.  
                 (Para 4.10)  
The Committee, therefore, recommends DHR -ICMR to formulate regulatio ns 
and national policy for oversight of dual -use research- which refers to experiments that 
are conducted for peaceful purposes but can be modified or altered to cause harm. The 
Committee further recommends DHR/ICMR to conduct trainings and develop strict 
and exhaustive guidelines on bio-safety and bio-security.  
                 (Para 4.11)  
CLINICAL TRIALS IN INDIA 
The Committee takes note of the challenges faced by the ICMR in 
operationalising clinical trials in India during the COVID -19 pandemic and com mends 
for its efforts to carry out clinical trials in the face of such difficulties. The Committee 
also appreciates ICMR for developing a pan -India clinical trial network (Indian 
Clinical Trial and Education Network -INTENT) which will provide a single, re ady 
platform for conducting rapid but good quality randomized clinical trials.  
                 (Para 4.14)  
The Committee, however, believes that there is a general lack of public 
confidence in healthcare/pharmaceutical industry and the Committee apprehe nds that 
slackening public confidence might pose a hurdle in conducting clinical trials. The 
Committee recommends ICMR to work in cohesion with the pharmaceutical industry to 
regain trust of the general public by engaging more transparently. The ICMR shoul d 
also make more efforts to educate and inform patients and the society at large about the 
benefits of engaging in clinical research and allowing authentic collection of patient 
data. 
                 (Para 4.15)  
ICMR -Ayush MINISTRY PARTNERSHIP 
The Committee takes note of the projects undertaken by ICMR along with 
different organizations of the Ayush.  The Committee is of the view that Ayurveda, 
Homeopathy, Siddha, Unani and herbal medicines have wide socio cultural acceptance 
with large chunk of population subscribing to the alternative mode of treatment, but the 
Committee believes that scientific research is needed, so that these regimens can be 
incorporated as standard treatment regimens. Research on Ayush preparations must be 
conducted scienti fically so that it is in line with the modern patenting system. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends ICMR to work towards linking various disciplines 
of Ayush with each other and also with modern system of medicine, as it may lead to a 
possible convergence of various disciplines for global benefit. 
                 (Para 4.17)

ICMR-REVIEW AND COMPARISON 
The Committee notes that the external evaluation and analysis conducted by 
Thomson Reuters for ICMR wherein ICMR was compared to similar organizations of 
US, UK, Australia, China and Brazil was done six years ago. Considering that since 
then the world has seen 3 waves of Covid-19 pandemic, the Committee believes that now 
is an opportune time for another similar external evaluation.  The Committee 
recommends ICMR to conduct such an evaluation  wherein ICMR is compared to other 
competitors on parameters like, trends in budgetary allocation and utilization, 
comparison between public and private investment, utilization avenues and research 
work undertaken, health  research infrastructure and its development, numbers of 
scientific professionals and clinical researchers, patents filed, number of fellowships and 
grant for research and future outlook.   
                 (Para 4.19)  
Analyzing the data from the 2016 eva luation the Committee observes that, there 
is wide gap in funding between health research institutions of countries like US, UK, 
Australia, China and Brazil and ICMR. The Committee further observes that though 
annual output (in terms of paper published) of  ICMR has continued to grow with 
respect to its 2005 output and in 2014, ICMR produced 108% more papers than it did 
in 2005, however, the Committee believes that this won't be a just comparison with NIH 
(National Institute of Health, US) as NIH is already working at a very high output, and 
given a larger base, it's rate of growth (in terms of paper published) cannot be as high 
as ICMR.  The Committee further notes that just the number of paper published cannot 
be a sufficient indicator for performance, as t he quality of research, its significance in 
public health and its viability are far more important factors in performance analysis.  
                 (Para 4.20)  
The Committee notes that regular assessment of ICMR's functioning is being 
carried out, as after the 2017 assessment another Committee under the chairmanship of 
Prof. (Dr.) Vinod K. Paul, Member NITI Aayog has been constituted in 2021 for 
conducting the ne xt evaluation/Peer review of the performance of ICMR. The 
Committee desires to be apprised of the Action Taken Note on the recommendations 
made by Performance Evaluation Committee (PEC) headed by MK Bhan which 
submitted its report in 2017.
