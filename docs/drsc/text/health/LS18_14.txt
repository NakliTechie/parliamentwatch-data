II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.5 The Committee notes with satisfaction that with the increase in allocation, capacity of the 
Department to utilise the same is also increasing. The Committee would, however, like to point out 
that trend of utilisation is not evenly spread in all the quarters of the financial year of 2004-05. Against 
plan allocation of Rs.200.00 crore in 2004-05, the Department could utilise Rs.121.33 crore by the end 
of third quarter, i.e. 31st December, 2005. By 7th March, 2005 it increased to Rs.179.49 crore and by 
the 28
th March, 2005, utilisation figures were Rs.195.26 crore. This clearly shows that out of total 
allocation of Rs.200.00 crore in 2004-05, an expe nditure of Rs.74.00 crore was made in the last 
quarter of the year. The Committee is  of the view that the pace of utilisation should be as per the 
prescribed financial norms. Expenditure on the high side in the last quarter of the financial year cannot 
be considered good for the health of budget planning. 
Action Taken 
 It is true, that with the increase in allocation, capacity of the Department to utilise is also 
increasing. As per the prescribed financial norms, the Department is supposed to spend 67% of the BE 
by 31st December, 2004 and if the Department coul d spend Rs.121.33 crore by 31st December, 2004, 
it had spent 70.13% of BE. The RE Budget was enhanced to Rs.200.00 crore after the approval of the 
Parliament. On receipt of the said approval, the Department took special initiative/made special efforts 
to utilise the said Budget by the close of financial year i.e. March, 2005, which was on higher side of 
the prescribed percentage. However; the Deptt. appreciates the concern of the Committee and in 
taking suitable measures/ steps to make further improvement in this regard during the year 2005-06. 
Recommendations/Observations

IV.  CENTRAL  PHARMACY  COUNCIL  FOR  AYUSH 
Recommendations/Observations 
4.1 The Committee has been commenting on no action being taken in the setting up of Central 
Pharmacy Council for AYUSH for the last many year s. Committee has observed  that during 2002-03 
token allocations was made for two pharmacy councils i.e. Indian Homoeopathy Pharmacy Council 
and ISM Pharmacy Council. However, in the next year it was decided to have one Pharmacy Council 
and accordingly Rs.19 lakh were provided therefor. The money allocated remained unspent as the Bill 
was yet to be flnalized. The Committee is constrained to observe that during 2004-05 also, position 
remained the same. The Committee finds that during  2005-06 Rs.1 crore has been provided for the 
proposed Council. The Department has informed that the Bill has been prepared and the comments of 
the Ministries of Finance, HRD and Health and Family Welfare obtained. The draft Bill is almost 
ready and will be introduced in Parliament shortly. The Committee reiterates that there should be no 
further delay for introducing the Bill in Parliament. . 

Action Taken 
 The draft Cabinet Note has been approved by the Cabinet in its meeting held on 23.6.05 and the 
matter has been referred to Ministry of Law for vetting. The Bill will be introduced in the forthcoming 
Session of the Parliament.

XVIII. MISCELLANEOUS 
Recommendations/Observations 
18.1 Mainstreaming AYUSH Institutions and Practitio ners in National Health Care Delivery Systems 
is one of the thrust areas of the Tenth Plan. The Committee notes that a proposal in this regard has 
been sent for consideration under the new initiative of National Rural Health Mission and National 
Commission on Macroeconomics and Health. The proposal envisages involvement of AYUSH 
doctors and Medicines in primary health care network.  The proposal is at present under consideration 
of different forums. State Government have also been asked to do the needful in terms of posting of 
ISMH physicians in PHCs. The Committee welcomes this initiative. The Committee is of the view 
that the Department should pursue this proposal with all concerned authorities. 
Action Taken 
 To pursue the matter of integration of AYU SH Joint letter from Secretary (Health) and 
Secretary (AYUSH) is being sent to the State Chie f Secretaries and Health Secretaries. Similarly, 
Union Minister for Health and Family Welfare is also writing a letter to State Chief Ministers and 
Health Ministers to include AYUSH systems at Sub Centre, PHC, CHC and District Hospitals. 
Recommendations/Observations 
18.2 The Committee has been informed that a capsule containing the basic concepts and 
fundamentals of AYUSH for incorporation in MBBS curricula has been sent to MCI by the 
Department. Similarly Nursing Council of India has also been approached for inclusion of 120 hour 
yearly programme in the nursing degree course. The Committee notes that the proposal regarding 
MBBS curricula has not been found acceptable by the MCI. According to MCI, inclusion of the basic 
principles and concepts of ISM&H in the MBBS course may only lead to acquiring inadequate 
knowledge of these systems by MBBS students. The matter has however been referred back to MCI 
for reconsideration. Response of NCI is awaited with regard to inclusion of a 120-hour course 
programme of AYUSH. The Committee feels that a de cision in this matter should be taken after 
considering the views of all stakeholders. 
Action Taken 

 Department of AYUSH has taken up the issue to introduce an introductory Module of AYUSH 
systems in MBBS course. Medical Council of India has not agreed to our proposal. Department of 
AYUSH is again taking up this issue with the Depa rtment of Health. Nursing Council has greed to 
include AYUSH component in the B.Sc. Nursing co urse, but the formal reply from the Nursing 
Council is still awaited. 
Recommendations/Observations 
18.3 The Committee notes that the Department in collaboration with CSIR and ICMR is to work 
together for scientific validation of some of the existing Ayurveda medicines. 
Action Taken 
 Department of AYUSH is working closely with CSIR and ICMR under a Golden Triangle 
Partnership (GTP) Project. Under this proposal, Ayur vedic Medicine will be standarised on scientific 
lines for 13 Diseases of National importance. F our Task forces meetings on Benign Prostate 
Hypertrophy, Sleep disorders, Joint disorders, and Rasayana have met in the month of July 05 to 
identify the gaps and effective Medicine to be researched upon under G.T.P. 
Recommendations/Observations 
18.4 Thirteen priority areas have been identified on  which drugs are to be developed in a time bound 
manner. These priority areas includ e some of the chronic problems like diabetes, Vision disorders, 
Cardiac disorders and Bronchial allergy etc. Th e Committee welcomes this ambitious partnership 
programme and hopes that research work to be undertaken under this project will yield fruitful results 
for all concerned. 
Action Taken

II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.2 The Committee was informed that during the fi rst two years of Tenth Plan, allocation of Rs.150 
crores in each year remained underutilized due to various reasons. The two main reasons were delay in 
approval of some Centrally Sponsored Schemes and lack of awareness among the implementing 
agencies about the Schemes. The Plan allocation of the Department during the year 2004-05 was 
Rs.173.00 crore (excluding Rs. 8.00 crore for the Department of Urban Development). Considering 
the performance of the Department and increased requ irements of funds to implement various Central 
Sector and Centrally Sponsored Schemes. BE allocation was raised to Rs. 200.00 crore at RE Stage. 
Expenditure reported upto 28th March, 2005 is Rs. 195.26 crore. 
Action Taken 
 During the first year of the Tenth Plan, i.e. 2002-03 the expenditure was Rs.89.78 crore, and 
during 2003-04 it was Rs.133.95 crore against Rs.150. 00 crore, for both. This was also due to cut 
imposed at the RE stage to Rs.105.00 crore and Rs .135.00 crore respectively. The reasons for less 
utilisation are as given by the Committee. The Actual Expenditure for 2004-05 has been Rs.198.76 
crore as against the RE: (plan) allocation of Rs.200 .00 crore. The reasons for less utilisation are the 
same as given by the Committee. 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.3 The Committee was given to understand that an enhanced provision has been made for 2005-06, 
in view of genuine requirements of the Department under the various on-going programmes and its 
capacity to utilize the proposed provisions. The De partment was confident of spending the increased 
budget provisions in 2005-06 as the Centrally Sponsored Schemes under implementation are fully 
subscribed. The Department was rather facing pauc ity of budget provisions in implementing even 
feasible proposals under some of the schemes. 
Action Taken 
 Budget provision for 2005-06 shows 75% increase over RE 2004-05. 
Recommendations/Observations 
2.4 The Committee notes that against an allocation of Rs.364.43 crore during the Ninth Plan, funds 
amounting to only Rs.293.72 crore could be utilised. An enhanced allocation of Rs. 775 crore has 
been made for the Tenth Plan. The Committee notes that although utilisation position of allocated 
funds in the first year of the Tenth Plan i.e. 2002-03 was not satisfactory, during the second year, the 
Department could utilise the entire allocation provided at the RE stage. For 2004-05, provision of 
Rs.173 crore was enhanced to Rs. 200 crore. The De partment justified this enhanced allocation by 
utilizing Rs. 195.26 crore by 28
th March, 2005. 
Action Taken 
 The initial allocation during the Ninth Plan wa s Rs.266.35 crore and the final funds allocated 
became Rs.364.43 crore. During 2004-05 the Deptt. by making the special efforts, could spend 
Rs.198.76 crore (almost 100%) under Plan against the allocation of Rs.200.00 crore at the RE stage. 

III. STATUTORY INSTITUTIONS 
Recommendations/Observations 
3.2 Initial allocation for Ninth Plan for the two Councils was Rs. 2.46 crores, which by the end of 
the plan period was brought down to Rs. 1.47 cr ores. The Committee observes that whereas CCIM, 
New Delhi could utilise the reduced allocation for the Ninth Plan, in the case of CCH, New Delhi, 
expenditure was restricted to only Rs.2 lakhs during the entire Ninth Plan and even this amount was 
spent during 1998-99. The Committee observes that situation remained the same during the Tenth 
Plan also. During the first two years, i.e 2002-03 and 2003-04 only a token amount of Rs.1 lakh was 
kept for CCH, New Delhi which remained unspent. During 2004-05 BE allocation of Rs.1 lakh was 
increased to Rs.10 lakhs. This money was to be used for conducting workshops and meetings which 
failed to materialize. 
Action Taken 
 During 2004-05, BE allocation of Rs.1.00 lakh increased to Rs. 10.00 lakhs at RE stage which 
was fully utilised by CCH for conducting workshops and meetings in connection with holding of 
elections in various States. 
Recommendations/Observations 
3.3 In contrast, in the case of CCIM, there has been optimum utilisation of allocated funds varying 
from Rs. 10 to 12 lakhs during this period. The Committee also notes that CCH, New Delhi has been 
utilizing the non-plan funds fully which are on the same scale as provided to CCIM, New Delhi. The 
Committee fails to understand the rationale for variation in the provision of plan funds to both the 
Councils which have been set up with similar objectives. 
Action Taken 
 The CCIM covers/regulates the functioning of Ayurveda, Unani and Siddha whereas CCH 
exclusively looks after the Homoeopathic Institutes/Colleges and there is a variation in allocation of 
Plan funds to these Councils keeping in view coverages/ areas falling in jurisdiction.

Advanced Ayurvedic Centre for Mental Health in NIMHANS 
Recommendations/Observations 
5.1 An Advanced Ayurvedic Centre for Mental Health was proposed to be set up in NIMHANS, 
Bangalore. Accordingly Rs.1.45 crore were provided in Ninth Plan. The Committee observes that 
although Rs. 97 lakhs were spent during the Ninth Plan period, nothing seems to have materialized in

05, Rs.1 lakh has been provided for this Centre. The Committee strongly feels that any scheme/plan 
proposed to be implemented should only be taken up after due consideration of its feasibility and other 
allied aspects. Advanced Ayurvedic Centre for Mental Health in NIMHANS, Bangalore seems to have 
been forsaken by the Department. The Committee would like to be apprised about the actual status of 
this Centre. 
Action Taken 
 During the Ninth Plan, it was proposed to upgrade the Central Research Unit (CRU) of 
Ayuveda, a unit of the CCRAS s ituated within the Compound of the NIMHANS, Bangalore, to the 

level of the Advanced Ayurveda Centre (AAC) for Mental Health and Neuro Science with the setting 
up of a 30 bed IPD and strengthening of the existing research activities. 
 Since the CRU is a small unit having no expertise to implement Civil construction activities as 
such the responsibility was entrusted to the NIMHANS, which is having its own Civil Engineering 
wing. The new building with 30 beds IPD has been completed by the NIMHANS in May, 2003 and 
handed over to the CCRAS. The AAC has shifted to its new building under the administrative control 
of the CCRAS, as it was prior to its upgradation. 
 The budget for AAC is being provided as a component of the budget of the CCRAS and the 
notional allocation of Rs.l.00 lakh, for NIMHANS, has been made erroneously. 
 The AAC, is a specialized unit of the CCRAS and is being strengthened by providing additional 
budget and Personnel.

XVII. EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS 
Recommendations/Observations 
17.1 There are 221 Ayurveda Colleges and 180 Homoeopathy Colleges that have the approval of 
State Governments and are by and large affiliated to the Universities. A State-wise breakup of these 
Colleges indicates that the maximum number of Ayurve da Colleges is concentrated in two States of 
Karnataka and Maharashtra. Similarly in case of Homoeopathy Colleges, Maharashtra and Madhya 
Pradesh have the highest number of Colleges. On bei ng asked about the reasons for this situation, the 
Department clarified that this was largely due to the higher degree of private initiatives in these States. 
The Central Government has been taking all preventive measures to check the mushroom growth of 
sub-standard medical Institutions of Ayurveda and Homeopathy. The Department has also informed 
that no College can be opened without a NOC from the State Government and consent of affiliation 
from the concerned University. The Committee is not fully inclined to accept the clarification given 
by the Department. The Department itself has indicated that all these Colleges are ‘by and large’ 
affiliated to Universities. 
Action Taken 
 After the Indian Medicine Central Council (Amendment) Act, 2003 and the Homoeopathy 
Central Council (Amendment) Act, 2002 came into force with effect from 7.11.2003 and 28.1.2003, 
the power of granting permission to open new Medical College, to open new higher courses of study 
or training (including Post Graduate Courses) and to increase admission capacity in the Colleges, was 
vested in the Central Government. Before the said Amendment, the Central Council of Indian 
Medicine and the Central Council of Homoeopathy, used to grant necessary permissions to the 
prospective Medical Colleges/Institutions. But after the enforcement of the said Amendment in Acts, 
the applications of the Colleges /Institutions are received in the Department of AYUSH directly. The 
applications received during the pres cribed schedule for receipt thereo f, are scrutinized thoroughly in 
the Department of AYUSH. After scrutiny of each appl ication, a copy of the same is forwarded to the 
CCIM in respect of the courses in Indian Medicine or to the CCH for courses in Homoeopathy, as the 
case may be. While doing so, the deficiencies found in the applications are indicated in the forwarding 
letters to the CCIM and CCH and copies of the same  endorsed to the applicants with the advice to 
rectify the deficiencies in consultation with the CCIM or the CCH, as the case may be. The CCIM and 
the CCH do the technical scrutiny of the applications referred to them and make their 
recommendations for disapproval or approval of the scheme in each case. The CCIM and the CCH 
depute their officers/ experts to the Colleges/Institutions for on the spot study of the infrastructure and 
facilities and minimum standards of educatio n as per the respective Regulations/Guidelines 
formulated by the Central Government. Even after the receipt of the recommendations from the CCIM 
or CCH, the Central Government may make further inquiries from the prospective Colleges and / or 
conduct visitations to the Colleges before taking decision to grant permission. 
Recommendations/Observations 

17.2 The Committee also notes that number of G overnment Colleges are very less when compared 
with the Private Colleges. Out of 221 Ayurveda Co lleges, only 54 are in the Government sector. 
Similarly, out of 180 Homoeopathy Colleges, 149 are private Colleges. This is the position in case of 
Unani and Siddha Colleges. The Committee has been informed that the Central Council of Indian

Action Taken

CCH as well as Government of India. Colleges which have rectified the deficiencies have been 
granted extension of recognition and others are being pursued to rectify the deficiencies to process the 
case for extension of recognition.

same with the Planning Commission-Department of Expenditure. (Para 14.2) 
XV.  STRENGTHENING  OF  PHARMACOPOEIAL  LABORATORIES 
Comments of the Committee 
 The Committee views with serious concern the state of affairs in both the Labs; i.e. HPL 
and PLIM. The Committee was given to unders tand that the buildings for HPL and PLIM 
would be ready by Februa ry, 2005. However, the date for comp letion again shifted to June-July 
-2005 and as per the latest info rmation, the buildings were to be  ready in all respects with all 
mandatory certificates by October, 2005. The Committee is not aware about the latest status but 
hopes that targets must have been achieved. Also , as regards the proposal for creation of posts, 
the Committee opines that the Depa rtment should not only submit the modified proposal to the 
Department of Expenditure at the ea rliest but also pursue the same so  as to get it arrive of the 
logical conclusion. All efforts should be made to get the post of Director, PLIM and HPL filled 
up urgently. The Committee hopes that the De partment would work towards removing all 
bottlenecks in the whole process beca use the objective of these Labs i.e. laying down the 
standards for drugs of AYUSH and also for their te sting/analysis is of immense importance for 
users. (Para 15.2 to 15.5) 
XVI. DRUGS QUALITY CONTROL 
Comments of the Committee 
 The Committee is happy to note that two st atutory bodies under the Drugs and Cosmetics 
Act, i.e. Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani Drug, Technica l Advisory Board a nd Ayurveda, Siddha, 
Unani, Drug Consultative Committee have been  constituted by the Department. ASUDCC will 
be meeting every six months, with all the State Drug Controllers participating. This Committee 
will take care of all aspects of ASU (Ayurveda,  Siddha and Unani) drugs and will also initiate 
preventive/deterrent action whenever required. Th e Committee hopes that these two statutory 
bodies will be activated at the earliest. (Para 16.5) 
Comments of the Committee 
 The Committee is given to understand that dr ug quality control is the responsibility of the 
Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) and the state licensing authorities. The Committee 
would however like to point out that as per th e Annual Report of the Department for 2004-05 

Drug Control Cell in the Department is also in volved in the control of  misbranded/adulterated 
and spurious drugs of AYUSH, besides other ma tters. It seems that this Cell has not been 
handling the cases of misbranded/a dulterated and spurious drugs so  far. If not, the Committee 
would like to be apprised about the work done by the Cell during last two years. The Committee 
notes that the Department now proposes to get the post of Additional Drug Controller (AYUSH) 
and four Drugs Inspectors (AYUSH ) revived and filled up to assist  the DCGI in the task of 
quality control of AYUSH drugs. The Committee emphasizes that the Department should take 
all the required steps to implement the proposal at the earliest. (Para 16.6) 
Comments of the Committee 
 The report of scientists of USA mentioning the presence of metals in Ayurvedic/Herbal 
Medicinal products more than permissible limits is directly related to the efficacy and credibility 
of Ayurvedic medicines. The Committee appreciates  that the Department has initiated a study 
to see the toxicity/safety profile of metal base d bhasmas. A project to estimate the presence of 
various metals in normal habita t in raw drugs and classical Ay urvedic formulations has also 
been started. The Committee feels that findings of the study/project wo uld be available after 
some time only. However, it seems that Ha rvard Medical Associatio n has not been kept 
informed about the follow-up action taken by th e Department. If that be so, the Committee 
would impress upon the De partment to do so without any fu rther delay. Feed back from the 
manufacturing companies should also be expedited. (Para 16.7)

ANNEXURE - II 
STATUS NOTE ON THE SCHEME FOR EXTRA MURAL RESEARCH IN AYUSH 
 The research in AYUSH sector was limited to the efforts made by the Central Research Councils, wh ich were largely in the natur e of clinical 
research and fell short of conforming to the modern scientific temper. Therefore, the Ministry felt the need for collaborative research arrangements with 
reputed research institutions and University Departments so as to generate a reliable and scientifically acceptable outcome. Ac cordingly, a Centrally 
Sector Scheme was launched in 1997-98. As the response to the Scheme was not very encouraging, the Scheme was revised in the ye ar 2001 by 
specifying the priority areas of research of the Department of AYUSH. Under the Scheme, funds are given to accredited research institutions, both 
Government and Non-Governmental Organizations, for undertaking time-bound research projects of one to three year durations. 
 Under the Scheme, a total of 67 projects have been sanctioned and total amount of Rs.7.26 crore has been utilized for undertak ing these projects so 
far. Year-wise break-up of funds utilization is given below:-
