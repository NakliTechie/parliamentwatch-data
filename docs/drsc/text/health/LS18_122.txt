Recommendation 
 
1.3 The Committee takes note of the increasing volume of export of medicinal plants but is not 
satisfied with the pace of its growth. The Comm ittee feels that India is rich in unique and

priority needs to be given. 
(Para 3.9) 
Action Taken  
 
1.4 In order to encourage Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs ) and Good Field Collection 
Practices (GFCPs) in medicinal plants and enhance quality and safety of their produce, the 
Government has launched the Voluntary Certification Scheme for Medicinal Plants Produce

1.5 Regional Workshops are being organised among stakeholders to create greater awareness 
about VCSMPP Schemes. 
  
1.6 For global promotion and support to Indian Medicinal Plants sector, the Ministry of 
AYUSH, Government of India has developed a standard “Memorandum of U nderstanding

121190 under Merchandise Exports from India (MEIS) scheme for boosting country’s exports of 
herbs.  Under the said scheme incentive @ 3%, 5% and 7% is provided.

III. NATIONAL MEDICINAL PLANTS BOARD 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.8 The Committee would also like to emphasize that India is known to be a treasure trove of 
medicinal plants. What is required is to identify, conserve and gratefully use them for medicinal 
purposes. Theref ore, focus of the Board should be on utilizing the rich biodiversity on a 
sustainable basis. The Committee would also like to highlight the pivotal role that the local 
community can play in development, conservation and sustainable use of such plants. The 
Committee, in this regard, recommends the NMPB to popularize a programme for sustainable 
development of Medicinal Plants amongst the local community. 
(Para 3.13) 
Action Taken 
 
1.9 For Sustainable Development of medicinal Plants amongst the local communitie s (Joint 
Forest Management Committees (JFMCs)/Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs), 
Panchayats/Van Panchayats etc.), there is a provision under “Central Sector Scheme on 
Conservation, Development and Sustainable Management of Medicinal Plants” of NMPB .  As 
per the said scheme guidelines financial assistance of Rs.15.00 Lakh per JFMCs/BMCs etc. is 
provided for creation of primary processing facilities in the form of Drying   Yards, Storage 
Godowns etc. for value addition of medicinal plants raw material and thereby generating 
livelihood to the local communities. 
  
1.10 Under “Central Sector Scheme on Conservation, Development and Sustainable 
Development of Medicinal Plants”, National Medicinal Plant Board supports for Medicinal Plants 
Conservation Areas (MPCDAs) and resource augmentation as project mode.   MPCDAs cover on 
an average area of 200 hectares or smaller areas in case of endemic and critically endangered 
species.  As per guideline, financial assistance of @20,000 per hectare is provided. 
  
1.11 In addition, resource augmentation of medicinal plant species assisted through natural 
regeneration and artificial regeneration of local populations of medicinal and aromatic plant.   
Active interest and engagement of rural communities in such a conservation program is 
instrumental to address sustainability of the medicinal plant sector as a whole, hence financial 
support also provided for community mobilization through entry point activities etc. 
  
1.12 Under “Central Sector Scheme on Conservation, Developmen t and Sustainable 
development of Medicinal Plants”, under which there is a provision to provide project based 
support to various Government and Non -Government organizations to establish various types of 
herbal garden to popularize the usefulness of commonl y available and frequently used Medicinal 
Plants among the various stakeholders and sensitize the public about our traditional knowledge. It 
is also a way of conserving Rare, Endangered and Threatened (RET) species of Medicinal 
Plants.  Setting up of Herba l Gardens are promoted under the scheme to create awareness about 
traditional usage of medicinal plants.

IV.  QUALITY CONTROL OF AYURVEDA, SIDDHA, UNANI AND 
HOMOEOPATHIC (AYUSH) DRUGS 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.13 The Committee time and again has been emphasizin g upon a stringent regulatory 
framework for quality control of ASU&H drugs. The Committee believes that for AYUSH 
systems to emerge as a strong alternative system of medicine, it is imperative that its credibility is 
enhanced through proper monitoring, qua lity control of drugs by scientific validation, proper 
parameters for clinical trials, upgradation of drug testing labs, GMP compliance by manufacturing 
units, control of misleading advertisements, etc. The Committee taking note of all the 
achievements would like to recommend that quality control of ASU&H drugs is one of the most 
important areas within the AYUSH ambit which needs to be accorded utmost priority. There is 
also a need for integration of AYUSH system of medicine with the modern system which is only 
possible through scientific validation and strict quality control for safety and efficacy of this 
system of medicine. The Committee strongly recommends for expediting and bringing forth the 
legislation for a regulatory umbrella for the education and p rofession of Pharmacy in Indian 
Medicine and Homoeopathy.   
(Para 4.4) 
Action Taken 
 
1.14 The Ministry has taken note of the recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee and 
would like to inform that Pharmacopoeia Commission of Indian Medicine and Homeopathy, 
Pharmacopoeia Laboratory of Indian Medicine and Homeopathic Pharmacopoeia Laboratory 
have been set up. These organisations are mandated to develop the quality standards of Ayurveda, 
Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy (ASU&H) Drugs and publish the Pharmacopoeia.  
Pharmacopoeia are the regulatory document for the purpose of quality control of ASU&H drugs.  
  
1.15 The Cabinet in its meeting held on 03.06.2020 has approved the establishment of 
PCIM&H as subordinate office under Ministry of AYUSH by merging PLIM & HPL  both 
subordinate offices and PCIM&H an autonomous organization under Ministry of AYUSH. 
 
1.16 Similarly, the setting up of the regulatory framework through Pharmacy Council of Indian 
Medicine and Homoeopathy Bill, 2020 is being steered for approval of the  Cabinet and thereafter 
introduction of the same in the Parliament.   
 
 
V. PHARMA COVIGILENCE INITIATIVES FOR ASU DRUGS 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.17 The Committee observes that a very low amount of allocation is being made for the scheme 
bearing a very vital mand ate. Even though the utilization of this paltry allocation is not quite 
satisfactory, the Committee is of the view that adequate and an enhanced allocation for this scheme 
is essential to create a strong Pharmacovigilance network across the country so as t o ensure 
effective control of misleading advertisements, safety monitoring of ASU&H drugs, 
documentations and analysis of Adverse Drug Reactions, etc. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends sufficient enhancement of funds for the scheme at RE stage. 
(Para 5.3) 

4 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.18 The network of Pharmacovigilance centres of ASU&H drugs is getting established and 
more peripheral pharmacovigilance centres have been added to the network. Accordingly, the 
recommendations of the Hon'ble Parliamentary Committee has b een noted and   provision of Rs. 
2.83 crore has been made in the B.E. 2020 -21, which can be appropriately enhanced in 
accordance with the proposal of requirement of funds from Pharmacovigilance Centres.   
 
Recommendation 
 
1.19 The Committee noting the vario us initiatives being taken under the scheme of 
Pharmacovigilance initiatives, observes that the Ministry is in the process of building a 
network/structure for carrying out strict monitoring/vigilance of quality control of ASU&H drugs. 
The Committee feels that this 3-tier network of pharmacovigilance needs to play pro -active role in 
reporting adverse drug events along with surveillance of misleading advertisements. With the 
Steering & Monitoring Committee, Technical Advisory Committee and Central Signal Dete ction 
& Causality Assessment Committee have been set up to assist in efficient implementation action of 
the said scheme, the Committee hopes that the task relating to the well -being of patients 
consuming ASU&H drugs will be taken care of. The Committee emp hasizes upon the need for 
imparting training and capacity building and also the awareness generation of ASU&H 
Pharmacovigilance so as to educate all involved in the network of pharmacovigilance for 
successful implementation of the scheme. 
(Para 5.5) 
Action Taken 
 
1.20 In order to successfully implement the scheme and for imparting training and generating 
awareness, three (3) CME training programs were conducted by the Intermediary 
Pharmacovigilance Centres of Ayurveda and Unani. Forty (40) public awareness programs have 
been conducted by the Peripheral Pharmacovigilance Centres to develop better understanding 
about the safety monitoring of ASU&H drugs. National  Pharmacovigilance Centre at All India 
Institute of Ayurveda, New Delhi conducted advance training program in collaboration with 
Inidan Pharmacopoeia Commission, Ghaziabad in March, 2019 and the Postgraduate Teaching 
and Research in Ayurveda, Jamnagar organised National Conference and Workshop 
on Pharmacovigilance in November, 2019.

II.  BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
 
Recommendation 
 
3.1 The Committee is disappointed to note that against the projected demand of Rs. 5035.27 
crore for 2020-21, the Ministry of AYUSH has been allocated Rs. 2122.08 crores reflecting huge 
shortfall of Rs. 2913.19 crores. The budgetary estimates of 2020 -21 when compared with RE 
2019-20 indicate a marginal increase of Rs. 265.08 crores only for the Ministry. The Committ ee 
appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry to get enhanced allocation but could not succeed. The 
Committee is constrained to note that several schemes of the Ministry would be affected due to 
shortfall of funds. The Committee apprehends that the objec tives of important schemes like 
National Ayush Mission, Health and Wellness centres etc would not be fulfilled due to financial 
constraints. Moreover, several research projects, construction work of National Institutes and 
Research Councils would also get affected. The Committee takes note of submission of the 
Ministry to make up the shortfall of funds by seeking a loan of Rs.1000 crore from Higher 
Education Funding Agency (HEFA), to support, in particular, the 8 construction projects of 
National Institutes. The Committee is surprised to note that financial constraints have forced the 
Ministry to raise loan which shows that Government is not very serious towards promoting 
AYUSH and letting it emerge as an alternative and economical system of medicine. The 
Committee, however, is of the view that that the Ministry, being a relatively new one requires 
adequate funds and resources for proving its efficacy and worth. The Committee, in its previous
