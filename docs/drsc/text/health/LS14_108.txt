I. BRIEF BACKGROUND
1.1 The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy (ISM&H) was established in the
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in March, 1995. It was re-named as Department of Ayurveda,
Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy with acronym as AYUSH in November, 2003. In
keeping with the policy of the Government to lay focused thrust on the Indian Traditional Systems of
Medicine, the Department of AYUSH was granted the status of Ministry w.e.f. 09.11.2014. The Ministry
is responsible for policy formulation, development and implementation of programmes for the growth,
development and propagation of Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy
(AYUSH) systems of Health Care. Sowa Rigpa is the recent addition to the existing family of AYUSH
systems. A request has also been sent this year by the Ministry to Rajbhasha Vibhag (MHA) and the
Commission for Scientific and Technical Terminology to recognise AYUSH as a word with the meaning:
"Traditional and non-conventional systems of healthcare and healing which include Ayurveda,
Yoga, Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa, Homoeopathy etc.
1.2 It is the vision of the Ministry to position AYUSH systems as the preferred systems of hiring and
practice for attaining a healthy India. The Ministry has identified its mission in terms of seven broad
thematic areas of AYUSH activities. The thematic areas are as follows:-
(i) Effective Human Resource Development
(ii) Provision of Quality AYUSH Services
(iii) Information, Education and Communication
(iv) Quality Research in AYUSH
(v) Growth of the Medicinal Plants Sector
(vi) Drug administration to accelerate the Pharmacopoeial/standardization work on AYUSH drugs
to ensure availability of high quality AYUSH drugs, to strengthen regulatory infrastructure in
Central and State Governments etc.
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminar/Workshops on AYUSH.

2.4 The Committee is constrained to note that despite the projected requirement of ````` 2797.96
crore for 2018-19, the Ministry of AYUSH has been allocated ````` 1626.37 crore leaving a shortfall
of ````` 1171.59 crores. The Committee also takes note of the meagre increase in allocation for
central sector and centrally sponsored schemes from 2017-18 to 2018-19. The Committee, time
and again, has been recommending for increasing the budgetary allocation for the Ministry
keeping in view the scope and reach of AYUSH in the life of the people. Given the popularity,
cost effectiveness and as an alternative to modern system of medicine i.e. Allopathy, the Ministry
of AYUSH through its various schemes/programmes/vision can help people through its various
remedies/cure and emerge as a solid system of medicine in healthcare delivery. Accordingly, the
Committee is of the considered view that there needs to be a quantum jump in the budgetary
allocation of the Minstry of AYUSH so that the very objectives of the Ministry to position AYUSH
systems as the preferred system of living and practice for attaining a healthy India is realized.
However, in view of the shortfall of funds for 2018-19, the Committee recommends to the

2.6 The Committee is anguished to see the meagre allocation given to Ministry of AYUSH
compared to Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Since the year 2013-14, allocation for the
Ministry of AYUSH is between 3% to 4% of the Health Budget. It is 3.08% of the Health Budget
for the year 2018-19. The Committee would like to emphasize that the Department of AYUSH
was granted the status of Ministry on 9th November, 2014 as it envisaged development and
implementation of programmes and policy formulation for the growth, development and propagation
of AYUSH systems of Healthcare. In view of the fact that the Ministry is being given a miniscule
budgetary allocation compared to Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, the Committee believes
that the Ministry of AYUSH would not be able to realize its objectives and tap the full potential
of the AYUSH systems. The Committee would like to reiterate that adequate funding is essential
for proper development of the AYUSH systems of medicine which has the capacity to emerge as
an alternative system of medicine through quality research, quality AYUSH services, standardization
of AYUSH drugs etc. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that given the vital importance
of the AYUSH systems, atleast 5% of the total Health Budget should be allocated for Ministry
of AYUSH mandatorily to boost up this sector. The Committee therefore recommends that the
Ministry of AYUSH should chalk out a solid fiscal roadmap for optimal utilisation of budgeted
funds and thereafter take up with the Ministry of Finance the issue of earmarking atleast 5%
of the total Health Budget for Ministry of AYUSH on priority basis. In the same vein, the
Committee would recommends that the Ministry should exercise due diligence in its
budget formulation, so that ambitious budgeting is commensurate with its ability or capacity to
absorb the enhanced funds and scarce resources deployable elsewhere do not remain idly
parked.

avoided if the Ministry had proactively pursued the issues concerned and streamlined its monitoring
mechanism. The Committee takes cognizance of the steps taken by the Ministry for optimum
utilisation of funds but would like to emphasize that if the Ministry needs more budgetary
allocation for proper implementation of its various schemes/programmes, it should be able to
show judicious and full utilization of allocated funds thereby justifying its demand for additional
funds. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Department to closely monitor the utilization
of funds for any scheme and take corrective measures as so to expedite matters where chances
of procedural delay is anticipated.

III. Quality Control of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathic (AYUSH) Drugs
3.1 Ministry of AYUSH has a Drug Control Cell (DCC) to administer regulatory and quality control
provisions for Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani & Homoeopathy (ASU&H) drugs. The Drug Control Cell deals
with Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules thereunder and the associated matter pertaining to
ASU&H Drugs. In this regard, the Cell coordinates with the State Licensing Authorities and Drug
Controllers to achieve uniform administration of the Act and for providing regulatory guidance and
clarifications. The Cell also manages the implementation of the Drug Quality Control component of the
National AYUSH Mission (NAM) through which grant in aid is provided for improving infrastructural and
functional capacity of the State Drug Testing Laboratories Pharmacies and Regulatory Frameworks. The
Drug Control Cell interacts with Central Drug Standard Control Organization (CDSCO) Directorate
General Foreign Trade, Ministry of Environment and Forests, Quality Council of India and National
Medicinal Plants Board and Drugs Manufacturers' Associations for various matters of ASU&H Drugs and
related issues. In order to oversee effective implementation of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules for
approval of ASU&H drugs, Technical Officers of the Ministry have been given additional charge as
Central Drug Inspector, Assistant Drug Controller and Deputy Drug Controller.
3.2 The major achievements/initiatives of the Ministry with respect to ensuring quality control of
ASU&H drugs in the interest of public safety are as follows:
• The Cabinet Note along with the "The Pharmacy Council of Indian Medicine and spelling Bill,
2017" has been sent to PMO and the Cabinet Secretariat for concurrence.

9
• The Central Sector Scheme of Pharmacovigilance of ASU&H drugs has been approved in the
SFC meeting held on 1st November, 2017 for implementation during 2017-18. The scheme
is envisaged for documentation of adverse drug event related to ASU&H drugs as well as
surveillance of misleading advertisements related to these systems and develop a institutional
mechanism for safety monitoring of AYUSH drugs.

3.7 The Committee would also like to express concern about the issue of failed samples of ASU
drugs and the consequent action taken in this regard. The Committee is of the firm view that
merely issuing show cause notices, departmental action etc. is not enough to control the menace
of spurious drugs of AYUSH. Despite repeated recommendations of the Committee to have
stringent measures to tackle the problem of failed samples, the Ministry seems not to have done
anything in this regard. The Committee takes serious note of the lackadaisical approach of the
Ministry in not taking concrete action against defaulters in order to ensure effective regulation
of ASU&H drugs. The Committee would like to emphasize that such erring manufacturers need
to be brought to book and dealt with strictly for misleading and duping innocent patients and
playing with their lives. The Committee, accordingly, recommends to the Ministry to lay down
stringent regulations which can act as a deterrent for erring manufacturers.

VI. National Medicinal Plants Board
6.1 India is a unique country of distinction by having codified system of traditional medicine like
Ayurveda and Siddha wherein the use of medicinal plants for preventive and curative health care are

21
described. India is also unique in possessing a substantial amount of biodiversity of medicinal plants,
thanks to its varied agro climatic regions and biodiversity hotspots. In India, there are around 17000
flowering plants of which 8000 plant species have shown medicinal value and all these plants are being
consumed by traditional healers, local people and ASUs Industry. India's herbal sector is representing
about 8500 licensed herbal units, thousands of extractors or semi processors, export houses and cottage-
level units of herbal based products, millions of folk healers and household users of thousands of herbal
raw drugs. As per the report of Pharmaxil, India is the world's second largest exporter of medicinal
plants after China and both the countries together produce more than 70% of the total global demand
for herbal products.
6.2 Based on the demand and supply study conducted by ICFRE supported by NMPB, it was found
that 1192 plant species are traded for their medicinal value of which 249 species are traded in large
quantity i.e. more than 100 MTs per year. The study also revealed that the total demand for medicinal
plants throughout the country is 5 lakh which is increasing at the rate of 7% per year. The report also
highlighted that with the demand for export for medicinal plants increasing, some of the medicinal plants
have found their way into farmer's field. The efforts of National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB) in
supporting Research and Development (R&D) related activities and support for Quality Planting Material
(QPM) and cultivation of MPs have led to bringing at least 50 species under cultivation. This has also
contributed to increasing the share of medicinal plants obtained from cultivation source to be upto 40%
by volume.

Help Groups (SHGs)/(BMCs).
(d) Research, Technology Development and Quality Assurance.
(e) Marketing Intervention.
(f) Awareness Building, Exposure visits, Education and Capacity Building of Stakeholders through
Information, Education and Communication (IEC) strategy.
(g) Promotion of Herbal Gardens, Home Herbal Gardens, School Herbal Gardens, Institutional and

(h) Establishment of Regional Centres of NMPB.

6.9 Attention of the Committee has also been drawn to the issue of creation of 65 new posts

II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
The Committee is constrained to note that despite the projected requirement of ````` 2797.96
crore for 2018-19, the Ministry of AYUSH has been allocated ````` 1626.37 crore leaving, a shortfall
of ````` 1171.59 crores. The Committee also takes note of the meagre increase in allocation for
central sector and centrally sponsored schemes from 2017-18 to 2018-19. The Committee, time
and again, has been recommending for increasing the budgetary allocation for the Ministry
keeping in view the scope and reach of AYUSH in the life of the people. Given the popularity,
cost effectiveness and as an alternative to modern system of medicine i.e. Allopathy, the Ministry
of AYUSH through its various schemes/programmes/vision can help people through its various
remedies/cure and emerge as a solid system of medicine in healthcare delivery. Accordingly, the
Committee is of the considered view that there needs to be a quantum jump in the budgetary
allocation of the Minstry of AYUSH so that the very objectives of the Ministry to position AYUSH
systems as the preferred system of living and practice for attaining a healthy India is realized.
However, in view of the shortfall of funds for 2018-19, the Committee recommends to the
Ministry to judiciously utilise the allocated funds and deploy the available resources in an
efficient manner. (Para 2.4)
The Committee is anguished to see the meagre allocation given to Ministry of AYUSH
compared to Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Since the year 2013-14, allocation for the
Ministry of AYUSH is between 3% to 4% of the Health Budget. It is 3.08% of the Health Budget
for the year 2018-19. The Committee would like to emphasize that the Department of AYUSH
was granted the status of Ministry on 9th November, 2014 as it envisaged development and
implementation of programmes and policy formulation for the growth, development and propagation
of AYUSH systems of Healthcare. In view of the fact that the Ministry is being given a miniscule
budgetary allocation compared to Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, the Committee believes
that the Ministry of AYUSH would not be able to realize its objectives and tap the full potential
of the AYUSH systems. The Committee would like to reiterate that adequate funding is essential
for proper development of the AYUSH systems of medicine which has the capacity to emerge as
an alternative system of medicine through quality research, quality AYUSH services, standardization
of AYUSH drugs etc. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that given the vital importance
of the AYUSH systems, atleast 5% of the total Health Budget should be allocated for Ministry
of AYUSH mandatorily to boost up this sector. The Committee therefore recommends that the
Ministry of AYUSH should chalk out a solid fiscal roadmap for optimal utilisation of budgeted
funds and thereafter take up with the Ministry of Finance the issue of earmarking atleast 5%
of the total Health Budget for Ministry of AYUSH on priority basis. In the same vein, the
Committee would recommend that the Ministry should exercise due diligence in its budget
formulation, so that ambitious budgeting is commensurate with its ability or capacity to absorb
the enhanced funds and scarce resources deployable elsewhere do not remain idly parked.
(Para 2.6)

The Committee has been given to understand that the funds remained unutilised for some

PENDING UCS
The Committee is constrained to note that the issue of pendency of UCs has become a
perennial problem. Despite recommendations of the Committee to liquidate the pending UCs,
there are still 1082 pending UCs amounting to ````` 524.56 crores. Out of 1082 pending UCs, 622
pertain to National Medicinal Plants Board amounting to ````` 94.78 crore and 244 pertain to
National AYUSH Mission amounting to ````` 391.34 crore. The Committee, therefore, strongly
recommends to the Ministry to liquidate the pending UCs and sort out this issue especially for
the two schemes indicating high pendency. The Ministry should make concerted efforts to
operationalize the portal as early as possible for online transmission of UCs. The Committee also
recommends to the Ministry to have a monitoring mechanism in place to check high pendency
of UCs with respect to certain schemes having pending UCs since 2001-02. (Para 2.16)
III. QUALITY CONTROL OF AYURVEDA, SIDDHA, UNANI AND HOMOEOPATHIC (AYUSH)
DRUGS
The Committee is of the view that a strong and strict regulatory framework is essential for
quality control of ASU&H drugs. Since the States are responsible for quality control and regulation
of AYUSH drugs, they need financial assistance so as to strengthen their regulatory framework.
The Committee notes that the financial assistance given to States is not much and therefore
reiterates its recommendation as given in 101st Report for significant enhancement of financial
assistance to the States. (Para 3.6)
The Committee would also like to express concern about the issue of failed samples of ASU
drugs and the consequent action taken in this regard. The Committee is of the firm view that
merely issuing show cause notices, departmental action etc. is not enough to control the menace
of spurious drugs of AYUSH. Despite repeated recommendations of the Committee to have
stringent measures to tackle the problem of failed samples, the Ministry seems not to have done
anything in this regard. The Committee takes serious note of the lackadaisical approach of the
Ministry in not taking concrete action against defaulters in order to ensure effective regulation
of ASU&H drugs. The Committee would like to emphasize that such erring manufacturers need

26
to be brought to book and dealt with strictly for misleading and duping innocent patients and
playing with their lives. The Committee, accordingly, recommends to the Ministry to lay down
stringent regulations which can act as a deterrent for erring manufacturers. (Para 3.7)
Attention of the Committee has been drawn to the fact that last year, 29 Drug laboratories
and 46 Pharmacies were available in the public sector in the States and 6 States did not have any
manufacturing unit. The Committee in its 101st report had recommended that there should be
atleast one Drug Testing Laboratory in each State and more ASU&H drug manufacturing units
in the country. The Ministry in its submission has not given any information about the increase
in State Drug Testing Laboratories, Pharmacies and ASU&H drug manufacturing units. The
Committee is constrained to note that the progress of GMP compliance by manufacturing units
across the country has also not been furnished by the Ministry. The Committee, accordingly,
recommends that the Ministry should strive for increasing the number of State Drug Testing
Laboratories for effective regulation of ASU&H drugs and manufacturing units which should be
GMP compliant for producing quality drugs for the people. The Ministry should also furnish the
relevant information to the Committee properly. (Para 3.9)
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry of AYUSH for monitoring of
AYUSH advertisements. Due to the monitoring by Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI)
and the Grievances against Misleading Advertisements (GAMA), 65% of the advertisements
found misleading were withdrawn or rectified and the rest forwarded to State AYUSH Drug
Regulators. The Committee is of the view that collaborative efforts of the Ministry along with
Department of Consumer Affairs, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting and State Governments
have certainly given results as misleading advertisements with respect to ASU&H drugs which
have been unregulated so far have been controlled to an extent. The Committee feels that it is
the need of the hour to strictly monitor and regulate the misleading advertisements for promoting
the sale of AYUSH medicine. Hence, the role of the Ministry of AYUSH is pivotal in proactively
pursuing the issue with other Departments/Ministries/ASCI on a regular basis for effectively
preventing the publication of inappropriate/misleading advertisements. (Para 3.12)
With respect to the new legislation containing the existing and proposed provisions of both
Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisement)
Act, 1954, the Committee is dismayed to note that the new legislation is still in the process of
being framed by the Department of Health and Family Welfare. It has been more than two years
since the new legislation is being framed and it speaks volumes about the laidback approach of
the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and also of the Ministry of AYUSH in pursuing the
matter. The Committee would like to strongly recommend that a definite time line within which
this new legislation will see the light of the day may be indicated and the Ministry of AYUSH
should aggressively pursue the matter with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare so that

27
relevant provisions are incorporated in the legislation and enacted so as to prohibit misleading
advertisements and strengthen the effective regulation of ASU&H drugs. (Para 3.13)
PHARMACOVIGILANCE INITIATIVE FOR ASU DRUGS
The Committee is relieved to finally see that the Central Scheme of Pharmacovigilance of
ASU&H medicines is taking shape as three-tier network of national, intermediary and peripheral
centres for pharmacovigilance. However, the Committee feels that once the required structure
is in place, the real work of pharmacovigilance of AYUSH drugs will initiate. The activities listed
out by the Ministry to be carried out in 2018-19 only seem to be preparatory exercises. The
Committee hopes that given the mandate of the scheme which entails quality control and
standardization of AYUSH drugs it will certainly give a big boost to the AYUSH systems of
medicine thereby upholding and increasing their credibility. (Para 3.17)
The Committee is also of the view that since no objection has been received from the
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare for creation of a vertical structure in CDSCO, all out
efforts need to be made by the Ministry to create this structure at the earliest under the CDSCO
for regulation and quality control of ASU&H drugs. The Committee also hopes that the recruitment
of Drug Controllers is completed as early as possible so that incumbents take charge of their
duties of pharmacovigilance. (Para 3.18)
IV  NATIONAL AYUSH MISSION
The Committee appreciates the Ministry of AYUSH for having co-location of AYUSH facilities
at PHCs, CHCs and District Hospitals, participation of AYUSH doctors in National Health
Programme and Public Health Outreach Activities and research activities undertaken to facilitate
integration of Ayurveda and Allopathic system of medicine. The Committee would, however, like
to point out that it is often seen that practitioners of modern system of medicine do not have
much faith in AYUSH system and often question their credibility and scientific validation. The
Committee feels that it is a known fact that our country has been a repository of knowledge of
traditional systems of medicine since ages. The need of the hour is to streamline the AYUSH
systems of medicine by standardization and quality control of ASU&H drugs and improving the
quality of education and healthcare services through AYUSH. At the same time, the curricula of
both modern systems of medicine and AYUSH system need to be changed in such a way as to
strive for integration in the study of medical education itself followed by cross system referrals
and integrated healthcare services. The Committee is of the view that such an integrated approach
towards both systems of medicine would help in understanding the strengths of each system of
medicine and also increase the credibility of AYUSH systems of medicine. The Committee,
accordingly, recommends to the Ministry to take concrete steps in introducing modules of modern
system of medicine in the curriculum and pursue the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare for
introducing similar modules of AYUSH systems of medicines in their MBBS course.
(Para 4.10)
