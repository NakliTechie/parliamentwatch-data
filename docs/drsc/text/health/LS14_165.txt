INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health 
and Family Welfare, having been authorised by the Committee, present this 165th Report of the 
Committee on the Demands for Grants (Demand No.4) of the Ministry of Ayush for the year 
2025-26. 
 
2. The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees are entrusted with the 
constitutional role of ensuring accountability of executive to the Parliament.  In this regard, 
Rule 270 (a) of Rules & Procedure and Conduct & Business in the Council of States (Rajya 
Sabha) empowered the Department-related Standing Committees to consider the Demands for 
Grants of the related Departments/Ministries with the objective of ensuring financial 
accountability of the executive and report thereon. In exercise of its constitutional mandate, the 
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health & Family Welfare scrutinized 
the Demands for Grants for the year 2025-26 (Demand No. 4) of the Ministry of Ayush. 
 
3.  The Committee, in its meeting held on 17st February, 2025, heard the Secretary, 
Ministry of Ayush and other Officers on the Demands for Grants (2025-26) of the Ministry of 
Ayush. 
 
4.  The Committee while making its observations/recommendations has mainly relied upon

concerned; 
(vii) Economic Survey 2024-25; and 
(viii) Budget Speech of the Finance Minister. 
 
5.  The Committee, in its meeting held on 11th March, 2025, considered the draft report and 
adopted the same.

CHAPTER I 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
The Ministry of Ayush formed in 2014, evolved from the Department of Indian System of 
Medicine and Homoeopathy (ISM&H) formed in 1995. The Department was responsible for the 
development of ancient system of medicine prevalent in Indian subcontinent in times of yore. It 
was renamed as the Department of Ayurveda, Yoga, and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and 
Homoeopathy (Ayush) in November 2003 with focused attention towards education and 
research in Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homoeopathy. Sowa Rigpa, a 
traditional medicine system in many parts of Himalyan regions was also brought under the 
umbrella in year 2011.  
 
1.1 Ayurveda is a comprehensive traditional system of medicine evolved in India thousands of 
year ago in medieval and ancient times. Ayurveda emphasizes on holistic approach through 
health promotion, disease prevention and curative roles. Ayurveda considers health as a 
dynamic integration of environment, body, mind and soul and accordingly advocates 
personalized approach for prescribing diet, lifestyle or treatment regimen based on its 
fundamental concept that everyone has distinct psychosomatic constitution and health status. 
Homoeopathy, has also been in practice in India for around 100 years, provides long term 
palliative treatment in both acute and chronic cases owing to its effectiveness, lack of side 
effects and low cost of treatment. The National Health Policy (NHP-2017) advocates 
mainstreaming the potential of Ayush within a pluralistic system of integrated healthcare. In 
convergence with NHP and National Health Mission, the National Ayush Mission (NAM) 
envisages better access to Ayush Services, operationalization of Ayush Health and Wellness 
Centre, quality improvement of Ayush Education and Research in order to achieve Sustainable 
Development Goals and enhanced public health. 
 
1.1.1 The Ministry of Ayush works with the vision to position Ayush systems as the preferred 
systems of living and practice for attaining a healthy India. Such a vision has gradually emerged 
through the thought processes of experts concerned with health care provisioning in the country. 
The Ministry strives to promote these systems through robust policy frameworks, widespread 
awareness, and comprehensive educational initiatives. The core objectives include driving 
research and innovation, ensuring access to quality Ayush products, and fostering global 
acceptance of these practices. The Ministry is dedicated to developing skilled professionals, 
bolstering the medicinal plant sector, and ultimately, enhancing public health through 
sustainable and accessible Ayush solutions.

Thematic Areas of Ayush  
 
1.2 The Ministry envisages its Mission in terms of seven broad thematic areas of Ayush 
activities. The thematic areas are as follows: 
 
(i) Effective Human Resource Development 
(ii) Provision of Quality Ayush Services 
(iii) Information, Education and Communication 
(iv) Quality Research in Ayush 
(v) Growth of the Medicinal Plants Sector 
(vi) Drug Administration 
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminars/Workshops in Ayush sector 
 
 
FUNCTIONAL AREAS OF THE MINISTRY 
1.3 With an aim to position India as a Global Health care leader, the Ministry has identified the 
key areas of focus as Globalization and Market Share; Research; Technology; Policy Regulation 
and Standardizations; Manufacturing and Services; and Healthcare and Education. 
 
1.3.1 The Major Activities of Ministry of Ayush are carried out by several autonomous 
institutions, and various institutes/councils under direct control of the Ministry; Centre Sector 
Schemes and the Centrally Sponsored Schemes where grants are provided through National 
Ayush Mission (NAM) to States/UTs to take up Ayush Activities. 
 
1.3.2 The Ministry of Ayush executes its mandate through its 5 Research Councils, 12 National 
Institutes, the 2 Regulatory Commission (i.e. NCISM & NCH), its subordinate offices PCIM&H 
and the Public Sector Undertaking IMPCL.

1.3.3 The Committee takes into account the thematic areas of Ayush activities and the 
institutional framework to achieve the objectives of those activities. The Committee 
desires that each and every institution must strive towards achieving the Mission 
Objective of the Ministry of Ayush within the specified time framework. The Committee 
further observes that the broad thematic area of Ayush activities is the key to accomplish 
the mission objectives of the Ministry i.e., improve access to high quality and affordable 
Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Sowa-Rigpa (AUS&SR) & Homeopathy medical education, 
availability of adequate and high quality AUS&SR & Homeopathy medical professionals 
in all parts of the country, promote equitable and universal healthcare that encourages 
community health perspective. The thematic area of Quality research in Ayush is 
undertaken through pioneer research councils under MoA such as CCRAS, CCRUM, 
CCRS, CCRH and CCRYN. The Committee, however, feels that in order to address 
emerging challenges of healthcare, there is a need for functional integration of Ayurveda 
and conventional medicine with its affordable and evidence based use to boost Ayurveda 
in the paradigm of contemporary scientific, technological and medical parameters.

1.8 The Committee appreciates the milestones achieved by the Ministry in year 2023-24 & 
2024-25 and takes note of initiatives proposed for the coming FY 2025-26. Given the 
operational efficiency, professional pursuit and proper & adequate institutional support 
for research councils, educational institutes, regulatory bodies, etc., the Committee 
observes that the Ayush system is striving towards establishing itself an alternative system 
of medicine. The Committee desires that the Ministry should make concerted efforts for 
optimal utilization of the potential of available AYUSH resources and establish the Ayush 
system within a pluralistic system of Integrative healthcare and re-emphasizing the need 
for integrating AYUSH in the National Health Mission/National Health Priorities, 
research and education in convergence with the objectives of National Health Policy 
(NHP) 2017. The Committee recommends the Ministry to lay focus on the implementation 
of its schemes, maintaining high standards of education and research and strengthen its 
monitoring mechanism vide its institutional statutory and regulatory bodies to have 
Always  Better Control (ABC) over quality and safety of AYUSH drugs. The Committee 
appreciates the new initiatives for the coming year and is of the view that Ayurveda and 
Yoga should reach every household for a Healthy and Fit India.

PHARMACOPOEIA COMMISSION FOR INDIAN MEDICINE AND 
HOMOEOPATHY (PCIM&H) 
 
3.3 Pharmacopoeia Commission for Indian Medicine & Homoeopathy (PCIM&H), is a 
subordinate office under Ministry of Ayush. Development of Pharmacopoeias and Formularies 
as well as acting as Central Drug Testing cum Appellate Laboratory for Indian systems of 
Medicine & Homoeopathy are the key fields of activity of PCIM&H. The vision of the 
PCIM&H is to be the Nodal Agency in establishing Quality standards for drugs of Indian 
Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy and the mission objective is to formulate Quality 
standards for drugs used in Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy and to act as the 
Central Drug Testing cum Appellate Laboratory thereof. Apart from this, other key fields of 
activity related to Indian systems of Medicine & Homoeopathy include Imparting training 
towards Capacity building of stakeholders; establishing repositories for Raw Drug Reference 
Materials & Chemical Reference Materials and Development of Medicinal Plants Garden, 
Herbarium, Museum etc.

3.3.4 PCIM&H acts as the Central Drugs Laboratory for Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy for 
the purpose of testing or analysis of ASU&H Drugs. PCIM&H on behalf of Ministry of Ayush 
lays down the formulary specifications and pharmacopoeial standards for Ayurveda, Siddha, 
Unani and Homoeopathy (ASU&H) drugs which serves as official compendia for ascertaining 
the quality  (identity, purity and strength) of the ASU&H drugs. AS per the Drugs & Cosmetics 
Act, 1940 and rules there under, the compliance to this quality standards are mandatory for the 
production of ASU&H drugs being manufactured in India. So far, 2259 quality standards on raw 
materials (single drugs of plant/ animal/ mineral/ metal/ chemical origin) used in ASU&H 
drugs, 405 quality standards of ASU formulations and 2666 formulary specifications of ASU 
drugs   has been published. In addition to above, supporting documents in the form of Macro-
Microscopic & TLC Atlas on 351 single drugs incorporated in Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of 
India (API) has also published. Rule 160 A to J of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 
provides the regulatory guidelines for approval of Drug Testing Laboratory for carrying out 
such tests of identity, purity, quality and strength of Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs. As on 
date, 34 State Drug Testing Laboratories have been supported for strengthening their 
infrastructural and functional capacity.  Further, 106 laboratories are approved or licensed under 
the provisions of Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 for quality testing of Ayurvedic, Siddha and 
Unani drugs and raw materials. 03 Drug Testing Laboratories of CCRAS approved under Rule 
160E of drugs and cosmetics Rules 1945 for quality testing of Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani 
drugs and raw materials. 
 
3.3.5 The Committee strongly recommends the consolidation of all AYUSH drug-related 
standard-setting processes under a single authoritative body viz. an independent drug 
controller of AYUSH, in alignment with the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, and its 
associated rules. To achieve this, the Ministry should establish a streamlined and inclusive 
mechanism that actively involves stakeholders in the development of pharmacopoeial 
standards, ensuring greater efficiency and uniformity. Additionally, the Committee 
emphasizes that PCIM&H and CCRAS may come together to coordinate and collaborate 
in this initiative to enhance the scientific testing and evaluation of a larger number of 
ASU&H drug samples to ensure safety, efficacy, and quality strengthening the foundation 
of research and standardization in the sector.

UNIFORM &STANDARD LICENSING  
4.3 The Ministry has informed that it is working in coordination with Ayush Grid and state 
licensing Authority, Gujarat to make e-AUSHADHI portal more user-friendly and robust for 
online licensing of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy drugs. 
4.3.1 The Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Drugs Rules, 1945 have exclusive regulatory 
provisions for Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani, and Homoeopathy drugs. Provisions relating to 
Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani Drugs are contained in Chapter IVA and Schedule- I of the Drugs 
and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and in Rules 151 to 169, Schedules E(I), T & TA of the Drugs and 
Cosmetics Rules, 1945. Further, second schedule (4A) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 
provides standards for Homoeopathic drugs and  Rules 2dd, 30AA, 67 (C-H), 85 (A to I), 106-
A, Schedule K, Schedule M-I of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 pertain to Homoeopathic 
drugs. It is mandatory for the manufacturers to adhere to the prescribed requirements for 
licensing of manufacturing units & medicines including proof of safety & effectiveness, 
compliance with the Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) as per Schedule T & Schedule M-I 
of Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 and quality standards of drugs given in the respective 
pharmacopoeia. 
4.3.2 The matter related to uniform pattern of license was discussed in the meeting of 
Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Consultative Committee (ASUDCC) held on 14/03/2024, 
wherein   Committee recommended that all the State Licensing Authorities (SLAs) should give 
uniform pattern of license numbers and to make necessary provisions under the rules to display 
manufacturing license number of the particular product in all the advertisements- electronic, 
social media or print media. The said recommendation was placed in ASUDTAB meeting held 
on 24/07/2024, wherein board recommended for draft notification for certain amendment in the 
Drug Rules for uniform license throughout the country. 

57 
 
4.3.3 The Committee is of view that the Ministry must have a standard licensing process 
based on uniform parameters across all the States so that drug formulations do not vary 
from State to State. A standardized approach will eliminate variations in drug 
formulations from state to state, thereby enhancing the quality, safety, and efficacy of 
AYUSH medicines. This will prevent discrepancies in ingredient composition, dosage, and 
therapeutic claims, ensuring that AYUSH drugs meet the same regulatory and scientific 
benchmarks nationwide. It will also strengthen consumer confidence by guaranteeing that 
all licensed AYUSH drugs adhere to nationally recognized quality standards, ultimately 
fostering wider acceptance and integration of AYUSH medicines in mainstream 
healthcare. 
4.3.4 The Committee, keeping in view the obscure and diverse pattern of licensing that 
creates hurdles for the manufactures for seeking license for production of Ayush products. 
The Committee therefore recommends that arrangement be made to have simplified 
procedure for seeking uniform and standard license that will ensure ease of doing business 
for Ayush manufacturers thereby seeking the objective of AtmaNirbhar Bharat and Make 
in India mission. Moreover, the manufacturers will be encouraged to setup AYUSH 
industry in the country for production of AYUSH products that will ultimately promote 
AYUSH sector in domestic as well as the global market. 
 
Clinical Trials Registry- India (CTRI) 
4.4 The Clinical Trials Registry- India (CTRI), hosted at the ICMR’s National Institute of 
Medical Statistics is a free and online public record system for registration of clinical trials 
being conducted in India that was launched on 20th July 2007. The CTRI also has data sets 
specific to the AYUSH system of medicine where AYUSH-specific information is being 
captured during the trial registration process.  The Committee has been apprised that the 
information pertaining to R&D protocol, approval and subsequent outcomes is maintained 
separately at respective institutes, research councils and other autonomous bodies etc. Clinical 
Trial of CCRAS is being monitored through an elaborated mechanism “Electronic Data 
Capture(EDC)” which is scrutinized at the headquarters level. CCRH is registering all its 
clinical trials on Clinical trial Registry of India before initiation of its research project. NIA has 
established the PMU (Project management Unit) to develop a robust CTMS and also launched 
IMR(Intra Mural Research) Policy for better R&D outcomes. NIA has also developed DDDU ( 
Drug development and Discovery Unit) for development of New Drugs. 
 
4.4.1 The Committee is of considered view that implementing a robust Clinical Trial 
Management System (CTMS) will significantly improve documentation, efficiency, and 
compliance in AYUSH clinical research. All the research councils, institutes and other 
autonomous bodies engaged in R&D, clinic trials and related activities should be brought 
onto to this single platform. Automation of key trial processes, including patient 
recruitment, randomization, monitoring, adverse event reporting, and regulatory 
submissions, will enhance efficiency. By automating workflows, it will reduce manual 
errors, enhance regulatory transparency, and accelerate trial approvals. Standardizing 
research protocols with ICMR/GCP guidelines will ensure uniformity across AYUSH 

58 
 
disciplines, strengthening the scientific validation of traditional medicine. AI-driven 
analytics will support data-driven decision-making, enabling better tracking of adverse 
events and treatment outcomes. Furthermore, a well-integrated CTMS will allow multi-
center trials, facilitating global acceptance of AYUSH-based interventions and positioning 
India as a leader in integrative medicine research. 
4.4.2 The Committee further desires that information on reported claims regarding Ayush 
treatment for controlling cholesterol levels using tablets or injections, supported by 
research-based evidence, if available, may be provided.

Clinical Trials Registry- India (CTRI) 
The Committee is of considered view that implementing a robust Clinical Trial 
Management System (CTMS) will significantly improve documentation, efficiency, and 
compliance in AYUSH clinical research. All the research councils, institutes and other 
autonomous bodies engaged in R&D, clinic trials and related activities should be brought 
onto to this single platform. Automation of key trial processes, including patient 
recruitment, randomization, monitoring, adverse event reporting, and regulatory 
submissions, will enhance efficiency. By automating workflows, it will reduce manual 
errors, enhance regulatory transparency, and accelerate trial approvals. Standardizing 
research protocols with ICMR/GCP guidelines will ensure uniformity across AYUSH 
disciplines, strengthening the scientific validation of traditional medicine. AI-driven 
analytics will support data-driven decision-making, enabling better tracking of adverse 
events and treatment outcomes. Furthermore, a well-integrated CTMS will allow multi-
center trials, facilitating global acceptance of AYUSH-based interventions and positioning 
India as a leader in integrative medicine research.

FUTURE PLANS 
The Committee appreciates the larger vision of Ayush @2047 and the future plans of 
making AYUSH a multi-billion dollar market, yet the Committee is wary about the 
present situation of the healthcare in the country and observes that yearly short term 
goals be given a priority and direction to provide best AYUSH healthcare to the public by 
establishing PHCs/CHCs at district level etc. The Committee is in agreement of the 
Ministry of Ayush that the target of 30 lakh Ayush professionals in country by 2047, 
setting up one Ayush HQ in every district with at least one Ayush College, creation of over 
50 lakh new jobs, increasing contribution of Ayush to up to 7.7% of GDP etc. by the 2047 
is achievable by converting the long duration ambitious targets into short-term plan with 
smaller objective & goals and having co-ordinating and controlling monitoring 
mechanism over the implementation progress of projects/schemes/programmes ensuring 
achievement of set targets/goals/objectives. The Committee, in this regard, recommends 
the Ministry to make concerted efforts to implement the projects/schemes/programme 
through development of botanical drugs, entering into Free Trade Agreements (FTA) with 
favourable countries for enlarging global markets & enhancing exports level to a new 
height, establishing global hub of traditional medicine alongside WHO GTMC, production 
& promotion standard & quality drugs, ensuring widespread availability of 
AyushAushadi Kendra etc.
