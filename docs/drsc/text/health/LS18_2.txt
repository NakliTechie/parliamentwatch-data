I.     INTRODUCTION
1.1   India, the largest Democratic Republic in the world and the second most populous country after China, possesses
2.4% of the world’s land area and every year it adds about 16 million people to its large base of population. In terms of
population growth rate, there has been decline during the decade 
1991--- 2001 as compared to previous decade 1981--- 1991 and it has been on the declining trend in the context of the
proposed Family Planning Campaign. According to the provisional results compiled by RG India for the Census of

1.2   During the Tenth Five Year Plan (2002--- 07), the main approach of the Family Welfare Programme is:
           (i)     To assess the needs for reproductive and child health of the community and to provide need based, client
centered and demand driven RCH care.
           (ii)    To strengthen the infrastructure for service delivery and bridging the gap in essential infrastructure and

II.   BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1   The Department proposed an outlay of Rs. 43,996.58 crores for the Tenth Plan, against which the approved outlay
is Rs. 27,125.00 crores. The Committee notes that against a proposed Plan allocation for 
Rs. 6444.40 crores in 2004-05, outlay approved was Rs. 5500.00 crore. The Committee was informed that reduction in
the proposed outlay for 2004-05 did not result in reduction in the EAP component. However, the DBS component of Rs.
3512.47 crore for 2004-05 is even less than the DBS component of Rs. 3631.10 crore for 2003-04, in spite of total
allocation being increased by Rs. 570.00 crores.
2.2   The Committee was given to understand that the cut in allocation will result in curtailing the release of funds in the
following major schemes:
           *      Grant-in-aid to the States for the maintenance of infrastructure units
                   •   Sub-Centres
                   •   Urban FW Services
                   •   Direction and Administration
           *      Procurement of Supplies and Materials under RCH Programme
           *      Social Marketing of Contraceptives
2.3   The Committee was informed that the matter was taken up with the Planning Commission which has agreed in
principle to additional allocation of Rs. 280.00 crores under DBS and enhance  DBS component by Rs. 400.00 crores by
corresponding reduction in EAP component. This will effectively increase the total Plan size of the Department to Rs.
5780.00 crores from the present level of Rs. 5500.00 crores.
2.4   The Committee notes that this additional allocation of Rs. 680.00 crores under DBS is to be utilized for the
following schemes:----
           *      Rural FW Services ---- Rs. 448.00 crores for maintenance of Sub-Centres
           *      Social Marketing of Contraceptives ---- Rs. 56.00 crores
           *      Increasing the counterpart funding under DBS in RCH Programme ---- Rs. 136.00 crores
           *      Empowered Action Group (EAG) ---- Rs. 40.00 crores (This amount will be used for schemes in high
fertility districts)
2.5   The approved outlay of the Department for the Ninth Plan was Rs. 15,120.00 crores. Against this, the total funds
available were Rs. 14,102.35 crores and the expenditure incurred was Rs. 13,968.72 crores.
        The Committee notes that the approved outlay for Tenth Plan is Rs. 27,125.00 crores, almost double of the Ninth
Plan provision of Rs. 14,102.35 crores. The Committee observes that there was a shortfall in the utilisation of Ninth
Plan allocation. Utilisation position has not improved in the first two years of the Tenth Plan also. In both the years,
Plan allocation had to be reduced at the RE stage and even this reduced allocation remained under-utilised. The
Committee observes that there is a difference of Rs. 800.00 crores between the RE allocation of 2003-04 and BE
provision of 2004-05. Additional Rs. 680.00 crores have been agreed to by the Planning Commission. The Department

III.   RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES
3.1   In order to provide comprehensive primary Health care services at the grass root level, Sub-Centres are established
for every 5000 rural population (3000 population in the tribal and hilly areas). These 
Sub-Centres provide basic health and family welfare services to the rural population.
3.2   There is a marginal decrease in the allocation of Plan funds for Rural Family Welfare services. Against BE
provision of Rs. 1563.50 crores in 2003-04, allocation for 2004-05 stands at Rs. 1532.71 crores. The Committee,
however, notes that additional allocation of Rs. 448.00 crores is proposed to be earmarked for Rural Family Welfare
Services to which Planning Commission has agreed to in principle.
3.3   Government of India bears the salary of ANMs and honorarium to health worker (Female), besides rent liability
and contingency whereas the salary of the Male Worker is borne by the State Government PHCs are established and
maintained by the State Government under the Minimum Needs Programme/Basic Minimum Services Programme.
PHC acts as a referral unit for six Sub-Centres. CHCs are being established and maintained by the State Government
under MNP/BMS Programme. CHC serves as a referral centre for four PHCs.

3.6   The activities of PHCs involve curative, preventive and also Family Welfare Services. CHC provides facilities for
obstetric care and specialist consultation. The Committee is constrained to observe that the existing shortfall in the
number of PHCs and CHCs means that as many as 9996 Sub-Centres and 9164 PHCs are deprived of the facilities of
PHCs and CHCs respectively. The Committee therefore, feels that the Department should approach the State
Governments for establishing the required number of PHCs and CHCs. The Committee is of the view that if the Sub-
Centres are not served by PHCs and CHCs, then the very purpose of establishing and running them gets defeated. If a
well established and functional setup of Sub-Centres, being served by PHCs and CHCs is there, heavy burden on the
secondary and tertiary care hospitals will automatically lessen, resulting in better services being provided by them.
3.7   The Committee notes that out of the 22,926 PHCs functioning in the country, 14435 are in Government building
and remaining 8491 PHCs are functioning in rented/rent free Panchayat building. Similarly, out of 3106 CHCs
functioning in the country, 2387 are in Government building and remaining 719 CHCs are functioning in rented / rent
free Panchayat buildings. The Committee observes that the Government has taken a number of steps for strengthening
the centres which include revision in the norms for rent, drug supply, additional fund for sub-centre under Pradhan
Mantri Gramodyaya Yojana (PMGY), Financial assistance for minor repair works, construction/repair of Operation
Theatre, Labour Room etc. The Committee feels that the ideal position will be if all the PHCs and CHCs are functioning
in the Government buildings. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department may explore the possibility
of having more PHCs operating from Governments building.

IV.  REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH PROGRAMME
4.1   Reproductive and Child Health Programme is the flagship programme of the Department of Family Welfare with
combined trinity of the objectives i.e. reproductive health, child survival and fertility regulation. The programme is
primarily offered thorough primary health infrastructure. It has been extended to all the districts in the country and is
currently in the seventh year of implementation.
4.2   Plan allocation for RCH Programme for 2004-05 is Rs. 783.16 crore, out of which DBS component is Rs. 69.50
crore and EAP component is Rs. 713.66 crore. The Committee notes that almost similar provision was made during
2003-04. However, BE 2003-04 of Rs. 712.41 crore was reduced to Rs. 442.34 crores at RE Stage and the actual
expenditure incurred is only Rs. 388.53 crore. Two of the main reasons cited for reduction in the allocation at RE stage
and even lesser actual expenditure are non-submission of utilisation certificates and Audit reports and availability of
unspent balance with the States/UTs. The Committee is disturbed to note that out of total allocation of Rs. 2217.00 crore
under this programme since its inception in 1997-98, only Rs. 1304.47 crore could be utilized and unspent balance is Rs.
912.8 crore. The position is very alarming in respect of the following states.

VI.   VANDE  MATRAM SCHEME
          The aim of the Vande Matram Scheme launched on 9 th February, 2004 is to reduce maternal mortality of
pregnant women by involving and utilising resources of specialists/trained workforce available in the private sector. It
has been informed that till now 1320 doctors and 517 nursing homes/maternity centres have volunteered for enrolment
under the Scheme. Besides, Federation of Obstetrics and Gynaecological Society of India has submitted a list of 685
FOJSI members enrolled under the Scheme. The Committee welcomes this initiative and hopes that private involvement
will continue to increase.
VII.  STRENGTHENING OF IMMUNISATION PROGRAMME & ERADICATION OF POLIO
7.1     Plan allocation for 2004-05 has been kept at Rs. 1186.40 crore as against Rs. 702 crore provided in 2003-04.
Higher outlay in BE 2004-05 is mainly meant for intensifying the Pulse Polio Immunization Programme.
7.2     The Committee notes that the total number of polio cases detected in the country during 2001, 2002 and 2003 are
268, 1600, 225 respectively. During 2004 as on 10.7.04, 18 Polio cases have been recorded, out of which 6 each are
from U.P. and Bihar. The Committee was informed that the reason for occurrence of more cases in Uttar Pradesh and
Bihar are that these States have been the primary reservoir of the virus in the past. During the same period in 2002 and
2003, Uttar Pradesh reported 678 cases (2002) and 30 cases (2003) and Bihar reported 279 cases (2002) and 8 cases
(2003).
7.3     The Committee was informed that in three districts of U.P.----  Badayun, Muradabad and Bulandshahr, Pulse-
Polio campaign is yet to succeed. The Department has planned five more National Immunization Rounds with one on
the 23 August, 2004. The Committee was given to understand that the visible high level support at national and State
level, political and public leaders as well as intensive work in these areas will result in success in elimination of Polio.
The Committee hopes that with the completion of
these rounds, the Sates of U.P. and Bihar would ultimately become polio free.
7.4     The Committee notes that the current coverage under the Routine Immunization is only 57% with States like
Bihar and U.P. having a coverage below 30/40 percentage. Introduction of AD syringes, provision for carrying
medicines to the immunization sites rather than making the ANM fetch the vaccine from PHC, arrangement for
mobilization of children on the fixed immunization day, setting up of National Vaccine Cell for ensuring timely supply
of vaccine to the States are some of the initiatives proposed for making the coverage for under routine immunization go
up from 57 to 90%. The Committee appreciates the initiatives proposed to be taken for increasing the coverage under
routine immunization and hopes that this programme would also receive the same level of satisfaction as Pulse-Polio.

IX.     COMPENSATION
9.1     Plan allocation under ‘Compensation’ for 2004-05 is Rs. 200.15 crore against an allocation of 
Rs. 186.46 crore in 2003-04. The increase in the allocation for 2004-05 has been proposed in view of expected increase
in the number of acceptors of sterilization/IUD insertion specially in EAG States. The Committee was informed that on
the basis of inputs received from these States and also to meet the excepted level of achievement as indicated in the
Tenth Plan, compensation package for undergoing Tubectomy/Vasectomy in the EAG States has been revised from Rs.
300/200 respectively to Rs. 400 per sterilization for the year 2003-04. The State Governments/UT Administrations have
been given full flexibility for drawing up their schemes, including the amount for diet, transport, payment to motivators
and service providers, within the total amount. The same package has also been extended to accredited private/NGO
health facilities in EAG States.
9.2     The Committee notes that there has been over all improvement in the achievement level of various family
planning measures in 2003-04 (April-December, 2003) as compared to the corresponding period of 2002-03. With
regard to sterilization methods there was coverage of 3.05 million persons in 2003-04 against a coverage of 2.90 million
in 2002-03. The Committee, however, notes that breakup of acceptance of Vasectomy and Tubectomy has not been
indicated for the year 2003-04 as done in the previous years. The Committee feels that separate details regarding
acceptance level of these two methods will alone give the actual position of the success rate of family planning
measures.
X.     ANNUAL REVIEW REPORT OF THE DEPARTMENT FOR 2002-03
10.1   As per Annual Review Report for 2002-03 of the Central Internal Audit Party of the Department, financial
irregularities/variations amounting to Rs. 92.95 crore have been noticed. Out of 21 defaulter States/UTs, financial
irregularities amounting to Rs. 27.61 crore and Rs. 32.73 crore in Andhra Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh respectively are
worth-noting. Some of the irregularities noticed in the Report are indicated below:----
           *      Misutilisation/delayed utilisation of Miscellaneous Purpose Fund.
           *      Irregular charging of States general side expenditure to the Family Welfare Budget.
           *      Non-adjustment of temporary advances drawn on abstract contingent bills.
10.2   The Committee views with serious concern the irregularities in Annual Review Report 2002-03 of the
Department. The Committee strongly feels that irregular charging of States general side expenditure to the Family
Welfare Budget is a very disturbing trend. The Committee apprehends that this shortcoming might have been noticed in
previous years also. If that be so, the Committee would like to be apprised about the corrective measures taken in this
regard.

12.1   The Committee notes that monitoring and evaluation of the performance of the Family Welfare Programme is
conducted by eight Regional Evaluation Teams located at Delhi, Bangalore, Bhopal, Pune, Patna, Lucknow, Kolkata
and Chennai. Each evaluation team undertakes tour of 20 days every month and covers two adjoining districts selected
randomly covering an average of 700 acceptors of family planning.
The Committee would like to be apprised about the feedback received regarding U.P., Bihar, Orissa, M.P. and NE
States.
12.2   The Committee feels that availability of funds is therefor carrying out Family Welfare Programmes. However,
problem lies in their effective implementation. The Committee is of the view that a number of initiatives need to be
taken by the Department in this regard. Instead of routine publicity campaigns on the need of family planning, the
Department should have a publicity programme which is region-specific. Mass leaders, leading actors etc. need to be
involved for popularizing positive aspects of family planning methods. Surrogate advertisements in the form of
dialogues in films can be another measure. The Department may coordinate with Ministry of Information &
Broadcasting in this regard. Similarly Panchayati Raj Institutions and Community at large can play an effective role in
this field. The Committee is of the view that the Department should evolve a mechanism where under Panchayati Raj
Institutions as well as Anganwadi Centres are actively involved in Family Planning measures. A beginning can be made
by ensuring availability of condoms etc. in Anganwadi Centres/Panchayats. Cooperation of the Department of
Panchayati Raj /Women & Child Development may be sought in this regard. Another effective initiative can be about
the NGOs who are actively involved in the implementation of family welfare measures. The Committee feels that in
order to make them more accountable and also to assess the work being done by them, it would prove to be very fruitful
if a list of NGOs working in a particular area/district is given to the local MP. The Committee is of the view that instead
of depending only on the State reports indicating achievement in implementation of different programmes, the
Department should evolve a strategy where under all the agencies as indicated above make useful and far-reaching
contributions in an area which is of prime importance for the growth and development of the country.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
II.    BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
        The Committee feels that in view of status of utilisation of allocated funds in the first two years of Tenth Plan, the

05 is the third year of the Tenth Plan. The Committee feels that this is the appropriate time for making a mid-term
appraisal of all the programmes being run by the Department and taking corrective measures wherever required.
                                                                                                                                             (Para 2.5)
III.   RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES
        The Committee notes that the requirement of Sub-centres is as per 1991 population norms. Requirement must have
increased as per Census 2001. Shortfall in the number of Sub-centres is targeted to be fulfilled during the Tenth Plan.
The Committee is not aware about the progress made so far in the achievement of this target. The Committee would,
however, like to emphasize that the Department should make all efforts to get established the targeted Sub-centres
during the Tenth Plan. The Committee feels that 2004-05, being the mid year of the Tenth Plan period, is the right time
to make an assessment about the shortfall in the number of Sub-centres as per Census 2001 and start preparatory
exercise for specific allocation of funds for this purpose from the first year of the Eleventh Plan. At the same time, the
Department should also approach the concerned State Governments so that ground work for establishing the required
Sub-Centres is not delayed for want of preparedness on the part of State Governments.                                                  
(Para 3.5)
        The Committee is constrained to observe that the existing shortfall in the number of PHCs and CHCs means that as
many as 9996 Sub-Centres and 9164 PHCs are deprived of the facilities of PHCs and CHCs respectively. The
Committee therefore, feels that the Department should approach the State Governments for establishing the required
number of PHCs and CHCs. The Committee is of the view that if the Sub-centres are not served by PHCs and CHCs,
then the very purpose of establishing and running them gets defeated. If a well established and functional setup of Sub-
centres, being served by PHCs and CHCs is there, heavy burden on the secondary and tertiary care hospitals will
automatically lessen, resulting in better services being provided by
them.                                                                                 (Para 3.6)
        The Committee feels that the ideal position will be if all the PHCs and CHCs are functioning in the Governments
buildings. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department may explore the possibility of having more
PHCs operating from Governments building.                                                                                    (Para 3.7)
        The Committee fails to understand the reason for existing vacancies of ANM and LHV, specially because the
expenditure on salary of these workers is the responsibility of the Governments. The Committee would like to be
apprised about the state-wise shortfall in the number of these functionaries. The Committee views with serious concern
that there is acute shortage of MPW with almost half the vacancies of required strength not being filled up. The
Committee is of the view that the Department should pursue with the concerned State Governments for bridging this
glaring gap.(Para 3.9)
        The Committee feels that there is an urgent need of filling up the number of vacancies in the posts of different
functionaries of Centres in NE region without any further delay. The Department should approach the concerned State
Governments for actively pursuing this aspect.
                                                                                                                                           (Para 3.10)
        The Committee feels that maintenance works of 478 training schools and 42 promotional training schools will take
a long time. The Committee would like to have the information about the number of training schools provided funds
during 2002-03 and 2003-04. This work long over due now needs to be looked into without any further delay. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should implement this scheme under a time bound action plan.
At the same time, the Committee would like to emphasize that there is a need for getting an assessment done of the 144
training schools being managed by the State Governments and NGOs.                                                                  (Para
3.13)
