CHAPTER I 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 The acronym Ayush stands for Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa 
and Homoeopathy, therefore, Ayush systems of medicine envelops Indian Systems of Medicine 
as well as Homoeopathy. Ayurveda, literary meaning 'Science of Life', is a comprehensive 
scientific system of medicine evolved in India. Ayurveda emphasizes on holistic approach 
through health promotion, disease prevention and curative roles. It advocates personalized 
approach for prescribing diet, lifestyle or treatment regimen based on its fundamental concept of 
‗Prakriti‘ (Human constitution). Based on sound c oncept and practices, Ayurvedic System of 
Medicine is well documented and includes every aspect of health and disease. Homoeopathy, in 
practice in India for around 100 years, provides long term palliative treatment in both acute and 
chronic cases due to ef fectiveness, lack of side effects and low cost of treatment. The National 
Health Policy (NHP -2017) advocates mainstreaming the potential of Ayush within a pluralistic 
system of integrated healthcare. In convergence with NHP and National Health Mission, the  
National Ayush Mission (NAM) envisages better access to Ayush Services, operationalization of 
Ayush Health and Wellness Centre, quality improvement of Ayush Education in order to achieve 
Sustainable Development Goals. The Ministry of Ayush is mandated to develop, promote and 
propagate Indian System of Medicine and Homeopathy through information, education and 
communication in Ayush to achieve the objective of 'Health for All'. 
 
1.1.2 The Ministry of Ayush works with the vision to position Ayush systems as the preferred 
systems of living and practice for attaining a healthy India. Such a vision has gradually emerged 
through the thought processes of experts concerned with health care provisioning in the country. 
 
1.2 The Ministry has identified its Mission in  terms of seven broad thematic areas of Ayush 
activities. The thematic areas are as follows: 
 
(i) Effective Human Resource Development 
(ii) Provision of Quality Ayush Services 
(iii) Information, Education and Communication 
(iv) Quality Research in Ayush 
(v) Growth of the Medicinal Plants Sector 
(vi) Drug Administration 
(vii) International Exchange Programme/Seminars/Workshops in Ayush sector 
 
FUNCTIONAL AREAS OF THE MINISTRY 
1.3 With an aim to position India as a Global Health care leader, the Ministry has identified the 
key areas of focus as Globalization and Market Share; Research; Technology; Policy Regulation 
and Standardizations; Manufacturing and Services; and Healthcare and Education. 
 
1.3.1 The Major Activities of Ministry of Ayush include autonomous institutions, activities 
performed by various institutes/councils under direct control of the Ministry; Centre Sector 
Schemes and the Centrally Sponsored Schemes where grants are provided through National 
Ayush Mission (NAM) to States/UTs to take up Ayush Activities.

CENTRALLY SPONSORED SCHEMES:- NATIONAL AYUSH MISSION (NAM) 
 
1.3.5 The Union Cabinet has approved on 14.07.2021 for the continuation of National Ayush 
Mission (NA M) as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme with financial implication of Rs. 4607.30 
Crores (Rs. 3000.00 Crore as Central Share and Rs. 1607.30 Crores as State Share) with effect 
from 01.04.2021 to 31.03.2026 for ensuring access to Ayush Services through increase in 
number of Ayush Hospitals and Dispensaries and an outlay to the tune of Rs. 1200 crore has 
been allocated to NAM in BE 2023-24 for the undertaken activities. 
 
1.4 The Committee notes that the broad thematic area of Ayush activities is the key to 
accomplish the mission objectives of the Ministry i.e., improve access to high quality and 
affordable Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Sowa -Rigpa (AUS&SR) & Homeopathy medical 
education, availability of adequate and high quality AUS&SR & Homeopathy medical 
professionals in all parts of the country, promote equitable and universal healthcare that 
encourages community health perspective. The broad thematic area of AYUSH activities in 
2022 under theme “Har Din Har Ghar Ayurveda” with sub themes as weekly based themes 
are namely Ayurveda for Holistic Health, Ayurveda for millennials, Ayurveda Aahar and 
Ayurveda for senior citizens. The thematic area of Quality research in Ayush is undertaken 
through pioneer research councils under MoA such as CCRAS, CCRUM, CCRS, CCRH 
and CCRYN. The Committee, however, feels that in order to address emerging challenges 
of healthcare, there is a need for functional integration of Ayurveda and conventional  
medicine with its affordable and evidence based use to boost Ayurveda in the paradigm of 
contemporary scientific, technological and medical parameters.

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK 
 
1.6 Responding to the Committee‘s query whether the existing regulatory framework in the 
Ministry is all comprehensive and effective to oversee, support and monitor the activities of the 
Ministry in effective manner or there is need for further modification or fresh legislation, the 
Ministry stated that the NCH, 2020 has constituted a National Commission for Homeopathy and 
its 3 autonomous boards while the NCISM Act, 2020 constituted the Na tional Commission for 
Indian System of Medicine and its 4 autonomous boards, however, the matter of appointment of 
sub members in both the Commissions is under process. Two Acts namely NCISM Act, 2020 
and NCH Act, 2020 have been brought into force by Minis try of Ayush to provide for a medical 
education system that improves access to quality and affordable medical education, ensures 
availability of adequate and high quality medical professionals of Indian System of Medicine and 
Homoeopathy in all parts of th e country; that promotes equitable and universal healthcare that 
encourages community health perspective and makes services of such medical professionals 
accessible and affordable to all the citizens. 
 
1.6.1 The Committee believes that the mission objective of NCISM Act, 2020 and the NCH 
Act, 2020 should be translated into action in all parts of the country including rural, urban, 
slum, hilly and tribal areas. Then and only then the impact assessment of both the 
legislations could be well understood  by both the policy maker and targeted beneficiaries. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to chalk out strategic course of action 
to accomplish the cherished goals as enshrined in two legislations.

BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
 
2.1 The Ministry of Ayush has been allocated an amount of Rs. 3647.50 crore  for the financial 
year 2023-24 as against a projected demand of Rs. 4092.33 crore, thus leaving a shortfall of Rs.

3.3 Pharmacopoeia Commission for Indian Medicine & Homoeopathy (PCIM&H), is a 
subordinate office under Ministry of Ayush. Development of Pharmacopoeias and Formularies 
as well as acting as Central Drug Testing cum Appellate Laboratory for Indian systems of 
Medicine & Homoeopathy are the key fields of activity of PCIM&H. The vision of the PCIM&H 
is to be the Nodal Agency in establishing Quality standards for drugs of Indian Systems of 
Medicine and Homoeopathy and the mission objective is to formulate Quality sta ndards for 
drugs used in Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy and to act as the Central Drug 
Testing cum Appellate Laboratory thereof. 
3.3.1 The Committee has been apprised that the capital outlay earmarked to the tune of Rs. 2.09 
crore in BE 2023 -24 is intended to be utilised for upgradation of infrastructure by procuring 
sophisticated analytical instruments and machinery for adopting latest technology in the 
laboratory and procuring computers and related items for optimum utilisation of IT 
advancements in day to day administration of PCIM&H. The e xpected outcome of upliftment of 
laboratory infrastructure is development of pharmacopoeial standards and Quality assurance as 
appellate authority. The Ministry also stated that the variation in Revenue Outl ay to the tune of

NATIONAL HEALTH POLICY, 2017 
5.1 National Policy on Health 2017 aims to bring a scientifically valid and useful synergy 
between traditional knowledge of different streams of AYUSH and modern tools of technology.  
The institutes focus on fundamental research, drug development, quality control, safety 
evaluation and scientific validation of AYUSH medicines and practices. National Health Policy 
(NHP) 2017 advocates mainstreaming the potential of AYUSH within a pluralistic system of 
Integrative healthcare and re -emphasizing the need for integrating AYUSH in the National 
Health Mission, research and education.  Goo d Health and Well -Being for people is one of the 
important Sustainable Development Goal. 
 
5.1.1 The Committee takes note of the key policy principles in the National Health Policy 
and recom mends CCRAS, CCRS, CCRH to create awareness on health, hygeine and 
ayurveda based diet and lifestyle. The Committee recommends the Ministry to standartize 
and validate Ayurvedic medicines and establish a robust and effective quality control 
mechanism for AUSH drugs. 
5.1.2 The Committee has been given to understand that tw o systems i.e. Ayush -Alopathy 
currently work separately without any coherence, therefore, the referral mechanism 
between the pathies is required to be developed. The Allopathy as well as Ayush 
practitioners can be trained in holistic care in an integrated fashion and be placed in all 
primary caregivers in the government as well as private settings under National Health 
Mission. 
5.1.3 The Committee, taking note of the provision under National AYUSH Mission (NAM), 
for co-location of AYUSH facilities at Primar y Health Centres (PHCs), Community Health 
Centres (CHCs) and District Hospitals (DHs), recommends the Ministry to give impetus to 
the engagement of AYUSH Doctors/Paramedics and their training is supported by the 
Department of Health & Family Welfare.

“ONE NATION, ONE HERB AND ONE STANDARD” 
 
5.5 The Ministry of Ayush put forward the concept of ―One Nation, One Herb and One 
Standard‖. The main aim of the mission is to merge the multiple standards of a herb into one. As 
a first phase, Pharmacopeia Commission for Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy (PCIM &H) has 
taken up the task to harmonize the monographs published/being published through Ayurvedic 
Pharmacopoeia of India (API), Siddha Pharmacopoeia of India (SPI), Unani Pharmacopoeia of 
India (UPI), Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia of India (HPI) and Indian Ph armacopoeia (IP). 
PCIM&H and Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) have signed a MoU for developing 
―ONE HERB -ONE STANDARD‖. Each monograph published through this collaboration will 
have Indian Standards along with International quality requirements, so th at all Indian standards 
become contemporary with global standards for the same botanical. 
 
5.5.1 The Committee is of the considered view that PCIMH, under the directives of
