INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, having been authorised by the Committee to 
present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Three Hundred and Seventy Fifth 
Report of the Committee on the Demands for Grants (Demand No. 26) of the Department of 
Higher Education, Ministry of Education for the year 2026-27.  
2. In accordance with the constitutional requirement under Article 113, the estimated 
expenditure of the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India projected under 
various Demands for Grants for the upcoming financial year needs to be voted by Parliament. 
As a sequel thereto, Demands for Grants of the relevant Ministries/Departments stand 
referred to concerned Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee to make a close 
scrutiny thereof under Rule 270 of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the 
Council of States (Rajya Sabha). While making scrutiny of the Demands for Grants, the 
Committee has made an appraisal of the performance, programmes, schemes and policies of 
the Department of Higher Education vis-a-vis expenditure made out of Consolidated Fund of 
India in the current financial year. 
 
3. The Budget allocations of the Ministry of Education have been sought under two 
demand Numbers, i.e, Demand No. 25 pertains to the Department of School Education and 
Literacy and Demand No.26 pertains to Department of Higher Education. In this Report, a 
scrutiny of Demand No. 26 pertaining to the Department of Higher Education of the Ministry 
of Education for the Financial Year 2026-27 has been made by the Committee. While 
scrutiny of Demand No. 25 pertaining to Department of School Education and Literacy is 
covered in Report number 376th of the Committee. 
 
4.  During the course of examination of Demands, the Committee heard the views of the 
Secretary, Department of Higher Education and other Senior Officers of the Department in its 
sitting held on 18th February, 2026. The Department also made available inputs from various 
organizations/agencies, namely, University Grants Commission (UGC); All India Council for 
Technical Education (AICTE), NAAC, IGNOU, JNU, DU, IIT Delhi etc under the 
administrative control of the Department of Higher Education.

upon the following documents:-

INTRODUCTION 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT OF HIGHER EDUCATION 
1.1 Higher education plays a pivotal role in the growth, stability and overall progress of a nation 
and its society. It serves as the cornerstone for the development of human capital, fostering 
intellectual advancement, innovation and socio-economic transformation. The Higher 
Educational Institutions (HEIs) act as centres of excellence where knowledge is created 
preserved and disseminated. HEIs equip students with advanced skills, critical thinking abilities 
and professional competencies necessary to meet the evolving demands of a globalized and 
technology-driven world. In essence, higher education not only drives economic growth and 
employment generation but also shapes the moral, intellectual and cultural fabric of society. It is 
in this backdrop, the Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports considered 
the Demand for Grants 2026-27 of the Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education. 
1.2 The Ministry of Education (MoE) is the apex body responsible for framing and implementing 
the National Education Policy, overseeing educational programmes at all levels, and developing 
systemic educational infrastructure across the country. The Department of Higher Education is 
responsible for the overall development of the higher education sector in India, encompassing 
universities, colleges, and institutions of national importance. 
The Ministry of Education comprises of two Departments: 
(i) Department of School Education and Literacy (DoSEL); and  
(ii) Department of Higher Education (DoHE) 
1.3 Mission and Vision of the Department 
Mission: 
 Provide greater opportunities of access to higher education with equity to all eligible 
persons, particularly to vulnerable sections. 
 Expand access by supporting existing institutions, establishing new institutions, and 
supporting State Governments and non-Government Organizations/Civil Society. 
 Initiate policies and programmes for strengthening entrepreneurship, research and 
innovations and encourage institutions to engage in stretching the frontiers of knowledge. 
 Promote the quality of higher education by investing in infrastructure and faculty, 
promoting academic reforms, improving governance and institutional restructuring. 
Vision: To realize India's human resource potential to its fullest in the higher education sector 
with equity and excellence.

Short Analysis of budget allocated to the Department 
2.9 A consistent upward trend is observed in the overall budget allocation for the Department of 
Higher Education. The allocation in BE 2026-27 at Rs. 55,727.22 crore is higher than the BE 
2025-26 by Rs. 5,649.27 crore (11.28%). This significant increase is attributable to the following 
factors: 
 Substantially enhanced allocations for Autonomous Bodies such as Central Universities 
(+4.49%), IITs (+6.82%), NITs (+10.07%), and IIMs (+15.92%), reflecting the growing 
requirements of academic and research infrastructure. 

9 
 
 Increased allocations under flagship schemes such as PM-ONOS (Rs. 2,200 crore), 
MERITE (Rs. 300 crore, 36.36% over BE 2025-26), Centres of Excellence in AI (Rs. 250 
crore, 25% increase), and PM Research Fellowship (Rs. 600 crore). 
 New allocations for PM Research Chair (Rs. 200 crore) and CoE in AI for Education (Rs. 
100 crore) introduced in BE 2026-27.

World Class Institutions 
3.2 Under the World Class Institutions scheme, the selected institutions are granted the status of 
“Institute of Eminence (IoE)”. It is a Central Sector Scheme aimed at establishing world-class 
institutions each in the public and private sector, by providing an enabling regulatory 
environment that allows them to achieve the highest levels of global excellence in teaching and 
research. The scheme grants selected institutions significant regulatory and administrative 
autonomy to facilitate their rise in global rankings. One of the major objectives of the scheme is 
that the institutions should appear in top-500 of any of the renowned global education ranking 
frameworks in a period of ten years of becoming an IoE.

 Public Institutions (08): IIT-Delhi, IIT-Bombay, IIT-Madras, IIT-Kharagpur, IISc 
Bangalore, Banaras Hindu University, University of Hyderabad and University of Delhi. 
 Private Institutions (04): Manipal Academy of Higher Education, Birla Institute of 
Technology & Science, O.P. Jindal Global University and Shiv Nadar University.

applications for the education sector. It will support development of indigenous, scalable AI-

Pradhan Mantri Vidyalaxmi Scheme (PM-VL) 
3.88 PM Vidyalaxmi (PM-VL) was approved by the Union Cabinet on 6th November 2024 as a 
new Central Sector Scheme. Its objective is to ensure that no meritorious student is denied the 
opportunity to pursue higher education due to financial constraints. The scheme facilitates 
collateral-free and guarantor-free education loans to students securing merit-based admission in 
top Quality Higher Education Institutions (QHEIs), identified through NIRF rankings. More than 
917 QHEIs have been identified, covering over 22 lakh students annually. 
Key features of the scheme are as under: 
 Collateral-free and guarantor-free education loans covering full tuition fees and course-
related expenses. 
 Credit guarantee of up to 75% of outstanding default for loans up to Rs. 7.5 lakh. 
 Interest subvention of 3% during moratorium period on loans up to Rs. 10 lakh for 
students with annual family income up to Rs. 8 lakh. 
 Total outlay: Rs. 3,600 crore for 2024-25 to 2030-31, covering an estimated 7 lakh 
students. 
 Dedicated online portal operational since 25th February 2025; all 12 Public Sector banks, 
20 leading private banks, 24 Regional Rural banks integrated on single portal. 
 Loans are made accessible through a simple, transparent, student-friendly and entirely 
digital application process; PM Vidyalaxmi Digital Rupee App (CBDC Wallet) for 

56 
 
disbursing interest subvention; the amount is credited to CBDC wallet of the beneficiary, 
then can be redeemed into bank account. 
 As on 22.01.2026, 25,385 grievances received on portal and 23,623 has been resolved. 
 Average Turn Around Time (TAT) for loan sanction ahs been reduced to less than 9 
days. 
 Simple 2-page application format (reduced from earlier 7-8 pages).

University Grants Commission (UGC) 
4.1 The University Grants Commission (UGC), established under an Act of Parliament in 1956, 
is a statutory body responsible for the coordination and maintenance of standards in universities 
throughout India. UGC provides financial assistance to eligible universities and colleges, 
promotes autonomous colleges, and advises on matters related to higher education. UGC is also 
responsible for introducing and implementing various academic reforms, including CHOICE-
Based Credit System (CBCS), Four-Year Undergraduate Programme (FYUP), academic 
autonomy, and curriculum reforms under NEP 2020. Its role and functions include coordination 
between universities; maintenance of standards; promotion of research; granting recognition to 
universities; disbursing scholarships/fellowships; framing regulations for higher education; 
promoting quality assurance through NAAC; and overseeing SWAYAM, ABC, and NIRF 
frameworks. 
4.2 Key initiatives undertaken by UGC: 
 154 Non-Technology Post-Graduate MOOCs developed for SWAYAM (2017 onwards); 
415 universities have adopted SWAYAM Credit Framework Regulations 2021; 4,55,956 
students earned credit transfers through SWAYAM (2021-2024). 
 Framework for Universities to conduct examinations for SWAYAM Courses notified 
(August 2024); 191 Universities adopted. 
 SWAYAM Outreach Workshops conducted at 8 locations (Jan-Mar 2025), with 
participation of over 7,000 stakeholders. 
 Guidelines for Incorporating Indian Knowledge in Higher Education Curricula released. 
 Mulya Pravah 2.0 introduced – focusing on human values and professional ethics. 
 Guidelines for Transforming Higher Education Institutions into Multidisciplinary 
Institutions (2022). 
 UGC (Institutions Deemed to be Universities) Regulations 2023 notified. 
 Foreign Higher Educational Institutions Regulations 2023 – facilitating branch campus 
establishment.

Research and Innovation 
5.1 Research and innovation are at the heart of any vibrant higher education system. Universities 
are not only places for teaching but also spaces where new ideas are explored, tested, and 
transformed into solutions for real-world problems. Strong research culture helps institutions 
contribute meaningfully to national priorities such as healthcare, agriculture, technology, and 
sustainable development. It also enhances academic excellence and global standing. By 
encouraging curiosity, critical thinking, and collaboration with industry, research-driven 
institutions prepare students to become innovators and leaders. In a rapidly changing world, 
continuous investment in research and innovation is essential to ensure long-term growth, 
competitiveness, and self-reliance for the nation. Key stats with regard to research and 
innovation scenario in HEIs in the country: 
 In the last 5 years, 2021 to 2025, over 1.1 Lakh patents have been filed by educational 
institutions of the country. Around 3,000 patents have been granted to centrally funded 
institutions under the Department of Higher Education during this period. 
 The IRINS (Indian Research Information Network System), which is a part of NMEICT 
has on boarded more than 1,800 higher education institutions and provided access to the 
comprehensive research profiling platform to them. The portal hosts more than 37.89 
lakh publications and approximately 2.75 lakh researcher profiles, including Assistant 
Professors, Associate Professors, and Professors. Collectively, these publications have 
garnered over 3.9 crore citations, reflecting the vast research footprint of Indian 
academia. 
 Research and innovation are central to institutional benchmarking within the Indian 
higher education ecosystem. Research & Innovation is a key criterion for Accreditation 
under National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC). Research is also among 
the five core weighted parameters of the National Institutional Ranking Framework 
(NIRF) for ranking Indian HEIs. 
 Around 6000 Institutions/Universities have established R&D Cells to catalyse 
multidisciplinary/ trans-disciplinary and translational research culture. 16,408 IICs have 
been set up in HEIs to develop a culture of research and innovation in Indian HEIs. 
 Presently 8,412 start-ups are incubated at HEIs and it is targeted to 50,000 start-ups by 
2030. 
5.2 Key initiatives taken by the Department in field of research and innovation under various 
schemes: 
 PMRF: 3,688 scholars admitted; 10,300 journal publications; 7,966 conference 
publications; 834 patents applied, 221 granted (as on 31.12.2025). 
 SPARC: 28 partner nations; research under 12 thematic areas aligned with G20 priorities;

5.4 The Committee recognizes that strengthening research and innovation ecosystems is 
central to India in achieving a knowledge-driven economy. The Committee recommends 
that the Department, in coordination with DST, DBT, CSIR, and ANRF (Anusandhan 
National Research Foundation), develop a unified National Research Mission for higher 
education with a five-year rolling budget, clearly delineating the roles of each agency to 
avoid duplication and ensure synergistic investment. A target of increasing India's GERD 
(Gross Expenditure on R&D) from 0.64% of GDP to at least 1.5% of GDP by 2030 should 
be embedded in this Mission. 
5.5 IIT data shows 9,393 patents applied and 5,733 granted between 2019 and 2025, but 
commercialization of these patents remains limited. The Committee is of the view that a 
Technology Transfer and Commercialization Office be established as a central facilitation 
body, interfacing with the private sector, DPIIT, and Start-up India may be established, to 
convert publicly-funded research into market-ready products and processes. 
5.6 While CFIs like IITs and IISc have world-class research infrastructure, State 
universities - which account for over 80% of enrolled students - have negligible research 
capacity. The Committee recommends that a dedicated 'State University Research 
Enhancement Fund' under PM-USHA with an annual outlay of at least Rs. 500 crore to 
develop Research and Innovation Clusters should be established at State universities, with 
matching contributions from State Governments. 
5.7 Consistent with NEP 2020's vision of multidisciplinarity, the Committee recommends 
that as per UGC notified guidelines Multi-Disciplinary Research Centres in every Central 
University should be established by 2027, covering at least three convergence areas such as 
AI and healthcare, sustainability and urban planning, or materials science and agriculture 
to facilitate establishing seed centres. 
5.8 The Committee notes that the Department does not currently publish a consolidated 
research output report mapping budget allocations to publications, patents, and citations 
across schemes. The Committee recommends that the Department should introduce an 
Annual Research Outcomes Report covering PMRF, SPARC, STARS, CoE-AI, MERITE, 
and all research-linked schemes, enabling Parliament and the public to assess the return on 
India's research investment in higher education.

85,000 per month, to stimulate a research culture beyond the elite institutions. 
5.16 The Committee notes that UGC fellowship data shows year-on-year increase in SC, 
ST, and OBC fellowship holders; however, the proportion relative to enrolled population in 
these categories remains unclear. The Committee recommends that the Department should 
conduct a comprehensive gap analysis - mapping the number of eligible 
SC/ST/OBC/PwD/Minority students enrolled in PhD programmes against the number 
actually receiving fellowships - and take corrective action to bridge the gap at the earliest.
