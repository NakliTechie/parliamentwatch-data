 REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       Role of medical education in a developing country like India is very important.   Nobody can deny the fact that
health care and medical education play a complimentary role.   For the effective implementation of all the National
Health Programmes, contribution of the teaching hospitals is very vital.   However, what is being observed is that
majority of young medical graduates opt  for urban hospitals and clinical practice, the trend towards specialization is
high, vacancies in rural health centres are on the increase, professional interest both at the level of practitioner and
educator continues to be in illness care at the secondary and tertiary level rather than in the challenges of primary health
care and public health.   The  increasing commercialization of medical education and erosion of norms in medical ethics
is also a  matter of serious concern.
1.2       Against this backdrop, the Committee on Human Resource Development appointed a Sub-Committee on
Medical Education on 6
th August, 1998 with the objective of assessing and analyzing the status of medical education in
the country from all conceivable angles.   However,  the Sub-Committee  had  to leave its task unfinished due to the
dissolution of 12th Lok Sabha.   Subsequently  a new Sub-Committee  was  appointed  on 26th May, 2000 which carried
forward the work of the previous Sub-Committee.
1.3       The Sub-Committee invited comments and suggestions from individuals, experts, doctors, medical teachers on
the status of medical education in the country through a Press Release issued on 18 th August, 1998.   An overwhelming
response was received from all over the country.   The Sub-Committee visited the States of Andhra Pradesh, Tamil
Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra and Union Territory of  Pondicherry to have a first-hand information on the
subject.   The Sub-Committee had the opportunity of  visiting medical colleges – both Govt. and Private and held
extensive and fruitful discussions with members of faculty and student community.   During these visits, the  Sub-
Committee also met a very large number of experts, medical professionals and Principals of  medical colleges which
could not be visited by it.  The Sub-Committee also held wide-ranging deliberations with State authorities to understand
their view-point on the subject.   Besides that, the Sub-Committee also visited AIIMS, Maulana Azad Medical College,
Lady Hardinge Medical College, University College of Medical Sciences and  ICMR in Delhi.   Representatives of PGI,
Chandigarh  were invited by the Sub-Committee to Delhi for knowing their views.   Due to time-constraint, it was not
possible for the Sub-Committee to visit any more State.   However, a detailed questionnaire was sent to all the Govt.
medical colleges in the remaining parts of the country and response from a large number of colleges was received.  
Besides that, State Govts  were also  requested to give their suggestions and quite a few State Govts  responded. 
Finally, the Committee heard the Secretary, MCI and  Secretary, Department of Health on 17
th July and 14th September,
2001.    The Committee shared its impressions/experiences with them and sought clarifications on various aspects of
medical education in the country.
II          OVERVIEW

2.1       It has been observed  that there has been a rapid increase in the   number  of medical colleges during the last two
decades with the maximum number  of colleges coming up in Southern and Western parts of the country.   The present
number of medical colleges in the country is 183, ( vide Annexure ) out of which 69 are private medical colleges. 
Mushrooming  of medical colleges has led to a  situation of regional imbalance  as very few medical colleges have come
up in northern states and the North Eastern region is almost bereft of  this  facility.   The Committee has been given to
understand that  contribution from the private sector notwithstanding, mushrooming of private medical colleges
continued to flourish with the tacit  support of State Govts for a considerable  period.   The Committee observes that the
amendments made in the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956 in 1993 by laying down very stringent norms for
establishment of new medical college, increase in the intake capacity and introduction of a new or higher course of
study  has  checked   the mushroom growth of  medical colleges in the country to some extent.   However, these norms
need to be enforced uniformly  and  with an objective approach.   Sporadic proposals for starting a very large number of
medical colleges at a stroke  still continue to be noticed.
2.2       The Committee had the opportunity to visit both Govt and private medical  colleges and interact with the
management, faculty members and student representatives.   Feedback with regard to  functioning of both Govt. and 
private  medical colleges was also received from different quarters.   While it is true that  common parameters about the 
two categories of medical colleges can not be drawn, the Committee  has the following observations to make.  Private
medical colleges, in the initial period, barely reach the standards and  norms set up by MCI  with regard to
infrastructure, manpower etc.   Similarly, in the Govt. medical  colleges set up in different parts of the country, with the
passage of time there has been a steady deterioration in the infrastructure because of   inability of State Govts  to
provide any assistance  whatsoever due to their very poor financial position.   Other major   constraints noticed in the
smooth functioning of Govt. medical colleges are control of too many authorities, lack of accountability, prolonged
procedure of recruitment  and promotion of teachers.   Inadequate  staff, unwarranted interference by management and
manipulation in the admission of students can be said to be major drawbacks of private medical colleges.    The
Committee also noticed that access to clinical facilities having large patient attendance was there in Govt. medical
colleges.   In contrast in private medical colleges, in spite of having impressive      hospital  buildings, required clinical
material  was lacking.
III        MEDICAL COUNCIL OF INDIA (MCI)
3.1       Medical Council of India (MCI) is a statutory body which was established under the  Indian Medical Council
Act, 1956.   It is a nodal body assigned the task of processing the proposals for establishment of a new medical college,
starting  of a new or higher course of study or training and increasing of admission capacity in any course of study  or
training.      Other major function  of MCI is regulating the standard of medical education by carrying out regular
inspections of different medical colleges  located all over the country in accordance with the  provisions of the Act and
Regulations made thereunder.    The Council is also empowered to recommend withdrawal of recognition in respect of
any college based on its inspections.   Thus, it can be said  that MCI acts as the watchdog  for maintaining  the standard
of medical education in the country.
3.2       MCI is composed of both elected and nominated members.   As laid down in Section 3(1) of the IMC Act, 1956
under the elected category, one member of the medical faculty from each university, one member from each State in
which a State Medical  Register is maintained and seven members to be elected  from amongst themselves by persons
enrolled on any of the State Medical Registers are included.   Under the nominated category, one member from each
state is nominated by the Central Govt. in  consultation with the State Govt.  and eight members are to be nominated by
the  Central Govt.   Thus,  composition of MCI was envisaged to be well represented having members from all the
required  categories.
3.3       The Committee has been informed that  as on 21
st June, 2001, the  total membership of MCI is 113 out of which
77 are filled  up and 36 are vacant.   Out of 77 members in position, 34 belong to the nominated category and 43 are
from the elected category.    Out of the total number of 36 vacancies, 35 are from the  elected category.   The Committee
observes that the  present composition of  MCI cannot be said  to be of an All India  character.   This is all the more
evident as only  fifteen members form the quorum  of the Council and all the acts of the Council are   decided by a
majority of the members present and voting.
3.4       The Committee notes that out of 36 vacancies in the  113-member Council, 21 are in the seats reserved for
Universities.  Reason for vacancies in this category, as intimated by    the Department is non-constitution/non-

reconstitution  of Senate or Court of the Universities or  meetings of the Senate/Court not being held for a long time.   
What is a cause of  serious concern  is that Universities are simply not responding to the reminders  sent by the Council
and the Central Govt. from time to time and vacancies are continuing in some cases for the last 10-15 years.
3.5       In some states, a number of Universities now fall under the jurisdiction of Health Universities  established in the
recent years.   This development has led to a peculiar situation.   By virtue of coming under the jurisdiction of a Health
University, such Universities   have lost their right of being represented in the Council.   On the other hand, Health
Universities  cannot send their nominee to the  Council as they do not have any Senate.   Thus, as many as 21
Universities over and above 56 eligible Universities are not represented in the Council.   Health Universities   having a 
large number of medical colleges under their  jurisdiction, specially  in southern  and western states have no say in the
decision-making apex body on medical education.
3.6       Out of 15 seats representing the Registered Medical Graduates (RMG) Constituency, 7 are lying vacant for
varying periods of time.   The Committee has been informed that the  main reason for vacancies   in this category is that
Returning Officers who are quite often the Registrar, State Medical Council or the State Director of Medical Education,
keep on changing due to change of incumbents  and in some cases voters’ list has not been prepared up-to-date.   The
Committee is constrained to observe that in spite of funds towards expenses for conducting the  election being provided
by the Council, the Returning Officers  are simply not responding to the reminders of the  Council.
3.7       Situation is equally disturbing so far as seats representing Licentiate Group are concerned.   At present, all the
seats are lying vacant.   The Committee  has been given to understand that Asstt. Director General, Directorate General
of Health Services was appointed as the Returning Officer on 21
st January,1997.   However, as late as on 12 th June,
2000, the Assistant Director-General approached the Central Govt. with a   request to be spared from the responsibilities
of Returning Officer due to his transfer to Lady Hardinge Medical College.  Thereafter,  ADG of Health Services was
appointed as the Returning Officer w.e.f. 19 th June, 2000.   It  has been informed that he has so far received lists of
registered licentiates from 12 State Medical Councils and the remaining 6 State  Medical Councils have also been
reminded.   The Committee feels that chances of election process being completed in the near future seem to be very
dim.
3.8       The Committee is of the strong opinion that procedure for election of different categories of  posts to the Council
is too rigid.   There is an urgent need for streamlining  the election procedure, more so, if the representative composition
of the Council as envisaged in the IMC Act, 1956 is sought to be maintained.   The Committee has been given to
understand that a proposal of the Council for bringing out amendment in the relevant provisions of the Indian Medical
Council Rules, 1957 for expediting election of members from Medical Faculty of Universities is pending with the
Central Govt.  since 15
th February, 1999.    The Committee recommends that the Central Govt. should take immediate
steps in the matter.   The Committee would also urge upon the Central Govt. to take up the issue of non-completion of
election process for other categories of seats with the concerned State Govts and impress upon them  to expedite the
same.   The Committee recommends that the Central Govt. should review the various provisions for constituting the
Council laid down in the Act and the Regulations made thereunder for removing the anomalies therein.
3.9       A basic problem in the  MCI structure is that since education is a State subject, it has in principle only  a
recommendatory role, not a regulatory one.  In the  absence of regulatory powers, MCI’s ability to maintain standards
has been severely compromised by State Govts/private medical colleges.   The Committee has been given to understand
that although with the amendment of IMC Act in 1993, sufficient powers have been vested with MCI so as to supervise
the maintenance of  standard of  education in medical colleges across the country, it has not proved to be effective.
3.10     The Council explained that minimum requirements in respect of staff, infrastructure, equipment etc as prescribed
in various Regulations are being checked periodically through inspections.  So far the Council  has not found any
difficulty in implementing its regulations and warnings given to medical college authorities for removal of deficiencies
have proved to be effective.
3.11     The Committee is not inclined to agree with the viewpoint  of the Council.  In its all the study visits  and
interactions with the various   functionaries comprising the entire medical education   spectrum, one view  that has been
consistently put across before the Committee, was that with the increase  in the number of medical colleges over the
years, it is practically not possible for a centralized body like MCI to constantly monitor the standard of medical
education.    The Committee observes that there is no regional council of the MCI.   The State  Medical Councils are
autonomous bodies established by State Govts that too not by all the State Govts.    These  State Medical Councils have

no   authority in the maintenance of standards of medical education.   In such a  scenario with 183   medical colleges
spread across the country with chances  of future expansion, the Committee strongly feels that regional  councils  with
MCI continuing as the apex body need to be set up in the country.   The Committee is of the view that both the Central
Govt. and MCI should explore the possibility of setting up four regional councils  in  Southern, Northern, Eastern and
Western parts of the country.   This would ultimately lead to better monitoring of the standard of medical  education as
laid down in the IMC Act, 1956  and Regulations made thereunder.     The Committee also desires that the Department
may take up the matter of establishing  State Medical Council in the States where they are not existing.   Role of State
Medical Council may also be reviewed so as to involve them in the exercise of maintaining the standards of Medical
Education in the States under the guidance of MCI.
IV        PROCEDURE/NORMS PRESCRIBED FOR ESTABLISHMENT OF A MEDICAL COLLEGE, STARTING
A NEW  OR HIGHER COURSE OF STUDY OR TRAINING AND INCREASING THE ADMISSION CAPACITY
4.1       Section 10A of the IMC Act, 1956 deals with the procedure for obtaining permission for establishment of a  new
medical college, starting a new or higher course of study or training and  increasing the admission capacity. There also
exist Minimum Standard Requirements for a Medical College for 50 admissions  annually Regulations, 1999 and similar
regulations for 100 admissions and 150 admissions separately.   These regulations provide  for a specific area
requirement for various departments both in the medical colleges and teaching hospitals, staff strength required and
equipment for various departments.  
4.2       By and large, the feedback which the Committee received with regard to the procedure and various
norms/conditions prescribed for opening a new medical college, starting a new course and increasing the intake capacity
emphasized their efficacy and necessity for checking the mushroom growth of medical colleges.  However, it was
pointed  out time and again that these norms need to be implemented stringently and objectively.   Besides that, some
reservations were also voiced about the practicability of some of the norms, like requirement of minimum land specially
for hilly, tribal and metropolitan  areas, very high amount of bank guarantee, requirement of infrastructure in the
medical college/teaching hospital etc.    The Committee recommends that  the MCI should undertake an exercise for
reviewing the various norms so as to  examine their viability in the present scenario and bringing  about necessary
modifications therein without compromising the quality.
4.3       The Committee finds that a detailed framework  for starting a new medical college has been  laid down in the
IMC Act, 1956 and the Regulations made thereunder.   The Committee has been given to understand that the formal
permission for starting a new medical college given by the Central Govt on the recommendation of the MCI  includes a
time-bound programme for the establishment of the medical college and expansion of the hospital facilities.  A clear cut
definition of preliminary  requirements to be met in respect of buildings, infrastructural facilities, medical and allied
equipment, faculty and staff etc. before admitting the 1
st batch of  students is also included in the permission, alongwith
annual  targets to be achieved to commensurate with the intake of students during the following years.   The Committee
was also informed that this permission is granted initially for a period of one year to be renewed on yearly basis subject
to verification of the achievements, annual targets and revalidation of the performance bank guarantees.
4.4       It can be said that with  the final authority vested in the Central Govt and MCI, mushrooming of medical
colleges leading to regional imbalance and commercialization of medical education has been checked considerably.  
However,  the Committee observes that there are certain practical problems also which go against the student interest.  
There are instances where medical college once started is not able to  fulfill the commitments made at the initial stage,
thereby losing recognition and thus playing with the future of students enrolled in that college.   The Committee is of the
view that there is a need for building up a mechanism whereby maximum standards are to be met at the very
beginning.   Reports are there where college is started on the basis of Essentiality Certificate and conditional approval.  
However, in the very second year, conditions are not met.    In such a scenario, either the college is allowed to function
so as to safeguard the interest of students or students are shifted to a recognized medical college.   Both the situations
cannot  be considered to be desirable and this should not be allowed to happen.
4.5       The Committee is, therefore, of the view that both the Central Govt. and MCI should have a more cautious
approach while giving approval for a new medical college.   There is a need for assessing  the background of a  trust,
specially its involvement in medical field and non-commercial approach before grant of any permission.   The
Committee, therefore, recommends that definition of the term ‘trust’ should be incorporated in the relevant provisions of
the IMC Act, 1956.

4.6       The Committee has been given to understand that a general view of the requirement of medical manpower is
taken by the State Govts  while providing for establishing a new medical college.   The prescribed form of Essentiality 
Certificate included in the  Establishment of Medical Colleges Regulations, 1999 requires the State Govts to indicate
details of existing medical manpower in the State, doctor-population ratio in the State and the justification for  opening
of the proposed medical college.
4.7       The Committee is not inclined to agree with the view of the Department that although it is necessary to have an
All India assessment of medical manpower requirement, it cannot be made a  pre-condition for starting a new college.  
The Committee strongly feels that the responsibility of the Central Govt does not end with the production of Essentiality
Certificate by State Govt.   The  Committee would like to refer to Sub-Section (7) of Section 10A of the IMC Act, 1956
which deals with recommendation of the Council and approval/disapproval by Central  Govt. for opening of a new
college.   One of the factors to be considered as enumerated in clause (f) is the requirement of manpower in the field of
practice of medicine.   The Committee is, therefore, of the view that both Central and the State Govts should specifically
ensure whether there is a requirement in the  real sense for establishing a medical college in a particular area before
giving its approval for the same.
V         INSPECTION

colleges.   The Council has been modifying its Regulations and the inspection proforma from time to time keeping in
view the advancement of technology.   The Minimum Standard Requirement for a medical college for 50/100/150
admissions annually Regulations published in 1999 provide for various requirements like staff, accommodation,
equipment for various departments etc.    Normally, not less than three inspectors carry out the inspections.   The
inspectors are senior level persons like Reader/Professor or even Vice-Chancellors of Universities who are medical 
personnel/Director of Medical Education/Principals and Deans of medical colleges etc.

5.3       During the  course of extensive discussions  with various medical functionaries, several reservations were
pointed out about the inspections carried out by MCI teams from time to time.   The main drawback cited was that the
inspection procedure was not fool-proof and lacked transparency.   General observation was that in majority of medical
colleges, required  staff as per MCI norms did not exist, infrastructure was totally inadequate, there was no proper
building and clinical material was much less than what was required.  At the time of inspection, cover up exercise was
generally carried out.    Reservations were also voiced about very high rate of inspection fee specially in the case of
Govt. medical colleges due to their lack of financial resources.
5.4       The Committee notes that a number of steps  have been taken by MCI to make the procedure of inspection
foolproof.   For example, the medical colleges are now supposed to maintain a list of teachers on their strength 
alongwith their photographs  duly verified by the Dean of the College.    The Committee, however, is of the view that
there is a need for  streamlining the inspection procedure so as to make it more stringent and effective.  The Committee
understands that so far inspections are being carried out only  after giving prior  notice.   The Committee is of the view
that MCI should initiate measures  so as to build up  a  mechanism for carrying out surprise inspections and inspections
at a very short notice.   This would indirectly lead to a  situation where medical college authorities would work towards
having a proper set up as per the prescribed norms.   The Committee is also of  the view that rate  of fees charged  for
different  categories of inspection should be reviewed.   Specially, in spite of   the so-called annual inspections being 
carried out only once in five years,  annual fee of Rs. 30,000 is prescribed  which  seems to be on the high side.

5.6       The Committee is surprised to note that this elaborate procedure does not   seem to be serving the required

meeting held on 17
th March, 1998 had recommended to the Central Govt. de-recognition of BRD Medical College,
Gorakhpur, UP.   It is  pertinent to point out that inspection of this college was carried out in 1995.   It took more than
three years for the Council to arrive at a decision which was communicated to the Central Govt. on 6
th July, 1998.   
Since then there have been correspondence between the Central Govt., State Govt. and MCI and the matter hangs    
there.   The Committee takes a very serious view of this situation.   Nobody  seems to have any concern-neither the
college nor the State Govt./University/Central Govt.    No doubt, students’ interest is safeguarded as de-recognition if
approved by the Central Govt. would be made effective from prospective date.    The Committee is of the view that in
the name of safeguarding the interest of students, interest of college authorities is being secured.   The Committee feels

that such developments defeat the very purpose of carrying out an inspection.   The Committee, therefore, strongly
recommends that a time-bound and result-oriented inspection procedure is required to be built up if the standard of

VI        POOR CONDITION OF GOVT. MEDICAL COLLEGES
6.1       The Committee had the opportunity to visit a number of State-run medical colleges, besides interacting with
medical professionals associated with such colleges.  The Committee observed that overall condition of these medical
colleges was very bad.  The Committee was given to understand that there had been no improvement in the
infrastructure facilities  for the medical colleges established long time back.   Even though there has been  manifold
increase in the number of undergraduate and postgraduate seats, the facilities remained virtually the same.    Not only
this, many new  paramedical courses have also been started without going into the availability of basic  infrastructure.  
No new classrooms have been added, labs have tremendous scarcity of space and classrooms were still equipped with
the minimum teaching aids.   Over the  years, what has happened is that all the modern technology developments have
not reached such colleges and as a result both teachers and doctors are not fully exposed to the latest techniques.  
General opinion was that there was a need for having a moratorium  on establishing new medical colleges in states
having large concentration.  Instead attention was required to be focused on updating the infrastructure of existing
medical colleges.
6.2       The Committee is inclined to agree with this view-point.   The Committee  is of the view that there is an urgent
need for undertaking a review of  infrastructure status of all state-run medical  colleges in the country.   Non-existent
required facilities tend to create an atmosphere which  has a demotivating effect on the teachers/doctors/students.    The
Committee understands that Central Govt. has  already launched a pilot project for providing financial assistance to
some State Govt.  medical colleges/hospitals.   The Committee feels that a time-bound action plan for revamping the
infrastructure facilities should be drawn by the Central Govt. which could be implemented by State Govts in active co-
ordination with Central Govt. and MCI. 
6.3       The Committee has been given to understand that in the present set up, educational level of technical/Para-
medical  staff is not appropriate to handle the hi-tech equipments and to pick up newer  techniques.   An indirect fall-out
of such a situation is that more and more work is being handled by the medical staff themselves at the   cost of time to
be devoted for teaching and training of students.  The Committee is of the view  that against the backdrop of
technological development, basic qualifications required for technical staff need to be reviewed.   Besides that, regular
in-service training, workshops and Continuing Medical Education (CMEs) for technical staff should be made a regular
feature.   The Committee also feels that feasibility of having Medical Learning Technique (MLT) colleges imparting
diploma/B.Sc degrees being attached to medical colleges can be explored.
6.4       The Committee also observes  that NE region of the country  is virtually starved of spread of  medical education
as very few medical  colleges  have been established  there so far.    The Committee has been informed that besides the
scheme for setting up of North-Eastern Indira Gandhi Regional Institute of Health and Medical Sciences which has been
approved by the Central Govt. at a  total cost of Rs. 422.60 crores, two proposals from State Govts. of  Meghalaya and
Tripura are under consideration.  The Committee is, however, of the view that Central Govt. initiative is very much
required for setting up medical colleges in NE states.   The Committee hopes that the Central Govt. would initiate
immediate action in this direction.

7.1       MCI Regulations on Graduate Medical Education, 1997 lay down the Eligibility criteria for admission to MBBS
course  and different categories of entrance tests as indicated below :-

ii)
                   in states, having more than one university/board/examining body conducting the qualifying examinations, a
competitive entrance examination to be held;
iii)
                 where there are more than one college in a state and only one university/board conducting the qualifying
examination, then a joint selection board to be  constituted; and
iv)
                 a competitive  entrance examination in the cases of Institutions of All India character.

7.2       The Committee was informed that all the State Govts. conduct entrance examination for admission to MBBS
course in Govt. medical colleges as well as merit seats in private medical colleges.   As per  the Supreme Court
directive, 15% of the MBBS seats in Govt. and some private medical colleges all over the country are already being
filled on the basis of merit through the All India entrance examination conducted by the CBSE.    Entrance tests for
admission to MBBS course can thus be broadly classified in three categories -  entrance  test conducted by respective
State Govts, entrance test conducted by CBSE for 15% seats and entrance tests conducted  by national level Institutions
like AIIMS,  JIPMER, AFMC, BHU, Aligarh Muslim University, Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences,
Wardha etc.   Committee’s attention was drawn to the practical problems and inconvenience faced by the students
sitting for a number of entrance tests every year.    The Committee feels that a  uniform entrance test covering all the
medical colleges – both Govt. and private would be the ideal position.   The Committee, however, is of the opinion that
in view of the different patterns of secondary level education  in different states, uniform admission procedure would
not prove to be feasible.   The Committee  feels that this issue needs to be debated by the medical fraternity, its pros and
cons thoroughly established and evaluated first.  The Committee is of the view that Central Govt./MCI  should take the
initiative to examine this issue in detail so that number of entrance tests is minimized to the extent possible  thus
lessening the burden of students. 
7.3       The Committee notes that the students admitted against the 15% seats in medical colleges  filled by the
management have to fulfill the minimum eligibility criteria namely, obtaining the qualification equivalent to the pre-
eligibility qualifications prescribed  for Indian students for admission to medical courses prescribed by the MCI.   
However, the Committee has been given to understand that in view  of the different backgrounds they come from, it is
for the management concerned to judge the merit of these candidates, having regard to all relevant factors.   The 
Committee has its reservations on such a procedure.   The Committee strongly feels that there has to be a mechanism for
judging the basic knowledge and aptitude for medical profession even for NRI students.   The Committee,  therefore,  is
of the view that a separate entrance test should be prescribed for NRI students so as to somewhat  eliminate the element
of commercialization in admission through this category.  The Committee  has no hesitation in saying that track record
of such students will justify such an initiative.
7.4       During its interactions with faculty members and experts, it was time and again emphasized that only motivated
and committed students should be selected for medical courses.   Marks alone or performance in the entrance test should
not be one and only criterion for eligibility for admission to MBBS.   Objectives of medical education being not simply
personal but social also, aptitude of students was very relevant.   The Committee, therefore, is of the view that entrance
test for admission to MBBS should also include a mechanism like aptitude test  for assessing the prospective student’s 
inclination for medical profession.
VIII      NRI QUOTA IN GOVT. MEDICAL COLLEGES
8.1       The Committee notes that the  Central Govt. in a communication dated 7 th April, 1999 had allowed the State
Govts to take   their own decision with regard to admission of NRI students in State Govt. medical colleges upto 15% of
the total intake capacity after  keeping in view the requirements of the local students and   subject to the condition  that
there shall  be no commercialization of medical education and that their contribution of seats towards the All India
Entrance Examination Quota will not be affected.   The Council has also no objection to this procedure  subject to 
admission of 15% of the total seats by NRI/foreign students.   The funds collected by these admissions should be
utilized for the development of the particular institution.  The Committee understands that in some of the States, this
practice has been started which is likely to be followed in other States also.   The Committee has its own reservations
about this Central Govt. directive.   While noting that contribution of seats towards the All India Entrance Examination
Quota by the State Govt. medical  colleges will not be affected by this move, the Committee apprehends that
requirements of local students are bound to be hit hard.   The Committee wonders as to how the interest of local students
will be taken care  of with the  total number of seats remaining the same.   The Committee is of the view  that relaxation
should only be resorted to if equal number of seats are increased.   In that event also, availability of additional required
infrastructure will also have to be ensured.    The Committee also feels that there are little chances of funds thus
generated being utilized for the development of a particular institution.  Instances  have already  come to the notice  of
the Committee where initial understanding with State Govt. was  that money generated from NRI admissions was to be
deposited with college and a special committee was to be authorized to utilize these funds.   However, subsequently,
State Govt directions were given  that such funds are to be deposited with the State Exchequer to be allotted  afterwards

8.2       As per the Postgraduate Medical Education Regulations, 2000, there is a  provision for adopting any of the
following procedures for admission to PG courses :-
i)          on the basis of merit as determined by a competitive test conducted by the State Govt. or by the competent  
authority appointed by the State Govt. or by the university/group of universities in the same state; or
ii)

8.3       The Committee has been given to understand  that the present procedure of allocation  of seats in different
disciplines of postgraduate courses was mainly based on an entrance examination conducted at the state or the national
level.   Thus, the merit position decided the choice of discipline which was  not  considered to be an ideal position.   It
resulted in frustration among students as there was every possibility of students not getting the choice of their discipline 
ultimately leading to lack of commitment on their part.   An indirect consequence of this system of PG admission was
that an important component of MBBS training  i.e. internship was totally  neglected by the students due to their
remaining busy in the preparation for admission to Postgraduate course.   The  Committee, therefore, feels that
alongwith entrance test, feasibility of having  individual cumulative performance at the first, second and third MBBS
examination and also aptitude of the students being  made the criteria for admission to Postgraduate courses may be
explored.
IX        EXAMINATION PROCEDURE
9.1       MCI Regulations on Graduate Medical  Education, 1997 deal extensively with the examination procedure to be
followed for obtaining MBBS degree.   Attendance, internal assessment and university examinations are the three basic
factors on which performance of a student in essential components of training is to be assessed.   Detailed procedure for
internal assessment and conducting university examination has been laid down.   Distribution of marks to various
disciplines has also been specified.   Criteria for appointment of examiners – both external and internal -  is   also there.  
There is a separate chapter on ‘Internship’ outlining beside other aspects the specific objectives, distribution of
compulsory and elective posting in various disciplines and the methodology for assessment of internship etc.    Even
model time-table for different semesters has been given.
9.2       During the course of its discussions  with various authorities, the Committee had the opportunity to hear
divergent views on various aspects of examination procedure for MBBS course.   Semester system was generally
welcomed.   However,  there were views both in favour and against internal assessment.  One view was that internal
assessment of students should be given due weightage as it would result in improved attendance.  However,
apprehensions were also there as it was felt that element of partiality  was bound to be there in internal assessment.   It
was also pointed out that examiners  were not being  appointed as per MCI norms.   Views were also expressed both for
and against multiple choice system in question papers.
9.3       The Committee is of the view that MCI norms are not being strictly adhered to by many of the medical
colleges.   Had it been so, there would not have been such reservations and also variations in examination patterns being
followed by different institutions/universities.  The Committee would also like to point out that uniformity of
examination pattern to the maximum extent possible can only be   achieved if the teaching facilities and  courses are
also uniform.  The Committee, therefore, is of the view that steps need to be initiated for evolving a forum for
interaction amongst medical faculties of different universities on this aspect of medical education.

MBBS course.   The entire course has been divided into three  phases.   Phase I (two semesters) consists of pre-clinical
subjects i.e. Human Anatomy,  Physiology including Bio-Physics, Bio-Chemistry and introduction to Community
Medicine  including Humanities.   Phase II (3 semesters) consists of para-clinical/clinical subjects.  Para-clinical
subjects shall consist of Pathology, Pharmacology, Microbiology, Forensic Medicine including Toxicology and part of

Community Medicine.   Phase III will be of 4 semesters.   The clinical subjects to be taught during Phase II and III are
Medicine and its allied specialities, Surgery and its allied specialities, Obstetrics and Gynecology and Community
Medicine.
10.2     The Committee has been informed that after the   revision of the curriculum for undergraduate course in 1997,
all the Universities in the country were requested to ensure that the revised curriculum is implemented effectively.  
Without exception, all the  universities are now following the revised curriculum which is being monitored by MCI
through periodical inspections.
10.3     The Committee notes that although proposal for bringing out changes in the curriculum for undergraduate course
was initiated in 1993, changes could be finally brought about only in 1997.  The Committee has been given to
understand that due to this time-consuming process, some of the changes in the undergraduate curriculum have become
outdated by the time of implementation. 
10.4     Based on implementation of 1997 Regulations, suggestions have already been received for amendments which
have been considered by the Council.    The Committee notes that these amendments are at present under discussion
between  Central Govt., DGHS and MCI.  The Committee notes that no time-frame has been specified for upgrading 
and updating the  curriculum.    The Committee  is of the view that revision of MBBS curriculum  should be made a
regular feature, within a specific time-frame strictly adhered to so as to keep pace with the advancement in medical
sciences.
10.5     During the course of its inter-actions, the Committee’s attention was drawn to the fact that although MCI has
laid down detailed regulations on curriculum for MBBS, these were not being followed by all the universities.   There
was non-uniformity of  curriculum even in one state resulting in lot of problems being faced by the students.   The
Committee emphasizes  the need for adoption of curriculum for MBBS  in letter and spirit by all the universities in spite
of there being variations in standard of medical education being imparted in different medical colleges due to one reason
or the other.  
10.6     There were extensive discussions on various components of MBBS curriculum.   Divergent feedback was
received as detailed below :-
i)          Reduction in the period of study for Basic medical sciences  from 18 months to 12 months was considered to be
a move in the right direction as exposure of students to clinical subjects was for longer duration now.   It was also 
pointed out that   applied aspects of Basic medical Sciences are re-taught during clinical teaching.   However,
reservations were also  there as this move had resulted  in longer period of daily study and drastic cut in the duration of
vacations for students.  
ii)
                   A strong plea was made for restoration of Amphibian Practicals in  undergraduate course.   It was  felt that
testing on animals was necessary before going for treatment of patients.
iii)
                 Need for inclusion of behavoural aspects  of medical care in the undergraduate course was emphasized.   One
view was that no amount of training will make the student committed to provide the best medical care to the patient
until and unless his attitude and aptitude towards patients is positive and his zeal for patient care is paramount.    The
other view was that to sensitise students to behave with compassion and concern with patients, teachers  are the role
models for students while they examine patients.   Addition  of a course on individual and group behaviour could
supplement  this process of sensitization.
iv)
                 Another drawback pointed out was that curative aspect of treatment was given more  emphasis  in the revised
curriculum.  Despite strong indications that what is needed is more training in primary care and more investment in
critical public health issues, precious time is spent in investigating diseases that afflict only a limited number of people.
v)
                  Another  fall-out of this approach was lack of desired capacity in the present day medical graduates in  making
proper clinical diagnosis.  By and large there was more concentration/ dependency on high-tech methods and
sophisticated instruments for identifying a disease and treatment thereof.    There is a need  for having a balanced
approach.
vi)                 More emphasis on  problem-based learning.
10.7     The Committee is aware that  quantum of curriculum for undergraduate course is already quite high resulting in
great pressure  on students.   The Committee feels that a line has to be drawn somewhere and one should not forget that

XI        TEACHERS
11.1     During the course of its  extensive interactions every where, one fact which has been constantly highlighted is
the acute shortage of medical teachers as a whole, and especially in Basic medical sciences.   There were many colleges
where even the sanctioned posts were not as per the MCI norms and out of these sanctioned posts also, almost 50%
sanctioned posts were lying vacant.   The  Committee was informed that situation was slightly better in some private
colleges.  The Committee was given to understand that following factors were mainly responsible for shortage of faculty
strength in the Govt. medical colleges –
a)
                  Poor service conditions of teachers
b)                  Transfer policy
c)                  Lack of job satisfaction
d)                  Attraction of  better-paid and non-transferable jobs in private medical colleges.
e)                  Lack of sufficient incentives for research/presentation of papers in national and international seminars
f)                    Unavailability  of teachers against the reserved category of posts.
11.2     A full time medical teacher has to perform a number of duties which may be broadly classified as Clinical 
teacher,  administrator, research analyst and provider of emergency services.   On the  one hand he has to impart
medical training to his  students and on the other hand, he is also responsible for the maintenance of health services in
the hospital.
11.3     During its inter-actions everywhere, Committee’s  attention was also drawn to the very poor service conditions
of Govt. medical college teachers as compared to private medical college teachers.  Committee  was given to understand
that instances of teachers of Govt. medical colleges leaving for private medical  colleges had become a regular feature. 
Very lengthy  process of initial  recruitment of medical teachers also worked as a demotivating factor.   Besides that, the
Committee was given to understand that the practice of  interchange between teaching and non-teaching cadres  in some
states  was adversely affecting good quality medical education.   A suggestion given was that promotion of teachers

112th Report

should not be  based on seniority alone.   Merit should play a more important  role.   Transfer policy being followed by
many of the State Govts. was also creating dissatisfaction in the teaching community.   It was also pointed out that on an
average, faculty in medical college had very little means of quality improvement.   Updating of knowledge, training in
advanced technology  were poorly organized.   Shortage of funds was also there for undertaking research activities.  
Teachers were also not getting enough chances to participate in national/international seminars.
11.4     The Committee finds that in states like Maharashtra and Gujarat, shortage of faculty strength in Govt. medical
college has been addressed to some extent by having two types of teachers -  Full-time and Honorary teachers. 
Although divergent views were given with regard to honorary teachers, the Committee had a feeling that both the
categories of teachers  were playing a complimentary role.   The Committee observes that in spite of there being a
provision for only full-time teachers in MCI Regulations, on the request of some State Govts., system of honorary
teachers has been further extended by the Central Govt.  upto 30
th June, 2002.   The  Committee is of the view that
Central Govt./MCI should undertake a detailed review of Honorary System of teachers in the concerned States.   The
Committee is of the view that after removal of all the  loopholes in this system and also safeguarding the interest of Full
time teachers, other State Govts. can be approached to take benefit of Honorary System of teachers to the extent
possible.
11.5     The Committee finds that the UGC guidelines regarding extension of retired medical teachers of all medical
colleges/institutions on yearly basis upto the age of 65 years have been agreed to by the Ministry of Health and Family
Welfare in 1997.   The Committee feels that this directive should be given wide publicity  so that the Govt. medical
colleges may take benefit of the same.  Besides that, possibility of hiring the services of  medical professionals –
Visiting Professors and practicing doctors without compromising the standard of medical education can also be
explored.
11.6     The Committee observes  that MCI has not made any Regulations regarding separation of teaching and non-
teaching cadres.   The Council Regulations only provide for a required number of teachers and other non-teaching staff
required for each department in the medical college and attached  hospital  according to the seat intake capacity in
MBBS course.   The Committee is of the view that practice of inter-change between teaching and non-teaching cadres is
indeed creating problems for medical faculty in some of the states.   The Committee,  therefore, recommends that the
MCI should examine this practice and make specific provision in the best interest of medical teachers in its Regulations.
11.7     The Committee observes that the duties and responsibilities of medical teachers are different, immediate and
exacting in nature when compared with other categories  of professions/Govt. employees.    The Committee would like
to emphasise that there is an urgent need for providing the  best possible service conditions to the medical teachers.  
Their pay scales and other incentives need to be of a higher scale as compared to other teachers.
11.8     The Committee notes that the Central Govt. or the MCI have not prescribed any uniform pay scales for teachers
in medical colleges.   Some of the state govts. have adopted the UGC scales while some have adopted the revised central
pay scales based on 5
th Pay Commission recommendations.  Feedback received by the Committee points to the fact that
such state govt.  initiatives are very few.   By and large, pay scales of medical teachers are dependent on the resource
position  of the state govts.
11.9     The Committee is not convinced by the stand of the Central Govt. that in the absence of central funding, it is in
no position to prescribe uniform salary and allowances for the teachers in medical colleges.  The Committee strongly
feels that uniform pay scales  in accordance with the manifold duties performed by medical teachers across the country
would be the ideal  condition.   In order to maintain the standard of medical education, there is imperative need for
safeguarding the interests of medical teachers in every conceivable manner.   The Committee is surprised to note that
the proposal for having a  Central agency on the  pattern of UGC and AICTE was initially discussed in a meeting taken
by the Member, Planning Commission and the broad guidelines for establishment of such a Commission  were also 
worked out.   However,  subsequently in 1997 with the approval of the then Health Minister  it was decided to keep the
proposal in abeyance.   The Committee is of the view that a Medical Grants Commission  is required  to be set up
without any further delay.  The Committee is happy to note that draft National Health Policy, 2001 at present under
circulation to all concerned for their comments envisages the setting up of Medical Grants Commission for funding new
Govt. medical colleges and upgradation of the existing Govt. medical colleges.     The service conditions of medical
teachers should also be covered under the mandate of Medical  Grants Commission.

XII       TRAINING
12.1     During its interactions, Committee’s attention was drawn time and again about there being need for having some
kind of training for medical teachers.   The majority view was that it is not necessary that a good medical practitioner is
also a good teacher.  The Committee observes that most postgraduate teaching programmes have a component of
students taking classes of undergraduate courses and also  conducting seminars as part of their training. In spite of that,
it was emphasized that training to prospective teachers  specially for communication skills was very much required. 
The Committee strongly feels that training in educational technology should be made the eligibility criteria for
appointment of a medical teacher.   If that is not considered feasible, extensive training programme of short duration
should be mandatory before   the prospective teachers join their duties. 
12.2     Committee’s attention was also drawn to the fact that there was also a constant need to have in-service training
programme/refresher course for doctors involved in teaching to update their knowledge especially during the present era
when rapid scientific developments are taking place and a wide variety of  diseases are being recognized.   The
Committee finds that Graduate Medical Education Regulations provide that Medical Education Units/departments be
established in all medical colleges for faculty development and providing learning resource material to teachers.   The
Committee is, however, constrained to note that these directives are not being adhered to as only about 50-60 medical
colleges in the country  have established such units so far.   The Committee is of the opinion that fulfillment of this
condition should be pursued with all medical colleges both Govt. and private. 
12.3     The Committee would also like to point out that there is also a need for encouraging the participation of more
and more medical teachers in CME Programmes, workshops, conferences, seminars etc. by making available the
required funds for this purpose.   MCI/Central Govt. should pursue this aspect also with the concerned authorities in the 
states.
12.4     Committee’s attention was also drawn to the fact that young doctors are not inclined to join teaching
profession.   General  perception is that at the time of joining medical education, most of the students make up their
mind to do private practice since it provides financial gain of varying nature to the practitioners in comparison to the
doctors going for teaching profession.   However, it can also be said that teaching job actually serves as a good
launching pad for private  practice after gaining sufficient experience, confidence and credibility.   Interest in teaching is
also dependent on the attitude and commitment of the young pass-outs.   The Committee is inclined to agree with these
views.   The Committee finds that at present no incentive schemes are there to attract bright students towards teaching
and there is no such proposal also being contemplated by MCI.   In view of medical practice being more income
generating, the Committee is of the view that a  system of identifying and providing incentives  like earmarking funds
for research projects and job guarantee to students interested in teaching needs to be evolved on the pattern of various
schemes being implemented by the Govt. /AICTE/UGC for attracting young talent towards teaching.   The Committee
strongly feels that this is specially required for students of non-clinical and para-clinical subjects.
XIII      ASSESSMENT OF TEACHERS
13.1     During the course of its discussion with  both teaching and student community  and  also experts, one issue on
which divergent views emerged was the need for assessment of medical college teachers by students.   The Committee
was given to understand that from an idealistic and futuristic point of view, assessment of teachers by students is
acceptable.   But there were very strong reservations also.   One view was that the concept is not suitable due to non-
uniformity in the selection procedure of teachers.   Besides, social and political set up will also have further adverse
effect.   The other view was that it can be implemented but only by those students who are sincere, intelligent and
regular in their studies.  The Committee is of the opinion that a scientific and professional mechanism for conducting
such an assessment in a fair/transparent and equitable manner needs to be evolved before taking any initiative in this
direction.
XIV     PRIVATE PRACTICE
14.1     The issue of private practice by medical college teachers was raised time and again during the deliberations of
the Committee with all categories of medical professionals – doctors, teachers, state authorities, student community.  
Besides that, the Committee also received lot of feedback on this issue from various parts of the country.   The
Committee observes that at present, no uniform system with regard to private practice by teachers is prevailing in the
various medical colleges.   There are colleges where teachers of clinical subjects  are allowed to do private practice and

112th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/112threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:15:22]
teachers of non-clinical subjects are eligible for non-practising allowance.   Besides that, in some states, there is a total
ban on private practice by medical teachers.    Instances are also there of teachers, in spite of  getting NPA,  doing
private practice.
14.2     The Committee finds that there is no provision in the MCI Regulations on this issue except that all teachers
should be full-time in medical  colleges and attached hospitals.   The Committee has been informed that in pursuance of
Central Govt. directions, a number of state govts. adopted the policy of grant of NPA to medical staff  including the
teachers and have abolished private practice.
14.3     The Committee strongly feels that the teaching institutions should have non-practicing staff.   Private practice
wherever in vogue is largely affecting the quality of functioning of the Institute as well as proper patient care  because
teachers are more interested in private practice rather than in imparting teaching and research.   Keeping these factors  in
view, the  Committee emphasizes that it is very essential that the teachers are  well paid so as to compensate them for
their extra time and dedication to their profession.   The Committee has been given to understand that the varying rates
of NPA are prevalent in different State Govt. medical colleges which are less than Central Govt. rates.  The Committee
is of the firm view that  there should be a clear policy regarding private practice and non practicing allowance
throughout the country.   MCI/Central Govt. should take the initiative in this direction without any further delay.
XV       PROBLEM OF SHORTAGE OF DOCTORS IN RURAL AREAS – REMEDIES THEREFOR
15.1     Acute shortage of doctors in rural areas is a problem being faced in the entire country.   No state can be said to
be not facing this problem, only its acuteness can be varying in different states.   The Committee finds that the main
reason for the paucity of doctors in rural areas is the lack or inadequacy of facilities available in PHCs/hospitals.  
Nobody would deny the fact that almost all PHCs in the country are ill-equipped.  They lack necessary facilities for
treating even minor ailments whereas ground reality is that generally very complicated cases are handled there.  
Another demotivating factor is the disparity in the living conditions in the rural areas vis-à-vis  urban areas. 
Geographical location is not convenient, and  even basic amenities like  proper roads, electricity and water, education
for children, entertainment etc. are not there.  The situation gets more aggravated because doctors who man the PHCs
are generally young pass-outs possessing only theoretical knowledge, practical exposure of ground realities virtually
being nil.
15.2     A number of suggestions were made for strengthening the rural services.   One suggestion made was that there
can be two types of doctors  - one for rural areas and the other for urban areas.   Services of diploma doctors (three years
course) can be utilized in rural areas.  However, the Committee is not inclined to agree with this view mainly because
equal standards are required to be maintained for both rural and urban areas.   One must not forget the fact that rural
masses although generally illiterate are gradually realizing the harm caused by unregistered doctors  practising in rural
areas.   The Committee strongly feels that creation of a class within a class will not be appropriate.  Such a distinction
would be detrimental to the entire medical profession.
15.3     The other suggestions offered to the Committee are cited below :-
a)
                  Out of the one year internship period for UG students, six months should be set aside for  compulsory rural
service. For better  service  such interns should be given intensive orientation training.  
b)
                  Before being registered as a doctor, a specific period of rural service should be made compulsory for the fresh
pass-outs.   Degree should only be awarded thereafter.
c)
                  Some seats in PG courses can be reserved for students who opted for rural services.
d)                  In the  recruitment of doctors, some posts should be earmarked for rural posting.
 
15.4     The Committee has been given to understand that some State Govts. have  adopted the policy of making rural
service compulsory in the initial posting of medical officers before they are transferred to urban areas or permitted to do
postgraduate medical education.   There is no shortage  of doctors as such, but there is mal-distribution between the 
rural and urban areas.   The Committee is of the view that compulsion alone will not have the desired results.   The
Committee, therefore, recommends that alongwith compulsory rural service for a limited specified time, incentives like
special allowance, better housing and other allied facilities and special relaxation for admission to postgraduate courses
are also necessary for attracting young doctors towards rural areas.

15.5     The Committee would also like to emphasise that during internship period, PHC posting should be strictly
adhered to and monitored as it would motivate them towards rural service later on.  The Committee also feels that  there

15.6     There was also a need for developing proper infrastructure in PHCs.   The Committee is of the view that the

XVI     BRAIN DRAIN
16.1     The Committee observes that the problem of brain drain is being faced at two fronts – one being from one
institution to another inside the country and the other being flight of young talent to foreign institutions.   The
Committee has been given to understand that this malady has penetrated even premier medical institutions.   On the one
side,  national level institutions like PGI, AIIMS are facing shortage of faculty strength due to faculty members joining
foreign institutions primarily for job satisfaction.   The other cause of serious concern is the silent brain drain in the
form of voluntary retirement or taking  up regular jobs by Govt. medical  college teachers in private medical colleges.  
Better working conditions, monetary benefits and more opportunity for undertaking research activities can  be  cited as
the main factors responsible for this phenomenon.   The Committee observes that there is another aspect of this
problem.   Young pass-outs do not get to know all the opportunities  which are available in the country.   There is a need
for evolving a facility  on the pattern of Recruitment agencies coming to the campuses of premier technical institutions
for placements.   The Committee is of the firm view that  remedial steps need to be taken for creating best job
opportunities  thereby retaining our young talent in the country.  The Committee also feels that if necessary, students
may be asked to pay back in early installments, wherever Government  funding is there.    Modalities in this regarding
may be explored.
 
 
XVII    MUSHROOMING OF LABS/DIAGNOSTIC CENTRES
17.1     Diagnosis of diseases based on clinical impressions alone can have fallacies even in experienced hands.   No
medicare system can ignore state of the art hi-tech methods of diagnosis.    The Committee  however, observes that too
much dependence of present-day doctors on technical know-how has indirectly led to a  mushroom growth of
Labs/Diagnostic Centres  conducting CT Scan and MRI and other sophisticated tests all over the country.   The
Committee has been given to understand that such centres are not generally manned by qualified medical    personnel.  
The doctors treat their patients on the basis of reports given by such centres which sometimes give out incorrect
interpretation of patient’s state of health.   The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for bringing out a
comprehensive legislation to check  the mushroom growth of  such centres.
17.2     The Committee has been informed that the Department of Health has circulated the draft Clinical Establishment
Regulation Act to the State Govts. in February, 2001 to obtain  the recommendations of the respective state legislatures
to enable the Central Govt. to get the law enacted by the Parliament.   The main features of the proposed legislation,
inter-alia, include mandatory registration of a  clinical establishment, requirement of minimum  standards in respect of
building, equipments, qualified doctors and other staff  etc. and the powers to frame the rules under the Act to prescribe
the minimum  standards and procedures to conduct tests- pathological, chemical, bacteriological  and others.   The
Committee hopes that the procedural  formalities are completed at the earliest so that the proposed legislation comes
into force without any further delay.
XVIII   INDIAN MEDICAL SERVICE
18.1     The Committee observes that  teachers of Govt. medical colleges are governed by varying service conditions, by
and large, dependent on the financial health of the Institution.  As a result, although MCI Regulations specifically
provide for Full-time medical teachers, there are different categories  of medical teachers – Full-time, Honorary,

teacher also vary from state to state.  There are states where inter-change between teaching and non-teaching cadre is
permissible,  other states are there where  district identity of teaching cadre is being maintained.   Besides that, Govt.
medical college teachers  are also liable  to be transferred within the state.   Another problem pointed out is that these

18.2     During the course of its deliberations, the issue for having an Indian Medical Service on the lines of IAS/IPS

XIX     THINK TANK
19.1     Divergent views have been received by the Committee about the need for having a ‘Think Tank’ of eminent
medical professionals  for streamlining medical education in the country.   While majority opinion was in favour of
having such an entity, there were few voices of caution also. 
19.2     The Committee notes that major policy  decisions in the field of medcial  education  are taken by the Govt. after

19.3     The Committee is aware about the mandate of MCI and also the over-all involvement of the Central Govt. in
regulating the standard of medical education in the country.   The Committee, however, is given to understand that the
required academic input for strengthening the quality of medical education is somewhat lacking.    The Committee,
therefore, strongly feels that there is a need for having a ‘Think Tank’ of eminent Professionals of both medical care and
medical education.   Such a body should, however, be solely involved in the work of assessment and analysis of medical
education  so as to provide various policies based on the academic knowledge and professional experience of its
members for its growth in the right direction.

XXI     ROLE OF NATIONAL LEVEL INSTITUTIONS
21.1     Premier institutions like AIIMS, PGI, JIPMER are centres of excellence and are supposed to be only referral
hospitals.   But over the years, there has been a steady increase in the rush of patients to such referral hospitals.   As a
result, research and academic activities of such institutions are affected.
21.2     The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for strengthening the peripheral hospitals and PHCs.  
No doubt, health being a State subject, it is expected of the State Govts. to upgrade the quality of medical services
provided so that a majority of the patients could be treated  at the State level and only those patients who cannot be
attended at the State level should be referred to the All India Institutions like AIIMS, PGI and JIPMER.
21.3     The Committee feels that in  view of the weak financial position  of States, Central Govt. has to take an initiative
in this direction if referral character of All India Institutions is to be maintained.   The Committee is happy to note that
the Central Govt. has launched a pilot project this year for strengthening the diagnostic facilities of State hospitals.   The
Committee has been given to understand that this scheme is being implemented for the last two-three years.   States like
M.P., Haryana, Bihar, Tripura, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, UP, Kerala, Gujarat and J&K have already benefited from
this scheme during this period.   While appreciating the initiative, the Committee would like to emphasise that such
grants should also be strictly monitored by the Central Govt. so as to assess the  fulfillment of the objective of this
project.
21.4     The Committee has been given to understand that  income generated by  national institutions  from their internal
resources results in reduction of equivalent amount from their budget allocation.   The Department has clarified that in
respect of Govt. institutions which are depositing the income generated by them to the Govt. Revenue, no reduction
from their budget allocation is made.   The Committee strongly feels that Govt. institutions generating income should be
allowed to retain such an income in the maintenance and up-gradation of their institution.   If the whole income being
placed at their disposal is not possible, the Committee finds no harm in a specific percentage of income being retained
by them.  This facility  need not result in deduction of equivalent amount of funds  from their budget allocation.
21.5     The Committee is of the opinion that India being a vast country and in view of  phenomenal increase in its
population, only three-four national level institutions can not be considered to be serving the purpose.   The Committee
therefore, feels that the Central Govt. should explore the possibility of establishing more national level institutions.
21.6     The Committee observes that problem of faculty shortage is also being faced by premier institutions like AIIMS
and PGI due to one reason or other.   The Committee understands that besides     a number of posts lying vacant, quite a
few  professors are working on ad hoc basis.  Besides that, in the institutions   under the control of Central Govt.,
recruitment to all the four sub-cadres i.e. General Duty Medical Officers, Teaching Sub-cadre, Specialist Non-teaching
Sub-cadre and Public Health Sub-cadre  of Central Health services is  made  through UPSC.   The Committee has been
given to understand that this is a very long drawn affair.  Not only this, promotional avenues for all the sub-cadres of

XXII    JOINT CONSULTATIVE MACHINERY
22.1     The Committee is deeply disturbed by frequent incidents of doctors/teachers, para-medical staff, ancillary staff

XXIV     TEACHER-STUDENT RELATIONSHIP
24.1     There has been gradual erosion in the traditional relations between students and teachers in medical education.   
Over the years, what has happened is that  with the increase in the intake capacity in medical colleges all over the

 OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
OVERVIEW
            The Committee observes that the amendments made in the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956 in 1993 by laying
down very stringent norms for establishment of new medical college, increase in the intake capacity and introduction of
a new or higher course of study  has  checked  the mushroom growth of  medical colleges in the country to some
extent.   However, these norms need to be enforced uniformly  and  with an objective approach.   Sporadic proposals for
starting a very large number of medical colleges at a stroke  still continue to be noticed. (Para 2.1)
Private medical colleges, in the initial period, barely reach the standards and  norms set up by MCI  with regard
to infrastructure, manpower etc.   Similarly, in the Govt. medical  colleges set up in different parts of the country, with
the passage of time there has been a steady deterioration in the infrastructure because of   inability of State Govts  to
provide any assistance  whatsoever due to their very poor financial position.   Other major   constraints noticed in the
smooth functioning of Govt. medical colleges are control of too many authorities, lack of accountability, prolonged
procedure of recruitment  and promotion of teachers.   Inadequate  staff, unwarranted interference by management and
manipulation in the admission of students can be said to be major drawbacks of private medical colleges.    The
Committee also noticed that access to clinical facilities having large patient attendance was there in Govt. medical
colleges.   In contrast in private medical colleges, in spite of having impressive      hospital  buildings, required clinical
material  was lacking. (Para 2.2)
MEDICAL COUNCIL OF INDIA (MCI)
The Committee observes that the  present composition of  MCI cannot be said  to be of an All India  character.  
This is all the more evident as only  fifteen members form the quorum  of the Council and all the acts of the Council
are   decided by a majority of the members present and voting. (Para 3.3)
            The Committee notes that out of 36 vacancies in the  113-member Council, 21 are in the seats reserved for
Universities.  Reason for vacancies in this category, as intimated by    the Department is non-constitution/non-
reconstitution  of Senate or Court of the Universities or  meetings of the Senate/Court not being held for a long time.   
What is a cause of  serious concern  is that Universities are simply not responding to the reminders  sent by the Council
and the Central Govt. from time to time and vacancies are continuing in some cases for the last 10-15 years. (Para 3.4)
Thus, as many as 21 Universities over and above 56 eligible Universities are not represented in the Council.

INSPECTION
The Committee notes that a number of steps  have been taken by MCI to make the procedure of inspection
foolproof.   For example, the medical colleges are now supposed to maintain a list of teachers on their strength 
alongwith their photographs  duly verified by the Dean of the College.    The Committee, however, is of the view that
there is a need for  streamlining the inspection procedure so as to make it more stringent and effective.  The Committee
understands that so far inspections are being carried out only  after giving prior  notice.   The Committee is of the view
that MCI should initiate measures  so as to build up  a  mechanism for carrying out surprise inspections and inspections
at a very short notice.   This would indirectly lead to a  situation where medical college authorities would work towards
having a proper set up as per the prescribed norms.   The Committee is also of  the view that rate  of fees charged  for
different  categories of inspection should be reviewed.   Specially, in spite of   the so-called annual inspections being 
carried out only once in five years,  annual fee of Rs. 30,000 is prescribed  which  seems to be on the high side. (Para
5.4)
            The Committee is surprised to note that this elaborate procedure does not   seem to be serving the required 
purpose.   The Committee would like to quote one example in this regard.   The General Body of the Council at its
meeting held on 17
th March, 1998 had recommended to the Central Govt. de-recognition of BRD Medical College,
Gorakhpur, UP.   It is  pertinent to point out that inspection of this college was carried out in 1995.   It took more than
three years for the Council to arrive at a decision which was communicated to the Central Govt. on 6
th July, 1998.   
Since then there have been correspondence between the Central Govt., State Govt. and MCI and the matter hangs    
there.   The Committee takes a very serious view of this situation.   Nobody  seems to have any concern-neither the
college nor the State Govt./University/Central Govt.    No doubt, students’ interest is safeguarded as de-recognition if
approved by the Central Govt. would be made effective from prospective date.    The Committee is of the view that in
the name of safeguarding the interest of students, interest of college authorities is being secured.   The Committee feels
that such developments defeat the very purpose of carrying out an inspection.   The Committee, therefore, strongly
recommends that a time-bound and result-oriented inspection procedure is required to be built up if the standard of
medical education is to be maintained.   (Para 5.6)
POOR CONDITION OF GOVT. MEDICAL COLLEGES
            The Committee is inclined to agree with this view-point.   The Committee  is of the view that there is an urgent
need for undertaking a review of  infrastructure status of all state-run medical  colleges in the country.   Non-existent
required facilities tend to create an atmosphere which  has a demotivating effect on the teachers/doctors/students.    The
Committee understands that Central Govt. has  already launched a pilot project for providing financial assistance to
some State Govt.  medical colleges/hospitals.   The Committee feels that a time-bound action plan for revamping the
infrastructure facilities should be drawn by the Central Govt. which could be implemented by State Govts in active co-
ordination with Central Govt. and MCI.  (Para 6.2)
The Committee is of the view  that against the backdrop of technological development, basic qualifications
required for technical staff need to be reviewed.   Besides that, regular in-service training, workshops and Continuing
Medical Education (CMEs) for technical staff should be made a regular feature.   The Committee also feels that
feasibility of having Medical Learning Technique (MLT) colleges imparting diploma/B.Sc degrees being attached to
medical colleges can be explored. (Para 6.3)
The Committee also observes  that NE region of the country  is virtually starved of spread of  medical education
as very few medical  colleges  have been established  there so farThe Committee is, however, of the view that Central
Govt. initiative is very much required for setting up medical colleges in NE states.   The Committee hopes that the

112th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/112threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:15:22]
Central Govt. would initiate immediate action in this direction. (Para 6.4)
ADMISSION PROCEDURE
 
The Committee feels that a  uniform entrance test covering all the medical colleges – both Govt. and private
would be the ideal position.   The Committee, however, is of the opinion that in view of the different patterns of
secondary level education  in different states, uniform admission procedure would not prove to be feasible.   The
Committee  feels that this issue needs to be debated by the medical fraternity, its pros and cons thoroughly established
and evaluated first.  The Committee is of the view that Central Govt./MCI  should take the initiative to examine this
issue in detail so that number of entrance tests is minimized to the extent possible  thus lessening the burden of
students.  (Para 7.2)
The Committee notes that the students admitted against the 15% seats in medical colleges  filled by the
management have to fulfill the minimum eligibility criteria namely, obtaining the qualification equivalent to the pre-
eligibility qualifications prescribed  for Indian students for admission to medical courses prescribed by the MCI.
   However, the Committee has been given to understand that in view  of the different backgrounds they come from, it is
for the management concerned to judge the merit of these candidates, having regard to all relevant factors.   The 
Committee has its reservations on such a procedure.   The Committee strongly feels that there has to be a mechanism for
judging the basic knowledge and aptitude for medical profession even for NRI students.   The Committee,  therefore,  is
of the view that a separate entrance test should be prescribed for NRI students so as to somewhat  eliminate the element
of commercialization in admission through this category.  The Committee  has no hesitation in saying that track record
of such students will justify such an initiative. (Para 7.3)
The Committee, therefore, is of the view that entrance test for admission to MBBS should also include a
mechanism like aptitude test  for assessing the prospective student’s  inclination for medical profession. (Para 7.4)
NRI QUOTA IN GOVT. MEDICAL COLLEGES
The Committee understands that in some of the States, this practice has been started which is likely to be
followed in other States also.   The Committee has its own reservations about this Central Govt. directive.   While
noting that contribution of seats towards the All India Entrance Examination Quota by the State Govt. medical  colleges
will not be affected by this move, the Committee apprehends that requirements of local students are bound to be hit
hard.   The Committee wonders as to how the interest of local students will be taken care  of with the  total number of
seats remaining the same.   The Committee is of the view  that relaxation should only be resorted to if equal number of
seats are increased.   In that event also, availability of additional required infrastructure will also have to be ensured.   
The Committee also feels that there are little chances of funds thus generated being utilized for the development of a
particular institution.  Instances  have already  come to the notice  of the Committee where initial understanding with
State Govt. was  that money generated from NRI admissions was to be deposited with college and a special committee
was to be authorized to utilize these funds.   However, subsequently, State Govt directions were given  that such funds
are to be deposited with the State Exchequer to be allotted  afterwards to the college. (Para 8.1)
The  Committee, therefore, feels that alongwith entrance test, feasibility of having  individual cumulative
performance at the first, second and third MBBS examination and also aptitude of the students being  made the criteria
for admission to Postgraduate courses may be explored. (Para 8.3)
EXAMINATION PROCEDURE
            The Committee is of the view that MCI norms are not being strictly adhered to by many of the medical
colleges.   Had it been so, there would not have been such reservations and also variations in examination patterns being
followed by different institutions/universities.  The Committee would also like to point out that uniformity of
examination pattern to the maximum extent possible can only be   achieved if the teaching facilities and  courses are
also uniform.  The Committee, therefore, is of the view that steps need to be initiated for evolving a forum for
interaction amongst medical faculties of different universities on this aspect of medical education. (Para 9.3)
CURRICULUM
The Committee notes that these amendments are at present under discussion between  Central Govt., DGHS and
MCI.  The Committee notes that no time-frame has been specified for upgrading  and updating the  curriculum.    The

Committee  is of the view that revision of MBBS curriculum  should be made a regular feature, within a specific time-
frame strictly adhered to so as to keep pace with the advancement in medical sciences. (Para 10.4)
The Committee emphasizes  the need for adoption of curriculum for MBBS  in letter and spirit by all the
universities in spite of there being variations in standard of medical education being imparted in different medical
colleges due to one reason or the other. (Para 10.5)
The Committee feels that a line has to be drawn somewhere and one should not forget that in medical profession,

TEACHERS
            The Committee finds that in states like Maharashtra and Gujarat, shortage of faculty strength in Govt. medical
college has been addressed to some extent by having two types of teachers -  Full-time and Honorary teachers. 
Although divergent views were given with regard to honorary teachers, the Committee had a feeling that both the
categories of teachers  were playing a complimentary role.   The Committee observes that in spite of there being a
provision for only full-time teachers in MCI Regulations, on the request of some State Govts., system of honorary
teachers has been further extended by the Central Govt.  upto 30
th June, 2002.   The  Committee is of the view that
Central Govt./MCI should undertake a detailed review of Honorary System of teachers in the concerned States.   The
Committee is of the view that after removal of all the  loopholes in this system and also safeguarding the interest of Full
time teachers, other State Govts. can be approached to take benefit of Honorary System of teachers to the extent
possible. (Para 11.4)
            The Committee finds that the UGC guidelines regarding extension of retired medical teachers of all medical
colleges/institutions on yearly basis upto the age of 65 years have been agreed to by the Ministry of Health and Family
Welfare in 1997.   The Committee feels that this directive should be given wide publicity  so that the Govt. medical
colleges may take benefit of the same.  Besides that, possibility of hiring the services of  medical professionals –
Visiting Professors and practicing doctors without compromising the standard of medical education can also be
explored. (Para 11.5)
            The Committee observes  that MCI has not made any Regulations regarding separation of teaching and non-
teaching cadres.   The Council Regulations only provide for a required number of teachers and other non-teaching staff
required for each department in the medical college and attached  hospital  according to the seat intake capacity in
MBBS course.   The Committee is of the view that practice of inter-change between teaching and non-teaching cadres is
indeed creating problems for medical faculty in some of the states.   The Committee,  therefore, recommends that the

112th Report

MCI should examine this practice and make specific provision in the best interest of medical teachers in its Regulations.
(Para 11.6)
            The Committee observes that the duties and responsibilities of medical teachers are different, immediate and
exacting in nature when compared with other categories  of professions/Govt. employees.    The Committee would like
to emphasise that there is an urgent need for providing the  best possible service conditions to the medical teachers.  
Their pay scales and other incentives need to be of a higher scale as compared to other teachers. (Para 11.7)
            The Committee is not convinced by the stand of the Central Govt. that in the absence of central funding, it is in
no position to prescribe uniform salary and allowances for the teachers in medical colleges.  The Committee strongly
feels that uniform pay scales  in accordance with the manifold duties performed by medical teachers across the country
would be the ideal  condition.   In order to maintain the standard of medical education, there is imperative need for
safeguarding the interests of medical teachers in every conceivable manner.   The Committee is surprised to note that
the proposal for having a  Central agency on the  pattern of UGC and AICTE was initially discussed in a meeting taken
by the Member, Planning Commission and the broad guidelines for establishment of such a Commission  were also 
worked out.   However,  subsequently in 1997 with the approval of the then Health Minister  it was decided to keep the
proposal in abeyance.   The Committee is of the view that a Medical Grants Commission  is required  to be set up
without any further delay.  The Committee is happy to note that draft National Health Policy, 2001 at present under
circulation to all concerned for their comments envisages the setting up of Medical Grants Commission for funding new
Govt. medical colleges and upgradation of the existing Govt. medical colleges.     The service conditions of medical
teachers should also be covered under the mandate of Medical  Grants Commission. (Para 11.9)
TRAINING
The Committee strongly feels that training in educational technology should be made the eligibility criteria for
appointment of a medical teacher.   If that is not considered feasible, extensive training programme of short duration
should be mandatory before   the prospective teachers join their duties. (Para 12.1) 
The Committee is, however, constrained to note that these directives are not being adhered to as only about 50-
60 medical colleges in the country  have established such units so far.   The Committee is of the opinion that fulfillment
of this condition should be pursued with all medical colleges both Govt. and private.  (Para 12.2)
            The Committee would also like to point out that there is also a need for encouraging the participation of more
and more medical teachers in CME Programmes, workshops, conferences, seminars etc. by making available the
required funds for this purpose.   MCI/Central Govt. should pursue this aspect also with the concerned authorities in the 
states. (Para 12.3)
The Committee finds that at present no incentive schemes are there to attract bright students towards teaching
and there is no such proposal also being contemplated by MCI.   In view of medical practice being more income
generating, the Committee is of the view that a  system of identifying and providing incentives  like earmarking funds
for research projects and job guarantee to students interested in teaching needs to be evolved on the pattern of various
schemes being implemented by the Govt. /AICTE/UGC for attracting young talent towards teaching.   The Committee
strongly feels that this is specially required for students of non-clinical and para-clinical subjects. (Para 12.4)
ASSESSMENT OF TEACHERS
The Committee is of the opinion that a scientific and professional mechanism for conducting such an assessment
in a fair/transparent and equitable manner needs to be evolved before taking any initiative in this direction. (Para 13.1)
PRIVATE PRACTICE
The Committee strongly feels that the teaching institutions should have non-practicing staff.   Private practice
wherever in vogue is largely affecting the quality of functioning of the Institute as well as proper patient care  because
teachers are more interested in private practice rather than in imparting teaching and research.   Keeping these factors  in
view, the  Committee emphasizes that it is very essential that the teachers are  well paid so as to compensate them for
their extra time and dedication to their profession.   The Committee has been given to understand that the varying rates
of NPA are prevalent in different State Govt. medical colleges which are less than Central Govt. rates.  The Committee
is of the firm view that  there should be a clear policy regarding private practice and non practicing allowance
throughout the country.   MCI/Central Govt. should take the initiative in this direction without any further delay. (Para
14.3)

PROBLEM OF SHORTAGE OF DOCTORS IN RURAL AREAS – REMEDIES THEREFOR
However, the Committee is not inclined to agree with this view mainly  because equal standards are required to
be maintained for both rural and urban areas.   One must not forget the fact that rural masses although generally illiterate
are gradually realizing the harm caused by unregistered doctors  practising in rural areas.   The Committee strongly feels
that creation of a class within a class will not be appropriate.  Such a distinction would be detrimental to the entire
medical profession. (Para 15.2)
The Committee is of the view that compulsion alone will not have the desired results.   The Committee,
therefore, recommends that alongwith compulsory rural service for a limited specified time, incentives like special
allowance, better housing and other allied facilities and special relaxation for admission to postgraduate courses are also
necessary for attracting young doctors towards rural areas. (Para 15.4)
The Committee would also like to emphasise that during internship period, PHC posting should be strictly
adhered to and monitored as it would motivate them towards rural service later on.  The Committee also feels that  there
should an orientation programme before doctors posted in rural areas join their duties. (Para 15.5)
There was also a need for developing proper infrastructure in PHCs.   The Committee is of the view that the
Department should pursue this issue with the State Govts.  The Committee understands the limitations of the Centre,
health being a state subject.   However, PHCs are being maintained through Central Grants.  Hence, the need for Central
monitoring about upkeep of PHCs is also there. (Para 15.6)
BRAIN DRAIN
   The Committee observes that there is another aspect of this problem.   Young pass-outs do not get to know all
the opportunities  which are available in the country.   There is a need for evolving a facility  on the pattern of
Recruitment agencies coming to the campuses of premier technical institutions for placements.   The Committee is of
the firm view that  remedial steps need to be taken for creating best job opportunities  thereby retaining our young talent
in the country.  The Committee also feels that if necessary, students may be asked to pay back in early installments,
wherever Government  funding is there.    Modalities in this regarding may be explored. (Para 16.1)
MUSHROOMING OF LABS/DIAGNOSTIC CENTRES
The Committee  however, observes that too much dependence of present-day doctors on technical know-how
has indirectly led to a  mushroom growth of Labs/Diagnostic Centres  conducting CT Scan and MRI and other
sophisticated tests all over the country.   The Committee has been given to understand that such centres are not generally
manned by qualified medical    personnel.   The doctors treat their patients on the basis of reports given by such centres
which sometimes give out incorrect interpretation of patient’s state of health.   The Committee strongly feels that there
is an urgent need for bringing out a comprehensive legislation to check  the mushroom growth of  such centres. (Para
17.1)
The Committee hopes that the procedural  formalities are completed at the earliest so that the proposed
legislation comes into force without any further delay. (Para 17.2)
INDIAN MEDICAL SERVICE
The Committee observes that although it is a welcome idea, there are some inherent problems which may arise
later on.   First is the difference between  the nature of jobs.   Medical Service is mostly research-oriented, teaching job
whereas IAS/IPS   is administration based service.   Secondly, it may not be  feasible as regional interest are not likely
to be served.   The Committee  would also like to point out that there were strong reservations about  transfer policy
being followed in the States.   Feedback which  the Committee received during its study-visits was that teachers, by and
large were not in favour of being transferred even within a  State.   Not only this,  Health being a State subject, it will be
very difficult to create such a cadre and its implementation would, therefore, be difficult for obvious reasons.   The
Committee, therefore, is of the opinion that this issue needs to be subjected to wide-scale discussions in the medical
fraternity before taking any decision. (Para 18.2)
THINK TANK
            The Committee is aware about the mandate of MCI and also the over-all involvement of the Central Govt. in

112th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/112threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:15:22]
regulating the standard of medical education in the country.   The Committee, however, is given to understand that the
required academic input for strengthening the quality of medical education is somewhat lacking.    The Committee,
therefore, strongly feels that there is a need for having a ‘Think Tank’ of eminent Professionals of both medical care and
medical education.   Such a body should, however, be solely involved in the work of assessment and analysis of medical
education  so as to provide various policies based on the academic knowledge and professional experience of its
members for its growth in the right direction. (Para 19.3)
POWERS OF UNIVERSITY/HEALTH UNIVERSITY
            The Committee is of the opinion that observations of local University  should invariably  be obtained before
permission being given for starting a new college/increasing the intake capacity/adding new courses.  Similarly, the
Committee finds no harm if University  representative is also part of the inspection team.   The Committee has been
informed that a Sub-Committee was constituted by the General Body of the Council to go into the establishment of
Health Universities.    The Sub-Committee has recommended discussion by a broader group in a workshop.  The
Committee hopes that the issues raised before the Committee with regard to Health Universities would also be taken
care of and a clear perception of the feasibility of Health University  would emerge as a result of discussion to be
undertaken by the broader Group. (Para 20.4)
ROLE OF NATIONAL LEVEL INSTITUTIONS
            The Committee feels that in  view of the weak financial position  of States, Central Govt. has to take an initiative
in this direction if referral character of All India Institutions is to be maintained.   The Committee is happy to note that
the Central Govt. has launched a pilot project this year for strengthening the diagnostic facilities of State hospitals.   The
Committee has been given to understand that this scheme is being implemented for the last two-three years.   States like
M.P., Haryana, Bihar, Tripura, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, UP, Kerala, Gujarat and J&K have already benefited from
this scheme during this period.   While appreciating the initiative, the Committee would like to emphasise that such
grants should also be strictly monitored by the Central Govt. so as to assess the  fulfillment of the objective of this
project. (Para 21.3)
The Committee strongly feels that Govt. institutions generating income should be allowed to retain such an
income in the maintenance and up-gradation of their institution.   If the whole income being placed at their disposal is
not possible, the Committee finds no harm in a specific percentage of income being retained by them.  This facility 
need not result in deduction of equivalent amount of funds  from their budget allocation. (Para 21.4)
The Committee therefore, feels that the Central Govt. should explore the possibility of establishing more
national level institutions. (Para 21.5)
The Committee observes that problem of faculty shortage is also being faced by premier institutions like AIIMS
and PGI due to one reason or other.   The Committee understands that besides     a number of posts lying vacant, quite a
few  professors are working on ad hoc basis.  Besides that, in the institutions   under the control of Central Govt.,
recruitment to all the four sub-cadres i.e. General Duty Medical Officers, Teaching Sub-cadre, Specialist Non-teaching
Sub-cadre and Public Health Sub-cadre  of Central Health services is  made  through UPSC.   The Committee has been
given to understand that this is a very long drawn affair.  Not only this, promotional avenues for all the sub-cadres of   
Central Health Services  are not of a satisfactory level.   The Committee hopes that after the  ongoing exercise of cadre
review is over,  things would improve for the better.   The Committee  is of the view that there is also a need for
evolving some system of co-ordination among the concerned authorities, i.e. UPSC, Directorate of Health Services/
Directorate of Medical Education and the concerned college. (Para 21.6)
JOINT CONSULTATIVE MACHINERY
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should pursue the setting up of such a mechanism
with all the State Govts/management of both national level and private institutions. (Para 22.1)
            The Committee is also of the view that a cell for receiving complaints and inviting suggestions from general
public and affected persons should be set up in the MCI to work as a watch-guard and also facilitate the functioning of
MCI. (Para 22.2)
PROBLEMS BEING FACED BY STUDENT COMMUNITY
            The Committee understands that a specific  time schedule for admission  to MBBS has been laid down by MCI.

The Committee finds that due to  delay in the admission procedure, training schedule gets disturbed and as a result,
instances are there that results are delayed.  The Committee, therefore, strongly feels that MCI should strictly monitor
the adherence of  time schedule by medical  colleges all over the country.  (Para 23.2)
The Committee is of the view that the   development of coaching classes in some states in addition to the normal
colleges cannot be considered to be a healthy sign.   MCI should, therefore, undertake a review of this development in
concerned states and pursue the State Govts. to take required steps for  putting a check  to this unhealthy practice.    The

available in Govt. medical colleges strictly on merit and without disturbing  students  of such colleges may be explored

for periodic review  of stipend payable to students and bringing about uniformity to the extent possible.   The
Committee would also like to point out that there is a need for standardization   of medical textbooks having a balanced
mixture of foreign and national components. (Para 23.3)
TEACHER-STUDENT RELATIONSHIP
The Committee feels that the situation should be  such that the teachers are able to share their experiences with
the students.   Teachers also  get an opportunity to evaluate the students better in such an environment.   The Committee
finds that  availability of video cassettes and internet access has vastly improved interactive learning wherever
available.   The Committee is of the view that steps should be taken for compulsory introduction of such hi-tech
facilities in all medical colleges  in the country. (Para 24.1)
MEDICAL RESEARCH
The Committee is of the considered view that steps should be taken  to step up research activities in the   Health
sector, with the active involvement of both public and private sector.   (Para 25.1)
