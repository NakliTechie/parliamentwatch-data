I           INTRODUCTION 
The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy was set up by the Government of 
India as an independent Department in March, 1995 under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.  
The main objective of the Department is to provide focused attention for the proper development and 
propagation of ISM&H, namely, Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Homoeopathy, Yoga and Naturopathy and 
to fully utilise the potentials of these systems. 
The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy (ISM& H) is headed by a Secretary 
to the Government of India who is assisted by a Joint Secretary and four Directors/Deputy Secretaries, 4 
Advisers and several Deputy Advisors of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani &. Homoeopathy. Senior level posts 
such as Director (Ayurveda & Siddha), Adviser (Ayurveda), Adviser (Unani) & Adviser (Homoeopathy) 
have been created for providing expert advice on policy formulation and execution, complex technical 
and pharmacopoeial matters. There are 3 subordinate offices and 15  autonomous bodies under this 
Department and more than 4000 personnel are working in these institutions/ organizations/subordinate 
offices. A manufacturing company is also working under the Department. A Medicinal plants Board is 
functioning under the Department to co-ordinate activities relating to conservation, cultivation, 
marketing, export and drawing policies and strategies for the development of medicinal plants sector. A 
Medicinal Plants Cell (MPC) is working under the Department for implementing the Central Scheme for 
development and cultivation of Medicinal Plants and developing agro-techniques. A  nucleus Patent Cell 
is working under the Department for dealing with developing Traditional Knowledge Digital Library
(TKDL) and matters relating to Intellectual Property Rights as also coordination with Government of 
India Ministries/Departments concerned with IPR.

V         AYURVEDA 
5.1       Ayurveda (Ayu +Veda) means the "Science of Life". The documentation of Ayurveda is referred 
to in Vedas (5000 BC). The origin of Ayurveda or the Indian Science of life is linked with the origin of 
Universe and developed out of the various Vedic hymns describing fundamentals/philosophies about the 
world and life, diseases and medicines. Around 1000 B.C, the knowledge of Ayurveda was 
comprehensively documented in Charak Samhita and Sushruta Samhita. According to Ayurveda, health 
is considered a pre-requisite for achieving the goals of life, dharma, artha, kama &. moksha (salvation). 
Ayurveda takes an integrated view of the physical ,mental and spiritual and social aspects of human 
beings, each impinging on the other. The philosophy of Ayurveda is based on the theory of 
Panchmahabhutas (five element theory) of which all the objects and living bodies are composed of. The 
combination of these five elements are represented in the form of Tridosha eg., Vata (Ether+ Air), Pitta 
(Fire) and Kaph (Water + Earth). These three 'Doshas' are physiological entities in living beings. These 
are also known as three humours. The mental, spiritual and physical attributes are described as Satva, 
Rajas and Tamas. The doctrine of Ayurveda aims to keep these structural and functional entities in a 
functional state of equilibrium which signifies good health (Swastha). Any imbalance due to internal or 
external factors causes disease and restoring the equilibrium through various techniques, procedures, 
regimen, diet and medicine constitute the treatment. Ayurveda considers the human being as a 
microcosm (Yatha pinde tatha brahmande), a replica of macrocosm (Universe). The treatment in 
Ayurveda system is individualized. Treatment in Ayurveda has two components; (a) Preventive; and (b) 
Curative: Preventive aspect of Ayurveda is called Svasth-Vritt and includes personal hygiene, regular 
daily routine, appropriate social behaviour and Rasayana Sevana, i.e, use of  rejuvenative materials/food 
and rasayans drugs. The curative treatment consists of three major categories of procedures (i)Aushadhi 
(drugs); (ii) Anna (diets) and (iii) Vihara(exercises and general mode of life) 
The Plan allocation for Ayurveda in 2003-2004 is Rs.14.46 crores. The Committee has been informed 
that amongst the European Countries only Hungry  has recognized the system of Ayurveda. Countries 
like, Russia, Italy, Spain, Belgium, U.K. and U.S.A. though have shown keen interest for the system, are 
yet to recognize it.  These Countries, though are positive about recognizing the system of Ayurveda but 
want assurance about the quality and safety of drugs.  The Department has further informed that for 
ensuring quality control it has recently introduced good manufacturing practices and land down certain 
norms and standards for manufacture of Ayurvedic Medicine. 
   The Committee feels that the European Countries, USA and other developed and prosperous countries 
have tremendous potential to become an extremely lucrative market for the Ayurvedic drugs of our 
country and can generate a lot of foreign exchange for the Country. However, in order to achieve this, as 
rightly pointed out by the Department, the confidence of such countries w.r.t. the safety and quality of 
such drugs has to be won. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Department should 
keep a tight vigil of the quality and safety of the Ayurveda drugs manufactured and used in India or 
exported out of India so that more and more countries recognize our traditional system of medicine.

The Committee observes that people opt for traditional medicine because they are affordable and less 
harmful. However, the experts have voiced concerns about the safety and quality, licensing of providers 
and standards of training in connection with traditional medicines. 
            The World Health Organization has come out with a traditional medicine strategy for 2002-2005 
which focuses on these issues. According to WHO, 70 per cent of Indian population use traditional 
medicine for their primary health needs.  
            A group of experts recently met in Delhi to chalk out an adequate strategy for integration of 
traditional medicine with the health care system in India. According to these experts, a set of standards 
for regulating traditional medicine and setting up of a body to provide to medical claims is necessary. 
            The Committee feels the safety and efficiency of traditional medicine has been established 
through centuries of use. However, in order to make it acceptable to the outside world it may be 
necessary to validate its scientific basis. Here, the Committee is of the view that we need not tinker with 
our system for this purpose. Chernobyl incident convinced Russians about the efficacy of our system of 
medicine when the victims’ burns were miraculously healed. Likewise there are many simple plants and 
products like neem and turmeric, the efficacy of which tempted some countries abroad to have these 
products patented for commercial exploitation. The Committee therefore reiterates that the system need 
not be tinkered with to convince the outside world.  
            The attention of the Committee has been drawn to the fact that there are many traditional 
medicine practitioners who have claimed a cure for some disease or the other. In most cases they end up 
making rounds of Government agencies or try to sell their formula to pharmaceuticals but to little avail 
e.g. the person who claimed to have developed a herbal contraceptive in the 
 1980, passed away by the time Government decided to follow it up. The Committee observes the need 
to avoid such situations. The Government machinery should be suitably geared up and be responsive in 
such situations. 
VI        HOMOEOPATHY 
6.1       Homoeopathy is a specialized method of drug therapy of curing natural diseases by 
administration of drugs which has been experimentally proved to possess the power of producing similar 
artificial symptoms on healthy human beings . In Homoeopathy, medicines are prepared from natural 
sources Vegetable, Mineral, Animal, etc. There is no toxic or poisonous effect in these medicines since 
it is not the chemical or physical properties that are acting on the human system. Homoeopathy has its 
own areas of strength in therapeutics. Its curative capability extends to allergic manifestations, auto-
immune disorders and viral infections. Many Surgical, Gynaecological & Obstetrical conditions, 
ailments, affecting eyes, nose, ear, teeth, skin, sexual organs etc. are amenable to the homoeopathic 
treatment. Behaviour disorders, neurological problems, metabolic diseases are successfully handled by 
the physicians of this system. Homoeopathy has effective answer to addiction to drugs, tobacco and 
alcohol and is highly efficacious in ridding of addicts and their craving for these noxious substances)  
  Plan allocation for Homoeopathy was Rs. 10.21 crores in BE 2002-2003. However it has been reduced 
to Rs. 9.17 crores in BE 2003-2004. The Secretary while deposing before the Committee informed that 
presently there are 80 colleges of homoeopathy in the Country and out of which 17 colleges are 
imparting PG education.  However, there is no separate university for Homoeopathy. 
6.3       The Committee feels that in the absence of a separate university, the Homoeopathy education is 
perhaps not getting the attention it deserves. The Committee further feels that the quality of imparting 
Homoeopathy education can be vastly improved if an exclusive Homoeopathy university is established 
to regulate and monitor the functioning of the medical colleges throughout the Country. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Department should consider establishing a separate Homoeopathy 
University and the allocation under the head `Homoeopathy should be increased accordingly. 
            At the same time there have been illiterate persons who acquired marvelous proficiency in 
developing this system of medicine.  The Committee, therefore, feels that there should be room for 
persons with average qualification to study and develop their skills in the field of Homoeopathy.  Part 
time colleges should also be encouraged to help people come forward to acquire knowledge and 
expertise in this system of medicine.

VII       YOGA AND NATUROPATHY 
7.1       Yoga is primarily a way of life propounded by Patanjali in a systematic form. It consists of eight 
components, namely, restraint, observance of austerity, physical postures, breathing exercise, restraining 
of sense  organs, contemplation, meditation and samadhi. These steps in the practice of Yoga have 
potential for improvement of social and personal behaviour, improvement of physical health by 
encouraging better  circulation of oxygenated blood in the body, restraining the sense organs and thereby 
inducing tranquility and a serenity of mind. The practice of Yoga prevents psychosomatic 
disorders/diseases and improves individual resistance and ability to endure stressful situations. 
Meditation, one of the eight  components, if regularly practiced, has the capacity to reduce wholesome 
bodily responses to a bare minimum so that the mind can be directed to perform more fruitful functions. 
7.2       The Plan allocation for Yoga and Naturopathy for the year 2003-2004 is Rs. 5.10 crores.  The 
Committee has been informed that there are 6 colleges in the country which are imparting training in 
yoga and naturopathy and there is also a Central Council for Yoga and Naturopathy which provides 
money for research and running of various schemes.  The Secretary while deposing before the 
Committee informed that the Department of ISMH is planning to pass an Act in the Parliament so as to 
empower the Council to grant recognition to the council so that Naturopathy and Yoga are regulated like 
other disciplines. 
            The Committee observes that the pros and cons should be considered before taking a decision to 
introduce legislation  in this regard.   
VIII          QUALITY CONTROL OF DRUGS 
8.1       A Drug Control Cell (ISM &. H) is working in the Department to deal with the matters 
pertaining to licensing and regulation of drugs and control of misbranded/adulterated and spurious 
manufacturing of Ayurvedic, Unani and Siddha Drugs and other matters related thereto. Besides, 
Information, Education & Communication Cell and Facilitation Centre have also been set up in the 
Department. 
8.2       Quality Control of ISM&H drugs is one of the priority areas of the Department.  The Secretary 
informed the Committee that the state Drug Licensing Authority has been designated as the ‘Licensing 
Authority’ and are mandated to take  all measures to ensure quality control.  However, the machinery 
and infrastructure required for testing of drugs is very poor in most of the States.  The Secretary has 
further informed that during the 10th Five Year Plan, the Department of ISM&H would be providing 
some assistance to the State Government to strengthen the machinery. 
            The Committee is happy to note that despite all odds, the Department has been able to provide 
assistance to the State Governments.  However, the Committee feels that mere strengthening  of 
machinery would not serve any useful purpose unless the State Drug Control Authorities vigorously 
monitor the quality of drugs produced.  Otherwise the machines will remain idle.   At the same time, as 
pointed out in the foregoing paragraph,  the Committee is of the view that the machinery should be 
responsive, devoid of corruption and preserve the system and should not unnecessarily promote too 
much of commercialization by diluting the principles.

II          STRATEGIES  FOR  TENTH  FIVE  YEAR  PLAN 
2.5       The Committee feels that non submission of utilization certificates by the States is a perennial 
problem which is derailing several developmental activities in the States.  The Committee is happy to 
note that Ministry of Finance has formulated  guidelines on furnishing of utilisation certificates by the 
States. The Committee recommends that the Department should constantly keep on hammering the 
states to submit utilization certificates and vigorously  implement the Finance Ministry’s Guideline. 
The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Department for the 10th Plan.  However, the Committee, 
feels that unless the causes for low utilization of funds as identified in the 9th  Plan are  addressed, the 
objectives of the 10th Plan may not be fully realized.  As far as non submission of utilization certificates 
by the ‘Implementing Agencies’ is concerned, the Committee feels that there could be possibilities of 
diversion, misutilisation or in some cases misappropriation of Government funds. The Committee, 
therefore,  recommends that the Department should make an investigation as to how the 1st Instalment 
of money has been utilized and fix responsibility in cases where  funds  have been  found to be 
misappropriated .  The Committee is dismayed at the fact that most of the civic work required for the 
development of the country gets trapped into labyrinths  of laws and by laws.  More laws means more 
beauracratization and more red tape and thereby causing unnecessary postponement of or delay in 
implementing various developmental schemes.  The Committee, therefore, feels that it is high time for 
the  Government including its various Department and Ministries to put their head together, simplify the 
rules and procedures and also consider giving publicity to the schemes and programmes aimed at the 
welfare of the people.  
(Para 2.5) 
III        BUDGETARY ALLOCATION  
    The Committee is constrained to observe that in all the abovementioned 11 schemes, the allocation 
was reduced at  RE stage. The Committee feels that prior to submitting its demands before Parliament, 
the Department should conclude all necessary consultations with the Planning Commission and Ministry 
of Finance and obtain their clearance so that its budgetary allocation, once approved by  Parliament,  is 
not reduced subsequently. (Para 3.2) 
IV.       PRACTICE OF INDIAN SYSTEMS OF MEDICINE AND HOMOEOPATHY IN PRIMARY 
HEALTH CENTRES 
4.3       While the Committee appreciates the  steps taken by the State of Himachal Pradesh and feels that 
such steps should not only solve the problem of non-availability of allopathic doctors in PHCs but 
would also popularize Indian System of Medicine and make career prospects in this field more attractive 
even though this may not be legally advisable step.  The Committee, recommends that the Department 
should persuade the States to follow the example of Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Kerala.  The 
Department should also do away with legal hudles, if any, in this regard. (Para 4.3) 
V         AYURVEDA 
5.5      The Committee feels that the European Countries, USA and other developed and prosperous 
countries have tremendous potential to become an extremely lucrative market for the Ayurvedic drugs of 
our country and can generate a lot of foreign exchange for the Country. However, in order to achieve 
this, as rightly pointed out by the Department, the confidence of such countries w.r.t. the safety and 
quality of such drugs has to be won. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the 
Department should keep a tight vigil of the quality and safety of the Ayurveda drugs manufactured and 
used in India or exported out of India so that more and more countries recognize our traditional system 
of medicine. 
The Committee observes that people opt for traditional medicine because they are affordable and less 
harmful. However, the experts have voiced concerns about the safety and quality, licensing of providers 
and standards of training in connection with traditional medicines. 
            The World Health Organization has come out with a traditional medicine strategy for 2002-2005 
Page 11 of 15137thReportofHRD
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/Report137th.htm

which focuses on these issues. According to WHO, 70 per cent of Indian population use traditional 
medicine for their primary health needs.  
            A group of experts recently met in Delhi to chalk out an adequate strategy for integration of 
traditional medicine with the health care system in India. According to these experts, a set of standards 
for regulating traditional medicine and setting up of a body to provide to medical claims is necessary. 
            The Committee feels the safety and efficiency of traditional medicine has been established 
through centuries of use. However, in order to make it acceptable to the outside world it may be 
necessary to validate its scientific basis. Here, the Committee is of the view that we need not tinker with 
our system for this purpose. Chernobyl incident convinced Russians about the efficacy of our system of 
medicine when the victims’ burns were miraculously healed. Likewise there are many simple plants and 
products like neem and turmeric, the efficacy of which tempted some countries abroad to have these 
products patented for commercial exploitation. The Committee therefore reiterates that the system need 
not be tinkered with to convince the outside world.  
            The attention of the Committee has been drawn to the fact that there are many traditional 
medicine practitioners who have claimed a cure for some disease or the other. In most cases they end up 
making rounds of Government agencies or try to sell their formula to pharmaceuticals but to little avail 
e.g. the person who claimed to have developed a herbal contraceptive in the 
 1980, passed away by the time Government decided to follow it up. The Committee observes the need 
to avoid such situations. The Government machinery should be suitably geared up and be responsive in 
such situations. (Para 5.5) 
VI        HOMOEOPATHY 
6.3       The Committee feels that in the absence of a separate university, the Homoeopathy education is 
perhaps not getting the attention it deserves. The Committee further feels that the quality of imparting 
Homoeopathy education can be vastly improved if an exclusive Homoeopathy university is established 
to regulate and monitor the functioning of the medical colleges throughout the Country. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Department should consider establishing a separate Homoeopathy 
University and the allocation under the head `Homoeopathy should be increased accordingly. 
            At the same time there have been illiterate persons who acquired marvelous proficiency in 
developing this system of medicine.  The Committee, therefore, feels that there should be room for 
persons with average qualification to study and develop their skills in the field of Homoeopathy.  Part 
time colleges should also be encouraged to help people come forward to acquire knowledge and 
expertise in this system of medicine. (Para 6.3) 
VII       YOGA AND NATUROPATHY 
            The Committee observes that the pros and cons should be considered before taking a decision to 
introduce legislation  in this regard. (Para 7.2)  
VIII          QUALITY CONTROL OF DRUGS 
8.3       The Committee is happy to note that despite all odds, the Department has been able to provide 
assistance to the State Governments.  However, the Committee feels that mere strengthening  of 
machinery would not serve any useful purpose unless the State Drug Control Authorities vigorously 
monitor the quality of drugs produced.  Otherwise the machines will remain idle.   At the same time, as 
pointed out in the foregoing paragraph,  the Committee is of the view that the machinery should be 
responsive, devoid of corruption and preserve the system and should not unnecessarily promote too 
much of commercialization by diluting the principles.  (Para 8.2)
