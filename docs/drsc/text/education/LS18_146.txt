II            BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee expresses its serious concern over reduction in allocation of funds for 2003-2004. It observes
that the Planning Commission should take a realistic view of the basic requirements of various Schemes before
restricting the allocation of funds from the projected Demands. The Committee recommends that the Department should
make all efforts to achieve the targets both physically as well financially and also utilize the existing allocation
judiciously and should not allow any scheme either being dropped or left neglected due to this curtailment in allocation.
 
Action Taken
 
This recommendation of the Committee has been brought to the notice of the Planning Commission.  The
Department makes every effort to ensure that no scheme suffers for want of funds within the available resources.
 
VI        DAY CARE CENTRES
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee feels there is tremendous need to introduce ISO standards. At the same time, it is necessary to
keep these crèches free from all kinds of infection for which utmost cleanliness is necessary as children are more prone
to infection. The Committee, therefore, recommends that top priority be accorded for maintaining cleanliness.
 
Action Taken
           
The recommendation of the Committee has been noted and the implementing agencies –2 NGOs (Bhartiya Adim
Jati Sevak Sangh & ICCW) and the CSWB have been  suitably advised
 
IX            BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA (BSY)
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee observes that the overall performance under this Yojana has been very poor. This sends a signal
that the State Governments are not concerned with the welfare of the vulnerable sections of the society. The Committee
recommends that the Department must take up the matter with the States to see whether they can sincerely implement
the Yojana and review the same in light of their response.
Action Taken
 
A Study has been entrusted to NIPCCD to evaluate the working of the scheme, and to come out with such
recommendations as necessary to improve its efficacy at the grass root level.

XVII            TRAFFICKING IN WOMEN AND CHILDREN
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee recommends that preventive measures should be undertaken in accordance with the following
strategies:
 
(i)
                 Prevention of trafficking through the legal and criminal system, training of law enforcement officers,
(ii)                Control and suppression of prostitution through  legal system,
(iii)              Rescue and rehabilitation for women and girl victims of trafficking,
(iv)              Protection for and awareness-raising among women and girls to prevent trafficking,
(v)               Enhance public awareness, particularly in relation to forced and child prostitution and other measures,
(vi)              Awareness-raising among parents and guardians,
(vii)            Provision of alternative employment and income-earning opportunities for women and girls, and
(viii)           Through campaigns targeting parents on the long-term advantages of girls’ education.
 
Action Taken
 
The strategies suggested by the Committee have  been noted for guidance.   The steps taken by the Department
of Women and Child Development to prevent trafficking of women and children are given as follows:  Helpline
services  are also being provided from 54 centres all over India.
 
The Government has taken the following steps to prevent trafficking of women and children.
 
(i)
                 Drawn up a National Plan of Action (1998) and constituted a Central Advisory Committee to combat
trafficking, rescue and rehabilitate victims of trafficking and commercial sexual exploitation, and activate
legal and law enforcement system to strengthen implementation of the immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act,
1956.

II            BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee notes that the trend to utilize the funds in the last month of financial year is a regular feature and
observes that it is not a healthy trend and reflects adversely on the credibility of planning to utilize about 3% of the total
allocation in 15 days or a month before the close of the financial year despite the fact that the Committee recommended
in its earlier Reports that the Department should take corrective measures to avoid last moment spending so that the

146th report of Committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/146threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:19:22]
funds could be judiciously utilized.
 
Action Taken
 
            The first instalment would be released at about 30% of approximate annual requirement of States without taking
into account last year’s savings (+/-).  The second instalment, at about 80% of approximate annual requirement, duly
adjusting last year’s savings would be released only after receipt of Statement of Expenditure (SOE) in respect of funds
released last year and utilization certificate(UC). The third instalment, at about 100% of approximate annual
requirement of States, duly adjusting last year’s savings, would be released after receipt of utilisation certificate of the
first instalment.  Normally, during the financial year, the first instalment would be released in   April -May, the second
in August-September subject to receipt of SOE and UC of last year and the third in December-January during the year
again subject to receipt of UC of first instalment.
 
III            INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee notes that there has not been any significant improvement in the number of Projects, which were
operationalised.  The states of Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Uttaranchal have not shown any increase in the
number of operational projects even after gap of an year.  This shows the indifferent attitude of the State Governments
towards the operationalisation of the sanctioned projects.
           
The Committee recommends that the Department must renew its efforts and approach the State Governments to
persuade them to operationalise these projects. 
 
Action Taken
 
In Andhra Pradesh, the number of operational Projects became 347 as on 31.12.2002, as against 251 reported
earlier.  This error occurred on account of Andhra Pradesh not having reported   projects operationalised  with the
 assistance of  World Bank.  For Uttar Pradesh and Uttaranchal 215 and 45 Projects respectively were permitted for
operationalisation in 2001-02. As far as Bihar is concerned, there has been constant emphasis on the State Government
to accelerate the pace of operationalisation.

III            INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
 
Recommendation/Observation
 
            The Committee notes with concern the findings of the Report which indicate that the maximum benefit of the
proposals made by the Department is almost out of reach of the targeted sections of population as it is evident from the
low rate of utilization of facilities by the beneficiaries. The Department should find out the reasons for the reluctance of
the people to avail of benefits of Schemes and take remedial steps accordingly.
The Committee recommends that there is an urgent need to sanction additional ICDS projects for the uncovered
blocks/urban slums.  In addition to this more anganwadi centres should be opened and feasibility for relocation of
existing centres should be explored.
 
Action Taken
 
One of the important components of the ICDS Scheme, to ensure optimum coverage of the targeted
beneficiaries, is adequate provision for supplementary nutrition at the Anganwadi Centres to all eligible beneficiaries as
per prescribed nutritional norms. Provision of supplementary nutrition is the responsibility of the State Governments. It
has been repeatedly emphasized upon the State Governments to earmark sufficient funds for supplementary nutrition
and ensure implementation of this component as per the prescribed nutritional norms. In this regard, elaborate
guidelines to clarify the basic financial, nutritional and registration norms under ICDS Scheme were circulated  by this

146th report of Committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/146threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:19:22]
Department in consultation with the Planning Commission. The Planning  Commission has also been requested to stress
upon the State Governments to make adequate provision of funds for supplementary nutrition under the ICDS Scheme
during the States’ Annual   Plan discussions. The States have also been requested to make full use of the funds available
under the  nutrition component of Prime Minister Gramodya Yojna.
 
2.         In addition, the Department has  taken a number of steps to make the ICDS Scheme more effective and result-
oriented, which includes decentralization of Medicine Kits and Pre-School Education Kits at the State level; effective
training of ICDS functionaries and Anganwadi Workers; revision of  training syllabi/curricula with a provision of 25%
State-specificity to be incorporated by the State Governments themselves;   improvement in the working conditions of
Anganwadi Workers and Helpers and substantial increase in their honoraria; constant emphasis on States to make
adequate budget provision in the State Plan Outlays for supplementary nutrition to all eligible beneficiaries; issue of
instructions/guidelines to States, in consultation with the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare for proper coordination
and convergence of health inputs; issue of instructions, in consultation with the Department of Elementary Education &
Literacy, to strengthen synergy between Early Childhood Care and Education under District Primary Education
Programme and Early Childhood Education under the ICDS Scheme; issue of instructions to States to relocate
Anganwadi Centres, wherever appropriate, within the overall ceiling of existing Projects and Centers to optimize
coverage of beneficiaries within the available resources; etc. 
 
3.         Various steps taken by the Department have shown positive results including increase in the number of
beneficiaries utilizing various services under ICDS Scheme. This would be evident from the fact that the supplementary
nutrition beneficiaries have increased from  2.90 crore  in March 2001 to 3.88 crore in March 2003, with  the number of 
beneficiaries per Anganwadi Centre  increasing from 70 in March 2001 to 83 in March 2003.
 
            The Plan allocation of the Department (and ICDS Scheme) was not increased in view of resource constraints. 
Therefore, no expansion activities under the ICDS Scheme are contemplated during the X Five Year Plan.  However,
with a view to optimizing  coverage of beneficiaries within the available resources, States have been requested to review
and relocate the Anganwadi Centres, wherever appropriate, within the overall ceiling  of existing Projects and Centres.
 
Comments of the Committee
 
The Committee notes various steps being taken by the Department to make ICDS Schemes more effective and
result-oriented.  But these efforts have not yielded any tangible results so far.  For example, increase in number of
beneficiaries per Anganwadi workers of supplementary nutrition from 70 in March, 2001 to 83 in March 2003 can
hardly been said as significant, looking at the number of targeted beneficiaries.  The Committee, does appreciate the
problem that the Department is facing in persuading the States to adopt/implement laid down norms, financial and
others.  The Department must be under severe resource constraint due to non - increase in its plan allocation.  The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should concentrate in strengthening, streamlining the existing
Schemes under ICDS so as to cover more & more beneficiaries during the year.

Acquisition of land for construction of hostels in the biggest bottleneck in the implementation of the scheme. 
The scheme’s  guidelines are under process for amendment  to provide for hiring of buildings for running working
women’s hostels.
 
Comments of the Committee
 
The Committee is of the view that acquisition of land might be a bottleneck for the implementation of this
Scheme only in big and metropolitan cities.  Demands for such hostels is increasing in middle size cities like state
capitals and other industrial towns in the country.  The Committee recommends that these aspects should also be looked
into while implementing this Scheme.  Local bodies may also be encouraged to participate in setting up of women's
hostels.

Recommendation/Observation
 
The Committee expresses its concern over the non -utilization of even the reduced allocation at RE stage in the
years 2000-2001, 2001-2002 and 2002-2003. The Committee is inclined to believe that the Scheme lacks credibility
among the people. Hence, the lukewarm response and non-utilization of funds. The Committee recommends a thorough
review of the Scheme.
                                   
Action Taken   
 
The Scheme Swayamsidha has three phases, namely (i) Formation of Self-Help Groups (ii) Group Stabilization
and Growth and (iii) Sustainability and Self-reliance.  At present, the Scheme is in its first phase, i.e. group formation
which does not require much funds.  Expenditure under the Scheme is expected to pick up when the Scheme enters the
second phase and when the Self-Help Groups start taking up various socio-economic activities.
 
Comments of the Committee
 
            Implementation of this Scheme, no doubt, is slow and the progress in group formation is also tardy . The Scheme
has remained in the first stage for the last three to four years .   In this manner, the idea of self-reliance of women would
remain a distant dream.  The Department should have to gear itself up for taking this Scheme as a challenge. 
Performance of one of our neighbouring country has been tremendous in this regard .
