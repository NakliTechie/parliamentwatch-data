REPORT
 
I        INTRODUCTION
 
1.1     The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (MoYAS) is entrusted with the responsibilities relating to
the development of Youth and Adolescents, as also the promotion of Sports and Physical Education in
the country. There is a close relationship between these two aspects as the Sports and Physical
Development are vital ingredients of an inclusive Development of the youth, apart from its other aspects
pertaining to competitive Sports.
 
1.2
             Accordingly, the MoYA&S is implementing a large number of schemes for the development of
youth and adolescents development as well as promotion of sports in the country.  As regards those
relating to youth, the schemes are proposed to be re-oriented as a part of an integrated framework
comprising three key thrust areas viz., Personality Development, Preparation for Work
Participation/Employability, and Empowerment.
 
1.3     The Committee appreciates the reorientation of the fragmented Schemes into an integrated
framework with a multi-pronged approach towards youth empowerment and their participation
in the planning of their development.  The Committee is of the view that youth development being
a multidimensional activity will be better achieved if we have a holistic approach towards it.  That
appears to be the main reason that we have not had significant achievements in this regard, so
far.   The Committee expects that now the Ministry would be able to focus on all the thrust areas
of the Youth Development simultaneously to achieve the desired objectives under an umbrella
scheme.

1.4     Under the Constitution of India, sports figures in the State List of the 7  schedule of the
Constitution.  Though sports is primarily the responsibility of the States, the Central Government, while
supplementing their efforts in this direction as well as for tapping the talent, will focus specific attention
on the objective of achieving excellence of the national and international in coordination with SAI, IOA
and Sports Federations Association.  The role of Central Government in supplementing the efforts of the
State Governments in the sphere of broad basing of sports has been reduced after the transfer of the
sports infrastructure related schemes to the States during by X Plan.  In turn, the allocation of resources
for sports by the State Governments has, at best, been peripheral and marginal.   At yet another level, a
third set of key stakeholders, viz., the National Sports Federations, enjoy considerable authority, both
from the Centre as well as the States.
 
1.5     The Committee is worried about an apparent lack of coordination among the Central
Government, State Governments and National Sports Federations.  The Committee feels that this
situation should not be allowed to continue any further.  It would be against the interests of the
sports particularly, keeping into account the forthcoming Commonwealth Games, 2010. The
Committee recommends that all the 'key stakeholders' should regularly interact by integrating
their synergies for the promotion of games, motivation of players and development of 'sports
infrastructure'.
 
1.6     The National Youth Policy affirms the commitment of the nation for holistic and all round
development of youth and seeks to establish an all India perspective to fulfill their legitimate aspirations
and empower them to successfully accomplish the task of nation-building.   The Committee was
informed that the population of youth and adolescents below the age of 35 years is about 70 per cent of
the total population.  This is the largest ever percentage of younger people in our population which, no
doubt, is a potential demographic dividend for the country but at the same time could be a challenge of
mega proportions, if not properly harnessed. 
 
1.7     Keeping into account our vast youth population, the Committee recommends that the
Ministry should make all out efforts to review the National Youth Policy incorporating in it the
key areas such as education, health, sustainable development, self-confidence, social harmony and
national integration.  The Committee also recommends that special attention should be focused on
those youth and adolescents who are living under poverty line in rural and urban areas.

III.    NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN         (NYKS)
 
3.1     Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS) is an autonomous body of the Ministry of Youth Affairs
and Sports.  NYKS has its presence in 500 districts of the country.  It has become one of the largest
grass-root level organizations in the world, catering to the needs of more than 8 million non-student rural
youth enrolled through about 2.58 lakh village based youth Clubs.  These Youth Clubs work in the areas
like education and training, awareness generation, skill development and self-employment,
entrepreneurial development, thrift and co-operation, besides development of the body through sports
and adventure and mind through sustained exposure to new ideas and development strategies.   For
implementation of the programmes, every district NYK has a trained cadre of District Youth
Coordinator, National Service Volunteers and Youth leaders.   The strength of NYKS is its vast network
of Youth Clubs at the grassroots level.   Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan has now 46 regional offices and
18 zonal offices working in 500 districts of the country.  It is proposed to expand its activities in the
remaining 123 districts.

IV      NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME (NSS)
 
4.1     National Service Scheme (NSS) is a centrally sponsored scheme with the objective to develop
character and personality of student youth in schools and colleges.   The Scheme is implemented through
the State Governments and the operations of NSS are at the University/College and Higher Secondary
School level.  The objective of NSS is to arouse social conscience of the students, and to provide them
an opportunity to work with the people in the villages and slum dwellers.  The motto of NSS “Not me
but you” seeks to imbibe a spirit of volunteerism and community service in the minds of young students. 
The scheme seeks to achieve its objectives through twin approach i.e. “Campus to Community” and
“College to Village”, which also helps in developing leadership qualities and personality development of
students.  The purpose of the scheme was the human resource development of those who become NSS
volunteers.  They are all students; they do voluntary work and get involved with some sort of social
activity; and community service during their student life.  The imprint on those individual members who
are NSS volunteers is enormous.

quite insufficient to conduct better quality programmes.   The duration of 10 days is also becoming an
inhibiting factor for students who are primarily devoted to competitive study.  These norms were last

V.      NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEER SCHEME
 
5.1     The National Service Volunteer Scheme launched in 1969 is a Youth leadership development

XV.   PANCHAYAT YUVA KRIDA OR KHEL ABHIYAN
 
15.1   The Committee has time and again been pointing out severe inadequacy of basic sports
infrastructure at the grass root level specially in the rural areas.  The Government has recently approved
a new Scheme Panchayat Yuva Krida or Khel Abhiyan (PYKKA).  It aims at providing basic sports
infrastructure at Panchayat level and organising rural sports competition in village, block and district
level in a phased level during Eleventh and Twelfth Plan.  The scheme will be implemented through the
State Governments as a Centrally sponsored scheme with one time seed capital assistance and limited
recurring grant as Central Grant-in-Aid.  The one time seed capital grant will be shared between the
Central Government and the State Government on 75:25 in respect of normal States and 19:10 in respect
of special category states.
 
15.2   A budgetary provision of Rs 110.50 crore has been made for implementation of Panchayat Yuva
Krida or Khel Abhiyan during 2008-09.  In addition, Rs. 14.50 crore has been provided under NE
Component thus the total provision made for PYKKA during 2008-09 is Rs. 125 crore.  The Secretary
informed the Committee that an amount of Rs. 1500 crores has been approved by the Cabinet for the
Scheme in Eleventh Plan.  He was optimistic that with the launching of the Scheme, it would be possible
to provide universalised access to Sports and sports infrastructure throughout the country over a period
of time.
 
15.3   On a query as to how the villages and blocks would be identified under the Abhiyan, the Secretary
clarified that the scheme would be provided in those Panchayats in which playing grounds were there.
Preference would be given where previous investments have been made but they are not being utilised.  
Central Government would provide Rs. 1.00 lakh to each Panchayat and Rs. 5 lakhs to each Block for
creating playing facilities including grounds.  One of the criteria for selection of village/block Panchayat
would be their ability to mobilise matching amount or more through other resources like State
Government contribution, MPLAD, MLALAD , Backward Region Grant , NREGA assistance, private
contribution etc.   On a query as to how as to why other resources are required when Government
resources are there.  The Secretary clarified that the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance
has advised to take advantage of these schemes which provide labour intensive work to ensure that good
playgrounds are created.   The convergence exercise of the schemes of other Ministries can be done by
way of incentives with a view to create better facilities.   However, all the norms and conditionalities of
each scheme will have to be followed by the local Panchayat.

framework with a multi-pronged approach towards youth empowerment and their participation
