INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, having been authorised by the Committee to 
present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Three Hundred and Sixty Fifth Report of 
the Committee on the Demands for Grants (Demand No.101) of the Ministry of Women and Child 
Development for the year 2025-26.  
2. In accordance with the constitutional requirement under Article 113, the estimated 
expenditure of the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India projected under various 
Demands for Grants for the upcoming financial year needs to be voted by Parliament. As a sequel 
thereto, Demands for Grants of the relevant Ministries/Departments stand referred to concerned 
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee to make a close scrutiny thereof under 
Rule 270 of rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha). 
While making scrutiny of the Demands for Grants, the Committee has made an appraisal of the 
performance, programmes, schemes and policies of the Ministry of Women and Child 
Development vis-a-vis expenditure made out of Consolidated Fund of India in the current 
financial year. 
3. In this Report, a scrutiny of the Budget allocations of the Ministry of Women and Child 
Development as sought under Demand No. 101 for the Financial Year 2025-26 has been made by 
the Committee. 
 
4. During the course of examination of Demands, the Committee heard the views of the 
Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development and other senior officers of the Ministry. 
Inputs from organizations/agencies, namely, National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child 
Development (NIPCCD), Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA), National Commission for 
Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) and National Commission for Women (NCW), were also 
made available to the Committee in its sitting held on 18th February, 2025. 
 
5. The Committee, while making its observations/recommendations, has mainly relied upon 
the following:- 
(i) Detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Women and child Development for the 
year 2025-26; 
(ii) Expenditure Budget 2025-26 and Output Outcome Framework 2025-26; 
(iii) Annual Report 2024-25; 
(iv) Presentations made by the Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development and 
inputs provided by Senior Officers from National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child 
Development (NIPCCD), Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA), National Commission for 
Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) and National Commission for Women (NCW); 
(v) Written replies furnished by the Ministry of Women and Child Development to the 
Questionnaires (Issues and Schemes & Budgetary Allocations) prepared by the committee; and to 
the points/issues raised by Members in the meeting of the Committee, etc. 
 
(ii) 
 

6. The Report is based on facts, figures and submissions (both oral and written) tendered by 
Ministry of Women and Child Development before the Committee. 
7. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the Secretary and officers of the Ministry of 
Women and Child Development as well as Heads/Officers of the organizations/bodies under the 
administrative control of the Ministry of Women and Child Development for appearing before the 
Committee and furnishing the requisite information in connection with the examination of 
Demands for Grants of the Ministry. 
8. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its meeting held on 26th March, 2025 
and presented to both Houses of Parliament on 28th March, 2025. 
9. For the facility of reference and convenience, the observations and recommendations of

1. INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 Women and Children together constitute about 67.7% of the country's population, as per 
2011 Census. Empowerment and protection of women and children and ensuring their 
wholesome development is crucial for sustainable and equitable development of the country. The 
Ministry of Women and Child Development is the apex body of Government of India for 
formulation and administration of regulations and laws related to welfare and development of 
women and children in the country. It came into existence as a separate Ministry with effect from 
30th January 2006. Earlier, it was the Department of Women and Child Development set up in 
the year 1985 under the Ministry of Human Resource Development. The Ministry was 
constituted with the prime intention to address gaps in State action for women and children and 
for promoting inter-ministerial and inter-sectoral convergence to create gender equitable and 
child centered legislation, policies, and programmes.  
1.2 Vision : Empowered women living with dignity and contributing as equal partners in 
development in an environment free from violence and discrimination; and, well-nurtured 
children with full opportunities for growth and development in a safe and protective 
environment. 
Mission – Empowerment of Women: Promoting social and economic empowerment of 
women through cross-cutting policies and programmes, mainstreaming gender concerns, creating 
awareness about their rights and facilitating institutional and legislative support for enabling 
them to realize their human rights and develop to their full potential.  
         Mission – Safe and Secure Childhood: Ensuring development, care and protection of 
children through cross-cutting policies and programmes, spreading awareness about their rights 
and facilitating access to learning, nutrition, institutional and legislative support for enabling 
them to grow and develop to their full potential. 
1.3 The list of the Institutions/ Organizations/ Bodies under the administrative control of the 
Ministry which received support from the Consolidated Funds of India is as under: 
(i) National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD) 

2 
 
(ii) Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA) 
(iii) National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) 
(iv) National Commission for Women (NCW) 
 
 Government has decided to close down Central Social Welfare Board (CSWB), Food and Nutrition 
Board (FNB) and Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) as per recommendations in the report on 
‘Rationalization of Government Bodies’ prepared by the Principal Economic Advisor to the 
Government of India in November, 2020. 
 
1.4 The stated mandate of the Ministry is:  
 Holistic development of Women and Children 
 Formulation of plans, policies, programmes and legislations  
 National Nutrition Policy, National Plan of Action for Nutrition and National Nutrition 
Mission 
 Guiding & coordinating efforts of both governmental organisations and NGOs 
 Ministry’s programmes play a supplementary and complementary role to other 
programmes in health, education, rural development etc.  
 Empowering women both economically & socially to become equal partners in national 
development. 
 
1.5 A brief note on the various Schemes and Bodies under the administrative control of 
the Ministry is as under:  
Saksham Anganwadi and POSHAN 2.0: The schemes of Anganwadi Services, Poshan 
Abhiyaan and Scheme for Adolescent Girls of the erstwhile Umbrella ICDS have been 
revamped and brought under the Umbrella Scheme of Saksham Anganwadi and POSHAN 
2.0. The sub- schemes of the scheme are as under: - 
Anganwadi Services: The scheme provides a package of six services, viz., supplementary 
nutrition, non-formal pre-school education, nutrition & health education, immunization, health 
check-up and referral services.  

3 
 
Poshan Abhiyaan: Poshan Abhiyan was launched by the Hon’ble Prime Minister on 8th March, 
2018. It aims to reduce the level of stunting, under-nutrition, anemia and low birth weight in 
children through use of technology, targeted approach and convergence. It also aims to address 
the issue of malnutrition among adolescent girls, pregnant women and lactating mothers.  
Scheme for Adolescent Girls: The Scheme for Adolescent Girls (14 to 18 years) has been 
revised and addresses the inter-generational problem of malnutrition by focusing on adolescent 
girls keeping in view the life cycle approach.  
Mission Vatsalya: The scheme is being implemented with a view to create a safe and secure 
environment for comprehensive development of children who are in need of care and protection, 
children in conflict with law and other vulnerable children.  
Mission Shakti: The erstwhile scheme of ‘Mission for Protection and Empowerment of 
Women’ has been revamped and brought under the Umbrella Scheme of Mission Shakti from 
the year 2021-22. The Mission Shakti has been divided into 2 sub-schemes as under: - 
SAMBAL: This sub-scheme includes components viz. Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, One Stop 
Centre, Nari Adalat and Women Helpline. 
SAMARTHYA: The sub-scheme includes various components viz Shakti Sadan, Shakhi Niwas, 
Palna, Pradhan Mantri Matru VandanaYojana, National Hub for Women Empowerment, Gender 
Budgeting, Research/ Skilling/ Training/Media Advocacy etc. 
National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD): NIPCCD  
is an autonomous body under the Ministry which conducts research and evaluation studies, 
organizes training programmes, seminars, workshops, conferences, provides information 
services in the field of public cooperation and child development and also caters to the need 
of training and research consultancy.   
Central Adoption Resource Agency (CARA): Central Adoption Resource Authority is a 
statutory body of Ministry of Women & Child Development, Government of India. It 
functions as the nodal body for adoption of Indian children and is mandated to monitor and 
regulate intra country and inter country adoptions.  

4 
 
National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR): The NCPCR was set up 
under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005 to ensure better protection 
of the rights of the child through the monitoring of constitutional and legal rights of children 
and monitoring programmes relating to the survival, welfare and development of children.  
National Commission for Women (NCW) : National Commission for Women is a statutory 
body constituted under National Commission for Women Act 1990. It has the mandate to 
investigate and examine all matters relating to the safeguards provided for women under the 
Constitution and other laws.

2.4 The Committee observes that Ministry has been allocated Rs. 26,889.69 crores 
under BE 2025-26 which is 0.53% of the total budget allocation of the Government of 
India. Further, allocation at BE 2025-26 is 3.05% more than BE 2024-25 and 15.98% more 
than RE 2024-25. The Committee notes that Ministry of Women and Child Development 
caters to 67% population of the country as per the Census 2011, however, allocation of only 
0.53% of the total budget allocation for FY 2025-26, is very meagre amount to facilitate 
implementation of  various schemes and plans. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Ministry to seek more funds from Ministry of Finance at the RE stage so that the 
schemes/plans do not shifted due to shortage of funds. 
2.5 The Committee notes that Ministry was allocated Rs. 26,092.19 crores at BE 2024-
25 which was reduced by 11.14% to Rs. 23,182.98 crores at RE 2024-25, which is Rs. 
1835.48 crores less than the Actual utilization by the Ministry during 2023-24. Moreover, 
the Actual utilization during 2024-25 as on 17.02.2025 is only 18,497.21 crores which is 
70.89% of BE 2024-25 and 79.79% of the RE 2024-25.The Committee feels that in view of 
the inflationary trends in the country, the allocation and Actual utilization should increase 
at least 8% to 10% over the allocation/utilization during previous year to accommodate the 
inflation and to maintain the existing standards for expenditure by Ministry and to avoid 
further decline in actual allocation/expenditure. Further, the Ministry of Finance should be 
taken into confidence so that substantial reduction in allocations at RE stage is avoided in 
order to make the schemes efficacious. 
2.6 The Committee notes that Actual utilization by the Ministry over allocation at RE 
and BE  had been 89.22% and 62.47%, respectively during 2020-21, 91.73% and 87.10%, 
respectively during 2021-22, 98.32% and 93.40%, respectively during 2022-23 and 98.31% 
and 98.31% during 2023-24. However, Actual utilization as on 17th February, 2025 over RE 
and BE 2024-25 has been 79.79% and 70.89%, respectively. In this regard, the Committee 
would like to refer to Rule 56(3) of General Financial Rules (GFR), which states that rush 
to expenditure particularly in the closing months of financial year should be avoided in 
future. As per the guidelines, the last quarter expenditure must be limited to actual 
procurement of goods and services and reimbursement of expenditure already occurred. 
Ministry of Finance's instructions seeks to restrict last quarter expenditure to 33% ceiling 
and last month (March) expenditure to 15% ceiling only.  
In backdrop of the Actual Expenditure by Ministry of Women and Child 
Development during 2024-25 and Rule 56(3) of General Financial Rules (GFR), the 
Committee recommends that the Ministry should adhere to Rule 56(3) of GFR, streamline 
its expenditure during 2025-26 to optimize the utilization of funds allocated to it and to 
avoid piling up of expenditure in the last quarter of the financial year.

3.4 MISSION SAKSHAM ANGANWADI AND POSHAN 2.0 
 
3.4.1 The Ministry has informed that the Saksham Anganwadi (The Anganwadi Services) is 
one of the flagship programmes of Government of India and represents one of the world’s largest 
and unique programmes for early childhood care and development. It is the foremost symbol of 
country’s commitment to its children and nursing mothers, as a response to the challenge of 
providing pre-school non- formal education on one hand and breaking the vicious cycle of 
malnutrition, morbidity, reduced learning capacity and mortality on the other. The Anganwadi 
Services is a universal self-selecting Scheme available to all the beneficiaries who enroll at the 
AWCs. The beneficiaries under this scheme are children in the age group of 6 months to 6 
years, Pregnant Women and Lactating Mothers and Adolescent Girls in the age group of 14 to 18 
years in Aspirational Districts of States and North Eastern Region. 
 
3.4.2 The Ministry has also  informed that earlier known as Umbrella Integrated Child 
Development Services (ICDS) Scheme was launched in 1975, has now been revamped as 
Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 (hereinafter referred to as Saksham Anganwadi) which aim 
to address Government’s commitment to address the challenge of child malnutrition and 
maternal under-nutrition through a strategic shift in nutrition content and delivery and create 
conditions and a convergent eco-system to develop practices that nurture health, wellness and 
immunity. The Scheme seeks to address the challenging situation of malnutrition levels among 
children, pregnant women and lactating mothers and adolescent girls as revealed by initial 
National Family Health Survey (NFHS)-5 findings and optimize the quality and delivery of food 
under the Supplementary Nutrition Program. 
  
Objectives of Mission Poshan 2.0 are as under: 
  
i. To contribute to human capital development of the country; 
ii. Address challenges of malnutrition; 
iii. Promote nutrition awareness and good eating habits for sustainable health and wellbeing; 
and 
iv. Address nutrition related deficiencies through key strategies. 
  
3.4.3 The existing Components of the Scheme have been reorganized under Saksham 
Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0. The primary verticals of the Scheme are as under: 

15 
 
a) Nutrition Support for POSHAN and for Adolescent Girls. 
b) Early Childhood Care and Education [3-6 years]. 
c) Anganwadi Infrastructure including modern, upgraded Saksham Anganwadis; and 
d) Poshan Abhiyan

3.4.9  The Committee was informed that current norms require one Anganwadi center for every 
400-800 persons (with a lower population ceiling for tribal, riverine, desert, hilly, and other difficult 
areas). The Committee was also informed that there are 13.99 lakh AWCs in the country. It 
therefore observes that the current number of Anganwadis, assuming that each serves 800 people, 
would cover only 111.92 crore persons. However, the population of India has grown considerably 
since the last Census in 2011, and the projected population for 2025 as per the Report of the 
Technical Group on Population Projections is almost 141.1 crore persons. Therefore, there may be 
a need for additional Anganwadi centers. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry 
should investigate the need for the same keeping in mind the population projections rather than the 
Census 2011 figures.  
3.4.10 The Committee also notes that AWCs are being established on a population basis. However, 
the Committee notes that to achieve universal primary school education, the RTE mandated a 
geographic criterion that there must be one primary school (Grade I-V) within 1 km of every 
neighborhood. The Committee observes that the goal of achieving universal ECCE, as envisioned in 
the NEP 2020, might require that the Department establish a geographic criterion for setting up 
new AWCs as well. 
3.4.11 In response to a query of  the Committee regarding rationalization of Anganwadi 
Centres in view of the fact that there are Centres where ample number of children are not 
coming into the Anganwadi Centres, the Secretary informed the Committee that this is an 
exercise which the State Governments, after taking local feedback, have to carry out. 
Further, there are about 5,000 Anganwadis which have not opened for even one day in last 
six months and there are several thousand Anganwadis where the number of beneficiaries 
is much less than the potential. In this regard, action needs to be taken and the States are 
the best authorities to do it. The Committee therefore, recommends that since the funding 
is provided by the Union Government, Ministry should enquire into the non-opening of 
5,000 Anganwadis for a single day in last 6 months and  should proactively take care of  the 
issue of rationalization of Anganwadi Centres with the State Governments and ensure that 
all the Anganwadi Centres open daily with their full potential otherwise the purpose for 

17 
 
running the scheme will be defeated. The Committee also recommends the Ministry to keep 
the Committee apprised of the action taken in this regard and the outcome thereof.

3.4.36 Ministry has informed that Mission Poshan 2.0 is a Centrally Sponsored scheme, is 
implemented by the States/UTs in accordance with the guidelines issued by the Government 
from time to time. Under the 15th Finance Commission, various components like Anganwadi 
services, Poshan Abhiyaan and Scheme for Adolescent girls (of 14-18 years in Aspirational 
Districts and North-Eastern region) have been subsumed under the umbrella Mission Saksham 
Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 (Mission Poshan 2.0) to address the challenge of malnutrition. It is a 
Centrally Sponsored mission, where the responsibility for implementation lies with the States. 
This mission is a universal self-selecting umbrella scheme that is being implemented across the 
country including Aspirational districts. Under this scheme, Supplementary Nutrition is provided 
to all beneficiaries Children (6 months to 6 years), Pregnant Women, Lactating Mothers and 
Adolescent Girls (in Aspirational districts and North east regions) to beat the intergenerational 
cycle of malnutrition by adopting a life cycle approach. Supplementary nutrition is provided in 
accordance with the nutrition norms contained in Schedule-II of the National Food Security Act. 
These norms have been revised and upgraded last year. The old norms were largely calorie-
specific; however, the revised norms are more comprehensive and balanced in terms of both 
quantity and quality of supplementary nutrition based on the principles of diet diversity that 
provides quality protein, healthy fats and micronutrients. Fortified rice is being supplied to 
AWCs to meet the requirement of micro-nutrients and to control anaemia among women and 
children. Greater emphasis is being laid on the use of millets for preparation of Hot Cooked Meal 
at least once a week and Take Home ration at Anganwadicenters.However, the food items for 
Supplementary Nutrition (Take Home Ration and Hot Cooked Meals) are specific to the States in 
accordance with the local palate and include locally grown/available wholesome fruits and 
vegetables, cereals, pulses, millets (coarse grains), nuts and fat.

3.5 Mission Vatsalya 
 
3.5.1  Ministry of Women and Child Development is implementing Centrally Sponsored 
Scheme namely ‘Mission Vatsalya’ through the State/ UT Governments on cost sharing basis 
between the Central and the State Governments with effect from 01.04.2022  for delivering 
services for children in difficult circumstances and include institutional care and non-
institutional care services. Mission Vatsalya scheme aims to foster a sensitive, supportive, and 
synchronized ecosystem for children as they transit different ages and stages of their 
development.  Mission Vatsalya scheme provides preventive, statutory care and rehabilitation 
services to children in need of care and protection and those in conflict with law as defined 
under the JJ Act, 2015 (as amended in 2021).  The primary responsibility of implementation of 
the Scheme lies with the States and UTs. The aims of Mission Vatsalya Scheme are as under:- 
(i) Support and sustain Children in difficult circumstances; 
(ii) Develop context-based solutions for holistic development of children from varied 
backgrounds; 
(iii) Provide scope for encouraging innovative solutions; and 
(iv) Cement convergent action. 
The Objectives of Mission Vatsalya Scheme are given as under:- 
(i) Prioritisation of children in the scheme of Administration keeping Centrality of the Child 
during all the activities and actions taken under the Mission. 
(ii) Best interest of the Child while designing or delivering projects and programmes and to take 
affirmative action to ensure right to grow in happy family environment with strong social 
safety net to support families. 

37 
 
(iii) Ensuring Children’s right to Survival, Development, Protection and Participation. 
(iv) To establish essential services and strengthen emergency outreach, non-institutional care 
within the family and community, and institutional care counselling and support services at 
the national, regional, state and district levels. 
(v) To ensure appropriate inter-sector response at all levels, coordinate and network with all 
allied systems to promote convergent efforts for seamless service delivery to children. 
(vi) To strengthen child protection at family and community level, equip families and 
communities to identify risks and vulnerabilities affecting children, create and promote 
preventive measures to protect children from situations of vulnerability, risk and abuse. 
(vii) Encourage private sector partnerships and interventions to support children within the 
framework of law. 
(viii) Raise public awareness, educate public about child rights, vulnerabilities and measures for 
protection sponsored by government and engage community at all levels as stakeholder in 
ensuring the best interest of children. 
(ix) To build capacities of duty holders & service providers at all levels. 
(x) Monitor progress on objective parameters against well-defined Outputs and Outcomes, and 
(xi) Participation of Panchayats and Municipal Local Bodies at the village level and at the ward 
and the urban cluster level within the urban municipal ward, for sustained assessment of the 
issues deserving attention, implementation of appropriate interventions, regular monitoring to 
develop a robust social safety net for children. 
 
 3.5.2 As informed by the Ministry the components of the Mission are as under:- 
  
a) Support for statutory services :(i)    Child Welfare Committees (CWCs), (ii)    Juvenile 
Justice Boards (JJBs), (iii)    District Child protection units (DCPUs),  (iv)   State Child 
Protection Society (SCPS) and  (v)    State Adoption Resource Agencies (SARAs). 
b) Structures for Institutional and Non-Institutional care :These include family and 
community-based care, emergency outreach, counselling and support services at the 
national, regional, state and district levels, for providing support to children in need of 
care and protection and children in conflict with law. 
c) Service Delivery Structures: The State Child Protection Society (SCPS) is responsible 
for situational analysis, planning, convergence and implementation of the scheme in the 
States and UTs, while the State Adoption Resource Agency (SARA) coordinates with the 
Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) and the District Magistrates, for adoption 
related matters.  The District Child Protection Unit (DCPU) assists the District Magistrate 
in ensuring service delivery and taking all necessary measures in the district, for the 
Child in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP). The CWCs are mandated to be established 
(at least one per district) under the JJ Act, 2015, to oversee the best interest of children in 
need of care and protection and take action for their care, protection, treatment, 
development & rehabilitation.  Similarly, the Juvenile Justice Board (at least one per 

38 
 
district) is mandated under the JJ Act, 2015 to hear the cases pertaining to Children in 
Conflict with Law (CCL) and take decision for their treatment and rehabilitation.  
  
d) Institutional Care: The scheme supports Child Care Institutions (CCIs) (Children 
Homes, Specialised Adoption Agencies (SAAs), Open shelters, Observation Homes, 
Special Homes and Place of Safety) for boarding, lodging and comprehensive 
rehabilitation of children in need of care and protection and Children in conflict with law.  
  
Separate homes based on age, gender or special needs of children are established/ 
supported by the State/District, having similar facilities in terms of infrastructure and 
services. The CCIs could be either for fifty (50) children or twenty-five (25) children, 
depending upon the need of children in a particular area.   
  
Specialised Adoption Agencies (SAAs) provides care and protection of ten (10) 
children in the age of zero to six (0-6) years. These SAAs can also be established near 
or within jail premises, to provide care and protection to young children of jailed 
mothers.  
  
The Open shelters provide care and protection to children of working parents requiring 
short time day or night care support.  
  
Under Mission Vatsalya per child per month maintenance grant @Rs.2500/- in SAAs & 
Open Shelters and @Rs.3000/- in other Child Care Institutions (Children Homes, 
Observation Homes, Special Homes and Place of safety) is provided. 
  
e) Non-Institutional Care:  The Scheme provides for Non-Institutional Care of vulnerable 
children by way of Sponsorship, Foster Care and After Care (upto the age of 21 years).  
Rs.4000/ per child per month in Non-Institutional Care (Sponsorship/ foster care/ 
Aftercare) is provided.   
f) Child Helpline:  Child-Help line (1098) is an emergency phone outreach service for 
children in crisis which links them to emergency and long term care and rehabilitation 
services. The service can be accessed by any child in crises or an adult on behalf of/for a 
child, by dialing a four digit toll free number - 1098. Under Mission Vatsalya Scheme, 
Child Helpline is running in coordination with State and District functionaries and 
integrated with the Emergency Response Support System 112 (ERSS-112) helpline of the 
Ministry of Home Affairs.  The Ministry of Women and Child Development has issued 
the Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) to all States/UTs on 31.03.2023. A round the 
clock WCD Control Room (WCD-CR) have been established in all the 36 States/UTs. 
Ministry has approved 762 Child Helpline Units at all the DCPUs.  Child Help Desks 
have also been approved for 135 Railway Stations and 16 Bus Stands. 
g) Mission Vatsalya Portal: As per Guidelines of Mission Vatsalya, a Digital Platform for 
various MIS related to children in difficult circumstances which include missing, 
orphaned, abandoned, and surrendered children has been developed by Ministry of 
Women and Child Development in consultation with States/UTs. The different portals 
under the erstwhile Child Protection Services Scheme and JJ Act, 2015 namely, 
TrackChild portal for Missing/Found Children; ICPS portal for monitoring the scheme 

39 
 
and; Khoya-Paya Citizen centric application for Missing and Sighted Children are being 
integrated under one single portal. The Mission Vatsalya Portal is utilized by the various 
stakeholders namely the SCPS, DCPU, CWC, JJB, CCI, SJPU through their respective 
logins.  
h) SAMVAD: Ministry has collaborated with the National Institute of Mental Health and 
Neurosciences (NIMHANS) which has established a National Initiative and Integrated 
Resource Center for Child Protection, Mental Health, and Psychosocial care named 
SAMVAD (Support Advocacy and Mental Health interventions for Children in 
Vulnerable Circumstances and Distress) in NIMHANS, at Bengaluru. Under 
SAMVAD, total 1,69,106 (till 30.09.2024) stakeholders comprising of law enforcement 
and judicial officers, child protection functionaries, education functionaries, health and 
mental health professionals have been trained and sensitized towards care and protection 
of children in vulnerable circumstances. 
i) Online Training Modules for Child Protection:  Ministry of Women and Child 
Development in partnership with LalBahadurShastri National Academy of 
Administration (LBSNAA) has developed an online training module which is available 
on the KARMAYOGI iGOT platform.  The initiative is for the capacity building of 
multiple stakeholders for strengthening implementation of the JJ Act, 2015.

(a) ONE STOP CENTRE 
3.6.8 One Stop Centre is implemented as Centrally Sponsored Scheme with effect from 01 
April 2015 for supporting women affected by violence and those in distress. The scheme is 
funded by Nirbhaya Fund. One Stop Centre provides 100% financial assistance to the States and 
the Union territories. The following specifications are made under the scheme are as under: 
(i) One Stop Centres can be set up either in pre-existing government buildings or newly 
constructed building; 
(ii) The centres are required to be established within a hospital or medical facility or 
within 2 km from a hospital or medical facility;  
(iii) The scheme aims to set up at-least one One Stop Centre in each district while 
covering all the districts in the country; and 
(iv) One Stop Centre operated with staff of 13 functionaries including Center 
Administrator, Psycho-social Counsellor, Case Worker, Para-medical personnel and 
Paralegal Worker. 
 
3.6.9 The objectives of the OSC is to provide integrated support and assistance to women 
affected by violence and in distress, both in private and public spaces under one roof and to 
facilitate immediate, emergency, and non-emergency access to a range of services to fight 
against any forms of violence against women. The physical progress of the OSC is as under:- 
(i) Total number of approved OSCs – 888 
(ii) Total number of operational OSCs – 802 
(iii) Approved OSC coverage - 785 districts 
(iv) Total women assisted– 10,61,337 women (since inception till 31st December  2024) 
 
3.6.10 The Committee appreciates the objectives of One Stop Centre (OSC) Scheme which 
aims at supporting women affected by violence and those in distress. However, the 
Committee notes that staffs at OSCs are not properly trained. They are being hired on 
contractual basis by State Governments and their salaries are very less. Further, they are 
not being paid salaries on regular basis. Moreover, OSCs are not manned as per the 
population density of respective area/locality. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Ministry should take efforts that OSCs should be manned as per population density of 

46 
 
respective areas/locality. Further, the Ministry should consider enhancing the salaries of 
personnel deployed at OSCs and sensitize the State Governments to disburse their salary 
on regular basis. Moreover, the Ministry should ensure proper training of personnel 
deployed at OSCs for effective implementation of the scheme. 
 
3.6.11 The Committee observes that One Stop Centers (OSCs) and Women Helplines need 
sufficient integration with law enforcement. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Ministry should take the following initiatives: 
(i) AI-Integrated Women’s Helpline: Deploy AI-powered analytics to identify high-risk 
cases and escalate responses to authorities in real time. 
(ii) Expand Mobile One Stop Centers (mOSCs): Introduce mobile OSCs to reach 
underserved areas and provide immediate support for survivors. 
(iii) Integrate Gender Sensitization in Police Training: Make it mandatory for police

(iv) Create Community-Led Safety Networks: Establish women-led neighbourhood 
watch groups trained in self-defence and crisis response.

fast-tracking of the operationalization of the remaining OSCs and their integration with 
WHLs.

(c) Beti Bachao Beti Padhao 
3.6.14 The Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (BBBP) scheme, launched on 22ndJanuary 2015, as a 
collaborative effort between the Ministries of Women & Child Development, Education, and 
Health and Family Welfare, aims to prevent gender-biased sex selection, ensure the survival 
and protection of girl children, and promote their education. Now, the Ministry of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship and Ministry of Minority Affairs have also been added as 
partners with a view to undertake a special drive and awareness programme for promoting 
skilling among girls. The scheme aims to work towards the protection and education of girl 
child through multi-sectoral interventions and encouraging greater spend on activities that have 
on ground impact, e.g., promotion of sports among girls, awareness about PC-PNDT Act, and 
skilling of girls etc. The objectives of the scheme are as under:- 
(i) Improvement in the Sex Ratio at Birth (SRB) by 2 points every year; 
(ii) Improvement in the percentage of institutional deliveries or sustained at the rate of 95% 
or above; 
(iii) 1% increase in 1st Trimester Anti-Natal Care (ANC) Registration per year; 
(iv) 1% increase in enrolment at secondary education level and skilling of girls/women per 
year; 
(v) To check dropout rate among girls at secondary and higher secondary levels; and 
(vi) Raising awareness about safe Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). 
 
3.6.15 The progress made in the scheme is as under:- 
(i) One of the key indicators of the Scheme is monitoring Sex Ratio at Birth (SRB). The 
latest reports of Health Management Information System (HMIS) of Ministry of Health 
&Family welfare (MoHFW) reveal that SRB is showing improving trends and has 
increased from918 in 2014-15 to 930(Provisional) in 2023-24 at national level; and 
(ii) Further, Gross enrolment ratio of girls in the schools at secondary level has increased 
from 75.51 percent in (2014-15) to 78 percent in (2023-24) [as per UDISE-data, MoE]. 
 
(d) Nari Adalat 
3.6.16 Nari Adalat, a component of the "Sambal" sub-scheme under Mission Shakti, is an

dispute resolution, grievance redressal, counselling, evidence-based decision-making, pressure 
group tactics, negotiation, mediation, and reconciliation. Addressing a broad spectrum of 
gender-based issues, the initiative focuses on themes like domestic violence, dowry-related 
disputes, child custody, and workplace gender disparities, intending to raise awareness, provide 
support, and advocate for women's rights. Nari Adalat forms with the formation of Women 
Collectives, comprising members selected from diverse categories, within this collective 
seven/nine individual are designated as Nyaya Sakhi, with one elected as the Mukhya Nyaya 

48 
 
Sakhi to lead and coordinate activities. Regular fortnightly meetings at the panchayat level 
provide platforms for discussion, planning, and action on women-related issues, with 
meticulous records maintained for historical reference. The progress made under the scheme is

(i) Nari Adalat initiative is being run in 50 Gram Panchayats each in the State of Assam and 
UT of Jammu & Kashmir. It is also being piloted in 10 Gram Panchayats each in the state

(ii) Till 31st December 2024, in both the state of Assam and UT of Jammu & Kashmir the

3.6.17 The Committee observes that Nari Adalat scheme which aims at empowering women 
and ensuring justice, offers services such as alternate dispute resolution, grievance 
redressal, counselling, evidence-based decision-making, pressure group tactics, negotiation, 
mediation, and reconciliation etc., is very slow and its implementation is not up to the 
mark. Further, this scheme is not operational in every district of the country. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to review the implementation of Nari 
Adalat scheme and also to ensure that each and every district of the country should have a 
Nari Adalat accessible to women. The Committee also recommends that this scheme should

(a) Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) 
 3.6.19 The Government of India has approved Pan-India implementation of Pradhan Mantri 
Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) with effect from 01.01.2017. PMMVY envisages payment of 
maternity benefits of ₹5,000/- in beneficiary’s bank/post office account linked to her Aadhaar 
Number in Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) mode in three installments. The eligible beneficiary is 
to receive the remaining cash incentive as per approved norms towards Maternity Benefit under 
Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) after institutional delivery so that on an average, a woman gets 
₹6000/-. The scheme is not implementing in Telangana State. The benefit is available to a 
woman for the first two living children provided the second child is a girl. In case of the first 
child the amount of ₹5000 in two installments and for the second child, the benefit of ₹6000 will 
be provided subject to second child is a girl child in one installment after the birth. However, for 
availing benefits for second child, registration during the pregnancy shall be mandatory. This 
would contribute to improve the Sex Ratio at Birth and to prevent female foeticide. 
 

49 
 
3.6.20 The objective of the Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) is to provide 
cash incentive for partial compensation for the wage loss so that the woman can take adequate 
rest before and after delivery of the child; and to promote health seeking behaviour among the 
Pregnant Women & Lactating Mothers (PW&LM).  Providing partial compensation for the wage 
loss in terms of cash incentive so that the woman can take adequate rest before and after delivery 
of the first child. The cash incentive provided would lead to improved health seeking behavior 
amongst the Pregnant Women & Lactating Mothers (PW&LM). Under PMMVY, maternity 
benefit of not less than rupees five thousand will be provided to women aged between the group 
of 18 years and 7 month – 55 years, belonging to socially and economically disadvantaged 
sections of society. However, any additional incentive available under any other scheme such as 
Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) shall continue to be availed. The eligible beneficiaries would 
receive the remaining cash incentives as per approved norms towards maternity benefit under 
Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) after institutional delivery so that on an average, a woman will get 
₹ 6000/-.

3.6.26 The Committee notes that the PMMVY is currently only available to all woman 
aged 18-55 years for their first child, and to women who have a second child only if the 
child is a girl. The Committee was informed that the PMMVY Rules, 2022 were notified 
under sub-section 3 of section 39 of the National Food Security Act, 2013 on 22nd 
December, 2022. The NFSA says that “every pregnant woman and lactating mother shall 
be entitled to…maternity benefit of not less than rupees six thousand, in such instalments 
as may be prescribed by the Central Government.” The Act is therefore unequivocal that 
the maternity benefit is due to every pregnant woman and not only to those pregnant with 
their first child and/or second girl child. The Committee recommends that the Department

not just those with their first child and/or a second a girl child, particularly of women from

3.7.1 The Ministry has informed that a dedicated non-lapsable fund called 'Nirbhaya Fund' has

women in the country. Under the Framework for Nirbhaya Fund, an Empowered Committee 
(EC) of officers has been constituted under chairpersonship of Secretary, Ministry of Women & 
Child Development (MWCD) that appraises and recommends the project proposals of Ministries 
/Departments and state governments for funding under Nirbhaya Fund. After the projects are 
appraised and recommended by the EC, the responsibility of its execution lies on the concerned 
Ministries/ Departments, who after obtaining approval of their respective Competent Financial 

62 
 
Authorities (CFA), get the allocation of funds made in their respective Departmental Budget for

directly implemented by Central Ministries/ Departments; majority of the projects are 
implemented through State Governments/ Union Territory Administrations (UTs) in which the 
nodal Central Ministry/ Department (for example: the Ministry of Home Affairs is the nodal 
Central Ministry for the projects forwarded by the Home/ Police Department of the States/ UTs) 
releases funds to States/ UTs as per prescribed fund-sharing pattern of respective projects/ 
schemes. Thereafter, implementation on ground is done by the States/ UTs in the given 
timelines.

4.0 AUTONOMOUS BODIES 
 
4.1 The National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD) 
 
4.1.1 The National Institute of Public Cooperation and Child Development, popularly known 
as NIPCCD, is an autonomous body and is devoted to promotion of voluntary action, research, 
training and documentation in the overall domain of women and child development. NIPCCD 
conducts research and evaluation studies, organizes training programmes, seminars, workshops, 
conferences, provides information services in the field of public cooperation and child development and 
also caters to the need of training and research. The mission of NIIPCD includes gender sensitization of 
law enforcement agencies, partnership initiatives of Government/social organizations in the area of child 
development, Manpower Development in Social Development sector, Capacity Building of civil 
society organizations, etc. The vision of NIPCCD is to be seen as an Institute of global repute in 
child rights, child protection and child development by developing partnerships and linkages 
with National and International agencies and making its training and research activities relevant 
to the needs of its varying stakeholders. The objectives of the Institute are to:  
i. Develop and promote voluntary action in social development;  
ii. Promote and develop relevant need-based programmes in pursuance of the National Policy 
for Children; 
iii. Evolve measures for coordinating between governmental action and voluntary action in 
social development;  
iv. Develop framework and perspective for organizing children’s programmes through 
governmental and voluntary efforts; and  
v. Establish liaison with international, National and regional agencies, research institutions, 
universities and technical bodies engaged in activities similar to those of the Institute.

4.2 National Commission for Women (NCW) 
4.2.1  The mandate of the Commission is derived from the ‘Statement of Objects and 
Reasons’ appended before the National Commission for Women Act, 1990, it says: “The main 
task of the Commission is to study and monitor all matters relating to the constitutional and 
legal safeguards provided for women, to review the existing legislations and suggest 
amendments, wherever necessary. It will also look into the complaints and take suo-moto notice 
of the cases involving deprivation of the rights of women in order to provide support, legal or 
otherwise, to helpless women. The Commission shall monitor the proper implementation of all 
the legislations made to protect the rights of women so as to enable them to achieve equality in 
all spheres of life and equal participation in the development of the nation.” Under Section 
10(4), the Commission has all the powers of a Civil Court while dealing with the complaints.  
4.2.2  Redressal of grievances and complaints concerning deprivation of women’s 
rights and non-implementation of laws enacted to safeguard their rights is one of the important 
activities undertaken by the Commission. Complaints are received in writing, through online 
mode i.e., www.ncw.nic.in, suo-moto cognizance on the basis of print, electronic and social 
media reports or information received by the Commission from any other source. The 
Commission, while handling/processing complaints, leverages its association with the State

4.3 The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) 
4.3.1 The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) has been constituted 
by the Government of India, under the Commission for Protection of Child Rights (CPCR) 
Act,2005 to exercise and perform the powers and functions assigned to it under CPCR 
Act,2005.The commission consists of the following members namely, a chairperson who, is a 

77 
 
person of eminence and has done outstanding work for promoting the welfare of children and Six 
members, out of which at least two are woman, are appointed by the Central Government from 
amongst person of eminence, ability, integrity, standing and experience in Education; Child 
health, care, welfare or child development, Juvenile justice or care of neglected or marginalized 
children or children with disabilities; Elimination of child labour or children in distress; Child 
psychology or sociology; and Laws relating to children. the functions of the National 
Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) under the CPCR Act, 2005 are to: 
(i) Examine and review laws related to child rights and recommend effective 
implementation measures. 
(ii) Present annual and periodic reports to the Central Government on the status of 
child rights safeguards. 
(iii) Investigate violations of child rights and recommend necessary legal actions. 
(iv) Address issues affecting children impacted by violence, natural disasters, 
HIV/AIDS, trafficking, abuse, and exploitation. 
(v) Recommend measures for children in distress, conflict with law, or without 
family, including children of prisoners. 
(vi) Review international treaties and recommend their effective implementation for 
children's welfare. 
(vii) Promote research and spread awareness about child rights through publications, 
media, and seminars. 
(viii) Inspect juvenile homes and institutions housing children and suggest remedial 
actions if required. 
(ix) Handle complaints or take suo moto notice of child rights violations, non-
implementation of laws, or hardship mitigation failures. 
(x) Analyse laws and policies for compliance with the Convention on the Rights of 
the Child and provide feedback on proposed legislation. 
(xi) Conduct formal investigations upon concerns raised by children or others on their 
behalf. 
(xii) Promote the inclusion of children's voices in decision-making processes. 
(xiii) Compile and analyse data on children for informed policymaking. 
(xiv) Advocate for incorporating child rights education into school curricula and 
personnel training programs.

4.4 Child Adoption Resource Authority (CARA)   
 
4.4.1 Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) is a Statutory Body constituted by the 
Government of India under Section 68 of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) 
Act, 2015 (as amended in 2021) and is working under the aegis of the Ministry of Women & 
Child Development, Government of India. It functions as the Nodal Body for adoption of 
Indian children and is mandated to facilitate in-country adoptions and regulate inter-country 
adoptions in the country. CARA is designated as the Central Authority of India to deal with 
inter-country adoptions in accordance with the provisions of the Hague Convention on Inter-
country Adoption, 1993, ratified by the Government of India in 2003. It primarily deals with 
adoption of orphan, abandoned, and surrendered children through its associated/ recognised 
adoption agencies. CARA is facing the following challenges to implement the adoption 
programme. The mandate of CARA as defined in Section 68 of Juvenile Justice (Care and 
Protection of Children) Act, 2015 (as amended in 2021) is as under:- 
 
a) To promote in-country adoptions and to facilitate Inter-State adoption in coordination 
with State Agency 
b) To regulate inter-country adoptions 
c) To frame regulations on adoption and related matters from time to time as may be 
necessary 
d) To carry out the functions of the Central Authority under the Hague Convention on 
Protection of Children and Cooperation in respect of in inter-country adoption. 
e) Any other function as may be prescribed.

4.4.4 The Ministry has informed that while adoption procedure has been streamlined under the JJ 
Act 2015 with online application system (CARINGS), the Ministry has come across grievances 
mostly due to long waiting list of Prospective Adoptive Parents. No of people wanting to adopt is 
much more than legally free children available for adoption. Moreover parents want to adopt 
younger children only thus leaving behind a large number of older children. With new guidelines 
on Foster care and development of Foster Care Module on CARINGS, efforts are being made to 
find families for older children through foster care route. Hon’ble Supreme Court of India vide 
its order dated 20/11/2023 directed States/UTs to carry out identification drive every two months 
in the category of orphan, abandoned, surrendered, no-visitation and unfit guardian. As result, 
14468 total numbers of children (mostly older children) have been identified in the category of 
orphan, abandoned, surrendered, no-visitation and unfit guardian. A separate Identification Cell 
has been set-up by CARA which is coordinating with the States to ensure determination of legal 
status of all such children and their early rehabilitation in adoption and foster care as the case 
may be. A number of older children have been identified for adoption, foster care and foster 
adoption. Since older children have long been in the institutional care counseling of such 
children is important and in this regard CARA is coordinating with the States and other 
stakeholders to ensure mandatory counseling in case of all such children. Although it is difficult 
to place such children in adoption, efforts have been made for their early placement in adoption 
after determining health status of such children through Chief Medical Officers (CMOs). It has 
been observed that the level of Child Welfare Committees are taking longer than the stipulated 
time for declaring their legal status for various reasons. CWC members are informed by CARA 
to expedite in each pending case and in this regard CARA officials are holding regular meetings 
with the stakeholders. CWC Members have also been given login Ids on CARINGS portal for 
them to be able to see pendency at their respective levels.

infants and young children. However, the time depends on the selection of preferences - region, 
age group, gender, category, seniority, and availability of children in the selected criteria by the 
PAPs. 
 
4.4.6  The Committee was informed that there are two systems of adoption prevalent in 
the country - one through CARA, and the other through the Hindu Adoption and 
Maintenance Act (HAMA). The Committee was notified that currently, it takes about 3.5 
years for a prospective parent to adopt a child. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Ministry/CARA as under: 
(i) The Committee recommends a revisiting of current protocols for family placement of 
OAS children, as well as a review of the existing dual system of adoption laws to plug any 
loopholes in the same; 

84 
 
(ii) Fast-Track Special Needs Adoptions: Ministry/CARA should provide financial and 
legal support for parents adopting children with disabilities or health challenges 
(iii)Expand Post-Adoption Support Services: Ministry/CARA should introduce mental 
health counseling, peer support groups, and legal assistance for adoptive families; 
(iv)Streamline Inter-Country Adoptions: Ministry/CARA should reduce red tape and align 
with international best practices to increase the number of successful adoptions; and 
(v)Awareness amongst public on legal adoption: Ministry/CARA should leverage social 
media to spread awareness and extensive training and outreach activities with respect to 
adoption. 
4.4.7 The Committee also expresses its concerns that instances of direct adoptions and

violates the legal frameworks in place but also puts the children at significant risk. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry/CARA to take effective steps to stop 
such instances of direct adoptions and the sale and purchase of children from biological 
parents.

Rs. 28.00 crores and the Actual utilization as on 17th February, 2025 is Rs. 27.54 crores. 
NCW has been allocated Rs. 28.00 crores at BE 2025-26 which is lower than Actuals of 
2022-23 and 2023-24. The Committee recommends the Ministry to allocate more funds to 
NCW in view of inflationary trends in the country. The Committee also desires that 
Ministry should furnish the reasons and source of Actual utilization 2023-24 higher than 
RE allocation for that year. 
(Para 4.2.6) 
99 The Committee observes that NCW has been entrusted with duties of providing 
constitutional and legal safeguards for women, to review the existing legislations and 
suggest amendments, wherever necessary, to look into the complaints and take suo-motu 
notice of the cases involving deprivation of the rights of women in order to provide support, 
legal or otherwise, to helpless women. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
Ministry should ensure following at the earliest to enhance the efficiency of the 
Commission: 
(i) Strengthening and popularising 24X7 Helpline developed by NCW; 
(ii) Providing more focus to public awareness on women’s rights through seminars, 
workshops and consultations; 
(iii)Frequent meetings with State Women Commissions to ensure effective functioning 
in collaboration with NCW;  
(iv)  Filling up of vacant posts in the Commission and State Women Commissions; and  

116 
 
(v) Ministry should proactively take up with Ministry of External Affairs for Mutual 
Legal Assistant Treaty with the remaining countries for safeguarding the interest of 
women in respect of NRI marriages. 
(Para 4.2.8) 
100 The Committee notes that out of total sanctioned strength of 64, there are 35 posts 
lying vacant in NCW which is 54.69% of the total strength. The Committee also takes note 
of the submission of the Ministry that all the posts in NCW are filled on deputation basis, 
the category-wise break-up under Women/Third Gender/SC/ST /OBC/EWS and PWD 
workforce is, therefore, not maintained. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Ministry/NCW to fill up the vacant posts by regular/permanent appointments in a time-
bound manner latest by December, 2025 to ensure proper and efficient functioning of the 
Commission. The Committee further recommends that NCW should appoint own 
personnel on regular/permanent basis instead of deputation/Foreign Service basis, so that, 
continuity in functioning of the Commission is ensured. 
(Para 4.2.11) 
101 The Committee takes note of the contractual appointments and appointments on 
daily wages basis in NCW and observes that a number of persons are working on 
contractual and daily wages basis in the Commission for more than 20 years. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that contractual appointments and appointments on 
daily wages should be stopped and the Ministry should consider to regularize those who 
are working there for more than 10 years on contractual and daily wages basis and all 
facilities associated with permanent appointees should be extended to them. 
(Para 4.2.13) 
102   The Committee observes that NCW plays a pivotal role in advocating women’s rights, 
but it faces gaps in legal outreach, enforcement, and awareness campaigns. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends the Ministry/NCW as under: 
(i) Expand Legal Aid Networks: Ministry/NCW should establish dedicated legal aid 
cells in each district to assist women facing gender-based violence; 
(ii) Create a National Gender Justice Index: Ministry/NCW should develop a ranking 
system for states based on gender equity and women’s rights enforcement; 
(iii)Strengthen Corporate Engagement: Ministry/NCW should collaborate with private 
companies for workplace gender sensitization and equal pay audits; and 
(iv) Increase Digital Outreach: Ministry/NCW should launch AI-powered chatbots to 
guide women on their legal rights and available support mechanisms. 
(Para 4.2.14) 
 
103  The Committee acknowledges the Reports of the National Commission on Women 
from its enquiry visits to Manipur.  The reports note that women are the worst affected in a 
crisis, and many instances of violence against women are not being reported due to the fear 
of social stigma. Further, the NCW Report shares that in a meeting with women 

117 
 
associations and student federations, “they narrated their loss of faith and confidence in 
the system.”In such a situation, the Committee recommends that the NCW stay apprised of 
issues relating to law and order in Manipur and proactively ensure that the concerns of 
women are registered and acted upon by the relevant authorities. 
(Para 4.2.15) 
104 The Committee notes that the Reports of the NCW from its consultations in Manipur 
consistently emphasized the need for a women-led peacebuilding and cooperation effort. The 
Committee notes that the NCW Northeast Cell’s activities has largely been focused on other states 
in the Northeast during the last two years, but that its resources can be leveraged to support 
peacebuilding and conflict resolution between local communities in Manipur. 
(Para 4.2.16) 
 
The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) 
105   The Committee notes that BE 2025-26 for NCPCR is Rs. 25.00 crores which is slightly higher 
than BE 2024-25 of Rs. 24.79 crores and far lower than Actual utilization during 2023-24 by 
NCPCR which is Rs 34.85 crores. The Actual utilization as on 17th February, 2025 is Rs. 22.66 
crores against the RE 2024-25 of Rs. 23.63 crores. The Committee observes that there is 
inconsistent allocation and utilization and recommends that the Ministry should appropriately 
allocate funds to NCPCR and ensure optimum utilization in view of the fact that it is apex statutory 
body for protection of child rights in the country.  
(Para 4.3.3) 
106 The Committee takes note of the number of grievances received and closed in the 
Commission during 2022-23 and 2023-24. The Committee observes that closure/ disposal of 
grievances is far lower than number of grievances received by the NCPCR. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends NCPCR to evolve effective mechanism for speedy 
disposal of grievances received in the Commission and to reduce pendency in a time-bound 
manner. 
(Para 4.3.5) 
107  The Committee observes that the NCPCR is responsible for monitoring child rights 
and ensuring the effective implementation of child protection laws. However, gaps in 
outreach, inadequate tracking of complaints and limited enforcement capacity reduce its 
effectiveness. The Committee, therefore, recommends as under: 
(i) Establish a National Child Rights Dashboard: Ministry/NCPCR should establish a 
real-time digital platform for tracking cases of child rights violations, with geo-
tagging of complaints and AI-driven case prioritization; 
(ii) Strengthen Child Protection Committees: Ministry/NCPCR should ensure every 
district has an active committee that conducts quarterly reviews of child rights 
cases; 
(iii)Increase Legal Aid for Child Rights Cases: Ministry/NCPCR should partner with 
legal aid groups to provide pro bono services for cases of abuse and exploitation; 

118 
 
and 
(iv) Mandatory Child Impact Assessments: Ministry/NCPCR should ensure that all 
policy decisions impacting children undergo an NCPCR-led impact assessment 
before implementation. 
 
(Para 4.3.6) 
108 The Committee notes that there are 32 vacancies against the total sanctioned administrative 
and non-administrative posts of 36, which is around 88% of total sanctioned strength. The 
Committee also notes that only 5 persons of permanent appointments of administrative and non-
administrative staff have been done in NCPCR since 2019 to 31st December, 2024. The Committee 
surprised to note that NCPCR has mentioned that there was no such hindrance as the 
targeted programme and activities were carried out with the assistance of available regular 
staff and by engagement of contractual employees including retired government employees. 
In this regard, the Committee would like to refer to the data provided by NCPCR 
regarding pending grievances at Para 4.3.4 and recommends that vacancies of 
administrative and non-administrative staffs should be filled up in time-bound manner 
latest by December, 2025. 
(Para 4.3.10) 
109 The Committee takes note of contractual appointments in NCPCR since 2019 till 
31st December, 2024 mentioned at Para 4.3.7 above and recommends that contractual 
appointments in NCPCR should be stopped in view of the fact that it undermines the rights 
of SC, ST, OBC, EWS and PwD for reservation in Government jobs as provided in the 
Constitution of India. 
(Para 4.3.11) 
Child Adoption Resource Authority (CARA)  
110 The Committee notes that BE 2024-25 for CARA was Rs. 11.40 crores which were 
increased to Rs. 23.47 crores at RE stage. However, the Actual utilization as on 17th 
February, 2025 is Rs. 25.95 crores which is higher than RE 2024-25. The Committee 
further notes that allocation at BE 2025-26 of Rs. 14.49 crores  is around 45% lower than 
Actuals of 2024-25 as on 17thFebruray, 2025. The Committee recommends that Ministry 
should ensure allocation to CARA at least at par with actual utilization during the previous 
year i.e. 2024-25. The Committee also desires that the Ministry/CARA should furnish the 
reasons and source of Actual utilization for 2024-25 which is higher than the RE allocation 
for that year. 
 
(Para 4.4.3) 
 
111  The Committee was informed that there are two systems of adoption prevalent in 
the country - one through CARA, and the other through the Hindu Adoption and 

119 
 
Maintenance Act (HAMA). The Committee was notified that currently, it takes about 3.5 
years for a prospective parent to adopt a child. The Committee, therefore, recommends the 
Ministry/CARA as under: 
(i) The Committee recommends a revisiting of current protocols for family placement of 
OAS children, as well as a review of the existing dual system of adoption laws to plug any 
loopholes in the same; 
(ii) Fast-Track Special Needs Adoptions: Ministry/CARA should provide financial and 
legal support for parents adopting children with disabilities or health challenges 
(iii)Expand Post-Adoption Support Services: Ministry/CARA should introduce mental 
health counseling, peer support groups, and legal assistance for adoptive families; 
(iv)Streamline Inter-Country Adoptions: Ministry/CARA should reduce red tape and align 
with international best practices to increase the number of successful adoptions; and 
(v)Awareness amongst public on legal adoption: Ministry/CARA should leverage social 
media to spread awareness and extensive training and outreach activities with respect to 
adoption. 
 
(Para 4.4.6) 
 
112 The Committee also expresses its concerns that instances of direct adoptions and 
the sale and purchase of children from biological parents have come to light. This not only 
violates the legal frameworks in place but also puts the children at significant risk. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry/CARA to take effective steps to stop 
such instances of direct adoptions and the sale and purchase of children from biological 
parents. 
(Para 4.4.7) 
113 The Committee observes that there are 23.8% posts of Group A, 85.71% posts of 
Group B, 33.33% posts of Group C are lying vacant in CARA. The total vacancies in 
CARA is 37.83% as mentioned at Para 4.4.8 above. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends the Ministry/CARA to fill-up all the vacancies including the backlog 
vacancies in a mission mode by regular/permanent appointments at the earliest latest by 
December, 2025. 
(Para 4.4.13) 
 
114 The Committee takes note of the contractual appointments at Para 4.4.9 above and 
recommends that contractual appointments in statutory bodies like CARA should be 
stopped as it violets the constitutional provision of reservation in Government jobs to SC, 
ST, OBC, EWS and PwD etc. 

120 
 
(Para 4.4.14) 
115 The Committee was informed by the Ministry that some of the reasons for delay in OAS 
children being declared legally free for adoption are –  
a. Non-fulfilment of Child Welfare Committee’s quorum 
b. Acute shortage of staff at district and state level authorities 
c. Non disbursement of salary to the staff in a timely manner 
d. Lack of proper training to the staff 
The Committee observes that these are issues that can be solved by the Ministry and States 
and encourages them to take proactive measures to solve the same. 
(Para 4.4.15)
