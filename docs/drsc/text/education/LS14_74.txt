PREFACE

I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Human Resource Development, having been authorised by the Committee to present the
Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Seventy-fourth Report of the Committee on
Vocational Education.

2. The Committee in this connection appointed a Sub-Committee under the convenor-
ship of Dr. M. Aram in its meeting held on 16th January, 1997. A new Sub-Committee was
constituted on 4th June, 1997 under the Convenorship of Shri V. Kishore Chandra S. Deo to
look into the various aspects of Vocational Education for making an in-depth study on the
subject and to present a Report thereon to the Committee at an early date.

from the Department of Education, Ministry of Human Resource Development and also
heard oral evidence of the Officials of that Department on the subject and got the first hand
information during its visits.

15th September, 1998. The Committee at its meeting held on 19th November, 1998 decided
to present the Report in the House during the Winter Session of Parliament.

REPORT
1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The Kothari Commission focussed its attention on breaking the barrier between the
“World of Learning’ and the ‘World of Work’ and to bring one as close to the other as
possible. Recognising the importance of Vocational Education, the National Policy on
Education, 1986, launched a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Vocationalisation of Secondary
Education in February, 1988. The main objectives of the scheme are:—

(a) To bridge the gap between the ‘World of School’ and the ‘World of Work’;
(b) To enhance individual employability;

(c) To reduce mismatch between demand and supply of skilled man power by pre
qualified middle level man-power superior to skilled workers;

(d) To provide an alternative to those pursuing higher education without any aim or
purpose and to direct the energies of youth towards constructive purposes;

(e) To bring out the importance of working usefully with one’s hands and imbibe the
concept of dignity of labour; and

(f) To reduce the number of drop-outs, failures and frustration in students.

1.2 The National Policy on Education (NPE) — 1986 had fixed the target of diverting
10% of students at the +2 level to the Vocational stream by 1990 and 25% by 1995. The
revised policy i.e. programme of Action (POA) — 1992 revised the target of diversion to
10% by 1995 and 25% by 2000 AD. The Department in its reply stated that even though the
scheme was launched in February, 1988, it was operationalised in actual terms during
1989-90. Therefore the target was revised to 10% by 1995 and -5% by 2000 AD. The
Department further stated that the target was achieved in terms of capacity sanctioned for
diversion of students at +2 level. However the findings based on All India Evaluation
Report on Vocational Education states that about 5% diversjon of students at the +2 level
against the 10% of NPE target has been achieved.

1.3 The Committee while looking into the disbursal of funds of different States observed
that funding has been most irregular in certain cases. The statement showing year-wise and
State-wise details of funds released is at Annexure I. From the Statement, the Committce
has noticed that many States are not being released sufficient funds. Also some States show
little interest in claiming for more funds and for implementing the programme. The
Committee has also been given to understand that some States have not been released funds
because of non-submission of audit reports. The Committee was also given to understand
that the scheme has not been given proper attention in North-Eastern States as well.

1.4 The Committee during its visits was further informed that due to strict guidelines
given by the Centre on the mode of expenditure, there is limited autonomy to function in
regard to expenditure to be incurred. It was stated that sufficient autonomy was not given to
divert funds to other areas within Vocational Education which were of urgent necessity. The
Committee during its visit found that the Management structures have remained weak in
many areas.

1.5 The Department informed that there are about 150 Vocational Courses introduced
in six broad areas, namely, Agriculture, Business and Commerce, Engineering and
Technology, Health and Para-Medical Services, Home Science and Humanities and Others
(List at Annexure II). The Committee observed that the selection of schools for starting

1



Vocational Education is done on the basis of District Vocational Surveys. But the
Committee was constrained to note that till now (As per the information collected from the
Department in November, 1997) only 166 District Vocational Surveys have been conducted
in the Country and out of the 25 States of the Country, only 6 States, such as Andhra
Pradesh (23), Himachal Pradesh (12), Madhya Pradesh (45), Rajasthan (31), Tamil Nadu
(19) and Uttar Pradesh (31) and National Capital Territory of Delhi (5) have conducted the
District Vocational Surveys. However in rest of the States/UTs these surveys had not been
carried out as has been pointed out by the All India evaluation Report on Vocational
Education. Due to this the Schools were selected at random in both rural and urban areas.
The Committee was also informed that the number of courses listed is very less and is
required to be broad based. The Committee was further informed that there still remained a
wide gap between the enrolment of boys and girls, especially in the field of Engineering and
Technology and Health Courses.

1.6 The Committee was given to understand that the link between the Industry and
Vocational Education is the weakest one. There still existed a mismatch between the
industry’s need and the products of the educational institutions and Vocational Schools.
Educational inputs are mostly outdated and not relevant, not to speak of quality of
institutions and their outputs. Consequently, industry is unable to absorb the products of the
institutes and a large number of educated youths remain unemployed.

1.7 The representatives of the Industrial Associations informed the Committee that in
order to stimulate private sector participation, there is a need for adopting the “German
dual System” of Vocational Education. Under this system, firstly, Vocational training is
carried out simultaneously with school studies and secondly, every trainee is sponsored by an
industrial unit, with a contractual obligation by the student to work for that industrial unit
for a certain period after completion of training. This model could be operative in India also
with a close coordination between the industry and the institutes, they felt. The Committee
has also been given to understand that the courses have more of a theoretical base rather
than of the practical base.

1.8 The Committee during its visits was informed by some of the schools that the
Vocational training for certain specific courses need not have to be exclusively at the Higher
Secondary level. Instead of being taught after the 10th standard, it could be lowered down
to upper primary level. The trainees in the field of carpentry, plumbing etc. need not be
highly qualified. What is necessary here is perfection of skills. The Committee has also taken
note of the fact that the school drop-outs are an important chunk of student population who
remain out side schools. According to statistics, drop-out rates in 1995-96 was 38.95% in
classes I—V and 52.26% in classes I—VIII.

1.9 The Committee while getting the first hand information during its visits came to
know that the vocational courses are being used as launching pad for vertical mobility. The
Committee was given to understand that one of the weak areas of the Vocational Education
Programme is the shortage of trained teachers and field experts.

1.10 The Committee while discussing with the NGOs and others during its visits was
informed that special attention is required to be provided to women, handicapped children
and spastics and above all to rural people. It was also stated that there is a need for a
humanitarian consideration to be given for the orphans and street children while enrolling
them in the Vocational Education.

1.11 The Apprenticeship Act 1961 was amended in 1986 to include pass-outs of


3

vocational courses at the +2 level. The All India evaluation Report found that there is
limited interaction between Board of Apprenticeship Training (BOAT) and State
Directorates. This is leading to under utilisation of seats for apprenticeship training by
vocational passouts.

1.12 The Committee was informed that a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Pre-
Vocational Education was launched in 1993-94, with the objective of imparting training in
simple marketable skills to the students of classes IX & X. The Committee has however
been informed that many States are still taking preparatory actions and the results have not
started flowing in as yet.

Il. OBSERVATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

2.1 The Committee emphasises that vocational education has very ancient roots in our
country. There used to be ‘craft schools’ in villages for training in crafts. The Committee
appreciates the emphasis given to vocational education by various Commissions and the
National Policy on Education 1986. Even though all these efforts culminated into launching
the scheme in 1988, yet the scheme has not made much headway so far. The‘Committee is
constrained to note that the scheme has not been given due weightage and has also not been
implemented to the extent it should have been done.

2.2 The Committee is deeply anguished to note that only 5% of the targets fixed has
been achieved. The Committee is of the view that even if this figure is to be relied on, there
remains another 20% to be covered by 2000 AD for diversion of students at +2 level which
stands as a tough task ahead. The Committee therefore strongly recommends that the
Department should make serious efforts to give a meaningful thrust to the Scheme. The
Committee also feels that the programme needs to be totally re-oriented.

2.3 The Committee is also not happy to note that inspite of 9 years of implementation
of the Programme, the need and area based approach has not yet been strictly followed. The
Committee recommends that community participation and need and area based approach
must be followed to accelerate the process of diversion of students at the +2 level so as to
achieve the target.

2.4 The Committee is pained to note that the programme has not received adequate
priority in most of the States. There is also a considerable time-lag between release of grants
from the Centre and its disbursement to the schools. The Committee is of the view that
when no response is received from State Governments on yearly basis, it should be followed
up and accountability fixed. The Committee was given to understand that funds are
generally released to reimburse the expenditure made, which is a major hurdle in the
successful implementation of the scheme. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
funds be released in advance on six monthly basis.

2.5 The Committee finds that in many a cases, the Central assistance released to
Vocational education is used by the States as a ways and means expenses andr diverted for
the purposes other than the ones for which the assistance was released. The Committee feels
that this is not a healthy trend. The Committee is of the considered view that this problem
can be tackled if separate funds are earmarked for Vocational Education by the Planning
Commission and it is impressed upon the State Govts. that non-utilisation of funds for
Vocational Education would be treated as misappropriation for which the concerned State
Government would be held responsible. The Committee also recommends that for proper
monitoring, there should be a Joint Monitoring Mechanism which would have

4

representatives from Central Government, State Government concerned, members of
Parliament of that particular State and MLAs of that area to ensure successful
implementation of the Scheme.

2.6 As the scheme of Vocational Education is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme and funds
allocated depend on proposals received from the States, the Committee is of the view that
sufficient awareness is required to be created in this regard. The Committee is also of the
view that proper initiative is lacking on the part of the Central Government due to which the
problems are arising. The Committee stresses that the Department should take all necessary
steps to activate the State Directorates and increase awareness among the State about the
utility and importance of the Scheme.

2.7 The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction over the neglect of District Vocational
Surveys. These surveys are an important ingredient, because the need and are based
approach of the Programme depends mainly thereon. The yearly District Vocational Survey
should be made compulsory and community participation in it should be encouraged so that
assessment of man-power requirements of the area, range of available occuptions, trend of
emerging vocations etc, are properly recognised. Without this, it is difficult to introduce area
and need based courses,

2.8 The Committee finds that the courses that are offered are generally not very
popular in many of the States. If out dated courses are offered, the people would have very
limited choice. Similarly, if a course which is not suitable to an area is introduced, students
would not be attracted by such course. It is therefore, essential that the programme should
include the courses which are useful in the tertiary sector. The Committee, is however,
constrained to note that the approach of the Department is very peripheral in this regard.
There are many examples such as plumbing, carpentry, agriculture related skills, pump set
repairs, para medical course and many other new courses which can be introduced on
requirement. There is a tremendous scope and demand for such skills, particularly in the
tural areas. The Department should keep in mind the fact that the vocational education
programme is meant for improving the skills of the people who are not able to go for higher
education, rather than making people engineers/rchitectscientists etc.

2.9 The Committee is of the view that unless the gap is bridged in regards to enrolment
of girls and boys, the country would be losing the talent of the major chunk of its
population. Hence the Committee recommends that steps must be taken to encourage more
and more number of girls to be enrolled in Vocational Education in general and in
Engineering and Technology and health Courses in particular.

2.10 The Committee feels that there is a necessity on the part of the Vocational Schools
to formulate updated packages which must be multifunctional through tradesSpecialised skill
from secondary school levels. There is also necessity of a placement cell to establish,
maintain and disseminate placement status record at the school and provide a sense of
direction to the students. The Principals of the Vocational Schools and Heads of the
tespective Departments should’ be in constant liaison with sector specific industry personneY
representative deputed by the collaborating industry.

2.11 The Committee further recommends that industrial sector should be involved in
curriculum preparation and renewal. Demonstration of basic skills should be done by
industrial personnel also. Faculty visits to the industry for specialised skill training,
nominating an industrial representative for the monitoring and inspection team, deputation
of the external examiner from the industryinvolvement of industry in evaluating students
performance, on-the-job training provision for about 4—6 weeks, apprenticeship training for

one year on completion of the course (based on selection criteria) by the collaborating
industry and above all a profile of job requirements by the industry for the purpose of
placement to be provided by the collaborating industries and their recommendation for
absorption in organisations located elsewhere, are the aspects which should be seriously
considered in collaboration with the industry. The Committee, however, feels that as there is
a lack of proper planning in this regard, there is an urgent need for Perspective Planning.
Not only this, efforts should also be made to assimilate the agro-based industries in the
curriculum of vocational education, so as to make the task of achieving the goals easier.

2.12 Looking at the present scenario, the Committee feels that there is a need to devise
a system of imparting vocational education which while building a sound theoretical base
could also ensure connectivity to the market and job security.

2.13 The Committee is of the opinion that since skill is a major requirement for
vocational education, the 10th standard as essential qualification can be brought down to
class 8th. The Committee is constrained to note that about 52.26% of the children drop out
in classes I—VIII (As per the 1995-96, figures). Many of those drop-outs are quite
vulnerable and indulge in undesirable activities. This manpower can be utilised for
constructive purpose if education is made attractive and relevant. Vocational education is the
best alternative. The parents will also be interested in sending their children to schools
because of the usefulness of courses. The Committee expresses its concern over the school
Drop-Outs who form a major chunk of the rural masses and urban poor and desires that
special courses and literature be developed for this target group. In the Rural Areas, there
can be greater need for multidisciplinary courses like agriculture-related Vocational subject
which include repairing of Pump sets, agricultural machinesimplements etc. Similarly
fisheries can also be given due attention. Small trades like TV Mechanic and Radio can also
generate employment in rural areas and these courses can also be incorporated under the
Scheme. The Committee is of the view that in Urban areas, there are greater number of
Industries and job facilities and the Industrial units should be linked to the courses offered
which'can take into account the requirements of the Industrial units. If this is done, larger
number of students including drop-outs can be accommodated after the completion of their
courses in the jobs of the Industrial units. The Committee therefore recommends that such
trades be considered for differential needs of Urban and Rural areas.

The Committee in this connection takes note of the Pre-Vocational Course which is
being implemented. But it is going at a snail’s pace and is required to be accelerated. This
scheme may also be slightly modified to establish proper linkage with the main scheme.

2.14 Similarly, many students who are good at practical and vocational courses may be
weak in the regular subjects such as language, mathematics, science etc. Such students may
not be able to pass out the regular examination to get the school certificate. It is therefore,
necessary that a separate certificate for vocational course is given to enable such students to
get gainful employment. In case a student could not complete the other subjects but secured
good marks in Vocational papers, another year of practical training may be given and a
certificate in vocational course could be given to him. There could also be two levels of
vocational certificate course. The lower level should cover the students who take up
Vocational Education after class VII or class VIII and Higher level certificate could be given
to those who take up Vocational Education after class X. The certificate from Vocational
Education centres would act as a proof of acquired knowledge and skill. There can be a
separate Institute or Board for issuing the certificate for vocational education. The certificate
can be a recognised one just like the ITI certificate.

“2.15 The Committee is also constrained to note that lack of trained teachers is one of



6

the weakest links. The Committee desires that the States should be asked to give priority to
teachers’ training as this programme cannot be successful without trained teachers. The
Committee is also concerned on the lack of willingness on the part of the old teaching staff to
accommodate new courses which stands as a stumbling block in the progress of Vocational
Education. Students would be attracted towards Vocational Education only when new and up-
to-date courses are offered. The Committee feels that there is an urgency of Re-oricntation
Training for existing teachers to adapt themselves to new courses and also induction of more
new teachers for updated courses. However, the Committee feels that an element of
indecisiveness with regard to continuance of this Scheme as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme
persists. This has created a sense of skepticism in the minds of the State authorities
implementing this scheme. That is why teachers taken under this scheme are given only an
adhoc appointment. In this respect, the Committee is of the view that once the teachers are
appointed on a permanent basis they would be actively involved in the training programmes
offered. In this way the Teachers training would be more meaningful. The Committee is of the
opinion that by this course of action, the unemployment problem can be solved to some
extent.

2.16 The Committee is constrained to note that even if seats are reserved for the Dalits
and other deprived sections in Vocational Institutes many of them are not in a position to
finally clear the courses offered. The Committee also observes that in Rural areas, due to poor
infrastructural facilities and lack of other developmental facilities many a students fail to
compete with the urban educated students. Hence, the Committee is of the view that unless
some sort of coaching classes are provided to these groups, it would be difficult to achieve the
desired goal. The Committee therefore recommends that coaching classes be arranged for the
disadvantaged sections of society.

2.17 The Committee is of the opinion that mentally and physically handicapped children
and other deprived section of the society need to be given special attention. A more
humanitarian approach should be followed while dealing with the neglected and disabled
section of society where output need not be judge by the adherence to law rather by the
number of people benefited and settled.

2.18 The Sub-Committee was informed during its visit to Chennai (24th to 26th
September, 1997) that in Tamil Nadu Vocational Education is being used as launching pad for
securing admission into Polytechnics. ‘Students who fail to qualify the entrance test of
Polytechnics, join Vocational Education and after one year of its completion, through the
quota fixed for Vocational Education, get admission in 2nd year of Polytechnics. In this way,
the basic aim of Vocational Education to espouse the concept of self employability goes into
the background. Steps should therefore be taken to see that Vocational Education fulfils its
objective like individual self employability, supplying skilled manpower and acting as an
alternative to those pursuing higher education without any aim. The Committee is, therefore,
of the view that only those students from Vocational Education who are very brilliant and
secure the qualifying standard for joining polytechnics should be allowed to go for it.

2.19 The Committee expresses concern over the under utilisation of seats for the
Apprenticeship training by vocational passouts. This has stood as a weak link and has
adversely affected the acceptability of the Vocational trainees. The Committee is of the
opinion that steps must be taken to encourage the students for going through Apprenticeship
Training after completing Vocational Education which would make the Vocational Trainees
more and more acceptable to the industries. There should be proper placement of students.
Hence the Committee is of the view that the placement of students under Apprenticeship
Training needs to be vigorously implemented. There should also be proper monitoring of it.


2.20 The Committee has been given to understand that many Institutions running the
vocational courses in the Private and Voluntary Sectors make use of the courses for
financial exploitation and exorbitant fees is charged from the students. Steps are therefore
required to be taken to stop such exploitation and misuse of Vocational Courses by the
managements.

2.21 The Committee is of the opinion that Voluntary Organisations must submit their
audited report year wise. The Committee desires that the evaluation by Joint Evaluation
Team be conducted every year and voluntary agencies must be made accountable to the
local community for their acts of omissions and commissions.

2.22 The Committee has come to know that in the Chief Ministers Conference held in
July, 1996, a decision was taken to transfer the scheme of Vocational Education at +2
stage to the States. However, as this decision has no endorsement of the National
Development Council, the implementation of the Scheme is presently held up. The
Committee feels that this State of indecisiveness has adversely affected the scheme. The
Committee finds that Rs.60 crores were allocated for this scheme in the BE of 1997-98.
However, in the light of the decision of the Planning Commission to transfer the scheme to
States, no funds were released to the StatesUTs. As a result, budget provision was
brought down to Rs.20 crores and actual expenditure was only Rs.4 crores during 1997-98.
Allocation for 1998-99 is Rs.10 crores only. The Committee is of the view that the drastic
cut in funds in the first two years of the ninth Five Year Plan has adversely affected the
scheme of Vocational Education in general. Not only this, the scheme functioning
somewhat satisfactorily in some of the States has suffered due to this. The Committee
expresses its dissatisfaction on this state of indecisiveness. It is of the considered view that
since the scheme of vocationalisation of secondary education is of special significance, it
should continue as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme and drastic cuts in funds for this scheme
should not be effected.

2.23 The Committee was given to understand that under the Vocational Education
Scheme, Certificate is issued by the Board after the completion of two years course. The
Department, however clarified that there were some courses which did not require two
years to complete it. The Committee is of the view that the courses which can be
completed in less than two years should not be prolonged purposelessly and permitted to
be completed within the required period only. By putting a strict range of two years for
Vocational Education for a course which could be completed much earlier, the scheme
itself has created a barrier for the students. Hence, the Committee recommends that there
should be flexibility in the term of the courses and necessary changes in the Vocational
Education Scheme may be effected accordingly.

2.24 The Committee is of the considered view that the Vocational Education Scheme
is still continuing as a Pilot project. It has come to the notice of the Committee that four
external agenciesinstitutions were entrusted the work of conducting a region-wise
evaluation of the Vocational Education Scheme and the final report has been submitted by
Operation Research Group (ORG). The Committee recommends that in the light of the
findings of this report, the StatesUTs should be approached by the Department for taking
corrective measures for successful implementation of Vocational Education. Scheme.

2.25 The Committee finds that at present a number of Vocational Schemes are being
handled by different departments like Department of Education, Rural Employment and
Poverty Alleviation and Ministry of Labour. However, Committee feels that there being no

4661/RS—SA



8

coordination among these Departments, achievement rate of these schemes has not been
quite satisfactory. The Committee is, therefore, of the considered view that a common
strategy should be evolved so as to integrate all these Vocational Schemes and take the
maximum advantage of the facilities available with the different departments.

2.26 The Committee observes that there has been regional disparity so far as setting up
of ITIs is concerned. Eastern region, Jammu and Kashmir and also the Backward and Tribal
areas are the most neglected lot. The Committee feels that such areas need to be given
special assistance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that steps should be initiated for
provision of 100% Central Assistance for developing the institutional mechanism in these
areas. The Committee finds that the organised sector has been the major beneficiary of ITIs.
The unorganised sector comprising about 90% of population mainly from rural areas which
also includes large number of Drop-outs and Non-Formal Education students has been
totally left out. This is due to the fact that there is a mismatch between the labour market
requirement and the trades and skills being offered in ITIs. There being no proper outlet,
average students are left with no alternative but to opt for higher studies. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that urgent steps need to be taken for broad-basing the Vocational
education scheme by inducting area specific courses under ITIs.

2.27 The Committee finds that under the Training of Rural Youth for Self Employment
(TRYSEM) Scheme, there exists no exhaustive list of trades. The State Govts. have the
option to select their trades in accordance with the illustrative list of trades furnished by the
Central Govt. The Committee, however, feels that Area Specific and Need-Based approach
has to be developed to generate the interest of Rural Youth in these courses. The
Committee was given to udnerstand that in most of the cases funds were being released
divertly to the implementing agencies. Chances of diversion of funds by the States were thus
very less. i

2.28 The Committee is constrained to note that although physical achievements so far
as training aspect of both the schemes is concerned ‘ure quite satisfactory, this is not the
position if we look at the figures relating to number of Youths Self EmployedAvage
employed. The Committee would like to emphasise that generation of employment both self
and wage is the ultimate objective of both these schemes. The Committee, accordingly,
recommends that some pilot projects should be started on trial basis where all kinds of
facilities are made available to trainees so as to make them gainfully employed.

2.29 The Committee finds that no recent evaluation of both the schemes of ITIs &
TRYSEM has been carried out. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
Department should carry out surveys so as to find out the inherent lacunae in the schemes
and take necessary remedial action. The Committee would also like to point out that
effective monitoring mechanism should be evolved so as to carry out effective
implementation of the Vocational Schemes. This monitoring Committee may consist of
CentralWState Govt. Officials and M.P/MLA and other local representatives.
