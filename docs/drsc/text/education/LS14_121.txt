 REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Department of Women and Child Development was set up in 1985 as a part of the Ministry of Human
Resource Development to give the much needed impetus to the  holistic development of women and children.
1.2       In its capacity as a nodal agency looking after the advancement of women and children, the Department 
formulates plans, policies and programmes, enacts and amends legislation affecting women and children and guides and
coordinates the efforts of both governmental and non-governmental organizations working in the field of women and
child development.   Apart from this, the Department implements certain innovative programmes for women and
children which are in the areas of training and capacity building, employment and income generation, welfare and
support services, awareness generation and gender sensitization. 
II
          PERFORMANCE DURING NINTH PLAN
2.1       Approved outlay during the Ninth  Plan for the Department  was Rs. 7810.42 crores.   Allocation of Rs. 900.00
crores in the first year of the Plan (1997-98) showed an upward trend with Rs. 1650.00 crores being provided in 2001-
02 which was the   last year  of Ninth Plan.   With the exception of first year, the Committee observes  that the
Department failed to utilize the allocated funds fully.  There was invariable reduction at the RE stage and that too
sometimes remained under-utilised.   The main factors responsible for this trend which were noticed by the Committee
were non-performance of implementing agencies, be it State Govts or NGOs, unspent balances lying with them, non-
submission of Utilisation Certificates, non-receipt of viable proposals, release of funds in the last quarter of financial
year etc. 
2.2       The Committee is constrained to note that as compared to children-related schemes, women-related schemes
need special focusing.  Although amount of funding for women-related schemes is much less, the same remained
grossly under-utilised during the entire Ninth Plan period.   So far as  achievement level  of physical targets for different
schemes relating to both women and children is concerned, the Committee is surprised to note that they remained more
or less fulfilled, in some schemes, achievement  level is exceeding the targets fixed.   This is despite the fact that trend
of under-utilisation of funds was noticed with respect to women-related schemes.   The Committee apprehends that
there is  likelihood of over-achievement of physical targets being on paper only,   ground reality being  totally
different.   The Committee also feels that there might be  a possibility of physical targets being fixed without proper
assessment.   The Committee is of the view that in either case, an effective inbuilt monitoring mechanism at all required
levels can reveal the real picture.

IV        INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
4.1       The ICDS Scheme seeks to provide an integrated package of health, nutrition and educational services to
children up to six years of age, pregnant women and nursing mothers.   The package includes supplementary nutrition,
immunization, health check-up, referral services, nutrition and health education and non-formal pre-school education.  
Number of sanctioned ICDS Projects is 5652 including 922 World Bank assisted Projects.  Out of these, 4587 Projects
including 752 World Bank assisted Projects are operational as on 31.12.01.    The Scheme is a centrally sponsored
Scheme.  While the Central Govt. bears the full cost of meeting the operational requirements, the States  provide for
funds for supplementary nutrition component.
4.2       The Committee notes that there is considerable gap between sanctioned and operational projects in some of the
States as indicated below:-

VI        TRAINING PROGRAMME UNDER ICDS (UDISHA)
6.1       Training is a crucial element in the ICDS Scheme.  ‘UDISHA’ is a comprehensive training programme being
undertaken through the National Institute of Public Co-operation and Child Development, selected training  institutions,
and the State Govts.   World Bank assistance is available for  part of the expenditure for this programme.  The
functionaries trained in the programme include the Anganwadi Workers, Supervisors, Additional Child Development
Project Officers (ACDPOs), Child Development Project Officers (CDPOs), Medical Officers and para-medical staff.  
UDISHA has an outlay of Rs. 600.55 crores over a  five-year period from 1999-2000 to September, 2004.
6.2       The Budget allocation in BE 2001-02 was Rs. 40.00 crores which was enhanced to Rs. 55.00 crores at RE
stage.   Provision of Rs. 72.00 crores has been made for BE 2002-03.   The Committee notes that during 2001-02, as
against the total release of  Rs. 36.15 crores (till 28.2.02),  the expenditure reported by the States/UTs and the NIPCCD
is Rs. 26.00 crores (upto December, 2001). 
6.3       The Committee has been given to understand that the training facilities are now better organized at  the State/UT
level and the  various training activities are being taken up in a big way in order to clear the training backlog during the
project period.  The Committee is, however, not inclined to agree with the contention of the Department.  The 
Committee observes that upto 31.12.01, against a target of training of 605 CDPOs, only 252 CDPOs have been trained.  
Similarly, out of 3428 Supervisors to be trained, only 1199 Supervisors have been imparted training. 
6.4       The  Committee finds that against a total release of Rs. 109.56 crores upto 25.2.02, total expenditure incurred by
States/UTs is Rs. 97.60 crores  and Statements of Expenditure received amount to Rs. 88.30 crores.   States/UTs of
Jharkhand, Uttaranchal, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu and Lakshdweep have
failed to furnish  the SOEs in spite of repeated requests.   States of Assam, Goa, Gujarat, Manipur, Nagaland, Sikkim
and Tamil Nadu are lagging behind in submission of SOEs.   The Committee is perturbed to note that against total
release of Rs. 413.41 lakhs to Tamil Nadu, expenditure figures are Rs. 205.92 lakhs and total SOEs  received are only
for Rs. 57.94 lakhs.   Not only majority of States have failed to utilize fully funds allocated to them, pendency is there in
respect of submission of expenditure details too.   The Committee strongly feels that a midterm review of this Training
Project should be undertaken without any delay so that assessment about the constraints/shortcomings  can be
made.       
VII       DAY CARE CENTRES
7.1       The Scheme aims to  provide day care services for children below 5 years, belonging to weaker sections of
society, whose family income does not exceed  Rs. 1800 per month.   The crèches running under the scheme provide
health care, supplementary nutrition, medical check-up and immunization, etc. to the children whose parents are away at

work sites or are incapacitated due to sickness and are unable to look after them.   The Scheme is implemented  through
the Central Social Welfare Board and two other national level voluntary organizations throughout the country.    The
Scheme covers 12,470 creches benefiting about 3.11 lakhs beneficiaries.  CSWB is running only 9738 creche units
allocated by the Department and rest of the crèches are being run by the two NGOs. 
7.2       Allocation for 2002-03 is Rs. 12.00 crores whereas the BE and RE 2001-02 was Rs. 7.45 crores.   The
Committee has been informed that out of additional funds provided in 2002-03, Rs. 3.50 crores are meant for
expansion/revision of norms.  While appreciating the considerable enhancement in Plan allocation for 2002-03, the
Committee would like to point out that during 2001-02, against a plan allocation of Rs. 7.00 crores, expenditure
reported upto 28.2.02 is only Rs. 0.70 lakhs.   Under non-plan provision also, against an allocation of Rs. 14.50 crores,
an expenditure of only Rs. 10.51 crores has been incurred upto 28.2.01.   The Committee has been given to understand
that as on 1.4.2001, the unspent balance under the Crèches Scheme with CSWB was negligible.   The Committee,
therefore, fails to understand the reasons for Plan allocation remaining under-utilised during 2001-02.
7.3       The Committee notes that out of 9738 creche units allotted to CSWB, only 9561 are operational.   Remaining 
177 units are withheld  out of which 119 units are withheld due to administrative reasons  and 58 units are to be allotted
to State Boards/NGOs to cover uncovered areas.   The Committee recommends that these non-operational creche units
should be started at the earliest.
VIII      BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA
8.1       The Scheme covers upto two girl children born on or after 15
th August, 1997 in a BPL family.  Post-delivery
grant of Rs. 500/- is deposited in an interest bearing account in a bank or post office in the name of the girl child.   In
addition, the girl child is also entitled to receive scholarship for each class of study ranging from Rs. 300/- for Class I to
Rs. 1000/- for Class X which is also deposited in the above account.   The Scheme is being implemented through ICDS
infrastructure  and in non-ICDS areas through district level agencies.
8.2       Plan allocation of RS. 25.00 crores in BE 2001-02 was reduced to Rs. 16.00 crores at the RE stage and
expenditure  reported upto 28.2.02 is Rs. 13.04 crores.   While examining Demands for Grants for 2001-02, the
Committee had noticed that fund utilization position under the  Scheme was as low as 31.41%  of total allocation and
non-adherence to financial norms was there  in respect of all States/UTs.   The Committee has been  informed that the
funds were continually  released to the States/UTs.  BSY being a  continued scheme, there is no bar to utilize the funds
released   in the  following years.   However, future releases will only be made on  receipt of Utilisation Certificates.  
Instructions have also been given for streamlining the procedure for disbursement of funds through ICDS infrastructure.
8.3       The Committee notes that there has been considerable improvement in the status of utilization of funds by the
States/UTs.   However, 50% of total allocated funds  still remain to be utilized.  States/UTs of Bihar, Maharashtra,
Sikkim, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Daman & Diu and Lakshadweep continue to be the major defaulters with
utilization percentage ranging from 13.63% to 33.65%.
8.4       The Committee  notes that no allocation has been proposed for BSY  for 2002-03 as during the  Tenth Plan, the
Scheme is to be transferred to the State Govts.   The Committee has been informed that the Department had conveyed
its acceptance to the transfer of BSY to States for the following reasons –
-
                     Obtaining of utilization certificates for the funds released has been extremely tardy.
-                     Operational details of disbursement of the scholarship, maintenance of accounts would be best left to the
State Govts. as each State would have to devise its own system.
8.5       The Committee has been given to understand that the approval of the National Development Council is yet to be 
obtained on the proposed transfer.   The Committee is, however,  not convinced  by the rationale for proposed transfer
of the Scheme to States.  The Committee strongly feels that in view of poor financial condition of all the States, there is
little hope of this kind of Scheme being handled by State Govts. out of their own resources.    Instead of taking
corrective measures right from inception of the Scheme,  this kind of proposal can be simply considered to be evading
one’s responsibility.

not exceed Rs. 16,000/- p.m. in ‘A’ class cities and Rs. 15,000/- p.m. in other cities.           Financial assistance is given
to the extent of 75% of the cost of construction of the hostel building and 50%  of the cost of the land by the Centre to
eligible voluntary organizations, universities, public trusts etc.    States/UTs are also eligible for assistance if suitable
organizations are not available in the area.

forthcoming, allocation had to be reduced at the RE stage.   The Committee finds that an allocation of Rs. 13.50 crores
has been made for 2002-03 in order to cover the requirement of women seeking employment, expansion of the Scheme
and revision of norms proposed to be undertaken during the year.   The Committee does not have  much faith in  these
proposed plans in view of working of the Scheme during Ninth Plan.  Utilization of funds under the Scheme has been
optimum during the Ninth Plan with the exception of last year, 2001-02.     However, the same cannot be said about the
achievement  level of physical targets during Ninth Plan.   Although 87 hostels have been sanctioned against the  target
of 93  hostels, the Committee apprehends that very few would have been constructed and made operational.   This is

hostels at regular intervals.   Last reminder has been sent on 11.10.2001 whereby all Chief Secretaries/Women and
Child Development Secretaries were asked to provide list of incomplete hostels.   District Collectors  and CDPOs were

would also like to be  enlightened about the number of  Hostels sanctioned, under construction and operational hostels
(State-wise).   The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for conducting an  evaluation of this Scheme so

The Committee observes that  against an approved outlay of Rs. 45.60 crores for the Scheme in the  Ninth Plan,
only Rs. 31.50 could be released and out of that an expenditure of only Rs. 19.04 crores has been incurred upto
28.2.02.   Although  allocation during the first three years of the Ninth Plan  was on the high side, utilization was very
poor and as a result, allocation during the remaining two years was brought down.  The main reason attributed for such a
poor performance is unspent balances lying with  the implementing agencies.   The Committee finds that as a result of

121st Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/121streport.htm[01-07-2016 15:17:27]
special drive for settlement of previous accounts and persistent interaction with State Board functionaries, situation has 
improved considerably.   The Committee, however, feels that there is still considerable scope for improvement .   Goa,
Gujarat, J&K, Nagaland, Lakshsdweep and  Orissa are yet to submit their accounts.   Although some State have updated
their pendency position considerably, in respect of some other  States, accounts are long pending.   The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Department should continue the exercise of special drive with State Boards for
settlement of accounts under the Scheme of Condensed Courses for Women.   The Committee has been given to
understand that funds under the Scheme are released by the CSWB to state Boards who maintain NGO-wise  position of
releases and utilization.  The Committee would like to be informed about details of unspent balances lying under the
Scheme (State Board-wise). (Para 9.2)
The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction on undue delay in taking corrective measures based on the two
evaluation reports.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should expedite the same. (Para 9.3)
X         SUPPORT TO TRAINING AND EMPLOYMENT PROGRAMME FOR WOMEN (STEP)
The Committee is not in favour of release of funds during the last month of the financial year.   The Committee,
therefore, recommends that necessary  measures should be taken for expeditious disposal of proposals.  (Para 10.2)
            The Committee observes that during the Ninth Plan overall performance so far as achievement level of Physical
and financial targets is  concerned has been satisfactory.  The Committee, however, feels that the Scheme is being
implemented by only ten to twelve States as  is evident from the details of funds released during the years 1998-99,
1999-2000 and 2000-01.  The Committee feels that efforts should be made to expand the Scheme to the uncovered
States also.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that required steps in this direction should be taken.   The
Committee is not aware   whether any exercise of evaluating the  Scheme has been undertaken so far.   If not, the
Committee feels that such an exercise should be initiated at the earliest. (Para 10.3)
XI        SOCIO-ECONOMIC PROGRAMME
            The Committee is in agreement with the aforesaid decision. (Para 11.2)
            The  Scheme has shown a very poor performance level as is evident from the achievement level of both physical
and financial targets during the Ninth Plan.   The Committee notes that as many as 3298 blacklisted NGOs are there due
to non-refunding of loan amount to the State Boards and non-settlement of accounts.   The Committee recommends that
the concerned State Boards should be  asked to vigorously pursue the matter with the blacklisted NGOs.  The
Committee is perturbed to note that the State Boards of Gujarat, UP, Bihar, AP, and Karnataka have failed to refund the
remaining amount of Revolving Fund, which accrued to them from the recovery of loans from the NGOs, in spite of
repeated reminders.    The Committee views this development with serious concern and recommends that all the efforts
should be made at the appropriate level to get back the funds lying with the State Boards.  (Para 11.3)
XII       HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN
The Committee  observes that enhanced allocation in BE 2001-02 was meant for expenditure on claims for 2
nd
instalment  for 29 Working Hostels sanctioned in 2000-01 and third and final instalment of several hostels sanctioned in
previous years.   However, due to anticipated  claims not forthcoming, allocation had to be reduced at the RE stage.  
The Committee finds that an allocation of Rs. 13.50 crores has been made for 2002-03 in order to cover the requirement
of women seeking employment, expansion of the Scheme and revision of norms proposed to be undertaken during the
year.   The Committee does not have  much faith in  these proposed plans in view of working of the Scheme during
Ninth Plan.  Utilization of funds under the Scheme has been optimum during the Ninth Plan with the exception of last
year, 2001-02.     However, the same cannot be said about the achievement  level of physical targets during Ninth Plan.  
Although 87 hostels have been sanctioned against the  target of 93  hostels, the Committee apprehends that very few
would have been constructed and made operational.   This is  borne out by the fact that achievement level with regard to
number of beneficiaries is much below the targeted figures. (Para 12.2)
The Committee is constrained to note that all this exercise has failed to achieve any results.   Information giving
very little details has been received only from the States of Rajasthan, J&K, Chattisgarh, Mizoram and Bihar.   The
Committee is, therefore, of the opinion that sanction for construction of new hostels should not be given at any cost.  
Instead all attention should be diverted to get the construction of all pending hostels completed at the earliest within a
prescribed and closely monitored time schedule.   The Committee would also like to be  enlightened about the number
of  Hostels sanctioned, under construction and operational hostels (State-wise).   The Committee strongly feels that there

is an urgent need for conducting an  evaluation of this Scheme so as to assess  the inherent drawbacks and suggest
necessary modifications therefor. (Para 12.3)
The Committee feels that there is a need for strict implementation of the  guidelines prescribed under the
Scheme so that such instances do not occur.   (Para 12.4)
XIII      SHORT STAY HOMES
            The Committee, however, observes that against an allocation of Rs. 10.00 crores in 2001-02, expenditure
reported upto 28.2.02 is only Rs. 5.32 crores.   Overall performance of the Scheme during Ninth Plan cannot be
considered to be satisfactory.   Due to unspent balances available with the  implementing agencies,  the trend of
reduction of funds at RE stage and that too remaining underutilized has been observed .   The Committee feels that
maintenance and supervision of the existing SSHs being handed over to CSWB in 1999-2000 has not led to any
noticeable improvement.  (Para 13.3)
            The Committee notes that  as on 8.3.02  there are 374 Short Stay Homes spread over in 26 States/UTs  with the
maximum number concentrated in AP, Bihar, Orissa, UP and W. Bengal.  The Committee would like to be apprised
about the number of fully functional SSHs (state-wise) and also about the feedback received in respect of functioning of
above-mentioned States during 2001-02 as a result of inspections conducted by the Department Officials. (Para 13.4)
XIV     CENTRAL SOCIAL WELFARE BOARD (CSWB)
            The Committee notes that in some cases CSWB expenditure has exceeded the amount provided by the
Department during 2001-02.    In some schemes, although no funds have been provided by the Department, Board has
shown expenditure thereunder.   In some other cases, the Board has some amount as opening balance at the beginning of
the financial year.   It seems that the  Board was having unspent balance with it.  The Committee would like to be
enlightened in this regard. (Para 14.2)
The Committee strongly feels that   serious efforts should be made   for bringing out  improvement in the
functioning of CSWB.   (Para 14.3)
XV       NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
The Committee finds that sexual harassment complaints whenever referred to the Commission are sent to the
concerned organizations and their disposal  is monitored by the Commission.   The Committee would like to be
enlightened as to whether the Commission has initiated any exercise for ascertaining the compliance of aforesaid SC
judgement regarding setting up of complaint mechanism in all workplaces so far. (Para 15.2)
The Committee, however, feels that this is not the  ideal position and the concerned States/UTs should be
approached once again for setting up  Women Commissions. (Para 15.3)
XVI     NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR CHILDREN
The Committee notes that the proposed National Commission for Children is to  monitor all the schemes
pertaining to children at present being handled by different Departments.   This would facilitate the handling of  all the
children related problems in a holistic manner.    (Para 16.1)
XVII    RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH
            The issue of augmenting the corpus of RMK had been going  on for considerable time.  The Committee is happy
to note that EFC had finally recommended augmenting  RMK’s corpus fund to Rs. 100.00 crores with the year wise
scheduling of the disbursement to be need-based.   The Department has informed that RMK’s initial corpus of Rs. 31.00
crores already stands augmented to Rs. 52.06 crores.   Accordingly Rs. 47.94 crores   is the additionality to be provided
as a consequence of the EFC decision.   The Committee, however, observes  that the year-wise scheduling is yet to be
finalized and EFC recommendations are under process of sanction.   The Committee hopes that all the procedural
formalities will be expedited for augmenting the corpus fund of RMK. (Para 17.2)
