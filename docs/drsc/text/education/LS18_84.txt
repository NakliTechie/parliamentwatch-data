REPORT 
I INTRODUCTION  
1.1 The Department of Culture plays a vital role in the promotion and dissemination of art and 
culture. The aim of the Department is to develop ways and means for instilling basic values and 
perceptions in the minds of the people, particularly the young. The Department also undertakes 
the prgoramme of preservation, encouragement and dissemination of various manifestations of 
creative activity, both past and present. The Department is engaged in excavation, conservation 
and promotion of ancient monuments and historic sites; administration of libraries, museums and 
institutions of anthropology; collection, preservation and ethnology of national character; catering 
to institutions and organisations in the areas of Buddhist and Tibetan Studies; promoting literary, 
visual and performing arts; observing centenaries and anniversaries of important personalities 
and entering into cultural agreements with foreign countries. The Department carries out its 
various programmes through two attached offices, six subordinate offices and 26 autonomous 
organisations.  
1.2 During the Ninth Five Year Plan period, the Department proposes to focus on a 
comprehensive plan for preservation of the archeological heritage and development of the 
monumental complexes in the country. Efforts would also be directed towards the preservation of 
archival heritage and the promotion of classical, folk and tribal art and crafts and oral traditions 
which are threatened with extinction.  
IIBUDGETARY ALLOCATION  
2.1 The Committee finds that the total allocation of the Department for the year 1999-2000 is Rs. 
376.21 crores which includes Rs. 165.00 crores under Plan and Rs. 211.21 crores under Non-Plan. 
The Department had initially proposed a Plan outlay of Rs. 201.22 crores to the Planning 
Commission. But the finally agreed amount is Rs. 165 crores. Similarly, the original proposal for 
Non-Plan was for Rs. 268.57 crores and the allocation finally agreed to by the Ministry of Finance 
is Rs. 211.21 crores. The Ninth Five Year Plan outlay has been kept at Rs. 920.41 crores against 
the proposed requirement of Rs. 1124.40 crores . According to the Department, the reduction of 
Rs. 203.99 crores in the Ninth Plan allocation which in almost 18 percent of its modest proposal 
would result in substantial cut in the promotional and support activities in the area of promotion 
and dissemination of arts and culture, Archaeological Survey of India, Libraries and other 
expenditure sector.  
2.2 The Committee feels that the Department of Culture has been entrusted with certain 
important activities and responsibilities and it is necessary that it carries on its activities smoothly. 
The Committee would therefore, urge upon the Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance to 
consider grant of enhanced allocation to the Department so that its main activities are not 
adversely affected.  
III NATIONAL CULTURE FUND  
3.1 The Committee in its 67th Report had noted that the incentives for contributions to National 
Culture Fund (NCF) were inadequate and felt that a total Income Tax exemption for the 
contribution made to NCF would prove to be a much desired incentive for the possible donors. It, 
Page 4 of 12REPORT
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/84repcul.html

therefore, recommended that the contribution to the NCF be fully exempted under section 80 G 
(2) of the Income Tax Act.  
2. The Committee is happy to note that the Govt. of India vide the Finance Act, 1998 
notified on 1st August, 1998 have provided that the donations to the NCF would be 
eligible for tax benefits under section 80 G(2) of the Income Tax Act. It also feels that 
adequate publicity needs to be given about NCF and the incentive to the donors, so 
that more donors could contribute to this Fund.  
3. The Secretary explained that under NCF it has been decided to select and execute a 
few selected projects of modest scale with participation of local community or 
corporate entities. So far, two projects, namely Shaniwarwada Project, Pune for 
restoration of the ancestral palace of Peshwas and the Project for renovation of Water 
Works of the Humayun’s Tomb, New Delhi have been selected. In addition, expansion 
of Prince of Wales Museum, Mumbai is also under consideration.  
4. The Committee has also been apprised of certain constraints in this regard such as 
rigidities and red-tapism, inflexibility in the operation and use of Funds of NCF. The 
Committee expects that the Department would do something concrete to avoid these 
apprehensions in the minds of the people and see that the NCF is able to achieve the 
objectives for which it has been set up. For this, complete transparency is must.  
IV ARCHAEOLOGICAL SURVEY OF INDIA (ASI)  
1. Against a projected requirement of Rs. 37.75 crore for 1998-99, only Rs. 20.00 crore 
were allocated to ASI. Only a limited number of monuments Taj Mahal, Khajuraho, 
Ajanta, Ellora, Konark, Bodhgaya, Nalanda, Vaishali, Sarnath and Hampi were to be 
brought up to world standards during 1998-99. However, the Department has 
informed that due to financial constraints, this task could not be accomplished. The 
Committee, however, finds that out of the allocation of Rs. 20.00 crore, enhanced to 
Rs. 22.00 crore at the RE stage, only Rs. 12.48 crore have been utilised upto 28th 
February, 1999. Allocation for 1999-2000 has been enhanced to Rs. 28.00 crores. The 
Committee strongly feels that the Department should make concerted efforts for 
maximum utilisation of the funds. Priority should be given to such monuments which 
need urgent attention.  
4.2 The Committee has been informed that Maharashtra Tourism Development Corporation, 
Govt. of Maharashtra has been requested to explore the feasibility for organising Sound and Light 
Programme at Ajanta and Ellora. Their response is awaited. The Committee is of the opinion that 
the matter needs to be pursued vigorously by the Department with the Govt. of Maharashtra. As 
these caves attract a large number of foreign tourists also, the Committee feels that there is a need 
for the Department also to take some initiative in this matter.  
4.3 With regard to the preservation of monuments, the Committee has been informed that there 
are 3598 centrally protected monuments in the country and if the total number of individual 
structures is taken into consideration, this figure exceeds 5000. These monuments are in the care 
of just 3443 Monument Attendants. As per the Action taken note of the Department, the Planning 
Commission has rejected the Department’s proposal for creation of 5000 posts of Monument 
Attendants for protecting these monuments. The Planning Commission has instead suggested that 
Archaeological Survey of India should go in for other solutions of engaging/hiring private agencies 
wherever they are available instead of adopting a mass employment strategy. The Committee has 
been further informed that the matter has been taken up afresh with the Planning Commission for 
reconsideration primarily on grounds that the Monument Attendants do a multifarious job which 
private security agencies cannot perform, apart from being expensive. The Committee is of the 
Page 5 of 12REPORT
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/84repcul.html

considered opinion that on an average provision of only one Monument Attendant per monument 
would not be adequate enough, in view of the increasing trend in the cases of theft and vandalism. 
Even this provision is not being fully met at present. The Committee, therefore feels that where 
there are no Monument Attendants at present, engaging/hiring/private agencies may be 
undertaken. However, this can be done as a temporary measure till the time a viable solution is 
worked out.  
4. The Committee does not wish to go at length with regard to the preservation of 
monuments, in view of the fact that the Sub-Committee on Preservation of monuments 
is already looking into the various aspects relating to the preservation of monuments. 
However, the Committee is of the view that certain aspects like maintenance of 
monuments, provision of public conveniences at monumental sites, encroachment in 
and around the monuments, progress/policies for attracting tourists – both domestic 
and foreign to our cultural sites and monuments etc. need to be given greater 
attention. The Committee is in no doubt about the practical difficulties and problems 
which are facing the ASI in this regard. Nevertheless, the Committee would be happy 
if the ASI is able to overcome the inherent difficulties/constraints to the maximum 
extent and fulfil its responsibilities and obligations which have been entrusted to it.  
V. MUSEUMS  
1. The Secretary informed the Committee that during the year 1998-99, the scheme for 
promotion and strengthening of regional and local museums was revamped with the 
objective of promoting in a professional manner the setting up and modernisation of 
museums at the regional, state and local level. As per the new strategy, regional 
responsibility has now been placed on certain nodal museums, viz, Indian Museum, 
Calcutta for East and North-East, Salarjung Musuem, Hyderabad for South, National 
Museum, New Delhi for North, Allahabad Musuem for Central and Prince of Wales 
Museum, Mumbai for Western India. Besides this, action has also been initiated to 
prepare a computerised National Register for Antiquities in the Central and State 
Museums in the country by the end of the year 2000. In due course, the Register would 
be extended to cover all districts and local museums.  
2. Upgradation of conservation facilities is another activity which has been given the 
highest priority and a high level Scientific Advisory Committee has been constituted 
with experts drawn from all fields of conservation and from Science and Technology, 
the Committee was informed.  
3. On a specific query as to whether there was any proposal to exhibit the Nizam’s 
Jewellery for public, it was informed that plans were there to exhibit the same on I5th 
August, 1999. It was further explained that National Museum is the only Museum 
where with some modifications the Nizam’s Jewellery can be displayed for public 
viewing. In the meanwhile, a request has been received from the Chief Minister of 
Andhra Pradesh stating that the Nizam’s Jewellery being historically connected with 
Hyderabad, it should be kept there only. The matter is, at present, under submission 
to PMO.  
4. The Committee appreciates all the new initiatives taken by the Department during 
1998-99. The Committee hopes that decision on exhibition of the Nizam’s Jewellery 
would be taken at the earliest.

VI ARCHIVES  
6.1 It has been reported that there are estimated three crore Manuscripts in several languages 
which are languishing with individuals and institutions in several parts of the Country. For 
protection of this endangered intellectual property, a National Mission for Manuscripts is 
proposed to be launched in 1999-2000. The first phase will comprise compilation and publication 
of a Directory of Institutions/ Individuals possessing these manuscripts and collecting data on 
their historical importance and physical state of these manuscripts and the preservation facilities 
available.  
6.2 The Committee welcomes the new initiative. The Committee hopes that this mission would be 
accomplished under a time-bound action plan so that these age-old Manuscripts enshrining the 
ideas and thought processes of our ancestors are protected.

VII LIBRARIES  
1. As per the Constitutional provisions, Library is a State subject. The Raja Rammohan 
Roy Library Foundation which acts as the nodal agency of Government of India has 
therefore to work in close, active and willing cooperation with State Governments and 
Union Territory Administrations in the direction of strengthening the Public Library 
movement in the country with special emphasis upon inculcating the reading habit 
among the rural masses. This objective is very laudable and in order to achieve this 
objective the Foundation has framed equally laudable schemes like "Scheme of Book 
Assistance’ and "Books for the millions at their doorsteps"etc. The Committee is not 
very sure as to what extent the Department has been able to achieve this lofty 
objective, in view of the fact that the National average literacy rate is around 62 per 
cent. In this connection the Committee also notes the rejection of the Department’s 
proposal for setting up of Rural Libraries at the Panchayat level by the Planning 
Commission and instead suggesting the urgent need for developing Community 
Information System which would encompass the Rural Library System and 
Continuing Education under National Literacy Mission. The Committee is of the 
considered view that as the concept of Public/Rural Library is linked to the literacy of 
the masses throughout the country; a holistic and integrated approach would have to 
be taken in consultation with all the concerned agencies of the Department such as 
Department of Education, Planning Commission, Ministry of Finance and States and 
Union Territories Administrations.

VIII AKADEMI  
1. The Committee in its 67th report had expressed its concern over large number of 
publications of Sahitya Akademi laying unsold. The Committee had also suggested 
that besides devising means to reduce the accumulation of unsold publications, steps 
should also be taken for creating and increasing interest of public in the publications 
of Sahitya Akademi, so that they donot accumulate. The unsold publications were also 
suggested to be distributed to the libraries. The Department has informed that the 
suggestions of the Committee have been communicated for action to the Akademi.  
2. The Committee has been further informed that during 1998-99, the Sahitya Akademi 
participated in a number of books exhibitions in India, it also initiated Special Sales 
Promotion Schemes to increase the sales for its publications. As a result of these 
schemes, the sales of the Sahitya Akademi publications have steadily increased during 
the year 1995-96, 1996-97 and 1997-98. While appreciating the efforts of Sahitya 
Akademi, the Committee feels that there is a need to popularise the publications of 
Sahitya Akademi through organising books exhibitions in different parts of the 
country besides making their availability easier in the libraries and National Book 
Trust.

Secretary explained that all the cases are recommended by the State Govts. In the case

XI. Committed Liability  
11.1. While examining the Demands for Grants 1997-98 of the Departments under its purview, the 
Committee noted that the committed liability of the Plan Schemes has not been reflected in the 
budgetary allocations.  
11.2. The Committee, in this, connection considered the report of the Tenth Finance Commission. 
The Tenth Finance Commission have observed in this regard as under:-  
"Although the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their terms 
of reference in deciding the cut off date for transfer of committed 
liability on account of plan scheme, the incremental liabilities that 
would arise in 1997-98 on account of 8th Plan scheme completed in the 
next two financial years would have to be provided for. The 
Commission are, therefore, of the view that the Planning Commission 
may consider providing for the maintenance of such scheme till 1999-
2000 in the Plan itself as was done for the schemes of the two Annual 
Plans of 1990-91 and 1991-92". 
11.3. The Committee also sought clarifications from the then Secretary (Expenditure). He 
informed the Committee that even though communications were sent to various Ministries, the 
Ministries could not identify the committed expenditure on time. Accordingly a lumpsum amount 
has been provided under Demand No. 31. The Secretary also assured that if required, more funds 
for this purpose will be provided at the RE stage. He also stated that the Departments may 
approach the Department of Expenditure for their share.  
11.4. The Committee, accordingly, directed the Department to approach the Ministry of Finance 
in this regard. However, while considering the Demands for Grants 1998-1999, the Committee 
noticed that no progress in this regard seemed to have taken place. The Committee, therefore, 
reiterated its stand. In spite of the Departments initiating some exercise, the matter could not be 
sorted out. The Committee, therefore, heard the Secretary (Expenditure) on 27th March, 1999.  
11.5. The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, submitted that at the end of the 8th 
Five Year Plan the Planning Commission issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the 
committed expenditure from plan to the non plan side. The Ministry of Finance (Budget Division) 
also issued a letter to all the Financial Advisors of the Department requesting them to indicate the 
amount to be included from plan to non plan.  
11.6. The Secretary submitted that as on 10th February, 1997, 24 Ministries/Departments 
responded indicating a figure of Rs.450.28 crore. However, some Ministries/Departments like 
Education, Culture, Women and Child Development did not respond. Accordingly a lumpsum 
provision of Rs.500 crores in the Ministry of Finance Budget was provided for this purpose. As a 
result of this exercise, a sum of Rs.150 crores was indeed transferred from plan to non plan for the 
various Ministries as committed expenditure during the year, on the basis of scrutiny with 
reference to the Planning Commission’s guidelines. The rest of the amount was surrendered as the 
Page 11 of 12REPORT
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/84repcul.html

proposals were not found acceptable.  
11.7. The Secretary agreed in principle that whatever is the committed expenditure, it should be 
transferred from plan to non plan. Otherwise the plan has no meaning.  
11.8. The Secretary assured that the proposals pertaining to UGC (Rs.27 crores) and Department 
of Education (Rs. 80 crores) which were under consideration would be cleared. Moreover, the 
Tenth Finance Commission Report has given a breathing time till 1999-2000 for this transition.  
11.9. The Committee would like to impress that the real picture should be projected before 
Parliament.  
11.10. The Secretary also apprised the Committee that another Sub Committee has been 
appointed by the NDC, consisting of representatives from seven states and Ministry of Finance. 
This Sub-Committee will look into the structural distortions which have entered into the finances 
of the states on account of 5th Pay Commission and to suggest a suitable package linked to fiscal 
reforms.  
11.11. The Committee observes that a similar problem exists in the case of Central Ministries also. 
The Committee, therefore, desires that a similar exercise be carried out in the case of Central 
Ministries also.
