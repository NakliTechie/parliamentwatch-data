Observations/Recommendations
NATIONAL MISSION FOR EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN (NMEW)
The Committee observes that the objectives of the National Mission for Empowerment of Women
Scheme are of vital importance as they aim to empower them socially, economically and educationally.
However, the pace of the expenditure under the scheme has been quite low. The Committee is of the
concerted view that the under-utilization of funds is indicative of the fact that the benefits of the Scheme
are not reaching the targeted beneficiaries. The Committee feels that the Ministry should play a pro-
active role in achieving the objectives of the Scheme by completing the process of putting all the
necessary structures in place and receipt of viable proposals from the States/UTs. The Committee hopes
that the pace of expenditure would pick up in 2017-18 and the allocation of 
` 70.00 crore for the year
2017-18 would be fully utilized. (Para 11.8)
Action Taken
The Comments of the Committee are noted for compliance. The revised EFC Memo for continuation
of NMEW Scheme during 2017-18 up-to 2019-2020 has been prepared for necessary appraisal and
approval. Regular meetings with the nodal officers of the respective State/ UT Administration is being
held to ensure proper utilisation of funds.

BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
The Committee also takes note of the fact that the total allocation for the child development
schemes has increased from

whereas for women welfare schemes, the allocation increased from ` 821.40 crore in RE 2016-17 to
` 1089.02 crore in budget 2017-18. A comparison of allocations to the child development schemes and
women welfare schemes of the last five years is as follow:

As is evident from the table, the allocation for child development schemes has always been higher than
that of the women welfare schemes. The reason for such a huge difference between the two, according
to the Ministry, is that the child development schemes are implemented on a pan India basis whereas the
women welfare schemes are implemented in selected areas/districts of the country. Further, a close
scrutiny of budget and revised allocations for both the child development schemes and women welfare
schemes reveals that at revised stage the allocation for women welfare schemes has always been reduced
substantially in comparison to child development schemes. The Committee feels that women welfare
schemes deserve equal attention and importance and required funds should be allocated to women welfare
schemes as well if the objectives of social and economic empowerment of women are to be achieved.
Therefore, the Committee would appreciate if the reason for this continuous trend of reduced allocations
at revised stage is checked. (Para 2.3)
Action Taken
The observation of the Committee on checking the reasons for the trend of reduced allocation of
fund at RE stage has been noted for compliance.

The scheme of Setting up of One Stop Centre (OSC) is being implemented since 1st April 2015.
Under the scheme, it has been envisaged that One Stop Centres would be set up in phased manner and
will cover pan India. In the first phase, one Centre was sanctioned per State/UT. Further, 150 additional
Centres are taken up in second phase during 2016-17. As the setting up One Stop Centre, involves a
number of activities ranging from identification of place & land, construction of building, hiring of
personnel, registration of scheme in PFMS, opening of bank account in the name of DM/DC, transfer
of funds, etc., the scheme could not take off and during first phase only 15 One Stop Centres could
be setup. However, after second phase, various issues have been resolved and 158 One Stop Centre have
been set up so far.
While operationalzing the WHL scheme, it has been found that technical issues are involved such
as, (i) procurement of certain hardware, (ii) bifurcation of telecom circles, (iii) augmentation of existing
helpline in 181, etc. In many States, 181 is popularly functional as CM helpline where all grievances are
taken up including women issues and call demarcation of women issue is challenging for general calls.
Also tendering process for hiring the agencies is time consuming and in certain cases retendering has
been done. Now the issue has been resolved and as on today, women helpline has become functional
in 25 States/UTs. The target during the year 2017-18 is to universalise women helpline in all States/UTs.
Observations/Recommendations
The Committee also notes that for the child development schemes there is an appreciable increase
in the overall budget allocation in 2017-18 but for women welfare schemes there has been no substantial
increase in the budget allocation of 2017-18. The Committee feels that the socio-political empowerment
of women should be a core development goal for any society if it wishes to be a developed and equal
society. This goal requires gender equality which in turn needs critical policy steps in favour of women.
Indian women have traditionally been vulnerable and marginalized section in the society. This is the
reason that the constitution of India not only prohibits discrimination on the grounds of sex but also
permits positive discrimination in favour of women. Seen in this context, the formulation and implementation
of women centric schemes acquires utmost importance. It is the implementation part of the schemes that
suffer due to lack of funds. Therefore, it is Committee's concerted opinion that sufficient funds should
be provided for the women welfare schemes as well if the objective of socio-economic development of
women is to be achieved. Further, the Committee feels that there is a need to universalize women welfare
schemes also so as to ensure their participation in social, economic and political decision making process.
(Para 2.4)
Action Taken
The observation of the Committee on providing sufficient funds for the women welfare schemes
and need to universalize women welfare schemes to ensure their participation in social, economic and
political decision making process have been noted for compliance.

Observations/Recommendations
The Committee observes that in almost all the major schemes except ICDS, ICPS and the schemes
for adolescent girls, the allocated funds have largely remained underutilized. The Committee feels that
there should be optimum utilization of funds under each of the schemes of the Ministry if the desired
goals of various schemes were to be achieved. Since all the schemes of the Ministry are meant for the
development and welfare of children and women, it is of utmost importance that allocated funds are
utilized optimally to cover targeted beneficiaries. The Committee accordingly, recommends that the
factors responsible for under utilization of funds be identified and remedial action taken in a time bound
manner. The Committee further desires that a status note be furnished on the schemes where utilization
of funds has been abysmally low in respect of the budget allocations.
The Committee also observes that under-achievement of financial targets in a number of schemes
is indicative of the fact that physical targets of those schemes remained under-achieved as well. The
Committee believes that schemes of children and women are of vital importance and under-achievement
of these schemes would necessarily mean that the benefits of the schemes have not reached the targeted
beneficiaries or they have been denied the benefits of welfare schemes. In this context, the Committee
feels that problems faced in the achievements of both financial and physical targets should be identified
and addressed for efficacious implementation of the schemes. The Ministry should also take up the matter
of under-achievement with the concerned State Governments/UTs for resolving Constraints.

OSC Scheme : The One Stop Centre Scheme was approved in March, 2015 for setting up on One
Stop Centre's in each State/UT to facilitate access to an integrated range of services including
medical aid, police assistance, legal aid/case management, psychosocial counselling, temporary
support services to women affected by violence. The Scheme is being implemented from 1st April,
2015. Under OSC scheme it has been envisaged to set up one centre in every State/UT in first phase
and its expansion in another 150 additional locations has been sanctioned in second phase. It may
be stated that in February 2016, the Ministry has envisaged that the 150 additional One Stop Centres
would be set up in newly constructed building. Hence an amount of `45.00 crore was kept for the

8
purpose of creation of Capital Assets. However, after series of discussions, it was concluded that
the proposal for setting up 150 OSCs in newly constructed building would not be materialised.
Therefore, it was decided to set up approx 60 Centres in newly constructed building and 90 OSCs
in existing building/facility which was approved by Secretary, WCD on 19.08.2016.
Setting up One Stop Centre involves a number of activities ranging from identification of place &
land, construction of building, hiring of personnel, registration of scheme in PFMS, opening of bank
account in the name of DM/DC, transfer of funds, etc. and the proposals of most of the States/
UTs were received after second half of the financial year. Identification of documents such as
survey/khasra No., plot demarcation diagram, construction agency, building estimates of OSC by
identified construction agency have to be prepared by respective State. This process is time
consuming. Land demarcation of OSC and its revenue document are also time consuming.
As such, the 2nd instalment of the recurring grant for 150 centres could not be released to States/
UTs. The proposals from 3 States/UTs namely West Bengal, NCT of Delhi and Lakshadweep were
not received even besides repeated reminders.
Letters to States/UTs has been issue at various levels for speedy implementation of the scheme and
also to complete the pendencies. Review meetings on status of implementation of OSC were held
in the month of May 2017, June 2017, July 2017 and August 2017 through Video Conferencing.
A letter on requirement of land documents for OSC Construction towards release of funds was sent
on 30.06.2017 to States/UTs from Joint Secretary, MWCD. Letters from Hon'ble Minister to Chief
Ministers have also been issued. As a result, 158 OSCs have become operational in 31 States/UTs
so far. Number of women assisted as on 31.08.2017 under One Stop Centre is 21297.

Observations/Recommendations
UNDERWEIGHT CHILDREN AND ANAEMIA IN WOMEN
As can be seen from the data mentioned above there has been some improvement in the percentage
of underweight children in NFHS-4 (2015-16) over NFHS-3 (2005-06). However, in certain States/UTs
such as Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Gujarat, Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra and Rajasthan
the percentage of underweight children is still on the higher side. Further, in case of anaemia in women,
the data reveals a grim scenario. In almost all the States/UTs, the percentage of anaemic women in
NFHS-4 (2015-16) is on extremely higher side. There has not been much improvement between NFHS-
3 (2005-06) and NFHS-4 (2015-16). These States/UTs are Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman
& Diu, Haryana, MP , Rajasthan, Telengana and Uttarakhand. The Committee observes with serious
concern that the services of supplementary nutrition, nutrition and health education and health check
up under ICDS are not yielding results as required. The Committee accordingly recommends that serious
attempts be made to reduce the percentage of high underweight children and anaemic women on an
urgent basis. (Para 3.15)
Action Taken
The Government is concerned about the high percentage of anaemia among women in the country
and level of underweight children in the States mentioned by the Committee. In the recent past, a number
of measures have been taken to combat the menace of underweight among children and anaemia among
the women. These measures include de-worming twice a year, fortification of food, setting up of four
regional laboratories under the Food & Nutrition Board to test the quality of nutrition provided to the
ICDS beneficiaries, ICT based Real Time Monitoring, proposed measurement of height of children at the
AWCs, advocating consumption of healthy balanced diet through Nutrition Education Programme, issue
of negative alerts to the parents of underweight children, etc. It is hoped that these steps besides various
schemes of MH&FW would yield positive results.

BETI BACHAO BETI PADHAO (BBBP)
The Committee notes that the Child Sex Ratio (CSR), an indicator of gender discrimination towards
girl child stands at 918 girls per 1000 boys in the age group of 0-6 years, much lower than the figure
of 927 in 2001 Census. The unabated decline in CSR since 1961 is a matter of grave concern as it
reflects the low status of women in our society and indicates her disempowerment. There are many areas/
pockets in the country where the child sex ratio has been below the national average or declining and
such areas/pockets have been expanding due to various socio-economic factors. As per the information
available to the Committee by the Ministry, out of 640 districts across the country, 429 districts have
declining CSR. The Committee feels that the declining CSR is a major social problem requiring urgent
remedial action before it is too late. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should
make concerted efforts to check this declining CSR in a mission mode manner and also to put more
aggressiveness, energy and resources to reverse this trend. (Para 5.3)
Action Taken
The observations of the Committee have been noted. BBBP scheme addresses the declining Child
Sex Ratio (CSR) and related issues of women empowerment over a life-cycle continuum through a
Nation-wide Advocacy and Media Campaign and multi-sectoral, multi-departmental, convergent action in
selected 161 districts.
Arresting decline in Child Sex Ratio is a challenging process as it is a symptom of socio-cultural
mindset and gender bias against girl child that results from conditioning through socialization of several
years. Thus, mindset change is a slow process and changes on ground would take reasonable amount
of time to be visible.
The scheme is being implemented through the State Govt./UT Administration. MWCD is the nodal
Ministry for the implementation and monitoring and line ministries i.e. MoHRD & MoH&FW are responsible
for ensuring relevant sectoral inputs for the effective implementation of the scheme.

Secretary, WCD which provides guidance and support. At State level by State Task Force headed by the
Chief Secretary and in UTs the Task Force is headed by Administrator to coordinate the Multi-Sectoral
implementation of the Scheme.
The Multi-sectoral District Action Plans have been operationalized in all State/UTs. Currently the
Scheme is getting implemented in 161 districts with 100% funding to districts by GoI. Capacity-building
programmes and Trainings have been imparted to Master Trainers to further strengthen capacities of
district level officials and frontline workers/stakeholders in all 161 districts.
The stewardship of DMs/ DCs has enabled meaningful innovations at the field level and several
initiatives focusing on advocacy and community engagements have been undertaken by the Districts
across the country.
Advocacy and Media Campaign at PAN India level with a 360 degree approach with includes Radio
and TV campaign, Cinema Halls, community engagement through Song & Drama Division, and online
digital media. Radio, TV and IVR based 139 (the universal railway enquiry number) campaigns.

workers, they are paid monthly honoraria as decided by Government from time to time.
The Ministry has been insisting upon the States/UTs to enhance the honorarium of AWWs/AWHs
from their financial sources especially keeping in view the enhanced allocations made to them under the

is on the lower side. Further, States/UTs pay different amounts as honorarium. It is Committee's
concerted opinion that AWWs and AWHs should be compensated properly.
