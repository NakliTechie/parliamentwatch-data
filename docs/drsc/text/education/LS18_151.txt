REPORT
I       INTRODUCTION
1.1    The development of women in India, who constitute around half of the country’s population, has occupied the
centre-stage in our development planning since independence. However, it was in 1980s that women were recognised as
a separate target group and given their rightful place in developmental planning by including a separate chapter viz.,
‘Women and Development’ in the Sixth Plan Document (1980-85). This marked the breakaway from a welfare
approach to women’s problems in the earlier years. Since then, all efforts of the government have been directed towards
bringing women into the mainstream of the national development process by raising their overall status -social,
economic, political and legal - at par with that of men. The Government realised that real development cannot take roots
if it bypasses women, who represent the very kernel around which social change must take shape.
1.2    Setting up of the Department of Women and Child Development in 1985, in fact, was a big step in providing a

focused and coordinated impetus to the holistic development of women and children who together constitute 70% of the
total population of the country.
1.3    The Department formulates plans, policies and programmes, moots proposals to enact and amend legislation
affecting women and children and guides and coordinates the efforts of both governmental and non-governmental
organizations working in the field of women and child development. Apart from this, the Department implements
certain innovative programmes for women and children which are in the areas of training and capacity building,
employment and income generation, welfare and support services, awareness generation and gender sensitization.
II      BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1    Plan allocation for the Department for the year 2004-05 is Rs. 2400.00 crores against a projected of Rs. 2500
crores. The Committee was informed that the amount demanded for the World Bank assisted ICDS projects, ICDS III
and UDISHA in 2004-05 is Rs. 330.00 crore against the demand of Rs. 685 crores in 2003-04 due to completion of
these projects in September 2004. The Committee was also given to understand that slow pace of expenditure during the
first six months of 2002-04 and resultant reduced allocation at the RE stage was basis of allocation in 2004-05 by the
Planning Commission.
2.2    The Committee notes that the plan allocation for 2003-04 was Rs. 2600.00 crores which was reduced to Rs. 2150
crores at the RE stage and the actual expenditure had been only Rs. 2053.33 crores. The Committees notes that

The reasons given for such reduction were mainly non-receipt of utilization certificates, non-approval of
restructuring/strengthening of WDCs and societies under Swashakti, time taken to set up new infrastructure, civil
construction and identifying suitable project implementing agencies.
2.3    Financial performance of the Department in respect all the programmes both for women and children during the
Ninth Plan presents a very dismal picture. In respect of many programmes, outlay had to be reduced at the RE stage, in
some cases even less than half the allocated amount and that drastically reduced amount also remained under utilized.

III.    INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
3.1    The ICDS is the main scheme of the Department which aims at enhancement of survival and development of the
children upto 6 years of age. It also takes care of pregnant women and lactating mothers by providing the services of
supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-ups, referral service, pre-school and non-formal education, health
and nutrition education. It is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme in which Central Government is responsible for providing
operating costs on infrastructure and the payment for .Anganwadi workers/helpers. The States are responsible for
implementation and providing supplementary nutrition. NOGs are involved through contract system as far as food
content is concerned.

3.4    Due to slow pace to operationalise all pending projects and Anganwadi Centres, Supreme Court had to direct the
States to operationalise all sanctioned Anganwadi Centres by 30.6.2004. The Supreme Court has also directed the
Government of India to file an affidavit indicating the time by which it proposes to increase the number of Anganwadi
Centres to cover all the 14 lakh habitations. The Common Minimum Programme of the Government has also envisaged
universalisation of the ICDS Scheme. The Committee was informed that States have, therefore, been requested to
furnish their requirement of additional Projects/AWCs to enable the Department to formulate a suitable proposal for
approval.
3.5    The Committee notes that so far information has been received only from 17 States. Information from Bihar, U.P.
Tamil Nadu and Jharkhand is yet to be received. The Committee observes that only on receipt of information from all
the States, proposal for additional Projects/AVCs will be formulated and processed for approval. The Committee
apprehends that by the time this whole exercise is completed, financial year would also be coming to end. The
Committee therefore recommends that the Department should vigorously pursue this issue with all the remaining states,
specially those states lagging behind.
3.6    The Committee observes that despite the full utilization of funds during the last three years, the scheme is not
reaching the targeted group and is lacking in desired impact at the ground level. A large number of children under the
age of five die every month in India and most of these deaths were due to malnutrition. In response to a query about
increasing deaths of the children in this age category, the Department stated  that the under five mortality rate in the
country is 94.9 per 1000.
3.7    The fact that nearly 95 out of 1000 children under five die is nothing less than shocking. The latest position of
IMR is not known as this figure is for the year 1998. Such a high IMR is certainly a very sad reflection on our efforts for
child development. ICDS’s main aim, in fact, is to lower the infant mortality because it focuses not only on child care
but on pregnant and lactating mothers as well. If the INIR is taken as indicator, the performance/effectivity of the ICDS
tells a sad story. The inability to operationalise ICDS projects, the Committee feels, is the main reason for this situation.
The requirement must be much more as the sanctioned projects are as per the 1991 census. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department should take up this issue with the States/implementing agencies at higher level to
chalk out an action plan to be taken up at emergency level and in time bound manner. The Committee also emphasizes
the need for constant and effective monitoring of implementation of this scheme. Having independent channel of getting
feedback is also oI’ prime importance which needs to be put in place immediately.
3.8    Under the I.CDS Scheme, the State/UTs are responsible for providing supplementary nutrition out of States
Outlays. On being asked about the reason of negligible supplementary feeding being received from certain States and
the constraints faced by them in providing this, it was informed by the Department that the possible reasons could be the
fund-crunch in the States, procedural delays in procurement and transportation of supplementary nutrition, absence or
lack of Anganwadi Workers/Helpers etc. The Government of India has been emphasizing on States, from time to time,
to make adequate provisions in the budget and to ensure that supplementary nutrition was provided to all eligible
beneticiaries as per the norms. The Department informed the Committee that in a PIL, the Supreme Court had also
directed the States to ensure that supplementary nutrition was provided to all beneficiaries as per the norms of the
scheme for 300 days in a year.
3.9    The Committee notes that the number of beneficiaries covered so far was not commensurate with the huge
expenditure incurred on the project. State’s share in SNP was neither adequate nor timely. The Committee further notes
that the benefits, if any, were not actually reaching to the beneficiaries. Obviously, the scheme was not being
implemented earnestly. lt points towards a very unfortunate situation. The Committee recommends that a specific plan
of action in consultation with the State Governments, public representatives at village/block levels and NGOs should be
prepared to ensure that provisions of supplementary nutrition is tackled effectively.
3.10  The Committee notes that the Central Government has sanctioned construction of 7600 Anganwadi Centres at a
cost of Rs. 95 crore in 2002-03 to cater the needs of North ­Eastern States. With this, the ICDS Projects in North-
Eastern States have been brought at par with the World Bank-assisted projects where civil works are the main
additionality. While appreciating this development, the Committee would like to point out that the norm of one
Anganwadi Centre for 700 population in tribal projects needs to be relooked into/revised in the case of Manipur. Instead
of one AWC for 700 population, one centre per hillock should be the criteria for Manipur and other States having

3.11  The Committee was informed that schemes relating to nutrition and health of children were being implemented by
many other Department /Ministries than the Department of Women & Child Development. The Ministry of Health and
Family Welfare was responsible for immunization, eradication of polio, nutritional anemia control and Reproductive
Child Health programme. The Department of Secondary and Higher Education was providing Mid-day meal for
children in schools which ,had an allocation of Rs. 1175 crores during 2003-04. Prime Minister Gramodaya Yojana also
had nutritional component in it. Ministries of Food as well as Rural Development also have schemes having nutritional
component for children.
3.12  The Committee is surprised to know about so many schemes of child nutrition being implemented simultaneously
by different Departments/Ministries. Incidence of large scale malnutrition in children indicates that there is serious
lacuna in the existing system. It is incomprehensible to the Committee as to why there is malnutrition in the children
despite the number of schemes operated through different Departments as also by the State Governments. The
Committee feels that multiplicity of schemes as well as Departments is also creating confusion leading to wastage and
duplication of resources and efforts. The Committee recommends that a coordinated approach amongst all the
concerned Departments/Ministries is the need of the hour. The Department of Women and Child Development should
be a nodal agency for all such scheme to achieve optimum results. Inter-departmental meetings should be held regularly
to review the progress on nutritional level of the children.
3.13  The Committee notes that ICDS takes the major share of the Budgetary allocation of the Department. Success of
the scheme largely depends on the functioning of Anganwadi workers (AWWs). The Committee is, however, not
satisfied with the plight of these workers. The meagre pecuniary benefits and poor facilities do not attract people to
work as AWWs. The Committee is surprised to see that their role still remains undefined; they are asked to do all kind
of odd works such as census operation, distribution of drugs etc. This puts extra pressure on them and their main role is
diluted.
3.14  The Committee was also given to understand that status of Anganwadi workers was not uniform in respect of their
method of recruitment, salary/honorarium, duties assigned etc. The Committee strongly feels that the Department should
undertake a review of status of Anganwadi worker and bring out uniformity as far as possible. The Committee is also of
the opinion that their role needs to be defined precisely. They should be provided adequate supports, physical &
financial and incentives to fulfil their responsibility. The government should make them symbols of ‘Asha and Aastha’
for others.

V.     CRECHE/DAY CARE CENTRES
5.1    The Scheme of running Creches/Day care centres was started in 1975 to provide day care services for the children
(0-5 yrs) of mainly casual, migrant, agricultural and construction labourers. The scheme is framed to cater to very low
economic group and, therefore, only those children whose parent’s total income does not exceed Rs. 1800/- p.m. are
covered under the scheme. The scheme is being implemented by the Central Social Welfare Board and two other
national level voluntary organizations, namely Indian Council for Child Welfare and Bharatiya Adimjati Sevak Sangh
all over the country.
5.2    Allocation for the scheme during 2004-05 is Rs. 27.00 crores. The Committee notes that during 2003-04, the plan
provision of Rs. 18.00 crores was reduced to Rs. 7.00 crores at the RE stage and the actual expenditure was just Rs. 6.03
crores. The reason given for reduction at RE stage was that additional funds allocated during the year keeping in view
the anticipated revision of financial norms could not be utilized as no final decision could be made. The other reason
given is non-release of sanctioned grants due to late receipt of accounts and utilisation certificates from many State
Boards.

VI.    INFORMATION, MASS MEDIA & PUBLICATION
6. l    The Committee notes that Rs. 35.00 crores was earmarked for this scheme during the, Tenth Plan. Year-wise
allocation in the first three years of the Plan period, the Committee notes, had to be invariably reduced at RE stage and
even the reduced allocation could not be utilized fully.
6.2    The Department explained that a sum of Rs. 230.92 lakhs was sanctioned to DAVP (against their demand) towards
the cost of advertising in newspapers, production and broadcast of weekly sponsored radio programme and telecast of a
TV serial and they could use only Rs. I 3 l .76 lakh. Some of the activities like production of a tele-serial at a cost of
Rs.125.00 lakh, display of Computerized Moving Animation Display System (CMADS) and publicity through
hoardings/wall painting could not be executed as the proposal for serial production was not approved and the publicity
through CMADS was reported as not having the desired impact as Municipal laws did not permit hoardings and
paintings on walls. Therefore, the expenditure could not be utilized in the projected manner.
6.3    Information, Mass Media and Publication, the Committee believes, are very important components of any
activity/scheme to help fulfill its objective. In the absence of publicity, even a very good scheme would be rendered
ineffective, if beneficiaries were not aware of it. Apart from this, it has educative role as well, clearing many a
myths/misconception that people may be having about implication etc. of a scheme. The Committee finds
implementation of this scheme tardy. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends to take up its implementation
with full vigour. Services of Akashwani having access to 35 crores audiences should also be considered.
VII.  BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA

community attitudes towards her. The Committee was informed that a proposal for transfer of this Scheme to the State
Governments during the Tenth Plan is at present before the National Development Council.
7.2    The Committee notes that only token allocation is being made for this scheme since the beginning of the Tenth
Plan. This is the third year of the Plan period and still no final decision has been taken. Not only this, it seems that the
Department itself is undecided about the fate of this scheme. In response to a specific query by the Committee, the
Department has informed that interaction with the State Secretaries of the major implementing States has been initiated
in order to make the scheme more effective. The recast version of the scheme will be circulated for comments of the
State Govts. The budget allocation for the scheme will be enhanced at the RE stage, after a complete review of the
scheme has been made.

XVII. OVERLAPPING OF SCHEMES
17.1  The Committee notes that there are two similar schemes for women’s welfare, one is Swawlamban Scheme whose
objective is to promote women development through provision of employment by training them in traditional and non-
traditional trades such as computers, electronics, embroidery and weaving etc whereas the objective of the scheme of
Support to Training and Employment Programme (STEP) for women, is to provide training for skill upgradation in
traditional sectors like agriculture, animal husbandry, handlooms, handicrafts khadi and village industry etc.
Implementing agencies for both the schemes include Women Development Corporation, Public Undertakings,
Cooperatives, Women Development Centers of Universities, Voluntary Organizations etc.
17.2  On being asked about the rationale behind having two parallel schemes with identical purpose, target and
implementing agencies, the Department informed that both the Swawlamban, earlier known as NORAD, and Support to
Training and Employment Programme (STEP) Schemes aim at providing training and skill upgradation to women to
facilitate them obtain some kind of employment or self employment. The two schemes, however, differ in the following
aspects :
(a)   Unlike the Swawlamban Scheme which is a kind of stand alone scheme for training of women for a fixed
period ranging from 3 months to 12 months, STEP is a more comprehensive programme which is usually
undertaken on a project basis for a period of 2-3 years (b) The number of beneficiaries in a training project
under Swawlamban Scheme ranges from 30 to 100. Whereas the STEP Scheme, produces between 500 to
1000 beneficiaries in a single project. (c) The Swawlamban Scheme caters to both traditional and non-
traditional sectors on the one hand, the STEP scheme covers only the traditional sectors.
17.3  The Committee reiterates that the Department should revisit the schemes having overlapping objectives with a
view not only to avoid inevitable confusions but also to have focused and optimum utilization of the scarce resources.

VIII. ASSISTANCE FOR CONSTRUCTION/EXPANSION OF HOSTEL BUILDINGS FOR WORKING  WOMEN
WITH DAY CARE CENTRES FOR CHILDREN.
         The Committee notes that a number of remedial measures have been taken by the Department. State Level
Empowered Committees (SLECs) have been set up which include technical experts in the field to examine the
proposals, and to recommend only good and viable proposal to the Department. Universities/colleges/local urban bodies
have also been made eligible. In order to attract good and viable proposals, the scheme is being publicized in the
national/regional newspapers.         (Para 8.4)
         The Committee finds this was yet another good scheme which somehow was not being successfully operated.
After so many years of its existence, there exist only 914 Working Women Hostels with 56,214 beneficiaries in such a
vast country by the year 2003-04.                                                                                                           (Para 8.5)
         The Committee notes that the scheme is under a process of revision which is at an advanced stage of
consideration. The Committee hopes that the revised scheme will be implemented in the current year, attracting a good
number of viable proposals. The Committee has been given to understand that there exist a system whereunder
performance of implementing agencies is reviewed through periodical reports from State Govts., review meetings and
field visits by the area officers. The Committee is of the view that surprise visits /inspections will strengthen the
monitoring mechanism.(Para 8.6)
IX.    SUPPORT  TO  TRAINING  AND  EMPLOYMENT  PROGRAMME  FOR  WOMEN  (STEP)
         The Committee did not find any reasons for reducing the RE in any document supplied to the Committee including
Performance Budget. The Committee recommends that the Department should be careful in furnishing the reasons for
variation and should give substantive reply in support of reduction/enhancement of allocation.          (Para 9.4)
         The Committee is baffled to see that achievement in the year 2001-02 and 2002-03 were many time more than the
target fixed. Whereas in the year 2003-04, it was just other way i.e. against the target of 40,000 only 16,650 could be
achieved. Confusion is worse confounded to see that in years 2001-02 and 2002-03 when achievement was shown
manifold, were the years when the funds utilization was weak. A convincing explanation for this would certainly make
the Committee wiser next time.(Para 9.5)
         The Committee recommends that random checks should be made to verify the authenticity of figures and also
recommends that efforts should be made to implement the scheme in all the States/ UTs without fail.(Para 9.6)
X.     NATIONAL  COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
         The Committee notes that a large number of complaints were pending over the years as shown in the table above
— “Complaints on which no action was found feasible /necessary or under process/pending”. It would have given a true
picture had complaints on which no action was found feasible/necessary been put separately from those under
process/pending. Reasons for delay in processing should be identified and removed at the earliest.             (Para 10.2)
         The Committee recommends that all the States should be pursued to set up Women
Commission at the earliest.                                                                                            (Para 10.3)
         The Committee believes that these amendments were long over due. It, therefore, recommends that immediate
steps be taken to complete consultations and other formalities for bringing drafts of these laws before Parliament with
no loss of further time.(Para 10.5)
XI.    SWASHAKTI
         It is baffling to the Committee again that under this scheme also achievement far exceeded the targets in the year
when funds could not be utilized. The Committee has been informed that the reason for under-utilisation of funds was
the proposal for restructuring/strengthening of women development corporations/societies in nine project States being
not approved by the World Bank and IFAD, the funding agencies. The Committee expresses its serious concern over
this development which indicates that the funding agencies were not apprised about the anticipated/proposed measures
for the smooth running of the scheme at the initial stage itself. The Committee hopes that such instances will not occur
in future.(Para 11.3)
XII.  SWADHAR
         The Committee is not happy to note that the scheme was limited to only ten States/UTs and the Central Social
Welfare Board. It appears that the scheme was not getting good response despite the three tier monitoring mechanism.

The Committee recommends that all out efforts should be made to popularize such useful scheme in all States/UTs. A

XIII. RESCUE OF VICTIMS OF TRAFFIKING
         The Committee finds this scheme as need of the hour. With the changing scenario all over, cases of commercial
exploitation of women and children are being reported frequently on unprecedented scale. The Committee would like to
caution the Department to take up its implementation seriously in the light of experiences it might have had with the
Swadhar.
                                                                                                                                     (Para 13.3)
XIV.GENDER BUDGET ANALYSIS
         The Committee expresses dissatisfaction over the indifferent attitude of the Department towards this aspect. It is
sad state of affairs when the Department whose mandate is to analyse women specific schemes is not reflecting it in the
Budget, the effort to analyse budget from a gender-based perspective by all the Ministries will be just eyewash. If the
Department is sincere in achieving its objective, the Committee recommends that it should figure out the gender-­based
allocation/targets prominently in its Budget. The Department may also take up the matter with other Ministries on the
issue.           (Para 14.3)
XV.  INSURANCE FOR WOMEN WORKERS
         The Committee observes that Insurance is an essential economic support to women during crises and recommends
that efforts should be made to link insurance with health programmes. The Committee would like the Department to
explore feasibility of linking insurance to other financial services - savings and credit - which promotes long-term
insurance coverage and work out the modalities in consultation with some renowned NGOs who have already started
this exercise.  (Para 15.2)
XVI.NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR CHILDREN
         The Committee was informed that the Bill needed some revision with a view to strengthening the Commission.
The Committee recommends that the revision of the Bill be expedited at the earliest and introduced as soon as
possible.        (Para 16.2)
         The Committee feels that formulation of the National Plan of Action for Children is a very important document
which includes not only goal & objectives but strategies as well for the current decade for improving nutritional status
of children, reducing IMR and MMR, increasing enrolment ration, reducing dropouts, increasing coverage of
immunization etc. Formulation of this has taken a long time since it was announced in August 2002. The Committee
recommends that all the formalities relating to this document should be completed at the earliest and brought before the
Parliament for its approval.         (Para 16.3)
         The Committee finds that the National Children’s Board was formed for the planning, review and proper
coordination of the multiplicity of services aimed at improving the needs of children and is headed by none other than
the Prime Minister himself. The Committee is not aware of the reasons as to why the Board could not be constituted
after its term expired in May 1999. And despite this, a token amount of Rs. 1 lakh is being made every year thereafter
including 2004-05. It sufficiently indicates that there is no idea for doing away with this body or shifting its
responsibility to some other body. The Committee would like to be apprised of latest position on the constitution of the
National Children’s Board.(Para 16.4)
XVII. OVERLAPPING OF SCHEMES
         The Committee reiterates that the Department should revisit the schemes having overlapping objectives with a
view not only to avoid inevitable confusions but also to have focused and optimum utilization of the scarce
resources.       (Para 17.3)
XVIII.  FUNCTIONING OF NGOs
         The Committee was informed that some of the schemes of the Department were being implemented through
NGOs. Idea of NGOs involvement, no doubt, is a noble one, functioning of some of the NGOs, however, leaves much
to be desired. Complaints of misuse/ misappropriation of government grants by some NGOs are reported from time to
time. There are allegations that in some cases different organization were being run by one person and same NGO was
receiving grants under different names. The Committee is aware that not all NGOs were defaulters and many of them

were rendering yeoman service. Department gave a list of NGOs blacklisted by it. The Committee feels that simply
blacklisting such NGOs would not deter the delinquent ones and they might come back with change names/addresses.
Government needs to streamline the procedure of their selection as well as regular monitoring of their work. We should
have penal provisions for recovering the grants given to defaulting NGOs. The NGOs doing good job needs to be
suitably rewarded so as to motivate others.    (Para 18.1)
         The Committee feels that there exist no restriction for having a maximum number of NGOs in a particular area.
The Committee has noticed that out of 60 NGOs provided financial assistance amounting to Rs. 2.53 crores under the
National Creche Fund during 2003-04, 31 are from Andhra Pradesh with 18 NGOs based in one district alone. The
Committee is of the view that there should be specific guidelines whereunder number of NGOs in a district (subject to
area) should be indicated.                                                                                                                      (Para 18.2)
         The Committee has also observed that out of 463 NGOs provided financial assistance under Swawlamban Scheme
during 2003-04, 98 were provided funds for running computer institutes and 55 for readymade garments. The
Committee is of the view that NGOs involved in imparting technical education, even though of a very low standard
should be certified for running such courses.                                                                                                     (Para 18.3)
         The Committee’s attention has also been drawn to the fact that feedback from NGOs implementing various
schemes of the Department is not being received on a regular basis. The Department itself had admitted that under
‘Swawlamban’ and ‘STEP’, feedback regarding status of employment of the training after the completion of the training
programme has not been forthcoming. Under ‘Swawlamban’ 1650 utilisation certificates and under ‘STEP’ 60
utilisation certificates are awaited which confirms Committee’s apprehension. The Committee strongly feels that
besides strengthening the monitoring mechanism, there is a need for making the sanctioning/approving authority
accountable for the non-performing/under-­performing NGOs.   (Para 18.4)
