INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME
Recommendations/Observations
Honorarium of AWWs and AWHs
3.27 The ICDS Scheme envisages AWWs and AWHs as honorary workers who are paid a
monthly honorarium. The Committee has been informed that with effect from 1 November, 2011,
Honorarium of AWWs of AWCs and that of AWHs and AWWs of Mini-Anganwadi Centres had been
enhanced by `750.00 per month respectively on the last honorarium drawn. Anganwadi Karyakartni
Bima Yojana (AKBY) under the LIC’s Social Security Scheme is one of the welfare measures
extended to the grassroots functionaries of the ICDS Scheme.
3.28 The Committee appreciates the initiative of the Ministry to enhance the honorarium of
AWWs and AWHs. However, the Committee is still concerned about the welfare of the AWWs and
AWHs in view of the fact that the inflationary trends have increased the cost of living due to rising
prices of not just food items but other things too. Given this reality, even the enhanced honorarium
seems to be a paltry sum. The Committee believes that the Ministry should review the enhancement
of the honorarium of AWWs and AWHs. Also, the State Governments should be urged to give
additional honorarium to them so that the AWWs and AWHs do not find it difficult to make both
ends meet. The Committee has, time and again, favored all support, from the Centre and States
to be given to the AWWs and AWHs. The Committee believes that these functionaries of the ICDS
Scheme, working at the grassroot level perform multiple tasks assigned to them and their
commitment in providing quality service to the targeted beneficiaries would only be firm if their
honorarium and service conditions are made better.
Action Taken
With regard to Paras 3.27 and 3.28 it is stated that Government of India has been enhancing
the honorarium of AWWs and AWHs from time to time. Honorarium of AWWs has been last
increased by `1500/- on the last honorarium drawn by them and that of AWHs and workers of
Mini-AWWs by `750/- per month, w.e.f. 1.4.2011. The States/UTs also provide additional honoraria
to AWWs/AWHs from their own resources. Government of India also has been urging the States/
UTs from time to time to pay additional monetary incentives (letter no. 19-17/2010-CD-I dated
26.10.2011).
However, the recommendation of the Hon’ble Committee has been noted.
HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN
Recommendations/Observations
8.4 The Committee is dismayed to find that even after the revision of the scheme, there is no
2

3
good response from the States/UTs for constructing working women’s hostels. In the year 2011-12,
only one viable proposal was received from the Government of Kerala for construction of working
women’s hostels at Vanchikkavala, Idukki District by Kerala State Women’s Development
Corporation Ltd. The reason for a lacklustre response is attributed mainly on the condition of the
revised scheme of providing financial assistance for construction of hostel building to be given only
on public land. The Committee believes that this condition limits the scope of the scheme. Getting
public land for the construction of hostels for women is not easy. The Committee takes note of
the fact that the Ministry has also admitted that it might be necessary to re-consider the decision
to allow construction only on public land. The Committee believes that the Ministry has ruled out
the contribution and participation of the private players. In the age of Public-Private-Partnership
(PPP) model, this condition laid down by the Ministry is not in tune with the practices of the
present times where contribution and participation of the private sector in the development
processes cannot be ignored. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to rethink and
review features of the scheme in consultation with the States/UTs to come to a solution as early
as possible to remove the stumbling blocks in the effective implementation of the scheme.
Action Taken
Revised scheme has been approved by the Ministry of Finance with certain conditions.
Assistance for construction grant on public land only is one such condition. This provision has
been made to exercise some control on the property created by public fund and minimize misuse
or under-utilization of the hostel facilities. Keeping in view the non-availability of land and/or high
cost of land at metros and cities it has been decided that financial assistance shall be extended for
hostels to be run in rented premises also. To take the help of Corporate Houses, a matching grant
in the ratio 50:50 for the construction of the hostel building in public land has also been added
in the revised scheme. To enhance the coverage of beneficiaries, Panchayati Raj Institutions and
Cantonment Boards have also been added to the implementing agencies.
The State Governments/UT Administrations should ensure that public land for working
women’s hostels is earmarked in all institutional areas and economic zones being set up in the
States, keeping in view the employment potential for women in the area. For this purpose, the State
Government may direct all Development Authorities to undertake this exercise of earmarking public
land for such hostels in new/existing colonies.
Depending on the demand and need assessment, land for hostels under this scheme should
also be earmarked in smaller towns/non-metropolitan areas as part of town and country planning
by the State Government through the district administration. Recommendation for this purpose may
be made by the District Women W elfare Committee to the State Government/District administration.
State Government/UT Administration should consider enactment of legislation or amendment to the
existing laws, if deemed necessary, to ensure earmarking and availability of public land for the
hostels.

Recommendations/Observations
2.4 While appreciating the utilization trends of the Ministry with respect to allocated funds for
the last few years, the Committee also takes note of the daunting challenges before the Ministry
with respect to certain facts like declining child sex ratio, underweight children, marked increase
in anemia levels, which have to be focused upon in the Twelfth Five Year Plan, necessitating a
holistic approach toward enhancing the care and protection of women and children. Keeping in
view the three-pronged programmatic intervention of the Ministry viz. Child Survival, Development
and Protection, Holistic Empowerment of Women and Social Support and Support to Women and
Child related Programmes, the Committee is of the view that with the allocation at hand for the
year 2012-13, the Ministry might face constraints in implementing its existing schemes/
programmes, initiating new schemes and re-structuring the ICDS Scheme. The Committee feels
that this Ministry being committed to the cause and concerns of women and children should not
face any constraints in catering to these vulnerable sections of the society. Inadequate fund
allocation not in sync with the projected requirements of the Ministry should not be a bottleneck.
The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to take this matter with the Planning
Commission for additional allocation for the Ministry in consonance with the projected requirement
of the Ministry. Also, the Ministry needs to strategise and prioritise its targets and also take steps
for optimum and judicious utilization of allocated funds.
Action Taken
The Hon’ble Committee’s observations are noted for compliance.
Recommendations/Observations
2.7 Attention of the Committee has been drawn to the new schemes for women proposed to
be implemented in 2012-13. The new schemes and budgetary allocations for them are as follows:-
Women’s Helpline ( `2.00 crore), Development of Distance Learning Programme on the Right of
Women ( `10 lakhs), Assistance to States for implementation of Protection of Women from
Domestic Violence Act ( `20.00 crore) and One Stop Crisis Centre ( `5.00 crore). The Committee
finds that the new schemes proposed have been provided paltry allocation in this year. Even for
the existing schemes for women, the outlays for the current year seem to be on the lower side
with respect to schemes like Hostels for Working Women, STEP, Central Social Welfare Board and
National Mission for Empowerment of Women. The Committee is surprised to find that no funds
have been allocated for Swayamsidha which was supposedly the main vehicle of women’s
empowerment in the Eleventh Plan. The Committee feels that launching of new schemes without
adequate allocations for them is nothing but a cosmetic measure for addressing the concerns of
women. Improvement in the condition of women would only be seen if concerted efforts of policy
making are matched with substantial funds for witnessing positive outcomes for the welfare of
women.
Action Taken
With regard to the scheme for Working Women Hostel , the budget allocation for the
financial year 2011-12 was `10.00 crore and the revised estimate was `5.00 crore. The Scheme
has been revised and notified on 26.10.2010. As per revised scheme, construction grant could be
allowed on public land only. This provision has been made to have control on the property created

7
by the public fund and minimize misuse or under utilization of the hostel. The budget provision for
financial year 2012-13 is `10.00 crore. State Governments have been advised to forward proposals
after making need based demand survey for Working Women’s Hostel. The State Governments have
been reminded on 19th January, 2011 to expedite demand survey and send proposals as per the
schematic norms of the revised scheme so that benefits may be extended to more and more needy
working women. In absence of viable proposals, projects could not be sanctioned; hence the
expenditure on lower side.
The budget provision for financial year 2012-13 is `10.00 crore. The number of hostels to
be sanctioned and number of beneficiaries would depend on feasible proposals received in the
Ministry, complete in all respect.
With regard to NMEW, lower outlay for scheme in 2012-13 is `25.00 crore. NMEW
projected `63.00 crore in the MWCD budgetary demand to the Planning Commission for 2012-13.
However, the Planning Commission approved the Ministry’s demand with substantial cuts. The
demands of various programmes under MWCD were reduced which has been reflected in the final
allocation of `25.00 crore for NMEW.
The report has raised the issue of convergence of schemes (page No. 49, Section XVIII)
with the suggestion to have an evaluation done of its schemes which may require convergence and
coordination with schemes of other Departments/Ministries/State Governments. The NMEW has
been given the mandate to address issues of convergence of schemes and programmes impacting
women cutting across departments.
With regard to STEP Scheme, it was launched as a Central Sector Scheme in 1986-87
and was revised in 2009. The allocation of funds during 2011-12 was `11.50 crore (RE) and for
2012-13 is `20.00 crore (BE) which is adequate.
With regard to CSWB, the RE 2011-2012 (plan) was proposed as `214.17 crore and BE
2012-13 (plan) is `170.0 crore. During RE 2011-12 a sum of `36.99 crore was proposed for Short
Stay Home. However now it has been decided that this scheme would be merged with Swadhar
scheme of the Ministry, and named as ‘Swadhar Griha’. Accordingly NIL provision was made
during BE 2012-13 for this scheme by CSWB. Besides additional funds for the arrears of MACP
were provided at RE stage and released to Field Staff and salary arrears to Border Area Project
employees during 2011-12. The difference on this account was `150 lakhs. Allocation with respect
to other schemes has been projected on realistic basis keeping in view the actual expenditure during
the previous year.
With regard to new schemes for women  for 2012-13, the budgetary allocations kept are
only token provisions, since all these schemes are only at the proposal stage and before the
schemes could get implemented, they would require to go through different stages of approval
process, which are time-consuming. As per the present status of formulation of these schemes, the
schemes can be started only by the end of this financial year, after getting due approvals. Hence
the schemes will not suffer due to paucity of funds in the current year. From 2013-14 onwards
adequate outlays will be provided in the budget for effective implementation of the schemes.
Recommendations/Observations
2.8 The Committee would like to highlight another area of concern in the budgetary allocations
for children in the Union Budget. The ‘Child Budget’ as a proportion of total budget outlay by
the Union Government shows a slight upturn from 4.6 per cent in 2011-12 to 4.8 per cent in
2012-13. The Committee finds this percentage to be grossly inadequate for addressing the various
needs of children. Not just this, sector wise prioritization of the Child Budget continues to be highly
skewed in favour of Child Education and Child Development where around 72 per cent and 23 per

8
cent of funds were allocated out of the total Child Budget respectively. Allocation for Child Health
and Child Protection have been accorded low priority as only 3.8 per cent and 0.93 per cent have
given for these two sectors respectively. Given the poor status of children who are the most
vulnerable section of our society, the Committee feels that adequate budgetary allocation with
emphasis more on Child Health and Child Protection needs to be prioritized and given urgent
attention.
Action Taken
The magnitude of the Child Budget i.e. the aggregate outlay for child specific schemes
as a proportion of the total budget outlay has increased steadily over the years. From 3.8% in
2008-09 (Revised Estimates), this rose to 4.5% in 2011-12 (B.E.) and in 2012-13. Sectoral
allocations are compiled in Statement 22 of the Expenditure Budget, which captures all child
specific schemes in the Union Budget. From an initial eight “Demands for Grants” in 2008-09, the
Statement now covers over 19 “Demands for Grants”, marking a significant increase.
The Child Budget does not take into account allocations made for general services, some
of which are also available to children, as in the general Health infrastructure, and the Law
enforcement machinery and this accounts for the apparently skewed allocations for education and
development.
The Integrated Child Protection Scheme (ICPS) has been introduced from 2009-10 by the
Ministry. Since the scheme is being implemented though the States/UTs, allocation of funds under
the scheme is made as per requirement being projected by them. Some of the States/UTs are still
in the process of setting up of services delivery structures and identifying the need for further
services required for creating a safe environment for children. It is, therefore, expected that the
budgetary requirement and allocation under the scheme will gradually increase as more and more
structures and services become functional in all States/UTs.
INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME
Recommendations/Observations
3.5 The Committee is in full agreement with the initiative of the Ministry to restructure the
flagship scheme of ICDS so that several implementation issues of the scheme such as lack of
proper infrastructure, lack of technical expertise at field level, non-indexation of cost of
Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP), weak early childhood care and education component,
poor monitoring due to lack of staff, lack of support to AWWs on nutrition education and
monitoring of under nutrition etc are taken care of so that the benefits of the scheme reach to the
maximum number of women and children. The Committee notes the findings of nation-wide
evaluation of ICDS in 1500 selected AWCs by the Planning Commission and would like to highlight
that even though the benefits of ICDS Scheme are reaching the targeted beneficiaries, there is a
need to fine tune the service delivery system with better infrastructure and resources. The
Committee hopes that the re-structuring of ICDS would increase the outreach of the Scheme and
benefit women and children in far-flung areas and remote regions also.
Action Taken
With regard to Para 3.4 & 3.5, it is stated that the aforementioned observation by the
Parliamentary Standing Committee is only a statement of fact. There is no recommendation as
such. In order to address several gaps (Programmatic, management and institutional), Government
has approved on 24.9.2012 Ministry’s proposal for Strengthening and Restructuring of ICDS to be

9
implemented in Mission Mode within an overall allocation of `1,23,580 Crore as Government of
India share, during the Twelth Plan Period. Under this, a series of programmatic, management and
institutional reforms will be carried out which will, inter-alia , include flexible architecture –
flexibility to States in implementation for innovation, strengthening governance to partnerships with
civil society, PRIs, introducing APIPs, construction of buildings for 2 lakh AWCs, SNP cost
revision, decentralized planning, ICDS in a Mission Mode, provision of 5% AWC-cum-Creche,
revision of cost norms, etc.
Besides, the Ministry has drafted the ECCE policy, curriculum framework and standards and
put the same on the website for public comment.
This would be finalized and gradually mainstreamed in the ICDS.

3.8 The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry for satisfactorily fulfilling its financial
and physical targets. However, there are many areas of concern in the ICDS Scheme which halt
the progress and implementation of the scheme like inadequate infrastructure, reach of SNP to
women and children, availability of pre-school kits to children, poor monitoring, lack of staff etc.

Action Taken

INDIRA GANDHI MATRITV A SAHYOG YOJANA (IGMSY)
Recommendations/Observations
9.5 The Committee finds that the objective of the Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana of the

17
Ministry of the Women and Child Development is similar to the objective of the Janani Suraksha
Yojana of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Even though there are few differences in the
components of the two schemes, both aim to provide cash incentives to pregnant and lactating
mothers for their improved health and nutrition and also safe motherhood. The Committee believes
that with some kind of coordination mechanism between the two Ministries, a convergence of the
scheme for the welfare of pregnant and lactating women could be made. The Committee, therefore,
recommends to the Ministry to make conscious efforts for convergence of the two schemes
administered by two different Ministries to avoid duplicacy and ensure the reach of benefits to the
targeted women. Both these schemes are to be handled by AWWs and ANM functionaries of AWCs
and Primary Health Centres respectively. Here Panchayati Raj Institutions can play a very vital role,
not only in supervision of the schemes to ensure their effective implementation but also benefit the
targeted beneficiaries. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that all the stakeholders and
implementing agencies at the ground level may devise a mechanism for providing maximum benefit
to needy mothers.
Action Taken
Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) was launched in 2005 by Ministry of Health and Family
Welfare. It provides a differential one-time cash incentive to pregnant women for institutional/home
births through skilled assistance. JSY , however, does not address the issues regarding a woman’s
socio-economic compulsions to work right up to the last stage of pregnancy and resuming work
soon after child birth. Hence, introducing a modest maternity benefit to partly compensate for their
wage loss was recommended by the Planning Commission in the Eleventh Five Year Plan.
Before formulation of the details about the scheme by the Ministry, several rounds of
consultative meetings, discussions, etc. were held with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
to implement the IGMSY scheme under the larger umbrella of JSY having similar objectives. Since
the IGMSY scheme aims to compensate partial wage loss and promote better nutrition and health
behaviours so it was finally decided to implement the scheme by using existing ICDS structure.
In view of the above, the Ministry of Women and Child Development formulated a new
Scheme for pregnant and lactating (P&L) mothers called Indira Gandhi Matritva Sahyog Yojana
(IGMSY) – a Conditional Maternity Benefit Scheme. Under this Scheme, a  cash incentive of `4000
is to be provided directly to women aged 19 years and above for the first two live births subject
to a woman fulfilling specific conditions relating to maternal child health and nutrition. Cash
incentive is being provided in three instalments , between the second trimester of pregnancy till the
infant completes 6 months of age. However, there is no cash incentive under IGMSY at the time
of delivery since cash incentive for this is already provided under JSY .
Ministry of Women and Child Development is implementing the IGMSY Scheme in close
coordination with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. IGMSY is a demand-driven scheme and
depends on the availability of the health services on the supply side to enable the beneficiaries to fulfil
certain conditions like ANC check, IFA tablets, immunization, etc. to get the cash benefits. Hence,
there is convergence with the Health department as far as fulfilment of conditions is concerned.
In the light of above recommendations of the Standing Committee, the issue of further
convergence of JSY & IGMSY will be decided after the evaluation of the IGMSY scheme, which
Ministry is planning in the current financial year.

CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AUTHORITY (CARA)
Recommendations/ Observations
15.3 The Committee finds that the number of in-country adoptions has increased and has picked
up since 2010. As for inter-country adoptions, the numbers do not seem to be encouraging. The
Committee is of the view that the number of adoptions would increase considerably if a simple
system is put in place for prospective parents without much hassle. The decreasing number of
inter-country adoptions may possibly be due to reasons like delays in completion of legal and
procedural formalities before adopting the child. The Committee believes that the whole process of
adoption, be it in-country or inter-country should be made parent-friendly and child-friendly. The
facilitators of the adoption process should be sensitive to the sentiments and vulnerability of
orphaned or abandoned children and also of prospective parents.
Action Taken
It is well established that children grow and develop best in their own socio-cultural milieu
and thus, as also stipulated in the Hague Convention on Inter-country Adoption of which India is
a signatory, the Guidelines Governing Adoption of Children notified in 2011 give priority to in-
country adoptions over inter-country adoptions.
Also, with enhanced awareness, the demand for domestic adoptions is increasing as may
be seen from the rising number of such adoptions. As the number of children available in the
adoption agencies is limited, and there being a sizable number of cases of direct adoptions, the
availability of children for inter-country adoption has resultantly reduced.
However, for ensuring expeditious adoptions, the Guidelines provide for specific timelines
for various stages. These Guidelines are also aimed at making the process of adoption more parent
and child friendly, streamlined and transparent.
Further, the foreign prospective parents can now submit their applications online on CARA’s
web portal CARINGS, through an authorized adoption agency in their country. Applications received
centrally at CARA, after scrutiny, are transmitted to adoption agencies which have children available
for adoption. This not only expedites finding a suitable child, but also checks chances of
malpractice. The parents can track the progress of their application online though CARINGS.

21
To increase the number of children available for adoption, the Government is encouraging
setting up and management of additional adoption agencies under ICPS, by providing financial and
technical support. Further, linkages are also being developed between children’s homes and adoption
agencies so that adoptable children who are in the Homes are also considered for adoption.
However, delays have been noticed in some cases at the level of CWCs and the courts.
CARA is holding regular training and orientation programmes for functionaries of CWCs, adoption
agencies and members of the judiciary to help sensitize them to the needs of the children and
prospective parents.
Recommendations/Observations
15.4 Committee’s attention has been drawn to the reports about unauthorized adoption agencies
functioning in the country. The Committee knows that more than 250 adoption agencies are
registered on CARINGS. All existing child care institutions housing orphan, abandoned and
surrendered children are required to register with the State Government and apply for recognition
as Special Adoption Agency (SSA) as per provisions of the Juvenile Justice Act. After being
recognized as SAA, such agencies should register under CARINGS to be part of CARA network.
Presently, there are 72 RIPS’s which undertake both In-country as well as Inter-country adoptions
and 254 LAPAs which undertake only In-country adoption.
Action Taken
More than 250 adoption agencies are registered on CARINGS and are updating their data
on weekly basis. CARA is also providing training to all recognized agencies to minimize the
difference between the dashboard data and actual data. Central System of receiving adoption
agencies has been effective since January, 2012 to bring transparency in the adoption system.
Further, matching of special need children (mental/ physical) directly by foreign agencies for the
prospective parents has been introduced in the first week of June, 2012 to expedite rehabilitation
process of highly special needs children.

Recommendations/Observations
17.1 Gender Budgeting is a process that entails maintaining a gender perspective at various stages
like programme/policy formulation, assessment of needs of target groups, review of existing

23
policies and guidelines, allocation of resources, implementation of programme, impact assessment,
reprioritization of resources etc. Gender Budgeting involves dissection of the Government budget
to establish its gender-differential impacts and to translate gender commitments into budgetary
commitments.
17.2 Analysing the total magnitude of Gender Budget, there has been a marginal increase from
5.8 per cent in 2012-13. However, if the Budget Estimates figures of 2011-12 are compared with
the Revised Estimates, the magnitude of Gender Budget shows a decline from 6.2 per cent to 5.8
per cent. Also, new schemes launched by the Ministry do not get reflected in the Gender Budget
Statement.
17.3 The Ministry has submitted that so far 56 Ministries/Department which have confirmed
setting up of a Gender Budgeting Cell/nominating a nodal person including non social sector
Ministries like Power, Petroleum, Culture, Heavy Industries, Science and Technology,
Telecommunications, Defence etc. When the Eleventh Plan recommendations were drawn up, the
estimates for the Gender Budgeting Scheme were made for (i) Grants for Research &

Gender Budgeting Scheme was found to be much smaller for the reason that the expenditure under

The objective of Gender Budgeting work was to institutionalize the process at the Central
and State Governments in the first instance by organizing various workshops/training programmes

capacity building.
17.4 The Ministry of Women and Child Development has undertaken several initiatives by

Ministries/ Departments, capacity building to undertake gender mainstreaming of policies/schemes/
programmes and initiating the exercise of gender auditing. The Gender Budgeting Scheme of the
Ministry provides support for organizing workshops and disseminating the concepts, strategies and
tools of Gender Budgeting to the Central Government Ministries/ Departments, State Government
Departments and State Commissions for Women, State Institutes of Rural Development etc. and
preparation of resource/training manuals for facilitating adoption of Gender Budgeting by various

Action Taken
As per its mandate RMK provides micro-credit loans to poor women through intermediary
micro-financing organizations (IMOs) like NGOs, VOs etc .
The Loan Agreements are signed between RMK and the intermediary microfinance
organizations (IMOs) before release of loans. The IMOs also submit postdated cheques (PDC)
against the loan dues (principal plus interest). The IMOs are legally bound to perform the loan
obligations as per the Loan Agreement(s). The procedure for Monitoring and Recovery followed in
RMK is enumerated below.
Monitoring of Loans:
(i) The IMOs furnish the utilisation certificates (UCs) in respect of the loan amount
released to them giving entire details viz. name and address of the individual women
beneficiaries, loan amount and purpose and also affix duly attested photos of the women
beneficiaries.

58
After receiving the UC, a post-sanction visit is conducted by another officer (not associated
with the sanction) to verify the utilization of the first disbursement and need assessment for second
disbursement. The second release of 50% is made only after the Kosh is satisfied with the IMOs
utilization of the first release.
Recovery of Loans:
The RMK loans are recoverable from IMOs over a repayment period of 3 years (including
the grace period of 6 months) in quarterly installments along with interest @ 8% per annum on
reducing balance. RMK follows the following procedure for recovery of loans:
(i) The repayment schedule is defined in advance, envisaging quarterly payment of
principal and interest amount. The requisite PDCs are collected as a risk mitigation
measure.
(ii) Demand letters are issued before due dates to all IMOs at pre-defined due dates.
(iii) Quarterly list of defaults is reviewed regularly. Each defaulting NGO is contacted,
either telephonically or through letters, to enquire about the reasons for default and
the likely time for clearing of default.
(iv) The default loans accounts are handled as under:
(a) In the first instance notices are sent to the defaulting IMOs asking them to pay
the overdue amount, urgently.
(b) The defaulting IMO is then sent first reminder, followed by another reminder
and if required final warning (after 30 days from second reminder) to clear the
overdues. If overdues still persist blacklisting is resorted to in case of an NGO
commits default of 4 quarters of interest and/or 4 quarters of principal, or
both. This has been an effective measure of recovery. Blacklisting of the
defaulting IMOs is undertaken mainly to pressurize for urgent clearance of
overdue amounts. In case the IMO clears the loan overdue, it is removed from
RMK black list.
(c) Post-dated cheques given by NGOs are deposited in the concerned banks; if
returned unpaid, case(s) under section 138 of Negotiable Instrument Act (NIA)
are filed in courts.
(d) In case of chronic default, a ‘recall-notice’ is also issued calling the defaulting
IMO to pay upfront the entire outstanding amount of the loan dues, in a
month’s time. If the recall-notice is not complied with, a PDC is lodged for the
entire outstanding dues (loan instalments plus interest).
(e) Suits are filed or wherever applicable, Arbitration Process is followed, to
recover the dues from the NGO where the loan repayment period has been
completed, as per the loan agreement.
Comments of the Committee

recovery of loans is followed in Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) which provides micro credit
to poor women through intermediary micro-financing organisations (IMOs). The laid out

ONE STOP CRISIS CENTRE
Recommendations/Observations
13.1 The Committee observes that a pilot scheme which would serve as an integrated facility
where various needs of victims of violence, such as medical aid, legal assistance, assistance in

is quite concerned to note that while on the one hand two already existing schemes are proposed
to be merged for more visible impact, the Ministry proposes to launch another new scheme. The
Committee is of the view that Swadhar Grehs equipped with all envisaged facilities can very well
serve as a One Stop Crisis Centre. It is all the more intriguing as already an exercise to consolidate
two similar schemes is on, another new scheme with similar objectives but with a new fancy name
is in the offing. The Committee would like to point out that any new component of the news
scheme, One Stop Crisis Center can be easily made part of the scheme of ‘Swadhar Greh’ .
Action Taken
With regard to Para 13.1 to 13.3, it is stated that Swadhar Scheme was launched by the
Ministry during the year 2001-02 for the benefit of women in difficult circumstances with the
following objectives:
• To provide primary need of shelter, food, clothing and care to the marginalized women/
girls living in difficult circumstances who are without any social and economic support;
• To provide emotional support and counseling to rehabilitate them socially and
economically through education, awareness, etc;
• To arrange for specific clinical, legal and other support for women/girls in need; and
• To provide for helpline or other facilities to such women in distress.
Short Stay Home scheme is being implemented with the objective to rehabilitate women and
girls who are facing social, economic and emotional setback due to family problems, social
ostracism, exploitation, violence or other causes or being forced into prostitution and are in moral
danger.
As the target group and the objectives of Swadhar and Short Stay Home schemes were
similar, these have been merged into a new scheme called ‘Swadhar Greh’ with revised
financial norms in order to reach out to those women who are victims of difficult

60
circumstances and are in need of institutional support for rehabilitation so that they could lead
their life with dignity.
In order to deal with a situation of violence, women require support at various levels that
address their multiple needs. Recognizing this “One Stop Crisis Centre” for women is envisaged
which would serve as an integrated facility where various needs of victims of violence, such as
medical aid, legal assistance, assistance in filing a police case, counseling and emotional support,
temporary shelter for herself and her children and basic necessities for the period of stay can be
met, at a single place. There is a strong need to encourage setting up of One Stop Crisis centre
where women in distress can find immediate legal, medical and counseling services as well as a
help desk of the police under one roof and from where women can be immediately sent to a
shelter.
Victims of rape and sexual assault will also be able to benefit from these Centres where
they will be provided with immediate medical help to deal with her injuries and trauma and where
her statement can be recorded in a conducive and sensitive environment. Such a facility is not
available in Swadhar Greh which is meant to serve as shelter home only with skill development
facility. As such Ministry feels that there is no duplication between the proposed ‘One Stop Crisis
Centre’ and Swadhar and Short Stay Home or its latest manifestation, Swadhar Greh.
Comments of the Committee
Given the increase in the cases of rape, molestation, violence against women in
recent times, the Committee believes that One Stop Crisis Centre which has the objective
to serve as an integrated facility where various needs of victims of violence such as medical
aid, legal assistance etc. at one place assumes great importance. Since the scheme has been
launched in 2012-13, the Committee is of the view that an efficient, well-equipped and

exercise and formalities leading to effective implementation of the scheme.
As regards the ICDS Scheme, the Committee has been given to understand that the
ICDS Scheme was not receiving adequate budgetary provision at BE stage, resulting in
additional allocation through supplementary demands for grants and re-appropriation out of
savings from other schemes of the Ministry at the end of the financial year. This also
resulted in release of funds to States/UTs in the last week of March. There was no proper
fund flow mechanism/devolution of financial powers in most of the States/UTs for timely
utilization of funds, causing sub-optional delivery of services and implementation of scheme
including utilization of funds. The Ministry has pointed out that the system of financing the
scheme was partly responsible for unspent money or shortfall with the States causing
imbalances. Communication with the Chief Ministers/Administrators of the States/UTs had
been facilitated by the Ministry for utilization of funds for the ICDS Scheme. Excess
expenditure or unspent balance lying with the State during the last financial year was to
be adjusted at the time of release of funds during the third installment in the third quarter
of the financial year. The Government of India had approved the Ministry’s proposal for
strengthening and restructuring of ICDS on 24.09.2012 with an allocation of `1,23,580 crore
during the 12th Plan Period. The shortcomings in the scheme were likely to be taken care
of through instrument and modalities of financial management.
The Committee is of view that ICDS Scheme is one of the most important schemes
of the Ministry of Women and Child Development benefiting women and children.
Therefore, there should not be any dearth of funds for this scheme. Further, timely release
of funds to the States should be facilitated and utilization certificates should be sought for
the expenditure incurred in proper time. Reiterating on the importance of the scheme, the
Committee believes that serious and concerted efforts need to be made for efficacious and
timely implementation of the scheme. Since the strengthening and restructuring of ICDS
Scheme is being envisaged, the Committee hopes that all the shortcomings of the scheme
would be taken care of as assured by the Ministry. However, a strong sense of dedication
and will is required at the level of the Ministry to make the ICDS Scheme a success.
(Para 2.9)
The Committee notes that the Ministry is taking the issue of unspent balances with
regard to ICDS Scheme, SABLA and IGMSY seriously. The Committee has been given to
understand that the matter of unspent balances was regularly being taken up with the
States/UTs. Apart from monitoring and supervision mechanism, the States/UTs have to
submit physical and financial progress report in each quarter and Annual Progress Report
too. The Committee has been reiterating on proper utilization of funds so that the problem
of unspent balances does not crop up as it should not lead to hasty utilization of funds but
judicious and optimum use of funds has to be ensured as well. The Committee also
appreciates the idea of the Ministry to conduct evaluation of the SABLA and IGMSY
Schemes by an independent agency. (Para 2.10)

III. INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICE (ICDS) SCHEME
The Committee has been given to understand that the draft findings of the NCAER
study on ICDS especially in respect of supply of supplementary nutrition, status of AWC
infrastructure, availability of basic kits etc. were shared with the States for taking
necessary action on their part. Some of the shortcomings pointed out in the study were
again highlighted in the review of ICDS Scheme with State Secretaries held on 13-14
September, 2012. The Ministry had provided detailed comments on the draft report for its
finalization with NCAER in August 2010. The Ministry had not agreed to some of the
findings of the NCAER due to factual incorrectedness. The Committee would like to be
apprised about the points/aspects not agreed to by the Ministry. Further, the Committee
wonders about the follow-up action taken by the States/UTs and the Ministry after sharing
the findings of the NCAER study. The Committee is aware of the fact that the
Government has approved the Ministry’s proposal for strengthening and restructuring of
ICDS to address several gaps, systematic, management and institutional arrangement for
its better implementation. The Committee would like to point out that if no specific
efforts are being made by the Ministry to address the shortcomings in the ICDS Scheme,
especially relating to supplementary nutrition, infrastructure of AWC, availability of basic
kits etc. as highlighted by the NCAER study, there does not seem to be much hope for
the scheme’s success even when it would be restructured and strengthened. The success
of a scheme depends on identification of shortcomings, if any, in its implementation and
addressing them as early as possible by making serious efforts. The Committee,
therefore, hopes that necessary measures would be taken by the Ministry in this regard.
(Para 3.2 & 3.3)
The Committee had expressed its concern regarding non-availability of adequate
funds for the strengthening and restructuring of the ICDS Scheme. With an allocation of
half of `30,000 crore, which the Ministry had requested for, the Committee wonders as to
how the Ministry envisages to start the strengthening and restructuring exercise of the
ICDS Scheme. Against this backdrop, the issue of unspent balances becomes of critical
importance as the requirement of additional funds for this flagship scheme needs to be
justified by optimum and judicious utilization of resources. The Committee feels that mere
delegation of requisite financial powers to the officers in the State/UT Women and Child
Development Departments dealing with ICDS will not help. A proper monitoring mechanism
for supervising utilization of funds and getting statements of expenditure incurred on time
needs to be focussed upon. (Para 3.9, 3.10 & 3.11)

V. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS)
The Committee has been made sure that the Ministry on its part has been strongly
urging the State Governments/UTs from time to time to come forward expeditiously with
feasible proposals. The scheme has gained momentum and so far, 29 States/UTs have set up
State Child Protection Societies, 24 have set up District Child Protection Units and State
Adoption Resource Agencies. Only 5 States have completed the recruitment of staff while the
other States arc in the process of doing so. There has been a considerable increase in the
number of CWCs (548), JJB (561), Open shelters (121), Adoption Agencies (196) and Childline
locations (212). Financial support has also been provided for upgradation of 323 and
construction of 39 homes for children. The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry
for working as a facilitator for smooth functioning of the scheme. However, the Committee
feels that despite the efforts of the Ministry, a lot remains to be done. Even though the States
have set up the necessary institutional arrangement like the State Child Protection Societies
etc, they are non-functional due to the pending recruitment of staff to manage these centres.
Shortage of required manpower is a major stumbling block in the implemention of the ICPS
Scheme aiming at protection and care of vulnerable children. The Committee feels that even
though the necessary structures have been constructed for helping distressed children but,
they do not serve the desired purpose due to lack of manpower and other connected facilities.
The Committee feels that this scheme is of significant importance in protecting, helping and
taking care of children in vulnerable situations and the half-hearted efforts for
implementation of the scheme would not fulfill the objectives envisaged for the scheme. The
Committee recommends to the Ministry to give utmost priority to the scheme so as to protect
each child in dire situations. (Para 5.6)

recommends to the Ministry to co-ordinate with the States/UTs to utilize the funds properly
and in time for setting up of systems for implementation of the scheme. Further, the
Committee fecls that the exercise of convergence with various schedules of the Ministries
needs to be focused upon for strengthening the non-nutrition component of the scheme.

IX. INDIRA GANDHI MATRITV A SAHYOG YOJANA (lGMSY)
The IGMSY Scheme was approved in 2010 and the year 2011-12 was the first
complete year of implementation of the Scheme. The Committee has been given to
understand that there was under-utilization of funds due to the fact that the States/UTs had
to undertake a lot of preparatory exercises such as establishment of cells at States and
district levels, tie up with Banks and post-office etc. along with compliance with procedural
formalities involved in the cash transfer to the beneficiaries. The Committee understands
the fact that many preparatory exercises need to be completed before the proper
implementation of any scheme. However, these preparatory exercises need to be completed
within the first year of the implementation of the scheme itself or else the delay in its
completion would further cause delay in the implementation of the scheme. The Committee
hopes that by now the preparatory exercises and other formalities with respect to IGMSY
Scheme are supposedly complete and the actual implementation of the Scheme has begun
in the year 2012-13 leading to optimum utilization of funds. In case, it is not completed,
it should be taken up on priority basis. (Para 9.3)

benefits of the scheme reach the poor and asset-less women. (Para 10.5 & 10.6)

in time and that such procedural delays need to be avoided or curtailed, and that the

XIII. ONE STOP CRISIS CENTRE

aid, legal assistance etc at one place assumes great importance. Since the scheme has been
launched in 2012-13, the Committee is of the view that an efficient, well-equipped and

functioning through inspection. (Para 13.1, 13.2 & 13.3)

registered and operate in accordance with the prescribed guidelines. (Para 15.5)

Provisions in JJ Act for Monitoring of Homes
(a) Section 34 (3) of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2000 (JJ Act)
provides for mandatory registration of all Child Care Institution (CCI) housing children
in need of care and protection with the intent of enforcing minimum standards of care for
the services provided for children in these Homes.
(b) Section 35  of the JJ Act provides appointment of the inspection committees  by the State
Governments for the State, a district and city, as the case may be, for the Children’s Homes.
The Committee is required to have representatives from the State Government, Child Welfare
Committee, voluntary organisations as well as medical experts and social workers.
Rule 60  provides for constitution of inspection committee on recommendation of selection
committee under Rule 91. The Inspection Committee (not less than 3 members together) is
required to visit every institution once in 3 months , either with prior intimation or on
surprise visit. It is to oversee the conditions in the Homes and appropriateness of the
processes for safety, well-being, standards of care for children and also look out for cases
of child rights violation.
Reports are to be submitted to State Governments and DCPU.
So far inspection committees have been constituted by 15 State/UTs, namely, Andaman &
Nicobar Islands, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Goa, Haryana,
Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Mizoram, Nagaland, Punjab and Sikkim.
(c) Section 36 of the JJ Act provides for Social Audit  under the Act for monitoring and
evaluation of institutions. Rule 66  provides for annual monitoring .and evaluation by Central
or State Government of the implementation of the Act, including, inter alia , functioning of
institutions. Social Audit is to be done with support and involvement of organisations working
in the field of mental health, child care and protection NIPCCD, ICCW, CIF, SSWBs , Schools
of Social Work and Schools of Law.
(d) Chapter VI of the Model Rules framed under the JJ Act provides minimum standards of
care to be maintained in the Homes including physical infrastructure; staff; daily need of the
child including food; clothing; medicines; education; vocational training; management of
individual case file; provision of Inspection Committee and Children Committee etc.
(e) Rule 60  of the Model Rules framed under the JJ Act also prescribes comprehensive measures
to respond in case of any kind of abuse, including sexual abuse, neglect and maltreatment is
noticed in the CCI.
(f) Rule 25 (p) of the Model Rules framed under the JJ Act provides that Child Welfare
Committee shall visit each institution where children are sent for care and protection or
adoption at least once in three months to review the condition of children in institutions,
with support of the State Government and suggest necessary action.
(g) Rule 55 of the provides for setting up of a Management Committee for every institution
for the management of the institution and monitoring the progress of every juvenile and child.
The Management Committee is required to consist of the following personnel:

(h) Rule 56 of the Model Rules provides for facilitation of setting up of Children’s Committees

(i) Rule 55 (7) of the Model Rules provides for setting up of a complaint and redress

If there is a problem or suggestion that requires immediate attention, the Chairperson of the

required to review the Children’s Suggestion Book at least once in three months.

I am writing this to share my deep concern regarding the spate of incidents reported in the
media, of abuse of children in the Children’s Homes in various States in the country. The
revelation, that such incidents have been going on for long, even in Homes that are registered under
the Juvenile Justice Act (JJ Act), indicates that the systems for monitoring the functioning of Child
Care Institutions (CCIs) in the States are either not properly established or are dysfunctional.
There is an urgent need to identify and register all CCIs in the State and to establish
mechanisms for monitoring the management of the Homes through Inspection Committees and
management Committees as prescribed under the JJ Act and Rules. We have written to Chief
Secretaries and Principal Secretaries of State Governments in this regard and I request you to
kindly issue instructions so that speedy action is taken to ensure that all Homes are properly
managed and regularly inspected.
I take this opportunity to suggest that a quick review be undertaken by the State
Government through specially appointed teams, of all the Homes for taking steps for their
registration and for determining the quality of services. In case such reviews indicate a need for
upgradation of infrastructure in the registered Homes, my Ministry would be happy to consider
financial support under the Integrated Child Protection Scheme.
In cases where gross violations of rights of the children come to notice or where the
children are not receiving proper services, the State Government may like to explore steps to
transfer the management of such Homes to alternate agencies or to manage such Home temporarily
through the district administration.
It is understood that in many States, the social worker Members of JJBs/CWCs have not
been selected in accordance with the procedure laid down in JJ Act and Rules. I would, therefore,
request you to kindly review recruitment procedure of the CWCs/JJBs in your State and ensure
that verification of their antecedents has been carried out to establish that they are not in any way
unsuitable for dealing with children.

I request your intervention for ensuring an early action on the above suggestions so that

Dear Shri Dwivedi,
As you are aware, Child Welfare Committees (CWCs) are set up in all the districts by the
State Governments under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2000. While
assessing the functioning of the CWCs, it has been felt that it will be useful if the members are
given regular orientation. In this context, I would like to inform you that National Institute of
Public Cooperation and Child Development (NIPCCD), in consultation with the Ministry, has
developed a training module for members of CWCs which may be accessed on the Ministry’s
Website www.wcd.nic.in. While NIPCCD may be approached to organize training of trainers for
CWC members in your State, you may also like to get this training included in the Calendar in your
premier State Level Training Institutes. If required, Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA)
may also be approached to assist in identifying resource persons or training for trainers. You may
kindly like to consider that every CWC member, once appointed, first undergoes orientation
training/within two months of joining as member.
May I request your personal intervention in this regard?
