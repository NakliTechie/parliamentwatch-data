REPORT
 
I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       The  National Policies of Education in India have always underscored the Constitutional resolve to provide
quality education to all.  Hence, to focus on the urgency of universalisation of elementary education, a separate
Department of Elementary Education and Literacy has been created. Education is the key to all processes of
development, especially human development.  Having regard to the size of the population, diversity and economic
frailty prevailing in the country, the challenges in the path of providing Education for All are too many and enormous. 
Education being in the Concurrent List of the Constitution, its meaningful development implies an effective partnership
between the Union Government  and the States.
1.2       The Ninth Plan treats education as the most crucial investment in human development.  Approach Paper to the
Ninth Plan had indicated that `apart from carrying out the direction given by the National Policy of Education 1986 and
Programme of Action, 1992, making the nation fully literate by the year 2005 would be a committed goal.  However,
under the revised scheme of the National Literacy Mission it has been upheld by the literacy experts that full literacy for
India means a sustainable threshold level of 75% and the basic objective should be to seek to achieve high success rates
rather than `total literacy’.  Hence, there is a need that priorities should include universalisation of elementary education,
raising the quality of education at all levels, upliftment of the educational status of disadvantaged groups like
SCs/STs/girls/disabled, equality and removal of regional imbalances, vocationalisation of education and setting up of
centres of excellence.
1.3       During the course of his evidence, the Secretary, Department of Elementary Education and Literacy informed
the Committee that an allocation of Rs. 24,908 crore has been made in the Ninth Five Year Plan in the Central Sector as
against an outlay of Rs. 7,443 crore in the Eighth Five Year Plan. While this represents an increase of more than two
and a half times, it is less than one third of the actual requirement projected.   In view of an anticipated expenditure of
Rs. 11,951,03 crore in the Ninth Plan, an amount of Rs. 12,957.35 crore is expected to be available for allocation 
during the last two years of the current Five Year Plan.  Thus, 50% of the amount i.e. Rs. 6,478.67 crore should have
been allocated to the Department during 2000-2001.  However, actual allocation has been restricted to Rs. 5,450 crore, a
shortfall of approximately, Rs. 1,000 crore, the Secretary clarified.  Allocation for Elementary  Education is   Rs.
3,608.75 crore as against Rs. 3,035.13 crore in 1999-2000 (BE) which shows an increase of 18.9%.  Allocation for
Elementary Education is 66% of total allocation for Central Plan on Education in 2000-2001 as against about 65% last
year.  The Committee was informed  that although several Committees had suggested to increase  allocation for
Education to  6% of GDP, yet,  the present allocation is only 3.4% of GDP which is still less by 2.6%.  The

II.         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The total allocation in BE 1999-2000 for Elementary Education and Literacy was Rs. 3150.37 crore which was
reduced to Rs. 2936.88 crore at the RE stage.  However, there is a  substantive increase of around Rs. 800 crore in 2000-
2001.  The Committee was informed  that allocation of Rs. 3036.95 crore in BE 1999-2000 was reduced to Rs. 2854.24
crore at RE stage as the Schemes for Teacher Education and Non-Formal Education could not be implemented with 
revised norms.  Similarly, the new scheme of National Programme for Women Education could not be launched for
which Rs. 160 crore was provided.  In addition, States and UTs are yet to appoint a sizeable number of teachers
sanctioned under the ‘Operation Blackboard’ due to various reasons.
2.2       During the year 2000-2001, it is proposed to implement  the new Scheme of ‘Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan’ for which
Rs. 350 crore has been allocated.  Similarly, the National Programme for Women Education is also expected to be
launched during the year for which an allocation of Rs.160 crore has been made.  Besides, it is proposed to  implement
the revised schemes of Non-Formal Education and Teacher Education with its enhanced cost  parameters for which an
allocation of Rs. 300 crore and Rs. 219.53 crore respectively has been made.  Hence, the provision in BE 2000-2001 for
the Schemes in Elementary Education Sector has been increased to Rs. 3610.75 crore, the Committee was informed.
2.3       The Committee notes with serious concern that the main reason for reduction of allocation at RE 1999-2000 is
the absence of approval to the expansion, revised and enhanced norms in respect of some of the schemes of elementary
education.  The Committee observes that against the reduced allocation of RE 1999-2000, the reported expenditure upto
29.2.2000 is Rs. 2323.92 crore only.  Therefore, the Committee suggests  that adequate  measures need to be taken for
effective and proper implementation of the schemes of elementary education in  2000-2001.
2.4       Performance Budget should clearly reflect both the financial provision and corresponding physical achievements
of different schemes/programmes during the financial year.  The Committee, however, observes that Performance
Budgets of almost all the Departments are prepared in such a manner that one does not get a clear  idea in this regard. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take corrective steps in this regard, if the
Performance Budgeting is to have any meaning.
III.       LITERACY
3.1       The Committee  was informed that as a result of concerted efforts in the area of primary education, non-formal
education and adult education, there has been some increase in the literacy rate prevailing in the country.  Results of the
53
rd round of the National Sample Survey Organisation (NSSO) indicate that against the literacy rate of 52.2% in 1991,
this rate was 62% at the end of December, 1997 and is projected to touch 64% at the end of 1998.  This increase of
almost 2% may be seen  against the backdrop of an average decadal growth of only 8.5% between 1951 and 1991. 
Keeping the growth of around 2% per annum, it has been expected that by the year 2005 we could reach a target of 75%
literacy.  Therefore, the revised scheme of National Literacy Mission has put it at a practical level of 75% by 2005.
3.2       Male literacy rates have risen from 64.1% in 1991 to 73% in 1997 and the female literacy rates in the
corresponding period have risen from  39.3% to 50%.  Thus,  the gender gap in literacy rates is being narrowed in
overall terms.  Another encouraging trend is acceleration of literacy rates in rural areas.  While in 1991, the urban
literacy rate was 73.1% and rural literacy rate was 44.7%,  in 1997, as per the  NSSO Survey, urban  literacy rate was 
80% and the rural literacy rate was 56%.    In terms of performance in literacy, Mizoram leads the country with a
literacy rate of 95%.  Kerala now occupies the second place with a literacy rate of 93%.   Almost all the educationally
backward States have also shown reasonable improvement.
3.3       The Committee is inclined to believe that literacy/enrolment/Drop-out rates projected on the basis of surveys
conducted so far do not seem to be realistic.  There is a lot of difference  between assessment made by the grass root
level workers and the so called independent surveys.  The  Committee views with serious concern the difference
between male and female literacy in some of the States as indicated below:

IV.       UNIVERSALISATION OF ELEMENTARY EDUCATION (UEE)
4.1       The Ninth Plan envisaged Universalisation of Elementary Education to mean universalisation of access,
retention and achievement.   Significant strides in this direction have been made.  The Secretary informed that 94% of
the rural population living in 8.26 lakh habitations has now a school within a walking distance of one km. And 84% of
this population has now an upper primary school within a walking distance of 3 kms.  It was further informed to the
Committee that the revised target for achieving UEE puts emphasis on that - every child in 6-14 age group is either in
school, Education Guarantee Scheme centre or  back to school camp by 2003, five years of primary schooling for every
child by 2007 and eight years of elementary schooling by 2010. 
4.2       The Committee was also given to understand that the revised 83
rd Constitution Amendment Bill, 1997 to make
education for Children from 6-14 years of age a Fundamental Right has been referred to a group of Ministers for
detailed examination of financial and legal aspects and Central legislation for making elementary education free and
compulsory is under consideration. The Committee takes note of the efforts and hopes that the revised target of UEE
would be achieved as per the proposed plan and steps would also be taken to make elementary education free and
compulsory for children of  6-14 age group.
4.3       It has been taken note of by the Committee that the Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) in the primary stage (Class 1-
V) increased from 42.6 percent in 1950-51 to 92.14 % in 1998-99 and in the upper primary stage (class VI-VIII) from
12.7 % to 57.58 % over the same period. However, the Committee observes that gender disparities are conspicuous in
regard to enrolment and retention.   Girls still account for only 43.5% of enrolment at primary stage and 40.5% at upper
primary stage.  The Committee is constrained to note that Uttar Pradesh has the lowest enrolment ratio (49.31) in class
I-V for girls in the country.   So far as upper primary stage is concerned, Bihar and UP have the lowest enrolment ratio
for girls i.e. 23.13 and 26.41 respectively.  The Committee finds that gender disparities are equally evident  so far as
dropout rates are concerned. Dropout rates in classes 1-V for the year 1998-99 (provisional) for boys are  38.62% and
for girls 41.22% and in classes I-VIII   of the same year (provisional) stand for boys 54.40 % and girls 60.09%.  Here
again, states like Bihar, UP, Rajasthan and some NE states are a cause  for concern. 
4.4       Expressing its concern in this regard, the Committee last year had opined that concerted efforts in a mission
mode by all the implementing agencies were required to be made without any delay.   The Committee has been
informed that the State Education Ministers’ Conference held in October., 1998 had recommended that universal
primary education should be pursued in a mission mode .   Subsequently, on the basis of recommendations made by a
National Committee of State Education Ministers, the scheme of ‘Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan’ was evolved.
4.5       The Committee has been informed  that the basic idea is to incorporate existing programmes of elementary
education in the Central and Centrally Sponsored Category under the new scheme in consultation and partnership with
states.
The following goals have been set for the SSA :-
-           all 6-14 age children in school/EGS centre/bridge course by 2003;
-           all 6-14 age children complete 5 years primary education by 2007;and
-           all 6-14 age children complete 8 years of school by 2010.

VI.       TEACHERS’ TRAINING PROGRAMME
6.1       Restructuring and Re-organisation of Teacher Education was  launched in 1987 to create a viable institutional
infrastructure, academic and technical resource base for orientation, training and continuous  upgradation of knowledge,
competence and pedagogical skills of elementary school teachers in the country.
6.2       The  Committee was informed that the budgetary allocation for Teachers’ Training Programme was enhanced to
Rs. 290 crore in 1999-2000 as compared to Rs. 159 crore in 1998-99 anticipating revision of norms under the
Programme and the demands from the states for new projects.    The Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC) in its
meeting held on 19
th April, 1999 approved the revised norms.   However, for the new projects of DIETs/CTE, EFC felt
that priority should be given to consolidation and operationalistion of the already sanctioned projects and the new
projects should be sanctioned only in those areas which have shown satisfactory performance in this regard.   The
Department has informed that in view of the dissolution of the 12 th Lok Sabha, it was decided that the matter be
processed further for obtaining the approval of the Cabinet only after formation of the new Government.   Accordingly,
the  matter has now been processed in consultation with the Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission and the
proposal of revision of norms is presently before the Cabinet.  In view of the non-revision of norms due to the above
mentioned circumstances, it was felt that it may not be possible to implement the revised norms during the current
financial year.   Accordingly, the budget provision for Teachers’ Training Programme was reduced from Rs. 219 crore
at BE stage to Rs. 150 crore at RE stage.   An expenditure of Rs. 142 crore has already been incurred on continuation
and consolidation of Teachers’ Training Programme  during 1999-2000.  Though, there are proposals from the  state
govts. for opening up 57 new DIETs and 11 CTEs/IASEs, the new projects will be sanctioned only during 2000-2001
after the revised norms are implemented.  Preference will be given to those states who have already operationalised their
sanctioned projects.   The Committee hopes that the new norms will be approved and implemented without further loss
of time and the allocation made in 2000-2001 for this Programme will be fully and properly utilised.
6.3       The main objectives of the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE), established in 1995, are to achieve
planned and coordinated development of the teacher education system, regulation and proper maintenance of norms and
standards of teacher education.   The Committee observes that due to unspent balances lying with NCTE, allocation of
funds had to be reduced at RE stage in 1997-98 and 1998-99.   An unspent balance of Rs. 0.74 crore was allowed to be
carried forward for utilisation during 1997-98.   During 1998-99, an unspent balance of Rs. 2.02 crore was adjusted.  
An unspent balance of Rs. 26.00 lakhs has been allowed to be carried forward in 1999-2000 also.   The Committee
apprehends that the same situation may arise in 2000-2001 also.   The Committee takes serious view of such a trend
which is against all budgetary norms. The Department should take remedial measures in this regard without any further
delay.

6.4       The Committee notes that the scope of Special Orientation Programme for School Teachers (SOPT) is being
widened to include secondary teachers.   The Committee finds that the  number of teachers trained under this
Programme during 1997-98 and 1998-99 shows drastic variations.   Out of 6,55,661 teachers trained,  3,39,643 teachers
were from the state of Maharashtra.   In contrast, in a state like Bihar, only 8,302 teachers were trained, and not a single
teacher was trained in Madhya Pradesh during this period.   The Committee is of the view that benefits  of such a
Training Programme should reach backward and tribal areas where literacy rate is still much below the average  literacy
rate of the country.   Efforts should be made to strengthen the existing structures of Teachers’ Training in such states. 
6.5       The Committee notes that a pilot project for in-service training of primary school teachers through inter-active
television has been taken up in the states of Gujarat and MP in collaboration with the Department of Telecom,
UNESCO and ITU under which 8000 teachers, teacher educators and educational supervisors are proposed to be
trained.
VII.      NON-FORMAL EDUCATION SCHEME
7.1       The Committee has been informed that the centrally sponsored scheme of Non-Formal Education has been
modified, keeping in view the various findings of the Planning  Commission  and feed-back from various studies and
also in keeping with the announcement  made by the Finance Minister in his Budget Speech to introduce Education
Guarantee scheme.    The revised scheme has since been approved by EFC and the process of obtaining Cabinet
approval is on.
7.2       The proposed scheme to be known as the ‘Scheme of Alternative and Innovative Education’ seeks to provide
primary education in a flexible manner at a convenient pace and location through locally recruited and trained
instructors.  Priority will be given to 10 educationally backward states.  It provides for :-
-           Demand driven establishment of EGS Learning Centres/alternative  Schools in unserved habitations.
-           Residential camps to prepare children for primary school through condensed and short time courses.
-           Remedial short term teaching programmes in urban slums
-           A flexible approach for adolescent girls to meet diversity of their learning needs.
-           Decentralisation to PRIs.
-           The Centre-State ratio for sharing of expenditure would be 75:25
-           The unit cost per child per annum revised from the present Rs. 375/-  to Rs. 845/- for an EGS Primary
Centre and from  Rs. 580/- to Rs. 1200/- for an upper primary centre.
7.3       The  Committee hopes that the revised scheme would be operationalised in 2000-2001 for which necessary
provision of Rs. 300 crore has been made.  The Committee also hopes that the scheme will be implemented smoothly
with proper coordination between Central Government, State Governments  and voluntary agencies.   In view of the
enhanced financial norms in the revised scheme, there is a need for having an effective inbuilt monitoring mechanism. 
The Committee is of the view that alongwith regular inspections, a component of random surveys without prior notice
should also be evolved to have proper assessment of the implementation of the revised scheme and  also elimination of
undesirable NGOs.
VIII.     NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR NUTRITIONAL SUPPORT TO PRIMARY EDUCTION (MID-DAY
MEAL SCHEME)
8.1       The      programme launched on 15
th august, 1995,  is intended to give a boost to universalisation of primary
education while simultaneously catering to the nutritional needs of the students in primary classes.  During 1999-2000,
the estimated number of beneficiaries is 9.80 lakh children. The Committee was informed that the requirement of funds
for 2000-2001 will be Rs. 2300 crore in view of increase in the economic cost of foodgrains.   However, only Rs. 1090
crore have been provided which will need to be augmented at the RE stage.    The Committee observes that the position
was the same in 1998-99.
8.2       As per the guidelines, the distribution of foodgrains is an interim measure till arrangements are made by the
implementing agencies for providing hot cooked meal/ready to eat food.   Such arrangements were required to be
developed within a period of two years from the date of introduction of the programme.  At present, only the states/UT
of Gujarat, Kerala, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Pondicherry and MP (174 Tribal Blocks) are providing cooked meals. 
8.3       The Committee was given to understand that paucity of funds is the main reason for not switching over to
cooked meals by the states/UTs.  It has been estimated that  in the Ninth Plan  alone, Rs. 18,000 crore would be required

IX.       EXTERNALLY-AIDED PROJECTS DISTRICT PRIMARY EDUCATION PROGRAMME (DPEP)
9.1       The scheme launched in 1994 aims at operationalising holistic approach for achieving UEE in selected districts,
promoting decentralized planning and management with district as a unit.  The scheme is under implementation in 219
low female  Literacy districts  in 15 States.  The project is funded 85% by external agencies through the Central Budget
and the concerned  State Governments contribute 15% .   The Committee was given to understand that expansion of
DPEP in 41 additional districts of Uttar Pradesh, 10 districts of Rajasthan and 5 districts of West Bengal has since been
approved by the Expenditure Finance Committee (EFC).  Appraisal of project plans for expansion of DPEP in  8
additional districts of Orissa has  been initiated.  Further expansion of DPEP in 9 additional districts of Rajasthan and 6
districts of Gujarat is also envisaged.        The Committee desires that, as proposed, the new centres would be started
early.
9.2       The Committee was informed that provision of Rs. 750 crore in BE 1999-2000 was reduced to Rs. 600 crore,
keeping in view the various factors , including the availability of funds with the State Implementation  Societies and the
trend of expenditure.  However, since the expenditure has picked up in the later part of the year, provision at final grant
stage was enhanced to Rs. 700 crore by re-appropriation.  The Committee observes that pace of expenditure has been
slower than anticipated in 1997-98 and 98-99 also as the State Implementation Societies were having unspent balances
from the previous year’s releases. 
9.3       Last year, the Committee had observed that certain problems were arising in the implementation of DPEP in
Kerala.  The Department has informed that a joint inquiry  had been initiated by the State Govt. of Kerala to review the 
work and progress and also  to inquire into the affairs of the DPEP in Kerala.  The problem was on the revised textbooks
specially Malayalam textbooks in classes I and II.  There was also some controversy about the new  pedagogical
methods in DPEP in the state.   Text books which raised controversy have been revised based on the interim  report of
the Guptan Nair Committee appointed by the Kerala Government.  It has also been informed that resistance of
parents/teachers to the pedagogical changes has now worn off. The Committee would appreciate if final
recommendation of the Guptan Nair Committee as and when received, are made known to it. 
9.4       The Committee expresses its concern over the State-wise expenditure details  under DPEP for the last three
years.  It is surprising that while no money was spent  in Rajasthan in 1997-98 & 1998-99, only Rs. 2.13 crores has been
shown in expenditure details as upto January, 2000.   In contrast, expenditure in  Madhya Pradesh is Rs. 392.32 crores
in the last three years.  Unspent balances lying with the states indicate that the scheme is not  being properly
implemented there. Therefore, proper monitoring of the scheme is urgently called for. The Committee notes that delay
in the release of 15% state share  stay  on recruitment of teachers due to court cases, frequent transfer of Project
Directors in some States have sometimes adversely affected the pace of programme implementation in the concerned
project States.  Steps need to be taken in these areas for effective implementation.

LOK JUMBISH  PROJECT
10.2     LJP  was launched in 1992  with the objective of promoting UEE through people’s participation, decentralized
decision making and partnership with NGOs.   The Committee is informed that an allocation of Rs. 50.25 crores for
`Lok Jumbish Project’ in BE 1999-2000 was reduced to Rs. 40 crores at the RE stage.   However, it has been increased
to Rs. 56.10 crore in 2000-2001.   The  Department in its reply stated that in view of the post Pokharan development
there was uncertainty about continuation of SIDA-Swedish International Development Agency funding to Lok Jumbish
Project.   Hence, as an alternative to SIDA funding, DFID agreed to support this project with a grant of about Rs. 200
crore.   The phase III of the Project  has since been approved by the Expenditure Finance Committee.  Pending approval
of the project by the Cabinet and consequent signing of agreement by the two countries no expansion could take place
and the activities had to be kept at existing level.  Hence, the provision was reduced to Rs. 40 crore at RE stage.
10.3     As the activities of the project would expand to cover more blocks of 13  districts during 2000-2001, the budget
provision has been enhanced to Rs. 56.10 crore.  The Committee hopes that the plan would be extended and
implemented in maximum  blocks of the 13 districts during 2000-2001. 
XI.       MAHILA SAMAKHYA PROGRAMME
11.1     It  is a 100% Dutch assisted project which was launched in 1989.  The programme, at present, covers 4032
villages in 24 districts of U.P., Karnataka, Gujarat and Andhra Pradesh.   It aims at women empowerment at grass-root
levels.  The Committee took note of the fact that the Plan allocation for `Mahila Samakhya’ in BE 1999-2000 was Rs.
7.50 crore which was reduced to Rs. 6 crore at the RE stage.  Provision for 2000-2001 has been hiked to Rs. 10.00 crore.
11.2     The Department has informed that according to the projected expansion envisaged in EFC Memo, the Mahila
Samakhya project was to be expanded in 35 districts by the end of 1999-2000, but so far it has been possible to expand
the project into 24 districts only.  Thus to achieve the projected expansion an enhanced allocation of Rs. 10.00 crores
has been proposed.  Moreover, the plan size for districts increases after the third year of the project in that district.  The
Committee suggests that there should be monitoring of the expansions and it should be implemented in a time bound
manner.
XII.      EDUCATION DEVELOPMENT IN NE REGION
12.1     The Committee finds that whereas Enrolment Ratio in classes I-V in the NE region in respect of both boys and
girls is higher than the national average,  the same is not the position with regard to drop-out rates in classes I-V in these
states.   For classes I-V, the average drop-out rate is 48.06 as against the national average of 39.58.  It has been informed
that there is a need for increasing the number of habitations covered by primary schools, reduction in drop-out rates,
increase in the provision of drinking water and toilet facilities in NE states.
12.2     The Committee notes that the following centrally sponsored schemes are not being properly implemented in the 
NE region –
i)          out of Rs. 178.66 crores released to NE region beginning 1987-88 till date under ‘Operation
Blackboard’, only Rs. 142.13 crores have been utilised.
ii)         Under the ‘Teachers’ Education Programme’, out of 55 District Institutes of  Education and Training 
(DIETs) sanctioned to provide pre-service and in-service training to elementary school teachers and for
adult education and non-formal education personnel, only 32 are operational in NE region.
iii)         Under the Mid-day Meal Scheme, a quantity of 1,14,789 metric tonnes of rice was allocated to the NE
region in 1998-99, out of which only 26,327  metric tonnes (23%) has been lifted.  
iv)        DPEP is operated in NE region only in  Assam, covering all the nine low female literacy districts.
12.3     The Committee is constrained to note that full utilisation of the allocated funds has not been possible mainly
because of lack of viable proposals from the NE region, non-receipt of utilisation certificates and lack of absorption
