REPORT 
  
            The Protection from Domestic Violence Bill, 2002, seeks to protect the rights of women who are 
victims of violence of any kind occurring within the family and to provide for matters connected 
therewith or incidental thereto. 
The Statement of Objects and Reasons appended to the Bill states as under:  
“Domestic violence is undoubtedly a human rights issue and serious deterrent to development.  The 
Vienna Accord of 1994 and the Beijing Platform of Action (1995) both have acknowledged this.  The 
United Nations Committee on CEDAW (Convention on Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination 
Against Women) in its general recommendation No. XII (1989) has recommended that State parties 
should act to protect women against violence of any kind especially that occurring within the family.  
The phenomenon of domestic violence is widely prevalent but has remained largely invisible in the 
public domain.  Presently, where a woman is visited with cruelty by her husband or his relatives is an 
offence under section 498A of the Indian Penal Code, 1860. The civil law does not address this 
phenomenon in its entirety.   
With a view to providing a remedy under the civil law, which is intended to preserve the family and at 
the same time provide protection to victims of domestic violence, legislation is being proposed.  The 
main features as contained in the Bill are as follows:- 
it is being provided that any conduct of relative of the victim, which subjects her to habitual assault, or 
makes her life miserable, or injures or harms, or forces her to lead an immoral life would constitute 
domestic violence; 
the Judicial Magistrate of the first class or the Metropolitan Magistrate may take the cognizance of 
domestic violence and pass a protection order requiring the relative of the women to refrain from 
committing an act of domestic violence, or pay monetary relief which is deemed fit in the circumstances 
or pass any other direction as the Magistrate may consider just; 
the Magistrate may even require as an interim and urgent measure from the relative of the woman to 
execute a bond, with or without sureties, for maintaining domestic peace; 
the violation by the relative of the order made by the Magistrate would constitute an offence punishable 
with imprisonment up to one year, or with fine, or with both; 
it is being proposed to set up an institution of Protection Officer to help the victim of domestic violence 
in making application to the Magistrate and in availing of her other legal rights; 
a provision is being made for the appointment of Protection Officers  by State Governments and they 
shall possess such qualifications as may be prescribed by the Central Govt., and 
Protection Officer shall be deemed to be a public servant within the meaning of section 21 of the Indian 
Penal Code, 1860 and if he fails or refuses to discharge the duties as directed by the Magistrate, his act 
shall amount to an offence punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or with fine, or with both”.

3.         The Secretary, Department of Women and Child Development deposing before the Committee 
put forward the Department’s view for bringing the Bill. 
3.1       Outlining the basic features of the Bill, he stated that the existing civil, personal or criminal laws 
leave certain gaps in addressing the issue of Domestic Violence.  Under criminal law, if a husband 
perpetrates violence on his wife, she may file a complaint under Section-498 A of IPC.  Similarly, under 
the civil law, if there is disharmony in a family and the husband and wife cannot live together, any one 
of them may file a suit for separation followed by divorce.  However, the present Bill addresses such 
situation where there is some disharmony in the family but the situation has not yet reached a stage 
where either separation or divorce proceeding has become inevitable and the aggrieved woman also for 
some reasons does not want to initiate criminal proceedings against her perpetrator.  Therefore, the Bill 
seeks to give the aggrieved woman an alternative avenue whereby she can insulate herself from violence 
without being deprived of the basic necessities of life and without disintegrating her family. 
4.         The Committee in its sitting held on 13th November, 2002, took up `clause-by-clause’ 
consideration of the Bill.  The clauses where amendments were suggested  by the Committee have been 
discussed in the succeeding paragraphs. 
            Clause 2 (i) 
4.1.      Clause 2 (i) deals with the definition of the term  `relative` in the Bill. 
4.2       The definition of  relative as given in the Bill has two requirements.  First, the person has to be 
related by blood, marriage or adoption and second, he/she should be living with the respondent.  This 
definition obviously excludes women whose marriages are legally invalid, women in bigamous relations 
and other consanguineous relations. When asked about the reason for not including such women, the 
Department stated that only those marriages recognized in law were covered under this Bill.  Such 
women as have been living in relationship akin to marriage without legal marriages, were not included 
simply because the prevailing cultural ethos of the nation did not encourage such relationship. 
 4.3      The Committee  is aware of the fact that in our society there are numerous cases  where a man 
and a woman, though not legally married, live together as husband and wife and their relationship has 
got social sanction too.  Moreover, the issue of  domestic violence is more proximate  with the basic 
human rights of a woman to live a dignified life.  Therefore, providing relief under the present Bill to a 
woman whose marriage is not legally valid won’t be in conflict with the existing laws and will not give 
any legal sanction to the illegal marriages.  The Committee, therefore, suggests that Clause 2(i) should 
be suitably amended to include those women  also who have been living in relationship  akin  to 
marriages and in marriages considered invalid by law. 
Clause 4 (2) 
5.         Clause 4(2) provides the right of self-defence to the respondent if the pursuit of his conduct was 
reasonable for his own protection or for the protection of his or another’s property. 
5.1       As per this clause,  no action of the respondent shall amount to domestic violence if it is done to 
protect himself or to protect  his property or  someone else’s   property.  Almost all the organizations/ 
associations which appeared before the  Committee were of  unanimous view that this provision is 
extremely dangerous and retrogressive as it provides an escape route for the man who commits acts of 
domestic violence.  On this issue, the Department replied that the right to self-defence is recognized in 
criminal and civil laws and has accordingly been provided in the Bill. 
5.2       The Committee does not agree with the Department’s views  . It is of the considered opinion that 
there are sufficient provisions in the existing criminal  and civil laws to take care of the right to defence 
and there is no need to provide the same in the Bill and unnecessarily overburden it.  In view of the 
above, the Committee concurs with the views of the  organizations/associations which appeared before 
the Committee and strongly recommends  that Clause 4(2) should be deleted from the Bill. 
Clause 9 (1) 
6.         Clause 9(1) provides that the aggrieved person who is a victim of domestic violence or any other 
person on her behalf or the Protection Officer, may present an application to the Magistrate for seeking 
relief under section 14. 
6.1       The Committee feels that clause 9(1)  needs to be made specific  in terms of laying down the 
Page 6 of 29124thReportofHRD
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/Report124th.htm

territorial jurisdiction of the Magistrate in whose court the aggrieved person is competent to file an 
application seeking relief under clause 14,   though the same has been provided under the definition of 
‘Magistrate’ in clause 2 (c)  of the Bill.  Given that, in a number of cases where the woman who has 
been subjected to domestic violence either comes back to her parent’s home or finds some alternative 
accommodation, which may not be under the territorial jurisdiction of the same magistrate  where the 
alleged violence i.e. the cause of action has arisen.  The Committee is of the view that clause 9(1) should 
specifically provide for presenting an application in such courts under whose territorial  jurisdiction 
either  the aggrieved person resides or the respondent resides or the alleged violence has occurred or the 
aggrieved person is temporarily residing.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that clause 9(1) may 
be amended accordingly.  The Committee further recommends that necessary consequential amendment, 
if any, may also be made in the Bill. 
Clause 11(1) 
7.         Clause 11(1) provides that the Magistrate at any stage of the proceedings under this Act may 
direct the respondent or the aggrieved person, either singly or jointly, to under go mandatory counselling 
with any service provider. 
7.1       The Committee has been given to understand that counselling is one of the methods of 
behavioural  corrections and according to accepted principles of  counselling, it should be given only 
when the concerned parties either volunteer or are willing to receive it .  In other words, counseling 
should be voluntary and not compulsory.  In view of this, it would not be proper to empower the 
Magistrate to insist on  `mandatory’ counselling to either party.  The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that the word ``mandatory`` should be deleted from  clause 11(1). 
Clause  (13) 
8.         Clause 13 lays down that the proceedings may be held in camera, if the Magistrate considers that 
the circumstances of the case so warrant, and shall be so held if either party so desires. 
8.1       A number of organizations which\ appeared before the Committee were of the view that in 
camera trials of rape cases are generally helping abusers more than the complainants because the abused 
women are mostly left alone in court with only their lawyers to support. Thus `in-camera trials`` which 
is meant to protect  the privacy of the parties, in fact, is being utilized to unduly favour the respondent. 
8.2       The Committee feels that  holding the trial of a case in camera is a procedural matter which 
should be left to the wisdom of the Magistrate.  However, the use of the word `shall’ in Clause 13 would 
leave the Magistrate with no other choice but  to hold the trial in camera if either party so desires.  The 
Committee therefore, recommends that the word `shall’  be substituted by the word `may’ so as to 
provide room for judicial expediency.  
Clause 14(1) 
9.         Clause 14(1) enables the Magistrate to pass Protection Order when he is satisfied that the 
aggrieved person is being subjected to domestic violence. 
  
9.1       The Committee has been given to understand that the range of protection order that can be 
passed  under the Bill is not exhaustive as it does not include the right to residence of the aggrieved 
person in matrimonial home.  A large number of cases of domestic violence remain hidden as the victim 
faces the threat of immediate ouster  from her matrimonial home. 
9.2       When asked to respond to this, the Department submitted that under clause 14(1) (b) of the Bill, 
the Magistrate is empowered to pass any other direction as he deems fit in the circumstances of the case, 
such as the aggrieved person should not be evicted from the household or her assets should not be 
disposed of etc. 
9.3       The Committee however, feels that there should be specific mention about the right to residence 
of the aggrieved person in matrimonial home in the Protection order passed by the Magistrate.  It, 
therefore, recommends that clause 14(1) be amended suitably.   
9.4       The Committee also feels that in appropriate cases where the Magistrate is satisfied that the 
aggrieved person is being subjected to domestic violence, it should be made obligatory on his part to 
pass protection order.  The Committee, therefore, further  recommends that in clause 14(1)(a) the words 
Page 7 of 29124thReportofHRD
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/Report124th.htm

`may pass the protection order’ be substituted by the words “shall pass the protection order”.   
Clause 14 (5) 
10.       Clause 14(5) lays down that where the Magistrate is satisfied that circumstances stated in the 
application presented under section 9 are such so as to justify the immediate intervention of the 
Magistrate to restrain the respondent from committing domestic violence, the Magistrate may issue an 
interim protection order directing the respondent to restrain from engaging in any act of domestic 
violence, and the Magistrate may further require the respondent to show cause as to why he should not 
be ordered to execute a bond, with or without sureties, for maintaining domestic peace for such period as 
the Magistrate thinks fit. 
10.1     The Committee understands that the provision of Interim Protection Order has been made in 
clause 14(5) so as to ensure immediate  relief to the aggrieved person in appropriate cases.  The 
Committee feels that as domestic violence  may occur on any day and at any time, there should be a 
magistrate available  or approachable  round the clock to pass the interim protection order under clause 
14(5).  The Committee,  therefore, recommends that in order to further strengthen the provision of 
Interim Protection Order and making it more relevant, clause 14(5) should be suitably amended or a new 
sub-clause may be added to clause 14, so as to allow the filing of application seeking emergency relief 
both within and outside the court hours and on a day which is not an ordinary court day. 
Clause 21 
11.       Clause (21) provides protection to the protection officer from action taken by him in good faith. 
11.1     The  service providers, as defined in Clause 2 (k) of the Bill, are voluntary associations who have 
been entrusted with the task of protecting rights and interests of women by any means including legal 
aid, medical, financial or other assistance.   
The Committee has  been apprised  that under the present Bill where the service providers are expected 
to carry out a lot of duties , there is every likelihood  that they may be sued even for acts done by them 
in good faith.  The Committee feels that they need protection. The Committee , is therefore of the view  
that the service providers may be given similar protection under Clause-21 of the Bill  as given to the 
Protection Officers and recommends that suitable amendments may be carried out accordingly. 
12.       The Committee adopts the remaining clauses  of the Bill, including enacting formula and the title 
without any  amendment.  
GENERAL OBSERVATIONS 
  
13.       It has been submitted before the Committee that there is acute shortage of trained and 
experienced Service Providers, especially women, in the country.  The Committee, is, however, of the 
view that since the Bill seeks to protect women against domestic violence, it would be proper if the 
service provider provided in the Bill is preferably, a woman organization.  It, therefore, recommends 
that a provision that a Service Provider may be a woman organization, to the extent possible, may be 
made in the Rules.  
14.       The Committee is of the view that excessive use of physical, psychological and verbal violence 
in TV serials tends to lend legitimacy to domestic violence.  Besides that, undue focus on bigamy and 
extra-marital relations has undesirable impact on children. On being asked whether it would be feasible 
to have a suitable provision in this regard in the Bill, the Department replied that the aspect of excessive 
use of physical, psychological and verbal violence in T.V. serials was being taken care of by other laws.  
Only legal marriages were covered under the Bill.  Over legislation had been avoided to the extent 
possible. 
15.       The Committee notes that despite several legislations, violence on women still continues to be 
shown most audaciously in visual media.  The Committee, therefore, feels that the existing laws are 
perhaps not being implemented properly.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department 
should strictly monitor the depiction of women in print as well as visual media and whenever a woman 
is being portrayed objectionably, it should immediately take up the matter with the appropriate 
authorities/departments. 
16.       Committee’s attention has also been drawn to the plight of NRI women who are victims of 
Page 8 of 29124thReportofHRD
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/Report124th.htm

domestic violence. To a question, whether it would be feasible to include such victims under the Bill, 
the Department replied that the issue of providing protection to Indian girls who married NRIs staying 
abroad was being considered separately.  It would not be prudent to include all such issues in the 
legislation which was meant for protection of women from domestic violence. 
16.1     The Committee is sensitive towards  the cause  of Indian girls who are subjected to violence after 
marrying NRIs staying abroad and feels  that this is  a  very serious matter which needs immediate 
attention  and recommends that the Department should take appropriate measures in this regard. 
17.       The Committee recommends that the Bill may be passed after incorporating the amendments 
suggested above.  The Committee also recommends that the observations made by it should be given 
due consideration by the Department.

PARLIAMENTARY COMMITTEE ON HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT INVITES 
SUGGESTIONS ON THE PROTECTION FROM DOMESTIC VIOLENCE BILL, 2002 
  
The Protection from Domestic Violence Bill, 2002,  as introduced in the Lok Sabha on the 8th March, 
2002, has been referred to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human 
Resource Development,  headed by Shri Arjun Singh, M.P., for examination and report. 
  
2.         As per the Statement of Objects and Reasons appended to the Bill, the phenomenon of domestic 
violence is widely prevalent but has remained largely invisible in the public domain.  Presently, where a 
woman is visited with cruelty by her husband or his relatives is an offence under section 498 A of the 
Indian  Penal Code, 1860.  The existing civil law does not address this phenomenon in its entirety.  
Therefore, the main objective of the Bill is to provide protection under the civil law to a victim of 
domestic violence.

2.         Shri Rama Shankar Kaushik, M.P. Rajya Sabha  chaired the meeting in the absence of the 
Chairman.   At the outset, he welcomed  the Officials of the Ministry of Human Resource Development 
(Department of  Women and Child Development) and Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative 
Department and Department of Legal Affairs) .The Committee, then, took up  the Infant Milk 
Substitutes, Feeding Bottles and Infant Foods (Regulation of   Production, Supply and Distribution) 
Amendment Bill, 2002 for clause-by-clause consideration. The Committee adopted the Bill with a minor 
change in Section-6 of the Infant Milk Substitutes, Feeding Bottles and Infant Foods (Regulation of   
Production, Supply and Distribution) Act, 1992, which was not proposed in the Bill.  The Committee 
felt that as there was a mention of `infant food'  in section 6(1) of the Act, necessary consequential 
amendment should to be made in section 6(2) and 6(2)(b).  
3.         Thereafter, the Committee took up the Protection from Domestic Violence Bill, 2002  for clause-
by-clause consideration. The Committee suggested amendments/deletions in certain clauses of the Bill 
which are as  follows : 
Clause 2 (i)  
Clause 2(i) should be suitably amended to include those women also who have been living in 
relationship  akin  to marriages and in marriages considered invalid by law. 
 Clause 4 (2) 
Clause 4(2) should be deleted from the Bill. 
Clause 11(1) 
The word, "mandatory" should be deleted from Clause 11(1). 
Clause (13) 
            The word `may’  be used in place of `shall’. 
Clause 14(1) 
The Protection Order under Clause 14(1) should provide for specific right of residence to the aggrieved 
women in the matrimonial home.  
In Clause 14(1) (a), the words `may pass the  Protection Order’ be substituted by the words, "shall pass 
the Protection Order".   
Clause 21 
The Service Providers should also  be given protection under Clause 21 of the Bill in the same manner 
as given to the Protection Officers.  
The Committee adopted the remaining Clauses of the Bill including the enacting formula and the title 
without any amendment.
