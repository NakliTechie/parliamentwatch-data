CHAPTER-I 
OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE 
GOVERNMENT 
OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS (2025-26) OF THE 
DEPARTMENT OF SCHOOL EDUCATION & LITERACY (DoSE&L) 
Recommendation  
1. The Committee observes that there is 6.9% increase in BE allocation 2025-26 vis-à-vis BE 
allocation 2024-25 while there was an increase of 8.44% in BE 2023-24 vis-à-vis BE allocation 
2022-23 for Department of School Education and Literacy. The Committee feels that in view of 
the inflationary trends in the country, the allocation should increase at least 8% to 10% over the 
allocation during previous year to accommodate the inflation and to maintain the existing 
standards for expenditure by Department of School Education and Literacy and to avoid decline 
in actual allocation/expenditure for school education. The current allocations are, therefore, 
disappointing – especially in the context of the need for adequate resources to implement the 
NEP. 
(Para 2.4 ibid) 
2. From analysis of the Table 3,4 and 5 given above, the Committee observes that there is 
6.90 % increase in BE 2025-56 in comparison to BE 2024-25 and 16.28% increase over RE 2024-
25. The Committee further observes that there is an increase of 8.44% in BE 2023-24 vis-à-vis BE 
2022-23. The Department had made projections of Rs. 84,100.84 crores to the Ministry of 
Finance for 2025-26. However, the actual allocation to the Department is Rs. 78,572.1 crores 
which reflects the variation/decrease of -6.57 % between projections made to the Ministry of 
Finance and actual allocation made to Department of School Education and Literacy in BE 2025-
26. 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to undertake review of the 
Projections made to Ministry of Finance and actual allocations to the Department in respect of 
each scheme run by the Department wherein there is gap between Projections and allocations to 
make it realistic. The Committee also recommends the Department to monitor the physical and 
financial progress of the schemes, so that, these schemes do not suffer due to reduced allocations. 
Such an objective review will help the Department to seek additional funds at RE stage for the 
schemes being run. 
(Para 2.6 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 For the Department of School Education and Literacy, the Budget Allocation for the FY 
2025-26 of ₹ 78572 Cr is the highest ever for the Department of School Education & Literacy. 
The highlights of the Budget allocation are: - 
 There has been an overall increase of ₹ 5074 Cr (7%) in the Budget Allocation of 
Department of School Education and Literacy in the FY 2025-26 from BE 2024-25. As 
compared to RE of FY 2024-25, there has been an increase of ₹ 11,000 Cr (16.28%). 
 The highest ever Budget Allocation may be seen in the Autonomous Body of Kendriya 
Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) at Rs. 9,503 Cr. Allocation in KVS has increased by ₹ 201.17 
Cr as compared to Budget allocation of FY 2024-25. There has been an increase of ₹ 776 
Cr (9%) as compared to RE of FY 2024-25. 

3 
 
 Budget Allocation of FY 2025-26 in Flagship Schemes have increased i.e Samagra 
Shiksha (by ₹ 3750 Cr), PM-POSHAN (by ₹ 32 Cr) and PM-SHRI (by ₹ 1450 Cr) with 
respect to Budget Allocation (BE) of FY 2024-25. As compared to RE 2024-25, allocation 
in Samagra Shiksha has increased by ₹ 4240 Cr (11%), allocation in PM-POSHAN has 
increased by ₹ 2500 Cr (25 %) and allocation in PM-SHRI has increased by ₹ 3000 Cr 
(66%). 
 Out of the overall Budget Allocation in FY 2025-26 of ₹ 78,572 Cr, the Scheme allocation 
is ₹ 63,089 Cr and Non-Scheme Allocation is ₹ 15,483 Cr. 
 Increase in Scheme Allocation in BE 2025-26 is ₹ 5284 Cr (9.14 %) as compared to BE 
2024-25. As compared to RE 24-25, increase in Scheme Allocation is ₹ 10248 Cr (19%) 
and non-Scheme allocation has increased by ₹ 752 Cr (5%) in BE 2025-26. 
Further, Economic Survey 2024-25 notes that there is a rising trend in Government’s social 
services expenditure (SSE), as a percentage of total expenditure (TE).  While the SSE outlay of 
the Centre and State governments was Rs. 14.8 lakh crore in FY21, it has increased steadily to 
stand at ₹25.7 lakh crore in FY25 (BE). 
On Education, the Survey notes that the expenditure in the sector has grown at a CAGR of 12%, 
from Rs. 5.8 lakh crore in FY21 to Rs. 9.2 lakh crore in FY25 (BE).  
Budget proposals of the Department projected to the MoF are finalized only after due 
deliberations. Further, the physical & financial progress of the Schemes are continually monitored 
throughout the year – monthly review meetings are held in person/online with the States/UTs for 
this purpose. 
 
Recommendation  
3. From figures given at Table 8, it is evident that Rs. 73,498.1 crores has been allocated 
under BE 2024-25 which has been decreased to Rs.67,571.2 crores at RE stage. The Committee 
observes that this reduction at RE stage amounts to Rs.5,926.9 crores which is 8.06% less in 
comparison to allocation at BE 2024-25. Further, the Committee notes that the Actual 
expenditure of the Department is only Rs. 43,254.81 crores as on 18.02.2025 which is 64.01% of 
the reduced allocations of RE 2024-25. In this regard, the Committee would like to refer to Rule 
56(3) of General Financial Rules (GFR), which states that rush to expenditure particularly in the 
closing months of financial year shall be regarded as a breach of financial propriety and shall be 
avoided. As per the guidelines, the last quarter expenditure must be limited to actual procurement 
of goods and services and reimbursement of expenditure already occurred. Ministry of Finance's 
instructions seeks to restrict last quarter expenditure to 33% ceiling and last month (March) 
expenditure to 15% ceiling only. 
 
In backdrop of the Actual Expenditure by Department of School Education and Literacy 
during 2024-25 and Rule 56(3) of General Financial Rules (GFR), the Committee recommends 
that the Department should adhere to Rule 56(3) of GFR, streamline its expenditure during 2025-
26 to optimize the utilization of funds allocated to it and to avoid piling up of expenditure in the 
last quarter of the financial year. 
(Para 2.12ibid) 
Action Taken 
During the financial year 2024-25 the actual expenditure incurred by the Department was 
Rs. 64519.64 crores which represents over 95% expenditure against the RE of Rs. 67571.20 
crores. 
 

4 
 
A review and monitoring mechanism is already in place to regularly assess the utilization 
of funds by the States/UTs. This includes periodic review meetings and reporting through 
platforms such as the PRABANDH portal and PFMS portal. As per existing instructions of 
Ministry of Finance, Central share of funds under a centrally sponsored scheme like Samagra 
Shiksha is to be released in four instalments and before release of an instalment it is inter alia to 
be ensured that at least 75% of the funds released earlier (both Central and State share) have been 
utilized and reflecting in PFMS; 
That the bank balance in Single Nodal Agency (SNA) account is not more than 50% of the 
funds proposed to be released and that conditions of previous sanction have been complied with. 
Monthly review meetings (in person/virtual) with the States/UTs are regularly held for this 
purpose.  The Department has requested States & UTs to exercise financial discipline for the timely 
submission of proposals (i.e. one proposal in each quarter for release of Central Assistance) and to 
make adequate budgetary provisions. 
 
SCHEMES/PROGRAMMES OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SCHOOL EDUCATION & 
LITERACY 
 
Recommendation  
4. The Committee notes that the Department was allocated for Centrally Sponsored Schemes 
like SSA, PM-POSHAN, NILP, STARS and PM SHRI, a total of Rs. 57,427.39 crores under BE 
2024-25 which was reduced to Rs. 52,480.00 crores at RE stage. Out of the said allocation 
Department has been able to spend Rs. 29,241.67 crores only as on 18th February, 2025 under 
these flagship schemes which pegs at 50.9% & 55.7% of the BE & RE 2024-25, respectively. The 
Committee expresses its concern over the poor fiscal management and underutilization of 
allocated funds under various flagship schemes of the Department like SSA, PM-POSHAN, NILP, 
STARS and PM SHRI. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take 
proactive and effective measures to utilize the funds allocated under BE 2025-26 for these 
Centrally Sponsored Schemes, so that, the benefits are percolated down to the target groups. 
(Para 3.2ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 Public Financial Management System (PFMS) is used for all releases and expenditures of the 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme. Of the Department. The Department has taken the following steps to 
ensure utilization of funds at an improved pace and enhance the efficacy of the Centrally 
Sponsored Schemes:  
1. All the PAB meetings have been concluded and minutes have been issued to enable the 
release of funds in time. 
2. Enabling ease in release of instalments to States/UTs through regular coordination and 
timely resolution of issues. 
3. Monitoring of recurring expenditure through a separate PRABANDH Portal developed by 
NIC, enabling monitoring of utilisation and outcomes under various components approved 
by PAB. Also review meeting with States / UTs for the Monitoring of PRABANDH Portal 
for regular updation is held. 

5 
 
4. Conduct of regular monthly reviews with States/UTs to assess progress. 
Mid-term review meetings with States/UTs is held  
Recommendation  
5. The Committee observes that resource allocation is grossly insufficient. Out of Rs. 41,250 
crores allocated to Samagra Shiksha, Rs. 35,000 crore goes to primary education. Only the 
remaining Rs 6,250 crore is for secondary education. This lopsided allocation raises questions on 
the Department’s seriousness in reaching the NEP’s stated goals of universal secondary 
education by 2030.Accordingly, the Committee recommends that the Department should 
significantly enhance the budget of the SSA and reserve these funds for the enhancement of 
secondary schools. 
(Para 3.3ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
Under Samagra Shiksha, all States and UTs submit their proposals for all interventions 
contained in the scheme through their Annual Work Plan & Budget (AWP&B). The proposals are 
appraised and approved by the Project Approval Board (PAB) as per the programmatic and 
financial norms of Samagra Shiksha in the Ministry, in consultation with the respective State/UT 
government and proportionate funds are allocated.  The funds, under the scheme of Samagra 
Shiksha, are released based on the fulfilment of guidelines prescribed by Ministry of Finance, 
such as transfer of central share to SNA, pace of expenditure, receipt of commensurate State share 
in SNA, statement on outstanding advances, up-to date expenditure statement, furnishing of 
information as prescribed in the Manual on Financial Management & Procurement and audited 
utilization certificate of previous year. 
In FY 2018-19, Rs. 3400.24 Crore was released as Central Share under Secondary 
Education while in 2024-25 it was 5971.28 Crore which is an increase of more than 75%. During 
annual PAB meetings with the States/UTs, they are sensitized for enhancing their allocation in 
Secondary education scheme of Samagra Shiksha. During the in-person meeting held on 17th 
January 2025 with States and UTs, they were guided to enhance allocation for secondary 
education also as per requirement. 
The Department is taking steps to ensure allocations are in a proportionate and focused 
manner. In the current year Rs. 5989 Crores has been allocated vide a supplementary PAB for 
saturating ICT, Smart Classes and Physics/Chemistry/Biology laboratories in secondary / Hr 
secondary schools, in addition to the regular allocation.                                         
 
SAMAGRA SHIKSHA ABHIYAN (SSA) 
Recommendation  
6. The goal of the Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan is to enable RTE implementation in state 
schools. However, the Committee is pained to note that 15 years after the RTE Act was passed, we 
have still not reached universal achievement of RTE norms by schools and teachers. In far too 
many schools, the pupil-teacher ratio, the number of classrooms, or the availability of water and 

6 
 
sanitation facilities and/or playgrounds is inadequate. The Committee notes the tremendous 
achievements of the Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan but calls for a revamping of the scheme to function 
on mission mode. The mission must be to achieve near-saturation of the RTE’s requirements by 
2029. The Finance Minister’s Budget announcement of achieving universal broadband 
connectivity for rural secondary schools within the year offers a model for the scale and timeline 
at which the Government should be operating at to upgrade the physical and human resources 
offered by schools. 
At the same time, it is not advisable to impose the RTE’s norms and requirements on 
schools without fore thought. There are several schools, especially in underserved, remote, and 
Adivasi-majority areas, which may be unable to meet the formal requirements demanded by the 
RTE. Recently, the only Gondi-language school in Maharashtra, in Gadchiroli district, was 
forced to shut down since it did not have the requisite approval from the State Government as 
mandated by the RTE. Similar stories have emerged from ST dominant areas in Assam. 
Recognizing that the only feasible alternative to these schools is no school, the Department must 
relax RTE norms in such instances. Relaxation of RTE norms can only be done with a view to 
preserve schools – not with a view to consolidate them or to continue to under-resource them. 
(Para 3.4.5 ibid) 
Action Taken 
Education is in the concurrent list of the Constitution and an overwhelming majority of the 
schools are under the jurisdiction of the concerned States & UTs. State and UT Governments 
are the appropriate Governments under the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory 
Education (RTE) Act, 2009, and have the responsibility and mandate to provide school 
infrastructure in schools in accordance with the norms laid down in the Schedule to the RTE 
Act, 2009 and as per respective State RTE Rules. 
  However, under Samagra Shiksha, States and UTs are supported for strengthening of 
existing government schools and for creation and augmentation of infrastructure facilities, 
based on the gaps determined from Unified District Information System for Education 
(UDISE) database and proposals received from respective State/UT. The requirement of 
infrastructural facilities in schools are worked out every year by the respective State/Union 
Territory on incremental basis depending on their need and priority and reflected in their 
Annual Work Plan and Budget (AWP&B). The budget plans are appraised/approved by PAB 
in consultation with the States and UTs as per the programmatic and financial norms of the 
Scheme and availability of budgetary resources. 
  DoSE&L is taking steps for saturation in providing Infrastructure facilities to the 
States. In this regard, the States/UTs have been requested in various forums including the 
meeting of the Project Approval Board, review meetings and other forums for filling up the 
gaps in school infrastructure.  
  In pursuance to other Para of 363rd Report of DRPSC, this Department has issued various 
advisory/guidelines in this regard to the States/UTs.  DO letter dated 03.07.2025 to all States/ 
UTs advising them to identify gaps in provision of safe and potable drinking water through 
taps and separate Girls’ toilets in all Govt/ Govt. Aided schools. 
  
Further, in convergence with DDWS and MoHUA this Department is enabling provisioning 
of safe and potable drinking water through taps and separate Girls’ toilets through their 
Schemes.  
  

7 
 
  The Status of infrastructure facilities as per UDISE+ 2023-24 is at Annexure – II. 
 
At the State/UT level, consolidation, opening and closure of schools is under State/UT 
domain and are being considered by States/UTs as appropriate to their regional requirements. 
Samagra Shiksha norms underline that consolidation/merger of school shall not in any 
case violate the right of access to school of a single child as per neighbourhood norms notified by 
the respective State/UT under the RTE Act 2009. 
 
Recommendation  
7. The Committee appreciates the increased allocation for SSA under BE 2025-26. However, 
the Committee is constrained to note that the actual utilization as on 18th February, 2025 is Rs.

Action Taken 
The Samagra Shiksha Scheme is aligned with the recommendations of the National 
Education Policy (NEP) 2020 and aims to ensure that all children have access to quality education 
with an equitable and inclusive classroom environment which should take care of their diverse 
background, multilingual needs, different academic abilities and make them active participants in 
the learning process. 
Framework for implementation of Samagra Shiksha has provisions of monitoring the 
progress including the quality of education at different level i.e. District level, State level and 
National level. Following mechanisms are being adopted to ensure quality education under 
Samagra Shiksha: 
 Performance Grading Index: The Performance Grading Index (PGI) has been designed to 
cater to the transformational change in the field of school education, where the focus has 
now shifted to the quality of education.  The index comprising of 73 indicators is propelling 
State and UTs towards undertaking multipronged interventions that will bring about the 
much desired educational outcomes.   
 UDISE+: This is an improved and updated version of Unified District Information System 
for Education (UDISE) for evidence-based planning and decision making. 
 Vidya Samiksha Kendra (VSK) - Vidya Samiksha Kendra (VSK) is aimed at leveraging data 
and technology to bring a bid leap in learning outcomes. This will cover data of schools, 
teachers and students to analyse them meaningfully using big data analysis, artificial 
intelligence and machine learning in order to enhance the overall monitoring of the education 
system and thereby improving learning outcomes. Support has been provided under Samagra 
Shiksha to set up VSK in all State and UTs.   
 National Achievement Surveys (NAS) now called PARAKH RashtriyaSarvekshan are 
conducted at regular intervals to check the health of the educational system and to provide 
information about the learning achievement of students.  
 Detailed guidelines for Social Audit of Samagra Shiksha have been issued and provision has 
been made for covering 20% government and government aided schools every year for 
conducting social audit. 
 Audit Mechanism- There are robust monitoring systems in place which includes an annual 
audit by an independent Chartered Accountant empanelled with the Comptroller and Auditor 
General (CAG) of India, a regular CAG audit, a system of concurrent financial reviews and 
regular meetings with State officials. A Manual on Financial Management and Procurement 
also assists States in streamlining implementation and financial management. 
 Periodic meetings with Stakeholders: The Ministry of Education convenes monthly meetings 
with Education Secretaries and State Project Directors to review the programme 
implementation.   
 Strengthening of Block Resource Centers and Cluster Resource Centers for periodic 
inspection and supervision of schools to observe the infrastructure and facilities  
 Community Level Monitoring- Monitoring under the programme is a three tiered one: 
monitoring at the local community level, at the State level and the National level. 
Community based monitoring is one of the strengths of the programme. The community, 
through its representative institutions like School Management Committees (SMCs), has 
been entrusted with the primary level of ensuring that the schools are functioning effectively. 
 NCERT is bringing out engaging textbooks to hold the attention of students and introduce 
them to experiential learning, in accordance with NCF-SE. 

17 
 
 Teachers are being trained to teach with better pedagogical skills. SCERTs and DIETs are 
being upgraded to Centres of Excellence. 
In pursuance of the recommendation of the NEP 2020, the National Assessment Centre, 
PARAKH (Performance Assessment, Review, and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic 
Development), was established on 8th February, 2023 by the Ministry of Education under the 
National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT). It serves as a central body for 
setting standards, conducting large-scale assessments, and providing evidence-based policy 
recommendations to improve the quality of education. Since its inception, PARAKH has 
undertaken the following activities with an aim to monitor the quality the education being 
imparted in schools and suggesting evidence based remedial measures: 
 
1. The PARAKH RashtriyaSarvekshan, which is a baseline study to understand the achievement 
of the stage specific competencies at the end of foundation, preparatory and middle stages as per 
NEP 2020, was conducted on 4.12.2024 in 781 districts of the country. The survey witnessed 
participation of 21,15,022 students and 2,70,424 teachers from 74,229 schools. The survey's data 
collection and analysis are aimed at strengthening accountability mechanisms, guiding future 
educational reforms and providing data-driven insights for policy interventions. 
2. Development of Holistic Progress Cards (HPC) - a competency-based assessment tool - which 
provides 360 degrees multidimensional holistic progress of learners across cognitive, affective, 
psychomotor and skill domains. Aligned with the curriculum structure as recommended by NEP 
2020, HPC spans across the foundational, preparatory, middle and secondary education stages. 
These progress cards help in identifying students’ unique abilities, strengths and areas for 
improvement, fostering a supportive learning environment. 
3. In an effort to standardize assessment practices, PARAKH has undertaken an empirical study to 
establish equivalence among different State education boards. The findings offer guidelines on 
critical aspects such as assessment, infrastructure, curriculum, inclusion and administration. 
Equivalence among difference boards will foster a level playing field for all students, enabling 
them to compete fairly in higher education admissions and job markets. 
4. PARAKH has also played a crucial role in advocating for the adoption of the State School 
Standards Authority (SSSA) and the School Quality Assessment and Accreditation Framework 
(SQAAF). The SSSA will establish a minimal set of standards based on basic parameters namely, 
safety, security, basic infrastructure, number of teachers across subjects and grades, financial 
probity, and sound processes of governance, which shall be followed by all schools. The 
framework for these parameters i.e. SQAAF, will be created in consultation with various 
stakeholders, especially teachers and schools. PARAKH has created a model SQAAF for 
adoption/adaption by States/UTs. 
5. PARAKH also implements an initiative namely “Udyam Sansaar” or World of Work (WoW), 
which complements the HPC framework. It promotes a multidisciplinary curriculum, skill-based 
learning, and exposure to emerging technologies like Artificial Intelligence, helping students 
develop competencies aligned with real-world needs. It reinforces the shift towards a competency-
based and inclusive education system, as envisioned in NEP 2020.

single Gondi-medium school. 
(i) Accordingly, the Committee recommends that the Department should create a special 
program within Samagra Shiksha that supports states in the following – 
(ii) Coordinate with NCERT, NBT, and SCERT to ensure that TLM and all other resources 
are widely available (online for free and marketable) in all key languages – especially 
those spoken by Adivasi and tribal communities; 
(iii) Develop and financially support bridge courses to teach the state’s dominant language as 
a second language to children belonging to linguistic minorities; 
(iv) Develop pedagogy, teacher training, and TLM for instruction in multiple languages; and 
(v) Support recruitment of new teachers from the local linguistic communities that the school 
caters to. 
(Para 3.4.51 ibid) 
Action Taken 
National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, brought out after a nationwide deliberation with 
all stakeholders, teachers, parents, students and others, has recommended the promotion of all 
regional languages in school education. The NEP 2020 underscores the importance of 
recognising the linguistic diversity of the country which has a bearing and benefit in education 
and for multicultural understanding. Multilingualism as the policy and strategy has been 
emphasised in order to promote and strengthen the use of all Indian languages in school 
education from the early years of learning. The language section of the Policy has been titled, as 
Multilingualism and the Power of Language.  
The policy focuses on the mother tongue-based multilingualism as it states “it is well 
understood that young children learn and grasp nontrivial concepts more quickly in their home 
language/mother tongue”. 
Inclusion of languages of all learners in school curriculum is one major objective of the 
National Education Policy 2020 and National Curriculum Framework for Foundational Stage 
(NCF-FS) 2022 and National Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF-SE) 2023. 
National Education Policy 2020 states, ‘The three-language formula will continue to be 
implemented while keeping in mind the Constitutional provisions, aspirations of the people, 
regions, and the Union, and the need to promote multilingualism as well as promote national 
unity. However, there will be a greater flexibility in the three-language formula, and no language 
will be imposed on any State.  
The National Curriculum Framework for School Education, prepared in pursuance of 
NEP 2020, aims to enable all our students to learn at least three languages, fully leveraging our 
socio-cultural context and resources. 

29 
 
As per NCF-SE, the language in which literacy is first acquired in school (R1) will be the 
language that the student already knows, because that enables the full use of the linguistic and 
cultural knowledge (and resources) from one’s home and community. Further, our students will 
learn two more languages (R2 & R3). At least two of these three languages, R1, R2, and R3, 
must be native to India. The state or other relevant bodies would decide the choices of R1, R2, or 
R3 that would be given to students. 
The CBSE has issued guidelines to help schools implement the language-related 
recommendations of the National Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF-SE) 2023 
from the academic session 2025-26. According to the guidelines, in the early years of schooling 
called the Foundational and Preparatory stages, children should be taught in a language they 
understand well. It should ideally be their mother tongue or home language (called R1). If that is 
not possible, then a familiar regional or state language should be used. This first language (R1) 
should be the medium of instruction for all subjects until students have learned to read and write 
in another language (R2). 
Children with special needs must be given extra support, including extra time in exams, 
the option of using scribes, and exemptions from second or third language studies, as per the 
Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.

Primers mainly emphasize on the introduction of sounds, alphabets, reading and writing 
practice for the children. They help in promotion of child's mother tongue, home language, local 
language, or regional language.

37. The Committee notes that teachers are at the heart of the NEP – and none of the ideas

draft version released in 2019, does not make any unequivocal statement on discontinuing the 
practice of hiring contract teachers. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department

37 
 
years. States must also be encouraged to count years on contract as being part of a teacher’s

61. The Committee notes that the mission has been re-focused recently to exclude oral 
reading fluency as one of its objective. This has ostensibly been done to align the Mission with 
the NEP’s 5+3+3+4 structure, since oral reading fluency was targeted for Grade 3 and Grade 3 
falls beyond the purview of the first 5 years of schooling under NEP. However the Committee 
notes that the Prime Minister himself highlighted the importance of oral reading fluency at the 
conclave on school education in the 21st century under NEP 2020 on September 11, 2020. Oral 
reading fluency is a foundational life skill critical to the success of students. The Committee 
strongly recommends that the Mission be refocused once again to include the attainment of 
universal oral reading fluency as one of its key objectives. 
(Para 4.3.8ibid) 
Action Taken 
NEP 2020 gives a framework of 5+3+3+4 where the initial 5 pertains to 3 years of preschool 
education and Grade 1 and 2, translating to age group of 3-8 years. The NCF-FS also covers the same 
5 years in the education continuum of 15 years and accordingly text books for FS have been made for 
upto Grade 2. The ECCE also covers the age of 0-8 years where 0 to 3 years is home-based. NIPUN-
Bharat Mission is therefore now aligned to the above and covers age 3-8 years for 3 years of 
Balvatika Grade1 & Grade2. The earlier L.Os were focused on Grade 3 for ORF  with emphasis on 
reading on the parameter of words per minute. They were based on findings of Foundation stage 
survey done by NCERT and varied for all languages. 
There was no similar parameter for Grade 2. In this background, the Learning 
Outcomes(L.Os) or Lakshyas on various parameters for NIPUN Grades  have been redefined by 
NCERT in alignment with the National Education Policy(NEP), 2020, NCF-FS and NIPUN 
Guidelines. These are on the parameters of oral language, Reading, Writing and Numeracy.

Recommendation  
1. The Committee observes that the total number of schools run by the State Governments

Action Taken 
Education is in the Concurrent list of the Constitution and the opening, merger/ closing of 
schools are within the purview of respective State /UT Government-which are the appropriate

Implementation of RTE Act 2009 comes under the purview of the appropriate govt be it

which declined to 28.32 crore in 2023-24 which is a decline of 1.87%. Therefore, population 
declined by 1.87% whereas schools declined by only 1.39% during 2020-21 to 2023-24.

Action Taken 
The GST Council has defined education as a social activity with constitutional obligation 
of providing free and compulsory education to every child upto elementary level. The GST Act 
distinguishes between core educational services provided and received by educational institutions 
or exempt and other services are sought to be taxed at the standard rate of 18%. The GST Rates on 
educational services provided by educational institution under Section 9992 (a) & (b) the GST 
rate is Nil. In other words GST provides exemption to educational institutions providing education 
up to higher secondary school or equivalent from the levy of GST.  Auxiliary services received by 
such educational institutions for the purpose of education up to higher secondary level is also 
exempted from GST. Other services related to education, not covered by the exemption would be 
taxed at a standard rate of 18%. This is summarised as under:- 
a)     Educational services provided by an Educational Institute to its students, faculty and staff - 
the GST rate is NIL. 
b)    Educational services provided to an Educational Institution by way of transportation of 
students, faculty and staff, catering including PM POSHAN, security or housekeeping services 
performed in Educational Institution, Services relating to admission, conduct of examination up to 
higher secondary,  services provided by IIM to their students by way of their Educational 
Programes except EDP- the GST rate is NIL. 
c)  Technical Bids for education, rehabilitation, occasional training and employment of the blind 
such as braille typewriters, braille watches, teaching and learning aids, games and other 
instruments and vocational aids specifically adopted for use of the blind braille instruments, paper 
etc.- the GST rate is 5%. 
d)  Instruments, apparatus and models designed for demonstration purposes-the GST rate is 28%. 
Thus, services provided by an Educational Institution to students, faculty and staff from pre-
school education up to higher secondary education and equivalent is exempt from GST.

Recommendation 
23. The Committee observes that few private schools force students and their parents to

102 
 
School shall endeavor to charge fees to the extent the expenses for running the School are met.

The Centrally sponsored Samagra Shiksha Scheme covers children with special needs 
(CwSN) from primary to senior secondary level across the country. The Scheme is regulated and

therapeutic interventions at the block level.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE -AT A GLANCE  
 
1. The Committee notes with appreciation that there is gradual increase in Centre and 
States expenditure in education which stood at 4.12% of GDP in 2021-22. However, there is 
a long way to go before it achieves the target of 6% of GDP. Given that the nominal GDP of 
the country at current prices is around 360 lakh crores which is likely to further increase in 
future, public investment and expenditure in education needs to be at least doubled from the 
current budget allocation of Rs 121118 crore in BE 2024-25 to achieve the target. The 
Committee therefore recommends the Department to argue its case assiduously before the 
Ministry of Finance for enhanced allocation for the Ministry. The Committee notes the reply 
of the Department that our spending in education as a percentage of DGP is higher than 
Russia and China. So far as Russia is concerned, as its population may be about 1/10 of 
India, its per capita investment or expenditure in education may still be much higher than 
us. The Committee, therefore, reiterates that the Department must draw up a time-bound, 
outcome-linked roadmap to progressively raise education expenditure to 6% of GDP, with 
clearly defined annual milestones. 
(Para 2.9& 2.10 ibid) 
2. The Committee is of the view that the figure of 11.7 lakh out of school children is 
considerably high. Such children have a constitutional as well as statutory right to have 
access to education and the state is obliged to provide the same. Therefore, the Committee 
recommends the Department to consider developing a national preventive protocol under 
RTE Rules, designating cluster-level Education Coordinators as attendance authorities to 
act within a predefined days of absence and involve Child Welfare Committees for chronic 
cases. Further, the Department should also consider making appropriate changes in 
centralized UDISE+ to integrate real-time OoSC tracking report, mandating monthly state 
reports and third-party audits. The Committee also recommends that the matter should be 
pursued with the states so that appropriate measures are taken in an expeditious manner for 
inclusion of such children in formal education system and inform the Committee 
accordingly. 
(Para 3.4.4 ibid) 
3. The Committee notes that the Department has acknowledged the issues in principle. 
However, the absence of enforceable timelines, accountability mechanisms, and funding-
linked incentives has resulted in stagnation in teacher recruitment. The Committee feels 
that keeping such large numbers of posts vacant for years together denies the students 
quality education and therefore is an infraction of the spirit of RTE Act. Given the 
magnitude of vacancies and their direct impact on learning outcomes, the Committee 
stresses that urgent action is needed in the larger national interest and for safeguarding the 
future of millions of school-going children. The Committee accordingly recommends that 
the Department should actively engage with the State Governments to sort out difficulties, 
if any, in filing up the vacancies as soon as possible.   
(Para 3.4.30, 3.4.31 & 3.4.32 ibid) 
 
4. The Committee notes that the funds due to Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, and Kerala 
have been duly approved by the Project Approval Board (PAB) of the Samagra Shiksha

bound action plan should be explored to accelerate coverage and measurable outcomes under 
the ULLAS programme. 
(Para 3.8.5ibid) 
20. The Committee takes note of the constitution of an Expert Committee and the advanced 
stage of finalisation of DHRUV guidelines. However, the Committee remains concerned over 
nil utilisation of funds in 2024-25 and the continued modest allocation despite sharp percentage 
increases. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department should finalise the 
guidelines without further delay. It should also strictly adhere to a clear, time-bound rollout of 
the scheme in 2025-26 and ensure front-loaded utilisation of funds. The Department should 
also review the adequacy of allocations based on pilot outcomes so that the objectives of 
nurturing gifted students are meaningfully achieved. 
(Para 4.2.4ibid) 
 
21. The Committee observes that the mere existence of regulatory provisions is 
insufficient in the absence of effective enforcement and deterrence. Continued 
commercialization of school education erodes public trust and defeats the constitutional 
vision of education as a social good. The Committee, therefore, urges CBSE to shift from an 
advisory-based approach to a monitoring-driven, transparent and accountable regulatory 
regime to ensure strict compliance with Clause 9.1.3 of the Affiliation Bye Laws. 
(Para 5.2.8ibid) 
 
22. The Committee while noting that the Ministry of Education (DoSE&L) has decided to 
take up the recruitment of KVS & NVS together and observes that procedural reforms without 
firm timelines and outcomes are insufficient to address the issue of vacancies in KVS. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that KVS should complete the whole recruitment process 
during the current year.  
(Para 5.3.9 & 5.3.11 ibid) 
 
23. The Committee appreciates that out of 163 KVs functioning from temporary 
buildings, land in 121 KVs has been transferred in favour of KVS wherein construction 
activities are at various levels. The Committee recommends the Department to complete the 
construction in a time bound manner during the ensuing financial year and inform the

Committee. The Committee further recommends that in respect of the remaining 42 KVs, 
the matter should be vigorously pursued with the sponsoring authorities for transfer of land 
in favour of KVS. 
(Para 5.3.13ibid) 
 
24. The Committee is not satisfied with the reply of the Department which has chosen 
not to give any clear replies to its recommendation. The Committee feels that the Members 
of Parliaments with their vast experience in public service will only provide significant 
inputs for proper administration of Kendriya Vidyalayas. The Committee therefore reiterates 
its earlier recommendation that the local Member of Parliament be added to the School 
Management Committee of the KendriyaVidyalayas falling within their constituency 
(Para 5.3.15ibid) 
25. The Committee notes that in the ATN wrt Para 5.3.11, the Ministry of Education has 
informed that it has decided to take up the recruitment of KVS & NVS together. The 
Committee is of the view that reliance on contractual teachers in schools like NVS for so long, 
is undesirable. The Committee, therefore, urges the Department to complete the whole process 
of recruitment during the year 2026 for the appointment of Teachers on regular basis in the 
NVS.  
(Para 5.4.9ibid) 
26. The Committee notes the reply of the Department that the process of filling up 
vacant posts through regular appointments shall be initiated once the revised Recruitment 
Rules are notified. It also notes that the draft Recruitment rules though have been prepared 
but are yet to be placed before the Competent Authority for compliance. Since the 
vacancies as per the revised  staff strength in NBB is almost 50%, the Committee urges the 
Department to complete all the necessary formalities for the notification of the Recruitment 
Rules followed by completion of the recruitment process during the year 2026.  
(Para 5.5.6 ibid) 
 
27. The Committee notes that while the Department has initiated Recruitment in mission 
mode and reduced vacancies from 63 to 37, progress remains insufficient. To fulfil NCTE’s 
statutory role in implementing NEP 2020 and teacher education programs, the Department 
should ensure withdrawal of the no-recruitment directive, fill all vacancies permanently, 
and institute regular monitoring. 
(Para 5.7.6 ibid)
