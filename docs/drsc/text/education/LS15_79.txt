PREFACE

I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Human Resource Development, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the
report on its behalf, do hereby present this Seventy-ninth Report of the Committee on
Action Taken by the Government on the Recommendations contained in its Fifty-first
Report on National Institute of Homoeopathy, Calcutta.

2. The Fifty-first Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee
on Human Resource Development was presented to Rajya Sabha on 3rd December, 1996
and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on 3rd December, 1996. Replies of the Government on
the recommendations contained in the Report, received from the Department of Indian
Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy were considered by the Committee. Clarifications
were also sought by the Committee from the Secretary and other Officials of the
Department.

3. The Committee considered the Draft Report and adopted the same on the 27th

August, 1997. The Committee at its meeting held on 19th November, 1998 decided to
present the Action Taken Report in the House during the Winter Session of Parliament.

Comments of the Committee

The Committee, is not convinced by this kind of arrangements. The reason for making the
Minister the President of the Governing Body seems to be that no reference should be called

Committee wonders as to how the Executive Committee which has been constituted by the
Governing Body has all the powers of the Governing Body. The Committee is, however, not
interested as to which body should exist but whichever body exists should have all the
powers to take immediate decisions to enable the Institute to work properly and regularly.
There should not be any delay in taking decisions. The Committee is of the view that‘ since
the National Institute of Homoeopathy is an autonomous body, the proposal for making the
pensionary scheme applicable to the Institute will not be agreed to by the Ministry of
Finance. The Committee feels that adoption of LIC Pension Scheme will prove
disadvantageous to the employees of the Institute. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the autonomous character of the Institute be done away and it should be brought at par
with other Government departments.

Further Comments

Commenting about the vacancy position in the Institute, the Secretary during her
evidence before the Committee on 17th August, 1998 stated that out of the 42 sanctioned
teaching posts, 20 posts have been filled so far. On being asked to specify the reasons for
delay in filling up of the posts, the Secretary informed the Committee that the Central

uncil of Homoeopathy have rationalised the norms and have accordingly fixed the norms
of 42 teaching posts as against 63 posts earlier, out of which at least 28 should be full time
teachers. The Secretary further clarified that these norms have not been incorporated in the
rules so far which has resulted in delay in filling up of the teaching posts.

Comments of the Committee

The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department in filling up the vacant
posts. However, the Committee emphasises that the norms fixed by the CCIH should be
incorporated in the rules without further delay. If that is not done, the Committee
apprehends that the students of the Institute will be the sufferers as 50% posts are still lying
vacant.

4.1 The Committee also feels that unless adequate pay-scales are given to the teaching
faculty or made at least at par with the other Central /State Government organisations, the
Institute will not be able to attract well qualified candidates, leaving the posts vacant as is
the case today. The Committee feels that the qualification required for the various teaching
posts should not be reduced in an attempt to fill up the vacant posts. However, the Institute
should take steps to immediately revise the pay-scales of the faculty members bringing them
at par with the Central /State Governments. The representatives from the Department of
ISM&H informed the Committee that they are awaiting the recommendations of the Sth Pay
Commission and as soon as the recommendations are made public, the Department will
revise the pay-scales of their faculty members on the lines of recommendations made by the
Pay Commission. The Committee did not agree with this plea as NIH, Calcutta is an
autonomous organisation and has no relevance with the recommendations of the Sth Pay
Commission set up by the Government. The pay scales of the teaching and non-teaching
staff have not been referred to the Sth Pay Commission at all. Therefore, the Department of
ISM&H should take immediate steps for revising the pay-scales of their teaching staff
without waiting for the recommendations of the Pay Commission.

Action Taken

4.1 Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance has decided not to entertain any
proposals pertaining to revision of pay scales, allowances, creation/ upgradation of posts,
Cadre Review/Cadre restructuring in all Ministries / Departments including autonomous
bodies pending decision on the recommendations of the Fifth Pay Commission.

Further Comments

The Secretary, during the course of oral evidence before the Committee on 15th
September, 1997 explained that the pay scales in the Institute were definitely lower. The
reasons was that there were very few post gradutes because post graduation in Homoeopathy
started late. Since they have Post Graduate Degree as the minimum qualification, for the
post of Director, the scale cannot be at par. The moment they raise the standard of Post
Graduation they will be able to give higher pay scales. On being asked as to whether any
reference was made to the Fifth Pay Commission for revision of pay scales etc., the
Secretary informed that they had not made any reference. She further stated that even
though they had not made any reference, they would be adopting the recommendations of
the Fifth Pay Commission because all Central Government rules were applicable. As regards
the pay scales of Director also she said that whatever is recommended by the Pay
Commission for the post of Director, it shall be applicable here. She further stated that a
decision had been taken recently in the Executive Committee to take steps for upgradation
of the post of Director, NIH from Rs. 3700—5000 to Rs. 5900—6700, but the Ministry of
Finance had not agreed for it and the proposal was turned down. The Secretary while
appearing before the Committee on 17th September, 1998 stated that efforts were now being
made to fill up the Post of Director on a regular basis. A few applications in response to an
advertisement in this regard have been received which will be processed shortly.

Comments of the Committee:

The Committee is not convinced by the replies given by Department and the Secretary
of the Department. The Committee is in total disagreement, that the pay scales of the
teaching staff could be revised only after a final decision of the recommendation of the Pay
Commission. Upgradation of the pay scales is different from the Pay Commission’s
recommendations. In this case the Deptt. and the Institute have committed a mistake of not
getting the pay scales revised for so long. The Committee recommends that the pay scales
should be upgraded without any further delay. As regards prescribing higher qualifications
for the teaching posts is concerned, necessary steps may also be taken immediately so that
higher pay scales could be prescribed. As regards the reference of the institute to Pay
Commission, the Committee feels that there is a total confusion. Nobody seems to be
interested in taking any decison. Either the Pay scales of the staff of the Institute should
have been referred to the Pay Commission and in case it has not been referred, independent
decision should be taken for upgradation of the pay scales of the staff. The Ministry of
Finance should also keep this in mind while taking decision in regard to the pay scales of the
teaching posts.

4.2 The Committee also feels that the Government may either consider allowing private
Practice to the Doctors or paying adequate non-practising allowances in order to attract well
qualified and competent doctors to join the Institute. The Committee also desires that
besides filling up the vacant posts, the Govt. should also sanction the 24 additional posts as
required. The Committee also feels that the Government should provide good promotional
avenues to the teachers to avoid stagnation in promotion. The Committee again reiterates
that the Government should not wait for the outcome of the Sth Pay Commission and take
immediate decisions on these long pending issues.


Action Taken

4.2 Govt of India’s decisions regarding private practice and the quantum of NPA are
applicable in the NIH, Calcutta and all other autonomous bodies working under the Deptt.
of ISM&H. Departure from these norms would create problems both within and outside the
Department of ISM&H.

The short-term goal of the Deptt. is to try and fill up all vacancies in the teaching
faculty. Regarding posts which have already been created some progress has been made in
this regard. The long term goal of the Department is to create and fill up all posts as per
Central Council of Homoeopathy norms

Further Comments

The Secretary further informed that the Department had made a reference to the
Ministry of Finance for reviving the posts in writing. On a query made by the Committee as
to whether the ban on filling up all the posts which remain vacant for more than one year
applies to teaching institutions or not, she stated that it was necessary to go to the Ministry
of Finance for reviving the posts.

Comments of the Committee:

The Committee feels that such a ban would put hurdles in the smooth functioning of the
teaching institutions. Teaching institutions should be made an exception from such a ban
because from the time post fall vacant till they are filled up, sometimes it might take very
long time and meanwhile the one year period may lapse. Therefore, the Ministry of Finance
should amend its rules and exempt the teaching institutions from applying such a ban.
