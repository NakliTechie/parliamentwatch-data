I. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The main objective of the Department is to ensure qualitative, and quantitative
improvements in Youth Programme’ and also on broad basing and bringing about awareness
of sports and physical fitness amongst school children. and youth. The programmes
undertaken by the Department are mainly Sports and Games and Youth Programme.

1.2 The Department finances various youth welfare programmes ranging from personal-
ity development to vocational training. Most of these programmes are implemented by
reputed institutions, non-governmental organisations as well as State Governments whereby
financial assistance is provided by the Mepartment as per the stipulated norms. The major
schemes for youth development are: National Service Scheme, Nehru Yuva Kendra
Sangathan, National Integration Programmes, Youth Hostels and National Service Volun-
teers Scheme, etc.

1.3 The Department also finances a number of: programmes which aim at promotion of
sports, including creation of sports infrastructure in all parts of the country, grants for laying
of synthetic surfaces, spotting and nurturing of talents through the schemes of Sports
Authority of India, grants to National Sports Federation, National Championships, organis-
ing of tournaments for rural youth, women and in the North-Eastern Region etc.

Department for the year 1997-98 was Rs. 159.33 crores in BE which was reduced to
Rs.158.39 crores at RE stage. The allocation made for the year 1998-99 is Rs. 214.76 crores
(Plan Rs.179.00 crores and Non-Plan Rs. 35.76 crores). -'

IV. APPROACH TO THE NINTH FIVE YEAR PLAN

4.1 The Committee has taken note of the approach on Youth Affairs and Sports during
the Ninth Plan period. The contents of relevant paras from the Approach Paper to the Ninth
Five Year Plan are reproduced as -below:—

“The thrust in Youth Affairs will be to consolidate and expand youth activities
by involving youths in the entire gamut of the developmental process as the youth
are a major resource in the task of nation building. Youth activities will focus on
environmental and health programmes such as greening of wastelands, solid waste
management, anti-smoking compaign, prevention of drug-abuse, health education
with emphasis on reproductive health and prevention of AIDS, population control as
well as various adventure activities. Greater access will be given to the rural and
marginalised youth in the vocational training programmes of Nehru Yuva Kendras by
involving the NGOs, self-help groups and community polytechnics in the task.

The thrust in sports will be on providing greater access to sport facilities through
substantial investments in physical education, infrastructure development including
centres of.sport physiology and sports medicine and in creating wide spread
awareness for physical fitness through nutrition, health education and yoga with
special focus on school children. Area specific sports programmes recognising the
traditional sports skills of the inhabitants, specially tribal population, will receive
priority consideration. Rural sports programmes will be revamped in order to tap
vast talents available in the rural areas. Special attention will be given for the
promotion of sports and games among the disabled. the need for a hélistic approach
that will integrate youth and sports activities and programmes within the context of

‘ education is well recognised and will guide all actions”.

Vv. NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN

5.1 Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan is an autonomous organisation under the
Department. It is the largest grass-root level organisation in the Asia and Pacific region,
catering to the needs of:more than 8 million non-student rural youth in the age group of
15—35 years enrolled through 1.82 lakh village-based youth clubs covering education,
training, employment promotion, income generation, self-employment, enterprise creation
etc. Allocation to Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan on plan side has been enhanced from
Rs. 12.00 crores in BE 1997-98 and Rs. 11.40 crores in RE 1997-98 to Rs. 17.35 crores in
BE 1998-99 10 take care of programme funds requirements. According to the Department,
due to financial constraints against proposed release of Rs. 1.00 lakh per Kendra only Rs.
35000/-per Kendra could be released for the regular programmes of NYKS during 1997-98.
The Committee disapproves any reduction in the allocation for NYKS as it would go against
the interests of the rural youth. With the enhanced allocation, it will be possible to give
about Rs. 1.35 lakhs per Kendra for regular programmes during 1998-99. The Department
also informed that in addition to its own programmes, the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan is
implementing programmes of various other State Governments, Central Government and
other national/nternational agencies.

5.2 The Committee during the course of discussion with the representatives of the
Department expressed the view that activities of NYKS are not being noticed at grass-root
level. Even people’s representatives of the area are not aware of the activities undertaken by
concerned NYKS. The Committee in this connection would like to suggest that efforts
should be made to involve people’s representatives of the concerned areas. It would also. be
in the interest of the programme because it would be conducted in a more democratic,
meaningful and transparent manner. The Committee feels that the Department should look
into the feasibility of forming a task force with a view to identify'any new schemes to be
included under NYKS.


VI. NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME

6.1 The National Service Scheme launched in Gandhiji’s Birth Centenary Year 1969,
with primary focus on the development of personality of students through community service
has at present over 13.75 Jakh student volunteers on the roll spread over 174 universities/+2
system. Since its inception, more than 1.52 crore students from the Universities, Colleges
and Institutions of higher learning have benefited from NSS activities. Due to its
overwhelming popularity and demand, the scheme was extended to the students of +2 level
in selected institutions.

6.2 The Department informed that the expenditure on programmes of NSS is shared
between the Cent¥e and the State Governments in the ratio of 7:5, except in the case of
Jammu & Kashmir and Union Territories without Legislature, where the entire expenditure
is met by the Central Government.

6.3 According to the information furnished to the Committee, for 1997-98 the target was
to enroll 13.75 lakh student volunteers which was fully achieved. 6764 special camps on
“Youth for Sustainable Development’ and ‘Wasteland and Watershed management” were
conducted. Besides, 6764 villages were adopted for literacy and other developmental
activities. Under AIDS Awareness Programme, 600 teachers and 60,000 peer educators were
trained and 30 lakh students were sensitised towards HIV/AIDS. There is a‘need to
reorganize NSS as third dimension of education. A number of universities have alrédy
integrated NSS into the curriculum. There is tremendous scope for further increase in the
number of NSS volunteers in the country but, due to resource crunch, it has not been found
possible to do so, the Department informed.

6.4 For 1998-99, the target is to enroll 15.13 lakh student volunteers and to conduct
7,500 special camps. Besides, it is also planned to adopt 6000 villages for literacy and other
development activities.

शा. YOUTH CLUBS

7.1 The scheme is being implemented through Nehru Yuvak Kendra Sangathan and
State Governments/Union Territory Administrations. It aims at assisting newly/set up youth
clubs to enable them to start their activities and\ get established. One time financial
assistance of Rs. 5000/- is given to registered youth clubs. During the year 1996-97, 960
youth clubs were given financial assistance.

7.2 The Committee is happy to note that the working group, constituted for the
formulation of the Ninth Five Year Plan, had recommended merger of this scheme with the
scheme for Assistance to Youth Club and steps in this regard have been initiated. The
Committee is also happy to note that as recommended by it earlier, the amount of assistance
to Youth Clubs is proposed to be enhanced from Rs. 5000 to 10,000.

शा. NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEER SCHEME

8.1 The National Service Volunteer Scheme, launched in 1977-78, aims at providing
apportunities to youth to involve themselves, .. a voluntary basis, in nation building

, activities for a year or two.

8.2 At Present, 3500 NSVs are deployed with the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan,
National Service Scheme, Bharat Scouts & Guides, ‘Youth Hostels Association of India,
IDARAs,"etc. The State Governments/Union Territories are also availing the services of
these volunteers.

8.3 Explaining the reason for allocation of Rs. 18 crore in BE 1998-99 compared to Rs.
2.85 crores in BE 1997-98, the Department informed that a new scheme as announced in the
Budget Speech of the Finance Minister for creation of a National Reconstruction Cedéps

. which will mobilige youth for community based nation building activities is being formulated

in consultation with concerned Ministries/Departments.

8.4 The Committee is of the view that funds for any new scheme may be allocated only
after completing the exercise of consultation among various Ministries/Departments and
scrutiny by Ministry of Finance. It would have been better if the scheme has been allocated
the required fund at RE stage after completing all rounds of deliberations. The Committee,
however, hopes that the scheme will be formulated and implemented during the current year
itself.

XII. PREPARATION OF NATIONAL TEAMS FOR THE MAJOR INTERNATIONAL
SPORTS EVENTS

12.1 According to the information furnished to the Committee, the Sports Authority of
India in collaboration with the National Sports Federation/ Association prepares the
national teams for different sports tournaments at the international level.

12.2 According to the Department, the Commonwealth Games and Asian Games are
the two mega sports events to be held during the year 1998. The status position with regard
to preparation of Indian teams for these games is indicated in the succeeding paragraphs:—

Commonwealth Games:

These games are scheduled to be held at Kuala Lampur in Malaysia in 14 disciplines
w.e.f. 11th to 21st September, 1998. India is likely to participate in 6 disciplines viz.
Boxing, Cricket, Hockey (men ¢: women), Squash, Shooting and Weightlifting where
medal prospects are better. The coaching camps for preparation of national teams for
the above games are being held at Patiala, Bangalore, Calcutta and Shillaroo.

Asian Games:

The Asian Games are slated to be held from 6th to 20th December, 1998 at Bangkok
(Thailand) in 36 disciplines. As per the present level of performance of our team,
there‘ are prospects of winning medals in Athletics, Billiards. and Snooker, Boxing,
Hockey, Kabaddi, Shooting, Tennis, Weightlifting and Yachting. The requirement of
sports equipment and the services of foreign coaches for training the national teams
for the above games have been finalized.

12.3 The Department further informed the Committee that in order to monitor the
preparation of our national teams for the ensuing Commonwealth and the Asian Games,
discipline-wise Monitoring Committees under the Chairmanship of Director-General and a
