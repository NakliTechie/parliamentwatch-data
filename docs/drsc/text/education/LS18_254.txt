I. INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1 The Ministry  of Women and Child Development is the nodal agency for all 
matters pertaining to the welfare, development and empowerment of women and children 
in the country and for promoting their survival, protection and development in a holistic 
manner.  The Department of Women and Child Development was set up in 1985 as a part 
of the Ministry of Human Resource Development and was upgraded into the Ministry of 
Women and Child Development on the 30 th January, 2006.  The Ministry was constituted 
with the intention of ad dressing gaps in State action for women and children and for 
promoting inter-ministerial and inter-sectoral convergence to create gender equitable and 
child centered policies, programmes, schemes and legislation.  The Ministry also aims to 
increase the dec ision-making potential of women and encourage child participation in 
matters concerning them.  The role of the Ministry of Women and Child Development 
includes policy formulation, programme implementation, co -ordination with other 
sectors, child and gender  budgeting, data management, training and capacity building, 
reporting and monitoring on status of children and women and their rights.  This 
enhances the coordinating role of the Ministry with all other sectors.  Promoting social 
and economic empowerment of women and ensuring survival, development, care and 
protection of children drive the Ministry's agenda.  Principles of equity, justice and non -
discrimination guide the Ministry's actions concerning women and children.   
 
1.2 The Special Secretary, Minist ry of Women and Child Development in his 
presentation before the Committee on the 26 th March, 2013 gave an overview of the 
requirements of the Ministry for the Twelfth Plan, the various programmes/schemes 
handled by the Ministry and achievements thereof.  He apprised the Committee that the 
funds allocated for the next year's Budget Estimates as well as the current year's Revised 
Estimates were substantially short of what was projected by the Ministry.  These were 
particularly much short in comparison to wha t the Planning Commission's Working 
Group had recommended as compared to the Ministry's original proposals before the 
Planning Commission.  The Ministry had suggested an overall budgetary allocation of 
`2.84 lakh crore for the Twelfth Plan against which a firm commitment of about `1.17 
lakh crore has been made which was only 40per cent of the projected requirement of the 

Ministry.  Out of the projected requirement of `2.84 lakh crore, `1.83 lakh crore was only 
for the Flagship Programme, ICDS Scheme which h ad been recently upgraded by way of 
restructuring and strengthening with the objective of universalizing the ICDS Scheme.  
Apart from the ICDS Scheme, `1.00 lakh crore was proposed for other schemes of the 
Ministry, against which the allocation for the Twe lfth Plan had been firmed up at `9,204 
crore.  He informed the Committee that traditionally, ICDS Scheme had been enjoying a 
share of more than 90 per cent of the overall Plan outlay of the Ministry.  The Ministry 
now wanted to bring the ratio between the ICDS Scheme and other schemes to 60:40 so 
that more emphasis could be given to other schemes, especially women -related ones.  
However, the approved allocation was similar to the traditional pattern as against an 
allocation of `1,08,000 crore for ICDS Schem e, the other schemes had been given only 
`9204 crore which was about 8 to 9 per cent of the total allocation. 
  
1.3 With respect to allocation, specifically for the year 2013 -14, the Special Secretary 
apprised the Committee that against the projected requi rement of `212 crore for Central 
Sector Schemes relating to children and `268 crore for women development schemes, the 
Ministry had got `165 crore for schemes relating to children and `154 crore for women 
development schemes.  It was roughly one -third less  than the projected requirements.  
For the other schemes, `389 crore was given against the demand of `550 crore.   
 
1.4 An important issue highlighted by the Special Secretary was the steeply declining 
child sex ratio.  Child sex ratio had been declining o ver the last four or fine censuses and 
particularly a steep decline in the census report of 2011 from 927 girls per 1000 boys in 
the age group of 0 -6 to 914 per 1000 boys had been reported.  Keeping in mind the fact 
that the overall sex ratio had gone up f rom 933 females against 1000 males to 940 in the 
same period, this was particularly alarming.  More alarming was the fact that this 
phenomena had spread to areas which were not known for disparity in the child sex ratio 
including tribal areas, states like J&K where these things were unheard of.  The trend was 

particularly acute in more developed areas of the country including metropolitan areas.  
The Ministry, therefore, wanted to target this issue in right earnest.  As a result, few 
interventions on a small scale at the district level were to be initiated to raise the sex ratio 
for age group 0-6 years from 914 in 2011 to 930 by 2016-17. 
 
1.5 The Committee has been given to understand that the Ministry has been 
given less than half of the projected requireme nts of funds for the Twelfth Plan.  
Since almost 90 per cent of the allocated funds is set aside for the ICDS Scheme, the 
remaining schemes do not get adequate attention with respect to fund allocation.  
The Committee agrees that other non -ICDS Schemes , especially women -related 
schemes need to be focused upon.  However, demand for enhanced allocation for the 
Ministry needs to be justified with satisfactory utilization  of funds given.  The 
Committee feels that the important schemes of the Ministry catering t o vulnerable 
sections of our society i.e women and children should not suffer due to dearth of 
funds.  The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to take up the 
matter of appropriate fund allocation with the Planning Commission for its 
important schemes.  
 
II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
 
2.1 There has been a considerable increase in the yearly outlays and the expenditures 
incurred vis -à-vis Budget Estimates/Revised Estimates for the development of women 
and children in the past recent years.  The Plan o utlay for the Ministry of Women and 
Child Development has gone up from `18,500 crore in 2012-13 to `20,350 crore in 2013-
14, registering an increase of 10per cent.  This increase in allocation has been attributed 
to substantial increase in allocation for t he ICDS Scheme from `15,850 crore in 2012 -13 
to `17,700 crore in BE 2013 -14 on account of restructuring and strengthening of ICDS.  
Besides that, the Ministry proposes to implement a new comprehensive Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme called the 'Umbrella Scheme on Protection & Empowerment of 
Women' during  the Twelfth Plan period.  The umb rella scheme contains major sub -
schemes/components like One Stop Crisis Centre, Restorative Justice for Victim of Rape, 

Scheme for implementation of PWDV Act etc for which enhanc ed allocation of  `300 
crore has been made for the year 2013-14. 
 
2.2 While the total allocation for child welfare schemes increased from `15,147.22 
crore in RE 2012 -13 to `17,143.70 crore in BE 2013 -14, the total allocation for women 
welfare schemes did no t witness any substantial increase.  Total allocation for women 
welfare schemes in BE 2013 -14 is `914.68 crore.  The Committee notes that total 
allocation for schemes for women welfare was reduced from `858.68 crore in BE 2012-13 
to `250.84 crore at the RE  stage.  The reasons given by the Ministry for such a drastic 
reduction in allocation at the RE stage was that it was expected that major schemes like 
National Women Helpline, Swadhar Greh, One Stop Crisis Centre, Restorative Justice for 
Victims of Rape etc would become operational during 2012-13 but due to procedural and 
administrative reasons, these schemes could not take off and hence the budgetary 
provision was reduced at the RE stage.

stage, utilization thereof exceeded that, allocation for women -related schemes was not 
only reduced at the RE stage,  utilization was even less than that as is evident from the

2.6 Protracted preparatory exercise prior to launch of a new scheme, unduly 
long time taken in getting necessary approvals and  non-receipt of viabl e proposals 
etc. are the main factors behind funds getting reduced at the RE stage.  The 
Committee feels that inordinate delays due to one procedural reason or another 
ultimately leads to delayed implementation of scheme s.  Ideally, least possible time  
should be taken for introduction and actual implementation of a new scheme so that 
the benefits  of the scheme reach the targeted beneficiaries at the earliest .  All 
preparatory exercises  should be completed  and necessary approvals should be 
sought within a specified time-frame so as to avoid any procedural or administrative 
delays.  It would be appropriate if a review of prodedural formalities prior to 
launch of a new scheme is taken so that unnecessary bottlenecks get eliminated and 
this exercise gets expedite d. The Commi ttee firmly believes that concerted efforts 
need to be made by the Ministry with active co -operation from and co -ordination 
with the State Governments for successfully initiating the implementation of 
schemes, leading to timely and proper utili zation of funds earmarked for the 
schemes in a financial year.

procedural formalities and accounting system need to be identified and addressed so

III. HIGHLIGHTS OF WORKING G ROUP REPORT ON WOME N'S 
AGENCY AND EMPOWERMENT 
 
3.1 Committee's attention has been drawn to the facts relat ing to women's position 
highlighted by the Working Group Report on "Women's Agency and Empowerment" for 
the Twelfth Plan.  The Report of the Working Group inter alia focuses on the various 
parameters reflecting the status and position of women in society, the important ones 
being mentioned below:- 
 the sex ratio as per the Census 2011: 940 females per 1000 males 
 the MMR (per 100,000 live births) SRS 2009-10 was 212 

 the literacy rate for females as per the Census 2011 was 65.46% 
 Worker population ratio (per 1000) is 336 females and 819 for males 
 Only 10.82% of MPs were women 
 Total number of crimes against women increased by 29.6% between 2006 and 
2010 as per the study of the data from National Crime Records Bureau 
 As per the study by the Ministry of WCD commis sioned in 2004, around 2.8 
million women were victims of trafficking, out of which 36% were children 
 As per the data from the 66 th round of the NSSO, there had been a decline in 
females work participation between 2004 -05 to 2009-10.  The share of women in 
visual status workers declined from 28.7% to 22.8%.  In rural areas, it declined 
from 32.7% to 26.1% and in urban areas from 16.6% to 13.8%.  Over 90 % of 
women are in informal employment.  
 
Even though literacy rates, enrolment and drop out rates in prima ry education, life 
expectancy, infant mortality, maternal mortality rates etc showed a progressive trend, 
various other parameters continue to be heavily skewed against women. 
 
3.2 The Twelfth Plan Working Group (WG) on Women's Agency and Empowerment 
built on the view that development was a process of expanding freedoms equally for all 
individuals and considers gender equality as a core development goal in itself.  The Plan 
advocates a shift from mere income poverty of women to the adoption of a 'multi -
dimensional' approach to poverty and well being.  The Multi -dimensional Penalty Index 
(MPI) complements the income poverty measures by reflecting all the other deprivations 
with respect to education, health and living standard that a poor person simultaneously  
faces.  The issue of multi -dimensional poverty was extremely relevant to the status of 
women in India today because although a lot of work had been done on the condition of 
women, the position of women still remained largely unaddressed. Further, the Plan  
would work towards making a transition from viewing women as victims of poverty to 
empowering them to change the contexts in which they live.  The issue of capability 
poverty was a critical imperative in this.  Poverty was not only a lack of material 
resources but also lack of power and choice. 
 
3.3 The Committee takes note of the following goals for the Twelfth Plan:- 
- Creating greater 'freedom' and 'choice' for women by generating 
awareness and creating institutional mechanisms to help women, question 
prevalent "patriarchal" beliefs that are detrimental to their empowerment. 

- Improving health and education indicators for women like maternal 
mortality, infant mortality, nutrition levels, enrolment and retention in 
primary, secondary and higher education. 
- Reducing the incidence of violence against women and providing quality 
care services to the victims. 
- Improving employability of women, work participation rates especially in 
the organized sector and increased ownership of assets and control over 
resources. 
- Increasing women's access to public services and programmes through 
establishing and strengthening convergence mechanisms at multiple 
levels, creation of physical infrastructure for women and improving the 
capacity of women's organizations and collectives. 
- Ensuring that the specific concerns of single and disadvantaged women 
are addressed. 
 
3.4 Keeping in view the present position of women in the country, the Committee 
believes that the goals and thrust areas of the Twelfth Plan assume great 
importance.  Even after more than sixty years of India becoming independent,  
women of the country continue to be the most vulnerable section of the society. They 
face discrimination of all kinds, face violence and abusive situations, restrictions on 
their mobil ity, questions about their safety and security, exclusion from public 
political sphere, unequal share of entitlements, resources and public services and all 
sorts of structural barriers. This discouraging situation continues to persist inspite 
of Indian wo men gaining new heights in every walk of life.  The Committee is, 
however, surprised to note that this grim scenario has not led to any pro -active 
action.  Not only the overall allocation for women related scheme s has been 
increasing at a snail 's pace in c omparison to child related scheme s, inadequate 
utilization of funds remains a cause of grave concern.  The Committee would like to 
emphasize that the schemes of the Ministry for the welfare of women contribute to 
women's economic empowerment (STEP , Hostels for Working Women,  RMK, 
Priyadarshini etc.) provide social support to women in difficult circumstances (One 
Stop Crisis Centre, Implementation of Protection of Women from Domestic 
Violence Act, Women's Help Line, Ujjawala, Swadhar, Scheme for Financial 
Assistance and support resources to victims of rape etc.) and schemes for advocacy 
and capacity building (Condensed Courses of Education for Women,  Awareness 

Generation Projects for rural and poor women , Training Programmes of NIPCCD

IV. INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES SCHEME 
 
4.1 The ICDS Scheme is a centrally sponsored flagship scheme of the GOI started in 
1975 to address the health, nutrition and development needs of 0 -6 year's children, 
pregnant and nursing mothers.   It is the world's largest and most unique and outreach 
programme providing a package of six services comprising (i) supplementary nutrition, 
(ii) immunization, (iii) health check -up, (iv) referral services (v) pre -school non -formal 
education and (vi) nutrition and health education.  The ICDS Scheme has been 
universalized with the recent 3rd phase of expansion of the Scheme by increasing the total 
approved number of ICDS Projects and AWCs including Mini -Anganwadi Centres to 
7076 and 14 lakh respectively with a provision of 20,000 Anganwadis on demand.  The 
Scheme preseltly operates t hrough a network of about 7006 operational projects and 
about 13.31 lakh AWCs as on 31.01.2013.

4.29 Committee's attention has been drawn to the findings of the CAG Report on the 
vacancy position of ICDS functionaries.  The Report categorically states that the 
State/UT Governments failed to recruit ICDS staff contemporaneously with th e 
expansion of the Scheme.  The vacancy position against the sanctioned strength had

4.30 The Committee has been given to understand by the Ministry that since the

and other ICDS functionaries was also done by the States/UTs.  However, the Ministry 
had been regularly taking up the issue relating to filling up of vacancies at various levels

considering the expansion and univers alization of the ICDS Scheme with no 
attention being given to adequate manpower to make A WCs functional seems to be

Committee hopes that the Ministry considers the problem of vacant positions of 
ICDS functionaries seriously and address es the same in consultation and co -
operation with the State Governments. 
 
4.32 The Committee would also like to emphasize that the initiative of the 
Ministry regarding having a separate cadre for ICDS st aff is indeed a welcome step

Government is giving 90 per cent of the funds to the ICDS (General), it also needs to

Role and responsibility and training of AWWs 
 
4.33 The Committee observes that there are manifold roles and responsibilities of 
AWWs which are as follows :- 
 To elicit community support and participation in running the programme. 
 To weigh each child every month, record the weight graphically on  the growth 
card, use referral card for referring cases of mothers/children to the sub -
centres/PHC etc., and maintain child cards for children below 6 years and produce 
these cards before visiting medical and para-medical personnel. 
 To carry out a quick su rvey of all the families, especially mothers and children in 
those families in their respective area of work once in a year. 
 To organize non-formal pre-school activities in the Anganwadi of children in the 
age group 3-6 years of age and to help in designin g and making of toys and play 
equipment of indigenous origin for use in anganwadi. 
 To organize supplementary nutrition feeding for children (0 -6 years) and 
expectant and nursing mothers by planning the many based on locally available 
food and local recipes. 
 T provide health and nutrition education and counseling on breast feeding/infant 
& young feeding practices to mothers.  Anganwadi Workers, being close to the 
local community, can motivate married women to adopt family planning/birth 
control measures. 
 To help and coordinate the pregnant and lactating mothers visiting the center to 
get the birth of their child registered and share the information with the village 
level functionary who has been notified as Registrar of Births. 
 To make home visits for educati ng parents to enable mothers to plan an effective 
role in the child's growth and development with special emphasis on new born 
child. 
 To maintain files and records as prescribed. 
 To assist the PHC staff in the implementation of health component of the 
programme viz. immunization, health check -up, ante natal and post natal check 
etc. 
 To assist ANM in the administration of IFA and Vitamin A by keeping stock of 
the two medicines in the centre without maintaining stock register as it would add 
to her administra tive work which would effect her main functions under the 
Scheme. 
 To share information collected under ICDS Scheme with the ANM.  However, 
ANM will not solely rely upon the information obtained from the records of 
AWW. 
 To bring to the notice of the Supervi sors/CDPO any development in the village 
which requires their attention and intervention, particularly in regard to the work 
of the coordinating arrangements with different departments. 

 To maintain liaison with other institutions (Mahila Mandals) and invol ve lady 
school teachers and girls of the primary/middle schools in the village which have 
relevance to her functions. 
 To guide Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHA) engaged under National 
Rural Health Mission in the delivery of health care services and  maintenance of 
records under the ICDS Scheme. 
 To assist in implementation of Kishori Shakti Yojana (KSY) and motivate and 
educate the adolescent girls and their parents and community in general by 
organizing social awareness programmes/campaigns etc. 
 AWW would also assist in implementation of Nutrition Programme for 
Adolescent Girls (NPAG) as per the guidelines of the Scheme and maintain such 
record as prescribed under the NPAG. 
 Anganwadi Worker can function as depot holder for RCH Kit/contraceptives and 
disposable delivery kits.  However, actual distribution of delivery kits or 
administration of drugs, other than OTC (Over the Counter) drugs would actually 
be carried out by the ANM or ASHA as decided by the Ministry of Health and 
Family Welfare. 
 To identify the disability among children during her home visits and refer the case 
immediately to the nearest PHC or District Disability Rehabilitation Centre. 
 To support in organizing Pulse Polio Immunization (PPI) drives. 
 To inform the ANM in case of emergency cases like diahorrea, cholera etc. 
 
4.34 The Committee observes that Anganwadi Workers are matriculate women 
from the local community who come forward to render their services on part -time 
basis.  Being honorary workers, they are paid a monthly honorarium of  `2500.00 
and the benefits like pension, gratuity and other allowances are not applicable to 
them.  They do not have any fixed tenure of service, with only the age of their 
disengagement being fixed at maximum 65 years.  However, they are expected to be 
a multi-tasking expert, being assigned as many as 22 duties, with many of them 
being wide -ranging.  The duties of an Anganwadi Woker as enumerated above 
indicate that not only is she supposed to carry out AWC -specific duties but is also 
expected to make home -visits, maintain a variety of records meticulously.  She is 
also mandated to assist the PHC Staff, ANM, to guide ASHA, to maintain liaison 
with Mahila Mandals, lady school teachers and girls of primary/middle schools, to 
assist in the implementation of Ki shori Shakti Yojana, Nutrition Programme for 
Adolescent Girls, to assist in Pulse Polio Drives and lastly to be in touch with her 
supervisors.

V. INTEGRATED CHILD PROTECTION SCHEME (ICPS) 
 
5.1 ICPS is a Cent rally Sponsored Scheme with a view to create a safe and secure 
environment for comprehensive development of children who are in need of care and 
protection, children in conflict with law and other vulnerable children.  The Scheme is 
being implemented from financial year 2009 -10 mainly through the State 
Governments/UT Administrations.  The Scheme has brought several child related 
schemes under one umbrella and also initiated new interventions.  The schemes brought 
within this umbrella scheme are Programme fo r Juvenile Justice, Integrated Programme 
for Street Children and Scheme for Assistance to Homes (Shishu greh) to promote in -
country adoption.  To encourage quicker establishment and procedures under the JJ Act, 
the Ministry introduced the ICPS Scheme for providing financial and technical support to 
the States/UTs.   
 
5.2 The Report of the Working Group on Child Rights for the Twelfth Plan gave a 
situational analysis of children and estimated that around 172 million or 40 per cent of 
India's children were vu lnerable to or experienced difficult circumstances. The major 
highlights of children in conflict with law, children in need of care and protection, 
children living on streets, etc are as given below:- 
 
- Increasing incidences of juvenile crimes from 1999 to 2009.  Under IPC 
crimes, the highest numbers of apprehensions were for theft (6540), hurt 
(4386), burglary (3210) and riots (2025).  These constituted 55.8 per cent of 
the reasons for children being in conflict with law under IPC. 
- Crime committed by childr en under special and local law included highest 
number of juveniles arrested under the Gambling Act (1216), Excise Act 
(613), Prohibition Act (476) and Arms Act (234).  These accounted for 54.4 
per cent of total juveniles arrested 
- Children in need of care and protection as 12.6 million child labourers in the 
age group of 5 -14 in hazardous occupations as per 2001 census.  As per a 
survey by NSSO, the number of working children was reported as 9.075 
million in 2004-05. 
- A study 'surviving the streets' (2011) o n children living on the streets in nine 
districts in Delhi found that there were 27.91 per cent of street living children, 
29.05 per cent of street -working children and 36.03 per cent of those from 
street families.  Nearly one per cent of the total number  of children in Delhi 
were living and working on the streets.  67.3 per cent children felt that they 
were in danger on the streets, 22 per cent children reported being dependent 
on some form of substance of which nearly 50 per cent were daily consumers. 

- Crimes against children increased by 18.57 per cent between 2007 and 2009 
as reflected in Crimes in India 2009.  The increase is attributed to an increase 
in kidnapping and abduction, infanticide, rape and murder during the period. 
- Child Abuse rampant in all  States.  Findings of the Child Abuse study by the 
Ministry in 2007 indicated that in States of Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, 
Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, M.P., Maharashtra, Mizoram, Rajasthan, UP, 
West Bengal, 68.99 per cent of children reported physical abuse in one or 
more situations, 20.90 per cent reputed one or more forms of severe sexual 
abuse, 50.76 per cent reported one or more forms of other sexual abuse, 48.37 
per cent reported one or more forms of emotional abuse and 70.57 per cent 
girl children facing one or more forms of neglect. 
 
5.3 Given the kind of data about the children in difficult circumstances and 
facing several forms of abuse and exploitation, the Committee firmly believes that 
the ICPS Scheme is of immense importance in providing a k ind of protective 
network across the country for such children.  Not just this, awareness, 
understanding and sensitivity were  required to address the grappling situations in

VI. RAJIV GANDHI SCHEME FOR EMPOWERMENT OF ADOLESCENT 
GIRLS (RGSEAG) - SABLA 
 
6.1 SABLA is a comprehensive scheme for the holistic development of adolescent 
girls introduced in the year 2010 by merging the erstwhile Kishori Shakti Yojana (KSY) 
and the Nutrition Programme for Adolescent Girls (NPAG) Scheme.  It is a Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme primarily for out of school adolescent girls (11 -18 years) 
implemented in 200 districts across the country on a pilot basis through the State 
Governments using the ICDS platform.  SABLA is being implemented through  the State 
Governments/UTs with 100 per cent financial assistance from the Central Government 
for all inputs other than nutrition provision for which 50 per cent central assistance is 
provided to States.  Anganwadi Centre is the focal point for the delivery of the services.  
SABLA aims at an all -round development of adolescent girls of 11 -18 years by making 
them self-reliant by facilitating access to learning, health and nutrition through various 
interventions such as health, education, vocational training etc. 
 
6.2 The Scheme has two major components, nutrition and non -nutrition component.  
Under the nutrition component, adolescent girls are provided with Take Home Rations 
(THR) or hot cooked meals as per t he feasibility.  While under the non -nutrition 
component, the girls are provided information on topics like improving the nutritional 
and health status, Family Welfare and Adolescent Reproductive and Sexual Health 
(ARSH).  The Scheme also provides for regu lar health check up, supply of IFA tablets, 
mainstreaming out of  school adolescent girls into school system, life skill education and 
vocational training.  Central Government is providing 100 per cent financial assistance 
for all inputs other than nutriti on.  For nutrition,50 per cent of Central assistance is 
provided to the States/UTs.  The Scheme is likely to cover nearly one crore adolescent 
girls annually.  Till September, 2011, nearly 40 lakh girls have been already benefited 
under the nutrition component of the Scheme.

VII. INDIRA GANDHI MATRITVA SAHYOG YOJANA (IGMSY) 
 
7.1 Indira Gandhi Ma tritva Sahyog Yojana (IGMSY): A Conditional Cash Transfer 
Scheme, a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for pregnant and lactating women was 
introduced in October, 2010 to contribute to a better enabling environment by providing 
cash incentives for improved health and nutrition to pregnant and nursing mothers.  The 
Scheme envisages providing cash to pregnant and lactating (P&L) women during 
pregnancy and lactation in response to individual fulfilling of specific conditions.  It 
addresses short term income support objectives with the long term objective of behavioral 
and attitudinal changes.  The Scheme attempts to partly compensate for wage loss to 
pregnant and lactating women, both prior to and after delivery of the child.  Being 
implemented on pilot basis in 53 sel ected districts using the platform of ICDS, 12.5 lakh 
P&L women are expected to be covered every year under IGMSY.  The beneficiaries are 
paid `4000/- in three installments per P&L woman between the second trimester and till 

the child attains the age of 6 months on fulfilling specific conditions related to maternal 
and child health.  Pregnant women of 19 years of age and above for first two live births 
are eligible under the Scheme.  The modalities of cash transfer are decided by the State 
Government/UTs.  The modes of cash transfer include -nationalized bank, post office, 
cooperative bank, business correspondent model of bank, etc.

suggestion for any kind of convergence and co -ordination was due to the fact that both 
the schemes were to be handled by AWWs and ANM functionaries of AWCs and PHCs

provided cash incentives for improved health and nutrition to pregnant and nursing 
women for improving health and nutrition and partially compensate for wage loss.  It was 
also pointed out that there was close convergence of Ministry of Health and Family 
Welfare and ICDS at the grass root level which facilitated dissemination of information 
about JSY and IGMSY schemes and availing benefits thereof.   
 
7.7 The Committee is constrained to observe that even though both the schemes, 
JSY and IGMSY cater to the needs of pregnant and nursing mothers, the Ministry 
seems reluctant to explore the possi bility of convergence of the two schemes.  The 
Committee is only making an attempt to highlight the fact that most of Indian 

women, especially those residing in rural areas are in poor state of health and are 
weak and anaemic.  Such women when pregnant nee d all kinds of support and care 
for their well -being during pregnancy and delivery and also during nursing. All 
these aspects concerning their health, nutrition and pre and ante -natal care are 
inter-connected and hence convergence of both the schemes would be desirable. The 
Committee, therefore, would like to reiterate its earlier recommendation and 
request the Ministry to explore the possibility of convergence of the two schemes for 
the well-being of pregnant mothers and avoid any over-lapping.

steps needed to be taken for making the whole process of adoption a much more 
simpler and easier one for prospective parents:- 
 

 Children Homes to be connected directly to the adoption agencies with 
complete information about all the children in their Homes indicating the 
availability of a number of children residing in a Home 
 A maximum time frame need to be specified so that prospective parents have 
a rough idea about the possible time that would be taken  in completing all 
procedures and adopting a child.  
 Delays at the level of Child Welfare Committee and the Court granting 
adoption need to be avoided and a specified time -frame should be specified 
for these agencies too to grant adoption , thereby minimizing the endless wait 
of the prospective parents 
  Check on all adoptions through unauthorized individuals and agencies. 
 Data base of missing children should be circulated amongst all Children 
Homes from time to time to trace missing children if they had some how 
reached in any Home so as to unite them with their parents. 
 
The Committee believes that the hopes and desire of prospective parents should not 
be dashed in view of the complicated adoption procedure and their endless wait for 
adopting a child.  Moreov er, encouraging child and parent friendly procedure 
would help many orphaned, abandoned or surrendered children to find homes and 
families to blossom with warmth and love. 
 
8.7 Committee’s attention has also been drawn to the recommendation of giving 
CARA the status of a statutory structure under the JJ Act made by the Working 
Group on Child Rights for the Twelfth Plan.  The Committee is in agreement with 
the Working Group observation that such a status would result in CARA 
addressing adequately the issues r elated to monitoring, accountability, conflict of 
interest, scrutiny of agencies,data management etc. It is also high time that presence 
of CARA is in all the States. 
 
8.8 Attention of the Committee has been drawn to media reports where cases of 
exploitation of children in inter -country adoptions have been reported.  The Ministry has 
submitted that the guidelines 'Governing the Adoptions of Children 2011'  ensured 
adequate safeguards for children placed in inter -country adoption as provided in the 
Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Cooperation in respect of Inter -country 
Adoption (1993) ratified by GOI.  Each case of inter -country adoption was approved by 
competent authority of the receiving country which was obliged to provide adequate 
safeguard to the adopted children. The progress reports of the children placed in inter -

country adoption were monitored for a period of two years and the child protection 
service of the receiving country took appropriate measures in case of any abuse or 
neglect.  C hildren placed in inter -country adopted were now supported with adoption 
orders under the JJ Act to enable their citizenship expeditiously.  Moreover, amendments 
to the JJ Act were supposed to have a separate chapter on adoption.   
 
8.9 The Committee is re lieved to know that inter -country adoptions with orders 
under the JJ Act enabled the adopted children in expediting their citizenship status.  
However, there are numerous concerns regarding the inter-country adoptions, more 
so because mostly children with special need are being  placed in such adoptions.  
These children, therefore, need  much more protection from abuse and neglect and 
safeguarding of their rights.  The Committee recommends to the Ministry to 
specifically lay down guidelines and norms with res pect to inter -country adoptions 
in the JJ Acts itself as early as possible with strict monitoring provisions to check 
any abuse/neglect of the adopted child.  The Committee also notes that during 2012 -
13, CARA had undertaken inspection of 45 adoption agencies up to December, 2012. 
The Committee would appreciate if such inspections are carried out on a regular 
basis followed by corrective measures. The Committee feels that surprise 
inspections should also be made as real position at the ground level would the n 
emerge.

asset-less women from availing benefits under the Scheme.  Further, a time period

reaching the beneficiaries in real need and being assisted by well -performing 
NGOs.. 
 
 
9.7 To a specific query about the status of restructuring of RM K and utilization 
of funds for RMK, the Committee has been given to understand that it was 
proposed to restructure and strengthen RMK from  the existing RMK single office 
society to Government owned Non Deposit Taking Systematically Improvement 
Non-Banking Finance Company under Section 617 of the Companies Act, with  a 
Pan India n Office network backed by need -based capital.  This was intended to 
enable RMK to respond to the growing need for micro -finance in low -income 
households on reasonable terms and condi tions and to reach out more effectively to 
poor women in the hither to less-covered or uncovered States/areas.  An EFC note 
dated the 12th August, 2011 was circulated by the Ministry to incorporate a new 
Public Limited Company, namely RMK limited by GOI by infusion of `100.00 crore 

in the first year and capitalized at `500.00 crore by the fifth year.  This was 
considered by EFC in its meeting held on the 18th June, 2012.  The EFC expressed 
concerns on whether the NBFC as proposed would be financially indepen dent as 
restructuring would add to operational costs; whether the proposed NBFC would 
access funds from the market and if so at what point and in what form; whether on 
dissolution of the Society, the funds in its accounts could be transferred to NBFC 
instead of being returned to Government account and also whether the entire 
business of the Society, including doubtful debts, decreed cases etc. could be taken 
over by NBFC.    In view of  the above, opinion of Attorney General through  the 
Ministry of Law on th e legal issue involved in the dissolution of RMK as a Society 
and transfer of its business to the proposed NBFC, was requested by this Ministry 
vide D.O. letter dated the 31st July, 2012.  The opinion/advice of the Department of 
Legal Affairs/Ld. Attorney General in the matter was awaited.  Hence, in the 
absence of approval of competent authority, expected expenditure of `100.00 crore 
(including `10.00 crore under NE) for incorporation of RMK Limited could not be 
made.  A sum of `20.00 crore (including `2.00 crore under NE) has been kept in BE 
2013-14 for incorporation of RMK Limited. 
  
9.8 The Committee takes a serious view of nil utilization of funds for RMK since 
the last three years due to the restructuring exercise of RMK which has yet not got 
the neces sary approvals.  The whole process of seeking approval for the 
restructuring exercise is moving at a snail's pace leading to no sanctioning of funds 
to States/UTs and nil performance of the scheme during the last three years.  The 
Committee can only hope t hat the restructuring exercise is expedited by the 
Ministry after seeking the necessary approvals so that women beneficiaries benefit 
from the restructured scheme with new components.

FICCI included under the revised Scheme. 
 
The State Governments had been advised to disseminate the revised guidelines of the 
Scheme and send project proposals accordingly.

which was reduced to `7.47 crore at the RE stage.  Out of this, `5.75 crore had been

implications of the revised norms of the Scheme and take corrective measures to 
improve the performance of the Scheme with respect to construction of more host els

Further, the Committee had suggested to the Ministry to explore the possibility of

Committee also feels that as observed by the Working Group that all the districts

XII. NATIONAL MISSION FOR THE EMPOWERMENT OF WOMEN 
 
12.1 The National Mission for Empowerment of Women (NMEW) was launched on 
the 8 th  March, 2010, with a view to empowering women socially, economically and 
educationally.  The Mission aims to achieve empowerment of women on all these fro nts 
by securing convergence of scheme/programmes of different Ministries/Departments of 
Government of India as well as State Governments.  Alongside, the Mission shall 
monitor and review gender budgeting by Ministries/Departments.  NMEW will facilitate 
the processes that ensure holistic empowerment of women with special focus: 
- to ensure economic empowerment of women  
- to ensure that violence against women is eliminated progressively 
- to ensure social empowerment of women with emphasis on health and 
education 
- to oversee gender mainstreaming of programmes, policies of participating 
Ministries and organizations 
- to undertake awareness generation and create, if required, structures at 
district, tehsil and village level with the involvement of Panchayats f or 
their fulfillment. 
 
NMEW has identified a three pronged strategy to achieve its objective of holistic 
empowerment of women through intervention at the policy level by reviewing 
studies/evaluations/impact assessment of programmes/schemes for gender equal ity, 
strengthening the implementation and delivery mechanism and empowering women to 
demand their entitlements.   
 
12.2 The Committee has been given to understand that during 2012 -13, the 
institutional mechanism under the mission was operationalized with t he setting up of the 
National Unit headed by the Additional Secretary and Mission Director.  It also had 
experts in the fields of poverty alleviation, social empowerment, health and nutrition, 
gender budgeting, gender rights and law enforcement, empowermen t of vulnerable and 
marginalized women, media, awareness generation, advocacy and mass communication 
and Information Technology services.  Further, the Central Monitoring Committee 
(CMC) and Inter -Ministerial Co -ordination Committee (IMCC) set up to monito r and 
facilitate convergence across Ministries met during 2012 -13.  Also, 21 States had set up

Mission.  Poorna Shakti Kendras (PSKs) were to be platform/forum for women  to come 
together and explore their potential and possibilities, raise awareness about their social, 
economic and political rights and facilitate access to schemes and entitlements and build 
capabilities for promoting women's participation in decision -making.  The first such pilot 
project was launched in Pali District, Rajasthan in September, 2011 with a new model of 
delivery i.e the Convergence Model with the opening of 150 village centres.  Apart from 
all this, the Mission conducted conferences, seminars,  events and other research studies 
with the objective of achieving convergence for empowerment of women.

XIII. NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN (NCW)

apex national level body under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990.  NCW 
reviews the provisions relating to the safeguards for women under the Constitution as 
well as in other laws.  It takes up petitions from women for redressal of their grievances, 
and does promotional research for the advancement of women.   
 
 The functions/activities of the Commission include:- 
 

- to investigate and examine all matters relating to the safeguards provided for 
women under the Constitution and other laws; 
- to call for special studies or investigations into specific problems of situations 
arising out of discrimination and atrocities against women; 
- undertake promotional and educational research so as to suggest ways of 
ensuring due representation of women in all spheres and identify factors 
responsible for impending their advancement; 
- participate and ad vise on the planning process of socio -economic 
development of women; 
- evaluate the progress of the development of women under the Union and the 
States; and 
- inspect or cause to inspect a jail, remand home, women's institution or other 
place of custody where women are kept as prisoners or otherwise and take up 
with the concerned authorities for remedial action, if found necessary.

problems or situations arising out of atrocities against women.  The disturbing rise in rape

an area of concern and focus.  The Committee would like to emphasize  that NCW 
needs to concretize it efforts in such a way so as to force change by striving to make 
laws much more stringent and also actively campaign for the safety and security of 
women force.  The focus should not only be on providing relief to the victims of rape 
and molestation but also on prohibiting the occurrence of such crimes against 
women through active policing and other women -orientated initiatives.  The 
Committee hopes that NCW would engage in active and aggressive safeguarding  of 
the interests of women.

reported from India and USA. 

 
13.8 The Committee has been g iven to understand that NCW largely adopted a 
convergence approach among different Ministries and efforts were made to coordinate 
jointly on taking up matters for the victims with respective authorities.  Recent initiatives 
taken by NCW regarding problems of Indian brides deserted by NRI/Overseas husbands 
included registration and processing of complaints of deserted women by NRI/Overseas 
husbands, attempt to spread awareness on the subject and to provide information on legal 
and other remedies to innocent India woman trapped in such marriages in informative 
booklet 'Abandoned Indian Women Trapped in NRI Marriages', 'Nowhere Brides -A 
report on NRI marriages' and a brochure 'Problems relating to NRI marriages -Dos and 
Donts', pro-active steps in co -operation with State Governments of Punjab and Gujarat, 
link with list of empanelled foreign NGOs under the scheme for providing legal/financial 
assistance/rehabilitation to Indian women deserted by overseas Indian spouses etc. 
 
13.9 The Committee appreciates the initiatives taken by the NRI Cell of the NCW.  
However, inspite of these, the Committee notes the increasing number of complaints 
state-wise and country -wise.  Out of 1308 cases, only 190 had been disposed off.  A 
large number of cases still remained pending.   The Committee is of the view that all 
out efforts need to be taken for disposal of cases within a specified time.  Further, 
the Committee feels that focus of the NRI Cell should be first and foremost to 
prevent such marriages wherein the NRI groom is alr eady married or planning to 
run away.  The Committee would like to suggest that before such a marriage takes 
place, the concerned country where the NRI resides can issue an Unmarried 
Certificate before he could be permitted to get married to an Indian brid e. The 
Committee also feels that there is a need for sensitizing the parents of prospective 
brides in this regard, especially in those states where such marriages are taking 
place more frequently.  State Commission for Women can be involved in this 
exercise.  This obviously necessitated co -operation with other countries and also 
changes in the Marriage Registration Form by the Office of the Registrar General 
of Marriage.  Further, all details of the NRI groom about his residence and 
workplace etc must be br ought on record before any marriage takes place so as to 
catch hold of the groom in case of desertion of Indian brides.  This measure would 

help in curbing ill -intentioned marriages of NRI grooms and doing justice with the 
Indian brides.   
 
13.10 The Committee has been informed that a five member Expert Committee and a 
four member Sub-Committee was constituted for addressing various issues and problems 
pertaining to NRI/Overseas marriages and its report submitted but the NCW felt that the 
issue needed in depth  research and consultations with different stake holders to come out 
with a reasoned document covering identification, assessment and management of the 
problem and suggesting specific amendments to the various provision of different laws of 
the country in order to benefit the affected women.  The final report, of the Committee is, 
however, awaited.  The Committee is relieved to know that a Committee and Sub -
Committee has been formed by NCW specifically to look into the problems of 
NRI/Overseas marriages .  The Committee hopes that the final report would 
definitely come out with stringent measures including sound and verified 
documentation of the NRI grooms so that the problems arising generally in such 
marriages are curtailed to a great extent.  The Commi ttee believes that in the 
interest of women going for overseas marriage, the NCW should expedite the report 
of its Committee and Sub -Committee so as to prevent other women from being 
duped or harassed by NRI grooms.  The Committee would like to emphasize t hat 
NCW needs to play pro -active role for disposal of pending cases/complaints and 
devise a mechanism for preventing overseas marriages which are ill- intentioned. 
 
XIV. NATIONAL NUTRITION MISSION (NNM) 
 
14.1 A National Nutrition Mission (NNM) was set up u nder the Chairmanship of the 
Prime Minister in 2003.  Subsequently in 2008, the Prime Minister's National Council on 
India's Nutritional challenges was constituted.  The Council decided to (i) strengthen and 
restructure the ICDS Scheme (ii) introduce a mul ti-sectoral programme to address 
maternal and child malnutrition in selected 200 high burden districts (iii) introducing a 
nationwide information, education and communication (IEC) campaign against 
malnutrition and (iv) making nutrition a focus in the prog rammes and schemes of line 
Ministries.

of a framework of multi -sectoral programme to address the maternal and children

to bring together with various national programmes through strong institutional and 
pragmatic convergence at the State, District, Block and Village levels.  Since the multi -
sectoral Nutrition Programme had been revised, i t was likely to be delayed further in 
view of necessary approvals.   The Committee hopes that all initial procedural and 
administrative steps are cleared without any further delay for the implementation of 
both the components of the scheme.

15.1 Awareness about women and children -related issues and variety of schemes 
initiated for their welfare and protection , both in the general public as well as 
implementing agencies is the most crucial requirement so as to sensitize all concerned.  

The Committee observes that substantive funds are being duly allocated  for this purpose  
every year.  On perusal of the trend of allocation of funds a nd utilization thereof,  the 
Committee notes that during the Eleventh Plan, against an allocation of  `213.00 crore, 
utilization reported at the end of the Plan period is only `135.39 crore.  During the first 
year of the Twelfth Plan period also, only `1.96 crore could be spent by the 31 st 
December, 2012, although provision of `50.00 crore was there. 
 
15.2 The Committee strongly feels that a comprehensive advocacy and awareness 
generation policy and plan of action has to be developed and implemented that wil l 
ensure awareness generation and public education of existing policies and 
programmes for women  and children at all levels.  In addition, a national 
awareness campaign having involvement of all concerned has to be launched.  Only 
then, public at large an d other stakeholders can be sensitized in the real sense.  The

of laws and allied schemes/programmes can register their impact only if awareness 
about them is there both in the designated authorities and implementing agencies 
and also targeted beneficiaries. 
 
XVI. GENDER BUDGETING 
 
16.1 Gender Budgeting is a powerful tool for addressing gender -based  disparities and 
achieving women empowerment through mainstreaming gender concern across sectors.  
The Ministry, the nodal Ministry for gender budgeting, has been pursuing with Central 
level Ministries/Departments and State Governments for adoption of gender budgeting 
for enhancing gender responsiveness of all programmes and schemes. 
 
16.2 The Committee notes that so far 56 Ministries/Departments  have set up Gender 
Budgeting Cells.  States like Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Bihar, 
Uttarakhand, Kerala, Odisha, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan and Jammu & Kashmir have 
undertaken var ious Gender Budgeting initiatives.  The Ministry proposes to take up 
Gender Audit of a few critical schemes with focus on the analysis of the 

programme/scheme guidelines, actual allocations, beneficiary incidence/impact analysis 
and the delivery mechanisms. 
 
16.3 The Committee finds that considerable progress has been made in this new 
area of fund management across the Ministries both at the Central and State level 
from the perspective of women specific concerns.  However, further steps have to be 
taken to further institutionalize the Gender Budget ing proceses.  The focus now 
should be to strengthen and empower the General Budgeting Cells to enable them to 
undertake the envisaged role.  It would be more result -oriented if 
Ministries/Departments are encourage d to undertake gender audits of major 
programmes, schemes and policies. 
 
16.4 Like Gender Budgeting, Child Budgeting can be considered another major 
initiative in sensitizing and institutionalizing child -related concerns.  For this 
purpose, the Committee n otes that the Ministry will have to build capacities to 
analyse the Central and State budgets and their impact on the outcomes for 
children. 
 
XVII. LEGISLATIONS RELATING TO WOMEN AND CHILDREN 
 
17.1 Over the years, a number of legislations have been enacted  both for women and 
children.  Inspite of our society having undergone a lot of changes, the status of women 
and children still remains quite vulnerable.  The process of enacting new laws keeping 
pace with the need for giving a better and well -protected en vironment to women and 
children continues.  The Committee observes that quite a few new laws are in the pipeline 
and there is a need also for having some more such laws.  While appreciating these 
developments, the Committee also strongly feels that time ha s come for reviewing the 
existing laws with a view to bring out changes wherever required.  The Committee finds 
that exercise in this regard has already been initiated by the Ministry in consultations with 
various stakeholders. 
 
17.2 A draft Amendment Bill  on the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 was prepared in 
consultation with the Ministry of Law and Justice, based on few amendments suggested 
by the National Commission for Women.  However, in view of a number of 
representations received against the proposed am endments, a series of consultations were 

held with State Governments and other stakeholders.  The Cabinet Note on amendments 
to the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961 is presently under finalization in the Ministry.  The 
growing incidence of trafficking for prost itution is a matter of growing concern.  Certain 
amendments are proposed to the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956 which includes 
deletion of those sections that revictimize the victims and more stringent punishment for 
perpetrators.  The National Comm ission for Women has taken up the review of the Medical 
Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 with a view to examine whether the provisions of the 
Act require any amendment. 
 
17.3 Committee's attention has been drawn by a number of very pertinent 
suggestions/recommendations made by the Working Group on Women's Agency and 
Empowerment for Twelfth Plan.  While suggesting  changes in some provisions of the 
Dowry Prohibition act, 1961, the Working Group has also recommended improving the 
implementation of the Act by appointing sufficient number of dedicated and full time Dowry 
Prohibition officers and also undertaking training and capacity -building of law enforcement 
agencies and the judiciary.  The Working Group has also advocated extensive amendments in 
the Preven tion of Immoral Trafficking Act.  Emphasizing the need for increasing control 
about trafficking menace, the Working Group has pointed out the need for undertaking a 
mapping of areas vulnerable to trafficking, along with having designated courts and fast 
tracking the judicial process.  Similarly, the Working Group has opined that a multi -pronged 
approach was required to implement the PCPNDT Act, to amend it wherever necessary, 
create awareness about the Act and to gender sensitize the persons in-charge of implementing 
the various provisions of the Act. Committee's attention has also been drawn to the 
recommendation for having a comprehensive stand-alone law to deal with killings and crimes 
in the name of honour and also a comprehensive Right to Marital Proper ty Act applicable to 
all communities. The specific suggestions made by the Working Group in this regard merit 
urgent attention of all concerned.   
 
17.4 The Committee also takes note of the recommendations of the Working Group on 
Child Rights for bringing out modifications in the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) 
Act, National Commission for Protection of Child Rights Act and also having a review of the 
impact of the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006. 
 
17.5 The Committee observes that the task  before the Ministry so far as review of 
existing laws relating to women and children and also enacting some new laws is 

concerned is quite an elaborate exercise.  The Committee would appreciate that this 
exercise is carried forward and completed in a miss ion mode.  At the same time, the 
Committee would like to emphasize that there are quite a few laws which  inspite of 
being enacted some years ago have virtually remained un -implemented as the proposed 
law-enforcing authorities in States are simply not in pl ace.  One such example is the 
Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 as only six State Governments 
have appointed independent Protection officers so far.  A Scheme for assistance to 
States for implementation of this Act has been drafted only recently and yet to be 
activated.  Similarly, State Commissions for the Protection of Child Rights are yet to be 
set up by many State Governments.   
 
17.6 The Committee would also take the opportunity to impress upon the Ministry 
not to allow recently enac ted laws like the Protection of Children from Sexual Offence 
Act and Protection of Women from Sexual Offences at Workplace Act remaining 
unimplemented for want of implementing authorities at various levels. 
 
XVIII. CONCLUSION 
 
18.1 The Ministry of Women an d Child Development is the nodal Ministry having 
the mandate to take care of all areas of women and children related concerns.  To be the 
designated authority of that segment of the population of the country which translates 
into more than 70 per cent of t he total population and that too the most vulnerable is 
indeed a very heavy responsibility for a Ministry.  The Committee is well aware that the 
Ministry has been carrying forward its multi -faceted mandate inspite of having very 
apparent and unavoidable li mitations.  The Committee also knows that all the schemes 
and policies formulated by the Ministry and various laws enacted so far are to be 
implemented by various State Governments, that too across the country.  Nobody can 
doubt the well -intention behind a ll the policy measures which inspite of being well -
conceptualized generally have to face practical problems at ground levels, sometimes 
due to very genuine State –specific limitations.  Another area of concern for the 
Committee is very low level of awarenes s about women and children related schemes, 
non-availability of institutional mechanisms at various levels.  The Committee strongly 
feels that a sustained effort at all India level has to be built up for increasing the 
awareness level.  Awareness has also to be built up for bringing about a change in the 

thinking and mind set of our public so as to adequately sensitize them about women and 
children related concerns.
