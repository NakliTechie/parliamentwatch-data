PREFACE
          I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human Resource
Development, having been authorised by the Committee, do hereby present on its behalf Two Hundred
Tenth Report of the Committee on 'Implementation of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA), Mid-Day Meal
(MDM) Scheme and Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme in Union Territories'.
2.       Over the years the implementation aspects of three major social schemes i.e. SSA, MDM and
ICDS have invariably been engaging the attention of the Committee during the examination of Demands
for Grants of various Ministries / Departments. The background of this study can actually be traced back
to the 4
th of October, 2004 when the Committee heard the Secretaries of the Department of School
Education and Literacy and Department of Women and Child Development in respect of different
Centrally sponsored schemes being operated by those departments in the country. Based on their
evidence, the Committee identified SSA, MDM and ICDS for detailed examination. Subsequently, the
study was focused to North-Eastern States and Jammu & Kashmir. The Committee has already presented
a report (193rd Report) on the implementation of these three schemes in North-Eastern States and Jammu
& Kashmir.
3.       In its meeting held on the 29 th November 2007, the Committee decided to continue its study on
these schemes with special focus on Union Territories.   The Committee undertook on the spot visits to
Union Territories of Puducherry, Lakshawdeep, Daman & Diu and Dadra & Nagar Haveli in two phases
in January and February, 2008 respectively. The Union Territory of Andaman & Nicobar Islands was a
part of the itinerary in the first phase of the Committee’s visit but was cancelled in view of the inability
expressed by the UT Administration due to some logistic problems. The Committee then heard the
representatives of the UT Administration of Andaman & Nicobar Islands along with the representatives
of Chandigarh and Delhi in the later half of May, 2008. 
4.       The Committee gained useful insights on the problems of implementation of the said schemes
during the on the spot study visits to Puducherry, Lakshawdeep, Daman & Diu and Dadra & Nagar
Haveli. The Committee would have liked to better appraise the implementation status through on-the-
spot visits of Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh and Delhi but for the paucity of time, their
representatives were called at Delhi for holding a discussion on these schemes.
5.       The Committee would like to express its gratitude to the Officers of the Department of School
Education and Literacy and the Ministry of Women and Child Development, Government of India as
well as to the Officers of Departments of Education and Social Welfare of Puducherry, Lakshadweep,
Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Chandigarh and Delhi for their
cooperation in furnishing the required material and information in connection with the examination of
these subjects and also making arrangements for the visit of the Committee.

I.       INTRODUCTION
 
1.1     A brief introduction of the schemes considered by the Committee i.e. Sarva Siksha Abhiyan, Mid
Day Meal Scheme and Integrated Child Development Services Scheme followed by the status of their
implementation in the Union Territories is given below.
 
II.      SARVA SHIKSHA ABHIYAN (SSA)
 
BRIEF ON SSA
 
2.1     Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA) is a national flagship programme of Government of India for
achieving the goal of Universalisation of Elementary Education (UEE) through a time bound approach,
as mandated by 86
th amendment to the Constitution of India making Free and Compulsory Education to
the Children of 6-14 age group, a Fundamental Right.
 
2.2     Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA) was launched in 2001-02 to provide useful and relevant education
for all children in 6 to 14 age group in partnership with States and local bodies by the year 2010. SSA
cover seeks to ensure access, retention and quality improvement in elementary education. The
Programme also aims to bridge social, regional and gender gaps, with the active participation of the
community in the management of schools. The assistance under the SSA was in the sharing ratio of
85:15 between the Union and the States during the IX Plan and it was 75:25 during the X Plan. During
the XIth Plan, this ratio is being proposed to be brought down to 50:50 sharing between the Central
Government and State Government but in a phased manner i.e., funding pattern has been proposed as
65:35 for the first two years of the Plan period, 60:40 for the third year, 55:45 for the fourth year and
50:50 in the fifth year.
 
2.3     The Programme covers all the districts of the country and addresses the needs of 19.4 crore
children in 12.3 lakh habitations. The Programme seeks to open new schools in those habitations which
do not have schooling facilities and strengthen existing school infrastructure through provision of
additional class rooms, toilets, drinking water, maintenance grant and school improvement grants.
Existing schools with inadequate teacher strength are provided with additional teachers, while the
capacity of existing teachers is being strengthened by extensive training, grants for developing teaching-
learning materials and strengthening of the academic support structure at the cluster, block and district
level. SSA seeks to provide quality elementary education including life skills. SSA has a special focus on
girl education and children with special needs. SSA also seeks to provide computer education to bridge

the digital divide. The approach is community-owned and even the village education plans are prepared
locally in consultation with the Panchayati Raj Institutions.
 
IMPLEMENTATION OF SSA IN PUDUCHERRY
 
2.4     The Union Territory of Puducherry as per Census 2001 has a total population of 9,74,345 (486961
males and 487384 females). It comprises of four districts viz. Puducherry, Karaikal, Mahe and Yanam;
with six blocks and twenty five clusters.
 
2.5     The Committee undertook a study visit of Puducherry from the 20
th to the 22nd January, 2008 for
having first-hand experience about the implementation of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan there. The Committee
visited several schools in Puducherry. Some of the schools visited by the Committee include the Govt.
Middle School, Lawspet; Govt. Primary School, Sanarapet, Govt. Primary School, Thattanchavady; and
Kannagi Govt. Girls Hr. Sec. School, Vilianur.   It also interacted with the UT Administration officials,
teachers, students and community leaders to ascertain the practical difficulties being faced in the
implementation of the Programme.
 
2.6     The Committee was informed that the Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) at primary level decreased to
101% during 2006-07 which was 107.02% in 2005-06. However, there has been an improvement in GER
at upper primary level which was 99.7% in 2006-07 in comparison to 97.37% in 2005-06. The number of
out of school children had increased from 1724 children during 2005-06 to 2673 children during 2006-
07. Also, the drop out rate at primary level increased from 4.35% during 2005-06 to 4.43% during 2006-
07. The Committee finds these developments worrisome and it recommends to the Department and
the UT Administration to take corrective measures urgently.
 
2.7     Committee’s attention was drawn by the unsatisfactory level of education indicators specially in
respect of the district of Yanam. The Committee noted that Yanam had a very high drop-out rate of
17.33%. The district also suffered from a low GER of 80.2% at primary level. In Committee’s opinion,
such a high level of drop-out rate against quite a low GER called for corrective measures without any
delay. The Committee, accordingly, recommends to the UT Administration to take sincere steps to
improve the situation in Yanam. There was an urgent need to identify specific problem areas in
relation to Yanam followed by appropriate action.
 
2.8     The Committee noted that Puducherry is one of the few States/UTs which has failed to fully
realize the benefit of Alternative Schooling mode under SSA. In fact, the Committee has pointed out this
fact in its 201
st Report wherein it had observed that against a target of bringing 1724 children in
Alternative & Innovative Education (AIE) fold, only 94 children (5.45% of the target fixed) could
actually be covered till 31 st March, 2007. Also, during 2007-08, against the physical target of 2673
children under EGS/AIE scheme, the achievement level at the time of Committee's visit in January 2008
was nil. The Committee is unhappy over the lackadaisical approach of the UT Administration,
particularly when the drop-out rate and Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) are becoming adverse. The
Committee hopes that the UT Administration will take necessary steps to ensure that all the
components under SSA are optimally implemented so as to ensure maximum coverage of children
in the UT thereunder.

2.9     An outlay of Rs. 21.61 lakhs was provided for 2701 interventions for Children with Special Needs
(CWSN) during 2007-08. The Committee learnt that the UT Administration has completed identification
of CWSN. The Committee was informed that besides other initiatives like providing aids & appliances
and giving financial assistance, home based care and teaching were being given to 92 mentally retarded
students. The Committee appreciates such steps being taken for CWSN, especially mentally
retarded students. However, the Committee failed to meet any teacher/volunteer given specialized
training for taking care of the educational needs of such children during its study visit. The
Committee hopes that the UT Administration must have organised specialized training
programmes for teachers of CWSN.
 
2.10   The Committee notes that against a physical target of 2701 CWSN to be covered under the
Scheme of Integrated Education for Disabled during 2007-08, only 948 CWSN could be covered till
the 9
th January, 2008. Committee’s attention has been drawn by the physical targets remaining
under-achieved under this vital component during the previous three years also. The Committee
finds this unacceptable and recommends that the UT Administration should make all efforts for
inclusion of all the identified CWSN and not repeat the past performance of under-achieving the
physical targets. Necessary funds for the purpose may be provided.
 
2.11   The Committee was informed by the UT Administration that all the schools were provided with
good infrastructure facilities, especially building and furniture under SSA. However, the Committee
noted that progress under civil works has been dismal, especially in respect of construction of school
buildings and additional class-rooms. The following table illustrates the position:-

2.12   The Committee finds that this dismal trend has continued in 2007-08 also with only four school
buildings being completed against a target of ten school buildings. The Committee is at a loss to
comprehend the factors responsible for such an approach of the UT Administration. In fact, due to
such a tardy progress in civil works, the Project Approval Board did not agree to any fresh civil
works for the UT of Puducherry for the year 2007-08. The Committee also finds that pace of other
civil works like construction of toilets, drinking water facilities, Block Resource Centres (BRCs)
and Cluster Resource Centres (CRCs) has also been discouraging. The Committee is all the more
disturbed by the fact that inspite of funds being duly allocated all these years, UT Administration
has simply failed to take benefit of the same. The Committee would like both the Department and
the UT Administration to take corrective measures so as to ensure removal of all the problem
areas. Obviously, it is high time to focus on the quality of education under SSA. The civil works
cannot be allowed to linger on indefinitely simply because good infrastructure is a pre-condition
for retention of students and quality education.
 
2.13   The Committee was given to understand that all the teachers have been given training for a period
of 8-10 days per year mainly on curriculum enrichment, ICT and other innovative practices. This fact, is

2.14   Committee’s attention has been drawn by the findings of survey of implementation of SSA in
Puducherry conducted by the designated Monitoring Institute, i.e. Puducherry University. The
Committee notes that this study was conducted in 36 schools out of 382 schools covered under SSA
Programme in Puducherry during the period from 1

Govt. Schools and only one school run with Govt. Aid. While overall performance level of SSA
Programme in the schools visited by the monitoring team was found to be very encouraging, a number of
areas requiring improvement have been pointed out in the Study Report. Some major shortcomings are
indicated below:-
 
-                     Civil works are very weak in few schools, many primary schools need new class rooms.
-                     Education Inspector needs to be empowered to monitor frequently the implementation of SSA
Programme.
-                     Training component of teachers not very satisfactory. Need for having more training

-                     Lack of awareness about the aims and objectives of SSA noticed in teachers.
-                     Twice a year Refresher Training Programme required for the school Headmaster and School
Co-ordinator.
-                     No visits to schools so far by the CRC and BRC Co-ordinators.
-                     VECs not being involved in the running of schools as envisaged under SSA.
-                     Cash books in schools not being maintained properly and records also not being updated.
-                     Funds allocated to the schools as maintenance/ school grants were not being used in time. In
many schools, account balance was too high.  Proper guidelines need to be issued in this regard.
-                     VEC/SSA meetings being not attended by the Headmasters, instead representatives being sent
by them, leading to communication gap.
-                     Accounts about TLM amount not being submitted by the Teachers.
 
2.15   The Committee observes that although the survey conducted by the Puducherry University is
only a sample study, it gives a fair idea about the practical problem areas evident at the ground
level.  The Committee hopes that required follow-up action must have been taken by the concerned
authorities both at the UT and Central level by now. The Committee would like to be apprised

IMPLEMENTATION OF SSA IN LAKSHADWEEP
 
2.16   The Union Territory of Lakshadweep comprises of 27 islands in the Arabian Sea between 8 o and
12o -30' north latitude and between 71 st and 74 th east longitude at a distance ranging from 200 kms to
400 kms from mainland. Of the 27 islands, only ten islands are inhabited while the other islands are
small and exist as satellites of the inhabited islands. The total population of Lakshadweep is around
60,650. Lakshadweep is the 4 th densely populated area after Delhi, Chandigarh and Puducherry. Area-
wise, Andrott is the largest island with a population of 10727 while Bangaram is the smallest of them all
with a population of only 65.   
 
2.17   The Committee visited the Union Territory between the 4 th to 6th February, 2008. The Committee
had the opportunity to visit some schools in the two main islands in Lakshadweep, namely Kavaratti
(which happens to be the second largest inhabited island) and Agatti (the fifth largest inhabited island). 
 
2.18   The Lakshadweep Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan State Mission Authority was established in 2002 to
fulfill the aim of Universalisation of Elementary Education. A separate Management system has been set
up to look after the implementation of SSA in the Directorate of Education in January, 2007.

2.20   The Committee is happy to note that the UT Administration has been regularly providing its
share for the SSA after not having done so in the initial years. What however worries the
Committee is the trend of utilisation of the allocations so far. While the allocations have been rising
over the years, actual expenditure incurred has been far from satisfactory. The Committee,
therefore, urges both the Centre and the UT Administration to work towards optimum and
judicious utilisation of allocated funds within the stipulated time period in future.
 
 
Civil Works
 
2.21   The Committee was informed that the Project Approval Board had approved the construction of 3
new primary schools, 2 upper primary schools, 20 toilets, 20 drinking water facilities, 20 additional
classrooms, 2 Block Resource Centres (BRC) and 10 Cluster Resource Centres (CRC) during 2007-08.
An amount of Rs.114.60 lakhs had also been approved for this purpose by the Board. However, the
Committee noted that action for construction of only 2 upper primary schools and 10 additional
classrooms had been taken up. Land in this regard had been identified and funds released to the Public
Work Department for the construction. The action in respect of the other civil works had not started.
 
2.22   The Committee further noted that the Department had no clue whatsoever about the progress in
respect of the civil works. As per the Department, not a single civil work had been undertaken by the UT
Administration till January, 2008. This is a matter of serious concern for the Committee. The
Committee feels that such a situation is a proof enough to conclude that the monitoring mechanism
that the Centre has supposedly put in place to take stock of the progress being made by the UTs
has failed miserably in Lakshadweep. This is also indicative of the fact that there is absolutely no
coordination between the Centre and the UT in the implementation of such an important
Programme. Feeling disturbed over the present state of affairs, the Committee recommends to the
Department to urgently review its procedures in the matter. The Committee feels that the pace of
construction of civil works as per the requirements of SSA Programme needs to be speeded up at
the earliest.

2.25   The UT Administration informed the Committee that they had been able to achieve 71 per cent
success in bringing down the Out of School Children figures from 168 to 48 during 2007-08. This has
been possible due the services of 10 educational volunteers appointed by the Administration to provide
home-based education to these children. The 48 Out of School Children were suffering from acute

even reaching out to them with the help of the educational volunteers.
 
2.26   The Committee welcomes the initiative taken by the UT Administration to achieve cent
percent enrolment as envisaged under the SSA. It hopes that the UT Administration will be able to
achieve this target well within the stipulated time.
 
Teachers’ appointment/training
 
2.27   The Committee was informed that the Project Approval Board had sanctioned eight posts of
primary school teachers during 2007-08. However, only four teachers could be appointed. The
Committee was given to understand that the remaining posts could not be filled up for want of opening
the newly sanctioned schools. Such a situation concerns the Committee as in its opinion, mere
sanctioning of civil works and the requisite infrastructure would lead to nowhere as long as these
are not completed in time. The Committee strongly recommends that the
implementation/performance level under SSA in the UT needs to be speeded up with the required
support and strict monitoring by the Department.  
 
2.28   On the training front, the Committee was informed that all the teachers recruited were trained
teachers. Hence, the Administration has focused on providing 20 days in-service training to all the
teachers. During 2007-08, 281 teachers were given an average of 7 days training in Approach and
Methodology against the target of 20 days training programme to 620 teachers. A Subject Resource
Group comprising of 29 teachers had been appointed as Resource Persons to impart in-service training to
teachers. The Administration had informed the Committee that the target of imparting training to the
remaining teachers would be achieved during April-May, 2008.
         
2.29   The Committee notes that the UT was way behind its target of training of 620 teachers even
in the last quarter of 2007-08. 55 per cent of the target group of 620 teachers i.e. 331 teachers had
not being given any form of training. The remaining 45 per cent of the teachers had also only been
imparted a shorter version of the training in place of the mandatory 20 days training as is required
under SSA. This surely does not augur well with the implementation of SSA in Lakshadweep and
there was an urgent need on the part of both the Centre and the UT Administration to ensure that
this target was achieved within a reasonable period of time.

removed/taken care of. This would facilitate the inducting of such special children into the
mainstream within the shortest possible time.

Community Mobilisation
 
2.32   As per the UT Administration, Village Education Committees (VEC) and School Management
Committees (SCM) had been formed in all the islands. Community awareness programmes were also
being conducted in all islands by the Block Resource Centre/Cluster Resource Centre Co-ordinators of
SSA. The Committee, however, during its interaction with the UT Administration officials found that the
role of VEC/SCM in the management/formulation of the development plans needed to be strengthened.
The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Department and the UT Administration to jointly
look into this aspect and see to it that the involvement of the VECs and SCMs is appropriately
enhanced.

III.    NATIONAL PROGRAMME OF MID-DAY MEALS IN SCHOOLS
(MDM SCHEME)
 
BRIEF ON MDM SCHEME
 
3.1     With a view to enhancing enrollment, retention and attendance and simultaneously improving
nutritional levels among children, the National Programme of Nutritional Support to Primary Education
(NP-NSPE) (renamed as 'National Programme of Mid-Day Meals in Schools' from the year 2008-09)
was launched as a Centrally sponsored Scheme on 15
th August, 1995, initially in 2408 blocks in the
country and subsequently to all blocks across the country. The Scheme has been revised since then on
three occasions viz., in September, 2004, in July, 2006 and recently in October, 2007. During its last
revision, the coverage of the scheme has been extended to children in upper primary (classes VI to VIII)
initially in 3479 Educationally Backward Blocks and subsequently to all areas across the country from
2008-09.
 
3.2     The objectives of Mid Day Meal Scheme are: -
(i)      Improving the nutritional status of children in classes I-VIII in Government, Local Body and
Government aided schools, and EGS and AIE centres.
(ii)      Encouraging poor children, belonging to disadvantaged sections, to attend school more
regularly and help them concentrate on classroom activities.
(iii)     Providing nutritional support to students of primary stage in drought-affected areas during
summer vacations also.
 
3.3     Under the scheme, a cooked meal with the following nutritional content is provided to all eligible
children :-

IV.     INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS) SCHEME
 
BRIEF ON ICDS SCHEME
 
4.1     The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme launched in 1975 aims at holistic
development of children (0-6 yrs) and pregnant and lactating mothers from disadvantaged sections, by
providing a package of integrated services comprising of supplementary nutrition, immunization, health
check-up, referral services, pre-school, non-formal education and nutrition and health education.
 
4.2     The objectives of the ICDS Scheme are:
(i)      to improve the nutritional and health status of children below the age of six years and
pregnant and lactating mothers;
(ii)      to lay the foundation for proper psychological, physical and social development of the child;
(iii)     to reduce the incidence of mortality, morbidity, malnutrition and school dropouts;
(iv)     to achieve effective co-ordination of policy and implementation amongst the various
departments to promote child development; and
(v)     to enhance the capability of the mother to look after the normal health and nutritional needs
of the child through proper nutrition and health education.
 
4.3     Under the Scheme, 100 per cent financial assistance is provided by the Union Government for all
inputs other than supplementary nutrition for which Central assistance is to the extent of 50 per cent of
the actual expenditure incurred by the States or 50 per cent of the cost norms, whichever is less.

V.      GENERAL OBSERVATIONS
 
5.1     Policy framework for the Centrally-sponsored Schemes of SSA, MDM and ICDS has been
laid down by the Central Government and the different State  Governments and UT
Administrations are the implementing agencies. During the  course of its interactions with various
stakeholders in the  UTs of Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli,
Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh and Delhi, the Committee has observed some very
specific and pertinent problem areas which are common to all the three aforesaid Schemes in all 
these Union Territories. The Committee would like to make the following observations in this
regard.
 

REPORT
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/210th%20Report.htm[01-07-2016 15:54:01]
5.2     Intricate details touching upon various aspects of the components and parameters are the
very basis for effective implementation of a Scheme.  In Committee’s opinion, such details once
finalised become the foundation stone for implementing as well as carrying out necessary
improvements and modifications in the Scheme. The experience of the Committee in this regard
while interacting with the various stakeholders or analysing the material placed before it both by
the Department and the concerned UT Administrations has not been very satisfactory. The
Committee has noticed that the Department was solely dependent on the feedback being furnished
to it by the concerned UT Administration. While being aware that this is the practical proposition,
the Committee would like to point out that it is also the duty of the Department to cross-check and
analyse the various data being forwarded to it to the extent possible. The Committee is, however,
constrained to point out that this aspect has not been receiving the required attention. On many
occasions in the past, discrepancies between the submissions made by the Department and UT
Administration came to the notice of the Committee which made it difficult for the Committee to
make the exact assessment about the running of the Scheme in a particular UT. The Committee
strongly feels that every conceivable effort should be made to have the accurate and latest data not
only for placing the same before a Parliamentary Committee but also for better implementation of
a Scheme.
         
5.3     Lack of coordination between the Department and the UT Administration at different levels
has invariably been noticed both in the written feedback given to the Committee and also during
interactions with the representatives of the Department and the UT Administrations.  The
Committee feels very disturbed by such a situation prevailing in respect of Flagship Schemes of the
Government.  It becomes all the more glaring when such a communication gap is noticed while
dealing with UTs located at a great distance from the mainland.  The Committee strongly feels that
these are the areas where both the Department and the UT Administration need to work in perfect
harmony.  The Committee acknowledges that this lack of coordination would have been somewhat
justifiable  in the initial stages of implementation of these Schemes but now since these Schemes
are operational for a number of years, such a discouraging situation should not have been allowed
to continue.
         
5.4     During the course of discussions with the representatives of the UT Administrations, many
UT specific problems have come to the notice of the Committee.  The Committee feels that in all
such cases, the problems or hindrances being faced by the UT Administration could have been
easily resolved much earlier.   However, it is very disturbing to note that in majority of the cases,
the Department was not even aware of the same.   The question of resolving such problems,
therefore, does not arise.  The Committee would like to emphasise that such a situation should not
be allowed to continue further. The Department being the Nodal Authority should immediately
take action whenever a problem is brought to its notice.  Ideal position would be that there is
regular channel of communication open between the Department and the concerned State/ UT
authorities.
 
5.5     The Committee understands that a monitoring mechanism working at different levels –
District, Zonal, UT/ State and the Department in respect of these Schemes has been evolved and is
reported to be working very effectively. It was, however, very surprising to observe that ground
realities were entirely different.  The general impression which the Committee has gathered was

REPORT
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/210th%20Report.htm[01-07-2016 15:54:01]
that wherever monitoring was done, be it by the UT Authorities or the Department or independent
agencies, no follow up action worth mentioning is being taken.  The Committee would appreciate if
all such monitoring is duly followed by the required follow up action.  Committee opines that in
this era of IT advancement and easy connectivity, such monitoring and resultant corrective
measures can be very fast and effective. 
         
5.6     While examining the implementation of ICDS Scheme, the Committee has observed that
under the component of Supplementary Nutrition, the UT Administrations, more or less, have
been actively participating by providing more than the required funds.   The Committee is,
however, not aware as to whether any assessment on the targetted beneficiaries especially the
malnourished children who are being covered under this component has been made so far. The
Committee feels that such an exercise should be initiated without any further delay.
 
5.7 Some very innovative measures within the broad policy framework of the Schemes by some UT
Administrations were noticed by the Committee during its visit.  The Committee had the
opportunity to see the visible impact of such  initiatives from close quarters.  The Committee is of
the opinion that such path-breaking measures need to be placed as a case study before the other
State Governments so that they are also encouraged to adopt the same to the extent possible.  The
Committee would appreciate if the Department takes up such an exercise.
 
5.8     The Committee has been informed that the Executive Committee of the National Mission for
Sarva Shiksha abhiyan (SSA) has taken the initiative of constituting District Level Committees
comprising of public representatives to monitor the implementation of the Programme in districts
in July, 2007.  It is also understood that the States/UTs have been informed of the amendments in
August, 2007.  It has, however, come to the notice of the Committee that most of the States/UTs are
yet to initiate the necessary follow up action in the matter.  The Committee, therefore, desires to
have a status note on the follow up action taken by each States/UTs.  Wherever these Committees
have been constituted, the meetings held so far and the outcome of the meetings may also be
furnished.

IMPLEMENTATION OF MDM SCHEME IN PUDUCHERRY
 
3.8     The concept of Central Kitchens, the Committee notes, is something unique in Puducherry. 
The Committee in its visits to these kitchens observed that cooking was being managed in a better
and professional manner. What further impressed the Committee was the fact that by engaging
such Centralized Kitchens for preparing the food, the UT had saved itself from the hassles being
faced by the other States/UTs in the implementation of the scheme at the ground level. This
exercise had also no doubt saved the UT Administration of the cost of engaging individual cooks
for every school in the UT. The engagement of Co-operative Societies and SHGs in the preparation
and distribution of mid-day meal makes the entire process very transparent. Besides highlighting
the management skills of UT Administration, it also promotes the cause of Co-operative Societies
and SHGs indirectly. The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Department to examine the
viability of having similar arrangements in other States/UTs and prepare appropriate guidelines in
this regard.
 
3.11   The Committee appreciates the additional inputs through which the UT Administration has
substantiated the MDM Scheme.  The Committee is inclined to agree with the assessment of the UT
Administration that provision of mid-day meal comprising of variety of food items of nutritious
value prepared in hygienic conditions by professionals has led to a positive impact on both student
enrolment and their retention. It is also indirectly improving the health of students and
contributing to gender equality. It only hopes that the example set by the UT encourages the other
States/UTs to come forward in a similar way so as to see to it that the objectives of the scheme are
achieved within the shortest possible time.

3.21   The Committee was given to understand by the Department that out of 157.92 MTs of foodgrains
allotted to the UT in 2007-08, lifting had been nil till the Committee visited the UT in February, 2008.

made to them under the scheme. The lifting was, however, slow. The Committee feels disturbed by the
ground realities.  The Department not being aware of the latest details of the implementation of the
scheme in the UT points to the fact that there is lack of coordination between the various agencies
involved in the implementation process. This is not acceptable to the Committee. The Committee

the Department to immediately put its act together and see to it that such loopholes are plugged
once for all.
 
3.22   On enquiring about the slow pace of lifting of foodgrains, the UT Administration officials apprised
the Committee of the fact that there was no Food Corporation of India godown in Lakshadweep. The
foodgrains allotted to the UT were required to be lifted all the way from Mangalore in Karnataka. This
was proving to be the main hindrance in lifting of the foodgrains as it was not always possible to get the
foodgrains via the sea route due to various reasons like topographical and geographical constraints of the
islands, bad climate, rough sea, etc. Moreover, the transportation cost was also very high and was not
being adequately compensated by the subsidy provided for this purpose by the Department. These
constraints had resulted in the slow lifting of the foodgrains under the scheme. The Committee believes
that the problem being  faced by the UT was genuine and must, therefore, be given a favourable
consideration by the Department.  The matter needs to be resolved in a feasible and appropriate
manner without any further delay.

made every possible effort to get a godown in one of its islands. The UT Administration had taken up the
matter with the Ministry of Food & Civil Supplies. They had even offered land for the construction of the
godown. Attempts to offer a godown on lease to the Union Ministry of Food & Civil Supplies were also
made. However, nothing was found acceptable by that Ministry. The Committee shares the concern of

4.65   It was also found that there were 3 posts of Supervisors lying vacant in the UT. The Committee
urges the UT Administration to fill up these vacancies at the earliest. 
 
V.      GENERAL OBSERVATIONS
 
5.1     Policy framework for the Centrally-sponsored Schemes of SSA, MDM and ICDS has been
laid down by the Central Government and the different State  Governments and UT
Administrations are the implementing agencies. During the  course of its interactions with various
stakeholders in the  UTs of Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli,
Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh and Delhi, the Committee has observed some very
specific and pertinent problem areas which are common to all the three aforesaid Schemes in all 
these Union Territories. The Committee would like to make the following observations in this
regard.
 
5.2     Intricate details touching upon various aspects of the components and parameters are the
very basis for effective implementation of a Scheme.  In Committee’s opinion, such details once
finalised become the foundation stone for implementing as well as carrying out necessary
improvements and modifications in the Scheme. The experience of the Committee in this regard
while interacting with the various stakeholders or analysing the material placed before it both by
the Department and the concerned UT Administrations has not been very satisfactory. The
Committee has noticed that the Department was solely dependent on the feedback being furnished
to it by the concerned UT Administration. While being aware that this is the practical proposition,
the Committee would like to point out that it is also the duty of the Department to cross-check and
analyse the various data being forwarded to it to the extent possible. The Committee is, however,
constrained to point out that this aspect has not been receiving the required attention. On many
occasions in the past, discrepancies between the submissions made by the Department and UT
Administration came to the notice of the Committee which made it difficult for the Committee to
make the exact assessment about the running of the Scheme in a particular UT. The Committee
strongly feels that every conceivable effort should be made to have the accurate and latest data not
only for placing the same before a Parliamentary Committee but also for better implementation of
a Scheme.
         
5.3     Lack of coordination between the Department and the UT Administration at different levels
has invariably been noticed both in the written feedback given to the Committee and also during
interactions with the representatives of the Department and the UT Administrations.  The
Committee feels very disturbed by such a situation prevailing in respect of Flagship Schemes of the
Government.  It becomes all the more glaring when such a communication gap is noticed while
dealing with UTs located at a great distance from the mainland.  The Committee strongly feels that
these are the areas where both the Department and the UT Administration need to work in perfect

REPORT
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/210th%20Report.htm[01-07-2016 15:54:01]
harmony.  The Committee acknowledges that this lack of coordination would have been somewhat
justifiable  in the initial stages of implementation of these Schemes but now since these Schemes
are operational for a number of years, such a discouraging situation should not have been allowed
to continue.
         
5.4     During the course of discussions with the representatives of the UT Administrations, many
UT specific problems have come to the notice of the Committee.  The Committee feels that in all
such cases, the problems or hindrances being faced by the UT Administration could have been
easily resolved much earlier.   However, it is very disturbing to note that in majority of the cases,
the Department was not even aware of the same.   The question of resolving such problems,
therefore, does not arise.  The Committee would like to emphasise that such a situation should not
be allowed to continue further. The Department being the Nodal Authority should immediately
take action whenever a problem is brought to its notice.  Ideal position would be that there is
regular channel of communication open between the Department and the concerned State/ UT
authorities.
 
5.5     The Committee understands that a monitoring mechanism working at different levels –
District, Zonal, UT/ State and the Department in respect of these Schemes has been evolved and is
reported to be working very effectively. It was, however, very surprising to observe that ground
realities were entirely different.  The general impression which the Committee has gathered was
that wherever monitoring was done, be it by the UT Authorities or the Department or independent
agencies, no follow up action worth mentioning is being taken.  The Committee would appreciate if
all such monitoring is duly followed by the required follow up action.  Committee opines that in
this era of IT advancement and easy connectivity, such monitoring and resultant corrective
measures can be very fast and effective. 
         
5.6     While examining the implementation of ICDS Scheme, the Committee has observed that
under the component of Supplementary Nutrition, the UT Administrations, more or less, have
been actively participating by providing more than the required funds.   The Committee is,
however, not aware as to whether any assessment on the targetted beneficiaries especially the
malnourished children who are being covered under this component has been made so far. The
Committee feels that such an exercise should be initiated without any further delay.
 
5.7 Some very innovative measures within the broad policy framework of the Schemes by some UT
Administrations were noticed by the Committee during its visit.  The Committee had the
opportunity to see the visible impact of such  initiatives from close quarters.  The Committee is of
the opinion that such path-breaking measures need to be placed as a case study before the other
State Governments so that they are also encouraged to adopt the same to the extent possible.  The
Committee would appreciate if the Department takes up such an exercise.
 
5.8     The Committee has been informed that the Executive Committee of the National Mission for
Sarva Shiksha abhiyan (SSA) has taken the initiative of constituting District Level Committees
comprising of public representatives to monitor the implementation of the Programme in districts
in July, 2007.  It is also understood that the States/UTs have been informed of the amendments in
August, 2007.  It has, however, come to the notice of the Committee that most of the States/UTs are

yet to initiate the necessary follow up action in the matter.  The Committee, therefore, desires to
have a status note on the follow up action taken by each States/UTs.  Wherever these Committees
have been constituted, the meetings held so far and the outcome of the meetings may also be
furnished.
