I. INTRODUCTION  
1.1. The main responsibility of the Department is to ensure qualitative and quantitative 
improvements in Youth Programmes and also on broad basing and bringing about awareness of 
sports and physical fitness amongst school children and youth. The Programmes undertaken 
by the Department are mainly Sports and Games and Youth Programmes.  
1.2. The Department finances various youth welfare programmes which ranges from 
personality development to vocational training. Most of these programmes are implemented 
by reputed institutions, non-governmental organisations as well as State Governments 
whereby financial assistance is provided by the Department as per the stipulated norms. The 
major schemes for youth development are National Service Scheme, Nehru Yuva Kendra 
Sangathan, National Integration Programmes, Youth Hostels and National Service 
Volunteers Scheme,etc.  
3. The Department also finances a number of programmes which aim at promotion 
of sports, including creation of sports infrastructure in all parts of the country, 
grants for laying of synthetic surfaces, spotting and nurturing of talents through 
the schemes of Sports Authority of India, grants to National Sports Federations, 
National Championships, organisation of tournaments for rural youth, women in 
the North-Eastern Region.  
II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION  
1. The total outlay, both Plan and Non-Plan taken together, provided to the 
Department for the year 1998-99 was Rs. 214.76 crores in BE which was reduced 
to Rs. 200.00 crores at RE stage. The allocation made for the year 1999-2000 is 
Rs. 230.09 crores (Plan Rs. 190.00 crores and Non-Plan Rs. 40.09 crores). When 
asked about the reaction to the marginal increase, the Department submitted that 
for the year 1999-2000, it has projected a demand of Rs. 282.09 crore (Rs. 230.00 
crores under Plan and Rs. 52.09 crores under Non-plan) and the marginal increase 
of Rs. 16 crores will be only sufficient to take care of inflationary impact. Scale 
of operation of various scheme would remain as same as of the previous year. It 
was also informed that the Department had requested for an enhanced allocation 
of Rs. 51 crores under Plan over the BE of 1998-99, out of which Rs. 33 crores 
are required for Sports Authority of India, for upgradation and maintenance of 
existing infrastructure as well as for creating new facilities, particularly in view of 
Afro-Asian games to be held in 2001.  
2. The Committee is of the view that in fact there is no real increase in the 
allocation of the Department for 1999-2000 and if there is no substantial 
Page 4 of 18REPORT
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/83rsport.html

allocation, various youth and sports activities being undertaken by the 
Department may be adversely affected. The Committee, therefore, finds 
enough justification for higher allocation in the RE.  
3. The Committee has also noted in this connection, that a tendency to keep 
higher allocation in the BE and then reducing it in the RE has developed. 
The Committee does not approve this. It is of the considered view that once 
an allocation is made and the same is approved by Parliament no one has the 
privilege to curtail it subsequently. In this regard, Parliament’s authority is 
supreme.  
III. APPROACH TO THE NINTH FIVE YEAR PLAN  
3.1 The Committee has taken note of the approach on Youth Affairs and Sports during the 
Ninth Plan period. The contents of the relevant paras from the Approach Paper to the Ninth 
Five Year Plan are reproduced below:-  
"The thrust in Youth Affairs will be to consolidate and expand 
youth activities by involving youth in the entire gamut of the 
developmental process as the youth are a major resource in the 
task of nation building. Youth activities will focus on 
environmental and health programmes such as greening of 
wastelands, solid waste management, anti-smoking compaign, 
prevention of drug abuse, health education with emphasis on 
reproductive health and prevention of AIDS, population control as 
well as various adventure activities. Greater access will be given 
to the rural and marginalised youth in the vocational training 
programmes of Nehru Yuva Kendras by involving the NGOs, 
self-help groups and community polytechnics in the task.  
The thrust in sports will be on providing greater access to sport 
facilities through substantial investments in physical education, 
infrastructure development including centres of sports physiology 
and sports medicine and in creating widespread awareness for 
physical fitness through nutrition, health education and yoga with 
special focus on school children. Area specific sports programmes 
recognising the traditional sports skills of the inhabitants, 
specially tribal population, will receive priority consideration. 
Rural sports programmes will be revamped in order to tap vast 
talents available in the rural areas. Special attention will be given 
for the promotion of sports and games among the disabled. The 
need for a holistic approach that will integrate youth and sports 
activities and programmes within the context of education is well 
recognised and will guide all action". 
3.2 The Committee feels that the objectives are laudable. However, unless dedicated 
and sincere effort are made to achieve these objectives, they will remain on paper only.

IV. NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN  
1. Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), an autonomous organisation under the 
Department, has about 500 district level offices throughout the country. It is the 
largest grass-root level organisation in the Asia and Pacific region catering to the 
needs of more than 6.4 million non-student rural youth in the age group of 15-35 
years enrolled through 1.79 lakh village-based youth clubs covering education, 
training, employment promotion, income generation, self-employment, Health 
awareness etc. The strength of NYKS is its vast network of youth clubs at grass-
root level. Allocation to Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan on plan side has been 
enhanced from Rs.17.35 crores in BE 1998-99 and Rs.19.30 crores in RE 1998-
99 to Rs.19.85 crores in BE 1999-2000. Explaining the enhancement of fund 
allocation under plan as well as Non-Plan, the Department informed that it was to 
take care of increased expenditure on salaries of Nehru Yuva Kendras Sangathan 
employees on account of granting benefits of Fifth Central Pay Commission. In 
Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan there are 196 Kendras under Plan and 304 
Kendras under Non-Plan.
  
4.2 The Committee was also informed that there are 500 functional District Nehru Yuva 
Kendras in the country and each District Nehru Yuva Kendra should have a District 
Advisory Committee on Youth Programme (DACYP). However, out of 500 District Nehru 
Yuva Kendras, the DACYP is functional in only 436 Kendras. According to the Department, 
the reason for non-functioning of DACYP in remaining 64 districts is primarily because of 
holding of additional charge by other Districts Youth Coordinators. The Committee in this 
connection would like to impress upon that District Youth Coordinators should be 
relieved of their additional charge as this additional charge may be affecting the 
activities of their District Yuva Kendra and DACYP in the remaining 64 districts 
should be formed without further delay.  
3. Regarding representation of women in the DACYP, the Committee was informed 
that as far as possible, 30 percent of the total membership of DACYP would go to 
women and this could be assessed after receiving the information from District 
Nehru Yuva Kendras. The Committee would like that sincere effort would be 
made to give due representation to women and the information in this 
regard would be shared with the Committee.

V. ASSISTANCE TO YOUTH CLUBS AND SPORTS CLUBS  
 
   
   
6.1 The schemes is being implemented through Nehru Yuvak Kendra Sangathan and 
State Governments/Union Territory Administrations. It aims at assisting newly set up 
youth clubs to enable them to start their activities and get established. One time 
financial assistance of Rs.5000/- is given to registered youth clubs.  
6.2. During the financial year 1998-99 (upto to 1.3.1999) an amount of Rs.69.50 lakh 
was released to NYK Sangathan for further disbursement to 1318 Youth Clubs against 
the target of disbursing Rs.75 lakh among 1500 Youth Clubs. During the year 1999-
2000, the Department proposes to disburse Rs.125 lakh among 1000 Youth Clubs.  
6.3. The Committee feels satisfied that the Scheme of Assistance to Youth Clubs, 
Sports Clubs and Awards to Outstanding Youth Clubs have been merged 
together and the scale of assistance has also been enhanced from Rs.5000/- to 
Rs.10,000/- per youth club.  
6.4. The Committee would however like to caution the Department that with the 
enhancement of assistance amount there is every possibility of misuse of funds as 
well as existence of some clubs only on paper. The Committee in this connection 
would like to suggest to the Department to be very vigilant in proper monitoring 
of this scheme .  
VI. NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEER SCHEME  
7.1. The National Service Volunteer Scheme launched in 1977-78, aims at providing 
opportunities to youth to involve themselves, on a voluntary basis, in nation building 
activities for a specific period of one or two years. Each volunteer is paid a stipend of 
Rs.500/- p.m. and a fixed travelling allowance @ Rs.200/- p.m. They are also given a 
contingency grant of Rs.100/- per year.  
7.2. At present, 8500 NSVs are deployed with the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan, National 
Page 9 of 18REPORT
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/HRD/83rsport.html

Service Scheme, Bharat Scouts & Guides, Youth Hostels Association of India, etc. The State 
Governments/Union Territories can also avail the services of these volunteers.  
7.3. Explaining the reasons for allocation of Rs.18 crore in BE 1999-2000 compared to the 
same figure in BE 1998-99 and a drastic reduction upto the level of Rs.5.84 crores at RE 
stage during 1998-99, the Department informed that the allocation of Rs.18 crores in BE 
1998-99 included the amount earmarked for the new scheme viz. National Reconstruction 
Corps. As the approval for the scheme could not be received during 1998-99, the allocation 
was reduced to Rs.5.84 crores. The enhanced allocation of Rs.18.00 crores in 1999-2000 
includes Rs.12.00 crores for National Reconstruction Corps Scheme. The Expenditure 
Finance Committee has met on 25th January, 1999 to consider the draft scheme for creation 
of National Reconstruction Corps and it is proposed that scheme will be implemented during 
1999-2000 on pilot basis, it was further stated.  
4. The Committee has been noting this type of increase in BE and subsequent 
decrease in RE since 1997-98. The Committee last year had expressed that a 
scheme should be included in the Budget after completion of preliminary 
examination of the scheme. The Department vide its ATR also noted the 
observation of the Committee. The Committee is pained to note that again a 
provision of Rs.12.00 crores has been made before completing all necessary 
formalities regarding approval of scheme.. It would have been better if the 
scheme could have been finalized first before its inclusion in the 
Departments’ demands.. The Committee takes a strong exception on the ad-
hoc approach of the Department in this regard.  
VIII. NATIONAL INTEGRATION PROGRAMME  
8.1. The Scheme for promotion of National Integration aims at providing financial assistance 
for a variety of youth programmes aimed at fostering national integration and communal 
harmony. The funds are channelised through a number of voluntary youth organisations. 
During the year 1997-98 there were 79 National Integration Camps and 9 inter-states youth 
exchange programmes. In 1998-99, 148 National Integration Camp for rural and urban youth 
and 7 inter-state youth exchange programmes were organised for which a financial 
assistance of Rs. 279 lakhs was given upto 1st March, 1999.  
2. The Committee has noted that since 1996-97, allocation for this programme has 
been constant at the level of Rs. 3.00 crores, thereby meaning that in real term, 
the allocation for the programme has been decreasing over the years if we take 
into account the normal inflation. The Department does not appear to be serious 
about the implementation of this programme. The Committee is of the view that 
national integration is the need of the hour and due emphasis needs to be 
given on this.

IX. YOUTH HOSTELS  
9.1. The Scheme is a joint venture between Central and State Governments. So far, 56 youth 
hostels have been constructed and 18 are under construction under the scheme. While the 
Central Government bears the cost of construction, the State Government provides 
developed land free of cost with water, electricity approach road and staff quarters. The 
youth hostel is looked after by the Warden and Assistant Warden, a husband wife team, and 
paid a honorarium of Rs. 3000/- p.m.  
9.2. During the year 1999-2000, a sum of Rs. 4 crores has been provided against last years’ 
BE of Rs. 4 crores and RE of Rs. 3 crores.  
9.3. So far as utilisation of fund is concerned, the Committee would like to draw the 
attention of the Department on varying figures mentioned in its different papers 
furnished to the Committee. In the Anuual Report for the year 1998-99, it has been 
mentioned that during the financial year 1998-99, out of the budget provision of Rs. 
300.00 lakhs, Rs. 217.00 lakh has been released for construction and furnishing of 
Youth Hostel buildings. Annual Action plan 1998-99 mentions this figure as Rs. 257.15 
lakhs (upto 28.2.99). On the other hand, Action Taken Report on the Sixty Fifth Report 
of the Committee states that during the financial year 1998-99 the Department released 
the entire fund to 5 youth hostels at Visakhapatnam (A.P.) Nagraota (J& K), Patiala 
(Punjab), Tezpur (Assam) & Buldana ( Maharashtra) which are nearing completion.  
9.4. The Committee has been given to understand that on many occasions construction 
work of Youth Hostels get delayed due to centralised procedure. The Committee would 
like the Department to explore the possibility of decentralise the same.  
9.5. The Committee has been informed that the Department organised a conference on 
youth hostels in which the officers of State Governments dealing with youth hostels and 
wardens of various youth hostels, participated and discussed issues relating to progress 
of construction, maintenance and management of youth hostels. The Committee in this 
connection would like to know the response of the States which are not providing 
facilities as envisaged in the Scheme.

XIII. Committed Liability  
13.1. While examining the Demands for Grants 1997-98 of the Departments under its 
purview, the Committee noted that the committed liability of the Plan Schemes has not 
been reflected in the budgetary allocations.  
13.2. The Committee, in this, connection considered the report of the Tenth Finance 
Commission. The Tenth Finance Commission have observed in this regard as under:-  
"Although the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their 
terms of reference in deciding the cut off date for transfer of 
committed liability on account of plan scheme, the 
incremental liabilities that would arise in 1997-98 on account 
of 8th Plan scheme completed in the next two financial years 
would have to be provided for. The Commission are, 
therefore, of the view that the Planning Commission may 
consider providing for the maintenance of such scheme till 
1999-2000 in the Plan itself as was done for the schemes of the 
two Annual Plans of 1990-91 and 1991-92". 
13.3. The Committee also sought clarifications from the then Secretary (Expenditure). 
He informed the Committee that even though communications were sent to various 
Ministries, the Ministries could not identify the committed expenditure on time. 
Accordingly a lumpsum amount has been provided under Demand No. 31. The 
Secretary also assured that if required, more funds for this purpose will be provided at 
the RE stage. He also stated that the Departments may approach the Department of 
Expenditure for their share.  
13.4. The Committee, accordingly, directed the Department to approach the Ministry 
of Finance in this regard. However, while considering the Demands for Grants 1998-
1999, the Committee noticed that no progress in this regard seemed to have taken 
place. The Committee, therefore, reiterated its stand. In spite of the Departments 
initiating some exercise, the matter could not be sorted out. The Committee, therefore, 
heard the Secretary (Expenditure) on 27th March, 1999.  
13.5. The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, submitted that at the end of 
the 8th Five Year Plan the Planning Commission issued very detailed guidelines to 
carry forward the committed expenditure from plan to the non plan side. The Ministry 
of Finance (Budget Division) also issued a letter to all the Financial Advisors of the 
Department requesting them to indicate the amount to be included from plan to non 
plan.
