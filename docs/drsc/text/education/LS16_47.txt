I. INTRODUCTION

1.1. The Department of Women and Child Development is engaged in the welfare and
development of Women and Children. The Department is responsible for sefeguarding
certain provisions of Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy with
reference to Women and Children.

1.2. The Department in its nodal capacity formulates plans, policies and programmes;
enacts/amends legislations; guides and coordinates the efforts of both Governmental and
non-Governmental organisations working in the field of Women and Child Development.
For a holistic child development, the Department implements the widely acclaimed
programme of Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS). The ICDS provides an
integrated package of services comprising supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health
check-up and pre-school and non-formal education and health and nutrition education for
women.

1.3. The other major schemes implemented by the department includes funding of
Hostels for working women, Support, Training and Employment Programme, scheme of
Training-cum-employment-cum Production Centres (NORAD), Socio-Economic Programme,
the programme of condensed courses of education and vocational training etc. Most of the
programmes of the Department are run through non-Governmental organisations.

1.4. The new policy initiatives taken up by the Department, are setting-up of the
Rashtriya Mahila Kosh, adoption of National Nutrition Policy, setting up of National Creche
Fund and Mahila Samriddhi Yojana and launching the Indira Mahila Yojana. The
Department also propose to launch a comprehensive National Policy for the empowerment
of women.

1.5. The total 8th Plan outlay made for the Department was Rs. 2010 crores. The
foreign aid component during the 8th Plan period upto 1996-97 is about Rs. 556 crores, and
the total expenditure incurred during the period of 1992—96 by the Department is Rs.
2530.41 crores. However, there are certain schemes started in the middle of the 8th Five
Year Plan, for which there was no allocation in the 8th Plan. The number of such schemes is
7 and the amount spent on them upto 1995-96 is about Rs. 153 crores out of the total
expenditure incurred.

been non-expanding, are to be phased out and merged into Integrated Child Development

also stated that the Schemes of ‘Short Stay Homes for Women and Girls’ and ‘Education
work for the Prevention of Atrocities against Women’ are to be transferred/shifted to
Central Social Welfare Board beginning from 1997-98 as suggested by the Planning
Commission. The Committee, however, feels that the Early Childhood Education Scheme is
a good scheme and is also utilising the funds fully well. This may not, therefore, be merged.
The Balwadi Nutrition Programme is not utilising the funds fully. This scheme may,
therefore, be looked into. The Committee also feels that immediate steps are required to be
taken to review and revamp all the schemes.

1.11. The Committee observes that the Schemes funded by the Central Government are
not properly and effectively implemented by the States. The State Governments are not
showing much interest in the schemes. The Committee, therefore, desires that the

Il, INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)

2.1. The Scheme aims at providing integrated package of health, nutrition and
educational services to children below six years, pregnant women and nursing mothers. This
package comprises supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health check-up, referral services,
nutrition and health education and non-formal pre-school education. The Scheme is
implemented through State Governments/UTs. So far, 5023 Centrally sponsored ICDS
projects and 317 TNIP Projects have been sanctioned. External Assistance from NORAD,
SIDA and the World Bank is also available for part of the expenditure.

2.2. The revised Estimates for 1995-96 was Rs. 669.30 crores which included the
provision under General ICDS (Rs. 567.64 crores), World Bank-assisted ICDS projects
(90.00 crores) and Training of ICDS Functionaries (Rs. 11.66 crores). Again, Budget
Estimates for 1996-97 is Rs. 681.90 crores which include provision for General ICDS Scheme
(Rs. 515.94 crores). World Bank assisted ICDS Projects (Rs. 154.30 crores), and Training of
ICDS Functionaries (Rs. 11.46 crores).

2.3. From the above, it is clear that there is a significant decline in the Budget Estimate
for General ICDS Scheme, which has come down from Rs. 567.64 crores in RE for 1995-96
to Rs. 515.94 crores in BE for 1996-97. There is, however, a significant increase under the
Externally Aided Component for the World Bank-assisted projects from Rs. 90 crores during
1995-96 to Rs. 154.30 crores. The Department has stated that the additional amount under
World Bank. ated ICDS projects has been provided as per the requirements of the
projects in line with the agreement with the World Bank. A significant decline in the
provision for General ICDS has been mainly on account of the decision of the Government
to peg the provision of 1996-97 at the level of Budget Estimate for 1995-96 and not at
Revised estimates for 1995-96. It was also stated that the total requirement for General
ICDS Schemes during 1996-97 is Rs. 640.46 crores, against which the Budget Estimate is Rs.
515.94 crores meaning thereby a significant shortfall of Rs. 124.52 crores. The Committee
feels that this shortfall is likely to affect the over all performance of the project. The
Committee realising the importance of the scheme, recommends that the Planning
Commission may, bridge this gap to the extent possible and provide the required funds at
RE stage.

2.4. The newly sanctioned I.C.D.S. Project takes minimum 18 months for operationa-
lisation. The Committee last year recommended to take steps to minimise the time limit. On
this the Department stated that the States are required to undertake a number of related
activities for operationalisation of a sanctioned project. The system called ‘Plan of
Introduction of Services” (P.I.S.) sets the time limit for various activites. With a view to
ensure early operationalisation of the sanctioned projects, the P.I.S. schedule has been re-
designed and the maximum time limit has been reduced from 18 months to 12 months for
operationalising new projects, the Department added.

2.5. The Department further stated that the States have been requested to take urgent
action in operationalising the new projects at the earliest. The Department had also
convened a meeting of State Secretaries on 9-10 July, 1996 to impress on the States to take
urgent steps to operationalise the sanctioned projects within a period of 12 months. The
States have, assured to take steps towards operationalising the sanctioned projects.
However, most of the States have indicated that lack of availability of funds for
supplementary nutrition in their Budgets is the chief reason constraining early operationalisa-


tion of the projects. Provision of Central funding for supplementary nutrition, atleast upto
50% of the requirement, was endorsed by the Conference of Chief Ministers which would
further facilitate operationalisation of the projects within the revised time schedule of 12
months, the Department added. The Committee recommends that the Department should
put this proposal in the 9th Plan.

2.6. The Secretary informed the Committee that the Department is very short of funds
for the activities, they are required to undertake. The major lacuna is their responsibility to
remove malnourishment among the children. The Secretary also informed that the
Department gives honorarium for the Anganwadi Workers and the cost of the supplemen-
tary nutrition is to be borne by the State Governments. As already explained in the
preceding paragraph, the State Governments do not provide adequate money for giving the
supplementary nutrition. The Committee understands that the rate of growth is closely
related to nourishment given to the children, particularly at their very young age. After the
fourth month, supplementary food should be given to the children. The Committee is
however, constrained to note that children in a large number suffer from malnutrition
irrespective of the region. Every year hundreds of children die due to malnutrition. Tribal
areas are the most affected places. Mortality rate is very high in backward areas such as 110
per thousand in Orissa and the National average is 73 per thousand. The Secretary also
stated that the Department has been pleading with the Ministry of Finance and Planning
Commission that they must put in some amount for nutrition so that, they can at least match
the money given by the State Governments. Appreciating this move of the Department, the
Committee feels that lack of resources should not affect the nourishment and the Planning
Commission should take a realistic view of the situation and make a proper allocation at
least in the 9th plan. So far the tribal children are concerned, total funding needs to be
ensured. For this purpose, the Committee desires that the Department should explore the
possibility of mobilising funds from Tribal Development Authorities for providing sup-
plementary nutrition to vulnerable groups in tribal areas.

2.7. When the Committee sought to know about efforts made to secure convergence of
inter-sectoral services in Anganwadi Centres, the Department stated that specific areas of
convergence relating to Departments of Health and Family Welfare, Rural Development,
Education, Welfare, Food and Urban Development have been identified. Instructions have
also been sent by the Central Sectoral Ministries to their State counterparts for giving
priority to the identified areas for ensuring efficient delivery of sectoral services in the
Anganwadi Centres, the Department added. The Committee desires that, the Department
may closely monitor the status of convergence in their respective States at regular intervals.

2.8. The Committee, however, recommends that all the States should be asked to
review the’status of convergence in their respective States. The concurrent evaluation of
ICDS Scheme which presently is being carried out on a pilot basis in the five States of West
Bengal, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka and Himachal Pradesh should also be utilised for
assessing the status of convergence.

2.9. The Department informed the Committee that it has taken steps, on the
Committee’s recommendation made last year, for assessing the impact of ICDS Scheme-on
delivery of services, adequacy of staff strength and infrastructure. The Department, in close
coordination with National Council of Applied Economic Research (NCAER) and other
researchWoluntary organisations concerned with Child Development, has finalised 3 detailed
questionnaires for assessing the requirements and impact at all block4ectoral level,
household level and anganwadi level. Under the umbrella cover of NCAER, concurrent
evaluation is in progress in the:5 States of West Bengal, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka


and Himachal Pradesh. The Committee desires that the evaluation should be completed at
the earliest. The evaluation should also be conducted in all the States.

2.10. The issue of non-reporting ICDS projects is a matter of great concern for the
Committee. The Deparment stated that during the quarter ending June’96, of 3945
operational projects, reports have not been received from 826 projects. Based on the analysis
carried out by the Department on the Quarterly Progress Report (QPR), all the State
Secretaries have been specifically requested to issue necessary instructions to their CDPOs to
ensure 100% reporting during each quarter, on their performance. The Committee feels that
the State Governments are required to be very serious in this regard. The Department
should, therefore, strengthen its monitoring mechanism and pursuade the State Government
to ensure regular reporting by all the projects.

2.11. The Committee has noticed a malady of consistent under-utilisation of funds
during successive years of 8th Plan by the sub-scheme called “Training of ICDS func-
tionaries”. Out of the total 8th Plan allocation of Rs. 58 crores, only Rs. 41.07 crores were
spent upto 1995-96. The main reason for this malady is non-deputation of trainees by the
State Govts. Non deputation of trainees by the State Government and non reporting of
trainees and training centres under ICDS is a cause of concern to the Committee. Explaining
about the steps taken to sort out the problem of non-deputation and non-reporting of
trainees at training centres as also to cope up with the likely increase in the number of
trainees, the Department stated that instructions have been issued to the State Governments
to designate one of the officers of State Governments as ‘Nodal Officer’ for ICDS Training
Programme, to prepare Action Plans and Calendar of Training at the State level on time and
adhere to them and to depute trainees on time and in adequate number.

2.12. In the review meetings held in 1995-96, performance of the States was reviewed
and it was observed that there was a need to identify alternative training methodologies to
meet the training requirement, the Department added. It was also added that alternative
training methodologies such as Field Based Training approaches Distance Education through
satellite television, etc., are being considered to meet the training requirements as a result of
universalisation. Distance Education approach through satellite television, which is in the
final stages of preparation, is likely to be beamed in October, 1996, on a pilot basis. Other
innovative techniques e.g. Field based training approaches and Mobile team training
approach, which are of shorter duration and have been adopted in some States, are being
considered to be extended to other States. These methodologies are being adopted to cope
up with the non-deputation of trainees, the Department added.

2.13. The Committee feels that designating Nodal Officer would not solve the problem
of non-deputation of trainees unless State Governments are serious about implementing the
programme. Similarly, distance education cannot totally replace the direct training. The
Mobile training team is a good proposal and the Department should adopt this proposal
extensively at the earliest.

2.14. The Secretary also stated that there are a large number of vacancies which is also
a reason for non-attending of training by the I.C.D.S. functionaries. For example, as per the
status report of ICDS in June, 1996, the sanctioned strength of CDPOs was 4314 out of
which 1430 were vacant. Similarly, the sanctioned strength of Anganwadi Workers was
4,00314 out of which 97,248 were vacant. This is mainly because of the procedures adopted
by the State Governments and various Stay orders by the Courts. Taking a serious view on
this, the Committee strongly recommends that the State Governments should be persuaded
to make serious efforts to get the Stays vacated and also to simplify the system so as to


remove the procedural delays. Another reason given by the Secretary is that the
functionaries are called for various training programmes such as, those organised by Health
Department, Education Department etc. Therefore, their attendance at the centre gets
reduced. The Secretary has, therefore, explained that due to these reasons, the Department
would like to undertake mobile training at a large scale. There will be teams which would go
from place to place and also give on the spot training to these persons. Appreciating the
idea of mobile training the Committee, as already stated above, recommends that
Department should take up this on a large scale.

2.15. The Honorarium fixed for Anganwadi Workers is of two types. Those who are
having matriculation qualification are paid Rs. 400 per month and those who do not have
this qualification are paid Rs. 350 per month. The Committee feels that this is a very meagre
amount and may be increased to a reasonable extent.

2.16. On a suggestion that there should be a maternity centre at the Anganwadi, the
Secretary stated that under the JRY scheme, buildings for Anganwadi are constructed and
the Department had suggested that they could construct one extra room for Anganwadi
buildings for the purpose of using this as a maternity centre. The Committee appreciates this
idea and desires that the matter may be taken up with the Ministry of Rural Development
for early implementation of the proposal.

गा, CRECHES AND NATIONAL CRECHE FUND

3.1. The Department informed the Committee that there has been no expansion of the
scheme of “Assistance to Voluntary Organisations for Creches for Working and Ailing
Women” for the last 5-6 years due to non-availability of additional funds. The Budget
provision under the scheme is sufficient only to meet the requirement of the existing (12470)
creches, the Department added. Therefore, in March, 1994 the Department set up a
National Creche Fund with a corpus of Rs. 19.90 crores, and under this, 1243 creches (944
general creches and 299 Anganwadis-cum-creches) have been sanctioned till March, 1996, it
was stated.

3.2. It is a matter of great concern to the Committee that India today requires 8 lakh
creches for its children and against this the Department has been able to set up only 13,713
creches under both the schemes mentioned above leaving an enormous gap which should be
filled at the earliest. An additional amount of Rs. 1767 crores, as per existing norms, will be
required to set up the balance number of creches, and Rs. 1453 crores will be required every
year thereafter to run these creches, the Department added.

3.3. The Committee recognising necessity of the scheme, recommends that the
Department may prepare a perspective plan for this purpose and make projections
accordingly for the 9th plan. Planning Commission also should consider this.

3.4. The Committee, however, is not able to agree with the Department's view that the
scheme of “Creche and Day-care Centres” is not having sufficient funds to meet the
requirements of existing creches. The Committee has noticed that the scheme has been
suffering from the consistent under utilisation of funds. This is evident from the fact that out
of the total 8th plan allocation of Rs. 33.30 crores, only Rs. 15.90 crores were spent upto
1995-96. Explaining about the under-utilisation of funds, the Department stated that the
Plan provisions for these schemes during the 8th Plan were made only to meet the additional
costs on account of revision of norms such as honorarium to Creche Workers, Balwadi
Workers, cost of supplementary nutrition etc. The likely shortfall in the expenditure is on
account of the fact that there was some delay in sanctioning ‘the revised norms, the

IV. CENTRAL SOCIAL WELFARE BOARD (CSWB)

4.1. The Committee recommended last year that the Department may appoint a
Committee for assessing the functioning of the Central Social Welfare Board. On this the
Department stated that a Committee has been appointed for the purpose and will be
meeting very shortly. The Committee desires that the assessment of CSWB should be
completed at the earliest.

4.2. The Revised Estimate for Central Social Welfare Board for 1995-96 is Rs. 22.95
crores. It has been reduced to Rs. 17.01 crores for the year 1996-97. On this, the
Department stated that as per direction of Planning Commission Government has decided to
present only an interim budget and obtain a Vote on Account on the basis of the interim
budget. Net Budgetary Support (NBS) for annual plan 1996-97 would be kept at the level
approved for the budget estimate of the year 1995-96. Accordingly the budget estimate of
Rs. 17.01 crores for the year 1996-97 of the CSWB has been kept, the Department added.
The Committee recommends that the Planning Commission may restore the required funds
at RE stage.

V. MAHILA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA

5.1. The Committee recommended last year that the Mahila Samriddhi Yojana should
be extended to the urban areas also with suitable modification. On this, the Department
stated that the matter was taken up with the Planning Commission at an appropriate level.
Since the Programme Evaluation Organisation of Planning Commission was already
evaluating the scheme it was advised to wait till the findings of evaluation report were
received. Moreover, Planning Commission desired that it was too early to take up any
modification in the scheme as the scheme had not even completed three years of its
implementation, the Department added.


5.2. Since the launching of the scheme on 2nd October, 1993 till 30th June, 1996, 183.22
lakh MSY account have been opened. Targets & achievements upto 1995-96 are as follows:

Target Achievement

5.3. The Department informed the Committee that during the 1st one and a half years
of the inception of the scheme, due to the various teething problems like shortage of
stationery on the part of Department of Post, orientation of Postal employees etc. the target
could not be achieved. The target of 3 crore accounts upto March 1996, proposed in the
initial EFC Memorandum was subsequently revised to 2 crore accounts. However, the
ultimate target of 5 crore accounts upto March, 1997 has been retained, the Department
added. The Committee feels that the targets being set are not realistic. The Department
should try to keep realistic targets and should try to achieve them also.

5.4, When the Department was asked as to whether the amount of Rs. 300/ for
opening an account under the Mahila Samriddhi Yojana was sufficient enough to keep the
scheme going, the Department stated that suggestions from the field have been received that
Rs. 300/ as a ceiling for MSY was proving as a disincentive to saving. However, a decision
to approach Planning Commission would be taken after the Programme Evaluation
Organisation report has been studied, the Department added. The Committee suggests that
an early decision may be taken in this regard.

VI. RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH (RMK)

6.1. Rashtriya Mahila Kosh is the newly added scheme of the Department. It looks-after
disbursal of credit to women through the NGOs. A large number of NGOs working in the
thrift and credit field are given loans by the Rashtriya Mahila Kosh. The RMK was
established with an initial fund of Rs. 31.00 crores. By the end of March, 1996 the fund has
increased to Rs. 4300 crores.

6.2. The Secretary has, however, stated that the main problem in this scheme is that
they can reach only those N.G.O’s which are working in thrift and credit. As a remedy to
this problem, she has explained that Block level societies and Anganwadi Level Indira
Mahila Centres are being set-up under the Indira Mahila Yojana to meet those require-
ments. The Committee, however, desires that the out reach of the Rashtriya Mahila Kosh
should be extended. The Committee also desires that the Department should impress upon
the Banks to provide loans over and above the Fund amount of Rashtriya Mahila Kosh.

VII. TRAINING CUM PRODUCTION CENTRES

7.1. The Scheme of setting up of Employment-cum-Income Generating Units for
Women was launched in 1982-83 with a specific objective of extending training and
employment opportunities for women in non-traditional and upcoming trades. The allocation
for “Training-cum-production Centres” has been increased from Rs. 6.20 crores in Revised
Estimates for 1995-96 to Rs. 18.00 crores for 1996-97.

7.2. Justifying the increase in the allocation, the department stated that during the years
1994-95 and 1995-96, the scheme has expanded considerably both in magnitude and physical
coverage and a number of new trades and occupations have been covered. The target fixed
for 1995-96 was 5000 beneficiaries and the achievement was 13,000 beneficiaries. The Eighth
Plan target was 25,000 beneficiaries and the achievement upto 1995-96 was 35,797.


Therefore, it was felt that the scheme should be considerably expanded to cover 22,500
beneficiaries in the year 1996-97 with a total outlay of Rs. 18 crores assuming the per capita
assistance of Rs. 8,000 per beneficiary, the Department added.

7.3. The Committee has noted that the State Women Development Corporations are
being given a major role in implementing this scheme this year, and would act as nodal
agencies for receiving applications from NGOs under the scheme. The Committee, feels that
the State Government should take more interest in implementing the schemes funded by the
Centre. The Department should be very vigilant in this regard. For making the state women
corporations as nodal agencies very successful in the matter the Central Government should
strengthen and tighten its monitoring mechanism. The funds being provided should be
properly and fully spent.

VIII. SCHEMES ON NUTRITION

8.1 The schemes aim at production and fortification of nutritious foods and beverages
and carrying out research and development studies in the field of nutrition by providing
grants-in aid to the implementing agencies; imparting nutrition education and monitoring of
ICDS supplementary feeding programmes with regard to quality and regularity by the field
units of Food & Nutrition Board.

8.2. The Committee has noted that there are about 13 schemes under implementation at
present. All are very small schemes. The Committee has noticed very unimpressive
performance by these schemes. The utilisation of funds by the Central Schemes in this
Division is however, satisfactory. Out of the overall 8th plan allocation of Rs. 6.75 crores,
Rs. 6.15 crores were spent upto 1995-96. But the centrally sponsored schemes have put up a
dismal performance. Out of the 8th plan allocation of Rs. 3.25 crores, only Rs. 0.85 crores
were spent by these schemes. During 1995-96, the central schemes spent Rs. 1.90 crores out
of plan allocation of Rs. 2.25 crores and the centrally sponsored schemes spent Rs. 0.09
crores out of Rs. 0.23 crores allocated.

8.3. The Committee feels that instead of having many schemes without getting the
desired results, it would be better to run a few schemes with better performance. Even
though the Department has initiated some measures in this direction, they are not sufficient.
The Committee feels that a complete over-hauling of the schemes is required in view of the
9th plan approaching now.

IX. EDUCATION WORK FOR THE PREVENTION OF ATROCITIES AGAINST WOMEN

9. The Eighth Plan allocation made for this programme was Rs. 2.25 crores whereas the
expenditure made upto 1995-96 was only Rs. 1.48 crores. On this, the Department stated
that the shortfall is because of non-receipt of adequate proposals in this regard from
Voluntary Organisations. The Committee is constrained to note that even though, the fund
was available, the Department could not attract sufficient proposals from the Voluntary
Organisations. The Department does not appear to have taken enough steps to increase
awareness about this scheme even though this scheme is very important to safeguard the
interests of women. The Committee takes a strong exception to this casual approach on the
part of the Department and recommends for stepping up of efforts to attract more NGO’s.

X. WORKING WOMEN’S HOSTELS

10. The Secretary informed the Committee that so far 777 Working Women Hostels
have been sanctioned which provide accommodation to 53,000 Working Women. The
present scheme excludes girl students. The Committee, strongly, feels that there is greater


10

necessity for the provision of hostel facilities for girls pursuing higher studies because all
colleges and universities do not have enough accommodation for girls. On this, the Secretary
informed that the Department is planning to change the scope of the scheme so as to include
students doing higher education, professional courses or diplomas, so that it could facilitate
them to stay in the towns and acquire more skills. However, the Secretary stated that the
availability of land and the cost of land are the main problems faced in this scheme. The
Department has not touched even 1/3 of the requirements. The NGO's response is not quite
encouraging. They are not coming up with sufficient number of applications. Besides, the
cost of construction has gone up but, the Department has to go by the C.P.W.D. approved
cost, she added. She has however, stated that the Department would like to sanction more
hostels but, it could not be done, not because of the lack of funds, but because there are not
many applications. The Committee feels that it is a very strange state of affairs. It appears,
there is lack of awareness among the NGO’s to come forward for constructing the hostels.
As regards the construction cost is concerned, the Committee recommends that the
Department may take up the matter with C.P.W.D. for revision of the cost so as to enable
more number of NGOs to come forward.

XI. STRENGTHENING OF WOMEN BUREAU

11. The Eighth Plan allocation made for this scheme was Rs.75 lakhs and not a single
rupee has been spent so far under this. The Department has explained that because of the
ban of creation of posts, no expenditure could be incurred on this. The Committee is not
happy with this sorry state of affairs in the Department. The Committee fails to understand
the reason given by the Department that the ban on creation of post led to non-utilisation of
funds. All is not well in this and the Committee recommends that the Department should
properly enquire into this matter.

XII. FORTIFICATION OF MILK WITH VITAMIN-A

12. The Eighth Plan allocation made for this scheme is Rs. 15 lakhs and the expenditure
made so far is only 4 lakhs. The Department stated that the shortfall in the expenditure is
due to the fact that reimbursement bills have not been received from the dairies and they
are being pursuaded to send the same. It is a very misleading statement on the part of the
Department. During 1994-95 Rs. 2 lakhs were allocated and similarly in 1995-96 Rs. 1 lakh
was allocated and in both the years the full amount was spent. In 1993-94 Rs. 2 lakhs were
allocated and nothing was spent. And in 1992-93 Rs. 2 lakhs were allocated and Rs. 1 lakh
was spent. Even, if the total amount allocated in all the four years is put together, it comes
to only 7 lakhs which is not even half of the Eighth Plan allocation. The Committee desires
that the Department should properly examine the matter and ensure full utilisation.
