REPORT

I. INTRODUCTION

1.1. The Museum, the oldest institution of its kind in Asia was founded in 1814. At
present, it comprises six sections such as Art, Archaeology, Anthropology, Geology,
Zoology and Economic Botany with a number of galleries under each section. Many rare
and unique specimens both Indian and Trans-Indian relating to Humanities and Natural
Seciences are preserved and displayed in the galleries of these sections.

1.2. The Parliamentary Standing Committee was shown to round the Museum many
rare objects of art collection made over the years. The rich repository of,cultural and natural
heritage make the museum responsible to the society to exhibit its wealth from time to time
through various exhibitions for the enjoyment and dissemination of knowledge to the people.

1.3. The Parliamentary Committee was also taken to visit the galleries of the Museum
of its select treasures of antiquities and art objects acquired over a long span of years
between 1836 to 1993. The relic containers of the lord ‘Buddha’ carved in stone and crystal
unearthed at the ancient site of belonging to the Mughal emperors; gold coins issued by
Kanishka, Samudragupta, Kumaragupta-I, Akbar and Jahangir—the great emperors of
India; the delicate Mughal miniatures; illustrated leaves from Shahnama, the miniatures
from Kangra valley, and the paintings by the great masters of Bengal school, viz.
Abanindranath Tagore, Nandalal, Bose and Jamini Roy were on show there.

1.4. Of the decorative art objects a Chinese cup carved in rhino horn, ‘Burmese silver
bowl, Japanese musical instrument, Nepalese lamp-stands and a copper gift umbrella
donated by a ‘Burmese monk to the temple of ‘Bodhgaya are added attractions of this
Museum. Besides, a valuable socio-economic document inscribed on a small piece of stone
from Mahasthangarh, Bangladesh, supposed to be the royal communique of a benevolent
ruler, a hubble bubble in silver filigree—a Orissan craftsmanship, a Tibetan eleven headed
and thousand armed icon of Avalokitesvara as well as the decorative ceramic pots from
Bombay, Jaipur, Multan and Madras adorn this Museum.

1.5. Assembled together from the vast treasures of its Art, Archaelogy and Anthropo-
logy Section the exhibits—a visual feast to the eyes of the art-lovers and connoisseurs, offer
a rare glimpse of the Museum’s exquisite collection acquired through sustained efforts over
the past one hundred and eighty years.

1.6. The Museum Directorate has eight co-ordinating service units such as Education,
Preservation, Publication, Presentation, Photography Medical, Modelling and Library. These
programmes should be intensified and expanded and adequate budget provisions must be
made for this purpose.

II. EXHIBITION OF ART OBJECTS IN ROTATION

2.1. During the visit of the Museum the Committee was informed that only 10 precent
of the vast treasures of its antiquities and art objects have been exhibited presently due to
lack of space. As per norms 40 per cent antiquities and art objects are required to be
exhibited. The Committee, was however, given to understand that there are large number of
art objects which are unique and rare lying in the godowns of the Museum located in the

are properly taken care of and preserved, they may wear out with the passage of time.

2.2. The Committee, therefore, recommends that these objects should be properly
preserved and exhibited in the Museum by rotation so that the life span of these valuable
antiquities and art objects could be enhanced. The Committee also recommends that the
space occupied by the Zoological Survey of India, Botanical Survey of India and
Anthropological Survey of India in the building of Museum should be got vacated, so that
the space meant for Museum could be utilised for the exhibition of the antiquities and art
objects acquired over a long span of time. Even after the vacation of the space by these
Departments if the space is not found to be adequate to display at least 40 per cent of the
collections, then the Government should consider construction of a new building so as to
accommodate the display of at least 40 per cent of the collections available with the
Museum. For the vacating Departments, 1.6. Zoological Survey of India, Botanical Survey of
India and Anthropological Survey of India, some building will have to be constructed to
accommodate them according to their requirement.

Ill. STRENGTHENING OF SECURITY ARRANGEMENT

3. During Committee’s visit to the Museum, the Committee was informed that the
security system of the Museum was periodically examined and up dated with the
introduction of some new measures and revamping of the old systems. On the recommenda-
tion of a high power security committee and intelligence department the entry and exist
points of the galleries were minimised. Vehicular traffic was restricted within the campus and
loopholes at all vulnerable points were plugged. Further, the Museum has, of late,
introduced walkie — talkie system and effective illumination of peripheral areas of the
museum complex as well as replacement of fire extinguishing apparatus in order to
strengthen the security and safety of exhibits. However, the Committee feels that the
security arrangements at the-Museum should be reviewed from time to time and adequate
number of fire extinguisheres may also be provided to take care of any untoward incidents.
The Government should consider installation of close circut T.Vs. at tht appropriate places
inside the premises of the Museum keeping in view the importance of the valuable art
objects displayed in the Museum.

IV. INTERNATIONAL EXHIBITION

4.1 The Committee was also informed that the Museum participates in International
Exhibitions form time to time. Recently the Museum participated in two international
exhibitions in the U.S.A. and Japan. The exhibits were taken from the collection of the
Museum. Two Jaina images carved in stone and bronze were sent to Los Angeles in
connection with the exhibition.

4.2 The Committee is happy to note that such International exhibitions serve as vehicles
for disseminating Indian Culture abroad. However, the Committee is of the opinion that the
various invaluable exhibits of bronze and silver idols which are taken abroad for exhibitions
in their original form invites security and safety risks. This practice should be discouraged
keeping in view the security and safety hazards involved in their transit and transportation.
The Committee feels that only the replicas of such idols should be allowed to be carried out
abroad for exhibitions.


9. INSTALLATION OF AUDIO VISUAL AIDES

5. The Committee notes that in many Museums the world over, particularly in the
advanced countries, there is a system of providing Audio and Visual programmes in each
section of the Museum explaining about the art objects kept in that Section. The Committee
desires that the Govt. may examine the feasibility of introducing the similar system in the
Indian Museum also.
