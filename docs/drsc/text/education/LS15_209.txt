REPORT
 
I        INTRODUCTION
         
1.1     The Ministry of Women and Child Development (MWCD) is the nodal agency for the welfare,
development and empowerment of women and children in the country.  In 1986, a separate
Department for women and children was constituted under the aegis of Ministry of HRD.  This
was more a recognition of the fact that the women population being valuable human resource
deserved more focused attention.  In January 2006, the Department was made a separate Ministry
with greater power and larger mandate.
 
1.2     The role of Ministry of Women & Child Development includes policy formulation, conception of
innovative programmes and their implementation, co-ordination with other sectors and State
Governments, gender-budgeting, training and capacity-building, monitoring the status of women
and their rights.  Various policies, programmes, schemes and enabling measures have been
evolved for the benefit of women and children concerning wide range of issues from gender
equality, women and child related legislation to their social and economic rehabilitation.   The
schemes/programmes of the Department are implemented mainly with the support of the State
Government, other Government agencies and non-governmental organizations. 
 
1.3     The Ministry has identified the following objectives in its Vision Statement for the Eleventh Plan:
-
-
         Favouring activities in favour of the endangered Girl child.
-         Social and economic empowerment of women through well-formulated schemes as well as
convergence and networking with programmes/schemes of other Departments and State

1.4     The Committee appreciates the lofty ideals and objectives envisaged through the Vision
Statement of the Ministry.  In the first instance, it appears as if the vision covers expansions
or review of some of the existing schemes of the Ministry.  This is a positive thinking on the
part of the Ministry.  Regular evaluation of the schemes indicate that feedback is being
obtained, monitoring is done and corrective measures taken.  Most of the schemes under the
Ministry are being implemented through State Governments or NGOs.  It is, therefore,
imperative that implementing agencies are kept in loop during the entire exercise of review
and revamping to be done after thorough consultation with field agencies.  The Committee
hopes that the Ministry has done all such preparatory exercises as were necessary in respect
of its schemes meant to be reviewed, expanded and revamped.  The Committee is optimistic
that Ministry would strive hard to achieve its mandate for development   of  women and
protection of children through focused schemes envisaged in their Vision Statement.

2.8     The Committee expresses its serious concerns over the major gaps between the targets and
the achievements thereof.  The Committee feels that the non-availability of funds is not an
issue, but utilization thereof seems to be the main problem resulting in non-achievement of
targets.   Apparently, there are some serious gaps at each level in the entire delivery system. 
The Committee believes that more impetus needs to be given to all the schemes that concern
the most vulnerable sections of our society i.e. women and children.  Implementing agencies
need to be motivated for more efficacious implementation of the schemes.  Simultaneously,
we must have effective periodical monitoring and review of the performance of the Schemes
with a view to overcome the problems encountered in implementation of these schemes.

3.4     The Committee observes that construction of AWC buildings is the most important
component of infrastructure development so that the services under the scheme could be
delivered efficiently.   The Committee understands that Ministry proposes to provide for
construction of AWC building in XIth plan.  This step, according to the Committee, should
have been taken long time back.  The Committee after having seen during its visits to the
AWCs in various parts of the country, is also convinced that the ICDS Scheme must provide
for construction of AWCs for making it more effective.   The Committee would like the
Ministry to explore the possibility of involving the Ministries of Rural Development, Tribal
Affairs, Social Justice and Empowerment and Health and Family Welfare under their
existing Schemes for construction of AWCs.  The Committee also reiterates its earlier
recommendation for fixing a percentage of its allocation for infrastructure development on
the lines of SSA.
 
3.5     Committee notes that not all AWCs are providing supplementary nutrition. Supplementary
nutrition is, infact, heart and soul of the ICDS Scheme that has to be given to the children
and to the pregnant and lactating mothers.  The Committee views it seriously and it does not
know the reason about this.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to make
sure that all the AWCs must provide supplementary nutrition.

IV      RAJIV GANDHI NATIONAL CRECHE SCHEME FOR THE CHILDREN OF
WORKING MOTHERS
 
4.1     Rajiv  Gandhi National Crèche Scheme for the children of Working Mothers was launched with
effect from 1
st January, 2006.  It provides crèche services to the children in age group of 0-6 years
including supplementary nutrition, emergency medicines and contingencies.   As of 14.03.2008,
31737 crèche were sanctioned covering 793425 children as beneficiaries.
 
4.2     The Scheme is being implemented through the Central Social Welfare Board and two national
level voluntary organizations, namely, Indian Council for Child Welfare and Bharatiya  Adim Jati
Sevak Sangh.  The Scheme provides financial assistance of Rs.42,384/- per crèche per annum,
which includes honorarium for two crèche workers @ Rs.1,000/- per crèche worker per month,
supplementary nutrition to 25 children @ Rs.2.08 per child per day for 26 days in a month and
Rs.350 per month for contingencies and emergency medicines.  The income eligibility limit of
family has been enhanced from Rs.1800 per month to Rs.12,000 per month.  According to the
schematic pattern the implementing organization receives 90% of the total expenditure as grant
except the honorarium of two crèche workers. A non-recurring grant of Rs.10,000/- per crèche is
sanctioned to the implementing organization at the time of setting up of a crèche.

XVIII          NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
 
18.1   The National Commission for Women (NCW) was constituted on 31
st January, 1992 as a statutory
body at the national level, in pursuance of the National Commission for Women Act, 1990 to
safeguard the interests of women.  It has a wide mandate covering almost all aspects of women's
development viz, to investigate and examine the legal safeguards provided for women under the
constitution and other laws and recommend to Government measures for their effective
implementation look into complaints and take suo moto notice of matters relating to deprivation of
women's rights etc and take up the issues with appropriate authorities etc.  Its activities include
right from participating and advising on the planning process of socio-economic development of
women to inspecting or causing to inspect a jail, remand home, women's institution or other place
of custody where women are kept as prisoners or otherwise so as to improve the living condition
of women and take up the issue with the concerned authorities for remedial action.

XIX   ROLE OF NGOs
 
19.1   Most of the schemes of the Ministry are being implemented through NGOs and their role in
development of women and children is quite important.   Some of the NGOs have done their job
with a missionary zeal.  On a query about monitoring aspect of NGOs, the Secretary clarified that
the Ministry asked the State Governments to furnish the list of NGOs whom grants should be
released.  It is the State Government which monitors the working of NGO.  Central Government
does not have the resources to execute direct monitoring for inquiring about the financial capacity
and the credibility of NGOs and the State Government is relied upon.  However, enquiry is
conducted by Central Government in case of complaint against an NGO.
 
19.2   The Committee appreciates the contribution of NGOs in the field of social and economic
upliftment of women.  Few of them are also doing notable work for development of children.   But
there are complaints or misappropriation of funds, and few NGOs have been blacklisted.   These
blacklisted NGOs sometime manipulate to seek funds and continue draining the nation kitty. Mere
blacklisting will not suffice.  The Committee would like the Ministry to initiate criminal
proceedings against erring NGO as a strong deterrent in the future.  The Ministry should
include the follow up of this recommendation in the Action Taken Replies.

must have worsened further as the last census was conducted in the 2001. The Committee is of the
opinion that the need of the hour is to introspect about our approach, efforts and resources
devoted to overall development of children so far.  The Committee feels that we have a long way to

they grow up into confident and self-respecting individuals.

to generate required impetus to our endeavour in providing a safe environment for the holistic
development of children, in need of care and protection.   It expects the Ministry to complete all

however like the Ministry to first address all the shortcomings of the three existing schemes to be

“National Website on Missing Children” to facilitate training, recovery and rehabilitation of
children who either run away or are missing envisaged under ICPS should also be made functional

existing Juvenile Children Homes needs improvement and upgradation in the quality of care
provided to children in the institutions, full staff strength, regular and timely release of Central
Grants etc.  The Ministry should focus on these aspects of the existing Juvenile Homes under the
ICPS.

justice to the aggrieved woman.

       The Committee is of the view that the members and Chairpersons of the Women

multifarious jobs that are mainly juridical and legal in nature. The Committee is of the opinion

XX    CONCLUSION
 
The Committee during the general review of the Schemes implemented by the Ministry has
noted that there has been under utilization of funds in respect of many of the schemes of the
Ministry.  The Committee is aware that most of the Schemes of the Ministry are being

the only reason.  Under utilization of funds could also be due to many other reasons ranging from
lack of vision, direction and ideas.  Lack of will to execute the Schemes cannot be ruled out.  The
Committee believes that utilization of funds should not necessarily be wasteful or hurried - it
should be spent fairly as well as evenly through out the year with a closer and stricter monitoring.  
The Committee strongly emphasizes the need for ensuring that funds are utilized in a proper,
smooth and productive manner.  This would also pave way for more realistic projections and
achievement of targets.  There is a need to evolve a delivery mechanism with no bottlenecks and
other problems being encountered in the existing system.
