REPORT
 
I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       The size of the population treated as youth i.e. between the age group of 15-35 constitutes nearly one-third of the
total population of India.  The main responsibility of the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports is to ensure qualitative
and quantitative improvements in Youth Programmes and also on broad basing and bringing about awareness of sports
and physical fitness amongst school children and youth. 
1.2       The Department finances various youth welfare programmes which range   from personality development to
vocational training.  Most of these programmes are implemented by reputed institutions, non-governmental
organisations as well as State Governments whereby financial assistance is provided by the Department as per the
stipulated norms. The major schemes for youth development are National Service Scheme, Nehru Yuva Kendra 
Sangathan, National Integration Programme, Youth Hostels and National Service Volunteers Scheme, etc.
1.3       The Department also finances a number of programmes which aim at promotion of sports, including creation of
sports infrastructure in all parts of the country, grants for laying of synthetic surfaces, spotting and nurturing of talents
through the schemes of Sports Authority of India, grants to National Sports Federations, National Championships,
organisation  of tournaments for rural youth, women  and in the North-Eastern Region.
II.         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The total outlay, both Plan and Non-Plan taken together, provided to the Department for the year 1999-2000 was
Rs. 230.09 crores in BE which was reduced to Rs. 220.09 crores at RE stage.  The allocation made for the year 2000-
2001 is Rs. 260.00 crores  (Plan Rs. 215.00 crores and Non-Plan Rs. 45.00 crores).
2.2       When asked to comment on the increase in the allocations, the Department submitted that it was not
commensurate with the projected requirements.  Against a projected demand of Rs. 410.00 crores under Plan for the
year 2000-2001, a provision  of only Rs. 215.00 crores has been made.  The projections included a higher requirement
for Sports Authority of India (SAI) keeping in view the proposed Afro-Asian Games 2001 to be hosted by India and
other requirements  as well. Besides that, higher allocation was also required for National Service Scheme, National
Reconstruction Corps Scheme and Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan.  The marginal increase of only Rs.25.00 crores
would enable the Department to only operate the on-going schemes at the current level.
2.3       The Committee observes that although allocations for the Department have been below the projected
requirement, trend of expenditure during the period 1997-2000 clearly indicates that plan allocation has been invariably
reduced at the RE stage both for Youth and Sports Schemes.  This reduced provision also could not be fully utilised.  In
contrast, provision under Non-plan has been enhanced during this period for both the Schemes and utilisation level has
also been optimum.  The Committee does not approve of this trend of allocation and expenditure. The Department
should make concerted efforts for optimum and judicious expenditure of allocated funds.

district level Kendras throughout the country.  It is the largest grass-root level organisation in the Asia  and Pacific
region catering to the needs of more than 8 million non-student rural youth in the age group of 15-35 years enrolled

IV.       NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME
4.1       The objective of the National Service Scheme is  the development of personality of students through community
service. NSS has, at present, over 15.87 lakh student volunteers spread over 175 universities and 22 Senior Secondary
Councils. Since its inception in 1969, more than 1.52 crore students from the Universities, Colleges and Institutions of
higher learning have benefitted from NSS activities.  Due to its overwhelming popularity and demand, the scheme has
been extended to the students of +2 level in selected institutions.
4.2       The Committee has noted that the allocation made for National Service Scheme (NSS) under plan has been
steeply fluctuating.  Allocation of Rs. 24.50 crores was brought down to Rs. 18.63 crores at RE stage and again Rs.
26.15 crores have been earmarked for 2000-2001.  Explaining this trend, the Department informed that higher allocation
at the BE 1999-2000 stage was proposed keeping in view the proposed increase in the scale of assistance for regular
activities and special camps. The Committee, however, notes that the proposed increase, though approved by the EFC,
is yet to be   approved by the Cabinet.   As the proposed increase was not likely to take effect during 1999-2000, the
allocation was reduced to Rs. 18.63 crores at the RE stage.  However, considering that the proposed increase will be
approved during the year 2000-2001, the allocation has been enhanced.
4.3       The Committee has been informed that for effective implementation of the Programme, there is a three tier
monitoring mechanism under which  Progress Reports in prescribed proforma are sent by the Institutions to the
concerned NSS Regional Centres and the State Liaison Cells.  After compilation, these reports are sent to the

V.        NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEERS SCHEME
5.1       The National Service Volunteers Scheme launched in 1977-78, aims at providing opportunities  to any person
below 25 years of age, who has completed his first degree course to involve himself, on a voluntary basis, in nation
building activities for a specific period of one or two years.  Each volunteer is paid a stipend of Rs. 500/- p.m. and a
fixed travelling allowance @ Rs. 200/- p.m.   He is also given a contingency grant of Rs. 100/- per year. At present,
5727  NSVs  are  deployed with the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan, National Service Scheme, Bharat Scouts & Guides,
Youth Hostels Association of India, etc.   The State Governments/Union Territories can also avail the services of these
volunteers. 
5.2       The Committee has  noted that the allocation of Rs. 18.00 crores for National Service Volunteers Scheme in BE
1999-2000 was reduced to Rs. 6.00 crores at RE stage.  Same allocation has been made for 2000-2001.  It  was informed
that the allocation of Rs. 18.00 crores for National Service Volunteer Scheme for the year 1999-2000 also included the
provision of Rs. 12.00 crores for the new scheme,  National Reconstruction Corps (NRC).  However, due to non-
opening of the new budget Head for the NRC for want of Parliamentary approval, no expenditure could be incurred
during 1999-2000.  The Parliamentary approval for opening a new Budget Head for NRC scheme was received in
February, 2000 and accordingly the provision for NRC Scheme has been kept separately in the year 2000-2001.

VI.       NATIONAL INTEGRATION PROGRAMME
6.1       The Scheme for promotion of National Integration aims at providing financial assistance for a variety of youth
programmes aimed at fostering national integration and communal harmony.  The funds are channelised through a
number of voluntary youth organistions. 
6.2       The Committee was informed that Rs.285.00 lakhs out of the allocation of Rs.300.00 lakhs for the year 1999-
2000 has been spent as on 21.1.2000.  184 National Integration Camps and 9 Inter-State Youth Exchange Programmes
have been given administrative approval during this period.
6.3       Programme of National Integration provides the framework for greater involvement of voluntary agencies in the
task of national integration and communal harmony.  The Committee finds that out of total Rs.2,65,27,551 allocated in
1999-2000, Union Territory of Delhi has been the major beneficiary with getting Rs.89,35,513.  During 1998-99 also,
out of the total allocation of Rs.3,18,34,783, funds released to Delhi amounted to Rs.89,97,653.  In contrast, States
having communal problems are not actively involved in this Programme.  The Committee finds that as a result of efforts
made by the Department, 58 proposals from the States of Jammu and Kashmir, Punjab and North-Eastern States have
been received during 1999-2000. The Committee hopes that the programme of National Integration would be expanded
to States affected by terrorism and communal disturbance as a result of efforts made by the Department.
VII.      ASSISTANCE TO YOUTH AND SPORTS CLUBS
7.1       The principal objective of this scheme is to encourage and assist newly established Youth Clubs so that they can
effectively take part in nation building activities.   Under this scheme, financial assistance of Rs. 10,000/- per youth
Club in normal areas and Rs. 15,000/- per youth club in tribal areas is given to those Youth Clubs which are registered
under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or corresponding State Act .  Under this scheme, NYKS has given financial
assistance to 1369 Youth Clubs in the country. 
7.2       On the basis of recommendation of the working groups constituted for the formulation of IX Five Year Plan, it
has been decided to merge the scheme of Assistance to Sports Clubs with this scheme. With the aim to promote Nodal
Voluntary Sports Clubs/Sports Centres, one in each Block, in a phased manner assistance for non-consumable and
consumable sports equipments with one time grant upto Rs. 30,000/- per club is given, which will be followed by grant
of Rs. 5000/- per annum for subsequent two years. 
7.3       The Committee observes that against an allocation of Rs.125.00 lakhs during 1999-2000, no expenditure has
been reported upto 29.2.2000.  An enhanced provision of Rs.155.00 lakhs has been made for 2000-2001. The

XI.       SPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA
11.1     Sports Authority of India is the apex body in the country  responsible for the development of sports.  A number
of Plan schemes have been taken up by SAI for implementation. These include spotting and nurturing of sports talent,
promotion of sports in special areas, women’s sports, rural sports etc.
11.2     The Committee has noted that the Plan allocation of Rs. 54.50 crores for Sports Authority of India in BE 1999-
2000, enhanced to Rs. 61.50 crores at RE stage has been further enhanced to Rs. 70.25 crores in 2000-2001. When
asked to comment on the increases in RE 1999-2000 and BE 2000-2001, the Department submitted that the additional
amount of Rs.7.00 crores in 1999-2000 was meant for meeting the requirement of the essential infrastructure projects,
maintenance of Indira Gandhi Stadium and increased requirement for meeting  salaries under the National Coaching
Scheme etc. So far as increase of Rs.8.75 crores in 2000-2001 is concerned, the Department informed that it was
required for Centres of Excellence, strengthening of Physical Education Programme and also strengthening of Sports
Science Units in Six Regional Centres etc.
11.3     The Committee observes that against an allocation of Rs.61.50 crores in 1999-2001, expenditure of only
Rs.40.89 crores has been reported upto 29.2.2000. The Committee feels that chances of meeting the expenses as
envisaged during 1999-2000 do not seem to be there in view of the expenditure position.  The Committee hopes that the
Department would make concerted efforts for maximum and judicious utilisation of funds for the programme as
proposed for 2000-2001.
11.4     The Committee was informed that in consultation with the Ministry of Finance, it was decided to allow SAI to
utilise the revenue generated by letting out space in various stadia.  All the proposals for letting out vacant space are
considered by a Sub-Committee and finalised after approval of the Department.  Space is being rented out on short term
basis and there is no provision for renewal of contract after expiry of the stipulated period of five years. The Committee
is of the view that these conditions should be strictly enforced and letting out space in various stadia should not be at the
cost of sports activities.  The Committee has been given to understand that at present net income generated by SAI is
being adjusted against allocation of funds. The Committee feels that this is not justified.  Income generated by SAI
should be treated as a saving which could be gainfully utilised for upgrading/creating additional sports facilities.
XII.      GRANTS TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS
12.1     National Sports Federations are autonomous bodies engaged in the promotion and development of various sports
disciplines with a special interest in promoting national and international competition in their respective disciplines.
Financial assistance is given to them by the  Department for sending their team abroad for training and participation in

XIII.     RURAL SPORTS PROGRAMME
13.1     The Scheme of Rural Sports Programme was launched in the year 1970-71 with a view to broad basing games in
schools and tap the hidden talent in rural areas.  The scheme is being operated by SAI.   The Scheme lays emphasis on
arranging of tournaments in rural areas and at the  block, district, state and national levels.   For the organisation of
State/UT level tournaments, an assistance of Rs. 30,000  per discipline is given to the  State Governments and Rs.
15,000 per discipline to the Union Territories.
13.2     The Committee is perturbed to note that allocation of Rs. 125.00 lakhs in 1999-2000 was reduced drastically to
Rs. 66.00 lakhs  at RE stage and expenditure incurred upto 29.2.2000 is only Rs. 42.01 lakhs.   The Committee had
expressed its serious concern last year on the trend of reduction of allocated funds at RE stage and final expenditure
being much below that.   The Committee had suggested that the States lagging behind should be encouraged to come
forward.  In response, the Department has informed that communications have been sent to States/UTs from time to
time for coming forward for implementing the Rural Sports Programme vigorously. 
13.3     The Committee finds that the Programme has been  revised w.e.f. 1.4.98 whereby assistance is being provided
only for holding competitions at  state and national level.   The States are now to hold district and block level
tournaments  out of their own resources.   The Committee fails to understand how the States already facing resource
constraints would be able to bear this additional liability.   The Committee feels that holding of district and block level
tournaments is essential for  spotting the hidden talent in rural areas.   Unless such tournaments are made a regular
feature,  holding of state and national  level tournaments would not serve any purpose.   The Committee is, therefore, of
the view that the Department should give serious thought to this aspect.
XIV.    SPECIAL AREA GAMES
This Scheme deals with scouting and nurturing talent for modern competitive  games and sports from tribal,
inaccessible rural and coastal areas of the country.   The selected tribal and rural children are admitted in SAG Centres
located in different parts of the country.   At present, there are 8 SAG centres.    The Committee has been informed that
during 1999-2000, number of trainees were proposed to be increased to the level of 685 from 380.   There was a
proposal  to set up 6 more SAG centres during 1999-2000.  The Committee would like to be apprised about the progress
made so far, in this regard.  The Committee would also like to have details about the sportspersons from tribal areas who
have brought fame to the country after being trained in SAG  centres.

XVII.   COMMITTED LIABILITY
17.1     The issue of committed liability has been engaging the attention of the Committee since 1997-98.  At the end of
the 8
th Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission had issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the committed
expenditure from Plan to the Non-Plan side .   A lump sum provision of Rs. 500 crores in the Ministry of Finance
Budget for 1997-98 was provided for this purpose.   As a result of this exercise,  however, only a sum of Rs. 150 crores
was transferred from Plan to Non-Plan for the various Ministries as committed expenditure during 1997-98.   Rest of the
amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable.   Secretary (Expenditure) while deposing before
the Committee on 27 th March, 1999 had informed that the Tenth Finance Commission had given a breathing time till
1999-2000 for this transition.
17.2     The Department has informed that a committed liability to the extent of Rs. 30.51 crores for the period 1997-98
in respect of its major schemes, i.e. NYKS and SAI has been identified and it was conveyed to the Ministry of Finance. 
However, even after reminders no response has yet been received from the Ministry of Finance.   Department will again
be approaching the Department of Expenditure for its share of committed expenditure at the Revised Estimates stage.
17.3     The Committee notes with serious concern that while grants for Plan Schemes are getting reduced, Non-Plan
allocation and expenditure is steadily increasing.   What is happening is that the committed expenditure which should
appropriately be charged to the Pool, is being passed on to the respective Departments which is totally against the
guidelines of Tenth Finance Commission/Planning Commission.  If the committed expenditure is charged to the Pool, it
will not have any burden on the specific schemes of the Department.  Secretary (Expenditure), who appeared before the
Committee last year was in full agreement with the stand taken by the Committee.   The Committee  is, however,
constrained to observe that in spite of Committee’s repeated efforts/directives to assist the Departments, no progress has
been made in the matter.   It seems that breathing time till 1999-2000 given by  the Tenth Finance Commission has not
been availed of by any Department.    There is total lack of initiative on the part of the Departments.  The Committee
again emphasises  that the Department should get this matter resolved without any further delay.   The way the 
Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the
Finance Commission and the recommendations made by the Committee.  This tendency, the Committee is constrained
to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.   The Committee again reiterates that the
Department should pursue the matter and report to the Committee about the outcome thereof.
