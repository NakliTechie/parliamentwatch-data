1.0 One of the most psessing problems that is being faced today by the country is the
alarming rate of growth of population. The current high population growth rate is mainly
due to three major factors as enumerated below:

1, the large size of the population in the reproductive age group;
2. higher fertility due to unmet need for contraception; and
3. high wanted fertility due to prevailing high Infant Mortality Rate (IMR).

The all India average annual exponential growth rate during the last decade has been of
the order of 2.14% which is marginally lower than 2.22% during the previous decade, but it
is still very high when compared with the developed nations of the World. Similarly, the
female to male ratio is 927-1000 which acts against women. Reasons for this will have to be
found out and steps to arrest the trend should be taken.

1.1 With a view to check the growth of population, the Family Welfare Programme was
taken up with the introduction of Planning in the country. By implementing the Family
Welfare Programme the Country has been able to avert an estimated 197.39 million birth
upto March, 1996. This has brought down the exponential growth rate of population from
2.71% per annum to 2.14% in the 1991 census, 1.93% in 1995 as per sample Registration
Sy stem and the latest being 1.8% as per UNDP Report.

{e 1.2 The Committee is constrained to note that even though there are some States which
are doing very well such as Tripura—1.11%, Kerala-1.17%, Tamil Nadu—1.23%, etc., the
national growth rate is adversely affected by States such as Goa—7%, Rajasthan—2.41%,
U.P.—2.43% etc. (National rate 1.93% in 1995—source: Sample Registration System) The
Committee, therefore, strongly desires that effectives steps are required: to be taken in these

States.
