REPORT
INTRODUCTION
 
1.1       The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports has, over the years, undertaken a number of initiatives and organized a

large number of programmes for youth development as well as sports promotion and development of sports and physical
education. The Ministry implements about 18 schemes in the former category and about 23 in the latter category.
 
1.2       Some of the important schemes run for youth welfare are: Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), National
Service Scheme (NSS), National Service Volunteers Scheme (NSVS), Rastriya Sadbhavana Yojna, Promotion of
Adventure and National Integration, etc. Schemes for sports promotion are:  Sports Authority of India (SAI), LNCPE,
Special Awards to Winner in International Sports Events, Assistance to National Sports Federations, National Welfare
Fund for Sports persons, etc.
 
II.        NATIONAL YOUTH POLICY, 2003
2.1       In order to fulfill the legitimate aspirations and to empower the youth in the age group of 13-35 years to
successfully accomplish the challenging tasks of national reconstruction and social change that lie ahead, the National
Youth Policy has been launched which envisages that all Ministries/Departments of the Central and State Governments
particularly, in the social sectors will strive to make identifiable allocations in their budgets for youth development
programmes.  The Committee welcomes the idea of making identifiable allocation for youth programmes by not only the
Central Ministries/Departments but by States also.  This would provide a clear picture about the efforts and resources
devoted for this purpose.  Such youth specific allocations be shown by all the Ministries/Departments from the year
2006-07 itself.
 
2.2       An Inter-sectoral Committee (ISC) has been set up under the chairmanship of Secretary, Ministry of Youth
Affairs and Sports to prepare a draft National Plan of Action under the National Youth Policy.  The ISC was constituted
on the 13
th December 2004.  The ISC has representatives from concerned Ministries / Departments, NGOs as well as
experts as members. Its first meeting was held on the 17 th March 2005. The Committee notes that no time frame has
been fixed for this Inter-Sectoral Committee to complete its mandate.  Committee’s  experience has not been very good
in this regard as it causes undue delays.  The Committee, therefore, underlines the need for having time-frame to prepare
the POA by the ISC.  It is necessary that all the stake-holders are taken into confidence by the ISC in this exercise.  It
must evolve strategies to empower youth by way of ensuring their active participation in the decision making process at
all levels.  They should be treated as valuable partners in the process of national planning and development.
 
III.       
NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR YOUTH
3.1       The National Commission for Youth (NCY), which was set up in March, 2002 adopted its report in its meeting
held on 5 July, 2004 and submitted the same to the Ministry on 17 July, 2004. The report of the Commission was laid on
the Table of both the Houses of Parliament on 23
rd December 2004.  A number of recommendations have been made by
the Commission out of which 14 recommendations have been accepted in principle by the Ministry. The Committee
would like to be apprised about the recommendations of the Commission  that have been accepted for implementation.  
The Committee hopes that youth - the future leaders of the country could be made instrumental towards achieving socio-
economic changes and technological excellence and thus contribute towards national development.
 
IV.       
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS
 
4.1       Total outlay, both Plan and Non-Plan taken together provided to the Ministry for the year 2004-05, was
Rs.466.00 crores.  It was decreased to Rs.441.00 crores in RE 2004-05. The budget allocation for 2005-06 is Rs.506.99
crores (Plan Rs.438.99 crores and Non Plan Rs.68.00 crores) against a projected demand of Rs. 691.00 crores (Rs.
610.27 crores under Plan and 80.81 crores under Non-Plan). The Plan projections, the Committee was informed, were
made in view of the requirements for the Commonwealth Games 2010 and proposal for extending the National Service
Scheme to all the schools in the country in accordance with the commitment made in the Common Minimum
Programme of the Government. The Committee feels that the Ministry's projection for the annual plan, 2005-06 was
quite justified because it constituted only 10% increase over the previous year’s allocations and also because the
Ministry has to fulfill its obligation for the preparations of the Commonwealth Games, 2010 and expansion of NSS upto
the school level.  There appears to be no justification in applying cut to its allocations, particularly when the Ministry
was able to spend more than 66.72% of its allocation upto December, 2004, as per the existing norms in this regard. 
 
4.2       The Committee was informed that allocation made to the Ministry in 2005-06, constituted just 0.98 per cent of
the entire Budget of the Government of India.  The Committee considers it disappointingly low in view of the mandate

group, which constitutes nearly 40 per cent of total population of India, is the most vibrant and potent segment of our

spending Rs. 558.50 crores in Ninth Plan period against an outlay of Rs. 472.61 under Sports and Physical Education
also indicates towards fiscal (mal)practices such as unspent balances or re-appropriation etc.  The Committee is of the
view that these situations - under-utilization as well as unspent balances - indicate that both our delivery mechanism as
well as system of flow of funds are not foolproof and need to be streamlined further.  It reflects a poor physical
performance because it is directly related to the financial performance.
 
4.5       While appreciating the formulation of quarterly Action Plans during Tenth Plan by the Ministry, the Committee
feels that it has not been able to achieve the desired results. The Committee would like to emphasize the need for the
judicious and optimum utilisation of funds by the Ministry allocated for various schemes by the Ministry.
4.6       The Committee is of the view that a detailed review of the schemes showing poor performance should be
undertaken so as to identify the shortcomings and initiate remedial measures. The Ministry should involve the Planning
Commission, the State Governments and other implementing agencies in this exercise.
 
4.7       The Committee is aware of the structural problems inherent in our system from Union level down to the
Panchayats in implementing the central sector as well as centrally-sponsored schemes.  Ours being basically a
cooperative federation, States have got to play a more pro-active and coordinated role in this exercise for obtaining the
desired impact of the schemes at the ground level.
 
V.        NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN (NYKS)

5.1       The Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS) was established to harness and channelise the power of youth on the
principles of voluntarism, self-help and participation. It is the largest grassroot level non-political organisation in the
world catering to the needs of non-student rural youth.  Nehru Yuva Kendras are spread to over 500 districts with a
network of over two lakh Youth Clubs, Mahila Mandals and Rural Sports Clubs with the membership of more than 8
million rural youth (female and male) in the age group of 13-35 years. Various Ministries and Departments and other
organisations are utilising this grassroot level organisation for improving the outreach of their programmes.
 
5.2       A new initiative entitled Gandhi Gramodaya Sankalp Abhiyan – Model Village Project was launched in 2004
under which NY Kendra will adopt one village with a view to develop it as a model self-reliant village. 497 NYK
District have been identified for developing self-reliant villages on the principles of Gram Swaraj as propagated by
Mahatma Gandhi. A new scheme entitled Rashtriya Sadbhavana Yojna has been approved for empowering rural youth
clubs and promoting leadership among them.  The Committee feels that adequate publicity should be given to all youth
programmes, particularly this new scheme, through print and electronic media.
 
5.3       An allocation of Rs.34.00 crores has been made for NYKS under plan in BE 2005-06 against a provision of
Rs.28.97 crores in BE 2004-05. The Committee, however, notes that BE allocation for 2004-05 was reduced at RE due
to plan cut of the Ministry.  The allocation has been increased in the year 2005-06 mainly on account of enhanced norms
for regular/special programmes conducted by NYKS in each district both in normal as well as difficult areas.  The
Committee was assured that the Ministry would be able to fully utilise that fund.  The Committee, however, notes from
the table below that the physical performance of the NYKS has been quite dismal.

5.4       The Committee notes that out of 600 districts in the country, NYKs are there only in 500 districts, out of which,
103 are in difficult areas and 397 are in normal areas.   The committee would like to be apprised about the states, which
have districts yet to be covered by NYKs.  The Committee emphasizes the need for opening NYKs in remaining districts
also.  Its total outreach in terms of beneficiaries is also very low which needs to be expanded in a phased manner.
 
5.5       The Committee is of the view that in order to fulfill its social responsibility, the Youth Clubs and Mahila
Mandals should be encouraged to work as watchdog agencies against drug and human trafficking, HIV/AIDS etc. They
need to be encouraged to actively participate in awareness generation and promoting activities relating to national
integration.
 
5.6       The Committee notes that apart from Youth Affairs & Sports, various Ministries/Departments and other
organisations such as Rural Development, Elementary Education, Health, Home Affairs, Department of North East,
Labour and Employment, NACO, UNFPA and various State Governments are utilising the services of NYKS for

increasing the outreach of their programmes. For monitoring of such initiatives a regular Management Information
system has been developed. Besides this, four Information Development and Research Agencies for continuous
monitoring of the programmes undertaken by NYKS in their allocated areas and send a report to the Ministry thereof are
in place.
 
5.7       While appreciating the idea of utilising the services of the NYKS by other Ministries/ Departments for improving
the outreach of their programmes, the Committee has a word of caution in this regard.  The basic mandate of the NY
Kendras should not get diluted/ignored, the possibility of which cannot be ruled out in this situation.  The Committee
feels that a well-coordinated monitoring mechanism would be required, if NY Kendras were to participate in schemes
run by eight to nine Ministries/agencies. 
In view of the 97 vacant posts of District Youth Coordinators, the Committee expresses its reservations about an
effective implementation of such programmes in addition to the programmes being run by NYKS at present.   
Moreover, nearly 100 districts are yet to have NY Kendras in the country.  The Committee recommends that all the
existing District Kendras should be equipped with requisite infrastructure and manpower for handling such a large
number of programmes.    Committee would like to be apprised of details of various State level programmes being
undertaken by NYKS.   The Committee has been given to understand that state govts. have been asked to converge their
programmes with NYKS.   The Committee feels that this is the right initiative taken by the Ministry.  The Committee is
of the view that a study can be initiated by the Ministry whereunder State and Centre-run programmes could be analysed
and element of overlapping if any, in schemes may be eliminated. 
 
5.8       When asked about the response of Ministry of Finance in regard to the proposal for sanctioning 97 posts of
District Youth Coordinators, the Committee was informed that instead the Ministry was asked to further reduce the
number of posts.  The ERC had, in fact, suggested winding up of NY Kendras at the central level and decentralising
NYKS upto State/UT level.  The Committee of Secretaries, however, decided to abolish regional levels of NYKS but the
Ministry of Finance is pressing to further reduce the number of posts.
 
5.9       The Committee finds this situation quite amazing where NYKS are expected to provide additional services to
different Ministries at the national plane and yet there is an attempt to reduce their number, leave aside expanding them
in remaining Districts of the country.  Even filling up of 97 vacancies is not being allowed.  The Committee is of the
view that in the context of number of schemes relating to education, health, rural development, women and child
Development, HIV/AIDS, etc. being supported by the Union Government, the role of NY Kendras assumes special
significance.  The NYKS network can be used for monitoring, getting feedback, awareness generation etc.  NYKS
network extended upto village levels, in fact, has proved to be quite useful .  In view of this, the Committee feels that
while allocating funds for this scheme, only money aspect was kept in mind, and not the utility of it. The Committee,
therefore, calls upon the Ministry of Finance to clear the proposal of the Ministry for not only filling up the 97 vacancies
of Youth Coordinators but also for opening Kendras in remaining districts in the country and allocate adequate funds at
appropriate stage.  This scheme needs to be strengthened as an instrument for channelising the youth power to
constructive activities.
 
5.10     The Committee also suggests that Department should make every possible efforts to clear the
confusion/uncertainties in respect of recruitment test that was held for selecting Youth Coordinators.
 
5.11     During the meeting, some members of the Committee, narrated their personal experiences that elected
representatives like MPs and MLAs were not being involved, formally or informally, in the activities of the NY
Kendras.  The names recommended by them for constituting District Advisory Committees are also not being honoured
by the concerned District Collectors.  The Committee was also informed by them that meetings are not taking place at
the district level for years mainly because District Collector who has to chair those meetings does not have time for this
purpose.  As a result, monitoring of the implementation of the scheme is not being done in a regular and effective
manner. In view of this, the Committee emphasizes the need for involving people's representatives of the area in an
effective and formal manner.  Members of Parliament may also be suitably associated with a view to imparting
regularity in holding meetings etc. to review the activities of the NY Kendras and receiving guidance from them. 
The Committee also impresses upon the Planning Commission to provide support for having some officers at the
regional level who would be able to monitor as well as coordinate the activities of the NY Kendras in those regions.

functionaries.
 
6.12     Evaluation of this scheme was got done by the Ministry with the help of the IIPA.  The Committee would like to
know the steps proposed to be initiated in the light of the findings of the IIPA report.
 
6.13     To a query about the major constraints faced while implementing the NSS scheme, the Committee was informed
that (i) some States/UTs delay the settlement of account resulting in holding of further releases of grants and (ii) State
Liaison Cells which are fully funded by the Ministry are not functioning in some of the States/UTs.  The Committee is,
no doubt, aware of this perennial problem of undue delay in settlement of accounts by the States in implementing Central
sector schemes.  It is the main bottleneck in implementing them.  It further feels that this problem is projected more often
than not as insurmountable.  Similarly, the problem of State Liaison Cell not functioning in some States and UTs also
falls in the same category.  The Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry should take up these two problems with the
concerned States at the highest level with a view to sort out the same and report the action taken in this regard to the
Committee.
 
VII.     NATIONAL DISCIPLINE SCHEME (NDS)
7.1       The National Discipline Scheme was decentralised in 1972 and the employees under the NDS were transferred
and absorbed in the services of the State Governments on the condition that the Central Government would reimburse
their pay and allowances so long as they remained in the service.   Accordingly, about 6145 NDS Instructors were
transferred / absorbed to/ under  various State Governments. The Committee observes that due to inadequate budgetary
provisions, the Central Government has never been able to bear the liability to full extent which, over the years, has
continued to grow upto about Rs.300 crores.  On a specific query about any assessment about the period till when this

163rdreport of committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/163rdreport.htm[01-07-2016 15:23:35]
provision is required to be made, the Committee was informed that a   reference was made to all concerned State
Governments to furnish requisite information.  However, except Rajasthan, Gujarat, M.P. and Punjab, requisite
documents from other States are awaited.  The case of Rajasthan has also been referred back for revised documents. The
Committee was given to understand that only on receipt of these documents from the State Governments, it will be
possible for the Ministry to make an assessment of the total amount, which is to be paid by the Government of India to
the various State Governments.
 
7.2       The Committee notes that against a non-plan  allocation of Rs. 5 crores in  2004-05, expenditure incurred upto
28
th Feb., 2005 is Rs.3.90 crores.  During 2002-03 and 2003-04, an expenditure of Rs. 3.50 crore and Rs. 3.00 crores has
