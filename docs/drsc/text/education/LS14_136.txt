I           INTRODUCTION 
            The Census of 2001 had indicated the population of India as 1027 million with 531 million 
males and 496 million females.    In the decade 1991-2001, nearly 181 million persons were added to the 
population of India.   Population increased at an annual exponential growth rate of 1.93 percent in the 
decade.  As per the latest Sample Registration System (SRS) estimates, the annual growth rate of 
population is 1.74% (1999).    
The eight States of Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Jharkhand, Uttaranchal, 
Chhatisgarh and Orissa, which currently constitute nearly 44% of the total population of India, are 
projected to comprise 48% of the total population in 2016.   In other words, these States alone will 
contribute an anticipated 55% increase during the period 1996-2016.    Demographic outcome in these 
States will determine the timing and size of population at which India achieves population stabilisation. 
The three most significant factors that can curtail the growth of population are increase in the use of 
contraceptives, improvement in the mother’s health and survival of children.  All the programmes of the 
Department of Family Welfare are accordingly planned and being implemented for pursuing a 
combination of these three issues.  
            During the Tenth Five-Year Plan the main thrust of family welfare programme would be: 
To assess the needs for Reproductive and Child Health (RCH); 
To provide need based, client centered and demand driven RCH care; 
To strengthen the infrastructure for service delivery and bridging the gap in essential infrastructure and 
manpower;

III        RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES 
            The Rural Family Welfare Centres (RFWCs)  were set up at all block level PHCs 
sanctioned upto 1.4.1980 to provide Family Planning and MCH services in rural areas. The 
scheme of Rural Family Welfare Centres (RFWCs) has been merged into the Primary Health 
Care System of the States.   As per information available from States/UTs, all the 1,37,311 Sub-
Centres are functional in the country. Government of India funds all these Centres with effect 
from 1.4.2002.  97,757 Sub-Centres were funded by Department of Family Welfare till March 
2002, and the remaining Sub-Centres i.e. 39,554 Sub-Centres were funded by State Governments. 
The Department was targeting to establish 8669 Sub-Centres during the Tenth Five -Year Plan.  
All these 8669 Sub-Centres (which include 461 for the desert areas of Rajasthan) would be  set up 
during 2003-2004. 
The Department has also informed that a facility survey was conducted in 12 Districts/ Sub-Divisions of 
the States in the year 1999 to assess the availability of health care facilities and its utilization at Primary 
Health Centres (PHCs)/Community Health Centres (CHCs)/First Referral Units (FRUs)/District 
Hospitals and in all, 221 districts were covered under this.  The survey assessed the availability and 
utilization of facilities at these primary health care institutions.  The survey in the remaining 372 
districts covering Primary Health Centres/Sub-Centres including Indian System of Medicine & 
Homeopathy facilities on sample basis started in 2002. The results of these surveys are expected to be 
available shortly.    
While the Committee keenly awaits the outcome of survey referred to above in respect of 
PHCs /CHCs/FRU/ District Hospitals etc., it can assess the plight of these units based on the 
grossly inadequate and poor health care system that prevails in Metros and other big towns of the 
country.    The Committee, however, recommends that steps should continue to be taken to 
strengthen these units to provide maximum health care facilities as also in terms of man power.

V         REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH (RCH) PROGRAMME 
            The Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) programme that is being implemented by the 
Government of India is expected to provide quality services and achieve multiple objectives. There has 
been a positive paradigm shift from the method-mix target based approach to client centered, demand 
driven quality services. The main objective of the Scheme is to strengthen reproductive and child health 
care services in the country with the aim of reducing maternal and infant mortality and creating a greater 
awareness about the rights of the population in health care. Four major initiatives were taken by 
Department of Family Welfare to address critical problems of high infant and maternal mortality and 
relatively low contraceptive prevalence which are: (i) Provision of capital investment in form of non-
recurring grant for construction, repair and refurbishing health infrastructure.; (ii) Provision of financial 
assistance to higher medical and para medical personnel on contract basis; (iii) Supply of drugs, 
vaccines and equipment of delivering RCH including immunization services; (iv) Community based 
initiatives such as cash assistance for providing transport to pregnant women to referral centers, 
community incentives for involving the PRIs, training and provision of training to Trained Birth 
Attendants(TBAs) in conducting safe deliveries, conducting of outreach camps in underserved areas etc; 
and  (v) Computerization and improved data management for Family Welfare. 
The allocation in BE 2002-2003 was Rs. 800.53 crores but in RE it was reduced to Rs. 445.54 crores . 
The allocation in BE 2003-2004 has been increased to Rs. 712.41 crores for this Scheme.

VI        REPRODUCTIVE TRACT INFECTION/SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED INFECTIONS 
Reproductive Tract Infection/Sexually Transmitted Infections are very common among the women in 
the reproductive age (15-44 years).   In order to overcome the problem, RTI/STI clinics were being 
setup in the First Referral Units (FRUs) in a phased manner.   429 RTI /STI clinics in the FRUs have 
been identified by the states for management of RTI/STI cases exclusively. 
Since it is linked to HIV / AIDS, the planning and implementation of services for RTI/STI has been 
done in close collaboration with National AIDs Control Organisation (NACO). 307 Laboratory 
technicians have been appointed in 25 States/UTs for this purpose and based on the proposals  received 
from States funds to the tune of   Rs. 450.14 lakhs have been released to them.   Training arrangements 
in RTI/STI were being set up under the RCH Programme organized through the National Institute of 
Health and Family Welfare.  
            The Committee notes with concern that only 307 Laboratory  Technicians have been 
appointed  for 429 identified clinics.   The Committee  observes that it is  not  sufficient to redress 
the problem.   It  therefore, recommends that the appointment of Technicians for rest of the clinics 
be expedited.

VIII      TRANSPORT 
            Mobility plays a crucial role in effective supervision of and providing outreach to the service 
delivery system. The Department is supplying vehicle to the States and Union Territories as a 
component of the Family Welfare Programme. 
The allocation in BE & RE 2002-2003 was Rs. 101.70 crores.   It is has been reduced to Rs. 49.50 crores 
in BE 2003-2004. 
            The Department has informed that the allocations under “Transport” Scheme has been reduced in 
BE 2003-2004 as provision for replacement of old vehicles was earlier being made in the budget of the 
Department of Family Welfare.  The higher outlay during 2002-2003 was to clear the backlog before 
passing on responsibilities to the States since, as per Cabinet decision, it is now left to the States to take 
a view on the matter of replacement and purchase of vehicles according to their requirement and 
specifications.  During 2002-2003, the expenditure was to the tune of  Rs.101.70 crores. 
            Under the scheme, interest free loan was also  provided to Auxiliary Nurse Mid- wife (ANMs) to 
purchase a moped of her choice and use the same for her official duties. The loan was to be recovered 
from the ANMs in instalments by evolving a revolving fund at the level of State Government.  However, 
the Scheme, taken up during three financial years i.e. from 1999-2000 to 2001-2002, did not make much 
headway and no funds were released thereafter. 
            The Committee observes that the impact of  non-release of funds for providing transport to 
ANMs and others be assessed and the scheme reconsidered in light of this assessment for 
reintroduction with such modifications as are considered necessary.

scheme to strengthen the logistics system for receiving, storing and distributing drugs, vaccines and 
contraceptives for Family Welfare Programme in the States has been initiated. Under the scheme States 
are being asked to draw state specific plans for managing the stores along with distribution network till 
the Sub-centre to ensure availability of right material at the right time. For this purpose organizational 
systems and infrastructure for procurement, storage and distribution is required to be strengthened. On 
the basis of experience of system developed in Tamil Nadu, studies were underway to have in place a 
practical logistic system in the State of Karnataka, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh and Orissa. 
The budget allocation under ‘Logistic Improvement’ in BE 2002-2003 from Rs 9.00 crores was reduced 
drastically at RE stage to Rs. 2.00 crores.   A notional amount of Rs. 0.90 crores has been put in the BE

Hindustan Latex Ltd has undertaken number of studies.  Some of the study reports, which have been 
received were being examined and would be discussed further with the State Governments.  Since, it 
would take time to examine these proposals, provision has been reduced in the last year as well as for 
the year 2003-2004. It is, however, proposed to address the issues related to logistics in a more 
comprehensive manner under the RCH-II Project. 
The Committee expresses its displeasure that the Scheme was finalized and budget allocated 
without making details of the procedure to be followed to run the Scheme. The Department should 
fix a timeframe to study the reports and implement the modified Scheme. Since, the Department is 
proposing a lot for RCH II Project, it should be ensured that all the steps are taken in a manner to 
make the project practically viable to achieve the target.

The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Immunization Status (% coverage) has

            While the Committee appreciates the constraints of budgetary exercise of matching the 
known resources of revenue  with the requirements of outflow of funds, it expresses its concern

country.    Abdication of responsibility by way of non allocation is fraught with disastrous 
consequences.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that Planning Commission should review

programmes and Schemes is ensured so that it does not affect the pace of steps being taken to 
right-size the population of the country. (Para 2)

 While the Committee keenly awaits the outcome of survey referred to above in respect of
