III.            BUDGETARY  ALLOCATION
Recommendation/Observation
3.6       The Committee is of the view that successful implementation of a scheme can be undertaken with the
partnership of both the Central and State Governments/UTs. If the funds are released by the Centre to the States/UTs
and if the same are not utilised/spent by them and utilization certificates are not furnished, the Centre would have
perhaps no option but to withhold further release, resulting in under-utilisation of funds. However, in certain cases, the
reason behind non-utilisation of funds by some States/UTs may be genuine, particularly financial crunch. In that case,
the Committee feels that the Department will have to give a thought to it and find some way out for such States/UTs.
Action Taken
3.6       The suggestion of the Committee has been noted for appropriate action.
IV.            OPERATION  BLACKBOARD
Recommendation/Observation
4.7       The Committee notes that against Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 1955.04 crore under Operation Blackboard,
utilization figures were only Rs. 1745.34 crore which is 89.2 per cent of total allocation. Trend of underutilization of
funds was due to unspent balances lying with various State Governments due to one reason or other. The Committee
hopes that such situations have been taken into account and would not recur in the Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan into which
OB have been subsumed.
Action Taken
4.7       Against Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 1955.04 crores, a sum of Rs. 1751.27 crore (about 90%) has been utilized
by the States/UTs.
            The entire unspent balance with States/UTs is towards procurement of Teaching Learning Equipment (TLE)
which is being monitored regularly.
            While formulating norms of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, the Department has taken into account the shortcomings
noticed in the implementation of OB.
V.            SARVA  SHIKSHA  ABHIYAN
Recommendation/Observation
5.6       The Committee recommends that the Department pursue the matter of deficient funding with the Ministry of
Finance vigorously and suggests that it take recourse to external aid as well and get some funds earmarked from the dis-
investment proceeds apart from ensuring that there was no leakage whatsoever during the implementation of the
Scheme. It also recommends that at least 40 per cent of the contributions to Bharat Shiksha Kosh be earmarked for the
Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan. The Committee also feels distressed with the unplanned launching of the Scheme as it appears
that the Department did not take into account the financial requirements of the Scheme vis-a-vis the availability of
funds.
Action Taken
5.6       The Ministry agrees with the concern of the Committee about financial crunch in implementation of the

VII.            NATIONAL  PROGRAMME  OF  NUTRITIONAL  SUPPORT  TO  PRIMARY  EDUCATION  (MID-
DAY-MEAL  SCHEME)
Recommendation/Observation
7.5       The Committee has been informed that the programme has been successful in attracting children to school in
those areas where a cooked meal programme has been implemented. In fact, the Supreme Court has recently in its order
dated 29 November, 2001 directed that under the Scheme, cooked meals be provided in a time bound manner by those
States/UTs which are not providing the same. But, only 10 States/UTs have fully implemented the Scheme so far in
accordance with the direction contained in the orders of the Court. In 8 States/UTs, the programme is being
implemented partially. The problems is not serving the cooked meal in other States have been the lack of funds to meet
the conversion cost and other peripheral requirements by the concerned State/local Governments. The Committee has
also been informed that the Ministry of Rural Development have agreed to Ministry of HRD's (Department of
Elementary Education and Literacy) proposal to construct kitchen sheds. About 4 lakhs schools in the rural areas were
expected to benefit from this decision.
Action Taken
7.5       As per the available information (as on 1.7.2003) the position o serving of cooked meals by States/UTs is as
under :–
(a)             States/UTs implementing the programme fully—
            Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala, Rajasthan, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, 
Meghalaya, Tripura, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu, Chandigarh and
Pondicherry.
(b)        States implementing the programme partially—
            Goa (1 Block), Madhya Pradesh (Tribal Blocks), Maharashtra (17889 Schools), Orissa (Tribal areas), Punjab (17
Blocks), Uttaranchal (26 Blocks), West Bengal (1100 Schools), Delhi (MCD Schools).
            The position has thus improved substantially. The States/UTs which have not yet launched a cooked meal
programme, are being constantly persuaded to switch to a cooked meal programme.
Recommendation/Observation
7.6       Further, the Committee has been given to understand that for National Programme of Nutritional Support to
Primary Education (Mid-day Meal Scheme) Rs. 1175 crore was allocated during 2002-03. This allocation was meant for
reimbursing the cost of foodgrains supplied by FCI to State/District agencies and BPL rate and admissible transportation

charges for movement of the foodgrains from the nearest FCI's depot to the schools/distribution points. The Department
had proposed for allocation of Rs. 1371 crore at the RE 2002-03 keeping in view the overall requirement. However, due
to the budgetary cut imposed by Ministry of Finance, the RE has been reduced to Rs. 1101.50 crore. This has resulted in

Rs. 1099.03 crore leaving a gap of Rs. 2.47 crore that remained unspent. This gap was from the allocation for North-
East Region for which there was no further proposal. The Department could have utilized about Rs. 400 crores as FCI
bills (other than for NE Region) were ready.
            In furtherance of above submission, the Department shall propose for additional funds during supplementary
demands.
VIII.            MAHILA  SAMAKHYA
Recommendation/Observation
8.7       The Committee is, however, of the view that there has been a continuous reduction at RE stage and this too has
been coupled with under-utilisation of funds in 2000-01, 2001-02, 2002-03 for various reasons like non-filling of vacant
posts in MS society, non-spread to the targeted districts, etc. The Committee recommends that such tendency of under-
utilisation be curved and it suggests that all the constraints in complete utilization of funds be done away with by filling
the vacant posts and granting due honoraria to its office bearers as well as by taking other necessary measures.
Action Taken
8.7       The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. The State Mahila Samakhya Societies have been
requested to fill the vacant post of Mahila Samakhya functionaries at the earliest. The Department had moved an EFC
note for continuation and expansion of the MS programme which was approved by the EFC and has been sent to the
Ministry of Finance for approval.
Recommendation/Observation
8.8       At the same time the Committee appreciates the outcome of the Mahila Samakhya Programme which is seen in
an increase in demand for literacy among women and through the vehicle of education, their recognition and visibility
within the family and the community has increased. The Committee recommends that the programme may be extended
across the nation without any undue delay but with all preparations and monitoring system in place beforehand.
Action Taken
8.8       The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. Efforts are being made to expand coverage under the
Mahila Samakhya Programme.

Recommendation/Observation
2.2            However, gender difference in Elementary Education, urban-rural gap and low literacy among SCs/STs and
among educational minorities still remain a cause of concern for the Committee Effective monitoring and

Action Taken

III.            BUDGETARY  ALLOCATION
Recommendation/Observation
3.3            Alternatively, the Working Group Report may be re-examined to see whether the projections with regard to
fund requirements can be brought to realistic levels. In any case budgetary support has to be provided without
compromising with the goals set forth in regard to Universalisation of Elementary Education for 2003-2004.
3.4       The Committee deprecates the tendency reflected over the years for various schemes under Elementary
Education where under budget projected at BE stage is quite high which is considerably reduced at the RE stage.
However, what is more distressing is that even this reduced allocation is not fully utilized.
Action Taken
3.3 & 3.4  HRM has written to Finance Minister for additional support for SSA for 2003-04.
            In 2002-03, the RE of SSA was reduced at half year stage because of insufficient expenditure at that point of
time due to late receipt of SSA Plans from the States. However, the expenditure picked up subsequently and the reduced
RE was not enough to meet the entire commitments. In 2003-04 it is being ensured that States send the plans well in
time. So far this year, District Elementary Education Plan of 348 Districts have been approved by the Project Approval
Board.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee feels that the Department should pursue the matter more vigorously and should also look out for
alternative sources of funding to fulfil the goals set forth. The Committee is concerned that elementary education plans
of only 348 districts have been approved as yet. The Committee feels that it is very less and more such plans should
have been approved by now. The Department should try to know the reasons behind this slow response and bring it to
the notice of the respective Chief Secretaries and get a reply from them.
            As to the aspect of non-utilisation of funds, the Department is silent. The Committee wants the Department to

IV.            OPERATION  BLACKBOARD
Recommendation/Observation
4.11     The Committee feels that there are many other States placed in situations like NE States which do not have the
requisite funds to carry out the salary liability of teachers appointed under Operation Blackboard. In fact, this has been a
major factor in non-appointment of teachers in many States. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the action plan
for NE States be extended to other States also. This is all the more necessary as many States in India have poor literacy
rate than those in North-Eastern region.
Action Taken
4.11     As regards salary of teachers appointed by States/UTs other then NE States, funds towards payment of salary to
teachers appointed under OB are released only for the plan period in which appointments were made. After that the
liability is borne by the States.
            In pursuant to the decision taken in the first meeting of Education Ministers of NE States held on 17.5.2002, the
Planning Commission have decided that the Central Government would meet the salaries of teachers in NE States under
OB scheme for one more plan period i.e. Tenth Plan period from Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan funds in the NE States only
with the sharing ratio of 75 : 25 between Central and State Government during Tenth Plan period.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee finds that the Department has not responded to the sugestion of the Committee to extend the
action plan for NE region to other most backward States of the country so that the literacy level in these States also
could improve resulting in higher standard of life in long run.
V.            SARVA  SHIKSHA  ABHIYAN
Recommendation/Observation
5.7       The Committee is concerned about the drop-outs and it feels that the goal of universal retention by 2010 should
be given special emphasis. In fact, the Committee believes that universal retention is only possible if the students and
their parents/guardians believe that the education that is being imparted would help the students lead a self-sufficient
life. The Committee would like the Department to do its best to ensure that causes behind the drop-outs are remedied.
Action Taken
5.7       The Ministry will also take all necessary steps to ensure that percentage of drop out is reduced considerably and
to ensure achieving the goal of retention of all children by 2010.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee would like the Department to go into the root of the problem and initiate concrete measures like
introducing "Earn and Learn" scheme to attract as well as retain more to education and also create a mass awareness
programme in favour of education. The Committee is aware that the problem was complex and difficult but it is
certainly not impossible, provided there is a will and commitment for achieving the result.
VI.            TEACHERS'  TRAINING  PROGRAMME
Recommendation/Observation
6.6       The Committee notes with satisfaction the utilisation of expenditure and observes that the cut in the Budget
provision for Elementary Education Bureau should not have been made.
6.7       The Committee is also constrained to observe that the trend of under utilisation of funds has been reflected
during the entire Ninth Plan period. Against total Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 976.53 crore, expenditure reported upto
21.3.2003 is only Rs. 763.06 crore i.e., only 78% of allocation has been utilised. The main reason advanced for funds
not being released was non-submission of requisite information including the approval of the appropriate authority by
some States/UTs. As a result, physical targets fixed under the scheme for Ninth Plan period almost remained on paper.
The Committee finds that against the target for setting up 100 new DIETs, 125 CTEs and 15 IASEs, only 39 DIETs, 9
CTEs and 4 IASEs could be set up. The Committee also notes with concern that against 492 DIETs sanctioned, only

144th Report of Committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/144threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:19:21]
431 were reported to be functional.
Action Taken
6.6 & 6.7  Implementation of the Teacher Education Programme primarily depended on the initiative taken by the States
and UT Governments to utilise the funds in time and develop the infrastructure at the earliest with Central assistance.
The implementation of the programme had been uneven. Though many States had shown better results in
implementation of the scheme, some States legged behind and took long time to utilise the non-recurring funds released
to undertake construction work in institution buildings for various reasons. Further releases of grants were restricted and
funds were not released where the progress had not been satisfactory. This resulted in under utilisation of funds
provided.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee is worried at the tendency of some States and UTs not utilizing funds in time and not being able
to create infrastructure for this programme. The Committee feels that the Department laying the blame on to the States
can hardly be justified. It is of the opinion that the Department should make all out efforts to convince and persuade the
States that they will have to deliver. The Committee is strongly of the opinion that a carrot and stick policy be adopted
to convince/motivate the States and its officials. Further, roping in of local people, NGOs, teacher can be thought of in
case of indifferent response to State Government.
Recommendation/Observation
6.8            However, the Committee notes with satisfaction the flexibility offered to the States as to their specific needs
and priorities in the revised guidelines of the scheme. The Committee further notes that all State Governments are
required to prepare a State Perspective Plan for the entire Plan period for Teacher Education keeping in view the needs
and requirement of the States and grants would be released after appraisal of the plans at the national level. At the same
time, States would be required to put in place basic conditions for success of the scheme such as a proper
recruitment/deployment policy; facilitating linkages of DIETs with schools etc.
6.9       The Committee observes that despite there being a huge infrastructure for teachers' training, it is yet to see a
fraternity of quality teachers dedicated to their profession. It appreciates the measures being taken to strengthen teacher's
training but apprehends that appraisal of State's Perspective Plan at national level, as provided in the revised guidelines
to the programme would take away the flexibility being offered to the States to strengthen their teacher training
activities as per their requirements.
Action Taken
6.8 & 6.9  Keeping in view the progress made in implementation of the scheme by various States, the Working Group
on Tenth Plan has recommended that the States and UTs prepare their own plan according to their need so that projected
target could be achieved during the Tenth Plan period. The central appraisal of the State Plans and guidelines are in
broader terms so that there is uniformity in the implementation of the scheme and timely utilisation of funds can be
effected and infrastructure so created are put to better use for a qualitative improvement in the teacher education
programme. Appraisals are not expected to curtail the flexibility offered to States to plan their own programmes.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee notes the position; but it feels that basic conditions for the success of the scheme like proper
recruitment/deployment policy, facilitating linkages of DIETs with schools, etc. put the entire exercise back to square
one as these very pre-requisites have been found to be the stumbling block to an effective utilization of funds as well as
to an effective teacher's education. this aspect needs to be considered by the Department.

Education may get defeated, if the trend continues. Moreover, the Committee fails to understand that while the number
of children covered under the scheme has come down, the lifting of foodgrains has increased, though marginally.
Further, the Committee notices a decline in expenditure due to reduced overall budgetary allocation, which is bound to
affect the lifting of foodgrains and its distribution, thereby affecting the scheme adversely ultimately.
IX.            DISTRICT  PRIMARY  EDUCATION  PROGRAMME  (DPEP)
Recommendation/Observation
9.8       The Committee is perturbed over the drop out rates and recommends that in order to check such a tendency the
Department should try to involve the parents/guardians of the children in school in the gainful employment under JRY
and other similar schemes. The assurance of employment under different employment schemes to a family member of
those who are in school would be an incentive for the people to send their children to the schools. The Committee is also
surprised that even after three years of the above survey, the Department has not yet come out with the final diagnosis of
the reasons for high dropout rate and its remedial prescriptions.
Action Taken
9.8       The DPEP is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme in which 85% project cost is met by the Government of India and
the concerned State Government shares the remaining 15%. The Government of India share is sourced through external
agencies like EC, DFID, UNICEF and the Government of Netherlands. The Programme objectives of DPEP do not
envisage providing employment to parents/guardians of the children in project schools. However, as a step against
reducing high drop out rate among children, the community is being increasingly involved in providing land for schools,

144th Report of Committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/144threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:19:21]
running of the schools, etc. The contribution made by the community towards school improvement in several States has
been remarkable. In addition to academic activities, non-academic activities have also been planned for
parents/guardians, Gram Panchayats, etc. With the increased community participation in the building and running of
schools, it is expected that the drop out rate would come down in the coming years.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee appreciates the concept of community participation but it would like to emphasise that the idea
of community having a stake in the school improvement may not be enough. Such an idea presupposes that drop-out
among children is the result of non-participation of the local community. Problem of drop-out in fact is a multi-
dimensional one, which therefore, has to tackled by adopting a multi-pronged strategy. The Committee feels that such a
holistic approach to this problem is yet to be chalked out.
Recommendation/Observation
9.9       The Committee also takes note of the fact that about 1,77,000 teachers, including para-teachers/shiksha karmis
are involved in teaching activities. The Committee believes that such teachers do not fit in the pedagogical calculus of a
great education system. The committee would like that these para-teachers/Shiksha karmis should be given necessary
training by Teachers Training Institutes before they make forays into the novel and pristine field of teaching the young
minds.
Action Taken
9.9       Para-teachers are being appointed by the States on a large scale to bring down the adverse pupil-teacher ratio and
to provide teachers in remote inaccessible areas. Training courses for such teachers have to be of longer duration and
more intensive. Under the DPEP Programme, teachers training has improved both quantitatively and qualitatively.
During the year 2002-03, 2 million teachers and 3 million community members were trained.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee has been informed by various experts from the field of education that the concept of the para
teachers was not working well as these teachers do not have any training or experience in teaching as well as child
learning methods. They do not take their work seriously. While the Committee appreciates the idea behind this scheme,
it also has apprehension about the quality of teaching by such underpaid and untrained teachers. The Committee
recommends that serious efforts be made for training these teachers appropriately.
X.            ADULT  EDUCATION
Recommendation/Observation
10.7     The Committee, while appreciating the progress made towards literacy, also expresses its serious concern over
the functioning of various schemes of Adult Education. At the end of Ninth Plan, NLM was expected to achieve around
70% literacy but as per census 2001, literacy rate is 65.38%. The Committee notes with dismay the cut in allocation in
the Ninth Plan outlay in priority schemes of adult education viz., Literacy Campaign and Operation Restoration have
been reduced to Rs. 32.00 crore from 140.00 crore. Even this allocation has been underutilized by 26 per cent. However,
for continuing Education for Neo-literates, there was an increase in the allocation of Rs. 261.57 crore from Rs. 229.39
crore. However, the funds under the scheme remained unutilized to the extent of 11 per cent. The Committee takes these
under-utilizations seriously and feel that these under-utilizations have come in the way of attainment of 70 per cent
literacy by the end of Ninth Plan. The Committee hopes that the Department would make concerted efforts for optimum
utilization of allocated funds under the various components/projects of Adult Education Programme.
Action Taken
10.7     As per the National Sample Survey Organization survey, Organization survey, the literacy rate was 62% at the
beginning of the Ninth Plan. At that time, the projected rate of growth was 1.5% per year. It was accordingly estimated
that at the end of the Ninth Plan, the projected achievement of NLM would be around 70%. However, as per Census
2001, the literacy rate increased to 65.38% as against 52.21% in 1991. During the Tenth Plan, the goal of National
Literacy Mission is to attain a sustainable threshold literacy level of 75% by 2007.
            The funds earmarked under various Schemes of Adult Education could not be fully utilized during the Ninth

144th Report of Committee on HRD
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/144threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:19:21]
Plan due to various reasons, such as non-submission and audited accounts and utilization certificates of the earlier
grants, non-receipt of viable proposals, etc. Efforts are being made to ensure that the allocation made under the various
schemes is optimally utilized so that the goal of NLM could be achieved. To ensure effective utilization of the grants,
periodic review meetings are being held by Secretary (Elementary Education and Literacy) and DG (NLM) with the
States. The Area Officers also visit the States to monitor the implementation of the various programmes. The position
relating to utilization of funds has improved during the last two years. In the year 2001-02, as against the budget
allocation of Rs. 200.00 crores, an expenditure of Rs. 173.66 crores (86.83%) was incurred. Similarly, in 2002-03, as
against the budget allocation of Rs. 233.00 crores, an expenditure of Rs. 216.29 crores (92.84%) was incurred. The
expenditure in 2002-03 would have been higher but for the earmarking of 10% funds for North-East and suitable
additional schemes for North-East were not available in the year. In 2003-04, schemes would be formulated to spend
10% funds for the North-East.
Comments of the Committee
            The Committee notes a gradual improvement in respect of utilization of funds under this scheme. It, however,
feels that increase in the rate of literacy remained so slow as it was 65.38% against the target of 70% during the Ninth
Plan. For Tenth Plan period this target has still been fixed at 75%. The Committee is of the opinion that unless all out
efforts were made to take NLM scheme as a movement, it would be very difficult to achieve the target.
