Observations/Recommendations 
The Committee recommends that the talent identification and selection 
procedure may be made simpler and single window selection procedure may be  
provided after which the selected sportsperson may be sent for training. 
(Para 22) 

Action Taken 
The talent selection Process is as per the international best practices 
considering that the objective is to select best available talent. Therefore, a two -tier 
selection and filtering process has been felt necessary with a High Powered 
Committee (HPC) set as a nodal committee to accord approval. 
 
Observations/Recommendations 
The Committee, therefore, recommends the Ministry to focus attention on  
these States also especially the tribal areas as they are fertile grounds for discovering  
athletes especially in sports like Hockey, Archery, Athletics and Football to ensure  
uniformity across the States in creating Sports Infrastructure. 
(Para 25) 
Action Taken 
Noted for compliance. Also 
(i) Talent selection is the culling of players with the current ability to participate and  
be successful in events taking place in the near future. Talent identification, on the  
other hand, is the prediction of future performance based upon an  evaluation of  
current physical, technical, tactical and psychological qualities. Talent selection is  
simpler, but talent identification is an art. One yields great results to -day; the other  
builds elite athletes and winning teams for the future. 
(ii) Talent identification also takes a long term approach to selection and 
development and focuses on training large numbers of players, instead of pruning  
and selecting a few on the basis of current performance to win in near future. 
(iii) SAI regularly conducts talent identification in the disciplines of Archery, 
Athletics, Football and Hockey. At present, the capacity of SAI Training Centres  
(STCs) for accommodating athletes in these disciplines is 2823 athletes (1443 Boys  
& 1380 Girls). These athletes are provided int ernational level training facilities,  
boarding, lodging, education, sports kits, sports equipment etc. 
Regarding special scheme developed to create Infrastructure at District/State level, it  
was further informed that:- 
(i) Khelo India State Centre of Excellence (KISCE) 
In an endeavour to scale -up State/UT‟s existing centre up to world standard level,  
one existing training centre in each State/UT will be designated as Khelo India State  
Centre of Excellence (KISCE) under the State Level Khelo India Centr e (SLKIC)  
vertical of the Khelo India scheme. The notified KISCE will be provided viability  
gap funding for Sports science back -up, engagement of coaches & support staff,  
sports equipment, etc. As of date, the KISCE‟s in 23  State‟s / UTs have been  
notified. 
ii) Khelo India Centre (KIC) 

1,000 KIC‟s are proposed to be established over a period of 04 years across all  
districts of the country which includes Tribal areas as well . Such KICs will be  
provided One-time initial grant of INR 5.00 lakhs per discipline and recurring 
annual grant of INR 5.00 lakhs for each sport discipline up to a maximum of 03  
sports disciplines (Support will be provided for training in only the 14 Identified  
Sports and football and indigenous games for remuneration to coach/past champion 
athlete, support staff, purchase of sports equipment, sports kit, consumables, 
competition/event participation etc.

Observations/Recommendations 
That the Department should make concerted efforts in first utilizing the 
allocated funds rationally and then getting/mobilizing the adequate resources. It 
should approach private and corporate sector for mobilizing the resources in the  
sports sector. The Department should also make efforts to go in for Public Private  
Partnership mode to create sports infrastructure in the country for broad -basing of  
sports and for achieving excellence in sports at National and Intern ational Levels.  
The Committee also  recommends that necessary  convergence of  Khelo India with  
the MPLADS Scheme should be expedited. 
(para 17) 
Action Taken 
The recommendation of necessary convergence of Khelo India Scheme with  
the MPLADS Scheme will be duly  examined at the time of revising the Scheme, as  
the present scheme is coming to an end by March 2020. 
It has been  further informed that the Khelo  India Scheme is  currently in  
operation till 31.03.2021. Draft EFC Memorandum for New Khelo India Scheme has  
been circulated to various Ministries/Departments vide O.M. dated 25.11.2020, 
seeking their inputs for incorporation in New Khelo India Scheme. Upon receipt of  
comments, the EFC for New Khelo India Scheme will be placed before Ministry of  
Finance for its approval and the new Scheme will be notified thereafter. 
The provision of convergence of MPLADS funds with Khelo India funding  
has been kept intact in the proposed New Khelo India Scheme as well. 
Observations/Recommendations 
That the Public Partnership mode for creating sports infrastructure in the 
country be adopted and focused strategies to rope in public-private sector as has 
been done in the State of Odisha are needed, where the State Government and  
corporate sector have come together to fund ten high performance centres like 
badminton and shooting etc. Also that the Department should motivate the States to  
replicate this appreciable example of Public Partnership in sports sector. 
(para 18) 
Action Taken 
Feasibility of incorporating suitable features of such models will be duly 
examined at the time of revising the Scheme. 
It has been further informed that under the Component of Support to National 
/ Regional / State Sports Academies of Khelo India Scheme, 232 Academies are 
accredited as of now which include 156 Non SAI Academies (54 Govt. & 102 

Private). Funding to the tune of Rs 5 Lakh per annum is provided to these academies  
for each Khelo India Athletes towards sports science, coaching, lodging and 
boarding, equipment support, competition exposure etc. As of Now 1296 Athletes 
are undergoing training in these academies. 
In addition to above recently an initiative has been undertaken to fund 500 
Private Academies through National Sports federations (NSFs).

sports where children are allowed to nurture and develop their talent in sports. Such  
centres may be established in every district of the country.

Observations/Recommendations 
That the sports should be made an integral part of our education system, as it  
will enable the system to catch the talented children at a very young age. It will also  
imbibe discipline in character and generate great leadership qualities among the  
children of the country. 
(Para 32) 
Action Taken 
a) Sports is already an integral part of the Health and Physical Education, which  
is compulsory subject for classes I to X and optional at classes XI and XII. 
b) A communication has been sent to Principal Secretary (Education) / 
Education Secretary of all the State / Union Territories on 20.02.2018  to issue 
necessary instructions to all concerned in their respective State to take appropriate  
action to accord primacy to Sports in the Schools in the concerned State/Uni on 
Territory. 
c) As per the National Curriculum Framework (NCF) - 2005, 'Sports is an 
integral part of Health & Physical Education, which is compulsory subject area from  
class I to X and optional subject at Senior Secondary stage. 
d) The CBSE has introduced a streamlined and well-designed Health and 
Physical Education in their affiliated schools, especially for students for classes IX  
and X with effect from 2018 -19 session. The Programme is compulsory for all  
affiliated schools of CBSE. 
e) NCERT has developed a detailed Guideline to introduce Sports and Physical  
Health as a subject in all school classes up to class X. These guidelines have also  
included the provision of appropriate time in school timetable for this new subject. 
Observations/Recommendations 
The Co mmittee recommends the Department to take more steps to help the  
persons with disabilities to interact with other sportspersons and encourage them to  
exchange ideas on health and fitness. This would act as a motivation to other persons  
with disabilities who are engaged in any sports related activities. It will also help in  
changing the attitude of the society towards persons with disabilities and encourage  
the society to motivate and support such sports persons. 
(Para 36) 
Action Taken 
The revamped Khelo India Scheme notified vide Gazette of India dated 
09.10.2017 inter-alia, aims to encourage and promote sports among the persons with  
disabilities from the grass root level. The revamped Khelo India Scheme notified 
vide Gazette of India dated 09.10.2017 inter-alia, aims to encourage and promote  
sports among the persons with disabilities from the grass root level. The scheme has 

a vertical “promotion of sports among persons with disabilities”, for focus on the  
subject. 
The operational guidelines on promotion of sports among person with 
disabilities had been issued on 01.11.2018 and necessary steps to implement the  
scheme are being taken through the respective organizations dealing with para 
athletes. 
Further, to promote sports among the Paralympic sportspersons, Sports 
Authority of India had established a Para Centre under its SAI Training Centre  
Scheme at SAI Regional Centre, Gandhinagar in the 2016 -2017. Accordingly, it was  
decided to identify talented Para Athletes in Athletics, Swimmi ng & Power Lifting  
and to train them on regular basis for representing the country in the international  
competition. 
The SAI Regional Centre, Gandhinagar has recently been declared as a Centre  
for Excellence for Paralympics and accordingly the sports infra structure available at  
the Regional Centre, Gandhinagar has been upgraded and made friendly for regular  
training of Para Athletes.

Observations/Recommendations 
That the Department should consider to appoint an eminent sportsperson or  
sports administrator as the Chief Executive Officer in the General Council of the 
Khelo India Scheme to oversee the implementation of the scheme as he/she would 
be more sensitive to the problems and needs of the budding athletes and also act as 
an interface between the needs and aspiration of the athletes and the administrative  
system for bringing best results. 
(para 12) 
Action Taken 
The General Council of the Khelo India Scheme is a policy making body  
which will advise the mission regarding implementation of the scheme. Since the  
General Council is a committee which may hold meetings 2 or 3 times in a year,  
there may not be any requirement of full time Chief Executive Officer. The 
implementation of the scheme is being done by the Ministry and Sports Authority of  
India and General Council is not directly involved in direct impl ementation of the  
scheme. 
It was further informed that the first  meeting of General Council of Khelo  
India Scheme was held on 04.08.2020 and minutes of the meeting are at Annexure- 
A. 
Further comments of the Committee 
The Committee is not convinced by the reply of the department. The  
Committee would like to point out that Khelo India Scheme is an ambitious  
scheme of the government and the General Council is the policy making body  
for the implementation of this programme. The Committee also feels that the  
General Council should meet at least 2 to 3 times in a month to oversee the  
implementation of the scheme. The Committee further reiterates that an 
eminent sportsperson or sports administrator be appointed as the Chief 
Executive Officer in the General Council  of the Khelo India Scheme as he/she  
would be more sensitive to the problems and needs of the budding athletes and  
also act as an interface between the needs and aspiration of the athletes and the  
administrative system for bringing best results.

2. Due to non-finalization of the cadre restructuring in SAI and to meet the urgent

3. All most maximum vacant post of coaches falling under direct recruitment

4. Olympians and Para -Olympian who represented the country are given direct

It has been further informed that there was a ban on promotion and regular 
appointment in SAI from 1996. Subsequently SIU report followed by ERC report  
reduced the number  of sanctioned post in  SAI at various cadre.  As a result,  no 
regular recruitment could take place till 2010 since the additional posts were 
adjusted against the vacancies and the promotions were carried out with the 
condition of  restricting the strength  specified by the ERC.  Due to  the delay  in 
promotion, the coaches have not completed the required qualifying service for 
promotion to higher grade resulting in vacancy in the higher grade. However, 
promotion is already carried out by giving relaxation for qualifying service within 
the RRs wherever possible. In view of the large number of vacancies due to reason  
explained above, it has been decided to fill all vacant posts through contract by  
amending the RRs suitably to ensure adequate availa bility of coaches. Accordingly a  
proposal is being moved. Further, in order to fill the vacancy especially at the higher  
level, it is already decided to engage outstanding sportsperson / coaches working in  
other organization on deputation. It is expected t hat, the filling up of vacancies will  
be completed in six to nine months. 
In addition, Coaches are also being hired on Contract under Khelo India 
Scheme. 

Further comments of the Committee 
The Committee is not happy with the reply of the Department and would  
like to point out that out of 1524 sanctioned posts, 428 posts are still vacant. The  
Committee would like to re -emphasize that for achieving sporting excellence at  
international level, adequate facilities and focused training is necessary. The 
role of coaches in training the athletes for these events becomes very 
paramount. The Committee further points out that there are a large number of  
contract coaches in assistant coach grade. The Committee feels that the 
Department should pro vide more opportunities to these contract coaches to  
enhance their skills and make them permanent coaches against vacant posts.  
The Committee further recommends that the Department should fill the vacant  
posts of coaches so that the training of the athletes does not get affected. 
Observations/Recommendations 
That the Government should also identify new places for training in already  
existing schools, colleges and academies which have infrastructure of sports and  
hostels. These institutions should be affiliated by SAI under Khelo India Scheme 
and be provided with Coaches so that these identified sports persons can be trained  
all over India as training has far reaching impact. 
(Para 42) 
Action Taken 
The Expression of interest is open to any Institution (Educational/Sports), 
Academy or any private establishment for applying for accreditation as Khelo India  
Academy. The accredited academy will be funded as per expenditure norms. 
It was further informed that in Khelo India scheme under the vertical “Support  
to National / Regional / State Sports Academies” private sporting academies are  
accredited upon receiving expression of interest from the private academies after  
following the due process. 
Regarding details of the EoI received and action taken on them, it  was further 
informed that accreditation of academies is an ongoing process and open to all  
(Govt/Pvt) Sports academies. As on date, a total of 171 EOI applications received  
from various academies for accreditation, out of which 103 academies have been  
accredited after a due process of accreditation. 
Further comments of the Committee 
The Committee notes the steps taken by the Department to make schools,  
colleges and academies having infrastructure of sports and hostels apply for  
accreditation as Khelo  India Academy. The Committee would however, like to  
point out that under this Scheme the Department should have a robust 
monitoring mechanism for funding such academies and adequate provisions  
should be made to make them accountable financially.

also act as an interface between the needs and aspiration of the athletes and the  
administrative system for bringing best results. 
(para 12) 
The Committee is not happy with the reply of the Department and would like to  
point out that out of 1524 sanctioned posts, 428 posts are still vacant. The  
Committee would like to re -emphasize that for achieving sporting excellence at  
international level, adequate facilities and focused training is necessary. The 
role of coaches in training the athletes for these events becomes very 
paramount. The Committee further points out that there are a large number of  
contract coaches in assistant coach grade. The Committee feels that the 
Department should provide more opportunities to these contract coaches  to 
enhance their skills and make them permanent coaches against vacant posts.  
The Committee further recommends that the Department should fill the vacant  
posts of coaches so that the training of the athletes does not get affected. 
(para 40) 
The Committee notes the steps taken by the Department to make schools, 
colleges and academies having infrastructure of sports and hostels apply for  
accreditation as Khelo  India Academy. The Committee would however, like to  
point out that under this Scheme the Department should have a robust 
monitoring mechanism for funding such academies and adequate provisions  
should be made to make them accountable financially. 
(para 42)

At the outset, the Chairman, welcomed the members of the Committee and informed them 
that the meeting had been called to consider and adopt the following draft reports: 
(i) No. 317 on the subject "Preparation for Olympics - 2021" 
(ii) No. 318 reg ATR on 311th Report on Khelo India Scheme; 
(iii) No. 319 reg ATR on 315th Report on Demands for Grants 2020-21 (Demand No. 101) of 
the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports. 

2. Thereafter, the convener of the Sub Committee made a power -point presentation to the  
Committee on the draft 317th Report on Preparations for Olympics-2021and informed that they had 
made 48 recommendations under 11 headings for short, medium and long term action plan to lay a  
road map for increasing India's medal tally in the coming Olympics. The Committee then 
discussed the draft report and adopted it. 
 
 
3. The Committee then took up for discussion the draft 318th & 319th Reports and adopted  
them after some discussion. 
4. The Committee keeping in view the fact that only  a few months were remaining for the  
Olympics authorized the Chairman, to present the Reports to the Hon'ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha 
in the inter -session period so that the Ministry can take action on the recommendations of the  
Committee. Concluding the meeting, the Chairman sought suggestions on  the subjects to be taken  
up for consideration and asked them to send them by e-mail. 
 
5. The meeting then adjourned at 12.20 P.M.
