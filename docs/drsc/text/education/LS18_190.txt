RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY GOVERNMENT
 
II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
Recommendations/Observations
 
2.4    The Committee can afford to conclude that even the formulation of a quarterly annual action plan for 2005-2006 by the
Ministry has not been able to achieve the desired results. It’s the case of failure in planning. It appears to the Committee that the
Ministry remained engaged in projecting higher requirements and launching new schemes without any considerations of the
judicious and optimum utilization of funds allocated for the existing schemes. It is quite contradictory that on one hand the
Ministry talk of resource crunch and on other hand the allocated resources are not fully utilized. Huge unspent balances are lying
with the Ministry under various schemes. This fiscal disorder in the Ministry has done more harm. The case for higher allocations
has weakened to a great extent. The Committee is of the view that a detailed review of all the ongoing schemes should be
undertaken so as to assess the shortcomings being noticed and take remedial steps for removal of the same. Active involvement of
the State Governments and other implementing agencies in this exercise is called for. The Committee would like the Ministry to
adopt better fiscal management practices to avoid the last minute spending for achieving the targets.
 
Action Taken
 
The advice of the Committee is accepted.

XIV.
        SCHEME FOR DOPE TEST
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
14.1     The scheme aims at prevention of drug abuse in sports by providing technical facility for Dope Test. Under the scheme, it
has been decided to set up two independent autonomous agencies namely National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) and National
Dope Control Laboratory (NDCL) and provision has, accordingly, been made keeping in view their technical and administrative
requirements. SAI has developed the Sports Science Centre at Patiala, Bangalore, Calcutta, Delhi and Gandhinagar. These Centres
mostly provide scientific support to the national campers, SAI Training Centre inmates and are also involved in research and
teaching.
 
14.2 The Committee was informed that there was some delay in procurement of equipments for Dope tests since the scheme was
under revision. The financial allocation under the scheme was also reduced due to postponement of accreditation process by
WADA and consequent less purchase of equipments. An allocation of Rs.5.00 crore has been made for 2006-07 against an
allocation of BE Rs.10.20 crore and RE Rs. 3.70 crore during 2005-06.    
 
14.3
          The Committee is constrained to note that at present, SAI has only 38 Sports Scientists including medical doctors. This
strength of scientific personnel is not enough to provide optimum scientific support to the sports needs in the country. Sports-
related scientific research and development in India has not been able to keep pace with international developments in various
fields like Physiology, Psychology, Bio-mechanics etc. Greater attention is required to be given to research and development of
sports science, in case India has to excel in sports at international level.  
 
14.4
          The Committee is happy to note that in view of the Commonwealth Games, 2010 the anti-doping programme has been put
on fast track. A number of important steps to check the increasing incidences of doping are proposed to be taken by the
Government which include:-
 
(i)
          According high priority to education of athletes;

14.5
          The Committee is aware of the recent doping incidents during international sports events. It is understood that out of 11
athletes 9 were found to be positive. This has become a recurring feature during the international championships and during every
international event the country’s prestige is thus put at stake. Perhaps our sports administration has failed to make the players
aware of the dire consequences if they are tested positive. These performance enhancing drugs have an adverse effect on their
health and could even ruin them professionally. With the advent of technology, dope testing methods have also faced new
challenges. Undoubtedly it is a constant battle with technology. Test methods have become more and more sophisticated. To
generate awareness amongst the players, education is the best cure. The Committee feels that though the Ministry claims that
overall the doping instances have decreased from 4% to 2 ½% but the recent instances put a shadow on such claims. The
Committee hopes that the Ministry would endeavour to achieve all the international standards of dope testing towards fighting the
malice of doping which degrades the prestige of the nation
 
Action Taken (14.1 to 14.5)
Doping
As far as Doping is concerned, it is submitted that the pressures of modern day International Competitions and the high
stakes of winning medals appears to be contributing to the menace of doping.  The methods of doping and masking the effects of
doping are getting more sophisticated.  It is a challenge to keep the methods of detecting in line with this trend.  It is also
important that individual athletes and National Sports Federations also cooperate in the education and extension efforts so that the
long term harmful effects of doping and the consequences of detection are widely appreciated.  As observed by the Committee,
several measures to tackle the problem, by way of establishment of the National Anti Doping Agency (NADA) and the National
Dope Control Laboratory (NDCL), are under way.  NADA would inter-alia, accord high priority to education of athletes, to
support personal and national federations, improve and systematize sample collection procedures, put in place a comprehensive
result management programme, development of registered testing pool and test distribution plan for each important discipline.  As
regards the NDCL most of the required equipments have been purchased, and systematic efforts are being made to ensure
permanent accreditation from WADA in a time bound manner, well before the Commonwealth Games, 2010.
 
Scientific and Technical Support
 
It is true that the strength of Sports Scientists in SAI is not enough to cope with the scientific support requirements of
sports in the country.  Proposals for creation of additional posts in the scientific cadre and also for lifting the ban on direct
recruitment are under consideration of the Ministry.  However, till the ban on direct recruitment is lifted, or new posts are created,
10 posts of Junior Scientific Assistants (against the vacant post of JSO) on contract basis for 1 year were advertised.  In response,
24 applications were received.  However after interview, only 3 candidates 1 each in Physiology, Biochemistry and
Anthropomerty were found suitable & selected for appointment and the posts are being re-advertised.  In order to promote
Research & Development in Sports Sciences the SAI has a scheme of Sports Sciences Research Fellowship wherein Research
Fellows in different Sports Science disciplines are selected for a period of one year extendable for one year and paid a
contingency grant of Rs.20,000/- per annum and stipend of Rs.6,000/- per month to conduct Research & Development in Sports
Science.  The whole subject of  upgradation and strengthening of Scientific support and Technical backup systems is being
discussed and considered in detail in the context of the ongoing exercises for formulation of the XIth Plan.
 
XVI.         SCHEME RELATING TO HIV/AIDS  
Recommendations/Observations
 
16.1     The Committee notes that a new scheme relating to HIV/AIDS has been introduced and is under formulation. The scheme
is aimed at controlling the spread of HIV/AIDS in India. An Action Plan has been made in a comprehensive way for 2006-2011
and one year for implementation during 2006-07. The total budget projected for one year (2006-07) is Rs.82.34 crore in which the
Ministry will dovetail its own programme fund of Rs.55.50 crore relating to NSS, NYKS and SAI. The budgetary provision has
been kept at Rs.10.00 crore, which will be utilized after its in-principle approval by the Planning Commission and the balance

190th report of human resource development
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/190threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:35:20]
Rs.16.84 crore will be outsourced from other development partners and international organizations.
 
16.2     The Action Plan aims to mainstream HIV/AIDS in the existing activities and programmes of the MYAS. It has been
suggested that the proposed activity of generating awareness among the youth of the country would be best implemented under
the Ministry’s umbrella. The Action Plan is to be implemented through the Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), the National
Service Scheme (NSS), Rajiv Gandhi National Institute for Youth Development (RGNIYD) and voluntary agencies such as
Bharat Scouts and Guides (BSG) and National Cadet Corps (NCC), Indian Red Cross and Sports Councils at State level. The
objective of the proposed new scheme is that every adolescent and youth has access to information, education, counseling and
services to protect themselves against the deadly disease and to help others to do so.
 
16.3
          The Committee is happy to note the introduction of the scheme aimed at achieving victory over AIDS in India. The
Committee feels that proper strategy should be developed to achieve the objectives of the scheme. Adolescent/youth clubs and
youth information centres require proper back-up of counseling and resource materials. The Committee underlines the need for
establishment of linkages amongst all the field organizations with health facilities and VCTCs for service access. The Committee
would like the Ministry to pursue the Planning Commission to seek in-principle approval in respect of the scheme. 
 
Action Taken
The new scheme of HIV/AIDS is in the process of being finalized.  The suggestions/recommendations made by the
Committee shall be given due consideration.  Action is being taken to obtain approval of the Competent Authority for the Scheme.

3.1   Report of the National Commission for Youth was laid on the Table of both the Houses of Parliament on 23.12.2004.
Fourteen recommendations made by the Commission have been accepted by the Ministry in principle which include:-
Setting up of Rashtriya Yuva Kosh on the pattern of the Rashtriya Mahila Kosh;
 
Expansion of National Service Schemes in all universities and colleges and scheme to be introduced at class IX level and
amalgamation of NSS & NCC.
 
 Comments of the Ministry of Defence to be asked for and to make serving either NSS or NCC mandatory for every student
to become eligible for award of degree.
 
3.2
  The Committee finds the recommendations made by the Commission very useful in empowering the youth. Unfortunately not
much progress has been made in the implementation of these recommendations. No follow-up action appears to have been taken
after the meeting with the officials of the Rashtriya Mahila Kosh on the feasibility of formulation of Rashtriya Yuva Kosh.
Similarly the proposal with regard to amalgamation of NSS and NCC sent to the Planning Commission simply remained a
referral. The Committee would like the Ministry to expedite the action on all recommendations particularly with regard to setting
up of Rashtriya Yuva Kosh. The Committee feels that either the NCC or NSS should be made compulsory from class IXth. 
Action Taken (3.1 &3.2)
Regarding setting up of Rashtriya Yuva Kosh, it may be mentioned that  issues concerning bylaws, corpus fund and the
procedure of releasing grants and their recovery were discussed in detail with officials of Rashtriya Mahila Kosh.  Since a large
number of issues are involved, it was decided to have further consultation with the Planning Commission, Department of Revenue
and other Ministries concerned before finalizing the proposal for consideration of Ministry of Finance.  It was also suggested to
explore other possible mechanism of funding, since at least Rs.500 crore would be required towards the seed money for the
proposed National Youth Bank. Unfortunately it has not been possible to conclude this exercise.  The Ministry is currently
engaged in the formulation of 11
th Five Year Plan, and the issue of setting up a  Rashtriya Yuva Kosh will also be considered as a
part of this.
 
As regards the expansion of NSS and the Committee’s concern that either NCC or the NSS should be made compulsory
from Class IX, it is mentioned that a proposal for expansion of National Service in all Universities, Colleges and schools, as well
as to enroll students of class IX and X as NSS volunteers was sent to the Planning Commission for seeking their ‘in principle’
approval.  On their advice, the views of Ministry of Human Resource Development were obtained in the matter, who had advised
the constitution of a Committee comprising representatives of the Ministry of HRD, CBSE, NCERT, NCTE, to work out an
Action Plan for expansion/strengthening the NSS.
 
            The committee was constituted and its recommendations have since been communicated to the agencies concerned for
appropriate follow up action.  Based on this, Secretary, Ministry of Education has written to all the Chief Secretaries of the States
in November, 2006 to consider introduction of NSS in all schools under the jurisdiction of the State Government.  Further, the
States have also been requested to consider that NSS may be made part of the curriculum in the school system wherein certain

VII.         YOUTH HOSTELS 
Recommendations/Observations
7.1       Youth Hostels are built to promote youth travel and to enable the youth to experience the rich cultural heritage of our
country. The scheme of Youth Hostels has been conceived as a joint venture of the Central and State Governments. While the
Central Government bears the cost of construction, the State Governments provide developed plot of land equipped with water,
electricity and approach roads. After completion, Youth Hostels are handed over to the State Governments for management.
7.2       The Committee notes that so far 72 Youth Hostels (as on 31.12.2005) have been constructed in different States. However,
Committee’s attention has been drawn to the trend of expenditure on Youth Hostels during the Tenth Plan as per details indicated
below: -

         14.93               9.65

The Committee notes that the allocations have not been utilized optimally. Main areas of concern have been the
inordinate delay in construction process of Youth Hostels. The status of 18 Youth Hostels under construction indicates shocking
revelations. There are Youth Hostels which were approved long back in 1986 and even installments of central funds for
construction were released. For instance an installment of Rs.15.00 lakh was released in March 1989 in respect of a Youth Hostel
in Himachal Pradesh and the State Government has not even handed over the developed plot of land for construction of the
project. Construction of Youth Hostel in Allahabad was approved in principle in April 1988 and State Government identified the
land in 1998. First installment of central funds amounting to Rs.20.00 lakh was released in March 1990 and the construction is yet
to be completed. Position with regard to the remaining Youth Hostels is more or less the same. The Committee notes that even
pursuing the issue with the concerned State Chief Secretaries has not brought any noticeable impact. The Committee observes that
with the passage of time, estimates for construction have kept on increasing steadily, leading to criminal wastage of Government
funds.

to be taken in respect of some places which ultimately will have its impact in other places also. Even the extreme step for recovery

has now decided to give priority to the on-going projects, and only thereafter will other approved projects be taken up.  No further
proposals are being approved or considered at present.

6.1       The National Discipline Scheme was originally started in 1954 by the Ministry of Rehabilitation, which was later
transferred to Ministry of Education and Social Welfare in 1958. Consequent upon the recommendations of Dr. Kunzru
Committee in 1963, introducing discipline programme at school stage, the integrated scheme called “National Fitness Corps”
(NFC) was evolved. The Scheme was transferred to the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports (now Ministry of Youth Affairs
& Sports) in eighties when a separate Department was formed. 
6.2       On the basis of the recommendations of Dr. Kunzru Committee, the decision to transfer NDS Instructors to various State
Governments was taken in consultation with the State Education Secretaries. In the year 1969, the Central Cabinet took a decision
that the employees under the NFC would be transferred to the services of the State Governments on the condition that the Central
Government would reimburse their pay and allowances so long as they remained on the strength of the State Governments. The
Committee was informed that 6145 NDS Instructors were transferred to various State Governments after decentralization of NDS
scheme.  
6.3       Though physically the NDS Instructors were absorbed in the services of the State Governments, the Central Government
was committed to bear the liability of their pay and allowances and this system has been continuing since seventies. Due to
inadequate budgetary provisions, the Central Government has never been able to bear its liability to the fullest extent and thereby
the accumulated liability continued to grow to unmanageable level. The Committee was informed that the total liability towards
pay and allowances for States of M.P., Punjab, Haryana, H.P., Rajasthan, Kerala and Maharashtra from which information has
been received is to the tune of Rs.184.21 crore. Information from remaining ten States is yet to be received. The total projected
liability in respect of all the States is to the tune of Rs.300.00 crore. A budget support would be required on that account. 
6.4
                   The Committee regrets that repeated reminders from the Department to some States have not evinced any response and
the issue of committed liabilities remains unresolved for years together. The Committee feels that this situation of uncertainty
should come to a halt. A time has come when the Ministry should fix a final deadline for such States to avoid this never-ending
chaotic scenario. The Ministry should settle the issue once for all after paying the liabilities to those States who have furnished the
information. The States who do not respond may have to forgo their claims. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would plan
the whole process to ensure settlement of committed liabilities within this financial year itself. The Committee would also like the
Ministry to appeal to the States to avail this opportunity and expedite their claims without further loss of time.
Action Taken (6.1 to 6.4)
            While proposals have been received from 11 States despite reminders detailsed information indicating the total liability in
the prescribed proforma has not been received from many other States. Due to non-receipt of complete information/documents
from all the concerned 17 State Governments, the committed liability towards pay & allowances of NDS Instructors could not be
assessed and as a result it has not been possible to make the requisite budgetary provisions. They have again been reminded. It is
also proposed to call a meeting shortly of officials from States who have not responded to expedite the required information.
While these delays on the part of some State Governments has resulted in its not having been possible to make the required budget
provision, every effort will be made to expedite releases to States who have complied with the prescribed procedures.   
Comments of the Committee (6.1 to 6.4)
The Committee has been observing on the status of the National Discipline Scheme in its earlier Reports.  The Committee
is, however, constrained to observe that no progress seems to have been made.  The Committee can only hope that the proposed
meeting with the officials of concerned State Governments must have been held by now and a final decision taken in the matter.
