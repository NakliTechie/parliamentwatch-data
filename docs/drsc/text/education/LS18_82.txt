REPORT
INTRODUCTION
1.1. Education is the key to all processes of development, especially human development. Having regard to
the size of the population, diversity and economic frailties prevailing in the country, the challenges in the
path of providing Education for All are too many and enormous. Education being in the Concurrent List,
its meaningful development implies an effective partnership between the Union Govt. and the states. While
the role and responsibility of states to provide education continues to remain essentially unchanged, the
Union Department of Education has assumed a larger responsibility to reinforce the national and integrated
character of education, to look after the international aspects of Education and in general, to promote
excellence in all sectors of education throughout the country.
The Ninth Plan treats education as the most crucial investment in human development. Approach Paper to
the Ninth Plan indicates that "apart from carrying out the directions given by the National Policy on
Education, 1986 and NPA, 1992, making the nation fully literate by the year 2005 will be a committed
goal. Priorities, therefore, include universalisation of elementary education, raising the quality of education
at all levels, upliftment of the educational status of disadvantaged groups like SCs/STs/girls/disabled,
equality and removal of regional imbalances, vocationalisation of education and setting up of centres of
excellence".
1.3. The Secretary, Department of Education informed the Committee that an allocation of Rs. 20,381
crores has been made in the 9th Plan in the Central Sector as against an outlay of Rs.7,443 crores in the 8th

Plan, which represents an increase of more than two and a half times : 66.1% of the total allocation has
been earmarked for Elementary Education during the 9th Plan as compared to 38.7 % in the 8th Plan .
Attention of the Committee was, however, drawn to the fact that total allocation for Education both in the
Central and State Sector if taken together is less than 4% of GDP. Suitable budgetary provision in the 9th
Plan period is required if it is to be raised to the tune of 6% of GDP by 2000 AD. The Secretary explained
that the Approach Paper to the 9
th Plan had indicated that GDP at market prices would grow from
Rs.12,40,000 crore in 1996-97 to Rs. 17,50,000 crore in 20001-2002. Six per cent of that would be
Rs.1,05,000 crore in 2001-2002. The current year’s allocation should thus have been about Rs. 15,500
crore . The Committee is of the view that greater budgetary support is needed if 6% of GDP for
Education is to be made a reality.
II. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1. The budgetary allocation for 1999-2000 is Rs. 7936.74 crores of which Rs. 4699.04 crore is on the
Plan side and Rs. 3237.70 crore is on the Non-Plan side. Compared to last year’s Plan allocation of
Rs.4244.18 crores at BE which was reduced to Rs. 4044.18 crore at RE stage due to economic
considerations and also under-utilisation in some of the schemes, the plan allocation for 1999-2000
Rs.4699.04 crore shows some increase. The Secretary explained that allocation for 1999-2000 although at
an enhanced level still falls short of the requirements.
2.2. The Committee, however, observes that against a total allocation of BE Rs.4244.18 crore in
1998-99 reduced to Rs. 4044.18 crore at RE stage, expenditure upto 26.2.99 shows an amount of only
Rs.2650.29 crore. There are little chances of full utilisation of funds at this stage. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that Department should take all appropriate steps for maximum utilisation
of allocation made for 1999-2000. The Committee would like to be informed about the States where
funds could not be utilised fully. The Committee apprehends that in some of the States, there may be
instances where funds allocated by the centre for a particular scheme are being diverted for other
purposes. If that be so the Committee recommends that the Department should take up this matter
with the concerned States.
III. Literacy
3.1. The Secretary informed the Committee that as a result of concerted efforts made in the area of primary
education, non-formal education and adult education, there has been some increase in the literacy rate
prevailing in the country. Results of the 53
rd round of the National Sample Survey Organisation (NSSO)
indicate that against the literacy rate of 52.2% in 1991, this rate stands at 62% at the end of December,
1997 and has been projected to touch 64% at the end of 1998.
3.2. Male literacy rates have risen from 64.1% in 1991 to 73% in 1997 and the female literacy rates in the
corresponding period have risen from 39.3% to 50% . Thus, the gender gap in literacy rates is being
narrowed in overall terms. Another encouraging trend is acceleration of literacy rates in rural areas. While
in 1991, the urban literacy rate was 73.1% and the rural literacy rate was 44.7%, in 1997, as per the NSSO
Survey, urban literacy rate was 80% and the rural literacy rate was 56%.
3.3. In terms of performance in literacy, Mizoram leads the country with a literacy rate of 95%. Kerala
now occupies the second place with a literacy rate of 93%. Almost all the educationally backward states
have also shown reasonable improvement.

IV Elementary Education
4.1. Universal access, universal retention and universal achievements are the broad parameters to achieve
UEE. Meticulously formulated strategies have been adopted in the form of Operation Blackboard (OB),
Non-Formal Education (NFE), Teachers’ Education, District Primary Education Programme (DPEP) to
address the specific problem areas, gender and regional disparities. Out of the total budget of Rs. 7936.72
crore for the Department for 1999-2000, Rs. 3034.95 crore have been allocated for Elementary Education
alone. This is a considerable enhancement when compared to allocation of Rs.2780.03 crore for
Elementary Education in 1998-99.
4.2. The number of teachers which was 7.51 lakhs in gross terms in 1950-51 has risen to 47.57 lakhs in
1996-97, showing a substantial increase in the number of female teachers from 1.15 lakh to 16.52 lakhs.
However, the Committee views with serious concern that despite the increase in infrastructural facilities
and the concomitant increase in the number of teachers, the teacher-pupil ratio (TPR) has increased from
1:24 to 1:42 at the primary stage and 1:20 to 1:37 at the upper primary level. The Committee is in
agreement with Department’s observation that increase in TPR indicates that demand for
elementary education has increased and justifies the need for opening more educational institutions
to meet the demand of the school going population . However, simultaneous efforts are also required
to be made for creation of required number of posts of teachers specially in the Rural areas.
3. Drop-Out Rates
4.3.1. The progress in universalisation of elementary education during 8th Plan has not been
insignificant. Declining trend in drop-out rates is one of the parameters in this direction. The
decline is from 42% in 1991-92 (First year of 8th Plan) to 39% in 1996-97 (last year of 8th
Plan), showing an overall decrease of 3% at the primary level. At the upper primary stage, the
drop out rate was 58.7% in 1991-92 and it declined to 52.3% in 1996-97, showing an overall
decrease of 6.4%. The 9th Plan envisaged UEE to mean universalising access, retention and
achievement.
4.3.2. Target set out for Universal retention for 9th Plan is reduction of drop out rates between
classes I to V and classes I to VIII from the existing 36.3 % and 56.5% to 20% and 40%
respectively. The Committee however, notes that figures for drop-out rates on completion of
1997-98 (1st year of 9th Plan) stand at 39.58% and 54.14% for primary and upper primary
level respectively. Very vigorous efforts are required if targets set out for 9th Plan for control
of drop out rates are to be achieved. The Committee also observes that there has been no
marked improvement in respect of educationally backward states. Drop-out rates in classes I
to V for the year 1997-98 in respect of Bihar are as high as 59.65% Meghalaya has the
highest figures of drop-out rates 62.44% in classes I to V in the country. Situation is more
grim in classes I to VIII. Bihar has the highest drop-out rate of 77.11% with girls having a
dropout rate of 80.48%.
4.3.3. The Committee notes that for increasing the retention rate at primary and upper
primary level there are a number of interventions through a variety of schemes in which
active participation of both state govts. and NGOs is evident with funding provided by

REPORT

the Govt. and also foreign agencies. However, drop-out rates have not shown an
appreciable declining trend. The Committee is of the firm view that concerted efforts in
a mission mode by all the implementing agencies are required to be made without any
delay, failing which targets set out for 9th Plan would remain on paper only. The
Department has to take an initiative in the right direction by targeting the weak areas in
different states and taking necessary remedial steps.
4. Operation Blackboard
4.4.1. The Department informed that under ‘Operation Blackboard’, 5.23 lakh primary schools and 0.76
lakh upper primary schools have been provided funds for development of academic infrastructure
(teaching learning material). On a specific query as to the exact number of teachers appointed against the
sanctioned posts, the Committee was informed that against 55,951 posts sanctioned for 3
rd teacher in
primary schools, 47,081 have been appointed so far. Against 45,089 posts of additional teachers sanctioned
in upper primary schools, 32,662 teachers have been appointed. Further, against the target of 47,000 posts
during 1998-99, only 24,369 posts have been sanctioned in primary/upper primary schools to date.
4.4.2. The Secretary informed the Committee that reluctance on the part of most of the states to create
additional posts of teachers was primarily responsible for large amount of funds either remaining
unutilised or not being released. The Committee views with serious concern utilisation of only Rs. 80.26
crores upto 26.2.99 against an allocation of Rs. 304 crores in 1998-99. The Committee was given to
understand that states were generally apprehensive about sharing extra burden under non plan due to
creation of additional posts once the plan period was over. There was a tendency on the part of State
Govts. to redeploy teachers against plan posts. The Committee feels that it is an undesirable activity. The
Committee is of the opinion that the State Govts. have a primary role to play for the successful
implementation of `Operation Blackboard’. They cannot shirk away from their responsibility.
4.4.3. Difficulties were also there due to procedural delays in centralised system of procurement of
teaching-learning material. As a result, approximately Rs. 365 crores were lying as unspent balance with
the state govts. Procurement level was as low as 38.63% in the case of Nagaland, 45% in respect of
Gujarat and 55.32% in MP. The Committee feels that there is a need for decentralisation of the system of
procurement of teaching learning material. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department
should take appropriate action in this regard.
4.4.4. While appreciating the fact that funds are now being released only on the basis of creation of
posts, the Committee strongly feels that there is a need for chalking out a well-planned strategy in
active coordination with state govts. so as to assess the actual requirement of teachers before
sanction of posts under ‘Operation Blackboard’. In view of the enhanced allocation of Rs.400 crores
for ‘Operation Blackboard’ in 1999-2000, the Committee is of the view that a meeting of all the State
Education Ministers with the representatives of the Central Govt. would be an ideal venue where
issues like non-utilisation of funds, difficulties being faced in implementing programmes etc. can be
discussed at length with possible remedies for the problem.

4.6 Externally-aided Projects
4.6.1. District Primary Education Programme (DPEP), an externally aided programme aims at
operationalising the strategies for achieving UPE/UEE through district specific planning and disaggregated
target setting in 1ow female literacy districts and builds on successful TLC campaigns. The Department
informed that the Programme is under implementation in 163 low female literacy districts in 14 states.
Expansion of the project to another 30-35 districts in UP, 10 districts in Rajasthan, 8 districts in Orissa and
5 districts in West Bengal is in the pipeline, the Committee was informed. The Committee notes that
against a projected requirement of Rs. 950.00 crores, an allocation of Rs. 750 crore has been made for
DPEP for 1999-2000. However, as per the decision of the Planning Commission, funds for externally
aided projects like DPEP would be provided based on the requirements of ongoing projects as well as
those in the pipeline. The Committee finds that against an allocation of Rs.550 crores for DPEP in 1998-
99, utilisation figures stand at Rs.340 crores as on 26.2.99. DPEP is a very important intervention for
achieving UEE. The Committee would like to point out that there is an urgent need for utilising the
funds without delay but in a judicious manner. Provision of Rs. 10 crores in 1999-2000 as a
‘Domestic Component’ so that DPEP could reach those States/districts where external funding may
not be available is a welcome step.
2. The Committee was given to understand that certain problems were arising in the
implementation of DPEP in Kerala where very few districts have been covered under
this programme. The Committee desires that the Department may look into it
4.6.3. The other externally aided projects are the Lok Jumbish Project and Rajasthan Shiksha Karmi

7. Mid-day Meal Scheme
4.7.1. Mid-day Meal Scheme launched in August, 1995 covers all the children studying in primary classes
in all Govt., local body and Govt.-aided schools in the country. As per the 1998-99 figures, 9.75 crore
children in about 6.88 lakh schools were beneficiaries of this Scheme. The State Govts are to supplement
the Central efforts by providing the conversion costs for serving cooked meals for which financial
assistance is available to them under the JRY/EAS funds. However, the Committee was informed that only
5 states and 1 UT, i.e. Gujarat, Kerala, Orissa, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh (in 174 Tribal blocks only)
and Pondicherry serve cooked meals. What is more surprising is that two states, Haryana and J&K which
used to, had to revert to the distribution of the food-grains in 1996-97 due to resource constraints,
inadequate cooking arrangements and resentment among teachers. Non-serving of cooked meals defeats
the very objective of the Scheme. The Committee views with serious concern the total lack of state
initiative in the implementation of a scheme designed to give a boost to the universalisation of
primary education. The Committee strongly feels that successful implementation of Mid-day Meal
Scheme by 5 states and 1 UT can well be followed by the remaining states of the country. The
Committee is of the view that there should be supply of food grains to the States only on the
condition of serving of cooked meals to the children.
4.7.2. The Committee was informed that the Mid day Meal Scheme was facing a severe resource crunch.
During 1998-99, there has been disruption in supply of foodgrains due to paucity of funds and non-
reconciliation of lifting figures between states and FCI. As a result, lifting of foodgrains during the current
year has been lower than the average of 70-75% allocation during the last three years. The Committee was
given to understand that allocations for 1997-98 and 1998-99 were much below the requirements under
this scheme. The proposed allocation of Rs. 1031.10 crore for 1999-2000 would be too meagre to meet the
requirement of approximately Rs. 3000 crores to continue coverage of all the children of primary classes in
the country. Consequently, FCI may again resort to suspension of supply of foodgrains. This is an
alarming position which warrants urgent action on the part of the Govt. for augmentation of allocation of
funds under this scheme.
4.7.3. The Committee views with serious concern the proposed move of the Govt. to restrict the Mid
day Meal Scheme to Girls, SCs/STs only. Instead of restricting this scheme to a limited category, the
Department should explore all the possibilities for removing the bottlenecks – both physical and
financial – in close coordination with state govts. and implementing agencies. The Committee hopes
that findings of the evaluation study of Mid day Meal Scheme presently being conducted by the
Operation Research Group in 10 states would prove to be useful in taking a decision in the right
direction.

4.8 Non-Formal Education
4.8.1. The implementing agencies for this Programme are both State Govts. and NGOs. During 1997-98,
the number of NFE Centres being run in the State Sector was 2.41 lakh which remained the same in 1998-
99 too. However, there was increase in the number of centres being run by NGOs. Against 49,000 centres
run by 740 NGOs in 1997-98, there were 58,000 centres managed by 825 NGOs during 1998-99.
However, enhanced provision ;of Rs. 309.21 crores in BE 1998-99, in view of the revision of NFE Scheme
with enhanced cost parameters had to be reduced to Rs. 160 crores at the RE stage. The Committee has
been given to understand that unspent balance of Rs. 61.88 crore lying with State Govts and delay in the
finalisation of revised NFE Scheme were the two factors responsible for drastic reduction at the RE stage.
However, the Committee finds that even this reduced allocation is not likely to be utilised fully as there
has been an expenditure of only Rs. 108.61 crores upto 26.2.99.
4.8.2. The Committee views with serious concern the conclusion arrived at by the evaluation study
conducted in 6 states by the Planning Commission that NFE Programme has not made any notable
contribution. Some of its major findings as detailed below are very disturbing :-
o        erratic and much less than required flow of funds to NFE centres in the State
Sector.
o        Acute shortage of teaching /learning material in NFE Centres;
o        Attendance and enrolment in girl centres are low. Large number of girl centres
have male instructors;
o        Lack of coordination between State Govts and NGOs; and
o        Very weak supervision and monitoring of NFE Programme.
4.8.3. The Committee has been informed that proposed revision of the NFE Scheme incorporating the
modifications as suggested by the Planning Commission, views of experts and NFE practitioners and State
Govts.is at an advanced stage of preparation for consideration by EFC. The Committee strongly feels
that Department should make concerted efforts for finalisation of revised NFE Scheme without any
further delay and full and judicious utilisation of enhanced allocation of Rs. 350.00 crores proposed
in BE 1999-2000.
4. The NFE Programme initiated way back in 1979 on an experimental basis, was scaled up
in 1987 into a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for educationally backward States and was
further expanded during the 8th Plan period. It took 20 years to arrive at the conclusion
that NFE Programme has been perceived as a low cost, inferior and ineffective alternative
for full-time school meant for the poor people. The Committee, therefore, strongly
recommends that the revised NFE Programme should have an effective in-built
mechanism for monitoring with full coordination between implementing agencies
i.e.. State Govt/NGOs and the Central Government.

VI Secondary Education
6.1. The Department informed the Committee that the number of secondary and senior secondary schools
has increased from 0.83 lakh in 1991-92 to 1.07 lakh in 1997-98 representing a growth of 29% in the
period from 1991-92 to 1997-98 resulting in not only an increase in the enrolment but also an increase in
the number of teachers. The demand on the secondary education set up is bound to increase during the
coming years because of universalisation of elementary education causing a greater transition to secondary
education. However, the Committee observes that in most of the programmes under the Secondary
Education, allocation of funds proposed in BE 1998-99 has been drastically reduced at the RE stage
and from the details of expenditure upto 26.2.99 made available, it is evident that there are little
chances of full utilisation of the allocation by the close of the current financial year. The Committee
would like to be informed about the states where the funds could not be utilised fully and the reasons
therefor.
6.2 Kendriya Vidyalayas
6.2.1. The Committee notes that plan allocation for Kendriya Vidyalayas was considerably enhanced in
1998-99 as compared to 1997-98 budget provision. However, this enhancement has not proved to be of
much help. Against Rs.200 crores projected under Plan for 1999-2000, proposed allocation of Rs. 90.00
crores would be too meagre to meet its requirements, the Committee was informed. The Committee finds
that there has been no expansion in the number of Kendriya Vidyalayas. Not only this, out of 708 Kendriya
Vidyalayas under Civil and Defence Sector, only 378 Vidyalayas are in permanent buildings and 73
buildings are under construction 257 schools still remain without buildings. Against the projected
requirement of Rs. 700 crores during 9
th Plan for construction activities only Rs. 175.50 crores have been
provided during the first three years of 9th Plan. As things stand, there will be sufficient number of
Vidyalayas without permanent buildings even at the end of the 9th Plan. The Committee observes that
whereas on the one hand funds are lying unutilised in some of the schemes on the other hand KVS is
in dire need of funds for construction of a large number of its school buildings. The Committee is of
the emphatic view that urgent and positive action is called for, for providing basic infrastructure to
KVS, an organisation which has been providing exemplary service in the field of education. Higher
pass percentage of KVS students appearing at the CBSE exam. as compared to National Average of
CBSE is an ample proof in this regard.
6.2.2. The Committee notes that out of Rs. 90.00 crores proposed for 1999-2000 Rs.68.80 crores are meant
for construction activities only. This is bound to have adverse impact on academic and other related
activities of KVS.

VII Higher Education
7.1 University Grants Commission
UGC provides financial assistance to 16 Central Universities both for maintenance and development
purpose, development grants to 107 State Universities and development or maintenance grants, as the case
may be, to the eligible Deemed Universities. UGC also provides development grants to 4815 eligible
colleges. The Committee was informed that 8th Plan allocation for UGC was only 8% of the budget of the
Department of Education as against 16% in the 7th Plan, which was already down from the previous Plans
averaging 22 to 25%. The Committee notes that an allocation of RS. 1002.62 crores against the 8th Plan
proposal for Rs.1767.70 crores based on actual requirement not only resulted in an estimated unpaid
liability of Rs.400 crores but also led to freezing of several schemes, including the prestigious ones.
7.2. The Committee was given to understand that due to pressure from the Planning Commission, the UGC
was forced to prepare its 9th Plan outlay at Rs.2500 crores. However, an allocation of only Rs. 2050 crores
has been made. As a result, schemes like Vocational education, academic staff colleges, Junior Research
Fellowships, Special Assistance Programme etc. would be severely affected. What is more worrying is that
some research related schemes would have to be closed down.
7.3. The Committee notes that BE under Plan for both 1997-98 and 1998-99 for UGC remained the
same. The Committee is in agreement with UGC’s argument that things could be managed somehow
as most of the new schemes were in the finalisation process during the first two years of 9th Plan.
However, an allocation of Rs.418 crores against an projected requirement of Rs.658.59 crore for
1999-2000 would worsen the situation further.
7.4. The Committee is constrained to note that the following activities would be the worst affected as
a result of drastic cut in the allocation for UGC in 1999-2000 –
a. number of major research projects pending for want of approval due to financial
constraints;
b. revision of remuneration of Research Assistants, Project Assistants/fellows/retired
teachers;
c. revision of amount of research fellowship awards;
d. upgradation of libraries/laboratories in selected universities/colleges; and
e. networking of universities.
7.5. The Committee is aware of the overall condition of fund constraints prevailing in the country.
The Committee would however, like to point out that in view of considerable expansion in the realm
of higher education during 8th Plan period, allocation of funds should reflect higher allocation in
anticipation of similar expansion, if not quantitative , at least qualitative, during 9th Plan period too.
It has to be borne in mind that the country has an existing infrastructure which despite its various
problems, is still one of the best in the developing world.
7.6. The Committee finds that situation is equally grim on the non-Plan side also for UGC. There is a

a. major repair works of older universities with ageing buildings;
b. funds required for 2100 vacant posts (986 teaching + 1114 non-teaching) in Central

7.7. There are three universities, namely, Banaras Hindu University,. Jamia Millia University and

Committee is, however, constrained to note that whereas the first two universities have been given
the status of a National University long time back, Tilak Mahavidyalaya is yet to be declared a
National University. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that needful action in this regard
may be taken by the Department.
VIII. General
8.1. The Committee appreciates that a number of interventions are being made/schemes being
implemented, many of them achieving their targets in a time bound manner so as to enable the
disadvantaged groups, i.e. women/children belonging to SC/ST/minority/belonging to economical weaker
sections of the society/physically handicapped.
8.2. The Report on the Constitution (83
rd Amendment) Bill 1993 for making the right to free and
compulsory education for children of 6-14 years age a Fundamental Right was presented to both the
Houses of Parliament on 24th November, 1997. The Committee is, however, constrained to note that the
Bill in the revised form as recommended by it is yet to come toParliament. The matter is being further
examined in the consultation with the Ministry of Law . The Committee would take the opportunity to
point out that its recommendations on the Constitutions (83rd Amendment) Bill, 1997 are based on
detailed interaction with a number of educationists/NGOs/representatives of public. The Committee
fails to understand the need for initiating a wider public debate on the issue.
8.3 Educational development in the N-E Region
The Committee notes that a number of interventions are being made by the Department in the North-
Eastern region so as to bring the people of this remote region of the country to the mainstream. In view of
high percentage (35%) of untrained teachers in N-E region, a special programme called ‘Diploma in
Primary Education’ has been launched there jointly by IGNOUand NCERT. In the realm of higher and
technical education, 26.37% of total budget allocation for the Central Universities during 9
th Plan, has
been allocated to NEHU, Nagaland, Assam and Tejpur Universities. Setting up of 6 engineering colleges,
19 polytechnics, 6 MBA and 2 MCA level colleges have been sanctioned by AICTE during the last year
for NE region. The Committee is happy to note that special emphasis is being laid on
initiation/implementation of various centrally sponsored schemes for the NE region.

IX. Committed Liability
9.1. While examining the Demands for Grants 1997-98 of the Departments under its purview, the
Committee noted that the committed liability of the Plan Schemes has not been reflected in the
budgetary allocations.
9.2. The Committee, in this, connection considered the report of the Tenth Finance Commission. The
Tenth Finance Commission have observed in this regard as under:-
"Although the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their terms of
reference in deciding the cut off date for transfer of committed liability on
account of plan scheme, the incremental liabilities that would arise in 1997-
98 on account of 8
th Plan scheme completed in the next two financial years
would have to be provided for. The Commission are, therefore, of the view
that the Planning Commission may consider providing for the maintenance
of such scheme till 1999-2000 in the Plan itself as was done for the schemes

of the two Annual Plans of 1990-91 and 1991-92".
9.3. The Committee also sought clarifications from the then Secretary (Expenditure). He informed
the Committee that even though communications were sent to various Ministries, the Ministries
could not identify the committed expenditure on time. Accordingly a lumpsum amount has been
provided under Demand No. 31. The Secretary also assured that if required, more funds for this
purpose will be provided at the RE stage. He also stated that the Departments may approach the
Department of Expenditure for their share.
9.4. The Committee, accordingly, directed the Department to approach the Ministry of Finance in
this regard. However, while considering the Demands for Grants 1998-1999, the Committee noticed
that no progress in this regard seemed to have taken place. The Committee, therefore, reiterated its
stand. In spite of the Departments initiating some exercise, the matter could not be sorted out. The
Committee, therefore, heard the Secretary (Expenditure) on 27
th March, 1999.
9.5. The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, submitted that at the end of the 8th Five
Year Plan the Planning Commission issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the committed
expenditure from plan to the non plan side. The Ministry of Finance (Budget Division) also issued a
letter to all the Financial Advisors of the Department requesting them to indicate the amount to be
included from plan to non plan.
9.6. The Secretary submitted that as on 10
th February, 1997, 24 Ministries/Departments responded
indicating a figure of Rs.450.28 crore. However, some Ministries/Departments like Education,
Culture, Women and Child Development did not respond. Accordingly a lumpsum provision of Rs.500
crores in the Ministry of Finance Budget was provided for this purpose. As a result of this exercise, a
sum of Rs.150 crores was indeed transferred from plan to non plan for the various Ministries as
committed expenditure during the year, on the basis of scrutiny with reference to the Planning
Commission’s guidelines. The rest of the amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found
acceptable.
9.7. The Secretary agreed in principle that whatever is the committed expenditure, it should be
transferred from plan to non plan. Otherwise the plan has no meaning.
9.8. The Secretary assured that the proposals pertaining to UGC (Rs.27 crores) and Department of
Education (Rs. 80 crores) which were under consideration would be cleared. Moreover, the Tenth Finance
Commission Report has given a breathing time till 1999-2000 for this transition. The Committee would
like to impress that the real picture should be projected before Parliament.
9.9. The Secretary also apprised the Committee that another Sub Committee has been appointed by
the NDC, consisting of representatives from seven states and Ministry of Finance. This Sub-
Committee will look into the structural distortions which have entered into the finances of the states
on account of 5
th Pay Commission and to suggest a suitable package linked to fiscal reforms.
9.10. The Committee observes that a similar problem exists in the case of Central Ministries also.
The Committee, therefore, desires that a similar exercise be carried out in the case of Central
Ministries also.
