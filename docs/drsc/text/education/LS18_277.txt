     Chapter-I 
 
1. The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports has two s eparate Departments under its ambit. 
They are Department of Youth Affairs and Department of Sports. The Ministry of Youth Affairs & 
Sports functions under the overall guidance of the Minister of State (Independent Charge) and 
each Department is headed by a Secretary to the Government of India. 
 
2.0 The Department of Youth Affairs has one subordinate  office, namely the National Service 
Scheme (NSS). There are also two autonomous organis ations, namely, Nehru Yuva Kendra 
Sangathan (NYKS) and Rajiv Gandhi National Institut e of Youth Development (RGNIYD), 
Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu (notified as an Institute  of National importance by an Act of 
Parliament in 2012). 
 
2.1 Programmes for the Youth get significant invest ment from the Government of India not 
only through the Department of Youth Affairs but al so various Ministries/Departments of Govt. of 
India. Besides these, the State Governments and a n umber of other stakeholders   are working to 
support youth development and to enable productive youth participation. 
 
2.2 The Youth represent the most dynamic and vibran t segment of the population of India. The 
Indian Youth has abundance of energy, courage, spir it for adventure, imagination, hope and 
ambition. These should not be allowed to go waste o r used for destructive purposes. The young 
man and women of Indian should be fully involved in  the creative work of nation-building and 
reconstruction. India is one of the youngest nation s in the world, with about 65% of the population 
under 35 years of age. The youth in the age group o f 15-29 years comprise 27.5% of the 
population. India is expected to become the 4 
th  largest economy by 2025, only after the United 
States, China and Japan, contributing about 5.5% - 6% to the world GDP. While most of these 
developed countries face the risk of an ageing work force, India is expected to have a very 
favourable demographic profile. It is estimated tha t by the year 2020, the population of India 
would have a median age of 28 years only as against  38 years for US, 42 years for China and 48 
years for Japan. This ‘demographic dividend’ offers  a great opportunity for nation-building and 
developmental activities. 
 
2.3 In order to harness the youth-power of the coun try, a National Youth Policy, 2014 was 
launched in February, 2014 to instil in the Youth a  deep awareness of national ideals of 
secularism, non -violence, integration and our anci ent historical and cultural heritage. It also aims 
at developing qualities of discipline, self-relianc e, leadership, justice, fair play, sporting spirit and 
scientific temper. 
 
2.4 With the above-mentioned objectives in view, ad venture institutions, cultural centres, etc, 
have been established in various cities of the coun try. There are special schemes for the tribal 
youth to give them vocational training and to updat e their skills to help them in self-employment. 
There are Youth Hostels spread all over the country  to promote travel among young men and 
women by providing cheap accommodation when on educ ational tour and own excursions to 
historical and cultural places. Under the internati onal scouting and guiding movement, Scouting 
and Guiding Organisations are inculcating in Indian  Youth a spirit of loyalty, patriotism and 
thoughtfulness for others. 
 

3.0 Physical education, games and sports have been rece iving attention over successive Plans. 
National Sports Policy, 1984 was the first move tow ards developing an organized and systematic 
framework for the development and promotion of spor ts in the country, and the precursor of the 
present National Sports Policy, 2001. The twin plan ks of the National Sports Policy 2001 are 
“Broad-basing of Sports” and “Achieving Excellence in Sports” at the national and international 
levels.  
 
3.1  The autonomous organisations functioning under  the administrative control of Department 
of Sports are as : 
 
/head2right Sports Authority of India (SAI). 
/head2right Lakshmibai National Institute of Physical Education (LNIPE), Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh. 
/head2right National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA). 
/head2right National Dope Testing Laboratory (NDTL)  
 
3.2 Sports and games have always been seen as an in tegral component in the all-round 
development of the human personality. Apart from be ing a means of entertainment and physical 
fitness, sports have also played a great role in ge neration of the spirit of healthy competition and 
bonding within the community. Needless to mention t hat achievements in sports at the 
international level have always been a source of national pride and prestige. 
 
3.3 With modern sports being highly competitive, th e use of modern infrastructure, equipment 
and advanced scientific support has changed the sce nario of sports at the international level. 
Keeping in line with the growing demands for advanc ed infrastructure, equipment and scientific 
support, Government of India has taken several init iatives and is providing the necessary 
assistance to sportspersons by way of training and exposure in international competitions backed 
up with scientific and equipment support.

RASHTRIYA YUVA SASHATIKARAN KARYAKRAM (RYSK) 
 
6.0 Rashtriya Yuva Sashaktikaran Karyakram is an Um brella Scheme with an allocation of 
`350 Crore in Plan side and `52.50 Crore  in the No n-Plan side of the budget allocation. Utimately, 
a provision of `402.50 Crore has been made in this Umbrella Scheme, wherein first time following 
eight Schemes have been amalgamated:- 
/head2right Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan(NYKS); 
/head2right National Youth Corp (NYC); 
/head2right National Youth Programme for Youth and Adolescent Development (NPYAD); 
/head2right International Cooperation (IC); 
/head2right Youth Hostels (YH); 
/head2right Assistance to Scouting & Guiding Organisations; 
/head2right National Discipline Scheme (NDS); and 
/head2right  National Young Leaders Programmme (NYLP); 
 
6.1 It has been informed that Rashtriya Yuva Sashak tikaran Karyakram (RYSK) will, 
henceforth, be the Flagship Programme of the Depart ment for development and empowerment of 
the youth to enable them to realise their potential  and in the process, to contribute to the nation-
building process. The Committee has been further apprised that the merger of a number of Scheme 
into a single flagship scheme, inter-alia, offers the benefits as:  
 
(i) Currently, only Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NY KS) and National Youth Corp (NYC), 
which are already integrated administratively, have  administrative presence at field level. Other 
Programmes do not have ground presence. Hence, thei r implementation, as standalone 
programmes, poses problems in effective implementat ion and supervision. Merger of these 
programmes into the new umbrella scheme will enable  the Department to effectively leverage the 
administrative structure of NYKS/ NYC for effective implementation of other  Programmes 
(NYKS/ NYC are being leveraged even now to some ext ent, but merger of the schemes into a 
single flagship scheme will make the process seamless and more effective).  
 

(ii) Under the National Youth Programme for Youth a nd Adolescent Development (NPYAD), 
assistance is given to NGOs for youth development p rogrammes. Integration of this programme 
with NYKS/ NYC will enable the Department to  lever age NYKS set up for effective 
monitoring of the activities carried out under the assistance given to NGOs. Moreover, it will be 
possible to ensure that NYKS set up (NYKS offices/ NYC volunteers and Youth Clubs) and the 
NGO work in close collaboration with each other, which will improve the  effectiveness of 
programme implementation. It will also be possible to closely monitor the activities of Scouting 
and Guiding Organisations being assisted by the Department. 
 
(iii) There are 83 running Youth Hostels, set up wi th the objective to promote youth travel with 
the country. Youth Hostels are directly managed fro m the Department. As a result, close 
supervision has not been possible. The capacity of the Hostels is not being fully utilised. 
Integration of Youth Hostels programme with NYKS wi ll help in effective management of Youth 
Hostels through NYKS functionaries at field level.  
 
(iv) ‘International Cooperation’ includes youth exc hange programmes with various countries. 
Youth Delegations from various countries visit Indi a and they are taken to various cities for 
showing them places of interest and arranging their  interaction with Indian Youth. Integration of 
these Programmes with NYKS will help in organising these programmes in a more effective 
manner at the field level. 
 
(v) National Young Leaders Programme (NYLP), which has important components 
 including neighbourhood youth parliament, shramada an and youth development through 
support from National Youth Development Fund, will also benefit from complete integration with 
NYKS, since the NYKS administrative set up can then  be fully leveraged for effective 
implementation of this Programme. 
 
6.2 The Committee, hence, was given to understand that in nutshell, 8 Schemes of the 
Department that were operating as stand alone Schem es, will now operate as components of a 
single cohesive Scheme, which will help in achievin g better synergy in implementation of these 
Schemes. All the Schemes are Central Sector Schemes  and the new Scheme will also be a Central 
Sector Scheme and hence, there is no change in fund ing pattern. There is also no change in 
operational guidelines of existing Schemes that are  being merged except that they will now be 
implemented in an integrated cohesive manner as par ts of a single umbrella Scheme. There are no 
constraints as such.  
 
6.3 The Committee hopes that current structure of the U mbrella Scheme, which is a 
Central Sector Scheme, will achieve the desired res ults for what it has been meant for in the 
current fiscal. It has been envisaged that by subsu ming the standalone programmes which 
lack the ground presence, would be effectively leve raged by the administrative presence and 
structure of NYKS and NYC.  The key focus would be NYKS set up comprising NYKS 
offices and NYC volunteers as well as Youth Clubs. The Committee recommends that the 
Department will not leave anything unturned by taki ng all requisite initiatives to accomplish 
the target within the stipulated time frame and als o overcoming all hurdles coming in the 

way of execution of the Umbrella Scheme so as to ma ke it a note-worthy Central Sector 
Scheme.  
 
NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN (NYKS) 
 
7.0 NYKS launched in 1972, is one of the largest Youth Organisations in the world. The main 
objective of the programme is to develop the person ality and leadership qualities of the youth and 
to engage them in nation-building activities.  This  is being done through promotion of Youth 
Clubs.  The Youth enrol as members of these Youth C lubs. NYKS reaches these Youth Clubs and 
their members though various programmes. NYKS has i ntroduced a signified procedure of on-line 
registration of Youth Clubs.

NATIONAL YOUTH CORPS (NYC) 
 
8.0 The Scheme of National Youth Corps (NYC) was la unched in the country during the year 
2010-11 and the same is being implemented through NYKS. The main   objectives of the 
Scheme are as follows :- 
• To setup a group of disciplined and dedicated youth  who have the inclination and 
spirit to engage in the task of nation-building. 
• To facilitate the realization of inclusive growth (both social and economic). 
• To act as points for dissemination of information, basic knowledge in the 
community.  
• To act as group modulators and peer group educators. 
• To act as role models for the younger cohort, espec ially towards enhancement of 
public ethics, probity and dignity of labour. 
 
8.1 The Committee on the basis of the replies to th e Questionnaire on DFG 2016-17 provided 
by the Department of Sports, came to know that unde r this scheme, Youth in the age-group of 
 18-25 years are engaged as volunteers to serve upt o maximum 2 years in nation- building 
activities. After 2 years, new NYC volunteers are r ecruited. Normally, 2 NYC volunteers are 
deployed in each Block. NYC volunteers act as an ex tended arm of NYKS administrative set-up 
(which has presence upto District level in the form  of District Nehru Yuva Kendras) for 
implementation of programmes and initiatives of NYK S. NYC volunteers are paid honorarium 
@Rs.2,500/- per month. The normal annual requiremen t of funds for 12,000 volunteers is Rs.36 
crores, but expenditure may sometimes be less due t o time taken in selection of new NYC 
volunteers on existing ones completing 2 years. 
 
8.2 Further, NYKS actively involves these volunteer s in motivating and guiding the village-
level youth clubs while helping in organizing vario us NYKS programmes.  The Department had 
got an independent evaluation study of NYC Scheme conducted through M/s HARYALI  Centre 
for Rural Development, New Delhi.  During this Stud y, it came out that the main grievance of 
NYC volunteers is that their Honorarium is very low  and the same should be increased. 
HARYALI Centre for Rural Development, New Delhi had  recommended that the Honorarium be 

increased to `5,000/- per month. The NYC volunteers  have no issues as far as operational aspects 
of functioning under NYKS are concerned. 
 
8.3 The Committee also came to know that the Board of G overnors of NYKS had 
recommended to the Department increasing the Honora rium of NYC volunteers to `5,000/- per 
month and increasing the number of NYC volunteers t o 4 volunteers per Block. Acceptance of 
these recommendations is contingent upon  availabil ity of funds, as this will increase the funds 
requirement more than four-fold  from the current l evel. The BE 2016-17 for NYC Scheme 
does provide for any additional requirement. An SFC  Proposal has been moved for increasing 
 Honorarium of NYC volunteers to `5,000/- per month , but this will eventually depend on 
availability of additional funds.  
 
8.4 Further, providing employment opportunity/improving  employability in not the main 
objective of NYC Scheme. The objective is to engage NYC volunteers in nation- building 
activities for a period of two years. However, the NYC volunteers gain valuable experience, 
working with the community in general and youth in particular, on various nation-building 
activities. It develops their personality and leade rship qualities, which opens many employment 
avenues for them.  NYC Scheme also has provision fo r providing skill training to the NYC 
volunteers, from Scheme funds, towards the end of t heir 2-year tenure, so as to help them in 
finding suitable job/ self-employment opportunities . Some volunteers even go for higher studies. 
NYKS also sponsors some NYC volunteers for post-graduate courses in RGNIYD. 
 
8.5 The Committee intends to draw the attention of the Government that the amount of 
fund allocation at BE 2015-16 to the tune of `33.00 crore was reduced to `21.65 crore at the 
stage of RE due to less number of registered volunt eers  for imparting training/programme

NATIONAL DISCIPLINE SCHEME (NDS) 
 
13.0  The National Discipline Scheme was originally  introduced in 1954 by the Ministry of 
Rehabilitation, which was transferred to the Minist ry of Education and Social Welfare in 1958. 
Consequent upon the recommendation of Dr. Kunzru Co mmittee in 1963, introducing discipline 
programme at school stage, the integrated scheme ca lled National Fitness Corps (NFC) was 
evolved. The Scheme was transferred to the Departme nt of Youth Affairs and Sports (now 
Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports) in eighties when a separate Department was formed. 
  
13.1   On the basis of the recommendations of the D r. Kunzru Committee, the decision to transfer 
NDS Instructors to various State Governments was ta ken in consultation with the State Education 
Secretaries. In the year 1969, the Central Cabinet took a decision that the services of the 
employees under the NFC would be transferred to the  State Governments and so eventually in 
1972, the National Discipline Scheme was decentrali sed and the employees under the National 
Discipline Scheme were transferred and absorbed in the services of the State Governments on the 

condition that the Central Government would reimbur se their pay and allowances so long as they 
remained on the strength of the State Governments. About 6145 NDS Instructors were transferred 
to various State Governments after decentralisation  of the NDS Scheme. This is a non-plan 
Scheme under which pay and allowances of NDS Instru ctors are reimbursed to concerned State 
Governments.

Under National Discipline Scheme (NDS), the only ac tion at present is to reimburse expenditure

of India, for which the Government of India had agr eed to reimburse the expenditure. Currently, 
no other activities/ programmes are being taken up by the Department under the Scheme. Hence, 
there is no achievement etc. under the Scheme. NDS,  in fact, is a Non-Plan Scheme. Regarding 
reimbursement of expenditure, there is serious mism atch between the committed liability on this

NATIONAL YOUNG LEADERS PROGRAMME (NYLP)   
 
14.0 National Young Leaders Programme (NYLP) was la unched in December, 2014. The 
financial year 2015-16 was the first full year for implementation of the Programme. NYLP is an 
ambitious pan-India programme, with the following components, namely: 
 
a) Neighbourhood Youth Parliament (NYP):   Neighbourhood Youth Parliament is 
to provide a platform to the youth to articulate th eir views on various socio-
economic development issues of concern to them. Youth clubs under NYKS (Nehru 
Yuva Kendra Sangathan) are being enabled to hold ‘n eighbourhood youth 
parliament’ programmes. The Programme will help in developing leadership 
qualities among the youth and in actively involving them in the governance process.  
 
b) Youth for Development Programme (YFDP) :  The Programme aims at 
channelising the immense youth energy towards the n ation-building, by involving 
youth in Shramadaan  (voluntary labour). The Programme will help in dev eloping

NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME (NSS) 
 
15.0 National Service Scheme (NSS) was introduced i n 1969 with the primary objective of 
developing the personality and character of the student youth through voluntary  community 
service. 'Education through Service'  is the purpos e of the NSS. The ideological orientation of the 
NSS is inspired by the ideals of Mahatma Gandhi. Ve ry appropriately, the motto of NSS is " NOT 
ME, BUT YOU'. An NSS volunteer places the 'community' before 'self'. 
 
15.1 The Committee was given to understand that in case of NSS the change in funding 
 pattern would be only with effect from 2016-17. Cu rrently, NSS is being implemented 
 as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS), with the fo llowing funding pattern:  
 
a) In case of J&K and UTs (without legislature), the C entral Government provides 100% 
funding. 
b) In case of NE States (including Sikkim), Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand, the sharing of 
expenditure between Centre and States is in the ratio of 75:25. 
c) In case of all other States/ UTs, the sharing of ex penditure between Centre and States is in 
the ratio of 7:5. 
 
 However, it has been decided that from 2016-17, Na tional Service Scheme (NSS) will 
 be implemented as Central Sector Scheme, i.e. with  100% funding from Government 
 of India. The budget allocation has also been enha nced accordingly. The Operational 
 Guidelines and the Monitoring Mechanism for the Sc heme will continue as before. 
 There are no difficulties or constraints as such.  The Programme is being effectively 
 monitored and it is the endeavour of the Departmen t to constantly improve the quality 
 of programme implementation. 

 
15.2 There are no new components in NSS. The only c hange is that from 2016-17, NSS will be 
implemented as a Central Sector Scheme (it was a CSS earlier). The change in funding pattern will 
result in substantial increase in funding requireme nt for 2016-17. During the year 2015-16, the 
funds requirement was projected at `180.50 crore  ( Plan: `170 crore and Non-Plan: `10.50 crore), 
against allocation was Rs.95.98 crore (Plan: `83.16  crore and Non-Plan: Rs.12.82 crore)due to 
which funds could not be released to some States/ U Ts as per their entitlements, adversely 
affecting the programme implementation. For the cur rent year, the projected funds requirement 
was `151.90 crore (Plan: `135 crore and Non-Plan: 1 6.90 crore), against which the BE 2016-17 is 
`137.50 crore (Plan: `120 crore and Non-Plan: `17.5 0 crore) only. This will be grossly inadequate, 
especially considering that there is substantial ou tstanding liability from the current year.  Matter 
will be taken up for increase in budget allocation at RE stage. Regarding Physical Targets, the 
target for 2015-16 is 34 lakh NSS volunteers and th e target for 2016-17 will also be the same. The 
enrolled NSS volunteers carry out designated regular, special camping and other  activities as per 
scheme guidelines. During current year, best possible efforts are  being made to achieve the 
targets despite shortage of funds.  
 
15.3 The Committee views that the NSS is very well struc tured programme and hence, there are 
no implementation issues as such. The only constrai nt is inadequate availability of funds. NSS is a 
novel scheme which aims at developing the personali ty and character of student youth through 
voluntary community service. This is part of the third dimension of education , namely, value 
education , which is becoming increasingly important. There i s need to universalise the 
programme, but currently, the NSS covers less than 10% of the student population eligible to join 
it. The States/ UTs have been asking for higher allocations for expanding the coverage of NSS, but 
the same has not been possible due to inadequate av ailability of funds.  The Department proposes 
increase in budget allocations for NSS every year, but the same has remained almost stagnant 
since 2007-08. The BE for 2016-17 has been enhanced  to Rs.137.50 crore, but this is mainly due 
to change in funding pattern. It does enable the De partment to enhance the coverage of NSS. The 
financing norm for allocations under NSS, i.e., @Rs .475 per volunteer per year, is abysmally low, 
considering the regular and special camping activities that are required to be undertaken.  
 
15.4 The Department has taken a number of initiatives to  enhance the effectiveness of 
implementation of NSS.  In fact, the Department had  got an Evaluation Study of the NSS 
conducted through TISS (Tata Institute of Social Sc iences). TISS gave a number of important 
recommendations on various aspects of the Programme , including (i) Planning, implementation, 
impact and sustainability of NSS activities, (ii) T raining/ capacity building of NSS functionaries, 
(iii) Motivating NSS functionaries/ volunteers, and  (iv) strategies for expansion of NSS. These 
recommendations have been accepted and are being im plemented. Some administrative measures 
being taken for close monitoring of the Programme a nd for improving its effectiveness are as 
follows: 
 
/square4 Timely release of grants/ funds to the States/ UTs for NSS activities.  
/square4 Training/ Capacity Building of NSS Programme Office rs through Empanelled Training 
Institutes (ETIs) for effective implementation of NSS programmes. 
/square4 Close monitoring of the ongoing programmes/ activities of NSS through the network of 
NSS Regional Centres and State NSS Cells. 

/square4 Regular Meetings of NSS Advisory Committees at vari ous levels, for effective 
implementation of NSS programmes and for removing bottlenecks, if any. 
 
NSS has been very pro-actively involved in all flag ship programmes of the Government. NSS 
volunteers have been actively involved in various p rogrammes including Swatchh Bharat, Digital 
India, Yoga Programmes, Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan, Bet i Bachao Beti Padhao, Saansad Adarsh 
Gram Yojana, etc. Further, to motive more and more students to opt for NSS, the NSS has now 
been made an Elective Subject (with credits). The U GC has issued necessary Advisory to all 
Universities in this regard, on the request of Department of Youth Affairs.  
 
15.5 The Committee recommends that in view of the prevai ling situation in NSS, there is 
an urgent need for substantial enhancement in budge t allocations for NSS and the 
Department will take the requisite initiatives in t his regards. On the other hand, the 
Committee further recommends for effective executio n of this scheme so as to achieve the 
desired goals. The Committee also wishes that verac ity of details of backlog expenditure 
incurred by the State Govts/UTs is ascertained by t he Department and a status note be made

ASSISTANCE TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS 
30.00  Financial assistance is given to recognized National Sports Federations for sending the teams 
abroad for training, participating in international  events, conducting international events in India, 
national championship events, and for procuring spo rts equipment. Financial assistance is also 
extended through SAI for organizing coaching camps,  for preparing national teams and for engaging 
the services of foreign coaches. Increase in RE 201 5-16 is due to expenditure made on conduct of 
12 
th  South Asian Games, Guwahati from 5-16 Feb, 2016. 
 
30.01  The Government of India, Ministry of Youth A ffairs and Sports (MYAS) recognizes only 
one federation for each sport discipline at the nat ional level. The recognized National Sports 
Federation (NSF), in turn, is responsible for the p romotion and development of the sport. The NSF 
has to discharge the responsibilities in accordance  with the principles laid down in the Olympic 
Charter or in the Charter of Indian Olympic Associa tion (IOA), or the relevant International 
Federation.  The Government only supplements the ef forts of National Sports Federations (NSFs) 

by providing financial assistance for participation  in international events abroad, conducting 
national/international tournaments and training/coa ching of national teams under Indian and 
Foreign Coaches/Experts etc. under the Scheme of ‘A ssistance to National Sports Federations’. In 
all these matters, SAI closely coordinates with NSFs. 
 
30.02  The Ministry regularly consults NSFs in majo r decision making, e.g.,  the NSFs were 
consulted while formulating criteria for Sports Awa rds, revision of Schemes of the Ministry, etc. 
The Ministry also either directly or through Sports  Authority of India (SAI) consults the NSFs 
while implementing its various schemes. Further, th is Ministry has evolved an 
evaluation/performance monitoring mechanism of the NSFs. Release of grants would be, inter-
alia, based on the evaluation of NSFs.                                                                                                                       
 
30.03 Government has also issued instructions to NSFs to mandatorily declare information on their 
website pertaining to their audited accounts, balan ce sheet, selection criteria for athletes, etc. The  
NSFs receiving grant of  `10 lakhs or more has been  declared as Public Authority within the RTI 
Act. Also, Government has issued guidelines imposin g age and tenure limits in respect of office 
bearers of National Sports Federations, including t hose of the Indian Olympic Association, these 
have further been reiterated in the National Sports  Development Code of India, 2011, which has 
been made effective from 31.1.2011.  As per the gui delines, the Government has, inter-alia, 
provided the following age and tenure limits in respect of office bearers of the NSFs: 
 
i. The President of any recognized National Sports Fed eration, including the Indian Olympic 
Association can hold the office for a maximum period of twelve years with or without break. 
ii. The Secretary (or by whatever other designation suc h as Secretary General or General 
Secretary by which he is referred to) and the Treas urer of any recognized National Sports 
Federation, including the IOA, may serve a maximum of two successive terms of four years 
each after which a minimum cooling off period of fo ur years is required to seek fresh election 
to either post. 
iii. The President, the Secretary and the Treasurer of a ny recognized National Sports Federation, 
including the Indian Olympic Association, shall cea se to hold that post on attaining the age of 
70 years. 
 
30.04  Compliance to the directions of the Governme nt by the NSFs has been made mandatory to 
receive government recognition and thereby become e ligible to receive financial as well as other 
forms of assistance from the Government of India su ch as railway concessions, income tax 
exemption, custom duty exemption, etc. and to deriv e the authority to perform the public functions 
of selecting and deputing the national teams for pa rticipation in recognized international sports 
competitions which involve representation of member  countries and to represent the country in 
international associations, events, meets, conferen ces, etc. No National Sports Federation which 
has not adhered to these instructions for the elect ions held after the issue of these Guidelines of 1-
5-2010 has been given annual recognition by the Government. 
 
30.05 Action has been taken against the NSFs for no n-compliance to the guidelines, such as, the 
Archery Association of India has been de-recognized  since it refused to accept the Government 
guidelines; the recognition of Indian Amateur Boxin g Federation was suspended and subsequently 

the same was not renewed.  Korfball Federation of I ndia was not accorded recognition since 2012 
for not accepting Government Guidelines.  Gymnastic s Federation of India and Athletics 
Federation of India were asked to hold fresh electi ons which they have complied with. Annual 
recognition to All India Tennis Association and the  Judo Federation of India, for the year 2016, 
has not been given since these NSFs violated the ag e and tenure guidelines in the elections of their 
Presidents.  
 
30.06 The Committee is of consistent opinion that t here is a distinct lack of adequate 
coordination and monitoring mechanism between the G overnment  and National Sports 
Federations. The sportsperson of conflicting Federa tions strive for stability in their 
performance and secured participation in distinguis hed sports events. The Committee in the 
perspective of erring Federations as well as career  of the sportspersons, emphatically shows 
its concern to evolve a sustainable mechanism so as  to curb the meance of erring sports 
Federations and at the same time protecting the tra ining participation and performance of 
affected sportsperson. 
 
30.07 The Committee, ultimately recommends that com e what may, the preparation as well 
as participation and also performance of sportspersons are not adversely affected on account 
of erring Sports Federations.
