OVERVIEW 
1. The National Health Policy (1983) has reiterated India's commitment to attain "Health for 
All (H.F .A.) by 2000 A.D.". The Eighth Plan envisages that health facilities must reach the 
entire population by the end of the Plan period. The B.F.A. paradigm, according to the 8th 
Plan, must take into account not only the high risk vulnerable groups i.e. mothers and children, 
but must also focus sharply on the underprivileged segments within the vulnerable groups. Health 
for under privileged will be the key strategy for the HFA, by the year 2000 A.D., a declaration 
to which India is a signatory. This can only be achieved by associating the pre-defined segments 
of population with not only the health planning process but also with the method for the 
, evaluation of programmes. 
2. India is facing highly complex and challenging health problems. There are health hazards 
of communicable diseases like leprosy, T.B. and malaria, besides challenges of post-transitional 
diseases like cardio-vascular and cancer. Above all these, we are also faced with horrendous 
threat of AIDS. These health problems are further aggravated by wide spread poverty, which 
leads to malnutrition, unhygienic sanitation, illiteracy and ignorance. Population growth is playing 
further havoc on the availability and quality of health services. 
3. Department of Health deals with the health administration of the country, including drugs 
control and prevention of food adulteration . 
4. The main functions of the Deparment of Health are to undertake national programmes of 
health for the prevention and eradication of communicable diseases; to promote education 
research and training in various medical disciplines; to prevent adulteration of food as well as 
drugs; to promote research and treatment on scientific lines in Indian Systems of Medicine; to 
provide primary health care at the doorsteps of the people; to implement better health care 
programme for tribals, and to collaborate with WHO and other international agencies. 
5. For the financial year 1994-95, Budgetary allocation of the Department of Health is Rs. 
1255 crores, this is 18.34 per cent more than the previous year's allocation. This increase, as 
stated by the Department, is due to steep escalation in the prices of consumable items, machinery 
and equipments, publications, raiVair traffic fares, etc. Grant s of additional instalment of D.A., 
Interim Relief, increased number of patients in Government hospitals and increase in cost of 
insecticides, anti-leprosy and anti-T.B. drugs, etc. 
6. According to the Ministry's documents, the major thrust of Budg~t provisions in 1994-95 is 
on National Leprosy Eradication Programme, National Programme for Control of Blindness, 
National Tuberculosis Control Programme and National AIDS Control Programme. It also 
emphasised on setting up of a separate Directorate of Indian System of Medicine and 
Homoeopathy. 
7. The public expenditure in the health sector, both at the Centre and the States put 
together, has been a little over 1.5 per cent of G.D.P. The WHO had recommended that public 
health care expenditure should cross at least 5 per cent of GDP, if equity and universal coverage 
are to be realised. The Committee hopes that concerted efforts will be made to increase health 
expenditure upto 5 per cent of GDP in the next year's budget. 
8. However, the Budget for 1994-95 does not reflect that the Department is heading towards 
Health for All by 2000 A.D. It is noted that more funds are allocated for curative functions 
rather than for prevention of diseases. Programmes of health are uneven in States which may be 
corrected by the efforts of the Department. State should also participate actively in achieving the 
goal of Health for All. However, Department should also provide help to States by releasing 
money on time.

the Department should see that quality of services improves to the satisfaction of be­

this have been under active consideration. It is, however, premature to make an allocation for 
the proposed National Institute.

and when they fall vacant/placement in the surplus cell. The decision to abolish the posts is on 
account of the fact that most of the States have established their own Forensic Science Institute 
Laboratories. 
79. The Committee feels that the reason given for abolition of technical posts is not 
plausible. As per the report of the Department number of medico-legal cases in forensic serology 
coming form the states has not gone down, showing that the demand for such services continues 
to be there. Also, this is not the only work done by the Department. The Department is the only 
indigenous producer of VDRL antigen and different antisera of internationally recognized 
standard; they are earning revenue and saving foreign exchange. Before the decision is 
implemented the Committee feels that assessment of the work done by the Department must be 
undertaken to see whether a massive curtailment of posts would not hamper this work.
