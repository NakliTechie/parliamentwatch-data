I. INTRODUCTION

1.1 One of the most pressing problems that is being faced today by the country is the
alarming rate of growth of population. Population of the country has multiplied by more
than four times during the century. It was 23.8 crore in 1901 Census while it became 84.6
crore in the 1991 Census and is presently expected to be 97.6 crore. India has only 2.4% of
the world land mass while its population constitutes 16% of the present world population.

1.2 The current high population growth rate is mainly due to three major factors as
enumerated below:

1. the large size of the population in the reproductive age group;
2. higher fertility due to unmet need for contraception; and
3. high wanted fertility due to prevailing high Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)

The all India average annual exponential growth rate during the last decade has been of
the order of 2.14% which is marginally lower than 2.22% during the previous decade. As
per the latest 1996 SRS estimates it is 1.85%, but it is still very high when compared with
the developed nations of the world.

1.3. The Committee notes that while there are creditable achievements and hopeful
signs in many States, the overall situation warrants concern. States of Kerala, Tamil Nadu
and Goa have already controlled birth rates and they are now at below the replacement level
of fertility. The situation in many other States like Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh,
Maharashtra, Gujarat, Punjab and West Bengal holds out hope that they could also control
birth rates sufficiently to reach below the replacement level of fertility. The situation in
regard to other large States is not yet so good as to warrant optimism. The Technical Group
on Population Projections appointed by the Planning Commission, reported in August, 1996
that if the trends indicated by the 1981—1991 Census continued, the country as a whole can
be expected to achieve replacement levels of fertility by the year 2026.

1.4 The Secretary stated that according to the UNFPA Population projections estimate
India would have a population of 162 crore by the year 2050 and it will surpass China as the
most populous country in the world.

1.5 Commenting on the Population Programme, the Department stated that there is
growing awareness in the country about the population situation. It is one among the most
critical issues before the country and unless this is controlled, there will be less scope for
optimism in other sectors. Taking note of this fact during the special sitting of the
Parliament coinciding with the celebrations for 50th anniversary of national independence
the population situation was one of the five themes highlighted in the document placed
before the Parliament for special debate. The resolution adopted by the Parliament, stated:

“that a vigorous national campaign be launched by all political parties to combat

economically unsustainable growth of population, recognising that such growth lies
at the root of most of our human, social and economic problems....”

II. NINTH PLAN PROPOSALS

2.1 The main objectives during the Ninth Plan Period, will be (a) to meet all the felt
needs for contraception and (b) to reduce the infant and maternal morbidity and mortality
so that there is a reduction in the desired level of fertility.

As per the Ninth Plan approach paper for achieving the above objectives, the following
broad strategies will be adopted.

(a) The needs of reproductive and child health care at PHC level will be assessed and
area specific microplanning will be undertaken.

(b) Attempts will be made to provide need based, client centred demand driven high
quality, Integrated Reproductive and Child Health Care.

The goal to be achieved by 2002 A.D. (end of Ninth Five Year Plan):—
1. Reduction of Total Fertility Rate to 2.6-2.9 from. the existing 3.5.

2. Reduction of birth rate to 23-24 per thousand population and infant mortality rate
to 50-56 against the current rate of 72 (1996).

3. Increasing the couple protection rate to 51-60% against the current figure of
45.4% (March, 1997).

2.2 The Committee observes that for the Ninth Plan Period which began in 1997-98, an
outlay of Rs. 16750.36 crore of Domestic Budgetary Support (DBS) and Rs. 5296.58 crore
of External Assistance (EAP) for implementing the Family Welfare Programme was

Ill. BUDGET ALLOCATION

3.1 The Committee notes that the allocation to the Department has been substantially
increased from Rs. 1844.81 crores, in B.E. 1997-98 to Rs. 2510.51 crores in the current year.
Out of Rs. 2510.51 crores, Rs. 2489.35 crores, forms the plan component and Rs. 21.16
crores is the non-plan component. The Domestic Budgetary Support is Rs. 1726.85 crores
and the Externally Aided component is Rs. 762.50 crores.

3.2 Commenting on the allocation made to the Department, the Secretary stated that
Family Welfare Programme is a 100 per cent centrally funded programme and this time
realising the importance of the programme, the Planning Commission provided Rs. 660
crores more than that of the last year. The Secretary further stated that out of Rs. 660
crores, Rs. 410 crores will be used for infrastructure maintenance which is a welcome sign.
Asked about the settlement of arrears to the State Governments the Department stated that
on the basis of statements received upto 1st April, 1997 Rs. 192 crores was due as arrears
payable to the States and during the year claims worth Rs. 361 crores was received. It was
also stated that only an outlay of Rs. 79 crores was provided in the Budget and another Rs.
187.11 crores was reappropriated towards liquidation of arrears by cutting down on other
programmes and as on 1-4-1998 an amount of Rs. 287.24 crores remains to be settled.

3.3 The Secretary while deposing before the Committee stated that the Department
made lot of efforts to solve the issue and the matter was taken up at the political level also.
An assurance was given that while determining Annual Plan outlay for the Department, the
Planning Commission would take a realistic view of the arrears and actual level of
expenditure, for maintaining infrastructure and provide this as first charge for the
Department. As a result an amount of Rs. 250 crores has been set apart this year for
liquidating the arrears. The Secretary also expressed his hope that although this will help in
the control of the problem of arrears, it will take a minimum of 2-3 years, to overcome it.

3.4 The Committee feels that non-payment of arrears claims of State Governments is
unfair to the State Governments because it is the responsibility of the Central Government
at whose insistance the infrastructure was created. The Committee finds that this long
pendency of dues has resulted in non-filling up of posts of para-medical and medical staff by
some States so as to reduce their financial liabilities which in turn is hurting the Family
Welfare Programme. The Committee is of the opinion that urgent and concrete steps need
to be taken in this regard.

3.5 The Department also submitted that from last year, the Planning Commission
started indicating the Domestic Budgetary Support (DBS) and External Assistance
Programme (EAP) component separately in order to make full use of external assistance.
However, the Domestically Funded Programme which was otherwise also underfunded was
further squeezed.

IV. RURAL FAMILY WELFARE CENTRES

4.1 Rural Family Welfare Centres are entrusted with the responsibility of implementing
the Family Welfare Programme i.e. Planning, mobilization, monitoring, administration and
supervision in rural areas. 5435 such centres are working at all block level PHCs and the
number has been stagnant at the same level since 1.4.1980. These centres have been marged
with the Primary Health Centres thereafter.

4.2 The budget allocation made to these centres in 1998-99 is Rs. 265 crores which is
slightly higher than that of last year. When the Committee sought to know whether the
increased allocation would be sufficient to maintain the existing centres, the Department
replied in the negative.

4.3 The Committee is of the opinion that it is the duty of the Department to provide
sufficient funds for maintenance of the infrastructure created, as these centres serve the rural
poor and they should not starve for funds.

V. RURAL SUB-CENTRES

5.1 Rural Sub-Centres are the peripheral Health institutions available to the rural
population for the delivery of Health and Family Welfare Services. These centres have been
opened for every 5000 rural population in plain areas and 3000 population in the tribal and
hilly areas. There are at present, 1,31,900 sub-centres functioning in the country out of
which 97757 sub-centres are funded by Government of India as per approved pattern and
the rest are funded under Minimum Needs Programme.

5.2 This year an allocation of Rs. 340 crores has been provided for the centres. When
the Committee sought to know about the opening of new 1500 rural sub-centres as envisaged
during the 1997-98, the Department submitted that the State Governments are not being
assisted for opening new sub-centres during VIII Plan period because there has been no such
scheme and outlay was insufficient to maintain even the existing ones.

5.3 The Committee was informed that from 1997, the Government decided that the
State Governments should be given freedom to use additional Central assistance transferred
to them by the Planning Commission for any item covered by the Basic Minimum Services.
The Committee feels that this is not the correct approach. The Committee apprehends that
there is a possibility of diversion of funds and therefore recommends the revival of the old
pattern. Around 386 sub-centres have been opened by three State Governments during the
year 1997-98.

5.4 The Committee notes that as per 1991 census the shortfall in the sub-centres is
7,727 and by the end of the Ninth Plan it will be 22, 987. The Committee feels that opening
of only 386 sub-centres is not going to serve any purpose. The Committee, therefore, desires
that the Department should urge upon the States to open fixed number of centres each year
and to set apart some provision in their Budget for the purpose.


VI. URBAN FAMILY WELFARE CENTRES/HEALTH POSTS

6.1 Urban Family Welfare Centres provide Family Welfare and Maternity Child Health
Services in urban areas. At present 1499 centres are functioning, of which 208 are run by the
Central sector i.e. Ministry of Railways, Defence, Labour, etc.

6.2 The allocation for the centres has been stepped up from Rs. 50 crores last year to
Rs. 64 crores this year. On the question of methodology adopted for establishment of Health
Posts, the Department stated that the proposed city to be covered under the (Urban
Revamping) scheme should have a slum population of about 40% and the existing staff of
the centres in the area were to be adjusted against the proposed sanctioned posts to be
created under the scheme.

6.3 Asked about the progress made by the Committee to suggest appropriate
programmes to reorganise health posts, the Department stated that the expert group
appointed to formulate MCH and F.W. Services (RCH package) in urban slums has
submitted its recommendations and the Department has formulated the scheme guidelines.

6.4 The scheme broadly envisages establishment of fixed health posts one each for slum
population of 15000, mobile health units for clusters of smaller slums and maternity clinics
run by medical NGOs with a lumpsum grant to the NGOs, to provide all primary health
care and spacing methods of contraception. These facilities will be managed by Municipal
Bodies. The funds for the same would come from the World Bank Project and the scheme
has been given to them for their concurrence.

6.5 The Committee feels that slums are proliferating at a faster pace in the urban areas.
The Committee, therefore, suggests that the Department should get the concurrence of
municipal bodies expeditiously so that the scheme could be implemented at the earliest.

VII. INFORMATION, EDUCATION AND COMMUNICATION (IEC) -

7.1 To provide full and integrated support to the community oriented services for
mother and child health and to encourage adoption of small family norm, IEC activities
were reoriented during the 8th Plan by carrying the Family Welfare Programme towards the
community.

reduced from Rs. 61.80 crores in B.E. 1997-98 to Rs. 55.05 crores in R.E. 1997-98 and

no reduction in the overall allocation for IEC activities. The reduction from Rs. 61.30 crores
in B.E. 1997-98 to Rs. 55.05 crores in R.E. 1997-98 is mainly due to discontinuation of some
of the ongoing activities like Swasthya Mela, IEC for primary school health check-up
programme etc.

7.3 The Secretary while deposing before the Committee stated that action has been
initiated in consultation with National Literacy Mission and Department of Education to

district level, the District Collectors have been requested to formulate the proposals and
forward the same to the Department for approval. The Secretaries, Family Welfare and

The Department of Education has also advised the State Directorates of Education

VIII. TRAINING OF AUXILIARY NURSE MIDWIVES/LADY HEALTH VISITORS

8.1 An allocation of Rs. 25 crores have been provided during the current year. The
amount allocated under the scheme is towards the maintenance of the existing ANM Training
Schools on the basis of approved pattern of assistance. There are at present 509 ANM
(Female Health Workers) Training Schools with an admission capacity of 16000. The
Committee notes that against 1,58,349 sanctioned posts of ANMs, there are 1,33,282 LHVs,
only 18409 LHVs are in position.

8.2 When Committee sought to know the steps taken by Department to fill up the large
vacancies that are existing, the Department stated that the appointment of ANMs and LHVs
is done by the State Governments, and the Department monitors only the health manpower
availability through quarterly reports, review meetings with the State officials and visits to the
States and UTs. From time to time, the State Governments are reminded to fill up the
vacancies to provide quality services at the field levels.

8.3 The auxiliary midwife is the most effective functionary in the system to man the
Primary Health Centres, Rural Family Welfare Centres and Health Posts. The Committee,
therefore, feels that the Department should impress upon the State Governments to fill up the
posts at the earliest. The Committee feels that keeping such an important post vacant for a
long time, will hurt the programme and quality of services may also be effected at the field
level.

IX. VILLAGE HEALTH GUIDE SCHEME

9.1 An allocation of Rs. 10 crores has been made for the scheme this year. When the
Committee sought to know the details about the Expert Committee constituted to suggest
ways for making use of Health Guides more meaningful, the Department submitted that the
expert Committee is expected to submit its report in August, 1998. The Committee hopes that
an early decision on the expert Committee recommendations will be taken by the
Department.

X. STERILISATION BEDS SCHEME

10.1 For providing facility for tubectomy operation in hospitals run by Government, local
bodies and Voluntary Organisations where such cases could not be admitted due to non-
availability of beds, a maintenance grant of Rs. 4500 per bed per annum is paid to each
institution subject to the condition that a minimum 75/60 tubectomy operations per bed are
achieved. At present, 3170 beds are functioning.

10.2 An allocation of Rs. 1.7 crore has been made this year, against a provision of Rs.2
crore made last year. The Committee notes that around 16,45,367 tubectomy operations,
4,62,274 laproscopic female sterilisation, 2514 No-scalpel Vasectomy have been performed
during 1997-98.

10.3 The Department submitted to the Committee that with a view to increase the male
acceptance of family planning Government of India has recently launched a programme for
popularising the new method of vasectomy known as the No-scalpel Vasectomy. This project
consists of training of 1,500 doctors in different parts of the country in the conduct of the No-
scalpel Vasectomy.

XI. SOCIAL MARKETING OF NIRODH

11.1 For social marketing, a provision of Rs. 80 crore has been made in B.E.
1998-99 as against a provision of Rs. 54.05 crores in 8.8. 1997-98.

11.2 The Department contended that the actual expenditure incurred under the head
during the last year was Rs. 65.36 crore, that is why higher provision has been made this

to strengthen the social marketing where availability of contraceptives is a problem.

XII. REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH PROJECT

12.1 “Reproductive and Child Health” programme was launched in 1997-98. The main
objectives of the programme are (a) improve the performance of the Family Welfare system
in implementing policy change; (b) strengthen the quality and effectives of a defined
essential package of services, and improve project implementation through the application of
performance based criteria for continued funding.

12.2 There has been a steep hike in the budget provision for ‘Maternal and Child
Health’ from Rs.450.10 crores in B.E. 1997-98 to Rs. 758 crores in B.E. 1998-99, justifying
the increase for the programme to achieve the objectives of the Reproductive and Child
Health (RCH) Programme the Department submitted that all interventions of Fertility
regulation and Material and Child Health (MCH) have been integrated. Under the ongoing
Child Survival and Safe Motherhood (CSSM) Programme, the focus was on integrated MCH
services and establishment of First Referral Units (FRUs) for providing emergency obstetric
care services at sub-district level health facilities. The input provided under CSSM
Programme was the equipment kits. However, under the RCH Programme the focus will be
on strengthening maternal care at all levels by:

(i) strengthening essential obstetric care by providing contractual staff viz. additional
ANMs in the remote/tribal/backward areas specially in the backward States of
Assam, Bihar, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Orissa, Nagaland, Rajasthan and Uttar
Pradesh, Staff Nurse:at PHC level etc.


8

(ii). enhancement of RCH facilities in PHCs by providing of infrastructure upgradation,
drugs support and contractual trained manpower.

(iii) facilities of MTP and IUD insertions are also strengthened in PHCs and Sub-
centres.

(iv) strengthening and enhancing of essential obstetric care at FRU level by provision of
Lab. Technician, Anaesthetist, drug support and provision of equipment in
remaining sub-district hospitals.

(v) assisting States in conducting ‘diploma Courses’ in Anaesthesia.
(vi) strengthening 24 hour delivery services in PHCs.
(vii) strengthening the new born care service by providing equipments.

(viii) a special programme for detection and treatment of Reproductive Tract infection

12.3 The department further submitted that the Programme also aims at improving the

been left out of the planning process e.g. urban slums, tribal population and adolescents.
The Committee approves the idea. However, the Committee feels that there should be a
monitoring Committee comprising of representatives of Central/State/Local bodies and
people’s representatives of the concerned area for effective implementation of the
programme. The Programme also envisages enhanced role and participation of NGOs and
Voluntary Organisations, practitioners of Indian System of Medicine. Panchayati Raj system
will have a greater role in planning, implementation and assessment of client satisfaction.

12.4 The additional amount under the Programme will be utilised for the additional
inputs brought under the RCH Programme like drugs, equipments, NGO participation, ISM.
Special Project on urban slums, tribal areas and adolescents, referral services, upgradation of
infrastructure, contractual staff and strengthening facilities under Local Capacity
enhancement component of the programme.

12.5 The Committee observes that out of the total budget of the department, 1/3rd of
the budget has been allocated to this programme. The Committee hopes that the department
would be able to achieve the objectives as envisioned in the programmes when sufficient
allocation has been made.

XIII. THE CONSTITUTION (SEVENTY-NINTH) AMENDMENT BILL, 1992

13.1 The Bill seeks to amend the Directive Principles of State Policy to provide that the
State shall endeavour to promote population control and to include to the Fundamental
Duties a commitment to promote and adopt the small family norm. The Bill also proposes
that a person shall be disqualified for being chosen as and for being a Member of either
House of Parliament or the Legislature of a State, if he has more than two children. The Bill
will, however, have prospective effects.

13.2 On the steps being taken by the Government to ensure an early passage of 79th
Constitutional Amendment Bill, the department stated that the Minister of State for Health
and Family Welfare (Independent Charge) desires that the Constitution (Seventy-ninth
Amendment) Bill needs to be reconsidered by the Government. The matter is accordingly
being submitted to Cabinet for directions.


9

13.3 The Committee is constrained to note that despite the Committee submitting its
report on the matter in 1995, no decision has been taken so far. The Committee is deeply
anguished to note that the bill is being reconsidered.

XIV. NATIONAL POPULATION POLICY

14.1 The Draft National Population Policy prepared by the Swaminathan Committee,
was presented to the Parliament in June, 1994. However, the final document has not yet
been finalised.

14.2 Asked about the latest position, the Department stated that the National
Population Policy was approved by Cabinet on 20th November, 1997, with directions to
table the Policy in both Houses of Parliament. On account of the two Houses of Parliament
being adjourned sine-die on the date on which tabling of this Policy document was scheduled
in the business of two Houses, followed by the dissolution of Eleventh Lok Sabha, the
Policy could not be tabled in Parliament. The Direction of the new Government have been
sought on the contents of the National Population Policy. The Minister of State, Health and
Family Welfare desires to consult experts before a view is taken.

14.3 The Committee notes that it is quite a long time since the draft policy has been
submitted to the Government. The Committee feels that necessary consultation with the
experts may be completed soon to enable the department to table the policy in the next
session of Parliament.

14.4 In view of the total neglect of Family Planning and population control, the
Committee feels that it deserves urgent attention, and the policy is a step in the right
direction. The Committee desires that all the political parties should evolve a consensus
approach in the matter.

XV. FREE DISTRIBUTION OF CONVENTIONAL CONTRACEPTIVES

15.1 An allocation of Rs. 92 crores has been made for the current year, as against
Rs. 109.90 crores in B.E. 1997-98. When asked to explain the reasons for reduction, the
Department stated that the actual expenditure incurred during the year was
Rs. 80.66 crore, this was due to the measures taken to avoid unnecessary wastage of
condoms. The Department also stated users of oral pill increased by 20.6% whereas the
condom users remained the same.

15.2 The Committee was also informed that a new scheme for allowing the field level
workers for charging service fee of Re. 1/- for packet of 10 condoms from the beneficiaries
and allowing the field level workers to retain the proceeds is proposed to be implemented
throughout the country.

15.3 The Committee feels that the scheme should be implemented in the-current year
itself, as it will not only promote the use of condom at the field level but in turn reduce
wastage.

XVI. INVOLVEMENT OF VOLUNTARY ORGANISATIONS

16.1 The allocation for involvement of other Departments and Voluntary Organisations,

XVII. ACCURACY AND AUTHENTICITY OF DATA

17.1 The Committee was informed by the Secretary that the rate of Growth of
Population in 1996 as per (SRS) estimates is 1.85. On being questioned about the accuracy
of the figures, the Secretary stated that from 1996 a National Family Health Survey have
been started at the District Levels and figures are obtained from that survey which is
authentic. The Secretary also stated that this will be done every year. The Committee
welcomes this idea.

XVIH. COMMITTED LIABILITY

18.1 The Committee in its 60th Report had recommended that the department should
make a thorough review of the schemes and identify the committed liability. In this matter,
the Committee considered the Report of the Tenth Finance Commission. The Tenth Finance
Commission have observed as under:

“Although the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their terms of reference in
deciding the cut-off date for transfer of committed liability on account of plan scheme,
the incremental liabilities that would arise in 1997-98 on account of 8th Plan scheme
completed in the next two financial years would have to be provided for. The
Commission are, therefore, of the view that the Planning Commission may consider
providing for the maintenance of such schemes till 1999-2000 in the Plan itself as was
done for the schemes of the two Annual Plans of 1990-91 and 1991-92.”

18.2 The Committee in this connection heard the views of the Secretary, Department of
Expenditure last year to clarify the position. He informed the Committee that even though
communications were sent to various Ministries, the Ministries could not identify the
committed expenditure on time. Accordingly a lumpsum amount has been provided under
Demand No. 31. The Secretary also assured that if required more funds for this purpose will
be provided at the RE stage. He also stated that the Departments may approach the
Department of Expenditure for their share.

18.3 The Department submitted that a proposal to shift the committed expenditure
under Family Welfare Programme to the Non-Plan budget was moved to Ministry of Finance
in February, 1997. In response, Ministry of Finance suggested that instead of transferring to
Non-Plan budget, the maintenance expenditure be transferred to the State Government’s
budget in consultation with the Planning Commission. Accordingly, a letter was addressed to
the Planning Commission seeking their advice on the issue in July, 1997 but no response has
been received from them so far. The issue of transfer of committed liabilities to the State
budget was included in the agenda for the fifth meeting of the Central Council of Health and
Family Welfare. The Council felt that the time then was not opportune to transfer the
liabilities to States.

18.4 The Committee views with concern that the Finance Ministry has not honoured
such clear cut commitment made before the Parliamentary Committee. As the
recommendations of the Tenth Finance Commission have been accepted by the
Government, it is the commitment of Government too. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department of Family Welfare should get the assurance implemented
without any delay.
