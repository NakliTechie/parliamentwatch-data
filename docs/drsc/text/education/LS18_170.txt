VIII.      BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA (BSY)
 
RECOMMENDATIONS /OBSERVATIONS
 
8.5         The Committee expresses its serious concern about the way the Balika Samridhi Yojana is being implemented. 
Scheme launched in the first year of Ninth Plan, i.e. 1997-98 had to be recast after two years of its implementation.  In
the third year, the scheme had to be reviewed again and recast.  The Committee is perturbed to note that Tenth Plan has
also completed three years but the air of indecisiveness still hangs around the Scheme meant for the welfare of the girl
child.  The Committee is left with no other alternative but to conclude that the scheme suffers from inherent drawbacks,
which continues to be there in spite of review of thescheme being done not once, but on two occasions.  The Committee
doubts whether the funds released so far have reached the targetted beneficiaries.
 
ACTION TAKEN
The scheme launched in 1997 was providing Rs. 500/- each, in cash, to the mother of girl child. The scheme was
recasted so that girl child must receive benefit. Rs. 500/- is now deposited in interest bearing accounts in Banks or Post
Offices which girl child receive at the age of 18 years and remain unmarried. In addition, scholarship ranging from Rs.
300/- from class I to Rs. 1000/- for class X is paid to the girl child.
 
              During 2004-05 an amount of Rs. 39.75 crore have been released to 16 states /UTs who submitted Utilization
Certificates for the grant released in past years. The states have submitted details of accounts opened in the Banks/ Post
Offices and details of cash certificates given to beneficiaries. However few states like Bihar and UP have not submitted
details of Utilization. These states are being reminded regularly.

9.4  Committee’s attention has been drawn by the audit report No.2 of 2004 (for the period ended March 2003).  The
audit has pointed out failure of the Department to monitor the utilization of grants totaling Rs. 39.58 crore given to
NGOs and local bodies for construction of hostels for working women that rendered the expenditure infructuous. This
clearly indicates that no effective monitoring mechanism exists under the Scheme .  The Committee would like to have
the feedback in this regard.
 
ACTION TAKEN
Realizing the difficulties in the monitoring of the Scheme throughout the country, from July, 2003, the
Department has made it mandatory that the proposals for construction of WWH (the initial proposal as well as those for
subsequent instalments) are to be routed through the State Government accompanied by an Inspection Report by a State
DWCD official.  Subsequent instalments are being released only on receipt of physical and financial progress reports
through the concerned State Governments.  The utilization of grants totalling Rs.39.58 crore released for Working
Women Hostels has been taken up with the concerned StateGovernments for reporting the status and initiating action to
ensure completion of incomplete hostel buildings and their proper utilisation.

XII.       SHORT STAY HOMES
 
RECOMMENDATIONS /OBSERVATIONS
 
12.1  NGOs are funded under this scheme to setup Short Stay Homes for those women who are victims of marital
conflict, family mal-adjustment etc.   The Scheme provides funds for suitable  rented accommodation for at least 30 in
one Home.   The Committee observes that achievement level both in physical and financial terms has been satisfactory
during the Tenth Plan.   The Committee, however, observes that number of Short Stay Homes (299) is too small in a
country like India.    Not only this, these Short Stay Homes are not evenly spread across the country.   Maximum
numbers of Short Stay Homes are in Tamil Nadu (42), followed by 30 each in Orissa and West Bengal.   Out of the total
allocation of Rs. 15.74 crore during 2004-2005, Rs. 2.89 crore were sanctioned in Tamil Nadu.   States like Himachal
Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Meghalaya and Sikkim do not have even one short stay home.   The Committee feels that there is
a need for persuading such states to take benefit of the scheme of Short Stay Homes.    It cannot be that these states are
not having needy women to rehabilitate themselves at Short Stay Homes.   CSWB through whom funds are released
should play a more pro-active role in this regard.
