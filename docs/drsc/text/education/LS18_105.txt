I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The development of women and children to their full potential is an essential component in the country’s total
human resource development.  In its capacity as a nodal agency looking after the advancement of women and children,
the Department of Women & Child Development formulates plans, policies and programmes, enacts/amends legislation
affecting women and children and guides and coordinates the efforts of both Governmental and non-Governmental
organizations working in the field of Women and Child Development.   Apart from this, the Department also
implements certain innovative programmes for women and children in the areas of employment and income generation,
welfare and support services, gender sensitization, training for employment and income generation and awareness
generation.  All these efforts are directed to ensure that women are empowered both economically and socially and thus
become equal partners in national development alongwith men.
1.2       For a holistic child development, the Department implements the Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)
which provides an integrated package of services comprising supplementary nutrition, immunization, health check-up
and pre-school and non-formal education and health and nutrition education.  The ultimate objective of all these
programmes is to ensure that children grow and live in a healthy and secure environment.
II         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       Total outlay for the Department during Ninth Plan is Rs.7810.42 crores. Plan allocation for 2001-2002, the last
year of Ninth Plan is Rs.1650.00 crores which represents a step-up of 22.2% over the RE allocation of previous year
(Rs.1350.00 crores). The Committee has been informed that this increase does not match the projected demand of the
Department which was Rs.2776.79 crore. Higher allocation was required for expanding the existing programmes and
also for starting new programmes, i.e. National Nutrition Mission, new schemes for women during the Women’s
Empowerment Year. However, due to lesser allocation, the Department has to restrict its programmes within the
available funds.
2.2       The Committee observes that during 2000-2001 also although the Department had projected a requirement of
Rs.2034.14 crore, an amount of only Rs.1460.00 crore was made available which was reduced to Rs.1350.00 crore at
the RE stage. What is more disturbing is that expenditure reported upto 13.3.2001 is only Rs.1199.67 crore. Situation
was virtually the same during 1998-99 and 1999-2000. The Committee had pointed out that maximum level of
expenditure during 1998-99 was in the month of March (against a total plan expenditure at Rs.1131.84 crore, figures for
March stood at Rs.474.52 crores) The Committee has noticed that there has been slight improvement during 1999-2000.
Against RE  allocation of Rs.1249.86 crore, expenditure reported upto 31.1.2000 was Rs.998.34 crore which went up to
Rs.1244.79 crore by the end of financial year i.e. 31.3.2000.
2.3       The Committee has been informed that in order to spread out the expenditure throughout the year, regular review
meetings were undertaken at various levels in the Department. Frequency of the meetings of the Project Sanctioning
Committee was also increased for expeditious clearance of the projects. The Committee is, however, constrained to
observe that these corrective measures have not proved to be of much help as large gaps still continued between the
issuance of the sanctions and the actual release of fund due to the following reasons:-
non-submission of utlisation certificates by NGOs and State Governments.
Non-furnishing of Pre-Stamped Receipts (PSR) and the Bonds duly signed by the recipient organizations, despite
repeated reminders.
The Committee views this with serious concern. The Committee recommends that the Department should vigorously
pursue fulfilment of required conditions both with the defaulter NGOs and States. The Committee is of the view that
stringent action needs to be taken against such NGOs and further grant should not be released in case of non-submission

III        INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS) (GENERAL)
3.1       The ICDS Scheme aims at providing an integrated package of services to the children below 6 years, pregnant
women and nursing mothers. The services include supplementary nutrition, immunization health check-up, referral
services, pre-school and non-formal education and health and nutrition education. The Scheme is a centrally sponsored
scheme with funds allocated to State Governments/UT Administrations for delivery of services at grass root Anganwadi
Centres. At present the scheme is operational in 4384 blocks and expected to cover 4550 blocks by March, 2001 and
5171 blocks by March, 2002.
3.2       The allocation of ICDS for 2001-2002 is Rs.1198.00 crore against a requirement of Rs.1307.00 crore.  Last year,
the Committee was informed that under the revised ICDS Scheme, 10 Mini Anganwadi Centres can be opened in lieu of
a full-fledged Anganwadi so that small hamlets/villages in tribal blocks having less than 300 population and situated in
far-flung areas can be accommodated. The Committee notes that proposals for opening of 21,1017 and 80 mini-
Anganwadi Centres have been received from Mizoram, Manipur and Assam respectively, which are under consideration
of the Department.  The Committee recommends that required procedural formalities should be expedited so that these
mini-Anganwadi Centres are opened at the earliest. The Department should also pursue other concerned state
Governments in this regard.
3.3       It has been reported that various studies have proved the efficacy of ICDS Scheme with reference to its impact
on Infant Mortality Rate. As per the figures about 17 States made available to the Committee, IMR among the ICDS
beneficiaries has been found to be significantly lower in comparison with general population as per the Sample
Registration Survey (SRS) (1998), National Family Health Survey-II (1998-99) and National Council of Applied
Economic Research (NCAER) (2000-2001). While inclined to agree with the reported figures, the Committee is
perturbed to note that situation is quite alarming in the two major States as indicated below-

IV        WORLD BANK ASSISTED ICDS
World Bank provides credit to centrally sponsored ICDS Projects. During 2000-2001, a provision of Rs.180.00 crore
was made for implementation of ICDS-II Project in Bihar, M.P., Chattisgarh, Jharkhand and A.P. and ICDS-III Project
in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharasthra, U.P. and Rajasthan. However, this provision was reduced to Rs.140.00 crore at the
RE stage.  Besides other reasons, the States  being not in a position to utilise all the funds due to carry-over balance of
earlier years has been cited as the main reason.   The Committee views with concern an expenditure of only Rs.48.00
crore upto 15.1.2001. Latest figures  reveal reported expenditure of Rs.105.88 crore upto 13.3.2001. The Committee is
not satisfied with the uneven spread of expenditure Rs.57.88 crore seem to have been spent in a period of two months
against an expenditure of Rs.48.00 crore in more than nine months. The Committee recommends that the Department
should initiate remedial measures at the earliest in view of proposed enhanced allocation of Rs.220.00 crores under
World Bank Assisted ICDS in 2001-2002.

VI        CRECHES/DAY CARE CENTRES FOR CHILDREN OF WORKING MOTHERS/AILING MOTHERS
6.1       The Scheme is being implemented by Central Social Welfare Board (CSWB) through voluntary Social Welfare
Organisations and by two other national level organizations, namely, Indian Council for Child Welfare (ICCW) and
Bhartiya Adim Jati Sevak Sangh (BAJSS). 12,470 Creche units are being run covering around 3.11 lakh beneficiaries.
6.2       Last year, the Committee had noticed that due to unspent balances available with the implementing agencies as
on 1.4.99, budget provision of Rs.20.50 crore was reduced to Rs.17.00 crore at the RE stage. An amount of Rs.15.49
crore was utilised upto 31.3.2000. The Committee finds that situation has not improved during 2000-2001. A lower
provision of Rs.18.50 crore was made due to unspent balances of about Rs.4.00 crore available with CSWB. Funds
released upto 13.3.2001 is Rs.10.74 crore.
6.3       The Committee takes serious view of the fact that major unspent balances are with CSWB. Out of total 12,470
Creches being run, CSWB is handling 9364 Creches. Besides that, 374 creches have been withheld by CSWB, reasons
for which are not known to the Committee. The Committee would like to have full details about these crèches, statewise
alongwith action taken by CSWB so far. The Committee, in view of the fact that higher allocation of Rs.21`.95 crores is
proposed for 2001-2002, would also like to be apprised about the other alternatives contemplated by the Department in
the event of CSWB failing in full utilisation of funds.
VII       CONDENSED COURSES OF EDUCATION FOR ADULT WOMEN
7.1       Under this scheme, grant is given to NGOs for conducting courses of two year duration for preparing candidates
for primary, middle and matric level examinations and one year duration for matric-failed candidates.
7.2       The Committee has been informed that an unspent balance of Rs.6.36 crores was available with CSWB as on
31.3.2000. Decision to consider grant for Middle and Higher level courses to only those NGOs engaged in educational
activities led to reduced number of eligible organizations resulting in release of only Rs.12.95 lakhs for 60 courses
during initial months of 1999-2000. However, as per a decision taken on 20.7.2000, institutions engaged in women
welfare activities (as being done earlier) were also considered for funding. As a result, during the period (July-
Dec.,2000), more than 200 courses were sanctioned and Rs.1.50 crores utilised.
7.3       The Committee observes that against an allocation of Rs.1.50 crores under ‘Condensed Courses for Women
Education’, expenditure figures upto 13.3.2001 are shown as only Rs.18.00 lakhs. The Committee feels that unspent
balances were adjusted during 2000-2001 also. This is borne out by the fact that due to non-appointment of regular
Chairpersons in some of the State Boards, regular work suffered resulting in non-settlement of their accounts. All the
State Boards have now been directed by CSWB to settle the accounts of NGOs on priority basis so that past liabilities
may be cleared and unspent balances, if any, may be adjusted in further releases. The Committee would like to be
enlightened about the progress made in this matter. The Committee strongly feels that accounting procedure for this
programme requires to be made fool-proof and transparent. On no account unspent balances should be allowed to be
lying with the implementing agencies, be it CSWB/State Boards/NGOs. The Committee is of the view that the scheme
of ‘Condensed Courses of Education for Adult Women’ needs to be evaluated by an independent agency. Achievements
made by candidates prepared under this scheme in primary, middle and matric level examination should be a component
of this evaluation.

IX        HOSTELS FOR WORKING WOMEN
9.1       The objective of the scheme is to provide cheap and safe hostel accommodation to working women, women
getting training for employment and girl students studying in post school professional courses.  Allocation of Rs. 9.00
crore has been proposed for 2001-2002.  The Committee notes with concern that there has been an expenditure of only
Rs.4.81 crores upto 13.3.2001 against an allocation of Rs. 7.02 crores during 2000-2001.  There has been shortfall in
achievement of Physical Targets also.   Only 1275 beneficiaries have been reported against a target of 2500
beneficiaries.  The Committee is dismayed to find that out of 861 hostels sanctioned, only 613 hostels have been
completed so far.  248 hostels are incomplete which include cases where assistance upto 90% has been released.  The
Committee is shocked to learn that response is still awaited from the concerned State Governments  about status
position of Working Women Hostels asked for by the Department in September, 1999, followed by reminders in
November, 1999 and November, 2000.  The Committee feels that the matter should be taken up at a higher level. 
9.2       Instances have also come to the notice of the Committee that in some States Working Women Hostels are not
functioning well.  All types of complaints are there against them.  The Committee hopes that Area Officers designated
by the Department, besides pursuing the cases of incomplete hostels, would also look into such complaints.  Ideal
position would be if a mechanism can be evolved whereby area MP/MLA is also involved in such exercises.

XI        SUPPORT TO TRAINING AND EMPLOYMENT PROGRAMME (STEP)
11.1     The programme, launched in 1986-87, consists of a series of action-oriented projects intended to enhance the
productivity and income-generation of women in the traditional sectors such as agriculture, animal husbandry, dairying,
fisheries, handlooms, handicrafts etc.  Women beneficiaries are organized into viable and cohesive groups or co-
operatives.  The Programme is implemented through Public Sector Undertakings and Voluntary Agencies.  Since, the
inception of the Programme, about 4, 91, 795 women have been covered under 96 projects launched in the states of AP,
Bihar, Gujarat, Haryana, HP, Kerala, Karnataka, Orissa, MP, Maharashtra, Manipur, Tamil Nadu, Tripura, UP and West
Bengal.
11.2     Under this programme the plan allocation has been increased from Rs.13.00 crores in 2000-2001 to Rs.18.00
crores in 2001-2002.  Justifying the increase ,the Department has stated that the number of projects being received and
approved has increased to a great extent as compared to previous years.  The allocation will be utilized for sanctioning
of new projects and giving instalments for second and third year of the projects sanctioned in the previous years.
11.3     The Committee notes that an expenditure of Rs. 9.13 crore has been reported upto 13.3.2001 against an
allocation of Rs. 13.00 crore.  The Committee has been informed that under the scheme, about 70% of the projects
implemented so far have been in the dairy sector and there are more than 6000 women’s dairy co-operatives in the
country now.  While appreciating the impact of STEP on dairy farming initiatives taken by women, the Committee feels
that women should be encouraged to involve themselves in other sectors like animal husbandry, fisheries, handlooms,
handicrafts, khadi and village industries, sericulture etc.  as envisaged under this programme.

the products of NGOs.

XIII      RASHTRIYA MAHILA KOSH
13.1     The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) has been given a one time corpus of Rs. 31.00 crore which is being used as a
revolving fund to provide loans to poor women mainly through NGOs  well versed in credit management.  It also
channelises loans through women development corporations and co-operative societies.  The Committee has been
informed that since its inception on 30.3.93, upto 31
st January, 2001, RMK has been able to sanction credit limits of Rs.
94.54 crore through 820 NGOs to benefit 3,93,345 women.  The Committee observes that out of Rs. 94.54 crore

13.2     Last year the Committee was informed that the total provision in 1999-2000 would remain unutilised as the EFC
proposal for augmenting the corpus fund of RMK was still under examination.  The Committee is dismayed to note that
there has been virtually no progress during 2000-2001 as the proposal still remained to be finalized and no expenditure
against an allocation of Rs. 3.00 crore has been incurred upto 13.3.2001.  The Committee finds that during 2001-2002
also in view of uncertainly about approval of the proposal for augmentation of corpus  funds, a provision of only Rs.
1.00 crore has been proposed. The Committee fails to understand the reasons for undue delay in taking a final decision
in the matter and recommends that the Department should make serious efforts to get clearance for EFC proposal during
2001-2002.

XV       INDIRA MAHILA YOJANA (IMY) (RENAMED AS INTEGRATED WOMEN EMPOWERMENT
PROGRAMME)
15.1     IMY was launched on 20.8.1995 initially in 200 blocks.  IMY is a strategy to create an organisational base in all
parts of the country for women to come together and analyse their needs and understand the processes of fulfilling their
needs through existing developmental programmes of the states and Central Government.   The Committee has been
informed that  a proposal to recast and expand Indira Mahila Yojana (IMY) from the existing 238 blocks to 650 blocks
by the end of the Ninth Plan was under consideration.  A   BE of Rs.18 crores was accordingly kept in 2000-2001 which
was reduced to Rs.2.21 crores at the RE stage due to non-finalisation of the proposal.
15.2     The Committee notes that the Cabinet Committee of Economic Affairs have given the approval on 27.2.2001 for
restructuring of IMY into an Integrated Programme for Women’s Empowerment and to expand it from the existing 238
blocks to 650 blocks by the end of Ninth Plan.  The Committee has been given to understand that Mahila Samridhi

Yojana has also been merged with IMY and the MSY account holders would be given a choice to either close their
accounts or convert the same into Post Office Savings Bank Account.  An allocation of Rs. 19.50 crore has been
proposed for 2001-2002 which may be further stepped up depending upon the pace of expenditure.
15.3     The Committee observes that under IWEP women are proposed to be given an opportunity  to plan and execute
small and localized initiatives through a provision made for community oriented innovative intervention.  While
appreciating the objectives of IWEP, the Committee has a word of caution.   Experience has been that due to there being

XVI     NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
16.1     The National Commission for Women has the mandate to safeguard the rights and interests of women and to
review all Central and State laws relating to or affecting women.  It also receives petitions from women for redressal of
their grievances.  The Committee has been informed that a higher provision of Rs. 5.00 crore has been made in 2001-
2002 for activities to be undertaken as part of Women’s Empowerment Year, 2001 and for additional sanctioned posts.
16.2     The Committee notes that NCW received 5268 complaints during 2000 which were referred to the concerned
State Women Commissions/State Departments where State Women Commissions are not established.  The Committee
was given to understand that inquiries conducted by NCW do not have the desired impact due to lack of powers given to
NCW at present.  The Committee notes that there is a proposal to enhance the powers of the Commission as given to
NHRC.  The Committee would like to be enlightened on the progress made on this issue.  The Committee would also
like to be apprised about the procedure for selection of members of NCW.
16.3     The Committee finds that NCW is presently reviewing 39 Central Legislations affecting women with a view to
remove provisions discriminatory to women.  So far 21 legislations have been reviewed by NCW.  The Committee
would like to have an idea about follow-up action taken, if any, after review of 21 legislations by NCW.

Development is also represented in the Committee.  The Committee would like to be apprised about the
