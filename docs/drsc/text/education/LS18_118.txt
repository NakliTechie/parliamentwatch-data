REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy was set up by the Government of India as an
independent Department in March, 1995 under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.  The main objective of the
Department is to provide focused attention for the proper development and  propagation of ISM&H, namely Ayurveda,
Siddha, Unani, Homoeopathy, Yoga and Naturopathy and to fully utilise the potentials of these systems.
II          PERFORMANCE  DURING  NINTH FIVE  YEAR  PLAN
2.1       The thrust areas identified for intervention during the Ninth Plan period were as indicated below:-
Effective use of ISM&H systems for health for all and for better patient care system.
-
                      Upgradation of medical educational facilities and improvement in standards of ISM&H medical education.
-                      Focus on research and development efforts
-                      Increasing availability of raw material
-                      Setting up of National Institute for each system.
-                      Development/Strengthening of pharmacopoeial  laboratories and Strengthening of State Government
pharmacies
2.2       Keeping in view the thrust areas, the following schemes were prepared by the Department  for implementation
during the  plan period:-
-
                      Scheme for strengthening of Pharmacopoeia Committee on ISM
-                      Scheme of grant-in-aid for  development of ISM&H UG colleges
-                      Scheme of grant-in-aid for Re-orientation for in-service training programme

2.4       From the above table, the Committee infers that  the  Department has fully utilised the allocated funds during the
first two  years of the Plan. Thereafter, the trend of BE allocation being reduced at RE stage and that too remaining
under-utilised started. The Committee was given to understand that tremendous increase in the Plan allocation for 2000-
01 as compared to previous year's allocation, put a strain on its absorption capacity. The Committee observes that
instead of there being any improvement in 2001-02, the last year of Ninth Plan, situation has worsened. The Committee
strongly feels that although the Department succeeded in getting enhanced allocation by projecting higher requirements
meant for expansion of ongoing Schemes and launching of many new Schemes, the Department failed to achieve both
the physical and financial targets during 2000-01 and 2001-02. The Committee notes that a large number of new
schemes were proposed to be launched during Ninth Plan period. However, so far as their implementation is concerned,
performance has been dismal.   In the last year of the Ninth Plan, i.e.2001-02, as many as eleven new schemes were
there, out of which some were yet to be finalised due to procedural bottlenecks and in the case of others, due to late
approval, schemes were yet to take off.
2.5       The Committee observes that as many as nineteen new schemes have been identified by the Department for
initiation during the Tenth Plan period. Scheme of CGHS Expansion in dispensaries is one of such Schemes. The
Committee is, however constrained to note that this Scheme was proposed to be launched during the Ninth Plan.  
However, for reasons unknown to the Committee, the Scheme was dropped during 1999-2000.  The Committee is
surprised to note that this Scheme is again included in the list of new schemes  to be launched during Tenth Plan.   In
contrast, fate of Schemes like 'Health for all through preventive and promotive Programme of ISMH', 'Speciality clinics
on ISMH',  ‘Indian Homoeopathy Pharmacy Council' and 'ISM Pharmacy Council’ is not known. The Committee hopes
that proper planning and preparatory exercise with regard to finalization and approval in respect of all these proposed
new schemes must have been done before making budget provision for the same in 2002-03. The Committee would like
to be enlightened in this regard.
III.      BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
            3.1       The total allocation both Plan  and Non-Plan taken together provided to the Department for the year
2001-2002 was Rs. 165.56 crores which was reduced to Rs. 134.84 crores at the RE stage.  However, the
Committee observes that while there has been minimal reduction in the non-Plan allocation at RE stage from
Rs.45.56 Crores to Rs. 44.84 Crores,  Plan allocation at the RE stage was reduced drastically from Rs. 120 crores
to Rs. 90 crores.  This year the Department has been allocated  Rs. 150 crores under Plan and Rs. 50.86 crores
under Non-Plan.
3.2
              The Committee notes that the Department has been given an increase of about 25% over the last year.  Asked to

systems will not develop and the purpose for which the Department has been created will get defeated.  The Committee,
is, therefore, of the opinion that the Department should make all out efforts to utilise the fund  earmarked for the
purposes  for which they are meant.

4.4       On the question of misleading advertisements on Ayurvedic medicines appearing in newspapers/magazines
noticed by the Department and preventive action taken by the Department , the Committee was informed that a meeting

Advertisement) Act and Rules, 1954. Drugs Control Cell in the Department is also checking misleading advertisements.
Besides that, Monitoring  Cell is going to be activated with Adviser (Ayurveda) being asked to submit quarterly report
to the Secretary.
4.5       The Committee notes that in spite of a number of initiatives being taken by the Department, not much

improvement seems to have been made. This is evident by the fact that no specific action-taken reports on 160

cover 57 publications including 30 national publications in Hindi, English and other regional languages and the
advertisements will be forwarded to all the major states and UTs.  The Committee appreciates the proposed  initiative.
4.7       The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee informed that the Department has decided to establish a
state-of-the-art Ayurveda Hospital, beyond Sarita Vihar,  Delhi with specialized treatment facilities, for  which it has
paid the cost of 4.5 acres of land to DDA.It was proposed to  establish the hospital  in collaboration with the private
sector and the Department has invited `expression of interest’.  The Committee notes that this proposal seems to have
been initiated during 2000-01 and an expenditure of about Rs.217 lakhs was incurred during 2001-02. The Committee
feels that scrutiny of expression of interest may be completed soon  and steps may be taken to establish it at the earliest.
4.8       Elaborating on Ayurveda and its impact on other countries, the Secretary informed the Committee that there is
resurgence of interest in our system, as people prefer gentler form of treatment, plant based medicines without adverse
side effects.  In this regard, Gujarat Ayurveda University has entered into collaboration with several institutions abroad
for the development of Ayurveda teaching and research and an MOU has been signed with the Govt. of Russian
Federation.  Hungary has recognized Ayurveda and 40 Ayurvedic drugs are already in  use and have requested for
technical support for starting  Ayurveda teaching in three universities, the Secretary added.
4.9       The Secretary further informed that some problem was noticed in U.K., where Ayurveda system was
downgraded to category III implying thereby that Ayurveda has no scientific basis.   To dispel the doubts, a scientific
presentation was made last year before the House of Lords Sub-Committee headed by Lord Walton which resulted in  
Ayurveda being  placed in  category for single drugs. South Africa has also decided to amend their law to accept
Ayurveda medicines and they also propose to start under-graduate and post-graduate course in Ayurveda.
4.10     Further elaborating, the Secretary stated that a  number of medical schools in the USA are receptive to the idea
of introducing a capsule course on Ayurveda for sensitizing their medical students.   The World Health Organisation
recently held a consultation for finalization of guidelines for establishing Panchkarma centres in the countries of South
East Asian region. This will help establishment of Panchkarma centres in these areas. A consultation on methodology
for research and evaluation of traditional medicine was held in Hong Kong in which USA,UK, Germany, Japan, China
and several other countries participated. The Consultation was arranged to decide methodology for research in
traditional medicine which could provide wider acceptance in the participating countries.
4.11     The Committee notes that the Department has taken adequate steps to popularise the system of  Ayurveda in
different countries.  The Committee hopes that such  initiatives will continue in  future also.
4.12     The Secretary informed the Committee about the efforts being made by the Government for the popularization
of ayurvedic system of medicine in foreign countries.  While the Committee notes the efforts made by the Government
in this direction, but observes that adequate measures for providing ayurvedic system of treatment to  public at large and
to the CGHS beneficiaries in particular are not adequate.  The Ministry was asked about the measures taken for
promoting inter alia
 ayurvedic system of medicine by the Government to the interested beneficiaries of the CGHS.  In
response to that, the Ministry has stated that apart from Arya Vaidashala Kottakkal, Kerala, there are 32 other
institutions/centers imparting ayurvedic treatment in different cities of the country and the same are also recognized
under CS(MA) Rules for medical attendance and treatment of all Central Government employees and members of their
families.  However, the Delhi unit of  Arya Vaidashala Kottakkal, has not been recognized so far.
4.13     The Committee further notes that ayurvedic hospital at Lodhi Road is under CGHS, Department of Health.

Perhaps, the decision about recognition of other ayurvedic institutions/centers for CGHS beneficiaries lies with the
Department of Health.  The Department of Indian System of Medicine and Homoeopathy was created in 1995.  It is,
therefore, surprising that the hospitals/other institutions of the Central Government providing ayurvedic treatment still
continue to be under the Department of Health.  The Committee fails to understand the rationale behind this.
4.14     As regards the standard of facilities at the Lodhi Road ayurvedic hospital, the Department has stated that the
facilities for ‘panchkarma’ treatment  at ayurvedic hospital at Lodhi Road  reportedly are not up to the mark due to
various reasons including unfilled up posts.  The Department in its reply has added that this hospital is under CGHS,
Department of Health.  The reply of the Department speaks volumes of the kind of step-motherly treatment that the
system of ayurvedic treatment is receiving by the Department of Health.  The Government’s stated policy for
propagating of Indian system of medicines appears to be only on paper and apparently no serious efforts are being made
by the Government to provide an alternative system of medicine, particularly to the CGHS beneficiaries.
4.15     The Committee feels that adequate steps have not been taken by the Government to provide ayurvedic treatment
facilities to the CGHS beneficiaries.  At the same time, no serious efforts are being made to strengthen the system as the
ayurvedic hospitals/centers continued to be under the Department of Health.  The Committee would, therefore,
recommend that all facilities of ayurvedic treatment should be transferred within a period of three months  to the
Department of Indian System of Medicine and Homoeopathy so as to ensure their proper management. Further, the
existing Government CGHS dispensaries/hospitals should be upgraded and private hospitals/dispensaries such as Arya
Vaidashala Kottakkal, Kerala, and its Delhi branch may also be brought under the facilities of CGHS beneficiaries.  The
Committee also feels that the Indian System of Medicine and Homoeopathy should be headed by a professional
adoctor(vaidya) at Secretary level rather than a civil servant who may not be adequately familiar with what kind of
policies need to be formulated in this area.  This will also facilitate taking decisions for cultivation of herbs which are
the basic raw-material for manufacturing  of all these medicines.

              For Homoeopathy, the plan allocation was Rs. 8.27 crores in BE 2001-2002. It has been increased to Rs. 10.21

5.3       The Committee notes that  in this connection cadre review committee had suggested creation of 44 posts in
various categories including teaching and non-teaching.  However, the Department proposes to create 47 posts.  The
Committee would like to know the reasons for creation of 3 extra posts by the Department on their own when an expert
committee has threadbare discussed the matter.  The Committee would also like to know whether these extra posts are
on the teaching side or  the non-teaching side.

VII       YOGA
7.1       The plan allocation for `Yoga and Naturopathy’ which was Rs. 5.99 crores in BE 2001-2002 got reduced to Rs.
3.83 crores at the RE stage.  The allocation has been increased to Rs. 7.60 crores in BE 2002-2003.  Clarifying this

Commission is expected shortly, higher Plan allocation of  Rs. 4.50 crores has been made for Morarji Desai National
Institute of Yoga during 2002-2003. The Committee is of the view that construction work of MDNIY should start
without any further delay.
7.2       The Department further stated that as less number of new projects were found viable for implementation and
keeping in view the opening balance of Rs. 12.15 lakhs, the plan allocation for Central Council for Research in Yoga

Naturopathy has been given higher Plan allocation from Rs. 0.80 crores last year to Rs.0.90 crores this year due to
increase in its activities.
7.3       The Committee had, last year, recommended that yoga be made part of regular curriculum in all the schools all
over the country.  The Department has stated that it had taken up with the Ministry of Human Resource Development  to
incorporate teaching of yoga, naturopathy and other Indian Health Traditions in the school curriculum and the Ministry
was reminded on 22.2.2002 to intimate the steps taken in this regard. The Committee feels that no effective steps seem
to have been taken in this regard and  desires that the Department may pursue this issue  more vigorously with the
Ministry of Human Resource Development .
VIII      NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF SIDDHA (NIS, CHENNAI)
8.1       Apprising the Committee about the progress in the case of National Institute of Siddha, the Secretary informed
the Committee that the project was pending for several years  and it  was finally approved in January, 2002.  An
investment of Rs. 47 crores is proposed to be made for PG teaching and research.  The Secretary also informed the
Committee that the expenses will be shared by the Centre and State in ratio of 60:40 in case of non-recurring and 75:25
in case of recurring expenditure.
8.2       On the question of popularizing Siddha  to other parts, the Department has stated that NIS, will help in
popularizing it, as many students from different areas will go in  for UG and PG courses and for research once the
institute is established.  The Committee feels that it could have been one of  the ways to popularise the system.    The
Committee is of the view that by publishing the efficacy of siddha medicines in vernacular  newspapers and also by
establishing more siddha dispensaries, the system can be made more popular.
IX        MEDICAL EDUCATION
9.1       At present, there are about 400 ISM&H colleges in the country.  However, standard of education in some of the
institutions is far from satisfactory and they are nowhere near the norms fixed by Statutory Bodies.  The Committee was
informed that high priority was given to this area.  About 56 colleges were assisted all over the country particularly for
teaching equipment, books, Journals and access to computers.

9.2       Commenting about the standards of ISM&H education and mushroom growth of ISM&H colleges, the Secretary
informed that a general concern has been expressed in various forums that they are being established without adhering
to laid down standard.  The Secretary stated that whereas the Department continued to support ISM&H institutions to
upgrade their facilities and the Central Council for Indian Medicine and Homoeopathy laid down minimum standards of

appropriate legislative measures in this regard.
X         QUALITY CONTROL OF DRUGS
10.1     Laying down the Pharmacopieal standards and quality control of ISM&H drugs is one of the priority  areas of
the Department.  The Secretary informed that Drug Standardization  received due priority during 2001-02.  Laying of
pharmacopoeial standards and preparation of formularies was also given adequate attention.  Substantial progress was
made in Ayurveda and Homoeopathy  where 40% and 65% pharmacopoeial work respectively has been completed.
Target has been fixed to complete all pharmacopoeial standards by 2005 and 35 laboratories are assisting the
Department in completing pharmacopoeial standards.  Pharmacopoeial Laboratory of Indian Medicine for Ayurveda,
Unani and Siddha and Homoeopathy Pharmacopoeia  Laboratory at Ghaziabad are being strengthened by augmenting
their infrastructure including technical manpower.  The construction work is expected to start in 2002-2003. The
Committee appreciates the steps taken in this regard.  However, the Committee feels that the Pharmacopoieal work
relating to Ayurveda needs to be further expedited, as only 40% has been done. 
10.2     The Committee is constrained to note that BE allocation of Rs. 42.00 lakhs and Rs. 26.oo lakhs for PLIM,
Ghaziabad and HPL, Ghaziabad had to be reduced to Rs. 8.00 lakhs and Rs. 23.00 lakhs respectively at the RE stage.
What is more shocking is that even against this reduced allocation, expenditure reported upto February, 2002 is only Rs.
3.58 lakhs and Rs. 19.79 lakhs respectively for the two laboratories .   The Committee notes that non-creation of new
posts and non-filling of some posts are the two main reasons for under-utilisation of funds. This situation has been
continuing for the last few years. The Committee fails to understand as to how in the absence of required staff, the
primary task of testing/analysis of drugs is being carried out in these labs. The Committee would like to be apprised
about the required and sanctioned strength of posts and vacant posts in both these labs. The Committee hopes that
construction work in both the labs would be started at the earliest.

Manufacturing Practices Rules were notified two years ago and will become law from 23
rd June, 2002 after a grace
period of two years.  The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules have  been amended to make an enabling provision to recognize
private laboratories as public test laboratories to expand the facilities for the testing of ISM&H drugs.  Schedule `K’ of

medicines must be certified by a person having necessary qualifications.

XI        NON-AVAILABILITY OF HERBS AND MEDICINAL PLANTS
11.1     The efficacy of most of the ISMH drugs is dependent upon the use of pure and genuine raw material obtained
from medicinal plants.  However, due to over exploitation, unscrupulous collection and denuding of forests, supplies
have been dwindling. 
11.2     Elaborating on the steps initiated in this regard, the Secretary informed the Committee that non-availability of
raw material of required purity and containing alkoids and essential oils etc. has been engaging the attention of the
Department.    Ministries and Departments of Central Government are separately addressing to conservation, cultivation
and research in medicinal plants areas.  Previously there was no agency to co-ordinate all functions and focus the plants
development for domestic consumption and exports.  Now  a Medicinal Plants Board has been set up under a
Government Resolution to co-ordinate all activities and draw up policy and schemes for all round development of this
sector.  The Board is assisting  various schemes and grant of Rs. 11.70 crores has been released.  The Committee is
happy to note that about 17 States have set up state level Medicinal Plants Board. The Committee desires that the
Department should persuade rest of the States /UTs also to set up medicinal plant board at the earliest to stop
adulteration and to make optimum use of available resources in their respective states/UTs.
XII       PUBLICITY
12.1     The Committee notes that the Department has initiated a number of steps to popularise ISM&H   313 blocks in
the country were selected for implementation of IEC Scheme through various NGOs.  The activities of the NGOs were
to organize community awareness, meetings, training workshops for ISM&H practitioners, health melas, organisation of
Yoga classes in primary schools and training of cultivation of medicinal plants.  Folders on Home remedies in Ayurveda
and Unani were sent to State Govts. for distribution to Panchayats to make the systems more popular at grass root level. 
Health Melas were also organized, in which  free  diagnosis and dispensing of medicines was also arranged.  First ever
comprehensive exhibition AROGYA, 2001, on Indian systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy at Pragati Maidan, New
Delhi from December 15-18, 2001 in association with India Trade Promotion Organisation was organized which was a
great success.  6 NGOs were assisted in North East during 2000-2001.
12.2     The Committee was also informed that the Ministry of Tourism has agreed to propagate Panchkarma in hotels
for which guidelines have been already provided to them.  Ministry of External Affairs has also asked all Missions to
help propagate ISM&H. 
12.3     The Committee notes the steps taken in this regard.  The Committee feels that simply sending the folders on
Home remedies in Ayurveda and Unani to State Govts for distribution to Panchayats will not serve any purpose  unless
the folder is in vernacular language and the instructions printed in the folders are communicated properly. The
Committee, therefore, desires that a demonstration on how to prepare the home remedies may be held at the block level.

II          PERFORMANCE  DURING  NINTH FIVE  YEAR  PLAN
            From the above table, the Committee infers that  the  Department has fully utilised the allocated funds during the
first two  years of the Plan. Thereafter, the trend of BE allocation being reduced at RE stage and that too remaining
under-utilised started. The Committee was given to understand that tremendous increase in the Plan allocation for 2000-
01 as compared to previous year's allocation, put a strain on its absorption capacity. The Committee observes that
instead of there being any improvement in 2001-02, the last year of Ninth Plan, situation has worsened. The Committee
strongly feels that although the Department succeeded in getting enhanced allocation by projecting higher requirements
meant for expansion of ongoing Schemes and launching of many new Schemes, the Department failed to achieve both
the physical and financial targets during 2000-01 and 2001-02. The Committee notes that a large number of new
schemes were proposed to be launched during Ninth Plan period. However, so far as their implementation is concerned,
performance has been dismal.   In the last year of the Ninth Plan, i.e.2001-02, as many as eleven new schemes were
there, out of which some were yet to be finalised due to procedural bottlenecks and in the case of others, due to late
approval, schemes were yet to take off. (Para 2.4)
            The Committee observes that as many as nineteen new schemes have been identified by the Department for
initiation during the Tenth Plan period. Scheme of CGHS Expansion in dispensaries is one of such Schemes. The
Committee is, however constrained to note that this Scheme was proposed to be launched during the Ninth Plan.  
However, for reasons unknown to the Committee, the Scheme was dropped during 1999-2000.  The Committee is
surprised to note that this Scheme is again included in the list of new schemes  to be launched during Tenth Plan.   In
contrast, fate of Schemes like 'Health for all through preventive and promotive Programme of ISMH', 'Speciality clinics
on ISMH',  ‘Indian Homoeopathy Pharmacy Council' and 'ISM Pharmacy Council’ is not known. The Committee hopes
that proper planning and preparatory exercise with regard to finalization and approval in respect of all these proposed
new schemes must have been done before making budget provision for the same in 2002-03. The Committee would like
to be enlightened in this regard.(Para 2.5)
