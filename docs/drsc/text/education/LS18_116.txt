REPORT
I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       Main objectives of the Department of Health are :
(i)
                  to undertake national programmes of health and to intensify measures for the prevention, control and
eradication of communicable diseases;
(ii)                to promote education, research and training in various medical disciplines, to reorient the medical
colleges in the delivery of health services in rural areas;
(iii)               to prevent adulteration of food as well as drugs;
(iv)              to provide primary health care at the doorsteps of the people and to train the required number of
personnel for this purpose;
(v)                to take steps for better implementation of Health Care Programmes for tribal and other weaker sections
of the Society; and
(vi)              to collaborate with member countries of United Nations and International Agencies like WHO, UNICEF
in matters relating to health promotion.
1.2              The Department of Health incurs expenditure either directly or through various institutions and establishments
run by it and by way of grants given to autonomous and voluntary organizations.  Grants to States and Union Territory
Administrations are mainly for Centrally Sponsored Health Schemes being implemented thorough the State
Governments and Union Territory Administrations.  They include national programmes for prevention, control and
eradication of diseases.
1.3              The Department finances, wholly or substantially, the following autonomous Institutions engaged in patient care

II          BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       Plan allocation for the Department of Health for 2002-03 is Rs. 1550.00 crores  showing an increase of 7% over
the Plan allocation of Rs. 1450.00  crores for 2001-02.  On a specific query  about the projected demand of the
Department for 2002-03, the Committee was informed that a plan outlay of Rs. 2009.60  crores was proposed.  
Additional allocation was meant for new initiatives emanating from the draft National Health Policy-2002, such as
distributing drugs to Primary Health Centres, Urban Health, setting up of a Medical Grants Commission.   The
Committee was, however,  given  to understand that the meager increase in 2002-03 would not be sufficient even for
maintaining the tempo of the existing programmes.
2.2       The Committee has observed that during the Ninth Plan period, every year Plan allocation has been less than the
projected demand of the Department, which has been  reduced without a break at the RE stage and even that reduced
allocation continued to remain under-utilised.  The Committee has been informed that major shortfall in expenditure
during the initial years of Ninth Plan was due to non-finalisation of procedural formalities observed in the case of
National Anti-Malaria Programme and National TB Control Programme.   Utilization of funds increased from 55% in 
1998-99 to 88% in    2000-01.   The Committee is, however, not fully inclined to agree with the explanation given by
the Department.   The Committee observes that the trend of under-utilisation has continued during the Ninth Plan  due to
variety  of reasons like non-setting up of required infrastructure  for implementation of various disease control
programmes, non-release of funds due to unspent balances lying with the implementing agencies, slow performance of
district societies, less number of proposals from NGOs, non-completion of required formalities etc.   During 2001-02,
the last year of Ninth Plan, Plan allocation of Rs. 1450.00 crores was reduced to Rs. 1320.00 crores at the RE stage, and
anticipated expenditure at the end of the year is Rs. 1300.00 crores.   The Committee hopes that all procedural
formalities under ongoing  and proposed Programmes/Projects during Tenth Plan have been completed so that optimum
and judicious utilization of allocated funds is ensured right from 2002-03, the first year of Tenth Plan. 
III.       CENTRAL   GOVERNMENT   HEALTH   SCHEME
3.1
              The Scheme was started in 1954 with the objective of providing comprehensive medical care facilities to
Central Government employees and their family members.  The Scheme which initially started in Delhi now covers 23
cities of which Bhubaneswar and Ranchi CGHS cater only to staff of Accountant General posted there and their
pensioners.
3.2
              Rs. 12.00 crores under plan has been  provided to  CGHS for 2002-2003.  The Committee notes that Plan

allocation of Rs. 12.00 crores in 2001-02 had to be reduced to Rs. 9.00 crores at the RE stage.  Explaining the decrease,
the Department replied that the CGHS earlier had proposals for expansion of its services by opening more CGHS
dispensaries which involves  creation of new posts. Fresh proposals for creation of new posts  under CGHS can be
referred to the Department of Expenditure only after the recommendations contained in the SIU Report of November,
1999 are  fully implemented.  However, due to a stay on the recommendations contained in the SIU Report by CAT,
New Delhi, it was not possible for the CGHS to open more dispensaries by creation of new posts and this resulted in
less expenditure during the year 2001-2002. 
3.3       The Committee notes that it has not been possible to carry out the expansion programme of CGHS dispensaries
to new cities as was planned by the Ministry of Health during the Ninth Plan Period, due to the fact that the Ministry of
Finance had not agreed to the creation of new posts of setting up of new dispensaries  pending finalisation of the SIU
report on the functioning of CGHS dispensaries.  The Committee is deeply disturbed to note that provision of medical
facilities to the government employees which is a basic necessity  is trapped into avoidable legal wrangles.  The
Ministry should have taken up the matter with the Central Administrative Tribunal for early resolution of the case(s) so
that the expansion programme of dispensaries was implemented expeditiously.  The Committee, therefore, would like
the Ministry to look into this and submit a report to the Committee within a period of three months  of the action taken
and the present status of opening new CGHS dispensaries area-wise.
3.4       The Committee was apprised that in places where there are no Central Government-run hospitals, the facilities
provided for in-patient treatment to CGHS beneficiaries are woefully inadequate.  Very few beds are reserved for them. 
Consequently, the patients are put to avoidable inconvenience and have to seek treatment through private facilities
which are very expensive.  The Committee, therefore, would like the Ministry to look into this aspect and furnish details
of the facilities available to the CGHS beneficiaries in private-run hospitals for CGHS beneficiaries and measures taken
to augment the same.

view that in addition to regular inspections, surprise inspections will bring out the ground reality.  The Committee
welcomes the decision regarding formulation of guidelines for at least  one inspection of each CGHS dispensary in the

3.7       The Committee notes that as per the procedure laid down , specialist of CGHS/Govts. hospitals/ CMO In-charge
of CGHS dispensaries have to indicate the line of medical treatment/procedure/investigations in the prescription based

Governors, etc.) after the initial advice of a specialist has been obtained the permission letter for such treatment would

do the needful in this regard at the earliest.
IV.       SAFDARJUNG  HOSPITAL
4.1
              The Plan allocation for Safdarjung Hospital for the year 2002-2003 is Rs.42.00 crores against a   projected
demand  of  Rs.57.69 crores.   The Department has explained that out of Rs.42.00 crores allocated for Safdarjung
Hospital during the year 2002-2003, Rs. 37.50 crores are meant for Safdarjung Hospital and Rs. 4.50 crores are
allocated for the recently established college, i.e. Vardhman Mahavir Medical college (VMMC) at Safdarjung Hospital. 
Out of Rs. 37.50 crores provided for Safdarjung Hospital, only Rs. 10.30 crores are for procurement of machinery and
equipment and rest of the amount is meant  for meeting the salary/wages expenses of the staff and other routine
expenditure. 
4.2       The  Committee notes that the required faculty and infrastructure is being made available for the VMMC.   As
the  first session of the new college has already commenced, the Committee  urges upon the Department to take
concerted efforts to make the college fully functional at the earliest so that the students are not put to any problem. 
4.3       The issue of vacant posts in Safdarjung Hospital  has been engaging the attention of the Committee for quite
some time.  The Committee notes that out of total sanctioned strength of 4647, staff in position is 4120.   Latest position
as given by the Department is that the Screening Committee at its meeting held on 4.10.01 had recommended 22 posts
for filling up as per the guidelines of the Department of Personnel & Training O.M. dated 11.5.2001.  To fill up the
above 22 posts, N.O.C.  from Surplus cell has been obtained in January, 2002 and further action for  filling up these
posts is under process.  The whole exercise will be completed by the end of May, 2002.  The Medical Superintendent
has also been directed to undertake a comprehensive review of various posts lying vacant/likely to fall vacant and come
up with a proposal for filling up of additional posts within the overall ceiling prescribed by the Department of Personnel
& Training.  Besides, all out efforts are being made to fill up the other vacant posts by taking up the matter with
Ministry of Finance and other competent authorities on priority.
4.4       The Committee is of the view that procedural formalities should be expedited so that the 22 posts recommended
by the Screening Committee are filled at the earliest.   Besides that,  ongoing comprehensive review of various vacant
posts/likely to fall vacant by the Medical Supdt. Should not be unduly  delayed in procedural wrangles.  The Department
should also impress upon the Ministry of Finance the urgency for filling up the required posts of Safdarjung Hospital
which is the biggest Hospital in the capital catering to the needs of not only local patients but also from the 
neighbouring States.
4.5
              Given the unsatisfactory services being provided by the Safdarjung Hospital, the Committee in its earlier Report
recommended that the Department should resort to strict measures so that employees are made to carry out the duties
assigned to them.  The Department has replied that the employees of the hospital are constantly supervised and
controlled by their supervisors, so as to get the best out of them. Necessary disciplinary/administrative actions like
warning, deduction in salary are being taken against the erring staff.  The Department has also given a list of
disciplinary/administrative actions taken during the past three year against the erring staff .

4.6       On the perusal of the list, the Committee notes that most of the erring employees fall in the category of Para-

conducted on regular basis for them.
V.        DR.  RAM  MANOHAR  LOHIA  HOSPITAL
5.1
              Plan  allocation for Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital, New Delhi in  BE 2001-2002  was Rs.21.00 crores which
has been decreased to Rs.17.20 crores in 2002-2003. Explaining  the decrease in allocation, the Department has
informed that Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital has procured MRI System costing around Rs.7.00 crores during the
year 2001-2002.   Since the hospital has no such project for the year 2002-2003, the plan allocation has been reduced for
the year 2002-2003.
5.2
       The Project for establishing a Trauma Centre at the  Hospital has remained at the proposal stage for a
considerable period.   The Expenditure Finance Committee at its meeting held on 1st June, 2001 has finally approved the
proposal.   Administrative approval and expenditure sanction to the proposal was subsequently issued on the 25 th July,
2001.  The drawings of the project have been submitted to the appropriate civic authorities for their necessary approval. 
The Minister for Health and Family Welfare laid the foundation stone on 31st December, 2001.
5.3       The Committee is happy to note that some progress has been made in this regard during 2001-02 and   finally the
project has started taking shape. However, the Committee is of the opinion that  given the increase in number of road
accidents every year in Delhi and also considerable time taken in getting the EFC proposal, the Department should make
concerted efforts to get the Trauma Centre established within the  fixed time frame i.e. upto 31.3.2004.
VI.       CENTRAL  INSTITUTE OF PSYCHIATRY,  RANCHI
6.1
        The Plan  allocation for the Central Institute of Psychiatry, Ranchi in BE 2001-2002 was Rs.7.00 crores which
was drastically reduced to Rs.2.50 crores at the RE stage. Plan allocation for 2002-03 is Rs. 3.50 crores against the
projected demand of Rs. 8.30 crores made by the Institute.  On being asked to specify the reasons for drastic cut made
in  the allocation, the Department has clarified that the Plan budget of the Department of Health was reduced by Rs.104
crores at RE stage which   had to be apportioned amongst the Institutes and Programmes. The amount of Rs.4.80 crores
for purchase of equipments for the Institute  was sanctioned only in December, 2001, by which time the RE had been
decided and allocation of the Institute was cut due to low expenditure upto that date. 
6.2       The exercise of purchasing the required equipments for the Institute has been continuing for a considerable
period of time.   The Committee has been informed that the amount of Rs. 4.80 crores for purchasing equipments was
sanctioned in December, 2001.   The Committee is, however, constrained to observe that the formal letter conveying the
approval of the Competent Authority in this regard could be issued only on 19
th February, 2002.   It is understood that at
that time the Institute was having an unspent balance of Rs. 3,01,76,510 against which some of the equipments out of
the sanctioned equipments could have been purchased during 2001-02 itself.  The Committee apprehends that this
amount could not have been utilized for want of completion of procedural formalities.   This becomes clear from the
request of the Institute  for increased allocation of  Rs. 8.30 crores for meeting the  expenditure towards purchase of
equipments amounting to Rs. 4.80 crores.   It seems that the Institute will have to wait upto the RE stage as the
allocation of only Rs. 3.50 crores has been made for 2002-03.   The Committee is deeply anguished at this state of
affairs. The Committee, strongly recommends that the Department should suitably enhance the allocation of CIP,
Ranchi so that these equipments which are essential to meet the needs of the patients can be procured at the earliest.
6.2
        The Department has further informed  that a proposal for creation/recreation  of 41 posts at the Central Institute
of Psychiatry based on the reports of the two Study Groups (one under the Chairmanship of Smt. Sunila Basant, then
Joint Secretary in the Ministry and another Expert Group from NIMHANS, Bangalore) has been under the consideration
of DGHS.  Out of these 41 posts, Directorate General recommended for creation of only 24 posts, which are primarily
required for augmenting the Institute.  It was  also indicated by the Ministry  that 13 posts could be abolished to find
some savings towards creation of the 24 posts, the expenditure for which after abolition of 13 posts was computed at
Rs.38.00 lakhs per annum.  The proposal was forwarded to the Department of Expenditure on 17.7.2001, which sought
some additional information in a statement showing cases handled in the last 10 years, posts sanctioned in various

grades and in existence, number of posts now to be created discipline-wise.  The said information has been sent to the
Department of Expenditure through the Internal Finance in  January, 2002 for their approval and the approval is
awaited.
6.4       The issue  of creation of required posts for CIP, Ranchi started with an IWSU study conducted as early as in 
1997.  Thereafter, another study was conducted by NIMHANS IN 2000.   Final outcome has been that out of the
proposal for creation/recreation of 41 posts, only 24 posts have been recommended for creation by DGHS.
6.5       On a specific query about the scaling down of number of proposed posts, the Department has clarified that
austerity  measures and the ban on creation/revival /filling up of posts led to revision of the proposal.   The Committee
expresses its serious reservations on this development.  What is more shocking is that as a bargain for creating these 24
posts, the Ministry has wished for abolishing 13 existing posts.
6.6       The Committee strongly feels that the position is very bad in CIP, Ranchi.   Creation of required posts apart,
even sanctioned posts are not filled up.    Out of 19 teaching  posts of Associate/Assistant/Professor, only 6 are filled
up.   Similarly, out of 24 non-teaching posts, only 9 are in position.   The Committee is of the view that drastic steps
need to be taken by all the concerned authorities so that the Central Institute  of Psychiatry, Ranchi  can provide the
services in the area of mental health care which is very much required in the present scenario.
VII.      ALL INDIA INSTITUTE OF MEDICAL SCIENCES (AIIMS)
7.1
               All India Institute of Medical Sciences was established in 1956 by an Act of Parliament as an institution of
national importance .  AIIMS is conceived to be a Centre of excellence in modern medicine with comprehensive
training facility.
7.2
              Plan allocation for the All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi was Rs.100.00 crores for BE 2001-
2002 which was reduced to Rs.95.00 crores at the RE stage.  However, plan allocation for 2002-2003 has been stepped
up to Rs.105.00 crores.  On being asked about the reasons for reduction in RE 2001-2002, the Department has replied
that the cut was effected because of reduction in the overall plan ceiling of the Department.  The Department has also
informed that AIIMS has asked for an allocation of Rs.124.46 crores for 2002-2003 but keeping in view the pace of
expenditure during the Ninth Plan, a plan ceiling of Rs.105.00 crores has been fixed for AIIMS for the year 2002-2003.
7.3
       The Committee notes that plan allocation of Rs.100.00 crores in BE 2001-2002 was meant for creation of
additional wards, labs and ICUs at Cardiothoracic and Neurosciences Centre for equipping the organ retrieval bank  and
for starting work on the Apex Trauma Care Centre. The Committee fails to understand as to how with the cut in
allocation at the revised stage in 2001-2002, the proposed work in AIIMS as mentioned above would have been
completed.  The Committee would like to be apprised of the progress made in this regard.
7.4       The Committee has been informed that the proposal for setting up of an Apex Centre for Trauma Care at AIIMS
has been approved at an estimated cost of Rs. 54.14 crores.  The Centre would be multi-disciplinary, which apart from
providing holistic Trauma Care Services would also be used as a role model for other Trauma Centres in terms of
conceptualization, project formulation and operation.  The construction work entrusted to HSCC, New Delhi, has started
in the month of January, 2002,  and is expected to be completed by January, 2003.  The Committee is happy to note that
the work of establishing Trauma Centre at AIIMS  has finally begun and hopes that  it would be completed by the target
date.
VIII.     POST GRAUDATE INSTITUTE OF MEDICAL EDUCATION AND RESEARCH, CHANDIGARH
8.1
       Plan allocation for PGIMER, Chandigarh was Rs.45.00 crores in BE 2001-2002, which was substantially reduced
to Rs.25.00 crores at the RE stage and the same allocation has been made for the year 2002-2003.  Explaining the

 XX      PREVENTION  OF  FOOD  ADULTERATION  PROGRAMME
20.1  The subject of the Prevention of Food Adulteration is in the Concurrent list of the Constitution.  However, in
general, the enforcement of the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954 is done by the State/UT. Governments.  The
Central Government primarily plays an advisory role in its implementation besides carrying out various statutory
functions/duties assigned to it under the various provisions of the Act.
20.2     Four Central Food Laboratories have been established under the Act, which work as appellate laboratories for
the purpose of analysis of samples of food lifted by the Food Inspectors of the States/U.Ts. and local bodies.  There are
82 Food Laboratories under the administrative control of various State/Union Territory Governments and Local Bodies.
20.3
             On being asked about experience of the Department in respect of administration of Prevention of Food
Adulteration Act and the Rules made thereunder, the Department has informed that some of the problems being faced
relate to inadequate enforcement at the level of the States.  There is a shortage of trained manpower, especially at the
level of food inspectors. Number of samples drawn for test and analysis has declined as also the cases in which
prosecutions have been launched or conviction obtained.  Capabilities of the State Food Laboratories are also required
to be augmented so that they can properly undertake analysis of contaminants.
20.4           The Department has also informed that the Government of India has been trying to assist the State Governments
by training of State Laboratory analysts and food inspectors and by supply of equipments to the laboratories.  The States
have been requested to increase their manpower for proper enforcement of the Act; to increase the budget to provide
proper facilities in the states laboratories and to utilize  the equipments provided by the Government of India properly;
to conduct investigational market surveys in a systematic way, with particular reference to items of mass consumption
and to keep a proper data bank of the results. 
20.5     From the feedback given by the Department, the Committee is left with no alternative but to believe that the
situation is indeed very grim so far as implementation of the   Prevention of Food Adulteration Act is concerned.   It
seems that with the load of so many Disease Control Programmes, this aspect of health management is  not getting the
due impetus from any State in the country.   The Committee is therefore, of the view that drastic measures are required
to be taken.   The Committee recommends that an overall review of Prevention of Food Adulteration Programme with
special focus on status of State laboratories, requirement of more labs and necessary staff therefor should be initiated
without any further delay.

have constituted State  Health Malaria Control Societies.   Remaining States are yet to follow suit.   The Committee
enjoins upon the Department to vigorously  pursue with the defaulter States.  (Para 12.9)
XIII      KALA-AZAR CONTROL  PROGRAMME
The Committee  hopes that with the beginning of the  Tenth Plan, things will improve in Bihar.   The
Committee, however, is of the  opinion that mere provision of enhanced Central assistance will not be  sufficient.   What
is required is intensive  monitoring in co-ordination with the State Govts. of Bihar and W. Bengal.  (Para 13.2)
XIV.   JAPANESE ENCEPHALITIS (JE)
            The Committee finds it rather surprising that there is no separate budget provision available for JE prevention  
and control.   The Committee strongly feels that States tackling these problems out of their  resources  does not  make
any sense due to their generally poor   financial position.   The Department must be fully aware about lack of motivation
on the part of most of the States in implementation of health Programmes in spite of central funding.  Diversion of need-
based support for  outbreak containment out of resources available under NAMP  cannot be considered a sound
strategy.   What is required is a well-planned programme with separate funding for control of JE. (Para 14.3)
XV.      NATIONAL  T.B. CONTROL  PROGRAMME
The Committee finds that achievement level of Physical Targets  under Sputum Examination and Sputum
Positive Detection has also  been much  below the fixed targets  during the Ninth Plan period. (Para 15.2)
The Committee recommends that the Department should pursue the submission of Utilisation Certificates with
all concerned State Govts. (Para 15.3)
The Committee would like to emphasise  that participation of Public Representatives in formal functions will not
achieve the desired results.   What is required is an exercise to obtain the feedback from the local Public Representatives
regarding the various ongoing activities  of the Programme in their areas.   Besides that, there is a need for having
random surveys/surprise inspections by Central teams.  Last year the Committee had desired that the Department should
take action for evolving a more effective monitoring mechanism.   The Committee strongly feels that mere taking note
of the advice regarding random surveys/surprise inspections does not serve any purpose.  The Department has to take
initiative  in this direction without any delay. (Para 15.5) 
The Committee strongly feels that in a country like India which accounts for nearly 1/3
rd of the global TB
burden, where every year there are approximately 22 lakhs new TB cases, and where one  person dies from TB every
minute and more than 5 lakhs every year, all the inherent  flaws in the Programme at all levels need to be eliminated at
the earliest. (Para 15.6)
XVI.    NATIONAL  LEPROSY  ERADICATION  PROGRAMME
The Committee notes that there has been optimum utilization of funds under the Programme during Ninth Plan.  
Against total allocation of Rs.388.00 crores, reported expenditure figures are Rs. 384.26 crores.  Similarly,  achievement
level of Physical Targets as per the parameters of Case Detection, Case Treatment and Case Discharge have been many
times more. (Para 16.3)
The Committee observes that there is no dearth of funds under the Programme and a large number of NGOs are
also involved with quite a few reported to be doing good work.  Special focus under 2
nd Phase of World Bank Assisted
Project is also there on the eight high endemic States.   The Committee, however, feels that Bihar being  the most
affected State,  there is a special need for extensive monitoring of   implementation of all the components of the Project
in Bihar.   The Committee is of the view that all efforts should be made to provide training in leprosy services to all
General Health Services Staff, specially in the most affected districts  of the State.   Campaigns for enhancing public
awareness about the disease would also contribute a lot. (Para 16.4)

XVII    NATIONAL  PROGRAMME  FOR  CONTROL  OF  BLINDNESS
The Committee recommends that all these proposals should be activated  at the earliest so that remote and tribal
areas do not remain deprived of the benefits of National Programme for Control of Blindness.  (Para 17.2)
The Committee strongly recommends that the Department should closely monitor the implementation of
different components of the National Programme for Control of Blindness in States where achievement level continues
to be very low.   Every effort should be made to ascertain the inherent constraints being faced by  the implementing
agencies.  (Para 17.5)
The Committee would like to be apprised about the findings of the Survey regarding prevalence and causes of
blindness and impact of the Blindness Control Project in these districts. (Para 17.6)
XVIII        NATIONAL  AIDS  CONTROL  PROGRAMME
            The Committee would like to be apprised of the findings of the sample survey. (Para 18.3)
            The Committee notes the efforts made by the Department in expanding the base of NGOs and recommends that
the Department should take up this  issue with other States where NGO activity is not there. (Para 18.4)
The Committee notes that the importance of Reproductive tract infection (RTI) including sexually transmitted
diseases (STD) has increased with the introduction of HIV/AIDS epidemic in the country.  (Para 18.5) 
The Committee is constrained to note that there is a very wide gap between target population covered and
population actually attended the camps. The Committee feels that this low achievement level is a clear indication that
there was a lack of awareness amongst people about the Family Health Awareness campaign.  The Committee,
therefore, recommends that a very extensive publicity  drive should precede the launching of such Family Health
Awareness Campaigns.  Only then maximum coverage would be there.  (Para 18.6)
            The Committee is of the view that there is apparent contradiction between the two clarifications given by the
Department.   As per  the ATN, the World Bank administration has agreed to reduce the requirement for production
capability from 25% to 10% of the tendered quantity.  As per the second clarification, World Bank has raised the past
supply criteria to 50% from 25%.  In the earlier reply, country has been divided into four regions.   However,  second
clarification mentions eight regions.  The Committee would like to be enlightened in this regard. (Para 18.9)
IX        NATIONAL  MENTAL  HEALTH  PROGRAMME
The Committee notes that shortage of trained psychiatric manpower and lack of awareness regarding treatment are
the two main barriers to the provision of mental health services in the country.  The Committee has been given to
understand that about 4000 clinical psychologists,  psychiatric  social workers  and  psychiatric nurses are working in
the country which is inadequate to meet the total needs of  persons  with mental disorders in the country.  The shortage
of psychiatric manpower is felt in all the States, specially in rural areas.  The Committee strongly feels that there is a
need for having systematic evaluation regarding Statewise requirement of manpower.  The Committee also observes
that mental health programmes are being implemented in a varying degree by different States.   The Committee has
been informed that the District Mental Health Programme is to be enlarged and made more effective covering the  entire
country during the Tenth Plan.   The Committee feels that there  is an urgent need for expanding the mental health
services.  While  appreciating the substantive allocation of funds for this programme for 2002-03, the first year of Tenth
Plan, the Committee would like to emphasise that there should be regular and extensive monitoring at all levels to
ensure optimum and judicious utilization of funds.       (Para 19.3)
 XX      PREVENTION  OF  FOOD  ADULTERATION  PROGRAMME
From the feedback given by the Department, the Committee is left with no alternative but to believe that the
situation is indeed very grim so far as implementation of the   Prevention of Food Adulteration Act is concerned.   It
seems that with the load of so many Disease Control Programmes, this aspect of health management is  not getting the
due impetus from any State in the country.   The Committee is therefore, of the view that drastic measures are required
to be taken.   The Committee recommends that an overall review of Prevention of Food Adulteration Programme with
special focus on status of State laboratories, requirement of more labs and necessary staff therefor should be initiated
without any further delay.  (Para 20.5)
XXI        DEVELOPMENT OF NURSING SERVICE

               The Committee feels that in a situation when large number of the people of this country particularly living in
remote and far flung areas are outside the reach of Health Care Delivery System, continuing with such a process which
makes the opening  of new Nursing Schools a long drawn one is not desirable.  The Committee, therefore, recommends
that appropriate steps should be taken so that avoidable procedural requirements are done away with.  The Committee
would also like to be apprised about the full details regarding Projects of Nursing Schools/colleges sanctioned and lying
pending (Statewise). (Para 21.2)
XXII    DRUGS  STANDARD CONTROL  PROGRAMME
               The Committee feels that the silence on the part of State Governments in furnishing the requite information is
perhaps due to their inability to  fill up the vacant posts.  The Committee views this very seriously and recommends that
the Department should pursue this matter relentlessly  with the State Governments. The Committee notes that there are
no drug testing laboratories in Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Pondicherry, Andaman & Nicobar
Islands, Chandidgarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Uttaranchal, Jharkhand and Chattisgarh.  Drug testing labs in States in
general except for the labs in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh fall below the required
levels.  The Committee finds that a countrywide auditing of all drug testing laboratories including Govt. testing
laboratories has been initiated.   The Committee welcomes this initiative  and hopes that this will result in proper
assessment of requirements in this vital sector and resultant follow-up action. (Para 22.3)
XXIII      HOSPITAL WASTE MANAGEMENT
On the perusal of the list, the Committee is shocked to find that  in respect of the States/UT  of Bihar, Manipur, 
Rajasthan and Chandigarh no action has been initiated by the State Pollution Control Boards or Pollution Control
Committees against the erring hospitals/nursing homes.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that this matter should
be taken up with the concerned Ministry, without any delay.  (Para 23.3)
XXIV   IMPLEMENTATION OF HEALTH PROGRAMMES IN  NORTH EAST SECTOR
Status of various National Health Programme in North-Eastern States is as follows :
NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR CONTROL OF BLINDNESS:-
         The Committee observes  that Cataract Surgery performance rate  is low in all States except Tripura.   Assam is
one of the lowest performing States in the country with Cataract Surgery rate as low as 162 operations per lakh
population against the national rate of 400. (Para 24.2)
         The Committee notes that State Blindness Control Societies have been constituted so far in the States of
Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura only.   The Committee has been given to understand that eye care
infrastructure is weak in  NE region, particularly  in Assam.   Very few NGOs are located in the region.   Number of
Eye surgeons is also very inadequate.   The Committee feels that in spite of adequate funds  being provided, the
programme is not functioning well.   Non-setting up/non-functioning of District Blindness Control Societies and lack of
NGO involvement seem to be the two major handicaps.   The Committee recommends that the  Department should
vigorously  pursue  the implementation of the Programme with all the NE States. (Para 24.3)
The Committee apprehends that reason for the drastic cut in allocation of funds may be unspent balances lying
with the State Govts.   If that be so, it is  a cause of serious concern.   The Committee would like to be enlightened on
this aspect. (Para 24.8)
NATIONAL AIDS CONTROL PROGRAMME 
The Committee is shocked to find that not a single North-Eastern State has been able to fully utilize the funds
allocated to them and the States of Assam and Tripura failed to utilize even a single penny.  The Committee feels that
there is something seriously amiss in respect of these States and recommends that the Department should make an
enquiry into the matter.  (Para 24.10)
