I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Department of Culture, which was set up in 1985, was part of the Ministry of Human Resource
Development before its transfer to the Ministry of Culture, Youth Affairs and Sports recently.  The Department is
primarily responsible for promoting art and cultural mutual understanding and goodwill and foster closer relations with
foreign countries.  The main functions/activities of the Department can be broadly classified under the following heads:-
 
(i)      Maintenance and Conservation of heritage, ancient monuments and historic sites.
(ii)    Administration of Museums and Libraries.
(iii)   Observing Centenaries and anniversaries of important national personalities and events.
(iv)  Promoting institutions and organisations of Buddhist and Tibetan Studies.
(v)    Entering into cultural agreements with foreign countries and holding of Festival of India            abroad.
(vi)  Anthropology and Ethnology.
(vii) Archives and Archival Libraries.
(viii)  Akademies and National School of Drama
(ix)  Promotion and dissemination of Culture.
(x)    Memorials
1.2       In the coming years, the Department besides, carrying out its normal activities, proposes to focus its attention
particularly with respect to:-
(a)Conservation and preservation of our Cultural Heritage.
(b)                        Scheme on the National Culture Fund.
(c)Linkage between education and culture.
(d)                        Development of a Public Library Movement.
(e)Strengthening of Museums and Art Galleries.
(f)               Promotion of Culture and Dissemination.

II.         BUDGETARYALLOCATION
2.1       The Committee has been informed that against the modest demand of Rs. 1124.40 crores, the Department has
been allocated Rs. 920.41 crores during the Ninth Five Year Plan.  The reduction of Rs. 203.99 crores in the allocation

have been only Rs. 625 crores (i.e. around 68% of the total Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 920.41 crores).  The Department
is not hopeful of getting an allocation of Rs. 295 crores  in 2001-2002 taking into consideration  difficult ways and
means position of the Government.   The Committee recommends that the Department should be allocated the balance
allocation of Rs. 295 crores during the last year of the Ninth  Plan (i.e. 2001-2002) to enable it to carry out its normal
activities and meet its commitments.

3.4       The matter of shortage of Monument Attendants for 3598 Centrally Protected Monuments in the country has
been engaging the attention of the Committee for the last two-three years.  The Committee regrets that the matter has
not yet been sorted out and is still lingering on.  As per the latest information submitted by the Department, it has been
advised by the Planning Commission to identify the monuments for de- protection and transfer them to the State
Archaeology Departments for maintenace in order to reduce its liability.  This suggestion has, however, not been
accepted by the ASI as it constitutes a major policy decision.  Instead a proposal has now been sent by the ASI to the
Planning Commission to create 1564 posts of Monument Attendants for regularising the temporary status of daily
wagers who have been working on the monuments for more than a decade.  In reply to a query as to what is the
experience of the ASI with regard to availability of funds with the State Archaeology Departments, the Committee was
informed that the State Departments of Archaeology are adversely affected because of inadequate allocation of funds. 
In case it is so, the suggestion for de-protection and transfer of the national monuments to State Archaeology
Departments seems to be absurd as the situation will then be worse. 
3.5       The Committee would also like to point out that the original requirement of Monument Attendants as projected
by the Department was 5000.   The latest proposal for creation of 1564 posts of Monument Attendants is meant for
regularising the temporary status of daily wagers.  Approval of this proposal by the Planning Commission would not
result in any increase in the existing strength of Monument Attendants.  The Committee is of the view that the stand of
the Planning Commission would indirectly result in further deterioration of the Monuments.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Planning Commission should adopt a positive attitude in the matter in order to preserve our
national heritage
3.6       The Committee expresses its deep concern over the illegal constructions and nefarious activities around the
protected monuments. The law requires that there should not be any construction within 100 metres of a monument. 
However, it is not being adhered to and in most of the cases, violation takes place.  The Committee is conscious of the
fact that one of the main reasons for these illegal activities is shortage of Monument Attendants. The Committee
wonders whether the various circles of the ASI can be activated in this regard.  They, with better coordination with the
local Administration, may perhaps do a bit in minimising this menance to some extent.

X.        ZONAL CULTURAL CENTRES
The Committee has somehow got an impression that most of the activities and programmes of the Department
are held mostly in cities.  Most of the organisations are also situated in metropolitan cities.  The programmes that are
implemented have hardly anything to do with the rural population.  There is no emphasis on rural and tribal arts.  The
Committee is of the view that there is a need to promote rural and tribal art and culture for a country like India where
majority of the population lives in villages.  Zonal Culture Centres can play significant role in this regard which were set
up to create cultural awareness and to identify, nurture and promote folk art traditions in the rural and semi urban areas
of the country.  In this connection, the Committee feels that recommendations of the Ananthamurthy Committee should
be examined and implemented expeditiously.

XIV.     NATIONAL  CULTURE FUND
With regard to National Culture Fund, the Committee was informed that during the last one year enormous
impetus had been given to the NCF, particularly after 100% tax exemption was declared.  Work on the restoration of the
monumental remains of `Shaniwarwada', the palace of the erstwhile Peshwas has commenced and is nearing
completion.  For the first time, after independence, all encrochments and unauthorised establishments there have been
removed.  This project has been fully funded by NCF.  Apart  from this, a major breakthrough has been avchieved
whereby Companies in the Public Sector have evinced a keen interest to be associated in protecting, promoting and
preserving the culture heritage of the country. The Indian Oil Corporation has launched the 'Indian Oil Foundation' with
a corpus of Rs. 50 crores.  Every year they will give a grant of  Rs. 10 crores.  They propose to take up atleast one major
monument in each State and Union Territorry.  So far, 8 States have been identified.  The Committee feels that there
should be more participation of corporate sector and even the small monuments, which are comparitively lesser known
and important should also be taken up.  The Committee hopes that the NCF will pick up much more momentum in the
coming years.

XVI.      COMMITTED LIABILITY
16.1       The issue of Committed Liability has been engaging the attention of the Committee since 1997-98.  At the end
of the Eight Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission had issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the
Committed Expenditure from Plan to the non-plan side.  A lump sum provision of Rs. 500 crores in the Ministry of
Finance Budget for 1997-98 was provided for this purpose.  As a result of this exercise, however, only a sum of Rs.150
crores was transferred from plan to non-plan for the various Ministries as committed expenditure during 1997-98.  Rest
of the amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable.  The Secretary (Expenditure) while deposing
before the Committee on 27
th March, 1999 had informed that the Tenth Finance Commission had given a breathing time
till 1999-2000 for this transition.
16.2     The Department has now informed that it has reviewed the position regarding transfer of committed liabilities of
the Eighth Plan to the non-plan side. It has been noted that unlike other Ministries and Departments, the programmes
and activities of the  Department of Culture are continuous in nature and most of the plan schemes of the Eighth Plan
can be carried forward to the Ninth Plan.  (without many committed liabilities). 
16.3     The Committee notes with serious concern that while grants for Plan Schemes are getting reduced, non-plan
allocation and expenditure are steadily increasing.  What is happening is that the committed expenditure which should
appropriately be charged to the Pool, is being passed on to the respective Departments which is totally against the
guidelines of Tenth Finance Commission/Planning Commission.  If the committed expenditure is charged to the Pool, it
will not have any burden on the specific schemes of the Department.  Secretary (Expenditure), who appeared before the
Committee last year, was in full agreement  with the stand taken by the Committee.  The Committee is, however,
constrained to observe that in spite of Committee’s repeated efforts/directives to assist the Departments, no progress has
been made in the matter.  It seems that breathing time till 1999-2000 given by the Tenth Finance Commission has not
been availed of by any Department.  There is total lack of initiative on the part of the Departments.  The Committee
again  emphasises that the Department should get this matter resolved without any further delay.  The way the
Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the
Finance Commission and the recommendations made by the Committee.  This tendency, the Committee is constrained
to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.  The Committee again reiterates that the
Department should pursue the matter and report to the Committee about the outcome thereof.
XVII.   GENERAL
The Committee notes with concern that some Departments are not furnishing the required information/papers
and Hindi version thereof sought by the Committee on time and sometimes the papers are not even properly arranged
and indexed.  This results in a lot of inconvenience to the Committee.  In such a situation the Members either get very
little time or no time at all to go through the papers and actively participate in the deliberations of the Committee. The
Committee, is therefore, constrained to observe that the communications sent to the Ministries or the Departments by
the Secretariat on behalf of the Committee should be given due attention and all efforts should be made to furnish the
requisite information/ paper to the Committee expeditiously  and within the time limit.

The matter of shortage of Monument Attendants for 3598 Centrally Protected Monuments in the country has
been engaging the attention of the Committee for the last two-three years.  The Committee regrets that the matter has
not yet been sorted out and is still lingering on.  As per the latest information submitted by the Department, it has been
advised by the Planning Commission to identify the monuments for de- protection and transfer them to the State
Archaeology Departments for maintenace in order to reduce its liability.  This suggestion has, however, not been
accepted by the ASI as it constitutes a major policy decision.  Instead a proposal has now been sent by the ASI to the
Planning Commission to create 1564 posts of Monument Attendants for regularising the temporary status of daily
wagers who have been working on the monuments for more than a decade.  In reply to a query as to what is the
experience of the ASI with regard to availability of funds with the State Archaeology Departments, the Committee was
informed that the State Departments of Archaeology are adversely affected because of inadequate allocation of funds. 
In case it is so, the suggestion for de-protection and transfer of the national monuments to State Archaeology
Departments seems to be absurd as the situation will then be worse.  (Para 3.4)
            The Committee would also like to point out that the original requirement of Monument Attendants as projected
by the Department was 5000.   The latest proposal for creation of 1564 posts of Monument Attendants is meant for
regularising the temporary status of daily wagers.  Approval of this proposal by the Planning Commission would not
result in any increase in the existing strength of Monument Attendants.  The Committee is of the view that the stand of
the Planning Commission would indirectly result in further deterioration of the Monuments.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Planning Commission should adopt a positive attitude in the matter in order to preserve our
national heritage. (Para 3.5)
