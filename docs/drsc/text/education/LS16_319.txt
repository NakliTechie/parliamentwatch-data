Department of Sports 
Budgetary Allocation 
Observations/Recommendations. 
1. The Committee notes that Lakshmibai National Institute of Physical Education, 
Gwalior has been given Rs.55.00 crore in BE 2020 -21 i.e. and enhancement of Rs5.00 crore 
(10%) from Rs.50.00 crore in RE 2019 -20. The Committee notes the role played by LNIPE, 
Gwalior in promoting the programmes of Physical Education and Sports in the country and 
suggests that efforts may be made by the Ministry to help the Institute for preparing aspirants 
desiring to excel in the field of Sports at National and International level. 
(Para-5.3) 
Action Taken 
The observations of Committee have been  noted for compliance. It is mentioned here that 
under able guidance of this Ministry, LNIPE, Gwalior has become an important institute in 
the field of Physical Education and Sports over the years. It became a “deemed University” in 
1995 and it has an off -campus in North East Regional Centre at Guwahati (Assam). LNIPE 
has achieved many successes in recent past. The Institute was accredited by A++ with CGPA 
of 3.79 by the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC) in 2017 and it has 
been graded as category –I deemed to be University by University grants commission (UGC) 
in 2018. The students strength of the institute was around 1400 during academic session 
2019-20.The institute has experienced faculties who run various courses at UG / PG / 
Diploma in the field of Physical Education and Sports. All the requirement of funds of the 
institutes is met by the Ministry through grant in aid which is released to them based on their 
requisition from time to time. It is expected that LNIPE being an Autonomous b ody to 
generate revenue manage its expenses in long run. 
 
Observations/Recommendations 
2. The Committee is of the view that National Sports Federations (NSFs) play an 
important functions relating in promotion and development of various Sports at national a nd 
international level. The committee finds that the allocation for assistance to various 
federation has been reduced from Rs.300.85 crore in RE2019 -20 to Rs.245.00 crore in 
BE2020-21 and it must be ensured that the decrease in allocation does not impact t he 
activities being performed by various federation. The committee feels that the Department 
should judiciously utilize the allocation and organize more and more events as they help in 
identifying talent. 
(Para-9.8) 
Action Taken 
Budgetary allocation of Rs. 245 crore for FY 2020 -21 for scheme of Assistance to NSFs is 
adequate and sufficient for preparation and training of Indian athletes and teams. If required 
the Ministry will seek additional funds at RE stage. Further expenses on training of elite 
athletes for their customized training are being taken care of under Target Olympic Podium 
(TOP) Scheme under the ambit of National sports Development Fund. The funds will not be a 
constraint for Indian athletes preparing for participation in international sports e vents 
including forthcoming Olympic Games. 
Observations/Recommendations 

 
3. The Committee also notes that despite various codes and guidelines being in place, all 
is not well in respect of functioning of NSFs, which adversely affects sportspersons and 
eventually the sports. The Committee recommends that regular elections should be held and 
the Federations function towards promotion of sports. The Committee further recommends 
that the functioning of the Federations should be regularly monitored and audited to  ensure 
that they stay corruption free and act impartially for promoting sports. 
(Para-9.9) 
Action Taken 
The Government insists upon holding of elections on regular basis by the recognised NSFs as 
per the Model election guidelines issued by the Government.  It is incumbent upon the NSFs 
to hold elections regularly and before the expiry of the tenure of existing office bearers. 
Instructions in this regard have been issued to NSFs from time to time. 
From the year 2010, the Government has introduced a system of  annual recognition 
procedure for the NSFs, under which recognition is granted to NSFs on year to year basis. 
The annual recognition of the NSFs, which fail to comply with the Government guidelines, 
are not renewed making them ineligible to receive assista nce from the Government under 
various schemes. The accounts of the NSFs are to be audited by the Chartered Accountants, 
who are in the panel of Comptroller and Auditor General of India (C&AG). Also, the NSFs 
receiving grants of Rs. 1.00 crore or more are subject to the audit by C&AG. 
In case of failure by any NSF to adhere to the guidelines issued by the Ministry from time to 
time, appropriate action against such NSFs are taken by the Ministry which include 
suspension / de -recognition / non -renewal of annua l recognition, etc. Also, whenever a case 
of any gross irregularity in an NSF is brought to notice, action as per rules is taken by the 
Government.

Department of Youth Affairs 
Observations/Recommendations 
1. The Committee feels that the Youth of the country play a very vital role and gain 
valuable experience for their future while working as volunteers. The Committee feels that 
there is steep cut in BE 2020 -21 for National Youth Corps as  Rs.90 crore have been given 
against projected demand of Rs.300.50 crore which needs to be enhanced and recommends 
that the Department should seek appropriate allocation for increasing the number of 
volunteers. 
(Para-5.6) 
Action Taken 
As far as NYC Scheme is concerned, a demand of Rs.300.50 crore was made in BE 2020 -21 
with the proposal for deployment of 15000 volunteers with monthly honorarium of 
Rs.15000/- per volunteer and the cost of volunteers training and administrative expenses. The 
enhancement of nu mber of volunteers was proposed due to the proposal of deployment of 
additional one volunteer in each 1115 blocks of 115 aspirational districts as well. 
However, budgetary provision has been made in BE 2020 -21 only for Rs.90.00 crore i.e. for 
deployment of  13251 volunteers with monthly honorarium of Rs.5000/ - and the cost of

6. The Committee observes that National Sports Development Fund (NSDF) is a corpus

sports. It has given Rs.52.81 crore to such organization / institutes / universities in the year 
2017-18 for various sports activities. The Committee is constrained to note that in the 
financial year 2019-20, contribution from Private and Public Secto r to NSDF is only RS 5.00 
lakh, and there is a steep fall in the contribution from Private and Public Sector in NSDF 
which is a cause of concern. The Committee is of the view that PSUs being government 
undertakings their funds belong to Government and shou ld be directly sought under CSR 
from Public Sector Companies or else the Government should take it directly from PSUs into 
its financial resources and then give it to the Department of Sports. 
(Para-16.8)

administrative Ministries of the concerned PSUs / PSBs to generously contribute to NSDF 
from their CSR funds.
