III.              INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
3.13   The Committee notes that ICDS takes the major share of the budgetary allocation of the Department.  Success
of the scheme largely depends on the functioning of Anganwadi workers (AWWs).  The Committee is, however, not
satisfied with the plight of these workers.  The meagre pecuniary benefits and poor facilities do not attract people to
work as AWWs.  The Committee is surprised to see that their role still remains undefined; they are asked to do all
kind of odd works such as census operation, distribution of drugs etc.  This puts extra pressure on them and their
main role is diluted. 
 
Action Taken
 
            The role and responsibilities of the AWWs and AWHs are well defined in the ICDS Scheme.  However, over the
years, AWWs and AWHs have been increasingly called upon to discharge duties in relation to various other
schemes/programmes of Government of India, State Governments and local bodies, in addition to the task assigned to them
under ICDS Programme.  In view of this, a Core Committee comprising  of Secretary, D/WCD as Chairperson and
representatives from concerned Departments and State Governments has been set up to review the role and responsibilities
of AWWs and AWHs.  Three meetings of the Committee have already been held so far.
 
V.      CRÈCHES  & DAY CARE CENTRES
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
5.6.   The Committee is happy to note that the Department proposes to merge the National Crèche Fund Scheme  with the
Scheme of Creches/Day Care Centres to take care of the problem  of reduced cash flow due to fall in interest rates from the
corpus of the National Crèche Fund.   The Committee feels that this is the right decision also because objectives of both the
schemes are identical and implementation of two parallel schemes was only resulting in confusion and uneconomical use of
funds.  The Committee emphasizes that a final decision in the matter should be taken at the earliest so that implementation of
the scheme does not suffer.
 
Action taken

XII. SWADHAR
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
12.3    The Committee is not happy to note that the scheme was limited to only ten States/UTs and the Central Social
Welfare Board.    It appears that the scheme was not getting good response despite the three tier monitoring
mechanism.  The Committee recommends that all out efforts should be made to popularize such useful scheme in all
States/UTs.  A wide publicity campaign should be launched across the country to highlight the activities and benefits
under the scheme.
 
Action Taken
           
During the current financial year, detailed advertisements, inviting applications for projects under the scheme of
Swadhar, have been brought out in the national newspapers all over the country to popularize the scheme.  Besides, senior
officers of the Department have been organizing workshops all over the country.  So far workshops had been held in
Hyderabad, Patna, Chandigarh and Bangalore to encourage and guide the organizations to submit proposals under the
scheme.  The Department is fully confident that budget earmarked for the scheme will be fully utilized.

X.   NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
10.5     The Committee notes that some very important laws need immediate attention viz., Domestic Violence Bill, Dowry
Prohibition Act, Indecent Representation of Women, Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act 1956 etc.  The   Committee  
believes that    these amendments were long over due.  It, therefore, recommends that immediate steps be taken to complete
consultations and other formalities for bringing drafts of these laws before Parliament with no loss of further time.
 
Action taken
 
Protection from Domestic Violence Bill 2002
 
            
After re-examining the provisions of the Protection from Domestic Violence Bill 2002 (which was pending before the
13th Lok Sabha and subsequently lapsed on dissolution of the House) and taking into consideration the recommendations made
by the Parliamentary Standing Committee in its 124th Report on the said Bill, the views expressed by the Prime Minister’s Office
(Vide PMO’s U.O. Note dated 19.8.2004) and women voluntary organizations, the Department has drafted a Bill on the subject. 
The various provisions to be included in the Bill have been approved by the Minister of Human Resource Development.  The
draft cabinet Note was circulated to the concerned Ministries such as Ministry of Home Affairs, Department of Justice,
Department of Legal Affairs etc. for their comments.  The comments of Ministries/Departments  have been received.  The draft
Bill has been referred to Ministry of Law and Justice for examination and vetting.  Their views are awaited.
         
The status of review of the 4 women-specific Acts administered by the Department is as under:
 
The Commission of Sati (Prevention) Act, 1987
 
The recommendations made by NCW and the Task Force on amendments in the Act were circulated to the State
Governments and Union Territory Administrations and the concerned Ministries/Departments  have been compiled.  HRM
has approved the recommendation of the Task Force on Women and Children.   A Note for the Cabinet  has been  circulated
to other  relevant Ministries/Departments for comments.
 
The Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986
 
The Task Force on Women and Children suggesting for amendment of the definition  of Indecent  Representation of
Women has been  referred to the Ministry of Law and Justice.  Their views are awaited.

The Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956 Amendment Bill
 
This Amendment Bill has been approved by Hon’ble HRM on 25.8.2004 and has been circulated to all concerned
Ministries in Government of India. We have received comments from all of the concerned Ministries.  The Bill will now be
drafted by the Law Ministry and, thereafter, a note will be placed before the Cabinet for approval.
 
Provision in Indian Penal Code relating to Rape
 
NCW had recommended certain amendments in the provision of the Indian Penal Code, 1860; the code of Criminal
Procedure 1973; the Indian Evidence  Act, 1872, relating to the offences of Rape.  Ministry of Home Affairs has circulated a
draft note for Cabinet containing proposed amendments to these provisions based on Law Commission’s review and
recommendation of the legal provisions relating to rape. Most of the recommendations of NCW have been covered therein.  
Department of Women and Child Development has concurred with the  draft note for Cabinet circulated by the Ministry of
Home Affairs, at the level of HRM, as well as reiterating the remaining recommendations of NCW.
 
The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961
 
National Commission for Women’s recommendations on the amendments need examination of this Department. 
Further  clarifications have been sought from the NCW.
 
In so far as the laws to give equal rights to women in ownership of assets is concerned, the laws relating to intestate
succession among Hindus are codified under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956. The NCW has reviewed the said Act and one
of its major recommendations was to amend Section 6 of the Act with a view to make recommendations for amending the
Act so that women get an equal share in the ancestral property. The recommendation of NCW was forwarded to the
Legislative Department, which is concerned with the legislative aspects of the said Act. Legislative Department informed
that the Law Commission of India, in its 174th Report on `Property Rights of Women : Proposed reforms under the Hindu
Law’ recommended amendments to section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956. That Department has circulated a draft
Cabinet Note for amending the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 to implement the recommendations made by the Law
Commission of India. The Department has examined the draft Cabinet Note and with the approval of the Minister of Human
Resource Development conveyed the concurrence to the proposals in it and also requested the Legislative Department to
consider the recommendations made by the NCW regarding amendments in the Act.

XV.  INSURANCE FOR WOMEN WORKERS
 
Recommendations/Observations
 
15.2   The Committee observes that Insurance is an essential economic support to women during crises and recommends
that efforts should be made to link insurance with health programmes. The Committee would like the Department to explore
feasibility of linking insurance to other financial services - savings and credit – which  promotes long-term insurance
coverage and work out the modalities in consultation with some renowned NGOs who have already started this exercise.
 
Action Taken
 
Life Insurance Corporation of India offers Group Insurance Scheme called Janashree Bima Yojana for those working
in the informal sector and are below poverty line or slightly above poverty line.  The scheme provides for an insurance cover
of Rs.20,000 on natural death.  On death/total permanent disability due to accident, the benefit is Rs.50,000.  On partial
permanent disability due to accident, the benefit is Rs.25,000.  The premium for the scheme is Rs.200 per member, 5o per
cent  premium under the scheme will be met out of  Social Security Fund.  The balance premium is to be paid by the

          The Ministry of Labour  had already moved a draft Cabinet Note for enactment of the ‘Unorganised Sector Workers

and are engaged in the scheduled  employments. The Act provides for schemes to be formulated by the appropriate

Reasons for shortfall and remedial measures
 
            The scheme of Condensed Course stipulates that the objectives of the scheme is to provide educational opportunities
to the girls and women who are school  drop-outs or who could not join mainstream education system due to their adverse
circumstances.  The CSWB, during the initial years of 9th Five Year Plan i.e. the year 1997-98, 98-99 & 2000-01 took a
decision.
 
i)
                     Not to consider proposals of such voluntary organizations for above Primary level courses who did not have
educational experience.  Even proposals from the institutions with experience of literacy or non-formal education were not
considered for sanction, though the scheme makes it clear that the proposals of the institutions who were engaged in women
welfare activities can be considered.  By imposing such restrictions many institutions could not avail the grant under the
programme which led to non-utlization of funds.
 
ii)
                   The CSWB also took a decision not to sanction residential courses whereas there was persistent demand from the
voluntary organizations functioning in rural, tribal and backward areas for residential course.
 
iii)            Though, there is no provision in the scheme to stipulate the condition of 50% result in examination, the CSWB took
a decision that continuation courses (IInd & IIIrd batches) should not be considered for the institutions whose result of earlier
batch was less than 50% in spite of the fact that in many States results of even candidates from regular schools in
CBSE/Higher Secondary Examination was even less than 50%.
 
The above conditions restricted the scope of implementation of the scheme as per objective of the programme. 
However, the CSWB took remedial measures and the Executive Committee of the CSWB in its 136th Meeting held on
20.7.2000 had decided that 50% passed percentage for continuation courses shall not be insisted upon.  The average State
result of girl candidates shall be taken as a criterion for giving continuation grant.  The Executive Committee has also taken a
decision that educational experience for middle and matric/equivalent level courses should not be insisted upon as voluntary
institutions having experience in conducting women’s programme are also eligible for assistance as per the scheme.  It was
also decided that the applications of residential courses should also be considered particularly for the courses which are to be
conducted in tribal, rural and backward area.

             INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
 
Recommendations/Observation
 
3.7       The fact that nearly 95 out of 1000 children under five die is nothing less than shocking.  The latest position of IMR
is not known as this figure is   for the year 1998.  Such a high IMR is certainly a very sad reflection on our efforts for child
development.  ICDS's main aim, in fact, is to lower the infant mortality because it focuses not only on child care but on
pregnant and lactating mothers as well.  If the IMR is taken as indicator, the performance/effectively of the ICDS tells a sad
story. The inability to operationalise ICDS projects, the Committee feels, is the main reason for this situation.  The
requirement must be much more as the sanctioned projects are as per the 1991 census. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department should take up this issue with the States/implementing agencies at higher level to chalk out
an action plan to be taken up at emergency level and in time bound manner.  The Committee also emphasizes the need for
constant and effective monitoring of implementation of this scheme.  Having independent channel of getting feedback is
also of prime importance which needs to be put in place immediately.
 
Action Taken
 
There are various factors like low birth rate, pneumonia, birth asphyxia etc. which are attributable to death of
children.  One of the major findings of the National Council of Applied Economic Research (NCAER), which  conducted an
independent survey of ICDS projects in 1998, was that the infant mortality rate (IMR) was lower in ICDS areas than in non-
ICDS areas.
 
Regarding operationalisation of ICDS projects/AWCs, there has been continuous emphasis upon States to

COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
The Committee is aware that the problem of malnutrition is so huge and complex that inter-sectoral efforts in the
areas of food security, health and education are necessary.  The Committee reiterates that different schemes of child
nutrition being implemented by various Ministries/Departments and also at State level need to be brought under one nodal
agency with special focus on tribal and remote areas of the country.  Interdepartmental meetings for setting nutrition goals
and framing the strategy for achieving the same do not seem to have led to any visible improvement.  The Committee,
therefore, reiterates that the Department mandated to look after the welfare of children and women has to play a more pro-
active role. (Para 3.12)
 
IV.     UDISHA –ICDS TRAINING PROGRAMME (1999-2004)
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
           
            The Committee notes that there has been some improvement in the training of different categories of ICDS
functionaries.  However, figures as on June, 2004 indicate that considerable gap still remains to be fulfilled.   The
Committee hopes that all ICDS functionaries would be trained by 30.06.05 under the extended ICDS-III Project as indicated
by the Department.   The Committee, however, reiterates that the Department should formulate an alternative programme of
training which could be initiated immediately after the completion of ICDS III project on 30.6.05.  (Para 4.4)

COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE

The Committee notes that the recommendations of the national level Conference on Crèche Programme held in July,

VII. BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
The Committee observes that after a prolonged phase of indecision about the fate of Balika Samriddhi Yojana, a
decision has been taken not to transfer the scheme to the States and it shall be operated by the Central Govt as being done
earlier.  The Committee would like to emphasize that the scheme was not running well under the administrative control of
Centre.  There were huge unspent balances lying with the State Govts.  The Committee apprehends that pendency level
continues to remain the same.  The Committee is not satisfied with the stand taken by the Department that only States
submitting satisfactory utilization will be sanctioned funds.  The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for
evolving an effective monitoring mechanism to ensure that financial assistance as envisaged under the scheme reaches all
the targeted beneficiaries. (Para 7.2 & 7.3)
 
IX.   SUPPORT TO TRAINING AND EMPLOYMENT PROGRAMME FOR WOMEN (STEP)
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
           
If the Committee was to accept the explanation given by the Department of Women and Child Development, then
there must be some problem with target fixing.  Does it mean that lower targets are fixed deliberately to avoid large gaps in
achievements at the end of the year?  The Committee feels that a more realistic approach needs to be taken by the
Department of Women and Child Development. (Para 9.5 & 9.6)
X.   NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR WOMEN
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
The Committee is not aware whether there exists any mechanism to get the feedback on the complaint sent to the
Department of Women and Child Development of State Governments/State Women Commission.  The Committee
recommends that the feedback should be obtained on quarterly basis.  The Committee also recommends that those
complaints which do not come under the mandate of the Commission may be treated as disposed off and should not be
shown as pending complaints. (Para 10.2)
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
         
The Committee is of the view that, Department of Women and Child Development has to play a proactive role for
this purpose by working as facilitator for setting up the State Women Commission in the remaining States. Mere writing
letters or taking meetings has not worked so far. (Para 10.3)

XVIII.  FUNCTIONING OF NGOs
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
            The Committee feels that there must be at least one representative from the Centre also in the SLECs in the States. 
Idea of involving eminent social workers of the area can also be considered.
 
            Involving University Women Departments in monitoring the NGOs needs to be strengthened further.  It can work
wonders.  Despite existing penal provisions for erring NGOs, not many of them are being penalized, the Committee feels.
(Para 18.1)
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
Committee had expressed its concern about there being no criteria for having a specific number of NGOs in a
particular area.  The Committee had, accordingly, recommended that there should be specific guidelines whereunder number
of NGOs in a district (subject to area) should be indicated.  The Committee feels that mere noting of Committee’s
recommendations for compliance will not serve the purpose.   The Committee reiterates that there is an urgent need for
having such guidelines otherwise instances of excess number of NGOs operating from a limited area will continue to occur.
