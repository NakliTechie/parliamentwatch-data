INTRODUCTION
1.1. The National Health Policy  of  1983 visualised the importance of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy
and its role in the national health care system.    People of our Country have faith in the traditional systems especially
when the services provided are cheaper and available in  very  small  place  and rural areas.  Moreover, the remedies are
free from side-effects.  India has  a  large resource  of ISM&H practitioners of Homoepathy whose services need to be
fully utilised in the national health care delivery  system.    For this  purpose,  a  new  Department  of  Indian Systems of
Medicine and  Homoeopathy (ISM&H) has been set up to promote and develop the  Indian systems of Medicine
(Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Homoeopathy and drugless Therapies like  Yoga  and  Naturopathy). 
II BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
2.1. The total allocation, both Plan and Non-Plan  taken  together, provided to  the Department for the year 1998-99 was
Rs.  85.60 crores  in BE which was enhanced to 89.12 crores at RE stage.    The  Committee  has  observed  that  the 
increase at the RE stage in 1998-99 was made only in Non-Plan head i.e.  from Rs.  36.60  crores  to  40.12 crores. 
2.2. On a specific query regarding amount sought by the Department for 1999-2000, the Committee was informed that
an  amount of Rs.  106.88 crores (Rs.   63.45  crores Plan  + Rs.  43.43 crores Non-Plan) was sought and the approved
amount was Rs.  100.96 crores (Rs.  59.13 crores Plan  +  Rs.    41.83 crores  Non Plan).  Thus there is an increase of
Rs. 15.36 crores in 1999-2000 as compared to the allocation made in BE 1998-99. 
2.3 The Committee notes that Plan allocation of Rs. 260 crores for the Department for Ninth Plan is at a considerably
enhanced level when compared to Eighth Plan allocation of Rs. 104.44 crores.  However, the fact remains that it is
much below the projected requirements of the Department.    The Department of ISM&H’s share of Plan allocation has
remained about 3.4% of the total health sector allocation.  If various systems under ISM&H have to develop properly,
there is a need for substantial higher allocation of funds for the Department.   Reduction  of  amount particularly  under 
plan head could severely affect the activities of the Department aimed at development of the ISM&H. The Committee
therefore recommends that Plan allocation made for the  year 1999-2000 should be increased at RE stage. 
 
III. AYURVEDA
3.1. The allocation  for  Ayurveda under Plan in BE 1998-99 was Rs. 10.54 crores which was enhanced to Rs. 13.16
crores at RE stage and again reduced to Rs. 12.09 crores in 1999-2000.    Explaining  the  reason for the uneven
increase/decrease in the plan allocation for Ayurveda,  the  Department  submitted  that  it  was  mainly due  to  
payment of arrears of salary and allowances  caused by implementation of Vth Pay Commission recommendations 
to      Ayurvedic Physicians  and  personnel.    The Department also submitted  that this additional liability was not
required to be   paid  during  1999-2000, hence the less provision. 
3.2 The Committee has noticed that there is a growing demand of Ayurveda treatment in the Western countries.  Some
private parties are reported to have shown interest in starting Ayurveda Health Care Centres so as to attract foreign
tourists.  The Committee is of the view that the Department should draw an action plan in this regard which will
popularise the Ayurvedic System of treatment in and outside India in due course  which in turn will attract more tourists.
3.3 Committee’s attention has also been drawn to a lot of misleading advertisements regarding some Ayurvedic
medicines which are not scientifically tested.   On a specific query as to what preventive action has been taken in this
regard, the Secretary informed that all the State Govts. have been requested to take stringent action in this regard. 
Action was also being taken for bringing about necessary amendments in the Magic Remedies Objectionable
Advertisements Act.  However, the Secretary admitted that there was a need for strengthening the drug control
administration at the state level.  The Committee is of the view that there is a need for building up a mechanism for
taking preventive action so that public at large is not misled. 
3.4 The Committee is aware that there are a number of persons who are doing some good work in the Ayurvedic system
of treatment.  However, due support for their achievements seems to be lacking.  The Committee strongly feels that the

IV. UNANI
4.1   Under  the allocation for the Unani System, Plan provision was reduced from Rs.  8.55 crores in BE 1998-99 to
Rs.  6.98 crores in the RE.  However,  an allocation  of Rs.  8.55 crores  has been made  in  BE  1999-2000. 
4.2. The Committee was informed that additional funds were allocated in 1998-99 for construction of buildings for
Regional  Research Institute of Unani Medicine at Srinagar and Karimganj and also for strengthening of RRIUM,
Hyderabad.  However, as these projects could not be started, amount of allocation had to be reduced at RE stage in
1998-99.  The Committee finds that approval of the appropriate authority is awaited for RRIUM, Srinagar.  For
RRIUM, Hyderabad and Karimganj, estimates are being obtained from the concerned authorities.  The Committee
hopes that necessary formalities will be completed at the earliest and the projects will not be delayed further. 
4.3. The Committee has been informed  that in view of the change in the  objective and the funding pattern, fresh
approval of EFC has become necessary for National Institute of Unani Medicine, Bangalore.  The Committee would like
to impress upon the Department to expedite the procedural requirements so that construction work of the main building
of  NIUM, Bangalore is started without any further delay.
V. HOMOEOPATHY
5.1. For Homoeopathy, the  plan provision was Rs. 6.77 crores in BE 1998-99 which was increased to Rs. 7.02 crores at
RE stage.  In BE of 1999-2000 it has been reduced to the level of Rs. 6.27 crores.  When the Department was asked to
explain the reason for these variations, it was informed that in the RE 1998-99, a provision of Rs. 7.02 crores was made
primarily to meet the arrears on amount of salary and allowances  to Homoeopathy physicians/personnel.   Since this
additional  liability is not to be paid during 1999-2000,  less provision has been made. 
5.2. The Committee notes that the increase of bed capacity for in-patient department of National Institute of
Homoeopathy, Calcutta has been linked up with the introduction of post graduate course in homoepathy which  is going
to commence from Feb, 1999. Last year, the Committee had suggested that research on detoxication  of arsenic
poisoning being conducted by NIH, Calcutta should be intensified as this problem had severely affected West Bengal. 
The Committee is happy to note that NIH has submitted a project costing Rs. 14.04 lakhs under the Department’s extra
mural research scheme and an amount of Rs. 6 lakhs has already been sanctioned during 1998-99 for this research.  The
Committee hopes that the research will be completed within the stipulated time.
VI. YOGA AND NATUROPATHY
6.1. For Yoga and Naturopathy the BE for 1998-99 on the plan side was Rs. 3.95 crores, which was reduced to Rs. 3.63
crores at the RE stage.  However, it has been increased substantially to Rs. 6.20 crores in  BE 1999-2000.  When asked
to justify the same, the Department explained that enhanced allocation is on account of construction of building  for
Morarji Desai National Institute of Yoga (MDNIY).  During 1999-2000, an amount of Rs. 325.00 lakhs has been
earmarked to accelerate the construction work. 
6.2. The Committee, however, notes  that in the year 1998-99, the provision of Rs. 170.00 lakh for the purpose could not
be utilised resulting in no expenditure upto to Feb, 1999.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that effective action
should be taken for completion of construction work of the building of the Institute. 
6.3. The Committee has been given to understand that the Institute propose to undertake experimental study on the
effect of yoga in alcohol intake and the research project in this regard has been submitted for seeking grant in aid from
W.H.O.  The Committee desires that the project should be cleared as early as possible. 
6.4. Last year the Committee recommended that Yoga be taught in schools right from the primary level.  The
Committee finds that the response of the Department of Education is awaited. The Committee recommends that the
Department should pursue the matter with the Department of Education. 
6.5. The Committee was informed last year that comprehensive project report for the establishment of the National
Institute of Naturopathy, Pune will be placed before EFC.  However the Committee is pained to note that the approval
of the EFC is yet to be obtained.  The Committee in this connection has also noted that an expenditure of Rs. 36.50
lakhs could be made  against provision of Rs. 75 lakhs.  The Committee feels that this is not a happy situation and there
is a need to expedite the matter to avoid under-utilisation of the provision made for the year 1999-2000.

VII. MEDICAL EDUCATION 
7.1. At present, there are 311 undergraduate and 47 postgraduate ISMH colleges  in the country.  In order to improve the
standard of education in these institutions, the Department has  adopted multi pronged strategy:- 
i) To set up model ISM&H Educational Institutes under the Department with the modern facilities and to maintain very
high standard of Education;
ii) To provide financial assistance to improve UG & PG ISM&H colleges run by State Govts./NGOs/Semi Govt.
organisations; and 
 
iii) To establish statutory regulatory bodies to regulate the education by laying down the minimum standards of
education and curriculum and register the practitioner of these systems.
7.2. Last year, the Committee was informed that views of the State Govts. have been sought by the Central Council of
Indian Medicine on norms for establishing a new Ayurveda  college/prescribing the minimum standard for the existing
colleges.  However, the Committee is constrained to note that the views of the State Govts. are yet to be received. 
Position with regard to Homoeopathy education is more worrying as no action seems to be initiated by the Central
Council of Homoeopathy so far.  The Committee would like to reiterate its earlier recommendation that speedy action is
called for checking the growth of sub-standard colleges and also for having the minimum standard of education in the
existing colleges. 
7.3. Regarding para- medical education in pharmacy and Nursing, the Department informed the Committee that they
have proposed to introduce a scheme as an add-on component to the existing schemes of improving and strengthening
of the existing UG colleges of ISMH for  financial assistance for setting up of pharmacy/nursing education.  Pharmacy
Council of India and Nursing Council of India were approached for making suitable modifications/amendments in their
Acts for facilitating introduction of pharmacy and nursing education in Ayurveda, Homoeopathy, Unani and Siddha
systems.  Though the response from the Nursing Council was encouraging, the Pharmacy Council has shown their
inability.  Setting up of a separate Pharmacy Council is therefore under-examination, the Committee was informed.  The
Committee hopes that these issues would be decided without further delay. 
7.4. So far as  teaching of yoga and naturopathy is concerned, the Department informed that the task of regulating
education in Yoga and Naturopathy has not yet been entrusted to the Central Council for Research in Yoga and
Naturopathy.  Setting up of a regulatory body for education in Yoga and Naturopathy was under active examination of
the Department.  The Committee therefore, recommends that a separate regulatory body be formed at the earliest so that
research activities in yoga and Naturopathy are fostered. 
7.5. Regarding need to check huge amounts of donation/capitation fee being taken by some colleges under ISM&H, the
Committee was informed that a fee structure to be charged by private colleges as per guidelines of Supreme Court
Orders is being finalised actively and the schemes submitted by the respective Councils on fee-structures in Private
Professional ISM&H colleges have been returned to the Councils for taking opinion from the State Governments.  The
Committee recommends that the Department should pursue the matter with State Govts. so as to finalise this issue at the
earliest. 
7.6 The Committee was informed that 30 institutions have been identified to impart training under RCH programme of
Department of Family Welfare to ISM&H physicians and Para- Medics and Director ISM&H of State Governments and
other personnel.  During the year 1997-98, 2310 professionals have been trained and during 1998-99, 1450 professionals
have been trained upto February, 1999.    The Committee appreciates the initiative taken by the Department.
VIII. POOR QUALITY OF DRUGS 
8.1. In view of the Committee it is necessary to prescribe standards for ensuring efficacy of ISM&H drugs  and also for
checking their  genuineness and purity in the market  as there is general skepticism about the quality of ISM&H drugs. 
The Committee was informed that the Department was giving higher priority for standardization of drugs.  It has been
resolved that the standardisation work on drug may be completed during the Ninth Plan period.  On a specific query as
to what progress has been made so far, the Department informed as under :-

(iii) Drug standardisation work on 915 homoeopathic single drugs have been finalised.  Standards on 710 homoeopathic

8.2. The Committee has also noted that at present two drug testing laboratories are providing the technical back up to
these Pharmacopoeial Committees and it is proposed to strengthen these laboratories.  There is also a need for setting up
of Regional Drug Testing Laboratories.  In order to accelerate the work of  evolving of standards, 31 Research
Institutions’ laboratories have been provided financial assistance for working on the standards for 10 drugs per year. 
8.2. The Committee hopes that the Department would be able to complete the standardisation work of drugs by Ninth
Plan period and recommend that for this purpose, sufficient funds should be provided to the Department. 
VIII. NON-AVAILABILITY OF HERBS AND MEDICINAL PLANTS 
9.1 80 % of ISMH drugs are based on medicinal plants.  However, due to over exploitation, unscrupulous collection and
denuding of forests, which are the source of medicinal plants, supplies have been dwindling.  The Department informed
the Committee that it has initiated various steps to enhance the supplies of medicinal plants. These are conservation and
preservation of medicinal plants through in situ and ex-situ cultivation, developing tissue culture, storage in gene banks,
developing large-scale gardens of medicinal plants in coordination with concerned Ministries/Departments. A special
scheme to provide assistance to research organizations for development of agro-techniques has also been introduced.  26
such organizations are participating in the scheme and 97 species of plants have been allotted to them. A component of
R&D  has also been included under the scheme to achieve better result. The  Department further informed that setting
up of a National Board for Medicinal plants is under consideration and a budget provision of Rs. 1.00 Crores  has been
provided for the purpose. The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the department and hopes that desired results
would be achieved in this regard.
X INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
10.1. The Department informed the Committee that a major area of concern is the keen interest being taken by foreign
countries in the development of herbal products and intellectual  property rights. Some of the well known medicinal
plants of India has been patented abroad and heritage of Ayurveda needs to be protected. There is also a lot of
traditional folklore which also needs to be protected. In order to put a check on these attempts, a patent Cell has been set
up in the Department. 
10.2. The Department  vide its ATR on 70th Report of the Committee submitted that to make Patent Cell more viable, a
detailed proposal has been submitted to the Finance Division for creation of posts under various groups. 
10.3 The Committee is constrained to note that that the Patent Cell is still functioning  with inadequate manpower and
various posts are lying vacant. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the vacant posts should be filled up
without further delay. The Committee would also like that the Patent Cell should work in close coordination with other
concerned Departments/ Agencies.
XI. Committed Liability
11.1. While examining the Demands for Grants 1997-98 of the Departments under its purview, the Committee noted
that the committed liability of the Plan Schemes has not been reflected in the budgetary allocations. 
11.2. The Committee, in this, connection considered the report of the Tenth Finance Commission.  The Tenth Finance
Commission have observed in this regard as under:- 
“Although  the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their terms of reference in deciding the cut off date for transfer
of committed liability on account of plan scheme, the  incremental liabilities that would arise in 1997-98 on account of
8th Plan scheme completed in the next two financial years would have to be provided for.  The Commission are,
therefore, of the view that the Planning Commission may consider providing for the maintenance of such scheme till
1999-2000 in the Plan itself as was done for the schemes of the two Annual Plans of 1990-91 and 1991-92”. 
11.3. The Committee also sought clarifications from the then Secretary (Expenditure).  He informed the Committee that

even though communications were sent to various Ministries, the Ministries could not identify the committed

11.5. The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, submitted that at the end of the 8th Five Year Plan the
Planning Commission issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the committed expenditure from plan to the non
plan side.  The Ministry of Finance (Budget Division) also issued a letter to all the Financial Advisors of the Department
requesting them to indicate the amount to be  included from plan to non plan. 
11.6. The Secretary submitted that as on 10th February, 1997, 24 Ministries/Departments responded indicating a figure
of Rs.450.28 crore. However, some   Ministries/Departments like  Education, Culture, Women and Child Development
did not respond.  Accordingly a lumpsum provision of Rs.500 crores in the Ministry of Finance Budget  was provided
for this purpose. As a result of this exercise, a sum of Rs.150 crores was indeed transferred from plan to non plan for the
various Ministries as committed expenditure during the year, on the basis of scrutiny with reference to the Planning
Commission’s guidelines.   The rest of the amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable. 
11.7. The Secretary agreed in principle that whatever is the committed expenditure,  it should be transferred from plan
to non plan.  Otherwise the plan has no meaning. 
11.8. The Secretary assured that the proposals pertaining to UGC (Rs.27 crores) and Department of Education (Rs. 80
crores) which were under consideration would be cleared.  Moreover, the Tenth Finance Commission Report has given
a breathing time till 1999-2000 for this transition.   The Committee  would like to impress that the real picture should be
projected before Parliament. 
11.9. The Secretary also apprised the Committee that another Sub Committee has been appointed by the NDC,
consisting of representatives from seven states and Ministry of Finance.  This Sub-Committee will look into the
structural distortions which have entered into the finances of the states on account of 5th Pay Commission  and to
suggest a suitable package  linked to fiscal reforms. 
11.10. The Committee observes that a similar problem exists in the case of Central Ministries also.  The Committee,
therefore, desires that a similar exercise be carried out in the case of Central Ministries also.
