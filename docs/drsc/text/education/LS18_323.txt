INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department -related Parliamentary  Standing Committee on 
Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, having been authorized by the Committee to 
submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Three Hundred and Twenty -third 
Report on the Demands for  Grants (2021 -22) pertaining to the Department of School 
Education and Literacy, Ministry of Education. 
2. Consequent upon the re -naming of the Ministry of Human Resource Development as 
the Ministry of Education by amending the Government of India (Allocation of Business) 
Rules, 1961 vide the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Three Hundred and Fifty 
Sixth Amendment Rules, 2020, as notified in the Gazette of India dated 1 7th August, 2020, 
the name of the Committee was revised from the Committee on Human Resource 
Development to Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports w.e.f. 8th 
October, 2020 by making amendments in the Third Schedule to the Rules of Procedure  and 
Conduct of Business in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) by the Chairman, Rajya Sabha in 
consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha in accordance with the proviso to sub -rule (2) of 
Rule 268 of the ibid Rules. This was notified in the Rajya Sabha Parl iamentary Bulletin Part-
II No 60287 dated 6th November, 2020.  
3. In accordance with the constitutional requirement under Article 113, the estimated 
expenditure of the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India projected under 
various Demands for Grants for the upcoming financial year needs to be voted by Parliament. 
As a sequel thereto, Demands for Grants of the relevant Ministries/Departments stand 
referred to concerned Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee to make a close 
scrutiny thereof under Rule 270 of rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council 
of States. While making scrutiny of the Demands for Grants, the Committee has made an 
appraisal of the performance, programees, schemes and policies of the Department of Scho ol 
Education and Literacy vis-a-vis expenditure made out of Consolidated Fund of India in the 
current financial year. 
4. The Budget allocations of the Ministry of Education have bee n sought under two 
demand Numbers, i.e, Demand No. 24 pertain s to the Department of School Education and 
Literacy and Demand No.25 pertain s to Department of Higher Education In this Report, a 
scrutiny of Demand No. 24 pertaining to the Department of School Education and Literacy of 
the Ministry of Education  for the Financial Year 2021-22 has been made by the Committee.  
While scrutiny of Demand No. 25 pertaining to Department of Higher Education is covered 
in Report number 324 of the Committee.

OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT 
 The Ministry of Education comprises two Departments , namely, (i) Department of 
School Education and Literacy and (ii) Department of Higher Education. The Department 
functions under the overall guidance of Minister of Education along with the Secretary, 
Department of School Education and Literacy. 
1.1 The Department  of School Education and Literacy decides the policy and 
programmes relating to school education, adult education and digital education with focus on 
quality, equity and inclusion. 
1.2 The following Institutions under the Department are involved in policy formulation 
and implementation relating to school education and adult education: 
A. National Council of Educational Research and Training(NCERT): 
1.3 The National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) is an 
autonomous organisation of the Gov ernment of India : It was established on 1 st  September 
1961 as a literary, scientific and charitable Society under the Societies' Registration Act, 
1860. It provides academic and technical support for qualitative improvement in school 
education and undertakes programmes related to educational research, develo pment, training, 
international cooperation, publication and dissemination of information.  
1.4 NCERT has one national level institution, namely, National Institute of  Education, 
(NIE) New Delhi and five  Regional Institutes of Education (RIE s) located at Ajmer, Bhopal, 
Bhubaneswar, Mysuru, and Urinam (Meghalaya).  It has an institute dedicated to vocational 
studies, namely, Pandit Sunderlal Sharma Inst itute of Vocational Education, (PSSIVE), 
Bhopal and Ce ntral Institute of Educational Technology (CIET), New Delhi dedicated to 
education and technology. 
1.5 The major objectives of NCERT and its constituent units are to undertake, promote 
and coordinate research in areas related to school education; prepare a nd publish model 
textbooks, supplementary material, newsletters, journals  and develop  educational kits, 
multimedia digital materials, etc. organise pre -service and in -service training of teachers; 
develop and disseminate innovative educational techniques and practices;  collaborate and 
network with State educational departments, Universities, NGOs and other educational 
institutions; act as a clearing house for ideas and information in matters related to school 

2 
 
education; and act as a nodal agency for achieving the goals of Universalisation of 
Elementary Education. 
1.6 Among the major initiat ives taken up by NCERT are PM eVI DYA, DIKSHA - 
National Digital Infrastructure for Teachers , Manodarpan, National Initiative for School 
Heads’ and Teachers’ Holistic Advancement (NISHTHA) , Tamanna, SWAYAM Prabha TV 
Channel, Shiksha Vani on All India Radio,  
National Achievement Survey (NAS)  for class III, V, VIII and X and Development of an 
Alternative Academic Calendar during the COVID pandemic, etc.                          
B. National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE): 
1.7 The National Council for Teacher Education(NCTE) is a statutory body set up under 
the National Council for Teacher Education Act, 1993 in 1995 to formally oversee standards, 
procedures and processes in the Indian education system. This Council functions for the 
Central as well as State governments on all matter s with regard to the Teacher Education. 
NCTE seeks to bring excellence in Teacher Education programmes through planned and 
coordinated development and maintenance of standard therein. 
C. Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE): 
1.8 The Board was given its present name ‘Central Board of Secondary Education’in 
1952. From 309 schools in 1962, the Board as of now has 24,007 schools in India and 261 
foreign countries. CBSE envisions a robust, vibrant and holistic school education that may  
engender excellence in every sphere of human endeavor. The Board is committed to provide 
quality education to promote intellectual, social and cultural vivacity among its learners. It 
works towards evolving a learning process and environment, which empowe rs the future 
citizens to become global leaders in the emerging knowledge society. The Board advocates 
and pledges to provide a stress -free learning environment that may develop competent, 
confident, and enterprising citizens who promote harmony and peace.

1.9 Being an autonomous body it does not receive any funding from the Ministry of 
Education and manage from the fees collected from schools and students. Recently the CBSE 
has been entrusted with the responsibility of organizing Programme for International Student 
Assessment (PISA), 2022.  
1.10 India aims to utilize PISA as a transformative tool to further the NEP’s vision of 
transforming the school education system from rote based to competency based in order to 
empower our children to compete at the global level.  
D.  National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS): 
1.11 National Institute of Open Schooling was set up as an autonomous organization in 
November 1989 under the Ministry of Education. NIOS is the largest open schooling system 
in the wo rld and is mandated to provide easy access to varied clientele for continuation of 
education up to pre -degree level through open and distance learning. NIOS seeks to provide 
“sustainable inclusive learning with universal and flexible access to quality scho ol education 
and skill development”. 
E. Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) 
1.12 Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) is an autonomous organization under the 
Ministry of Education with Headquarters at New Delhi. It was registered under the Societies’ 
Registration Act, 1860 on 15 th December, 1965. The primary aim of KVS is to  cater to the 
educational needs of the children of transferable Central Government employees including 
Defence and  Central Para -Military personnel by providing a common programme of 
education; to pursue excellence and set the pace in the field of school education; to initiate 
and promote experimentation and innovations in educati on in collaboration with other b odies 
like the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) and the National  Council of 
Educational Research and Training (NCERT), etc ; and t o develop the spirit of national 
integration and create a sense of “Indianness” among children. 
1.13 The KVS (HQ) administers its schemes through 25 Regional Offices and 124 5 
Kendriya Vidyala yas including 03  at Kathmandu, Moscow and Tehran . Each Kendriya 
Vidyalaya has its Vidyalaya Management Committee headed by a senior officer from 
Defence/Civil Sector or an educationist. All the Kendriya Vidyalayas are affiliated to the 
Central Board of Sec ondary Education (CBSE), Delhi. The policies of Kendriya Vidyalaya 
Sangathan are framed through its Board of Governors headed by the Minister of Education, 
Government of India. 
 

4 
 
F. Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS). 
1.14 Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS) is an autonomous organization under the Ministry 
of Education and has been registered as a Society, under the Societies’ Registration Act, 1860 
at NOIDA  on 28 th February, 1986. NVS establishes, runs and manages Jawahar Navodaya 
Vidyalayas (JNVs) all over the country through its 8 Regional Offices at Bhopal, Chandigarh, 
Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Patna, Pune and Shillong  and these schools are affiliated to 
CBSE. At present there are 661  JNVs out of which  585 are upto class X and  548 are upto 
Class XII. 
1.15 The vision of the organization is to provide good quality modern education, including 
a strong component of culture, inculcation of values, awareness of the environment, 
adventure activities and physical education, to talented children predominantly from the rural 
areas without regard to their family's socio-economic conditions. 
G. Central Tibetan Schools Administration (CTSA): 
1.16 When his Holiness the Dalai Lama along with his followers came to India in 1959, he 
showed his deep concern for education of T ibetan children in India. The then Prime Minister 
of India and His Holiness the Dalai Lama visualized the need of special schools for the 
Tibetan children and with their efforts CTSA was established in 1961. These schools mainly 
cater to the needs of Tibetan community. However, local Indian children to the extent of 10% 
are also admitted from Class VI onwards. CTSA is fully funded by Ministry of Education, 
Government of India. 
H. Directorate of Adult Education 
1.17 Directorate of Adult Education (DAE) is a subor dinate office of the Department of 
School Education & Literacy [ Ministry of Education, Government of India ]. It functions as 
the National Resource Centre for Adult Education and Literacy programmes in the country 
and provides professional, academic and tec hnical resource support and guidance for 
effective implementation of programmes launched under the aegis of the National Literacy 
Mission Authority (NLMA) from time to time. This Directorate prepares guidelines for 
development of teaching learning material , organizes training and orientation programmes, 
produces media material and harnesses all kinds of media and learner assessments. 
I. National Bal Bhavan (NBB): 
1.18 National Bal Bhavan is an autonomous body, fully funded by Ministry of Education. 
It seeks to let every child fully participate, contribute and strive towards a creative, humane, 

5 
 
innovative and joyful World in all its Wonder. The NEP, 2020 has recommended setting up 
of Bal Bhavans as special day boarding schools in States and Districts. 
J. National Education Policy, 2020 
1.19 The National Education Policy, 2020 is a basic long term policy aligned with 
aspirational goals of 21 st Century. It intends to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals 
(SDG) of the United Nations (SDG-4). With the change of name of the Ministry from Human 
Resource Development (HRD) to Ministry of Education on 18 th August, 2020 on the basis of 
suggestions c ontained in the NEP, 2020 , the policy has been  set in motion for 
implementation. The Ministry has modified existing schemes and programmes to align with 
the policy document. 
1.20  The Finance Minister on the floor of the Lok Sabha on 1 st February, 2020 has made 
announcement about 15000 exemplar schools, 100 new Sainik Schools,  750 Eklavya Model 
Residential School in tribal area  introduction of Higher Education Commission of India 
(HECI) Bill during Financial Year 2021 -22 , also amply indicates that the poli cy would be 
implemented from Financial Year 2021 -22.  The policy documents also mentions that the 
entire policy will be in operational mode during the decade 2030 -40 .This Policy will replace 
the existing Policy on Education of 1986 in phases. 
1.21 The following are the key highlights of the document:- 
 The 10+2 structure of School Curricula to be replaced with the 5+3+3+4 Curricula 
structure with five years of foundational education (upto Class 2); three years of 
preparatory stage (from Class 3 to 5); three  years of middle stage (from Class 6 to 8) 
and four years of Secondary School (from Class 9 to 12). In a nutshell, three year s of 
pre schooling/anganwadi has been proposed to be integrated with 15 years schooling 
under the NEP, 2020. 
 The Policy aims at universalization of education with 100 percent Gross Enrolment 
Ratio (GER) by  the year 2030 for school education and 50 percent by  the year 2035 
for higher education. 
 3.22 crore out of school children in the age group of 6 to 17 years to be brought back 
school to achieve 100% GER by the year 2030. 
 Additional Kendriya Vidyalayas (KVs) and Navodaya Vidyalayas (NVs) to be set up 
in aspirational districts and Special Education Zone (SEZ). 
 National Mission of Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (NMFLN) for attain ing 
universal foundational literacy and numeracy in all primary schools for all learners by 
Grade three by the year 2025. 
 National Curricular and Pedagogical Framework for Early Childhood Care and 
Education (NCPFECCE) for children upto the age of eight to be developed by the 
NCERT.  
 A Comprehensive National Curriculum Framework for Teachers Education (NCFTE) 
will be formulated by National Council for Teachers Education (NCTE) in 
consultation with NCERT, by the year 2021.

 Three language formula in school e ducation; mother tongue/regional or local 
language to be used as a medium of i nstitution till Grade Five and m ay be extended 
till Grade Eight and beyond.  
 Performance Assessment, Review and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic 
Development (PARAKH) to be set up as a standard setting body at national level; 
States to develop State School Standard Authority (SSSAs) and the SCERT to

 A common National Professional Standard for Teachers (NPST) to be developed by

 National Research Foundation (NRF) to set up as an apex body to foster research and

 Universities and Colleges to bloom into full fledged research centre of excellence

 An Academic Bank to Credit to be set up for digitally storing academic credit earned

 Higher Education Commission of India (HECI) to be set up as an overarching

 The Higher Education Commission of India will have independent bodies i.e.

 A National Mission for Mentoring to be set up with a large pool of outstanding

university/college teachers.  
 Bal Bhawans as special day boarding schools to be set up in States and Districts. 
 Gender Inclusion Fund (GIF) for equitable and quality education for all girl children 
and transgender and Special Education Zones (SEZ) for disadvantaged regions and 
groups.  
 Integration of Vocational Education with mainstream education  from primary to 
university level in a phased manner ; vocational education/internship from Class 6 
onwards.  
 Vocational Educational is received by 5% of youth in India in contradiction to 52% in  
USSR, 75% in Germany and 96% in South Korea. Policy lays emphasis to raise it to 
50% by 2025.  
 Holistic Education by removing distinction between Curricula, Co -Curricular and 
Extracurricular activities.  
 Standardized State School Examination for Grade 3, 5 and 8 and Board Exams for 
Grade 10 and 12.  
 Centre and States to work to increase public investment in education to reach six

 Multidisplinary Education and Research Universities ( MERUs) (with enrollment in

L.  Samagra Shiksha (Centrally Sponsored Scheme): 
1.27 Samagra Shiksha , an overarching programme for the school education sector 
extending from pre -school to class 12, is being implemented with the broader goal of 
improving school effectiveness measured in terms of equal opportunities for schooling and 
equitable learning out comes. The vision of the Scheme is to ensure inclusive and equitable 
quality education from pre -school to senior secondary stage in accordance with the 
Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) for Education. The main outcomes of the Scheme are 
envisaged as Universal Access, Equity and Quality including Vocational Education, Inclusive 
Education, increased use of Technology and strengthening of Teacher Education Institutions 
(TEIs) and treat school education holistically as a continuum from Pre-school to Class 12. 
M. Strengthening Teaching-Learning and Results for States (STARS):  
1.28 STARS is an externally aided project (supported by World Bank) which will be 
implemented as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.  The total cost of the project is approximately 
₹ 5718 Crore, with US$ 500 million (approximately ₹. 3700 Crore) to be funded by the 
World Bank and rest coming as State share from the participating States. The project covers 6 
States, viz., Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala and Odisha. 
The identified States will be supported for various interventions for improving the quality of 
education. 
N. National Programme of Mid-Day Meal in Schools (MDM): 
1.29 The National Programme of Mid -Day Meal in Schools, popularly known as the Mid -
Day Meal Scheme, is an on -going Centrally -Sponsored Scheme which covers all school 
children studying in Classes I -VIII of Government, Government -Aided, Special Training 
Centres including Madarsas and Maqtabs supported under Samagra Shiksha. During 2019-
20, 11.80 crore children studying in 11.20 lakh institutions have been benefitted under the 
Scheme. The NEP,2020 have also advocated introduction of breakfast under the scheme. 
O. National Means-cum-Merit Scholarship Scheme (NMMSS): 
1.30 The Central Sector Scheme, ‘National Means -cum-Merit Scholarship Scheme’, was 
launched in May, 2008 with the objective to award scholarships to meritorious students of 
economically weaker sections @ ₹6000/- per annum (₹500/- per month) per student to arrest 
their dropout at Class VIII and encourage them to continue the ir studies at secondary stage. 
The rate of scholarship has been enhanced from ₹6000/ - to ₹12000/ - per year with effect 
from 1st April, 2017.

P. Padhna Likhna Abhiyan 
1.31 A new scheme of adult education , namely; Padhna Likhna Abhiyan , has been 
launched for implementation during the FY 2020 -21. The main objective and thrust of the 
scheme is on basic literacy, i.e., on making illiterate adults literate. The scheme has a flexible 
approach and innovative methodologies such as involving school and college stude nts and 
other volunteers of Schemes, such as, National Cadet Corps (NCC), National Service Scheme 
(NSS) and Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), for imparting basic literacy.

Implementation of the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 
3.12 The Committee was apprised that our country is one  of the 135 countries which has  
made education a fundamental right. With the enactment of the Right of Children to Free and 
Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (also known as RTE, Act) education has been included 
under Article – 21A of Constitution. With its enactment of the RTE Act, children in the age 
group of 6 to 14  and Classes 1 to 8  have been provided free and compulsory education. The 
education for them is compulsory as the RTE Act cast an obligation on appropriate 
Government to ensure admi ssion, attendance and completion of education by all children 
between 6 to 14 age group leaving no child behind. It was also free in the sense that children 
are not required to pay any fee/charges/expenses which may prevent him/her to pursue 
elementary education. Free uniform, books have been provided to children. The said Act also 
reserves 25% seats for Children from Economically Weaker Section for  ensuring equitable 
education. 
3.13 The Committee noted that since NEP  lays down  Curricula Structure 5 +3+3+4, the 
child would be in the school from age of 3 and amendment to RTE Act could be necessitated 
to provide free and compulsory education to children from the age of 3 which is now from 
the age of 6.  The Department  accordingly may ensure that the children of age group of 6 to 
14 years entitled for free and compulsory education  under the RTE Act  may be changed to 
age group of 3 to 14 years to implement the NEP, 2020.

National Education Policy, 2020 
3.57 The National Education Policy way back in 1968 had emphasized upon public 
expenditure of 6% (by both Centre and States) of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) , which 
has been reiterated in Education Policy of 1986 and reaffirmed by the NEP of 2020. But the 
current expenditure of Government at Union and States is around 4% of Gross Domestic 
Product (GDP) , which is roughly 10% of  the total Government expenditure. Among the 
SAARC Countries, Nepal and Bhutan spent more than India on their Education. 
3.58 The corresponding figures stand at 7.5 percent 3 for Bhutan, Zimbabwe and Sweden; 7 
percent for Costa Rica and Finland; 6 percent for Kyrgyzstan, South Africa and Bra zil; 5.5 
percent for United Kingdom, Netherlands and Palestine; and 5 percent for Malaysia, Kenya, 
Mongolia, Korea and United States of America. 
3.59 The Committee has been apprised that a total of ₹ 93224.00 crore has been allocated 
for Ministry of Education for Financial Year 2021 -22 with 58.86 percent for Department of 
School Education and Literacy and 41.14 percent for Department of Higher Education, which 
comes to around 2.29 percent of  the total budgetary allocation by the Union  Government to 
all its Ministries/Departments. The budgetary allocations on Sainik Schools which comes

Pre Schooling Education 
3.60 The Committee was apprised that the 10+2 structure of school curricula is to be 
replaced with a 5+3+3+4 curricula structure with 5 years of foundational education (upto 
Class 2); three years of preparatory stage (from Class 3 to 5); three year s of middle stage 
(from Class 6 to 8) and four years of Secondary S chool from Class 9 to 12. First five years of 
foundational learning entails three years period at Anganwadi/Bal Vatika , which is proposed 
to be clubbed with first two grades of schooling. The State wise Specific Attendance Ratio 
(ASAR) for t he year (2017 -18) referred to in  Economic Survey (2020 -21) at Page 331 was 
brought to the notice of the Committee wherein attendance in the age group of 3 to 5 years 
(Early Childhood Education) at national level is 33.1% with Punjab having high est 
attendance and Karnatak a with the lowest attendance rate. The National Education Policy 
(NEP), 2020 has proposed for inclusion of pre- schooling in the school curricula. 
3.61 The Committee is happy to note that Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) 
has been proposed to be introduced in public schools which has been followed by some of the 
private schools in the form of play school and nursery. It is widely accepted that during this 
age, 85% of cumulative development of brain takes place , which has definitive bearing on 
acquisition of knowledge and skill s in later life.  The Committee recommends that as 
envisaged in NEP, 2020 the preparation of National Curricula and Pedagogical 
Framework for Early Childhood Care and Education (NCPFECCE) for children upto 8 
years of age should  be done on priority basis with due seriousness so as to ensure it’s 
early and effective implementation.
