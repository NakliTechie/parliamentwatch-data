REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports was initially set up as the Department of Sports in 1982 at the time of
organisation of the IX Asian Games in New Delhi.   Thereafter, during the celebration of the International Youth Year
in 1985, the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports came into being.  It became a Ministry on the 27
th May, 2000.
1.2       The Youth i.e. those falling within the age group of 15-35 years constitutes nearly 40% of the total population of
the country.   To optimally tap the constructive and creative energies of this most valuable human resource, the Ministry
of Youth affairs and Sports has taken a number of new initiatives since its inception.    The Ministry organises a large
number of programmes in the field of youth development,  sports and physical education.
1.3       The Secretary, while deposing before the Committee, highlighted the various activities undertaken by the

Ministry during the year 2001-2002.   A mention was made about the celebration of the International Year of Volunteers
and the 7th National Youth Festival at Hissar.    Committee's attention was drawn to the initiative taken in the setting up
of a six- member National Commission for Youth.   The Secretary also informed that a new National Sports Policy has
been approved by the Government on the 11 th September, 2001.    The Committee was given to understand that as a
result of detailed review of all the youth related schemes, action was initiated for merger of some overlapping
schemes.    Similarly, a decision was taken to merge all the sports related schemes under six umbrella schemes.    The
Secretary apprised the Committee about two new Plan Schemes relating to State Sports Academy and Dope Test,
proposed to be started during the Tenth Plan.
II.         BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       The total outlay, both Plan and Non-Plan taken together provided to the Ministry for the year 2001-2002 was Rs.
315.00 crores which was marginally enhanced to Rs. 322.14 crores in RE 2001-2002.   The allocation made for 2002-
2003 is Rs. 339.39 crores (Plan Rs. 285.00 crores and Non Plan Rs. 54.39 crores).
2.2       Expressing its dissatisfaction with the enhanced allocation under Plan in 2002-2003, the Ministry clarified that
this increase would only enable it to implement the ongoing schemes better as per the present norms.  Against a
projected demand of Rs. 327.54 crores under Plan for the year 2002-2003  a provision of only Rs. 285.00 crores has
been made.   The projections were made, taking into consideration, higher requirements for  the new schemes to be
implemented by the Ministry during 2002-2003.
2.3       The Committee is aware that under the Constitution, Sports is a State subject and in the implementation of the
different youth and Sports related schemes, State Govts./ autonomous organisations/NGOs have to play a very active
role.    However, the Committee has observed that during the Ninth Plan period,  although the allocation has been
generally  less than the projected requirements, allocated funds after being reduced at the RE stage remained under-
utilised.   Another disturbing trend noticed by the Committee was non-uniform spread of expenditure during the year.  
Last year, the Committee had pointed out that the expenditure on different schemes should be planned in such a way
that last moment spending is avoided.   The Committee is, however, constrained to point out that the situation seems to
have worsened in 2001-2002.   Against a Plan allocation of Rs. 255.00 crore, expenditure reported as on 1.3.2002 is
only Rs. 155.84 crores.   Specially under  the Youth Welfare Schemes, expenditure reported upto 1.3.2002 is less than
half of the allocated funds (Plan allocation - Rs. 91.25 crores Exp. upto 1.3.2002 is Rs. 42.47 crores).    The Committee
strongly feels that the formulation of a quarterly annual action plan for 2001-2002 has failed to achieve the  desired
results.    The Committee would like to point out that instead of projecting higher requirements and launching new
schemes without completing the requisite formalities, focus should be on the judicious and optimum utilisation of funds
allocated for various schemes.   The Committee is of the view that a detailed review of all the ongoing schemes should
be undertaken so as to assess the shortcomings being noticed and take remedial steps for removal of the same.   The
Department should endeavour to involve the State Govts. and various implementing agencies in this exercise.
III.       NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN
3.1       Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS),  an autonomous organization of the Ministry,   has its network in 500
districts of the country.   It is one of the largest grass-root level organisations in the world, catering to the needs of more
than eight million non-student rural youth enrolled through 1.89 lakh village based Youth Clubs.     Every district of
NYK has a trained cadre of District Youth Coordinator, National Service Volunteers and Youth Leaders.   NYKS
accomplishes its task through two categories of Programmes  i.e. Regular Programmes such as Youth Club
Development, Vocational Training, Work Camp, Awareness Generation, Cultural Programme, Sports Promotion etc.
and Seminars and Workshops and Celebration of National and International Days/ Weeks and Schemes assigned to it by
the Ministry for Implementation.
3.2       During the period ending December 31, 2001, 856 Youth Club Development Programmes, 905 Awareness
Generation Programmes, 2005 Vocational Training Programmes, 1875 tournaments for sports promotion, 444
workshops and seminars, 796 cultural programmes were organised and 4025 National days/weeks were celebrated.  
Besides that, Nehru Yuva Kendras of 13 districts (10 from U.P. and 3 from West Bengal), having  polio incidence,
conducted  extensive awareness campaigns in all blocks of these districts.    The Committee also notes that positive
response has been received to the Swarnjyanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana launched in November, 2000 in 8 districts of
Assam, Jharkhand, Bihar, Orissa, M.P. and Chattisgarh with the financial support from the Ministry of Rural
Development.   A proposal to expand the project in six more districts is at present under the consideration of the

Ministry of Rural  Development.
3.3       An allocation of Rs. 28.38 crores has been made for NYKS under Plan in BE 2002-2003 against a provision of
Rs. 23.23 crores in BE 2001-2002.   On a specific query about the reasons for enhanced allocation in the current year, it
has been informed that the hike of Rs.5.15 crores in Plan allocation was due to construction of an office-cum-training
complex at a satellite town of Delhi on land owned by the Sangathan and to meet the committed liability  of increase in
the salaries of the staff due to annual increment, DA etc.
3.4       The Committee, however, notes that an expenditure of only Rs. 11.12 crores has been incurred upto 1.3.2002
against an allocation of Rs. 23.23 crores in BE 2001-2002.   The Committee has been given to understand that during
2000-2001, the last instalment  of GIA  was released to NYKS only in the last week of March, 2001 as the Utilisation
Certificate submitted by the Sangathan was not in an appropriate  format.   The Committee  apprehends that non-
submission of Utilisation Certificate and statement of  accounts within  the prescribed time limit must be one of the
reasons for expenditure of only one-third of the allocated funds under NYKS during 2001-2002.   The Committee would
like to be apprised about the factors responsible for such a discouraging trend.  
3.5       Annual Report for the year 2001-2002  highlights a variety of regular programmes like Youth Club
Development,  Awareness Generation, Vocational Training etc.    organised and seminars and workshops conducted by
various Nehru Yuva Kendras and on each programme/workshop etc.   funds ranging from Rs. 4,000 to Rs. 30,000 per
Kendra have been either allocated or earmarked.  The number of these programme/workshops must have increased by
the year end i.e. 31
st March, 2002.    The Committee strongly feels that either these achievements in majority of the
cases are on paper only or unspent balances are lying with  many Nehru Yuva Kendras.  Either way, it cannot be
considered an ideal position.    The Committee is of the view that the Department should not entirely depend on the
progress reports forwarded by the NYKS.   An effective  monitoring  mechanism  with an element of surprise 
inspections should be evolved.    It is understood that instructions have already been issued for associating the local
MPs/MLAs with the District Advisory Committee on Youth Programmes.    The Committee would like to be informed
within  a period of three months about the implementation of these instructions together with the details of the inter-
action with local MPs/MLAs. 
3.6       The Committee notes that a total of 492 district NYKs have District Advisory Committee for Youth Programme
(DACYPs) and for the remaining 8 districts, DACYPs are likely to be formed by the first quarter of 2002-2003.    The
Committee observes that against the prescribed 30% representation of women in DACYPs, national average comes to
36.47%.    The Committee is, however, constrained to point out that in some of the States/UTs, representation of
women  in DACYPs is much below the prescribed 30% .   For example in Lakshdweep, it is as low as 11.1% and in
West Bengal only 13.63%.    The Committee recommends that NYKS in such States/UTs. should be approached for
doing the needful in this regard.
3.7       The Committee has been given to understand that a number of initiatives have been taken during 2001-2002 to
give wide publicity to all the youth programmes undertaken by NYKS.    The Committee hopes that these efforts would
have the desired results.
IV        NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME (NSS)
4.1       The main objective of the National Service Scheme is development of the personality of students through
community service.   The scheme is being implemented through universities, colleges and +2 level schools and
institutions of higher learning in all the States/UTs.   At present, NSS has over 17 lakh student volunteers spread over
176 Universities and 16 Senior Secondary schools.   From its inception, more than 1.52 crore students from
Universities, colleges and Institutions of higher learning have benefited from NSS activities.
4.2       The expenditure on NSS programme is shared between the Central and the State Governments in the ratio of 7:5
except in the case of Jammu and Kashmir and Union Territories without legislatures, North-Eastern States including
Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh and other hilly areas as notified by the Planning Commission.   The sharing of expenditure in
such States/UTs  is 3:1.  The Central Government also meets the entire expenditure on administration, training, research,
monitoring, evaluation and publications pertaining to the scheme and full cost of NSS State Liaison Cells set up in the
concerned Department of State Governments.   The existing pattern of expenditure on NSS `Regular Activities' and
`Special Camping Activities' is Rs. 160 and Rs. 300 per volunteer per annum respectively except for North-Eastern
States & Hilly Terrain /Areas including Sikkim and Himachal Pradesh for which the pattern of expenditure is Rs. 200
and Rs.400 respectively.

V         NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEER SCHEME
5.1       The objective of the scheme is to provide opportunities to students, generally speaking, those who have
completed their graduation to involve themselves, on a voluntary basis, in nation - building activities for a specific
period on a whole time basis.   Any person who has completed his/her first degree course and is below 25 years of age
may get himself/herself enrolled as a National Service Volunteer for one/two years.   The requirement of age and
educational qualification is relaxable in case of SC/ST and women volunteers.   Each of the volunteers enrolled under
the scheme is paid a stipend of Rs. 500/- per month and fixed travelling allowance @ Rs. 200/- per month.   They also
get a contingency grant of Rs. 100/- per year.   At present, the number of National Service Volunteers sanctioned is
5400.    The Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan, the National Service Scheme, State Governments and some reputed NGOs
are the implementing agencies.
5.2       The Committee was informed that the stipend /TA to NSVs has now been enhanced and fixed at par with NRC
Volunteers i.e. Rs. 1000/- While approving the enhancement of stipend for volunteers, EFC had imposed the condition
that this should be met from within the available budget allocation.   Thus, the BE/RE 2001-2002 and BE 2002-2003
have been kept at the same level i.e. Rs. 5.40 crores.   This may result in the reduction of the number of volunteers to be

VI        NATIONAL INTEGRATION PROGRAMME
6.1       This scheme provides the framework for greater exchange and understanding amongst the youth of the country
and greater involvement of voluntary agencies in the task  of national integration and communal harmony.   Two main
programmes which are covered under this scheme are:
(i)
                              National Integration Camp (NIC)  and
(ii)                            Inter-State Youth Exchange Programme (ISYEP)
6.2       The Committee was informed that the scheme of National Youth Award has since been merged with the scheme
of Promotion of National Integration.   The Committee notes that the Plan allocation has been reduced to Rs. 3.40 crores
in BE 2002-2003 from Rs. 4.50 crores in BE 2001-2002 and RE Rs. 4.00 crores.   Reduction in RE is due to less number
of applications for grant-in-aid.   As on 1.3.2002, expenditure of only Rs. 2.30 crores has been incurred under this
scheme.
6.3       Under the scheme of Promotion of National Integration, proposals are received from various State Govts. and
NGOs which are examined on merit and funds are released on finding them admissible in all respects.   The Committee 
notes that out of 258 National Integration Camps held during 2000-2001, none was held in NE States of Mizoram,
Arunachal Pradesh and Tripura, only one camp was held in each of the State of Meghalaya, Nagaland and Sikkim and
two camps in Assam.   Manipur is the only state where eight camps were held during this period.   The Committee notes
with constraint that the situation has deteriorated further during 2001-2002  with only one camp being approved for
Assam in the entire NE region and Sikkim.   While noting that every year about  75% camps are organized by NGOs,
the Committee understands that the Central Govt. also organizes such camps through its subordinate offices like the
NYKs and NSS units and also in collaboration with State Govts.    The Committee fails to understand the reasons for
lack of initiative on the part of the Central Govt. in holding such camps in uncovered areas like N-E region.   The
Central Govt. should pursue with the concerned State Govts. and NYKs and NSS units for holding the National
Integration camps there.    The Committee also feels that this scheme should be reviewed so as to assess its impact
specially in States/UTs like Bihar, UP, MP and Delhi where a very large number of camps have been held during 2000-
2001.
VII       YOUTH HOSTELS
7.1       Youth Hostels are built to promote youth travel and to enable the youth to experience the rich cultural heritage of
our country.   The scheme of Youth Hostels has been conceived as a joint venture of the Central and State Govts.  
While the Central Govt. bears the cost of construction, the State Govts.  provide developed plot of land equipped with
water, electricity and approach roads.   After completion, Youth Hostels are handed over to the State Govts. for
management.  
7.2       The Committee notes that so far 63 Youth Hostels have been constructed in different States.   Besides that, 21
Youth Hostels are under various stages of construction and 23 Youth Hostels have been approved in principle,
construction has yet to be started.   Undue delay in completion of construction process of Youth Hostels has been
engaging the attention of the Committee since last year.   From the details about 21 Youth Hostels under construction,
the Committee is shocked to learn that there are Youth Hostels approved as early as in 1986 where 1
st and even 2 nd
instalment of central funds for construction had been released at the earliest, with concerned State Govt taking ten years
in identifying suitable land for the Youth Hostel.   Construction of Youth Hostel in Lucknow was approved in principle 
on 21.8.87, and State Govt identified the land in 1998.   Three instalments of central funds amounting to Rs. 70.00 lakhs
released so far, with the first instalment released in March, 1988 and the construction is yet to be  completed.   Position
with regard to the remaining Youth Hostels is more or less the same.   So far as 23 Youth Hostels approved in principle
are concerned, the earliest dates back to 1986.   Not much progress, apart from correspondence with the State

Governments,  has been made.   It seems that these proposals will continue to remain for another ten years or so.   The

VIII      NATIONAL RECONSTRUCTION CORPS SCHEME (NRC)
8.1       The scheme aims at providing an opportunity to Youth who are matriculate and attained the age of 18 years  to
participate in the process of nation building.   The NRC volunteers will be deployed by NYKS on specific projects
relating to community and national development.   The deployment of volunteers will be for one year and they will get a
stipend of Rs. 1,000/- p.m. exclusive of travel expenses. There would be sufficient representation of women and SC/ST
/OBC youth.  
8.2       Under the Scheme, a Project Officer and 100 Volunteers have been selected by a duly constituted committee for
each district and for 80 districts, 80 project officers and 8000 volunteers have been deployed by the NYKS.   Seven
schemes of the Government have been identified for implementation by the NRC Volunteers in the targeted districts.  
These are (i) Swarnjayant Gram Swarojgar Yojna, (ii) National Anti-Malaria Programme, (iii) Swarnjayanti Shahri
Rojgar Yojana (iv) Integrated Watershed Development, (v) Renewable Energy System, (vi) Social Forestry and (vii) 
Promotion of Literacy.   The Committee has been informed that this scheme has became operational from 1
st April,
2001 in 80 selected districts of the country.   Forty more districts will be added during the second year of its
operationalisation.   The scheme will be evaluated after the second year and depending on the outcome of the
evaluation, the Ministry proposes to extend this scheme to all the districts of the country, either as a Central Sector
Scheme or a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.
8.3       The Committee hopes that the  scheme would fulfill its objectives. The Committee has, however, a word of
caution in view of different schemes relating to different Departments and Ministries sought to be implemented under
the umbrella of one single scheme.  The idea is a laudable one.  But the Department will have to play a pro-active role
by having proper co-ordination with all the Departments/Ministries and acting as the main linking force between the
implementing agency and the concerned Department.  The Committee would like to point out that a strong monitoring
mechanism also needs to be evolved from the very beginning for effective implementation of  the scheme of National
Reconstruction Corps.  The Committee also recommends that instead of launching all the targeted schemes in a
particular district simultaneously,  assessment should be made about their requirement and only thereafter schemes
should be implemented on priority basis. 
IX        PROMOTION OF YOUTH ACTIVITIES AND TRAINING
9.1       Following schemes for youth welfare were being operated during the Ninth Plan:
i)
        Financial Assistance to Voluntary Organisations working  in the field of Youth;
ii)      Training of Youth;

1999-2000.  Likewise from UP, 48 NGOs are having funds amounting to more that Rs. 75.00 lakhs with them since
1999-2000.  The Committee has been given to understand that action has been taken to stop further Grants to such
NGOs and the matter is being actively pursued with them.  The Committee does not have much faith in this exercise. 
The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation that all the inherent flaws particularly relating to handling of
earlier schemes by NGOs should not be reflected in the revised scheme.  At the same time, the Committee fails to
understand as to how NGOs not having the proper antecedents are released Govt. funds for running different schemes. 
The Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need for having an element of accountability at every level –
Centre/State/district.  Only then, things might improve.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that a fool proof
mechanism for selection of NGOs should be put into practice.  Efforts should also be made to avoid concentration of
many NGOs in one particular  district. (Para 9.3)
The Committee hopes that all the preliminary formalities for these two proposed schemes have been completed.
(Para 9.4)

XI        SPECIAL AREA GAMES (SAG) SCHEME
            The Committee would like to be apprised about the progress made in this matter.  The Committee hopes that the

whenever brought to its notice. (Para 11.3)

The Committee is not in favour of allocating a token provision for a yet to be approved scheme.  General trend
noticed by the Committee in such matters is that token provision is made year after year with the scheme continuing in a
proposal stage.  The Committee is of the strong view that only after completing all the requisite preliminary formalities

XIII      INCENTIVE SCHEMES
It is understood that these schemes are proposed to be brought under one composite scheme.  The Committee
welcomes the initiative as, by and large,  these schemes have failed to achieve their targets during Ninth Plan period. 
The Committee strongly feels that there is a need for giving wide publicity to such incentive schemes so that the
targeted persons can get the maximum benefit thereunder.  The Committee , therefore, recommends that the Ministry
should make concerted efforts in this direction. (Para 13)
XIV     GRANTS TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS.
The Committee has been given to understand that these federations being autonomous organisations, Govt. have
very little say in their affairs.  The Committee, however, is of the view that as Govt. funding is there, there has to be an
element of accountability also.  Out of 50 federations provided financial assistance during 2001-2002, allocations range
from Rs. 23,750 to Rs. 1,91,17,000.  The Committee strongly feels that submission of quarterly progress reports should
be made a pre-condition  for further release of funds.  The Committee finds that School Games Federation of India and
Tug of War Federation of India were provided Rs. 18,72,809 and Rs. 10,00,000 respectively in 2001-2002.  The
Committee would like to have an idea about the activities of these federations.  The Committee recommends that an
exercise for reviewing and evaluating the work done by all federations should also be initiated.  Such an exercise is
likely to clear the picture about the inherent flaws in the system and also come up with suggestions for improvement.     
(Para 14.3)
XV       GRANT FOR CREATION OF SPORTS INFRASTRUCTURE
The Committee finds that the main reason for such a situation is that some States have not incurred their share of
expenditure with the result the Department could not release the Central Govt’s share as the scheme prescribes incurring
of State Govt’s share  first.  Last year, the Committee noticed that 274 sports infrastructure projects sanctioned from
1990 onwards till 28.2.01 were in various stages of construction.  The  Committee has been apprised that during the
period 1998-2001, 65 projects have been completed after a lot of persuasion and constant correspondence.  The
Committee, however, feels that this has not made any  significant change in the number of pending projects.  The
Committee is not aware about the number of projects completed during 2001-2002.  The Committee has been given to
understand that no time limit has been prescribed under the scheme for completion of projects, indication of one  to two
years’ time for completion indicated in the application form and provision regarding claiming assistance within two
years in the approval letter notwithstanding.  The Committee is of the firm view that adherence to the time limit by both
the implementing agencies and the Ministry would have shown a better record.  These over-delayed projects have
virtually defeated the very purpose of launching of the scheme for Creation of Sports Infrastructure. (Para 15.2)
XVI     GRANT FOR INSTALLATION OF SYNTHETIC PLAYING SURFACES
            During the Ninth Plan period, allocation of funds and utilization thereof under this scheme has shown a
declining trend.  The Committee observes that situation has been very discouraging during 2001-2002, the last year of
the Ninth Plan.  Allocation of Rs. 5.40 crores was reduced to Rs. 3.85 crores at the RE stage and expenditure reported
upto 1.3.2002 is only Rs. 43 lakhs.  The Committee notes that the main reason for under-utilization of funds under this
scheme has been the difficulty being faced in providing the matching in share as well as failure of user agencies
furnishing the progress report and utilization certificates of the already approved projects.  The Committee has been
informed that during Ninth Plan, 16 synthetic hockey surfaces and 6 synthetic athletic tracks in different parts of the
country have been approved with Central assistance of Rs. 20.22 crores and the balance of Rs. 9.66 crores payable for
these projects.  The Committee has no other alternative but to conclude that the approval of 22 projects under the
scheme of `Grant for Installation of Synthetic Playing Surface’ indicates a very dismal picture as apparently not even a
single could be completed and put to effective use during the Ninth Plan period.  The Committee strongly urges the
Ministry to expedite the completion of all these pending projects without any further delay. (Para 16.2)
The Committee hopes that there will be no further delay in the launching of the revised scheme pertaining
to infrastructure. (Para 16.3)

contribute their respective share in due time to make the scheme of State Sports Academies a success.   The Committee
