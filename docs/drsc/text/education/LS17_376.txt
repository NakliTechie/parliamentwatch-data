INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, having been authorised by the Committee to 
present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Three Hundred and Seventy Sixth 
Report of the Committee on the Demands for Grants (Demand No. 25) of the Department of 
School Education & Literacy for the year 2026-27.  
2. In accordance with the constitutional provisions under Article 113, the estimated 
expenditure of the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India projected under 
various Demands for Grants for the upcoming financial year needs to be voted by Parliament. 
As a sequel thereto, Demands for Grants of the relevant Ministries/Departments stand 
referred to concerned Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee to make a close 
scrutiny thereof under Rule 270 of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the 
Council of States (Rajya Sabha). While making scrutiny of the Demands for Grants, the 
Committee has made an appraisal of the performance, programmes, schemes and policies of 
the Department of School Education and Literacy vis-a-vis expenditure made out of the 
Consolidated Fund of India in the current financial year. 
3. The Budget allocations of the Ministry of Education have been sought under two 
Demand Numbers, i.e, Demand No. 25 pertains to the Department of School Education and 
Literacy and Demand No.26 pertains to Department of Higher Education. In this Report, a 
scrutiny of Demand No. 25 pertaining to the Department of School Education and Literacy of 
the Ministry of Education for the Financial Year 2026-27 has been made by the Committee. 
While scrutiny of Demand No. 26 pertaining to Department of Higher Education is covered 
in Report number 375th of the Committee. 
4.  During the course of examination of Demands, the Committee heard the views of the 
Secretary, Department of School Education and Literacy; Commissioner, Navodaya 
Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS); Commissioner, Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS); 
Chairperson, National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS); Chairman, Central Board of 
Secondary Education (CBSE); Director, National Council of Educational Research and 
Training (NCERT); and Director, National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE) etc., in its 
meeting held on 20th February, 2026.

CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
OVERVIEW OF THE DEPARTMENT OF SCHOOL EDUCATION AND LITERACY 
1.1 School education forms the bedrock of a nation’s intellectual, social and economic 
development. School serves not merely as institutions of learning but as structured 
environments where children are nurtured to think critically, act responsibly and contribute 
meaningfully to the society. A strong school education system ensures the development of 
literacy, numeracy and cognitive skills, which are indispensable. Investing in school 
education yields long-term dividends in the form of a skilled workforce and sustainable 
economic growth. In essence, the quality and accessibility of school education directly 
influence the trajectory of national progress. It is in this backdrop, the Committee on 
Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports considered the Demand for Grants 2026-27 
of the Department of School Education and Literacy, Ministry of Education. 
1.2 The Ministry of Education (MoE) is the apex body responsible for framing and 
implementing the National Education Policy, overseeing educational programmes at all 
levels, and developing systemic educational infrastructure across the country. The 
Department of School Education and Literacy decides the policy and programmes relating to 
school education, adult education and digital education with focus on quality, equity and 
inclusion. 
The Ministry of Education comprises of two Departments: 
(i) Department of School Education and Literacy (DoSEL); and  
(ii) Department of Higher Education (DoHE) 
1.3 Vision and Mission 
Vision: To develop a comprehensive and holistic school education system anchored in the 
constitutional values of equality, justice, and social cohesion that prepare every child in India 
to be a creative, critical thinker and a responsible global citizen, in alignment with the vision 
of NEP 2020. 
Mission: To ensure universal access to quality schooling from Balvatika to Grade XII, 
promote foundational literacy and numeracy, reduce dropout rates, bridge gender and social 
gaps, and nurture innovation through technology-integrated learning, thereby enabling every 
child to realise his/her full potential. 
1.4 Objectives of the Department 
 To formulate the National Policy on school education and ensure its implementation. 
 To ensure planned development, universal access, and improvement in quality of 
school education across the country. 
 To provide special attention to disadvantaged groups — economically deprived, 
females, SC/ST/OBC, and minorities. 
 To provide financial assistance in the form of scholarships and other support to 
deserving students. 

2 
 
 To administer the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 
2009. 
 To implement the National Education Policy 2020 at school level through curriculum, 
pedagogy, and assessment reforms. 
 To promote digital education, foundational literacy and numeracy, and innovation in 
school education. 
 To encourage international cooperation in education, including with UNESCO and 
foreign governments. 
1.5 Autonomous and Regulatory Bodies under the Department 
The following Autonomous Bodies and Institutions are under the administrative control of 
the Department of School Education and Literacy, involved in policy formulation, curriculum 
development, teacher training, and implementation of various schemes: 
 National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) 
 Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) 
 Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS) 
 National Bal Bhawan (NBB) 
1.6 The Department of School Education and Literacy also administers the Right of Children 
to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009, as amended from time to time. The Act 
mandates the appropriate Government to provide free and compulsory elementary education 
to every child of the age group of 6 to 14 years in a neighbourhood school. 
1.7 The major Legislative and Policy Framework under the Department of School Education 
and Literacy are as follows: 
 Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009 
 National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 
 National Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF-SE) 2023 
 National Curriculum Framework for Foundational Stage (NCF-FS) 2022 
 National Food Security Act, 2013 (for PM POSHAN)

2.9 An analysis of the budgetary trends over the last four financial years reveals the following 
significant patterns: 
 The overall BE allocation has increased from Rs. 63,444.37 crore in 2022-23 to Rs. 
83,562.26 crore in 2026-27, representing a cumulative increase of approximately 
31.7% over four years. 
 The year-on-year increase in BE has ranged between 6.35% (2026-27) and 8.44% 
(2023-24). The Committee has previously noted that a minimum annual increase of 8-
10% is necessary to accommodate inflation and maintain real expenditure levels. 
 The Scheme allocation in BE 2026-27 is Rs. 66,641.02 crore (79.75% of total) and 
Non-Scheme allocation is Rs. 16,921.24 crore (20.25% of total). The persistent gap 
between BE and Actuals across all years indicates under-utilization, with the gap 
being most pronounced in CSS schemes, particularly Samagra Shiksha, PM 
POSHAN, and PM SHRI. 
 The Department had projected Rs. 98,331.90 crore to the Ministry of Finance for BE 
2026-27, but the actual allocation is Rs. 83,562.26 crore - a variance of 15.02%.  
 Public expenditure on education as a percentage of GDP remains below the NEP 2020 
target of 6%, currently estimated at approximately 4.17% (2021-22), indicating 
significant headroom for enhancement.

Pradhan Mantri Innovative Learning Programme (DHRUV)

personal mentorship and exposure to leading institutions and experts. By fostering curiosity, 
creativity and innovative thinking, DHRUV aims to develop future leaders, researchers and 
innovators who can contribute to national development. The initiative also promotes a culture 
of excellence in learning and encourages young minds to pursue knowledge, research and 
problem-solving for societal progress. The guidelines for the scheme are presently being 
finalised after extensive stakeholder deliberations.

National Means cum Merit Scholarship Scheme (NMMSS) 
3.9 The National Means-cum-Merit Scholarship Scheme was launched in the year 2008 with 
the objective of awarding scholarships to meritorious students from economically weaker 
sections to arrest the dropout rate at the Class VIII level and encourage them to continue 

12 
 
secondary education up to Class XII. The scheme provides scholarships of Rs. 12,000 per 
annum (Rs. 1,000 per month) per student at the Class IX stage, continuing up to Class XII, 
subject to the fulfilment of eligibility criteria. Scholarships are awarded to selected students 
of class IX every year and their continuation or renewal in classes X to XII for study in State 
Government, Government-aided and Local body schools under the scheme. As per the 
guidelines of the NMMSS, the beneficiary students should get a minimum of 60% marks in 
class-X for continuation of scholarship (relaxable by 5% for SC/ST candidates) for class-XI 
and class-XII. 
3.10 The Committee was informed that to ensure fair access to NMMSS test for students 
from diverse backgrounds, the NMMSS examination is not conducted at Central level but at 
the State-level. Each State/UT is assigned the task of conducting its own test, at State level in 
different languages as per the prescribed syllabus for Class VIII.  
3.11 The scheme is administered through the National Scholarship Portal (NSP), which 
operates on the PFMS platform, which was developed for streamlining and fast tracking the 
release of Scholarships and assuring the delivery of scholarships with efficiency, 
transparency and reliability across Ministries/Departments of the GOI. AII the qualified 
candidates across all States/UTs submit their applications for scholarship through NSP, which 
gets verified at two levels, i.e. at Institute Nodal Officer (L1) and District Nodal Officer (L2). 
Those applications which are finally verified at two levels are considered for sanction of 
scholarship amount. Aadhaar Payment Bridge (APB) gateway is used for disbursal of 
scholarship through NSP in DBT mode. The rate of successful payments under APB system 
is approximately 95% every year. 
3.12 As per the Evaluation study report of Institute of Economic Growth, 2025 the scheme 
has been successful in ensuring that the students continue to meet the continuation/renewal 
criteria to continue their scholarships in class-XI and class-XII. At the same time, this criteria 
has motivated the beneficiary students for better academic performance in class-X and class-
XII examination. As per the study, the scheme is effective and benefiting the meritorious 
students of economically disadvantaged sections in continuation of higher education and it 
has been strongly realized that this scholarship scheme should be continued in future with the 
prescribed policy changes and recommendation. This scheme is not only preventing school 
dropouts and early marriage of girl-children but also improving equity and quality in 
education. Overall, the enhanced outlay aligns with the objectives of the National Education 
Policy 2020 by promoting equity, reducing dropout rates, and ensuring continuity of 
education at the secondary level and achieving the objective of 100% GER in secondary 
education by 2030.

Atal Tinkering Labs (ATL) Scheme 
3.21 The Atal Tinkering Labs (ATL) Scheme is a flagship initiative of the Department of 
School Education and Literacy (DoSEL), Ministry of Education, announced in the Union 
Budget 2025-26. The scheme aims to establish 50,000 ATL in Government schools to foster 
a spirit of innovation, scientific temper, creativity, and problem-solving skills among school 
students from Class VI to XII, in alignment with the objectives of the National Education 
Policy 2020. The ATL programme seeks to create a nation of innovators by providing hands-
on learning experience through modern tools such as, AI, 3D printers, robotics kits, 
electronics, Internet of Things (IoT) devices, and other STEM-related equipment. The 
scheme is being positioned as a transformative intervention to develop a future-ready 
workforce. 
3.22 The experience of ATL 1.0 as a pathway to attract students to science and technology - 
has been used as evidence to scale this pedagogy systematically through ATL 2.0. Selection 
of ATL schools under ATL 2.0 prioritizes higher secondary government schools (classes 6–
12) as Hubs, which will then be mapped to nearby Spoke schools, ensuring that students from 
surrounding government, government-aided and even private schools can also benefit from 
the ATL ecosystem. ATL 2.0 proposes a more rigorous monitoring architecture building on 
ATL 1.0’s third-party assessment, which showed that 97.5% of existing ATLs were 
operational and that schools reported clear gains in 21st-century competencies. Under the 
new scheme, key performance indicators (KPIs) will be defined for both Hub and Spoke 
schools covering frequency and quality of ATL sessions, project completion, participation in 
competitions, mentorship engagement and inclusion of girls and disadvantaged groups. 
3.23 In response to a query of the Committee during the meetings, the Department of School 
Education and Literacy has informed that for national skill development and entrepreneurship 
linkages, the scheme explicitly plans to: 
 Leverage the Mentor India/Mentor of Change ecosystem and explore onboarding 
World Skills and National Skills Competition mentors as ATL mentors in 
collaboration with MSDE, thus connecting school-level innovation with national skill 
standards. 
 Build structured innovation pathways from ATLs to higher education institutions 
(IITs, NITs, engineering colleges, other HEIs of eminence) through formal mapping 
of Hub ATLs to local HEIs for mentorship, lab visits and knowledge exchange. 
 Institutionalize synergy with Dept of Science and Technology’s (DST) 
INSPIRE-MANAK scheme so that student awardees can use ATL infrastructure to 
convert ideas into workable prototypes and connect ATLs with DST’s NIDHI 
incubation ecosystem for incubation and scale-up of promising student innovations.

Samagra Shiksha (SS)

3.38 The Committee notes that there is persistent gap between the fund allocation and 
actual utilisation under Samagra Shiksha – for example 2025-26 BE of Rs. 41250 crore 
vs AE of Rs. 22634 crore as of 13.02.2026. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Department of School Education and Literacy should introduce a stricter release-
linked-to-expenditure mechanism and review the fund utilisation pattern of States/UTs 
on monthly basis in order to make the scheme efficacious. 
3.39 The Committee notes that the dropout rate for some States is persistently above the 
national average which is a matter of concern. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that the Department should identify at least ten districts with the highest secondary 
dropout rates in each of the States where secondary dropout exceeds 15% and prepare 
a district-specific intervention plan in collaboration with the State government under 
Samagra Shiksha that address the locally-dominant factors of dropout.  
3.40 The Committee is of the view that the target of universal school infrastructure 
(100% schools with toilets, drinking water, electricity, ramps, and libraries) should be 
achieved on a time-bound basis. In this regard, the Committee is of the view that special 
attention is needed for ramp availability which is currently 87.7% in 2024-25 and 
playground access which is currently 79.9%. 
3.41 The Committee notes that some States lag behind in closing the gender gap with 
respect to gross enrolment. In this regard, the Committee recommends that  gender gap 
in enrolment at secondary level must be addressed through targeted incentives, with 
special focus on States like Bihar, Jharkhand, and Madhya Pradesh where girls' 
dropout rates remain high. 
3.42 The Committee notes that the NIPUN Bharat Mission has seen some successes but is set to 
be wound up this Financial Year. The Committee recommends that it be extended for another 5 
years till 2032, with its ambit broadened to include children from Grades 3 to 5, with a focus on 

24 
 
oral reading fluency in addition to basic literacy and numeracy. The Committee also 
recommends that the Ministry seek to increase the annual budgetary allocation from Rs. 2,500 
crores to Rs. 6,000 crores to enable the Missions’s expanded focus and to enhance funding for 
Early Childhood Care and Education, teaching-learning materials, and capacity-building 
support for teachers. This increase would expand reach from 2.5 crore to 6 crore children 
annually while maintaining the Rs. 1,000 per-child investment and keeping total expenditure 
under 1% national school education budgets.  
 
3.43 The Committee further recommends that the Ministry consider a census assessment for all 
Grade 5 students (Government and private) to diagnose system health and enable early course 
correction post the completion of the expanded NIPUN Bharat Mission. The Ministry may also 
consider the use of Personalised Adaptive Learning (PAL) products, leveraging advances in 
technology to support education among children. 
 
3.44 The Committee notes that the NEP calls for universal Early Childhood Care and 
Education. The Committee notes that it is particularly important for the Ministry and the WCD 
Ministry to work on education for 5-year–old children, whose health requirements for 
vaccinations, etc. have largely concluded and who are ready to learn. The Ministry should 
develop a focused package for 5-year-olds with special attention to their educational needs, and 
ensure that any such learning protocol is universalized across Bal Vatikas and Anganwadis. 
 
Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN) 
3.45 Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN), earlier known as the National 
Programme of Mid-Day Meals in Schools (MDM), is one of the largest school nutrition 
programmes in the world. It is a rights-based Centrally Sponsored Scheme under the National 
Food Security Act, 2013. The primary objective of the scheme is to improve the nutritional 
status of children enrolled in Classes I to VIII and Balvatika in Government and Government-
aided schools, while simultaneously enhancing school attendance, enrolment, and retention 
rates. 
3.46 The scheme is one of the foremost rights based Centrally Sponsored Scheme 
implemented in partnership with the States and Union Territories (UTs) for providing one hot 
cooked and nutritious meal to all children studying in Bal Vatika (just before class-I) and 
classes I to VIII of Government and Government-aided schools.  The Scheme covers about 
11 crore children in more than 10.35 lakh schools and more than 24 lakh Cook-cum-Helpers 
are engaged for preparation of the meals in all the States/ UTs. The main objectives of the 
Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman Scheme are to address two of the pressing problems 
for the majority of children in India, viz. hunger and education: 
i. Improving the nutritional status of children studying in classes Bal Vatikas (just 
before class I) and I – VIII in Government and Government-Aided Schools. 
ii. Encouraging poor children, belonging to disadvantaged sections, to attend school 
more regularly and help them concentrate on classroom activities. 
iii. Providing nutritional support to children of elementary stage in drought-affected areas 
during summer vacation and during disaster times.

School Education and Literacy has put in place an automated system of data collection for 
real time monitoring of PM POSHAN. Such data (on number of meals served on that day and 
reasons if meals not served) is being continuously captured from schools. Under this system,

Strengthening Teaching-Learning and Results for States (STARS) 
3.56 The Strengthening Teaching-Learning and Results for States (STARS) Project is a 
Centrally Sponsored Scheme approved by the Cabinet in October 2020, implemented with 
World Bank assistance. The project aims to improve measurable educational outcomes and 
support school-to-work transition for improved labour market outcomes. Implemented in six 
States - Himachal Pradesh, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, and Rajasthan - 
the STARS project is aligned with NEP 2020's vision of quality-based learning outcomes. 
The project was originally approved for five years up to FY 2024-25 and has been extended 
for two additional years till March 2027. 
3.57 The key focus areas under the STARS Project include Foundational Learning and Early 
Childhood Education (ECE); Strengthening of Learning Assessment Systems; Teacher 
Development and Improvement of Classroom Practices; Governance and Decentralized 
Service Delivery; and School-to-Work Transition and Vocational Education. The expected 
outcomes of the STARS Project are as follows: 
 Increase in students achieving minimum proficiency in grade 3 language in select 
states 
 Improvement in secondary school completion rate in select states 
 Improvement in governance index scores in select states 

29 
 
 Strengthened learning assessment systems with fully operational National assessment 
centre and state assessment centres 
 Partnerships developed to facilitate cross-learning between states 
 State-level improved service delivery by Strengthening Early Years Education, 
Improving Learning Assessment Systems, Improving Teacher Performance and 
Classroom Practice, Strengthening Governance and Decentralized Management for 
Improved Service Delivery, and Skill education and training. 
3.58 The Committee was informed that under the STARS Project, institutions such as 
SCERTs, DIETs, and BRCs/CRCs are being strengthened 
to enhance academic support, teacher professional development, mentoring, and evidence-
based planning, with the district positioned as the primary unit for implementation and 
monitoring. The Project is also driving major assessment reforms through the establishment 
of an independent National Assessment Centre and State Assessment Cells, alongside 
adoption of competency-based assessments supported by online item banks.  Further,  the  
Project  is  institutionalizing  performance- based governance by incentivizing improvements 
in governance related PGI indicators, strengthening systems for teacher deployment, 
attendance, school inspection, and planning, and ensuring the eﬀective use of UDISE+ for 
accurate, validated, and publicly accessible data, thereby supporting a shift toward outcome-
oriented monitoring and governance. Main achievements of the scheme are as under: 
 National Assessment Centre-PARAKH (Performance Assessment, Review and 
Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic Development) was set up in NCERT as per the 
mandate under STARS on 8th February 2023. 
 Under the STARS scheme, states have launched impactful school readiness initiatives 
to strengthen early childhood education such as Pahile Paule initiative in Maharashtra 
engaging over 14 lakh children and forming 2.5 lakh mothers' groups across 70,000 
schools in School Readiness Melas, Pehli Shikshak-Maa program in Himachal 
Pradesh resulting in up to 40-47% improvements in cognitive, language, and socio-
emotional skills, Responsive Parenting Kit in Rajasthan distributed to over 25,000 
early learning centers. 
 All six STARS states have established State Assessment Cells. These Assessment 
cells have developed learning outcome-based online item banks, as well as teacher 
training modules for Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) and 
classroom-based assessment. They have also conducted teacher training on CCE and 
classroom-based assessment. 
 Increase in secondary school completion rate among the six STARS states from 
79.95% to 93.53% in FY 2022-23.(UDISE report 2022-23) 
 Increase in students achieving minimum proficiency in grade 3 language in six 
STARS states from 44.17% in NAS 2021 to 70.33% in PARAKH Rashtriya 
Sarvekshan (PRS) 2024.

PM Schools for Rising India (PM SHRI) 
3.64 PM Schools for Rising India (PM SHRI), earlier known as the Exemplar Schools 
scheme, is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme launched in 2022. The scheme aims to develop and 
upgrade more than 14,500 model schools across the country that will serve as exemplars of 
the National Education Policy 2020 in action - providing high-quality, equitable, and 
inclusive education to students across diverse backgrounds. These schools are expected to 
lead educational transformation in their respective regions and showcase best practices. A 
total of 13,076 schools have been selected as PM SHRI schools from 33 States/UTs along 
with KVS/NVS/NCERT. The total cost of the project is Rs. 27360 crore spread over a period 
of 5 years which includes central share of Rs. 18128 crore.

participation in the learning process in accordance with the vision of NEP 2020.

New India Literacy Programme (NILP/ULLAS) 
3.73 The New India Literacy Programme (NILP), branded as ULLAS (Understanding of 
Lifelong Learning for All in Society) / Nav Bharat Saaksharta Karyakram, is a Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme of Adult Education for the period 2022-27. The scheme is designed to 
impart foundational literacy and numeracy skills to all persons above 15 years of age who are 
not literate. It covers five components: Foundational Literacy and Numeracy, Critical Life 
Skills, Vocational Skills, Basic Education, and Continuing Education. The scheme is 
implemented in a hybrid mode through volunteerism based on the vision of making Bharat 
'Jan Jan Saakshar', driven by Kartavyabodh (a sense of duty) and Janbhagidari (collective 
responsibility), utilising School/HEIs platforms and community engagement through 
volunteerism. Volunteers include students of Schools, Teacher Education Institutions of 
NCTE, Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) etc. to teach the non-literates. The scheme is 
aligned with the recommendations of NEP 2020 on Adult Education and Lifelong Learning. 
More than 2.37 crore learners from 33 States/UTs have participated in the literacy test under 
the scheme. 
3.74 The scheme has five components, namely; Foundational Literacy and Numeracy, 
Critical Life Skills, Basic Education, Vocational Skills, and Continuing Education. The 
scheme covers basic reading, writing and numeracy, digital literacy, financial literacy and life 
skills relevant to daily life. Learning content is provided in regional languages through 
learner-friendly primers, digital platforms, and community learning centres to ensure 
universal access and inclusivity. A dedicated Mobile App of ULLAS was launched on 
29.7.2023 for registering learners and volunteer teachers and also enables teaching learning 
process by providing access to primers available in 27 languages. As on date, more than 3.14 
crore Learners and 46.98 lakh Volunteer Teachers (VTs) have been registered on Mobile App 
of ULLAS till date.

Himachal Pradesh have declared Full Literacy. The Literacy rate as per PLFS 2023 stands at 
80.9% as in 2023-24.

Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) 
4.1 Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) was established in 1965 as a registered 
autonomous body under the Ministry of Education to cater to the educational needs of 
children of transferable Central Government employees. The KVS operates Kendriya 
Vidyalayas (KVs) - Central Government schools providing education from Class I to XII - 
across India and abroad. KVS schools follow the CBSE curriculum. The organisation is fully 
financed by the Government of India and is India's largest chain of centrally-managed 
schools, with a presence in every State and UT. 
4.2 The main objectives of Kendriya Vidyalayas are as under: 
 to cater to the educational needs of the children of transferable Central Government 
employees including Defence and Para-Military Personnel by providing a common 
programme of education. 
 to provide, establish, endow, maintain, control and manage schools for the children of 
transferable employees of the Government of India, floating population and others 
including those living in remote and undeveloped locations of the country and to do 
all acts and things necessary for or conducive to the promotion of such schools. 
 to pursue excellence and set pace in the field of school education; 
 to initiate and promote experimentation and innovativeness in education in 
collaboration with other bodies like the CBSE, NCERT etc., and 
 to develop the spirit of national integration and create a sense of ‘Indianness’ among 
children.

Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS) 
4.12 Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS), established in 1986, is an autonomous organisation 
under the Ministry of Education, registered under the Societies Registration Act 1860. NVS 
establishes, runs and operates Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas (JNVs) - fully residential 
schools providing quality education from Class VI to XII – all over the country primarily for 
talented rural children, with at least 75% seats reserved for rural students. JNVs are free-of-
cost institutions targeting socially and economically backward gifted students who lack 
access to accelerated learning due to financial, social and rural disadvantages, where students 
receive food, lodging, uniform, books, and stationery at the expense of the Government. As 
per the policy of Government of India, one JNV is to be established in each district of the 
country. Keeping in view with this policy, JNVs has been sanctioned all over the country, 
except the State of Tamil Nadu, which has not yet accepted the Navodaya Vidyalaya Scheme. 
4.13 The main objectives of Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas are as under: 
 to provide good quality modern education to the talented students predominantly from 
rural areas irrespective of socio-economic status. 
 to nurture and promote the talent and creativity of the students 
 to develop integrated personality of the students under residential co-educational 
setup through value orientation and ultimately developing students to be good human 
beings and an asset to our Nation. 
 to make JNV as an excellent pace setting school in the District.

National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) 
4.24 The National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT), established in 
1961, is an autonomous organisation set up to advise and assist the Ministry of Education and 
State Governments in formulation and implementation of education policies. NCERT's core 
mandate encompasses curriculum development, preparation of textbooks, teacher education, 
research in education, and academic support to States. NCERT has recently developed new 
textbooks for Classes I-VIII aligned with the NCF-SE 2023 and NEP 2020 vision of 
competency-based, experiential, and multidisciplinary learning. NCERT is currently pursuing 
Deemed-to-be University status. 
4.25 The major objectives of NCERT are as follows: 
 to undertake, promote and coordinate research in areas related to school education and 
teacher education; 
 to prepare and publish model textbooks, supplementary reading material, newsletters, 
journals and develop educational kits, multimedia digital materials, etc.; 
 to organise pre-service and in-service training of teachers and teacher educators; 
develop and disseminate innovative educational techniques and practices; collaborate 
and network with state educational departments, universities, NGOs and other 
educational institutions; 
 to act as a clearing house for ideas and information in matters related to school 
education; and 
 to act as a nodal agency for achieving the goals of Universalisation of Elementary 
Education.

National Bal Bhawan (NBB) 
4.38 National Bal Bhawan (NBB), established in 1956, is an autonomous body fully financed 
by the Ministry of Education, Department of School Education and Literacy. It serves as a 
non-formal educational institution for children in the age group of 5 to 16 years. The 
institution was envisioned to promote learning through creative thinking, imagination and 
engaging activities for children. Its primary objective is to nurture and develop the creative 
abilities and skills of children by providing training in a wide range of activities such as art 
and craft, music, dance, stitching, handicrafts, weaving, clay modelling, science, museum 
activities, literacy, home management and physical education through a non-formal learning 
approach. It provides opportunities for cultural activities, fine arts, performing arts, science 
experimentation, and technology exploration, with special focus on children from 
economically weaker sections. 
4.39 The vision of National Bal Bhawan is as follows: 
 to let every child fully participate, contribute and strive towards a creative, humane, 
innovative and joyful World in all its Wonder. 

45 
 
 to provide opportunities that ignite curiosity and celebrate possibilities through 
immersive dynamic experiences in visual arts, scientific activities and physical 
activities. To foster values that mould self-confident, holistic and responsible citizens 
of the world.

ICT, AI and Digital Learning Initiatives 
5.1 Information and Communication Technology (ICT) and digital education occupy a 
central place in the implementation of NEP 2020. The Department of School Education and 
Literacy has made significant investments in ICT infrastructure, digital content, and AI-
integrated learning through its schemes. Key initiatives include the ICT component of 
Samagra Shiksha, PM e-VIDYA, DIKSHA (Digital Infrastructure for Knowledge Sharing), 
e-Pathshala, and the newly announced AVGC Content Creator Labs in secondary schools. 
The Finance Minister in her Budget Speech 2026-27 announced support to the Indian 
Institute of Creative Technologies, Mumbai, for setting up AVGC Content Creator Labs in 
15,000 secondary schools and 500 colleges. 
5.2 The Committee was informed during the course of meetings about the key digital 
initiatives undertaken in the field of ICT and Digital learning by the Department, are given as 
under: 
 DIKSHA (Digital Infrastructure for Knowledge Sharing): Serves as a comprehensive 
digital National platform for school education with digital learning content in multiple 
languages, accessed by crores of students, parents and teachers. It provides access to 
NCERT textbooks, energized digital textbooks with QR code integration, interactive 
learning materials, teacher professional development course and assessment tools. It 
acts as ‘One Nation, One Digital Platform’ under PM eVidya inititative. 
 PM e-VIDYA: Multi-mode platform integrating PM e-VIDYA DTH channels (One 
Class One Channel), online content, and digital textbooks. 
 NIPUN Bharat Digital Resources: Various FLN-focused digital content for 
foundational stage learners has been created under NIPUN Bharat. 
 AI in Curriculum: Budget Speech 2026-27 announced regarding specific measures for 
embedding AI in education curriculum from school level initiated in consultation with 
NCERT, CBSE, and IIT Madras. 
 AVGC Content Creator Labs: Announced in Budget 2026-27 - 15,000 secondary 
schools to get AVGC labs, linked to IICT Mumbai. 
 e-Jaadui Pitara (e-JP): Launched in February 2024, e-JP is a blend of technology and 
education to foster play-based pedagogy beyond classroom walls. This web based 
mobile application currently has more than 1,000 stories and includes songs, lullabies, 
riddles and activities available in 14 different languages. The application has 1lakh+ 
users till date. 
 NCERT: NCERT is conducting AI training programs that focus on foundational AI 
concepts, AI ethics, data literacy and the practical use of AI tools for lesson planning 
and assessments. Over 46,000 teachers have participated in AI awareness workshops 
and training. The Completion rate for teacher AI modules on the DIKSHA platform is 
63.23%.

OBSERVATIONS/ RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
1. The Committee observes that against a combined fund allocation of Rs. 62,660 crore 
in BE 2025-26 for Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS), AE as on 13th  February 
2026 stands at Rs. 32,296.54 crore which is barely 51.5% of the BE and only 60.1% 
of the RE of Rs. 53,730.02 crore in the same financial year. The Committee further 
notes that in Samagra Shiksha alone, the largest scheme run by the Department, AE 
is Rs. 22,633.88 crore against a BE of Rs. 41,250 crore which is 54.9% utilisation of 
allocated funds with less than six weeks left in the financial year. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends identifying the specific administrative, procedural and inter-
governmental bottlenecks responsible for under-utilisation in CSS schemes. 
(para 2.11) 
 
2. In backdrop of the Actual expenditure by the Department of School Education and 
Literacy during the FY 2025-26 and Rule 56(3) of General Financial Rules (GFR), 
Ministry of Finance, the Committee recommends the Department to adhere to Rule 
56(3) of GFR and to streamline its expenditure during FY 2026-27 to optimize the 
utilization of funds allocated to it and to avoid piling up of expenditure during the 
last quarter of the financial year. 
(para 2.12) 
 
3. The Committee observes that the Department had projected Rs. 98,331.90 crore to 
the Ministry of Finance for BE 2026-27, against which the actual allocation in BE 
2026-27 stands at Rs. 83,562.26 crore which is a shortfall of Rs. 14,769.64 crore i.e. 
15.02% compression. The Committee is of the view that the Department should 
project the shortfall, if required, at the time of Revised Estimates so that the scheme 
does not suffer due to shortage of funds. 
(para 2.13) 
 
4. The Committee is of the view that the capital expenditure, which stands at a 
negligible amount of Rs. 0.85 crore in BE 2026-27, should be substantially increased 
to support critical infrastructure requirements, particularly for schools in 
Aspirational Districts, LWE-affected areas, and tribal areas. 
(para 2.14) 
 
5. The Committee welcomes the steps taken by the DoSEL in pursuance of the 
recommendation given in 363rd Report and a MoU has been signed with the State of 
Kerala for implementation of PM SHRI Scheme. The Committee, therefore, 
reiterates its recommendation from 363rd Report to resolve the issue expeditiously 
and amicably with the States of Tamil Nadu and West Bengal. Further, the 
Committee is of the view that SNA-SPARSH, now being rolled out in States/UTs, 

53 
 
should be expanded to all States expeditiously to enable just-in-time release of 
funds, improved monitoring, and enhanced transparency in fund utilization.
