1.         The University of Allahabad Bill, 2004 seeks to restore the status of the University of Allahabad as a Central
University with the objective of strengthening this prestigious university and improving the quality of teaching and
research.
 
2.         The University of Allahabad , established in 1887, is one of the oldest and prestigious Universities in the
country.   It enjoyed a national and international reputation as a premier institution of higher learning. It used to be
referred as the Oxford of the East for its attainments and contributions in teaching, research and the formidable
academic reputation of its faculty members.   The alumni of the University included many eminent and distinguished
personalities, several in almost every area of life.
 
3.         The Committee was informed that the University of Allahabad was established originally as a Central University
but after the Government of India Act, 1919 made education as a provincial subject, it became a provincial university in
1921, and was reorganized as a unitary, teaching and residential university under the Allahabad University Act, 1921.  
Presently, it is governed by the Uttar Pradesh State Universities Act, 1973 (UP Act No. 10 of 1973).
 
4.         According to the Department of Secondary and Higher Education the University was facing difficulties owing
mainly to its rigid governance and structure, financial inadequacy, inappropriate student-faculty ratio etc. Various kinds
of pressures and counter pressures were acting against its all India character and national outlook. Therefore, there had
been demands for restoration of its Central University status ever since Independence.  This move picked up momentum
in 1990s and had the support of different Governments from time to time as per the chronological background given
below:-
(i)
                  In February, 1991, the then Prime Minister publicly endorsed the claim of the University to be taken over
by the Central Government;
(ii)                in October 1993, the then Minister of Human Resource Development made an announcement in the Rajya
Sabha and a team of University Grant Commission was sent to Allahabad  for an exploratory visit.  
Subsequently, the Central Government agreed for restoration of central university status to the University in-
principle, on the condition that the Government of Uttar Pradesh would hand over all assets;
(iii)
               in 1998, the Governor of Uttar Pradesh and Chancellor of the University raised the issue of giving central
university status for the University;
(iv)              there was a special mention in the Lok Sabha in December 2000 on the need to declare the University a
Central University;
(v)                in September 2001, the Chairman, University Grants Commission conveyed the strong support of the
Commission to provide central university status to the university;
(vi)              in December 2001, the Principal Secretary to the Governor, Uttar Pradesh forwarded a proposal of the
University for its conversion into a Central University;
(vii)             in January 2002, the then Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh, in a letter to the Minister of Human Resource
Development, requested that the University be accorded the status of a Central University; and
(viii)           the Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh, in her letter dated the 23 rd May 2003 to the Prime Minister, made a
similar request.

5.         The Department argued that in view of the persistent demands from various quarters for restoration of its Central
University status and the difficulties being faced by it,  the Government felt it expedient to provide the central status to
the University of Allahabad with a view to restore its glorious past and the excellence in academics and research in
different fields.  The University of Allahabad Bill, 2004 was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on the 20 th December 2004
for the purpose.
 
6.         The salient features of the aforesaid Bill are as under:-
 
(i)
                  The Bill seeks to dissolve the existing University of Allahabad, a state university of Uttar Pradesh,
to declare the University of Allahabad to be an institution of national importance and to provide for
its incorporation and matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.
(ii)                The University would be a unitary teaching and residential University with its Headquarters at
Allahabad having territorial jurisdiction extending to the area within a radius of sixteen kilometers
from the Convocation Hall of the University.
(iii)               It would function broadly on the pattern of the existing central universities.
(iv)              The existing Constituent College of the University, namely the Motilal  Nehru Medical College and
Swarup Rani Nehru Hospital, Allahabad, would continue to comprise the Faculty of Medicine of the
University as a University College.
(v)                The Kamala Nehru Post Graduate Medical Institute, Allahabad would be admitted to the privileges
of the University as a Constituent Institute of the University along with the other two existing
recognized Institutes, namely the Govind Ballabh Pant Social Science Institute, Allahabad and the
Harish-Chandra Research Institute of Mathematics and Mathematical Physics, Allahabad.
(vi)              All the eleven existing Associated Colleges of the University would be admitted to the privileges of
the University as Constituent Colleges of the University.
(vii)             The teachers of the University would be classified into those appointed by the University, and those
recognized by the University.  Both categories would have representation in the various decision-
making bodies of the University.
(viii)
           The annual accounts of the University shall be audited by the Comptroller and auditor-General of
India and shall be laid before both Houses of Parliament alongwith the audit report and the annual
report of the University.
(ix)              During the transitional period, the appointment of the first Officers like Chancellor and Vice
Chancellor and the constitution of the first bodies like the Court, Executive Council, and Academic
Council etc. would be made by the Central Government for specified terms.
 
7.         The Secretary, Department of Secondary and Higher Education during his presentation on the Bill before the
Committee informed inter-alia that the jurisdiction of the University of Allahabad, when it was established, was not
confined to the north only; it extended up to Nagpur in south also.   The University in fact, occupied a very important
place in the history of this nation.  The support being given by the U.P Government for the university including its own
income was highly inadequate. Therefore, need for central status was felt. During the process of consultation, the
Ministry had discussions with different interest groups including the officials, the students and the old distinguished
alumni as well, who by and large, supported making it a Central  University.  Apprehensions in the minds of the

stakeholders in the university have been cleared during the process of consultation with them before the draft Bill was
finalised.  The Secretary assured the Committee that the prime position of the University would not be disturbed.
 
8.         The Committee heard the Vice-Chancellors of the two prestigious Central Universities - Jawaharlal  Nehru
University and Delhi University on various provisions of the Bill in the light of their experiences over the years.  They
pointed out that the present Bill was basically a replica of the Delhi University Bill, which was established as far back as
in the year 1922. They felt that the provisions of the Bill provided same type of structure, authorities etc. as provided
some 40 to 45 years ago when Delhi University was set up.  Their argument was that lot of changes have taken place in
the field of education and university and higher education was not same today as it used to be when Delhi University or
Jawaharlal Nehru University was established.  No two institutions can be similar; they have to have their own
specifications keeping in terms of time and place, they argued.  With these sentiments many  suggestions were made by
the two Vice-Chancellors for making Allahabad University more effective and meaningful.
 
9.         Thereafter the Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sitting held on the 11
th March
2005 and suggested following amendments to various clauses of the Bill.
 
10.       The Committee recommends that in clause 7, line 45 of page 4, the number of sub clause is wrongly printed as
(xvi) which may be corrected as (xiv).
 
Clause 7(xii)
11.       Clause 7(xii) gives the power to the University to admit to its privileges colleges and institutions situated within
the territorial jurisdiction of the University, as University Colleges, Constituent Institutes and Constituent Colleges, and
to withdraw all or any of those privileges in accordance with such conditions as may be prescribed by the Statutes.
11.1     According to sub-clause xii of clause 7 the university can admit any number colleges, institutes etc to its
privileges.  The Committee feels that the ever-increasing size is something that affects both governance and standards
after a time.  This may dilute the quality of the University and for a Cental  University it is not advisable. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that sub-Clause (xii) of clause 7 may be deleted and the sub-clause xiii to xxii may
be renumbered as  Sub-clauses xii to xxi.
11.2
          The Committee recommends that the consequential changes necessitated due to the deletion may be made in
various clauses and Statutes of the Bill.
 
Clause 7 (xvii)
 
12.       Clause 7 (xvii), page 5, empowers the University to establish and recognize hostels and supervise residences of
the students of the University, make arrangements for promoting their health and general welfare and guide the
Constituent Colleges to like ends in respect of the students enrolled thereat.
 
12.1     Since the Bill contains provisions for the constituent institutes also, therefore, the Committee feels that
University should have similar powers with regard to hostels in constituent institutes also.  The Committee accordingly
recommends that in Clause 7(xvii) in line 7 after the words ‘Constituent Colleges’ the words “and Constituent

Institutes” should be added.
 
Clause 10(3)
 
13.       Clause 10(3), page 5, provides that the Visitor shall have the right to cause an inspection to be made, by such
person or persons as he may direct, of the University, its buildings, laboratories and equipment, and of any college or
institution maintained by the University or admitted to its privileges; and also of the examinations, teaching and other
work conducted or done by the University and to cause an inquiry to be made in like manner in respect of any matter
connected with the administration or finances of the University, and the said colleges and institutions.
 
13.1     The Committee feels that libraries are an important and integral constituent of the University system and they
have not been included in the above provision for inspection.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that in Clause
10(3) in line 48 after the word ‘buildings’, the word ‘libraries’ should also be added.
 
Clause 12
 
14.       Clause 12, page 7, provides that the following shall be the officers of the University: -
(1)
       the Chancellor;
(2)       the Vice-Chancellor;
(3)       the Pro-Vice Chancellor;
(4)       the Deans of Faculties;
(5)       the Registrar;
(6)       the Finance Officer; and
(7)       such other officers as may be declared by the Statutes to be officers of the University.
 
14.1     The Committee feels that the Controller of Examinations is an important functionary of any University and
therefore, he also should be included as one of the officers of the University, in the Act itself.
 
14.2     The Committee, accordingly, recommends that after ‘the Registrar’, the Controller of Examinations may be
added as the sixth Officer and the existing 6 and 7 items may be renumbered as 7 and 8.
 
Clause 14(3)
 
15.       Clause 14(3), page7, provides that the Vice-Chancellor may, if he is of the opinion that immediate action is
necessary on any matter, exercise any power conferred on any authority of the University by or under this Act and shall
report to such authority the action taken by him on such matter.
 
15.1     The Committee while accepting the rationale behind providing emergency powers to the Vice-Chancellor to deal
with urgent matters feels that the Vice Chancellor should be required to report such actions taken by him to the
appropriate authorities at the earliest available opportunity.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that in Clause
14(3), in line 22, the words ‘at its next meeting’ should be added after the word ‘authority’.

Clause  21
16.       The Committee's attention was drawn towards the corresponding provision in the Delhi University Act, where
the supremacy of the Court has been expressly provided. The Committee feels that the same may be added as Clause
21(3) in Allahabad University Act also. Therefore, the Committee recommends that after sub clause (2) of clause 21 a 
new sub-clause (3) may be added as follows:
(3)The Court shall be the Supreme authority of the University and shall have the power to review the acts of the
Executive Council and the Academic Council (save when these authorities have acted in accordance with the
power conferred upon them under this Act, the Statutes, or the Ordinances) and shall exercise all the powers for
the University not otherwise provided for by this Act or the Statutes.
Clause 25(2) & (3)
17.              The Committee further notes that Delhi University Act provides for a Board for looking after health, residence
and disciplinary aspects. The Committee feels that such Board should be provided in Allahabad University Act also.The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the following two sub clauses may be added to Clause 25.
25(2) The University shall include a Residence, Health and Discipline Board and such other Boards as may be
prescribed by the Statutes.
25(3)    The constitution, powers and duties of the Residence, Health and Disciplinary Board, and all other
Boards of the Universities shall be prescribed by the ordinances.
 
Clause 45(1)(a)
18.       Clause 45, page 13, provides for transitional provisions.  Clause 45(1)(a) provides that the first Chancellor and
the first Vice-Chancellor shall be appointed by the Central Government in such manner and on such conditions as may
be deemed fit and each of the said officers shall hold office for such term, not exceeding five years, as may be specified
by the Central Government.
 
18.1     The Committee feels that transitional period being the formative years would be very crucial when future of the
University would take shape.  Therefore, the transitional period should be as short as possible and the regular set-up
should be established at the earliest.   The Committee feels that the term of Chancellor and the Vice-Chancellor
appointed by the Central Government should be reduced to three years from the proposed five years.  The Committee,
therefore, recommends that in existing clause 45(1)(a) line 38 the word ‘three years’ may be substituted in place of ‘five
years’.

Second Proviso to Clause 4(1) of the Statute
19.       Clause 4(1) at page 16, provides that the Executive Council may, on the recommendation of the Vice-
Chancellor, appoint a Professor to discharge the duties of a Pro-Vice-Chancellor in addition to his own duties as a
Professor.
 
19.1     The Committee feels that a time frame has to be fixed for the continuance of the adhoc appointment to discharge
the duties of a Pro-Vice Chancellor  otherwise it may lead to adhocism indefinitely.  The Committee also feels that dual

charge may also affect the academic work of the professor adversely.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that at
the end of proviso two of clause 4(1) of the statutes, after the words “as a professor” the words “ for not more than six
months” may be added.
 
Clause 6(3) of the Statutes
 
20.       First Proviso to Clause 6 (3) at page 18, of the Statutes provides that the Registrar shall retire on attaining the
age of sixty years
 
20.1     The Committee learnt that retirement age of Registrar in other Central Universities was 62 years.  Sometimes a
senior professor might also be appointed as Registrar of the University, who retire at the age of 62 years.  It might, the
Committee feels put them at disadvantage. The Committee, therefore feels that the retirement age of the Registrar
should be made as sixty-two.  The Committee accordingly recommends that in Proviso to Clause 6(3) line 1 of the
statutes the words ‘sixty years’ should be substituted by ‘sixty- two years’.  Consequently, the ‘sixty years’ in the
succeeding provision should also be made as sixty-two years.
 
Sub Clause 6 of Clause 6 of the Statutes
21.       Clause 6(6) at page 18, provides that the Registrar shall be ex officio Secretary of the Court, the Executive
Council and the Academic Council, but shall not be deemed to be a member of any of these authorities.
21.1     The  Committee observes that Registrar is an important functionary in the day to day administration of the
University.   Therefore, he should be an integral part of every major decision-making body in the university set up.   The
Committee therefore, recommends that the existing sub-clause  (6) of Clause 6 of the Bill may be substituted as
follows:-
(b)  The Registrar shall be ex-officio  Member Secretary of the Court, the Executive Council, and the Academic
Council.
 
Clause 8(1)(a) of the Statutes
 
22.       The Committee notes that the Deans of Faculties occupy a position above the members of the Executive Council
in the hierarchy. Therefore, they may be placed above the members of the Executive Council. The Committee
accordingly recommends that in Clause 8(1)(a) the item no.(v) may be placed after item no. (iii) and renumbered as item
no. (iv)and the existing item (iv) may be  renumbered as item (v).
 
Clause 8(e)(i) of the Statutes
 
23.       Clause 8(1)(e)(i) of the Statutes at page 21, provides for three representatives of Parliament, two to be nominated
by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha and one by the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, to the Court of the University.
 
23.1     The Committee feels that Members of Parliament to the Court should be elected by the respective Houses rather
than nominated by the Speaker/Chairman. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Clause 8(1)(e)(i) of the
Statutes and the Proviso thereof should be substituted by the following:
 
Three members of Parliament, two to be elected by the Lok Sabha from amongst its own members, in such

manner as the Speaker may direct and one to be elected by the Rajya Sabha from amongst its own members in
such manner as the Chairman may direct.
 
23.2     Consequently, in the proviso to this clause the word ‘nomination’ shall be replaced by words, ‘election to the
Court’.
 
Clause 9(1) C of the Statutes
24.       Clause 9(1) (c) of the Statutes at page 21, provides three Deans of Faculties to be selected by rotation as
members of the Executive Council in the order in which the Faculties are enumerated in clause 14(1) of the Statutes.
 
24.1     The Committee feels that the Executive Council is an important decision-making body of the University.  It
notes that there are five Faculties, of which only three are proposed to be on the Executive Council at a time. No
rationale could be provided for leaving the other  two. The Committee feels that it is essential that whenever decisions
are taken about a particular faculty, the points of view of that faculty also should be taken into account. Therefore, there
was no harm in providing representation to remaining two other faculties also in the Executive Council.  The Committee
accordingly recommends that the existing Clause 9(1) (c) of the Statutes should be substituted by the word  "the  Deans
of Faculties". 
 
Clause 9(2) of the Statutes
 
25.       Clause  9(2) of the Statutes at page 22, provides that the term of office of members of the Executive Council
under sub-clauses (c) to (g) of clause (1) shall be two years, and of members under sub-clauses (h) to (j) thereof shall be
three years.
 
25.1     The Committee recommends that as a consequential change of the recommendation made in Clause 9(1) (c),  the
words  "sub-clauses '9(c) to (g)",should be substituted by the words " 9(d) to (g)" in  line 12 of Clause  9(2) of the
Statutes.
 
Clause 9(4) of the Statutes
26.       Clause 9(4) of the Statutesat page 22,provides that seven members of the Executive Council shall form a quorum
for a meeting of the Executive Council.
 
26.1     Consequent to the recommendation of the Committee to include two more Faculties in the Executive Council its
total membership shall increase from 25 to 27.  Accordingly it would be appropriate if the quorum for the meeting of
Executive Council is increased from 7 to 9.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that in line 20 of Clause 9(4) of the
Statutes the word ‘Seven’ may be substituted by the word ‘Nine’.
 
Clause 10 (2)(ix) of the Statutes
 
27.       Clause 10 (2)(ix) of the statutes at page 23, empowers the Executive Council to invest any money belonging to
the University, including any unapplied income, in such stocks, funds, share or securities, from time to time as it may
think fit or in the purchase of immovable property in India, with the like powers of varying such investment from time

to time.
 
27.1     The Committee feels that the stock market is a highly volatile arena.  In view of uncertainties of Stock market,
chances of incurring heavy losses cannot be ruled out. An academic body like a University should not be allowed to
invest in stock market.   The Committee therefore recommends that in line 7 of Clause 10(2)(ix) of the statutes, the
words  ‘stocks’ and  ‘share’ may be deleted.
 
Clause 11(1) (n) of the Statutes
 
28.       Clause 11(1) (n)of the statutes at page 24, provides thatfive persons not being persons in the service of the
University or a college or an institution admitted to the privileges of the University, to be co-opted by the Academic
Council.
 
28.1     The Committee, for obviating the possibility of non-academic persons entering into the Academic Councils,
feels that the persons appointed under this sub clause should be from the academic field only.   The Committee,
therefore, recommends that in line 13 of Clause 11(1) (n) of the Statutes after the words' ‘five persons', the words ‘of
academic eminence' may be added.
 
Clause 16 (i) of the Statutes
 
29.       Clause 16 (1)of the statutes at page 29,  provides thatnotwithstanding anything contained in Statute 15, the
Executive Council may invite a person of high academic distinction and professional attainments to accept a post of
Professor or Reader or any other academic post in the University, as the case may be, on such terms and conditions as it
deems fit and on the person agreeing to do so appoint him to the post.
 
29.1     The Committee notes that this sub clause empowers the university to appoint persons of eminence in the
academic faculty by way of special mode of appointment. The Committee, however, feels that such appointments may
be restricted only to the level of professors or equivalent posts. The Committee accordingly recommends that in  line 12
of Clause 16 (i) of the statutes the words ' or Reader’ may be deleted and the word ‘equivalent’ may be added after the
word ‘other’.  Also the words ‘as the case may be’  after the word 'University’ be  deleted.
 
30.       The Committee adopts the remaining clauses of the Bill without any amendments.
31.       The enacting formula and the title are adopted with consequential changes.
32.       The Committee recommends that the observations made by it should be given due consideration by the
Department. The Committee also recommends that the Bill may be passed after incorporating the amendments
suggested by it.
