PREFACE
 
          I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human Resource
Development, having been authorised by the Committee on its behalf, do hereby present this Hundred Eighty-third
Report of the Committee on Major Issues Concerning Kendriya Vidyalayas.
 
2.         The Committee after having done an extensive study of the Functioning of Kendriya Vidyalayas had presented
the Hundred sixty-second Report to Parliament on 28 th April, 2005.  Thereafter, the Committee also considered the
Action-Taken Notes on the Report received from the Department of Secondary and Higher Education. While doing so,
the Committee found that some important issues raised by the Committee either were not explained convincingly or
further clarification were required on those. It was, therefore, decided to present another report highlighting those major
issue on which the Committee wishes to make further emphasis and obtain Government’s response.
 
3.         The Committee held extensive discussion with the representatives of the Department and Kendriya Vidyalaya
Sangathan on 3
rd and 13th January, 2006.  The Committee also heard the representative of CPWD on 6 th January, 2006
to get a correct assessment about the construction of KV buildings.
 
4.         The Draft report prepared on the basis of the feedback received from the Department and the discussions held
with the concerned authorities was considered and adopted by the Committee on the 27
th September, 2006.

1.1       The Report of the Committee focuses on certain vital issues linked directly or indirectly with the functioning of
Kendriya Vidyalayas (KVs).  Broadly speaking, these areas are: efficacy of existing norms for opening of Kendriya
Vidyalayas, problem of Project Vidyalayas, present status of KV buildings – both sanctioned and functional; existing
mechanism for construction/maintenance of KV buildings; problem relating to admissions; recruitment procedure for
KV Teachers; vacancy position; and grievances redressal mechanism and others.
 
II.        NORMS FOR OPENING OF KENDRIYA VIDYALAYAS
 
2.1       Opening of Kendriya Vidyalayas is based on specific norms prescribed for this purpose by the Government. 
One of those was the provision of 15 acres of land to be provided free of cost by the sponsoring agencies.  The
Committee was informed that this criterion had now been drastically reduced to 4.00 acres in a metropolitan city; a
contiguous plot fairly leveled measuring 6.00 acres in hilly terrain; 8.00 acres in urban area; and 10.00 acres in
rural/semi urban area.  The Committee appreciates that in view of the ever-increasing shortage of land in the present
scenario, lowering the land requirement is the need of the hour.  However, the Committee would like to emphasize that
there should be no deviation from the need to demarcate 40% as building area and 60% as play field of the 4.00 acres
provided in a metropolitan city.
 
2.2       The Committee came to know that out of 931 KVs in the country, there are 38 KVs where land had not been
provided by the sponsoring agencies even after several years, which in some cases is more than 20 to 25 years.    In the
case of as many as 27 KVs, sponsoring agencies were the Armed Forces.  The Committee notes that with the exception
of two KVs opened in the year, 2005-06, rest were opened between 1977-78 to 1997-98.  In the remaining 11 KVs also
the situation was, more or less, the same; the earliest KV was opened in 1973-74. Details of such KVs reveal a very
discouraging picture.  The Committee hopes that with the revision of land norms, situation would improve.  The
Committee would like the Department of Secondary and Higher Education and KVS to vigorously pursue the allotment
of land with the concerned agencies and report the progress made in this regard to the Committee within three months.
2.3       Besides the provision of land, the other basic criteria for opening a new KV is the availability of 200 children
(500 in big cities) and minimum concentration of 1000 Central Government employees.  In all the cases, proposal has to
emanate from State Government/ Departments/ Ministries/Defence authorities/PSUs.  Committee’s attention has been
drawn towards the opening of 98 KVs in a short span of two years, i.e. during 2003-2004 and 2004-2005.  On a specific
query in this regard, the Committee was informed that only three KVs-- two in the project sector and another one in
Tehran were opened on specific requests/proposals. As regards the remaining 95, the Committee noticed a very
disturbing aspect.  In the normal course, demands for opening of new KVs come from various sources, ranging from the
elected representatives to the State/project authorities.  However, the Committee was informed that these 95 KVs were
opened without there being a formal proposal from the KV Sangathan.  These KVs were opened on communications
received from the then Chairman, KV Sangathan at different points of time.  Opening of these KVs was apparently
justified by the availability of surplus teachers and need for utilizing their services. The Committee was given to
understand that ex-post-facto approval was proposed to be obtained from the Cabinet after the matter was discussed and
approved in EFC meeting.  That could, however, not be done.
2.4       The Committee takes a serious view of this development because the prescribed pre-sanction procedure for the
purpose was simply given a go by in these cases.  The Committee is perturbed to note that these 95 KVs were opened
without taking any prior approval/sanction.  Even the ex-post-facto approval could not be taken.  The Committee is not
happy with such an approach of short circuiting the established procedures.   In this case, in the absence of any formal
approval and there being no fund allocation specifically for these KVs., plan funds are utilized to fulfill the salary and
other requirement of staff members, of these KVs. This is not a healthy fiscal practice.
2.5       The Committee is of the view that the role of the Government should be to ensure that prescribed norms for
opening of new KVs were strictly adhered to.  Instead of giving priority to such districts where KVs are genuinely
required and where there are no KVs, setting up such schools arbitrarily is certainly deplorable. 
2.6       As per the information made available to the Committee, out of such 95 KVs, 13 KVs are yet to be functional. 
The Committee finds that the status of remaining 82 KVs cannot be considered as fully functional. The Committee fails
to understand the basis of there being surplus teachers and their adjustment in these 95 KVs.  Details given by the
Department indicate that out of 36 sanctioned posts of PGT, only 29 were in position; and there were 55 vacancies in
the sanctioned posts of TGT.  What is more disturbing is that, only 5 KVs had PGTs.  On a specific query in this regard,
the Committee was informed that rationale of surplus teachers was based on sanctioned posts.  However, in view of the
fact that number of posts filled up being less than the sanctioned posts, the Committee finds this justification untenable.

The Committee views it with serious concern and deplores such adhocism.
2.7       Details about schools made available to the Committee clearly indicate that enrolment status in some of the
Vidyalayas was far from satisfactory.  The Committee would like to emphasize that the Department ought to have been

making efforts on their own in this regard.  The Committee would like the Department to take up the matter at the
highest level to sort out the problem and submit a report to it within three months.
III.       PROJECT VIDYALAYAS
3.1       Committee’s attention was drawn towards the closure of a large number of project vidyalayas due to failure of
the sponsoring agencies in providing the required funds.  The Committee reiterates that the Department/and KVS,
besides approaching the project authorities for re-opening of closed vidyalayas. Those Project Vidyalayas which could
not be revived may be taken over by the Government and run as normal KVs.            
3.2       The Committee notes that there are 129 project sector KVs functioning at present.  Outstanding dues amounting
to Rs. 16.08 crores are to be paid by the sponsoring agencies.  These dues may be against both closed as well as running
project sector vidyalayas. The Committee understands that the Ministry is pursuing with the concerned agencies for
payment of dues/pending against them.  However, these efforts seem to have failed, as only three PSUs have responded
and these too have expressed their inability in liquidating their dues.  The Committee finds the situation quite
discouraging.  The Committee is of the view that this matter needs to be taken up at the highest level.  There is also a
need for evolving a mechanism for release of funds by project authorities in advance on yearly/half yearly basis.  Only
then, this problem is likely to be solved, otherwise, this financial burden on KV Sangathan could continue to persist or
may even increase.
IV.       KENDRIYA VIDYALAYA BUILDINGS
4.1       The Committee has made detailed observations in its earlier reports on the abnormal delay in the construction of
KV buildings.  The Committee had asked for a status report on the constructions indicating the year when projects were
cleared, funds allocated, and when the construction started; reasons for delay as also the revised estimate.   The
Committee found that 38 KVs were yet to be allotted land; construction work was going on in 65 KVs and in 28 KVs,
school buildings were at the planning stage.  A close scrutiny of the ongoing construction works in 65 KVs revealed that
11 different construction agencies were involved in constructing KV Buildings, with CPWD having the largest number
of 24 projects.  These included KVs sanctioned from January 2002 to December, 2005.  In as many as 40 KVs, work
was either at the planning stage or progress was very slow due to one reason or the other. Situation in this regard was
really bad in the N.E. Region.  Out of the 14 KVs buildings under construction in this region, status of the following
projects is self revealing:-

4.2       The Committee is concerned about the position in this regard in the NE Region and feels that special efforts
should be made to complete all the projects in a fixed time period.
4.3       The Committee observes that considerable time is taken in the various stages of construction including the delay,
sometimes, at the planning stage.  What is more disturbing is that a large number of these KVs, had been opened way
back in eighties.  The Committee understands that after a protracted procedure involving States/sponsoring agencies and
overcoming a number of hurdles, these KVs must have reached the stage when finally construction project could be
sanctioned.  However, it appears that series of other procedural formalities are yet to be fulfilled, before the students
could pursue their studies in a proper environment having required infrastructure.
4.4       The Committee found that construction of another 28 school buildings was at the planning stage.  The
Committee finds that these KVs are scattered in different States of the country, having various sponsoring agencies. 
The Committee is concerned to note that out of these 28 KVs, no progress is expected in 19 KVs, due to various reasons
beyond the control of KV Sangathan.  In some cases construction agencies have not submitted even preliminary
drawings and estimates. They are reminded from time to time to expedite it.  In such a situation the Committee is
constrained to conclude that the construction status of these KVs would continue to remain at the same stage.  It appears
that no agency is responsible and KV Sangathan itself seems to have abdicated its responsibility/duty.
4.5       The Committee made an attempt to understand the factors primarily responsible for such a dismal situation with
a view to find a solution to break this impasse. The Committee summoned the DG, CPWD to discuss this problem as
also to understand his constraints.  The Committee was given to understand that KV Sangathan had a well laid-out
monitoring mechanism functioning at different levels.  The Committee, however, feels that this monitoring mechanism
has not proved to be very effective so far.  The Committee observes that an initiative has been taken to simplify
procedural formalities.  Henceforth, all sanction orders issued by KVS are to be forwarded to the concerned CPWD
authorities by name.  This would result in eliminating undue delay occurring at various levels in KVS as well as in
CPWD. 
4.6       Another problem noticed by the Committee was lack of co-ordination between the civil and electrical wings of
CPWD.  This also, the Committee found, leads to unnecessary delays in this regard.  The Committee is happy to note
that CPWD has since introduced a composite tendering system, whereunder electrical works inside the building are to
be made part of the main contract.
4.7       Committee’s attention was also drawn towards another problem being faced by CPWD with regard to time taken
in handing over of works by CPWD to KV authorities, which varied from state to state. The Committee is of the view
that there is an urgent need for a complete review of existing procedure at every stage---pre-construction, construction
and post-construction of KV buildings. Although some initiative has been taken by KVS and CPWD, the Committee
feels that this would not serve the purpose.  The Committee, therefore, would like to emphasize that all unnecessary
procedural formalities need to be done away with urgently.  A regular and effective monitoring mechanism involving all
the concerned authorities, the Committee feels needs to be put in place urgently.  The Committee would like to impress
upon KV Sangathan, CPWD and other construction agencies to complete all the pending construction works in a pre-
approved fixed schedule so that there is no further cost escalation as also there was no inconvenience caused to the
students and teachers.
V.        ADMISSION CRITERIA FOR STUDENTS
5.1       The Committee notes that the present admission policy results in overcrowding of certain KVs in different parts
of the country and it therefore, feels that this problem needs to be tackled without any further delay. In this regard,
Committee’s attention has been drawn by the Report of the Baldev Mahajan Committee given in 2000. One of the main
recommendations of the Mahajan Committee, the Committee was informed was that freezing of sections was neither
academically sound nor administratively feasible.   The Committee is also of the view that problem of increasing
number of students cannot be solved by the freezing of sections.  The Committee was also informed that the Board of
Governors of KV Sangathan was in agreement with the recommendation of the Baldev Mahajan Committee that the
Commissioner, KVS should have full powers of opening new sections and sanctioning additional staff.  The Board of
Governors had, accordingly, decided to recommend the same to the Government.   The Committee, however, finds that
as per the latest response received from the Department, the recommendations of the Baldev Mahajan Committee have
since been rejected in principle.   The Committee fails to comprehend the reasons for the change in the stand of the
Government.    The Committee would like to point out that number of students in a class/section being more than the
prescribed ratio, does affect the quality of education adversely; it needs to be avoided. The Committee is also in favour
of more and more students getting admitted to the KVs.   But overcrowding is certainly not conducive for focused
attention on students performance. KVs cannot be an exception to this. The Committee, therefore, feels that all efforts

VI.       VACANCY POSITION
6.1       In its 162 nd Report, the Committee had dealt with the problem of vacancies existing in different categories of
teaching staff in KVS.   The Committee found that the position remained very discouraging for a long time.   The
Committee notes that the selection of 1571 teachers in December 2003, in which certain procedural irregularities were
alleged to have been committed, continued to remain undecided till September 2006.  As a result, the appointment of the
teachers was withheld. In the meantime, a Satyam Committee was appointed to look into the issues and the selected
teachers were given conditional appointment on ad hoc basis, till the Committee submitted its report. Thus, the teachers
remained ad-hoc for more than three years. In the meanwhile, the Committee learnt that the report of the Satyam
Committee has since been submitted to the Government. The Committee notes with serious concern that the process of
selection of teachers that had started in February 2003, could not be completed till the time of the writing of this Report.
The Committee is at a loss to see that 1571 posts of teachers continued to remain vacant for several years.  The
Committee, while appreciating the move for giving the ad hoc appointment to the selected teachers pending the inquiry,
demands strict action against those found responsible for this fiasco.  The Committee calls upon the government to
consider the recommendations of the Satyam Committee for an early implementation so that future of those teachers is
decided without further delay.  The Committee is also aware that on an average about 7.5% ad hoc teachers have
contractual appointment.  The Committee would like to point out that teachers appointed for a short period would have
no motivation to give good results, for obvious reasons.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that ad-hoc
appointment should be to the barest minimum and the vacancies must be filled as soon as possible.   The Committee
also has serious reservations about the prolonged recruitment procedure that goes on for years.   There is an urgent need
for having a review of the same for streamlining, decentralising and eliminating the unnecessary procedural formalities.
6.2       The Committee in its 162
nd Report had strongly advocated the need for having a decentralised - zone-wise
recruitment for KV teachers, instead of the existing centralized procedure.  The most obvious reason for having a
decentralised system is to avoid the inherent problems faced in a centralized recruitment system by the KVS and the
candidates coming from different parts of such a vast country. The Committee is of the firm view that centralized
recruitment system is one of the main reasons for continuance of vacancies for long time affecting the academic
environment of the Vidyalayas. The Committee therefore, reiterates its recommendations of decentralization of the
recruitment process for selection of candidates at zonal level so that it becomes convenient to people of different regions
to participate in the process which otherwise may not be possible for them.  It would also ensure proper representation
of the candidates from different parts of the country, particularly from NE States and J&K.
VII.     GRIEVANCES REDRESSAL
7.1       The Committee observes that some initiative has been taken by the KV Sangathan for strengthening the
Grievance Redressal Machinery.  Senior officers are visiting Regional Offices to attend to the grievances of employees. 
Every day, specific hours have been fixed at the KV Sangathan’s Headquarters when aggrieved employees can meet the
concerned officers including the Commissioner without prior appointment. While appreciating the initiative taken by
KV Sangathan, the Committee would like to point out that there should also be a mechanism whereby a track of efficacy
of such an outlet available to employees can be constantly monitored. The Committee feels that the ground reality in
this regard seem to be somewhat different. Even public representatives such as MPs sometimes, fail to contact the
concerned officers because most of the time they are not available. Details regarding number of Grievances received and
those dealt with also do not indicate a significant improvement.
            The Committee has learnt that teachers union in KVS has been derecognized. The Committee would like to have
a brief note on the issue for its consideration.
VIII.        SURVEY OF KVs
8.1       The Committee is of the firm view that this is the right time for making a comprehensive assessment of KVs
spread over different parts of the country.  Reason behind making such a suggestion is that Kendriya Vidyalayas having
evolved into a reputed educational system since its inception in 1960s have seen many ups and downs.  Inspite of the
best efforts put forth by all concerned, quite a few constraints continue to persist at the ground level.  Committee’s
specific recommendation for conducting a detailed survey was meant to take corrective measures based on its findings
so that KV Sangathan is better equipped in attaining its goals. Committee’s recommendations/observations on the
various aspects of functioning of KVS are also meant for this purpose only.  
8.2       The Committee is unhappy to note that the spirit of Committee’s suggestions have not been given the importance
that they deserved by the Department/KV Sangathan. The Committee is afraid that development of an Information
