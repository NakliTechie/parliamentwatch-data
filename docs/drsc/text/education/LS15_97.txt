PREFACE
            I,  the Chairman of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human Resource Development having been
authorised by the Committee, on its behalf, present this Report of the Committee on the Delhi University (Amendment) 
Bill, 2000.
2.         The  Delhi University (Amendment) Bill, 2000  which was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on the 18
th April,
2000 provides for deletion of  Section 5(2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922 in order to facilitate the affiliation of
institutions and colleges with the  Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya or any other university that may be set up in Delhi.

REPORT
            The University of Delhi, a premier university of the country, is known for its high standards in teaching and 
research and attracts a  large number of students not only from all over  India but also several other countries.  
Established in February, 1922 under the provisions of the Delhi University Act, 1922 as a unitary and residential
university with a nucleus of three colleges, two faculties and about 750 students, it has grown  over the years into one of
the largest universities in the country. 
2.         The Delhi University (Amendment) Bill, 2000 provides for deletion of sub-section (2) of section 5 of the Delhi
University Act, 1922.  The aforesaid provision  prohibits the affiliation of any educational institution or college with a
university other than the Delhi University within the limits of the National  Capital Territory of Delhi, unless it is
specifically permitted by an order of the  Central Govt.    As per the Statement of Objects and Reasons attached to the
Bill, the Delhi University  already has a large number of institutions/colleges affiliated with it and may  not be in a
position to take on the continuous  additional burden of  new institutions/colleges as well as  increasing student
population.
3.         The Committee was  given to understand that  the Govt. of India was already contemplating for setting up of
self-financing universities  (private universities) to cater to the needs of the ever increasing demand of higher education. 
However, section 5 (2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922 had been causing difficulties/restraints in affiliation of
different colleges/institutions being set up in Delhi with any other university to cope up with the increased influx of
students.   
4.         The Committee was informed that the Indraprastha Vishwavidyalaya Bill was passed by the Legislative
Assembly of the National Capital Territory of Delhi in 1998 and while the Bill was being examined in the Ministry of
Home Affairs for presidential assent, the Department of Legal Affairs brought to the notice of the  Ministry of Home
Affairs the implication of Section 5 of the Delhi University Act, 1922.
5.         The Committee was given to understand that the proposed deletion of  Section 5,  if passed by the Parliament,
would help the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya in its extension and also avoid complications regarding affiliation of
institutions/colleges with the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya within the jurisdiction of Delhi University.   It was also
submitted that the necessary  Cabinet approval had already been obtained for the proposal relating to deletion of Section
5 (2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922 before the Bill was moved in the Parliament.
6.         The Committee in its meeting held on the 12
th June, 2000  had a detailed discussion on the  proposed
amendment  in the Delhi University Act, 1922 with the  representatives of the Department of Secondary Education and
Higher Education, Chairman, UGC and also representatives of the Delhi University and Indraprastha Vishwavidyalaya.
7.         On a specific query about the number of colleges affiliated to the  Delhi University, the Committee was

informed that so far 68 colleges had been affiliated to the Delhi University, out of which 8 colleges were maintained by
the Central/State Govt., 10 by the Delhi University and 1 by the Govt. of Bhutan. 21 colleges were sponsored by Private
Trusts and 28 were sponsored by the Delhi Govt.  The Committee was given to understand that the Delhi University was
in a position to attend to the requirements/conditions as laid down in the relevant Act and rules made thereunder so far
as its affiliated institutions/colleges were concerned.
8.         So far as Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya  was concerned, 8 teaching departments had been established and 35
colleges had been affiliated to the University   under the relaxation provided in  proviso to Section 5(2) of the Delhi
University Act, 1922. The Committee was also informed that with the exception of 2 Govt. aided colleges,  rest were
self financing private institutions.  
9.         Very strong apprehensions were raised about the implication of the resultant situation with  the deletion of sub-
section 5 (2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922.  It was clarified by the representatives of the Department   that even
after the proposed deletion, only such of the universities which were to be set up  by the Delhi Govt. would have the
power to affiliate colleges.  Attention of the Committee in this context was drawn to the Statement of Objects and
Reasons of the Amendment Bill which clearly stated that in order to facilitate the affiliation of institutions and colleges
with the Indraprashta Vishwavidyalaya or any other university that may be set up in Delhi, sub-section 2 of section 5 of
the Delhi University Act, 1922 was required to be omitted.  It was further clarified that no Govt.  or Union Territory
where there was a legislature would set up universities in the territorial jurisdiction of another State or Union Territory. 
10.       In contrast, the contention  of the  Delhi University was that this kind of blanket deletion of the provision would
have an adverse effect on the quality of education in Delhi. It was  pointed out that the Bill did not clearly reflect the
objectives mentioned in the Statement of Objects and Reasons but went beyond that.  With the deletion of Section 5(2),
not only control of the Central Govt. would be removed but there would also be no barrier for any university outside
Delhi to affiliate colleges set up in Delhi. The Committee was given to understand that a university outside Delhi had
already been given permission by the Central Govt. to affiliate a college in Delhi.  It was suggested that  the objective of
the proposed amendment namely to enable the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya to give affiliation to colleges could be
achieved through an amendment in the Delhi University Act. 
11.       The Committee noted that as per the provisions of Section 4 of the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya Act, 1998, no
college or institution situated within the jurisdiction of the University shall be compulsorily affiliated to the University
and affiliation shall be granted by the University only to such college or institution as may agree to accept its statutes
and ordinances.   However, in view of  section 5 (2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922, direct affiliation was being
granted by the Central Govt. to such colleges/institutions, opting for  affiliation to the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya. 
On a specific query whether there was any conflict so far as AICTE and other councils were concerned in view of the
direct affiliation being granted by the Central Govt. to such colleges, it was clarified to the Committee that  recognition
by the concerned council was  a mandatory provision and affiliation could only be granted thereafter.
12.       The Committee voiced its concern about a number of universities functioning in different parts of the country
which had been established  without any permission from the  Govt. and were running professional courses or granting
affiliation to institutions/colleges without the required approval from the concerned council.   The Committee was
informed that 20 such fake universities were functioning in different parts of the country which were  being approached
by the UGC for fulfilling the norms from time to time.  State Govts. were also being asked to take action against them. 
It was also mentioned that some of the fake universities had  moved the courts against the UGC.   Under the existing 
provisions of the UGC Act, only a fine of Rs. 10,000 could be imposed against them.   The Committee was, however,
given to understand that more punitive action had been proposed against fake universities under the new UGC Act
which was under contemplation at present.
13.       Doubts were also raised about the future status of Delhi University in view of the proposed deletion of section
5(2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922. The Committee was of the view that deletion of section 5(2) would lead to
commercialisation of education particularly in Delhi.  There was a need for a safeguard in  the form of  Govt. control,
specially in view of the existing fake universities and likelihood of more such universities coming up in future.  
Continuance of   section 5(2) was very much required as there was genuine apprehension that there would be ample
opportunities for some unscrupulous elements to start universities and colleges which would hamper the prospects of
Delhi University and in the larger perspective, of higher education in the country.
14.       The Committee was not in agreement with the contention of the representatives of the Department and UGC that
with the deletion of section 5(2), all institutions would have a free choice to get affiliated either to the Indraprashtha

Vishwavidyalaya or to the Delhi University or to any other university to be set up in future by the Govt. of Delhi or by
the Central Govt. The Committee noted that it had been duly clarified in the Statement of Objects and Reasons to the
Delhi University (Amendment) Bill 2000 that only the Indraprastha Vishwavidyalaya or any other University that may
be set up in Delhi would have the power to affiliate institutions and colleges.  However, the Committee felt that  with
the Bill becoming an Act, Statement of Objects and Reasons would not have any relevance.  This would result in not
only the removal of the Govt. control but also there would be no bar for universities from other states from affiliating
institutions and colleges in Delhi.
15.       Attention of the Committee was also drawn to a difficult situation which may arise with the omission of section
5 (2). It was felt that with the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya  being given the authority to affiliate self-financing
institutions, it may happen that the colleges already affiliated with the  Delhi University may opt for affiliation with
some other university. There was,  therefore, a need for having checks and balances so that only future institutions could
be affiliated to any university established in Delhi. 
16.       In its meeting held on the 10
th July, 2000, the Committee heard the Secretary, Department of Secondary
Education and Higher Education and Vice-Chancellors of  Delhi University and Indraprastha Vishwavidyalaya. 
Secretary of the Department informed the Committee that in view of the many apprehensions raised by the Committee 
with regard to the deletion of section 5(2),   particularly the loss of control of the Central Govt. in regulating the
affiliation of Delhi colleges and also the  establishment of  self-financing/private institutions in Delhi, the Department
of  Secondary Education and Higher Education held wider consultations with the Vice-Chancellors of Delhi University
and Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya and also  other stake-holders concerned with the amendment of Delhi University
Act, 1922 on the 23
rd June, 2000.
17.       The Committee was given to understand that there were conflicting views on the issue whether colleges already
affiliated to Delhi University might shift their affiliation to Indraprastha Vishwavidyalaya.   After much deliberations, it
was felt that    this issue should be better left open specially as a ban may not be really required and called for. There
already was an empowering element in section 5(2) which gave the Central Government the authority to direct
affiliation  of an institution/college with some other university.  It was also felt that the amendment would not result in
any dilution of status so far as the Delhi University was concerned.  There was little likelihood of any college leaving
the Delhi University for joining an infant university in future in view of  the fact that the  Delhi University  was a central
university and  was getting very liberal grants from the Central Govt.   Not only this, from the academic point of view,
Delhi University enjoyed a unique status, having 82 departments in different disciplines.  Any college affiliated to the
Delhi University would have access to all its departments engaged in the teaching of graduate/post-graduate/
professional courses, research etc.   
18.       It was, therefore, unanimously decided in the aforesaid meeting that section 5(2) of the Delhi University Act,
may be retained and the following proviso may be added to section 5(2):-
‘Provided that the provision of the above sub section shall not apply in the case of Guru Gobind Singh
Indraprashtha University incorporated by the Government of NCT of Delhi.’
19.       It was explained that the above cited proposed amendment in the Delhi University Act, 1922 would obviate the
need for Central Govt. orders in case of institutions/colleges seeking affiliation to the State Govt. created Indraprashtha
Vishwavidyalaya which had been set  up with an objective of catering to the increasing needs of higher education in
Delhi.  The Committee was also informed that  the proposed change in the legislation had been approved by the 
Minister for Human Resource Development.
20.       The Committee  after a detailed  discussion on the  various aspects connected with the proposed deletion of
section 5(2)  of the Delhi University Act, 1922  recommends that  section 5(2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922  
should not be deleted so as to    prevent the loss of control of the Central Govt.   in regulating the establishment of self-
financing/private institutions  in Delhi which may seek affiliation  to the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya or any other
university within or outside the National Capital Territory of Delhi.  The Committee also  recommends the addition of
the following proviso to section  5(2) of the Delhi University Act, 1922 so as to  obviate the need for  Central Govt.
orders in case of institutions/colleges seeking affiliation to the Indraprashtha Vishwavidyalaya :-
 ‘Provided that the provision of the above sub section shall not apply in the case of Guru Gobind Singh
Indraprashtha University incorporated by the Government of NCT of Delhi.’

paragraph.  The Committee also recommends that a fresh Bill may be introduced in Parliament incorporating the above-
