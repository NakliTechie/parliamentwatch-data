REPORT
I.      INTRODUCTION
1.1    The primary responsibility of the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports is to ensure qualitative and quantitative
improvements in Youth Programmes and also to broad base and bring about awareness about sports and physical fitness
amongst school children and youth.  The Programmes undertaken by the Department are mainly categorized
under ‘Youth Welfare’ and Sports and Games.

152nd Report of Committee on HRD

1.2   The  Ministry has formulated the National Youth Policy which redefines the age group  of youth
from 13 to 35 years and recognizes four thrust areas i.e.  (i)  youth empowerment (ii) gender justice, (iii) inter-
sectoral approach and (iv) information and research network. The policy identifies eight key  aspects like
education, health, environment, sports and recreation, art and culture, training and employment of youth. It also seeks
to accord priority to the groups of youth-rural and tribal youth, out of school youth, adolescents particularly female,
youth with disability and those under difficult circumstances.
1.3    The Committee notes that most of these programmes are implemented by the State Governments, UT
administration statutory/local bodies, autonomous organizations of the Ministry as well as the NGOs.

increased and believes that  it would hardly suffice to implement the ongoing projects, leave alone new

requirements for this year, before the Ministry of Finance/Planning Commission justifying its need for higher
allocations at the RE Stage.
2.4    The Committee, at the same time, would impress upon the Ministry to ensure that allocations were properly and
fully utilized.
III.      GENDER BUDGET ANALYSIS OF BUDGET ALLOCATIONS
3.1    Women empowerment without gender equality would be nothing but an illusion. The Gender based analysis
of the budget allocations under different  schemes of the  Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, it is learnt, was
initiated during the year 2003-04 only.  Gender based analysis of Budget allocations for the year 2003-04
under its different schemes has been done.
3.2    The Committee notes that percentage of funds spent on women component is not upto the mark under various
schemes of the Ministry, except the NSS, NSVS and sports scholarship. In some schemes, specially those run by SAI, it
is very low. It is matter of great concern to the Committee that only 33.60% of the total allocation of the Ministry was
spent on women component during  the year 2003-04. The very fact, that data have been assumed for some
schemes, shows that gender analysis of its scheme is being done neither systematically nor regularly. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the gender-based analysis of the allocations of the Ministry should be done regularly, as it
would provide an idea about the percentage of funds being spent on the women who constitute about half of our
population.

4.1   The  total outlay, both plan and non-plan, for the Ministry under youth welfare scheme for the year
2003-04, was Rs. 136.54 crores (Rs. 109.23 crores plan and Rs. 27.31 crores non-plan). This was reduced to Rs. 100.38
crore (Rs. 73.94 crores plan and Rs. 26.44 crores non-plan) in the Revised Estimate 2003-04. Provision for the year
2004-05, is Rs. 137.27 crores (Rs. 107.50 crores plan and Rs. 29.77 crores non-plan).
4.2    The Committee again notes that against an allocation of Rs. 352.24 crores made under youth welfare schemes for
the Ninth Plan, an expenditure of only Rs. 336.94 crores (including Rs. 6.35 crores for NER) could be incurred, leading
to a balance of Rs. 15.30crores.
4.3    The  Committee notes with concern that the allocations for youth welfare schemes could not be utilized
during the entire Ninth Plan period, despite the fact that the main thrust of the Ninth Plan was on harnessing the Yuva
Shakti. Tenth Plan outlay for Youth Welfare Schemes has substantially increased to Rs. 677.64 crores, as
compared to only Rs. 352.24  crores in Ninth Plan. The Committee, however, observes that position
remains unchanged during the first two years of Tenth Plan. During 2002-03, against an allocation of Rs. 106.98
crores, expenditure incurred was Rs. 98.70 crores. Enhanced allocation of Rs. 121.98 crores in BE 2003-04 had to be
reduced to Rs. 82.20 crores at the RE  stage and even that remained underutilized. Low utilization of
funds, the  Committee feels, should be viewed seriously because it reflects nothing but the lack of sincerity and
determination in implementing the Youth Welfare Schemes.
4.4   The  Committee notes that the Tenth Plan proposed to target the adolescent youth in  the 
10-19 years age group which is 230 million, accounting for 22.8%, through specific intervention strategies
that will focus on their power, gender justice, health and responsible living.
4.5   The  Committee is of the opinion that for achieving these avowed objectives youth will have to be
provided proper educational and training opportunities, access to information on employment opportunities including
entrepreneurial guidance and financial credit, proper platforms for developing qualities of leadership, tolerance and
open mindedness, patriotism, etc. But with this financial performance, the Committee feels, the Ministry will hardly be
able to achieve these targets. The Committee, therefore, urges upon the  Ministry to play a more pro-active
and catalytic role in optimally utilizing the allocations in various youth-related schemes in the coming years.
V.     NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME
5.1    National Service scheme popularly known, as NSS is a Centrally sponsored scheme, which is being implemented
through universities, colleges and other institutions of higher learning.
5.2    Today, NSS has more than 20 lakh student volunteers on its roll spread over 178 Universities and 39 (+2)
Senior Secondary Councils and Directorate of  Vocational Education covering more than 8600
institutes/colleges of higher  education and 6350 Secondary schools all over the country. Since its inception,
more than 2.51 crore students from universities, colleges and institutes of higher learning have been benefited under the
NSS activities.
5.3    The Ninth Plan outlay of Rs. 104.56 crores has been enhanced to 172.00 crores in the Tenth Plan. During the
Ninth Plan, the actual expenditure incurred under the scheme was Rs. 96.51 crores.
5.4 Allocation  of Rs.25.20 crores (Plan) has been made under the scheme for 2004- 05. It was 23.40 crores
at BE 2003-04 which was reduced to Rs. 20.59 crores at RE. The allocation in BE

5.5    The Committee was informed that during the year  2003-04 against a target to enroll 20.82 lakh

with the cooperation of the States.
VI.     NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN
6.1 Nehru  Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS), an autonomous organization of the Ministry caters to the needs
and concerns of more than 8 million non-student rural youth enrolled through more than 2 lakh villages based Youth
Clubs. Five hundred districts have a network of trained cadre of District Youth  Coordinators, National
Service Volunteers and members of Youth Clubs/Mahila Mandals. The activities of the NYKS are organized through
two categories of programmes, regular programmes and special programmes.
6.2    The plan allocation for NYKS in BE 2003-04 was Rs 31.18 crores, which was reduced to Rs. 29.75 crores at RE
stage. It has been further decreased to Rs. 28.97 crores in BE 2004-05. On a specific query about the reason for
decreasing the allocation for NYKS in 2004-05, the Ministry gave the following reasons :
           1.     Deen Dayal Upadhyay Swawlamban Yojana was on a pilot project basis and ended on March 2003.
           2.     During 2002-03 and 2003-04 payment of arrears were made to the employees of NYKS on account of the
benefit of Assured Career Progression (ACP).
           3.     The allocation was pruned as there is a ban on filling up of posts and the resultant vacancies on account of
retirement, death etc. are not being filled in.
6.3    The Committee notes that out of 500 posts of district youth coordinators, 134 posts are lying vacant. The
Ministry has set a target to expand its activities to all the districts in the country. The Committee finds the
Ministry in the catch-22 situation. Funds have been reduced and there is ban on filling up the posts, let alone
creating new posts. Then, there is the target to cover the remaining districts also. The Committee is dismayed at
the casual manner the scheme is being run with such a less number of District Coordinators and how the targets
were going to be achieved. The NYKS having all India network, the Committee feels, its volunteers can play vital
role in areas like literacy, family welfare, health awareness, national integration, gender equality etc. in rural
areas. The Committee feels very much concerned at the way  this scheme is sought to be implemented. The
Committee, therefore, recommends to initiate effective measures for improving the situation and taking this
scheme out of the morass it is presently in.
6.4    Committee has been given to understand that a recruitment process to fill up the vacancies of District Youth
coordinators was initiated sometimes back. The Committee is not aware of the final outcome of the process. The
Committee would like to have a status note on the same.

VII.NATIONAL RECONSTRUCTION CORPS
7.1    The National Reconstruction Corps, which was launched as a pilot project on 30.6.1999, aims at providing
opportunity to youth who have completed their matriculation to participate as volunteers in the process of nation
building. The Committee notes that against a Ninth Plan outlay of Rs. 36.00 crores, expenditure incurred was only
Rs. 14.41 crores.
7.2    The Committee notes that this scheme was to end in March 2003, therefore, only Rs. 10 lakh was earmarked as
against Rs. 10.90 crores in the previous year. The scheme has been evaluated and its continuance is under consideration
in consultation with the Ministry of Finance and the Planning Commission.  The Committee notes that in the
absence of a final decision, provision of Rs. 10.90 crores in 2003-04 had to be reduced to Rs. 10.00 lakhs. In
anticipation that this scheme will be continued, allocation for this scheme has been increased to Rs. 10.80 crore in 2004-
05. The Committee notices an element of ad-hocism  as well as uncertainty  about the scheme and urges
the Department to expedite its evaluation as well as decision to continue/discontinue this scheme at the earliest so
that it could be implemented timely and effectively.
VIII.    NATIONAL INTEGRATION PROGRAMME
8.1    Under this scheme, assistance is given to voluntary Organisations, educational institutions, NSS and NYKS for
organizing National Integration Camps in different parts of the country, for interstate visits, seminars, conferences,
research, publication, youth festivals, cultural programmes and other activities, all of which aim at combating
communalism, regionalism, linguistic chauvinism and other divisive tendencies. The scheme was revised in 2001, and
the organisation of the National Youth Festival and celebration of National Youth Day and also National Youth Awards
have been made integral part of it.
8.2    The budget allocations made during last three years were fully utilized except, the year 2001-02. The Ministry
explained that this shortfall was due to non-submission of documents by some implementing organisations. The
allocations in the RE 2002-03 were increased as more proposals were received and most of them were found viable and
were approved. An increased amount has been sought for the year 2004-05. The Committee is happy that
the scheme appears to be picking up after facing some initial teething problems. Now, the number of viable proposals
were increasing. The Committee, however, wonders as to how the scheme of such a great national significance could be
successfully operated with a marginal enhancement of Rs. 0.45 crores in the year 2004-05. The Committee is of
the view that a scheme  for promoting national integration should not suffer due to financial constraints. The
Ministry should, therefore expand this scheme, by encouraging more and more organisations to operate it. Financial
allocations also for this scheme need to be suitably enhanced.
8.3The  Committee observes that voluntary agencies play a vital role in organizing both National Integration
Camp and Inter-State Youth Exchange Programmes. The Committee would like to be apprised of the assessment of
work done by them under these Programmes, specially of those agencies being allocated the maximum grants.
IX.   YOUTH  HOSTELS
9.1    Youth Hostels are built to promote youth travel within the country. While the Central Government bears the cost
of construction, the State Governments provide fully developed land, free of cost, with water, electricity,
approach road and staff quarters. After completion, Youth Hostels are handed over to the State
Governments for management. As of December 2003, 65 Youth Hostels have been constructed and 20 are under
construction.
9.2    The Committee notes that the allocation under this scheme in BE 2003-04, was drastically reduced at RE stage
from Rs. 260 lakh to Rs. 60 lakh. The reason given for this was that there was reduction in the overall allocation of the
Ministry made by the Ministry of Finance. This year’s allocation has been Rs. 270 lakh to meet the expenditure on the
pending proposals which could not be cleared in 2003-04 due to paucity of funds. The Committee is surprised to see that
due to overall reduction of Ministry’s allocation, share of this scheme was drastically reduced from Rs. 260 lakh to just
Rs. 60 lakh. This general reduction in Ministry’s allocation, committee feels, should have been evenly spread to the

other schemes as well.
9.3    The Committee notes with concern that out of Rs. 14.93 crores for the Ninth plan outlay, the actual expenditure
was only Rs 9.65 crores i.e. only 67%.
9.4    The Committee notes that performance of this scheme is not satisfactory, in view of the expenditure pattern
in the entire Ninth Plan period as well as 2003-04. The fact that the allocation of the Ministry was
reduced from Rs. 260 lakhs to 60 lakhs last year, gives an impression that this scheme was not given the
priority that it deserved. Just 85 youth Hostels upto December 2003, in a country of continental dimensions, the
Committee feels, is highly inadequate which needs to be increased in the coming years. The Committee notes that
the spread of these 85 hostels state-wise is also highly uneven. In view of this, the Committee recommends that
the Ministry must try to see that this scheme picks up and gets adequate resources so that the pending
projects are completed in time. It is also necessary to fix physical targets for this scheme every year.
9.5    On being asked about publicity of the scheme, the  Ministry replied that the relevant information
pertaining to youth hostels was available on Internet and the Youth Hostels Association of India had been
bringing out a booklet providing  necessary information in respect of youth hostels. The Committee
feels that this scheme lacks adequate publicity, as a result, youth, particularly from rural areas, were not
able to make use  of hostel facilities. The Committee, therefore, is of the opinion that  existence of
youth hostel facilities should be given wide publicity particularly  by Akashvani which has large
audience in rural areas. It also recommends to strengthen the scheme as the present coverage of the scheme was
too less to show any impact.
9.6    The Committee does not know how much of the existing facility of youth hostels was being
utilized by the women youth. Generally women, particularly from rural areas, do not come out due to reasons of
security and safety. The Committee, therefore, recommends that separate girls hostels should also be constructed
on the lines of youth hostel.
X.    NATIONAL COMMISSION FOR YOUTH
10.1 The Commission was set up in March, 2002 for a period of six month to study the problems of youth and suggest
remedies. Initially, an allocation of Rs. 40.00 lakh was provided for it in 2001-02, by way of supplementary grants. In
2002-03, no provision was initially made for the Commission. However an amount of Rs. 100.00 lakh was obtained
later from the Contingency Fund of India. Since the Commission could not even start its work due to various
constraints, its tenure was extended for a period of one year i.e. upto 15 th September 2003. A provision of Rs. 150.00
lakhs was provided by way of re-appropriation in the year 2002-03, as the Ministry of Finance had not agreed to provide
any additionality for the Commission. In 2003-04, a sum of Rs. 100.00 lakh was provided for the Commission in the
budget to meet its requirements upto 15 th September, 2003. The tenure of the Commission was extended further in the
middle of the financial year. However, no budget was available for it. The Ministry of Finance also did not agree to
provide any additionality. An additional amount of  Rs. 306.00 lakhs was therefore, arranged by diverting
funds from other schemes of the Ministry. In 2004-05 (Interim Budget), only a token provision of Rs. 10.00 lakh was
made, as there was no request from the Commission for any further extension. However, in 3 rd week of March, on
request from the Chairman of the Commission, its tenure was again extended upto  30 th September 2004 and
so a total provision of Rs. 193.00 lakh has been made in the regular budget to meet the expenses upto September 2004.
The Commission has now submitted its report and the process of its winding up is underway.
10.2 The Committee gets an impression from what has been stated above that the Commission was not taken with due
seriousness although it had laudable mandate. Its initial tenure of six months, the Committee feels, was not realistic and
the Commission was not provided with the necessary wherewithal and assistance for starting its work in time. The
Committee disapproves this ad-hocism as also the manner in which funds were arranged first by re-appropriation and
then from the Contingency Fund. The Commission has since submitted its report after two years and four months which
it was to submit within 6 months initially. The Committee expresses its unhappiness at the manner everything was done.
However, it would like to have a note on the report of the Commission at the earliest.

assistance to youth clubs; and scheme for youth development centre. The last component has since  been
merged with the other components of the scheme. The Committee notes that the allocation for the scheme could not
be utilized last year because some funds had to be diverted to meet the expenditure on national youth festival.
Allocation this year has been increased by Rs.  55 lakh as compared to the previous year. The Committee was

XII.NEW SCHEMES
12.1The  Committee came to know that two schemes: (i) Scheme for Welfare and Development of
Adolescents and (ii) Establishment of National/State Youth Centres were proposed to be implemented during the Tenth
Five Year Plan. It is surprising that till today both these schemes could not be implemented. An amount of Rs. 90 lakh
was made for the scheme for welfare and development of  adolescent in BE 2002-03, which remained
unutilized. Despite this fact, Rs. 15.30 crore was provided in 2003-04, which again remained unutilized. Rs. 6.30 crore
has been provided for this scheme for 2004-05.
12.2  The Committee finds it appalling that the funds are being allocated for the scheme for welfare and development of
Adolescents for the last three financial years but these went unspent every year mainly because the scheme could not be
finalized. Similar was the story of the proposed scheme of  Establishment of National/State Youth
Centres. In this case also the amount  allocated since 2002-03, remained unutilized again because this
scheme also  could not be finalized till today. This reflects lack of foresight and planning in the Ministry. The
Committee deprecates this tendency of allocating amounts even when the schemes were not finalized. This is against
the norms of fiscal discipline. The Committee, therefore,  feels that Ministry should ask for allocations
only after the finalization of  the schemes. It, therefore, recommends that these two schemes should be finalized
and implemented without further delay and ensure that funds provided are fully utilized.

XVI.SPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA
16.1   Sports Authority of India is the apex body in the country, which is responsible for the development of sports.
A number of schemes for  spotting and nurturing of sports talent, promotion of sports in special areas,
women’s sports, rural sports etc. are implemented by the SAI. It is also responsible for maintaining and utilizing
stadia in Delhi. SAI has six Regional Centres and four Sub-Centres. SAI also has two academic wings-one for training
of coaches and research and development in sports at the Netaji Subhash National Institute of Sports, Patiala and the
other for physical education at the Lakshmibai National College of Physical Education, Thiruvananthapuram.

         An allocation of Rs. 123.83 crore has been made in BE 2004-05 for the ‘Sports Authority of India’ as against BE
Rs. 110.50 crore in 2003-04, which was reduced to Rs. 98.50 crore at RE stage. This reduction was mainly due to
general cut applied by the Ministry of Finance. When asked to comment on the increase in the allocation for 2004-05,
and the thrust areas for which the Ministry proposes to spend the enhanced allocation, the Committee was informed that
main works to be taken up in 2004-05 are: replacement of ACs in IG Stadium and Dr. S.P. Mukherjee Swimming Pool;
upgradation of the fire-fighting system in all the SAI Stadia; installation/replacement of the synthetic surfaces; creation
of Fitness Centres in all SAI Stadia and outstation units/regional centres of SAI where National Coaching Camps are

infrastructure etc.

expenditure of the SAI allocations does not present an encouraging picture. The Committee would like the Ministry to

(iii)     Scheme for Dope Test
           The anti-doping programme in the country is managed by SAI through the Dope Control Centre located at the
JLN Stadium, New Delhi and is funded under the Central Sector ‘Scheme for Dope Test’ launched during the 10 th Five
Year Plan. The Committee is happy to learn about the temporary accreditation by the IOC to Dope Control Centre run
by the SAI. It has got ISO 9001:2000 & ISO: 17025 Certificate and efforts are on for permanent accreditation for its
laboratory by the IOC and World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA).
16.6    Last few years have seen a number of sportspersons specially the weightlifters and athletes testing positive for
banned drugs. These instances have brought shame instead of fame to the nation. Our scheme for dope test was
launched only three years ago. The SAI has anti-doping lab set up a decade ago in New Delhi, it has still not acquired
the requisite international certification to gain legal sanctity. So, even if anybody fails a test, he or she can successfully
challenge it in a Court. Such a situation allows the over-ambitious ones to take the easy route.
16.7    In view of cases in Athens Olympics and earlier experiences, the Committee notes that our weightlifters are most
hit by the scourge of doping. Every time an Indian contingent prepares to leave the country for a mega event, doping
tests are hastily conducted and surprisingly they do not test positive here but fail the test in International events.

16.8   The  Committee was surprised to know that nobody seems to take the responsibility for the same. Even

16.9    The Committee feels extremely unhappy about the continuing indifference shown by all sports bodies - the

spotting the talent at younger age, and nurture them by giving every support including scientific training. But the
Committee finds that the impact of these schemes is not visible at any level, despite these being implemented for a very

16.15   The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that performance of these schemes should be evaluated and

forefront.
16.16   The Committee agrees with the Ministry that sporting standards in country have not declined. It, however,
believes that it  cannot be said to have improved either. Our performance in international sports is not at
all satisfactory and commensurate with the size of our country as well as the population.
16.17The Committee  is of the opinion that training is the core aspect of good performance.  Rigorous

16.19   The overall  performance as well as functioning of the SAI, the Committee feels, has been far from
satisfactory. Our performance in international sports as it is, is highly disappointing. The SAI obviously has not
been able to make any significant impact as was initially visualised when it was set up. It is said to have developed
into an unwieldy bureaucratic organization, which is not being managed by sportspersons themselves. The Committee

appears to be little hope from this scheme. The Ministry must therefore, revisit this scheme at the earliest.   (Para
20.4)
XXI.  SPECIAL AWARDS TO WINNERS IN INTERNATIONAL SPORTS EVENTS AND THEIR
           COACHES
           The Committee is of the opinion that the pension scheme for sportspersons is a good scheme but it needs to
be further streamlined and strengthened. Besides, the Committee suggests that other facilities like CGHS
benefits should also be extended to them. It has come to the notice of the Committee that many cases of
sportspersons for pension are pending. The  Committee would like the Ministry to expedite the
disposal of pension cases pending with it as early as possible.                                                                                (Para
21.3)
XXII.ASSISTANCE TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS
           The Committee is aware about questions being raised from different quarters particularly in media about
the functioning of National Sports Federations. While the Committee feels concerned about those
questions, it, however, thinks that National Sports Federations are the  lynchpin in the entire
sports structure of the country. Organisations like Indian Olympic Association are under certain
international obligations as well. While recognizing the key contribution of the NSFs in the promotion and
development of sports in the country, the Committee is of the opinion that they should also see that their
functioning does not leave any cause of complain. If some of the NSFs were not functioning in transparent
manner and the matter is reported in the newspaper, it does not mean that functioning  of all the
NSFs are questionable.           (Para 22.5)
        The  Committee feels that if the Ministry had initiated some steps to start a  dialogue on the
issues of concern, the NSFs including IOA should have  cooperated in this endeavour of the
Ministry. In present scenario, some  revolutionary decision needs to be taken to achieve results in a
fixed timeframe as has been done in other countries like China, South Korea and,  Taiwan. Such a
strategy could be chalked out only after the Ministry, the  Federations, IOA and the corporate
houses and all the stakeholders in sports sit together to share each other concerns and identified
the problems.                                                                                                                   (Para 22.6)
        The  Committee is happy to know that soon after the conclusion of the Commonwealth and the
Asian Games in 2002, the Ministry undertook the exercise of finalizing  Long Term Development
Plans with various NSFs. The exercise has been completed and a review undertaken in respect of
some of the Federations also.  The Committee would like to know the result of that review for its
knowledge.                                                                                               (Para 22.7)
        The  Committee would like to mention about the alleged non-submission of UCs by some
sports federations that has become a recurrent feature and the Ministry has not been able to tackle
the problem. Besides, statement of accounts is also not  being published by some sports federations.
The Committee would like the  Ministry to ensure the financial accountability of these federations.
The  Committee is surprised at the silence maintained by the Ministry on this aspect. This
approach ultimately would hamper the development of sports. The  Committee would like the

Ministry to adopt more pro-active approach in these  matters.                                     (Para 22.8)
XXIII.  STATE SPORTS ACADEMY
           The Committee feels that this scheme has not taken off at all mainly due to from poor response from
the corporate sector. The  Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Ministry to discuss the
problems with the private sector and based on discussions held so far the scheme has been proposed to be
revised. The Committee therefore, recommends that the process of revision be expedited as early as
possible.           (Para 23.3)
           The Committee calls upon the corporate houses to co-operate in this national endeavour as part of their
social responsibility. Any apprehensions/problems that they may be having in contributing for this noble cause,
the Committee feels, could be taken care of after mutual consultations with the Ministry as well as Federations. 
(Para 23.4)
XXIV.                                                                                                NORTH-EASTERN STATES
        North-Eastern States have been provided special emphasis by making separate provision for this
area both for the youth programmes as well as sports activities. These States have immense potential for sports
development and have produced many outstanding sportspersons who have brought laurels for the country.
Looking at the financial performance of the Ministry in this region, the Committee feels that these states didn’t
receive the attention they deserved. The Committee therefore, recommends to have a separate unit in the
Ministry to monitor the implementation of the schemes in this area and would like to have a status report after
every six months.                                                                                                             (Para 24.1)
XXV.  PROMOTION OF SPORTS AMONG DISABLED
           Under the scheme of Promotion of Sports among disabled a token provision of Rs. 4 lakh has been made
for BE 2004-05. The Committee is dismayed to observe that the scheme is being merged into the scheme relating
to events and the same is under formulation. This appears to be a strange move on the part of the Ministry. The
Committee recommends that the scheme of disabled should be finalized at the earliest and every possible steps be
taken to run it as a separate scheme.                                                                             (Para 25.2)
XXVI.MONITORING OF SCHEMES
           The Committee is surprised to see that there is no permanent mechanism in the Ministry of its own to
receive regular feedback as well as to monitor various programmes, both under Youth Welfare and the Sports.
This is an aspect without which, it is not possible to know about the performance and success of the schemes run
by the Ministry. The Committee was informed that monitoring was being done basically by two Committees one
formed under the DG, SAI and the other under the Minister of Youth Affairs and Sports. The Committee
disapproves a  monitoring activity which is so centralised that it is being done from the Centre. Sports being
the State subject, the Committee feels, any mechanism which is not from down-to-the-top is bound to be
ineffective. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should devise a decentralised mechanism of
monitoring of its schemes at the earliest.                                                                                          (Para 26.2)
