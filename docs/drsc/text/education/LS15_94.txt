REPORT
I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1
              Education is the most crucial investment in human development.  It is an instrument for developing an
economically prosperous society and for ensuring equity and social justice.  With this realization, the importance of
Secondary Education which provides skilled manpower for the economy and is a bridge for higher, technical and
professional education cannot be denied.  With the process of liberalization and globalisation the economy generates
higher demand for new skills. In this context Higher Education and need for it is imperative.  A holistic view was taken
in 1986 when the National Policy on Education (NPE) visualized education as a dynamic, cumulative, life long process,
providing diversity of learning opportunities to all segments of society. Prime Minister’s Special Action Plan (SAP) has
also stressed the need for expansion and improvement of social infrastructure in the field of education.  With the 
necessity of qualitative skilled manpower,  a separate Department of Secondary Education and Higher Education has
been created recently.
1.2
              During the course of his evidence, Secretary, Department of Secondary and Higher Education  informed  the
Committee that an allocation of Rs. 24,908 crore has been made in the Ninth Five Year Plan in the Central Sector as
against an outlay of Rs. 7,443 crore in the Eighth Five Year Plan. While this represents an increase of more than two
and a half times, it is less than one third of the actual requirement projected.  During the first three years of the current
Five Year Plan, an expenditure of Rs. 11,951,03 crore is likely to be incurred (Approx. 48%).  An amount of Rs.
12,957.35 crore is expected to be available for allocation  during the last two years of the current Five Year Plan.  50%
of the amount i.e. Rs. 6,478.67 crore should have been allocated to the Department during 2000-2001.  Actual allocation
has been restricted to Rs. 5,450.00 crore, a shortfall of approximately, Rs. 1,000 crore.  Allocation for Secondary
Education & Higher Education is Rs.1720.29 crore as against Rs.1553.91 crore in 1999-2000 (BE) which shows an
increase of about 11%. However, the Committee was given to understand that even though several Committees have
suggested for 6% of GDP to be made available for Educational sector,  yet the present allocation is only 3.4% of GDP
which is still less by 2.6%.  The Committee in this respect is of the considered view that when there is a general trend
for disinvestment  of the  public sector and cuts in subsidies, it should be the effort  of the Government to prioritize the
Social Sector,  especially Education and Health which stand as the heart and soul of human development.   The
Committee, therefore,  suggests that the Department should find greater ways and means for  further development and
promotion of education.

crore which  is less when compared to the BE of previous year.
2.2       Allocation for Secondary Education is Rs. 600 crore as against Rs. 558.45 crore in 1999-2000 (BE), reflecting an
increase of about 7%.  Similarly, enhancement in allocation for Higher Education from Rs. 501.85 crore as against Rs.

Budgets of almost all the Departments are prepared in such a manner that one does not get a clear  idea in this regard. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should take corrective steps in this regard, if the
Performance Budgeting is to have any meaning.
III.       SECONDARY EDUCATION
Against a provision of Rs. 558.45 crore in BE 1999-2000 for Secondary Education, allocation for 2000-2001
stands at Rs. 600 crore, thus reflecting a marginal increase.  The Committee, however, observes that trend of allocation
of funds and expenditure thereof during the period 1997-2000 clearly indicates that the funds provided were invariably
reduced at the RE stage and even the reduced allocation could not be fully utilised due to one reason or the other.  The
Committee feels that secondary education is a critical stage of education where the process of transformation from
childhood to adulthood occurs.  It can become gateway to higher education for the talented students and for others a
stage for entry into the world of work.  The Committee is,  therefore, of the view that special emphasis at this stage is 
called for.  The Department should actively monitor the successful implementation of the various schemes of the
secondary education sector.
IV.       NATIONAL COUNCIL OF EDUCATIONAL RESEARCH AND TRAINING (NCERT)
4.1       NCERT is an apex resource organisation to assist and advise the Central and the State Governments on academic
matters related to school education.  It provides academic and technical support for qualitative improvement of school
education.  The Secretary informed the Committee that keeping in view the requirements of the National Education
Policy,  steps to review the 10 year old National curriculum framework have been initiated by the NCERT.  NCERT has
recently initiated a national debate on its document on “National Curriculum Framework for School Education”, where
the focus is on:-
i)
                    Work experience/life coping skill development;
ii)                   Value Education;
iii)                 Reduction in content/load and load of non-comprehension ;and
iv)                 Information Technology (IT) literacy and increasing use of IT in teaching and learning.
It was clarified by the Secretary that this document was a discussion document which has only mentioned the
issues being raised by many educationists/experts in various quarters.   This document was being debated at present.  It
was further clarified that there was no change in the National  Policy on medium of instruction and teaching of
languages in schools and the discussion document will be finalised in line with the national policy.  The Committee
feels that mother tongue should be taught right from the first standard. However, the Committee finds that in reality this
is not happening.   Most of the privately managed schools are not following the national policy in this regard.
4.2       The Committee has been informed that allocation for NCERT in BE 1999-2000 was Rs.34.70 crore which was
increased to Rs.40 crore at the RE stage and in BE 2000-2001 it has further been enhanced to Rs.43 crores.  The
Committee is, however, constrained to observe that the real increase is reflected  only in the non-plan allocation. 
Provision of Rs. 20.70 crore (Non-plan) in BE 1999-2000 was enhanced to Rs. 30 crore at the RE stage.  Same
allocation has been  made in BE 2000-2001.  In contrast,  plan allocation of Rs. 14 crore in BE 1999-2000 was reduced
to Rs. 10 crore at the RE stage.  Rs. 13 crore have been given in 2000-2001 which is less than  BE 1999-2000.  Reason
given for reduction is slow pace of expenditure.  The Committee desires that steps should be taken for the optimum and
judicious utilisation of plan funds meant for various schemes for qualitative improvement of school education.

V.        KENDRIYA VIDYALAYAS
5.1       The main objective of  Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan set up in 1965 is to provide uninterrupted educational
facilities  to the wards of Central Govt. employees who are transferred from one region to another and from one
linguistic area to another.  At present, there are 871 KVs in India including two abroad at Kathmandu and Moscow. 707
KVs are in Civil/Defence Sectors and the remaining 164 are Projects and Institutes of Higher learning.
5.2       Against an allocation of Rs. 544.81 crore in BE 1999-2000, an enhanced provision of Rs. 590.59 crore has been
made in 2000-2001.  Rs. 95 crore is the plan component and Rs. 495.59 crore have been provided under non-plan. 
Additional allocation is proposed to be utilised to meet the increased expenditure on maintenance and repairs of school
buildings affected by severe cyclone in Orissa and other routine expenditure.
5.3       The Committee finds that out of 707 KVs in Civil/Defence Sectors, at present 403 are in permanent buildings
and the construction work is in progress in respect of another 79 KVs.  In respect of 89 KVs, the land has been
transferred and work is to be started.  Out of the total Ninth Plan outlay of Rs. 553.21 crore in the first three years, an
allocation of Rs. 217.18 crore has been made.  A requirement of Rs. 168 crore for each year, i.e. 2000-2001 and 2001-
2002 has accordingly, been projected.  The Committee observes that out of a plan allocation of Rs. 90 crore in 1999-
2000, Rs. 68.80 crore were earmarked for construction of School Buildings and Staff Quarters.  The Committee is,
however, constrained to note that whereas non-plan provision of Rs. 454.81 crore has been fully utilised upto 29.2.2000,
against a plan allocation of Rs. 90 crore, expenditure reported upto 29.2.2000 is only Rs. 45 crore.  The Committee
apprehends that there are chances of funds not being fully utilised for construction activities.  The Committee observes
that the Department should take appropriate measures for removal of constraints if any, in this regard.  The Committee
also recommends that allocation may be enhanced at the RE stage on the condition that it must be ensured that targets
set for construction activities are achieved.   The Committee would also like to point out that the Plan Schemes are
being transferred to Non-Plan, thereby adjusting the amount of committed expenditure.  The Committee strongly objects
to this kind of adjustment.
5.4       The Committee would also like to point out that children of local people  should get admission in Kendriya
Vidyalayas and the Committee recommends accordingly.  The Department should report to the Committee about the
action taken in this regard.

VII.      VOCATIONALISATION OF  EDUCATION
  7.1     The main objectives of this Centrally Sponsored Scheme introduced in 1988 are to enhance individual
employability, reduce the mismatch between demand and supply of skilled manpower and provide an alternative for
those pursuing higher education without particular interest or purpose. The Scheme is being implemented in all
States/UTs except Lakshadweep.  However, as the scheme has not  yielded desired results, it  was evaluated by four
research institutions under the co-ordination of the Operation Research Group (ORG). An evaluation study was
conducted by taking 25 per cent sample of vocational centres throughout the country and the Report was submitted in
1996-97.  Thereafter, a Working Group was set up under the Chairmanship of the then Director, NCERT.  A theme
paper was prepared on the basis of Working Group Report which has been circulated to all the States/UTs. So far
comments have been received from 15 States/UTs.
7.2       The Department has now informed that it has been decided to continue the scheme in its existing form and
expansion proposals have been invited from the State Governments. Further review of the working of the scheme has
been proposed to be taken up with State/UT. Government representatives in April, 2000.  The augmented budget of
Rs.31 crore proposed for  2000-2001 will be utilised for this purpose.
7.3       The Committee is not happy at the element of uncertainty surrounding the scheme. The Committee was given to
understand that a Sub-Committee of the National Development Council had been appointed to decide the fate of a
number of schemes of secondary education.  Scheme of Vocationalisation of Education was one of the schemes.  The
Committee feels that continuance of such a situation has adversely affected the implementation of this Scheme. Trend of

IX.       INTEGRATED EDUCATION FOR DISABLED CHILDREN (IEDC)
9.1       IEDC is a centrally sponsored scheme launched in 1974 which aims at providing educational opportunities  to
disabled children in schools to facilitate their retention and ultimate integration in the general school system.   The
scheme is being implemented through State Governments and NGOs.  The Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection
of Rights and Full Participation) Act 1955 places a statutory responsibility on the Government to ensure that all disabled
children receive free education in an appropriate environment till the age of 18 years.
9.2       The scheme  is being implemented in 27 States/UTs.  It is currently estimated that more than 65,000 disabled
children have benefitted under the scheme.   However, the Committee is perturbed to note that figures regarding number
of beneficiaries have not been received from 13 States/UTs.   The Committee observes that participation of the states
also does not seem to be of uniform level.   While States like Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, and Orissa
are the major beneficiaries during 1998-99 and 1999-2000, no funds/nominal funds have been allocated to Bihar, J&K 
Punjab and Arunachal Pradesh.  The Committee is of the view that monitoring mechanism of this scheme should be
strengthened.   Matter should also be taken up with the states who have failed to furnish the details about beneficiaries.  
The Committee notes that NCERT  has been requested to conduct an impact study with a view to  ascertaining the
impact of the IEDC Scheme.

UNIVERSITY GRANTS COMMISSION (UGC)
14.2     UGC was established under an Act of Parliament in 1956 for the purpose of coordination and determination of
standards in Universities.  The Act empowers the Commission among other things to allocate and disburse grants to
Universities for their   maintenance and development and to establish and operate Inter-University Centres.  Allocation
for UGC for 2000-2001 stands at Rs. 1407 crore which is considerably enhanced when compared with provision made
in 1999-2000, i.e. Rs. 1323 crore.
14.3     The Committee had observed last year that as a result of allocation of funds much below the requirements under
the Non-Plan side of UGC, 2100 posts (986 teaching and 1114 non-teaching) remained vacant in Central Universities,
Deemed Universities & Delhi Colleges. The Non-Plan allocation for UGC for the year 1999-2000 in the RE stage has
been increased to Rs. 975 crores as compared to BE allocation of Rs. 640 crores showing an increase of about 52%. 
The Committee hopes that  the enhanced allocations made in 1999-2000 would have enabled the UGC to meet the
Committed Liabilities including filling up of posts in the various institutions. 
14.4
          The Committee was  given to understand that UGC has been implementing a scheme to encourage additional
resource mobilization by the Universities.  Under this scheme, grants amounting to 25% of the additional resource
generated by the Universities are paid by the UGC, subject to a ceiling of Rs. 25 lakhs in a financial year.  The
Committee hopes that with the active implementation of this scheme, financial position of many Universities must be
strengthened.  The Committee would appreciate if details of universities which have benefitted from this scheme are
made known to it.
