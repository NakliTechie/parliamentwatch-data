REPORT
I.          INTRODUCTION
            The development of women and children to their full potential is an essential component in the country’s total
human resource development.  In its  capacity as a nodal agency looking after the advancement of women and children,
the Department of Women & Child Development  formulates plans, policies and programmes, enacts/amends legislation
affecting women and children and guides and coordinates the efforts of both Governmental and non-Governmental
organisations working in the field of Women and Child Development.   Apart from this, the Department also
implements certain innovative programmes for women and children in the areas of employment and income generation,
welfare and support services, gender sensitization, training for employment and income generation and awareness
generation.   For a holistic child development, the Department implements the Integrated Child Development Services
(ICDS) which provides an integrated package of services comprising supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health 
check-up and pre-school and non-formal education and health and nutrition education.  The ultimate objective of all
these programmes is to make women independent and self-reliant and to ensure that children grow and live in a healthy
and secure environment.

III.       INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS) SCHEME
3.1       The Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) Scheme implemented through State Govts./UT
Administrations, aims at providing an integrated package of services to the children below 6 years, pregnant women and
nursing mothers.  The services include supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health check-up, referral services, pre-
school and non-formal education and health and nutrition education.  The Scheme is at present operational in 4200
development blocks in the country. 
3.2       The Committee was informed that the proposed enhanced estimate of Rs. 935.00 crores in 2000-2001 is mainly
due to revised financial norms and proposed expansion of the scheme in additional 260 Blocks in the country. 
However, an additional amount of Rs. 264.00 crores was required to meet the full requirement.  The Committee is
happy to note that the revised cost norms of ICDS from Rs. 17.06 lakh to Rs. 30.75 lakh per project which relates to
costs of salaries of project staff, contingency amounts, provision of medicine  etc.  approved by the Govt. of India in
January, 2000, would be made effective in due course.   A noteworthy feature in the revised ICDS scheme is the
proposal to cover the small hamlets/villages in the country in tribal blocks  having less than 300 population and situated
in far flung areas.  It is now proposed that 10 Mini Anganwadi centres can be opened in lieu of a full-fledged
Anganwadi.
3.3       The main objective of ICDS is to improve nutritional and health status of children 0-6 years old.   This scheme
covers a major part of the country and there has been continued enhanced allocation and expenditure under it.  However,
the Committee is disturbed to note that this significant expansion of ICDS has not been able to reduce the Infant
Mortality Rate (IMR).  Figures for 1998 indicate no improvement and in case of some states there is  an increase in IMR
which  is a matter of great concern.  Figures for  the period 1996-98  in respect of weak states are self-revealing :-

the concerned Departments.  The Committee was also  informed that the Central Planning Committee has been regularly

co-ordinated approach amongst all the concerned Central Departments and the State Govts. Initiative by the Department
of Women & Child Development in active cooperation from rest of the Departments can achieve results.            
3.5       The Committee was informed that as on 30.9.99, 3,70,908 Anganwadis were providing supplementary nutrition
to 257.88 lakh children and 51.61 lakh mothers and pre-school education was available to 126.95 lakh children.    
Nutrition supplement is the responsibility of the State Govts. which varies from State to State as per the local
requirements.   External  agencies like CARE and World Food Programme were providing  assistance to some of the
States.  However,  the Committee was given to understand that due to resource constraint, some State Govts. were not in
a position to fulfil this responsibility as per the prescribed norms.  The Committee is of the view that the Department
should prevail upon the State Governments to ensure that provision of nutrition-cum-supplement should not be
adversely affected due to lack of funds.  Steps may be taken for provision of assistance from external agencies to the
extent possible to such States.
3.6       The Committee would like to draw the attention of the Department to instances of nutritious supplement not
reaching the beneficiaries due to one reason or the other.   Anganwadi Workers find it difficult to collect the nutritious
supplement from CDPO office in the absence of any transport charges being given to them.  The Committee urges upon
the Department to look into the matter and take necessary remedial measures.
3.7       The effective delivery package of services envisaged in the ICDS scheme depends upon the functioning of
Anganwadi Worker, who is a community-based voluntary worker and her helper.  Feedback made available to the
Committee indicates that successful running of an Anganwadi centre, by and large, depends upon the initiative and
dedication of an individual Anganwadi worker. However, the Committee finds that as compared to her manifold duties,
honorarium paid to an Anganwadi Worker is too meagre.  The Committee was given to understand that an Anganwadi
Worker is a part-time worker, but ground realities are totally different.  There is large scale exploitation of Anganwadi
Workers in most of the States.   Anganwadi Workers, generally being uneducated are neither aware nor properly briefed
about the various services available for child/women welfare. 
3.8       The Committee  was informed that 14 States, where additional work was being done by Anganwadi Workers
and Helpers, have started giving additional remuneration ranging from Rs. 100 to Rs. 250 to as high as Rs. 650 in the
case of Pondicherry.   Three more States are likely to provide additional remunerations.  The Committee is of the view
that honorarium of Rs. 500 for AWW and Rs. 200 for Helper needs to be increased in the present scenario.  Lack of
resources cannot be held responsible for it.  It is for the Department to manage funds by streamlining the existing
schemes. 
3.9       The Committee finds that the report of the National Committee to review the benefits/facilities to AWWs and
Helpers is under finalisation and hopes that the implementation of the recommendations made by the aforesaid
Committee would lead to a significant improvement in the service conditions of AWWs and Helpers.  The Committee
would like to be apprised in this regard.
3.10     The Committee finds that there are Central directives for running of Anganwadi centres but the implementation
thereof  varies from State to State.  The Committee notes that as per the central directives, there has to be a local
committee for selection of Anganwadi Workers and it is for State Governments to decide the composition of the
Committee.  The Department submitted that the mode of involvement of PRIs in implementation of ICDS scheme
differs from State to State depending on the powers conferred upon them by the State Govts.   The Committee was
given to understand that in States where Panchayati Raj Institutions are traditionally strong like MP, AP, West Bengal,
UP, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Kerala etc., the supervision and management of the Anganwadi centres is actively done

IV.       WORLD BANK ASSISTED ICDS
4.1       The Committee notes that at present three on-going World Bank assisted ICDS Projects are there:
i)          ICDS-II Project in Bihar and MP (1993-2000)
ii)         ICDS-III in Kerala, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu and UP (1999-2004)
iii)         ICDS-APER – AP (1999-2004)
The  World Bank assisted ICDS-II Project  in Bihar and Madhya Pradesh became operational in September,
1993 for a period of seven years.   The Project covers 210 blocks in Bihar and 244 blocks in Madhya Pradesh in
predominantly tribal and difficult areas.   The total outlay for the Project, excluding the cost of Supplementary Nutrition
Programme is Rs. 596.23 crores – Rs. 257.13 crores for Bihar and Rs. 339.10 crores for Madhya Pradesh. 
4.2       The World Bank Assisted ICDS-III Project has been in operation  in five States of Kerala, Maharashtra,
Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh since March, 1999.  The approved IDA credit available for the ICDS-III
Project, including the UDISHA  Project is, US$ 300 million.  The total outlay for the Project is Rs. 1000.11 crores over
a five year Project period from 1998-99 to 2003-2004 (excluding the training component).  An amount of Rs. 75.90
crores has been released to the five States so far for implementation of the project.
4.3       Andhra Pradesh Economic Restructuring (APER) Project is being implemented in the State of Andhra Pradesh
since March, 1999.   The approved IDA Credit for the Project is US$ 75 million.   The Project would be covering a total
of 251 blocks at a project cost of Rs. 392.75 crores .
4.4       The Committee was informed that the additional components under World Bank assisted ICDS Projects are
construction of Anganwadi centres, construction of CDPOs Office-cum-Godowns, installation of hand pumps, enhanced
Information,  Education and Communication activities, provision of micro-nutrients for adolescent girls.  The
Committee was informed that as against proposed outlay of Rs. 293.81 crores,  Rs. 180.00 crores have been provided
for the year 2000-2001.
4.5       The Committee notes that so far Rs. 270.80  crores and Rs. 280.80 crores have been released to Bihar and
Madhya Pradesh (December, 1999) respectively under ICDS-II project.  The Committee has been given to understand
that despite a slow start, the project has picked up very well in both States.   Comparison of the National Family Health
Survey (1992-93) data with NFHS (1999) and India Nutrition Profile  (1998) data indicates that there has been
improvement in Bihar in growth and survival indicators over the life of the project.   However, the Committee has an
apprehension that ground realities may be somewhat different.   The Committee feels that an in-depth evaluation by an
independent agency with active interaction with field workers and beneficiaries and involvement of Panchayats/local
bodies and also with local MPs/MLAs wherever feasible in the most vulnerable areas (particularly in tribal and remote
areas) would give the real assessment of this Project.

VII.      CRECHES/DAY CARE CENTRES
7.1       The Scheme of running Creches/Day Care Centres started in 1975 aims to provide day care services for the
children (0-5 years) of mainly casual, migrant, agricultural and construction labourers.  The scheme is being

7.2       The Committee notes that during 1999-2000, the budget provision of Rs. 20.50 crores was reduced to Rs. 17.00

corpus amount has not yet been approved and only  token provisions are being made during the last several years.   The

made to consider enhancement at a later stage.   The Committee emphasises that concerted efforts should be made to
finalise the proposal regarding enhancement of corpus amount under the National Creche Fund.

IX.       BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA
9.1       The Scheme of Balika Samriddhi Yojana was launched on 2 nd October, 1997 with the objective of raising the
overall status of the girl child.  Re-designed in 1999-2000, the post-delivery grant of Rs. 500/- will now be deposited in
an interest-bearing bank/post office account in the name of the girl child  and a scholarship ranging from Rs. 300 to Rs.
1000 for each class of study successfully completed upto Class X. ICDS/Village Panchayats/Municipalities are the

XX.      COMMITTED LIABILITY
20.1     The issue of Committed Liability has been engaging the attention of the Committee since 1997-98.  At the end
of the Eight Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission had issued very detailed guidelines to carry forward the
Committed Expenditure from Plan to the Non-Plan side.  A lump sum provision of Rs. 500 crores in the Ministry of
Finance Budget for 1997-98 was provided for this purpose.  As a result of this exercise, however, only a sum of Rs. 150
crores was  transferred from Plan to Non-Plan for the various Ministries as committed expenditure during 1997-98. 
Rest of the amount was surrendered as the proposals were not found acceptable.  Secretary (Expenditure) while
deposing before the Committee on 27
th March, 1999 had informed that the Tenth Finance Commission had given a
breathing time till 1999-2000 for this transition.
20.2     The Department has informed that a proposal to transfer committed expenditure from Plan to Non-Plan for the
amount of Rs. 2.75 crores under the scheme of Short Stay Homes was submitted  to the Ministry of Finance in
November, 1997.  The proposal was revised in 1999-2000 due to revision of norms of the  scheme and the matter has
been pursued with that Ministry during 1999-2000.  The Ministry of Finance (Budget Division) has informed that the
matter will be considered during pre-Budget discussions while deciding the RE for 1999-2000.
20.3     The Committee notes with serious concern that while grants for Plan Schemes are getting reduced, Non-Plan
allocation and expenditure are steadily increasing.   What is happening is that the committed expenditure which should
appropriately be charged to the Pool, is being passed on to the respective Departments which is totally against the
guidelines of Tenth Finance Commission/Planning Commission.  If the committed expenditure is charged to the Pool, it
will not have any burden on the specific schemes of the Department.  Secretary (Expenditure), who appeared before the
Committee last year, was in full agreement with the stand taken by the Committee.   The Committee  is, however,
constrained to observe that in spite of Committee’s repeated efforts/directives to assist the Departments, no progress has
been made in the matter.   It seems that breathing time till 1999-2000 given by  the Tenth Finance Commission has not
been availed of by any Department.    There is total lack of initiative on the part of the Departments.  The Committee
again emphasises  that the Department should get this matter resolved without any further delay.   The way the 
Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the
Finance Commission and the recommendations made by the Committee.  This tendency, the Committee is constrained
to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.   The Committee again reiterates that the
Department should pursue the matter and report to the Committee about the outcome thereof.

Department including Ministry of Finance is trying to escape their responsibility is against the expressed wishes of the

to observe, is not in consonance with the principles which are well-established.   The Committee again reiterates that the
