I.          INTRODUCTION
1.1       The main responsibility of the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports is to ensure qualitative and quantitative
improvements in Youth Programmes and also on broad basing and bringing about awareness of sports and physical
fitness amongst school children and youth.  Over the years, the Ministry has taken a number of new initiatives and
organized a large number of programmes in the field of youth developments and sports and physical education.   The
Programmes undertaken by the Department are mainly Sports and Games, Physical Education and Youth Programmes.
1.2       The Ministry pursues the twin objectives of man making and nation building i.e. developing the personality of
the youth as also involving them in various nation building activities.  To achieve these objectives, Ministry of Youth
Affairs and Sports has undertaken various youth welfare programmes which range from personality development to
vocational training, to promotion of adventure and entrepreneurial development.  Most of these programmes are
implemented by State Governments, autonomous organizations of the Ministry and non-governmental organizations. 
Financial assistance is provided to all the implementing agencies by this Ministry as per the stipulated norms.  The
major schemes for youth development are National Service Scheme, Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan, National
Integration Programmes, Youth Hostels and National Service Volunteers Scheme, etc. and the same are financed by the
Ministry.
1.3       The Department also finances a number of programmes which aim at promotion of sports, including creation of
sports infrastructure in different parts of the country, grants for laying of synthetic surfaces, spotting and nurturing of
talents through the schemes of Sports Authority of India, grants to National Sports Federations, National
Championships, organisation of tournaments for rural youth, women in the North-Eastern Region.
1.4       In the recent past, the performance/standard of Indian Sports has definitely improved.  India’s performance in
2002 Commonwealth Games and Asian Games was commendable.  However, compared to International Standards and
pace of improvement in the International Standards, we have a long way to go.  The Ministry is making concerted
efforts to create infrastructure of a high standard in various parts of the country. 
            Since the promotion and development of sports is an ongoing process, Government of India, in consultation with
Sports Authority of India, Indian Olympic Association and National Sports Federations, is taking the following steps to
improve the standard of Indian sports:
Broadening the base-especially, for competitive sports;
Improvement in infrastructure;
Improving the knowledge of the Coaching Community;
Improving the practical knowledge of the Scientific Community to enable them to contribute along with the coaches to
enhance performance of sports persons;
Improved functioning and professionalisation in management of Sports Federations;
 Greater involvement of States in the development of Sports infrastructure and improved training of sportspersons;
Involvement of the private sector in adopting and sponsoring individual/specific sports disciplines; and
Creation of awareness in general public to have greater participation in sports activities.
Besides these, long term development plan prepared by NSFs also cover areas like athlete development, growth of
sports clubs, domestic and international tournaments, equipment and scientific support, management etc.
Youth constitute nearly 40% of India's population. It is, therefore, necessary not only to make this vibrant and strong
resource of the country beneficiaries of, but, also, valuable partners in the process of our planning and development. In
order that young men and women are able to contribute towards national development in a significant way, it is
important that they should be able to effectively participate in the process of decision-making. Towards this end, the
Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports has been constantly reviewing the policies and programmes concerning the Youth of
the country, so as to keep pace with the fast changing socio-economic scenario and also to address the emerging
concerns of the youth, a Draft Policy is stated to be in an advanced stage of finalization. A National Commission for
Youth has been set up to recommend to the Government measures to tackle effectively the problems faced by the youth
of India, with particular focus on youth unemployment. Two new schemes viz. Financial Assistance for the
Development and Empowerment of the Adolescents and Scheme for Establishment of National / State Youth Centres
are proposed to be implemented during the Tenth Plan.

While the role and contribution of youth is of vital importance in all countries, it is particularly significant in a
country like India where the proportion of the youth in the country’s overall social and demographic profile is
continuously increasing. According to the 2001 Census, there were approximately 355 million people in the 15 to
35 age group, 74 per cent of whom lived in rural areas. The number was expected to rise to approximately 510
million by 2016. Both central and state governments need to harness the energies of this large group, providing
them with adequate infrastructure of world standards for excellence in sports at national and international
levels.
The Committee is constrained to observe that no organized effort appears to be forthcoming over a national scale to tap
the potential of the Youth. Youth are the foundation for the development of the country and unless emphasis is laid on
youth programme and put the youth on the right track and right direction, the ultimate goal of nation building can not be
achieved.  The Committee feels that in order to develop the youth, a yoga programme should be started throughout the
country for different age groups with a definite capsule of different sets of exercises provided for different age groups. It
should be as near to the homes of the people as possible. Adequate number of volunteers should be trained for the
purpose who may conduct the exercises in Yoga suited to different age groups. These exercises may be conducted in an
hour say between 5 to 6 a.m. throughout the country. It should be designated a national hour. The trained volunteers
may be paid an allowance for the purpose. This would infuse new energy & fitness among the people and help build a
strong nation. Further modalities, if any, could be worked out. The services of any existing organization could also be
utilized for the purpose.
III        THRUST IN THE NINTH FIVE YEAR PLAN
The thrust in the Ninth Plan was on harnessing Yuva Shakti (Youth power).  The National Youth Policy emphasis:
Involving young persons in various community-based nation building activities and a new scheme called National
Reconstruction Corps (NRC) to involve youth volunteers in such activities;

Providing special attention to education, training and employment, health, environment, sports, recreation and leisure,
art and culture, science and technology, etc.; and
Developing the interest of youth in development-oriented programmes.
3.2       During the Ninth Plan, the NYKS was able to extend its activities to a larger number of villages and it now has
offices in almost 500 districts The NYKS took up several new initiatives to involve the rural youth - by motivating them
to organise themselves into youth clubs and self-help groups. Schemes like the NSVS, Financial Assistance to Youth
Clubs, Award to Outstanding Youth Clubs and Youth Development Centre have been successful in encouraging young
people to take up development-oriented programmes. The NYKS also took up major programmes under the
Swarnajayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana (SGSY), being implemented in collaboration with Ministry of Rural
Development. The objective of the programme was to bring selected Below Poverty Line (BPL) youth above the
poverty line in three years. Watershed Management Programmes were also being implemented by the NYKS. In
addition, the NYKS also provided assistance to disabled persons for providing aids and appliances, running of 100 old
age day care centres, 30 Gandhi Millennium Youth Peace Centres, Wildlife Watch Centres, National Youth
Cooperatives and United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)-NYKS Youth Volunteers Against Poverty Awards
Programmes. The coverage under NSS increased to more than 1.7 million students.
IV        APPROACH TO THE TENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN
Government’s approach on Youth Affairs and Sports during the Tenth Plan period is reflected in the following paras of
the Approach Paper to the Tenth Five Year Plan:
            “Adolescent youth in the 10-19 years age group numbered 230 million, accounting for 22 8 per cent of the
population. It is proposed to target this segment of the youth population through specific intervention strategies that will
focus on youth and power, gender justice, youth health and responsible living.
            The major thrust will be on involving the youth in the process of national planning and development and making
them the focal point of development strategy by providing proper educational and training opportunities, access to
information on employment opportunities including entrepreneurial guidance and financial credit, proper platforms for
developing qualities of leadership, tolerance and open mindedness, patriotism, etc. 
The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports will play a pro-active and catalytic role in exploring and identifying
employment opportunities for the youth in coordination with other Ministries and Departments so as to optimally utilize
the investments in various youth-related schemes The Ministry will also play an active advocacy role in promoting
gender justice by sensitising the youth towards gender issues.
            Youth activities will focus on providing quality health services and will ensure that the youth have access to
information relating to reproduction, health issues, managing alcoholism, drug addiction, etc. 
            Value-based education will be imparted which will focus on respect for elders, tolerance for others' beliefs and
religions, compassion towards the poor and needy. The youth will be involved in the protection and preservation of
nature. Steps will be taken to increase their awareness of India's cultural heritage and history.
Adolescents need particular attention and concentrated efforts are required to build a relatively stable place in society
for them and help them overcome the barriers to healthy development.
Efforts will be made for networking between Government and non-Government Organi­sations (NGOs) in youth
development.”
            The Committee feels that the objectives are laudable.  However, unless dedicated and sincere efforts are
made to achieve these objectives they will remain on paper only. These objectives cannot be achieved overnight.
Rather, ground should have been prepared much earlier for attainment of these objectives. To start with, the
Committee feels, that a nationwide Yoga programme should be started under the supervision of trained
volunteers in a morning hour say between 5.00 to 6.00 a.m. as already mentioned in the foregoing paragraphs, in
order to harness the energies of youth.  The Committee hopes that efforts would commence in right earnest. This
is expected to energise the nation and also help promote unity.
V         YOUTH AFFAIRS PROGRAMMES
5.1       The total allocation made for Youth Affairs for the year 2003-2004  is Rs136.54 crores. This amount includes
Rs.109.23 crores on Plan side and Rs. 27.31 crores on Non-Plan side.  The entire Budgetary allocation consists of Rs.
122.54 crores - Rs. 95.23 crores (Plan) and Rs. 27.31 crores (Non-Plan) made last year. Though the allocation on Plan
side this year has been increased by about Rs.14 crores, the Committee feels that the overall annual plan allocation is
not sufficient keeping in view the schemes being implemented by the Ministry and the objectives that have been set
forth in the Tenth plan document.   More so, the inflation pressure and the increasing youth population in the country are
likely to make things more difficult.

The Committee observes that there is an urgent need to address the existing scenario of youth problems which is
reflected in the unlawful activities of misguided youth of the country. Youth power, if directed properly, can change the
socio-economic scenario of the country.  Added to this lack of adequate employment opportunities, lack of direction
because of uncertain education system, inability to pursue education because of financial constraints are compelling
youth to take to the crimes to find some easy money to sustain life.   In these circumstances, the Committee is
constrained to note that the achievement of targets, both financial and physical, by the youth wing has not been
satisfactory.  Infact the budgetary support  during Ninth Five Year Plan remained unutilized.   Against the approved
outlay of was Rs.352.24 crores for the Ninth Plan expenditure incurred amounted to  Rs.336.94 crores.   The Committee
agrees with the view expressed by the Approach Paper to the Ninth Plan that the social sector programmes have not
performed upto the mark.   The Committee is therefore of the view that the monitoring and evaluation system either
does not exist and even if it exists, the findings of it are not being seriously taken. These are the reasons due to which
the targets could not be achieved on both financial and physical fronts. 
The Committee therefore recommends that the Ministry should tighten and strengthen its monitoring system to
ensure achievement of targets.  Two Vital factors like inflation, increasing number of youth and paramount need
to channelise the  energies of youth in right direction be kept constantly in view while making allocations to the
Department.
            On being asked that though the country has one billion people, what were the reasons for performing badly in
International events, the Ministry stated that India was the most populous country in the world after China. But our
sports playing population was very less.  It is estimated that out of one billion, only a few lakhs pursue sports for
recreation and even fewer for participation in competitive sports. Quality comes out of quantity and with a narrow base
of sports playing population especially for competitive sports; it was very difficult to find adequate number of athletes
of international level. Unlike China, the role and contribution of educational institutions/universities to Indian sports is
quite meagre.  Apart from lack of sports culture, the country also lacked the kind of resources necessary for achieving
excellence in sports. India being a developing country, limited budgetary resources were allocated for sports activities.
Efforts were being made to bridge the gap in resources by involving the private sector and getting assistance from some
public sector undertakings of some Central Ministries such as the Ministry of Coal.
            The Committee feels that in order to promote Sports in the country it is necessary to provide adequate
incentives, good job opportunities and recognition of talent and achievements through an independent
assessment and honest promotion of sports.  At the same time while it is desirable to enlist cooperation of private
sector, it may not be forthcoming unless this is linked to tax incentives.
VI        NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN (NYKS)
6.1       Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan was a Central autonomous body of the Ministry which has Nehru Yuva Kendras
in the districts functioning under it. It is one of the largest grass root level organizations in Asia-Pacific region, catering
to more than 8 million youth in the age group of 15-35 years. This organization was working for giving extra thrust to
the activities of non-student rural youth through programmes such as youth leadership, training, community service
programmes, vocational training, sports and recreation, cultural activities, non-formal education, etc.
6.2       NYKS accomplished its task through two categories of programmes i.e. Regular Programmes and Special
Programmes.  Plan Financial outlay made under this scheme was to the tune of Rs. 31.18 crores for the current year as
against a provision of Rs. 28.38 crores last year.  It has been stated that the marginal increase of Rs.2.80 crores was
necessitated on account of  the requirement of payment of arrears to the employees of Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan
who have been placed in  higher scales of pay, in terms of  the scheme of Assured Career Progression.    B.E. 2001-2002
and 2002-2003 provided enhanced allocation as compared to earlier years due to the sanction of a new project for self
employment of Youth and normal increase of expenditure necessitated by   inflationary pressures in the economy. 
The  Committee feels that the activities/programmes undertaken by NYKS were good in promoting communal
harmony but they hardly reach the grassroot levels.  Lack of awareness among people deprived them to fully
exploit the benefits of the programme.   The Committee,  therefore, feels that it is necessary to give wide publicity
to the activities of NYKS to invite increased participation.
Nevertheless, the achievements by NYKS under various schemes during the entire Ninth Plan period viz. number of
programmes, Financial Assistance for Promotion of Youth Activities and Training National Service Volunteer Scheme
and Assistance to Youth Clubs have been quite encouraging.
VII      NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME (NSS)
7.1       The scheme started in 1969-70 was being implemented through universities, colleges and other institutions of
higher learning. Expenditure on the scheme was shared on 7:5 basis between the Govt. of India and the States except in
case of Govt. of J&K and UTs without legislatures where the entire expenditure was met by the Central Govt. The

Scheme has two types of programmes, viz. "Regular Activities" and "Special Camping programmes" undertaken by its
volunteers. Under "Regular Activities", students were expected to work as volunteers for a continuous period of two
years, rendering community service for a minimum of 120 hours per annum. The activities included improvement of
campuses, tree plantation, constructive work in adopted villages and slums, work in welfare institutions, blood donation,
adult and non-formal education, health, nutrition, family welfare, AIDS awareness campaign, etc. Under "Special
Camping Programme", Camp of 10 days' duration was conducted every year in the adopted areas on specific themes
like "Youth for afforestation and Tree Plantations", "Youth for Rural Reconstruction", "Youth for Development",

"Watershed Management" in the New Millennium, the NSS theme for Special Camping was 'Youth for Healthy
Society" which was being launched from the year 1999.  The theme chosen for the year 2003-2004 is "Swacchta"
(Cleanliness).   These programmes provided ample opportunities to the students for group-living, collective experience
sharing and constant interaction with different communities. Adoption of a village/area was also one of the meaningful
programmes undertaken by the NSS Volunteers.  Village adoption programme ensured continuity of work, vis.-a-vis.,
sustained action, evaluation and follow up work.
NSS has over 19 lakh student volunteers spread over 176 Universities and 22 Senior Secondary Councils. From its
inception, more than 2.51 crore students from universities, colleges and institutions of higher learning have benefitted
from NSS activities.
A target of 9500, each,  for Adoption of Villages and Special Camping Programmes has been fixed for the year 2003-04
. The identification of villages for adoption and camp sites has been done at the unit level i.e. +2 School, college and
university having NSS units.
The activities of the NSS network would be expanded to cover all degree colleges and +2 schools while the NRC would
extend its reach to 500 districts by the end of the Tenth Plan period because  it is felt that because all categories of
youth, including rural youth, should get an opportunity to participate in various schemes like the National Cadet Corps
(NCC), NSS, Scouts and Guides and NRC.
            The Committee observes it is necessary to have another look on the three-tier system to determine the extent to
which it has helped.  The Committee notes that the utilisation of allocated funds under NSS continues to be the
optimum.  The Committee is surprised to learn how in the absence of Audited Accounts/utilisation certificates,
allocation of funds has been continuing for years together.
 The Committee, therefore, is of the view that before going for enhancement in the scale of assistance for regular
activities and special camps under NSS, it is necessary to develop an effective monitoring system to curb the
misuse of expenditure, if any.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
While the role and contribution of youth is of vital importance in all countries, it is particularly significant in a
country like India where the proportion of the youth in the country’s overall social and demographic profile is
continuously increasing. According to the 2001 Census, there were approximately 355 million people in the 15 to
35 age group, 74 per cent of whom lived in rural areas. The number was expected to rise to approximately 510
million by 2016. Both central and state governments need to harness the energies of this large group, providing
them with adequate infrastructure of world standards for excellence in sports at national and international
levels.
The Committee is constrained to observe that no organized effort appears to be forthcoming over a national scale to tap
the potential of the Youth. Youth are the foundation for the development of the country and unless emphasis is laid on
youth programme and put the youth on the right track and right direction, the ultimate goal of nation building can not be
achieved.  The Committee feels that in order to develop the youth, a yoga programme should be started throughout the
country for different age groups with a definite capsule of different sets of exercises provided for different age groups. It
should be as near to the homes of the people as possible. Adequate number of volunteers should be trained for the
purpose who may conduct the exercises in Yoga suited to different age groups. These exercises may be conducted in an
hour say between 5 to 6 a.m. throughout the country. It should be designated a national hour. The trained volunteers
may be paid an allowance for the purpose. This would infuse new energy & fitness among the people and help build a
strong nation. Further modalities, if any, could be worked out. The services of any existing organization could also be
utilized for the purpose. (Para 2.2)
APPROACH TO THE TENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN
The Committee feels that the objectives are laudable.  However, unless dedicated and sincere efforts are made to
achieve these objectives they will remain on paper only. These objectives cannot be achieved overnight. Rather,
ground should have been prepared much earlier for attainment of these objectives. To start with, the Committee
feels, that a nationwide Yoga programme should be started under the supervision of trained volunteers in a
morning hour say between 5.00 to 6.00 a.m. as already mentioned in the foregoing paragraphs, in order to
harness the energies of youth.  The Committee hopes that efforts would commence in right earnest. This is
expected to energise the nation and also help promote unity. (Para 4)
YOUTH AFFAIRS PROGRAMMES
            The Committee feels that in order to promote Sports in the country it is necessary to provide adequate
incentives, good job opportunities and recognition of talent and achievements through an independent
assessment and honest promotion of sports.  At the same time while it is desirable to enlist cooperation of private
sector, it may not be forthcoming unless this is linked to tax incentives. (Para 5)
NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN (NYKS)
The  Committee feels that the activities/programmes undertaken by NYKS were good in promoting communal
harmony but they hardly reach the grass root levels.  Lack of awareness among people deprived them to fully
exploit the benefits of the programme.   The Committee, therefore, feels that it is necessary to give wide publicity
to the activities of NYKS to invite increased participation. (Para 6.2)
NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry should realize the importance of sports and take necessary steps
towards utilization of vast potential manpower of students gainfully either in sports or in completion of projects
of national importance during holidays in forms other than NCC camps.  If dedicated efforts are made, the

YOUTH WELFARE ACTIVITIES
The Committee is aware that adolescents have special and distinct needs.  The most important issues concerning 
this segment of youth i.e. adolescents are: health, nutrition, education, both formal and non formal, vocation,
recreation and sports, child labour, children in difficult situations, alcohol and drug abuse.  The Committee
hopes that the Scheme would be implemented in letter and spirit within a stipulated timeframe keeping in view
the requirements related to this age group. (Para 11.3)
SPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA (SAI)
The Committee observes that SAI is required to be a purely professional entity devoid of bureaucratic control
and solely devoted to its objective of promoting sports so that country finds an honored place in the comity of
nations.  (Para 12.3)
To that end, among other things, the Committee observes that it is absolutely necessary that there is not only
adequate infrastructure in the matter of sports and also whatever infrastructure is created it should be of
international standard.  Besides, it is equally necessary to maintain it and providing timely renewals, repairs and
replacements which is generally not forthcoming.  (Para 12.3)
GRANT TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS
The Committee observes that the entire structure be it the creation of infrastructure, role of sports federations 
in the promotion of sports, selection of sportspersons  for national as well as international events smacks of
favoritism and not free from  corruption.  Even the role of sports federations does not seem to be objective.  The
Committee has been given to understand that there are cases where sports federation are in existence for name
sake and for receiving grants.  They are not concerned with the promotion of sports.  The Committee would like
to go into their working later.  (Para 13.3)
RURAL SPORTS PROGRAMME
The Committee recommends that Sports activities need to be encouraged keeping in mind the facilities available,
particularly in rural areas and the need to keep in youth fit and keep them engaged in sports activities. As far as
possible, disci­plines which did not require much equipment such as kho-kho, kabaddi, volleyball, track events
and long jump in athletics should be encouraged. Efforts should also be made to enlist the cooperation of  the
Ministry of Rural Development in the promotion of sports in rural areas. The Ministry should extend assistance
to the panchayats for the creation and maintenance of play fields and for providing sports equipment. (Para 14)
 SPECIAL AREA GAMES (SAG)
   The Committee recommends that efforts should also be made to tap potential for swimming in coastal areas,
and archery in tribal areas.(Para 15.2)
 ALL INDIA COUNCIL FOR PHYSICAL EDUCATION
The Committee welcomes the move of the Ministry for setting up a body of national stature and hopes that the
Council would fulfil the objects  for which it is proposed to be set up.  However, the Committee hopes that the
proposed council would not be a mere addition to the existing institutions and would  be a specialised body
professionally devoted to its objectives. (Para 16.2) 
 AFRO – ASIAN GAMES
            The Committee recommends that everything possible should be done to ensures that the games/events are
conducted successfully and no inconvenience is caused to visiting teams/delegations and the event goes down the
memory as a successful event.  Govt. should also try to have the dope test center duly accredited from IOC well
before the games start.          (Para 17.2)
 INCENTIVES FOR PROMOTION OF SPORTS ACTIVITIES/EVENTS
            The Committee was given to understand that National Welfare Fund for sportpersons was meant to
provide assistance to sportspersons living in indigent circumstances.  However,  it remained a fact that some of
our former sportpersons lived in abject poverty and were sometimes forced to do odd jobs, in order to eke a

living.  Except in cases of  a few popular sports, which attracted private sponsorship, the sports activities
remained devoid of any career opportunities for sportspersons. The Committee feels that  there is a need to have
definite incentives for sportspersons in the form of job opportunities. (Para 18.2)
 GRANTS FOR CREATION OF SPORTS INFRASTRUCTURE
The Committee reiterates the need for creation of adequate infrastructure and that too of an international
standard.  This should be thrown open to all and sundry individuals as well as groups on moderate charges.  This
would facilities the encouragement of sports.  (Para 19.2)
