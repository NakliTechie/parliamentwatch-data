increase, as explained by Secretary (Women & Child Development), in her ora! evidence tendered 
before the Committee, is mainly on account of World Bank assistance of Rs. 38 crores to ICDS and 
an increase in sanction for Mahila Samridhi Yojna. On all other schemes increase in allocations for 
1994-95 is only marginal. It is a matter of concern for the Committee that in the SAARC Decade of 
Girl Child and condition of women, there has not been adequate increase in the Budget allocations.

3. The Committee feels that gender inequality is one of the problems all over the world. Actual 
development cannot take place unless the Govt. takes initiative to remove this bias and guarantees 
equal status to women as enshrined in our Constitution. Committee is of the view that all out efforts 
should be made for greater involvement of women in the national development process by raising their 
status - social, economic, legal and political - at par with that of man. The Department of Women 
and Child Development has started a number of schemes for the development and welfare of women 
and children The Committee has deliberated only on some of the points relating to Demands for Grants 
(1994-95) which are enumerated in the following paragraphs along with the Committee's observations 
and recommendations. 
Women Welfare and Development 
(i) Rashtriya Mahila Kosh 
4. Rashtriya Mahila Kosh was set up in March, 1993 under the Registration of Societies Act, 
1869. With the main aim to help the poorest of the poor and assist women who are unable to utilize 
formal banking facilities but are in need of credit. For the purpose, a Corpus of Rs. 31 crores was 
sanctioned, to be utilised in a phased manner. 
5. The Committee observes that as against Rs. 31 crores sanctioned last year for this scheme, only 
a token amount of Rs. one lakh has been earmarked this year. It feels that unless 

schemes for the utilisation of sanctioned money are formulated well in time, setting up of such 
funds becomes infructuous. Therefore, it recommends that Department should, ensure that schemes 
are framed for prop er utilisation of the funds without delay. 
(ii) National Commission for Women and Commissioner for Women's Rights 
6. The Nation al Commission for Women was set up as a nodal agency to monitor the 
enforcement of lega l status of women, to review the existing legislation and suggest amendments 
and to liaise with the concerned Ministries, State Governments and NGOs, in order to create 
awareness and spread literacy. For this purpose, an amount of Rs. 1.75 crores in RE was 
provided in 1993-94, which has been increased to Rs. 2 crores in the Budget Estimates for 1994-
95. 
7. The Committee notes that there is substantial increase in the Budget provisions for the 
National Commission for Women for 1994-95, as compared to 1993-94. Govt. should ensur e full 
utilisation of the budg et of Rs. 2 crores by the Commission. 
8. The Committee recommends that Govt. should fulfil the assurance given to th e Parliament 
by creat ing the post of Commissioner for Women 's Rights and all consultations and other 
formalities be completed as early as possible. 
(iii) Hostels for working Women 
9. To protect young working women in urban areas from exposure to undesirable and anti­
social elements, financial assistance to the extent of 50% of the cost of land and 70% of the 
cost of building construct ion is provided to Voluntary Organisations. In 1993-94 Rs. 7.00 crores 
were provided which has bee n increased to Rs. 7.75 crores in the Budget for 1994-95. This 
year's target is to provide assistance to 60 hostles which would benefit about 3000 working 
women.

Hostels in the country, the Department could not achieve the physical targets during 1993-94, but 
ambit ious targets have been flXed for 1994-95. The Committee desires that effective steps should 
be taken by the Depar tment to achieve the targets set for 1994-95. 
12. As frequent complaints are received about mismanagement of the hostels rece1vmg grants 
and assistance from the Government the Committee is of the view that there is need for evolving 
some system of monitoring so as to control any sort of irregularities in these hostels. For this 
purpose, the Committee recommends that a Government representative be included on the Board 
of Management of each of such hostels.

should encourage construction of more such hostels not only through grants but also by allotment

(iv) Education for prevention of atrocities against Women 
14. The programme was sta rted in 1982 with the aim of creating public opinion against exploita tion 
of women and atrocities against them. This objective is sought to be achieved mainly through 
programmes, publicity and research work , as well as holdin g meetings, conferences, seminars , 
exhibitions, etc. 
15. Financial assistance extended in 1993-94 for this purpose was Rs. 35.00 lakhs and the same 
amount has been allocated during 1994-95. 
16. The Committee expresses its concern over the increasing numbers of cases of atrocities against 
women an d recommends that Govt. should conduct some review of these publicity programmes to find 
out whet her these are sufficient to create a significant impact on the public mind. 
17. The Committee recommends that the amount sanctioned for the year 1993-94 for legal aid in 
cases of atrocities against women should be increased. 
18. The Committee was informed of training and other awareness generation programmes 
undertaken by the Department. Production of legal literacy manuals in regi ona l languages and gender 
sensitization are part of this programmes. The Committee feels that such programmes should be 
extended to the grass root level also. 
(v) Indira Mahila Y ojna 
19. Indira Mahila Yojna, as originally conceived in 1991 and included in the President's Address of 
the year, has four basic components: (i) Awareness generation (ii) Income generat ion (iii) Convergence 
of inter-sectoral services; and (iv) Integration of field cadres of all related departments in the country. 
20. The Committee expresses its deep concern over the fact that though the scheme was announced 
in the President's Address in 1991, till this date it has not been operationalised. 
21. The Committee was informed by the Department that the original draft scheme for Indira 
Mahila Yojna contained components of awareness generation, income generation, integr ated system of 
delivery, inter-sectoral services and integration of field cadres of concerned Departm en ts, with an 
allotment of Rs. 1785 crorcs for the Eighth Plan. The concerned Departments could not, however, 
come to a consensus on integration of cadres and transfer of schemes from one Ministry to the other. 
Subsequently, there was a revised scheme to cover 200 blocks, with income generation and awareness 
generation components (Rs. 78 crores for the remainder of the 8th Plan.) But this also is yet to be 
approved by the Planning Commission. The Provision of Rs. 8 crores in the 1994-95 Budget may go into 
the World Bank-aided ICDS project, if the approval does not come in time. 
The Committee feels that such a big scheme should be implemented only when proper assessment of 
financial and infrastructural implications is made. Duplication of efforts should be avoided. whether a 
single-window delivery system would benefit women, in immensely varied circumstances, has also to be 
assessed. 
(vi) Mahila Samridhi Yojna 
22. This is a new scheme launched on 2nd October, 1993 to promote thrift, self-relia nce and certain 
amount of economic independence among rural women. It is implemented by the Depar t· of Posts 
through the network of 1.32 lakh post offices in rural areas. An amount of Rs. 10.5 cror cs \',as initially 
sanctioned for the scheme which has been increased to Rs. 19.89 crores in the Budgetar y orovis ions for 
1994-95. 
23. The Committee notes that significant increase in allocations for Mahila Samridhi Yojna has been 
made this year. The Comm.ittee, however, recommends that there should be proper monitoring to see 
that these funds are properly utilised. 
24. The Committee is of the view that various programmes under the Mahila Samridhi Yojna are 
implemented keeping in view the welfare of rural women, whereas poor women in urban areas , who are 
also in need of assistance, are not covered under it. The Committee recommends that requiremen ts of 
needy urban women belonging to the weaker sections of society should also be kept in mind while framing 
and implementing schemes for women's welfare. 

25. Committee also feels that there is not sufficient awareness among the rural people regarding 
the benefits of Mahila Samridhi Yojna. Secondly, the postmasters in the villages are not fully info.-med 
and trained about the scheme, and there is also dearth of forms. The need is, therefore, to train 
postmasters about the functioning of the scheme and to make the forms available so as to avoid 
inconvenience to the needy. 
26. There should also be a monthly review of the progress made under the scheme so as to take 
corrective measures wherever necessary. 
Nutrition and Child Development 
27. The Committee expresses its concern over the sharp decli~e in the Plan Budget estimates 
under the Head 2235 for Child Welfare. As against. Rs. 43.35 crores in 1993-94, it has gone down to 
Rs. 20.17 crores in 1994-95 and recommends restoration of the last year allocations. 
(i) Integrated Child Development Services (/CDS) 
28. The main thrust of ICDS programme is on the provision of Anganwadis in every village and 
urban slum areas. Generally, there is one Anganwadi for a population of 1000 in rural areas and 
urban slum areas and of about 700 in tribal area~, which is run by an Anganwadi worker, a female 
voluntary worker and a helper. By the end of 1993, 3120 ICDS projects were sanctioned which 
benefited about 1.94 crorc children and women. For the year 1994-95 an outlay of Rs. 426.00 crores 
has been approved for the ICDS and 200 new projects are likely to start. 
The Committee feels that a large portion of the increased outlay is likely to be absorbed in the 
increased honorarium to Anganwadi workers. Therefore, the Committee feels that the amount available 
for the projected expansion of the scheme to cover new areas seems to be insufficient. Of 5239 blocks, 
2119 block remains to be covered. At the rate of 200 projects per year, it will take another 10 years for 
all blocks to be covered. Thus is too slow. To expedite the process, any shortfall in the projected 
expansion by the end of the 8th Plan should be properly estimated and additional allocations made. 
29. The relative success of the ICDS scheme in improving child health and lowering IMR is due 
to its decentralized and popular structure. probably because of this, ICDS is being increasingly seen 
as an umberlla organisation within which other schemes could be incorporated. Thus, early childhood 
education is now a part of ICDS_. There is a new scheme for adolescent girls, creches and day care 
centres arc sought to be made a part of ICDS. Indira Mahila Yojana, in its revised form, will become 
a component of ICDS. Separate allocations have been proposed for these schemes, but the National 
Creche Fund has only been notified. Allocation of Rs. 8 crores for Indira Mahila Yojna has not yet 
been approved. Therefore, the Committee recommends that until and unless spearate additional 
allocations are obtained for these additional components, ICDS should concentrate on strengthening 
and expansion of its own structure with whatever allocations it has. The proposal to entrust a 
percentage of !CDS schemes to NGOs should be properly assessed before it is implemented, because in 
the absence of community-based monitoring arrangements, it would be difficult to ensure accountability 
of these NGOs. 
30. With the usc of ICDS infrastructure, a special scheme for adolescent girls has been 
formulated, mainly for those who have been out of schools, to meet their nutritional, educational and 
recreational needs. 
31. Regarding education of adolescent girls, the Committee recommends that there should be a 
scheme to help meritorious girls enabling them to pursue their studies further. 
32. In order to meet the requirement of trained personnel for implementing the programmes for 
pre-school children, the Bal Scvika training programme was started in 1961-62. There are 25 Bal 
Scvika training Centres and each centre is provided with a sum of Rs. 1,74,750/- to train 50 Bal 
Sevikas each year. 
33. The Committee observes that out of the three components of ICDS, namely executive, workers 
and recipients, two do not appear to be happy with the service. Therefore, monitoring system should be 
strengthened to ensure proper functioning of the ICDS Scheme to the satisfaclion of all its components. 

of the population through ICDS, as well as nutritional education. In this context, the Committee 
recommends revival of the scheme of supply of milk to the under-nourished school Children. 
44. The Committee also feels that, year after year, the focus of attention of the Deptt. keeps on 
changing from one scheme to another and there seems to be no long-term perspective. Therefore, the 
Committee recommends that instead of framing new schemes every year and letting the earlier ones to 
just continue, the Department should concentrate on some major schemes and work to achieve the 
targets fixed through suitable action plans. The Department was set up in 1985 but its strerigth has 
remained the same although many new schemes have been introduced during this period. The 
Committee feels that strengthening of Departmental infrastructure is necessary inorder to ensure the 
effectiveness of the schemes.
