REPORT
 
1.         The Central Institute of English and Foreign Languages University Bill, 2006 was introduced on the 20th March,
2006, in the Rajya Sabha and was referred to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Human
Resource Development on 27th March, 2006 for examination and report. 
 
2.         The Central Institute of English and Foreign Languages is currently functioning at Hyderabad offering courses
for the teaching/ study of English and major foreign languages.  It is a society registered under the Hyderabad Societies
Registration Act, 1950. In July 1973, it was conferred deemed university status under section 3 of the University Gants
Commission Act, 1956.
 
3.         This Bill seeks to accord the Central Institute of English and Foreign Languages at Hyderabad a Central
University status and dissolve the Institute as a society. 
 
4.         Statement of Objects and Reasons appended to the Bill justifies the need for converting this institute into a
Central University in the following manner: “With the advent of globalization and opening of world economies, the
mobility of people of all walks of life, across international boundaries, had phenomenally increased during the recent
years, resulting into added pressure on the institutions offering foreign language courses world over.   The
responsibilities of the Central Institute of English and Foreign Languages, Hyderabad have also increased manifold on
this count and it would require greater autonomy for discharging its added responsibilities.   At the same time, it needs to
be made more accountable and transparent in its functioning.  Both these requirements, may be met by conferring on it
the status of a full-fledged Central University”.
 
5.         During the course of deliberations on the Bill, apprehensions were voiced by some members of the Committee
that there appeared to be unjustified haste in according the central university status to this Institute. It was pointed our
that there may be any number of more reputed and better performing educational institutions in the country, more
deserving to get central status than this Institute.  These members felt that the objective could be better achieved by
strengthening/empowering the Departments of Foreign Languages in the existing institutions/universities.   Doubts were
raised that the proposed university might also go the same way as the Mahatma Gandhi Hindi Vishvavidyalaya has gone,
where even after a decade of its creation, it remains virtually a non-starter.
 
6.         The Committee is surprised to note that instead of there being any demand from any quarter/stakeholder for
making the Institute a Central University, this idea was mooted by the Central Government.  Not only this, no formal
inspection of the Institute- its academic and other infrastructural facilities has been carried out so far.
 
7.         The Committee sought clarifications on a number of issues connected with the functioning of the Institute and its
present status from the Department of Secondary and Higher Education.  Representatives of the Department also
appeared before the Committee and replied to various queries raised by the members. With the advent of globalization,
manifold increase in the responsibilities of the Institute, resultant need for expansion and diversification of its activities
and greater autonomy emerged as the main factors on which Committee held extensive discussions.  
 
8.         As regards the increased responsibility, the facts made available to the Committee indicated otherwise.  The
Committee found that there were only 300 regular students pursuing M.A., M. Phil & Ph.d from five Schools of studies
and 700 students were enrolled for different courses through distance education mode.  Besides that, the Institute was

also offering Part time 
Certificate/diploma/advanced diploma courses to about 650 students.  The Committee was given to understand that the
two regional Campuses of the Institute at Shillong and Lucknow were fully functional.  However, the Committee found
that although both the Campuses were set up in 1970s, they did not have their own buildings for academic,
administrative and hostel purposes.  The Committee also noted that large number of faculty positions were lying vacant-
44 at the main campus and 4 at the Lucknow campus. 
 
9.         The Committee was informed that over the years, the Institute had “grown in stature and had earned recognition
as a centre of excellence” in the study of English and major foreign languages.  The Department could, however, not
provide specific data/information, which could support this claim and provide an idea about the past performance of the
Institute.  Specific information indicating placement of the students passing out of the Institute also could not be made
available to the Committee by the Department, reason being that no formal study in this regard has been conducted so
far.   
10.       As regards the autonomy of the Institute, the Department argued that the CIEFL presently being a registered
society and an institution declared to be a deemed university under Section 3 of the UGC Act, was “susceptible to
erosion by the various authorities like Registrar of Societies, UGC and the Ministry of HRD.  Presently, the Institute had
to have prior approval of the UGC for starting any course, etc.  The Department contended that the Institute was
constrained tremendously by the procedures and guidelines laid down by the UGC.  The Bill, therefore, sought to
remove representation of the UGC from any authority of the proposed university and the VC will be appointed by the
President of India in his capacity as its Visitor.  The Department contended that the granting of central university status
would bring in systemic structural changes in the Institute with larger administrative and academic autonomy and
complete freedom from governmental control.
 
11.       The Committee then inquired about any specific case where the Institute could not take decision based on the
principles of autonomy.  The Committee further sought to know the structural bottlenecks that might have prevented
proper development of the Institute.  To this, the Department replied that any proceeding of the Institute may be annulled
by the Central Government, if it is not deemed to be in conformity with the rules and bye-laws of the Society.  It was,
however, argued that once the Institute was accorded the status of a central university, its autonomy would be
safeguarded in terms of the statutory provisions of the Act, where the Central Government or the UGC will not have any
say. 
 
12.       The Committee found these arguments/justifications given by the Department quite unconvincing. The
contention of the Department clearly implies that within UGC framework, there is no scope for autonomy and that UGC
works as road-block to development of educational institutions.  It further implies that the real autonomy and
development of an educational institution is possible only if it were a Central university.  The question arises: are the
State universities like Bombay University, Kolkata University and others are functioning without autonomy and
therefore, are not progressing?  The Committee finds this argument not only bizarre but also fraught with grave
implications.  It also raises question about the existing framework as well as functioning of the UGC.  It also underlines
the need for revisiting the whole concept/system of ‘deemed university’ because CIEFL is said to be ailing mainly due to
the UGC and the deemed university status and therefore, it needed no less than Central university status.          
 
13.       The Committee, despite all the questions and reservations on the aspects mentioned in preceding paragraphs,
would like to emphasize that it is not against empowering and strengthening an educational institution financially and
otherwise.  The Committee is aware of the importance of English and other foreign languages in the emerging scenario
at the global level.  India, today has become a force to reckon with in areas like science, information and communication
technology, medicine, space, nuclear energy, etc.  Apart from their/talent this can be upto a large extent attributed to the
compatibility of our young boys and girls with English and other languages.   In principle, therefore, the Committee
agrees with the strengthening of any institution promoting teaching and learning of English and foreign languages. 
Therefore, the Committee after expressing these sentiments suggests following amendments in the Bill:-
 
 Clause 1 (1)
14.       The Committee notes that the Act is named “The Central Institute of English and Foreign Languages University
Act, 2006”.  The Committee, however, feels that the use of words ‘institute’ and ‘university’ together should be
avoided.  Instead it would be more appropriate to call the proposed Act in Clause 1(1) as “The English and Foreign

Languages University Act, 2006”.
 
Clause 3(1)
15.       In view of its recommendations for clause 1(1), the Committee proposes that the name of the proposed University
be named “English and Foreign Languages University”.
 
Clause 6(viii)
16.       Clause 6 relates to Powers of the University.  As per clause 6 (viii) the University shall have the power to appoint
persons working in any other University or organization as teachers of the University for a specified period.
 
17.       The Committee is of the considered opinion that the word “organization” in this clause leaves enough scope for
including extraneous agencies/bodies as well. The Department clarified that the word ‘organization’ generally meant “a
teaching or a research institution, other than a university”.  The Committee feels that it needs to specified.  It therefore,
recommends that the word ‘organisation’ may be substituted by the words ‘educational institution’ so as to remove any
ambiguity about the nature of the organization.
 
Clause 30(1)(i) 
18.       Clause 30 deals with power to make Ordinances. The Committee finds that this sub-clause provides for making
special arrangements “for residence, discipline and teaching of women students.”  The Committee is of the considered
opinion that while this is valid in terms of  residence and specific courses for women students, the word “discipline”  in
this clause appears to be unnecessary and discriminatory.  Accordingly, the Committee recommends that the words
‘discipline and’ be deleted from line 12 of clause 30 (1) (i) of the Bill.
 
Clause 30(1)(j)
19.       This Clause provides for cooperation and collaboration by the proposed University apart from other universities
with “other agencies”. The Committee feels that there is a possibility of considering agencies, which are private and
might be for profit.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the words ‘not for profit’ may be added before the word
‘agencies’ in page 10, line 15 of Clause 30 (1) (j).
 
 Clause 35(3)  
20.       Clause 35 pertains to conditions of service of employees. According to sub-clause (3) of this Clause, the decision
of the Tribunal of Arbitration shall be final and no suit shall lie in any civil court in respect of the matters decided by it. 
This sub-clause gives an impression that the employees were debarred from all other judicial remedies against the
decision of the Tribunal.  The Committee is aware that no Act can debar the remedies available under Articles 226 and
32 of the Constitution.  The Bill also doesn’t intend to do this.  The Committee, however, recommends that the following
Explanation may be added in the said sub-clause for removing the erroneous impression in this regard.
             “This does not preclude the employees from availing the remedies provided for under Articles 226 & 32 of the
Constitution.”
 
Clause 39
21.       This Clause provides for the final authority in respect of any question regarding election or appointment of a
member of any authority or other body of the University.  The Committee notes that the Bill nowhere mentions about an
elected membership of an authority of the University.  The Committee is of considered opinion that ideally the
composition/membership of these bodies should have been provided in the Statute itself as was done in the case of
Allahabad University. In view of the fact that the composition of authorities of the university would be subsequently
decided by the university bodies, the Committee wishes to emphasise the need for having elected elements in all the
bodies/authority of the university.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that suitable mention for the purpose be
made in the Bill at appropriate places.  The Committee further recommends that an enabling provision must be provided
in the Act itself to ensure democratization in true spirit.
Clause 46
22.       Clause 46 pertains to Transitional Provisions. The Committee is of the considered opinion that since the first
officers and authorities of the university is to be appointed or nominated by the Central Government, their term of offices
as provided in transitional provisions should not be long enough. The Committee notes that in some cases it is five years
and in others it is three years.  It, therefore, recommends that the term of office of the first Chancellor, the first Vice-

THE SCHEDULE
 
Clause 6 and 7 (b), (c) and (d) of the Statutes
23.       The Committee notes that these provision of the Statute provided in the Schedule mention about College
Development Council but this body is not amongst the authorities of the university.  The Department clarified that it was
mentioned ‘erroneously’.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that name of this body be deleted from the above
clauses.
 
Clause 17 of the Statutes
24.       The Committee notes that in case of the ‘Finance Committee’, while its composition has been given, there is no
representation from the University Court.  The Committee opines that University Court being the largest body should
have representation in the Finance Committee. The Committee, therefore, recommends that  a new sub-clause “atleast
one person to be nominated by the Court” may be added immediately after line 11 of Clause 17 of the Statutes on page
22 of the Bill.
 
Clause 37 of Statutes
25.       The Committee is of considered opinion that the Act must provide for elected representation of students, teachers
and non-teaching staff in all authoritative bodies of the University and ensure their right to form associations.  However,
the Committee found no such provision for students.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the words ‘two
elected representatives of students” should find place in the constitution of the Student Council and Clause 37 be
redrafted accordingly.
 
26.       The Committee adopts the remaining clauses of the Bill without any amendments.
27.       The enacting formula and the title are adopted with consequential changes.
28.       The Committee recommends that the Bill may be passed after incorporating the amendment additions suggested
by it. 
29.       The Committee would like the Department to submit a note with reasons on the recommendations/suggestions,
which could not be incorporated in the Bill.

2.         The Committee took up for consideration the draft report on “The Central Institute of English and Foreign
Languages University Bill, 2006”.  The Committee suggested amendments/deletion in the Clauses 1(1), 3(1), 6(viii),
30(1)(j), 35(3), 39 and 46 of the Bill and Clauses 6, 7(b), (c), and (d), 17 and 37 of the Statutes contained in the Schedule
of the Bill. The Members also sought clarifications from the representatives of the Department to which they replied.  
The Committee, thereafter, adopted the draft report with some modifications.
3.         The Committee authorized the Chairman to fix a suitable date to present/lay the Report in the monsoon session of
Parliament and also nominate members from both the houses to present/lay the report.
4.         The Committee then adjourned at 12.50 p.m.
