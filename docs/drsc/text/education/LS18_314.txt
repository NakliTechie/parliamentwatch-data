REPORT 
 
I INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Ministry of Women and Child Development is the apex body of Government of India 
for formulation and administration of regulations and laws related to women and child 
development. It came into existence as a separate Ministry w.e.f  30th January, 2006.  It has the 
nodal responsibility to advance the rights and concerns of women and children who constitute 67.7 
per cent of the country’s population as per 2011 census.  The Ministry was constituted with the 
prime intention of addressing gaps in state action  for women and children and for promoting inter -
ministerial and inter-sectoral convergence to create gender equality and child -centred legislations, 
policies and programmes.  The function of the Ministry is to promote the empowerment and 
protection of women and children and to ensure their equitable and wholesome development.  
1.2 The role of the Ministry includes policy formulation, programme implementation, 
coordination with other sectors, child and gender budgeting, data management, training and 
capacity building, reporting and monitoring on the status of women and children and their rights. 
The Ministry is the prime mover of inclusive programmes, policies, schemes and legislations for 
women and children and stands solely for securing the best interest of  women and children.  
Promoting social and economic empowerment of women and ensuring survival, development, care 
and protection of children drive the Ministry’s agenda.  Principles of equity, justice and non -
discrimination guide the Ministry’s actions concerning women and children.  
1.3 The Secretary, Ministry of Women and Child Development, in his presentation before the 
Committee on the 13 th February, 2020 gave an overview of various programmes/schemes of the 
Ministry.  He stated that the Government attaches a lot of importance to the welfare of women and 
children, in accordance with which the budgetary allocations and spending of the Ministry have 
gone up significantly in the last three years.   It was also submitted that there has been gradual 
increase in the budgetary provisions since the year 2017 -18 and it has risen from Rs. 22095 crore 
in 2017-18 to Rs. 24700 and Rs 29165 crore in 2018 -19 and 2019 -20 respectively.  He informed 
the Committee that the Ministry has been allocated Rs. 30,007.10 crore in BE 2020-21 against the 
projected demand of Rs. 33,720.00 crore.  Emphasising on the need for increased outlay, he stated 
that for the past five years, the Ministry’s budget as percentage of GOI budget has remained 
unchanged at around one per cent only. 

8 
 
1.4 The Committee gathered from the presentation that the entire schemes/programmes of the 
Ministry are divided into three broad categories i.e. (I) Umbrella Integrated Child Development 
Services (ICDS) Scheme covering Anganwadi Services, Poshan Abhiyaan, Scheme for Adolescent 
Girls (SAG), National Creche Scheme, Pradhan Mantra Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) and 
Child Protection Services (CPS); (II) Women Empowerment and Protection (WEP) Schemes 
covering Beti Bachao Beti Pradhao (BBBP), One Stop Centre (OSC), Wo men Helpline (WHL), 
Mahila Police Volunteer, Working Women Hostels, Swadhar Greh, Ujjawala and Mahila Shakti 
Kendra; and (III) Autonomous Bodies such as Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA), 
National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) and National Commission for 
Women (NCW) etc.

2.7 Taking into account the details of percentage share of budget allocation vis -a-vis 
Government of India Budget, overall projected demands and actual allocations, the 
Committee observes that the Ministry of Women and Child Development’s budget 
remaining unchanged at one per cent of GOI budget  since the last five years is a cause of 
serious concern and introspection.   Women and children both in urban and rural India, 
inspite of the steady progress in many fields, are still largely marginalised, unserved and 
unprotected lagging far behind in hu man and social development.  The Ministry of Women 
and Child Development being endowed with the responsibility of creating an enabling 
environment for both women and children so that they have access to fundamental services 
such as pre -school non formal ed ucation, health, nutrition and protection etc. should have 
greater share of GOI budget.  Therefore, the Committee, recommends, that there is an 
urgent need to increase the percentage share of budget of the Ministry of Women and Child 
Development in the GOI  budget to meet the needs and requirements of growing population 
of women and children. So that schemes and programmes being run for them do not suffer 
any shortfall in reasources. 
2.8 The Committee further recommends that the gap between the projected dem and of 
the Ministry for each of its programmes and the actual allocations needs to be bridged 
completely.  The Committee understands that when the Ministry raises a demand they have 
an assessment of targets and outreach of beneficiaries.  Any gap would mea n either targets 
are being compromised or intended beneficiaries are not being covered.  Both the things 
would defeat the purpose of the schemes meant for the benefit of the most vulnerable section 
of the society i.e. women and children.  In this backgroun d, the Committee recommends the

demands met.

discrimination at every level. Then, there are manifold instances of violence and abuse 
against them requiring not only women centric policy formulations but also sustainable 
framework for protection.  In this context, it is imperative that women e mpowerment and 
protection schemes also given equal attention and priority, The Committee accordingly, 
recommends greater allocation, full utilisation and focussed implementation of various 
schemes related to women.  The Committee also recommends universali zation of women

(ICDS) 
III ANGANWADI SERVICES 
 
3.1 The Anganwadi Services Scheme is one of the flagship programmes of the Government of 
India and represents one of the world’s largest and unique programmes for early childhood care 
and development.   The scheme aims to improve the nutritional and health s tatus of children in the 
age group of 0 -6 years, pregnant women and lactating mothers and reduce the incidences of 
mortality, morbidity and malnutrition.   The scheme offers a package of six services viz. (i) 
supplementary nutrition; (ii) pre -school non-formal education; (iii) nutrition and health education; 
(iv) immunisation; (v) health check -up and (vi) referral services provided through a network of 
Anganwadi centres.   Immunisation, health check -up and referral services are related to health and 
are pro vided through National Rural Health Mission (NRHM) and public health infrastructure.   

15 
 
Anganwadi service scheme operates through a network of 7075 fully operational projects.   As on 
31.12.2019 out of 1399697 sanctioned anganwadi centres 1379158 anganwadi centres are 
operational. 
 
3.2 As per the annual report of the Ministry, construction of AWC buildings is being provided 
in convergence with the Ministries of Rural Development, Panchayati Raj and Women and Child 
Development for which revised guidelines dat ed 17 th February, 2016 have been issued.   Under 
this convergence 4 lakh AWC buildings are to be constructed across the country.   The Ministry 
has also issued directions dated 14 th May, 2019 to all States/UTs to examine the status of 
anganwadi centres vis-a-vis the infrastructure available in the primary schools and take all 
necessary steps to relocate the AWCs accordingly.   The States/UTs to ensure that the schools in 
which AWCs are to be co -located, should be present in the same habitation where the AWC  was 
previously present.

etc.   Among the remedial steps, the Ministry suggested the following steps:- 
- Holding periodical re view meetings, undertaking inspections, issuing guidelines, 
reviewing their programme from time to time especially at the time of approving their

8.1 Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (BBBP) was launched by the Prime Minister on 22 nd January,

AUTONOMOUS BODY 
XIV CENTRAL ADOPTION RESOURCE AGENCY (CARA) 
14.1 The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA), which was an Autonomous Body of 
the Ministry of Women and Child Developme nt, has now become a statutory body as per the 
provisions of Section 68, Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.  It 
functions as a nodal body at National level on adoption related matters of Indian children.  The 
mandate of CARA is to promote in-country adoptions in coordination with state agencies, regulate 
inter-country adoptions, frame regulations on adoption and to carry out the functions of the of the 
Central Authority under the Hague  Convention on Protection for Children and Co -operation in 
respect of inter-country adoption.  CARA has framed the Adoption Regulations 2017 which have 
been operationalized w.e.f 16th January, 2017.  It has also been informed that adoption procedure 
has further been simplified as it is entirely facilitated through on -line system.  CARA has engaged 
Helpline staff (toll free) to assist all prospective adoptive parents.  One counselling c entre at 
CARA is operational and there is a proposal  to extend it to the State and district level to ensure 
that Prospective Adoptive Parents (PAPs) have excess to counselling help. 
14.2 The Committee was informed about the constraints faced by CARA as we ll as prospective 
parents in (Inter-country) adoption issues : 
(i) There is a long waiting list of registered PAPs in both in -country and inter-country 
and there are few children legally free and available for adoption. 
(ii) The Specialised Adoption Agenci es (SAAs) are often found ill -equipped to handle 
inter-country adoption cases and uploading of documents on CARINGS. 
(iii) It has been observed that the Advocates appointed by the SAAs are not fully 
acquainted with the adoption procedures laid in the JJ Ac t 2015 and Adoption Regulations 
2017.  Hence, filing of adoption petitions gets hampered. 
(iv) There is often long delay in the disposal of adoption orders beyong the stipulated 
period of two months resulting in grievances of PAPs. 
(v) Specialised Adoption  Agencies (SAAs) have intimated that due to the elaborate 
documentation process involved in Inter -country adoption (notarization/appostile etc.), 
filing of the adoption petition (within 10 days by the SAA after NOC is received) gets 
delayed. 

44 
 
(vi) At times, the language becomes a barrier between SAAs and PAPs in case of Inter -
country adoptions. 
14.3 The Committee also took note of the steps proposed to be taken to make the adoption 
procedure short and simple:- 
(i) Developing linkages with the Child Care Institut ions (CCI) to bring in the 
available children into the fold of adoption (Section 66(2) of JJ Act 2015 & 
regulation 58 of Adoption Regulation 2017). 
(ii) Regular Inspections for monitoring and capacitating the agencies (Section 
54 of JJ Act 2015 & regulation 26 of Adoption Regulations 2017). 
(iii) Grievance Portal to address the grievances. 
(iv) To upgrade CARINGS into a Real Time Monitoring System. 
(v) CARA is organising various Awareness and Capacity Building Programmes 
for all the stakeholders including Judiciary. 
(vi) Meetings with the Judiciary for addressing delays in the adoption cases. 
(vii) Amendment in JJ Act, 2015 being proposed to finalize the adoption process 
at the level of District Magistrate.
