INTRODUCTION
1.1. India with just 2.4% of the world landmass constitutes 16% of the present world population. According to the
1998 United Nations Fund Population Activities (UNFPA) - estimates, population of India is increasing at the rate of
1.55 crores per year. And on current trends, in 2050 India will become the most populous country with population of
152 crores. Such an alarming scenario has big implications for the problem of unemployment and state of environment
in the country.
1.2. The current high population growth rate is mainly due to the three major factors as enumerated below:
1. large size of the population in the reproductive age group;
2. higher fertility due to unmet need for contraception; and
3. high wanted fertility due to prevailing high Infant Mortality (IMR).
The All India average annual exponential growth rate during the last decade has been of the order of 2.14% which is
marginally lower than 2.22% during the previous decade. As per the latest 1996 SRS estimates, it is 1.85%, but it is
still very high when compared with the developed nations of the world.
1.3. The main objective of the Family Welfare Programme since beginning has been to stabilise population of the
country at a level consistent with the requirements of national development. The objective is pursued by addressing
contraception issues, maternal health issues, child survival issues and by encouraging citizens through IEC to use
these services.

1.5. The Secretary informed the Committee that after critically reviewing the Family Welfare Programme in the
Eighth Plan and their outcome, some path breaking changes have been introduced and new programmes in the Ninth
Plan have also been initiated. During the year 1998-99, some of the programmes were operationalised by the
Department.
1.6. The Secretary stated that apprehensions were expressed in many quarters that giving up of contraceptive targets
would result in a sharp decline in the performance of the population programme. Contrary to such negative
predictions, after the initial slump, the overall performance, under all the family planning methods for the period
April-January, in both 1997-98 and 1998-99, has been registering considerable improvement, i.e. 4% in Sterilisation;
1.3% in IUD insertions, 8.3% in condom use and 3.8% in use of oral pills. There is thus 5.7% increase in all family
planning methods as compared to last year. The Committee presumes that this increase represents the
reproductive age group of couples only. Otherwise this increase has no relevance. Another encouraging factor
observed is that improvement has been taking place in both sterilisation and spacing methods, the Secretary claimed.
1.7. Five major States, i.e. Assam, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Bihar have also shown an
improvement of 24.2%, 25%, 31%, 11% and 1.6%, respectively in sterilisation over last year. In IUD, Assam, Bihar
and Uttar Pradesh have shown an improvement of 48.2%, 11.8% and 7.5%, respectively. Similar improvements were
noticed in most of the States in condom use, the Secretary added.
1.8. The Committee observes that U.P. has the highest birth rate, i.e. 33.5 among the States closely followed by

II. NINTH PLAN
2.1. The goal to be achieved by 2002 A.D. (end of Ninth Five Year Plan) are:-
1. Reduction of Total Fertility Rate to 2.6-2.9 from the existing 3.5;
2. Reduction of birth rate to 23-24 per thousand population and infant mortality rate to 50-56 against the
current rate of 72 (1996); and
3. Increasing the couple protection rate to 51-60% against the current figure of 45.4% (March, 1997).
2.2. Underlining the Ninth Plan strategy, the Secretary stated that it seeks to achieve population stabilisation through a
three-pronged effort, easy availability of and education on contraceptive choices and improvement in the health status
of women and children.
2.3. The Committee notes that for the Ninth Plan, an outlay of Rs.16,750.36 crore of Domestic Budgetary Support
(DBS) and Rs.5296.58 crore of External Assistance (EAP) for implementing the Family Welfare Programme was
proposed to the Planning Commission. Out of DBS of Rs.16,750.36 crore, Rs.10,270 crore was planned as the
requirement of the ongoing activities of the Department and Reproductive and Child Health Programme. Another
minimum of Rs.2000 crore was required for strengthening some of the key programmes. Thus, to achieve the targets
set on the domestic front, Rs.12,270 crore is the minimum requirement of DBS during IX Plan. Against this, the
Planning Commission has indicated a DBS of Rs.12000.00 crore. The corresponding EAP allocation of Rs.3717.00
crore is also far less than the amount projected.
III. BUDGET ALLOCATION
3.1. The Committee notes that the allocation to the Department has been substantially increased from Rs.2510.51
crore in B.E. 1998-99 to Rs.2940.60 crore in the current year. Out of Rs.2940.60 crore, Rs.2920.00 crore, form the
plan component and Rs.20.60 crore is the non-plan component.
3.2. Commenting on the allocation made to the Department, the Secretary, while deposing before the Committee
stated that underfunding has always been a major problem in the family welfare sector. Family Welfare Programme
being a 100 percent centrally funded programme, one result of underfunding has been the Department’s inability to
pay arrears claimed by the State Governments. Due to chronic resource crunch it has been impossible for the
Department to launch many new programmes. The Department has been able to bridge only certain glaring gaps in the
funding of the existing programmes.
3.3. Considering the severity of the problem of arrears and damage it was causing to the Family Welfare Programme,
the entire additionality in 1998-99 has been utilised to address to this most pressing problem. In normal circumstances,
the sizeable increase in the allocation for 1999-2000 would have solved about 75% of the problem of arrears for future
years. However, as per estimates, requirement on account of arrears would be around Rs.1360 crore in 1999-2000,
against which a provision of Rs.1291 crore has been made. Thus, though the size of arrears has been reduced to a
considerable amount due to the Department’s consistent efforts, yet the States’ requiremnt was likely to remain unmet
by another Rs.200 crore which was mainly on account of recommendations of the Fifth Pay Commission, it was
clarified.
3.4. The Committee notes the steps taken by the Department in this regard. The Committee strongly feels that
the Non-settlement of arrear claims is adversely affecting the Family Welfare Programme. Settlement of arrear
claims would result in better implementation of the programme. The Committee is, therefore, of the opinion

IV. RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES
4.1. Under the head `Rural Family Welfare Services’, the allocation has been raised from Rs.605 crore on 1998-
99 to Rs.875 crore in 1999-2000. Out of this, a provision of Rs.350 crores has been proposed for
continuation/maintenance of 5435 Rural Family Welfare Centres which have been merged with the Primary
Health Centres (PHCs) after 1980. An allocation of Rs.525 crores has been proposed for 97,557 Rural Sub-
Centres funded under the national Family Welfare Programme.
4.2. To a question, as to in what manner, the additional allocation was proposed to be utilised, the Department
submitted that the whole provision was for maintenance of RFW Centres and Sub-Centres which were earlier
being inadequately provided. As per the Audited Statements of Expenditure received from the State AG
Offices, the requirement for Rural Family Welfare Services in the existing pay scales work out to Rs.980 crore,
against which a provision of only Rs.875 crore has been made. The requirement will further hike once the
States implement the recommendations of the Fifth Pay Commission, the Committee was informed.
4.3. The Committee is of the strong opinion that the Rural Family Welfare Centres and Sub-Centres are
meant for the effective implementation of the Family Welfare Programme in rural areas where need is
the most. However, the Committee is given to understand that even in the existing centres/sub-centres
required facilities are not available. If such centres are not properly maintained for want of funds, it will
result in a severe set back for the programme as more than half of the population of our country lives in
rural areas. The Committee, therefore, desires that funds as required for these centres may be provided
at the RE stage.
4.4. To a question as to how many rural sub-centres were opened during the year, the Department stated that
389 sub-centres were opened during 1997-98 and 201 centres had been opened during the period 1
st April, 1998
to 31st January, 1999. In view of the fact that the shortfall in the number of rural sub-centres which was 7,727
as per 1991 census, would reach the figure of 22,987 by the end of Ninth Plan, the Committee feels that
opening of these few centres is not going to make much of a difference. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its
recommendation made last year that the Department should urge upon the States to open fixed number of
centres each year and to set apart some provision in their Budget for the purpose.
4.5. To another question about the States which have identified opening of sub-centres as a priority within the
seven items identified under the Basic Minimum Services Programme (BMS), being funded out of the
Additional Central Assistance being made available to the States/UTs by the Planning Commission, the
Department informed that the States of Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Maharashtra have identified
opening of sub-centres as priority within the seven BMS items. Concerned State Secretaries have been
requested to make adequate provision for opening of sub-centres under BMS, it was added.
4.6. Considering the severity of the problem, the Committee feels that the Department should persuade
all the states to give priority to opening of rural sub-centres alongwith required facilities among the seven
items under BMS and to allot reasonable share of funds therefor.

VI. TRAINING OF AUXILIARY NURSE MIDWIVES/LADY HEALTH VISITORS
6.1. An allocation of Rs.39.40 crore has been provided during the current year. The amount allocated under the
scheme is towards the maintenance of the existing ANM Training Schools on the basis of approved pattern of
assistance. There are at present 408 ANM (Female Health Workers) Training Schools with an admission
capacity of 16000. The Committee notes that against 1,59,777 sanctioned posts of ANMs, there are 1,34,112
ANMs only. Against the required posts of 22,962 LHVs, only 19,071 LHVs are in position.
6.2. When Committee sought to know the names of the states where the maximum number of posts were
vacant, the Department stated that maximum number of posts were vacant in the states of Bihar (2500), Uttar
Pradesh (1016), Karnataka (1006) and Andhra Pradesh (561) in respect of ANMs and in the states of Uttar
Pradesh (473), Tamil Nadu (463), Madhya Pradesh (337), Bihar (335), Karnataka (289), Assam (247) and
Gujarat (200) in respect of LHVs. The Committee believes that this problem persists in other States too.
6.3. The Committee notes with dismay that inspite of having the required number of trained personnel, a
very large number of posts are lying vacant in different States. ANMs and LHVs are the key
functionaries of the Primary Health Centres/Rural Family Welfare Centres/Health Posts and keeping
such important posts vacant for a long time is adversely affecting the quality of services provided at the
field level. The Committee feels that the matter needs urgent attention, if required, at the political level,
particularly because in some of these States, Family Welfare measures have failed to achieve the desired
results.

VIII. NO-SCALPEL VASECTOMY PROJECT
1. The Committee notes that to promote male participation in the Family Welfare Programme, a UNFPA No-
Scalpel Vasectomy Project is being implemented in the country under which 1500 medical personnel are to
be trained. A total of 214 doctors have been trained so far and 23,829 acceptors have undergone No-
Scalpel Vasectomy Operation. The Committee was also given to understand that No-Scalpel Vasectomy is
one of the most effective contraceptive methods for males with practically no side effects/complications.
2. The Department further submitted that NSV training was held in the States of A.P., Orissa, Maharashtra,
Haryana, U.P., T.N., West Bengal, Kerala, Sikkim, H.P. and Rajasthan and the feedback was good. In A.P.,
especially in Karim Nagar District, there has been a reversal of trend from female sterilisation to male
sterilisation.
3. The Committee notes that neither the time-limit within which the target for training of 1,500 medical
personnel has been indicated nor the period until which the UNFPA project will be financed has
been given. So far only 214 medical personnel have been trained against a target of 1500. The
Committee is of the view that if desired results are to be obtained, the training programme should be
completed at the earliest.
IX INFORMATION, EDUCATION AND COMMUNICATION
9.1. The Information, Education and Communication (IEC) component of National Family Welfare Programme is
mainly to have an effective communication strategy to create an awareness among the masses about the means and
measures of the Programme, educate them about the perils of overpopulation and motivate and persuade them to adopt
small family norm, using all possible channels of media.
9.2. The allocation for Information, Education and Communication (IEC), has been marginally increased from
Rs.29.70 crores last year to Rs.32.95 crore this year. This is in addition to the provision of Rs.88.80 crore made
available under RCH for IEC activities.
9.3. To a specific query as to whether there exists any mechanism to assess the impact of IEC activities on the various
Family Welfare Programmes being implemented in the country, the Department stated that the ongoing Second
National Family Health Survey will throw a great deal of light on impact of IEC activities, on quality, reach and
utilisation of Family Welfare Services and the resultant impact. A system of annual District Rapid Household survey
has also been initiated which will also monitor the effectiveness of IEC campaign. Besides that, evaluation of special
programmes and schemes like PPI, Mahila Swasthya Sangh etc. are also being taken up by the Department
occasionally. The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Department in this regard.
9.4. The Committee also notes that in order to improve the awareness of the general public towards RCH and
population issues, as one of the IEC activity, the Department plans to have 5-10 big hoardings in 13 towns and the
messages in the hoardings will be replaced quarterly. The Committee feels that such a measure will be too
insignificant in view of the vastness of our country. The Committee is of the view that instead of a centralised
initiative, this strategy can be worked out in States particularly where Family Welfare measures are not
reaching the public at large due to one reason or the other, with active coordination between State
Governments and Family Welfare functionaries-both Government and NGOs.
9.5. On the IEC activities, the Secretary stated that during 1998-99, the Department tried to reinvigorate the IEC
campaign by professionalising a large part of the national and State-level IEC campaign through audio/visual media,
while local specific IEC activities were left to the district set up. Private professional agencies were used under the
World Bank funded RCH programme, for telecasting and broadcasting spots and inter-active panel discussions on
various RCH issues.
9.6. Another effort in this direction was to involve the community in IEC activities by launching a programme for

IV. DISTRIBUTION OF CONTRACEPTIVES
COMMERCIAL DISTRIBUTION
10.1. The allocation for commercial distribution of contraceptives has been steeply increased from Rs.80 crore last
year to Rs.140 crore this year. Asked how this steep increase is proposed to be utilised, the Department submitted that
the additional allocation is for strengthening of social marketing in rural areas through NGOs.
FREE DISTRIBUTION
10.2. The allocation for `Free Distribution of Conventional Contraceptives’ has been increased from Rs.92 crore last
year to Rs.107.50 crore this year. To a specific query as to the manner of utilisation of funds and about the areas of
coverage, the Department stated that under this scheme, conventional contraceptives are supplied through all
PHCs/ANMs in the country since a long time and hence no area is left uncovered. The marginal enhancement in the
allocation is because of more requirement projected by the States and also due to the increase in the cost of
contraceptives and better packaging specifications for condoms.
10.3. The Committee notes that budget allocation on both free and commercial distribution of contraceptives has not
been fully utilised during the period 1996-99 as the expenditure is dependent on the demand for the items made by the
State Governments. The Committee is constrained to note that the contraception coverage as on 31
st March,
1998 for condoms was only 10.1% of the eligible males and that for oral pills it was only 3.8% of the eligible
females. The Committee feels that for stable population, this needs to be increased many times in the next few
months. The Committee emphasises that priority should be accorded to the identification of uncovered areas
particularly in States lagging behind and subsequent distribution of contraceptives in active coordination with
local community and Panchayati Raj Institutions.
10.4. The Committee was given to understand that free distribution of contraceptives has already reached a
saturation point and it is not preferred by the Department due to lot of wastage involved. The Committee,
therefore, fails to understand the reasons for increase in the allocation for free distribution of contraceptives in
1999-2000.

XIV. REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH PROGRAMME
14.1. For improving the effectiveness of Family Welfare Programme, the programme for mother and child care was
reorganized in 1997 in the form of Reproductive and Child Health (RCH) Programme. The programme aims to meet
the unmet needs of Family Welfare Programme by improving health status of women and children, especially the poor
and undeserved, by reducing Infant, Child and Maternal mortality and morbidity through:-
a. Sustaining and strengthening the ongoing Child Survival and Safe Motherhood Programme to ensure the
essential services as per package of services;
b. Improve the performance of the Family Welfare system in implementing policy change;
c. Strengthen the quality and effectiveness of the services under essential package of Reproductive and Child
Health Services; and
d. Improve project implementation through the application of performance-based criteria for continued
funding.
14.2. The Department stated that the allocation for the project in BE 1998-99 was Rs.758 crore which was decreased

XV. PULSE POLIO
15.1. Briefing the Committee about Pulse Polio campaign, the Secretary stated that in 1998-99, in order to
achieve the WHO mandated global goal of reaching polio eradication by 2000 A.D., the Government has
intensified the Pulse Polio Immunisation Programme. Even though more than 100 per cent was reported
coverage every year, it was found that about 8% of children were still left out. Therefore, the Government made
special efforts by supplementing the booth immunisation with house to house search and also through
surveillance efforts. The Cabinet has also approved a proposal for launching additional rounds covering the
whole States of U.P., Bihar, M.P. and Rajasthan as the performance of these States had come down
considerably, the Department informed.
15.2. The Committee hopes that by these measures the Department will be able to achieve zero incidence
of poliomyelitis by the end of the millennium.
XVI. NEW INITIATIVES
16.1. Elaborating on the new initiatives taken the Secretary stated that the Department has launched a scheme for
setting up drug storage centres on a regional basis in all major States and to improve the logistics of supply from the
storage point to the health institutions. The proposal which is being jointly funded by the World Bank, European
Commission and UNFPA is currently under circulation for consideration in the EFC. The Committee hopes the EFC
will clear the project at the earliest.
16.2. Among the other new initiatives taken is the launching of Urban Measles Campaign. A special drive for
elimination of Neo Natal Tetanus has been also launched in Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh. The Government proposes
to intensify these campaigns in the coming year. The Committee hopes these initiatives will yield the desired
results.

XVII. COLD CHAIN EQUIPMENTS
17.1. A large cold chain system has been established under the immunisation programme since its inception which
consists of a series of transportation and storage facilities for carrying vaccines from the manufacturers to the
beneficiaries. The equipments being used for storage, distribution and transportation of the vaccines are walk-in-
Coolers (WIC), Walk-in-Freezers (WIF), Ice-lined refrigerators, Deep Freezers, Cold Boxes, Vaccine carriers and day
carriers and Refrigerated vans and Vaccine delivery vans.
17.2. The Department submitted that new equipments are to be provided to PHCs and CHCs which have not been
provided so far. In addition, equipments which have broken down or require frequent repair need to be replaced.
Further, additional population to the extent it has grown since 1985 will need additional storage facilities for vaccines.
A sum of Rs.50 crores has been provided for procurement and replacement of Cold Chain Equipments this year.
17.3. The Committee feels that priority may be given for the replacement of existing equipments and thereafter
new equipments may be provided.

Commission have observed in this regard as under:-
"Although the Tenth Finance Commission have gone by their terms of reference in deciding
the cut off date for transfer of committed liability on account of plan scheme, the

in the next two financial years would have to be provided for. The Commission are,
