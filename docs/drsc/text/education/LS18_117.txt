REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Census of 2001 had indicated the population of India as 1027 million with 531 million males and 496
million females.    In the decade  1991-2001, nearly 181 million persons were added to the population of India.  
Population increased at an annual exponential growth rate of 1.93 percent in the decade.  As per the latest Sample
Registration System (SRS) estimates, the annual growth rate of population is 1.74% (1999).  
1.2       The eight States of U.P., Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Jharkhand, Uttaranchal, Chhatisgarh and Orissa,
which currently constitute nearly 44% of the total population of India, are projected to comprise 48% of the total
population in 2016.   In other words, these States alone will contribute an anticipated 55% increase during the period
1996-2016.    Demographic outcome in these States will determine the timing and size of population at which India
achieves population stabilisation.
1.3       The three most significant factors that can curtail the growth of population are increase in the use of
contraceptives, improvement in the mother’s health and survival of children.  All the programmes of the Department of
Family Welfare are accordingly planned and being implemented for pursuing a combination of these three issues.
1.4       During the Tenth  Plan the main approach of family welfare programme will be:
(i)
                  To assess the needs for Reproductive and Child Health (RCH);
(ii)                To provide need based ,client centered and demand driven RCH care;
(iii)               To strengthen the infrastructure for service delivery and bridging the gap in essential infrastructure and
manpower;
(iv)              Providing additional assistance to poorly performing districts and ensuring uninterrupted supply of drugs
and equipments;  and

II          BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1              The Committee has been informed that allocation for the Department for  2002-2003 is Rs. 4953.83 crores
against Rs. 4232.38 crores in BE 2001-2002 which was  reduced to Rs. 3721.67 crores at RE stage.   This represents  an
increase of Rs. 721.55 crores over the previous year.
2.2
              Commenting on the allocation, the Department submitted that the Annual Plan proposal for the year 2002-2003
was Rs. 7590.56 crores.  However, an allocation of Rs 4953.83 crores was approved by the Planning Commission.  As a
result of cut in plan proposal, funding of the infrastructural units in the States will be the most affected.   Besides that, 
other schemes like contractual appointments under RCH Programme, adolescent health care, supply of Dai kits,
promoting institutional deliveries, civil works at Sub Centres, urban slum project and strengthening of primary health
care infrastructure, may also be curtailed.
2.3
              The Committee  has also been given to understand that during 2001-2002, a cut of 710.00 crores (plan) was
proposed by the Ministry of Finance on the basis of expenditure  incurred by the Department till October, 2001
(51.19%).   However,  subsequently an assurance was given by the Ministry of Finance that if 75% expenditure was
incurred by the Department by December, 2001, the cut made in the outlay will be restored.    However, despite meeting
the targetted expenditure and the representations made at the highest level, the cut of Rs. 710 crores was only reduced to
Rs. 510 crores.  
2.4              The Committee agrees in principle with the Department's contention of there being no case for resorting to a cut
of Rs. 700.00 crores at the RE stage in 2001-2002 by the Ministry of Finance.  The Committee also notes that total plan
allocation of Rs. 3700.00 crores in 2001-2002 is anticipated to be utilised. Actual expenditure figures are not available
at present.  The Committee, however observes that during 2000-2001, against a higher projected requirement, plan
allocation was Rs. 3500.00 crores which was reduced to Rs. 3200.00 crores at the RE stage.  Actual expenditure was
even less i.e. Rs. 3071.35 crores.  The Committee strongly feels that inview of much higher allocation of Rs. 4930.00
crores in 2002-2003, the Department should ensure that funds allocated under various schemes are utilised optimally
and strictly as per the action plan to strengthen its case of higher allocation.
III        RURAL FAMILY WELFARE SERVICES
3.1
              The Rural Family Welfare Centres were set up at all block level PHCs sanctioned upto 1.4.1980 to provide
Family Planning and MCH Services in rural areas.  There are 5435 such centres functioning in the Country.  Besides
that, to provide basic health and family welfare services to the rural population 1,37,311 Sub-Centres  are currently
functioning out of which 97,757 Sub-Centres were funded by the Department till now. 
3.2              The Committee was informed that a Consultative Committee consisting of the representatives of the Planning
Commission, Department of Expenditure, States of Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan was constituted by
the Department in 1998.   The Committee examined the SWAP proposal between Rural Family Welfare Centres and
Sub-Centres and strongly recommended that in view of the importance of the rural Sub-Centres for the Family Welfare
Programme, and as the Rural Family Welfare Centres have lost their original identity and have become part of primary
health centre complexes at the block level, the Department of Family Welfare should assume the responsibility of
funding all 1,37,311 existing Sub-Centres.   In exchange, for accepting this responsibility the States should take over the
financial liability of maintaining the Rural Family Welfare Centres  (RFWC) numbering 5435.
3.3       The Department  also informed that the main purpose of the SWAP is to streamline the system of financing the
Sub-Centres. As the Sub-Centres were established by Central Government and State Governments during different time
periods it is difficult to assess the impact of the programme run by the Department.   Therefore, there was a need to
bring all the Sub-Centres under one umbrella for effective monitoring of the programme.   The Planning Commission
insisted  that a consensus be developed among the States on the issue of swaping these responsibilities between the State
and Department of Family Welfare.     The Committee in its 101
st Report had also recommended that the issue needed
to be discussed with the States/UTs before any decision was taken in this regard.  The Committee, however, notes that a
decision has already been taken to implement the SWAP proposal from 1.4.2002.  Therefore there  is no proposal for
requirement of Funds for RFWCs in the Tenth Plan.  Accordingly, no allocation has been made in BE 2002-2003.
3.4       The Committee is surprised to note that a final decision has already been taken by the Department inspite of
there being no consensus on this issue among the States.  According to the Department itself, outcome of
implementation of SWAP proposal will be that some states will be in an advantageous position by saving the funds and

outreach services, primary health care, maternal and child health and distribution of contraceptives.   The financial
assistance under this component is given for the salary of staff, contingency and rent as per approved norms.

4.3              In order to improve service delivery outreach services of Primary Health Care, Family Welfare and MCH
Services in urban areas particularly in urban slums, Urban Revamping Scheme was introduced under which
Government have accorded approval for establishment of 936 Health Posts and 14 Family Welfare Bureau in various
States of the country so far.   Out of which 871 Health posts and 10 Family Welfare Bureau have been sanctioned so far.

made at the earliest.    Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Department should make adequate provision for
reorganisation of Urban Family Welfare Centres in 2002-2003.
V         REPRODUCTIVE AND CHILD HEALTH PROGRAMME (RCH)
5.1       Reproductive and Child Health Programme is the flagship programme of Family Welfare, which combines
trinity of objectives viz. reproductive health, Child survival and fertility regulation. The Programme is primarily offered
through Primary Health Infrastructure.  The overall goals of the programme are to reduce maternal and infant mortality
and morbidity and unwanted fertility and contribute thereto stabilization of population.
5.2      Allocation for RCH project in BE 2001-2002 was Rs. 1126.95 crores which was reduced to 933.36 crores at the
RE stage.   Allocation for 2002-2003 has been further reduced to Rs. 800.53 crores.   Commenting on the reduced
allocation the Department has stated that the outlay for Polio Eradication Programme and vaccines for routine
immunization till 2001-2002 used to be routed through RCH programme.    The Planning Commission advised that the
Department  should club  all the immunization related activities under one head.   Acting on the advice of the Planning
Commission, the allocation for polio activities and vaccines for routine immunization related activities which stands at
Rs. 376 crores and Rs. 141 crores respectively have been separated from the RCH Programme which in-turn resulted in
decreased allocation  for RCH Programme in BE 2002-2003.
5.3      The Committee notes with appreciation the rationalization done by the Department in restructuring the RCH
Programme and feels that the Departmentshould, in the same vein carry similar rationalization/ restructuring,  
wherever possible, in respect of other programmes.
5.4       The Committee finds that funds have been released to all the States/UTs under RCH Programme during the
Ninth Plan.   However, utilisationof these funds has been very poor.   As per the figures made available to the

the end of Ninth Plan.   Balance amount of RS. 563.55 crores is lying as unspent balance with States/UTs.   While all

VII       POST PARTUM PROGRAMME
Post Partum Programme is a maternity centered hospital based approach to Family Welfare Programme covering
550 Post Partum Centres at district level and 1012 Post Partum Centres at sub-district level hospitals.   The objective of
the programme is to provide antenatal,  natal and post-natal services to expectant mothers and also to provide Family
Planning Services, besides diagnosing early cases of cervical cancer.   An allocation of  Rs 135.00 crores was made for
Post Partum Programme in BE 2001-2002.   However, no allocation has been made for the year 2002-2003.   Giving
reasons, the Department has replied that the Planning Commission has decided to transfer the Post Partum Programme
to the States/UTs w.e.f. 1.4.2002.  The Committee would be interested to know as to the precise reasons that weighed in
favour of transfer of this Programme to the States/UTs.
VIII      DISTRIBUTION OF CONTRACEPTIVES
8.1
              Allocation of Rs. 117.00 crores made under the head Free Distribution of Conventional Contraceptive in BE
2001-2002 has been increased to Rs. 181.80 crores in 2002-2003.  Explaining the reasons for increase,  the Department

the States has been increasing and   in order to meet the enhanced demand, increased budgetary provision has been
proposed.
8.2       The Department has further informed that as regards allocation of additional quantities of conventional
contraceptives in the States, these would be allocated proportionately in relation  to utilisation and demand of the
respective States.   The unmet need in condom under free supply scheme is nearly 30% whereas in Oral Contraceptive
Pill (OCP) it is around 10%.   Attempts will be made to fill up these gaps.   The additional allocation would be required
for the purchase of the raw material for OCP which is no more available under external assistance and the same has to
be met from domestic resources.

under free supply scheme as okay,  75% as good and 6% have rated as very good.    The Committee feels that the
quality of condoms goes a long way in its acceptance by the users.    The Committee is also of the view that poor quality
may lead to unwanted pregnancy and reproductive tract infections.    The Committee,  therefore, recommends that the
Department should ensure that only the best quality of latex is used in manufacturing of condoms.

The Committee notes with satisfaction that the percentage of coverage in 2000-2001 in respect of TT for
pregnant woman and BCG, Measles, DPT and Polio for infants have increased in comparison to the preceding year.

Planning methods.   (Para 1.6)
            The Committee understands that under the Community Needs Assessment Approach (CNAA), an action plan is
prepared each year at the district level based  on the need assessment done by ANMs of Sub-Centre at village level.  In
the beginning of the financial year, the health worker will first complete the house-to-house survey.  Based on this
survey, Eligible Couple Register is updated by the ANM.  The Committee has been informed that the State Govts. are 
impressed from time to time to ensure that the Sub-Centre action is got prepared for each Sub-Centre in the State.  The
Committee has its own doubts about the implementation of this annual exercise, which is most essential for successful
implementation of Family Welfare Programme, specially in the remote and inaccessible areas.  The Committee strongly
feels that surprise inspections in the performing as well as non-performing districts by Central teams/dedicated
NGOs/Social Workers can bring out the ground reality.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department
should initiate such an exercise in 2002-2003. (Para 1.7)
