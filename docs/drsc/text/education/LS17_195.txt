REPORT
 
I           INTRODUCTION
 
1.1       The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of India owes its origin to the Department of Sports, which
was set up in 1982 at the time of the 9
th Asian Games in New Delhi.  During the International Youth Year 1985 it was
upgraded to the Department of Youth Affairs & Sports.  On 17th May, 2000 it became a full fledged Ministry.
 
1.2       The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (MoYAS) is entrusted with the responsibilities relating to the development
of Youth and Adolescents, and the functions of the Central Government with regard to Sports and Physical Education.
 
1.3       The MoYA&S is implementing a large number of schemes for youth and adolescents development.   These
schemes are proposed to be re-oriented as a part of an integrated framework comprising three key thrust areas viz.,
Personality Development, Preparation for Work Participation/Employability, and Empowerment.

1.4       In so far as the subject of Sports is concerned, this figures in the State List of the Constitution.   In view of this, it is
the State Governments who are primarily responsible for the development and promotion of sports.  The MoYA&S in its
effort to complement the State's efforts aims at development of a broad based sports culture throughout the country, and
significantly expanding the pool of talented sportspersons who can be groomed to bring laurels to the country in
international events.  With this in view, a major initiative proposed to be launched by the Ministry is a scheme with the
title 'Panchayat Yuva Khel Abhiyan', which is aimed at creating basic sports infrastructure, and promoting sports activities
at the village/sub-district level, both as a necessary ingredient of youth development and as a means to substantially widen
the catchment for spotting of talent.   The Committee desires that Pancayat Yuva Khel Abhiyan should be finalized at the
earliest and the details in this regard may be furnished to the Committee.
 
1.5       The Committee was informed that the population of the youth and adolescents is nearly 45% of the total
population, and that of the number of persons below the age of 35 is around 75%.   This creates, the Committee feels, a
unique opportunity to take advantage of this "demographic dividend" and also highlights the urgency of addressing the
needs of the youth and adolescents in a comprehensive manner with the objective of creating responsible citizen as well as
a healthy and productive work force.

III        NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME
 
3.1       National Service Scheme launched in 1969 has primary focus on the development of personality of students
through community service.   Since its inception, more than 2.8 crore students from Universities, Colleges and other
educational Institutions have participated in NSS activities as student volunteers.
 
3.2       The Scheme is implemented through the State Governments. The basic unit of NSS consists of 50 or 100
volunteers and is headed by a Programme Officer at faculty/institution level.  At the University level, the Programme
Officers of affiliated colleges of the concerned University work under the guidance of Programme Coordinators.  At the
State level, all the Universities in the State work under the guidance of a State Liaison Officer.  The State Liaison Cell at
the State headquarters is responsible for ensuring timely release of grants under its jurisdiction and timely submission of
accounts thereof.  The State level Liaison Cell is fully funded by the Central Government.  There are 15 NSS Regional
Centres in the country.  These Regional Centres maintain liaison with the State Governments, Universities, +2 Councils,
Training and Orientation Centres and Training Orientation and Research Centre, Coordinators and Programme Officers.
 
3.3       The Committee notes that every student cannot join NSS as there is a ceiling on the number of volunteers a State
can have. The cap on the number of NSS volunteers, the Committee feels, appears to be incongruous to the objectives of
NSS.   Our efforts should be to provide every student who is desirous of joining NSS, opportunity to do so.  The
Committee feels that finance should not be a problem in achieving this as funds usually remain under utilized.
 
3.4       The Scheme is jointly funded by the Union and State Governments in a ratio of 7:5 in all States, except North East
Region, including Sikkim, and hilly areas, where the ratio is 3:1.  In J&K and all the Union Territories, Kendriya
Vidyalayas and Navodaya Vidyalayas, the programme is entirely funded by Government of India.  The funding to the
State Government for regular activities is Rs. 160/- per volunteer per annum for normal areas and Rs. 200/- per volunteer
per annum for North Eastern States including Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh and other hilly states as notified by the Planning

Committee also welcomes the introduction of diet component into this programme.   The Committee advises the Ministry
to ensure that this allocation does not remain underutilized.
 
3.7       The Committee, at the same time, is worried to note the precious fund lying unspent under NSS.  The Committee is
at a loss to see the huge backlogs in settling the accounts.   For example, four States/UTs have settled account up to
2001/02 only; nine States/UTs up to 2002-03, seventeen States/UTs up to 2003-04 and only five States/UTs up to 2004-
05.  The non-settlement of accounts and the unspent balances, in Committee’s view, are indicative of the poor health of
this scheme.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to take up the settlement of accounts with all States/UTs on
priority to clear the backlog during this financial year itself.
 
3.8       The Committee has been informed that a proposal for expansion of National Service Scheme (NSS) to all
universities, Colleges and Schools as well as to enroll the student of classes IX and X also as NSS volunteers was sent to

Ministry of HRD. In the meanwhile the issue was deliberated in the Working Group for the formulation of 11

Plan.  It has proposed that the NSS volunteer's strength currently at 2.6 million, be substantially increased in a phased
manner to 5.08 million by the end of the 11 th Five Year Plan.  In this regard first priority will be accorded to extend NSS
to uncovered Universities, Colleges, Technical Institutes and +2 Schools under Higher Secondary Councils/Boards,
followed by setting up more units in the existing NSS institutions, wherever required.  However, the feasibility of
extending NSS to classes IX & X will be examined separately in a larger context relating to the universal coverage of
educational institutions either through the NCC or NSS.  In the process of increasing the number of volunteers, emphasis
will be given to the State and regions where this is low.  Enrolment of women volunteers will be encouraged through a
targeted special drive in the 11
th Five Year Plan period. The Committee welcomes the proposal to increase the volunteer's
strength to 5.08 million by the end of the 11 th Five Year Plan.  It hopes that the target of 5.08 million NSS volunteers will
be achieved and proper planning will be done in this regard.

IV        NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN
 
4.1       Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS) is an autonomous body of the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports and
has its presence in 500 districts of the country.  It is one of the largest grassroot level organizations in the world, catering
to more than eight million non-student rural youth enrolled through about 2.53 lakh village based Youth Clubs.  These
Youth Clubs work in areas like education and training, awareness generation, skill development and self-employment,
entrepreneurial development, thrift and co-operation, besides personality development through sports and adventure and
sustained exposure to new ideas and development strategies.   For implementation of the programmes, every district has a
trained cadre of District Youth Coordinator, National Service Volunteers and Youth leaders.  The strength of NYKS is its
vast network of Youth Clubs at the grassroots level.
 
4.2       NYKS accomplishes its task through three categories of programmes:
(1)
               Regular programmes such as Youth Leaders and Youth Club Development, Panchayat Yuva Shakti Abhiyan,
Employment and Income Generation Programme through Rural Business Hub, Cultural Programmes, Mahila
Sashaktikaran Abhiyan, Work Camp, Local Need Based Project, Adventure Promotion Programmes, etc.
(2)               Special Schemes of Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports like National Service Volunteers, Youth
Development Centres, Awards to Outstanding Youth Clubs, Financial Assistance to Youth Clubs, Rural Sports
Clubs, Rural Information Technology Youth Development Centre (RITYDC), Scheme for Development and
Empowerment of Adolescents, Rashtriya Sadbhawana Yojana.
(3)
               Special Programmes undertaken in collaboration with other Ministries and Organizations.  Here in specific
schemes pertaining to Health, Education, Environment, Poverty alleviation, etc. are  undertaken by NYKS,
from time to time, as one of the implementing agencies on behalf of the concerned Ministries/Departments,
including Ministry of YAS.  In this role, the NYKS is practically seen as any other NGO.
 
4.3       Besides, the programmes covered under the above broad categories, NYKS also plans to observe/celebrate
Important National & International Days & Weeks.

V         RASHTRIYA SADBHAVANA YOJANA
 
5.1       The Rashtriya Sadhbhavana Yojana was launched in 2005 in place of the erstwhile National Reconstruction Corps
Scheme.  The Scheme aims at encouraging leadership among the rural youth.  Under the scheme average of around 10-20
volunteers in each of the 500 districts having presence of NYKS are to be enrolled as Nehru Yuva Sathees (NYSs).   These
NYSs are to be nominated by the selected best Youth Clubs and Youth Development Centres in the block and district
respectively.  During 2006-07, 4925 NYSs were deployed through NYKS.
 
5.2       The Committee notes that the performance of the scheme of Rashtriya Sadbhavna Yojna has been uneven in
different states.  The Ministry has informed that this is mainly on account of a requirement that the Youth Clubs, for this
purpose, should be registered for at least three years. However, this requirement could not be fulfilled since many Youth
Clubs could not get registered in many States like UP, Bihar as there are difficulties in getting registration in these States. 
As a result, the scheme suffered.  The Ministry has also submitted that there were some practical difficulties in the actual
selection of Nehru Yuva Sathees (NYSs).   The Ministry has not furnished any information as to what steps it took to
persuade the States to simplify the registration process.  The Committee only hopes that the Ministry must have taken the
matter of slow registration of clubs with concerned States to resolve it.  The Committee, in view of practical problems
being faced in selection of NYSs, regrets to say that these problems still persist.  The Committee recommends the Ministry
to take urgent steps to remove the difficulties encountered in the actual selection of Nehru Yuva Sathees.
5.3       One of the measures taken by the Ministry to improve the situation is to raise the level of honorarium for Nehru
Yuva Sathees from Rs. 1000/- to Rs.1500/-.  The committee welcomes this raise but is afraid that in the face of rising
inflation and other factors, even this raise may not be sufficient to encourage the volunteers to come forth as Nehru Yuva
Sathis.  The Committee is of the opinion that Rs. 1500 may not be at all adequate for areas like J&K, North-East,
Himachal Pradesh and Uttrakhand.
 
5.4       The Committee also welcomes the fact that Rashtriya Sadhbhavana Yojana has been included as a monitorable
programme in the revised 20 Point Programme.   It hopes that this inclusion will ensure proper implementation of the
Scheme.
 
5.5       The Committee notes that BE 2007-08 for the Yojana is pegged at Rs. 8.00 crore which is an enhancement to Rs.
5.00 crore that the Yojana got in RE 2006-07.  The Committee is, however, surprised to see that not a single rupee has
been spent till 31.12.2006 against an allocation of Rs. 5.00 crore.  The Committee would like to know the reasons behind
this complete non-utilization of funds.  It further desires to know as to how the Ministry could reach an achievement of
4925 volunteers against a target of 5911 fixed for the year 2006-07, without spending a single rupee until 31.12.2006.  The
Committee requests the Ministry to throw more light on this.

       NATIONAL SERVICE VOLUNTEERS SCHEME
 
7.1       The National Service Volunteer Scheme provides a platform for the youth to take up a leadership role in the

195th Report of Connittee On Human Resource Development

spheres of the social and community service at the grass roots level.
 
7.2       The Service Volunteer is a youth social worker who is willing to commit his/her energy and time to the service of
the community and the country. The National Service Volunteer Scheme provides for an opportunity for first work
experience after education and is aimed to inculcate leadership qualities in them.  During their deployment, the Volunteers
are paid Rs. 1000/- per month by way of honorarium.  The NSVs, together with the Nehru Yura Sathees (NYSs) under
Rsahtriya Sadhbhavana Yojana, are the backbone of the NYKS.
 
7.3       The Committee notes that the Plan allocation in BE 2007-08 for National Service Volunteers Scheme has been
increased to Rs. 13.50 crores as compared to Rs. 6.00 crores in 2006-07.  The Ministry has informed that the reasons for
seeking increase in allocation for 2007-08 is the proposal to enhance the stipends of the volunteers from the existing Rs.
1000/- per month to Rs. 2500/- per month.  The Committee welcomes the enhancement in the stipends of the volunteers
but it still feels that this enhancement is not sufficient, given the fact that as NSVs, they contribute significantly for social
work on a full time basis. The Committee recommends further enhancement in the stipend of the Volunteers. 
 
7.4       The Scheme aims at deploying 4400 NSVs during 2007-08.  Under the scheme, the volunteers are enrolled in the
beginning of the year and allotted to Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan and National Service Scheme.   The recruitment is
made by a District level Committee headed by the collectors. 
 
7.5       The Committee notes that there is a little awareness about the existence of such a scheme in the public.   The
Committee emphasizes the need for giving a wider publicity about the scheme, mode of recruitment of volunteers, etc.
 
7.6       The Committee further notes that 30 per cent of NSVs after the training remain unemployed and only 30 per cent
become social worker.  The Committee feels that with this achievement, the scheme has failed to create a desirable impact
on our youth particularly to join social sector in a big way.
 
7.7       The Committee notes that these NSVs are associated with NYKs.  However, given the fact that only 500 districts in
the country have NYKs, the youth of remaining districts are being deprived of the opportunity to become NSVs.   Further,
with the projection of only 4400 NSVs to be deployed during 2007-08, the number of volunteers for a district comes to
mere 8 volunteers per district.  The Committee feels that this is an inadequate number.  It only hopes that NSVs are being
selected from uncovered districts as well.
 
7.8       The Committee takes note of the fact that about 5% of NSVs leave midway for various reasons.  The Committee
would like the Ministry to inform it about the impact this drop out has on the scheme and the steps taken to control it.
 
VIII      YOUTH HOSTELS
 
8.1       Youth Hostels are built to promote youth travel within the country.  They have been conceived as a joint venture
between the Central and the State Government.   While the Central Government bears the cost of construction, the State
Government provides fully developed land free of cost with water, electricity, approach road and staff quarters.  After
completion, Youth Hostels are handed over to the State Government  for management.  Youth hostels are generally located
in areas of historical and cultural value, educational centre, tourist importance etc. where facilities for youth activities are
available.  As on 31
st December, 2006, 73 Youth Hotels have been constructed and 20 hostels are under construction and
32 have been approved in principle.   So far, 14 Youth Hostels have been constructed in the North Eastern Region, which
are functional.
 
8.2       The Committee notes that the budgetary allocation for Youth Hostels has decreased to Rs. 1.25 crore in BE 2007-
08 in comparison to Rs. 3.50 crore allocated in RE 2006-07.  This reduction has been made in view of lower actual
expenditure till September, 2006 under Youth Hostel Scheme.   The Committee notes with concern the continuously low
utilization of funds under this scheme.  Against an allocation of Rs. 4.50 crore in 2005-06, the Ministry could utilize only
1.49 crore.  Further in 2006-07, against an allocation of Rs. 3.50 crore, the Ministry could spend a mere 7 lakh only till
31.12.2006.  The extremely low utilization of funds has severely affected the construction of Youth Hostels.  The
Committee is perturbed over such a poor performance of an important scheme from its inception.   It recommends the
Ministry to make effort to improve the situation and devise specific measures to address various problems associated with
this scheme.

XVI     GENERAL OBSERVATION
 
16.1     The Committee after going through the replies and other documents relating to Demands for Grants of various
years including that of 2007-08 arrives at conclusion that the financial performance/management of the Ministry leaves
much to be desired.  Importance of the mandate of the Ministry – development, empowerment of youth as well as
promotion of sports – can hardly be over-emphasized.  The Committee has all these years been pleading for more funds for
these purposes.  Rs. 200 crore for development of youth who constitute 45% of our population and on whom the country is
pinning all its hope for future, is just a peanut.   Similarly, sports development gets Rs. 350 crore + Rs. 150 crore for
Commonwealth Games, 2010.
 
16.2     On the other hand, the Committee regrets to say that even these meagre allocations are not being utilized
completely and properly.  Every year, crores of rupees are left as unspent balance or being allowed to lapse due to various
reasons-non-settlement of accounts and pending UCs being the two most important ones.  As a result, crores of rupees are
blocked every year and our schemes languish.  Another disturbing trend noticed in this regard is the quantum jump in
spending of allocations in March every year.  Such a position leads to a situation where we have plan, programmes and
targets but there is no performance.   Flow of funds from Centre to States and other implementing agencies take 8 to 10
months, at times more.  Some schemes are prepared/declared to be revised/redesigned before they actually take off.    The
Committee has no option but to conclude that our schemes are suffering not merely because of paucity of funds but also
because of problems in our delivery mechanism, the procedure we adopt and above all the attitude in the administrative
machinery.  Lack of effective mechanisms for monitoring, authentic feedback, objective evaluation, etc. make it difficult
to gauge the performance of a scheme. 
 
16.3     These are larger questions which need to be considered at various levels to evolve a system as well as culture in
which we work as facilitator/enabler also not only as a controller, which has an effective system of transparency and
accountability and enough scope for correction and improvement.
16.4     The Committee has been expressing its reservation about the transfer of Sports Infrastructure Scheme to the States. 
The Committee has noted the assurance of the Ministry to consider it in the ongoing exercises for the formulation of the
Eleventh Plan.  The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation in 176
th Report for strengthening of monitoring and
effective implementation rather than transferring the scheme to the States.
