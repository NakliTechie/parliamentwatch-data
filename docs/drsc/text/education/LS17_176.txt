REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports was set up as the Department of Sports in 1982 at the time of
organisation of the IX Asian Games in New Delhi.   Thereafter, during the celebration of the International Youth Year
in 1985, it was converted into the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports. It became a full-fledged Ministry on the 27 th
May, 2000.
1.2       The Youth i.e. those falling within the age group of 15-35 years constitutes nearly 40% of the total population of

of Youth affairs and Sports has taken a number of new initiatives since its inception.    The Ministry organises a large
number of programmes in the field of youth development, sports and physical education.
1.3       The Joint Secretary (Sports), while deposing before the Committee, highlighted the various activities undertaken
by the Ministry during the year 2005-2006. He informed the Committee that Nehru Yuva Kendra played a major role in
providing relief materials, organizations of camps, counselling and career guidance to Tsunami victims.  SAI hostel,
Port Blair acted as both the center for housing inmates and for giving relief to Tsunami victims.  Contribution made by
NYK in providing relief to the earthquake victims in Kashmir was also worth-mentioning. Another initiative taken by
the Ministry was identification of Sports Federations performing well for giving them additional Funds.
1.4       A mention was also made about the new initiatives planned for 2006-07. A new Scheme ‘Yuva Shakti Abhiyan’
was going to be launched in Panchayats in all the States. Similarly all the Youth organizations like NYKS, NSS, NCC,
Bharat Scouts and Guides are being involved in the campaign on HIV/AIDS. States are going to be involved more and
more in the monitoring of different schemes.  He also drew the attention of the Committee towards the commendable
performance of Indian players who won a total of 50 medals and retained 4
th position in Commonwealth Games held at
Melbourne, 2006. These achievements were made possible to a large extent by the financial assistance made available to
the Sports Federations by the Ministry and also providing the necessary facilities to the participating players.

which include:-
-
                     Setting up of Rashtriya Yuva Kosh on the pattern of the Rashtriya Mahila Kosh;

IV.       NEHRU YUVA KENDRA SANGATHAN
4.1       Nehru Yuva Kendra Sangathan (NYKS),  an autonomous body of the Ministry which functions in Districts all
over India is engaged in conducting various youth programmes. Being the largest grass root level organization in the
world it is presently catering to the needs of more than 8 million non-student rural youth enrolled through about 2.30
lakh village based youth clubs.  The vast network of the youth clubs is the real strength of NYKS.
4.2       NYKS accomplishes its task through three categories of programmes i.e. Regular Programmes such as, Youth
Club Development, Vocational Training, Work Camp, Awareness Generation, Cultural Programme, Sports Promotion,
Adventure Promotion, Seminars and Workshops and Celebration of National and International Days/Weeks; second
category of schemes include National Service Volunteers, Financial Assistance to Youth Clubs, Awards to Outstanding
Youth Clubs at District, State and National levels, Rural Information Technology and Youth Development Centres
assigned to it by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports for implementation.  The third category being the special
programmes undertaken in collaboration with other Ministries and Organizations.
4.3       An allocation of Rs. 34.00 crores was made at BE and RE stage during 2005-06. The actual expenditure was Rs.
33.21 crores.  The allocation for 2006-07 is Rs. 45 crores against proposed outlay of Rs. 61.18 crores.  The Committee

V.        NATIONAL SERVICE SCHEME
5.1       The Scheme is targeted towards students of Universities, Colleges, +2 level schools and institutions of higher
learning in all the States/Union Territories.  Volunteers are enrolled for undertaking Regular activities and Special
Camping Programme under the Scheme which included social work like blood donation, Adult and Non-formal
education etc.
            The main objectives of National Service Scheme (NSS) are:
(a)
            understand the community in which they work;
(b)           understand themselves in relation to their community;
(c)            identify the needs/problems of the community and involve them in problem solving;
(d)           develop among themselves a sense of social and civic responsibility;
(e)            utilize their knowledge in finding practical solutions to individual and community problems;
(f)             develop competence required for group living and sharing of responsibilities;
(g)            gain skills in mobilizing community participation;
(h)            acquire leadership qualities and democratic attitudes;
(i)              develop capacity to meet emergencies and natural disasters and
(j)             practise national integration and social harmony.
 
5.2       An allocation of Rs. 31.50 crores has been made for NSS under plan in 2006-2007 against a provision of Rs.
26.10 crores last year. The allocation this year has been kept on higher side due to the increase in the enrolment of
students in various universities and colleges.  Also there is a proposal to introduce National Service Scheme from IXth
class onwards. The Committee would like the Ministry to consider introduction of NSS from class IX itself in the
light of noble objectives of the scheme.
5.3       The Committee notes that the motto of NSS “Not Me But You” reflects the essence of democratic living and
upholds the need for self less service.  NSS helps the students develop appreciation of other person’s point of view and
also show consideration to other living beings.  The philosophy of the NSS is well doctrined in this motto, which
underlines the belief that the welfare of an individual is ultimately dependent on the welfare of the society on the whole
and, therefore, the NSS volunteers  strive for the well being of the society.
5.4       The Committee understands that there is a need to deploy more number of National Service Volunteers who act
as link between the Kendra and village based Rural Youth Clubs.  The Committee strongly feels that it is high time for
acknowledging the good work being done by NSS volunteers. Their efforts need to be encouraged and commensurated
with good incentives. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the incentive given to NSS volunteers should be
enhanced and brought at par with the incentives provided to the NCC cadets so that they perform their duties with
greater zest and vigour.

VI.       NATIONAL DISCIPLINE SCHEME
6.1       The National Discipline Scheme was originally started in 1954 by the Ministry of Rehabilitation, which was
later transferred to Ministry of Education and Social Welfare in 1958. Consequent upon the recommendations of Dr.
Kunzru Committee in 1963, introducing discipline programme at school stage, the integrated scheme called “National
Fitness Corps” (NFC) was evolved.  The Scheme was transferred to the Department of Youth Affairs and Sports (Now
Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports) in eighties when a separate Department was formed.
6.2       On the basis of the recommendations of Dr. Kunzru Committee, the decision to transfer NDS Instructors to
various State Governments was taken in consultation with the State Education Secretaries.  In the year 1969, the Central
Cabinet took a decision that the employees under the NFC would be transferred to the services of the State Governments
on the condition that the Central Government would reimburse their pay and allowances so long as they remained on the
strength of the State Governments. The Committee was informed that 6145 NDS Instructors were transferred to various
State Governments after decentralization of NDS scheme.
6.3       Though physically the NDS Instructors were absorbed in the services of the State Governments, the Central
Government was committed to bear the liability of their pay and allowances and this system has been continuing since
seventies. Due to inadequate budgetary provisions, the Central Government has never been able to bear its liability to
the fullest extent and thereby the accumulated liability continued to grow to unmanageable level. The Committee was
informed that the total liability towards pays and allowances for States of M.P., Punjab, Haryana, H.P., Rajasthan,
Kerala and Maharashtra from which information has been received is to the tune of Rs. 184.21 crores. Information from
remaining ten States is yet to be received. The total projected liability in respect of all the States is to the tune of Rs. 300
crore. A budget support would be required on that account.          
6.4       The Committee regrets that repeated reminders from the Department to some States have not evinced any
response and the issue of committed liabilities remains un-resolved for years together. The Committee feels that this
situation of uncertainty should come to a halt. A time has come when the Ministry should fix a final deadline for such
States to avoid this never-ending chaotic scenario. The Ministry should settle the issue once for all after paying the
liabilities to those States who have furnished the information. The States who do not respond may have to forgo their
claims. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would plan the whole process to ensure settlement of committed
liabilities within this financial year itself. The Committee would also like the Ministry to appeal to the States to avail
this opportunity and expedite their claims without further loss of time.

VII.     YOUTH HOSTELS
7.1       Youth Hostels are built to promote youth travel and to enable the youth to experience the rich cultural heritage of
our country.  The scheme of Youth Hostels has been conceived as a joint venture of the Central and State Governments. 
While the Central Government bears the cost of construction, the State Governments provide developed plot of land
equipped with water, electricity and approach roads.  After completion, Youth Hostels are handed over to the State
Governments for management.
            The Committee notes that so far 72 Youth Hostels (as on 31.12.2005) have been constructed in different
States.However, Committee’s attention has been drawn to the trend of expenditure on Youth Hostels during the Tenth
Plan as per details indicated below: –
                                                                               (Rs. in crore)

             The Committee notes that the allocations have not been utilised optimally. Main areas of concern have been the
inordinate delay in construction process of Youth Hostels.  The status of 18 Youth Hostels under construction indicate
shocking revelations. There are Youth Hostels which were approved long back in 1986 and even instalments of central
funds for construction were released. For instance an instalment of Rs.15 lakhs was released in March 1989 in respect of
a Youth Hostel in Himachal Pradesh and the State Govt. has not even handed over the developed plot of land for
construction of the project.  Construction of Youth Hostel in Allahabad was approved in principle in April 1988, and
State Government identified the land in 1998.  First instalment of central funds amounting to Rs. 20.00 lakhs was
released in March 1990 and the construction is yet to be completed.  Position with regard to the remaining Youth
Hostels is more or less the same.  The Committee notes that even pursuing the issue with the concerned State Chief
Secretaries has not brought any noticeable impact.  The Committee observes that with the passage of time, estimates for
construction have kept on increasing steadily, leading to criminal wastage of Government funds.
7.3       The Committee recommends that specific action needs to be initiated to remedy the problem. Stringent action is
required to be taken in respect of some places which ultimately will have its impact in other places also. Even the
extreme step for recovery of the instalment to central pool may be contemplated in such cases where the construction
work has not started even 3 years after the release of instalments(s). The Committee would like to have a status note on
the utilisation of instalments released for all such incomplete projects.

Youth Exchange Programme administered by NGOs and other agencies.   The Committee notes that achievement level
for both physical and financial targets has been by and large satisfactory.    Additional funds are also being  provided 
due to the tremendous response being received from the implementing agencies.   During 2005-06, plan allocation of
Rs. 4.95 crore was enhanced to Rs. 5.50 crore and actual expenditure reported upto 20

this programme.   As many as 27 UCs, 110 UCs and 120 UCs for 2002-03, 2003-04 and 2004-05 respectively are yet to

matter. Under no circumstances, defaulter NGOs should be provided further assistance.

equipments and for creation of additional sports infrastructure.

11.5     Traditional/indigenous games have been the bane of Indian sports. Promotion of these sports particularly in our
rural and tribal areas has helped in tapping the sporting talent in the past. Encouragement of these games is also an
important component of our sports planning. The Committee notes that responsibility of choosing the discipline
amongst traditional/ indigenous games lies with the State Government Training Centres. These centres send their
proposals to Sports Authority of India (SAI). The funds are allocated to the Sports Federations dealing with the
promotion of traditional/indigenous sports.
11.6     The Committee is unhappy with the low expenditure on traditional/indigenous games during the last three years.
This fact is reflected in the drastic reduction of funds to these federations over the years. The Committee feels that there
are no clear-cut guidelines to either State Governments or SAI centres about the promotion of indigenous games. The
appraisal of these games is also not possible as no State-wise grant is released for the purpose. The continuous decline in
allocation over the years itself indicates reluctance of Government towards these disciplines. The Committee is
surprised on Ministry’s assertion that they are not short of funds. In fact, if it is so the Ministry should not have any
problem in persuading the federations to avail optimum funds from the Government for promotion of indigenous
games.
11.7     The Committee has been concerned about poor level of achievement in Olympics inspite of good performance at
Asian and Commonwealth Games. The representatives of the Ministry clarified  that pre-qualification standards in
Olympic Games are very tough.  To overcome the problem, the numbers of trainees are proposed to be increased from
10,000 to 15,000 in SAI training centers.  Efforts are also on to strengthen the rural sports.  At Panchayat level efforts
are initiated so that the children from rural areas could also be benefited and get encouragement to join sports.  It would
be helpful in upgrading the skills of the talented, which is in abundant in rural areas.  He assured that by adopting such
measures more medals could be achieved in 2010 and 2014 Games.
11.8     The Committee understands that there are 5 main SAI centers in the country.  However, of these centres i.e.
NERC, Imphal still continues to function as a sub-centre as there is no full-fledged Director. The Committee is surprised
at such peculiar status of the centre. The Committee is of the view that this centre would work better if it is declared as a
full-fledged centre with adequate manpower and infrastructure.
11.9     The Committee is aware that there is no dearth of sporting talent in the country. Proper hunting/searching and
nurturing of the talent is required. Talent is in abundance in schools and educational institutions.  States are allocated
funds for development of sports.  But educational institutions do not have physical instructors.  The Committee feels
that the talent search process is not able to get the reach it requires. People remain in dark about such exercise. Present
system of selection has not yielded the desired results and the talent has still remained unspotted.
The Central Government, State Government and the sport authorities should, therefore, give adequate publicity through
advertisement to such events.

XIV.    SCHEME FOR  DOPE TEST
14.1     The Scheme aims at prevention of drug abuse in sports by providing technical facility for Dope Test.  Under the
scheme, it has been decided to set up two independent autonomous agencies namely National Anti-Doping Agency
(NADA) and National Dope Control Laboratory (NDCL) and provision has, accordingly, been made, keeping in view
their technical and administrative requirements. SAI has developed the Sports Science Centre at Patiala, Bangalore,
Calcutta, Delhi and Gandhinagar. These Centers mostly provide scientific support to the national campers, SAI Training
Centre inmates and are also involved in research and teaching.  
14.2     The Committee was informed that there was some delay in procurement of equipments for Dope tests since the
scheme was under revision. The financial allocation under the scheme was also reduced due to postponement of
accreditation process by WADA and consequent less purchase of equipments. An allocation of Rs. 5.00 crores has been
made for 2006-07 against an allocation of BE Rs. 10.20 crores and RE Rs. 3.70 crores during 2005-06.
14.3     The Committee is constrained to note that at present, SAI has only 38 Sports Scientists including medical
doctors.  This strength of scientific personnel is not enough to provide optimum scientific support to the sports needs in
the country. Sports-related scientific research and development in India has not been able to keep pace with
international developments in various fields like Physiology, Psychology, Bio-mechanics etc. Greater attention is
required to be given to research and development of sports science, in case India has to excel in sports at international
level.
14.4     The Committee is happy to note that in view of the Commonwealth Games, 2010 the anti-doping programme
has been put on fast track.  A number of important steps to check the increasing incidences of doping are proposed to be

XVI.    SCHEME RELATING TO ‘HIV/AIDS’
16.1     The Committee notes that a new scheme relating to HIV/AIDS has been introduced and is under formulation.
The scheme is aimed at controlling the spread of HIV/AIDS in India. An Action Plan has been made in a comprehensive
way for 2006-2011 and one year for implementation during 2006-07. The total budget projected for one year (2006-07)
is Rs. 82. 34 crores in which the Ministry will dovetail its own programme fund of Rs. 55.50 crores relating to NSS,
NYKS and SAI. The budgetary provision has been kept at Rs. 10.00 crores, which will be utilized after its in-principle
approval by the Planning Commission and the balance 16.84 crores will be outsourced from other development partners
and international organizations.
 
16.2     The Action Plan aims to mainstream HIV/AIDS in the existing activities and programmes of the MYAS. It has
been suggested that the proposed activity of generating awareness among the youth of the country would be best
implemented under the Ministry’s umbrella. The Action Plan is to be implemented through the Nehru Yuva Kendra
Sangathan (NYKS), the National Social Service Scheme (NSS), Rajiv Gandhi National Institute for Youth Development
(RGNIYD), and Voluntary agencies, such as, Bharat Scouts and Guides (BSG) and National Cadet Corps (NCC), Indian
Red Cross-and Sports Councils at State level. The objective of the proposed new scheme is that every adolescent and
youth has access to information, education, counseling and services to protect themselves against the deadly disease and
to help others to do so.
16.3     The Committee is happy to note the introduction of the scheme aimed at achieving victory over AIDS in India. 
The Committee feels that proper strategy should be developed to achieve the objectives of the scheme. 
Adolescent/youth clubs and youth information centers  require proper back up of counseling and resource materials. 
The Committee underlines the need for establishment of linkages amongst all the field organizations with health
facilities and VCTCs for service access. The Committee would like the Ministry to pursue the Planning Commission to
seek in principle approval in respect of the scheme.

VII.     YOUTH HOSTELS
The Committee notes that the allocations have not been utilised optimally. Main areas of concern have
been the inordinate delay in construction process of Youth Hostels.  The status of 18 Youth Hostels under construction
indicate shocking revelations. There are Youth Hostels which were approved long back in 1986 and even instalments of
central funds for construction were released. For instance an instalment of Rs.15 lakhs was released in March 1989 in
respect of a Youth Hostel in Himachal Pradesh and the State Govt. has not even handed over the developed plot of land
for construction of the project.  Construction of Youth Hostel in Allahabad was approved in principle in April 1988, and
State Government identified the land in 1998.  First instalment of central funds amounting to Rs. 20.00 lakhs was
released in March 1990 and the construction is yet to be completed.  Position with regard to the remaining Youth
Hostels is more or less the same.  The Committee notes that even pursuing the issue with the concerned State Chief
Secretaries has not brought any noticeable impact.  The Committee observes that with the passage of time, estimates for
construction have kept on increasing steadily, leading to criminal wastage of Government funds. (Para 7.2)
The Committee recommends that specific action needs to be initiated to remedy the problem. Stringent
action is required to be taken in respect of some places which ultimately will have its impact in other places also. Even
the extreme step for recovery of the instalment to central pool may be contemplated in such cases where the
construction work has not started even 3 years after the release of instalments(s). The Committee would like to have a
status note on the utilisation of instalments released for all such incomplete projects.                             (Para 7.3)

IX.       NATIONAL INTEGRATION PROGRAMME
 
                        The Scheme provides the framework for greater exchange and understanding among the youth belonging
to different regions of the country.  The two components of this scheme are National Integration Camp and Inter State
Youth Exchange Programme administered by NGOs and other agencies.   The Committee notes that achievement level
for both physical and financial targets has been by and large satisfactory.    Additional funds are also being  provided 
due to the tremendous response being received from the implementing agencies.   During 2005-06, plan allocation of
Rs. 4.95 crore was enhanced to Rs. 5.50 crore and actual expenditure reported upto 20
th March 2006 is Rs. 5.11 crore. 
The Committee notes that provision of Rs. 10.00 crore has been made for the scheme for 2006-07. The Committee’s
attention however, has been drawn by the status of pendency of utilization certificates spread over different States under
this programme.   As many as 27 UCs, 110 UCs and 120 UCs for 2002-03, 2003-04 and 2004-05 respectively are yet to
be received from implementing agencies.   These are the figures in respect of NGOs.  Status of other implementing
agencies is not known to the Committee. The Committee strongly feels that urgent action is required to be taken in this
matter. Under no circumstances, defaulter NGOs should be provided further assistance.               (Para 9.1)

                   The Committee would like to reiterate its earlier recommendation that any new scheme should be

XI.       SPORTS AUTHORITY OF INDIA (SAI)
                        The Committee is not convinced with the Ministry’s excuse for inadequate resources, when the allocation

                        The Committee is dismayed to note that after conversion of NSTC Scheme from residential to day
boarding, the intake of children in the scheme in most of the adopted regular schools both in individual as well as team

                        The Committee is unhappy with the low expenditure on traditional/indigenous games during the last
three years. This fact is reflected in the drastic reduction of funds to these federations over the years. The Committee
feels that there are no clear-cut guidelines to either State Governments or SAI centres about the promotion of indigenous
games. The appraisal of these games is also not possible as no State-wise grant is released for the purpose. The
continuous decline in allocation over the years itself indicates reluctance of Government towards these disciplines. The
Committee is surprised on Ministry’s assertion that they are not short of funds. In fact, if it is so the Ministry should not
have any problem in persuading the federations to avail optimum funds from the Government for promotion of

by adopting such measures more medals could be achieved in 2010 and 2014
Games.                                                                                      (Para 11.7)

i.e. NERC, Imphal still continues to function as a sub-centre as there is no full-fledged Director. The Committee is
surprised at such peculiar status of the centre. The Committee is of the view that this centre would work better if it is
declared as a full-fledged centre with adequate manpower and infrastructure.                                                        (Para
11.8)
                        The Committee is aware that there is no dearth of sporting talent in the country. Proper hunting/searching
and nurturing of the talent is required. Talent is in abundance in schools and educational institutions.  States are
allocated funds for development of sports.  But educational institutions do not have physical instructors.  The
Committee feels that the talent search process is not able to get the reach it requires. People remain in dark about such
exercise. Present system of selection has not yielded the desired results and the talent has still remained unspotted.
The Central Government, State Government and the sport authorities should, therefore, give adequate publicity
through advertisement to such events.         (Para 11.9)

                        Only a token provision was made for the scheme in 2003-04, 2004-05 and 2005-06 as the scheme had not
been formulated.   The Committee has been given to understand that the scheme is likely to be included in the umbrella
scheme related to events.   Accordingly, no provision has been made in 2006-07.  The Committee takes strong
exception to this kind of approach.   This clearly indicates that the Ministry is not serious about the necessity of
providing any kind of incentives to the disadvantaged segment of our
society.                                                                        (Para 12.1)

XIV.    SCHEME FOR  DOPE TEST
                        The Committee is constrained to note that at present, SAI has only 38 Sports Scientists including medical
doctors.  This strength of scientific personnel is not enough to provide optimum scientific support to the sports needs in
the country. Sports-related scientific research and development in India has not been able to keep pace with
international developments in various fields like Physiology, Psychology, Bio-mechanics etc. Greater attention is
required to be given to research and development of sports science, in case India has to excel in sports at international
level.      (Para 14.3)
                        The Committee is aware of the recent doping incidents during international sports events. It is understood
that out of 11 athletes 9 were found to be positive. This has become a recurring feature during the international
championships and during every international event the country’s prestige is thus put at stake. Perhaps our sports
administration have failed to make the players aware of the dire consequences if they are tested positive.  These
performance enhancing drugs have an adverse affect on their health and could even ruin them professionally. With the
advent of  technology dope testing methods have also faced new challenges. Undoubtedly it is a constant battle with
technology. Test methods have become more and more sophisticated. To generate awareness amongst the players
education is the best cure.  The Committee feels that though the Ministry claims that overall the doping instances
have decreased from 4% to 2-½% but the recent instances put a shadow on such claims. The Committee hopes
that the Ministry would endeavour to achieve all the international standards of dope testing towards fighting the
malice of doping which degrades the prestige of the nation.                                     (Para 14.5)
 
XV.      GRANTS TO NATIONAL SPORTS FEDERATIONS
                        Undoubtedly the existing guidelines/byelaws do not have provision to recover the dues from the
federations but this issue of prolonged non-payment needs to be addressed with some sort of authority. The Ministry
should, therefore, revise its guidelines by either adding a clause for derecognition  of defaulting NSF or authorising SAI
to recover the dues from the NSF. A beginning should be made by recovery of outstanding electricity and water charges
from such federations.                     (Para 15.3)
XVI.    SCHEME RELATING TO ‘HIV/AIDS’
                        The Committee is happy to note the introduction of the scheme aimed at achieving victory over AIDS in
India.  The Committee feels that proper strategy should be developed to achieve the objectives of the scheme. 
Adolescent/youth clubs and youth information centers  require proper back up of counseling and resource materials. 
The Committee underlines the need for establishment of linkages amongst all the field organizations with health
facilities and VCTCs for service access. The Committee would like the Ministry to pursue the Planning Commission
to seek in principle approval in respect of the scheme.                                                                                   (Para 16.3)
XVII.      COMMONWEALTH GAMES, 2010
                        The Committee recommends that the concerned agencies should be asked to expedite its planning and
projections so that the annual break-up of funds required during next four years could be worked out.  The special plan
of action prepared in respect of imparting training to athletes should be finalized at the earliest.  Committee would like
to be apprised about the evaluation of performance of Indian athletes in the Commonwealth Games, 2006.     (Para 17.4)
