 REPORT
I           INTRODUCTION
1.1       The Department of Women and Child Development was set up in 1985, for providing a focused and coordinated
impetus to the holistic development of women and children who together constitute 70% of the total population of the
country.
1.2       The Department formulates plans, policies and programmes, moots proposals to enact and amend legislation
affecting women and children and guides and coordinates the efforts of both governmental and non-governmental
organizations working in the field of women and child development.   Apart from this, the Department implements
certain innovative programmes for women and children which are in the areas of training and capacity building,
employment and income generation, welfare and support services, awareness generation and gender sensitization.
 
II.        BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2.1       Plan allocation of Rs. 3875.29 crores has been made for the Department of Women and Child Development for
2005-2006 against Rs. 2400.00 crores allocated in 2004-2005, thereby increasing the allocation by Rs. 1475.29 crores. 
The Department has informed that the Plan Allocation has been enhanced in 2005-2006 for the schemes such as
Creches/ Day Care Centres for Children of Working/ Ailing Mother; Swadhar; General Grant to CSWB; GIA to
Research, Publication and Monitoring; Nutrition Education (FNB) and ICDS-General.
 
2.2       The Committee notes that against a Ninth Plan allocation of Rs. 7810.42 crores, expenditure figures stood at Rs.
6248.53 crores.    An enhanced provision of Rs. 13780.00 crores has been made for the Tenth Plan.   The Committee
observes  that during the first three years of the Tenth Plan, allocated funds remained underutilized. Major funding is
there for child welfare related schemes and there has been optimum  utilization of the funds with expenditure exceeding
the allocated funds during 2004-05.   However, status of women related schemes are entirely different.   Allocated
funds for Women related Schemes, in spite of being very less when compared with Child Welfare Schemes remained
underutilized in all the first three years of the Tenth Plan.
2.3       The Committee's attention has also been drawn by another disturbing trend in the manner of utilisation of
allocated funds during the year. Against a total plan allocation of Rs. 2400.00 crores in 2004-2005, expenditure
reported upto December, 2004 is Rs. 1944.48 crores.   However, in the last quarter of the year, expenditure figures

III.       INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
3.1       The ICDS Scheme aims at holistic development of children (0-6 yrs) and pregnant and lactating mothers from
disadvantaged sections, by providing a package of  integrated services comprising of supplementary nutrition,
immunization, health check-up, referral services, pre-school, non-formal education and nutrition and health education.
 
3.2       Introduced in 1975 in 33 blocks, on an experimental basis, the scheme has been gradually expanded to cover
5652 projects till the end of the Ninth Plan.
 
3.3       The Committee observes that Tenth Plan allocation of Rs. 10,391.75 crores for ICDS is more than double of the
provision of Rs. 4980.00 crores in Ninth Plan.   There has been optimum utilisation of allocated funds during the first
three years of the Tenth Plan.   The Committee  notes that against an enhanced  allocation of Rs. 1837.44 in 2004-2005,
expenditure reported upto 18
th March, 2005 is Rs. 1653.97 crores   The Committee, however, observes that during 
2003-2004 and 2004-2005 funds allocated to NE  states remained under utilized .BE provision of Rs. 214.00 crore
during 2003-2004 for NE states had to be reduced to Rs. 196.68 crores at the RE stage and actual expenditure reported
at the end of the year was only Rs. 125.21 crore.   During 2004-2005 against BE provision of Rs. 214.00 crore, only Rs.
144.69 crore could be utilized upto 28th  February, 2005.   In view of considerable  enhancement in the provision for
ICDS during 2005-2006, the Committee recommends that  operationlisation of ICDS projects/AW centres 
should be closely monitored in NE States.
 
3.4       The BE for 2005-06 for this scheme is Rs. 3142.25 crores.  Explaining the reasons for this significant increase in
the allocation for the year 2005-06, the Department informed that since many States were not providing adequately for
supplementary nutrition in view of resource constraints, it has been proposed to support States by providing 50% of
expenditure incurred by them on supplementary nutrition.  A sum of Rs. 1500.00 crore has been earmarked for this
purpose.  The Scheme is also proposed to be expanded to cover 467 additional Projects and 1,88,168 AWCs, in the first
phase.  Kishori Shakti Yojana, which is currently sanctioned for 2000 ICDS Projects, is also proposed to be extended to
all ICDS Projects.  This would involve estimated additional expenditure of about Rs. 300.00 crore in 2005-2006.

competent level.

only after  the feedback is received  from  them,  a final decision should be taken.

3.12     The Committee would also like to reiterate its earlier recommendation that the norm of one Anganwadi Centre
for 700 populations in tribal projects needs to be re-looked for revision in the case of inaccessible areas.  Instead of one
AWC for 700 population, one centre per hillock/ cluster should be the criteria for hilly or desert areas like Manipur and 
Rajasthan and same may be applied in other States having similar geographic locations. 
 
3.13     To run the ICDS programme effectively, Anganwadi workers are the key persons.  The Committee is  happy to
note that initiatives like  incentives/awards and insurance  cover have been taken to motivate the Anganwadi Workers. 
Not only this, a Core Committee has been set up in April, 2003 to review the role and responsibilities of Anganwadi
Workers and Helpers, as envisaged under the ICDS Scheme, and additional responsibilities being assigned to them in
implementation of other Schemes/Programmes of States/Central Government and to suggest a revised charter of duties. 
Its final recommendations are awaited.
 
3.14     The Committee shares the plight of Anganwadi Workers. The Committee, therefore, desires that the final
recommendations may be framed in a time bound period and the implementation process should also be formulated so
that the grievances of Anganwadi workers are settled.
 
3.15     The Committee is perturbed to note the vacancies in regard to the key positions of Anganwadi Centres. The
Committee wonders how the programme can achieve its desired result if so many vacancies existed.  So many
'vacancies must be telling upon', the performance of this major scheme. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
immediate steps should be taken to remove the shortcomings faced in the programme.
 
3.16     The Committee  notes that an intervention for adolescent girls (11-18 years)  Kishori Shakti Yojana was
launched in 2000-01 as part of the ICDS Programme.   The Scheme is being currently        implemented in 2000 ICDS
Projects.   A Pilot Project in 51 identified districts, 2 backward districts  each in major states have been launched by the
Planning Commission in 2002-03.   The purpose  of this Pilot Project is to provide free foodgrains to under nourished
adolescent girls, pregnant and lactating women.   A sum of Rs. 53.96 crore and Rs.40.04 crore was released to the States
in 2002-03 and 2003-04 respectively for the implementation of the Project.   It is proposed to continue the Pilot Project
in 2005-06 also.   The Committee would like to be apprised  about the findings of evaluation study, if done, of these two
interventions under the ICDS programme.
 
3.17     The Committee also notes that besides these two interventions, under  Wheat Based Nutrition Programme,
administered by the Department of Food and Public Distribution, foodgrains - wheat and rice  are allotted  to the
Department of Women and Child Development for further  allocation to States/UTs for supplementary nutrition under
ICDS.   Besides that, under PMGY  being administered  by the Planning Commission, additional Central assistance is
provided  for providing nutrition to children in age group 06 months – 3years through Anganwadi Centres.   From 2005-
06, States / UTs will  be provided assistance upto 50 % of expenditure incurred by them towards supplementary
nutrition.    The Committee feels that with such a variety of nutrition schemes there should be significant improvement
in the targeted beneficiaries in the districts covered.   The Committee would appreciate a Pilot Project for evaluation of
impact of these interventions is undertaken in one district each from all these States.

IV.       WORLD BANK ASSISTED ICDS PROJECTS
4.1       World Bank Assisted ICDS-II Project commenced with effect from 4.10.1999 and originally covered 1003
Blocks in five States of Kerala, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh alongwith a nationwide training
component (UDISHA).  Civil Works and Quality Improvement Activities like IEC, Innovative, Adolescent girls
scheme, etc. have also been provided under the said project in 11 States of Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Bihar,
Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan, Kerala, Orissa and Uttaranchal.  The project completed
its normal period of 5 years on 30.9.2004.  The project has been extended by the World Bank till 30.6.2005.  However,
a proposal to further extend the project by 18 months till 31.3.2006 is under consideration.
 
4.2
              After closure of the Projects, ICDS Blocks covered under the Projects, will be funded by Central Government
under Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) (General) Scheme, as per its approved norms, for which adequate
provisions have been made in the Tenth Five Year Plan.
 
4.3       The Committee hopes that all out efforts will be made for expediting the extension of the scheme without much
loss of time so that implementation of the schemes is not discontinued.

VI.       DAY CARE CENTRES
6.1       The scheme aims to provide day care services for the children (0-5 yrs) of mainly casual, migrant, agricultural
and construction labourers.    The scheme is being implemented by the Central Social Welfare Board and by two
national level voluntary organizations, namely Indian Council for Child Welfare, New Delhi and Bharatiya Adimjati
Sevak Sangh, New Delhi.   The scheme covers 12,470 creches covering 3.11 lakh children.
 
6.2       The allocation for the Tenth Plan for the scheme is Rs. 60 crores.  The Committee notes that although increased
funds are being allocated from the first year of Tenth Plan, utilization thereof has failed to show the same pace.  In
2003-2004, Plan allocation of Rs. 18.00 crore had to be reduced to Rs. 7.00 crore at the RE stage and final expenditure
figures at the end of the year were only Rs. 6.03 crores.  During 2004-2005, there was yet another increase at the
beginning of the year (Rs. 27.00 crore) which had to be drastically reduced to Rs. 15.92 crore and the figures upto 28
th
February, 2005 showed an expenditure of only Rs. 10.49 crore.  An enhanced allocation of Rs. 28.50 crores has been
made for 2005-06.
 
6.3       The Committee observes that delay in the revision of the Scheme is being cited as the main reason for
underutilization of funds.  This position has continued since the beginning of the Tenth Plan.  The Committee however,
finds that only progress  made since then is that this proposal  has received the approval of the Minister and an EFC
memo is under circulation.  Keeping in view the number of procedural formalities to be completed before revision of a
scheme gets final approval, the Committee apprehends that the scheme would continue to be at the revision stage in the
last two years of the Tenth Plan also.
6.4       The Committee finds that enhancement in allocated funds for the scheme of Day Care Centres was also meant
for expansion of the scheme by starting more crèches.  However, the number of crèches continues to remain the same. 
Another striking feature of this scheme is that out of 12,470 creches in the country, largest number (1,340) are
concentrated in Andhra Pradesh, followed by 1,327 in Madhya Pradesh and 1,171 in Maharashtra.   The Committee  is
not happy with the  coverage of children as well as number of crèches in the country.
 
6.5       This scheme is meant to provide day care services for the children (0-5 years) of mainly casual, migrant,
agricultural and construction laborers. The Committee wonders about the constraints being faced by the Central Social
Welfare Board and two national level voluntary organizations, Indian Council for Child Welfare and Bharatiya
Adimjati Sevak Sangh in establishing these crèches in underserved areas.  The Committee recommends that necessary
steps should be taken to increase the number of crèches and make then operational.
 
6.6       The Committee observes that a total amount of Rs. 18,480 per year per crèche is being extended to NGOs every
year.  With the number of creches remaining the same, variation in the level of expenditure every year does not seem to
be proper.  The Committee would like to be enlightened in this regard. 
 
6.7       Under the National Creche Fund, total number of crèches is 4,885, maximum number of crèches are set up in
Andhra Pradesh, followed by 594 in West Bengal and 400 in Maharashtra.  The Committee observes that under this
scheme, crèches are sanctioned to NGOs/Mahila Mandals for 5 years.  Thereafter, crèches are to be run on self-
supporting basis. The Committee would like to know about the assessment about crèches which have completed five

160threport of Committee on HRD

6.8       The Committee was also informed that  during the financial year 2002-2003, there has been a saving of Rs.

 OBSERVATONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
II.        BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
            The Committee expresses its serious concern over major part of the expenditure being rushed through in the last
quarter of the year.  Such a trend, in fact, points towards some inherent drawbacks both in the system of flow of funds
as well as in the delivery mechanism adopted by the Department.  The Committee fails to understand the purpose of
formulating Annual Action Plan for each and every scheme, clearly identifying both the physical and financial targets
to be achieved in every quarter.  The Committee calls upon the Department to discuss with the States the structural and
functional problems that are responsible not only for poor utilization of resources but also manpower and planning.
Resources both human and fiscal meant for the welfare of women and children - bedrock of our future society and
economy can not be allowed to go waste.   (Para 2.4)
 
III.       INTEGRATED CHILD DEVELOPMENT SERVICES (ICDS)
            In view of considerable  enhancement in the provision for ICDS during 2005-2006, the Committee recommends
that  operationlisation of ICDS projects/AW centres  should be closely monitored in NE States. (Para 3.3)

proposed additional projects/AWCs. (Para 3.7)
            The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department in providing more emphasis on supplementary
nutrition and expansion of the project to more areas from 2005-2006.  The Committee recommends that efforts be made
to utilize the amounts for these activities judiciously to avoid any underutilization. (Para 3.9)

survey. (Para 3.19)
            The Committee recommends that for construction of anganwadi centre, provision of drinking water etc.
feasibility of taking benefits from schemes of other Departments like Rural Development, Panchayati Raj etc could be
explored. The funds from MPLADS can also be utilized for this purpose. (Para 3.20)
Committee reiterates that different schemes of child nutrition being implemented by various
Ministries/Departments and also at State level need to be brought under one nodal agency with special focus on tribal
and remote areas of the country.  Interdepartmental meetings for setting nutrition goals and framing the strategy for
achieving the same do not seem to have led to any visible improvement.  The Committee, therefore, reiterates that the
Department mandated to look after the welfare of children and women has to play a more pro-active role. (Para 3.21)
 
IV.       WORLD BANK ASSISTED ICDS PROJECTS
            The Committee hopes that all out efforts will be made for expediting the extension of the scheme without much
loss of time so that implementation of the schemes is not discontinued. (Para 4.3)

VI.       DAY CARE CENTRES
            The Committee observes that delay in the revision of the Scheme is being cited as the main reason for
underutilization of funds.  This position has continued since the beginning of the Tenth Plan.  The Committee however,
finds that only progress  made since then is that this proposal  has received the approval of the Minister and an EFC
memo is under circulation.  (Para 6.3)
The Committee  is not happy with the  coverage of children as well as number of crèches in the country. (Para
6.4)
            This scheme is meant to provide day care services for the children (0-5 years) of mainly casual, migrant,
agricultural and construction laborers. The Committee wonders about the constraints being faced by the Central Social
Welfare Board and two national level voluntary organizations, Indian Council for Child Welfare and Bharatiya
Adimjati Sevak Sangh in establishing these crèches in underserved areas.  The Committee recommends that necessary
steps should be taken to increase the number of crèches and make then operational. (Para 6.5)
            With the number of creches remaining the same, variation in the level of expenditure every year does not seem
to be proper.  The Committee would like to be enlightened in this regard.  (Para 6.6)
Thereafter, crèches are to be run on self-supporting basis. The Committee would like to know about the
assessment about crèches which have completed five years of their establishment and are running on a self-supporting
basis. (Para 6.7)
            The Committee is not satisfied with the monitoring mechanism of Department.  The very fact that unspent 
balances are available with the implementing  agencies indicates that crèches are not  providing the services for which
they are meant.   The Committee desires that steps should be taken for removal of constraints being faced in the
implementation of this scheme by regular monitoring and interaction between  the State Boards and the CSWB.   The
Committee also recommends that a special drive for settlement of accounts should be initiated  to stop
misutilisation/underutilization of funds. (Para 6.9)
 
VII.     CONDENSED COURSES FOR WOMEN EDUCATION
 
The Committee is constrained to note that in a number of States, in spite of accounts being settled and
utilization certificates being received upto a particular year, unspent balances still remain with the State Boards.  The
Committee has been given to understand that funds for second year batch are released, subject to the condition of 50%
pass percentage, satisfactory performance and settlement of accounts.  The Committee is of the view that if this norm
had been strictly adhered to, unspent balances, would not have been there.  The Committee, recommends that special

VIII.    BALIKA SAMRIDDHI YOJANA (BSY)
            The Committee expresses its serious concern about the way the Balika Samriddhi Yojana is being implemented.

the third year, the scheme had to be reviewed again and recast.  The Committee is left with no other alternative but to
conclude that the scheme suffers from inherent drawbacks, which continues to be there in spite of review of the scheme
being done not once, but on two occasions.  The Committee doubts whether the funds released so far have reached the
targetted beneficiaries.  (Para 8.5)
The Committee also feels that in the event of a decision being taken for continuance of this Scheme, then very
effective monitoring mechanism and also sufficient publicity for making the targeted beneficiaries aware will have to be
an essential component of the Scheme. (Para 8.6)

            The Committee  notes that the rate of interest on credit to NGOs is 8% p.a.  However, the NGOs further give

Committee feels that the rate of interest charged is too high when compared to the market rate.  The Committee fails to
understand the rationale for such a difference in the rate of interest from 5 -18% to be charged by the Govt. from the
implementing agencies and beneficiaries.  The beneficiaries are supposed  to be poor women.    The Committee wonders
whether they can ever be in a position to pay back loan taken by them.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
rate of interest should be reduced to facilitate women to take advantage of the scheme. (Para 14.4)

improvement in the existing scheme of ‘Swadhar’. (Para 15.4)

was given to understand that there is a delay in obtaining Utilisation Certificates from NGOs/States. (Para 15.5)
            The Committee recommends that proper monitoring of the scheme is the need of the hour to set the process of
obtaining Utilisation Certificates from all the States/UTs. (Para 15.6)
