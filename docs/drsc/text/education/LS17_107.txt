I.             Methods of computing drop out rate
Observation of the Committee
            
The Committee during its visit had held discussions with many NGOs  and experts working in the field of education and
felt that there is a lot of ambiguity about the method of commuting the drop out rate.   In view thereof the Committee wishes
here to redefine the definition of the popular methods which are used for computing drop out rates.   The three methods for
determining drop-out rates are as under:-
a)                  Method based on grade-to-grade progression rates.
b)                  Method based on ratio of grade V enrolment to grade I enrolment of 5 years before; and
c)                  Methods based on individual students data on their year to year progress in schools.
The Committee recommends that the three above-mentioned methods may be used as parameters to locate dropouts for
which a Retention Register should be maintained.
Comments of the Department
Responses have been received from ten States and UTs (Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Andaman and Nicobar, Daman,
Tripura, Pondicherry, Goa, Maharashtra, Haryana, Arunachal Pradesh and Gujarat).   All have agreed with the recommendation
of the Parliamentary Standing Committee that the three methods mentioned should be used as parameters to locate drop out for
which a Retention Register should be maintained.
            On a specific query about the response of the remaining States, the Department informed that the issue was inter-alia
discussed in the meeting of the State Education Secretaries at New Delhi on August 28-29, 2000.    Most of the Secretaries were
in agreement with the recommendation of the Committee and felt that there was need to review the existing procedures in this
regard.   Tamil Nadu has completed a sample study based on retrospective cohort method.  Some of the States like Andhra
Pradesh, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, and Haryana have also initiated  such studies.
II.            Enrolment Ratio
Observation of the Committee
2.1        The Committee is of the opinion that enrolment ratio and drop-out rates are co-related.   Accurate drop out rate can be
arrived at only on the basis of actual enrolment ratio.  However, the  Committee understands that this is not the position in most
of the States.   At present, what is happening is that Headmaster of a primary school simply shows all school going  children in
the village as enrolled in his school in class I.    No consideration is being given to the percentage of children actually attending
the school on a regular basis.   Besides that, problem of double enrolment was often found in evidence by the Committee during
its State visits.   The Committee feels that accurate enrolment figures can be easily arrived at by involving the Village Education
Committees and local youth in this task.   The Committee also recommends that the admission age of the child in Class I should
be strictly adhered to in order to avoid the instances of fictitious enrolment.
Comments of the Department
            All States and UTs. whose response has been received endorse the recommendation of the Parliamentary Standing
Committee.   Village Education Committees are being involved to arrive at accurate enrolment figures.   In DPEP, involvement
of VECs for microplanning, which includes  household surveys, has been stressed.   Efforts will be made to incorporate the
valuable recommendations of the Committee while implementing the proposed Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan – an effort for UEE in a
mission mode following a holistic and convergent approach.
            The Department has further informed that the recommendation of the Committee has been incorporated in the draft

children and also universal retention by 2010.    The Educational Monitoring and Information System (EMIS) under Sarva

IV.            Opening of more Primary and Upper Primary Schools
Observation of the Committee
4.3        The Committee recommends that the norms for opening new schools in remote, hilly and desert areas should be relaxed
and the Village Education Committees (VECs) should be empowered to make survey of the existing situation, identification of
non-schooling areas and preparation of plan for facilitating the State Governments to open good quality non-formal education
centres.   Keeping in view the growth rate of population, the Central Government should also make plans for creating extra
upper primary schooling facilities equal to the primary schools in the country.
Comments of the Department
            Under the revised NFE scheme named Alternative and Innovative Education, provision to establish Education Guarantee
Centres in unserved habitations has been made. Under the proposed Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan upgradation of primary to upper
primary schools is an item of financing.  Vertical expansion of DPEP to cover upper primary is also under consideration.
            The Department further clarified that the  revised EGS & AIE scheme provides for setting up of alternative schools
commonly named as EGS(Education Guarantee Scheme)  schools in all habitations that do not have primary schools within a
radius of one Km.  This would specially benefit hilly, desert and remote areas.   It was expected that about 50,000 EGS schools
may be supported under the scheme during 2000-2001, if proposals were received from the States.  It also provides for running
of alternative schools for upper primary stage for which per pupil cost has been enhanced from Rs.580/- to Rs.1200/- per
annum.  The other intervention for older children in the age group 11-14  will also be possible in the form of residential camp,
remedial teaching and special camps for adolescent  girls.
            Under this scheme, the community and Village Education Committees(VECs of Panchayats) have been given a central
role in running of the schools including conduct of micro-planning, selection of the volunteer teachers, payment of honorarium
and overall management of the school/centre.  The scheme also provides for setting up of upper primary level centres.
            The proposed scheme of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan envisages :
-
                Upgradation and provision of upper primary schools/sections as per requirement based on number of children
completing the primary stage up to a ceiling of one upper primary school/section for every two primary schools.
-                Provision of one teacher and one classroom for every class and a room for the headmaster in every upper primary
school.
 
If also provides for running of alternative schools for upper primary stage for which per pupil cost has been enhanced
from Rs.580/- to Rs.1200/- per child per annum.  The other intervention for older children in the age group 11-14  will also be
possible in the form of residential camp, remedial teaching and special camps for adolescent  girls.
Observation of the Committee
4.4        The Committee hopes that a funding pattern under JRY worked out by the Department of Education in consultation with
the Ministry of Rural Development would accelerate the pace of construction of schools buildings.
Comments of the Department
From the savings under the JGSY, funds for primary school buildings can be provided on a 75-25  sharing with States.
V.       Joyful Learning
Observation of the Committee
5.1             The Committee during its visits has seen that the present system of imparting education to the children is very
traditional and rigid and is not based on scientific pattern.   The Committee is of the view that Text-books should be colourful
and attractive from the children point of view and oral joyful teaching learning mechanism should be introduced at least upto
IIIrd standard as the Committee observed that the maximum rate of drop out is from Class I to Class III.

5.2        The Committee feels that schools can be made an attractive place and learning a joyful experience, if teaching is made
child centred and activity oriented as has been done in the `Anandapath’  schooling system adopted by the Government of West
Bengal and also by production of colourful and attractive textbooks and low cost teaching aids.   The creation of these items
would automatically result in improving the retention rate.
Comments of the Department
            The task of making learning joyful has been undertaken in many states, especially under DPEP.   New textbooks and
workbooks, teacher training modules, etc.   have been developed to make learning joyful.
            Government of Gujarat has informed that already more than 30,000 Balmitra Vargas have been established that provide
activity based learning to students of class I.   New textbooks prepared under Lok Jumbish Project in Rajasthan and under DPEP
in Kerala, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradsh and Maharashtra have focused on
activity-based and child-centred learning.
VI.            Motivation of Teacher
Observation of the Committee
6.1        The Committee feels that lack of motivation on the part of a section of teachers is indirectly responsible for dismal
retention rate of students in the schools.   Dedicated teachers are required for making the stay of a child in school a joyful
experience.   However, at present this is not very much visible  primarily in the Government set-up of schools.   The Committee
is of the considered view that inculcation of motivation can be successfully attempted by making it an essential component of
teachers’ training programme. 
Comments of the Department
NCERT has noted the Committee’s useful suggestion of making motivational training a part of teacher training
modules.   NCERT is providing academic support to SCERTS to organize teachers training programme on an ongoing basis.
Observation of the Committee
6.2        The Committee finds that almost all the states have the required strength of teachers.   But mere filling up of the existing
vacancies of teachers is not the end, but it is just the beginning.   The Committee feels that motivated, dedicated and fully
trained teachers are the need of the hour.   The Committee finds that all the State Councils of Educational Research and Training
(SCERTs) are not fully equipped to impart adequate training to the teachers.   Even many of the SCERTs  personnel require
training by master trainers.   The Committee, therefore, recommends that NCERT should launch a pilot programme in close
collaboration with the SCERTs  for the training of teachers.   This becomes all the more necessary as studies have pointed out
that the utilisation of teaching learning material has not been very satisfactory because of lack of training of teachers.   The
Committee  is of the considered view that if teachers are imparted training on how to use available equipment effectively in a
classroom teaching situation, it is bound to have a positive impact on improvement in quality of teaching.
Comments of the Department
NCERT has informed that SCERTs and DIETs are being provided academic support by NCERT to equip them to
organize training programmes on a continuing basis.
Observation of the Committee
6.3        The Committee is in full agreement with the findings of  evaluation of the `Operation Blackboard’ that there is a need
to:-
(i)                 streamline the purchase procedure with inbuilt quality controls;
(ii)                Provide for greater involvement of teachers in selection of teaching aids;
(iii)              Develop better linkage between teaching aids and curriculum requirements; and
(iv)              Motivate teachers to improve teachers aids from inexpensive locally available materials.
Comments of the Department
            States are making efforts in this direction.   The Central Government has been emphasizing the need for decentralized
procurement of teaching aids with full involvement of teachers and representatives of PRIs.   In DPEP, a provision has been
made for  Rs. 500/- grant for the teacher to prepare/purchase teaching learning materials of his/her choice.   The teaching
learning material grants are contextually situated and developed as per the curriculum requirement in DPEP.

Observation  of the Committee
During the visit of the Committee to certain States, it observed that there were some districts maintaining very high
drop-out rates and among the low level drop-out districts, there were some blocks having high drop-out rate.  There are also
some villages with high drop-out rate in such districts.  Thus, the drop-out problem is prevalent throughout the country.  The
Committee feels that there is a need to locate the exact area of high drop-out rates.  In  Shiksha Karmi and Lok Jumbish
Projects, at the village level, the school mapping system exercise has proved very fruitful in enrolling all out of school children
in the age group of 6-14 years.   The Committee feels that this kind of school mapping exercise should be undertaken in all the
States in order to get the exact picture of the enrolled and drop-out children.   The Committee recommends that the local
bodies/PRIs/PTAs should be involved in this task. 
Comments of the Department
            The proposed Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan provides for school mapping and micro planning with the involvement of the local
community and PRIs.  School mapping and micro planning exercises have been undertaken in DPEP.
X.                 Setting up Junior Technical/Vocational Education Institute
Observation  of the Committee
10.1            The Committee was given to understand during the field visits by the State Governments that the drop-out rates are
very high in classes VII or VIII.   The primary reasons for this situation is that there is a general feeling among parents/students
that no purpose would be served by continuing studies after class VIII.   To check this high drop-out rate, the Committee is of
the opinion that some technical/vocational education institutes should be set up for imparting two years’ certificate courses in
the field of craft, carpentry, mechanical, food processing and poultry farms etc. after VIIIth Standard.   The Committee feels that
setting up of such institutes would not only give a pragmatic approach towards the educational system but it will also help in
earning their livelihood and for inculcating an attitude towards vocationalisation.
10.2
         The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Education and the Planning Commission should initiate
action for setting up of technical/vocational education institutes parallel to the secondary stage in some selected districts on an
experimental basis and make budgetary provision accordingly in the 9th Plan.
Comments of the Department
           The scheme of vocational education is under review at present.   The valuable suggestions of the Parliamentary Standing
Committee would be taken note of while formulating the revised scheme.
XII.     Mid-Day Meal Scheme
Observation of the Committee
12.1            The Mid-Day Meal Scheme was launched by the Central Govt. on 15 th August, 1995 to give a boost to the
universalisation of Primary Education by impacting upon enrolment, attendance, retention and the nutrition needs of children in
Primary Classes.   The Committee has noticed that cooked meal is not being served in all the States and instead 3 kg. Wheat/rice
is being distributed to the children in many states without adhering to the stipulated condition of 80% attendance in a month.  
The Committee was apprised that the attendance rate on the day of distribution only remained very high.
12.2
            The Committee feels that to have a direct impact of the Mid-day Meal Scheme on the retention side, to check the
problem of absenteeism and drop-out and also to provide nutrition to the school going children instead of distributing 3 kg.
Wheat/rice, cooked meal should be served.
12.3            During the study visits, the Committee also noticed that pilferages are taking place and the distribution system of
foodgrains has also not been developed systematically due to which untimely and short supply are the frequent phenomena
which could be judged from the fact that in some of the States/UTs.  like Rajasthan, West Bengal and Andaman & Nicobar
Islands, foodgrains have not been distributed to the tragetted group even once in the academic session of 1996-97.
12.4            The  Committee makes the following recommendations to correct the above- mentioned situation:-
(1)          Cooked meal should be served;
(2)          Strict monitoring system delegating the power to Village Level Education Committee or PTA should be evolved;
(3)          Co-ordination between the Civil Supply and Education Department of State Governments needs to be
strengthened;
(4)         Local youth may be involved for the preparation of the cooked food; and

107th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20HRD/107threport.htm[01-07-2016 15:15:18]
(5)         Mid-day meal should be provided through a separate mechanism without disturbing the normal academic
work of Teachers.
(6)         The Noon Meal Programme of Tamil Nadu Government may be adopted as a model for implementing
Mid-Day Meal Scheme.
Comments of the Department
            It was expected that all States would switch over to the serving of hot cooked meals within a period of two years of
commencement of the scheme i.e., by 1997-98.   However, most states/UTs have, so far, opted for distribution of foodgrains
under the programme.   Five States namely, Gujarat, Kerala, Orissa, Tamil Nadu and Madhya Pradesh (limited to 174 tribal
blocks) and Union Territory of Pondicherry are providing cooked meals.   Government of UT of Delhi has been supplying
processed food in urban slums.   Haryana  and J&K which started with a cooked meal reverted to distribution of foodgrains as
did Madhya Pradesh for the non-tribal blocks in the States.
            The State Govts. are expected  to bear the cost of conversion of foodgrains into a cooked meal though support for
construction of a kitchen shed and wages for a cook are available from funds from programmes of the Ministry of Rural Areas
and Employment.   The main reason for not switching over to hot cooked meals and instead providing foodgrains alone is the
paucity of funds.   It has been estimated that in the Ninth Plan alone, Rs. 18,000 crore would be required to convert foodgrains
to hot cooked meals, as against a requirement of Rs. 12,615 crores for the supply of foodgrains alone.
            The guidelines of NP-NSPE provided that the impelenting agencies of the programme will be Local Bodies/Authorities
such as panchayats and Nagar Palikas.  The Local Bodies will have flexibility to organize and to decide the type of food to be
provided and are also expected to develop institutional arrangements for providing cooked/pre-cooked food in association with
the Village Education Committees (VECs), School Parent Teacher Associations, women/DWACRA groups and other
organizations.
            At the State level a nodal Department is responsible for implementation of the programme.   In case, the designated
department is other than the Education Department, necessary linkages are established between the designated department and
the Primary Education Department.   Towards this end, it has been suggested to the State Governments that an institutional
mechanism of a Committee under the Chairmanship of the Chief Secretary/Additional Chief Secretary/Development
Commissioner be constituted to oversee the implementation of the programme.   The Committee should include the Secretaries
and Heads of Department of all the concerned Departments and is expected to ensure the convergence of primary education,
primary health care, early childhood care and education, nutrition and other related services.
            In order to provide logistic support, employment  schemes have already been linked with this programme.   As stated in
the guidelines, expenditure on remuneration of cooks/helpers and construction of sheds under this programme is to be met out of
the JRY/NRY funds of the Ministry of Rural Areas and Employment and Ministry of Urban Areas and Employment  for rural
and urban areas respectively.
            The Department has requested State Chief Secretaries and Education Departments to ensure that teachers are not
involved in the distribution of foodgrains to the extent possible and that adequate support be provided to them at school level
through local bodies and community based organizations such as Mahila Mandals, Women Groups and PTAs.
            Under NP-NSPE the implementing agencies/Local Bodies/authorities such as Panchayats and Nagarpalikas have the
flexibility to organize, to decide the type of food to be provided subject to the food being wholesome and having a calorific
value equivalent to 100 gms of wheat/rice per student per day.  Eating habits being different from state to state, it may not be
feasible to adopt a uniform model for all parts of the country.   The Giri Committee   on Mid-Day Meals had considered various
models including the Tamil Nadu model, before making recommendations for introduction of NP-NSPE on the existing
pattern.   Nevertheless, State Governments have the flexibility to adopt the scheme to their requirements.
            The recommendations of the Committee have been communicated to the States.   Steps have also been taken by Govt. of
India to ensure closer monitoring of the implementation of the programme.
            A study of the programme by an independent Consultant, Operations Research Group, New Delhi in ten states found that
in Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal there has been a boost to enrolment whereas in Gujarat, Haryana,
Jammu and Kashmir, Karnataka, Orissa and Rajasthan the programme has made a positive impact on attendance and retention.

XIV.            Distribution of Free Text Books

XV.      Girls Education/Women’s Development
Observation of the Committee
15.2      The Committee recommends for giving suitable incentives to girl child, like providing free uniform, free
teaching/learning materials and construction of toilet and providing drinking water facilities particularly in the girls school/co-
education schools.   The Committee also recommends that a Special Management Structure should be set up wherein women
should be placed in the key position and at the micro level women’s groups should be engaged for the development of the girl
child.
15.3      As women have traditionally played a subordinate role in society,  so they have been conditioned into thinking of
themselves as inferior to men, to be tolerated only if they toil, fetch, carry and procreate.   Financial burden is only hurdle in the
education of boys but it becomes an insurmountable barrier when the question of education of girls arises.   Sending her to
school would also mean an additional loss of income because the mother will have to stay away from work to look after the
younger ones and to do the household chores.   But education can be an enduring and strong factor in women’s empowerment.
15.4      The Committee feels that unless a systematic effort is made to improve women’s access to education it will not be
possible to achieve the objective of education for all by the year 2000.   Experience has shown that the rural women have  not
kept pace with the ‘Sathin’s’ progress.   It is now a recognized fact that instead of focussing on just one woman, `Sathin’ village
level activity should be central around a Group of women.   The Committee is of the view that this collective, the Samooh, after
adequate orientation and training would enable the village women to take an interest and play an effective role in community
activities especially related to Education.
Comments of the Department
            Most of the States are providing free education for girls upto upper primary/secondary level with provision of free text
books and uniforms for SC/ST girls.            In pursuance of the National Agenda for Governance (NAG) 1998, this Ministry has
proposed launching a scheme called “National Strategy for ensuring greater participation of women in educational field.”
The scheme envisages developing multiple facilities to provide access and other incentives for provision of girls education and
for their retention as also creation of assets in favour  of rural poor, particularly the SCs/STs and educationally backward
minorities.   These include girls’ hostels, book banks, Navodaya Schools and remedial coaching classes etc.

Observation of the Committee
4.1        The scheme of `Operation Blackboard’ was launched in 1987 with the objective to bring about both qualitative and

1551.68 crores from  1987-88 till 31st March, 1995.   However, till 31st March, 1995 only 81%  of the total Central funds i.e. Rs.

2617.26 crores from 1987-88 till 31 st March, 2000.  The utilization percentage of the funds sanctioned for teaching learning
equipment and salaries of teachers appointed under OB is 86.29%.   Thus, there is an improvement of over 5%.  Most of the

Recommendation of the Committee
            The Committee expresses its grave concern over non-utilisation of around 14% of the financial assistance under
Operation Blackboard given to the States by the Centre.  Basic infrastructure in most of the schools in the country is still lacking

4.2        Even after a decade of the launching of Operation Blackboard, the Committee finds that so far as basic infrastructure is

IX       Non-Formal Education Programme/Prehar Pathshala as a Backup Method
Observation of the Committee
9.1      Out of school children are being taken care of under the Non-Formal Education Scheme since 1979.   Almost all the
States are running NFE centres.   NGOs are also contributing a lot in this field.   However, even after two decades, the NFE
scheme could not achieve the desired target to bring back the non-formal students to the mainstream education.   There are a
varied number of factors responsible for it.    Poor infrastructure, lack of motivated and trained instructors, very low
honorarium for instructor, lack of community participation, no linkage between Formal and Non-Formal System of education,
NFE being treated as second grade education can be cited as the major reasons for NFE Scheme’s failure.
9.2      The Committee is of the considered view that revamping of entire NFE Scheme with special emphasis on the following
points is urgently required:-
a)
         Creation of Basic facilities at NFE Centres.
b)         Increase in honorarium for instructors.
c)         Involvement of community and  PRIs.
d)         Training of Instructors.
e)         Mechanism for effective supervision and monitoring of NFE Scheme.
Comments of the Department
            The revised scheme of NFE called Alternative and Innovative Education, is in the process of approval.   It has taken into
account the issues suggested by the Parliamentary Standing Committee.   The new scheme is based on different existing,
successful, replicable NFE models in the country.
            Under the revised scheme,
(i)         NFE and the formal system are to be dovetailed into one at the district level where a detailed school mapping
exercise will be undertaken to locate precisely the un served habitations and educationally less privileged areas.
(ii)        Both the community and the PRIs will be actively involved in sharing responsibilities.   They will create the
demand, identify teachers where necessary and provide learning space where required;
(iii)       Honoraria for instructors have been increased five fold.  All costing patterns have been made more realistic;
(iv)       Adequate training and supervision are built in.  The states are free to choose from a set of options.   Non-
academic monitoring and supervision can even be given to the PRIs (as has been done with the Education
Guarantee Scheme in Madhya Pradesh).  Regular training will be organized through the CRCs, BRCs and
DIETs.
             The Department was requested to clarify as to whether NFE under the revised scheme of Alternative and Innovative
Education will be able to provide recognition to non-formal education at  par with the formal education.  The Department
informed that the revised scheme aimed to provide education of a quality comparable /equivalent to the formal schools.   Several
inputs have been included in the scheme that would ensure quality including a minimum duration of 4 hours for the central
school, at least 30 days training for the volunteer teacher before the opening of the centre, regular academic support, 2 days
monthly review and planning meeting etc.
  Recommendation of the Committee
            The Committee notes that the Alternative and Innovative Education Scheme has received Cabinet approval and
modalities  for its  implementation are being finalized in consultation with the State Governments.   The Committee is of the
view that   a consensus on operationalisation of the scheme in various states should  be arrived at without further loss of time,  
so that the scheme is implemented, as considerable time has already been taken in clearance  of the scheme.

CONTINUATION OF PLAN PROJECTS
            The Committee notes that a long term financial partnership with States has been envisaged under Sarva Shiksha 
Abhiyan.   The Committee hopes that this provision would prove to be of much assistance to State Governments in
implementing the various components of  Sarva Shiksha  Abhiyan thereby resulting in maximum coverage of children under
elementary education. (Para14)
COMMUNITY INVOLVEMENT
            In the 71 st Report of the Committee on the Problem of Drops outs, the Committee had suggested for a meeting of the
Education Ministers of all State Governments to work out modalities to provide legal status to the Panchayati Raj Institutions.  
However, no such meeting in this regard has taken place till now.   The Committee in this respect again reiterates that a meeting
of the Education Ministers of all States should be convened to discuss this issue. (Para 17.2)
