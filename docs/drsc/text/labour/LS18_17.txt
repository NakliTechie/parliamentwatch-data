I. INTRODUCTORY 
1. The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE) 
continued its unwavering commitment towards strengthening India’s skilling 
and entrepreneurship ecosystem in the last year. The Ministry’s initiatives were 
aimed at not only expanding the reach and quality of skill development efforts 
but also ensuring deeper convergence across Ministries, sectors, and 
geographies and ensuring industrial alignment of training efforts. MSDE played 
a pivotal role in reinforcing the skilling narrative as an enabler of India’s 
demographic dividend, economic growth, and global competitiveness. Building 
on the foundation of a decade of the Skill India Mission, the Ministry ushered in 
a new phase of systemic transformation through convergence with flagship 
Government programmes, partnerships with industry and international 
stakeholders, enhancement of institutional capacities, and a strong focus on 
inclusivity and future-readiness.  
2. The National Policy for Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (NPSDE), 
launched in 2015 by the Ministry, acknowledges the need for an effective 
roadmap for promotion of skilling and entrepreneurship as the key to a 
successful growth strategy. The Vision of the Policy is to create an ecosystem of 
employment by Skilling on a large Scale at Speed with high Standards and to 
promote a culture of innovation-based entrepreneurship which can generate 
wealth and employment so as to ensure Sustainable livelihoods for all citizens 
in the country.  
3. The Mission of NPSDE is to: 
i) Create a demand for skilling across the country; 
ii) Correct and align skilling with required competencies; 
iii) Connect the supply of skilled human resources with sectoral 
demands; 
iv) Certify and assess in alignment with global and national standards; 
and 
v) Catalyse an ecosystem wherein productive and innovative 
entrepreneurship germinates, sustains and grows leading to creation 
of a more dynamic entrepreneurial economy and more formal wage 
employment. 
 
4. The National Skill Development Mission (NSDM) was approved by the 
Union Cabinet in July 2015. The Mission seeks to Create an end-to-end 
implementation framework for skill development, which provides opportunities 
for life-long learning. This includes: incorporation of skilling in the school 
curriculum, providing opportunities for qualities long and short-term skill 

2 
 
training, by providing gainful employment and ensuring career progression that 
meets the aspirations of trainees.  
5. The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship is supported by 
its associated organizations like - the Directorate General of Training (DGT), 
National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET), and National 
Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) in its endeavour to foster seamless 
interaction and collaboration to overcome the challenges within India’s skilling 
and entrepreneurship landscape.  
6. An analysis of the financial and physical performance of the Ministry of 
Skill Development and Entrepreneurship and its Attached and Subordinate 
Organizations in the process of implementation of certain Schemes/Projects has 
been made in the Report.

10. The Committee observed that there has been a significant increase in the 
BE during 2024-25 (28.50%), 2025-26 (34.95%) and 2026-27 (62.05%) as 
compared to the previous year. However, the RE was scaled down with drastic 
reduction from Rs.6100.10 crore to Rs.2703.54 crore during FY 2025-26. When 
asked to state the reasons along with the schemes/projects to be taken up with 
the enhanced allocation and the pre-emptive measures proposed to ensure that 
the budgetary allocation for 2026-27 is not reduced at RE stage, the Ministry 
responded stated as under:-  
“Reason for increase in BE and low expenditure during F.Y. 2024-25. 
Budgetary allocation for the year 2024-25 was majorly increased by Rs.1000 crore over BE-2023-24 for the new Centrally Sponsored Scheme namely “New ITI Upgradation Scheme” announced in the Regular Budget of F.Y 2024-25 and additional funds of Rs.400 crore in ‘Skill India Programme(SIP)’.  
‘New ITI Upgradation Scheme’ - After announcement, the Scheme was formulated & appraised by EFC in the F.Y 2024-25, however, the Cabinet approved this Scheme only on 07.05.2025 Therefore, the projection under RE-2024-25 was curtailed. This scheme has now 

4 
 
been renamed as “Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs” (PM-SETU).  
‘Skill India Programme’ – In anticipation of the Cabinet approval of this scheme, budget estimates were projected on the basis of year-wise allocations recommended by the EFC. However, the scheme was approved by the Cabinet only on 07.02.2025. Meanwhile, expenditure had to be limited to the extent allowed by DoE from time to time. Department of Expenditure (DoE) did not allow MSDE to incur expenditure between October, 2024 to January, 2025. This adversely impacted the training and only after Cabinet approval in February, 2025, the Ministry was able to book expenditure in the last quarter.  F.Y. 2025-26 
PM-SETU Scheme - Budgetary allocation of MSDE have been majorly increased for the years 2025-26 and 2026-27 due to additional allocation of Rs.2000 crore and Rs.3140 crore respectively in the PM-SETU Scheme. In this Scheme, release of Central Government contribution is contingent upon approval of Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs), on-boarding of Anchor Industry Partners (AIPs), and subsequent formation of industry-led Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs). As this is a State-driven, proposal-based mechanism, States/UTs are required to invite relevant AIPs and finalize SIPs before funds can be utilized through SPVs. Since, as on date, no SIP has been finalized and the process remains in the preparatory stage with EOIs and RFPs under evaluation, allocations were rationalized and subsequently decreased at the RE-2025-26 stage.  The enhanced allocation for FY 2026-27 is justified as the PM-SETU project has now moved beyond the preparatory phase. With groundwork completed, States/UTs are expected to progress past the Expression of Interest/ Request for Proposal (EOI/RFP) stage into evaluation of proposals submitted by Anchor Industry Partners (AIPs). In addition, five NSTIs are envisaged to onboard Lead Industry Partners (LIPs). As Sectoral Implementation Plans (SIPs) are approved, expenditure will be driven by the formation of Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) under Component I and Industry Management Committees (IMCs) under Component II. This progression marks the transition from planning to execution; hence this progression justifies the enhanced allocation of Rs. 6140.50 crore for FY 2026-27.  Skill India Programme - Following the Cabinet approval and drawing upon the learnings from the previous financial years, the Ministry has taken several corrective measures in FY 2025–26 to strengthen quality, compliance, and robustness in implementation before allocation of targets. Key measures undertaken include: - i. Issuance of a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to streamline the assessment and certification process.  ii. Revision of monitoring guidelines to strengthen oversight of 

5 
 
training delivery and ensure adherence to scheme norms.  iii. Rigorous scrutiny of proposals in compliance with extant guidelines prior to approval and target allocation.  Mandating face authentication and geo-tagged attendance for candidates, trainers, and assessors under PMKVY 4.0 to enhance transparency and monitoring.  iv. Strengthening monitoring and independent oversight through Kaushal Samiksha Kendras (KSK) enabling large-scale virtual and physical inspections, supplemented by regular field inspections by State and District coordinators. v. Initiation of strict disciplinary actions against non-compliant centres, including blacklisting, suspension of centres, stoppage of payments, issuance of Show Cause Notices, cancellation of Accreditation and Affiliation, and filing of FIRs, to deter future violations.  vi. Capturing candidate feedback through NIC’s Central Communication Layer (CCL).  To ensure optimal utilization of funds in FY 2025–26, the Ministry has undertaken concerted efforts to promote high-quality skill training through credible and well-established institutions including State Skill Development Missions (SSDMs), Institutes of National Importance (INIs), Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) and other reputed organizations. The Ministry has also hosted a structured online application form with necessary validations on the Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) to invite and process training proposals from industries / industry associations / SMEs, thereby strengthening industry participation and demand-driven skilling under the scheme.  
Integrated Scheme in Skilling Architecture – New Central Sector 
Scheme 
Initially total budget of the Ministry was Rs.9285.80 crore for the year 2026-27. Meanwhile, a new Central Sector Scheme namely “Integrated Scheme in Skilling Architecture” has been announced in the Regular Budget of F.Y 2026-27 with the total budget outlay of Rs.600 crore. This new scheme is under formulation. Consequently, total budget of the Ministry has been increased from Rs.9285.80 crore to Rs.9885.80 crore leading to 62.05% increment for F.Y 2026-27 as compared to last year’s budget”.

about the reasons for the high attrition at the assessment stage and whether it 
is attributable to a shortage of assessors, candidate disengagement, or perceived 
low certification value, the Minstry replied as follows:                                                                                  

45 
 
 
“The attrition observed at the assessment stage under PMKVY 4.0 is 
primarily attributable to candidate dropouts before the assessment is 
conducted. A significant proportion of enrolled beneficiaries, particularly 
out-of-school and out-of-college youth, exhibit irregular attendance 
patterns. As PMKVY 4.0 mandates a minimum 50% attendance for 
candidates to be eligible for assessment, many trainees who do not meet 
this requirement are unable to appear for assessments and consequently 
drop out of the programme. The observed gap is therefore largely linked 
to candidate irregularity and non-fulfilment of eligibility conditions,

65. Financial Utilisation vs. Outcomes 
FY 2024–25 saw 100% fund utilisation (Rs.1,538 crore) but only 13.3 lakh 
certifications against a target of 45 lakh (~30%). FY 2023–24 recorded 55% 
utilisation with just 2.63 lakh certifications (~6% of target). When enquired 
about justification behind the consistently low certification, the Ministry stated 
as under:                      
 
“It is submitted that the apparent mismatch between fund utilisation and 
certification numbers during FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 must be 
understood in the context of the phased rollout of PMKVY 4.0. Skill India 
Programme (SIP) (including PMKVY 4.0) was approved by the Cabinet on 
07.02.2025 for the period of 2022-26. Prior to this approval, the scheme 
was being implemented on the basis of phased approvals granted by the 
Department of Expenditure from time to time. Consequently, eﬀective 
implementation could be undertaken for only about 15 months (January-
March2023, September 2023-March 2024, and May–September 2024) out 
of the total 36 months during FY 2022-23 to FY 2024-25. This fragmented 
approval cycle significantly slowed fund flow, training commencement, and 
assessment timelines, directly aﬀecting both year-on-year utilisation and 
certification outputs. 

47 
 
 
Additionally, the Ministry highlights that skilling programmes require 
substantial upfront expenditure including mobilisation, centre onboarding, 
infrastructure setup, creation of digital systems, assessments processes 
which do not immediately translate into certifications within the same 
financial year. Certification numbers are further influenced by assessment 
and re-assessment cycles, third-party verification processes, and candidate 
availability and batch completion timelines. In many cases, certifications 
are recorded in the subsequent financial year, and annual targets allow for 
such roll-over, ensuring that candidates completing training later are still 
accounted for. Further, under PMKVY 4.0, funds are being released on 
achieving certain milestones such as 30 percent of funds are released upon 
commencement of training, another 30 percent after achieving at least 50 
percent AEBAS attendance and 50 percent course completion, and the final 
40 percent only after candidates clear assessments and receive certification. 
The relationship between fund utilisation also needs to be viewed in light of 
the training and assessment pipeline. For example, in FY 2022-23, 2.27 
lakh candidates were enrolled, of which 1.28 lakh were assessed only in FY 
2023-24. Similarly, 21.26 candidates enrolled in FY 2023-24, of which 
11.14 lakh candidates assessed in FY 2024-25.”

E. PM VISHWAKARMA SCHEME 
126. When asked about the challenges being faced by the Ministry in the 
implementation of the PM Vishwakarma Scheme and what initiatives have been 
taken to overcome the said challenges, the Ministry in their written reply stated 
as under:-                     
“Challenges in Implementation of PM Vishwakarma Scheme and 
Measures Taken by MSDE 
The PM Vishwakarma Scheme aims to support traditional artisans and 
craftspeople operating largely in the informal sector, across diverse trades 
and geographies. The nature of the target group and the wide operational 
spread present multiple implementation challenges. MSDE has adopted a 
combination of policy flexibility, institutional strengthening, and field-level 
interventions to address these challenges in a calibrated manner. 
1. Challenge: Irregular Participation and Retention of Beneficiaries 
Many beneficiaries depend on daily wages and seasonal occupations. 
Livelihood compulsions, migration, and family responsibilities often affect 
their ability to attend or complete training, despite initial willingness. 
Steps Taken: 
i. Pre-batch engagement of beneficiaries by States and training partners 
to clearly communicate training schedules, duration, and benefits. 
ii. Deployment of District Coordinators in every district to support 
mobilisation and follow-up at the local level. 
iii. Establishment of a dedicated call centre (20-member team) to guide 
registered beneficiaries, resolve queries, and encourage participation. 
iv. Automated SMS notifications to registered Vishwakarmas to improve 
awareness of training batches and timelines. 
 
2. Challenge: Low Candidate Density in Certain Regions and Trades 
In several State–district–trade combinations, particularly in remote, hilly, 
and sparsely populated areas, the number of registered candidates is 
insufficient for standard batch formation. 
Steps Taken: 

78 
 
i. Flexibility to States and implementing agencies to plan training 
based on actual availability of registered candidates and trade 
concentration. 
ii. Reduction of minimum batch size from 10 to 5 candidates in the 
North-Eastern Region and Union Territories to accommodate scattered 
registrations. 
iii. Empanelment of three specialised mobilisation agencies to improve 
outreach, consolidate candidates, and support batch formation. 
3. Challenge: Trade-wise and Location-specific Training Requirements 
The Scheme covers a wide range of traditional trades, each requiring 
customized tools, pedagogy, and scheduling aligned with local practices. 
Steps Taken: 
i. Trade-specific and location-specific planning of training batches by 
States and implementing partners. 
ii. Continuous coordination with Sector Skill Councils to ensure 
alignment of training content with traditional skills and local market 
needs. 
iii. Orientation and capacity-building of training partners on Scheme 
guidelines and trade-specific quality requirements. 
4. Challenge: Inadequate or Uneven Training Infrastructure 
Availability of suitable training centres for traditional trades varies 
significantly across regions. In some locations, existing centres require 
trade-specific strengthening. 
Steps Taken: 
i. Onboarding and strengthening of over 5,000 Training Centres, 
including ITIs and other government institutions, with emphasis on 
underserved regions. 
ii. Leveraging existing public training infrastructure and upgrading 
facilities to ensure relevance to traditional trades. 
iii. Balanced geographic distribution of Training Centres to improve 
accessibility for artisans. 
5. Challenge: Ensuring Consistency in Training, Assessment, and 
Certification 
Beneficiaries enter the Scheme with varying levels of prior experience, 
making standardization of training outcomes, assessment, and certification 
a challenge. 
Steps Taken: 

79 
 
i. Regular orientation of Training Centres and training partners on 
standardized assessment norms and certification processes. 
ii. Close engagement with Sector Skill Councils to maintain uniformity in 
assessment frameworks. 
iii. Periodic monitoring and feedback-based improvements in training 
delivery. 
6. Challenge: Coordination Among Multiple Stakeholders 
Implementation requires continuous coordination among State 
Governments, district administrations, training partners, Sector Skill 
Councils, and other line Ministries. 
Steps Taken: 
i. Periodic reviews at National, State, and District levels, supported 
by data dashboards, virtual meetings, and field feedback. 
ii. Active coordination with State Governments and district 
administrations to resolve location-specific issues. 
To conclude, MSDE has adopted an adaptive and responsive approach to 
ensure effective rollout of the PM Vishwakarma Scheme by mapping 
implementation challenges with targeted policy flexibility, institutional 
expansion, digital communication, and robust field-level coordination. 
These measures are helping bridge gaps arising from geographic diversity, 
informal livelihoods, and trade-specific requirements, thereby 
strengthening last-mile delivery of the Scheme.”

“Under the PM-SETU scheme, the upgraded ITIs will be managed through 
an industry-led Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) set up as a not-for-profit 
entity (Section 8 Company / Society / Trust). The SPV will act as the central 
body for coordination and implementation of the Hub-and-Spoke cluster. 
The SPV will be formed jointly by the Central Government, State 
Government/UT, and the selected Anchor Industry Partner (AIP). With the 
AIP holding 51% shareholding, the industry holds full autonomy in 
steering the operational and strategic direction of the SPV, while the 
Central and State Governments, sharing the remaining 49% equally, 
provide enabling support and oversight. This structure places industry 
leadership at the core of decision-making, with government participation 
safeguarding accountability and alignment with national skilling objectives. 
The SPV will be governed by a Board of 11 Directors, including at least two 
women. The Board will have a chairperson nominated by the AIP (in 
concurrence with Government), four Directors from the AIP, two each from 
the Central and State Governments, one from ITI leadership, and the CEO 
of the SPV. The Board will oversee strategy, approve plans and budgets, and 
monitor performance, thereby reinforcing the industry-driven autonomy 
of the SPV while maintaining collaborative governance.”

PART-II 
OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS 
I. General Overview 
1. The significance of various aspects of skilling identified by the Prime 
Minister has led to the creation of a new Ministry by the name of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship and specific programmes have been envisaged 
by the Ministry. However, the Committee feel that the Ministry are in dearth of 
enough personnel in both technical and administrative areas to implement the 
various schemes conceptualized by them and also to coordinate with the State 
Governments in this regard. Hence, the Committee desire that a National Level 
Skill Board may be established with all the States on board. The Committee feel 
that contribution and commitment by all the States in the effective 
implementation of the Schemes are significant and therefore, the share of State 
Governments has to be a part of all the important Schemes of the Ministry. The 
Committee observe that permanent institutionalization of Skill Development 
Centres is also highly essential to boost skilling among youth and further prepare 
them to contribute in the economic development of the nation. 
II.  Overall Budgetary Allocations and Utilization  
2. An analysis of the budgetary allocations of MSDE during the last three 
fiscals has shown significant increase of 28.50%, 34.95% and 62.05 % in the BE 
for FYs 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27 respectively. However, shortfall in the 
actual expenditure persists even though the BE allocations were slashed down at 
the RE stage. During FY 2025-26, only Rs 1252.97 crore constituting 46.35% has 
been utilized out of the RE of Rs. 2703.54 crore as on 12 February, 2026. The 
Committee are concerned over the repeated higher budgetary projections by the 
Ministry without full preparedness and capacity on their part to implement the 
schemes, particularly new ones and utilize the funds effectively.  In their 
considered view, savings and surrender of allocated funds amount to wasting of 
resources which could have been utilized for other development programmes of 
the Government of India. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry 
should evolve a mechanism for accurate forecasting of budgetary requirements, 
improved accountability and optimizing resource allocation by factoring in 
approval timelines and readiness of the stakeholders for implementation of new 
schemes, as well as absorptive capacity and past expenditure trends. No 
substantial allocation should be projected at the BE stage unless the scheme has 

105 
 
achieved adequate administrative and financial readiness.  Funds may also be 
sought based on the actual utilization under quarterly expenditure plans prepared 
for a fiscal year. A time-bound mechanism for obtaining SFC/EFC/Cabinet 
approvals may also be put in place to avoid delays in approval of schemes, 
minimize savings and avoid surrender of funds. 
3. The Committee note that the percentage of expenditure against the RE 
2025-26 has increased from 40.12% as on 07.01.2026 to 49.94% till 01.03.2026 
indicating that more than 50% of the allocated funds remains to be utilized in 
March, 2026.  Though the Ministry have stated that vigorous efforts are being 
made to ensure utilization of the allocated funds under PMKVY and PM-SETU 
where maximum   shortfall has been registered, full utilization of the allocated 
funds appears to be a herculean task in the last month of the current fiscal. The 
Committee further note that Rs. 2800 crore has been allocated for Skill India 
Programme (SIP) which has PMKVY as one of its component while the allocation 
for PM SETU stands at Rs. 6140.50 crore in BE 2026-27. Given that these two 
major schemes constitute more than 90% of the budgetary allocation for FY 2026-
27, the Committee urge the Ministry to draw upon the learnings from the current 
fiscal and work out a plan of action to ensure full utilization of the allocated funds 
for the upcoming fiscal. Quarterly Expenditure Plan should be meticulously 
prepared and expenditure strictly monitored on a monthly basis. Accountability 
may be defined and fixed for deviations so that expenditure is incurred in a 
balanced manner every quarter. The Committee also recommend 
institutionalization of a digital dashboard for real-time monitoring of financial and 
physical progress under these schemes along with early-warning mechanisms for 
potential savings.  A quarterly statement detailing scheme-wise   expenditure 
incurred by the Ministry may also be furnished to the Committee during FY 2026-
27. 
4. The Committee take note of the multiplicity of schemes and programmes 
under the Ministry and numerous efforts, including collaborations with different 
institutions for implementation of the schemes. The Committee, however, find 
that success has been limited and desire that the primary objective of developing 
skills across various sectors should not be lost sight of and the approach of the 
Ministry towards skilling needs to be simplified and focused to achieve the desired 
results. The Committee are of the view that reducing the number of schemes of 
the Ministry would provide focused attention to the core issue of skilling for 
livelihood opportunities and economic development.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommend proper rationalization of the schemes and focusing on few flagship 

106 
 
programmes while the rest should be left to the States which possess the ‘native 
intelligence’ relating to the local skilling requirement.  Simplified guidelines may 
be issued to the States and the Central Government should take on the role of a 
regulator/consultant providing support, wherever needed. The Committee urge 
the Ministry to work out a plan of action in this regard and apprise them of the 
status at the earliest. 
5. The schemes implemented by MSDE comprises of both Central Sector and 
Centrally Sponsored Schemes. The Committee are of the view that a pragmatic 
approach should be adopted towards implementation of the schemes for better 
outcomes and greater reach of the intended beneficiaries. Defining clearcut 
responsibilities between the Centre and the States would ensure more 
accountability, transparency and efficiency. This would also result in 
implementation of programmes depending on the capacity of the Department to 
deliver. The Committee expects that the Ministry will work towards this goal in 
the coming years. 
6. The Committee observe that the Pradhan Mantri – Skilling and 
Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs (PM-SETU) scheme, 
approved in May 2025, was finally launched in October 2025. As informed by the 
Ministry, the scheme underwent multiple iterations as it had to be designed as a 
PPP scheme with onboarding of industries and creation of an SPV for a cluster of 
ITIs. The Committee emphasize the need for careful planning, adequate 
preparatory work, identification of gaps and shortcomings, addressing anomalies 
and taking the States/stakeholders into confidence through extensive 
consultations while formulating new or revised schemes. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that new or revised schemes should only be launched when 
it is foolproof and all the stakeholders are onboarded to avoid delays in approval, 
implementation and utilization of the allocated funds. Funds sought for the new 
schemes should be realistic, starting with smaller budgets based on physical and 
financial targets fixed. In respect of revised or schemes proposed for merger, the 
Ministry should ensure that the old schemes continue till the new ones are 
approved. It should also be ensured that the transition from the old to the new 
scheme is seamless with no gap in implementation and funding.

III.  Scheme-wise Analysis of Performance 
(A) Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) 4.0 
7. Scrutiny of the physical progress under the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas 
Yojana (PMKVY) 4.0, which was launched on 7 February 2025 as part of the Skill 
India Programme (SIP) with enhanced emphasis on re-skilling and up-skilling 
through formal Recognition on Prior Learning (RPL), reveals a huge gap between 
the number of candidates enrolled, trained, assessed and certified. The Ministry 
have informed that the gap between the number of candidates trained and 
assessed is mainly due to the dropouts before the assessment. Further, the 
difference in assessed and certified number is due to candidates failing the 
assessment. The Committee are concerned that many candidates trained under 
PMKVY 4.0 have failed to meet the required competencies leaving question mark 
on their chance of finding employment. The Committee are of the considered view 
that unless correctives measures are taken, the very purpose of the scheme would 
be defeated. The Committee, therefore, recommend an independent evaluation of 
the efficacy of the training imparted under the scheme. The Committee 
emphasise that it should be conducted without delay to know whether dropouts 
under the training programme and failure in assessment indicate systemic failure, 
poor quality of training imparted or lack of focus on employment outcomes. 
8. The Committee note that MSDE has issued a Standard Operating Procedure 
(SOP) to streamline the assessment and certification process. The Committee 
desire that the Ministry should adopt a structured approach that integrates 
certification with career advancement and provides incentive for all the trained 
and assessed candidates to complete the certification process.  
9. The Committee note that for FY 2025-26, more than 8 lakhs training targets 
amounting to approximately Rs. 630 crore have been approved, with additional 
allocations currently under process. The Committee expect consequential 
increase in the number of candidates under the training programmes of PMKVY 
4.0 and urge the Ministry to ensure that training targets are set, infrastructure 
are ready and adequate number of training providers are available to cater to more 
than 8 lakhs training targets in the current fiscal. The Ministry should also draw 
up a plan of action for effective and optimal utilization of Rs. 630 crore and 
additional allocations currently under process. Considering the fact that the 
objective of the Scheme is to enable and mobilize a large number of Indian youths 
to take up skill training for employability and sustainable livelihood, the 

108 
 
Committee call upon the Ministry to intensify its efforts so that maximum number 
of youths are trained and certified. 
10. The Committee note that MSDE is formulating the next version of PMKVY 
i.e. PMKVY 5.0. The tentative date of launch is April 2026 depending on approval 
from Cabinet. The Committee urge the Ministry to ensure that this new version 
adequately addresses the shortcomings of the earlier versions and focus on 
outcomes, enhancing training quality, placement tracking, monitoring and 
transparency, outreach and accessibility in addition to increasing the relevance 
of courses to industry needs. Efforts may also be made for timely approval of the 
scheme and adequate measures should be taken for seamless transition to PMKVY 
5.0 with no gap in implementation and funding.   
 
(B) National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS)-New Version . 
11. The Committee note that under the PM-National Apprenticeship Promotion 
Scheme, the physical achievements in apprenticeship training during the last 
three fiscals i.e. 2022-23; 2023-24 and 2024-25 have been 73.80%; 84.73% and 
82.08 % respectively. The Committee note that while the physical target for FY 
2025-26 is to engage 13 lakh candidates, only 10.80 lakh apprentices could be 
engaged as on 15 February 2026. On the financial progress, Rs.588.56 crore has 
been utilized out of the RE of Rs.715 crore in FY 2025-26, as on 11 February 2026.  
The Ministry have stated that the remaining funds i.e. Rs. 126.44 crore would be 
utilized during the current financial year. The Committee urge the Ministry to 
make concerted efforts and honour their commitment so as to cover the 
remaining 2.17 lakh candidates within the remaining period of the current FY.  
12. The Committee note that 19.22 lakh candidates have completed their 
apprenticeship training, out of which 7.66 lakh apprentices have been assessed 
while 7.81 lakh apprentices have been certified. The Ministry have attributed 
factors like non-appearance of apprentices in final assessments, early exit from 
training due to personal reasons, and limited awareness among some 
establishments and apprentices regarding the importance of certification for the 
comparatively low level of assessment and certification. The Committee further 
note that certain measures like enhancing awareness campaign among 
apprentices and establishments about the value of obtaining national 
Apprenticeship Certificate (NAC), Certificate of Proficiency (CoP), and Sector Skill 
Council Certifications have been undertaken by the Ministry. Emphasis has also 

109 
 
been laid on timely completion of training and facilitation of assessments. The 
Committee desire to know the outcome of the measures taken by the Ministry to 
improve the level of assessment and certification as well as review meetings held 
under NAPS–New Version. In addition to proper sensitization of apprentices 
regarding the importance of assessment and certification, the Committee 
recommend introduction of system of incentives for completion of the assessment 
and certification process.   
 13. The Committee note that 4, 47, 926 apprentices have been engaged under 
social category, out of which 1,15,362 belonged to Scheduled Castes, 45, 415 
belonged to Scheduled Tribes while 2,87,147 are in Other Backward Classes 
category. The Committee emphasize the need for bringing all the eligible persons 
belonging to these categories under the NAPS to enhance their employability for 
sustainable livelihood as well as for developing a skilled workforce for promotion 
of economic growth. The Committee, therefore, urge the Ministry to sensitize the 
people inhabiting identified vulnerable pockets/areas regarding the benefits of the 
scheme including through local media and social media platforms. Periodic 
outreach campaigns and advocacy drives designed to inform and educate people 
belonging to these categories on specific programmes regarding apprenticeship 
training should be undertaken at regular intervals so that all the eligible persons 
are covered under the scheme. 
14. The Committee observe that 9257 apprentices have been engaged from the 
NER States during FY 2025-26 till 31 December 2025. The State-wise break-up of 
apprentices engagement in NER States - viz, Arunachal Pradesh-147; Assam - 
7803; Manipur - 168; Meghalaya - 136; Mizoram - 261; Nagaland - 14; Sikkim - 406 
and Tripura - 322 indicates negligible apprentices engagement except for Assam. 
Considering the geographical, infrastructural and economic challenges as well as 
lower awareness among employers in the North Eastern Region, the Committee 
urge the Ministry to adopt a targeted approach that addresses these challenges 
including sensitization of employers regarding the scheme.  The Committee also 
recommend for instituting stronger industry-academia linkages and region 
specific policies to boost engagement between employer and apprentices, 
including the youth and women in the NER. 
 
(C)    Jan Shikshan Sansthan  

110 
 
15. The Committee note that at present 294 Jan Shikshan Sansthans (JSSs) are 
functional in 26 States and Union Territories covering 286 districts. The Ministry 
have informed that during the financial cycle 2022–26, a provision had been made 
for the establishment of 250 new JSSs, and the budget outlay was accordingly 
approved. However, due to administrative and procedural constraints, the new 
JSSs could not be established and consequentially, the actual expenditure 
incurred during the period is lower than the projected outlay. The Committee, 
therefore, urge the Ministry to resolve the administrative and procedural 
constraints without further delay and establish the 250 new JSSs for which the 
budget had already been approved.  
16. The Committee observe that the priority groups under the JSS scheme are 
women, SCs, STs, OBCs and minorities in rural and urban low-income areas. Since 
FY 2018-19 till date, the number of divyangs, women, SC, ST, OBCs and minorities 
trained so far are 13,224, 28,77,825, 8,51,283, 4, 56, 765, 13,16,369 and 
2,64,639 respectively.  The Committee find the number of trained persons 
belonging to the above categories relatively low for a seven year period and urge 
the Ministry to bring about considerable improvements in the number of 
beneficiaries for the upcoming fiscal. A shift towards a more inclusive, accessible 
and market-linked skill development as well as improving outreach to rural areas 
and strengthening post-training placement support may be taken up and the 
progress achieved in this direction be communicated to the Committee. 
17. The Committee note that the sunset date for the Jan Shikshan Sansthan 
(JSS) Scheme under the umbrella of Skill India Programme is 31 March 2026. 
Accordingly, implementation of the revised provisions and other structural 
interventions will be taken forward subject to the outcome of the EFC appraisal 
and subsequent approval of the Cabinet. Considering that the vocational training 
initiatives under JSS have been specifically designed to address the socio-
economic needs of the marginalized and disadvantaged communities through a 
targeted, inclusive and livelihood-oriented approach, the Committee desire that 
the scheme should continue without any gap in execution and funding. The 
Committee, therefore, urge the Ministry to make concerted efforts for timely 
approval of the scheme and apprise the Committee of the progress made in this 
regard. 
 
(D)   PM Vishwakarma Scheme  

111 
 
18. The Committee note that PM Vishwakarma Scheme aims to support 
traditional artisans and crafts people operating largely in the informal sector, 
across diverse trades. MSDE has adopted an adaptive and responsive approach to 
ensure effective rollout of the PM Vishwakarma Scheme by mapping 
implementation challenges with targeted policy flexibility, institutional 
expansion, digital communication, and robust field-level coordination. The 
Committee, however, observe that  Nationalised/PSUs banking services are not 
forthcoming in supporting the implementation of the scheme by way of providing 
loans to the intended beneficiaries. The Committee are concerned that lack of 
cooperation from the Banks is depriving the traditional artisans and craft people 
of sustainable livelihood opportunities. The Committee, therefore, urge the 
Ministry to take up the matter with the Department of Financial Services, 
Ministry of Finance for issuance of necessary instructions to the 
Nationalised/Public Sector Banks to be more proactive in sanctioning loans under 
the scheme so that the financial problems relating to the Vishwakarma Scheme 
are resolved. MSDE may also obtain feedback from target groups on the problems 
being faced in availing of financial services. 
 
 (E)  Schemes under Directorate General of Training  
19. The Committee note that during the last three years, there has been 
persistent underutilization of allocated funds under various schemes of the 
Directorate General of Training (DGT).  Moreover, enrolment rate of candidates in 
Government ITIs is lower than that of the private ITIs. The Committee further 
observe that a number of ITIs have been closed down, number of seats are 
declining whereas the number of students are on the increase. The Committee are 
concerned at this unhealthy trend and urge the Ministry to address these issues 
in coordination with the State Governments and other stakeholders.  
The Committee are also of the considered view that upgradation of ITIs and 
improving the quality of training provided in these institutes should be taken up 
on priority. The Committee, therefore, desire that the Ministry should tie up with 
these institutions in the States, make investment in terms of money, machine 
and men, ensure the deployment of experts in various sectors and make adequate 
arrangement for training of trainers to enhance the quality of skilling in the 
country.  
 

112 
 
(F)  The Pradhan Mantri – Skilling and Employability Transformation through 
Upgraded ITIs (PM-SETU)  
20. The Pradhan Mantri – Skilling and Employability Transformation through 
Upgraded ITIs (PM-SETU) scheme was announced in Budget 2024-25 for 
upgradation of ITIs with an aim to enhance the quality and outcomes of skilling 
across India by upgrading 1,000 ITIs in a hub-and-spoke arrangement for over a 
period of five years. It involves upgradation of 200 hubs and 800 spoke ITIs. 
Additionally, the programme includes capacity augmentation of five National 
Institutes for training of trainers. The Committee observe that BE 2025-26 of Rs. 
3000 crore was reduced to Rs.356 crore at the RE stage, out of which a meagre 
amount of Rs. 1.38 crore has been utilized as on 07 January 2026. The Committee 
note that the release of Central Government contribution is contingent upon 
approval of Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs), onboarding of Anchor Industry 
Partners (AIPs), and subsequent formation of industry-led Special Purpose 
Vehicles (SPVs).  Since no SIP has been finalized so far, expenditure is negligible. 
The Committee urge the Ministry to expedite the finalization and approval of SIPs 
in active coordination with States/UTs. MSDE should ensure the appointment of 
Project Management Consultants (PMCs) within a fixed timeline so that States 
receive the necessary technical assistance to prepare and finalize SIPs 
expeditiously. Since the scheme envisages strong industry participation through 
AIPs and industry-led SPVs, the Committee direct the Ministry to intensify 
industry outreach and consultations to accelerate the short-listing and 
onboarding of AIPs so that the implementation framework becomes operational in 
the current fiscal. 
21. The Committee observe that no Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) which form 
the core implementation mechanism of the PM-SETU scheme, have been formed 
so far due to procedural constraints being faced by PSUs/industry partners in 
joining SPVs, including the requirement of approvals from the Department of 
Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM). The Committee desire that 
the Ministry should undertake extensive consultations with stakeholders to work 
out a feasible plan for creation of SPVs and onboarding of PSUs/industry partners 
under the scheme including flexible models of participation, tripartite 
arrangements for industry partners, etc. at the earliest 
22. The Committee note that the Cabinet has approved 25 Hub-and-Spoke ITI 
clusters for the initial pilot rollout under the PM-SETU scheme during FY 2026–
27 and 78 clusters comprising 427 ITIs (78 Hub and 349 Spoke ITIs) have already 
been identified across the country. However, the actual launch of these clusters 

113 
 
is contingent upon approval of Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs) and onboarding 
of Anchor Industry Partners (AIPs). The Ministry have stated that industry 
partners appear hesitant to participate in the management and operation of ITIs 
due to lack of expertise in skilling and training activities. As all industries do not 
possess the capacity or requisite skill, the Committee are of the view that a 
scientific and pragmatic approach should be taken as far as skilling is concerned.  
The Committee, therefore, urge the Ministry to identify specific industries which 
can be tasked with management and operation of the ITIs, in consultation with 
the States. A clear and defined operational framework outlining the roles and 
responsibilities of industry partners, training providers and government 
institutions should be devised so that participation by industry becomes more 
feasible and attractive. In addition, arrangements may also be worked out for 
providing   incentives for running the ITIs. Steps taken and progress achieved may 
be communicated to the Committee within three months of the presentation of 
this Report.  
23. The Committee further observe that the success of the Hub-and-Spoke 
model depends significantly on the adequacy of infrastructure and institutional 
capacity of the Hub ITIs and Spoke ITIs. The Committee urge the Ministry to 
ensure timely modernization of workshops, installation of advanced equipment, 
creation of smart classrooms, digital learning facilities and development of 
industry-aligned laboratories in the identified clusters so that the institutions are 
fully equipped to deliver demand-driven skill training. 
24. The Committee emphasize the need for bringing skilling to the next level 
and desire that a specialized expert team should be established within the MSDE 
to oversee skill upgradation in the country. As a long term goal, the Ministry 
should consider establishing National Training Centres of Excellence to be located 
in key economic centres such as Chennai, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Pune, 
Ahmedabad, Bhopal, Lucknow, Noida and Gurgaon. These National Centres of 
Excellence should conduct regular and comprehensive training activities. 
 
(G)   Integrated Scheme in Skilling Architecture   
25. The Committee note that a new Central Sector Scheme- Integrated Scheme 
in Skilling Architecture has been announced in the Budget of FY 2026-27 with a 
total outlay of Rs. 600 crore and the new scheme is under formulation. The 
Committee desire that sustained efforts should be made by the Ministry for early 

114 
 
formulation of the Scheme in coordination with the States, industry stakeholders 
and relevant Ministries. The scheme design should also clearly define its 
objectives, implementation framework, monitoring mechanism and expected 
outcomes to ensure that it meaningfully strengthens the overall skilling 
architecture of the country. The Committee also urge the Ministry to ensure 
timely appraisal and approval process so that the Scheme is implemented within 
the current fiscal and the allocation is not surrendered due to non-approval of the 
scheme.
