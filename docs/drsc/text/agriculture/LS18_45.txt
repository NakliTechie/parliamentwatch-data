A. Balanced Fund Utilization and Strengthened Quarterly Planning 
(Recommendation Serial No. 2) 
 
1.5  The Committee had observed/recommended as follows: — 
The Committee note that the percentage of actual expenditure in the first, 
second, and third quarters of FY 2025 –26, in comparison to the proposed quarterly 
expenditure under the DDG plan, has been uneven which is 33 percent in the first 
quarter, 68 percent in the second quarter, and 53 percent in the third quarter. The 
Committee also observe that re -appropriation of funds from one scheme to another 
has taken place mostly during the last quarter of the financial year. Further, the 
Committee note that while the proposed budget allocation for FY 2025 –26 was Rs. 
5267 crore, the funds allocated at the Budgetary Estimate (BE) stage were Rs. 
4364.22 crore, which were subsequently reduced to Rs. 3571.57 crore at the 
Revised Estimate (RE) stage. The actual expenditure incurred up to 31 January 
2026 amounts to Rs. 2183.60 crore, representing 61.14 percent of the RE, and 
nearly 40 percent of the RE allocation remains to be utilized in the remaining two 
months of the financial year 2025-26. 
In view of the above, the Committee recommend that the factors impede 
balanced utilisation of allocated funds during the first three quarters in the FY  
2025-26 may be identified and the Ministry may take necessary steps to ensure a 
more balanced and timely utilization of funds across all quarters of the financial 
year.

Under the Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing

Enterprises (PMFME) Scheme, during FY 2025 –26, the quarterly expenditure 
pattern remained uneven, with utilization at 7% in Q1, 38% in Q2, 16% in Q3, and 
24% in Q4. Lower expenditure in the initial quarter was primarily due to delays in 
approvals, implementation of SNA Sparsh model by the States, slower uptake by 
States/UTs, and procedural timelines associated with scheme implementation. 
However, with continuous monitoring and corrective interventions, the pace of 
expenditure improved significantly in the l ater part of the year. Under the Scheme, 
measures such as regular review meetings, timely interventions, and strategic 
reallocation of funds to better -performing States/UTs contributed to accelerated

future. 
Under the Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Food Processing  
Industry (PLISFPI), approved applicants are required to submit their incentive 
claims up to 31 st December of the respective financial year, as per the Scheme 
Guidelines. It is observed that a significant number of applicants submit their  
claims close to the prescribed deadline, resulting in a concentration of claims in the 
last quarter. The claim processing mechanism under the Scheme is robust, 
streamlined and functioning efficiently, and the processing of claims  is carried out  
in a timely manner upon receipt of complete documentation. Accordingly, the 
observed pattern of expenditure is primarily attributable to the timing of claim 
submissions by applicants rather than any procedural constraints in processing. It  
is submitted that out of the budget allocation of Rs. 1200.00  Crore (RE) for the year 
2025-26, Rs. 1199.96 Crore were utilised as on 31.03.2026, making it 99.99% 
utilisation. Ministry has been actively encouraging timely submission of c laims

Project Management Agency (PMA), and continuous monitoring of claim 
submission status. Further, suitable measures are being explored, in consultation 
with stakeholders, to facilitate more staggered submission of claims within the

B. Impact evaluation of the PMFME Scheme 
Recommendation Serial No. 8 
1.8 The Committee had observed/recommended as follows: — 
The Committee note the Impact Evaluation of the Scheme and the 
recommendations made therein. The Ministry has stated that the  recommendations 
of the evaluating agency are being considered in the proposal for continuation of the 
PMFME Scheme under the 16th Finance Commission cycle, subject to  approval for 
extension of the Scheme and the decisions of the competent authority. In this regard, 
the Committee desire to be apprised of the accepted recommendations, with 
particular emphasis on the proposal to raise the subsidy cap from Rs.10 lakh to 
Rs.35 lakh, so as to enhance the competitiveness and effectiveness of the Scheme. 
1.9  In its Action taken reply, the Ministry has stated: - 
The Component wise  recommendations of the evaluating agency along with 
the response of MoFPI for the same, are as under: 
The key recommendation includes the need to enhance the credit -linked 
subsidy limit from Rs.10 lakh to Rs. 35 lakh, in line with the revised definitions of 
micro-enterprises under the updated MSME guidelines. It was further recommended 
that an incremental subsidy of up to 50% be provided to beneficiaries belonging to 
SC/ST categories, women, and persons with disabilities, as well as to applicants 

7 
 
from the North Eastern Region and hilly states, subject to an overall subsidy cap of 
Rs. 35 lakh. To facilitate better access to modern equipment and complement 
existing funding avenues available to SHG members,  it was proposed that 
assistance under seed capital be increased from Rs. 40,000 to Rs.50,000. 
The study recommends the introduction of new components including the 
establishment of food testing laboratories, support for participation in domestic and 
international events, strengthening of marketing linkages through e -commerce 
platforms, promotion of national/central entities, and the creation of an umbrella 
brand. These measures were widely regarded as timely and essential for  enhancing 
the competitiveness of micro food processing enterprises in both domestic and 
global markets. 
The recommendations of the third -party evaluating agency are under active 
consideration for incorporation in the proposal for continuation of the Scheme during 
the 16th Finance Commission cycle, subject to the approval and availability of funds. 
1.10. The Committee note the submission  of the Ministry  that the 
recommendations of the third -party Impact Evaluation Study are under active 
consideration for incorporation in the proposal for continuation of the Pradhan 
Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PMFME) Scheme 
during the Sixteenth Finance Commission cycle, subject to the approval of the 
competent authority and the availability of funds. 
The Committee further note that the recommendations under 
consideration include enhancement of the credit -linked subsidy ceiling from 
Rs. 10 lakh to Rs. 35 lakh, revision of assistance under the Seed Capital 
component, and introduction of new components aimed at strengthening 
marketing support, food testing infrastructure and market access for micro 
food processing enterprises. 

8 
 
The Committee expect that an early decision on these important 
recommendations, particularly the proposal for enhancement of the credit -
linked subsidy ceiling, would significantly improve the competitiveness, 
financial viability and growth of micro food processing enterprises across the 
country. The Committee would, therefore, like to be apprised of the decisions 
taken by the Government on these recommendations after the proposal for 
continuation of the Scheme is finalised. 
C. Physical achievements under PMKSY 
Recommendation Serial No. 9 
1.11 The Committee had observed/recommended as follows: — 
The Committee note that under the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana 
(PMKSY), a 1607 number of projects have been sanctioned across its various 
components out of which total number of 1196 projects are completed and remaining 
411 projects are under implementation. 
Under the Scheme for Creation/Expansion of Food Processing and 
Preservation Capacities (CEFPPC), 505 projects have been sanctioned, out of which 
356 projects have been completed. Under the Scheme for Creation of Infrastructure 
for Agro Processing Clusters (APC), 63 projects have been  approved, of which 23 
projects have been completed and 40 projects are under implementation. In respect 
of Mega Food Parks, several projects are awaiting compliance with operational and 
leasing conditions for release of the fin al installment, while projects under the 
Integrated Cold Chain component are reported to be at advanced stages of 
implementation. 
The Committee further note the Ministry’s submission that delays in 
completion of projects are attributable to non -receipt of statutory clearances such as 
building plan approvals, Consent to Establish (CTE), Consent to operate (CTO), 
FSSAI licenses and land use permissions from State authorities, delay in 
disbursement of term loans by banks, and non-fulfilment of compliance conditions by 
Project Implementing Agencies (PIAs) . The Committee also note that fewer eligible 

9 
 
proposals under SC/ST and NER categories have affected achievement of targets 
under those components. 
The Committee take  note that Third -Party Impact Evaluation Studies have 
been undertaken for the ongoing components of PMKSY and that monitoring 
mechanisms, including engagement of Project Management Agencies (PMAs), 
periodic review meetings, inspections and tracking through the SAMPADA portal, 
have been instituted. Nevertheless, the Committee are concerned that the gap 
between sanctioned and completed projects reflects continuing implementation 
bottlenecks. 
In view of the foregoing, the Committee recommend that the Ministry take 
effective and time-bound measures to expedite completion of the projects sanctioned 
under PMKSY. The Committee are of the view that strengthened coordination with 
State Governments and concerned authorities is necessary to ensure timely grant of 
statutory clearances and approvals. 
The Committee further recommend that close monitoring of projects under 
completion be undertaken and regular follow -up with lending institutions and  Project 
Implementing Agencies should be ensured to meet the challenges or emerging 
situations hurdling the completion of the projects in targeted timeframe. 
The Committee also recommend that the findings of the Third -Party Impact 
Evaluation Studies and stakeholder feedback should be examined by the Ministry 
and also ensure its implementation sincerely to address the recurring bottlenecks 
and improve the pace of project execution under PMKSY. 
1.12 In its Action taken reply, the Ministry has stated: - 
As on 31.03.2026, under the various component schemes of PMKSY, a total 
of 1,722 projects have been sanctioned out of which 1,241 projects have been 
completed/operationalised, with a completion rate of about 72% (Variation in

due to various factors, like delays in obtaining statutory clearances/approvals from 
State Governments and other concerned authorities, such as Consent to Establish 
(CTE), Consent to Operate (CTO), building/lay out plans, Change of Land Use 

10 
 
(CLU), FSSAI licence, etc. In addition, delays in fulfilment of pre -disbursement

As per the Impact evaluation studies of the PMKSY component schemes,

The Ministry is making incessant efforts to ensure timely completion of 
projects and timely release of approved grants, particularly under component 
schemes where progress is slow. The Ministry has instituted a robust monitoring 
mechanism for projects sanctioned under PMKSY through engagement of Project

highlighted implementation challenges and provided recommendations for improving

Regular follow -up is also being undertaken with Project Implementing 
Agencies (PIAs), lending institutions and State Governments to address issues

bottlenecks. State Governments are also being requested to facilitate timely 
approvals and clearances for smooth implementation of projects. 
 
Further, ongoing projects are being closely monitored through periodic 

11

Party Impact Evaluation Studies, along with stakeholder feedback, are also being

to address recurring bottlenecks and improve the pace of project execution.

1. Budgetary Allocation for the Food Processing Sector 
(Recommendation No. 1) 
 
The Committee note  the proportion of budgetary allocation made in favour  
of the Ministry out of the total central plan outlay during the last five years is Rs. 
4064.16 crore. While allocations for the Ministry have increased in absolute terms, 
the proportion has remained stagnant throughout the period. 
The Committee further observe that the Gross Value Addition (GVA) of the 
Food Processing Sector has risen significantly from Rs. 1.34 lakh crore in 2014 –15 
to Rs. 2.24 lakh crore in 2023–24. With the Sector expected to grow at a 
compound annual growth rate of around 6 per cent over the next decade, and has  
a huge potential for employment generation, the Committee are of the view that the 
budgetary allocation to the Ministry for the current assessment year, 2026 –27 does 
not adequately reflect the emerging significance and contribution of the Sector to 
the national economy. 
In light of the above, the Committee urge the Ministry to coordinate with the 
Ministry of Finance to ensure that future allocations are commensurate with the 
Sector’s potential and aligned with objectives of the Sector. 
 
Reply of the Government: 
Budget allocated under 2026 -27 is after realistic assessment of the projects 
expected to be completed during the Financial year. Further, in order to assess the 
needs and requirements of the sector, the Ministry undertakes Third Party Impact 
Evaluation studies for the component schemes periodically. As regards budget 
forecasting review, the total scheme outlay is reviewed before the start of every 
Finance Commission Cycle, based on the recommendations of the impact 
evaluation studies and the assessment of t he project proposals received, approved 
and expected to be completed during the next Financial years. 
Further, Production Linked Incentive Scheme for Food Processing Industry 
(PLISPI) is a demand driven and performance -linked scheme, wherein incentives 
are disbursed to approved applicants based on achievement of eligible incremental 
sales and submission of claims in accordance with the Scheme guidelines. 
Accordingly, the budgetary requirement under the Scheme for the financial year 
2026-27 has been assessed on the basis of past trends of claims and the likely 
performance of approved applicants. 
It is further submitted that the position of fund requirement under the 
Scheme is continuously monitored, and a detailed assessment is undertaken  
based on actual claim submissions and performance of beneficiaries. Any additional 

13 
 
requirement, if necessary, will be projected at the Revised Estimate (RE) stage.

Reply of the Government: 
In the existing framework under the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing 
and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011, a differentiated  regulatory 
approach is already specified wherein petty Food Business Operators (FBOs) are 
required to obtain registration, while other FBOs are required to obtain State/Central 
Licenses, based on their scale, annual turnover and nature of operations. 
Further, the conditions of license as well as hygiene and sanitary 
requirements specified under Schedule 4 are graded and proportionate to the size 
and risk profile of the food business, with comparatively simplified requirements for 
petty FBOs. Additionally, procedural requirements such as documentation, fees, and 

45 
 
compliance obligations are relatively minimal  for registered FBOs  as compared to 
licensed entities, thereby reducing regulatory burden while ensuring basic food 
safety compliance. 
Furthermore, based on recommendations of NITI Aayog’s High Level 
Committee on Non -Financial Regulatory Reforms, the Food Safety and Standards 
(Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Amendment Regulations, 2026 
have been notified vide Gazette Notification dated 10.03.2026. The Amendment 
introduces following reforms: 
(a)  Increase in the turn over  threshold for registration and licensing : FSSAI vide 
order dated 13.03.2026 revised the turnover thresholds for categorisation  of food 
businesses, which is effective from 01.04.2026 for new applicants. The turnover 
threshold for registration has been increased from ₹12 lakhs to ₹1.5 crore, and for 
State licensing up to ₹50 crores, with Central licensing applicable beyond this limit. 
This rationalisation is intended to empower and strengthen the role of State 
authorities by enabling them to focus more effectively on oversight,  facilitation and 
enforcement of food safety regulations within their jurisdictions. For food business 
operators, particularly micro and small enterprises, the measure will result in simpler 
compliance requirements, reduced paperwork and fees,  elimination of pre-
inspection, and instant registration, thereby improving ease of operations. 
(b) .  Exemption to street food vendors from registration by FSSAI, if they are  
registered under the Street Vendors Act, 2014 : This will address the dual 
compliance requirements for street food vendors, registered with municipal 
corporation and town vending committee. It will benefit more than 10 lakhs street 
food vendors by eliminating the requirement of multiple registration across 
departments, the reform will significantly reduce compliance burden and enable 
street food vendors to focus on their livelihood, hygiene and business operations. 
(c) .  Perpetual License for the FBOs: To reduce regulatory burden, 
perpetual validity of FSSAI registrations and licences  has been adopted, subject to 
risk-based inspections. Earlier, registrations and licences were required to be 
renewed periodically, with validity ranging from one to five years. Under the revised 
framework, registrations and licences will have perpetual validity, eliminating the 
need for repeated renewals. This reform will substantially reduce compliance costs, 
paperwork and the need for  repeated interaction with licensing authorities for food 
business operators (FBOs), while improving continuity of operati ons. It will enable 
regulatory resources to focus more effectively towards enforcement, monitoring and 
capacity-building activities. 
(d) . A technology-enabled, dynamic risk-based inspection framework: Inspections 
will be carried  out based on  defined risk factors such as  risk associated with the 
nature of food commodity, past compliance record of the food business operator, 
performance during third -party audits, and inputs from enforcement and 
surveillance activities. It has been put in place to incentivise compliant food 
business operators and reduce repetitive inspections. This will ensure focused and 
transparent regulatory oversight while reducing unnecessary compliance burden on 
compliant businesses.

(i) Adulteration in milk and dairy products  should be tackled with 
mandatory rapid testing systems. 
Reply of the Government: 
Food testing infrastructure has been significantly strengthened, with 
expansion of NABL -accredited laboratories, referral labs, and state labs across the 
country. As on date, the number of NABL accredited Referral  laboratories is 24 and 
the Primary food testing laboratories is 252. 
The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India approve Rapid Analytical 
Food Testing (RAFT) kits/ equipment/methods for use in screening and  
surveillance by various stakeholders, including Food Business Operators (FBOs), 
food testing laboratories, and Food Safety Officers, etc. The list of FSSAI -approved 
RAFT kits is available in the public domain on the official FSSAI website. At 
present, 86 RAFT kits have been approved by FSSAI. 
Further, as per FSSAI advisory dated 19.11.2025, all dairy manufacturers 
engaged in the sale of milk and milk products through vending or kiosk machines 
have been directed to keep or make available FSSAI -approved/validated rapid test 

53 
 
kits at such locations for basic detection of common adulterants in milk and milk 
products. 
In addition, FSSAI undertakes awareness initiatives regarding rapid food and 
milk testing kits through publicity campaigns, training programmes, demonstration 
drives, and deployment of mobile food testing laboratories. These kits are also 
being made available to State Food Safety Departments, consumer organisations, 
and other stakeholders. In addition, FSSAI regularly sensitises Food Safety 
Commissioners of States/UTs on the use of rapid testing kits during meetings of the 
Central Advisory Committee. 
FSSAI has provided Mobile Food Testing Laboratory (MFTL) referred to as 
“Food Safety on wheels” (FSW) to States/UTs. It is a multipurpose vehicle that 
helps in dissemination of information and acts as a platform for getting food safety 
training and food testing. Mobile Food Testing Laboratories (MFTLs) provided to  
the States/UTs have been equipped with “Milk-o-Screen” equipment for on-the-spot 
analysis of milk samples. This equipment is capable of testing quality parameters 
viz., Fat, SNF, protein, adulterants such as added water, urea, sucrose, 
maltodextrin, ammonium sulphate etc. It is an important means to combat 
adulteration as FSWs are equipped with basic infrastructure for on spot testing of 
adulteration in various food commodities. Currently, 305 FSWs are deployed 
across 35 States/UTs to ensure last-mile testing and awareness, especially in 
remote areas. 
Further, the Food Safety Magic Box, meticulously curated  by FSSAI, 
facilitates the detection of adulteration in food. It consists of "quick screening tests" 
that a common person can perform at the household level, enabling a broad 
assessment of potential adulteration in their food in cases of doubt. It is an 
educational tool, which can ignite curiosity among students and empower them to 
perform quick tests to identify potential adulterants in food. It consists of a total of 
107 tests across 06 food categories, with 27 tests for detection of adulterants in 
Dairy and Dairy Products. The tests include detection of adulterants like starch, 
urea, detergent, cellulose, maltodextrin salicylic acid, glucose in Milk along with 
detection of artificial dyes in butter, khoa, cheese; and vanaspati/ hydrogenated 
edible fat in ghee etc. For detection of food adulterants at household by the citizens 
themselves FSSAI has developed Detect Adulteration with Rapid Test (DART) 
booklet. DART covers more than 50 tests that can be performed easily at 
household including test methods for milk and milk products.
