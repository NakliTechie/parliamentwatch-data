CHAPTER-I 
 
An Introduction to the Ministry of Cooperation 
 
 Cooperative Societies form a distinct separate  group among the societies.  The 
International Cooperative Alliance (ICA) defines a Cooperative as an autonomous 
association of persons united voluntarily to meet their common economic, social and 
cultural needs and aspirations through a jointly -owned and democratically -controlled 
enterprise.      
 
1.2 The Ministry of Cooperation was created vide Cabinet Secretariat’s Gazette 
Notification dated 06th July 2021.  The Ministry of Cooperation  is responsible for providing 
a separate administrative, legal and policy framework for strengthening the cooperative 
movement in the country. Its aim is to deepen co -operatives as a true people -based 
movement reaching up to the grassroots and develop a co operative based economy 
model where each member works with a spirit of responsibility.  
 
Mandate of Ministry of Cooperation 
1.3 The Mandate of the Ministry as contained in the Gazette Notification dated 6th July, 
2021 issued for creation of this Ministry are as under: 
1. General Policy in the field of Co -operation and Co -ordination of co -operation 
activities in all Sectors,  
2. Realization of vision “from cooperation to prosperity”, 
3. Strengthening of cooperative movement in the country and deepening its reach up to 
the grassroots, 
4. Promotion of cooperative-based economic development model, including the spirit of 
responsibility among its Members to develop the country, 
5. Creation of appropriate policy, legal and institutional framework to help cooperat ives 
realize their potential, 
6. Matters relating to National Co-operative Organization, 
7. National Co-operative Development Corporation, 
8. Incorporation, regulation and winding up of Co -operative societies with objects not 
confined to one State including administration of ‘the Multi-State Co-operative Societies 
Act, 2002 (39 of 2002)’, 

2 
 
9. Training of personnel of Co-operative Departments and Co-operative Institutions 
(including education of members, office bearers and non-officials). 
 
Schemes and Programmes of Ministry of Cooperation 
1.4     According to t he Ministry of Cooperation, the implementation of the Central  Sector 
Integrated Scheme on Agricultural Cooperation (CSISAC), which was transferred to it by 
the erstwhile Department of Agriculture, Cooperation & Farmers Welfare was ceased w.e.f. 
2022-23. The CSISAC Scheme comprised of following Sub-Schemes for providing 
assistance to National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC) , National Council 
for Cooperative Training (NCCT) , National Cooperative Union of India (NCUI)  and for 
Multi-State Cooperative Societies :   
i. Assistance to National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC) 
Programmes for Development of Cooperatives - under this scheme subsidy is 
provided to cooperative societies through NCDC. 
ii. Assistance to Cooperative Education and Training -This scheme is implemented 
through the National Cooperative Union of India (NCUI) and the National Council 
for Cooperative Training (NCCT). 
iii. Assistance to Multi State Cooperative Societies. 
1.5 The Ministry of Cooperation  had initiated an exercise to revamp  and replace the 
CSISAC Scheme with the proposed N ew Schemes of (i) Digitalization of PACS  ; (ii) 
Education & Training ; (iii) Credit Guarantee Fund for Cooperatives  and (iv) Sahkar se 
Samriddhi Yojana with following Sub-Components :           
a. Recapitalization of PACS 
b. Seed money for new PACS 
c. Revival of defunct PACS 
d. Transformation of PACS into multi-role cooperatives on the lines of FPOs 
e. Assistance to cooperative societies in branding, marketing and trade 
f. Capital subsidy for creation of basic infrastructure  
g. Creation of National Database of cooperative societies)

Autonomous Bodies and Implementing Agencies of Ministry of Cooperation 
1.8    When asked about the details of Autonomous Bodies and Implementing Agencies 
of the Ministry of Cooperation, the Committee were furnished a written reply as under : 
"National Council for Cooperative Training (NCCT)  - NCCT is an autonomous 
society under Ministry of Cooperation, conducts trainings through its 20 constituent 
institutes which are - a national -level Vaikunth Mehta National Institute of 
Cooperative Management (VAMNICOM) Pune, 5 Regional Institutes of Cooperative 
Management (RICMs), 14 Institutes of Cooperative Management (ICMs) located in 
different States. NCCT has been in existence for decades and has been imparting 
training to cooperatives across India through its network of 20 constituent institutes. 
It plays a cent ral role in the implementation of cooperative training & awareness 
schemes of the Ministry.  
National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC)  - NCDC is an apex 
Level Statutory Institution under the Ministry of Cooperation. NCDC was established 
in March 1963 under an Act of Parliament (NCDC Act, 1962) and amended in 1974 
and 2002. The scope of activities has been further enlarged time to time. 
A major objective of the Corporation is to promote, strengthen and develop farmer 
cooperatives for increasing pro duction and productivity and establishing post 
harvest facilities. The Corporation's focus is on programmes of agricultural 
marketing and inputs, processing, storage and marketing of agriculture produce and 
supply seeds, fertilizer and other agricultural i nputs etc. In the Non -farm Sector, the 
Corporation's endeavour is to equip cooperatives with facilities to promote income 
generating activities, with special focus on weaker sections such as dairy, livestock. 
handlooms, coir, jute, sericulture, poultry, fi shery, scheduled caste & scheduled 
tribes. women cooperatives etc. 
NCDC lends through the State Government or directly to cooperative societies on 
certain prescribed terms and conditions. NCDC provides financial assistance in the 
form of loan (both term lo an and investment loan) and subsidy (subject to 
availability from Government of India). 
NCDC has launched new schemes for cooperatives in various sectors such as 
‘Swayamshakti Sahkar’ for SHG; ‘Deerghavadhi Krishak Sahkar’ for long term 
agricultural credit; ‘Dairy Sahkar’ for dairy and ‘Neel Sahkar’ for fisheries. "

CHAPTER-II 
 
ISSUES AND CHALLENGES IN THE COOPERATIVE SECTOR OF THE COUNTRY 
 
COOPERATION POLICY 
 According to the Ministry of Cooperation New Cooperation Policy is being prepared 
through extensive consultations with stakeholders.  A two-day National Conference was 
organized by Ministry of Coop eration on 12 th & 13th April, 2022 in which Cooperation 
Secretaries / RCSs from all the States/UTs participated. During the conference, 
discussions were also held on legal framework, identification of regulatory, policy & 
operational barriers; ease of doing business; reforms fo r strengthening governance; 
promoting new and social cooperatives; revitalizing defunct ones; making cooperatives 
vibrant economic entities; cooperation among cooperatives and increasing membership of 
cooperatives. Suggestions on the draft Policy were also  sought from stakeholders 
including general public through my Gov portal and the website of the Ministry. 
 
2.2. On being asked about the present status of 'New Cooperation Policy', the Committee 
were furnished a written reply as under : 
"A National level committee has been constituted on 2nd September 2022 
under the chairmanship of Shri Suresh Prabhakar Prabhu, consisting of experts 
of the cooperative sector, representatives from National/ State/ District/ Primary 
level cooperative societies, Secretaries ( Cooperation) and RCSs from 
States/UTs, officers from Central Ministries/ Departments to formulate the New 
National Cooperation Policy. The formulation of New National Cooperation 
Policy will help in realising the vision of 'Sahakar se Samriddhi', promoting  the 
cooperative based economic development model, strengthening the 
cooperative movement in the country and deepening its reach up to the 
grassroots. In this regard, consultations were earlier held with stakeholders and 
suggestions for formulating the new  Policy were invited from the Central 
Ministries/ Departments, States/UTs, National Cooperative Federations, 
Institutions and also from the general public. The National level committee will 
analyse the collated feedback, policy suggestions & recommendation s to 
formulate the draft of the new Policy. A number of meetings have already been 
held by the Committee and its report is expected by the end of February, 2023. "

National Level Data Repository 
2.4. The Ministry of Cooperation has initiated the process of developing an exhaustive 
digital database of cooperative societies namely National Cooperative Database for having 
an authentic & updated data repository of cooperatives working in different sectors across the 
country. The proposed Database will facilitate all stakeholders in policy making & 
implementation to strengthen the cooperative movement in the country. It would also help the 
cooperative sector for its better positioning in the business environment. The proposed 
database would also bring synergy among various databases maintained by Central line 
Ministries and State Governments.  The data fields of database were finalised after extensive 
discussions and consultations with various stakeholders viz. State/UT RCS, National 
Cooperative Federations and other Stakeholders at various levels (Primary to Apex).  
 Ministry set up Project Monitoring Group (PMG) in collaboration with NIC for 
software development. 
 Ministry also constitut ed Steering Committee and Advisory Committee incl. State 
RCS, National Federations and regulatory Boards. 
 About 1100 Nodal Officers at District & State level were nominated by State 
RCS/Functional Registrars for monitoring and data validation.  
 In order to make cooperatives as a true people -based movement reaching upto the 
grassroots levels, it was decided to use services of 560 local interns enrolled on All 
India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) Portal for data collection.  
 
2.5. Ministry decided to develop National Cooperative Database in a phased manner. For 
Phase-I of National Cooperative Database, data collection of three cooperative sectors i.e. 
Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS), Dairy and Fisheries started from 1 st December, 
2022. Out of about 2.61 lakh registered PACS, Dairy & Fisheries Cooperative Societies, the

data of about 2.58 lakh (about 99%) Cooperative Societies have been collected so far. After 
validation and certification by various stakeholders, the database would be available soon.

Cooperation intimated inter-alia as under : 

10 
 
"At present, 83 defunct Multi -State Cooperative Societies including 05 Cooperative 
Banks are under liquidation and the proceedings are under way. The Office of 
CRCS is continuously monitoring and reviewing the status of proceedings of

Liquidation Process of Defunct MSCSs

stakeholders like depositors, employees, members etc. as well as time consuming 
legal proceedings in various courts of laws a nd other tribunals / forums, are 
ongoing.  
Further, to expedite liquidation, the O/o CRCS is providing legal assistance and

liquidation proceedings. O/o CRCS has also circulated relevant provisions of laws

3.12 In order to digitize the Primary Agricultural  Credit Societies (PACS), Government of 
India has approved Computerization of PACS project on 29 th June 2022 to computerize 
63,000 functional PACS with total budget outlay of Rs. 2516 Cr., across the country. This 
project entails bringing all the functiona l PACS onto an ERP (Enterprise Resource 
Planning) based common software, linking them with NABARD through State Cooperative 
Banks (StCBs) and District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs). Following are the major 
components of the project: 
1. National Level Comm on Software:  A National Level software will be developed 
which will link PACS with NABARD through District Central Cooperative Banks 
(DCCBs) and State Cooperative Banks (StCBs). The software will be owned by the 
Ministry. 
2. National Level Data Repository:  A National Level Data Repository would be set up 
at NABARD for storage of data. 
3. Procurement of Hardware:  Hardware will be procured by the States/ UTs for 
providing support to the PACS. 
4. Digitization of Legacy Data:  All the legacy data would be digitized and uploaded on 
the national software. 
5. Support System: A support system would be set up at State and District level for 
providing support to the PACS during the on-boarding process. 
6. Training and Awareness:  Training w ould be provided to the manpower available 
with PACS for smooth transition on the national level software. 
7. Project Management Unit: PMUs have been set up at Central and State level for 

22 
 
coordination and monitoring of the project. 
 
3.13 Computerization of PACS will provide various benefits such as increasing the 
efficiency of their operations, ensuring speedy disbursal of loans, lowering of transaction 
costs, reducing imbalances in payments, seamless accounting with DCCBs and StCBs 
and increasing transparency . It will also enhance trustworthiness in the working of PACS 
among farmers. Implementation of Common Accounting System (CAS) and Management 
Information System (MIS) would enable PACS to carry out their operations online and 
obtain refinance/ loans for the ir various activities from NABARD through District Central 
Cooperative Banks (DCCBs) and State Cooperative Banks (St.CBs). 
 
3.14 Proposals to computerize 50,241 PACS have been received from 22 States and 4 
UTs. In addition to this, additional proposals are  also likely to be received from other States. 
The development of software has started and process of procurement of hardware would be 
started shortly. Also, process for empanelment of system integrators would be completed by 
February, 2023. Thereafter, pr ocess for digitization of records would be started. Hence, to 
complete the abovementioned processes and to onboard 50,241 PACS on National level 
software, BE of Rs. 968.24 has been proposed and allocated the funds thus provided for is 
sufficient for proposed plan of action.

Schemes & Programmes Implemented by Other Ministries / Departments  
3.37 When asked about the  details regarding Schemes and Programmes by other 
Ministries/ Departments/ Institutions/ autonomous bodies of Government of India for the 
promotion of Cooperative Societies in the country, it was stated : 
"In order to promote cooperative societies in the country, particularly in the sectors of 
agriculture, dairy and fisheries, Ministry of Cooperation has identified the following 10 
existing schemes of various Ministries / Departments of Government of India, for 
convergence by leveraging the ‘whole-of-Government’ approach: 
 
1). Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare 
i. Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF)  – It envisages creation of post -harvest 
management infrastructure and community farming assets through 
incentives and financi al support to improve agriculture infrastructure in the 
country. All loans under this financing facility will have interest subvention of 
3% per annum up to a limit of ₹ 2 crore per project. 
ii. Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure (AMI)  – It is meant for hol istic 
development of agricultural value chain, including promotion of value 
addition and processing at farmer’s level. Subsidy at the rate of 25% to 
33.33% is available to eligible beneficiaries through channelizing agencies 
i.e., NABARD, NCDC, DA&FW, etc. 
iii. Agriculture Mechanization – It envisages setting up of custom hiring centers 
and hubs for hi -tech and high value farm equipment. Subsidy at the rate of 
40% is available for projects costing up to ₹ 60 lakhs.  
iv. Integrated Development of Horticulture – It is meant for holistic growth of the 
horticulture sector. Various activities, like horticultural mechanization, 
creation of post -harvest infrastructure including cold storage and marketing 
infrastructure get subsidy in the range of 25-55% under the scheme. 
 
2) Ministry of Food Processing Industries 
i. PM Formalization of Micro Food Processing Enterprises Scheme (PMFME)  
– It aims to create an organized supply chain for micro food processing units. 
Cooperatives can avail 35% subsidy (up to ₹ 10 Lakh) for upgradation of 
food processing units. ₹ 3 crore credit -linked grant is available for setting up 
of common infrastructure like common processing facility, premise for 
sorting, grading, warehouse and cold storage at farm-gate, etc. 
ii. Pradhan Mantri Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (P MKSY) – It aims to create 
modern infrastructure with efficient supply chain management from farm gate 
to retail outlet. Subsidy of up to 35% of the project cost is provided under the 
scheme which can go up to 50% in difficult areas. 
 
3) Department of Animal H usbandry and Dairying, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal 
Husbandry & Dairying 
i. National Programme for Dairy Development (NPDD)  – It aims to enhance 
quality of milk and milk products and increase share of organized 

33 
 
procurement, processing, value addition and marketing. Financial assistance 
in the form of subsidy is provided under the scheme.  
ii. Dairy Processing and Infrastructure Development Fund (DIDF)  - It aims to 
modernize and create milk processing infrastructure. Loans under this fund 
will involve an interest subvention of 2.5% per annum. 
 
4) Department of Fisheries, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying 
i. Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY)  – It aims to address 
critical gaps in fish production and productivity, quality, technology, post -
harvest infrastructure and management, modernization and strengthening of 
value chain. For beneficiary-oriented activities under the centrally sponsored 
scheme component, financial assistance up to 40% of the total project/ unit 
cost is provided. 
ii. Fisheries and Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund (FIDF) – It aims 
to create fisheries infrastructure facilities, both in marine and inland fisheries

Management of Multi-State Cooperative Societies (MSCS) 
 
6. The Committee note that as on date there are about 1481 registered Multi State 
Co-operative Societies (MSCS) in the country and 83 of them including five 
Cooperative Banks are under liquidation. The liquidation proceedings are time 
consuming due to legal p roceedings in various courts of laws and other tribunals / 
forums, involvement of multiple Law Enforcement Agencies, multitude of stake 
holders like depositors, employees, members, etc. The Committee also notice that 
no liquidation case of defunct MSCSs ha s been settled during the last 10 years. The 
Committee, taking this fact in view, urge the Ministry of Cooperation to take all 
necessary corrective measures to protect the interests of all the Members of these 
MSCSs at the earliest. The Committee also reco mmend that the Ministry of 
Cooperation may consider to introduce a suitable Capital / Credit Insurance Scheme 
for Cooperative Societies especially for the MSCS to protect financial interests of all 
stakeholders of Cooperative Societies specifically for dep ositors Members of 
MSCSs at the earliest. The Committee would like to be apprised of the action taken 
in the matter.
