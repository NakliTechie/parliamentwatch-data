The Ministry of Industry (Department of SSI and A&RI) was requested to furnish 
detailed background notes on the Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP), Prime 
Minister's Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) and Coir Industry, Mahila Coir Yojana & Integrated Coir 
Development Project (ICDP). The Ministry in their background notes have inter alia stated that : 
(i) Khadi and Village Industries play a key role in the strengthening of rural economy 
by generating large employment opportunities in the rural areas at low capital cost with the help 
of Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC). As such KVIC has played an important 
role in augmentation of the national economy. The functions of KV1C comprise of planning, 
promoting and assisting the implementation of programmes encompassing the development of 
Khadi and Village Industries. For this, they undertake financing of eligible agencies, training of 
presons concerned, supply of tools/implements, R&D in K VIC sector, promotion of sale and 
marketing of K VI products and encouragement of cooperative efforts among the persons engaged 
in Khadi and Village industries etc. 

The growth of KVIC can be gauged from the figures of I 955-56, when there were only 
2 State KVI Boards, 242 registered Institutions and 60 Cooperatives and the number has now 
increased to 30 State Khadi and Village Industries Boards, 4869 registered Institutions, 301 12 
Cooperatives, 7.80 lakh individuals and about 15000 Sales outlets operating in 2.48 lakh 
villages in the country. The production has increased toRs. 4516.26 crore (Khadi Rs. 626.40 
crore and village industries Rs. 3889.86 crore) in 1996-97 from the level ofRs. 16.47 crore 
(Khadi Rs. 5.54 crore and village industries Rs. I 0.93 crore ) in 1955-56. Similarly the 
employment level has increased to 58.17 lakh persons (Khadi 14.79 lakh and Village 
Industries 43.38 lakh and Village Industries 3.07 lakh) in 1996-97 from 9.64 lakh persons 
(Khadi 6.57 lakh and Village Industries 3.07 lakh) in 1955-56. There are 116 types of village 
industries as against 26 village industries covered earlier. 
KVIC extends financial assistance to its implementing agencies in the form of grants 
and loans for Khadi and Polyvastra activities. 
So far village industries are concerned, the beneficiaries are expected to to prepare 
viable projects for getting assistance from KVIC. The implementing agencies which avail of 
bank loans under K VIC sponsorship are required to pay 4% interest and the difference 
between the interest charged by the banks and 4%, is borne out by KVIC. 
Besides, certain Department Bhawans in major cities which have been established by 
KVIC, nearly 16000 sales outlets are being managed by NGOs working under K VIC. 
There is provision of rebates (standing and special rebates) on various varieties of 
Khadi. The basis is to make Khadi compete effectively with mill-made products and to 
increase the production and sale of Khadi. Standing rebate continues throughout the year 
while special rebates is provided on selective khadi for specific period during festivals. 
Generally the State Government also provide the matching rebate and the rebate policy is 
revised by the Government from time to time. KVIC get the fund from Ministry of Industry, 
Government of India both on plan and non-plan side. 
In order to give focus on accelerated development of K VI programme, the Govt. of 
Ind_ia in 1993, appointed a High Power Committee (HPC) under the Chairmanship of the then 
Prime Minister, Sh. P. V. Narasimha Rao which submitted its recommendations in 1994. 
One of the major recommendations of the Committee was to generate additional two million 
employment out of which 17.53 lakh of additional jobs were envisaged under village 
industries sector. In order to fulfil this target, KVIC has taken various initiatives and 
launched a programme called as Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP). This 
comprises mainly of: 
(a) DISTRICT SPECIAL EMPLOYMENT PROGRAMME 
Here the target was to generate sustainable employment for about I 0,000 persons 
per selected district, where the districts are generally in the backward category. 
Presently, this scheme is being financed from K VIC budgetary source as well as 
institutional finance. 
(b) 125 BLOCK DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME 
This programme was initiated in 125 blocks to commemorate 125'h birth 
anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi and was launched on the 2"d October, 1994. It 
targeted for minimum sustainable employment of I 000 per block over a period of time. 
This scheme was also to be implemented under the joint finance by KVIC, DRDA and 
Banks. 

(c) NATIONAL PROGRAMME ON SELECTED INDUSTRIES 
This programme includes handmade paper industry (HMP), leather industry, bee 
keeping, pottery etc. 
(d) STRENGTHENING OF VILLAGE INDUSTRIES PROGRAMME 
KYIC are also making efforts to strengthen the other on going village industries 
programmes and envisage to absorb more employment in time to come. 
(e) EXPANSION OF KHADI PROGRAMME 
KVIC proposed to increase the production of khadi both in terms of value and 
meterage. As a result of which it envisaged to create additional employment.

(ii) Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) was launched on October 2, 1993 and the 
objectiv~ of the scheme was to facilitate in setting up of 7 lakh micro enterprises by the 
educated unemployed youth during the 8
1
h plan period. It has been continuing during the 
current year. During the year 1993-94, the Scheme covered only urban areas of the country 
and since 01.04.94 it has been extended to whole of the country. 
The Scheme is a central sector scheme and the Central Govt. provides funds for subsidy, 
training , supervision and other support. The Scheme is monitored in the office of DC(SSI), 
New Delhi. The States and the banks are the implementing agencies at the field level. The 
beneficiaries under the Scheme are identified by the States and loan component for setting up 
the micro enterprises is provided by the banks to the beneficiaries. The unemployed youth, 
normally apply to DICs and the preliminary screening is conducted by a task force at the DIC 
level. Thereafter, the suitable applications are forwarded to the banks who undertak~ a 

detailed scrutiny and thereafter sanction the loan. A managerial tramtng programme is 
imparted to beneficiaries whose cases have been sanctioned before the actual disbursal of 
loan. 
The target for the current year is 2.20 lakh. The budget for : 998-99 is Rs. 94 crore for 
subsidy and Rs. 16 crore for entrepreneurial development, amouNting to Rs. I I 0 crore . 
(iii) The Ccir industry is primarily an agro based rural cottage industry and more than 90 per 
cent of the employment generated is in rural areas. Majority of the Coir workers are in the 
spinning sector, working in household units. The spinning activities are conducted in open 
yard in the rural areas and prolonged adverse weather conditions affect the production. 
The Coir industry started and flourished in Kerala, which has a long coastal line and 
abundant lakes, lagoons and back waters which provide the natural conditions required for 
retting. The Coir industry gradually started expanding into other coconut producing States, 
mainly in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. 
The thrust areas for developing the Coir industry are as follows : -
(a) modernisation of production infrastructure; 
(b) upgradation of skill of workers through training for increasing productivity 
and income; 
(c) expansion of domestic market, increase in export through publicity and 
propaganda; 
(d) promotion of new Coir products like Geotextiles, Coir ply, Coir pith etc.; 
(e) promotion of research and development in process improvement; 
(f) product diversification; 
(g) elimination of drudgery and pollution: and 
(h) extension of results of R&D to the concerned sections in the Coir industry. 
There is Mahila Coir Yojana, which is intended to provide self employment to rural 
women artisans in Coir fibre producing regions. 
3. The Integrated Coir Development Project is being implemented in the Coir producing 
States as a modernisation component of the centrally sponsored Scheme of Cooperativisation. 
The concept of the project is worth mentioning. The I COP is being implemented in the States of 
Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. All Coir producing States are being persuaded to implement 
ICDP in their States.

2. The Sub-Committee was informed by the Chairman, Coir Borad about the state of affairs 
of Coir industry. The Indian Coir Industry has been a cottage industry, traditionally and is 
confined to Handloom Sector. Coir Industry has two components namely, white and brown 
fibres . White fibre is extracted from husk of matured cocount after a process of retting. On the 
other hand, mechanical means like decorticator and defibring machines are applied to extract 
brown fibres directly from coconut husk. In Tamil Nadu, coconut crop is cultivated in an 
approximate area of2.7 l&kh hectares, wherein the annual production comprises of nearly 3282 
million nuts. In this State although the.coconut grows all around, but the coir industry _ is mainly 
confined in districts like Salem, Nagercoil, Thanjavur, Madurai, Tirunetveli and Cuddalore. 
According to the sources of the State Govt.'s Department oflndustries arid Commerce, there are 
4864 coir units in Tamil Nadu where they function both in organised and un-organised sectors. 

On a pointed query, the Sub-Committee was apprised that in the extraction of 1 Kg. of 
coir fibre, about 2 Kg. of coir pith is generated. Concerning coir pith, it was told that coir pith is a 
waste material and its accumulation in huge quantity will impose environmental problem. In it, 
25% lignin is found and as a result of which it fails to decompose easily. Central Coir Research 
Institute has developed a technology to provide a solution for this problematic waste material. 
Coir pith is converted into organic m·anure within 30 days by applying a fungus called as pithplus. 
This is the method of composting. Pithplus is being produced on commercial basis also by CCRI 
through its Pilot Scale Laboratory. 
The Sub-Committee was further apprised that brown fibre is produced from dry or green 
coconut husks without retting by mechanical means, mainly in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra 
Pradesh etc. In these States there is lack ofretting facilities. The quality of the green husk fibre it 
much inferior to the retted fibre. A technology has been developed by CCRI, under which green 
husk fibre is treated using 'Coirret' which is a bacterial cocktail. The coirret is also made 
available to coir-industry from the Pilot Scale Laboratory established at CCRI. 
The Sub-Committee was next informed that like coconut growers in Kerala , 
Cooperatives do not exist in Tamil Nadu for husk producers. This is because coconuts are sold 
directly by growers to the merchants. Only from these merchants , husks go to industri al fold. So, 
there is no direct connection between coconut growers and coir industry, mainly concentrates on 
fi.bre extraction . However , yarn spinning activities are carried out in the rural areas which are 
mostly in the un-organised sector. There are 47 coir spinning Cooperatives functioning all around 
the State. The installed capacity of these societies is of 6400 M.Ts of yarn . But for the district of 
Nagercoil, Coir workers in Tamil Nadu are not so much skilled as compared to coir workers in 
Kerala. Here the coir industry is being developed mainly by imparting training in yarn spinning 
and mattings through the assistance provided by coir Board, DRDA etc.

3. The Sub-Committee also held discussion with the representatives of NGOs, engaged in 
self employment, training, promotion of entrepreneurs etc. 
The Bengal Federation of Certified Khadi Institutions apprised of the persisting problems 
which have came as a stumbling-block towards the development of Khadi and Village Industries 
in the State of West Bengal. The KVICs shifting of policy in the matter of financial assistance to 
the Institutions has turned the situation from bad to worse. The Banks in the State are very much 
reluctant to come to the aid of Khadi and Village Industries programmes. In the changed policy 
of KVIC, the implementing Institutions are made to seek working fund loans from the banks, but 
inspite of every effort the Institutions have failed to secure working fund loans form the banks, 
which generally shows their lackadaisical attitude towards implementation of Khadi and Village 
Industries programmes. As a result, KVI programmes in the State are receiving serious set back 
throwing a large number of poor and hapless artisans and workers out of employment. 
The Sub-Committee was also informed about Institutions undertaking Khadi & Village 
Industries that there is a provision for renewal of KVIC certificates after every three years. But it 
was found that to their utter dismay, most of the Institutions are denied renewal of their 
certificates for one pretext or the other or for the nothing. There is a vicious circle active behind 
making the things complicated. The Institutions are put to· unnecessary harassment to get their 
certificates renewed. As a result, activities of the running Institutions are getting severe set back. 
Thus to ease the problem, the Sub-Committee was urged that the system of renewal of KVIC's 
certificates should immediately be dispensed with and instead, the certificates once granted 
should be made permanent. 
The Sub-Committee was further informed by the Secretary of Gramin Shilpodyog 
Sanstha, Murshidabad that this Institution, with a view to organise silk artisans of Murshidabad 
district, started it operation on production of silk in the year 1970-71 as a small category 
Institution. With the arduous work of staff, artisans and guidance of the officials of K VIC, this 
Institution has attained the category of major Institution within a span of twenty-seven years. The 
Sub-Committee appreciated the Institution, for the progress it has achieved in the fields of muslin, 
polyvastra, leather, rural engineering and village oil industry apart from silk.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE 
COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
1. In view of price-rise and inflation, the project cost limit under PMRY for the 
Services/Industry/Business sector, does not seem to be appropriate. The Sub-Committee 
accordingly recommends that a review of the project cost limit for Services/Industry/Business 
sector should be undertaken to adjust it suitably with the present cost of Rupees. The ceiling for 
project cost/loan should be realistic, taking into account the activity and location of the project. 
2. The Sub-Committee notes that under PMRY, there is subsidy ceiling of Rs.7,500/-. The 
Sub-Committee is of the view that the practice of subsidy ceiling may be dispensed with and 
instead 15 per cent subsidy may be made, admissible for all quantum of loan amounts. 
3. The Sub-Committee observes that the recovery under PMRY is far from satisfaction. 
The Sub-Committee, accordingly, is of the view that the recovery process ofPMRY loans at bank 
level can be strengthened by creating District level Recovery calls with every bank. 
4. The Sub-Committee feels that recovery of pending cases should be expedited by fixing 
accountability at district administration level also. In addition the record of pending cases be 
computerised and updated at regular intervals. There should be time bound specific targets to be 
monitored at District level by District Magistrates. 
5. The Sub-Committee impresses upon the Government to explore the feasibility of setting 
up of Special Courts for recovery of PMRY loans under defaults, as delays occur in serving of 
notices and appearance in the Court. 
6. The Sub-Committee observes that bankers generally raise their concern about the late 
communication of targets to them from the Government. For example, targets are being 
communicated in August/September wherein they are to be achievable by March (end of financial 
year). 
The Sub-Committee recommends that the targets for PMRY should be communicated to 
Banks from the Government, well in time to avoid any kind of time constraint. 
7. The Sub-Committee impresses upon the Government that DICs should avoid 
disproportionate sponsoring of applications under PMRY. This act, in return, will minimise the 
pendency rate of applications. 
8. The Sub-Committee observes that under PMRY, quality applications are not being 
sponsored to banks. As regards this shortcoming, the Sub-Committee feels that Branch Managers 
should be involved actively in selecting the beneficiaries and the activities.

9. As per the provisions made under PMRY, loans should be in the ratio of 50%, 30% and 
20% in respect of industry, services and business sector. During.the course of the discussion, the 
Sub-Committee came to know that mostly applications are received for business activities and 
applications regarding industry are few in numbers. The Sub-Committee feels that steps should 
be taken to ensure that the ratio of loans for all three activities ar:e maintained according to 
provisions contained in the Scheme. 
I 0. This is the general view that viability aspects of proposals are ignored by DICs while 
sponsoring the applications of PMRY. The Sub-Committee recommends that DICs should go 
thoroughly in details of applications including spot verification, technical feasibility and financial 
viability of each and every application. 
In this connection, the Sub-Committee further recommends that the plea of helplessness 
from DICs, for want of staff should not be taken as an excuse. 
II. The Sub-Committee has noticed that DICs fail to complete the identification process well 
in advance. As a matter of fact, in place of transmission of applications to banks under PMRY, in 
a phased manner, they do the same in bunching. 
The Sub-Committee therefore, recommends that it has to be ensured that the flow of 
applications from DICs to banks, take place in a phased manner and at any cost the bunching of 
applications should be stopped. 
12. The Sub-Committee notes that PMRY Club is a novel idea. As a result of 
implementation of this, majority of the problems such as creation of assets, marketing 
arrangement and repayment of bank's dues etc. will be easily overcome. The Sub-Committee, 
therefore, recommends that efforts should be taken to ensure that PMRY Clubs smoothly start its 
operation in all the prominent centres. 
13. The Sub-Committee notes with discontent that the reduction in disbursement as 
compared to loan amount applied for has been dampening the spirit and enthusiasm of borrowers 
of PMRY. So, the Sub-Committee impresses upon the Government to take suitable corrective 
measures as regards the cases, the quantum of which is sizeable, where it is presumed by bankers 
that borrowers are incapable of executing the projects or there is likelihood of less demand in the 
area due to saturation of activity and thus the loan applied for is reduced without following any 
yardstick. 
14. The Sub-Committee is unhappy to note that in severa l cases of PMRY loans, the 
disbursem ent is stopped or delayed on unjustified/flimsy grounds. Thus, the Sub-Committee 
recommends that suitable measures should be taken concernin g the stoppage /delay of 
disbursement ofPM RY loans on such grounds.
