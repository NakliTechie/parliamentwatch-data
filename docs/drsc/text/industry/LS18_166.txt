REPORT
The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Ministry of Agro and Rural
Industries  on the Recommendations contained in the  152 nd     Report (of the Department-related Parliamentary
Standing Committee on Industry) on  Demands for Grants (2004-05) pertaining to the Ministry of Agro and Rural
Industries .  The Report was presented /laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha on  the 26th  August, 2004.
2.         Action Taken Notes have been received from the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries    in respect of the  
recommendations contained in the Report.   These have been categorized as follows:
Chapter - I- The recommendations/observations, which have been accepted by the Ministry :- Paras : 2.4 ; 12.2;  12.5; 17.2;
20.5;  21.11; 21.14; 22.9;  25.6;  26.3 and  29.2
 The Committee is pleased to observe that  the   Ministry  has accepted its  recommendation as  mentioned in Chapter –I of the 
Report. 
Chapter - II- The recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the
Government's replies: - Paras :- 4.9  and 11.6
 In respect of these recommendations the Committee is  convinced  with the logic and explanation  advocated by the
Ministry  and therefore   does not want to  pursue  further. 
Chapter - III- The recommendations/observations in respect of which the Committee has not accepted replies of the Ministry: -
Paras: 12.4; 12.9; 14.2; 16.2; 21.16;  23.4;  24.2; 27.2; 28.2; 30.2 and 31.4
 The Committee expresses its concern over the non-implementations  of recommendations contained in  Chapter-III  and desires
that the Ministry should furnish  logical  explanation  in respect of these recommendations.  
Chapter - IV- The recommendations/observations in respect of which  final replies of the  Ministry have not been received :- 2.5
;  9.2; 10.7; 12.7; 15.2; 22.6 and 22.10
In respect  of recommendations placed in Chapter-IV, the Ministry has  either   furnished the interim replies or couched its
language in  vague terms like  noted for compliance/guidance, therefore, the Committee desires  that the Ministry should furnish
Action Taken Notes in  respects of recommendations categorized   in Chapter-IV  in detail and to the point, as per the intention
and spirit of the recommendations of the Committee.
3.         The Details of the ATR is being discussed in various Chapters in the succeeding pages.

Recommendation
1.0       Professionalism in finance, marketing, HRD, technology and manpower upgradation in the KVI sector
1.1       The Committee hopes that the creation of independent Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries would reinforce
and strengthen the functioning of the ARI sector as a whole, by highlighting its present status and ensuring timely policy
interventions when needed. The contribution of this sector can be further streamlined and strengthened by injecting
more professionalism in the areas of finance, marketing, HRD, technology and manpower upgradation in the KVI
sector, revival of sick khadi institutions, renovation and modernization of marketing outlets.

taken the following fresh initiatives in this direction:

1.7.1    The Committee recommends that the following Steps may be taken  to promote the marketing and sales of coir
products in the domestic market:-
 
(i)
                  Intensification of promotional measures like publicity through print and  electronic media and increased
participation in trade fairs and exhibitions
(ii)                Organisation of Buyer –Seller Meets in potential regions and  organisation of special sales campaign
and festival discount offer to promote sales.
(iii)               Organising collective participation of manufacturers in selected trade fairs and exhibitions and
(iv)              Popularisation of new products like coir geotextiles, garden articles, coir pith based products through
awareness creation programmes etc.
Action Taken
1.7.2    Various measures have been adopted to popularize coir and coir products in India. These include advertisement
through electronic and print media, erection of hoardings, distribution of pamphlets, distribution of calendars and
diaries, etc.    Product promotion advertisements have been given in leading magazines and journals, highlighting the
unique features of coir and coir products as an eco-friendly live-in- style product. The Coir Board participated in 54
exhibitions in the country during the year.  The participation in these exhibitions has contributed to popularizing coir  
products, particularly in areas where the use of coir products were less known.
Advertisements were also released  through the print media in local languages.  Display of banners at vantage points is
another publicity measure. The recommendations of the Committee are noted and the promotional measures will be
intensified.
1.7.3    The Coir Board participates in important exhibitions and also in specialized exhibitions for floor coverings,
handicraft items, gift items, etc., held in metropolitan cities.
Under the Market Development Assistance Scheme, assistance is provided to Apex Co-operative Societies, Central Co-
operative Societies, Public Sector Enterprises, etc. for innovative marketing strategies, including an element of
discounts.
1.7.4    The recommendation of the Committee to organize Buyer-Seller Meets is noted.
1.7.5    Assistance is provided to coir product manufacturers to participate in  exhibitions and fairs. In most cases,
participation in selected exhibitions is facilitated by  the Coir Board bearing the space rent fully/partially. The

RECOMMENDATION
1.8       R&D activities for Coir Industries
1.8.1    The Committee recommends that since the budgetary provisions for R&D for Coir Industries is sufficient all
efforts would be made for skimming out of benefits of R&D for the proliferation and promotion of Coir Industries.
ACTION TAKEN
1.8.2    Concerted efforts are being made for promotion of coir industry by propagating the new technologies developed
and the machinery fabricated for modernization of the industry. Technologies like application of Coirret has been
successfully transferred to enable  faster retting  and  to eliminate drudgery in retting operation, use of coir pith for
manufacture of coir Pith Organic Manure (C-POM) so as to avoid pollution being created by the coir pith hillocks in the
coir production centres, skill upgradation training in the spinning of coir yarn on motorised traditional ratt and
motorised ratts in the traditional sector, etc.
1.8.3    A new compact metallic handloom called "Anugraha" has also been developed for weaving of coir mattings,
even by women workers without  drudgery. The scientists who were instrumental for development of the loom were 
given technology awards by the National Research Development Corporation, New Delhi. Efforts have also been made
to transfer the technology for production of the looms and the design of the looms to the  six coir machinery
manufacturers registered with the Coir Board.     One of the major exporters from the coir sector has put up 36 compact
metallic looms for weaving coir geotextiles in a newly established coir unit in the Thirunelveli district of Tamil Nadu.
Assistance has also been extended to the exporter in fabrication of the loom as well as training the women workers in
weaving of coir geotextiles.  It is proposed to popularize the technology of the new loom in the non-traditional sectors
and include the loom inthe list of equipment eligible for payment of subsidy under the Mahila Coir Yojana.
1.8.4    Efforts are also being made to popularize diversified products in coir sector like coir ply boards, which is
considered to be a wood substitute, for its use in the Railways and other sectors. A collaborative project with the
Konkan Railway Corporation has already been taken up by the Coir Board for construction of foot- over bridge using
coir composites. The project may open up  a large market for coir composites.
1.8.5    Concerted efforts are being made for  popularization of coir pith    products. The export of coir pith from India
has made a quantum jump in the recent past.  During 2003-04, a total quantity of 29179   MTs of coir pith valued at
Rs.1975.92 lakh was exported from the country as against 21064  MTs valued at Rs. 1493.01 lakh during  2002-03. 
Apart from  its export, possibilities of using coir pith in  areas like extraction of valuable organic compounds and  as an
absorbents of toxic materials, for which a collaborate project has already been taken up by the Coir Board with the
Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Mumbai, are being explored. Regarding manufacture of C-POM using pithplus, a
technology for composting the pith in situ using perforated pipes in the hillocks of coir pith for increasing aeration has
been developed.  Using this method, the composting period of coir pith could be reduced to 21 days from 30 days.

Recommendation
1.11     Registration  and regularization  of Agro & Rural Industries  units
1.11.1  The Committee desires that government should initiate concrete steps for effective regularization and regulation
of Agro & Rural Industries.
Action Taken
1.11.2  In the initial phase of implementation of REGP, registration with Khadi and Village Industries Commission
(KVIC)/ State KVI Boards was not a pre-requisite.  However, with effect from 1 January 2003, registration of REGP
units with KVIC/KVIB has been made mandatory for grant of margin money.  As the projects are being financed with  
bank loans, the flow of funds and adjustment of margin money against the repayment scheme, etc., are regulated by
banks  as per  KVIC guidelines.  Further, regular meetings are also  held to review the progress of implementation of the
programme and  take corrective/remedial measures, wherever necessary.

112.1   The Committee recommends that constant attempts should be vigorously made for recovery of dues of finances

lengthy procedural formalities while accessing credit for their units.
Action Taken
1.12.2  With a view to  ensuring  recovery of loan dues, review  meetings   are  held with banks and  followed up with

conducted by a research institute viz., Vaikunthbhai Mehta Research Centre for Decentralised Industries, Mumbai,
shows that none of the beneficiaries  under REGP was considered as total defaulter by the financial agencies.  It can,
therefore, be said that under REGP, the recovery rate is 100 per cent.  It was further observed in survey that REGP
scheme, in majority of the cases,  resulted in good relations between the financial institutions and the entrepreneurs.  
KVIC has also launched the  scheme for Rural Industries Consultancy Services (RICS) Centres in order to help the
entrepreneurs in preparation of viable projects, thereby reducing the rejection rate. 
1.12.3  Several measures have been taken  for improving the recovery of loan over dues under the PMRY Scheme. 
These include  linking  additional targets with recovery performance, joint recovery drives by banks with DIC officials,
constitution of Recovery Cells in the Zonal offices of the banks, requesting State Governments to notify the recovery of
loan over dues under the PMRY Scheme at par with land revenue arrears and notify it under the respective land revenue
recovery laws.  During meetings with the banks and the State Governments emphasis has been laid on the recovery of
loan overdues.   It has been decided to restructure curriculum and methodology of training under the PMRY.  Under the
restructured curriculum and methodology, emphasis has been laid on making the entrepreneurs aware about the need for
timely repayment of loan taken under the PMRY and on selection of viable projects.  The need for selection of ‘viable
projects’ was also stressed in the meetings with the implementing banks and the States.  The RBI has advised the
implementing banks to fix area wise minimum unit cost of activity to tackle the under-financing of projects by banks. 
Several measures have been taken to cut down the time required for completing procedural formalities, e.g., issue of 
‘no-dues’ certificate within one month of the date of issue of verification request by the banks, time limit of fortnight for
disposing applications involving credit limit of Rs.25,000/- and those for over Rs.25,000/- within 8 to 9 weeks,
convening DIC Task Force Committee Meeting at least once in a month or more depending upon the applications
received in the district etc.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE
Lack of support from the financial institutions
           
            The Committee is perturbed to note that despite efforts made by the Ministry of SSI to enable KVIC to get
sufficient credit flow from the banking sector could not yielded into satisfactory result.   Moreover, financial institution
does not honour the Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) issued by KVIC in favour of khadi institution on the
basis of approved pattern of assistance resulting into insufficient flow of  working capital to khadi institutions. During 
2002-03, Rs. 378.07 crore worth of ISEC certificates were issued for providing working capial assistance to khadi
institutions.  Against this, the institutions could avail of Rs. 253.72 crore only , i.e., 67 per cent of the  amount in the
ISEC issued.  For 2003-04, the  amount of ISEC issued and  credit availed  of in the khadi sector was Rs. 387.09 crore
and Rs. 298.78 crore respectively, i.e., 77 per cent of the ISEC issued- although showing an improvement of 10 per cent
in availing  of the total ISEC  amount.
            The Committee also takes not of the fact that part of the working capital of the khadi institutions is also blocked
with various is also blocked with various State Governments in the form of arrears  of admissible rebates, therefore, the
Committee recommends that Ministry of ARI should apprise the Committee, state-wise the details of arrears on account
of admissible rates and take up follow up actions with the respective States Government and accordingly apprise the
Committee the outcome of its efforts so made.
The Committee is constrained to express its displeasure that the recommendation of the Committee has not been taken
in right spirit.  As the Committee has presented the 152
nd Report in August, 2004 with the recommendation that the
Ministry of ARI should come out with specific details of labour reforms for improving Agro and Rural Industries and
after the lapse of seven months in March, 2005 the Ministry of ARI come out with the idea that observation of the
Committee are noted for future guidance.   The Committee's recommendation in this regard intails a particular course of
action on the part of the Ministry of ARI i.e. brain storming session for chalking out the areas /  issues for reform in
existing labour laws affecting the ARI sector.  Here at the Committee has not issued any behavioral modification which
could have been noted for future guidance which performing a task.  Therefore, the Committee desires that the Ministry
of ARI should have taken its recommendation in right earnest and avoid replying in a very casual manner.
Stock Inventories 
The Committee observes that the reduction of stock inventories from Rs. 612.30 crore ( khadi – Rs. 551.19 crore and
Polyvastra –Rs. 60.84) as on 31
st March, 2003 to Rs. 611.11 crore ( Khadi – Rs. 549.45 crore and Polyvastra Rs. 61.66
crore) as on 31 st March, 2004 is a very minute achievement in regard to Action Plan for disposing of the stock
inventories in a time bound programme.  The Committee is of the considered view that unless and until stock
inventories is not cleared, the shortage of working capital would continue to be felt on the part of the KVIC.
The Committee expresses its pleasure  over the increasing trend of employment generation in the khadi sector.  During
2002-03 and 2003-04, the employment in the khadi sector has increased slightly to 8.59 lakh persona and 8.61 lakh
persons respectively.  However, for further widening the employment generation avenues in the khadi sector, the KVIC
and the Ministry of ARI should  pay adequate attention to production and sales planning, particularly product quality
and marketability, provide working capital to KVIC institutions, undertake suitable steps for disposing of inventories
stocks and undertake effective steps for  payment of arrear rebates by the State Governments.
Potential of coir industries in non-traditional states like Uttar Pradesh,  Orissa ,Bihar and Jharkhand.
The Committee appreciates the efforts undertaken by the Coir Board in proliferation of Coir Industry in the
major coconut producing States like Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh & Tamilnadu .  The Committee is also
appreciative of the initiative taken by the Coir Board in promoting coir industries in Orissa where a Rubberized Coir
Manufacturing unit at the cost of Rs. 417.00 lakh is being established under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme of Co-
operativisation.  The Committee hopes that the Coir Board would implement its strategy to set up coir units in the non-
traditional states like U.P., Bihar and Jharkhand by sourcing coir fibre from the nearest available place and imparting
training to the local artisans in spinning and weaving products.  The Committee desires that concerted efforts must be
made to establish coir units in the non-traditional areas by sourcing fibre from the nearest coir producing States.
National Programme For Rural Industrialisation
The Committee is of the considered view that by changing the name of the programme / scheme, from National
Programme for Rural Industrialization to Scheme Of Funds For Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) is not
going to provide any remarkable achievement in regard to the stated objectives of the scheme originally conceived.  The

Ministry of ARI should have extracted the benefits of SWOT analysis of NPRI and accordingly the Ministry should
have taken adequate and suitable steps for combating the problems of NPRI i.e. meager financial assistance of Rs 5.0
lakh per cluster for cluster-wide interventions, fill-in-gaps arrangements, non-availability of credit-linked subsidy
component, inadequate synergy with the Ministries/departments with their own schemes in this area etc. with all
seriousness.  The Committee is of the considered view that NPRI should remain in force as it contains the cherished
objectives having potential for employment generation and consolidation of rural economy and growth.  At best,
SFRUTI can be made one of the component of NPRI or it can launched as a separate scheme.  That is so because re-
generation of traditional industries may cover only the confined component of the earlier conceived  National
Programme for Rural Industrialization.
Fund for Modernization of Traditional Industries
            The Committee observes that the Government has been the witness to the withering away of the cherished
objectives and mission of consolidation of rural economy and arresting the rural migration to the urban areas under
National Programme for Rural Industrialization and thereafter, when the Government has drafted a substitute scheme
i.e. SFRUTI, although it does not cover all aspects of NPRI, Government is taking to much time in clearing the
SFRUTI.  Therefore, the Committee desires that SFRUTI may be launched without delay.
Impact of globalisation and liberalization on Agro & Rural Industries
            The Committee feels that the Ministry of ARI should take up the issue of exemption the of KVI products from
state level Value Added Tax (VAT) to the concern authorities advocating that the KVI products are not in a position to
face stiff competition from the organized sector.
Problems being faced by Agro & Rural Industries
The Committee, while appreciating the components of Product Development Design Intervention and Packaging
(PRODIP), recommends that the scheme should be implemented in letter and spirit.  The Committee would like to know
the details about the functions of Rural Industries Service Centre (RISC) and its distribution in various states.  The
Committee would further like to know the details of "transfer of technology and technological intervention from
Technology Information Forecasting and Assessment Council (TIFAC) and outcome thereof.
Revival of the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)
The Committee knows and understands that Revival of the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) falls within
the jurisdiction of Ministry of Rural Development, however, sine the Agro and Rural Industries may be one of the major
beneficiaries of RIDF, therefore, it should be the Ministry of ARI which should come out and make efforts in tandem
with the Ministry of Rural Development chalk out action plan to boost the development of the clusters in economically
backward states like Bihar, M.P., Chhatisgarh, Orissa and Jharkhand.
"New Deal" for rural India
The Committee is of the view that the Government, beside REGP, should come out with comprehensive scheme for
rural employment with the purpose of alleviating rural poverty and integrating rural economy with the national
economy.
