     RECOMMENDATION 
 
The Committee observed that BYNL as a holding company has not been able to assist its subsidiaries to come out of the wood. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that all the six subsidiaries of the BYNL should be merged into a single Company  so that the newly merged Company can pool together all the
resources of the subsidiaries and stand on a sound economic ground and this may help them in bidding for LSTK projects and by having effective control on its
Units act as the main agent for  securing  orders, rationalizing manpower, ensuring VRS grant from the  Government, etc. and eventually emerge as a pioneer to
the other Central /State  PSEs.
 
COMMENTS
 
BYNL was incorporated in 1986 as a wholly owned Company of Government of India. It started functioning w.e.f. 1.4.1987. Six subsidiaries namely, SHPV,
BPCL, B&R, R&C, TSL and TSPL came under its fold in April, 1987. This concept of holding company with subsidiaries under its fold came into existence
based on the recommendations of an expert Committee.
At present, the six subsidiaries under BYNL are operating in different areas of specialisation. Out of the six subsidiaries, three namely; BPCL, R&C and TSL are
sick and under reference to BIFR. Their revival plans sanctioned by BIFR are under review of BIFR and future of these companies will depend on the final
recommendations of BIFR. While three companies namely; BHPV, BPCL and TSPL have been identified for Joint Venture (JV) formation and action to convert
them into JV is already on. Once the exercise is complete, the status of these companies will undergo change. The recommendation to merge all the six
subsidiaries into a single company will bring into focus a set of problems with regard to inter-subsidiary seniority of the employees, different pay scales
applicable in Schedule 'A', 'B, and 'C' PSEs and other terms & conditions applicable to respective companies. It may lead to litigation by employees respective
Unions/Associations.
CHAPTER – III
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS IN RESPECT OF WHICH THE COMMITTEE HAS NOT ACCEPTED REPLIES OF THE
GOVERNMENT
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
           The first major problem, which warrants the attention of this Sub-Committee, is the delay in approval of the restructuring of the subsidiary companies of
BYNL .  It is felt that Department of Industry, Ministry of Heavy Industries  and Public Enterprises seems to have a very indifferent approach towards the
preparation, approval and implementation of the restructuring packages for these subsidiary companies .
 
         The Committee feels that any delay on the whole process involving the restructuring of a Company smacks of lack of commitment  on the part of the
Government . Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry , Ministry of Heavy Industries  and Public Enterprises, should make
conscious  efforts to help these subsidiary companies by approval , timely preparation, and implementation of their restructuring- packages .
 
COMMENTS
 
Out of six subsidiary companies of BYNL group, three companies namely, BPCL, R&C and TSL are sick PSEs under reference to B1FR. The rehabilitation
schemes sanctioned by BIFR in respect of these PSEs are under implementation since 1995. However, these companies have not been able to match the
projections given  in the BIFR scheme despite grant of all reliefs & concessions given by the Government. During the year 1999, Government appointed M/s
CRISIL Advisory Services as Consultant to undertake diagnostic study to suggest ways & means for revival of these companies. Based on the reports of M/s
CRISIL, draft Cabinet Note envisaging package of financial assistance for TSL has been prepared and circulated to all concerned for their comments. After
receiving comments from the various Ministries/Departments, the Note will be placed before the Cabinet for its decision in the matter. As suggested by the
Consultant, a package of assistance in respect of BPCL is also under preparation and will be finalised and circulated to all concerned for comments and further
action will be taken in the matter.
 
The remaining three subsidiaries of the group namely, BHPV, B&R and TSPL  as well as BPCL have been identified by the Government for Joint Venture (JV)
formations. BHPV, BPCL and TSPL's cases have already been referred to Department of Disinvestment (DOD) which has been mandated to handle
disinvestment in Central PSES. Restructuring is to form part of the disinvestment JV  formation.  Restructuring proposal of B&R is under consideration of this
Department as a prelude to JV formation in due course.
 
COMMITTEE’S OBSERVATION
 
            The Committee expects that implementation of revival packages will lead to the revival of B.P.C.L. and T.S.L. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that  their restructuring proposal should be finalized at the earliest. The Committee also suggests that a similar revival package should be initiated by the
Department of Heavy Industry in respect of R&C. The Committee notes that B.H.P.V., B &R,  T.S.P.L. and B.P.C.L. have been  identified by the Govt. for Joint-
Venture formation. The Committee feels that financial restructuring would help these companies in improving their performance  and also enable them to be in a 
position to get commercial borrowings. It will also help in getting attractive prices from the bidders. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the financial
restructuring of these companies may be implemented at the earliest.

RECOMMENDATION
 
          During the interaction with the Officials of the customer PSEs/ concerned Departments of these  PSEs the Sub-Committee was informed that the delivery

The Committee, therefore, recommends the need for timely execution of orders / projects in order to avoid subsequent delay in completion of the projects of the
customer PSEs / Departments as it ,in turn, helps them to avoid cost and time over-runs.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that all PSEs should evolve a
proper workable mechanism to meet the deadline for supply of orders.  The Committee further recommends that in case the delivery is before the deadline, there
should be some provision for rewarding the supplier PSE in some form.

PERMANENT MECHANISM FOR COOPERATION AMONGST THE PSEs
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee observes that there is a lack of coordination amongst the PSEs within and outside the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public
Enterprises.  In the post liberalisatioin era, coordination and cooperation are a must for the survival of PSE , which had earlier flourished under the protective
regime.
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises  should  explore the possibility of a permanent mechanism
for cooperation amongst the PSEs for export orientation, sharing of R&D  experience, forming of joint ventures   etc.  There should be regular meetings of these
PSEs under one umbrella for this purpose.
