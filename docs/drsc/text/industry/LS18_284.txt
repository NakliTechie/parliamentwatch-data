REPORT
The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Ministry of Micro,
Small and Medium Enterprises on the recommendations contained in the Committee's Two Hundred Eightieth
Report on Demands for Grants (2017-18) pertaining to the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises.
2. Action Taken Notes received from the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises in respect of
the recommendations contained in the 280th Report have been categorized as follows:-
Chapter-I The recommendations/observations, which have been accepted by the Ministry.
The Committee notes that the Ministry has accepted its recommendations as mentioned
in the Chapter -1 of the Report.
Chapter-II The recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue
further in view of the Government's replies.
In respect of these recommendations, the Committee is convinced with the logic and
explanations advanced by the Ministry and therefore does not want to pursue them
further for the present.
Chapter-III The recommendations/observations in respect of which the Committee has not
accepted replies of the Ministry and desires the Ministry to review their reply.
The Committee is not convinced with the replies of the Ministry in respect of
recommendations contained in Chapter-Ill and desires that the Ministry in the light of
further recommendations/observations of the Committee furnish explanations thereof.
Chapter-IV The recommendations/observations in respect of which the Committee desire further
comments of the Ministry.
In respect of recommendations placed in Chapter-IV, the Ministry has either furnished the interim
replies, therefore, the Committee desires that the Ministry may furnish Action Taken Notes in respect of
recommendations categorized in Chapter-IV in detail and to the point, as per the intention and spirit of the
recommendations of the Committee.
3. The details of the ATR of the Ministry and recommendations/observations of the Committee thereon
are given in the succeeding pages.

CHAPTER-I
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS, WHICH HA VE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE MINISTRY
Recommendation/Observation
The Committee expresses concern over narrow tax base among informal sector individual enterprises.
On the one hand MSME proprietorship/partnership firms demand their inclusion into proposed tax concession
to small enterprises; on the other hand they do not even file tax returns. This is an unacceptable situation
and the Committee recommends that the Ministry of MSME must take corrective but non-intrusive action in
consultation with Ministry of Finance. The Committee suggests that Ministry must seek PAN and TIN/ GST
details of the proprietors/partners in the UAM and, in association with the Ministry of Finance, link these
references for better tax monitoring through data networking of direct and indirect taxes. (Para 29)
Action Taken
As per the Udyog Aadhaar Memorandum (UAM), Permanent Account Number (PAN) of the organization
alongwith Aadhaar Number of the authorized signatory shall be required for filling UAM in case of a Company
or limited liability partnership or a cooperative society or a society or a trust. However, the suggestion of the
Committee to link the data with M/o Finance for better tax monitoring has been noted.
Recommendation/Observation
The Committee takes note that Scheme-wise cost of implementation has not been indicated despite
Ministry's agreement with Committee's earlier recommendation to this effect. In this regard the Committee
takes note of C AG's earlier Report (1 of 2015) wherein the national auditor has commented on lax monitoring
of utilization of Grants-in -Aids which constitute more than 90% of the Ministry's expenditure. The Ministry
depends on these agencies outside the Ministry's control for outreach and delivery of most of its schemes at
ground level. The Committee may be informed about the institutional, administrative and financial linkage
between the Ministry and the State agencies to implement Ministry's schemes considering that most of States
have parallel schemes that are primarily the responsibility of the State agencies. The Committee therefore
finds it imperative that the Ministry should run only those Schemes that it can monitor effectively on regular
basis through the institutions directly under its control or have an institutional MoU with the State agencies
to implement its Schemes. The Committee recommends that the Ministry must consolidate its Schemes which
is essential for better implementation and monitoring which is essential for efficient implementation of
Schemes. (Para 54)
Action Taken
The observation of the Committee is noted. However, Ministry is continuously making efforts to
streamline and rationalize various Plan schemes and monitors the implementation of these schemes regularly.
Recommendation/Observation
The Ministry may also consider putting the SCSP at the disposal of SC/ST Hub which has elaborate
implementing and monitoring infrastructure under the Chairmanship of the Minister. (Para 58)

The Committee is not sure that reduction in income tax rate as proposed in the Budget will make the

Recommendation/Observation
The Committee having observed the indifferent performance of other components on NMCP over last
5 years of 12th Plan seeks to know Ministry's roadmap for implementing ZED Scheme. Whether the Ministry
has any sector wise database of such technologies and its suppliers. The Committee finds sweeping
generalisation in broad objectives without much detail about availability of technological resources,
institutional architecture and financial resources that will be required for implementation. The Committee
would like to know the parameters fixed for selection of the intended beneficiaries. (Para 33)
Action Taken
The ZED scheme is an extensive drive to create proper awareness in MSMEs about ZED manufacturing
and motivate them for assessment of their enterprise for ZED (Bronze-Silver-Gold-Diamond-Platinum)
(from lower to higher) and support them. After ZED assessment, MSMEs can reduce wastages substantially,
increase productivity, expand their market as IOPs, become vendors to CPSUs, have more IPRs, develop
new products and processes etc. The core activity under the scheme is Assessment / Rating by empanelled
Credit Rating Agencies/ other Agencies. Under this 22222 MSMEs will be given financial assistance for
opting ZED assessment and certification. The assessment process consists of three stages as under:
(i) Online (e-Platform) self assessment
(ii) Desk Top assessment
(iii) Complete assessment
The ZED (Zero Defect Zero Effect) Scheme was launched by the Prime Minister on 18th October,
2016. Under ZED the MSMEs can get themselves rated for Zero Defect Zero Effect. ZED certification will
be in five categories viz., Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond and Platinum. The rated SMEs will get better
business opportunities.

"Financial Support to MSMEs in ZED Certification Scheme" is a voluntary scheme for MSMEs engaged
in manufacturing. The main intent of this scheme is to enhance global competitiveness of Indian MSMEs on
quality and environment related parameters of its systems and processes. ZED is a Maturity Assessment
Model that lays down parameters on various process and outcome based disciplines which assess, rate and
certify an MSME and provide a roadmap for improvement. The scheme provides an opportunity to an
MSME to adopt the ZED model, get itself assessed and avail handholding services. The key resources of
this scheme are the 'assessors' and the 'consultants'. The scheme has provisioned rigorous training of
eligible assessors and consultants who will carry out these activities through accredited rating agencies and
empanelled consulting organisations/consultants.
Objectives of the scheme: The scheme envisages promotion of Zero Defect and Zero Effect (ZED)
manufacturing amongst MSMEs and ZED Assessment for their certification so as to:
• Develop an Ecosystem for Zero Defect Manufacturing in MSMEs.
• Promote adaptation of Quality tools/systems and Energy Efficient manufacturing.
• Enable MSMEs for manufacturing of quality products.
• Encourage MSMEs to constantly upgrade their quality standards in products and processes.
• Drive manufacturing with adoption of Zero Defect production processes and without impacting
the environment.
• Support 'Make in India' campaign.
• Develop professionals in the area of ZED manufacturing and certification.
These objectives are holistic in nature and the scheme provisions institutional & financial support to
activities that support all these objectives. Some of the important activities are:
• Industry Awareness Programmes,
• Training Programmes,
• Development of e-Platform,
• Assessment, Rating & Certification,
• Gap Analysis and Handholding,
• International Benchmarking.
Through above activities the necessary wherewithal is being established to create an ecosystem of
Zero Defect Zero Effect manufacturing. This is an ongoing process. The Quality Council of India (QCI) has
been nominated as the National Monitoring and Implementing Unit for the scheme and financial support to
MSMEs for obtaining ZED certification are provided by the O/o DC(MSME) as per scheme guidelines. As
per the guidelines of the scheme, the only resources required to be created out of the scheme finances are
a pool of qualified Assessors, Consultants, Master Trainers and tools viz., e-platform, e-learning modules
and on line service support, domestically to achieve the objectives of the ZED scheme.

of rating/assessment agencies and training of their resources throughout the country is being carried out to
ensure availability of resources pan India. The assessment process consists of three stages as under:
(i) Online (e-Platform) self assessment: Nil Fee
(ii) Desk Top assessment: Rs 10,000/-per MSME
(iii) Complete assessment: (a) Rs 80,000/- for ZED rating per MSME; (b) Rs 40,000/-for additional
ZED Defence rating; (c) Rs 40,000/- for Re-rating
