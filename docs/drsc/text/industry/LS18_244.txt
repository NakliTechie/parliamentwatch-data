REPORT
Overview of the MSME sector
1. Fourth Census of MSME (2006-07) estimates provide insight into the composition and
structure of MSME sector in the country. Estimated 361.76 lakh units provide employment to
805.24 lakh persons at an average of 2.23 persons per unit with fixed investment of Rs 6.90 lakh
crore. 55 % of units are located in rural areas. 7.36% of the Units are owned by the women. 95%
of the enterprises are proprietary organizations.
2. 67% of the registered MSME enterprises were engaged in manufacturing whereas 33%
were involved in service sector. However, the situation was opposite in case of unregistered units,
of which 70% were engaged in service activities and remaining 30% in manufacturing business.
Among the Registered enterprises about 55% employment was located in urban areas whereas
remaining 45% was located in rural areas. The situation was opposite in case of unregistered
enterprises where rural enterprises provided for about 56% of employment, and remaining 44%
employment was in urban areas.
3. Between the third All India Census of SSI (2001-02) and the Fourth Census (2006-07), in
terms of number of enterprises and employment the sector grew at an impressive annual
compounded growth rate of 28% and 26.42% respectively. Though the scope of the sector was
greatly enhanced with the inclusion of micro, small and medium enterprises.
4. In terms of number of enterprises and employment potential retail trade including repair of
household and consumer goods is the leading industry. Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu
are the three leading states in terms of number of enterprises and employment.
5. Another important indicator of growing prominence of this sector in economy, is
exponential growth in filing of Entrepreneur Memorandum–II after the commencement of a project.
The number of EM II have grown from 1.74 lakh in 2007 after the commencement of the MSMED
Act, 2006 to 2.82 lakh in 2011-12. The most encouraging sign has been exponential growth of
EM-II in Micro sector, from 1.56 lakhs in 2007-08 to 2.42 lakh in 2011-12.
6. The Chairman of the Committee recognized the comment made in the Economic Survey
(2012-13) that “too many firms in India stay small, unregistered, unincorporated, largely informal
or in the unorganized sector because they can avoid regulations and taxes. These firms have little
incentive to invest in upgrading skills of largely temporary workers or investing in capital equipment
that could bring them into tax net, so their productivity stays low.”
7. The Committee also took cognizance of the incentives given to firms to grow, in this years
Budget. The Finance Minister in his Budget Speech announced “To encourage them to grow, I
propose that the benefits or preferences enjoyed by them will stay with them upto three years after
they grow out of the category in which they obtained the benefit. To begin with I propose that
the non-tax benefits may be made available to a MSME unit for three years after it graduates to
a higher category.” In his submission before the Committee, the DC, MSME sought extension of
Tax benefits as well.
8. The Committee recognises that Section 8 of the MSME Development Act, 2006 makes
filing of memorandum optional for micro, small and those medium enterprises engaged in
service sector. However, in view of large number of un-registered units, who are excluded
from the benefits given to registered units, the Committee recommends that registration of
MSME units should be encouraged. The Ministry should make registration simple and

2
nonintrusive.  It should facilitate online registration of unregistered units. The  Ministry
should also take the help of industry associations in this regard. The Committee also
recommends that in addition to non-tax benefits as announced in the Budget, tax benefits
too should be extended to the Units who have graduated from their category.
Allocations and Utilisation
9. In year 2010-11, Rs. 2648.70 crores was the allocated to the Ministry at BE stage which
was raised to Rs. 2678.95 crores at RE stage. However, actual expenditure was Rs. 2552.67
crores, with significant under-utilisation of Planned allocation.
10. Similarly in year 2011-12, total Rs. 3001.29 crores were allocated at BE stage, which was
reduced to Rs. 2759.25 crores at RE stage. The actual expenditure was Rs. 2240.42 crores, which
was about Rs. 500 crores lower than the Revised Estimates. Again, there was significant under-
utilisation of Planned allocation.
11. In 2012-13 too, the Ministry demanded about Rs. 6379 crores as Planned expenditure but
was allocated Rs. 2835 crores in BE, which was reduced at RE stage to Rs. 2542 crores and
provisional expenditure in the year has been only about Rs. 2257 crores, a gap of almost Rs. 4000
crores between demand and actual utilization.
12. It was informed that the Ministry had proposed a plan outlay of Rs.5263.61 crore for
2013-14. However, the Planning Commission has reduced the outlay and allocated Rs.2977.00 crore
only.
13. The Committee notes with reservation that despite its critical observation in 235th
Report on Demands for Grants for the Ministry for 2012-13, regarding non-utilisation of
funds and its recommendation that Ministry must use the money allocated to it for meeting
its targets, the situation in 2012-13 remained the same. Scarce funds still remain un-
utilised. The Committee has always supported Ministry’s demand for more funds as it
considered MSME sector critical for the inclusive and equitable socioeconomic development.
However, it observes a consistent under- utilization funds. The Committee reiterates its
reservation over possible pattern of inflated demands by the Ministry.
Achievement of targets as given in Outcome Budget (2012-13)
14. The Committee also noted that despite massive under-utilisation of funds, the annual
achievements have been exceeding the target. For year 2012-13, in case of Assistance to Training
Institutions upto 31st December 2012, only Rs. 35 crores out of allocated Rs. 71 crores were
spent. However, as per the Annual Report (2012-13) of the Ministry 1,75,315 persons were trained
against annual target of 55000. The Outcome Budget put the figure at 1.41 lakh persons. Under
Rajiv Gandhi Udyami Mitra Yojna, upto 31st December 2012, 8465 new Udyamis were registered
against the target of 3000 with an expenditure of Rs. 1.37 crores out of Budget allocation of
Rs. 3 crores.
15. In case of Village Industries Grant Rs. 20.77 crores out of Budget allocation of Rs. 74.84
crores was spent upto 31st December 2012, which was less than a third of allocation but 70%
of production and Sales targets and 90% of employment targets were already met.
16. Similarly, in case of Khadi grants upto 31st December 2012, only Rs. 64.64 crores was
spent out of Revised allocation of 168.90 crores, however 70% of the production target, 75% of
the sales targets and 90% of employment targets were met with that amount. On the other hand,
in case of PMEGP, Rs. 914.28 crores out of annual allocation of Rs. 1276.28 crores had been
spent by 31.12.2012 but only 35% target in terms of projects assisted and employment

3
opportunities were created. However if compared with performance in PMEGP for previous year
2011-12, 128% of the target in terms of project assisted and 115% of target in terms of
employment opportunities was achieved with Rs. 1057 crores as against Revised allocation of
Rs. 1215.95 crores. The Committee has reason to be circumspect about the figures provided
by the Ministry. There have been year-end rush in spending the allocation. There is
legitimate apprehension about the quality of expenditure. Also, in view of excessive
achievement with lesser actual expenditure, the Committee apprehends if annual targets are
kept at modest levels vis a vis annual budgetary allocations.
17. The Committee took note that the Economic Survey 2012-13 stated that the PM’s Task
Force on MSME would be the basis of XII Plan Policy Framework. It sought to know the status
of implementation of these recommendations of the Task Force during the XII Plan period
considering that the Task Force had even identified nodal Ministries/Departments to implement its
recommendations and had also indicated deadlines.
18. The Ministry informed that the Prime Minister’s Task Force (PMTF) made 85
recommendations under various areas including credit, marketing, labour, rehabilitation and exit
policy, infrastructure/technology/skill development, taxation and special measure for North-Eastern
Region and Jammu & Kashmir. Out of the 85 recommendations action on 39 recommendations
have been completed, recommendations dropped/delinked. It was informed that to monitor
implementation of the recommendations, a Steering Group under the Chairmanship of Principal
Secretary has been constituted.
19. However, the Development Commissioner, MSME, in its oral evidence before the Committee
on the 16th April, 2013, submitted that there were significant slippages in Task Force’s
recommendation in respect of 10% Year on Year hike in terms of micro enterprise accounts and
volume of credit. And that the micro enterprises accounts were actually coming down in case of
certain bigger Public Sector Banks.
20. The Committee may be informed about the eight recommendations that have been
dropped/delinked and rationale for dropping the same. The Committee may also be informed
about the status of implementation of remaining recommendations through the Action
Taken Note. The Ministry must closely work with the Steering Committee in implementing
the recommendations. The Committee takes serious note of the fact that the prominent
Public Sector Banks are not adhering to the recommendations of PM’s Task Force and RBI
guidelines in respect of credit to Micro sector. It recommends the Ministry to take up the
issue with RBI and Steering Committee.

Credit Support Programme
26. The Credit Guarantee Fund Scheme is the prominent scheme under this Scheme. The
Credit Guarantee Scheme operated by Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small
Enterprises (CGTMSE) guarantees collateral free and/or third party guarantee free credit facilities
upto Rs. 100 lakh per MSE borrower sanctioned by its Member Lending Institutions (MLIs). The
Trust has been receiving corpus contribution since inception from its Settlors i.e., GoI and
SIDBI in the ratio of 4:1. The corpus contribution received by CGTMSE and the net income
earned by the Trust have been invested in FDs of Banks/Institutions. In the event, the borrower
account turns into NPA, the MLIs can invoke the guarantee cover after expiry of lock-in–period
of 18 months and compliance of other terms and conditions for settlement of claim. The cost
of guarantee cover is to be paid by MLIs to CGTMSE. However, the amount equivalent to the
guarantee fee and/or the service fee payable may be recovered by the MLI, at its discretion from
the eligible borrower. A composite all-in Guarantee Fee of 1% per annum of sanctioned credit
facility shall be paid upfront to the Trust by the Institutions availing of the guarantee cover for
credit facilities sanctioned by MLIs on or after January 01, 2013 as per the modified provisions
of the scheme. This is lower at 0.75% per annum for credit facility upto Rs. 5 lakh and 0.85%
per annum above Rs. 5 lakh and upto Rs. 100 lakh for women, Micro enterprises and units in
NER including Sikkim.

58. The Committee noted that in 2012-13, the target was to cover 18000 units under the
scheme. The Committee also sought to know the reasons for reducing the allocation to Rs.71.10
crores at RE stage and Rs.65 crores in BE 2013-14, considering that in previous years 2010-11
and 2011-12 the allocation was fully utilized.

Infrastructure Development and Capacity Building
62. Scheme of MSME cluster Development and Growth Poles has been rechristened as
“Infrastructure Development and Capacity Building” as per the advice of Planning Commission.

Difficulty in doing the business
153. There Associations impressed upon the Committee that despite its socioeconomic

terms of the ease of doing business and ranks very low in comparison to other industrial peers.
The MSME sector in particular faces multiple approval and operational restrictions. The process of
setting up and exiting business is time consuming and complicated requiring expensive third party
assistance. Since states have the major role in administering MSME sector, the prevailing ecosystem
therefore varies from state to state.” It further acknowledges that the MSME sector suffers due
to costly finance, “Sourcing of finance at competitive cost is another major constraint for both the
organized and the unorganized MSME enterprises. Financing other than internal accruals is costly
and prohibitive.” The Survey also recognized the technological constraints faced by sector.

number of incubators operates in the country which is very low relative to other countries. New
incubators will need to be set up on a Public-Private Partnership basis.”

Summing up
177. The Committee reiterates the pivotal role MSME Sector will play to bring about
inclusive and equitable development, and in giving opportunity to self employed
entrepreneurs to express their skills and innovation. In the spectre of overall economic slow
down, only MSME could be a redeeming factor. With little support, it shows resilience to
withstand the recession. This sector provides the foundation on which new corporate sector
will be built. There are innumerable inspiring success stories, where small entrepreneurs
have achieved exemplary and inspiring success with their sheer resilience and acumen. As
the spectre of jobless growth and informalisation of job market continues, the Government
has recognized the vitality of this sector. Several Committees and instruments have been
set up to suggest ways to support this Sector. Government has increasingly been proving
budgetary support to schemes. The Committee has always supported the demand of the
Ministry for higher funds. However, the constraints not only about money, it is more about
management, monitoring and delivery of the schemes. It is issue of implementation and
financial discipline, which rests squarely on the Ministry and implementing agencies. The
structure of Schemes is complex and involves multiple funding agencies who more often
lack coordination. Scarce Finds remain unutilised for most part of the year, there is year-
end rush to spend. Balance unspent money is carried forward to subsequent year. Whether
there is a approval of Ministry of Finance, to roll over the unspent balance of a continuing
scheme to next year. Money is deposited in joint Bank accounts and remains un-used. Yet
the Budget estimates for next year are retained at same level. Umbrella allocations are
made in Budget, under which independent schemes are allocated independent meagre funds,
but same is not reflected in the Budget document. These funds remain unutilized supposedly
for lack of demand as indicated in Outcome Budgets. Yet the allocation is retained at same
level. As the Ministry seeks more funds for the MSME sector, the Committee is circumspect
about the argument of lack of demand.  There is a prevalent practice of seeking allocations
for successive years, for Schemes like Special Scheme on PM’s Task Force, Virtual SME
Univerisity etc. which does not have the approval of Planning Commission or other
competent authority. Also, there are recurrent discrepancies in figures provided by the
Ministry vis-a-vis the Budget figures.
