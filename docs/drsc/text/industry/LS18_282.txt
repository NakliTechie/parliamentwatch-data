After independence, grave socio-economic problems such as inequalities in income and 
low levels of employment, regional imbalances in economic development and lack of trained 
manpower, weak industrial base, inadequate investments and infrastructure facilities, etc. were the 
primary concerns for the Government to address. It was in this backdrop that the roadmap for 
Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) was developed as an instrument for self-reliant economic growth. 
Accordingly, India adopted planned economic development polices, which envisaged the 
development of PSUs. 
2. 	 Initially, the public sector was confined to core and strategic industries, subsequently, the 
second phase witnessed nationalization of industries, takeover of sick units from the private sector, 
and entry of the public sector into new fields like manufacturing consumer goods, consultancy, 
contracting and transportation, etc. Thereupon, protectionist Government policies envisaged active 
engagement of the State in development of industries for more than four decades. Thereafter, the 
introduction of the New Industrial Policy of 1991 was a watershed which opened up the economy 
and initiated disinvestment and privatisation. The Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) were 
also opened to competition from the private sector, both domestic and international. The World 
Trade Organisation (WTO) agreements and other Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) that followed . 
contributed to the globalised competition as well. In the present scenario, the Government now 
stresses the-need for CPSEs to .  p.eO-  orirri; to exist and the NITI Aayog has been entrusted to 
evaluate the CPSEs to determine their suitability for revival/restructuring, or closure/strategic 
disinvestment.

5. The Committee notes that during 2015-16, the total number of CPSEs stood at 320, 
which was a significant increase of 22 CPSEs, compared to 2014-15 when the total number 
was 298. At the same time, the number of operating CPSEs rose only by 8 during the said 
period. Further, the Paid-Up Capital, Capital Employed, Investment and Reserves & Surplus 
by the CPSEs rose steadily during the XIIth Plan period. Similarly, the dividend paid and 
the contributions made to the exchequer also saw an increase during the Plan period. The 
indictor that decreased the most in the XIIth Plan period was the foreign exchange earnings 
by the CPSEs. Another factor that is obvious is the decline in the number of people employed. 
As submitted by DPE, due to the decline in the number of permanent employees over the 
years, CPSEs are now hiring employees on contract basis. 
6. The Committee considers the fact that the role of CPSEs in India has undergone a lot 
of metamorphosis since the beginning of planned mixed economy. After playing their part in 
nation building in a protected environment till the advent of the New Economic policy of 
1991, the era of economic liberalisation dawned upon them. Now, the Government has done 
away with planned economy and has set priority areas wherein it would continue to be 
engaged in business through CPSEs. In the remaining spheres, only CPSEs who keep 
themselves afloat by being profitable are encouraged while closure or strategic 

disinvestment, as decided by the NITI Aayog, is being used by the Government to exit loss-
incurring CPSEs in non-priority areas. This approach is expected to free up capital for 
investment in the social and infrastructure sectors. In this regard, the Committee observes 
that one of the causes for which CPSEs were envisaged, i.e. for equitable economic and social 
development - is still relevant to our country and has to be kept in mind along with financial 
and business prudence while deciding the closure or strategic disinvestment of CPSEs. The 
Committee is of the considered opinion that employment generation is one factor that CPSEs 
can facilitate for some more time especially, in areas suffering from regional imbalances, 
besides the North Eastern Region. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that CPSEs 
should be encouraged to provide permanent employment to people and may be desisted from 
hiring personnel on contract basis. 
Department of Public Enterprises (DPE). 
7. In their 52 nd  Report, the Estimates Committee of 3 rd  Lok Sabha (1962-67) stressed the need 
for setting up a centralized coordinating unit, which could also make continuous appraisal of the 
performance of public enterprises. This led to the setting up of the Bureau of Public Enterprises 
(BPE) in 1965 in the Ministry of Finance. Subsequently, as a result of the reorganization of the 
Ministries/Departments of the Union Government in September, 1985, BPE was made part of the 
Ministry of Industry. In May, 1990, BPE was made a full-fledged Department known as the DPE. 
Presently, it is part of the Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises (Mo HI &PE). 
8. The DPE is the nodal department for all the CPSEs and formulates policy pertaining to 
them. It lays down, in particular, policy guidelines on performance improvement and evaluation, 
autonomy and financial delegation and personnel management in CPSEs. It further, collects and 
maintains information in the form of a Public Enterprises Survey on several areas in respect of 
CPSEs. 
9. In fulfilling its role, the Department coordinates with other Ministries, CPSEs and 
concerned organizations. As per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government, the 
following subjects have been allocated to the DPE: 
• Residual work . relating to erstwhile Bureau of Public Enterprises including 
Industrial Management Pool. 
• Coordination of matters of general policy affecting all Public-Sector Enterprises.

• Matters relating to Permanent Machinery of Arbitration for the Public-Sector

Undertakings under Voluntary Retirement Scheme.

10. The Committee notes that during the oral evidence, while making a presentation to 
the Committee, the Department submitted as part of the `CPSE highlights -2015-16' that the 
total number of CPSEs in the country to be 320, out of which 244 are operating and the 
remaining 76 are under construction. In the ensuing discussion, attention was drawn to the 
General Performance of CPSEs and the areas wherein CPSEs have excelled and the areas 
where they have failed. It was pointed out that the sectors where the top ten profit making 
CPSEs of the country operating currently were in the spheres where CPSEs can deliver and 
where they should venture more into by the Government. Likewise; when the top ten loss-
incurring CPSEs were analysed except BHEL, it was not difficult to find that they are 
operating in areas where technological changes are frequent and the market fluctuation were 
rampant due to varying factors. 
11. In view of the above, the Committee while observing the data relating to the profit 
making and loss incurring CPSEs, finds that there are certain captive sectors in the industry 
which are conducive for the Government to set up CPSEs in a profitable manner. In the 
opinion of the Committee, general parameters of such areas might iriclude stable technology, 
steady market, capital intensive nature and judicious utilization of public assets, etc. The 
Government can keep these broad principles in mind while identifying and setting up of 
A -r 

CPSEs in the future in strategic and priority areas. Besides this, the principles of social 
responsibilities of the Government should also be intertwined with the business 
considerations. 
12. Accordingly, as a preparatory step in this direction, the ComMittee recommends that 
DPE being the body in charge of the General Policy framework for CPSEs, should conduct a 
well-organized comprehensive research study to identify the areas of business wherein 
CPSEs can successfully/profitably operate and grow. A policy of public investment in CPSEs 
can be based on the findings of such a scientific study. This, in the opinion of the Committee 
should help to ensure that the 76 CPSEs that are being newly set up by the Government 
operate in a profitable environment and are not doomed to be shut due to extraneous factors 
like in case of their predecessors. 
13. As part of the examination of Demand for Grants (DFG) of DPE, the Department placed 
before the Committee the steps taken by them to facilitate the functioning of CPSEs during the last 
few years. According to DPE, while the day-to-day management of the CPSE's is being handled 
by their Boards and the respective administrative Ministries, DPE, inter-alia, coordinates matter of 
general policy, performance evaluation, corporate governance and capacity building issues of 
CPSEs and advises on the revival, restructuring and closure of CPSEs. The Department also shared 
with the Committee some of the important guidelines issued by DPE for CPSEs during the last 
three years which are placed below: 
a. Guidelines-  for 'Streamlining the mechanism for revival and restructuring of sick/ incipient., 
sick and weak CPSEs'. These guidelines would help administrative Ministries in monitoring 
those CPSEs which are moving toward sickness so that corrective measures can be taken in 
time. As per these guidelines, administrative Ministries are supposed to closely monitor the 
weak CPSEs so that they do not become sick/ incipient sick. 
b. Guidelines on 'Time bound closure of sick/ loss-making CPSEs and disposal Of their 
movable and immovable assets'. These guidelines would help in unlocking the amount 
invested in land and building as clear modalities for disposal of assets have been prescribed 
for CPSEs under closure. 
c.. Observance of transparency and due diligence in selection and implementation of activities 
under Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) by CPSEs. 
d. Guidelines on improvements in the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) process are 
being issued from time to time. Last guidelines on this subject were issued on 16.12.2016.

15. The Committee had a detailed deliberation on the Guidelines regarding the utilization 
of CSR funds by CPSEs. In this context, it was brought before the Committee that there 
were certain activities like the construction of toilets which were undertaken as part of the 
Swatch Bharat activities, but funds/efforts were not spared for their upkeep or maintenance. 
The Committee notes that in many areas where such constructions where taking place were 
remote in nature and lack of upkeep rendered them useless and most unhygienic within a 
short span of time. 
16. The Committee takes note that DPE's latest advisory on CSR activities mentions only 
transparency, selection and implementation of activities under CSR by CPSEs. However, 
based on the facts brought before the Committee, it finds that there are activities under CSR 
that become redundant in the absence of provisions for their maintenance by utilizing CSR 
funds. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that DPE guidelines/advisories on CSR 
should contain provisions/instructions to ensure the maintenance of constructed toilets and 
their related -lialifies under Swachh Bharat activities. This will also be in synchronisation 
with the similar provisions as contained in Swatch Bharat Kosh guidelines.

25. According to the Department, one of the reasons for under-utilisation of outlay of CRR 
Scheme was gradual decline in the number of VRS/VSS employees during the XII Plan period. In 
tune with this, from 2013-14 onwards target under the scheme was reduced to 2500-3000 as the 
VRS number during the corresponding period remained 1500-2000 per year. Though number of 
employees released may vary year to year in future, it is a fact that separation under VRS/VSS in 
CPSEs is likely to be a continuous process and closure of sick/unviable CPSEs could result in 
enhancement in number of VRS/VSS employees. 
26. Based on this, a query was raised about the Department's plan to address the need for 
augmentation of CRR scheme for the future. DPE replied in this connection that for the 
implementation of CRR scheme, a Moll  has  been signed on 28.09.2016 with National Skill 
Development Council (NSDC) under the aegis of Ministry of Skill Development Entrepreneurship 
(MSDE) to broaden the network of training providers to cover maximum possible number of 
separated employees and to impart them a standard and recognised training. NSDC, as facilitator, 
will ensure all training programmes identified as per National Council for Vocational Training 
(NCVT) certification and standardised course curriculum in line with NSDC's Qualification Pack 
(QP)/National Occupational Standard (NOS), standards set forth by the Sector Skill Councils 
(SSC) resulting in certification as per certification standards. National Occupational Standard • 
(NOS) specify the standard of performance, knowledge and understanding when carrying out a 
particular activity in the work place, while Qualification Pack (QP) is a set of NOSs, aligned' to a 
job role available in each industry sector. This would help in greater coverage and augmenting 
redeployability under the CRR Scheme and would ensure quality and market oriented skill 
development and facilitate proper matching of individual skills and aptitude of the typical target 
group.

MoU System for CPSEs 
42. The MoU is a mutually negotiated agreement between the Government of India/ Holding 
Company and the management of the CPSEs. Under this agreement,'the CPSEs undertake to 
. 	••• achieve the targets set in the agreement at the beginning of the year and submit themselves to • 
evaluation on the basis of its achievements at the end of the year. The Government of India 
19 

introduced the MoU system in the year 1986, based on the recommendations given by Arjun Sen 
Gupta Committee Report (1984). The report recommended that the CPSEs should enter agreement 
with their administrative Ministries for five years, with progress to be reviewed annually. The 
MoU system was given broader thrust by the Governments after the announcement of the New 
Industrial Policy of 1991. 
43. The institutional arrangements for the implementation of the MoU policy includes an apex 
High Powered Committee (HPC) headed by the Cabinet Secretary which gives guidance and 
directions with respect to the determination of the principles and parameters for evaluating the 
performance of CPSEs. Till now, there was a Task Force of experts to assist the HPC in their job. 
From the year 2016-17 an Inter-Ministerial Committee (IMC) has been put in place headed by the 
Secretary, DPE to approve the MoU targets and recommend its evaluated score and rating to the 
HPC. A Pre-Negotiation Committee (PNC), earlier known as the Standing Committee on MoU, is 
also there to assist the IMC in determining the most appropriate and relevant parameters for 
measuring improvement in performance and for fixing targets. 
44. The Committee asked the Department to provide an overview of the evolution of the MoU 
system for CPSEs over the years in terms of the method of implementation, evaluation parameters, 
consultative process to draw up the guidelines, etc. The Department was also sought to furnish the 
details of the modifications made in MoU guidelines during the. last three years and the current 
year, i.e., 2016-17 in response to the changes in the regulatory/ legal framework impacting CPSEs 
like, the Procurement Policy & Vendor Development Programmes and CSR. Responding to this, 
DPE submitted the following:- 
I. 	 Overview of the evaluation of the MoU System for CPSEs over the years: 
1 Method of implementation: 
Signing of MoU has been made compulsory as per the decision of the High-Powered 
Committee in 2007. Rating of MoU is used for rating Board level officials of the CPSEs, 
payment of PRP, giving Maharatna status, etc. This has resulted in increase in number of 
MoU signing companies from 144 in 2007-8 to 234 in 2015-16. 
2. Evaluative parameters: 
Evaluation of MoU of the CPSE is done after the end ofthe year and finalisation of their 
accounts. The evaluation is done based on the parameters fixed at the time of target 
setting: Evaluation of MoU, i.e., achievement of targets is .first approved by Board of 
Directors of the :CPSE concerned. Mali evaluation issent to DPE through.their .  
20 

administrative Ministry. The parameters for the targets are decided based on MoU 
guidelines for the respective years. 
3. Consultative process to draw up the Guidelines: 
For formulating the Guidelines, DPE consults administrative Ministries/ Departments and • 
CPSEs by holding workshops at various places. 
MoU 2016-17 Guidelines were finalised after consulting CPSEs in 4 workshops. These 
Guidelines were discussed in High Powered Committee (HPC) on MoU having 
Secretaries of various Ministries and Members. On the directions of HPC, these 
Guidelines were again discussed with administrative Ministries and Conveners of Task 
Forces on MoU. Thereafter, the Guidelines were again discussed and finalised in a 
meeting of High Powered Committee. 
MoU 2017-18 Guidelines were finalised after consulting CPSEs in 3 workshops, out of 
which one workshop was exclusively for senior officers of administrative Ministries/ 
Departments and Board level officers of Navratna and Maharatna CPSEs. The Guidelines 
were issued after the approval of Cabinet Secretary as Chairman of High Powered 
Committee on MoU. 
II. 	 Details of the changes made in MoU Guidelines during the last three years and the current 
year, i.e., 2016-17 in response to the changes in regulatory/ legal framework: 
1. Procurement and vendor development programme: 
Public Procurement Policy issued by Ministry of MSME was made compulsory 
parameter from the year 2014-15 onwards. Failure to comply with the Public Procurement 
Policy was given negative mark of 1 for the years 2014-15, 2015-16, 2017-18 and 
onwards. For the year, 2016-17, non-compliance of various conditions including Public 
Procurement Policy issued by Ministry of MSME would result in downgrading- of 
Excellent rated CPSEs to Very Good and 5 marks are to be deducted in respect of other 
CPSEs. 
During the year 2015-16, 131 CPSEs were given negative mark for non-compliance of 
Public Procurement Policy issued by Ministry of MSME. 
2. CSR: 
CSR has been a parameter from the year 2013-14 onwards. For the year 2015-16, non-
compliance of CSR parameter was given 1 negative mark. For the current year 2016-17, 
non-compliance of provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 which also includes 
compliance of CSR would result in downgrading of Excellent rated CPSEs to Very Good 
and 5 marks are to be deducted in respect of other CPSEs. 
During the year 2015-16, 39 CPSEs were given negative mark for non-compliance of 
CSR Guidelines. 
3: Other legal provisions:

- 21 

For the current year 2016-17, compliance of provisions of the Companies Act, 2013 or 
the relevant Act under which they had been regulated and compliance of provisions of 
Listing Agreement (in case of listed CPSEs) to the extent compliances are within the 
ambit of CPSEs, compliance of DPE Guidelines having financial implications, no adverse 
observation by the CAG on the annual accounts of the CPSE (beyond certain limit), 
holding of AGM without seeking extension of time, submission of MoU/ Evaluation by 
due date have been made compulsory parameters. Failure to comply would result in 
downgrading of Excellent rated CPSEs to Very Good and 5 marks are to be deducted in 
respect of other CPSEs. 
45. 	 When asked about the number of CPSEs that signed MoUs during the year 2016-17, their

for CPSEs in the country during the last one year, DPE submitted that 206 CPSEs have signed 
MoU for the year 2016-17 till date against a target of 234. Performance evaluation of the CPSEs 
that have signed MoU for the year 2016-17 is due for submission on 30 th  September, 2017. The 
modifications/ improvements made in the MoU Guidelines mechanism during last one year (2016-
17) were as follows: 
1. Result oriented parameters have been proposed. 
2. Parameter included for monitoring of CAPEX contracts/ projects running/ completed 
during the year without time/ cost overrun. 
3. CAPEX parameter weightage has been increased. 
4. Procurement policy issued by Ministry of MSME has been given importance by prescribing

46. 	 MoU is seen by the Committee as an instrument to assess' the financial and physical 
perf  mance and social obligations of CPSEs in the country. Evaluation of CPSEs through 
the MoU system is one of the core responsibilities of DPE. During the last year and the 
current year, the Committee finds that the targeted number of MoUs to be signed could not 
be met. Further, regarding the mechanism of MoU, the Committee takes into account that 
during 2016-17 there have been some notable changes in the system. Introduction of result 
oriented parameters and increased weightage for CAPEX are some of them. The parameters

Revival/Restructuring/Closure of CPSEs  
47. 	 The Government of India has recently taken many steps to make the mechanism and 
process for revival/ restructuring of CPSEs time bound, comprehensive, performance driven and 
efficient. The Government has decided to remove the multiple layers in the decision-making 
process to ensure timely revival/ restructuring of CPSEs. It has been decided that revival/ 
restructuring of sick /incipient sick CPSEs is to be merit based considering strategic, national and 
business concerns of the CPSEs. Accordingly, the Government has wound up BRPSE and 
administrative Ministries/Departments concerned are made responsible to monitor sickness of 
CPSEs functioning under them and to take timely redressal measures with the approval of the 
competent authority. On these lines DPE issued guidelines on 29 th  October, 2015 for 'Streamlining 
the mechanism for revival and restructuring of sick/ incipient sick and weak CPSEs: General 
principles and mechanism of restructuring' to be followed by the administrative Ministries/ 
Departments. Further, DPE issued another guideline on 7 th  September, 2016 for 'Time bound 
closure of sick/ loss-making CPSEs and disposal of their movable and immovable assets'. These 
guidelines are intended to expeditiously complete the various process and procedure related to the 
closure of CPSEs. 
48. 	 On the procedure for deciding restructuring / closure / strategic sale of a PSU and the role 
of NITI Aayog in this direction, DPE submitted that Chapter XIX of the Companies Act, 2013 
refers to Reviyal—arrd—Rehabilitation of Sick Companies and Chapter XX to Winding up of the 
Companies. The decision whether a company has become a sick company would be taken by the 
National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). The Act is administered by Ministry of Corporate 
Affairs ( MoCA). Further, a Committee has been constituted under the Chairmanship of Vice-Chairman, NITI 
Aayog to review the status of all loss making_ CPSEs. Secretaries of DPE, Department of Investment and 
Public Asset Management (DIPAM) and Department of Heavy Industry (DHI), CEO, NITI Aayog and Joint 
Secretary (Expenditure), Ministry of Finance are members of the Committee. The recommendatiohs of NITI 
Aayog with regard to revival, restructuring or closure of CPSE are processed by the administrative 	 . 
Ministry/Department concerned for obtaining the approval of the Cabinet/ Cabinet Committee on 
Economic Affairs (CCEA). The final decision of whether to accept these recommendations in part, . 
fully or with modifications, rests with the Cabinet/CCEA.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE-AT A GLANCE 
1. The Committee notes that during 2015-16, the total number of CPSEs stood at 320, 
which was a significant increase of 22 CPSEs, compared to 2014-15 when the total number 
was 298. At the same time, the number of operating CPSEs rose only by 8 during the said 
period. Further, the Paid-Up Capital, Capital Employed, Investment and Reserves & Surplus 
by the CPSEs rose steadily during the XIIth Plan period. Similarly, the dividend paid and 
the contributions made to the exchequer also saw an increase during the Plan period. The 
indictor that decreased the most in the XIIth Plan period was the foreign exchange earnings 
by the CPSEs. Another factor that is obvious is the decline in the number of people employed. 
As submitted by DPE, due to the decline in the number of permanent employees over the 
years, CPSEs are now hiring employees on contract basis. 
(Para No. 5) 
2. The Committee considers the fact that the role of CPSEs in India has undergone a lot 
of metamorphosis since the beginning of planned mixed economy. After playing their part in 
nation building in a protected environment till the advent of the New Economic policy of 
1991, the era of economic liberalisation dawned upon them. Now, the Government has done 
away with planned economy and has set priority areas wherein it would continue to be 
engaged in business through CPSEs. In the remaining spheres, only CPSEs who keep 
themselves afloat by being profitable—are encouraged while closure or strategic 
disinvestment, as decided by the NITI Aayog, is being used by the Government to exit loss-
incurring CPSEs in non-priority areas. This approach is expected to free up capital for 
investment in the social and infrastructure sectors. In this regard, the Committee observes 
that one of the causes for which CPSEs were envisaged, i.e. for equitable economic and social 
development - is still relevant to our country and has to be kept in• mind along with financial 
and business prudence while deciding the closure or strategic disinvestment of CPSEs. The 
Committee is of the considered opinion that employment generation is one factor that CPSEs 
can facilitate for some more time especially, in areas suffering from regional imbalances, 
besides the North Eastern Region. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that CPSEs 
should be encouraged to provide permanent employment to people .  and may be desisted from 
hiring personnel on contract basis. 
(Para No. 6) 

3. The Committee notes that during the oral evidence, while making a presentation to 
the Committee, the Department submitted as part of the `CPSE highlights 2015-16' that the 
total number of CPSEs in the country to be 320, out of which 244 are operating and the 
remaining 76 are under construction. In the ensuing discussion, attention was drawn to the 
General Performance of CPSEs and the areas wherein CPSEs have excelled and the areas 
where they have failed. It was pointed out that the sectors where the top ten profit making 
CPSEs of the country operating currently were in the spheres where CPSEs can deliver and 
where they should venture more into by the Government. Likewise, when the top ten loss-
incurring CPSEs were analysed except BHEL, it was not difficult to find that they are 
operating in areas where technological changes are frequent and the market fluctuation were 
rampant due to varying factors. 
(Para No. 10) 
4. In view of the above, the Committee while observing the data relating to the profit 
making and loss incurring CPSEs, finds that there are certain captive sectors in the industry 
which are conducive for the Government to set up CPSEs in a profitable manner. In the 
opinion of the Committee, general parameters of such areas might include stable technology, 
steady market, capital intensive nature and judicious utilization of public assets, etc. The 
Government can keep these broad principles in mind- while identifying and setting up of 
CPSEs in the future in strategic and priority areas. Besides this, the principles of social 
responsibilities of the Government should glso be intertwined with the business 
considerations. 
(Para No.11) 
5. Accordingly, as a preparatory step in this direction, the Committee recommends that 
DPE being the body in charge of the General Policy framework for CPSEs, should conduct a 
well-organized comprehensive research study to identify the areas of business wherein 
CPSEs can successfully/profitably operate and grow. A policy of public investment in CPSEs 
can be based on the findings of such a scientific study. This, in the opinion of the Committee 
should help to ensure that the 76 CPSEs that are being newly set up by the Government 
operate in a profitable environment and are not doomed to be shut due to extraneous factors 
like in case of their predecessors. 
(Para No. 12)
