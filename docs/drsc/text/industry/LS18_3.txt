THIRD REPORT OF THE COMMITTEE 
I, the Chairman, Department- Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry, 
having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this 
Third Report of the Committee on the Demands for Grants for 1993-94 of the Ministry of 
Mines. 
2. In pursuance of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Rajya Sabha 
pertaining to Department-Related Standing Committees, Hon'ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha 
and Hon'ble Speaker , Lok Sabha nominated 42 members of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha to 
the Committee on Industry for the functions assigned to it under the Rules. Further 
Chairman, Rajya Sabha and Speaker, Lok Sabha decided that all provisions of Rule 270 of 
the Rules relating to the functions of the Committee shall become operative with effect from 
30th March, 1993. 
3. The Committee on Industry deals with the subjects falling in the jurisdiction of the 
Ministry of Industry, Ministry of Steel and Ministry of Mines. 
4. The Committee accordingly held its first meeting on the 22nd April, 1993 and 
considered relevant documents and papers received from the Ministry of Mines on the 
Demands for Grants for the year 1993-94. 
5. The Committee also heard the oral evidence of the Secretary of the Ministry of Mines 
on the performance budget in the context of Demands for Grants of that Ministry. 
6. The Committee considered the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in three sittings and 
considered and adopted the Report at its sitting held on 4th May, 1993 (Annexure consisting 
of the minutes of the First, Fourth and Fifth sittings of the Committee). 
7. Demands for Grants as presented for the Ministry as under:-

8. The Committee had no time to consider in depth and arialyse the Demands for Grants 
of the Ministry and the other documents. The Committee is conscious of the fact that the 
Demands for Grants of various Ministries and Departments are to be put for vote of the 
Lok Sabha on the 4th May, 1993. We, therefore, offer general comments. We propose to 
consider the detailed expenditure at a later stage and will present our views in subsequent 
reports so as to enable the Government to recaste their expenditure priorities in the revised 
estimates.

Committee concludes as under:-

accepted the offer of the Kaiser Aluminium of USA on the basis of the delegated powers 
enjoyed by the company in this type of investments with the approval of the Ministry. The 
Committee would observe that with the Budgetary support of Rs. 4 crores in the Budget 
Estimates for 1993-94 the modernisation of Korba Plant Smelter is undertaken. The 
Financial delegation of powers to be enjoyed by the Board of Directors of the Company in 
awarding such contracts should therefore be precisely laid down. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that it should be clearly defined and indicated in appropriate chapter in the 
Performance Budget for correct appraisal of financial relations between the Ministry and the 
Undertakings functioning under its administrative control. The Committee do not intend to 
go into the merits and demerits and award of the content at this point of time. The 
Budgetary figures and the memorandum relating thereto should be self-explanatory in this 
behalf.

During the 7th Five Year Plan, an allocation of Rs. 85.03 crores was made for the 
promotional fund for exploration and Rs. 35.93 crores was made for capital outlay. The 
Mineral Exploration was therefore , able to undertake drilling of 18.39 lacs mtrs. and 54.600 
mtrs. of mining resulting in a reserve of 17,750.73 million tonnes of different minerals in 
roughly 140 projects. The Committee finds that against this background, an amount of 
Rs. 13 crores for the promotional work and one crore for capital outlay for the current 
financial year has been made. Contractual drilling and promotional activities are basic 
functions of the Corporation. The Committee feels that the reduced allocation for this item 
in the Budget estimates is likely to decrease the net profit and will hamper its growth. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry should re-examine the allocation and 
submit the revised re-appropriation for the consideration of the Lok Sabha. 
9.4 Zinc: Another area in production of non-ferrous metals falling under the jurisdiction of 
the Ministry is the Production of Zinc. The Committee has noticed that in the matter of 
purchasing metallurgical grade of Coke required for use in the smelter· of Hindustan Zinc 
Limited at Chanderiya in Chittorgarh District of Rajasthan the HZL ha~ not taken any note · 
of the indigenous metallurgical coke available with indigenous companies with the requisite 
grade and quality of Coke. It has come to the notice of the Committee that ten thousand 
tonnes of Coke of the required grade and quality was purchased by HZL after verifying the 
technical suitability of the Coke. The quality purchased from indigenous resources was 
however not even given a trial in the actual production in the smelter and instead the 
foreign-based companies in Singapore and Japan were awarded the contract for supplying 
the coke afresh. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the entire transaction needed 
to be thoroughly probed to identify and fix responsibility for purchasing coke from abroad. 

9.5 Bharat Gold Mines Ltd.: The Committee notes that a sum of Rs. 20 crores have been 
earmarked for restructuring/diversifying the activities of Bharat Gold Mines Ltd. (BGML) 
and out of Rs. 20 crores Rs. 8.5 crores have been earmarked for the year 1992-93. The 
Committee also had an overall view of the financial position of BGML. The Committee is 
given to understand that the Company is under heavy losses and has also beeh referred to 
BIFR. The Committee has been informed that some of the units of BGML have acquired 
optimum expertise in their respective fields of operation . The Committee proposes to study 
the subject in depth taking into account various factors and will submit its report -at -a later 
stage. However, the Committee like to observe that efforts should be made to experiment 
shallow mining and reduce establishment expenditure on the maintenance of township of the 
factory. Proceedings before BIFR may be kept in suspension or deferred till the Committee 
has studied the problem in greater detail. Based on these observations, the Committee 
recommends required Budgetary support for shallow mining of BGML on experimental 
basis. 
9.6 The Committee finds that Geological Survey of India is a premier organisation under 
the Ministry. The Committee finds that only Rs. 42 crores out of a total of Rs. 112.10 crores 
have been .earmarked as a plan expenditure for the GSI while the non-plan expenditure is 
Rs. 70.10 crores. The Committee, therefore, recommends that more funds for plan 
expenditure for different activities of the GSI like survey, mapping, mineral exploration, etc . 
may be provided. 
9.7 In regard to Mining, the Committee regrets to note that large scale closure of mines in 
.different States and Union Territories has become a normal feature. The Committee , 
therefore, recommends that every step should be taken by Government to ensure that this 
trend is immediately reversed and effective measures are taken to halt the deterioration in 
the operation of mines and for revitalising and rejuvenating the mines for better mineral use 
for our economic development. The Committee do not find any specific monetary allocation 
in the Budget for the purpose. 
9.8 The Cc.mmittee trusts that its observations and recommendations will receive serious 
consideration for implementation.
