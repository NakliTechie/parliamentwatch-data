REPORT
1.1     The Ministry of Small Scale Industries is the nodal Ministry for policy formulation, promotion, development and
protection of Small Scale Industries. It also monitors the implementation of these policies.
1.2     Ministry of SSI designs and implements the policies through its field organizations for promotion and growth of
small enterprises. The Ministry also coordinates with other Ministries/Departments on behalf of the Small Scale
Industries (SSI) sector.
1.3     Implementation of policies and various programmes/schemes for providing infrastructure and support services to
small enterprises is undertaken through its attached office, namely the Small Industries Development Organization
(SIDO).
1.4     The Indian economy has experienced profound changes and sustained growth in the last five decades. Industry
sector, by building core strength of the economy, has acted as an engine of growth. The Small Scale Industries (SSI)
sector has also contributed significantly in process of growth and has emerged as a vibrant partner. The importance of
the sector can be well assessed in terms of its contribution to the socio-economic objectives of growth in generation of
employment, output, exports.
1.5     The SSI sector, producing a wide range of products, accounts for around 95 per cent of the industrial units in the
country, contributing to 40 per cent of the manufacturing sector output and approximately one-third of the Nation's
exports. As at end-March, 2002, there were 34.42 lakh SSI units providing direct employment to around 192.33 lakh
persons. As per the estimates of the office of the Development Commissioner (SSI), the output of SSI sector at current
prices aggregated Rs. 6,90,316 crores during the year 2001-02 and exports at the level of Rs. 69.797 crores during the
2000-01. During the financial year 2002-03, the production of SSI sector at current prices is projected to reach at Rs. 7,
42,021 crores. The sector also acts as a nursery for promoting entrepreneurial talents and as a catalyst in the industrial
growth.
1.6     There has been a structural relationship between the SSI sector's growth and the growth of industry and the
overall economy. The comparative annual growth rates of the overall industry, manufacturing and SSI sectors show that
the small scale industry sector has been generally recording a rate of growth higher than that overall industry and
manufacturing sectors growth rates. During the financial year 2001-02, the SSI sector achieved a growth of 6.08 per
cent in comparison with a 3.3 per cent growth recorded by the overall industry sector and 3.4 per cent by the
manufacturing sector.

1.7     The SSI sector has been receiving due attention from the Government as well as other extension agencies through
various policies by way of financial and fiscal incentives, infrastructural facilities, technology, marketing and other
similar support measures. For credit dispensation, SSI sector has been accorded status of "priority sector" by banks.
However, with the onset of economic reforms and globalisation, a number of challenges and opportunities have
emerged for the sector. While, on the one hand, the process of liberalization has expanded the scope of cross-border
transactions, on the other, the new order has led to different perceptions about the protection and safeguards that the SSI
sector has hitherto enjoyed. The changed environment has necessitated technology upgradation, quality standardization
and strengthening of marketing facilities for various products, more particularly, the SSI products, to tap the expanded
horizons.
1.8     The process of liberalization and market reforms has created wide-ranging opportunities for the development of
small-scale industries. At the same time, changing world scenario has thrown up new challenges to the very existence of
the sector. Moreover, this sector has also been facing some problems related to credit, infrastructure, technology,
marketing, delayed payments, problems on account of complex rules and regulation etc. The need of the hour is to
suitably strengthen the sector so that it can adapt itself to the changed environment and face challenges boldly and
effectively. In order to enable this sector to avail the opportunities and play its role as an engine of growth, the Hon'ble
Prime Minister on 30th August, 2000 announced a Comprehensive Policy Package for the SSI Sector.
1.9     The small enterprises have emerged as a focus area for forging and promoting trade. Economic reform
programmes envisage the emergence of Indian Industry as a global competitor with substantial presence in the
international markets. Technology upgradation and modernization of SSI sector and promotion of export from this
sector are interlinked issues, which are important objectives behind promoting international cooperation. Nine countries
viz.; USA, Germany, Italy, South Korea, U.K., Taiwan, Japan, Singapore and Israel have been identified as the thrust
countries for promoting enterprise-to-enterprise cooperation to upgrade the capabilities of Indian SSI sector to make it
competitive in the global markets. This has assumed greater significance in the context of the liberalization process. In
fact, the potential of SSI sector for contributing to exports and creating employment remains untapped in the absence of
access to technology and other factors.

3.5     The Committee observes that in the era of globalisation and liberalisation of the economy, the Small Scale
Industries should be strengthened to compete in the international markets. Policy measures should be formulated and
relevant data should be obtained through surveys, investigations and studies. The Committee recommends that the role
of Development Commissioner for SSI should be oriented in accordance with the ever-changing scenario so as to make
the SSI sector internationally competitive.
3.6     The Committee further observes that it has become extremely essential to create awareness about India's
obligations under the WTO regime and their impact on various sectors. Having heard the representatives of Powerloom,

gap between the SIDO and SSI Associations. Therefore the Committee recommends that there should be regular
interaction between the Government and Small Scale Industries. A Committee at local level should also be formed
between Bankers and local chapters of SSI Associations to sort out their problems relating to credit related issues at
local level in all the States. The local committee should give quarterly report to the Ministry of Small Scale Industries,
Ministry of Finance (Banking Division) and Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry.

VI.     CREDIT TO SSI SECTOR
6.1     The Committee is of the opinion that one of the major problems of SSIs is that of finance, whether it is equity,
term loan or working capital. Financial institutions and commercial banks are often reluctant to lend to small scale
enterprises because of the high risks involved. Moreover, the commercial banks and financial institutions that provide
working capital and term finance to the corporate borrower generally demand personal guarantees from borrowers,
besides the collateral security. This adversely affects the borrowing capacity of the small entrepreneur. The lack of
coordination between commercial banks and financial institutions causes delay in availability of working capital and
hence in the commissioning of projects. The complex and time-consuming procedures and formalities of the
institutional lenders cause operational impediments for SSI units.
6.2     The Ministry apprised the Committee that the total advances to SSI from the Public Sector Banks has gone up in
absolute terms from Rs. 48,445 crores in March, 2001 to Rs. 49,743 crores in March, 2002. The percentage of SSI
advances to Net Bank Credit (NBC) however, has declined from 14.2% as at the end of March, 2001 to 12.5% as at the
end of March, 2002. The RBI's study of the specialized SSI/general branches has revealed that adequate credit is being
made available to all viable and bankable projects.

facilitate additional capacity generation, expansion of the existing units and improving their capacity utilization,
diversification and to enhance the competitiveness of the sector with a thrust on technology upgradation and
modernization.

XI.     VALUE ADDED TAX  (VAT)
11.1   Value Added Tax (VAT) has emerged as one of the most important fiscal innovation of the present century with
more than 115 countries opting it. Since the setting up of "Indirect Taxation Enquiry Committee" in 1976 under
Chairmanship of Shri L.K. Jha, the Central Government has been perusing the idea of VAT. With announcing its "long
term fiscal policy" in December 1985, the Government of India moved towards implementing the modified version of
VAT for manufacturing i.e. Modified Value Added Tax (MODVAT) in 1986 and later extending its scope under
CENVAT at federal level. The Central Government facilitated the formation of Empowered Committee of States
Finance Ministers for formulation of VAT in States. After a series of meetings and development, the State VAT 
was proposed to be introduced on 1st April 2003, and over 15 States have already accepted the implementation of VAT.
11.2   The Finance Minister in his speech on Finance Bill, 2003 on 30th April, 2003 stated that VAT was a modern, a
forward looking, and a path breaking reform that the States amongst themselves actually agreed to implement. There
could not be departures from the agreed list of commodities which would be subjected to 12 and a half percent rate of
Value Added Tax. The Empowered Committee had considered these issues. It was very important that the legislation of
the States were amended to conform to the decisions taken by the Committee itself and also to the agreed upon rates
latest by 5th of May. Otherwise, it would not be possible to introduce VAT by 1st of June as suggested by the States
themselves. Government had also, therefore, to take on board the question of adequate preparations in the administrative
machinery before introduction of this very important tax reform.
          The Empowered Committee had fixed some criteria and concerned officials had got full training. The
Government had said that it would try to produce a checklist right from the Centre upto district level. It has also been
stated that it was for the States to deliberate upon the apprehensions of the traders' organizations and industrialists.
11.3   The Committee however, feels that the Central Government should establish a nodal agency, keeping in view the
interest of SSIs in particular and industry in general, in Ministry of Finance (preferably headed by an officer not below
the rank of Addl. Secretary) to coordinate implementation of VAT in States. There is a need to list the operative
problems in inter-State transactions, which could only be solved with the intervention of the Central Government.
11.4   The Committee further observes that the Central Government should act swiftly and decisively to bring in the
homogeneity in the States' VAT Acts: homogeneity in basic principles and classification.
11.5   The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Finance should develop an extensive information base in
Ministry's web site on VAT (or a separate site altogether to solve confusion arising out of lack of updated information at
one point.
XII.    ANTI -DUMPING MEASURES
12.1   There is expected to be large scale dumping of different products and flow of cheaper imports to Indian markets.
Imported consumer goods being of international standards in quality and competitive in prices are likely to change the
tastes of Indian consumers. It may prove to be a major factor in shrinking demand for local products. SSIs are struggling
to restructure themselves, diversify production, adopt higher standards, and have collaborations for product designs or
brands or upgraded technology for their existence. There is ignorance among Small Scale entrepreneurs about
implications of WTO provisions in general and removal of QRs in particular. SSIs did not get much time to adjust and
prepare themselves for increased competition and are thus in a precarious situation.
12.2   The Ministry has informed that it was pointed out by the industry that the imports from Nepal are basically re-
exports without any significant value addition to take advantage of the facility of 0% customs duty. The Committee is
also concerned that goods like Vanaspati are being imported which is adversely affecting the domestic producers. In the
absence of proper rules of origin, Nepal has become a re-export point for many of the products from China and other
South Asian countries. The provision of certificate of origin was dispensed with in the Indo-Nepal Treaty, which gave
rise to a spurt in flow of Chinese goods in the guise of Nepali goods. The Committee recommends that adequate
safeguards be taken to stop misuse of Indo-Nepal Treaty for import of goods from China and other countries.
12.3   Keeping in view the above situation, the provisions relating to value addition and certificate of origin have since
been incorporated in the renewed Treaty so as to protect the interests of Indian industry.
12.4   The Committee was also apprised that the Government has been closely monitoring the situation in this regard
and has taken steps to curb unfair trade practices leading to imports of under priced consumer goods from China. The
Directorate General of Anti-Dumping and Allied Duties has recommended Anti-Dumping final duty in 49 cases and
provisional duty has been recommended in 8 cases against Chinese goods. Further, 6 cases initiated against Chinese
goods are under investigation for preliminary findings.
12.5   A notification has been issued by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry on 24.11.2000, requiring the
compliance of all the conditions of the Standards of Weights and Measures (Packaged Commodity) Order, 1977, on

imports.
12.6   The Union Budget 2002-2003 has incorporated Section 8C in the Customs Tariff Act 1975 in terms of which
Central Government has been empowered to impose transitional product specific safeguard duty on import from the
People's Republic of China.
12.7   The Committee was informed that India's imports from China have not increased significantly and the imports
from China were Rs. 4613 crores during 1998-1999, which increased to Rs. 6706.3 crores during 2000-2001. However,
exports of India to China have grown from Rs. 1797.04 crores (1998-1999) to Rs. 3788.8 crores (2000-2001).
12.8   The Committee observes that the major option for limiting imports that remains for use by policy-makers in a
QR-free regime is that of higher tariffs. The Committee recommends that QRs may be converted into tariff and non-
tariff equivalents to give an adequate protection to the domestic SSIs. While this measure would be a discouraging
factor for foreign producers to export their cheap goods to India, it would also mean increasing revenue for the
Government of India.
12.9   The Committee recommends that on account of almost all products coming under Open General License (OGL)
list, the Government may further strengthen the anti-dumping mechanism so that remedial measures may be initiated
expeditiously. Countervailing duties and safeguard duties may be imposed in order to protect domestic producers from
cheap imports.
12.10 The Committee, therefore, recommends tariff and non-tariff barriers may be imposed in the form of higher safety
standards, and sanitary and phyto-sanitary measures as are permissible within the WTO framework.
12.11 The Committee further recommends that compulsory printing of manufacturing date, maximum retain price (in
Indian rupees) and composition of the products, by foreign exporters should be introduced. This would, of course, be
compatible with the national obligation under WTO, as domestic manufacturers are already required to comply with
these requirements.

Government had announced a modernization package for powerlooms. The Government had also announced a financial

industry. It has done in order to complete the CENVAT chain, to eliminate deemed credit and, finally to improve the
country's international ranking in the textiles industry. The Government had a total textile production of Rs. 3,775 crore.
Out of that Rs. 3,600 crore, by these figures were being exported.
          It is possible that a number of powerloom owners who are self-employed have up till now not filed any income
tax return. But they may now wish, therefore, either to declare their stocks for CENVAT purpose or to join the chain.
Therefore, to encourage them and new assesses of the powerloom sector or the garments to come into the mainstream,
the Income Tax Department has been instructed to encourage the powerloom owners to declare their stocks. If it does
not exceed Rs. 10,000 per powerloom, such declaration will not be subject to any questioning, any scrutiny or any other
inquiry. The Government purpose to fully exempt excise duty on unprocessed fabrics upto the first clearance of Rs.
20.00 lakh provided they are woven by powerloom units having an annual turnover of Rs. 25 lakh. It means that the
powerloom owners or the operators operating some of the traditional powerloom, not the modern variety, and holding
up to almost 10 powerlooms, will be totally free from excise duty. It is totally exempted upto Rs. 20 lakh. The tax of Rs.
5.00 lakh will be charged on Rs. 20-25 lakhs. The Government has given very strict instructions that there would be no
visit and no inspection to these powerlooms. The Government has introduced a self-assessment scheme so that these
powerloom owners could give their own self-assessment.
          The Government also propose to fully exempt from excise duty unbranded woven and knitted readymade
garments upto the first level of Rs. 25 lakh provided that annual turnover does not exceed Rs. 30 lakh. Such units would,
however, be required to keep only one record and it is a record of the yarn or fabric that they have purchased. The
Government has a tax research unit.
          The tax research unit has the details of all garment producers in the country. The Government find that almost
about 85 per cent of the garment manufacturers are in this Rs. 20.00 lakh to Rs. 25.00 lakh bracket. Those that are above
this are really major garment producers. They are not self-employed. The Government also proposes to extend this
exemption scheme to garments of rubberized textile fabrics, cotton benching, mosquito nets and fabrics of mono-
filament."
15.6   The Committee hopes that the above incentives announced by the Government would to a great extend benefit the
small-scale industry units engaged in powerloom and readymade garment sector. The Committee recommends that the
Ministry of Small Scale Industry should make a study of the impact of the above incentives on the Small Scale Industry
units and inform the Department-related Standing Committee on Industry about the same.

XVII. TECHNOLOGY UPGRADATION
17.1   The technological upgradation of the SSI units in India has been quite slow. There are a number of factors that are
responsible for this. The Indian market has so far been basically a sellers' market. Therefore, entrepreneurs have not
made any efforts for modernisation. Moreover, there is non-availability of proper information regarding latest
technologies and their suppliers. There is inadequate availability of credit for investment in technological upgradation
and testing facilities are also not readily available. Even the technological institutions set up for assisting the small-scale
industries themselves lacks sophistication and coordination amongst each other.
17.2   The Ministry apprised the Committee that the number of units benefited under Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme for
Technology Upgradation upto 31st March 2002, was 6, and the amount of Subsidy disbursed was Rs. 25.95 lakhs.
However, during 2002-03 (upto February, 2003), 37 more units have availed subsidy totalling to Rs. 84.88 lakhs.
Though the progress of this Scheme has been slow initially, it has picked up momentum subsequently. However, there is
a need to further accelerate the pace of subsidy off-take under the Scheme. The following measures have been taken in
this regard :
          (i)     The progress of implementation of the Scheme is constantly monitored by a Governing and Technology
Approval Board (GTAB).
          (ii)    A Technical Sub-Committee has been constituted under the Chairmanship of Addl. Secretary and
Development Commissioner (SSI) for suggesting the inclusion of new sub-sectors/products and well
established and improved technologies under the Scheme.
          (iii)   Revised guidelines have been prepared by the Technical Sub-Committee. The salient features thereof are as
underlined:
                  a.     Number of eligible products/sub-sectors increased from 14 to 21.
                  b.     Number of eligible Primary Lending Institutions (PLIs) increased by addition of 34 Cooperative Banks
and 21 Regional Rural Banks for which NABARD will be the new Nodal Agency.
                  c.     In-house testing and on-line qualitative control equipments now eligible for assistance.
                  d.     Units availing subsidy under Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme for Technology Upgradation of the
Small Scale Industries (CLCSS) shall not avail any other subsidy for Technology upgradation from the
Central/State/UT Governments.
                  e.     Release of subsidy will be to the extent of 50% with the first instalment of the loan and the balance 50%
with the last instalment.
                  f.      The term "Proven Technology" replaced by "Well-established and Improved Technology".
                  g.     List of Proven (now termed as "Well-established and Improved") Technologies expanded by adding six
new technologies.
          (iv)   Seminars and awareness programmes are organized to popularize the scheme.
17.3   For effective technological upgradation in Indian SSI units, the Committee recommends that the partnership
approach to develop linkages between large and small enterprises be strengthened.
17.4   Technology Bureau for International Industrial Partnerships and Technology Bureau for Small Enterprises, Delhi
has been providing useful services for technology upgradation in the small-scale units. These organisations need to be

strengthened further and their activities need to be marketed/advertised in an effective manner. The small-scale

XVIII.        LABOUR REFORMS IN SSIs
18.1   The Committee has been getting requests from various Industry Associations on the need to implement labour
reforms so as to make the SSI sector competitive. The Association representing the SSI sector have also time and again
stressed upon the need to free the SSI sector of archaic labour laws and also to free the SSI sector from the Inspector
Raj.
18.2   In this context the Committee observes that the present structure of labour laws does not encourage efficient
performance by the SSIs Managements find it difficult to manage their human resources optimally because of the
constrained labour laws. As of now, the same set of labour laws is applicable to all industrial undertakings, irrespective
of their size.
18.3   The Committee, therefore, recommends that instead of a plethora of labour laws, a single unified Act with simple
provision understandable by the entrepreneurs of SSI units be legislated. SSI units employing upto 50 persons should be
kept outside the purview of the labour law. Instead of the complex Industrial Disputes Act, a simpler law to govern the
relationship between the Employer and the Employee has to be made applicable. The Shops and Establishments Act
may also be suitably modified so as to adapt itself to the needs of the present day SSI sector.
18.4   The Committee further observes that the existing social security legislations such as Employees' Provident Fund
& Miscellaneous Provisions Act, Employees State Insurance Act, Maternity Benefits Act, Workmen's Compensation
Act, and the Payment of Gratuity Act are very cumbersome for entrepreneurs of small-scale units to understand and
comply with. The Committee, therefore, recommends that instead of so many social security legislations, a
comprehensive Social Security Scheme be formulated on a priority basis for providing for one insurance cover to each
workman. The Committee strongly feels that the small-scale units should be able to devote more time to quality
management rather than inspector management.
XIX.  MULTIPLICITY OF AGENCIES
19.1   The Committee observes that at present, there are several Government agencies to deal with small-scale industries
in India. These include Ministry of SSI & ARI, Office of Development Commissioner (SSI), Small Industries
Development Organisation, Small Industries Service Institutes, National Small Industries Corporation, Small Industries
Development Bank of India, Product-cum-process Development Centres, Regional Testing Centres, District Industries
Centres, State Financial Corporations, State Industrial Development Corporations, State Small Industries Development
Corporations, etc.
19.2   The Committee, therefore, recommends that roles of all these organisations be re-evaluated and clearly defined. It
needs to be ensured that their activities do not overlap each other. There should also be co-ordination in the activities of
these agencies. Most importantly, all of these should be able to provide effective service to the small-scale sector.
XX.    DE-RESERVATION OF SSI ITEMS
20.1   The Union Minister for Commerce announced the removal of restrictions while announcing the EXIM policy for
2002-07. While the impact of the removal of QRs is being felt directly by industries, which were earlier protected, there
is likely to be greater impact on the SSIs, especially with regard to their competitive ability and integration with the
global markets against the opportunities of an expanding global market.
20.2   The policy of reservation, was not brought as a measure of protection alone, but was brought for some other

117th Report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/117threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:27:03]
factors as well as provisions of employment opportunities, development of industrial activities in the backward and rural
areas, utilization of local raw-materials, restraining migration of labour etc. Large industries and MNCs are not barred
from manufacturing the reserved items. They have only to adhere to the stipulation that 50% of their production of the
reserved items should be exported, which they could easily do with their latest technologies and their professional
management.
20.3   De-reservation of over 50 items which were marked for exclusive production in the SSI sector has hit hard such
affected and related units. The Committee fails to see any supportive and promotional measure in the latest Budget for
such affected units. It is to be noted that the Government had accepted the Abid Hussain Committee Report in full in
1997. The Abid Hussain Committee recommended that the abolition of reservation should accompany a support
package for the small industries, which include equity support, interest rate concessions, technology assistance etc. The
Abid Hussain Committee, therefore, recommended allocation of Rs. 500 crore per year i.e. Rs. 2,500 crore over the next
five years for the purpose.
20.4   The Ministry apprised the Committee that SSI reservation is in respect of manufacturing only. Export
opportunities on the other hand are a function of marketing for which there is no reservation. Even a reserved item can
be manufactured by a non-SSI unit when it undertakes to export more than 50% of its production. In any case, out of the
total of about 8,000 items being manufactured in the SSI sector, only 749 items are currently reserved and a further de-
reservation of 75 items has been announced in the Union Budget 2003-04.
20.5   Phased de-reservation of 881 items relating to laboratory chemicals and re-agents, leather and leather products,
plastic products, chemicals and chemical products and paper products is in tandem with the recommendations made by
various expert committees. It is desirable that the ceiling of investment in plant and machinery for manufacture of such
products is also increased to a limit of Rs. 5 crore to enable those units for taking up capacity expansion,
technology/upgradation/modernization and export promotion.
20.6   As such, no adverse impact on export can be attributed to the policy of reservation. Government constantly
reviews the situation through a conscious policy of fostering dialogue with stakeholders and putting in place support
measures as required.
20.7   The Committee feels that the Government should have given a support package to the affected units before doing
away with the reservation for such SSI units. The Committee recommends that this may include timely and low cost
credit; export assistance and proper infrastructure facilities. After giving the package, a time frame may be
simultaneously given to such affected units to enable them to fight for survival. In the absence of such package, the
Committee feels the units might be hard-pressed to survive. Moreover, effecting de-reservation without any package
will bring deleterious socio-economic changes as reservation directly affects employment and economic growth.
XXI.  INTEGRATED INFRASTRUCTURAL DEVELOPMENT SCHEME  (IIDS)
21.1   Small Industries Development Organisation (SIDO), Ministry of Small Scale Industries, Government of India has
been operating a number of promotional schemes for the growth of the small-scale industries in the country. The
Integrated Infrastructural Development Scheme is one such Scheme to facilitate the location of industries in rural
backward areas and to generate employment opportunities by establishing close linkages between agriculture and
industry.
21.2   IID Scheme is a Central Sector Scheme in which the Central Government and SIDO contribute to each IID Centre
Rs. 5.00 crore in the ratio of 2: 3 and the cost in excess thereof is met by State/UT Governments. Suitable land for this

21.3   The Committee recommends that unless State Governments play a proactive role in creating infrastructure for
industries, integration and harmonization of villages with industrial development cannot be achieved. Active
participation of State Governments, NGOs and SSI Associations should be ensured by providing appropriate
encouragement to them. This is in keeping with the Industrial Policy announced in 1991, to give special thrust to the
promotion and strengthening of small, tiny and village industries.

XXIII.        PURCHASE PREFERENCE FOR SSI SECTOR
24.1   In USA, there are special laws for small-scale sector. But in our country, there is no such provision for small-scale
sector. Moreover, 50 items have been de-reserved which were exclusively reserved for the small-scale sector. Marketing
facilities for the small-scale sector are also not adequate. Majority of the consumption of items manufactured by small-
scale sector is for domestic consumption. Only 8-10% of the items made by SSIs are exported.
24.2   The Committee reiterates its recommendation, made in its 75th Report, that in order to protect small scale sector
from multinational companies and others, a certain per cent of items, say 33% should be purchased from SSI sector by
Central and State Government Departments/Agencies specially when the items reserved for SSI sector have been
withdrawn and majority of the items have been put on OGL list. The policy of purchase preference system as followed
by USA Government for protection of indigenous industries may be emulated. Necessary action in this regard should be
initiated by the Ministry of SSI immediately.

XXV. IMPACT OF GLOBALISATION AND LIBERALISATION ON SMALL SCALE SECTOR
26.1   The SSI sector continues to be a major generator of employment, next only to the agriculture sector. The sector
has been consistently meeting the targets fixed for employment generation. Given the vast employment opportunities in
the SSI sector, the Government has envisaged creation of 4.4 million additional jobs in this sector during the Tenth Five
Year Plan period (2002-07).
26.2   The Government is constantly monitoring the impact of globalisation and WTO arrangements on the economy
including SSIs and has put in several measures to safeguard the interests of domestic industry. These include special
focus on areas such as technology upgradation, infrastructure assistance through cluster approach, enhanced fiscal
support, timely availability of credit, adoption of modern management facilities, use of electronic infrastructure and
other IT applications to face the emerging challenges of trade liberalization. Protection of these industries is available in
the form of raising customs duties upto the bound levels, imposing anti-dumping duties and countervailing duties,
taking safeguard measures in case of surge in import etc.
26.3   While the globalization and liberalization have exposed the SSI sector to greater competition both domestically
and globally on the one hand, they have on the other provided the SSI sector greater access to markets, cheaper
imported raw materials and technologies. Keeping in view the available trends in employment, exports, production,
number of units etc., which indicate high rates of growth, no major visible adverse impact has been noticed on the SSI
sector.
26.4   With a view to provide a level playing field and help the SSI sector to compete in the international market, the
following measures have been taken by the Ministry of Small Scale Industries:

2.       At the outset the Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee.
3.       The Committee then took up the following Draft Report for its consideration :–
          (i)     111th Report on Operational Efficiency and Survival of Alloy Steel Plant (ASP), Durgapur (Ministry of
Steel)
          (ii)    114th Report on Action taken by the Government on the Recommendations contained in the eighty-third
Report on MOU system in Allied Sector PSEs pertaining to the Department of Heavy Industries and Public
Enterprises (Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises).
          (iii)   115th Report on Action taken by the Government on the Recommendation contained in the Committee's
eighty-first Report on Sickness in SSI Sector of Cement Industry in Uttar Pradesh (Ministry of Small Scale
Industries).
          (iv)   116th Report on Action taken by the Government on the Recommendation contained in the Committee's
eighty-second Report on MOU System in Strategic Sector PSEs pertaining to the Department of Heavy
Industries and Public Enterprises.
          (v)    117th Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Ministry of Small Scale Industries.
          (vi)   118th Report on Action taken by the Government on the Recommendation contained in the ninetieth Report
on 'Sickness in SSI Sector in the city of Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh (Ministry of Small Scale Industries).
          (vii)  119th Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Department of Public Enterprises
(Ministry of Small Scale Industries).
          (viii)  120th Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Department of Heavy Industries
(Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises).
          (ix)   121st Report on Action taken by the Government on Recommendation contained in the Committee's
hundredth Report on Performance Review of National Mineral Development Corporation (Ministry of Steel).
          (x)    122nd Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Ministry of Steel.
          (xi)   123rd Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Ministry of Mines.
          (xii)  124th Report on Demands for Grants (2003-2004) pertaining to the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries.
          (xiii)  125th Report on Implementation Status of Modernisation Expansion and Financial-cum-Business
Restructuring Programmes of SAIL at a Glance (Ministry of Steel).
          (xiv) 126th Report on Revival of Nagaland Pulp and Paper Company (Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public
Enterprises).
4.       The Chairman requested the Members to give any suggestion or change in the Report, if required. After a brief
discussion the Reports were adopted.
5.       The Chairman nominated Prof. M.M. Aggarwal and in his absence Shri Khan Gufran Zahidi, Members Rajya
Sabha to present these reports to the Rajya Sabha. He also nominated Dr. B.B. Ramaiah and in his absence Shri A.C.
Jose, Member, Lok Sabha to present these reports in the Lok Sabha on 8th May, 2003.
6.         The Committee then adjourned at 5.00 P.M.
