CHAPTER - I
MOU SYSTEM
         The Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) is a negotiated document between the Government, acting as the owner
of Public Sector Enterprise (PSE) and a specific PSE. It contains the intentions, obligations and mutual responsibilities of
the Government and the PSE. Further, MoU makes an attempt to move the management of PSEs from management by
controls and procedures to management by results and objectives.
1.2    The genesis of the policy of Memorandum of Understanding can be traced to the report of the Arjun Sengupta
Committee. One of the recommendations of this Committee was for the introduction of the system of MoU for
measurement of performance of public enterprises. The MoU System was introduced on an experimental basis in 1987.
OBJECTIVES OF THE MOU SYSTEM
1.3    The objectives of the MoU system are to improve the performance of public enterprises by increasing autonomy and
accountability of the management; to remove the fuzziness in the goals and objectives of the enterprise to pursue through
clearly laid down performance targets in the MoU and to enable the evaluation of managerial performance through
objective criteria and provide a mechanism to reward good performance through performance incentives to stimulate
improved performance.
1.4    Under the agreement the enterprises undertake to achieve the targets set in the agreement at the beginning of the
year. Thus the MoU covers both financial performance as well as non-financial performance. Under this system
performance of the Company is ­categorized into five categories namely: excellent, very good, good, fair, and poor.
PREPARATION OF MOU
1.5    Based on the guidelines issued by the DPE in the month of October/November every year, the draft MoUs are
prepared by PSEs and forwarded to the concerned Administrative Ministry and DPE by December. The draft MoUs
indicate the performance targets on a five point scale as proposed by the PSE for the ensuing financial year for all crucial
parameters like production, sales, profits, etc. The PSEs are also expected to include dynamic parameters of significance
for strengthening the future potential of the enterprise, such as project implementation, R&D, Human Resource
Development, etc. Appropriate weights are assigned to each of these parameters. The draft MoU are discussed with the
PSE Administrative Ministries at the Adhoc Task Force (ATF) negotiation meetings arranged in DPE in the month of
March/soon after the April presentation of the Union Budget. Since the Budget has implications on performance targets,
the timing of the negotiation meetings is deliberately scheduled after the budget presentation. The appropriateness of the
parameters and weights and the soundness of the targets as also the commitments of the Government are examined in
detail at the negotiation meetings. The draft MoUs as finalised thereafter are placed before the High Power Committee

(HPC) comprising of the Cabinet Secretary as Chairman, Secretary, Finance, Expenditure, Planning Commission,
Programme Implementation, Chairman Public Enterprises Selection Board, Chief Economic Adviser and Secretary
Public Enterprises as Members, for approval. After approval, the respective Chief Executives and Secretaries.
EVALUATION
1.6    The evaluation of the MoU signing enterprises is done at the end of the year by the Ad-hoc ,Task Force (ATF) on
the basis of actual achievement vis-a-vis the MoU targets. Where the performance exceeds the target by the
predetermined stipulated level the enterprise is ranked as ‘excellent’ and when the target is just met, the enterprise is
ranked as ‘very good’ . In other cases, where the targets are not met the enterprise is ranked either ‘ good’ or ‘ fair’ or ‘
poor’ . Ranking depends on how the performance has been as compared to MOU targets.
1.7    The performance criteria should be assigned appropriate weights corresponding with their stated priorities in the
objectives. It should be ensured that they also reflect the perceived relative importance of the various criteria in the
judgement of the PSE/Ministry. The weight for physical criteria should be kept as low as possible. Instead, Efficiency and
Dynamic criteria should get higher weights. The weight for profit-related financial parameters would be 60 % in case of
PSEs operating under free market mechanism. The weight of 60 % would be distributed to; Gross Margin, Gross
Profit/Capital Employed and Net Profit/Net Worth, each having a weight of 20 % each. For PSEs operating under
administered prices, PSEs where Government is sole/almost sole customer & Trading, Consultancy, Engineering and
Financial PSEs, the weight would be 40%. The weight of 40 % would be distributed to, Gross Margin and Net Profit/Net
Worth, each having a weight of 20 % each. The PSEs which have been specially set up for implementing certain
Government policy directives will have no pre-determined financial parameters.
1.8    The targets set should be realistic and growth oriented. It should be ensured that the MoU targets flow from the
Corporate Vision/Plans formulated by the PSEs and there is linkage between them. In case there is any variation, reasons
thereof should be clearly specified. For qualitative indicators, the targets for each of the points on the 5-point scale should
be indicated in the performance matrix itself. If these targets are on a time scale, only such target, which falls within the
MoU year, is included.
1.9    The MoU should contain the past five years performance data on criteria included in the MoU in an appendix. It
should also indicate the projections for the next five years, as far as possible consistent with the corporate plan.
1.10  The report on MoU performance in respect of various PSUs is an exercise in the study of Memorandum of
Understanding of public sector undertakings undertaken by the Sub-Committee for Ministry of Heavy Industries and
Public Enterprises of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry. This study includes, Rashtriya Ispat Nigam
Limited, Dredging Corporation of India both at Visakhapatnam and National Mineral Development Corporation Limited,
Bharat Dynamics Limited both at Hyderabad. The Committee undertook a study visit to Visakhapatnam and Hyderabad
on 22-23rd September, 2003 and met with the CMDs and other officials of these PSUs. The deliberations of the
Committee is explained in the following chapters".
CHAPTER II
RASHTRIYA ISPAT NIGAM LIMITED
Visakhapatnam Steel Plant
2.1    Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited ( RINL) is the corporate entity of Visakhapatnam Steel Plant (VSP) The steel plant
is located 26 km south of Visakhapatnam  city. The Company has a blast furnace grade limestone captive mine at
Jaggayapeta, a captive mine for Dolomite at Madharam, a Manganese ore captive mine at Cheepurupalli All the captive
mines are located in the State of Andhra Pradesh.
2:2    The Sub-Committee for Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises visited the plant on 22nd September,
2003 as a part of its study visit to Visakhapatnam on the MOU performance in the Public Sector Enterprises The
Committee was apprised by the Chairman and Managing Director that the foundation stone of the steel plant was laid on
January 20, 1971 and the plant was dedicated to the nation on August 1, 1992. The plant has a capacity to produce 2.656
Mt of saleable steel, of which, 2.410 Mt is finished steel Besides receiving raw materials from the captive sources, the
steel plant meets its iron ore requirements from Bailadilla mines of National Mineral Development Corporation, coking
coal requirements through through and coal washeries of Coal India Limited, SMS grade limestone through imports
quartzite requirements through purchase and boiler coal from the coal mines of Mahanadi Coal Limited.
2.3    The product profile of the plant comprises wire rods, rounds, reinforcement bars (rebars), angles, channels beam

Steps taken to improve MOU performance
2.10  Consistent efforts are made for improving the performance of the Company. These efforts have resulted in
improved physical and fiscal performance, which have contributed to improved MOU performance.
2.11  Technological upgradation in various areas like cold and hot repairs of coke oven batteries; Bed height in sinter
machines; Oxygen enrichment in blast furnaces; Installation of amanoscope in Blast Furnaces; Introduction PLCs in BFs
; Modification of lance tip design in LD conveners; Slag splashing in LD converters; Installation of ladle furnace in steel
melt shop; Installation of gega cutting machine in continuous casting; Improvement of cooling system in continuous
casting; Implementation of higher levels of automation in BF, TPP, rolling mills, etc. Installation of fibre optic cable
network in the plant; Installation of server based RS-6000 computer; Implementation of cost reduction and waste
minimization initiatives; Increasing production of value added steels; Product-mix optimization; Thrust on training and
HRD ; Focus on research and development Completion of projects within time; Efficient financial management i.e.
swapping of high cost debts with lower cost debts, working capital management, etc.; and Customer relations
management.
2.12  RINL has a committed work force of 16,894 as on 31-3-2003. Out of these, 4,308 are executives and 12,586 are
non-executives. RINL has rationalized the designations of non-executive employees. The highly skilled category has been
designated as ‘charge man’, the skilled category as ‘ technician’ and the unskilled employees as ‘ khalasi’ RINL has
introduced multi-skilling concept since inception and the employees are trained as per this concept.
2.13  RINL follows a system of overlapping shifts, the first of its kind in the industry. This system ensures smooth change
over of the shifts and uninterrupted pace of operation of the plant during the shift change over. Another unique feature
followed at RINL is the uniform working hours for the ministerial and non-ministerial employees.
2.14  Training and human resource development is given a lot of importance at RINL. Each year, at least one-third of the
employees undergo various training sessions either at training and development centre or at centre for human resource
development (HRD) for sharpening their skills, with respect to both technical matters as well as human related issues.
Further, training is also imparted to the employees in safety, fire prevention, occupational health besides giving on- the
job training at shop floor.
2.15  Caring for stakeholders is one of the main thrust areas at RINL. Progress through systematization and through
empowering of employees is the main approach followed. Fearless environment with encouragement towards risk-taking
is helping the innovativeness at RINL. Motivation of employees towards production and productivity is achieved through
a well-designed-incentive scheme. Team approach is encouraged for all, the activities to achieve results. Process control
functions in the plant and control of product quality are carried out by the line operators. Quality assurance and
technology development (QA&TD) department facilitates these activities. Technology upgradation and product
diversification activity is carried out through R&D activities, which is not only the function of the research and
development (R&D) wing, but of each and every employee of the Company. Communication and knowledge sharing is
the route followed by the employees so that they can give their best to the Company.
2.16  The Company follows customer-centric approach in its activities since it is sincerely believed that the existence of
the Company is only due to its customers. It is also aware that in today’s competitive environment, unless and until it
gives its best, it cannot survive.
2.17  RINL takes all necessary actions for the fulfillment of regulatory requirements. It has dedicated departments for this
purpose. Energy conservation, environmental preservation, safety in workplace and occupational health gets highest
priority in the Company. It also follows strict financial discipline as prescribed under various Acts. It has an internal audit
department, which acts as a watchdog for the financial discipline within the Company. RINL also works in close co-
operation with the suppliers and service providers. The Company is conscious of its financial situation and recognizes
that it has to continue to improve its financial performance to invest in future.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AND CONCLUSIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
PERFORMANCE
2:19  The Committee takes note of the overall performance of the VSP particularly the financial crisis overcome by the
organization and reaching the ‘ Zero Debt’ level. The Committee was informed that the PSU was referred to BIFR in the
year 1998-99. It started recovering since then and its cash profit was likely to reach Rs.975 crores (approx.) in the year
2002-03. In terms of MOU performance also the PSU performed well and has been rated continuously ‘ Excellent’ since
2000-01. The production of value added steel products has also risen from a level of around 3 lakh tones in 2000-01 to
over 4.8 lakh tones in 2002­-03 and the efforts being taken by the PSU for new products, grades continued to be
developed every year to meet the market demand. The Committee appreciates the various steps taken to improve the
performance of the plant like, capacity utilization, optimum maintenance of equipments available and the ongoing
technological upgradations in various areas. The Committee also appreciates the thrust given by training and HRD
activities as well as the focus on the R&D taking place.
2.20  The Committee observes that the major raw material for production of steel is iron ore, which is supplied by NMDC
. The supply of Iron ore to VSP has been inconsistent for the past one year, especially in the rainy season. The ore is
currently supplied from Bailadilla and Dominalai Mines and the railway routes are severely affected during the rainy
seasons. The Committee is of the view that VSP should look out for expansion of its iron ores captive mines for smoother
operations of the plant, which will also help the VSP to achieve the set targets. The Committee further recommends that
the Mines identified by VSP like Chidi Mines and Ramaghat in Chattisgarh may be acquired by VSP to strengthen its
operations. The Government should render all necessary help in accomplishing this task.
2.21  The Committee further observes that as a part of its expansion plan, the focus in the first phase was on capacity
enhancement of 5 million tonnes with minimal investment, by exploiting the inherent potential of the existing facilities.
In this phase , the capacity of the Blast Furnaces was planned to be increased by using natural gas, which is likely to be
available in the Krishna-Godavari basin. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should ensure the
availability of natural gas from KG Basin at the earliest which is crucial for the Phase-I expansion programme of VSP.
EXPANSION ACTIVITIES
2.22  The Committee was informed that during the year 2003-04 the hot metal production was likely to cross 4.1 million
tonnes. The requirement of Coke for the additional hot metal production has to be sourced from the external agencies.
There is acute shortage of coke both in domestic and international market due to closure of some coke oven units and coal
mines in China. Whereas, VSP is having its own three coke oven batteries, which requires expansion. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the request of VSP for the fourth coke oven Battery should be cleared immediately to meet
the additional production target as well as the timely taking up of rebuilding of the present coke oven batteries.
2.23  The Committee observes that as a long-term measure and considering the inherent strengths of VSP a corporate
plan for expanding the capacity of VSP to 10 million tonnes by the year 2020 should be seriously taken up by the
Government so as to meet the increasing demand of steel in the country as well as the potential for export.
RAILWAY FREIGHT
2.24  The Committee was informed that the Railways have been charging 30% extra as surcharge for movement of iron
ore from Bailadilla to Visakhapatnam  for the past thirty years. Now, it has been reduced to 20% . As many years have

MoU PERFORMANCE IN RESPECT OF DREDGING CORPORATION OF INDIA (DCI)
3.1    In the 1960s, dredging requirements in the various ports were being catered to by the then Ministry of Shipping and
Transport which maintained a central dredging pool consisting of dredgers, hopper barges, tugs, survey launches and
other operational equipment. Subsequently, in 1968, considering the increased dredging requirements in the country,
various crafts and equipment were handed over to Shipping Corporation of India Ltd. (SCI) for managing on agency
basis. Over the years, the development of ports continued and taking into account the increasing dredging requirement in
the country , the Government decided to form a separate Company. Accordingly, in March, 1976, Dredging Corporation
of India Limited (DCI) was incorporated as a wholly owned Government of India Undertaking with the primary objective
of catering to the dredging requirements of various ports. However, the agency arrangement with SCI continued during
1976-77, and DCI commenced its full-fledged commercial operations from 1.4.1977.
3.2    The Sub-Committee for Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises visited Visakhapatnam on 22
nd
September, 2003 as a part of its study visit to PSUs on MOU performance. The Committee was apprised by the CMD,
DCI that DCI has its Registered Office at New Delhi, Head Office at Visakhapatnam  and Offices at almost all the major
ports. DCI possesses 10 Trailer Suction Dredging and 2 Cutter Suction Dredgers. The Company was formed with an
authorized capital of Rs.3000 lakhs, of which Rs.2800 lakhs was fully paid up taken entirely by the Government of India.
In 1992, the Government disinvested 1.44 % of its share-holding and presently shares of the Company are listed in Delhi,
Mumbai and Kolkata Stock Exchanges. The DCI has again listed 20% of its stock for disinvestment in March, 2004
alongwith other PSUs which received overwhelming response in the market. The Company has been earning profit
consistently since its inception, and its turnover from a modest Rs.1986 lakhs in 1977-78 has risen to Rs.51952 lakhs; at a
capacity utilization of Rs.102% in 2002-03.
         The dredging capacity of the Company has increased from 155 lakh CuM. in 1977-78 to 798 lakh CuM. in 2002-03:
DCI’s main customers are Major Ports; Non­ Major Ports; Indian Navy; Shipyards and Private Ports.
3.3    DCI has always strived to meet the requirement of its customers and, on some occasions; DCI’s efforts were
appreciated by the customers. DCI has been awarded Mini-Ratna (Category-I) status by the Government during 1999.
DCI was upgraded to Schedule-“B” category with effect from lst April, 2002. To augment the dredging capacity, the
Company has acquired three Trailer Suction Hopper Dredgers each of 7400 Cu. M. hopper capacity, which were added to
the fleet during 1999, 2001 and 2002, respectively. DCI’s 10th plan outlay of Rs. 365 crores, inter alia, envisages

consistent good performance.
Product Range of BDL
5.2    BDL has two manufacturing units. These are at Kanchanbagh (Hyderabad) and at Bhanur (Medak District). It
produces the following equipments: Milan Anti-tank Guided Missile (ATGM) (under Licence Agreement with Euro
missile of France) with a range of 2 KMs; Konkurs Anti-tank Guided Missile (ATGM) and Konkurs-M (Tandem
Warhead) under Licence Agreement from Russia, with a range of 4 Kms; Prithvi, Surface-to Surface Missile (SSM);
3UBK-20 (Gun launched Projectile) with a range of 5 Kms, under licence from Russia; Advanced Experimental Torpedo
(AET); Submarine Fired Decoy (SFD); and 9 MM Pistols. Training simulators for Anti Tank Guided Missiles (ATGM)
viz. ATGM Indoor and Outdoor Simulators, Konkurs simulators; Interactive Fire Arms Training Simulator for training in
Small Arms; Test Equipment viz., KLTE, KMTE, FLTE. BDL is also nominated as the Prime Production Agency for
Akash, Nag, Trishul (New version) Missiles under development by Defence Research and Development Organisation
(DRDO). These projects are in different stages of development. Further the Ministry of Defence has nominated BDL as
the production Agency for Refurbishment/Life Extension of various types of Missiles held in the inventory of the three
services. In addition, BDL has supplied Tow body to Aeronautical Development Establishment (ADE) and flow formed
tubes to Bhaba Atomic Research Centre (BARC).

         The Committee was informed that the price of various range of products of BDL is fixed by the Government. As the
Government is the sole purchaser of its products, thereby earning of the organization is controlled by the Government and
there is mandatory 20% dividend to be paid to the Government. The Committee is of the view that in the era of opening
up of economy, the Government should allow the BDL to fix the price of its products and make profit to promote its
developmental activities so as to compete in the international markets.                             (Para 5.26)
SAFETY
         The Committee was informed about the fire accident that took place in the year 2001 due to which it was rated
‘Fair’ in terms of MOU performance during the years 2000-01 and 2001-02. The Committee is of the view that in an
organisation of strategic importance, safety is an integral component of its performance. The Committee, therefore, urges
upon the Government to ensure safety measures to avoid such accidents in all strategic organisations.(Para 5.27)
FUTURE PLAN
         The Committee is of the view that the growth of a country rests upon its defence capability also. Therefore, any
developing nation should not compromise in defence sector because of pressure from any quarter. The Committee was
informed that the BDL envisages induction of various products in the medium term and long term periods. The
Committee therefore, recommends to the Government to ensure that the future plan of BDL be fulfilled to produce a
range of products to put the country in the list of developed countries.                                                        (Para 5.28)
