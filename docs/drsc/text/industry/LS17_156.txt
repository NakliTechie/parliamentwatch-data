REPORT
1.0     The present Report is Action Taken Report on the Committee’s 131 st Report (of Department-related
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry) on the Constraints in Industrial Development of North Eastern Region.
The said report was presented to the Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of the Lok Sabha on 22nd December, 2003.
1.2     The 131 st Report relates to the Industrial units located in North East Region which are facing locational and
logistical disadvantages besides the problems of lack of infrastructure development, insurgency, connectivity, working
system and the problem of communication and transportation. The Committee felt that at the strategic level the
problems facing industrial units in the NE region, notwithstanding whether it is in private or public sector are similar.
The existing industrial units were becoming increasingly uncompetitive on account of the general economic slow down
and adverse inbound and outbound logistics. It is, therefore, fair and reasonable that the excise duty exemption and
transport subsidy, as envisaged in the newly emerging units, be extended to the existing units, which are teetering on the
brink of bankruptcy and eventual closure. Therefore, in order to mitigate the locational disadvantages and the logistical
problems the Committee emphasized the physical concession, the excise exemption and the continuance of transport
subsidy for the sustenance of the industrial units mainly Hindustan Paper Corporation (HPC) and other PSUs like
Nagaland Pulp and Paper Mills Limited (NPPM), Cement Corporation of India Limited (CCI), Andrew Yule and
Company Limited (AYCL). The underline argument of the Committee was that the employment potential of the pulp
and paper industry and the plywood industry was undoubtedly one of highest, especially after taking into account the
spin off effects in unorganised informal sector which the predominantly tribal population found something akin to their
traditional occupation.
1.3     The succeeding paragraphs deals with Committee’s recommendations mentioned in the 131 st Report and the
Action Taken Notes as received from the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises and the same have been
examined and analysed vis-a-vis Committee’s recommendation followed by Committee’s further observations and
recommendations:----
RECOMMENDATION
2.0     Extension of 50 per cent excise concession
          The Committee, however, express its happiness as Secretary, Department of Revenue, informed the Committee
that 50 per cent excise concession would be given for one year and thereafter it would be reviewed. The Committee,
however, recommends that HPC’s case should be reviewed by the Government on the lines of refineries till it is
disinvested.          (Para 5.14)
ACTION TAKEN
2.1     The DHI took up the matter with the Department of Revenue on 15th January, 2004. The Department of Revenue
have informed that the recommendations as communicated by the DHI have been received late and it will be difficult
for them to implement the same at this stage. The Department of Revenue have however stated that the
recommendations will be considered at an appropriate time.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
2.1.1  The Committee is of the view that HPC is a highly employment generating unit with a multiplier effect in the
informal sector. HPC has already given back to the Exchequer to the tune of Rs. 1558 crores out of which excise duty
alone accounted for Rs. 615 crores. In the opinion of the Committee HPC is genuinely pleading for being equated with
the treatment that is given to any new units or a new unit undertaking substantial expansion under the new North East
Policy. And under the new dispensation when the disinvestment of HPC seems to a distant goal, it is worth while to
make effort to revive the HPC through fiscal measures on the lines of the recommendation of the Committee. Moreover,
the case of HPC requires a political decision rather than a revenue consideration in the overall interest of development
of North East Region. The special treatment which HPC requires should not be halted because of the overt concern of
the Government as to if the excise concession is given to the North East units, the other states like Uttaranchal and
Himachal Pradesh will also ask for similar facilities. Therefore, the Committee reiterates its recommendation that the

RECOMMENDATION
3.0     Conversion of metre gauge to broad gauge line in the Lumding - Silchar section as well as new line on
Kumarghat - Agartala section.
3.1     The Committee, however, agrees that the North Eastern Region should be given a separate footing with regard to
expansion of railway network specially for two projects i.e. conversion of meter gauge to broad gauge line in the
Lumding - Silchar section as well as new line on Kumarghat - Agartala section. The Committee therefore recommends
that Government of India should provide adequate funds for implementation of these two projects as on completion of
these projects not only the movement of essential commodities will be faster but also improve the industrial scenario in
the North East with the movement of raw materials required for industrial units. The economic lot of common people in
the North East depends on the industrial development which should not be lost sight
of.                                                         (Para 5.19)
ACTION TAKEN
3.1.1  Matter was taken up with the Ministry of Railways [Railway Board]. The Ministry of Railways has informed that
as per the policy of investment in the North Eastern Region, Railways are earmarking 
10 per cent of its gross budgetary support [GBS] for projects in the North Eastern Region which amounts to Rs.300-500
crore per annum. As against this, the ongoing new line and gauge conversion projects in the North Eastern Region
require an investment of Rs.6500 crore for their completion. Accordingly, in order to meet the requirement of funds for
expeditious completion of projects, the Department of Development of North Eastern Region [DoNER] was approached
to provide additional resources from non lapsable pool of funds. However, it was not found feasible to allocate funds
from non lapsable pool of funds in view of other pressing demands from various States. Considering the need of
expediting infrastructure development in the North Eastern Region, Hon’ble Prime Minister was further requested by
the Minister for Railways to declare Lumding - Silchar - Jiribham gauge conversion and Kumarghat - Agartala new line
projects as National Projects to be funded through additional budgetary support over and above the normal Railways’
plan. The matter has thereafter been considered by Planning Commission. Planning Commission has not favored
budgetary support outside the plan for the above projects. They have opined that the projects in the North East may be
assigned a higher priority with sufficient outlay for their speedy completion. The Ministry of Railways is already
earmarking 10 per cent of their GBS for the projects in the region. Furthermore w.e.f. 2002-03, State-wise allocation of
available funds is done by Railways based on a rational and transparent formula involving three major criteria namely,
area of the state, the population and the throw forward of projects in the States. Weightage given to these criteria is in
the ratio of 15 per cent, 15 per cent & 70 per cent respectively. In view of this Ministry of Railways have informed that
it is not feasible to allocate required funds for the completion of the projects in the North Eastern Region in next 4-5
years. Accordingly, the matter is being pursued by Railway Ministry for declaring these projects as National Projects.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
3.2     The Committee notes that the project relating to conversion of meter gauge to broad gauge line in the Lumding -
Silchar section as well as new line on Kumargram - Agartala section in the North Eastern Region require an investment
of Rs. 6500 crore for their completion. The Committee is pleased to know that the Minister for Railways has forwarded
the case of declaring Lumding - Silchar -Jiribham gauge conversion and Kumarghat - Agartala new line projects as
National Projects to the Hon’ble Prime Minister for arranging the fund through additional budgetary support over and
above the normal Railways’ plan. However, the Committee expresses its concern over the inability of the Planning
Commission for paying due attention in arranging the required fund through budgetary support. Considering the need of
expediting infrastructure development in the North Eastern Region, the Committee thinks that the project should have
been given green signal, the Committee is of the considered view that North Eastern Region requires special status so
far the projects on infrastructure development is concerned because not only the economy of the common people in the
North Eastern Region but the balanced national economic growth and development also depends upon the industrial
development which is concomitant to the infrastructure development. Therefore, concerted efforts should be made on
the part of the ministry of Railways to declare Lumding - Silchar - Jiribham gauge conversion and Kumarghat -Agartala
new line projects as National Projects.

FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
4.5     The Committee understands that close co-ordination between Department of Heavy Industries and Railways at the
Central level would provide a platform to discuss the mutual problems in removing the bottlenecks in the North East
Region for the establishment and development of industrial units. Mutual discussions provides opportunities for
consensus-ad-idem for exploring the possible solutions to the problems being combated by the authorities concerned.
That is why, the Committee reiterates its recommendation that DHI and Railways should continue to have cooperative
and collaborative approach towards solving the problems on the path of industrial development of North Eastern
Region. The Committee is of the considered opinion that the Railways can play a paramount role in providing
Infrastructural base for industrial projects and units and can thus play supportive part in the lives of the masses dwelling
in the region.
4.6     The Committee acknowledges the fact that the railways have very much appreciated and shared the concern in

RECOMMENDATION
5.0     Implementation of the recommendations made by the Committee in its 58th report
5.1     Since not much headway has been made with regard to the implementation of the recommendations made by the
Committee in its 58 th report, the Committee reiterates its observations made in the said report and recommends that
DHI should take up the following relating:
          (i)    to the allotment of degraded forest land for the development of captive plantation with the Government of
Assam and the Ministry of Environment and Forests.
          (ii)    to expedite approval for implementing working plans for extractions of Bamboo in the North East and
construction of dirt road in unexploited forest areas with the State Government and Ministry of Environment
and Forests.
          (iii)   reviewing of the agreement with private firms regarding extraction of Bamboo on a long term basis from the
forest areas reserved for HPC to see whether there are any violation of Forest Conservation Act.
          (iv)   entering into an agreement with State Forest Departments to mobilise plantation work with necessary
financial support with the condition that the first charge on the crop at the time of harvesting would be
HPC’s.
          (v)   problems of gregarious flowering which could lead to excessive rodent population, with Ministry of
Environment and Forests.
          (vi)   exploring the utilization of inland waterways in transportation of its products and raw materials for interiors,
with the Ministry of Shipping.
                                                                                                                                            (Para 5.22)
ACTION TAKEN
5.2     The Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises furnished the following information:-----
          (i)    Despite numerous meetings with Government of Assam, no decision has come regarding allotment of
degraded forest land for development of Captive plantation.
          (ii)    Hindustan Paper Corporation Ltd. (HPC) have informed that their working plans under reference have
already been submitted to Assam Government who have referred the matter to the Ministry of Environment
and Forests. This Ministry has taken up the matter with Inspector General [Forests], Ministry of Environment
& Forests. The matter is being followed up with that Ministry.
          (iii)   HPC has taken legal recourse to dissuade the concerned agencies to annul the agreement with the private
firms and Court has given verdict in favour of HPC in one of the case.
          (iv)   HPC is of the view that for a forestation efforts with State Forest Department, once the discussions were
held when they had indicated that HPC may give the funds to State Forest Department and they would do the
plantation work of their own, Since there is always resource crunch with the State Forest Department,
handing over the fund to them may not lead to any fruitful result which may be apportioned by the State
Forest Department for their other expenses. Therefore it has been considered better to go ahead with Farm
Forestry rather than trying a forestation through State Forest Department.
          (v)   Regarding gregarious flowering, 3 Committees have already been set up by the Ministry of Environment &
Forests [MoE&F]. CMD, HPC is a member of the Committee on Marketing of Bamboo Produce, headed by
Chief Secretary, Mizoram and the Committee is going into various facets of impending gregarious flowering.

FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
5.2     The Committee in its 131 st Report expressed its concern over the non-implementation of the recommendation
made by the Committee in its 58 th Report and the Committee reiterated its recommendations as made in the 58 th
Report. After taking the Action Taken Note thereto into the consideration vis-a-vis its recommendation:—
          (i)    The Committee considers the allotment of degraded forest land to HPC as indispensable to make HPC self
sufficient in meeting with the requirement of bamboo for two mills of HPC and to enlarge the forest resources
in general. Since the issue relates to the sustenance and revival of HPC located in Assam and the ecology and
environment in and around Assam, there should not be much hesitation and hassels on the part of the
Government of Assam and the Ministry of Environment and Forests. Besides, the activity so envisaged bears
the potential of employment generation and social upliftment. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
DHI should keep on pursuing with the Government of Assam and the Ministry of Environment and Forests
highlighting the social benefits, requirement and benefits accruing to HFC and gains in maintaining the
ecological balance in allotting the degraded forest land to HPC.
          (ii)    The Committee recommends that the DHI should take up effective follow up action with the Ministry of
Environment and Forests for getting the approval for implementing working plants for extraction of bamboos
in the North East. Similar follow up action should be under taken with the State Government and Forest
Department for construction of dirt road in unexploited forest areas.
          (iii)   The basic concern of the Committee is that HPC must be facilitated to meet its requirement of bamboo. The
Committee is pleased to note that court has given verdict in favour of HPC in one of the case relating to the
agreement with private firms regarding extraction of bamboo on a long term basis from the forest areas
reserved for HPC.
          (iv)   Relating to the issue of entering into an agreement with State Forest Department to mobilize plantation, the
Committee recommends that DHI should explore the possibility of creation of Plantation Consortium with
equal financial contribution from the State Government and HPC for plantation purpose and formation of a
Monitoring Committee having a representatives from State Forest Department and HPC to look after the
execution of plantation projects. This arrangement would ensure HPC in meeting with the requirement of
bamboo and at the same time generating additional revenue to the financially crunched state.
          (v)   The Standing Committee on Industry would like to know the outcome of the efforts made by the Committee
constituted by the Ministry of Environment and Forests, where CMD, HPC is a member, in combating the
menace of gregarious flowering to prevent the excessive rodent population.
          (vi)   As regards the utilization of inland waterways in transportation of HPC products and raw materials for
interiors, the Committee is of the view that the said means of transportation should be experimented keeping
in view the cost benefit analysis and the local climatic conditions. If the proposition is favourable and viable
the said means of transportation might be undertaken, otherwise alternative cheaper and viable mode of
transportation may be undertaken.                                                                                                           (Para
5.2)

Gardens] and CCI unit [Bokajan]. The scope for investments in Nagaland Pulp and Paper Mills Ltd is limited as the unit
is not in a operation for a long time and options of “revival or closure” are under consideration. All efforts are being
made to utilize the allocated funds for the North Eastern Region. For the year 2004-05, Planning Commission have
earmarked Rs. 13 crore for investment in the North East.
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
Capital Investment Programmes in North East Region
6.5     The Committee is of the view that Industrialization is the key to economic growth and development. For the
balanced national development, the economic development of North Eastern Region needs to be put on industrialization
track and due to this cherished goal and National priority the Government of India has the responsibility for the last mile
developments for the socio-economic infrastructure in the North Eastern Region. At the same time, the Government has
engineered its “gubernatorial will” to remove the bottlenecks being faced on the path of industrial development like
insurgency, connectivity and communication system, working system and Infrastructural development to induce
investment from private sectors. Proper and timely utilisation of the Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources would
ensure acceleration of industrial development. Moreover, the Department of Development of North East Region has to
facilitate the proliferation of new industrial units in the region by co-ordinating with various Departments/Ministries of
State and Central Government.
6.6     The sustenance and survival of existing industrial units i.e. Hindustan Paper Corporation (HPC), Nagaland Pulp
and Paper Mills Limited (NPPM), Cement Corporation of India Limited (CCI) and Andrew Yule and Company Limited
(AYCL) is equally important as the revival of these units would act as a catalyst to raise the confidence level of
investors in the region besides their contribution of these industrial units in the economic development of the region due
to their employment generation potential and opportunity for poverty alleviation. The Committee reiterates its stand that
the DHI should assist these PSUs in formulting the short term and the long term capital investment programmes and co-
ordinate with the DONER for rescuing these PSUs out of red and reassure the people of the region that PSUs still
remain the temple of modern India.

COMMITTEE — AT A GLANCE
EXTENSION OF 50 PER CENT EXCISE CONCESSION
          The Committee is of the view that HPC is a highly employment generating unit with a multiplier effect in the
informal sector. HPC has already given back to the Exchequer to the tune of Rs. 1558 crores out of which excise duty
alone accounted for Rs. 615 crores. In the opinion of the Committee HPC is genuinely pleading for being equated with
the treatment that is given to any new units or a new unit undertaking substantial expansion under the new North East
Policy. And under the new dispensation when the disinvestment of HPC seems to a distant goal, it is worth while to
make effort to revive the HPC through fiscal measures on the lines of the recommendation of the Committee. Moreover,
the case of HPC requires a political decision rather than a revenue consideration in the overall interest of development
of North East Region. The special treatment which HPC requires should not be halted because of the overt concern of

CONVERSION OF METER GAUGE TO BROAD GAUGE LINE IN THE LUMDING - SILCHAR SECTION
AS WELL AS NEW LINE ON KUMARGHAT - AGARTALA SECTION.
          The Committee notes that the project relating to conversion of meter gauge to broad gauge line in the Lumding -
Silchar section as well as new line on Kumarghat - Agartala section in the North Eastern Region require an investment
of Rs. 6500 crore for their completion. The Committee is pleased to know that the Minister for Railways has forwarded
the case of declaring Lumding - Silchar -Jiribham gauge conversion and Kumarghat - Agartala new line projects as
National Projects to the Hon’ble Prime Minister for arranging the fund through additional budgetary support over and
above the normal Railways’ plan. However, the Committee expresses its concern over the inability of the Planning
Commission for paying due attention in arranging the required fund through budgetary support. Considering the need of
expediting infrastructure development in the North Eastern Region, the Committee thinks that the project should have
been given green signal, the Committee is of the considered view that North Eastern Region requires special status so
far the projects on infrastructure development is concerned because not only the economy of the common people in the
North Eastern Region but the balanced national economic growth and development also depends upon the industrial
development which is concomitant to the infrastructure development. Therefore, concerted efforts should be made on
the part of the Ministry of Railways to declare Lumding - Silchar - Jiribham gauge conversion and Kumarghat -Agartala
new line projects as National Projects.(Para 3.2)
CLOSE COORDINATION BETWEEN DHI AND RAILWAYS AT THE CENTRAL LEVEL
          The Committee understands that close co-ordination between Department of Heavy Industries and Railways at the
Central level would provide a platform to discuss the mutual problems in removing the bottlenecks in the North East
Region for the establishment and development of industrial units. Mutual discussions provides opportunities for
consensus-ad-idem for exploring the possible solutions to the problems being combated by the authorities concerned.
That is why, the Committee reiterates its recommendation that DHI and Railways should continue to have cooperative
and collaborative approach towards solving the problems on the path of industrial development of North Eastern
Region. The Committee is of the considered opinion that the Railways can play a paramount role in providing
Infrastructural base for industrial projects and units and can thus play supportive part in the lives of the masses dwelling
in the region.                                 (Para 4.5)
          The Committee acknowledges the fact that the Railways have very much appreciated and shared the concern in
respect of two units of HPC admitting that North Eastern Region deserved a special treatment for various consideration
and in this regard efforts were made in the short term, medium term and the long term to take care of the bottlenecks
and improve the condition.   Besides keeping in view employment potential and the available raw material in the region
and the National priority for balanced Regional Development, the Committee is of the view that the North East Region
should be treated as a special case and not just from the economical or feasibility point of view. Therefore, the
Committee reiterates its recommendation that the Railways has to look into three aspects; first, whether they were able
to improve their lines; whether their meter gauge or the broad gauge were sufficient; and third, the transhipment should
be minimized by various, other methods. But in taking care of all these, the basic object was to see to it that there was an
improvement in the employment opportunities.      (Para 4.6)
IMPLEMENTATION OF THE RECOMMENDATIONS MADE BY THE COMMITTEE IN ITS 58 TH
REPORT
          The Committee in its 131 st Report expressed its concern over the non-implementation of the recommendation
made by the Committee in its 58 th Report and the Committee reiterated its recommendations as made in the 58 th
Report. After taking the Action Taken Note thereto into the consideration vis-a-vis its recommendation the Committee
comes to the following conclusions:-
          (i)    The Committee considers the allotment of degraded forest land to HPC as indispensable to make HPC self
sufficient in meeting with the requirement of bamboo for two mills of HPC and to enlarge the forest resources
in general. Since the issue relates to the sustenance and revival of HPC located in Assam and the ecology and
environment in and around Assam, there should not be much hesitation and hassels on the part of the
Government of Assam and the Ministry of Environment and Forest. Besides the activity so envisaged bears

the potential of employment generation and social upliftment. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the
DHI should keep on pursuing with the Government of Assam and the Ministry of Environment and Forests
highlighting the social benefits, requirement and benefits accruing to HFC and gains in maintaining the
ecological balance in allotting the degraded forest land to HPC.
          (ii)    The Committee recommends that the DHI should take up effective follow up action with the Ministry of
Environment and Forests for getting the approval for implementing working plants for extraction of bamboos
in the North East. Similar follow up action should be under taken with the State Government and Forest
Department for construction of dirt road in unexploited forest areas.
          (iii)   The basic concern of the Committee is that HPC must be facilitated to meet its requirement of bamboo. The
Committee is pleased to note that court has given verdict in favour of HPC in one of the case relating to the
agreement with private firms regarding extraction of bamboo on a long term basis from the forest areas
reserved for HPC.
          (iv)   Relating to the issue of entering into an agreement with State Forest Department to mobilize plantation, the
Committee recommends that DHI should explore the possibility of creation of Plantation Consortium with
equal financial contribution from the State Government and HPC for plantation purpose and formation of a
Monitoring Committee having a representatives from State Forest Department and HPC to look after the
execution of plantation projects. This arrangement would ensure HPC in meeting with the requirement of
bamboo and at the same time generating additional revenue to the financial crunched State.
          (v)   The Standing Committee on Industry would like to know the outcome of the efforts made by the Committee
constituted by the Ministry of Environment and Forests, where CMD, HPC is a member, in combating the
menace of gregarious flowering to prevent the excessive rodent population.
          (vi)   As regards the utilization of inland waterways in transportation of HPC products and raw materials for
interiors, the Committee is of the view that the said means of transportation should be experimented keeping
in view the cost benefit analysis and the local climatic conditions. If the proposition is favourable and viable
the said means of transportation might be undertaken, otherwise alternative cheaper and viable mode of
transportation may be undertaken.

CAPITAL INVESTMENT PROGRAMMES IN NORTH EAST REGION
          The Committee is of the view that Industrialization is the key to economic growth and development. For the
balanced national development, the economic development of North Eastern Region needs to be pat on industrialization
track and due to this cherished goal and national priority the Government of India has the responsibility for the last mile
developments for the socio-economic infrastructure in the North Eastern Region. At the same time, the Government has
engineered its “gubernatorial will” to remove the bottlenecks being faced on the path of industrial development like
insurgency, connectivity and communication system, working system and Infrastructural development to induce
investment from private sectors. Proper and timely utilisation of the Non-Lapasable Central Pool of Resources would
ensure acceleration of industiral development. Moreover, the Department of Development of North East Region has to
facilitate the proliferation of new industrial units in the region by co-coordinating with various Departments/Ministries
of State and Central Government.(Para 6.5)
          The sustenance and survival of existing industrial units i.e. Hindustan Paper Corporation (HPC), Nagaland Pulp
and Paper Mills Limited (NPPM), Cement Corporation of India Limited (CCI) and Andrew Yule and Company Limited
(AYCL) is equally important as the revival of these units would act as a catalyst to raise the confidence level of
investors in the region besides their contribution of these industrial units in the economic development of the region due
to their employment generation potential and opportunity for poverty alleviation. The Committee reiterates its stand that
the DHI should assist these PSUs in formulting the short term and the long-term capital investment programmes and co-
ordinate with the DONER for rescuing these PSUs out of red and reassure the people of the region that PSUs still
remain the temple of modern India.(Para 6.6)
