I. Coal as a source of energy plays the paramount role in the development and growth of 
economy, therefore, Coal is metaphorically known as Black Diamond. Coal is the source of 
electricity generation in the Thermal Power Station. Moreover , o ther Industries of the economy 
like steel, cement, fertilizers, chemicals, papers and thousand of medium and Small Scale 
Industries are dependent of Coal for their process and energy requirements. 
1.1 The main subjects allocated to the Department of Coal under the Government of India 
(Allocation of Business) Rules , 1961, as amended from time to time , are development and 
exploitation of coal and lignite reserves in India; all matters relating to production, supply 
distribution and pricing of coal, development and operati on of coal washeries, administration of 
Coal Bearing Areas (Acquisition and Development) Act, 1957, administration of Public Sector 
Enterprises dealing with coal and lignite and all business incidental to the administration of 
above activities including matters concerning the State Government etc . 
1.2 '!'he Department of Coal has under its direct administration control the following two 
public sector undertakings. 
(i) Coal India Ltd.; and 
(ii) Neyveli Lign ite Corporation Ltd .

2 . The Secretary, during the course of oral evidence highlighted the performance of the 
coal sector . He submitted to the Committee that 
"The coal sector, after having witnessed recession in the recent past started showing 
positive growth in this year. Coal production and offtake have staged a recovery in the current 
year , when compared to the negative growth in 1998-99. Coal production from April to 
February in the current year is approximately 266 million tonne from April to February last 

year, the actual performance for the same period in the current year is more than 272 million 
tonne. We are confident that the current year's target of298.90 million tonne of coal production 
and offtake of about 300 mill1on tonne will be fully met. This will be a record-breaking 
achievement." 
2.1 However, the Secretary also elaborated the concern of Coal sector as under:-
" Indian coal is getting out-priced by the imported coal at certain destinations, 
particularly coastal regions in the country. Low rates of import duty, high railway 
freights, and additional burdens of royalty/taxes are the main factors contributing to the 
rise of more imports over the years. The basic import duty on non-coking coal which 
was 85 per cent in 1992-93 was reduced to 15 per cent in 1999-2000. The railway 
freight on coal has been continuously going up over the years. It has gone up by 68 per 
cent smce I 992. further, it is estimated that coal freight is also loaded by about 30 per 
cent for subsidizing the passenger traffic by the Rail"ays . For example, the coal going 
from South Eastern Coalfields or the Mahanandi Coalfields to the coastal areas in 
Gujarat and Maharashtra, has to bear a freight as high as 200 per cent to 300 per cent of 
the coal price. It is ironical that coal traffic, whit.h con ~tltutes between 45 per cent of the 
Indian Railway's total revenue is being subjected to most unreasonable hikes and 
additional burdens. We have been addressing the Ministry of Railways and the Ministry 
of Finance also to rationalize the freight structure and provide a level playing field to 
indigenous coal vis-a-vis imported coal. Unfortunateiy, without any succ~ss."

7. 7 The Committee is of the view that higher the degree of risk involved greater is the 
necessity to give adequate and sufficient impetus to the measures undertaken for safety. 
7.8 The Committee is pleased to note that an arrangement has been made under the Coal 
Mines (Conservation & Development) Act, 1974 for supporting the activities of the Coal

protection works. It also notes that coal conservation and development advisory Committee 
(CCDA) carries out periodical assessment of the actual expenditure incurred by the Coal

express its displeasure over the non-utilisation of funds earmarked for the purpose. Against 
the provision of Rs.60.00 crore in 1998-99 the actual expenditure remained confined upto 
Rs.49.45 crore. In this regard, the role played by CCDA Committee is wanted pertaining to 
periodic assessment of the actual expenditure. The Committee is of the considered view that 
CCDA should play pro-active role in monitoring the actual expenditure for conservation of 
coal including stowing and protective work. 
7.9 Moreover, the Department of Coal should take up the issue with the Ministry of 
Finance and the Planning Commission for enhancement of fund for conservation of coal and 
safety measures. 
7.10 The Committee notes that the percentage recovery by mechanised opencast mining is 
upto 80% to 90% in situ reserve of coal and application of longwall methods in underground 
Mines to achieve higher underground production with Higher percentage of recovery . In this 
regard, the Committee is of the view that efforts should be made for evolution of better 
methodology of Coal exploration to achieve the highest percentage of recovery. 
7.1 1 The Committee is constrained to express its displeasure and to note that despite the 
mechanism of Mine safety and rules and regulation Jharia Coalfield is affected by 
incidence of fire. The Committee is of the considered view that the Department of Coal 
should make an all out effort to extinguish fire in various coalfield including Raniganj and 
Jharia. 
7.12 The Com.mittee is perturbed to note that Department pf Coal has not been able to 
implement the recommendation of High Level Committee as the problem of subsidence & 
fire in Raniganj, Jharia Coalfield persists. As is reflected in the Annual Report 1999-2000, 
enactment of suitable legislation banning construction over unsafe areas is still awaited from 
Government of Bihar. Moreover, Constitution of Sub-authority for . rehabilitation of non­
BCCL people from endangered areas is also awaited with the Government of Bihar. The 
Committee is of the strong opinion that the Department of Coal should prevail upon the 
Government of Bihar for enactment of suitable legislation and constitution of Sub-authority 
for rehabilitation of non-BCCL people for endangered areas. 
7.13 The Committee further hopes that the Department of Coal is making all out effort to 
protect the interest of inhabitants over the surface area of Jharia and Raniganj coalfields 
and due compensation is given to those whose dwellings are endangered due to fire incidents.

1 5. COMPENSATION FOR LAND ACQUISITION 
15.1 The Committee wanted to know the basic policy behind fixing of rate of compensation 
for land acquisition for exploration and Detailed Drilling. The Department of Coal informed that 
under Section 4 (1) of the Coal Bearing Areas Acquisition and Development Act, a notification 
is issued whenever it appears to the Central Government that coal is likely to be obtained from 
the land. Land is acquired only when it is established through the prospecting undertake'n in 
terms of notification u/s 4 ( 1) of the Act that coal is obtainable from the land and the coal 
companies find it commercially viable to exploit the coal deposits found therein. Section 13 of 
the Act lays down the principles and procedures for determination of compensation for 
acquisition of prospecting licenses, rights under mining leases , land, standing crops, trees etc. 
Section 13 (5) provides that compensation for land shall be determined after taking into 
consideration the market value of the land on the date of publication of the notification under 
Section 4 ( 1) of the Act. Fair market value · is assessed on the basis of the land transactions 
recorded in the office of local Registrar of the concerned area during the preceding three years. 

On the market value so assessed, solatium @ 30% and additional compensation @ 12% (for a 
maximum period of three years) is also payable. In case of delay in payment of compensation 
interest @ 9% for the first year and 15% for the subsequent years is paid the land losers to 
compensate for the delay. 
15.2 The Committee observes that regulatory provision for compensation for land acquisition 
for regional exploration and detailed drilling. Section 13(5) of th.e Coal Bearing Areas 
Acquisition and Development Act lapse down the principles and procedure for determination 
of compensation. The Committee is of the view that while making assessment of fair market 
value the Department of coal should take care the interest of persons whose land is being 
acquired and efforts should be taken for timely disposal of cases of compensation as this 
measures if implemented would give timely solve to the land owner as welt as save considerable

17. PRIVATE INVESTMENT IN COAL MINING INDUSTRY 
17:·1 During the course of oral-evidence the Committee wanted to know the policy framework 
for inducing private investment in Coal Mining Industry and the programme undertaken in this 
regard. The Department of Coal informed the Committee that suitable amendment has been 
effected in the coal Mines (Nationalisation) Act, 1973 to allow coal mining for captive 
consumption in power generation and such other end uses to be notified by the Central 
Government from time to time in addition to the existing provision for coal mining for captive 
consumption in iron and steel production. In exercise of the powers conferred on the Central 
Government under the June I 993 amendment, coal mining for captive consumption for cement 
production was allowed by a Gazette Notification issued on 15.3.96. These provision in the Coal 
Mines (Nationali sation) Act, 1973 as well as in the Gazette Notification apply to the public sector 
as well as private sector. Section 3 (3) (a) (iii) of the Coal Mines (Nationalisation) Act, !973 as 
amended in June, 1993 provides that no company other than a company engaged in power 
generation, etc. can do coal mining. As such, the literal meaning of this provision conveys that the 
coal mining company and the end-user company has to be the same entity. 
17.2 However, in September, 1995 the then Ministry of Coal obtained the advice of the then 
Attorney General of India that coal mining by associated coal companies can be allowed provided 
the end-user company (e.g: power generating company. iron and steel producing company, etc.) 
holds at least 25% of voting equity in the coal mining company, the same principle was also made 
applicable in a situation when a holding company was formed that two subsidiaries viz. (i) the 
end-user company and (ii) the associated coal company. In the later situation, it was stipulated 
that the holding company should have at least 25% equity share at all times in both the end user 
company and the associated coal company. 

17.3 The Committee was given to understand that a Screening Committee has been 
constituted in Department of Coal to consider the proposals by various parties for allocation of 
captive coal mining blocks. 24 coal blocks have so far been offered to various parties in the 
private and public sector. Out of these 24 blocks, previous approval of the Central Government 
has been accorded in respect of 6 blocks under the proviso to Section 5 (I) of the Mines & 
Minerals (Regulation & Development) Act, 1957, for grant of mining lease by the respective 
State Governments. The communications to the State Governments to convey the previous 
approval has been issued after the mining plans in respect of these blocks were approved by the 
Department of Coal. Two captive mining companies in the private secto r viz. (i) Bengal Emta 
coal Mining Limited and (ii) Jindal Steel and Power Limited have already started coal mining 
from their respective captive blocks. 
17.4 The Department of Coal informed the Committee that ratio of government and private 
participation in coal mining in the country is therefore 97.59% and 2.41% respectively as on 
31.3.99.
