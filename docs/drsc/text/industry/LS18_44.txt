                  The Workers sought financial assistance from the Government for restructuring one of its units..
 
2.
                  They also sought exemption from the  earnest money deposits in Government tenders for the unit.
 
3.                  Workers sought parity of their Dearness Allowance (DA) and long pending wage revision.

OBSERVATIONS / RECOMMENDATIONS
 
 
            As is evident from the subject of study assigned to the Sub-Committee, it was required to review the
performance of Central Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) under the administrative domain of the Department of
Heavy Industry, Ministry of Heavy Industries  and Public Enterprises.  Accordingly,  the Sub-Committee embarked
upon the most talked issue, namely, the performance of the Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)  in the changed scenario
of the Indian economy.  The Sub-Committee, while having interaction with the Workers and Management of select 6
subsidiary companies  of the Holding Company, i.e.,  Bharat Yantra Nigam Ltd. (BYNL), had an opportunity to analyse
the factors responsible for the present state of decline of some of these PSUs.  These PSUs, for long, had lived under a
protective regime and the opening up of the economy had meant opening of floodgates of unforeseen  problems for
some of them.  Transmission from a closed economy to a free economy has caught them and exposed their
vulnerability.  There is a host of reasons which culminated into present crisis.  State protection can no longer be there to
shield them against the onslaught unleashed by  their rivals in the present competitive and consumer-oriented market.  
It is apparent that the present crisis in some of these PSUs  is a simple case of gross managerial inefficiency and sheer
lack of  foresightedness on the part of those who have been at the helm of affairs. 
 
The first major problem, which warrants the attention of this Sub-Committee, is the delay in approval of the
restructuring of the subsidiary companies of BYNL .  It is felt that Department of Industry, Ministry of Heavy
Industries  and Public Enterprises seems to have a very indifferent approach towards the preparation, approval and
implementation of the restructuring packages for these subsidiary companies.
 
The Sub-Committee feels that any delay on the whole process involving the restructuring of a Company smacks
of lack of commitment  on the part of the Government.  Therefore, the Sub-Committee recommends that the Department
of Heavy Industry , Ministry of Heavy Industries  and Public Enterprises, should make conscious  efforts to help these
subsidiary companies by approval , timely preparation, and implementation of their restructuring- packages.

            The issue of  timely payment of arrears and strategy to reduce  the employees of these subsidiary companies
merit the Sub-Committee’s serious consideration as it has a direct bearing on the well-being of thousands of employees.
For years together, there has been no wage revision in some of these  PSUs as a result of which the moral of the
employees has touched its  nadir.  Some of these PSUs have  also defaulted in the payment of  statutory dues  like
gratuity, PF, etc. of the employees because of shortage of funds.  The argument advanced by the Government  for non-
payment of wage etc. was that the responsibility of payment to employees lies with the  companies  themselves and the
issue is being examined by a Group of Ministers. 
 
            The whole issue is a sensitive one and it affects the employees in various ways.  The Sub-Committee, therefore,
recommends that arrears and statutory  dues  should be paid  to the employees forthwith by earmarking separate funds
for this purpose .  The Ministry merely becoming a helpless spectator  in the matter  by saying that the matter is being
examined by a Group of Ministers does not end the responsibility of the Administrative Ministry. The Ministry  should
own the responsibility in this regard and take up  the matter  seriously with the Government at appropriate forum for the
release of  funds for the payments of wage arrears, statutory dues, etc. by arranging some separate fund for the purpose,
if necessary,  in conjunction with other Ministries  whose  PSUs are also not able to pay  salaries to their employees.
During the visit of the Committee to various subsidiaries companies it was found that there was not enough work
order with these subsidiaries as a result of  which  the entire  activities of  these subsidiary companies may come to a
grinding halt at any moment.
 
            The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry, Ministry of Heavy Industries
and Public Enterprises, should   take up the matter with the Union Cabinet and necessary instructions should be  issued
for those customer-PSUs and Departments for placing adequate orders on these  subsidiaries of  BYNL.
 
 Many subsidiaries are having excess manpower that has proved a handicap to function in the post liberalization
era . Although planned rationalization of manpower by offering attractive packages   could solve the problem,  the flow
of funds for the implementation of  Voluntary Retirement Schemes  (VRS)  still remains far below the required mark. 
The Sub-Committee, therefore, recommends that  wherever the  planed VRS can facilitate a firm ground , the
Government should make requisite financial support available to that particular  PSU  without delay.
 
 During the interaction with the Officials of the customer PSUs/ concerned Departments of these  PSUs the Sub-
Committee was informed that the delivery record of these PSUs is very poor. The Sub-Committee, therefore, 
recommends the need for timely execution of orders / projects in order to avoid subsequent delay in completion of the
projects of the customer PSUs / Departments as it ,in turn, helps them to avoid cost and time over-runs.  The Sub-
Committee, therefore, recommends that all PSUs should evolve a proper workable mechanism to meet the deadline for
supply of orders.  The Sub-Committee further recommends that in case the delivery is before the deadline, there should
be some provision for rewarding the supplier PSU in some form.
 
Through the interaction with various PSUs it also came to the notice of the Sub-Committee that fair amount of
money of various PSUs is locked-up with the State Government Agencies like State Electricity Board, Power houses,
State Irrigation Department, State PSUs, etc.  As a result the Company is not able to make payments to their suppliers
and it is also not in a position to pay  monthly wages to their employees.  The Sub-Committee is perplexed to see such a
helpless situation.  The Sub-Committee , therefore, recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry should take up
the matter with the State Governments for release of such dues .Otherwise, in the alternative , they should take up the
matter with the Planning Commission and inform them the details of dues yet to be released by the State Government
agencies and to impress upon the Planning Commission to consider this aspect before  allocation of funds to the State
Governments.       It has also come to the notice of the Sub-Committee that earlier these PSUs were getting orders from
the State Government Agencies but now the State Governments have started  placing their orders with their own PSUs
or any other private agencies for its requirements. The Sub-Committee feels that like the Central PSUs which are giving
some purchase preference to the Central PSUs, the State Government may also be requested to give some purchase
preference to the Central PSUs and the Central PSUs may also give  some purchase  preference to the State PSUs.  The
placement of work-orders   by State PSUs  to Central PSUs  and vice-versa will take Government PSUs  a long way in 
post  liberalization period  which will  usher in a  new chapter in the   relationship between the Central and State
Governments.

BRIDGE AND ROOF COMPANY(INDIA) LTD.
 
1.         The capital-restructuring proposal of Bridge and Roof Company  has been pending since 1991. The Capital
restructuring proposals of Rs.42.57 crores  has already been submitted to the Government.  However,  proposal  has not
been approved   by the Government   till date.  This inexplicable delay in the approval of the proposal is coming in the
way of the companies’ efforts to borrow from the open market to meet the total capital requirement. 
The Sub-Committee has taken a serious note of this undue delay and recommend that capital restructuring of the
Company should be approved by the Government at the earliest.
  2.       Bridge and Roof Company  was  awarded  a contract for `Design, engineering, supply, installation and
commissioning of storage facility at four locations,viz., Thankot, Amlekhgunj, Kathmandu and Pokhara in Nepal, by
Nepal Oil Corporation Limited (A Nepal Government Undertaking) valued at Rs. 17.77  cores in March, 1994. Out of
four sites works at three sites have been completed and Fuel has been stored in all the Tanks.  The work was
accomplished despite a lot of constraints and delay faced by Bridge and Roof Company  due to reasons beyond the
Company’s control . Fourth terminal at Pokhara has been abandoned/foreclosed by NOC on their  own due to local
villagers’ problem/land  dispute.
 
            Since commissioning of the storage facilities built by B&R NOC has been using  three  terminals for storage of
Fuel and other purposes, formally taken over the sites and issuing  substantial completion certificate as  per the  contract
condition.  They have also not  taken-over the fourth terminal at Pokhara though they have foreclosed the same. NOC
are not paying dues of approximately Rs.12.90 crores comprising schedule bills, extra work bills, escalation bills and
claim.
 
            Due to above situation, B&R’s Bank-Guarantees towards (a) Mobilisation Advance ((Rs.2.69 crores) and (b)
Performance Guarantee (Rs.3.54 crores) are blocked. As a result this, Bank-Guarantees have to  be extended a number
of times draining Rs.30 lakhs annually due to bank charges only. Consequently, Bridge and Roof Company  has to
perforce maintain the establishment at Nepal since they can not abandon the project unless the terminals are formally
taken over by NOC.  This involves  recurring expenditure around Rs.6 lakhs per month.  If Bridge and Roof Company 
do not continue to extend the   Bank-Guarantees  as demanded by NOC or decide to leave the project now, NOC is
likely  to encash  Bank-Guarantees and foreclosed  outstanding- payments.
The Sub-Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Heavy Industry should used the Good Offices
of the Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of External Affairs to help release of payments by Nepal Oil Corporation to
Bridge & Roof Company.

2.         The Convenor welcomed the Members of Sub-Committee-III and Secretary and Senior Officials of  the
Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises, Railway Board, Ministry of Railways and other organisations. At
the outset, the  Convenor briefed the officials about the  pertinent issues that could be sorted out within the concerned
Ministry itself and problems being faced by various PSUs to help strengthen their working of the PSUs. The Convenor 

44th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/44threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:12:21]
stated that there is a need for cooperation amongst various PSUs keeping in view the changed environment in the post 
liberalised and competitive era. Thereafter, he requested the Secretary, Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public
Enterprises to present his view point on the problems being faced by BYNL group of companies.
 
3.         Shri Vijay Raghvan,  Secretary,  Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises first of all thanked the
Ministry of Railways for supporting the BYNL group of companies. He said that the BYNL group of companies,
however, are facing some problems.  He informed that the Byculla Unit of Richardson and Cruddas is heavily
dependent on the Railways for the supply of points and crossings but the Railways have stopped giving them orders
and consequently the unit is lying idle   for last 9 to 10 months. Second problem relates to the railway siding at the
Byculla Iron Works of Richardson and Cruddas Ltd. pertaining to the rental charges. He requested the Ministry of
Railways for the restoration of development orders from Railways for the Manufacture of Wagons and Roof Mounted
Package Units for AC Coaches. Shri Raghvan revealed that in the past the Railways floated a number of tenders to the 
BYNL group of companies, but unfortunately, the tenders were either held up or cancelled. When these companies re-
tendered, the situation changed drastically in terms of amount.  He requested the Sub-Committee to intervene in respect
of these issues which need to be corrected.
 
4          Thereafter, the Convenor requested the Members, Railway Board, to offer their comments on the contentious
issues. Shri R.N. Malhotra, Member (Engineering) Railway Board,
presented his view point that in respect of tender submitted by R&C there were a lot of discrepancies. In their offer,
R&C had put certain conditions which were against  the conditions put by the Railways. Regarding purchase
preference, he stated that as per the Central Government instruction the Railways have been giving purchase preference
to the Central PSUs. But if there is difference of more than 10% of the tender rate, the tender is offered to  the other
party in the open market. 
 
5          Shri K.B. Sankaran, Member (Mechanical) Railway Board, submitted  that in so far as the Railways are
concerned, Railway Design Standards Organisation  (RDSO) inspects all equipments and spares (manufactured goods)
supplied by the outside agencies.  For the purpose of  inter-PSU cooperation the Railways cannot compromise  with
quality due to safety measures. The tenders have to meet certain technical specification. If they do not meet those
specifications the tenders are rejected out rightly.  He informed that in the past Indian Railways had placed
development orders to the BYNL Group of companies.   Shri Malhotra said that though approval from the RDSO is a
very cumbersome process which, in certain cases, takes months, they  invite the CMD of BYNL to come to their 
Office and sort  out  the issues whenever such discrepancies arise. On the issue of the problem relating to the rental
charges at the Byculla Iron Works of Richardson and Cruddas Ltd.,  Shri Malhotra stated that  the Ministry of
Railways has laid down the procedure for evaluation of land. As per the rules re-evaluation of the lands is done after
every five years. The Railways have been charging only 6% of the cost of the land as the license fee from the parties.
The land under reference has a commercial value. Therefore, the dues are charged from the Richardson & Cruddas.
 
6          When Shri Khan Ghufran Zahidi, M.P.,  expressed the apprehension  that  due to lack of orders from the
Railways the PSUs  may  face closure like situation, Shri R.N. Malhotra observed that  if the policy  decision to be
taken    that such  a preference  is to be given to the Public sector the Government  has to  take a decision and  specify 
that for the  development of PSUs  certain preferences are to be given.  The Secretary, Ministry of  Heavy Industries
and Public Enterprises informed that a cabinet  note can be prepared by the Department in this regard.      
          
7          Thereafter, the Convenor suggested the CMDs/Officials of PSUs present in the meeting that there is a lack of
coordination and cooperation amongst the central PSUs.  In the post-liberalisation era, cooperation and coordination
amongst the PSUs are a must for the survival of a PSU. He further stated that the Central PSUs should explore the
possibility of a permanent mechanism for cooperation in respect of work orders.
