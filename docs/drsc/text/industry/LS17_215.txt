REPORT 
 
1. The Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public 
Enterprises (Department of Heavy Industry) 2010-11 as presented are as under: 
 
 Demand No. 49, Department of Heavy Industry 
 Total Demands Rs.881.71 crore (Voted) 
 
2. Considering the documents and other material made available to it and the oral 
evidence tendered before it by the Secretary of the Dep artment of Heavy Industry and 
CMDs of various PSUs, the Committee examined in detail a number of issues, the 
conclusions reached are as under: 
 
Industrial Growth:   
 
3. Automobile Sector and Capital Goods Sector are the two major sectors which are 
being loo ked after by Department of Heavy Industry apart from 32 operating public 
sector undertakings.  The global recession actually affected our economy in September 
2008 onwards where the exports started declining substantially as well as growth of GDP 
also came down.  Considering the importance of these sectors, the Union Government 
announced three stimulus packages to protect the Industry.  The three stimulus packages 
were in the form of reducing the excise duty rate by 8%, fiscal concessions by reducing 
CRR, Repo rate etc. and reduction of cenvat for products falling in the 10% excise duty 
rate by 2%.  In addition to these stimulus packages, State Governments were provided 
assistance under Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission for purchase of 
buses for their urban transport system to protect the automobile industry from the global 
recession. 
 
4. The Secretary, DHI informed the Committee that in January 2010, compared 
to the corresponding period of last year the growth rate of industry has been 16.7%.  The  
manufacturing sector has grown up to 17.9%.  The Capital Goods Sector has shown 
tremendous growth rate of 56.2% as against 15.9% in last January and almost all the 
sectors of the industry have been doing well and have come out of the recession.  All the 
components of industrial production index have grown up to double digits.  


 
 
5. The Committee is of the firm opinion that despite signs of a recovery of 
industrial growth during the period from October 2009 onwards, the performance 
of the industrial sector is cause of concern as the growth is directly proportional to 
stimulus packages announced by the Government.  The Committee also expresses its 
concern on the sustainable growth of automobile sector in view of this years 
budgetary proposals and hike in the excise duty of petroleum products.  The 
Committee feels that the need of the hour is not merely in keeping on the stimulus 
packages but also to restructure the bases of taxes and import and export duties in 
such a manner that without affecting public expen diture, the industrial growth can 
sustain.  The Committee desires that this issue may be taken up with the Ministry of 
Finance and pursued seriously.  
 
Impact of Budget proposals on Auto sector   
 
6. The Committee was informed that due to stimulus packages a nnounced by the 
Government, the auto sector has shown tremendous growth.  During the festival season, 
all the manufacturers  came out with new models of passenger cars and country  is going 
to be the hub of global small compact cars and all the car compani es will satisfy the 
Euro-IV norms as on April 2010.  The Society of Indian Automobile Manufacturers 
(SIAM) informed the Committee that passenger vehicles segment during April 2009 to  
February 2010 grew at 26.13% over same period last year and the overall commercial 
vehicles segment registered positive growth at 35.52% during April 2009 to  February 
2010 as compared to the same period last year.  The Committee was informed by the 
Department that the procurement of buses by State Transport Undertakings throu gh 
funding under the JNNURM Scheme was a direct demand generation stimulus measure 
for the commercial segment.  The nodal Ministry for the implementation of procurement 
of buses under this scheme is Ministry of Urban Development.  The total number of 
buses envisaged to be procured under the scheme, as a one time special measure is about 
15000, against which orders for about 13600 buses have already been placed.  The 


Committee was further informed that this scheme was initially valid till 30 June 2009 and 
has been extended upto 31 March 2010.   
 
7. The Committee appreciates that such an initiative of the Department of 
Heavy Industry with the Ministry of Urban Development will not only boost the 
demand in the Auto sector but will also develop the public transp ort in the remote 
areas of the country.  The Committee, therefore, desires that the scheme of funding 
the purchase of buses for public transport by the States under JNNURM may 
continue for some more time in public interest.  The Committee at the same time,  
stresses upon the Department of Heavy Industry to monitor the progress of this 
scheme with the Ministry of Urban Development and concerned State Governments.  
 
8. The Committee was informed that around 70% of utility vehicles are sold in rural 
areas. These vehicles are the optimum form of mass transportation and also create jobs 
and entrepreneurial opportunities to people, especially in rural and semi urban areas.  The 
Automotive Mission Plan 2006 -2010 speaks for fiscal incentives for certain class of 
vehicles which included cars, utility vehicles etc.  The current year Budget proposal 
imposes a very high rate of excise duty at 22% plus additional levy of Rs.15000/- on such 
utility vehicles.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of 
Heavy Industry should take up the issue with the Ministry of Finance to ensure 
concessional excise duty structure for utility vehicles.    
 
9.  The Committee was informed that to encourage usage of environment friendly 
vehicles in the country, the price of such v ehicles has to come down significantly.  To 
achieve this, in the overall interest of the environment and the society, support from the 
Government by way of reduced taxes/duties and other alternative is required.  The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that i n order to promote use of Hydrogen/Fuel 
cell/Hybrid vehicles, CNG vehicles and other alternative fuel vehicles, in the interest 
of protecting environment, such vehicles and its parts/components thereof may be 
exempted from excise duty at par with electric vehicles.   
 
10. Regarding the development of auto industry, Committee was informed by the 
representatives of Auto Component Manufacturers Association that Automotive Vision 


Plan 2016 released by the Prime Minister of India states that "by 2016, India will  emerge 
as the destination of choice in the world for the design and manufacturers of automobiles 
and automotive components with output reaching a level of US $ 145 billion accounting 
for more than 10% of GDP and providing additional employment to 25 milli on people by 
2016".  The plan envisions that the auto component industry will invest US $ 1.5 billion 
on an annual basis for the next 10 years, leading to an incremental investment of US $ 15 
billion by 2016.  The Committee feels that this policy will boos t the auto sector 
during the coming years.  The Committee desires that Government should create a 
National Technology Development Fund for the development of auto and auto 
component industry.  This fund will not only provide impetus for modernisation of 
auto sector but will also create huge employment opportunities in the country.  
Further, this fund would provide financial support to companies by financing 50% 
of the project cost by way of a loan with minimum interest of 4 to 6%.  The 
Committee feels that such a fund will truly support auto companies to modernise 
and upgrade their technology in the globally competitive environment.  
11. The Committee was given to understand by the Automotive Components 
Manufacturers Association of India (ACMA) that the Supre me Court of India has made a 
judgment in CCE vs SKF India Ltd. 2009 that the interest is payable on payment of 
differential duty payable because of revision of price subsequent to removal of the goods.  
The Committee was informed by the Association in this  regard that the prices are usually 
fixed provisionally in the auto component industry and subsequently final prices are 
mutually decided after negotiations and supplementary invoices are raised after deciding 
the final price of the component so demand of interest is not justified.  It is due to the 
reason that at the time of removal there was no short payment or non -payment and 
differential duty was not determined by the Central Excise Department but the same was 
paid by the component manufacturers at thei r own.  Section 11A (2) of Central Excise 
Act, 1944 apply only in a situation where the concerned authority firstly determined the 
excise duty payable by an assesses and after such determination only, the assesses pays 
the differential duty.    The Committ ee finds that such practice is common in the Auto 
Component Industry and if there is a levy of interest on duty payable on the 
supplementary invoices raised due to revision of price, it would not only affect the 
manufacturers but will also increase the cos t of auto component.  The Committee,  


therefore, desires that Department of Heavy Industry should propose an 
amendment with Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) in Section 11 AB of 
the Central Excise Act, 1944 so that such anomalies may be sorted ou t. 
 
12. The Committee finds that there is no proposal in the Budget for upgradation of 
Auto Expo Centres in the country like Delhi; Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, U.P. etc.  
The Committee finds that most of the Auto Expos are generally held at Pragati Maidan  in 
Delhi which is already occupied with lots of promotional activities by Trade/Business 
Associations and it also lacks latest standard infrastructure facilities.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that Department of Heavy Industry should create a sepa rate 
fund for the upgradation of Auto Expo Centres like Pragati Maidan, Delhi etc. in 
consultation with ITPO and Ministry of Commerce & Industry.  The Committee 
further suggests the Department of Heavy Industry to also arrange such Auto 
Exposes in other su itable places in the country such as Greater Noida (U.P), 
Hyderabad (A.P.) and some other cities of the country where required 
infrastructure and facilities are available.  
 
NATRIP  
13. The Committee was informed that t he National Automotive Testing R & D 
Infrastructure Project (NATRIP), approved by the Government in 2005, envisages setting 
up of world-class, state of the art, automotive testing and homologation facilities at seven 
different locations in  the country with a total investment of Rs.1,718 cror e.  The detailed 
break up of Rs. 1718 crore for setting up of these state of the art facilities are as under:-

    & Loan Rs. 273 crore

Total:-     Rs. 1718 crore 
 
This project envisages setting up of world -class automotive testing and homologation 
facilities in country at different locations as under:-  
 
A full -fledged testing and homologation centre with the northern hub of 
automotive industry at Manesar in the state of Haryana; 
 


A full -fledged testing and homologation centre within the southern hub of 
automotive industry at Orgadam, near Chennai, the state Capital of Tamil 
Nadu; 
 
Comprehensive upgrdation of existing testing a nd homologation facilities in 
the western hub at Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI), Pune 
and at Vehicle Research and Development Establishment (VRDE), at

A world-class Proving Ground on more than 4,000 acres of land at Pithampur, 
near Indore in the state of Madhya Pradesh; 
 
A Centre for testing of Tractors and Off-Road Vehicles in the Northern region 
of the country, with national facility for accident data analysis and specialized 
driving training at R ae Bareilly, about 60 miles from Lucknow the state 
capital of Uttar Pradesh; and   
 
A Specialized Hill Area Driving Training Centre and an In -Use Vehicle 
Management Centre in the North Eastern region at Silchar in the state of 
Assam.

Scooters India Limited  
 
18. The Committee was apprised of the pathetic condition of Scooters India Ltd. 
which was the pride of the country in the area of manufacturing of two and three 
wheelers. The Scooters India Limited was incorporated in the year 1972 and was 
inaugurated by the Late Prime Minister, Smt. Indira Gandhi in April, 1973 at Lucknow.  
The production had started from 1975 by the company by making two wheelers at the 
initial stages with the brand name Vijai Super.  It was referred to BIFR in 1992 and BIFR 
sanctioned revival package in 1996.  The two wheelers production was stopped in 1997 
and company was manufacturing three wheeler in place of that.  The Company is 
presently facing a tough competition from companies like Piaggio and Mahindra and 
Mahindra.     The Committee  was informed that the reason behind the sickness of this 
unit is that SIL have set up the plant with obsolete second hand machinery which is 15 to 
20 years old so the scooters made by it face a lot of breakdown problems and Department 
of Heavy Industry has put it under sick unit.  The Committee is surprised to know that 
despite of its serious concern for the revival of Scooters India Ltd., the Department 
has made the provision of Budgetary support to this company to the tune of only 
Rs.4 crore which Commit tee feels is very meagre amount for its revival.  Committee,  
therefore, recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry should make 
adequate Budgetary support for this company at the time of RE stage and at the 
same time, should seriously pursue its 70 c rore rupee project proposal for revival 
through joint venture.  In fact, this important PSE has to diversify its activities 
because it is manufacturing three wheelers which are being phased out in various 
parts of the Country. Mini four wheelers for transp orting goods have fairly good 
market and with the help of Government this PSE may examine the diversification 
issue in right earnest.  
 


Capital Goods Sector  
 
19. Capital Goods Sector is the mother of all manufacturing Industry and is of 
strategic importance  to the national security and economic independence.  The 
Committee was informed that the Capital Goods Sector was on an upswing since March 
2002, due to investments having taken place in the infrastructure, oil & gas sector, power 
sector, steel plants, au tomobile industries etc.  The Capital Goods industry has evolved 
over these years in terms of competitiveness by consolidating.  Hence the number of 
players is few.  Due to its strategic importance for the country, it is essential to encourage 
manufacturing of capital goods rather than import and enhance the value addition and 
technology transfer. The XIth plan period foresees a 9% GDP growth, which would mean 
that the manufacturing industry should grow at least @12% per annum and the capital 
goods sector w hich is at the core of the manufacturing sector should grow at the rate of 
around 18%.   
20. The Committee notes that like for textile industry, the textile machinery forms the 
Capital Goods Sector and for the engineering industry, the machinery forms the capital 
goods sector so unless the capital goods sector develops, the manufacturing sector can not 
develop.  The Committee has been informed by Secretary DHI and other representatives 
during the course of discussion that in the textile machinery sector, th e industry is forced 
to import the automatic looms from China, which are second hand looms.  These looms 
are being dumped from China in India because our looms production has not come up 
with the latest technology.  Although Ministry are putting some restr ictions saying that 
the looms which are being imported should have a useful life left of 10 years, but with 
this certification, it is very difficult to assess the condition of the machinery.  The same 
thing is happening in the machine tools sector of the c ountry where second hand machine 
tools are being imported from Germany and Japan. The Committee feels that such 
imported machines/plants although imported at the lower cost but importers have 
to pay high prices for their maintenance contracts and spares.  The Committee notes 
that the DHI has raised such issue in the development council meeting.   The 
Committee recommends that suitable remedial measures be taken by imposing high 
import duty   and anti dumping duty on second hand machinery in consultation 
with Ministry of Commerce to grow our capital goods Industry.  The Committee 


impresses upon the Government to allocate sufficient fund for the engineering 
industry and R & D activities to sustain the Industrial growth in coming years.  
 
21. The Committee finds that one of the important constraints to the growth of Indian 
Capital Goods Sector is lack of good infrastructure.  Most of the industries especially in 
machine tools, textile machinery and Heavy Electrical equipment segment, are 
fragmented in nature. Ve ndors to these segments have issues related to timely delivery, 
standardization of process, cost, quality, instrument etc. which in turn affects the growth 
of the overall sector.  In order to overcome such  shortcomings, sector specific cluster or 
parks could be set up at suitable locations.   The Committee feels that the Department 
of Heavy Industry needs to provide a road map to establish these clusters and 
provide adequate funds to create facilities for these clusters.  
 
22. The Capital Goods Sector in gen eral and especially the segments like machine 
tools, textiles machinery, heavy electrical equipment etc do not have major R&D, testing 
and other common infrastructure facilities.  Lack of such facilities has been adversely 
affecting the competitiveness of the industry.   The Committee feels that the DHI 
should facilitate such common facility centers in Public Private Participation (PPP) 
mode.  Some of the Common facilities that can be provided are: -  
Common Foundry (for Machine Tool Industry);  
Common Heat T reatment facility (for Machine Tool Industry);  
Accredited Testing Laboratories;  
Design Centre; and  
Centers of Excellence  
 The Committee suggests that PPP based approach could be adopted for this 
purpose, under which group of entrepreneurs or industry asso ciations would be 
encouraged. 
 
23. The Department of Heavy Industries should also assess the logistics requirements 
of the Capital Goods Sector in areas like railways, roads, bridges, etc and ensure that 
infrastructure is developed to meet the needs of tra nsporting heavy individual 
consignments (upto approximately 500 tons) from manufacturing plant/part to the remote 


locations in the country (as in the case of Hindustan Paper Corporation at Cachar Paper 
Mill in North Eastern region) where power stations wou ld be situated.  The Committee 
suggests that DHI should initiate action in this regard in coordination with Ministry 
of Railways, Road Transport & Highways and Ministry of Power etc.  
  
Paper Industry      
24. The Committee was informed that the paper indus try in the country is facing 
problems mainly   because import of newsprint has been permitted without levy of any 
duty as a result imported newsprint is considerably cheaper in the domestic market than 
indigenous newsprint.  Further, the recession in the p aper market has resulted in decrease 
in sale price of paper.  The Committee was further informed that the combined 
performance of Hindustan Paper Corporation Ltd (HPC) and Hindustan Newsprint Ltd. 
(HNL) during the last five years is gradually decreasing due to increase in specific cost of 
productions with adverse inbound logistic, scanty supply of bamboo at Cachar Paper Mill 
and non availability of railway rakes on the MG section linking Cachar Paper Mill to BG 
section.   The Committee also finds that there  is only a token provision of Rs. 0.02 crore. 
towards budgetary support in respect of HPC during BE 2010 -2011 and Rs. 364.31 crore  
is made through I.E.B.R.  The Committee wonders how a huge amount of Rs. 364. 31. 
crore would be generated through I.E.B.R.   The Secretary, DHI informed the 
Committee that implementation of project at Jagdishpur paper mill is yet to be started 
because of non availability of approx 500 acre of land and out of which 250 area is 
required for the plant and rest for the township.  The Committee is of the view that the 
issue regarding   import duty should be discussed in details with the Ministry of 
Commerce and Industry, Ministry of Finance and Information and Broadcasting 
before a decision is taken.  The Committee at the same time r ecommends that some 
measures need to be taken to protect the paper industry in the country.  The 
Committee also recommends that the issue regarding supply of Railway rakes on 
the M.G. Section linking Cachar Paper Mills to B.G. Section may be taken up with 
the Ministry of Railways so that speedy completion of conversion of meter gauge  
can be made to help the paper industry in the North -Eastern region.  
 
HMT  


 25. Hindustan Machine Tools is one of the major industry in the country. There are 
three major manufacturing activities covered by HMT (i) watches (ii) machine tools and 
(iii) tractors.  The Committee was informed that there is already a rehabilitation plan for 
HMT holding company and this rehabilitation package is at the advance stage of 
approval.  The  Committee was also informed that HMT machine tools division is doing 
very badly along with its watches division. The poor performance of these units is 
because the HMT have not upgraded the technology for satisfying the emission norms 
and the skilled manp ower have left the company.  HMT bearing is also not doing well 
and HMT International is marginally making profits.  The Committee expresses its 
serious concern over the state of affairs of HMT Ltd. Despite Governments grant of 
two revival packages in the recent past and part of the revival package is still under 
implementation. Despite these revival packages there is no turn around of the units. 
Department of Heavy Industry is contemplating for a Joint Venture and the 
Committee is of the opinion that serio us attempts should be made for the revival of 
HMT Ltd. through the Joint Venture route as a last resort otherwise how long the 
public money can be pumped and drained in keeping the PSE alive. In that 
eventuality some other options including closure have to  be given a serious thought.   
The Committee, therefore, is of the view that necessary budgetary support should be 
given for the upgradation of technology to HMT Ltd. during supplementary 
demands 2010 -2011.  The Government should at the same time also impre ss upon 
bulk buyers in the Government Sector, such as Railways, Indian Airlines, 
Commonwealth Games Organization Committee etc.  to opt, wherever  feasible, for 
HMT products so that this once much achieved and prestigious unit would come out 
of the red.  
  
BHEL 
 
26. The Committee finds that for the BE 2010 -2011 no budgetary support has been 
made for BHEL. However, there is decrease in IEBR in respect of this company for BE 
2010-11. The Committee was informed that BHEL is doing well and it had a turnover of 
Rs. 28,033 crore last year which is an all time high turnover of BHEL. It is trying to re -
enter in to the wind energy sector. BHEL has decided to expand its capacity up to 20,000 


MW by 2011. Bharat Heavy Plate and Vessels (BHPV) has been taken over by the 
company and BHEL are outsourcing a number of things from Richardosn & Crudddas. 
The Committee has made its serious concern on its long waiting list for 
establishment of power plants in the country. Committee finds that it has a pending 
list for customers w ho are waiting for 2 -3 years.  During the discussion with 
representatives of BHEL, Committee has come to know that the main reason for such a 
long waiting list is due to its dependence on outside agencies for material inputs for 
castings, forgings, CRGO Ste el, other material etc. On the other hand BHEL is facing a 
problem of non-availability of skilled manpower like qualified boilers operators, welders, 
electricians, fitters at its various sites and problem with respect to standard turbines and 
boilers. The company is also facing a tough competition from L & T, MHI, Bharat Forge 
– Alstom, BGR and Geo Engineering. The Committee is of the view that fierce 
competition to BHEL from these companies is healthy kind of thing which improves 
the performance of BHEL da y by day. The Committee at the same time feels that 
Capital Goods Industry faces dearth of skilled and trained manpower both at shop 
floor level, as well as managerial level. With growing demand and growth of Capital 
Goods Industry it needs to be provided with adequate skilled manpower. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that DHI should consider providing the 
Industry with assistance for development of skills to employees of PSUs through 
approved training institutes.  For this purpose exclusive Industrial  Training 
Institutes may be established for BHEL or arrangements may be made with various 
ITIs to train batches of students in these vocations for BHEL. The Committee finds 
that no budgetary support has been provided to engineering sector and there is huge  
demand of secondary level engineers in the Capital Goods Sector. The Committee 
therefore recommends that BHEL themselves, meet various engineering clusters 
and support it through various up gradation schemes. The Committee at the same 
time also recommends  to Government of India that a separate fund may be 
earmarked for Ministry of HRD for opening of more ITIs throughout the country 
for producing secondary level engineers which are in great demand in the Capital 
Goods Industry.

OBSERVATIONS/ RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE  
AT A GLANCE  
 
The Committee is of the firm opinion that despite signs of a recovery of 
industrial growth during the period from October 2009 onwards, the performance 
of the industrial sector is cause of concern as the growth is directly proportional to 
stimulus packag es announced by the Government.  The Committee also expresses its 
concern on the sustainable growth of automobile sector in view of this years' 
budgetary proposals and hike in the excise duty of petroleum products.  The 
Committee feels that the need of the  hour is not merely in keeping on the stimulus 
packages but also to restructure the bases of taxes and import and export duties in 
such a manner that without affecting public expenditure, the industrial growth can 
sustain.  The Committee desires that this issue may be taken up with the Ministry of 
Finance and pursued seriously.  
(Para 5) 
 
The Committee appreciates that such an initiative of the Department of 
Heavy Industry with the Ministry of Urban Development will not only boost the 
demand in the Auto sect or but will also develop the public transport in the remote 
areas of the country.  The Committee, therefore, desires that the scheme of funding 
the purchase of buses for public transport by the States under JNNURM may 
continue for some more time in public interest.  The Committee at the same time, 
stresses upon the Department of Heavy Industry to monitor the progress of this 
scheme with the Ministry of Urban Development and concerned State Governments.  
(Para 7) 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the  Department of Heavy 
Industry should take up the issue with the Ministry of Finance to ensure 
concessional excise duty structure for utility vehicles.    
(Para 8) 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that in order to promote use of 
Hydrogen/Fuel cell/Hybri d vehicles, CNG vehicles and other alternative fuel 
vehicles, in the interest of protecting environment, such vehicles and its 


parts/components thereof may be exempted from excise duty at par with electric 
vehicles.   
(Para 9) 
 
The Committee feels that thi s policy will boost the auto sector during the 
coming years.  The Committee desires that Government should create a National 
Technology Development Fund for the development of auto and auto component 
industry.  This fund will not only provide impetus for m odernisation of auto sector 
but will also create huge employment opportunities in the country.  Further, this 
fund would provide financial support to companies by financing 50% of the project 
cost by way of a loan with minimum interest of 4 to 6%.  The Com mittee feels that 
such a fund will truly support auto companies to modernise and upgrade their 
technology in the globally competitive environment.  
(Para 10) 
 
The Committee, therefore, desires that Department of Heavy Industry 
should propose an amendment wi th Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) 
in Section 11 AB of the Central Excise Act, 1944 so that such anomalies may be 
sorted out. 
(Para 11) 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department of Heavy Industry 
should create a separate fund for th e upgradation of Auto Expo Centres like Pragati 
Maidan, Delhi etc. in consultation with ITPO and Ministry of Commerce & 
Industry.  The Committee further suggests the Department of Heavy Industry to
