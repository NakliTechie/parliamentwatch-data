Recommendation/Observation 
1. The Committee notes that for the Financial Year 2022 -23, the M/o 
MSME has been allocated Rs. 21422 crore as BE against the projection of 
Rs. 24979.6  crore which translates to 85.76%.  Therefore, there is a 
shortfall of Rs. 3557.6 crore in BE allocation.  The SME division has been 
allocated Rs. 200 crore against the demanded amount of Rs. 208 crore, 
which comes to 96.15%.  However, the Committee notes that allocation to 
both DC -MSME and ARI Division of t he Ministry has been curtailed 
substantially as DC -MSME has got 88.99% whereas ARI Division got only 
72.73% of the projected amount.  Both ARI Division and O/o DC-MSME are 
the backbone of the Ministry of MSME and are therefore crucial for 
development and g rowth of MSME sector. The Committee understands that 
O/o DC -MSME implements policies and various programmes/schemes for 
providing infrastructure and support services to MSMEs whereas ARI 
Division looks after the administration of KVIC, COIR Board, MGIRI, 
besides supervising flagship schemes of the Ministry such as PMEGP, 
SFURUTI, ASPIRE, etc.  Therefore, inadequate allocation of funds would 
jeopardize the smooth functioning of the Ministry and successful 
implementation of its schemes/projects.  The Committe e, therefore, 
recommends that the M/o MSME should impress upon the Ministry of 
Finance to get enhanced budget allocation for both ARI Division and O/o 
DC-MSME at RE stage. 
(Para 9)

The Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance, wh ile 
conveying approval of the Cabinet regarding continuation of Centrally 
Sponsored Schemes and Central Sector Schemes over the 15 th Finance 
Commission cycle i.e, FY 2021-22 to 2025-26, approved for continuation of  
SFURTI up to 30.09.2022 only. The proposal for budget enhancement under 
RE 2022 -23 will accordingly be taken up with the Ministry of Finance in 
view of the continuation of the scheme beyond 30.9.2022.

Action Taken Replies  
Raw Material Storage Facility & Marketing Outlets are permissible 
under ID Project component of MSE-CDP. 

 10 
      National Small Industries Co rporation Limited (NSIC), a Public Sector 
Undertaking under this Ministry, facilitates Micro, Small and Medium 
Enterprises (MSMEs) to meet their raw material requirement by making 
arrangements with bulk manufacturers for procuring the materials and 
supplying the same to MSMEs. 
 
        KVIC is supplying the required Raw Material i.e Sliver/ Roving 
through its Departmental CSPs to the KIs. The required Cotton for CSPs is 
being procured from Cotton Corporation of India (CCI) to ensure good 
quality of Raw Mate rial and transparency. In addition to this, some of the 
KIs are having In-house pre-processing units/ conversion facility to convert 
the cotton into sliver/Roving. The CSPs and the pre -processing units are 
ensuring unhindered and timely supply of Raw Material to the KIs. 
         As regards to supply of raw material at reasonable price is concerned, 
it is to inform that, though there is a steep increase in cost of cotton, the 
price of Sliver/Roving of CSPs has not been increased and the price 
difference shall be met from the Price Fluctuation Fund available with CSPs 
As far as Coir Industry is concerned, the coconut husk is the basic 
raw material of industry for manufacturing variety of value added products 
out of coir. Coconut husks are abundantly available  in the coconut 
producing States of country. Coir Board is taking various efforts to ensure 
availability of the required quantity of coconut husks to coir manufactures 
in the MSME sector. The Coir Board has already addressed the State 
Governments of major coconut growing States where the coconuts husks 
are abundantly available, to incorporate necessary provision for husk 
collection in their industrial policies. The Board is also encouraging and 
supporting the MSMEs in the coir sector for establishment of hu sk 
collection centres and raw -material banks for ensuring easy availability of 
the raw -materials to the manufactures and thereby increasing the husk 
utilization of coir industry. As  the coconut husk are used as fuel in certain 
areas, the Board has been ad dressing the Governments of coconut 
producing states for taking appropriate action to ban the usage of coconut 
husk as fuel by introducing mandatory provisions. Further, in order to 
address the financial difficulties faced by the coir units for procurement s of 
raw materials, the Board has been supporting such entrepreneurs for setting 

 11 
up husk collection units through the various schemes being implemented by 
the Board like PMEGP, SFURTI, Mahila Coir Yojana, etc.  
NSIC facilitates MSMEs to meet their raw mate rial requirement like 
Iron & Steel, Aluminum, Paraffin wax, Bitumen, Emulsion, and Cement 
etc. by making arrangements with bulk manufacturers for procuring the 
materials and supplying the same to MSMEs. NSIC also provides financial 
assistance to MSMEs unde r their Raw Material Assistance (RMA) Scheme 
against bank guarantee for payment to the suppliers. The issue of regular 
increase in prices of raw materials specifically post Pandemic was regularly 
taken up by NSIC with the bulk producers. NSIC does not have  any direct 
control on the prices of raw material sold by PSUs & Private Manufacturers 
and final decision on prices is taken by the Manufacturers themselves. 
However, NSIC has made several communications and held meetings with 
Bulk suppliers of Steel, Alum inum i.e. SAIL, RINL & NALCO regarding 
steep hike in Steel & Aluminum prices and persuade them to have some 
check on the price hike. NSIC is making every possible effort in this regard 
and approaching private big Suppliers of Iron & Steel to have price 
advantage for MSME sector.  
 
Recommendation/Observation 
10.  The Committee observes that under National SC/ST Hub, the Actual 
Expenditure often has been less than the BE & RE. The Committee is 
concerned about the under -utilization of allocated funds meant for  the 
marginalized communities of the society. The Committee recommends that 
Ministry should endeavor to improve the situation by ensuring that the 
budget earmarked for SC/ST Hub Scheme is utilized fully for the intended 
beneficiaries. Lack of awareness amo ng the SC/ST entrepreneurs may be 
one of the main reasons for lower availment of various benefits offered 
under the scheme, hence efforts may be made to create awareness among 
SC/ST entrepreneurs through various available modes like print or social 
media e tc. Handholding of SC/ST entrepreneurs may work well in 
improving the performance of this scheme. Underutilization of funds is a 
serious cause of concern for the Committee; therefore, Ministry is advised 
to ensure full utilization of funds. 
(Para 79) 
 
 

 12 
Action Taken Replies 
  Under National SC -ST Hub, a number of initiatives have been 
introduced for providing support to the SC/ST entrepreneurs for capacity 
building and making them competitive. Various awareness campaigns 
through electronic media, newspapers,  etc. have been done for wider 
outreach of the scheme. Scroll message on NSSH scheme has been run on 
“DD national channel” to connect with SC -ST community across the 
country. Further for adequate awareness generation of National SC-ST Hub, 
extensive networ k of CSC -VLEs and Post Offices is being leveraged. 
Posters of NSSH scheme have been displayed at 5000 CSC-VLEs and 9178 
Post Offices across the country. In SC -ST predominant geographical areas, 
500 Nukkad Nataks have been conducted for creating awareness o n NSSH 
scheme. Awareness is also being created regularly through social media and 
also engaged key stakeholders such as Banks, CPSEs, EPCs, Training 
institutes etc. for last mile impact.  
Further, under the revised guidelines of the Scheme issued on 
15.11.2021, service sector has been included and now all the SC/ST MSEs 
of manufacturing as well as service sector are eligible for 25% subsidy under 
SCLCSS. In addition, provision  of assistance for participation in virtual 
exhibitions, assistance for registration in Government promoted e-commerce 
portal have been added.  The existing scale of assistance under various 
interventions of NSSH has also been increased. With the above initiatives, it 
is expected that more  SC/ST entrepreneurs will avail the benefits of 
NSSH and the entire fund allocated under the scheme  in BE 2022-23 will be 
utilised.  
 
Recommendation/Observation 
 
11. The Committee noticed that overall credit gap in the MSME Sector is 
in the range of Rs. 20 lakh crore to Rs. 25 lakh crore.  The Committee
