1.8   Synchronisation of DSP and ASP 
  
The Committee views that the close proximity of Alloy Steels Plant and Durgapur Steel Plant, under the 
same organisations – SAIL, is a matter of great advantage. ASP is utilising scrap skull, C.O. gas, 
electricity, water, dolomite, oxygen from DSP. It also utilises Hot Metal / Liquid Steel, Ingot, Blooms & 
Billets as input materials. The two sister plants of SAIL are having common township and Hospital, 
under the control of same Managing Director. Therefore, the Committee feels that the possibility of 
merger of DSP and ASP may be explored. This plant could be made more cost effective by utilising the 
in-house facilities of the two SAIL Plants, ASP & DSP. This requires more close supervision and 
effective co-ordination. This should not be a difficult job since the two plants are under the control of 
same Managing Director. What is needed is the will of SAI to run both the Plants, making best use of 
the technical arrangements already prevailing to make both the Plants economically viable.

Recommendation   
  
2.1 Facilities Available at ASP 
  
The Committee, while noting the facilities available at ASP, is constrained to express its concern over 
the under-utilisation of facilities available at ASP. Reasons for under utilisation may be of different 
nature, i.e. varying from general recession in steel industry to specific and inherent problems of the 
plant. Some of the reasons might seem justified and might be well defended by stating that, factors 
forming the reasons, were beyond the control of management, the other reasons might be pointed out, 
could be dwindling market demand of ASP’s product. However, the facts remain that elaborate facility 
is available at ASP and expansion programme in ASP was completed as late as March, 1988 and 
infrastructure facilities of the plant has since been expanded from 1,60,000 to 2,60,000 tpa for producing 
1,83,000 tonnes of liquid Steel. 
                                                                                                                        (Para 3.3)
Action Taken 
  
2.1.1 The main r eason for low capacity u tilisation at ASP is that  the orders from sister
plant units of SAIL have been drastically redu ced as the products ea rlier supplied to them
by ASP are now being produced by the plants themselves.  
  
2.1.2 ASP with its stainl ess production through EAF −VOD route could no t compete with 
those produced through more co st effective AOD route.  ASP st arted producing less as the
market demand was not there for ASP products  and ASP started incurring losses. The net
loss is remaining at a level of around Rs.1 40 crores to Rs.180 cr ores per annum during the
past few years.  
  
2.1.3 Necessary steps have been taken to cash in the up-trend in the market, aided by the growth for 
alloy steels products, especially the long product which has increased substantially. For eg., Electro-
Magnetic Stirrer (EMS) in Continuous Casting Shop (CCS) has been installed to improve the quality 
and also to reduce the cost of production to cater to the needs of Automobile and Defence sectors.

3.1  Sales Performance 
  
The Committee expresses its concern over the operational status of Alloy Steels Plant (ASP). ASP is 
facing all odds. The plant has suffered huge accumulated loss, is incapable of utilising installed capacity 
and facilities available, scale of production is unviable, financial performance is dwindling, cost of 
production is uncompetitive and it has lost its market. The disinvestment efforts failed as ‘Expression of 
Interest’ (EOI) has evoked no response. Government agencies are not coming forward to help the plant 
out. The Committee is of the opinion that the plant may reach to the point of no return unless a 
comprehensively considered revival package is undertaken to improve the operational efficiency of the 
plant under guided supervision for the ultimate survival and sustenance of the plant. The confession of 
the Secretary that with the kind of technology and age of equipment that the ASP has, it would continue 
to be a loss-making unit, is a testimony to the fact that it is appropriate time for immediate upgradation 
of the existing equipments and technology for improving the operational efficiency of the plant.

The Committee understands that ASP was  facing all odds till recently and corrective steps taken by the

The Committee understands that the problems of ASP pose the real loggerheads for the viability of the 
plant. Because of its high variable cost of production, ASP is facing the problem of decreasing sales 
realization.  The Committee notes the strategy of the Ministry of Steel and the action taken by the SAIL 
Management strategy to improve the financial performance of the ASP. The Committee is of the view 
that since the financial position of the SAIL is improving with the general recovery in the Steel Industry, 
it is expected that SAIL would come forward to invest money for the purchase of required equipment 
(especially AOD).
