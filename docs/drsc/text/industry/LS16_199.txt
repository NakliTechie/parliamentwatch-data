                  1.0 AN    OVERVIEW
1.1    The Agro and Rural Industries Sector has been envisaged as an important avenue for creation of employment opportunities   in the rural non-farm sector
for the balanced economic growth of the country.  The importance of this sector lies in the fact that it seeks to establish village industries at low cost  based on
the local raw materials, skills and technology for providing employment opportunities to the local population and artisans who have been traditionally engaged
in village level economic activities.

1.3       The main contribution of ARI sector to the national economy has been in terms of production and employment generation
through setting up of village industries in the rural and semi-urban areas. Over the past five decades, there has been a big growth in
Village Industries (VI) Sector in terms of production and employment. The Committee was informed by the Ministry of ARI that
while in the year 1955-56, the production in terms of value under village industries was at the level of Rs.10.93 crore, the same had
gone up to Rs.11915.54 crore in 2005-06 and Rs. 12773 crore in 2006-07 (up to February 2007). Similarly, employment level in VI
sector has also increased from 3.07 lakh persons in 1955-56 to 77.65 lakh persons up to February 2007.
 
 
1.4       The Committee notes the performance of VIs but feels still much remains to be achieved keeping in view of size of  population
that lies in rural India and also the scope of   VI, have to grow.  The Committee,   therefore,   feels that the  contribution  of VI can be 
further strengthened and  streamlined by  ensuring timely and adequate delivery of credit by the banks, technology upgradation and 
marketing support in the    form  of  financial support  for exhibitions, product design intervention and packaging etc.
 
       
        The Ministry guides and coordinates with the implementing agencies to ensure development and achievement as per the set mandate.
 
1.5            To generate additional employment opportunities in rural areas, the Government launched the “Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP)”
throughout the country on 01.04.1995. In the 10th plan, a target of 2 million jobs under REGP was fixed.  This target was enhanced to 2.5 million jobs in 2003-04.
Accordingly, the employment targets were revised and fixed for 2003-04, 2004-05, 2005-06 and 2006-07 as 5 lakh, 5.25 lakh, 5.50 lakh and 5.90 lakh respectively.
During 10th Plan period (upto February 2007), 22.87 lakh job opportunities have already been generated under this programme. The Ministry informed that 3.56 lakh
additional employment opportunities (60 per cent of the target) have been generated as against the target of creation of 5.90 lakh job opportunities.  It has been
reported that a number of projects are at sanction stage and it is expected that the target set under the programme  would be achieved. During 2005-06, an
achievement of 5.68 lakh number of additional employment opportunities was made against the target of 5.50 lakh. In order to meet the enhanced employment target,
the budgetary allocation for REGP was also enhanced from Rs. 366.60 crore in 2005-06 to Rs. 372.63 crore in 2006-07 which has been further enhanced to Rs. 445
crore in BE 2007-08.  Based  on the expected performance in  2006-07, the outlay  to the extent of Rs.445 crore for 2007-08 under REGP is expected to generate
additional employment opportunities for 7.04 lakh persons as against 5.90 lakh  opportunities targeted for 2006-07.

one of the major organizations in the decentralized sector for generating sustainable rural non-farm employment opportunities at low per capita investment.  It
undertakes activities like skill improvement, transfer of technology, research & development, marketing, etc., in the process of generating employment/self-

7.2            The Khadi and Village Industries Commission functions in the rural and
 towns to provide employment opportunities at low cost to local artisans by utilizing the local raw materials. To support such traditional industries, Government
had been giving loans for Khadi and Village Industries to be set up in rural areas through the KVIC. The KVIC in turn was lending this money to Khadi and VI
institutions for meeting their working capital fund requirements.  As the Khadi institutions were to pay zero per cent interest on this loan till 2003 and 4 per cent
thereafter, KVIC have hardly been able to pay any interest or return the loan advanced by Government to KVIC. The amount of interest accrued to KVIC on loan has
been subsidized by the Government through payment of subsidy in lieu of interest over the years.  Thus, this subsidy in lieu of interest is not actually released to the
KVIC but is provided for in the budgetary provision both Plan and Non-Plan for book adjustments, against the interest payable by KVIC to Government. . The
subsidy is therefore towards this difference in the rate at which KVIC borrows from the Government and the rate at which it lends to beneficiaries. Since the amount
of interest subsidy has been accumulating every year, the budgetary provision for interest subsidy has been kept at the level of Rs. 39.10 crore to meet the
requirements of annual book adjustment. The budgetary provision for interest subsidy is only the book adjustment of interest on past loans.

promotion of exports and internal sales; marketing of coir and coir products in India and abroad; preventing unfair competition among producers and exporters;
assisting in the establishment of units for the manufacture of products; promoting co-operative organizations among producers of husk, coir fiber, coir yarn and

              The Chairman welcomed the Members of the Committee and asked the Committee to consider and adopt the Draft 198 th,
199th, 200th, and 202nd Reports of the Committee.  The Committee then unanimously adopted the Report and authorized Shri O.T.
Lepcha and in his absence Dr. K. Malaisamy to present the Reports in Rajya Sabha.  The Committee also authorized Shri Swadesh
Chakraborty and in his absence Shri V.K. Thummar to present the Reports in Lok Sabha.  The Committee also decided that the
Reports be presented in both the Houses of Parliament on the 8th May, 2007.

3.            The Committee also decided to undertake during June-July 2007, a visit to        Sikkim, Guwahati, Kolkata, Bhubaneswar,
Hyderabad, Banglore, Mumbai and Bhopal to for the on-spot study of the issues under its consideration.   It   authorized the
Chairman of the Committee to obtain necessary approvals and  finalise the details of such visit.
 
4.        The Committee then began its consideration of the issue of bank credit from Private and foreign banks to SSI sector and
sought to know from the representatives of the Banking Division (M/O Finance) and the RBI about the guidelines issued to these
Banks in this regard.  The Committee further asked the reason for shrinking share for SSI credit of overall Net Bank Credit and if
the new Credit Policy had new incentives for the SSI sector.  The representatives of the Ministries of Finance, SSI and the RBI
responded to the queries raised by the Committee.
