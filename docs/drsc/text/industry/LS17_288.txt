REPORT
The Micro. Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) is known as the pillar of economic growth
in many developed and developing countries in the world. It has also rightly been addressed as "Growth
Engine" in terms of Indian Economy because of the prominent role being played by it in the development
of the country as regards generating largest employment opportunities for both rural and urban population
at comparatively lower capital cost, next only to agriculture. MSME sector has so far employed more
than 11 crores scaling manufacturing capabilities, removing regional disparities and contributing a major
portion to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and export of the country. MSME sector is providing
goods and services at affordable costs by offering innovative solutions and sustainable development to
the economy as a whole. For a country to grow, the business enterprises should be promoted actively
and among business Enterprises, MSMEs deserve special attention.
2. There are around 6.34 crores enterprises in the MSME sector in India contributing an impressive
picture next only to China where this sector is providing employment to a large populace. MSME sector
by manufacturing varied products ranging from traditional to hi-tech items in the country registered a
growth much higher than large industries. For sustainable growth for any country, proper attention to
the MSME sector is imperative, which is also the need of the hour. More than 50% of the MSMEs are
in six States viz. Maharashtra, West Bengal, Tamilnadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh
and more than 20% MSMEs are owned by women and a large number are working under proprietorship
or partnership. While it contributes about 45% to manufacturing sector and perhaps 40% to exports, it
forms the highest share of employment sector in India i.e., around 69%. Post the dream of 'Make in
India', the process of incorporating a new business has been made very easy.
3. The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 which was notified on 2nd
October, 2006 defines the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises based on (i) the investment in Plant and
Machinery for those engaged in manufacturing or production, processing or preservation of 'Goods" and
(ii) the investment in equipment for enterprises engaged in providing or rendering of 'Services".

which included industrial sector also, took cognizance of the complexities and the problems being faced
by MSME sector to run their enterprises and doing away with Inspector Raj came forward with a new
proposal, duly approved by the Union Cabinet on 7th February, 2018 for classification of the Micro,
Small and Medium Enterprises based on the basis of Annual turnover which is as follows:

5. The Committee took note of the deliberations with the Stakeholders and process of finalization of
the criteria for defining MSMEs. The Secretary, MSME, during the course of his presentation to the
Committee, informed the Committee that the Ministry, after due consultations with stakeholders had
ensured that the definition of MSMEs should be such that there is ease of doing Business and the end
of Inspector Raj. After, weighing all the pros and corns, it was decided to take into consideration only
the Annual Turnover. MSME with Annual Turnover of ` 20 Lakh and above should mandatorily register
themselves with GST and accordingly their annual turnover can easily be ascertained. As for those with
less than ` 20 Lakh turnover, their annual turnover can be known from their Audit statement. In
consequence with the above criteria, a New Bill is likely to be introduced in the Lok Sabha.
6. The Primary responsibility of promotion and development of MSMEs is with the State Government.
The role of the M/o MSME and its organizations is to assist the States in their efforts to encourage
entrepreneurship, employment, livelihood opportunities and enhance the competitiveness of MSMEs in
the changed economic scenario.

Industry (ARI) Division, Integrated Finance (IF) Wing and Data Analytics and Technical Co-ordination
(DATC) wing, besides the Office of the Development Commissioner (DC-MSME) as an attached office
and other subordinate organizations namely Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), National
Small Industries Corporation (NSIC), Coir Board, National Institutes for Micro, Small and Medium
Enterprises (Ni-MSME) and Mahatama Gandhi Institute for Rural Industrialization (MGIRI). The major

9. The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises – Development Organisation (MSME-DO) is headed by
the Additional Secretary & Development Commissioner (MSME). The Office of the Development
Commissioner (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises) assists the Ministry in formulating, coordinating,
implementing and monitoring different policies and programmes for the promotion and development of
MSMEs in the country. It functions through a network of MSME-Development Institutes, Regional
Testing Centres, Footwear Training Institutes, Production Centres, Field Testing Stations and specialized
institutes. It renders services such as :
• Advising the Government in policy formulation for the promotion and development of MSMEs.
• Providing techno-economic and managerial consultancy, common facilities and extension services
to MSMEs.
• Providing facilities for technology up-gradation, modernization, quality improvement and
infrastructure.
• Developing Human Resources through training and skill up-gradation.
• Providing economic information services.
• Maintaining a close liaison with the Central Ministries, Planning Commission, State Governments,
Financial Institutions and other Organisations concerned with development of MSMEs.
• Evolving and coordinating Policies and Programmes for development of MSMEs as ancillaries
to large industries.

14. The Committee gave a patient hearing to the representatives of Associations from Industrial and
MSME sectors and was apprised that as far as the changes made in the Budget with regard to taxation
rates, which has been reduced from 30 to 25%, is concerned these have been acclaimed and further
added that this is a very positive development which would certainly leave more money in the hands of
entrepreneurs who could now utilize the money for their business growth. The changes in the definition
of MSME sector have also been appreciated. The Committee was further apprised that, MSME sector,
however, still lacks a dedicated national body or a controlling department to look into the grievances and
challenges being faced by MSMEs. Besides the issues of meeting indigenization targets, lack of financial
and technological support, Public Procurement Policy compliance in Government sector and adoption of
globally accepted technology were also placed before the Committee.

7
15. The Secretary, Ministry of MS&ME informed the Committee while making presentation on the DFG
(2018-19) that the year has been very important as the introduction of the GST has brought the majority
of MSMEs under the umbrella of formal economy from informal one. The number of registration of
MSMEs has thus increased by the use of UAM. The Committee was further apprised about the expenditure
in 2017-18 and enhanced expenditure under various Schemes in 2018-19, which would be utilized in
time. The Secretary further added that the number of beneficiaries under Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)
is highest as compared to other Ministries/Departments. The Ministry of MS&ME further adduced late
approval of Revised Estimate (2017-18) from Ministry of Finance as the reason non-utilization of funds.
To add further, the Committee was apprised that the corpus to the Credit Guarantee Fund has been
enhanced by bringing it up to ` 7,500 crores from ` 2,500 crores to ensure payment to the Banks in
case the loan amount becomes NPA. ( Under this fund, there is already a Credit Guarantee of ` 1.44
lakh crores.
16. During the presentation by the Secretary, Ministry of MS&ME, the Committee was also informed
about the compliance of Public Procurement Policy by PSUs and benefits to SC/STs owned Micro and
Small Enterprises under it, existence of MSEFC in States and cases settled by its intervention, turnover
limit with or without GST, functioning of Stock Exchange of MSMEs, functioning of "Sambandh" and
"Samadhan" Portals, restructuring of stressed accounts of SMEs and repayment, functioning of GeM
Portal, derivation of cost of job, linkages with Public Sector Banks, Credit Rating, low wages in KVI
sector etc.
17. The Ministry of MSME submitted during the examination of Demands 2018-19, that the budget
under certain Schemes could not be utilized properly as the schemes are demand driven.  The Committee
therefore suggests that proper assessment regarding financial requirement of the demand driven
Schemes should be made by the Ministry before sending the Budget proposals. Hence, it should
be ensured in future that the budget allocation made is utilized properly and overrated demands
are not projected by any of the organization /Institution under its jurisdiction.
18. It is observed that the allocation and utilization of funds under the plan schemes of the Ministry
during the last three years have been almost equal. The Committee is of the view that the funds for
meeting the salaries and pensions should be considered suitably without affecting the funds for
the development schemes. The Committee also desires that the Ministry may follow-up with the
Ministry of Finance for release of funds on timely basis.
19. The Committee during its course of interaction on DFG (2018-19) discussed on a few common
issues which rather do not fall purely under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of MS&ME but certainly
needs its intervention. The issues are like; problem of electrification in many of the villages to implement
Sambandh and Smadhan Portal, mal-functioning of GeM Portal - the Portal that facilitate Ministries and
its organisations to purchase from MSMEs, restructuring of stressed accounts of MSME units and credit
rating in MSME sector, in this regard, the Ministry of MSME has taken various initiatives including, inter-
alia, "MSME Samadhan Portal" to enable entrepreneurs to upload their problems in case there is delay
in getting payment from Public Sector Undertakings or any other Government Department; Public
Procurement Portal "MSME Sambandh" for monitoring the implementation of the Public Procurement
from MSEs by Central Public Sector Enterprises etc.

8
20. In the opinion of the Committee, most of the MSMEs are not paid timely by the Government
organizations and PSUs for their supply as a result of which MSMEs face serious financial crunch
and it becomes very difficult for them to survive in the market. On the other hand they have
to dole out a high rate of interest to the Banks against their loans for delayed repayment.
Further, procurement from SC/ST Enterprises has also not received adequate attention under the
Public Procurement Policy. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of MS&ME should
take immediate measures with the concerned Ministries/Departments to sort out these issues to
dispel the apprehensions of MSME sector.
21. The Committee reiterates its recommendation that the long lasting problem of delayed
payment to SMEs, although, resolved to a certain extent by introduction of a few portals for
MSME sector, yet, it should be settled effectively and a time limit for defaulting large Companies
be fixed along with some penal provisions for making delayed payments to MSME units so that
both Large and Small units are enable to flourish, side by side.
22. The Committee is of the opinion that Micro Entrepreneurs in Indian Society finance their
business through borrowings, credit from friends or through their own resource generation and
are away from access to the Capital whereas the big and powerful entrepreneurs in business
sector take advantage of loopholes in the Financial Institutions. It, therefore, desires that the
entire Industrial sector should ponder over the ways to check the slippages in the financial
Institution to make the system foolproof so that the Micro Entrepreneurs can also avail the
benefit.
23. The Committee observes that Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC)
constituted under MSMED Act, 2006 to resolve the delayed payment issue for MSEs have not yet
been set up in all states. There is a wide gap in the number of cases settled and reported to it.
MSEFCs have not yet met the success due to lack of any legal power for enforcing its decision.
The Committee recommends that the Ministry should take steps to constitute MSEFCs in all the
States so that the issue of delayed payments is resolved expeditiously.
24. The Committee further observes that MSME Exchange gives them the opportunity to raise
equity through an IPO and then get listed on the exchange. There are more than 300 companies
listed on BSE and NSE. Efficient SME platforms could prove to be a vital source of Capital for
MSMEs, but the two platforms are far behind in expansion, the way it were envisaged. It,
therefore, recommends that requirement of presence of a company in the market for three years
could be considered for reduction to a shorter period by SEBI. Tax exemption to the investors
in SMEs could add to the growth of MSME sector.
25. The Committee heard the Industry and MSME Associations representing enterprises from
various sectors in the country and is of the opinion that rising input cost is affecting the
competitiveness of the products of MSME sector especially, Micro and Small Enterprises, in
Domestic as well as International Market. This sector finds it very difficult to pass on the extra
cost to its consumers due to which margins of Micro and Small units decline and thus making
productions unviable for them. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of MS&ME should

9
raise the issue with Ministry of Finance and persuade them to address it at appropriate level so
that the input cost for SMEs remain within their reach and they are not forced to take loan at
exorbitant rates.
26. The Committee observes that there have been gradual changes in technological world and in the
globalized and liberalized economy, digital technology has crept in. Around the world, traditional
manufacturing industry is being replaced with digital one. MSME sector need to adopt this rapid change
if they are not to be left behind by development in their sector, by their competitors. The rapid use of
electronics and Information Technology is changing the level of production, competitiveness etc. It opens
the new ways for enterprises to understand customers needs and preferences and integrate them into
their development and production process thereby enhancing the quality and avoid faults in production.
Exposure to new age technology will in the long run facilitate MSMEs in India.
27. The Committee observes that in the present scenario of digitalization IT professionals are
in high demand and they are getting placement with reputed MNCs and other big establishments
in different sectors. Many employees in Micro and Small sector start from ground level after
working for many years they attain a certain level of expertise. These employees may not be IT
literate and often have high resistance to the rapid changes in the working process that they are
comfortable with after many years.
28. The Committee is of the opinion that smart manufacturing is now making its place in the
market which is dependent on industrial automation including breath taking innovation viz. use
of robots and machine intelligence, smart material self learning systems etc. MSME sector
requires integration of work force and digital manufacturing and production processes for better
performance.
29. The Committee observes that implementation of some bigger scale IT project especially
after the introduction of GST and changes in tax structure involving business process across
different Departments which requires large amount of data entries requiring human resource
during implementation. The SMEs are often in shortage of manpower and thus it becomes
difficult for them to carry out implementation as it is always conflicting to run business on day
to day basis with limited manpower and do the extra implementations work. This affects the
business enterprises in the nascent stage and forcing them to close the establishment many a
times.
30. The Committee is of the opinion that to fight with bankruptcy and insolvency, modern codes
in this area are need of the hour in the competitive markets and uncertainties in globalization
so that if an entrepreneur is encouraged to take risk and the creditors should also have an
assurance of disposal of case quickly and repossession of assets after the failure of business.
31. It was informed by the Ministry that in order to build the capacity of MSMEs, there is a need to
use energy efficiency technology to improve the acceptance of their products thereby making them
globally competitive. Therefore, there is a need to adopt energy efficient and clean development technology
available globally. The MSMEs also try to enhance their technology by collaborating with such MSMEs
all over the world having good reputation in their country.

10
32. The Committee observes that MSME sector having limited financial resources lags far
behind in adoption and acquisition of globally available expensive advanced technology and thus
fails to stand firmly in competitive and qualitative market. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that a Global Technology Acquisition Fund should be set up to enable the Indian Industries to
acquire high-end technology with its support to enhance their competitiveness globally and value
addition.
33. During the course of discussion with the associations in Industrial sector, the Committee has been
apprised of the meager expenditure on R&D and innovation in India as compared to the other developed
and developing countries. In India, the expenditure is only 0.5% of the turn over whereas in other
developed countries it is 5-6% of the total turnover. This needs to be enhanced because this factor would
decide the competitiveness through owning of advanced technology. It appears a major weakness in
production and requires overhauling. Indian MSMEs over the years have largely ignored R&D requirement
and have not been open to new product development and technological up-gradation at requisite place.
34. The Committee recommends that R&D and Technology related support facilities and services
including training and skill development programme should cover optimum educated unemployed
youths of the country having qualification and talent along with inclination through IITs, ITIs,
NITs and other research laboratories. The facilities available need to be modernized and should
be fine tuned and tailored as per the need and requirement of the MSME sector.
35. The Committee feels that there is an urgent need to evaluate the Research and Development
system to make it more effective and beneficial to the MSME sector which is crawling far behind
in technology adoption in comparison to MSMEs of developed countries. The Committee recommends
that Ministry of MS&ME being the protector of the units in this sector should allocate more
funds for R&D and qualified people working in the field be motivated and encouraged through
the best possible ways and means.
36. The Committee further recommends that technological and innovative preparedness of the
MSME sector need to be studied constantly and a mechanism is required to be evolved urgently
to fill the gaps or constraints being faced by MSME sector in view of various trade agreements
at International level including economic cooperation agreements.
37. MSMEs engaged in International business have developed co-operation, partnerships, linkages and
network with foreign companies and institutions. By way of import and export MSME enterprises boost
their efficiencies capabilities, competitiveness and their vision. FDI thus play a prominent role for

Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme
68. The Ministry apprised the Committee that ISEC Scheme was introduced in May, 1977 to mobilize

khadi and polyvastra as per Pattern of Assistance. For this, an interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate is

working capital. It is also supported to achieve additional productions and sales target in the khadi field.

69. Further, the Committee was informed about the details of SWOT analysis of the scheme is given
below:
• The ISEC scheme strengthens the KIs to avail the Bank Finance for the shortfall in working
capital. KIs can fulfil their requirement of funds to achieve the approved productions and sales
target during the prescribed time limit.
• The weakness of the scheme is that the flow of funds from Bank is delayed due to the lending
norms prescribed by banks. Therefore, the shortfall in their working capital leads to their
under performance.

18
• The ISEC scheme has given opportunity to the KIs for availing of shortfall in funds to meet
out required working capital. It is also supported to achieve additional productions and sales
target in the Khadi field.
• There is a wide gap between the ISEC issued and funds availed by KIs through banks. KVIC
takes a longer working cycle of 18 months while the Banks normally apply turnover formula
and consider one working cycle of maximum of 90 days. This is effectively comes to 25%
of annual turnover of SSI units. This is the main reason for the gap between ISEC issued by
KVIC and Bank Finance sanctioned by Bank.
• KVIC follows Debt Equity Ratio (DER) of 1:10 and Banks normally adopt 1:4. The flow of
credit to Khadi sector under ISEC is not at the desired level. This creates gap in working
capital requirement and actual fund availability. The KVI sector faces set back in its performance
due to shortage of fund.
70. Under the ISEC scheme, the certificate is issued to the extent of shortfall in working capital for
the financial year concerned. For the next financial year, ISEC is issued to the extent of shortfall assessed
based on previous year's performance. In this case, the ISEC shall cover bank finance already availed
and additional eligibility arrived.

Coir-Board
92. "Coir" is the native name of fibre extracted from coconut husk, the fibrous mass surrounding
coconut, which is a perennial plant cultivated extensively in tropics. Coir Industry plays a very significant
role in economic structure of the country as a source of employment, income and foreign exchange
earnings. The U.S., China, Netherland and South Korea are main buyers of coir products from India.
93. The Coir Board is a statutory body established under the Coir Board Industry Act, 1953 for
promoting overall development of the coir industry and improving the living conditions of the workers
engaged in this traditional industry. The activities of the Board for development of coir industries, inter-
alia include undertaking scientific, technological and economic research and development activities;
collecting statistics relating to exports and internal consumption of coir and coir products; developing
new products and designs; organizing publicity for promotion of exports and internal sales; marketing
of coir and coir products in India and abroad; preventing unfair competition between producers and

Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS) of Technology Upgradation of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs)

completed application is uploaded by PLI through Online Application and Tracking System to the attached
Nodal Agency, which, in turn, recommends the application online to Office of DC (MSME) for release
of subsidy. After processing of application and subject to availability of funds, due approval is accorded
from competent authority and concurrence of Internal Finance Wing, after which funds are released to
Nodal Agencies. Funds are then transferred by the Nodal Agencies to the PLIs where the account of the
MSE is operated.

The Committee observes that the allocations of the Ministry were either reduced or enhanced

Estimate. The Committee is therefore, of the opinion that adequate steps should be taken to
monitor the output of various Schemes, including individual components so that the output
targets are achieved timely. (Para 11)
The Ministry of MSME submitted during the examination of Demands 2018-19, that the
budget under certain Schemes could not be utilised properly as the schemes are demand driven.
The Committee therefore suggests that proper assessment regarding financial requirement of
the demand driven Schemes should be made by the Ministry before sending the Budget proposals.
Hence, it should be ensured in future that the budget allocation made is utilised properly and
overrated demands are not projected by any of the organization /Institution under its jurisdiction.
(Para 17)
It is observed that the allocation and utilisation of funds under the plan schemes of the
Ministry during the last three years have been almost equal. The Committee is of the view that
the funds for meeting the salaries and pensions should be considered suitably without affecting
the funds for the development schemes. The Committee also desires that the Ministry may
follow-up with the Ministry of Finance for release of funds on timely basis. (Para 18)
In the opinion of the Committee, most of the MSMEs are not paid timely by the Government
organisations and PSUs for their supply as a result of which MSMEs face serious financial crunch
and it becomes very difficult for them to survive in the market. On the other hand they have
to dole out a high rate of interest to the Banks against their loans for delayed repayment.
Further, procurement from SC/ST Enterprises has also not received adequate attention under the
Public Procurement Policy. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of MS&ME should
take immediate measures with the concerned Ministries/Departments to sort out these issues to
dispel the apprehensions of MSME sector. (Para 20)
The Committee reiterates its recommendation that the long lasting problem of delayed
payment to SMEs, although, resolved to a certain extent by introduction of a few portals for
MSME sector, yet, it should be settled effectively and a time limit for defaulting large Companies
be fixed along with some penal provisions for making delayed payments to MSME units so that
both Large and Small units are enable to flourish, side by side. (Para 21)
The Committee is of the opinion that Micro Entrepreneurs in Indian Society finance their
business through borrowings, credit from friends or through their own resource generation and
are away from access to the Capital whereas the big and powerful entrepreneurs in business
sector take advantage of loopholes in the Financial Institutions. It, therefore, desires that the
entire Industrial sector should ponder over the ways to check the slippages in the financial

turnover formula on one working cycle differs both at KVIC and Bank level, which appears to
be one of the lacunas for the gap existing between ISEC issued and Bank finance which needs
to be addressed at the appropriate level. (Para 74)
In fact this matter had also come up for discussion during the Committee's visit to Mumbai
and Ahmedabad in August, 2017, wherein the Committee had desires that the Ministry, KVIC
and the financial institutions may together hold talks with RBI and come up with a workable
solution. (Para 75)
The Committee observes that KVIC is not working impressively in North Eastern Region
and has a very small set up in these areas. The number of ISEC issued under Khadi and
Polyvastra is almost negligible except in Assam and Manipur in the last financial year. The
Committee desires that the reasons for the same other than the hardships faced due to hilly
terrain should be placed before it. (Para 76)
The Committee is of the opinion that the main objective of the SFURTI Scheme is to
encourage Traditional Industries and Artisan clusters of the country. This scheme is likely to
complete 10 years of its inception and, by now every district of the country should have had at
least one project, but the fact is that the scheme could not make its presence felt even in 10%

48
districts of the country. The Ministry of MSME as well as KVIC and Coir Board appear to have
failed on this front. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should come out with the
specific targets so that each district of the country will have at least one SFURTI project.
(Para 79)
In a recent Study Visit, The Committee came to know about the payment of low wages to
the workers in SFURTI cluster in Andhra Pradesh. The Committee therefore feels that derivation
of cost norms for job creation vis-a-vis investment and maintenance should be more realistic as
minimum wages to support the livelihood is a basic requirement. It appears that what the
Government spends on Khadi units/VI units for the benefit of artisans is not reaching them for
their financial upliftment. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the payment of wages
to the un-skilled, semi-skilled and skilled workers with Full Time Employment (FTE) in KVIC
in different pockets should be looked into properly. (Para 80)
The Committee is the opinion that KVIC which is mandated for providing employment
opportunities across the country must come out with a comprehensive plan considering the local
need for the Khadi as well as for village industries sector along with developing a marketing
network for rural Khadi artisans. (Para 86)
Since the majority of rural habitants are illiterate and/or not technically qualified, there is
as such a need for creating employment opportunities in the rural area. Khadi, being a sector
that required low investment, must be vigorously persuaded in the rural areas. Although, the
Government has emphasized on every occasion, the need of support to this sector, however
budget allocation for this sector shows a very notional increase in the last 3 years. The Committee
recommends that the reasons of slow progress in Khadi programme should be evaluated properly
and appropriate measures be taken for its development. (Para 87)
The Committee observes that first and foremost requirement is overhauling of the marketing
strategy for KVI products. Product development and marketing need a professional approach.
Even the attempts to boost sales using Khadi as brand name is unlikely to succeed unless some
fundamental changes are introduced. Bringing in, the private sector for marketing and product
development may be a good attempt and may be initiated as a pilot project. KVIC and M/o
MS&ME should think over the cost cutting and stoppage of rebate on sale. The Committee
desires that an enhanced B.E. of ````` 65 crores under MPDA for 2018-19 may be utilised judiciously
for organising Trade Fairs, Vendor Development Programmes etc. at domestic and international
levels so that a need to bring down the funds drastically at the RE stage does not arise.
(Para 90)
The Committee is of the opinion that MSMEs are an integral part of the economic development
of the country and therefore their growth and survival is vital for the Government. Hence, it
recommends that more and more MSME exposition, MSME Haats and MSME National/International
trade fairs/Exhibitions should be organised, including at District level, with the participation of
people's representatives, to enhance the market accessibility of MSMEs. (Para 91)

49
The Committee feels that one of the most important objective of the implementation of
various Schemes under Coir Board is the employment generation especially in rural areas. For
this, organisations like Coir Board is providing training on various aspects of production and
development of new/innovative products. The Committee recommends that Ministry of MS&ME
should give a consideration for recognition of such training courses/training institutions so that
more younger generation will be attracted to such training and the status of such training will
be elevated to the next level. (Para 100)
The Committee also desires that the Ministry in consultation with the Coir Board should
make all out efforts to reach out to the Coir Udyami Yojna applicants whose accounts are
frequently turning to NPAs resulting in Banks showing reluctance to sanction loans under the
Scheme. The Ministry of MSME should make efforts to ascertain the root cause of increasing
rate of NPAs under the Scheme. (Para 101)
Deforestation is one of the major threats for the maintenance of ecological balance. The
Committee, in order to avoid grave threats which are endangering the climatic conditions and
human life, recommends that the production of substitutes for wood, such as coir wood, has to
be encouraged and more coir clusters under SFURTI have to be established to meet the demands
of coir based furnishings and building materials from the domestic as well as international
market. To achieve this, more funds should be allocated by the Ministry of MS&ME to the Coir
Board with proper monitoring and utilisation of allocated funds. (Para 106)
The Committee is of the firm opinion that in order to have a better co-ordination of export
promotion of coir and its by products for aiding in multifarious activities such as soil erosion
control, landscaping, river embankment etc., establishment of overseas offices of the Coir Board
may be considered. This way, the Coir Industry would also grow internationally side by side with
the Khadi Industry. (Para 107)
The Coir Board is implementing multifarious developmental and promotional activities when
in turn is providing employment to a large number of people mostly in the rural areas of the
country. Sufficient manpower is however required for implementation and monitoring the
programmes and for providing research data/statistics in time for MIS. Hence, the Committee
recommends that in view of the increased activities of the Board, the necessary qualified manpower
should be provided to the Board for its smooth functioning. (Para 108)
The Committee is aware that there are various other schemes implemented by the
Government under which technology developed is transferred to the beneficiaries without any
charges. The Committee is therefore is of the view that the technology developed by the Coir
Board should be transferred to entrepreneurs, traders, clusters etc. free of cost to encourage
potential entrepreneurs to get into the coir sector. Besides, steps should also be taken to explore
the potential overseas markets for export of coir products particularly innovative coir products.
Also, 100% modernization of coir industry has to be encouraged with high funding support to
popularize the innovative coir products in domestic market as well. (Para 115)

50
The Committee is also of the opinion that Buyer Seller meets and Vendor Development
Programmes may be organized overseas to popularize coir products and to tap the virgin overseas
markets. (Para 116)
A maximum numbers of coir industries are in Micro and Small category and these are
dependant heavily on the Banks and other financial institutions for funds. Natural calamities like
tsunami/cyclones etc. which are beyond the control of human being, sometimes badly effects the
coir industries destroying them seriously. The Committee therefore, recommends that in such
cases, the coir sector should be supported financially in order to provide some relief to the
affected artisans/units and, for the purpose, a corpus fund specifically, for Coir Board may be a
good solution and could be considered positively. (Para 117)
Coir Industry is a traditional industry (at the same time an export oriented one also) and
using the raw material available cheaply in the coconut producing areas. Actually, it is a process
of making value added products from the waste, otherwise it would have been a threat to the
environment. The Committee is of the opinion that the GST imposed on coir and coir products
may be reduced further for encouraging the traditional coir industry in which livelihood of about
7 lakhs people is based in rural sector. The matter may be taken up with Ministry of Finance.
(Para 118)
The Committee is of the opinion that the skill required by the artisans in Coir sector can
be acquired easily and similarly the amount of investment required is negligible vis-a-vis other
similar village industries. Also the employment generated in the coir sector during the last
2-3 years is very negligible in view of the vast potential available in the sector. The Committee
recommends that more Entrepreneur Development Programmes (EDPs) should be organised so
that potential entrepreneurs are motivated for setting up of their own units as a result employment
opportunity are created for rural youth. (Para 119)
The Committee was informed about the Solar Charkha Mission for which an allocation of
````` 50 crores has been made for the first time during 2018-19. Though the Solar Charkha mode
will surely remove drudgery, the Committee is of the view that the KVIC should ensure that
there is no conflict in the definition of Khadi which originally was totally 'hand spun', but with
the introduction of Solar Charkha, it would become 'motorised'. (Para 121)
The Committee observes that the funds released to MGIRI during the last three years is
always less than the Revised Estimate for the respective years. The requirement of fund and its
utilisation for an Institution engaged in rural upliftment should be monitored properly by the
Ministry of MS&ME so that the targets are achieved timely. If there is any grey area, the
Ministry should take initiative to address it immediately. (Para 125)
The Committee notes that the MGIRI is the only Institution under the Ministry of MSME
which is working on empowerment of traditional artisans through R&D projects for alternative
technology using local resources and it has given excellent results, especially, with regard to the
project based on cow urine and waste human hair producing Amino acid as a plant growth

51
promoter. The Committee observes that the success of this pilot project would go a long way in
increasing employment generation in the rural areas of the country. The Committee is of the
view that there should not be any shortage of funds for the institution rather it should be
promoted and motivated. (Para 126)
The Committee is of the view that Joint Plant Committee which is looking after the
procurement and distribution of metals, coal etc. should ensure unhampered supply to NSIC and
see that allocation order is released by the respective Ministries in time so that there is no
dearth of raw material supply to MSME sector. (Para 139)
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of MS&ME should consider other available
options to provide necessary raw materials required by the MSMEs and for this, the other
concerned Ministries/Departments be persuaded for allocation of raw materials like, aluminum,
copper, paraffin wax, cement and bitumen etc. to fulfil the requirement of MSME sector.
(Para 140)
The Committee is of the considered opinion that the Incubation Centres run by NSIC
provide training to the prospective entrepreneurs by imparting training in the manufacturing as
well as service sector for skill development. In view of the huge skill demand existing in the
country, the Committee recommends that more Incubation Centres should be established in co-
ordination with State Government so that training in conventional trade as well as hi-tech areas
are imparted to entrepreneurs to accure skill in all areas of business operation for gainful
employment. (Para 141)
The Committee observes that NSIC through its Vendor Development Programme sensitize
and facilitate MSEs, including SC/ST MSEs, to become qualified vendors to public sector units and
other Government Departments as per the provision, of 20% mandatory procurement under
Public Procurement Policy. Of this, 4% should be from SC/ST MSEs. While the PSUs and
Government procurement from MSEs has been gradually improving, procurement from SC/ST
units is still very low. Therefore, the Committee recommends that there should be more VDPs
organised to publicise Public Procurement Policy among MSEs including SC/ST units.
(Para 142)
The Committee observes that as per reply furnished by the Ministry under the Single Point
Registration Scheme (SPRS), there are 527 MSMEs registered upto 15th February, 2018. However,
as per the Annual Report, 2017-18, there were 21922 cumulative number of units registered
under SPRS upto 31st December, 2017. The Committee desires that mismatch between the
figures should be clarified and placed before the Committee. (Para 143)
The Committee is pleased to note that the launch of the UAM has proved to be a stepping
stone for the ease of doing Business by the MSMEs. However, at the same time it also observes
that the online filing system under UAM based on self declared information shows an uneven
geographical distribution of UAMs across all Indian States and Union Territories. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Ministry should take the required steps to encourage MSMEs,

52
especially, the Small and Medium Enterprises to register under UAM. In this direction, the
stakeholders from the States/UT Governments, Industry Associations and Public at large should
also be encouraged to contribute significantly for the purpose, especially in the NER, in order to
afford ease of doing business in the MSME sector. (Para 148)
The Committee feels that use of standard raw material consistently; possible design
development, process and product development including planning for maximum output, maximum
efficiency, better finishing with accuracy can sharpen the competitiveness in manufacturing and
production sector. MSMEs face competition from developing countries due to globalization and
increased international trade but most of the Micro and Small Enterprises do not see financial
competition as major deterring factor but tend to face stiff competition in quality rather than
prices. (Para 152)
To enhance the marketing, targeted advertising through social network sites may prove a
right platform to reach out to prospective customers. The Committee feels that Ministry of
MS&ME should emphasize on setting up of more LBIs and TBIs with modern practice and
procedure for skill up-gradation of MSMEs. (Para 153)
The Committee feels that despite the efforts, the problem of unemployment, poverty, drudgery
and migration still exists in rural economy. The one of solutions to address these problems is
creation of employment opportunities in rural areas by setting up of Small Enterprises in Agro
based sector as nearly 56% of the population is still dependent on Agriculture. There is always
a scope for innovation, value addition and entrepreneurship development in this sector. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the skill up-gradation, handholding, credit support and
incubation aiming rural zones should be considered to provide employment in rural areas.
(Para 157)
The Committee would also like the Ministry of MSME to expedite the sanctioning and
setting up of the targeted number of LBIs and TBIs and also make the States and other
organisations in world, aware of usefulness of the Incubation Centres by holding talks with them
from time to time. In this way, the States would be encouraged to furnish more and more
proposal for setting up of Incubation Centres. (Para 158)
The Committee finds that there is a tangible gap in subsidy claims received and settled
every year. There is also a declining trend in the number of MSEs benefited. The Committee is
constrained to note the decreasing trend in subsidy claim amount. The Committee recommends
that the Ministry of MS&ME should come out with a concrete plan to get sufficient allocation
from the Ministry of Finance for clearance of subsidy claims. (Para 164)
The Committee observes that benefits under CLCSS are extended to the beneficiaries only
through 11 Banks. The Committee recommends that more Banks should be added to the list of
banks. Moreover, the list of 51 subsectors should be increased in consultation with Industry
Associations and GTAB. The Ministry of MS&ME should also ensure release of timely payment
to scheduled Banks. (Para 165)
