IL BACKGROUND OF MOU SYSTEM

2.1 The Department of Public Enterprises in its Background Note on the MoU system stated that the
MoU system was introduced as a major policy initiative by the Central Government as an important tool
to improve the performance of the Central Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs). The New Industrial Policy
of 1991 laid greater emphasis on the MoU system requiring all PSEs to be brought under the ambit of
the MoU system. Beginning with four PSEs in 1987-88, the MoU system has now been extended to a
large number of PSEs. By 2000-2001, 108 PSEs had signed the MoUs.

2.2 It was stated that the main objectives of the MoU system are to improve the performance of the
PSEs by imparting increased autonomy and accountability and to provide a method of evaluation of the
performance of the managements of the PSEs in an objective and transparent manner. Further, the
objective of providing clarity of goals to be achieved by PSEs is another dimension of the MoU system.

2.3. The Committee has noted that there is a High Power Committee (HPC) at the apex level, headed
by the Cabinet Secretary which is basically a policy making body for the MoUs. This HPC consists of
Secretaries of all nodal Departments of Government such as Finance, Expenditure, Planning
Commission and Programme Implementation, etc. The HPC undertakes the evaluation of the
performance of the PSEs and in addition the HPC also provides a forum to resolve inter-ministerial
issues.

2.4 To assist HPC in this task and also to provide the technical inputs and to finalise the MoUs, there
is an Advisory Body called Adhoc Task Force (ATF), it was stated. The members of the ATF are drawn
from various fields having vast experience in their areas and include retired Secretaries, Chief
Secretaries, top experts in different disciplines like finance, management, science and technology, and
prominent academicians. This body provides neutrality and objectivity to the MoU system.

2.5 The Department has also informed that under the present set up there is a small MoU Division
under the Department of Public Enterprises, Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises, which
co-ordinates the entire exercise of finalizing the MoUs in respect of all the PSEs numbering 108. In
addition to finalizing the MoUs, the MoU Division is also responsible for the evaluation of performance
of PSEs, both on the basis of the provisional as well as the audited results.

2.6 Explaining about the process of preparing MoUs, the Department have stated that the MoU
process starts with the issuing of guidelines by the DPE to the PSEs/Administrative Ministries for
drafting the MoUs. The PSEs prepare their MoUs on the basis of these guidelines. These draft MoUs
are then discussed with the Administrative Ministries and in the Boards of the PSEs. They are then
submitted to the DPE. DPE prepares background papers and its comments on each draft MoU keeping
in view the past performance of the PSE, its future potential, the industry scenario and the economic
environment of the PSE, it was added. It was further stated that these draft MoUs are discussed in the
MoU negotiation meetings where the ATF members, managements of the PSE, officials from the
Administrative Ministries and the representatives from nodal Government Agencies like Ministry of
Finance, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation and Planning Commission are also
present. The MoUs and the targets are finalised in these meetings through mutual discussions.

EXPORT CREDIT GUARANTEE CORPORATION
_OF INDIA LIMITED (ECGC)

L PROFILE

1.1 Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India Ltd. (ECGC) was set up in 1957 by the
Government of India to support and strengthen the export promotion efforts of the country. The
corporation provides insurance cover to exporters against non-receipt of the payment for their exports
due to commercial and political risks. Export Credit Insurance seeks to create a favorable climate in
which exporters get timely and liberal facilities from banks. For this purpose, the corporation issues
policies and guarantees. The Registered and Corporate/Head Office of the corporation is located at
Mumbai. There are five regional offices at Mumbai, Chennai, Bangalore, Kolkata and New Delhi and 26
branches are also located in various cities in the country.

1.2... ECGC offers various policies which include standard policies, specific policies, financial
guarantee, transfer guarantee, overseas investment etc. The corporation has designed following five
types of standard policies to provide cover for shipments made on short-term credit :

Shipments (Comprehensive Risks) Policy — to cover both commercial and political risks from the date of

shipment.
Shipments (Political Risk) Policy — to cover only political risk from the date of shipment.

Small Exporters Policy — to cover both commercial and political risks or only political risk from the date
of shipment for the use of Small Exporters.

Contract (comprehensive Risks) Policy- to cover both commercial and political risks from the date of
contract.

Contracts (Political Risks) Policy — to cover both commercial and political risks from the date of
contract.

1.3 Specific policies are offered to provide continuous cover on the shipments of raw material,

consumer goods and consumer durables for which credit period does not exceed 180 days. Contracts for
export of capital goods or tumkey projects or construction works or rendering services abroad are not of
a repetitive nature. Such transactions are therefore insured by ECGC on a case - to — case basis under

specific policies.

1.4. Inorder to encourage banks to give adequate credit and other facilities for exports and protect
them from losses on account of their lending to exporters, ECGC provides financial guarantees which
include packing credit guarantee, export, production, finance guarantee, post-shipment export credit
guarantee, export performance guarantee, and export finance (overseas lending) guarantee. The
company also provides transfer guarantee, overseas investment insurance etc.

Il MISSION OF THE COMPANY
To support the Indian Export Industry by providing cost effective insurance and trade related

services to meet the needs of the Indian export market.

tm. OBJECTIVES OF THE COMPANY
To encourage, facilitate and develop trade between India and other countries.

To provide adequate export credit insurance cover, comparable to similar covers available to exporters
in other countries so that Indian exporters would be able to save themselves from losses that may arise
due to credit risks both commercial and political and also be in a position to maximize their export

business.

To provide the banks in India with export credit guarantee with a view to enable them to extend
adequate credit facilities to the Indian exporters both in pre-shipment and post-shipment stages.

To provide investment insurance to Indian investors undertaking investments in foreign countries

To operate the various schemes in such a manner that the Corporation would generate enough surpluses
to enable it to meet any losses that may result from unforeseen political situations.

To introduce new product lines so as to diversify into trade related services.

V. Discussions with the management of Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC)

5.1 The Sub-Committee interacted with the management of ECGC at Mumbai. The CMD Shri

informed about the losses incurred by ECGC on account of high value claims in 1991 on account of

expected to declare a net profit before tax of Rs.75 crores for 2001-02. The Sub-Committee was also
informed of the corporate plan of ECGC, which included customer specific products to suit the needs of
the customer. The CMD also informed that ECGC had been enhancing its presence by expanding its
existing distribution channels by opening new branches, satellite centres and special promotion
agencies. Besides ECGC is also endeavoring to upgrade the knowledge, skill and expertise of its
manpower through regular training programmes. The Sub-Committee was also informed that ECGC had
introduced Maturity factoring for exporters since 17 April 2002. The CMD was asked to spell out the
future plan of ECGC in context of the liberalization of the Indian economy and in view of the opening of
the insurance sector within the purview of (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority) IRDA.
The Sub-Committee was informed that ECGC will try to transform itself into a multi-product
organization from a single product organization so as to service the requirements of a large number of
customers and give a fillip to its business.

5.2 With respect to the financial position of the company, the Sub-Committee was informed that
against a capital base of Rs.415 crores as on date, the total exposure in terms of maximum liability
undertaken by ECGC is Rs. 40,000 crores. The annual premium income has grown from Rs.89.51 crores
in 1991-92 to Rs.330.45 crore in 2001-02 showing a growth of 269 percent . On asked whether ECGC
is assuming risks disproportionate to its capital base, the Committee was informed that the Government
had agreed to provide a further sum of Rs. 25 crores towards the paid up capital of ECGC during the
current year to raise it from the present level of Rs. 415 crores to Rs. 440 crores.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE

6.1 The Committee is of the view that ECGC should expand the range of its activities to cater to
medium and small-scale exporters. The Committee recommends that ECGC should endeavour towards
specialization based on customer segmentation based on customer size, which has become a necessity in
the present circumstances.

6.2 The Committee after examining the performance of ECGC within the MOU parameters is of the
opinion that weightage should be given to the role played by ECGC in providing impetus to exports in
the small-scale sector. The Committee feels that the SSI sector makes a marked presence in the export
market and comprises a part 45 percent of the India’s exports. The Committee strongly recommends that
ECGC should introduce some special scheme to promote exports from the SSI sector.

6.3. The Committee during the course of the deliberations felt that the customer requirements are
changing and demand for cost effective and efficient service on global standards is growing. The
Committee strongly recommends that ECGC should take immediate steps to become a strong customer
oriented organization.

The Committee is of the opinion that the total exposure in terms of maximum liability underwritten by
ECGC is disproportionate to the present capital base of ECGC. The Committee recommends that

शा. NATIONAL FILM DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION (NEDC)

PROFILE
National Film Development Corporation Ltd. (NFDC) is a Public Sector Enterprise set up in

1975 under the companies Act, 1956.The company was restructured during 1980 by the merger of the
erstwhile Indian Motion Picture Export Corporation (IMPEC) and Film Finance Corporation (FFC) and
the company emerged as Central Agency to promote good cinema in the country. Over the years, NFDC
has provided a wide range of services essential to the integrated growth of Indian cinema Besides
producing and financing films NFDC exports Indian films all over the world and imports commercial
films, Tele-films and serials for distribution and exhibition in the country. Other technical services
provided by NFDC include recording and dubbing, production equipment hiring, subtitling of films,
video duplication, video production, postproduction etc.

VUI. MISSION OF THE COMPANY
To plan and promote an integrated and efficient development of film industry and to be a leader
in fostering excellence in cinema and to develop state of the art infrastructure as well as quality products

in the audio-visual and related fields

Ix. OBJECTIVES
Generation of revenue of Rs. 90.00 crores through the activities mentioned at serial nos. (a) to (e)

below :-
Distribution of Indian and Foreign films through various channels such as theatres and television to
ensure its wider reach.

(b) Promotion of Indian film and television software abroad, through exports by
participation in various international film markets and in other ways.

(c) Import and development of state-of-art pre-production and post- production technical
infrastructure such as camera, editing and recording facilities required by the film industry in Chennai,
Kolkata and Mumbai and hiring out of these facilities.

(d) A beginning to be made in providing appraisals services for the film industry.
(e) To ensure sustained financial appraisal services.
Financing/Producing of about 11 quality films.
Promotion of culture and understanding through film media by organizing foreign and Indian film
weeks in collaboration with Film Societies/State Film Development corporations in India and abroad.
Promotion of social security and welfare measures for needy Cine/Artists of yester years through
Cine Artistes Welfare Fund Of India - the Trust set up by the Corporation.

XII. OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE

The Committee was of the opinion that in today’s world the impact of mass media is tremendous.
However, commercial considerations should not be the sole criteria for production of films. The
Committee strongly feels that films, which are socially relevant, must get preference in NFDC.

The Committee during the course of deliberations was of the opinion that NFDC should play a more
active role towards the popularization of Indian Cinema at the international level. The Committee
recommends that NFDC should provide support to both Indian films and Indian Directors to enable
them to get international exposure and success.

The Committee is of the considered opinion that the film festivals being organized by NFDC are mainly
held in major metropolitan cities. The Committee recommends that film festivals should be held in
smaller towns so as to propagate and promote Indian cinema and to provide wider audience.

The Committee feels that NFDC should help in promoting regional films so as to enable them to get all
India viewer ship. The Committee recommends that NFDC should help in the distribution/exhibition of
regional films by providing Hindi/English subtitles.

The Committee is of the opinion that Indian Cinema cannot remain isolated from the global cinema and
hence steps to bring good quality foreign cinema must be made. The Committee recommends that
NFDC should bring in good quality foreign cinema to India.

The Committee was informed that resource constraint has been a major factor hampering the growth of
NFDC. The Committee feels that with the grant of Industry status to the film industry financial
institutions can finance the production of quality films The Committee strongly recommends that
government should take up the matter with the financial institutions so that more loans are extended
NFDC.
