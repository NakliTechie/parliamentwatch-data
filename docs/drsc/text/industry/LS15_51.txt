3.4 The Ministry of Steel pointed out that BSLC is a financially sick company. Against its 
paid up capital of Rs. 50.00 lakh the accumulated loss till 31 .3.2000 stood at Rs. 189 crores. It 
has various problems like demand constraints. excessive manpower. financial constraints. 
strained industrial relations and so on. The company continues its operations because of 
Government assistance in the form of Plan Loan, Non-Plan Loan, Grant-in-Aid for VRS etc. on 
consideration that mines are located in the backward region and most of the employees of BSLC 
belong to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Categories. If this Company is c l o~d down. 
there rna) be socio-economic problems in that region. The Board of Directors of the com pan). 
after detailed examination of the situation prevailing in BSLC suggested some alternatives as 
regards the future of the company. A meeting was held under the Chairmanship of Minister of 
Steel on 8.9.2000 to consider these alternatives. It was decided that some concrete steps i.e 
Reduction of manpo\\er from 1742 to 1300 by March, 200 I: Non revision of wages: and Off­
take of about 65000 tonnes of dolomite ·per month by SAIL Steel Plants should be taken to 
improve the Company's viability . The Ministry of Mines further stated that it is hoped that with 
these measures the Company would manage to raise enough resources to pay off its statutory 
dues and will not have to depend on Gover:nment of India for non-plan assistance. 
3.5 The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Steel should chalk out concrete 
revival proposal for Bird Group of Companies with the Ministry of Finance to get the 
required amount released from out of general corpus/pool of Rs. 7000 crores for 
restructuring of PSE as announced by the Minister of Finance in the budget 2001-2002 . 
The Committee further recommends that Bird Group of companies management 
should continue the rightsizing of manpower in order to enhance the labour productivity . 
3.6 As regards Bisra Stone Lime Company Ltd. (BSLC) the Committee , taking into 
account the geographical location of BSLC is of the opinion that the Ministry of Steel 
should leave no stone unturned for survival of the company. Besides the efforts made for 
having a clearer balance sheet , BSLC management should strive for seeking sufficient 
work order. rationalization of manpower in a specified timeframe and smooth industrial 
relations to have a better future. 

3.7 The Committee also recommends that as decided in the meeting held by the Minster to

24. Problems in Exports and Imports 
24.1 The Secretary, during the course of his oral evidence, informed the Committee " unless and 
until, we have export market for ourselves, our industry will not be able to sustain for itself. We 
have been able to export 3 million tonnes in a year. But, in the recent past, countries like the U.S. 
and Europe have imposed anti-dump ing duties. On the one hand, the U.S. talks about WTO 
compatibi lity, and whenever India takes any act ion to safeguard its industry, they say that it 
should be done as per the provisions of the WTO; whereas, on the ot her hand, in their own 
country, they are placing all sorts of restrictions and imposing very high duties the Secretary 
s~ated. He further stated that recently, in the Clinton regime, they passed an Act which provides 
that the revenue collected by way of anti-dumping duties could be passed on to the steel units. 
S:>, it is essential for us to keep our foothold in the internati onal market , the Secretary felt. 
Another problem is imports in the form of inflow of seconds and defectives at a very cheap rate 
the Secretary informed. That is also harming the domestic steel industry. The Ministry of Steel 
have taken up the matter with the Ministry of Commerce, and after a great deal of persuasion , the 
Ministry of Comme rce have agreed to impose some sort of non-tariff barriers, at least, for 
stconds and defectives, the Secretary added. 
24.2 The Comm ittee understand that exports are essential ingredieht of the growth of industry. 
Similarly, imports should not ca use glaring damag e to the indigenous industry. The Committee 
therefore recommends that Ministry of Commerce may take necessary steps to find solution, to 
the problems of our steel industry faced in internati ona l market in respect of exports and from the 
imports. Ministry of Steel may pursue the matter with Ministry of Com merce. 
25. Railway Freight 
25.1 The Secretary informed the Comm ittee that because of the two percent increase in the freight 
in the Railway Budget of 2001-2002 they have been adversely affected. In fact, it is only the 
public sector companies like SAIL and RINL which are using rail for transporting their finished 
products. The new steel plants are using road traffic for transporting their finished products. 

Even this two per cent hike in freight is going to cost the SAIL about Rs. 28 crores more and to 
RINL about Rs. I 0 crores more, the Secretary added. 
25.2. The Secretary also informed that another problem with the Railways is relating to 
transport of iron-ore from Bailadila to Vizag. When that railway line was constructed, the 
Railways incurred a lot of expenditure on constructing that line. At that time, it was decided to 
levy some additional freight on transportation of iron-ore. The additional freight was charged for 
the entire sector. It was also decided at that time that when the traffic would reach the level of 60 
lakh tonnes per annum, that additional freight would be withdrawn . Today, the traffic has 
reached to 120 lakh tonnes per annum. But it is still continuing. The Secretary also stated that 
they have taken up this issue with the Ministry of Railways and there is a strong case for 
withdrawal on this surcharge. 
25.3 The Committee understands that the two percent hike in railway freight would have a 
severe impact on SAIL and RINL. Ministry of Steel may take up the matter with Railways. The 
Committee also feels that the additional freight charged on transportation of iron ore from 
Baladila to Vizag should have been stopped after it reached the level of 60 lakh tonnes per annum 
as per the decision taken at that time. Now, it has reached 120 lakh tonnes. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry of Steel may pursue the matter with Railways and Railways also 
may consider removing this at the earliest. 
26. Anti Dumping 
26.1 The Secretary Ministry of Steel during the course of oral evidence stated ,"the USA has 
the largest market for our exports. The anti dumping duty is against a company specific and 
product specific . But, there is a legal battle. In some cases, we have won and in some cases we 
have lost. Suppose, some company is exporting against contract in USA. The moment anti­
dumping duty is imposed, that schedule is affected for next six months . What happens in USA is 
that they first impose preliminary determination in very large number of cases, and just on first 
hearing, they see what is the preliminary case and they impose a duty of 40-45% . Generally , 
after hearing both the parties 50% of case are disposed of in this way an early stage. 
Whether the duty is conformed or it is not confirmed , is decided later . When the Committee 
sought to know as to why this cannot be done in India, he informed the Committee they ," we 
have made a request before the Ministry of Commerce.... We want some amendment to the 
procedures. We have already requested the Ministry that provisional duties must be imposed ". 
26.2 The Committee observes with concern that the anti-dumping system that is prevailing in 
other countries is different from that of ours. In the anti-dumping system of our country, 
complaints are received and after examination of those complaints , the witnes ses are called . Then 
the whole thing is enquired upon and it takes a long time, may be one year or six months. Later 
on, the decision is taken. During this period of complaint and inquiry, the materials will 
automatically be coming into the country. Then after one year, if it is found that this import was 
not required, then imposition of certain restrictions is resorted to. But by that time, the damage 
would have taken place to the industry or to the trade . The Committee feels that the time has 
come for our country to think and act on it seriously. The Committee recommends that our anti­
dumping law also may be brought at par with that of other countries, particularly America.
