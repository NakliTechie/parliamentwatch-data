6.         In addition to the waivers of dues worth Rs. 2562.17 crores, a total of Rs. 1011.27 crores were invested as fresh capital input.
 
7.         The Committee observed that with a huge investment and waivers to the tune of Rs. 3573.44 crores, almost all the PSEs
relapsed into losses within couple of years of  their  revival. The Department of HI informed that the main reasons for the PSEs
relapsing into losses were lack of remunerative orders, working capital shortage, technological obsolescence, erosion in their
competitiveness, excess  manpower etc.
 
8.         The reply of D/O HI indicated that the revival was neither comprehensive enough to improve technological profile and
working capital constraints nor was it able to improve the competitive prospects of the PSEs which continued to rely upon the
Government and its PSEs for orders in a scenario where Government was itself opening up.
 
 
 
General growth and outlook for heavy industry and auto sector.
 
9.         During the year 2006-07, the PSEs under DHI recorded a growth of 26.65%.  In the last  year, 2007-08 (upto Feb’08) these
PSEs have clocked a growth of 14.52%.  However Auto Industry in the year 2007-08 had shown a decline of 2.3% in financial
terms.  As regards capital goods, growth in April’07 – Jan’08 has been 18.3% as compared to overall industrial growth of 8.7%.
           
10.       The Committee noted the assertion of Department that the PSEs are likely to end FY 08-09 with a production of Rs 31150.52
crores. The aggregate profit before the Tax in 08-09 is likely to rise to Rs 3702.76 crores.
 
11.       The Committee wondered if such a growth has factored in the huge waivers and subsidies given through the
Budgetary support. The Committee also observed that of the 34 operational units in case of  about 18 PSEs the order books
have either stagnated or shown decline in 2007 besides the fact that most of such orders are from the Government or PSEs.

Furthermore given the precarious financial conditions, these PSEs may require Government’s support for the working
capital. As such the Committee feels that the PSEs are functioning more as Government’s Departments instead of
autonomous sustainable commercial entities. The Committee also noted that the export earnings in 07-08 are likely to come
down and that only 9 PSEs are likely to show export earnings.
 
12.       Despite Department’s optimism the Committee finds the financial performance of heavy engineering and machinery
sectors as precarious considering that the capital goods sector recorded an impressive growth of 18.3 %. The Committee
recommends that the performance against the  MoU signed with the PSEs must also reflect   the cash and non-cash
Budgetary support given to the PSE.

18.       The Committee has observed with reservation the delay in making reference of BRPSE’s recommendations to the
competent authority. Considering that the BRPSE includes the Secretaries of the Ministry of Finance, Department of Public
Enterprises and that it relies significantly on the inputs of administrative Departments, the Committee finds the practice of
further referring its recommendations Inter-Ministerial Group or the Committee of Secretaries as superfluous and time
consuming. The views of the Government on the BRPSE’s recommendations are further ratified by the BIFR. The
Committee objects to such a practice that leads to further critical delays. On the one hand BRPSE is not provided with
adequate budget to make its own independent assessment and is made to rely upon the administrative Ministry, on the other
hand its recommendations are further referred to a collateral body of Secretaries.   In view of the Committee such a practice
only undermines the recommendations of the BRPSE.

21.       The Committee was further informed that revival plans also included One time settlement with Banks/FIs, Land/Plant
machinery sale and the Contribution by State Governments.  The BRPSE informed the Committee that  revival packages do
envisage infusion of funds from the Government for the payment of dues to the banks/FIs. The BRPSE had recommended infusion
of funds by the Government to the tune of Rs. 110.62 crores for the One Time Settlement ( OTS) with Banks in respect of CCI,
AYCL and TSL. As part of OTS the Banks/FIs also agreed to waive interest of Rs. 102.98 crores.
 
22.       The Committee has repeatedly observed against the current revival packages being recommended by the BRPSE which
largely aim at clearing the books of the Company, without any sustainable revival through capital infusion. In name of revival the
Government after clearing the books eventually disinvests its ownership in favour of a strategic partner through a JV. The
Committee therefore sought to know if the BRPSE had also recommended Capital infusion for the revival of PSEs. Details in respect
of individual PSEs were sought.

23.       The Committee recalled that the NCMP stated of the efforts to modernize and restructure the sick PSEs and that the
chronically loss-making Companies would either be sold off or closed after paying legitimate dues and compensation. However it
sought to know that after the passage of four years, if the BRPSE was able to evolve a criteria to distinguish between revivable sick
PSEs and the chronically loss making PSEs to be closed.   The Committee also observed that the cost of revival was mostly  met
through the Budgetary support and the PSEs were not able to generate Internal and Extra Budgetary resources for revival. The
Committee was also concerned over the safety and security of Government loans that are converted into equity as part of revival.
 
24.       In its written reply the BRPSE stated :
 
24.1.    “that  Sick CPSEs are unable to generate/raise funds either internally or from market due to their weak financial position of
incurring losses and negative net worth.  Therefore, it may be necessary to restructure their Balance sheet and improve their financial
position by way of conversion of loans into equity and waiver/write off of loans/interest, etc by Government.  If the financial
position of the PSEs is not strengthened, the accumulated losses, negative networth, etc will continue to remain in their books which
may pre-empt the prospects of their revival.  The financial restructuring of sick CPSEs will enable them to improve their profitability
in future and raise funds from Banks for their capital expenditure and working capital requirements.
 
24.2     The loans for CPSEs sanctioned by the Government are generally unsecured as the entire/majority ownership of the CPSEs is
vested with the Government.  The competent authority decides on case by case basis, the amount of infusion of founds in the form of
equity, loan, grant or a mix of them keeping in view the minimum amount required for the sustainable revival of the CPSEs. 
Further, proposals which envisage funding the revival package through sale of surplus land/assets are supported.  The CPSEs are
expected to post net profit (profit after meeting interest and other liabilities) after implementation of the revival package and repay
the loans taken from the Government.”
 
25.       Considering the performance of past revival efforts, the Committee is concerned over the security of fresh
Government investments in the revival of PSEs. The reply of the BRPSE does not indicate any mechanism in the revival
package to secure Government’s investment.

mistakes in the figures provided by the Department. The Committee takes cognisance of the reply presented by the Ministry
of Finance which concedes that while classification on the basis of Capital and Revenue expenditure was a constitutional
requirement adding to it another sub classification of Planned and Non-planned expenditure, just because it has been in
vogue and institutionalised has led arbitrariness, which even the representatives of the Department have found difficult to
elaborate. In absence of a convincing reply from the Department, the Committee gets an impression that the Department
and its relevant wing too could not comprehend the current format of classification. Absence of any standardised definition
of the four hybridised categories obviously has serious consequences and may have resulted in arbitrariness. The Committee
in agreement with the observation made in the Economic Survey regarding Planned and Non-Planned expenditure.  It is the

expenditures. The Committee therefore feels that if a classification can not be explained even by the representatives of the
Department and the Planning Commission, it must be done away with. The Committee recommends that in view of the reply
from the Ministry of Finance and the observations made in the Economic Survey,  the Financial Advisor to the Department
must persue with the Ministry of Finance to stop an institutionalised  practice in favour of constitutional requirement.

Concluding Observations
 
204.     In conclusion the Committee expresses its serious reservation over the performance of the Department in managing
its financial affairs. That significant part of the allocation remains unspent despite seeking higher RE, indicates an avoidable
tendency of hoarding the allocations and this has been the a predominant trend as far as the DHI is concerned.  That bulk of
the  expenditure is spent in last week of the FY indicates the quality of expenditure. Going by the replies given by the
Department in respect of allocations for the revival of PSEs, it appears that entire revival process is so tentative and ad-hoc
which makes even distribution of expenditure through the financial year,  impossible. That there has been a persistent and
serious imbalance in annual Planned and Non-Planned expenditures, with Non-Planned expenditure overshooting the annual
budget estimates every year in contrast to the Planned allocations that have remained unspent in most of the cases, is an
alarming pointer to the quality of revival   of the PSEs and the sustainability of such revival. Such apprehensions of the
Committee are corroborated by the revival packages of individual PSEs. Regardless the directions of the Ministry of Finance
regarding Government’s loans to the PSEs, the revival packages are charters of waivers and concessions without any
provisions to secure the Government’s investments. With such generous waivers and Budgetary support, it was only
expected that the PSEs shall start showing profit. However, the fact is that Order book positions of several of the revivable
PSEs have already started showing decline or stagnation.
 
205.     The performance of the two major Planned schemes namely NATRIP and Modernisation of Capital Goods Sector,
has been rather unsatisfactory. The two schemes have suffered crippling delays due to administrative bottlenecks and
extraneous considerations. The Funds in case of NATRIP have remained unspent, whereas in case of Modernisation of

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA

Capital Goods Sector, the Planning Commission has delayed the process.
 
206.     In view of above the Committee recommends that in additions to the observations made at appropriate places in the
Report,  the DHI must justify the allocations made the Demands, the Department must also ensure the quality and efficiency
of such expenditure.
 
207.     In view of the experience previous years, the Committee recommends to the Ministry of Finance to scrupulously
monitor the usage of allocations before committing any Supplementary Demands. In this regard the Committee notes that
the Department does not seek cash demands in the Supplementary Demands, instead Token and Technical allocations are
sought. The Committee sought to know from where these token and technical allocations were drawn. However, no reply was
provided by the Department.  The Committee apprehends if the savings from several under-utilised allocations are relocated
in the Supplementary Demands. It is desired that the DHI should apprise the source of such savings in its future

52.62 crores in respect of HPC/NPPC was waived till the February 2008.
(Para 126)
 
            Similarly, waiver of Loans in respect of NIL, BYNL and HPC/NPPC as reflected in RE 07-08 were sought in the first
Supplementary Demand in 2007. Yet as per the quarter-wise information provided by the DHI, only Rs 29.87 crores in
respect of HPC/NPPC were waived till February 2008.
(Para 127)
 
            In view of above the Committee takes strong objection to the practice of seeking Supplementary Demands in the
beginning of a Financial Year without actually utilising the same till the very end of Financial Year. The Committee
observed this recurrent trend in almost all the allocations made to the DHI.
(Para 128)
 
            The Committee also objects to the practice of keeping sundry Loans for settling VRS/Statutory and salary dues as
Planned expenditure, irrespective of the objective of such loans which may eventually be waived as Non-Planned
expenditure.
(Para 129)
 
            The Committee observed that in RE 07-08, Rs 226.43 crores were allocated towards writing down the equity in
AYCL, Rs 2.31 crores for BYNL and Rs. 8.31 crores for NIL. The allocations in these respect were sought in very first
Supplementary Demands, however as per the information provided by the DHI, such writing off was actually implemented
in the last month of Financial Year 07-08. Considering that the Competent Authority had approved revival of AYCL and
NIL in February 2007 and that writing off was implemented in March 2008, the Committee reiterates its concern over delay
in implementing the revival plans by the DHI. The Committee also expresses its serious reservations over the recurrent
pattern of spending bulk of allocations in the very last month of Financial Year. Such pattern poses question over quality
and efficiency of allocations.
(Para 135)
 
            The Committee also notes that NIL and BYNL are unlisted companies. As such what purpose could be served by
writing down the equity when its actual worth remains unknown.  The Committee may also be informed as to how the extent
of writing down the equity of an unlisted Company is determined. The Committee may also be informed about the
instructions of the M/O Finance in case of writing down of equity.
 (Para 136)
 
            Even if the conversion was only a book adjustment and did not entail cash out go, the Committee wonders that
against a token allocation of Rs 1 lakh indicated in RE 07-08, a  Supplementary Demands was sought to convert loan of Rs
116.52 crores into equity. Considering that such conversion had financial implications in terms of Government’s investment,
the full amount instead of Token amount should have been indicated in the SBE in the RE for 07-08. The Committee is
unconvinced over such budgetary practice of indicating the token amount in RE and seeks reply from the Department and
M/O Finance.
(Para 139)
 
            The Committee questions if it is proper on the part of Government to implement the revival in a way to avoid tax.
Should this at all be a consideration in a revival package.  The Committee only wonders if similar options are available to
private companies in respect of their liabilities towards FIs/Banks.
(Para 142)
 
            The Committee expressed is serious dismay over the delay in settling the issue between the technology developer

            The Committee also observes the uneven  release  of funds out of the Lumpsum allocation   for loans to settle

(Para 173)
 
            The Committee observed that while two lumpsum allocations are made  - Rs 250 crores to provide loans to settle the

used to serve the same purpose of settling the VRS/Statutory/salary dues. Given the commonality of objectives of both the
allocations, the Committee wonders if there were at a need to make two distinct lumpsum allocations. The Committee seeks a
detailed rationale for retaining the two distinct allocations for largely similar objective.
 (Para 182)
 
            The Committee observed that the terms and conditions prescribed by the Ministry of Finance have been repeatedly
relaxed in case of PSEs. The Committee should be informed of the applicability of the direction issued by the M/O Finance in
respect of availing the loans by the PSEs on their revival packages considering that most of the PSEs had availed waivers
during previous revival attempt in the 1990s. The Committee should also be informed if new budgetary outlays adjust the
defaulted amount as prescribed by the Finance Ministry.   The Committee recommends that terms and conditions on the
returns on the Governments loans must be incorporated in the MoU signed by the PSEs.
(Para 188)
 
            The Committee suggests if the total of the relocations from the lumpsum allocations to individual PSEs matches up
with the Rs 250 crores and Rs 150 crores, than what is the rationale of reducing the amount in RE, considering that the RE

misleading without appropriate explanatory details.
(Para 191)
 
 
            Considering that BRPSE had already given its recommendations in respect of most of the PSEs referred to it, the
Committee finds the reply of the Department intransigent in view of the fact that the relocations to individual PSEs out of
such lumpsum are made only towards the end of the Financial Year and that the option of seeking additional funds as per
the approved revival plan in the Supplementary Demands is always open and preferable instead of hoarding the funds. 
(Para 195)
 
 
            The Committee observed that Rs 25 crores were retained in the RE 07-08, yet only Rs 12.69 crores could be spent in

            In conclusion the Committee expresses its serious reservation over the performance of the Department in managing
its financial affairs. That significant part of the allocation remains unspent despite seeking higher RE, indicates an avoidable
tendency of hoarding the allocations and this has been the a predominant trend as far as the DHI is concerned.  That bulk of
the  expenditure is spent in last week of the FY indicates the quality of expenditure. Going by the replies given by the
Department in respect of allocations for the revival of PSEs, it appears that entire revival process is so tentative and ad-hoc
which makes even distribution of expenditure through the financial year,  impossible. That there has been a persistent and
serious imbalance in annual Planned and Non-Planned expenditures, with Non-Planned expenditure overshooting the annual
budget estimates every year in contrast to the Planned allocations that have remained unspent in most of the cases, is an
alarming pointer to the quality of revival   of the PSEs and the sustainability of such revival. Such apprehensions of the
Committee are corroborated by the revival packages of individual PSEs. Regardless the directions of the Ministry of Finance
regarding Government’s loans to the PSEs, the revival packages are charters of waivers and concessions without any
provisions to secure the Government’s investments. With such generous waivers and Budgetary support, it was only
expected that the PSEs shall start showing profit. However, the fact is that Order book positions of several of the revivable
PSEs have already started showing decline or stagnation.
(Para 204)
 
            The performance of the two major Planned schemes namely NATRIP and Modernisation of Capital Goods Sector,
has been rather unsatisfactory. The two schemes have suffered crippling delays due to administrative bottlenecks and
extraneous considerations. The Funds in case of NATRIP have remained unspent, whereas in case of Modernisation of
Capital Goods Sector, the Planning Commission has delayed the process.
(Para 205)
 
            In view of above the Committee recommends that in additions to the observations made at appropriate places in the
Report,  the DHI must justify the allocations made the Demands, the Department must also ensure the quality and efficiency
of such expenditure.
(Para 206)
 
            In view of the experience previous years, the Committee recommends to the Ministry of Finance to scrupulously
monitor the usage of allocations before committing any Supplementary Demands. In this regard the Committee notes that
the Department does not seek cash demands in the Supplementary Demands, instead Token and Technical allocations are
sought. The Committee sought to know from where these token and technical allocations were drawn. However, no reply was
provided by the Department.  The Committee apprehends if the savings from several under-utilised allocations are relocated
in the Supplementary Demands. It is desired that the DHI should apprise the source of such savings in its future
Supplementary Demands.
(Para 207)
 
            Further, in its previous Reports too the Committee had commented upon the ambiguous classification of expenditures
and their inadequate explanation in SBE. In view of the inputs given the  Ministry of Finance, Planning Commission and the
observation made in the Economic Survey, the Committee feels that it is time that the Government must remove the existing
ambiguities which can not be explained even  by the senior representatives of the Departments.
