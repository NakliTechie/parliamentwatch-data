REPORT
In the early 1950s, India had only five CPSEs with an investment of around ` 29 crore. Today,
the country has 320 CPSEs with ` 11, 71,844 crore cumulative investment. CPSEs were the harbingers
of industrial development, indigenisation and self reliance in India. They have played an unimpeachable
role in building infrastructure, capital intensive industries, national security, public services, etc. at an
affordable rate. The Public Sector Companies balanced social development along with economic prosperity
and brought in employment on a large scale. When the era of globalization dawned and the initial phase
of nation building was over, the Government of the day took a call to liberalize Indian Economy.
Accordingly, the New Industrial Policy, 1991 came about and it revealed the lack of market competitiveness
and inefficiency of our CPSEs in comparison to the private sector. To adapt to the new economic order,
the CPSEs underwent extensive disinvestment of Government equity, downsizing/retrenchment and other
measures of organizational recalibration. Redundant CPSEs were closed down and, now, specific areas
have been identified as "High Priority Area" for the Government to remain in business. Currently, even
profit making CPSEs face disinvestment if they do not"fall in the areas of business so identified.
However, despite extensive privatization in many areas, Government companies remain significant. There
are still many areas that have to be controlled by the Government in a country like India - basic
infrastructures like road transport and railways, electricity, banking, material recourses extraction, defence,
aerospace, shipbuilding, etc. are some. Further, promotion of R&D and innovations, delivery of public
goods and services, Make in India initiatives, balanced regional development and social security also
remain key areas for CPSEs to deliver, in the Indian context.

Department of Public Enterprises (DPE)
4. In their 52nd Report, the Estimates Committee of 3rd Lok Sabha (1962-67) stressed the need for
setting up a centralized coordinating unit, which could also make continuous appraisal of the performance
of public enterprises. This led to the setting up of the Bureau of Public Enterprises (BPE) in 1965 in
the Ministry of Finance. Subsequently, as a result of the reorganization of the Ministries/Departments of
the Union Government in September, 1985. BPE was made a full-fledged Department as the Department
of Public Enterprises (DPE). At that time it was made part of the Ministry of Industry. Presently, it is
part of the Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises.
5. The DPE is the nodal department for all the Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) and formulates
policy pertaining to CPSEs. It lays down, in particular, policy guidelines on performance improvement
and evaluation, autonomy and financial delegation and personnel management in CPSEs. It, furthermore,
collects and maintains information on several areas in respect of CPSEs.
6. As per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government, the following subjects have been
allocated to the DPE:-
1. Residual work relating to erstwhile Bureau of Public Enterprises including Industrial Management
Pool.
2. Coordination of matters of general policy affecting all Public Sector Enterprises.
3. Evaluation and monitoring the performance of Public Sector Enterprises, including the
Memorandum of Understanding mechanism.
4. Matters relating to Permanent Machinery of Arbitration for the Public Sector Enterprises.
5. Counselling, training and rehabilitation of employees in Central Public Sector Undertakings
under V oluntary Retirement Scheme.
6. Review of capital projects and expenditure in Central Public Sector Enterprises.
7. Measures aimed at improving performance of Central Public Sector Enterprises and other
capacity building initiatives of Public Sector Enterprises.
8. Rendering advice relating to revival, restructuring or closure of Public Sector Enterprises
including the mechanisms therefor.
9. Matters relating to Standing Conference of Public Enterprises.
10. Matter relating to International Centre for Public Enterprises.
11. Categorization of Central Public-Sector Enterprises including conferring 'Ratna' status.
12. Survey of Public Enterprises.
7. Regarding the publication of Public Enterprises Survey, it has been submitted to the Committee that
with a view to improve the analytical content, overall design and presentation of Public Enterprises
Survey, the Department invited bids from external agencies of national repute. The study to upgrade the

8. According to the Annual Report of the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises the
Vision and Mission statements of DPE are as follows:
Vision
"Effective, Profitable and Globally Competitive Central Public Sector Enterprises"
Mission
"To continuously improve management and performance of CPSEs through Corporate Governance,

9. The Committee is aware that the realm of CPSEs has recently under gone major changes,
especially, since the Government has decided to restrict itself only to a few selected priority areas
of business. Even the CPSEs that are profitable, but not belonging to specified areas of business,
are being disinvested. In this scenario, the Committee is of the considered opinion that the
CPSEs that would remain in operation, after hiving off of the earlier said non-priority companies,
would need clear guidance regarding efficient functioning and other inputs required to sustain
themselves successfully. Here, the Committee takes note that the mandate of DPE includes the
following:
'Measures aimed at improving performance of Centra! Public Sector Enterprises and other capacity
building initiatives of Public Sector Enterprises.
Rendering advice relating to revival, restructuring or closure of Public Sector Enterprises including
the mechanisms therefor."
In the opinion of the Committee, the above mentioned mandates of DPE under the Allocation
of Business Rules, entails DPE to be empowered to play a more proactive role than their current
profile. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that LPE should widen the horizon of its activities,
under the said ambit, to support and facilitate the revival/efficient functioning of CPSEs in
future. To achieve this, the Committee feels that DPE has to think beyond their current set of
activities, for which they can be well endowed with as per the business allocated to them, to
actually play the role expected of a nodal department.

Counselling, Retraining & Redeployment (CRR) Scheme for Separated Employees of CPSEs.
21. DPE is implementing CRR Scheme since the year 2001-02 as a social safety net to provide
opportunities of self/wage employment to the employees or dependents of CPSEs separated under
V oluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS)/V oluntary Separation Scheme (VSS) or retrenched due to closure/
restructuring of the enterprises. The main elements of the CRR programme are Counselling, Retraining
and Redeployment The Scheme aims at providing short duration skill development/entrepreneurship
development training programmes to equip the beneficiaries for self/wage employment. In September,
2016, a tripartite agreement Was signed between DPE, National Skill Development Fund (NSDF) and
National Skill Development Council (NSDC) under Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship
(MSDE) to initiate a skill development project with the objective to provide skill training under National
Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) to the target group of the CRR scheme. NSDC being a
facilitator ensures that training programmes are as per National Occupational Standard (NOS), which
have been set forth by the Sector Skills Council (SSC) resulting in certification and augmentation of

10
redeployability of the trainees under the Scheme. This step broadened the Network of Training providers
and also introduced standardized methodology of training design and delivery. During the oral evidence,
as part of the examination of Demands. 2018-19 of DPE. Secretary, DPE informed that the process of
third party evaluation of the CRR scheme has been initiated.
22. Objectives of the CRR Scheme are:
1. Separated employees of CPSE to be brought into the mainstream economy and thereby
contribute to national income.
2. Reorientation of VRS /VSS optees / dependent to enable them to adjust to new environment
and adopt new vocations.
3. Skill development for VRS optees / dependent for their redeployment.
4. Exposure to various industry associations, which is also a component of the CRR scheme,
to help the trainee in product / service selection.
5. Awareness of various central and state government loan assistance/subsidized schemes.

following criterion:
(i) Classification of CPSEs as 'high' or 'low' priority
The CPSEs have been considered 'High priority' for the Government if they fit in any of the
following criteria:
(a) National security
(b) Sovereign function at arm's length
(c) Market imperfection and public purpose
The CPSEs not covered in the above criteria are reckoned as 'low priority' for the Government
notwithstanding that they may be profit making companies. Tow priority' CPSEs have been considered
for Strategic Disinvestment notwithstanding that they may be profit earning.
(ii) Economic principles
The NITI Ayog was guided by basic economic principles that Government should have no business
to continue to engage itself in marketing/producing goods and services in sectors where competitive
market has come to age, and such entities are most likely to perform better in private hands due to
various factors e.g. technology upgradation and efficiency management practices and add GDP of the
country.
(iii) Tr eatment of subsidiaries and holding companies:
NITI Ayog has restricted its analysis to operating company for Strategic Disinvestment irrespective
of the fact whether it was holding company or a subsidiary. Where subsidiary CPSEs are tightly
connected into productions / manufacturing chain or business process of the parent company, such
entities have been considered alongwith parent company.
(iv) Unblocking the Value through Strategic Disinvestment
Further revenue optimization for the Government was also one of the factors, though not the only
guiding criteria. It was of the view that real unlocking of value through better/efficient management and/
or of latest technology by private sector may bring much higher dividends for the economy as a whole
in terms of GDP growth.

CPSEs, it is found that the major leveraging of CAPEX has been done by the Oil Sector PSUs,
the National Highway Authority of India (NHAI) and Power Generation and Distribution Companies.
Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) topped the performance by achieving a target of 218.88
%. The Committee observes that such investment of capital in building the infrastructure of
country will have multiplier effect on the growth of the economy. It also observes that DPE is
effectively playing their role by encouraging CPSEs to pledge more CAPEX by making the same
a MoU evaluation parameter. In this regard, the Committee is of the opinion that the relevance
of CPSEs, in the market driven economy with overwhelming private party presence, is highlighted
by the fact that when the private sectors were sceptical of such investment, it was the CPSEs
whom met the need of the hour of nation building by pooling in CAPEX for infrastructure
development during the recent past. (Para No. 34)
The Committee find that as the nodal Department of all CPSEs, the guidelines issued by
DPE governs important aspects of CPSEs like disposal of movable and immovable assets of
CPSEs identified for closure, revival /restructuring of CPSEs, etc. In this regard, the Committee
observes that CPSEs that are approved for closure, even before the issue of the DPE guidelines
on disposal of assets, have also decided to follow the proposed revised guidelines. Here, the
Committee also acknowledges the fact that the said guidelines of the Department are currently
under the consideration of Cabinet Secretariat for approval of the revision suggested by Committee
on Secretaries. The Committee desires that once the revised guidelines on time bound closure
of the sick/loss making CPSEs and disposal of movable and immovable assets are finalized, the
Committee may be apprised of the same. (Para No. 39)
With regard to disposal of land and other assets of CPSEs, the Committee recommends that
DPE being the nodal Department of all CPSEs should collect from the respective administrative
Ministries/Departments the relevant data and compile and maintain the same with them.
(Para No. 40)

Further , the Committee takes note of the information submitted by DPE as collected by
them from DIP AM about the strategic disinvestment of CPSEs and the proceeds that accrue to
the Government exchequer from sale/disinvestment of CPSEs. It is found that receipts from
disinvestment during 2017-18 is expected to be around ````` 1,00,000 crore , while an amount of
````` 80,000 crore is supposed to accrue to Government during 2018-19. The Committee, is aware
that the Government has decided to engage in business only in a few selected areas of 'High
Priority'. However, the Committee is of the firm opinion that while making a decision to disinvest
CPSEs, especially those are profit making, the Government should give due consideration to the
jobs that are supported by those CPSEs, the track record of their contribution to the national
economy, their CAPEX creation potential and also their role in balancing the social/regional
fabric. Regarding the revival/restructuring/modernization of CPSEs, the Committee observes
that timely approval of these plans, with accurate cost estimates, availability of funds with the
Government and the timely disposal of such finds are crucial factors. Accordingly, the Committee
recommends that a defined portion of the proceeds from disinvestment should be kept aside by
the Government for funding the revival/restructuring/modernization proposals of CPSEs as identified
approved by the Government. In this manner, the Government can extend a hand holding to the
select sick CPSEs that have the potential to turn around and sustain itself in future. (Para No.
41)
The Committee recommends that DPE may use its good offices to sensitise CPSEs to invest
their CSR fund on activities that are gainful to the society. (Para No. 43)
