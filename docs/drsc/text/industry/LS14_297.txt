REPORT 
 
The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Sector has emerged as a highly vibrant 
and dynamic sector of the Indian economy over the last few decades. MSMEs not only play crucial 
role in providing large scale employment opportunities at comparatively lower capital cost than 
large industries but also help industrialization of rural and backward areas, thereby, reducing 
regional imbalances, assuring more e quitable distribution of national income and resources. 
MSMEs are complementary to large industries as ancillary units and this sector contributes 
enormously to the socio -economic development of the country. They are also providing uniform 
development to t he society and can be a strong mean s to utilize the natural resources of India.  If 
this contribution is to be sustained, then their uniqueness needs to be nurtured in an overt and 
explicit manner. The MSMEs are producing diverse range of products and serv ices to meet demand 
of domestic as well as global markets. The MSME Sector is also proving  to be a boon for aspiring 
entrepreneurs in India.  
2. The Ministry of Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises also presented a progressive MSME 
Sector by promoting growth and development of the Sector, including Khadi, Village and Coir 
Industries, in cooperation with concerned Ministries/Departments, State Governments and other 
Stakeholders, through providing support to existing enterprises, adopting cutting -edge technologies 
and encouraging creation of new enterprises.

4. There are around 6.34 crore enterprises in the MSME Sector in India contributing around 
28.9% to the GDP. Out of the total MSMEs, around 31% are operating in Manufacturing Sector, 
36% are in Trading Sector and 33% are in Service Sector. MSME Sector is manufacturing a wide 
range of products ranging from tradi tional charkha to hi -tech spare parts that gives a sense of pride 
to MSME Sector to be associated with remarkable projects like Chandrayaan-II.  But for equitable 
growth of the MSME Sector, it is imperative that MSMEs have presence all over the country rat her 
than being limited to only few states. If MSME Sector is nurtured properly, it can provide a 
sustainable growth to the country and provide large scale employment opportunities, so it is 
imperative that proper attention is given to MSME Sector. 
5. The Primary responsibility of promotion and development of MSMEs is with the State 
Government. The role of the Ministry of MSME and its organizations is to assist the States in their 
efforts to encourage entrepreneurship, employment, livelihood opportunities a nd enhance the 
competitiveness of MSMEs in the changed economic scenario. 
6. In fact, the Committee on Industry, while reviewing the implementation of MSMED Act, 
2006 in the year 2013 had  taken up the issue in its 245 th Report also and had recommended ther ein 
that considering the inflation and dynamic market situation, the definition of MSMEs, as provided 
in the MSMED Act, 2006, may need to be revised suitably after every five years. Still further, in its 
258th Report, the Committee had recommended that, if  needed, the Act be amended to make 
„definition‟ flexible. The Committee, therefore, feels that this change in „definition‟ of investment 
should be revised and changed as a considerable passage of time since the year 2006 has elapsed 
and inflation effect has headed northwards with the passage of time. 
7. Hence, the Committee is finally of the opinion that a change in criteria of classification of 
MSMEs from existing criteria of investment in plant and machinery or equipment to a new criteria 
based on annual turnover of the enterprise is the need of the hour and accordingly recommends that 
the MSMED Act, 2006 be amended as recommended by the Committee in its 294th Report.  
8. The present classification of MSMEs based on investment in plant and machinery has 
been effective since the adoption of the MSMED Act, 2006. However, the extant financial 
limits fixed in 2006 for MSMEs are no longer relevant given the impact of inflation.  Most of 
the Industry Associations and stakeholders have, therefore, expressed the n eed for changing 

3 
 
the present criteria of investment in plant and machinery to annual turnover as criterion for 
classification of MSMEs. 
9. The Committee is of the view that the present classification based on investment in 
plant and machinery also has a nu mber of disadvantages as it prevents MSMEs to become 
competitive in the market, inhibits investment to modernize, upscale, improve productivity 
and technology upgradation due to rigor of investment thresholds. On the other hand, taking 
turnover as a criter ia shall overcome the said shortcomings as it can be pegged with reliable 
data available in GST Network and other methods of ascertaining, which will help in having a 
non-discretionary, transparent and objective criteria and will eliminate the need for 
inspections and make the classification system progressive and evolutionary. 
10. It is observed by the Committee that the growth of MSMEs is concentrated mostly in a 
few States only, which induces the people to migrate to those areas in search of employment 
and livelihood. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Ministry should work in the 
direction of removing all the existing geographical imbalances and ensuring that equitable 
presence of MSMEs is there in all the States so that there is industrialization and employment 
generation in all the States.  
11. The Ministry of MSME consist of Small & Medium Enterprises (SME) Division, Agro & 
Rural Industry (ARI) Division, Integrated Finance (IF) Wing and Data Analytics and Technical Co -
ordination (DATC) wing, besides the Office of the Development Commissioner (DC -MSME) as an 
attached office and other subordinate organizations namely Khadi and Village Industries 
Commission (KVIC), National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC), Coir Board, National 
Institutes for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (Ni -MSME) and Mahatma Gandhi Institute for 
Rural Industrialization (MGIRI).  In order to nurture the MSME Sector, the Ministry runs various 
schemes aimed at financial assistance, technology assistance and upgradation, inf rastructure 
development, skill development and training, enhancing competitiveness and market assistance of 
MSMEs. Thus, the Ministry is committed towards an agenda of inclusive development to ensure 
that demographically as well as geographically, weaker sections benefit from its work.

16. During the course of discussion on Demands for Grants (2020-21) pertaining to the Ministry 
of MSME, the Committee also heard the views of the Representatives of some  Associations from 
Industrial and MSME Sectors like FICCI and FISME. However, ASSOCHAM could not participate 
in the discussion but submitted its written response to the Committee. The Committee appreciates 
and is thankful to the Industry and MSME Associati ons.  Some important and meaningful 
points/suggestions were also shared by the Industry Associations.  
17. The Committee was apprised that the Budget does take a few steps towards addressing the 
decelerating growth rate, specifically in the areas of Govern ance, Export -Import Promotion, and 
Ease of Doing Business etc. for the Industry and Business. The Associations also acclaimed the 
Vivad se Viswas  tax facilitation scheme, increasing audit limits to five crore rupees, allowing 
NBFCs to participate in Bill discounting over the TReDS platform and financing working capital 
requirements through quasi-equity subordinate debts. 
18. The Committee was also apprised by the Associations that even after the government 
issuing policy guidelines, the guidelines are not followed. Most of the Banks do not give the benefit 
of CGTMSE to the MSMEs and insist on collateral security and harassment of the small 
entrepreneurs is a common thing without even giving opportunity and also with no justification. 
Accordingly, they suggested that officers dealing with financial matters and regulatory affairs be 
made more accountable and be allowed to support the MSMEs.  
19. It was also informed by the representatives of MSME Associations that the GST Act need to 
be simplified further for easy application, improvement and allow corrections of genuine mistakes 
and rate rationalization should be applied by the Government. MSMEs always  run on short of 
resources and GST and its maintenance have made MSMEs completely occupied with its processes 
and the returns. Besides, the Committee was also informed that MSMEs need to have separate GST 
registration in each state. As a result, even if th ey have limited or nil business in that State, they 
need to file return which is in totality a very tedious task for a unit who already lack resources. It 
was accordingly suggested that MSMEs should be allowed to have only one unified GST 
registration which is applicable in all states.  
20. Further, the Committee was informed by the representatives of the MSME Associations that 
though the products of Indian MSMEs have the immense potential in global markets,  the Indian 

6 
 
MSMEs are not able to export their go ods as per their true potential due to lack of resources and 
knowledge. Besides, they also do not have enough resources to have a dedicated research team to 
develop the products as per the demand of a certain region or country. In this regard, the 
representatives of MSME Associations suggested to set up an exclusive „Export Facilitation Centre‟ 
for MSMEs to promote exports of products and services from MSME sector. The centre can be 
established in each State with the cooperation of the State government, whi ch could be One Point 
Contact for export facilitation. 
Demands for Grants (2020-21) 
21. The Ministry of MSME informed the Committee while making presentation on the DFG 
(2020-21) that MSMEs provide employment to about 11.10 crore people and contribute around 
28.9% to the country‟s GDP. The Committee was also apprised that under PMEGP (till 15.02.2020) 
around 43,426 micro enterprises have been assisted and about 3.47 lakh employment opportunities 
have been created. Further, the Ministry apprised that the one -time cap of Rs. 200 lakh is removed 
and the borrower can avail incremental credit facil ities now under CGTMSE and so far 39.28 lakh 
guarantees have been approved with coverage of Rs.2.06 lakh crores under the Scheme. To add, it 
was apprised that the corpus to the Credit Guarantee Fund has been enhanced to Rs. 7,500 crores to 
ensure payment to the Banks in case the loan amount becomes NPA.  
22. The Committee was informed by the representatives of MSME Associations that many 
of the MSMEs were unable to meet the fixed deadline of 90 days in repaying their loan 
installments, due to the unique sys tem of their working cycle, which is much longer, due to 
their inability to meet the stipulated timeline for repayment, the MSMEs units are declared 
NPAs. To avoid such difficulties being faced by MSMEs, the Committee recommends that the 
Ministry should en deavor to see to it that time limit for declaring a MSME as NPA due to 
non-payment of loan amount should be enhanced from the current 90 days to 180 days. 
Dream of Five Trillion Dollar Economy and the MSME Sector 
23. The Economic Survey 2018 -19 and the Uni on Budget 2019 -20 have indicated the goal of 
making India a US$ 5 trillion economy by 2024 -25. MSMEs are widely spread across the country 
in all the sectors and the current size of Indian Economy is around $3 trillion in which MSMEs ‟ 
share is estimated to be around $0.9 trillion based on its share of around 30% in the GDP.   
24. In order to increase the contribution of MSMEs in India‟s GDP and to achieve the target of 
$2 trillion mark for MSME sector by 2024 -25, Government is focusing on the key factors of 

7 
 
economic growth i.e. Investment and Productivity (efficiency) and the main parameters of the 
MSME Strategy is:  
(i) investment in all the vertical of MSME Ecosystem viz. human capital, knowledge 
dissemination, access to finance, technology, infrastructure, mar kets, policy, governance 
and ease of doing business. 
(ii) consolidation and convergence of all the MSME related activities under Central/ State 
management 
(iii)exploring the possibility of crowd sourcing of knowledge services and capital to 
supplement the efforts of the Government.  
(iv) implementing projects with external aid from World Bank, ADB etc. to supplement the 
investment of Government of India.  
(v) strengthening linkages of large manufacturing and services sector with micro, small and 
medium enterprises (MSMEs) and  developing a firm value chain within and across the 
sectors.  
(vi) upgrading the rural and urban livelihoods into enterprises by providing them with access 
to capital and market. This will not only lead to formalization of these sectors but also 
capture their contribution in the GDP which is at present either remains unreported or 
underreported.     
25. The Committee welcomes the Government’s goal of making India a $5 trillion 
economy by 2024 -25. The Committee is however of the considered view that the target o f 
becoming $5 trillion economy by 2024 can be achieved by exponential growth of MSME 
sector. This sector has the capacity of building up at a low cost and utilizing the modern 
innovative technology can be made commercially productive at lower cost. This se ctor has 
also the capacity to generate employment and multiplication of production is far easy than the 
big industries.  The Committee , therefore, recommends that Government need to play a 
proactive and constructive role in promoting MSME S ector. More than ease of doing 
business, there is need of creating ease of running business. More information dissemination 
regarding the export requirements should be taken care of. Financial institutions should be 
proactive, friendly and forthcoming. Gov ernment regulators should do handholding and play 
the role of mentors. Besides, change in the attitude of the Government regulators will 
immensely help the MSME S ector. Together with the industry, skilled manpower and the 
support of government authorities, the desired target can be achieved.   
26. Artificial Intelligence is a rapidly emerging concept in the market across the globe , 
which has great potential for enterprises. Most of the entrepreneurs are making use of this 
concept for their benefit and to in crease their outreach.  The Committee recommends that 
Ministry of MSME should also explore th e concept of AI for strengthening and betterment of 
MSME Sector in the country.    

8 
 
27. Market Intelligence plays a crucial role in strengthening and improving the e nterprises. A 
dedicated market intelligence cell is very essential to gather information about latest trends, taste 
and other aspects of the market. It is imperative to keep an eye on International markets and 
MSMEs of developed countries for knowledge of latest technology and developments and to adopt 
good practices prevailing in the developed markets across the globe for benefit of indigenous 
MSMEs. The Committee, therefore, recommends  that a Market Intelligence Cell for MSMEs 
should be set up that would regularly map specific markets, foreign as well as domestic, for 
meeting the requirement of specific MSME clusters and accordingly provide information and 
training to them about consumer preferences, product specifications, technological 
developments and trade agreements. 
28. Taking into consideration the views expressed by some of MSME Associations during 
the course of examination of DFG (2020 -21), the Committee also perceives that a lot of 
reforms on the Tax front too need to be taken to promote and encou rage the MSMEs. The 
Committee accordingly, is of the view that tax rates for proprietary and partnership firms 
should be reduced from 30% to at least 22% on the lines of reduction in Corporate Tax. 
Likewise, exemption and relief under Capital Gain should b e accorded to MSMEs for 
investment in expansion of the existing MSME or for fulfilling their working capital 
requirements. The Committee accordingly, desires that Ministry of MSME may take up these 
issues with the Ministry of Finance and apprise the Committee of the outcome thereof. 
29. The Committee recommends that MSMEs should be allowed to have only one unified 
GST registration , which is applicable in all states. With respect to the sharing of Taxes 
between Central and State Governments, it should be don e at the back end as the 
Government already has all the information in this regard.  
30. The Committee recommends that multiple registrations with multiple government 
agencies for various purposes should be done away with for MSMEs. Inter -Ministries 
coordination should be improved to bring out one registration to carry out all business 
activities including exports, tax and day -to-day business activities to improve ease of doing 
business for MSMEs.  
31. The Committee recommends that Ministry should play a pr oactive role in promoting 
MSMEs abroad and the foreign entrepreneurs should have profiles of Indian MSMEs so that 
they could promote them at appropriate level amongst companies of their respective country. 

9 
 
There should be an exclusive ‘Export Facilitation Centre’ for MSMEs in PPP mode in each 
State to promote exports of products and services from MSME sector.  
32. The Committee observes that there have been instances of inadequate credit 
disbursement by banks to MSMEs vis-à-vis large industries whereas the case should have 
been opposite. In Economic Survey 2018 -19, it has been stated that MSME sector has vast 
potential and it should be nurtured fully to convert dwarfs into the giants. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry s hould take up the matter with the Ministry of Finance and 
RBI to ensure that there is no inadequacy of funds to MSMEs.                             
Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) 
33. The KVIC is entrusted with the functions to plan, promote organize and assist in the 
implementation of the programme by financing the eligible units in Khadi and Village Industries 
sector, imparting training for the employment, ensuring supply of raw materials to the 
units/entrepreneurs, promotion of sales of Kha di and Village Industries products and undertaking 
research and development activities for the growth and spread of Khadi and Village Industries to 
promote the sale and marketing of Khadi or products of Village Industries or handicrafts by forging 
links wi th established marketing agencies wherever possible; and to encourage and promote 
research in technology used in Khadi and VI, including use of non -conventional energy and electric 
power with a view to increasing productivity. The programmes of the KVIC ar e implemented 
through its Regional/States level Offices and State Boards (KVIBs).  The KVIBs implement about 
90% of VI programmes and receive funds from both KVIC and State Governments.  Khadi Sector 
programme in its entirety and a small part of Village In dustries programmes are being implemented 
by KVIC directly.  Both the KVIC and KVIB are promotional bodies which extend a network of 
institutional, infrastructural and financial support to Khadi and Village Industries units/institution.  
34. In response to  a query regarding the future plans of the Ministry for the Khadi Sector and the 
suggestions for the betterment of Khadi Sector, the  Ministry informed that the Khadi Gramodyog Vikas 
Yojana (KGVY) has been implemented during 2019-20 and assistance is provided under various components 
viz., Modified Market Development Assistance (MMDA) to the Khadi Institutions and Khadi Artisans, 
Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme, ensuring availability of working capital to Khadi 
Institutions, Work-shed Scheme for providing sufficient space and congenial environment to Khadi artisans, 
Strengthening of Weak Khadi Institutions, assistance for modernization of sales outlets of Khadi 
Institutions, marketing component for promoting sales and marketing, assista nce under science & 
technology for developing new technologies, and training to the artisans. The Ministry also informed that 

10 
 
the social welfare schemes like Janashree Bima Yojana etc., are implemented for the artisans. Also, to make 
Khadi a youthful and g lobal brand, it is proposed to set up design houses to introduce new and vibrant 
designs.

Scheme of Funds for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) 
64. The Committee was informed that KVIC is implementing centrally sponsored scheme 
known as Revamped Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) to make 
the traditional industries more productive, competitive and to facilitate for their sustainable 
development. It is basically a cluster development programme, designed to pr otect and preserve the 
arts and crafts of rural traditional artisans, in a cluster-based manner. The objective of the scheme is 
to organize traditional industries and artisans into clusters to make them competitive and provide 
support for their long -term s ustainability, sustained employment to enhance marketability of 
products of such clusters, to equip traditional artisans of the associated clusters with the improved 
skills, to make provision for common facilities and improved tools and equipment for artis ans, to 
strengthen the cluster governance systems with the active participation of the stakeholders and to 

19 
 
build up innovative products, improved technologies, advanced processes, market intelligence and 
new models of public-private partnerships. 
65. The Scheme covers three types of interventions: 
(i)   Soft interventions - Activities to build general awareness, counseling, skill   
      development & capacity building, exposure visits, market development initiatives, design 
and product development, etc. 
 (ii)  Hard interventions - Creation of common facility centers, raw material banks,    
        upgradation of production infrastructure, warehousing facilities, tools and     
        technological upgradation, etc. 
   (iii) Thematic interventions - Interventions on a cross-cutting basis for brand building,    
          new media marketing, e-commerce initiatives, research and development, etc. 
 
66. The financial assistance provided for any specific project shall be subject to a maximum of 
Rs. 5 crore to support Soft, Hard and Thematic interventions. 
67. The scheme is implemented through registered implementing agencies. Financial assistance 
is given for Khadi and Village Industries Institutions to create Common Facility Centres (CFCs) 
and new infrastructure, to purchase machinery, equipment, Charkha, Looms and tools, to construct 
Workshed, renovate sales outlets, to ensure Skill development of artisans and also to facilitate 
computer and computer related design, etc. Till December, 2019, 63 clusters were assisted and an 
additional 50 clusters are likely to be approved by March, 2020.  Further, 54 are functional 
benefitting 38970 artisans. The Ministry has so far approved 191 proposals for setting up clusters 
and approved an amount of Rs. 408.19 crores  as grant for implementation of clusters since 2015 -16 
to 2019-20 (till December, 2019). 
68. As per revised guidelines pattern of financial assistance, Rs. 2.50 crore is granted for regular 
Clusters (up to 500 artisans) and Rs. 5.00 crore is granted for ma jor Clusters (more than 500 
artisans). The financial assistance provided for any specific project shall be subject to a maximum 
of Rs. 5 crore.

74. Solar Charkha Mission is proposed to be set up under Ministry of MSME with a corpus of 
Rs. 951.43 crores to set up 500 exclusive solar charka based clusters (250 during 2018 -19 and 250 
during 2019 -20) across the country by the end of 2020 to help promote industrial growth, 
employment generation and technology up -gradation. It is completely innov ative concept of 
innovation of solar charkha to create employment opportunities for youth including at least 50% 
women beneficiaries in rural households across the country. The Mission targets to provide direct 
employment to 10.21 lakh youth during the projected three years period.

generate direct employment nearly to one lakh persons.

village and other surrounding villages in a radius of 8 to 10 Kilometers.  Further, such a Cluster will

Micro & Small Enterprises - Cluster Development Programme (MSE-CDP) 
105. The Ministry of MSME has adopted the Cluster Development approach as a key strategy for 
enhancing the productivity and competitiveness as well as capacity building of Micro and Small 
Enterprises (MSEs) and their collectives in the country. A cluster is a group of enterprises located 
within an identifiable and as far as practicable, contiguous ar ea or a value chain that goes beyond a 
geographical area and producing same/similar products/complementary products/services, which 
can be linked together by common physical infrastructure facilities that help address their common 
challenges.  
106. The mai n objective is to support the sustainability and growth of MSEs by addressing 
common issues such as improvement of technology, skills & quality, market access etc.; to build 
capacity of MSEs for common supportive action through formation of self help group s, consortia, 
upgradation of associations etc.; to create/upgrade infrastructural facilities in the new/existing 
Industrial Areas/Clusters of MSEs; to set up Common Facility Centres (for testing, training, raw 
material depot, effluent treatment, complement ing production processes, etc); and promotion of 
green & sustainable manufacturing technology for the clusters so as to enable units switch to 
sustainable and green production processes and products. 
107. The scheme supports financial assistance for establishment of common facility centres 
(CFCs) and to create/upgrade infrastructural facilities (IDs) in the new/existing industrial 
areas/clusters of MSEs. As far as the government grant is concerned CFCs, it is restricted to 70% of 
the cost of Project o f maximum Rs. 20.00 crore.  However, the grant will be 90% for CFCs in 
North-East & Hilly States, Island territories; Aspirational Districts/LWE affected Districts; Clusters 
with more than 50% micro/ village, women owned, and SC/ST units. 
108. Further, the Ministry informed that u nder the MSE-CDP, 1421 potential clusters have been 
identified by field establishments of Office of DC (MSME) namely MSME - Development 
Institutes, across the country. The project proposals along with the Detailed Project Reports (D PRs) 
are required to be forwarded by respective State Governments in respect of these identified clusters 
to office of DC (MSME). Ministry of MSME would accord necessary approvals for the submitted 
proposals and provide financial assistance for setting up of Common Facility Centres (CFCs) under 
the MSE -CDP subject to availability of budgetary provision. The Steering Committee under the 

30 
 
Chairmanship of Secretary, MSME is the Apex Body to sanction and monitor progress of MSE -
CDP.

Public Procurement Policy 
140. The Public Procurement Policy for Micro and Small Enterprises (PPP-MSE) Order 2012 has 
mandated that every Central Ministry/Department/PSU shall set an annual goal for procurement 
from the MSE sector at the beginning of the year, with the objective of achi eving an overall 
procurement goal of minimum 25 per cent of the total annual purchases from the products or 
services produced or rendered by MSEs, with sub-targets of 4 per cent for SC/ST-owned MSEs, and 
3 per cent for women -owned MSEs. The SAMBANDH portal  shows the procurement from the 
MSEs by the CPSEs as per the PPP-MSE Order, 2012 provisions.

least 25% of tendered value at L -1 subject to lowering of price by MSEs to L -1. 358 items are 
reserved for exclusive procurement from MSEs . For enhancing participation of MSEs in 
government procurement, Ministry/Department/CPSUs shall conduct Vendor Development 
Programmes or Buyer Seller Meets for MSEs especially for SC/ST entrepreneurs.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE – AT A GLANCE 
1. The present classification of MSMEs based on investment in plant and machinery has 
been effective since the adoption of the MSMED Act, 2006.  However, the extant financial 
limits fixed in 2006 for MSMEs are no longer relevant given the impact of inflation.  Mo st of 
the Industry Associations and stakeholders have, therefore, expressed the need for changing 
the present criteria of investment in plant and machinery to annual turnover as criterion for 
classification of MSMEs. 
 (Para 8) 
 
2.  The Committee is of the view that the present classification based on investment in

Certificates should have global acceptance. Besides, MSMEs should be given subsidies for 
testing facilities in the country.  The Committee further recommends that  the setting up of 
Tool Rooms and Technology Centres can be explored under the Public -Private Partnership 
(PPP) model. 
(Para 170) 

61 
 
61. The under utilization of Budget Allocations and lack of performance of various 
important MSME S chemes viz. PMEGP, CLCS -TUS etc. in NER  has been a recurring 
concern for the Committee, which was communicated to the Ministry from time to time in its 
earlier Reports. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should make concerted efforts 
for industrialization in NER region, be it in the field of traditional art, craft and artisan work.   
(Para 174) 
62. The Committee observes that many schemes of the Ministry are not working 
satisfactorily in North Eastern Region and has a very negligible presence in these areas.  The 
number of ISEC issued under Khadi and Polyvastra are almost negligible except in the 
previous financial years. The Committee recommends that serious efforts should be made by 
the Government/Ministry to benefit the populace of NER through various scheme s run by 
them with the help of State Governments. Particularly potential women and SC/ST 
entrepreneurs be encouraged to participate in the growth process. 
(Para 175) 
63. The Committee notes the initiative taken by the Ministry for honoring MSMEs for 
quality production, exports, innovation, product development etc.  for the economic growth of 
the country. However, the Committee is of the view that the award distribution functions 
should be organized in and around areas where the award winning MSMEs are locate d or in 
areas where concentration of MSMEs is less like the North Eastern Region so as to generate 
awareness and to encourage the potential entrepreneurs to set up new MSMEs in those areas. 
(Para 178)
