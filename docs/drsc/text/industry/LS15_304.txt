REPORT 
 
The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Sector has emerged as a highly 
vibrant and dynamic sector of the Indian economy over the last five decades. It contributes 
significantly in the economic and social development of the country by fostering 
entrepreneurship and generating large-scale employment opportunities at comparatively lower 
capital cost, next only to agriculture. MSME Sector has strong linka ges to large indu stries as 
ancillary units and contributes significantly in the inclusive industrial development of the 
country. MSME Sector is also  helping in establishment of industries in economically backward 
regions and removing regional  inequalities, promising justifiable dispersion of national income 
and wealth. MSMEs are now widening their domain across various sectors of the economy, 
producing diverse range of products and services to meet demands of domestic as well as global 
markets and also proving to be a boon for aspiring entrepreneurs in India. 
1. As per the 73rd round of National Sample Survey (2015-16), t he MSME Sector is 
providing livelihood to around 11.10 crore people . Around 6.34 crore MSMEs contributed 
approximately 30 per cent to the G ross Domestic Product. Its share in total manufacturing and 
export is 33 per cent and 49 per cent respectively.  The primary responsibility of promotion and 
development of MSMEs is with the State  Government. Nevertheless, the role of the Ministry of 
MSME and its organizations is to assist the States in their  efforts to encourage entrepreneurship, 
employment generation, and livelihood  opportunities and enhance the competitiveness of 
MSMEs in the changed economic scenario. 
The major Schemes of the Ministry of MSME are as under: 
 Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP); 
 Modified Market Development Assistance (MMDA); 
 Interest Subsidy and Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme; 
 Strengthening of Infrastructure of Existing Weak Khadi Institutions and Assistance 
for Marketing Infrastructure; 
 Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE); 
 Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS); 
 Scheme for Technology Upgradation and Competitiveness; 
 A Scheme for Promoting Innovation and Rural Entrepreneurship (ASPIRE); 
 Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI); 

2 
 
 Micro and Small Enterprises–Cluster Development Programme (MSE-CDP); 
 Science & Technology (S&T); 
 Mission Solar Charkha  
 Honey Mission;  
 Kumbhar Shashaktikaran Programme; 
 The National Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe Hub; and 
 Scheme for Promotion of MSMEs in NE Region and Sikkim. 
 
 
New Definition for MSME 
 
2. The Committee was informed by the Ministry that since the  economy has undergone 
significant changes, a revision in MSME criteria of classification was  necessary to establish an 
objective system of classification and to provide ease of doing  business. Accordingly t he new 
classification became effective from 1 stJuly, 2020. The earlier criterion of classification under 
MSMED Act, 2006 was based on investment in plant and machinery/equipment. However, the 
revised definition remove d the distinction between Manufacturing and Service enterprises. 
Besides, a new criterion of turnover has been added and it expected to bring about many benefits 
that will aid MSMEs to grow in size. It was decided that the turnover with respect to exports will 
not be counted in the limits of turnover for any category of MSME s whether Micro, Small or 
Medium. It will help in attracting investments and creating more jobs in the MSME Sector. 
Besides, the classification of MSMEs  has also removed the difference between manufacturing 
and services. The Ministry also apprised the Commi ttee that this was also the demand of 
the stakeholders for a long time and that it is a paradigmatic and significant change. 
 
 The new definition of MSMEs is as under:

Now the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) are classified as below: 

3 
 
(i) A micro enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or equipment does 
not exceed one crore rupees and turnover does not exceed five crore rupees; 
(ii) A small enterprise, where the investment in  plant and machinery or equipment does 
not exceed ten crore rupees and turnover does not exceed fifty crore rupees; and 
(iii) A medium enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or equipment 
does not exceed fifty crore rupees and turnover does not exceed two hundred and fifty 
crore rupees. 
 
3. The Committee hopes that th e new classification of MSMEs would  bring 
about positive changes in the MSME Sector. The Committee therefore, 
recommends that a study needs to be conducted to know the exact impact of 
COVID-19 Pandemic on the MSME Sector and to ascertain what benefits have 
accrued to the MSME Sector by change of criteria for classification of MSMEs  and 
introduction of various Sche mes. The Committee may also be apprised about the 
findings of the study for perusal. 
4. The Committee reiterates its recommendation contained in its 297 th Report that the 
growth of MSMEs is concentrated mostly in a few States only which induces the people to 
migrate to those areas in search of employment and livelihood. Therefore, the Ministry 
should work in the direction of removing all the existing geographical imbalances and 
ensure that equitable presence of MSMEs is there in all the States so that there is 
horizontal industrialization and adequate employment generation to tide over the situation 
created particularly due to COVID-19 Pandemic.  
 
Demand for Grants (2021-22) 
5. While making presentation on the DFG (202 1-22), the Ministry o f MSME 
informed the Committee  that the MSME Sector is providing livelihood to around 11.10 
crore people.  Further t he Committee was apprised that the MSME Sector is providing 
ample entrepreneurship opportunities to the aspiring entrepreneurs at a lower cost  and 
during the FY 2020-21, approximately 44,097 micro enterprises were assisted, generating 
employment opportunities for about 3.53 lakh people. 
6. Regarding the details of the Action Plan set for the Ministry for the year  2021-22, 
the Committee was infor med by the Ministry that an amount of Rs. 15699.65 crore has

Ministry focuses on strengthening the overall ecosystem of MSMEs by providing 
infrastructural support, marketin g avenues, proper access to finance,  technical support

Monitoring Framework Document of the Ministry for FY 2021-22.

Ministry of MSME in the BE of 2021 -22 under ‘Guarantee for Emergency Credit Line 
for MSME Borrowers’ and the details regarding utilization of the proposed 
allocation Rs 10,000 crore is being worked out in consultation with Ministry of  
Finance.

Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP)  
 
32. PMEGP is a bank appraised and financed credit linked subsidy programme for generation 
of employment opportunities through establishment of non -farm based microenterprises for self-
employment in rural as well as urban areas of the country. KVIC is the single nodal agency at the 
national level to implement the scheme across the country and at State/District level the 
implementing agencies are the KVIC and State Khadi and Village Industries Boards (State  
KVIBs) in rural areas, and District Industries Centers (DICs) in both rural and urban areas , and 
Coir Board for coir related activities. 
33. The Committee was informed that subsidy under the scheme is provided to 
General/Special Category -15/25% in urban areas and 25/35% in rural areas respectively. 
Besides, 2nd Financial Assistance up to Rs. 1crore is also provided with subsidy of 15% (20% for 
NER/Hill areas). Around 6.53 lakh micro units have been assisted so far with a total subsidy of 
Rs. 15,300 crore with estimated employment to 53.7 lakh persons.

Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate (ISEC) Scheme 
44. The Committee was informed by the Ministry that the Interest Subsidy Eligibility 
Certificate (ISEC) Scheme is one of the components of Khadi Vikas Yojana. It was introduced to 
mobilize the funds from banking institutions in order to cater to the additional  fund requirement 
of Khadi and Village Industries Institutions. For this, an Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate is 
issued to Khadi Institutions (KIs). The off take of the ISEC depends upon the production and 
sales capacity of Khadi Institutions and the nature of production i.e., Silk, Cotton, Woolen Khadi 
and Polyvastra. It strengthens the KIs to avail the bank finance for the shortfall in working 
capital. Under the Scheme 4% of the interest is to be borne by the KIs and the interest 
differential betw een the actual lending rate and 4% is provided by the Government of India  
through Khadi & Village Industries Commission (KVIC) to the lending Banks. The funds for 
this purpose are provided to KVIC under the Khadi Vikas Yojana Grant. 
45. Further, the Commit tee was informed that during FY 2019 -20, Rs. 38.02 crore was 
disbursed to 1289 Khadi Institutions under the ISEC to Khadi & Polyvastra. During FY 2020 -21 
(up to 31-12-2020), Rs. 24.96 crore was disbursed to 1,002 Khadi Institutions and the anticipated 
disbursement under ISEC is Rs. 40.00 crore to 1370 Khadi Institutions (up to 31 - 03 - 2021). 
Also it has been  observed by the Committee from the status of ISEC claim for the FY 2020 -21 
that the scheme is not performing satisfactorily in the states of Haryana,  Chhattisgarh, Assam, 
Orissa, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telengana and Karnataka (DO Hubli). It was informed by the 
Ministry that the probable reasons for underperformance in availing the ISEC Scheme could be 

22 
 
(i) COVID-19 Pandemic situation prevailed in the sta tes, (ii) reluctance of bankers to provide 
bank finance under the Scheme, (iii) non -fulfillment of criterion prescribed by the bankers, 
(iv) mindsets of bankers etc. In this regard, the State/Divisional offices of KVIC have been 
advised to take up the prob lem area pertaining to ISEC in the State Level Bankers' 
Committee (SLBC) and Block Level Bankers' Committee (BLBC) in the respective states. KVIC 
is also organizing awareness programme with bankers to make them aware of the ISEC Scheme 
and Khadi Programme.

Covid Pandemic and MSME Sector 
 
91. The COVID -19 spread very fast to almost all the countries of the world  and on  11th 
March, 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared it as a Pandemic. In India, the first 
COVID-19 case was reported from Kerala on 30th January, 2020, and subsequently the Pandemic 
spread to almost all the States/UTs. In order to contain the spread of COVID -19 Pandemic, the 
Government of India took various steps such as enforcing social distancing norms, self -isolation 
measures, travel ban, border closure and lockdown of the entire country. 
92. The Pandemic has significantly hampered economic activity all across the world. The 
restricted movement due to lockdown had severe effect on all the sectors of the economy 
especially on the MSMEs  in India . Most of them did not have the capacity to deal with 
something so unexpected and unprecedented. Consequently MSMEs, which rely m ostly on the 
cash availability and informal employment , had to face problems such as sudden dip in or ders 
and cash flows, uncertainty in demand, labour related issues , supply chain disruption etc. 
Particularly the Micro E enterprises that comprised the majority of MSME s suffered massive 
losses due to the lockdowns because they had to run their businesses with only limited resources. 

36 
 
A large number of MSMEs comprising manufacturing and service/retail firms had to shut down 
their business es during nationwide lockdowns due to the disruptions in the supply chain as 
procurement of raw materials at affordable rates became very difficult.

93. Taking cognizance of the gravity of the situation, the Government announced a series of 
measures under the Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyaan  for ameliorating the MSME s. The 
announcements were focused on various important aspects such as availability of credit, demand 
generation, grievance redressal and handholding of MSMEs etc. The steps inter-alia includes: 
 
(i) The Government has notified composite criteria of classification of MSMEs based on 
Investment in plant & machinery or equipment and Turnover of MSMEs as structural 
change to facilitate both existing as well as new entrepreneurs.  
 
(ii) Earlier process of filing  of Udyog Aaadhar  Memorandum has been replaced by

 97. Further, the Committee was informed that the CGSSD Scheme was launched on 
24thJune, 2020. As on 03.02.2021, a total number of 34 Banks have registered as Member 
Lending Institutions under this Scheme and have extended 272 guarantees amounting to Rs. 
30.84 crore. The objective of the scheme is to provide personal loan through banks to the 
promoters of stressed MSMEs for infusion as equity/quasi equity in the business and provides 
Sub-Debt support in respect of restructuring of MSMEs. The salient features of the Scheme 
are as under: 
(i)   it provides for Rs. 20.000 crore as subordinate debt for Stressed MSMEs. 
(ii) MSME entrepreneurs of such stressed units find it difficult to bring in this additional 
equity and that in turn severely constrains the survival of these units. 

38 
 
(iii) Infusion of capital in such units in the form of equity or sub-debt will be of substantial 
help in sustaining and reviving the MSMEs which have either become NPA or are on the 
brink of becoming NPA. 
(iv)  Promoter(s) of the MSMEs will be given credit equal to 15% of their stake (equity plus 
debt) or Rs. 75 lakh whichever is lower, Promoter(s) in turn will infuse this amount in the 
MSME unit as equity and thereby enhance the liquidity and maintain debt-equity ratio. 
(v)  90% guarantee coverage for this sub-debt will be given under the scheme and 10% would 
come from the concerned Promoters. 
(vi)  There will be a moratorium of 7 years on payment of principal whereas maximum tenure 
for repayment will be 10 years. 
(vii)  Till date, 34 Banks including all PSU Banks have come on-board by signing MoU with 
CGTMSE for becoming the Member Lending Institution (MLI) of the Scheme.

for such slow pace of expending in respect of such an important Scheme, which has been 
specially formulated for the marginalized section of the society. The C ommittee 
recommends that the Ministry should take adequate steps to speed up the spending 
process, so that allocated amount gets utilized to the optimum.  
(Para 84) 
 
37. The Committee observes that under National SC/ST Hub, the Actual Expenditure 
has always been less than the BE. The Committee is concerned about the under -utilization 
of allocated funds meant for the marginalized communities of the society. The Committee 
recommends that Ministry should endeavor to improve the situation by ensuring that the 
budget earmarked for SC/ST Hub Scheme is utilized fully for the benefit of the 
communities. Creating awareness among SC/ST entrepreneurs, ground level support and 
handholding of SC/ST entrepreneurs may work well in improving the performance of this 
scheme. Underutilization of funds is a serious cause of concern for the Committee; 
therefore, Ministry is advised to ensure full utilization of funds. 
(Para 85) 
 
38. The Committee recommends that the Ministry should create an All India Database 
of SC/ST entrepreneurs’ locations and vocations and share the same with CPSEs so that 
CPSEs can locate SC/ST units easily for availing their services. Also, the SC/ST Hub 
should run NSQF compliant skill development programmes for SC/ST technicians who 
could take contract jobs and services that CPSEs outsource. 
(Para 86) 
 
39. The trend under Public Procurement Policy has not been encouraging over the 
years. The Committee therefore, recommends that the trend should be reversed and 
adequate steps should be taken by the Ministry to ensure fair participation of SC/ST MSEs 
in Public Procurement Policy.  The Committee also recommends that more special VDPs 
should be organized for SC/ST entrepreneurs for improving their participation in Public 
Procurement Policy. The Ministry  should also adopt handholding approach while 
promoting SC/ST entrepreneurs.   
(Para 87) 
 
40. The Committee observes that many schemes of the Ministry are not working 
satisfactorily in North Eastern Region and has a very negligible presence in these areas.   

50 
 
Even the number of ISEC issued under Khadi and Polyvastra are also not impressive. The 
Committee recommends that serious efforts should be made by the Ministry to benefit the 
people of NER through its various schemes with the help of State Governments 
particularly potential women and SC/ST entrepreneurs be encouraged to participate in the 
growth process. 
(Para 88) 
 
41. The Committee recommends that Testing & Certifications should be made 
convenient and cheaper for MSMEs and they should be provided some sort of subsidy for 
availing such testing facilities. It is also required that a mechanism should be developed so 
that testing can be done at Indian Centres whereas certificates for the same test could be 
issued from various global testing centres, which are acceptable in respective 
countries/regions.  
(Para 90) 
 
42. The Committee notes that Ministry has launched CHAMPIONS Portal for grievance 
redressal of MSME units but many small units are not aware about this portal and its 
functions. The Committee recommends that adequate steps should be taken by the 
Ministry to create awareness about this portal and more budgetary alloc ations be made for 
publicity purposes. The use of the Portal may be made as simple as possible and also the 
portal should have an option for Hindi language as well as the Regional languages for ease 
of understanding. 
(Para 103) 
 
43. As a consequence of COV ID-19 Pandemic and subsequent lockdowns, numerous 
MSMEs became stressed and could not sustain their businesses due to unavailability of 
working capital, raw material and manpower. Accordingly the CGSSD Scheme has been 
designed to infuse capital in the form of subordinate debt to the MSMEs, which have either 
become NPA or are on the verge of becoming NPA,  to help them increase their liquidity. 
The Committee is of the view that the CGSSD Scheme has the capability of providing much 
desired liquidity to the MSMEs who are desperately in need of capital to sustain their 
business. The Committee therefore, recommends that all the procedures and technicalities 
for availing the benefits of the Scheme should be simplified;  irreducible minimum interest 
should be cha rged; repayment tenure of the loan should be kept at the maximum and; a 
friendly and favourable approach should be adopted by the banks to help revive the 
MSMEs. 
    (Para 104)
