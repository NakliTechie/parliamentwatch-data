RECOMMENDATION 
  
            The Committee observes that Indian Stee l Industry is marching ahead stead ily at a pace determined by
the process and forces of liberali zation and globalization since 1991.  Domestic Economic Reforms i.e. de-
regulation and removal of erstwhile li censing system, capital market refo rms, full conversion of currency on
trade account etc. led to gearing up of steel industry for its adherence to  the guidelines of WTO-regime. The 
globalization process opened up new opportunities for Indian steel industry in the world market. The Committee
is pleased to note that Indian Steel Industry is undergoi ng a sea change to adapt itself as per the requirement o
f
globalised and liberalized economy.  However, the Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry of Steel should
undertake steps to mull out the negative impact of lib eralization and globalizat ion of Steel industry an d
accordingly apprise the Committee in its action taken note on the presentation of this report. Adequate measures
may be taken to safeguard the Indian Steel Industry. 
                                                                                                                         
(PARA5.9)     
ACTION TAKEN 
  
1.6.1    In the current deregulate d and liberalised economic regime, whic h also abides by the rules of multi-
lateral trade of the WTO, the government is discharging th e role of a facilitator so as to assist development o
f
the iron & steel industry in the country.   Towards achi eving this end, the government has strongly taken up the
cause of the Indian steel industry faced with strong prot ectionist action in many parts of the world and has been
able to safeguard their interests to the extent possible.   
  
1.6.2    Further, to safeguard the interest of the do mestic steel industry, the G overnment is engaging in the
OECD discussions, which are aimed at formulation of a steel subsidy agreem ent. Every effort is being made to
preserve at least the special and differential treatment, provisions currently available to us under the WTO, in
any possible steel agreement  
  
1.6.3    The government has remained vigilant about the threat of 'dumped' or ‘subsidized’ materials taking ove
r 
the domestic market and has quickly and comprehensiv ely acted by taking anti-dum ping or other measures. 
Steps like imposition of floor price and higher import duty have also been taken to arrest the import o f
seconds/defective category of steel items into the countr y. This is one of the reasons  the prices in the domestic

RECOMMENDATION 
  
1.7       SWOT ANALYSIS OF THE STEEL INDUSTRY  
  
            The Committee recommends that the Minist ry of Steel should have the objective assessment of SWOT
analysis for improving the health of Indian Steel industr y. Since the SWOT analysis gives the details of the
strength, weaknesses, opportunities and threats to the objects undertaken, it is imperative that the strength is the
subject where the strategy maker has to bank on, while, weaknesses are to be overcome. Similarly, opportunities
are to be availed of and a resilient strategy has to be sketched up for combating the threats coming across. In this
context the Indian Steel Industry policy maker has to  bank on the low wage rates, low iron cost, superio
r
managerial skill while searching for an adequate ma rketing strategy. Further avenues may be opened up fo r
supplying semi steel products to small scale steel industries and ancillary steel industries in various parts o f
country (especially Chennai and Ludhiana where clusters of steel units are wide spread) 
  
                                                                                                                        (PARA 6.2) 
ACTION TAKEN 
  
1.7.1    The Economic Research Unit (ERU) attached to  the Ministry of Steel regularly assists the Ministry by 
carrying out studies on various aspe cts of the strength weakness, opportuni ties and threats facing the steel
sector. 
  
1.7.2    After the decontrol of pr ices and distribution of iron and stee l in the country, the government has no
direct role to play in pricing and ma rketing of steel.  Subjects like supp ly of semi-finished steel products to 
small scale steel industries and anci llary steel units are now part of the commercial policy of the steel
companies, whether these are in the private or public sector.  
  
            The government, however, has continued with its policy of supporting sm all scale industries which use
steel materials (not the pr ocessors of steel semis) by way of provi ding a rebate to the State Small Industry
Corporations (SSICs) on handling and freight costs incu rred by them in making available the required iron an
d
steel materials at the work place/ plants of the small industries. These are governed  by the allocation made by
the Ministry of Steel  (earlier by the office of the DCI&S).

4.9 IMPACT OF RATIONALIZAT ION OF TAX STRUCTURE MADE  IN BUDGET 2002-03 ON THE 
INDIAN STEEL 
  
            After having a detailed discussion with the representatives of Indian Steel Alliance, TISCO, Jindal Vijay
Nagar the Committee feels that the existing tax structure needs to be modified to become a source of support for
the steel sector.  The Comm ittee is of the view that the Ministry  of Finance should examine the issue o f
rationalizing the tax structure on the following lines:- 
  
(i)         Customs duty on charge nickel and Metc oke be fixed at 5% for all users keeping in view the
dominant user industry.   The Co mmittee agrees with the proposal of the Ministry of Mines that a
differential of in the import duty between Coking Coal - the raw material for production of Metcoke, and 
Metcoke should be maintained by reducing the duty on Coking Coal to 0% and retaining the duty of 5%
on Metcoke.  
  
(ii)        Prior to the budget 2003-04 integrated  steel manufacturers were paying Excise duty on the 
factory gate, even when goods were sold from the stoc kyard.  It is now proposed that Excise duty has to
be paid on transaction value at depot.  Withdrawal of  the existing facility would imply an increase in
price of construction steel in particular for supply from stockyard, affecting SAIL and TISCO
specifically.  Reinstatement of the existing notification is required.  
  
(iii)       While there is an exemption of customs duty on Stainless St eel coin blanks, domestic
manufacturers such as SAIL and other have to pa y excise duty and local ta xes on similar products made
in India. This product is bought by the Ministry of fi nance only. Indian companies are loosing tenders to
overseas companies. The Department of Economic affairs must allow exemption of excise duty and sales
tax for local producers supplying to them.  
  
(iv)       Restoration of status quo ante i.e. 5%  import duty, 4% SAD and CVED of 16% on import of ol d
ships for breakin g so that (a) tax evasion is reduced and revenue collection is increased and (b) the
Page 40 of 48137th Report on Industry
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/indus/137threport.htm

industry gets the requisite boost in production.  
  
(v)        Reduction of excise duty on GP/GC sheets from 16% to 8% for promoting rural mass
consumption 
  
(vi)       Withdrawal of Notification 345 date d 23.10.97 regarding clearance of recoveries on ship-
breaking and charging of custom duty on non-excisable  items like generators , refrigerators, under thei r
respective heading as is being done in case of bunk oil.  
  
(vii)             Exemption of excise duty on prefabricated item of steel used in construction in line with
