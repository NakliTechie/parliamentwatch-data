REPORT

Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL) commonly known as Visakhapatnam Steel
Plant (VSP), is the first shore-based integrated Steel Plant set up in India at a distance from
the major raw material sources, viz. metallurgical coal and iron ore . Being shore-based it
has the advantage of easier import of input materials like coking coal, lime-stone ete., and
casier export of finished products. The location is also expected to help industrialistion of
the region. VSP has been set up with several latest technologies for the first time in the
country, such as 100 percent continuous casting of liquid steel, dry quenching of coke
with auxiliary power gencration facilities, 100 percent Cast House Slag Granulation in
Blast Furnace and Torpedo Ladle for transporting liquid hot metal.

2.0 RINL INDEPENDENT FROM SAIL

21 RINL was originally conceived as a unit of Steel Authority of India (SAIL) to
augment its long product capacity and service its southem market, The facility was envisaged
with sourcing of raw material from National Mineral Development Corporation ( NMDC),
which was also a part of SAIL at that time, and a jetty was also envisaged for its imports and
exports. The announcement of VSP as part of SAIL was done in April 1970, the foundation
stone was laid in January,1971, the Indo-Soviet agreement was concluded in June, 1979 and
the revised DPR was prepared in 1980. In 1982, Government of India decided to carve a
separate holding company for VSP, and RINL was formed as a separate PSU under Ministry
of Steel in February ,1982.

2:2 RINL has been operating as a separate PSU primarily for the following reasons :

i to have healthy competition with the public sector
ii to improve efficiency of its operations in a competitive eavironment
iii to be accountable for its own financial performance, ete.

6.0 CAPACITY UTILIZATION

6.1 By producing 3.083 Mt of Liquid Steel and 2.757 Mt of Saleable Steel in 2001-02,
VSP has achieved 103 percent and 104 percent of capacity utilization respectively during
the current fiscal year. It is likely to increase its capacity utilization further, The first priority
of the plant is to convert maximum production of hot metal into saleable steel and to reduce
the component of semis in the saleable steel by producing more percentage of finished
products. With this the plant expects to improve its net sales realization per tonne of its
product. The plant has got scope to increase its capacity to 10 MT. RINL has a long term
Corporate Plan envisioning the producer of 7.0 Mt by 2013-14 and 10 Mt by 2019-20 with
addition of production units in both primary and finishing zones which will be put up for
Government of India’s clearance shortly. The plan envisages to increase the long products to
6.0 MT from the present level of 2.66 MT and to diversify into flat products segment with a
capacity installation of 3 MT.

6.2 The Committee is pleased to note that RINL has achieved 103 percent and 104
percent of capacity utilisation by producing 3.083 million tonnes of liquid steel and 2.757
million tonnes of saleable steel, respectively, during, 2001-02,

11.0 RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT

11.1. Research & Development plays a vital role in VSP, both for reducing the production
costs as well as for in developing new products. Some of the measures taken up to reduce the
cost are given below :

(i) Usage of nut coke and BF coke in Blast furnace

(ii) Usage of LD slag and metallurgical waste at Sinter Plant

(iii) Usage of Coke Dust as partial replacement of coke breeze at sinter plant

(iv) Usage of used silica bricks to replace quartzite lump at Blast Furnace

(v) Recycling of pitch at Coke ovens

(vi) Manufacture of Gas/air recuperators of reheating furnaces at engineering shops

11.2 With the use of developed 4-hole 17.5 degree lance the lining life of converter
increased to 1601 heats in one campaign and subsequently to more than 2000 heats. A
thermo chemical model of heat balance was developed for feed back monitoring of various
parameters with an aim to reduce coke rate in Blast furnace through optimization of these



parameters. The coke rate of 524 kg/thm in the 2001-02 is lowest so far. Based on the process
development specific consumption of hot metal was reduced during capital repair of Blast
Furnace-II_All these measures led to a saving of Rs.186 crore for the year 2001-02 helping the
company to improve its financial performance process parameters were developed and special
steel are being produced on regular basis. During 2001-02, thirteen new grades, two new
sections and seven new products were developed to enhance the product mix of RINL.

11.3 The Committee is pleased to note that R&D projects have been successfully
undertaken by RINL, in reducing the cost of production and developing new products. the
Committee recommends that intensive R&D programme in RINL should continue in
forthcoming years and adequate amount of finance should, be provided for the purpose.

+ the question that has been raised with regard to the KK Line related to movement of

to 35 per cent and now to 30 per centis, itself, a recognition of the understanding on which

should now go. This is the issue that has to be examined.

27.0 MAKING ANTI-DUMPING MECHANISM MORE EFFECTIVE

27.1 The anti-dumping mechanism is to be more effective to protect the Indian Steel
industry. As soon as the anti-dumping allegation arises, the required assessment of injury to
domestic industry is to be ascertained within the shortest possible time so that the industry is
protected against the onslaught of dumped material. The present mechanism takes longer time
to ascertain the injury to domestic industry thereby depriving the remedial action to be taken,

27.2 The Commnuttee understands that dumping of defective and secondary steel is
unflinching damage to the domestic steel industry, Western countries are quick to protect
their domestic steel industry by adopting various anti-dumping measures. The Commuttee,
although , appreciate the role of the Ministry of Steel, in Protecting the domestic industry
from the onslaught of dumping of steel, however it recommends that the Ministry of Steel
may explore the possibility of reducing the time in ascertaining the assessment of injury to
domestic industry, in case of dumping of steel takes place.

28.0 FUTURE OUTLOOK OF INDIAN STEEL INDUSTRY VIS-A-VIS
EMERGING GLOBAL STEEL SCENARIO.

28.1. While the West was restructuring steel industry by trimming their capacity to demand
forecast, East was growing by leaps and bounds and India also was looking towards the
Eastern pattern of growth. During the late 1970's Indian expectation was to achieve by 2000
AD, a steel making capacity of around 100 Mt, it was adjusted to 50 Mt in the 1980's and
Subsequently re-adjusted again to 37 Mt in. the early 90's, The current projections remain at
around 49 Mt by 2006.

28.2 This situation is alarming for a country of our size and population where the steel
consumption is not even 30 Kg/Capita/year against the consumption of over 400
Kg/Capita/year in the developed countries. If we apply the similar thinking of China as in the
late 1970's and 1980's and try to catch up with the world standard of living, then India should
have looked for a consumption of at least 100 Mt of steel per year, This level of consumption
for a country of 100 crore population will mean consumption level of only 100
Ky/Capita/year against the world average of 141 Kg/capita/year, and even the China’s
consumption level of over 100 Kg/capita/year. The surplus availability of steel at the present
level of consumption indicates a serious weakness in the Indian economy which needs to be
addressed immediately if the future development of the country is to be kept in mind,

28.3 Though the efforts on infrastructure development gave some breathing space for steel
industry, the exports have started falling down due to increased competition and lower prices,
With the result that steel makers have to look at the domestic market to find ways and means
to improve the demand. The export prices which were at one time giving good margins are
now at a level where the producers are able to meet only the production costs. Thus it can be
seen that Indian Stee! is currently going through a tough time and its ambitious growth plans

29.0 NATIONAL STEEL POLICY

29.1 Though the steel sector has a Position in top 10 PSUs of the country, they do not
figure in the highest top 10 profit earners of the country. This is due to steel being a capital
intensive industry and its gross margin is around the range of 15 to 17 percent . Fiscal relief
can help to limited extent. It calls for a set of major decisions so far as Indian Steel industry is
concerned both in public and private sector to come out of the current difficult situation,
Some of the issues which are to be addressed to come out of the current situation for the steel
industry in India are as follows :

1. Steel must be treated as an unique industry

ii Provide level playing field to domestic steel industry vis-a-vis cheaper imports.
WTO compliance should be such that domestic industry is not effected,

iii Industrial policy has to be looked at more closely to generate more demand for
the basic commodity like steel, It has to be taken up on war-footing.

iv Investment in infrastructure like railways, ports, highways and certain key sectors
of economy like petroleum refineries, housing, power and white goods sectors
has to get priority.

५. Steel plants have to be expanded to their optimum infrastructural levels which
can be sustained rather than looking for green field steel plants so that advantage
of economy of scale and incremental investment over existing base can be reaped
by the existing steel plants.

vi_ Technology selection has to be based on less direct employment to add economy
in operations

vii The Contract Labour Laws have to be looked at so that each unit is able to limit
itself to the key operations and maintenance activities and the rest of the
operations have to be sub-contracted.

viii Rationalisation of duty structure

ix Abolition of adverse railway freight changes

x Certain labour laws will need to be amended to bring the industry to its highest

levels of human efficiency without any recourse to disturbances in smooth
running of the steel plants.

xi Office automation has to get a thrust in addition to the automation in the plant
based on IT systems so that lesser number of employees do more elegant jobs,

xii Some of the unviable units have to be closed down and only those with potentials
have to be expanded to enhance their efficiencies.
