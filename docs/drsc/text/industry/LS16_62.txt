Report of the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry on the Demands For Grants 2000-2001 pertaining to the
Department of Public Enterprises  which was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on  the 14th  May, 2001.

CHAPTER-I
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY      THE GOVERNMENT
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
After hearing the Secretary, Department of Public Enterprises, the Committee expressed its unhappiness that the Expenditure Reforms Commission
has recommended that the task of preparing MoUs can be left as well to the concerned administrative Ministries.  The Committee feels that the said
Commission in an anxiety to reduce the Government expenditure undermined the role of a nodal agency, i.e., Department of Public Enterprises
without evaluating its performance over the years.
COMMENTS
 
The recommendations of the Committee were kept in view while reviewing the role and functions of DPE in the context of the recommendations
made by the ERC and it has been decided that the work relating to MOU can not be transferred to the Administrative Ministries and should continue
to be performed by the DPE in the interest of maintaining objectivity and credibility of the system apart from ensuring transparency and
accountability.
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee is also of the view that a unified central agency to implement the MoU system is certainly more cost effective rather than
having separate MoU Units in all the Administrative Ministries.  Similarly, pooling expertise for all the Ministries from the same ATF as is being
done at present by the Department of Public Enterprises is also cost effective.
 
COMMENTS
 
The recommendations of the Committee have been kept in view and it has been decided to continue with the present arrangement of implementing
the MOU System by the nodal Department namely, DPE rather than leaving the task of preparing MOUs to the concerned Administrative Ministries
as recommended by the ERC.  The present system of implementing MoU System through a unified central agency like DPE is not only cost-
effective but also imparts objectivity and credibility to the system.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee further feels that the Department of Public Enterprises provides a neutral forum for finalizing the MoUs, which is essential
to make it an objective system, and it should not be abolished.
 
COMMENTS
 
Government agrees with the view of the Committee that a neutral forum like the Department of Public Enterprises is necessary for finalizing MOUs
to make it an objective system. Government has no intention to dilute its nodal and neutral status as at present.
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee is also of the firm view that the object of signing MoUs would not be achieved in the absence of an over-seeing agency, i.e.
Department of Public Enterprises.

COMMENTS
 
The Government agrees with the views of the Committee. DPE’s status as a nodal and neutral over-seeing agency will continue.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the proposal of abolishing MoU Division may be shelved.  As the country is now on
the threshold of making PSEs more vibrant, productive and efficient, it is very essential that the MoU Division should be further strengthened
instead of being abolished.  Strengthening of MoU Division would go a long way in making PSEs profitable and responsive to competitiveness.
 
COMMENTS
 
The Government accepts the recommendations of the Committee. There is no proposal to abolish the MOU Division. As regards strengthening this
Division, position is reviewed from time to time  and appropriate action  taken wherever necessary.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee, therefore, again recommends that the Department of Public Enterprises should be strengthened so as to make it more
responsive.
 
COMMENTS
 
The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. Periodical review of the organizational structure of DPE is being conducted and
whenever necessary, action is being taken to strengthen the Department to make it more responsive.
CHAPTER-II
 
THE COMMITTEE DOES  NOT DESIRE TO PURUE IN VIEW OF THE GOVERNMENT' S REPLIES
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The committee is of the view that the present staff strength numbering about 136 of the Department of Public Enterprises needs to be
increased in view of the wide roles and functions being undertaken by the Department.  The Department needs to be strengthened.
 
COMMENTS
 
In the context of the recommendations of the Expenditure Reforms Commission (ERC), the role and functions of the Department were reviewed and
15 posts at various levels were abolished. The above recommendation  has been noted and the staff strength will be reviewed as per needs and due
strengthening and rationalisation will be done  where required.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The existing rate of Rs.9000 per head for non-recurring expenses towards infrastructure, office equipments, preparatory works, training
expenses, follow-up services, etc. of nodal agency, may prove insufficient.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the existing rate of Rs.9000
per head may be reviewed.  If necessary,  the allocation may be increased.
 
COMMENTS
 
This is the first year of implementation of  the Training programme  by the Department of Public Enterprises. The advice of the Committee is being
noted and while reviewing  the working of the scheme, the per head cost along with other aspects of the scheme would  be considered.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee feels that the Department of Public Enterprises is over burdened after being assigned the tasks of counselling, retraining and
redeployment of rationalized employees of Central PSEs.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Public Enterprises should
involve/appoint new technocrats, Research Officers, skilled workers etc.
COMMENTS
 
The recommendation of the Committee has been noted. The position with regard to implementation of the scheme would be reviewed periodically,
when the manpower requirement could also be reviewed.

RECOMMENDATION
 
            Since substantial new tasks have been assigned to the Department of Public Enterprises, the allocation of Rs.10.86 crores made for the year
2001-2002 does not appear to be sufficient.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the allocation made to the department by the Government
may be reviewed in the supplementary demands for grants.
 
COMMENTS
 
As far as the work relating to retraining and redeployment of rationalized employees of PSEs is concerned, this is the first year of implementation of
the Training Programme by the Department of Public Enterprises. The recommendation of the Committee is being noted and will be kept in view
while reviewing the Scheme.
 
 
COMMITTEE'S OBSERVATION
           
The Committee is of the view that the work relating to retraining and redeployment of rationalised employees of central PSEs recently assigned to
the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) is an important matter in the present context, which needs to be reviewed and monitored periodically.
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee, however, would like to know how far such an enlarged Board of Directors consisting of official/non-officials/part time
Directors has produced the desired results.  The Committee feels that the Department of Public Enterprises should undertake a study in the matter.
 
COMMENTS
 
While granting enhanced autonomy and authority under Navratna and Miniratna, one of the preconditions was that Boards of these Navratna and
Miniratna CPSUs should be professionalised by inducting of non-official part-time Directors through Search Committee. Accordingly the exercise
for professionalisation of Boards in case of existing 11 Navratna CPSUs was completed in April, 1999.  In case of Miniratna, Board of Directors of
17 CPSUs out of 46 have been restructured by co-opting part-time professional Directors. It may be mentioned that the Boards are not enlarged, but
only vacant positions have been filled.
A study was carried out in consultation with all the 11 navratna PSUs and those Miniratna PSUs, where non-official part-time Directors have been
inducted. It is revealed that the Boards have exercised the enhanced powers to a large extent. The induction of non-official Directors has helped in
professionalising the Boards of these PSUs and also to set up various sub-committees like Audit Committee. The non-official Directors with their
rich experience and expertise in various areas like management, administration, finance, marketing, technology, etc. have contributed substantially
in the Board deliberations. Thus the study has revealed that the by and large induction of non-official professional Directors has produced the
desired results.
 
COMMITTEE'S OBSERVATION
 
            The Committee feels that autonomy of  PSUs should not be limited by Govt. control and such Companies should be fully Board-managed
Companies in all matters. In an environment of stiff competition in the post liberalisation era, the PSUs can not function effectively without
sufficient freedom of operation and level playing field. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Boards of Directors of at least MOU-signing
Companies should be given sufficient powers to enable them to take quick decisions in their business related  matters, by reviewing the extent of the
current powers relating to limit on investment, access to capital market, repayment of foreign loans, foreign visits  by Boards of Directors for their
business position,  forming of joint ventures, exploring foreign markets etc. 
 
            The Committee feels that many PSUs remain headless without any CMD for a long time. This cripples the functioning of the PSUs.
Therefore, as and when any PSU is without CMD for three months, immediate action should be taken to appoint CMD. The Committee understands
that the appointments of CMDs of Central  PSUs is done by Public Enterprises Selection Board. Earlier this Board was under the Department of
Public Enterprises which is presently being controlled by the Ministry of Personnel & Training . The Committee, therefore, recommends that this
Board may be shifted from the purview of  the Ministry of Personnel & Training  to the Department of Public Enterprises for an effective 
monitoring  of  the functioning of the Central PSUs.
 
            Smaller PSUs at micro level are facing lot of challenges of the competition. The best way to make PSUs viable is to merge the related PSUs
so that the competition can be effectively faced. For this Government  may appoint a Committee to look into the issue of merger.
 
            The Committee further observes that only 108 PSUs out of 240 PSUs have signed the MoU system  with their Administrative
Ministries/Departments. The Committee stresses that all profit making PSUs should be brought under the ambit of the MoU system.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
            The Committee, however, feels that the Department of Public Enterprises should review the performance of such companies in order to
know whether such companies are maintaining the status for which they were awarded such elevated positions.  The Committee feels that the
procedure to accord the Navratna status is a tardy procedure which needs to be revised by the Government.  Secondly, the responsibility of
accordance of the status of Miniratna, presently being held by the respective administrative ministries themselves to the concerned PSE, must be
transferred to the Search Committee under the Department of Public Enterprises.

COMMENTS
 
In May, 2001 the Government  issued guidelines for review of performance of Navratna and Miniratna Enterprises and for divest/grant of Navratna
and Miniratna status as the case may be which covers the points raised by the Committee.  These guidelines include:
 
Apex Committee headed by the Cabinet Secretary in the case of Navratna enterprises and Inter-Ministerial Committee headed by the Secretary,
Department of Public Enterprises in the case of Miniratna enterprises would regularly review the performance of such companies.
A comprehensive review would be undertaken every three years by the above mentioned respective Committees with regard to maintenance of the
Navratna/Miniratna status.  The Apex Committee would lay down the format for such a review.
The Apex Committee would also review the operations of Navratna/Miniratna schemes every three years and would make recommendations in
regard to further delegation of autonomy to these enterprises
The recommendations of the Apex Committee, if any, for divestment of the Navratna status would be put up to the Minister(Heavy Industries and
Public Enterprises) for approval through the Minister in-charge of the administrative Ministry concerned.  In case of any disagreement, the matter
would be put up to the cabinet.
The recommendations of the divestment of Miniratna status would be dealt with by concerned administrative Ministry only.
 
The recommendations for accordance of Navratna status to a CPSU would, ordinarily, not be placed before the Cabinet.  Based on the
recommendations of the Apex Committee these would be placed before the Minister for Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises for taking a final
decision.  But as in (iv) above, in case of disagreement, the matter would be put up to the Cabinet.
 
This guideline has set in place separate Committee to review the performance of Navaratna and Miniratna PSEs. The periodicity of the review will
be every 3 years. It has also laid down the procedure for according Navaratna Status.
 
The recommendation of assigning the task of accordance of the status of Miniratnas to the Search Committee under DPE has been noted and will be
kept in view at the time of reviewing of the existing mechanism.
 
COMMITTEE'S OBSERVATION
 
            The Committee desires that an early decision may be taken regarding assigning the tasks of accordance of the status of Miniratnas to the
Search Committee under DPE. The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation that the procedure to accord Navaratna status may be revised
and simplified.

RECOMMENDATION ( 5.6 )
 
            The Committee is very much concerned to note that certain Central PSEs/Central Govt. Agencies are not including the Purchase Preference
clause in their Notice Inviting Tender(NIT) depriving the other Central PSEs to avail the benefit of the Government Policy.  This non-cooperative
attitude of some of the PSEs does not go well with the overall policy of the government to improve the health of PSEs.  The highlights indicated by
the Minister would remain only highlights without a major shift in their health status.  If there has to be a major reversal in the health condition of
the PSEs, all the PSEs  need to have mutual cooperation.  Purchase Preference Policy is one such grey area which, if followed scrupulously by all
the PSUs, would improve the performance of all the ailing PSEs.

COMMITTEE'S OBSERVATIONS ON THE PURCHASE PREFERENCE POLICY ------  ( 5.4,  5.6,  5.7 & 5.8 )
 
(1-5.4)             In this connection, the Sub-Committee-III for the Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises took up this matter for
consideration and held discussions with the Secretary, the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises.
 
The Secretary observed that if the PSEs were to be efficient, the crutches were to be removed. The Secretary informed that this policy provided
Purchase Preference in supply of goods and services to the government departments and other PSEs if the price quoted by the supplying PSE was
within 10% of the lowest valid bid price, other things being equal.  The policy was extended up to 31.3.02.  He pointed out that 51 PSUs took
advantage of the policy and 22 PSUs awarded the contracts under the policy.  There had been cases where Purchase Preference clause was not
included in the Notice Inviting Tenders (NIT).  The Administrative Ministries/Departments had been told to ensure that the guidelines issued by the
Government including D/o Public Enterprises were implemented by the PSEs.  He observed that there was slackness in following the guidelines and
it became difficult to correct action at a later stage wherever a violation of the guidelines took place.
 
            The Sub-Committee also held discussions with the CMDs of  many CPSUs and most of them have agreed that this policy should be
extended beyond 31.3.3002 and were also of the view that the policy should be strictly complied with by all PSUs.
 
            The Committee is  of the firm view that in the interest of the Nation and well being of the PSEs, the Purchase Preference Policy must
continue. It may be extended beyond 31
st April, 2002 at least for two more years.
 
 
(2-5.6 & 5.7)   The Committee also recommends that the Department of Public Enterprises should  vehemently oversee the coordination, monitoring
and implementation of the Purchase Preference Policy in true spirit.  The Committee further recommends that if  any Administrative
Ministry/Department/CPSUs/Autonomous Bodies  does not desire to incorporate the Purchase Preference Clause in their NITs, then they   should 
seek  exemption  from  the  Cabinet  through  the Department  of  Public Enterprises.
 
(3-5.8)             The Committee also recommends that all the Administrative Ministries/Department/CPSUs/Autonomous Bodies  must send a copy
of the NIT to the Department of Public Enterprises, well in advance before publication in newspapers, to enable the DPE to examine the NIT and
check whether the Purchase Preference Clause has been included in the NIT.  The Committee further  recommends that the monitoring cell of DPE
must submit a status note to the Cabinet Secretariat every quarter in this regard with a copy to the Department related Parliamentary Standing
Committee on Industry for effective monitoring of the Policy by the DPE.
