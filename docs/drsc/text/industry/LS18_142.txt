Chapter - I
 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS, WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE MINISTRY   OF MINES
 
RECOMMENDATION
1.1       Structural And Functional Pattern of the Department of   Mines
The Committee notes the structural and functional pattern of the Department of   Mines,   the  Secretariat  for the 
Department of  Mines and  is of the  view  that structural aspect must  match the functional requirement and working  
of  the  organisation with adequate degree  of co-ordination and collaboration   among  various units  thus contributing
on  their  part    in the overall functioning of the organisation in the interest of national  economy .
                                                                                                                        ( Para 1.8 )
                                                                                                                                               
ACTION TAKEN      
 
1.1.1    The structure of the Ministry  of Mines, together with the subordinate offices and other organisations under its
control is adequately geared to its functional requirements and responsibilities. The functions can be broadly categorised
into Survey  and related activities aimed at identifying  mineral resources on the  mainland and offshore, natural hazard
mitigation and environmental preservation; regulation of mining activity; policy for  promotion and development of
mineral resources and mobilising private sector  and foreign investment for the purpose;  Research and Development
with the aim of developing state of the art technology and processes in mineral development and mining activities.
These activities are being performed through the Geological Survey of India (GSI), Mineral Exploration Corporation
Ltd(MECL), the Indian Bureau of Mines(IBM),  Public Sector Undertakings,  3 R&D Organisations viz National
Institute of Rock Mechanics (NIRM), Jawaharlal Nehru Aluminium Research and Development  Design Centre
(JNARDDC) and National Institute of Miners Health (NIMH),  and institutional arrangements for regular interaction
with the State Government  and the Industry viz the Mineral Advisory Council(MAC), Quarterly Performance Review
meetings/Governing Body meetings of the various organisations under the Ministry and meetings of the MAC are being
held regularly  to monitor the operational aspects and review issues relating to Policy requirements.

RECOMMENDATION
 
1.3       Philosophy behind the budget proposals
 
            The Committee is pleased to note that  the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Ministry of
Mines is broadly based on the principle that extensive survey and investigation should be carried out in the field of
minerals in which the country is deficient or which are of strategic importance for the country. The Committee strongly
feels that the budget proposals for the year 2003-04 pertaining to the Ministry of Mines are expected to prove as an
instrument to march forward towards the national goal of overall development in the era of liberalized and globalised
economy.   The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Mines , discharging its core activities as well as its
responsibility and role as a facilitator in the liberalized and globalised economy must guide the organizations and its
PSUs to determine the national goals for growth and development of Mining Industry (i.e. identification of goals,
mineral specific industry wise), Chalking out set of programmes, course of Action required and overall monitoring  for
execution of the plans without time and cost over run.  Any effort for shrugging of its responsibility would raise a
question mark on  the raison-de-itre for   the Ministry of Mines. 
( Para 2.5.2 )
 
Action Taken   
 
1.3.1    The valuable suggestion of the Committee has been noted and would be observed for compliance. Efforts are
being made assiduously in the Ministry to ensure timely implementation of various projects and plan priorities through
regular review and monitoring of the activities of the various organisations and PSUs under its control.

1.10     Grant to Bharat  Gold Mines Ltd.
            The matter of BGML is sub-judice.  However, the confession of the Secretary, Ministry of Mines before the
Committee that in most probably , there is a gold reserve in nearby areas and information as furnished by the Secretary,
that some foreign companies have shown their willingness to do exploitation work in the nearby areas, reopens the
chapter of BGML.  At this juncture, since it is a case of closure or continuation of a century old gold mining company
and related with this is the fate of committed worker, and development of region and not only this, also the issue of
proper and commercial utilization of embedded a scarce resource (that is gold) is concerned, it is expected that the
Ministry of Mines  would  consider  its  earnest duty to  apprise  the  Court with  all these material facts with due
diligence and willingness to highlight the fact regarding the possibility of running the mill for possible exploration of
gold reserves in near by areas.  The committee strongly recommends that bureaucratic bottlenecks should not come on
to  the way of possible revival of BGML. 
                                                                                                                        ( Para 4.10.4)
1.11     Variation of non-plan expenditure
            The Committee is in agreement with the plea of Ministry of Mines as regards the reason for variation of non-
plan expenditure projected at Rs.83.62 crore in BE  2002-03 but the same was reduced to Rs.15.00 crore in RE stage
and to the fund of Rs. 83.01 crore for VRS purpose.
                                                                                                            ( Para 4.10.7)
1.12     Protection of the Interest of the Workers
            The Committee reiterates its recommendations that in the case of winding up/liquidation of the company the
interest of the workers/employees be protected in all respects.  The committee further made its recommends that the
large tracts of land/other assets should be properly utilized and the assets of the company should not be allowed to
deteriorate owing to inadequate/lack of maintenance.  Regular maintenance of the asset may be kept.  Proper utilization
of asset of the company in the case of winding up means  its proper disposal/strategic sale and mitigation of the
company’s liability of optimum appropriation of proceeds of such strategic sale and not the utilization of the plants and
machinery in the process of production even if  the company is closed. The Committee notes with concern the financial
sate of BGML.
                                                                                                            ( Para 4.10.10)
Action Taken   
1.12.1  The efforts made by Government to revive the company go back to the period when BGML was referred to
BIFR in 1992. BIFR had appointed  the Industrial Credit and Investment Corporation of India(ICICI) as the Operating
Agency (OA). ICICI had prepared a rehabilitation scheme and submitted the same to the Government. The
rehabilitation scheme prepared by ICICI involved a very substantial  fresh infusion of funds by the Government of
India. The matter was considered in detail and the Government decided in June, 1997 that the scheme was not feasible
and acceptable.
1.12.2  The Government also  explored  the possibility of rehabilitating BGML through joint venture route.  A
Committee under the chairmanship of the then Additional Secretary (Mines) was constituted for this purpose  The
Committee had appointed M/s S.B. Billimoria and Company as consultants and advertised in national and international
press calling for expressions of interest from potential  co-promoters for participation in the revival of BGML. In
response to the advertisement 20 parties had sent their pre-bid expression of interest, only 11 bought the bid documents
and only 3 submitted their proposal. The Committee finally concluded that none of the bidders were technically,
financially or otherwise capable of undertaking the revival process  through the joint venture route.
1.12.3  The Cabinet in its meeting held on 22
nd December, 1999 directed that BIFR be informed that BGML may now
be allowed to be wound up under the provisions of Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions), Act, 1985 after all
the employees on the rolls of the Company as on 31.3.1999 are offered the benefit of the separation scheme. In the wake
of this the BIFR recommended winding up of the company and, separately the Ministry of Labour issued  closure
orders. These orders were challenged in the High Court of Karnataka.
1.12.4  As regards gold reserves in area technical and geological assessment was carried out by different agencies and
experts including independent international agencies like UNDP who based their report on exploration work done in and
around KGF. However, it did not result in the discovery of  any economically  mineable gold deposits.
1.12.5  The Hon’ble high Court of Karnataka had been apprised of the position during the hearings. In its final order
dated 16.9.03, the High Court has upheld these orders, and has also made certain recommendations regarding the
Voluntary Separation Scheme etc. Further action is being taken in the light of the judgement.
1.12.6  Rs. 15 crores had been released during 2002-03 to BGML to release the employees of BGML in connection with

Special Terminal Benefit Package(STBP) which had been offered. 456 employees who opted for STBP have been
released.  Action for separation of the remaining employees  will now  be taken in pursuance of the final orders of the

1.12.7  While the Government has been announcing Voluntary Retirement   Scheme (VRS) from time to time for

time are being disposed off by auctions. Further action for disposal/utilisation of the larger assets of the Company will

1.13     Provision of Rs. 60.00 crore for keeping the operation of HCL
 
            The Committee is in agreement with the provision of Rs. 60.00 crore for keeping the operation of HCL
continuing on the presumption that prospective and strategic joint venture partner would come forward to give a new
lease of life to the company which is at the moribund stage and  heading towards point of no return. At this juncture the
provision of Rs. 60.00 crore for keeping the operation of HCL continuing would keep the hope alive.  The Committee
feels that the implementation of financial restructuring must be followed by massive rationalization of manpower for
rescuing HCL out of red.
                                                                                                                        ( Para 5.16)
Action Taken 
1.13.1  Rs.60.00 crores which was provided was mainly utilised to keep the company as a going concern and to keep the
net worth positive.  During the year 2002-03, 1570 employees have been separated from Surda mine and other
establishments  Since then orders for closure of Khetri mine have also been passed by the Ministry of Labour,  as a
result of which around  450 employees   would be separated on VRS. The total number of employees has already  been
reduced to 6455 as on 30.9.03 and will further come down to 6000 after closure of the Khetri Mines. Efforts to further
reduce manpower in various units are also continuing.

that while    it  is rational for  grants in aid to research projects  and provision of funds for awards  but at the same time
opines that autonomous and scientific  bodies should generate revenue to meet non-plan expenditure.  In  this regard 
JNARRDC,  National  Institute of  Rock Mechanics (NIRM) and  National Institute of Miner's Health (NIMH)  should
generate sufficient fund to meet their requirement of non-plan  expenditure.  In today's  commercial world service is
regarded as product so these scientific  institutions should open avenues  for  generating revenue by providing
consultancy services.  By  diversification and exploring the possibility of service  mix, these  institutions may go for 
service endowment  in the field  of  consultancy, training and ideas for project generation.  They should become the 
seller of    ideas  and once the measures click they may reign and  rule  in the    world   market of ideas.  On the  whole
the committee is of the  view  that scientific institutions  should  create  opportunity for extending consultancy service
and help Government  in pruning  of non-plan expenditure.

3.8       Mineral Advisory Council
 
            The Committee is of the view that the Mineral Advisory Council should meet  at least four times in a year to sort
out the problems being faced by Mining Industry and suggest possible solutions and if required modification in the
existing legal framework.  The Committee feels that interaction of the representatives of Central Government, State
Governments and investors in Mining Sector would solve the mutual problems and thus the consensus-ad-idem and
common efforts so evolved would lead to rapid growth and development of Mining Industry in general.
                                                                                                                        ( Para 8.4)
Action Taken   
 
3.8.1    Mineral Advisory Council  is a  High Powered Committee having the Minister in-charge of Mines  as chairman
and  it has  representation from  all concerned Central Ministries/Deptts. and  major minerals  producing States as
permanent member  and other states  as  members  on rotational  basis, besides  leading  mining companies  and
Federation of  Indian  Minerals  Industries(FIMI).  Normally,  Mineral Advisory Council  is  constituted  for  a period
of  four years  and its meetings  are held  annually.  Since  it is  a forum for  interaction among Central Government,
State Govts. and the Industry to sort out  problems being faced by States and the  Industry, views  of all  the Stake
holders are  solicited  and these are consolidated, processed and analysed before   convening  the meeting of  MAC. 
Interactions on various issues are being held in various other fora such as the  Conference of  the State Ministers of 
Mining & Geology,   tripartite meetings among the  Central Govt., State Govts. and investors and  issue specific
meetings and disucssions.    Based  on the discussions and decisions taken in these meetings  necessary amendments  in
the Act if required  and subordinate legislation  are also  carried out  in  consultation with  Legislative Deptt.  Keeping
all this in view,   it may not be possible, feasible or necessary  to hold  meetings of  MAC on a quarterly basis.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
 
3.8.2    The Committee is of the view that Mineral Advisory Council may play the proactive role in solving the mutual
problems being faced by Mining Industry and thus encourage common efforts of the policy maker and the Centre and
the State level only when it meets frequently or at least twice a year i.e. once for chalking out the necessary course of
action for accelerating the mining activities and second meeting to review the progress made.  Alternatively a
Committee consisting of representatives of Central Government, State Governments and investors in Mining Sector may
be constituted for brining the modification in the existing legal framework and striking the consensus-ad-idem to resolve
the mutual disputes and undertaking massive action for the rapid growth and development of mining industry in
general.  The said Committee may meet at least quarterly a year.

(Para 3.12.2)
 
3.13     Financial performance of MECL
 
            The Ministry of Mines being the administrative ministry, it is its responsibility to take all the necessary actions
and whatever follow up actions are required for getting the node of the Ministry of Finance in regard to the
Comprehensive Financial Restructuring of MECL for ameliorating the financial position of MECL.
(Para 3.13.2)

3.15     Impact of globalisation and liberalization
                        The Committee understands that statutory amendments have been made in the relevant laws and rules
which gives greater power to states for streamlining the mining regime in view of the ongoing liberalization process. 
The Committee is pleased to note that necessary provision has been enacted to prevent the negative fall out of the
globalization process however, the efforts may be taken for reaping the benefits of the globalization and liberalization
process in the mining sector.
 
(Para 3.15.3)
3.16     “ Key concern Areas of Mining Industry”
 
            The Committee would like to be assured by the Ministry of Mines that the cost of mining  exploration and
operation is the minimum in India while compared to the other parts of the world.    And in this regard adequate
research and development  should continue.
(Para 3.16.3)
3.17     Minimum time gap during the process of  grant of mineral concession,
 
            The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry of Mines in sensitizing  the State Governments and
various Central Ministries/Departments on the need to work in coordination through meetings of the Mineral Advisory
Council (MAC)   the Conference of the State Ministers of Mining and Geology  and the Tripartite meeting of the
Central Government, State Governments and the investors.  However, the Committee would like to know response of
the Ministry of Mines in regard to the suggestion for formulating a single window mechanism to minimize time gap
during the process of  grant of mineral concession, clearance from the Ministry  of Environment and Forest  and the
final commencement of actual operation.
(Para 3.17.4)
 
3.18     Taxation regime suitable to the mineral sector
 
                        The Committee is deeply anguished to find that the recommendations  of  the Multi Disciplinary
Committee MCD, constituted by the Ministry of Mines in 1998 to look into a taxation regime suitable to the mineral
sector, is long pending in the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry of Mines has already examined and accepted the
recommendations of Multi Disciplinary Committee and it has been found that the recommendations of the Multi
Disciplinary Committee contains the potential and strategy for making Mining Sector globally competitive. The
Committee , therefore, reiterates that the Ministry of Mines should pursue the matter with the Department of Revenue(
Ministry of Finance and Company Affairs).
(Para 3.18.2)
