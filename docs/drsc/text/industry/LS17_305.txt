REPORT 
The Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) are rightly hailed as „the temples of modern India.‟ 
They were conceived to be a critical component of our mixed economy and were put into 
operation for transforming the independent India into a modern and industrially advanced nation. 
Primacy given to the CPSEs arose out of the fact that the private enterprises were not adequately 
equipped to undertake the mammoth task of creating infrastructure and other facilities which 
were indispensable for nation -building. The contribution of PSEs towards the growth of Indian 
economy through measures such as generation of income, capital formation, generation of 
employment, development of infrastructure facilities, building a strong industrial base, export 
promotion and impor t substitution, contribution to central exchequer, removal of regional 
disparities, checking concentration of income & wealth etc. is really commendable. 
 The Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) in India 
2. The Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSE s) comprise enterprises established by the 
Government of India (GOI) as Government companies under Section 617 of the Companies Act, 
and wherein the equity holding of the GOI is more than 50 per cent. It also includes statutory 
corporations constituted und er specific statutes of the Parliament. The CPSEs do not, however, 
include departmental undertakings, banking institutions and enterprises where equity holding of 
the GOI is 50% or less.  
3. The number of CPSEs has increased from 5 during the first five ye ar plan to 366 as on 
31.3.2020. The cumulative investment (paid up capital plus long term loans), which was Rs. 29 
crore in 5 enterprises as on 31.03.1951, has gone up to Rs.  20.62 lakh crore in 366 CPSEs.  Out 
of these 366 CPSEs, 256 are in Operation, 96 CPSEs have yet to commence business and 14 are 
under liquidation or approved for closure. Out of 256 operating CPSEs as many as 171 CPSEs 
showed profit during 2019 -20, 83 CPSEs incurred losses during the year, and 1 CPSE had no 
profit no loss. The Central Public Sector Enterprises are under the administrative control of 
various Ministries/ Departments and decision with regard to major investments, expansion, 
diversification, joint ventures, modernisation, technology upgradation, etc. are taken by the 
CPSEs in consultation with their respective administrative Ministries/Departments. 
 Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) 
4.      In their 52nd Report, the Estimates Committee of 3rd Lok Sabha (1962 -67) stressed the 
need for setting up a centralized coordinatin g unit, which could also make continuous appraisal 
of the performance of public enterprises. This led to the setting up of the Bureau of Public 
Enterprises (BPE) in 1965 in the Ministry of Finance. Subsequently, as a result of the 
reorganization of the Ministries/Departments of the Union Government in September, 1985, BPE 
was made part of the Ministry of Industry. In May, 1990, BPE was made a full fledged 
Department known as the DPE. Presently, it is part of the Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public 
Enterprises (Mo HI &PE).                            
 

2 
 
5.      The DPE is the nodal department for all the CPSEs and formulates policy pertaining to 
them. It lays down, in particular, policy guidelines on performance improvement and evaluation, 
autonomy and financi al delegation and personnel management in CPSEs. It further collects and 
maintains information in the form of a Public Enterprises Survey on several areas in respect of 
CPSEs.        
6.      In fulfilling its role, the Department coordinates with other Min istries, CPSEs and 
concerned organizations. As per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government, the 
following subjects have been allocated to the DPE: 
 Coordination of matters of general policy affecting all Public-Sector Enterprises.  
 Residual work relating to erstwhile Bureau of Public Enterprises including Industrial 
Management Pool. 
 Evaluation and monitoring the performance of Public Sector Enterprises, including the 
Memorandum of Understanding mechanism. 
 Matters relating to Permanent Machinery of  Arbitration for the Public -Sector 
Enterprises.  
 Counselling, training and rehabilitation of employees in Central Public Sector 
Undertakings under Voluntary Retirement Scheme. 
 Review of capital projects and expenditure in Central Public Sector Enterprises. 
 Measures aimed at improving performance of Central Public Sector Enterprises and other 
capacity building initiatives of Public Sector Enterprises. 
 Rendering advice relating to revival, restructuring or closure of Public Sector Enterprises 
including the mechanisms therefore. 
 Matters relating to Standing Conference of Public Enterprises. 
 Matters relating to International Center for Public Enterprises. 
 Categorization of Central Public Sector Enterprises including conferring „Ratna‟ status. 
 Survey of Public Enterprises.

Categorization of CPSEs 
12.  CPSEs are categorized into four schedules  namely „A‟, „B‟, „C‟ & „D‟ based on criteria 
such as quantitative parameters, i.e., Investment, Capital Employed, Net Sales, Profit before Tax, 
Number of employees and units, Capacity Addition, Revenue per employee, Sales/Capital 
Employed, Capacity Utiliz ation, Value added per employee as well as qua litative  parameters, 

4 
 
i.e., National importance, Complexities of the problems being faced by the company, Level of 
technology, Prospects of expansion and diversification and competition from other sectors are t o 
be considered while examining such proposals. In addition, critical/strategic importance of the 
concerned CPSE as well as factors such as Share price, MoU ratings, Ratna status, ISO 
certification are also taken into consideration. 
13.        The present procedure of categorization of CPSEs involves consideration of the 
proposals in the administrative Ministry concerned and the Department of Public Enterprises 
which consults the Public Enterprises Selection Board. The categorization of CPSEs has 
implications mainly for organizational structure and salary of Board level incumbents of the 
concerned CPSE. It also plays a role in grant of autonomy to the Board s of CPSEs under „Ratna‟ 
scheme. A s informed by DPE, at present there are 66 Schedule „A‟, 65 Schedule „B‟, 44 
Schedule „C‟ and 5 Schedule „D‟ CPSEs. The schedule -wise list of CPSEs is given in 
Annexure-I.                                                                                          
   Autonomy of CPSEs 
14.     The endeavor of the Government is t o make Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) 
autonomous board managed companies. Under Articles of Association, the Board of Directors of 
CPSEs enjoys autonomy in respect of recruitment, promotion and other service conditions of 
below board level emplo yees. The Board of Directors of a CPSE exercises delegated powers 
subject to broad policy guidelines issued by Government from time to time. The Government has 
granted enhanced powers to the Boards of the profit -making enterprises under various schemes 
like Maharatna, Navratna and Miniratna by delegating powers in the areas of (i) capital 
expenditure, (ii) investment in joint ventures/subsidiaries, (iii) mergers & acquisitions, (iv) 
human resources management, etc.  
15. The main objective of the Maharatna s cheme which was introduced in 2010 is to 
empower mega CPSEs to expand their operations and emerge as global giants. The Maharatna 
Scheme empowers big sized CPSEs to expand their operations and emerge as global giants.The 
Government had introduced the Navra tna scheme, in 1997, to identify Central Public Sector 
Enterprises (CPSEs) that had comparative advantages and to support them in their drive to 
become global giants.  In October 1997, the Government had decided to grant enhanced 
autonomy and delegation of financial powers to some other profit making companies subject to 
certain eligibility conditions and guidelines to make them efficient and competitive. These 
companies, called Miniratna, are in two categories, namely, Category - I and Category -II. As 
informed by DPE, p resently there are 10 Maharatna CPSEs, 14 Navratna CPSEs and 73 
Miniratna CPSEs (61 Miniratna category-I and 12 Miniratna category-II) (Annexure-II).

Scheme of  Research, Development and Consultancy (RDC) on Generic issues related to 
Public Sector Enterprises : 
31.    The Scheme of RDC on Generic issues of Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) was 
introduced in the year 2007 -08 and is being implemented since 2008 -09. In view of instructions 
issued by the Ministry of Finance (MoF) with regard to rationalization of Schemes, the  Scheme 
for Skill Development/Training programme to executives/employees of State Level Public 
Enterprises (SLPEs) was merged with the Scheme of RDC on Generic issues of Public Sector 
Enterprises (PSEs) during the year 2016-17. 

10 
 
32.    RDC scheme was modified so that activities could be spread round the year and its scope 
was expanded and diversified to include more activities. Some of the features of revised scheme 
are elaborated as under: 
        i.          Thematic studies and consultancies on generic is sues like corporate governance, 
Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) system, professionalization, project management, risk 
management, human resource management, Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) etc. related

MoU System for CPSEs 
64.    The Government of India introduced the MoU system in the year 1986, based on the 
recommendations given by Arjun Sen Gupta Committee Report (1984) . The report 
recommended that the CPSEs should enter agreement with their administrative Ministries for 
five years, with progress to be reviewed annually. The MoU system was given broader thrust by 
the Governments after the announcement of the New Industrial Policy of 1991. 
65.    DPE monitors and evaluates the profitability and efficacy of MoU signing CPSEs 
through their MoU targets & their evaluation to measure the performance of the management of 
the CPSEs on key selected parameters so as to improve the critical performance indicators of the 
organization. 
66.    MoU guidelines are reviewed from time to time based on the feedback received from 
Administrative Ministries/ CPSEs and based on the changes occurred in the Regulations. 
67. Some of the main points  of the latest MoU Guideline issued by DPE on 17th January, 
2019 are as under:- 
1. Memorandum of Understanding: MoU is a negotiated agreement and contract between 
the Administrative Ministry/Department/ Holding CPSEs i.e. majority shareholder and the 
Management of the Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSE) on selected parameters having 
targets decided normally before the start of a new financial year and results evaluated af ter the 

20 
 
end of the year to measure  the performance. MoU system involves target set ting in financial and 
non- financial areas and performance evaluation of those targets. 
2. Purpose of MoU:  The purpose of the MoU is to measure the performance of the 
management of the CPSEs on key selected parameters against the targets agreed upon so as to 
improve the critical performance indicators of the organisation. 
3. Scope: All CPSEs (Holding as well as Subsidiaries) are required to sign MoUs. The 
Holding companies will signMoUs with their administrative Ministries/ Departments, while the 
Subsidiary companies will sign MoUs with their respective Holding companies. 
 
4. Exemption from MoU:The following CPSEs are exempted from MoU system: 
 
i.     CPSEs under liquidation where Liquidator has already been appointed. Administrative 
Ministry would provide the list of such CPSEs. 
ii.       CPSEs which are not in operation or having no employees or on any other ground on 
the recommendation of administrative Ministry with approval of InterMinisterial 
Committee or High Powered Committee. 
5.     Parameters: CPSEs work in various sectors under different conditions. In view of this, the 
following guidelines are laid down:- 
5.1    For 50% weightage, t here would be uniform parameters for measuring financial 
performances such as revenue from operations, operating profi t and return on investment (e.g. 
ratio of PAT/Net -worth). This would be applicable to all CPSEs, except CPSEs which are 
dependent on government grant or performing functions of distribution of grant etc. e.g. 
Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Coun cil (BIRAC). Hence, 3 financial parameters 
have been prescribed for all CPSEs with total weightage of 50% except for CPSEs like BIRAC. 
5.2    For the remaining 50% weightage, a menu of parameters has been suggested for selection 
depending on the sector in which the CPSE is operating. Separate Format has been given for 
Finance Sector and another Format for the remaining operating CPSEs. The parameters most 
appropriate and relevant for measuring performance shall be suggested by the Pre -Negotiation 
Committee (PNC) to the Inter -Ministerial Committee (IMC). In all the cases IMC shall take 
appropriate decision on the suggestions made by PNC. 
5.3    Chairman, IMC is authorised to modify the parameters or weightages of parameters in 
sector specific cases, if justified. 
6. High Powered Committee (HPC) : The High -Powered Committee (HPC) is the Apex 
body under MoU system for laying policy guidelines, approval of the benchmarks and indices 
recommended by IMC.   
7. Pre-negotiation Committee (PNC):  The role of the Pre -negotiation Committee (earlier 
known as Standing Committee on MoU) would be to assist IMC in determining the most 
appropriate and relevant parameters for measuring improvement in performance and for fixing 
targets. Meetings of the Pre -negotiation Committee wou ld be held in each case before the 
meetings of IMC to look at the trends, discuss, negotiate and recommend MoU parameters and 

21 
 
targets. The Pre -negotiation committee comprises Adviser (MoU), DPE, concerned Joint 
Secretary/Adviser of Administrative Ministry, concerned Adviser (NITI Aayog), Director (MoU) 
and representative from Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI). 
8. Inter-Ministerial Committee (IMC) : MoU targets are decided by the IMC. Inter -
Ministerial Committee on MoU consists of Secr etary, DPE as Chairman, Secretary of concerned 
administrative Ministry/Department or his representative not below the rank of Joint Secretary 
(Member), Secretary, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation or his representative 
not below the rank of Joint Secretary (Member), Additional Secretary, NITI Aayog or his 
representative (Member). Secretary, DPE may co -opt any officer who is a finance expert in case 
the need is felt. 
9. Time-lines for submission of MoU:  The draft MoU with all documents/ Ann exures 
should be submitted to administrative Ministry/ Department in respect of all CPSEs and their 
subsidiaries by 21st November of each year for the forthcoming year. The draft MoU after the 
approval of administrative Ministry/ Department should be sent to DPE by 15th December of 
each year for the forthcoming year with all documents/Annexures. Administrative Ministry/ 
Department would ensure that the targets are realistic, growth -oriented, aspirational and 
consistent with the latest Annual Plan, Budget and Corporate Plan of the CPSEs. 
10.   MoU Signing Process :MoU based on the parameters, targets and weightage 
recommended by IMC without any deviation shall be signed between CMD/ MD of CPSE and 
Secretary of administrative Ministry/ Department in case of holding/ independent CPSEs and 
between CEO/ MD of subsidiary company and CMD/ MD of holding CPSE in case of subsidiary 
by 31st March (i.e. before start of financial year in respect of which targets are fixed) or within 
21 days from issue of IMC meeting minutes, whichever is later. In case, deviation is detected, 
IMC minutes would prevail. 
11.   MoU Evaluation: Evaluation of MoU of the CPSE is done after the end of the year on 
the basis of actual achievements vis -à-vis the MoU targets. CPSEs (Holding as well a s 
Subsidiaries) are required to submit performance evaluation reports on the basis of audited 
accounts to Department of Public Enterprises after approval of the Board of CPSE and through 
the administrative Ministries/Departments on or before 30th September  (in respect of 
immediately preceding year) or any other date communicated by DPE. Figures and information 
in the MoU achievement which are not verifiable from audited accounts would be relied on the 
basis of certification by way of resolution of the Board given separately for each parameter. 
12. MoU Score  and  Rating:  MoU  score  is  an aggregate of score on all parameters with 
respect to performance vis -à-vis the targets. With a view to distinguish „excellent performance‟ 
from „poor performance‟, five d ifferent performance ratings have been fixed in the MoU, i.e., 
„Excellent‟, „Very Good‟, „Good‟, „Fair‟, and „Poor‟.  
13. CPSEs not signing of MoU or not submitting MoU evaluation:  The CPSEs who do 
not submit Draft MoU/ MoU evaluation through their adminis trative Ministries/ Departments 
within a stipulated time of the prescribed date will be rated as „poor‟. Stipulated time would be 
decided by Secretary, DPE (presently stipulated time is six months vide OM no M -

22 
 
03/0017/2016-DPE (MoU) dated 14.07.2017). The Prescribed dates are given in para 10 and 13

Revival/ Restructuring/ Closure and Disinvestment of CPSEs 
 
72. The Committee wanted to know the reasons for the l osses/ sickness in CPSEs. The 
Department in their written submission informed that some common problems for sickness in 
CPSEs include old and obsolete   plant  and machinery, outdated technology, low capacity 
utilization, low productivity, poor debt –equity structure, excess manpower, weak marketing 
strategies, stiff competition, lack of business plans, dependence on government orders, heavy 
interest burden, high input cost, resource crunch, etc. Further with privatization, liberalization 
and opening up of the economy, those CPSEs which did not change along with the fast -changing 
times lost ground to private companies and turned loss making and sick.  
 
73. The Committee hopes that the I nter Ministerial consultative process, together with 
DPE's role as a mentor  and guide would facilitate the incipient sick and weak CPSEs into 
taking mid -term course , corrections so that they are saved from entering into the 'sick' 
bandwagon and likewise the 'weak' CPSEs would be able to nurse t hemselves back into 
good financial health.  
 
74. The Government has taken many steps to make the mechanism and process for revival/ 
restructuring of CPSE time bound, comprehensive, performance driven and efficient.  The 
Government has decided to remove multiple layers in the decision making p rocess to ensure 
timely revival/ restructuring of sick CPSEs. It has been decided that revival/restructuring of sick/ 
incipient sick CPSEs is to be merit based taking into account strategic, national and business 
concerns of the CPSE. 
 
75. The DPE had on 2 9th October, 2015 laid down guidelines for streamlining the 
mechanism for restructuring/ revival or closure of sick or incipient sick CPSEs and weak CPSEs 
which were revised on 07th September 2016. Further, on 14th June 2018, DPE again issued 
revised guidelines on „time bound closure   of  sick/loss making CPSEs and disposal of movable 
and immovable assets. These replace the earlier guidelines issued by DPE on this subject. 
 
76. The guidelines are intended to expeditiously complete the procedures for closure  of 
CPSEs and lay down responsibilities   of the concerned Ministries/ Departments/ CPSE, etc., 
including the support required to be extended by nodal Departments/ Organizations like, 
Ministry of Finance, NITI Aayog, etc. The revised guidelines prescribe a matrix of timelines for 
step by step process to be completed in respect of a CPSE under closure and about disposal of its 
assets. The guidelines on time bound closure of sick/ loss making CPSEs uniformly provide for 
payment of VRS/VSS at 2007 notional pay scale to employees of CPSEs under closure 
irrespective of the existing pay scales of the company.

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) 
 
95. As per Section 135 of the Com panies Act, 2013, all profit making corporates, including 
CPSEs exceeding threshold limits  prescribed in the Act having  net worth of Rs. 500 crore, or 
turnover of Rs. 1000 crore or net profit of Rs. 5 crore, are mandated to spend at least 2% of the 
average net profits (Profit Before Tax) of the company made during the three immediately 
preceding years to undertake CSR activities. Such companies have to constitute a CSR 
committee of the Board, comprising three or more Directors with at least one Independent 
Director. 
  
96. The Companies (CSR Policy) Rules, 2014 notified by M/o Corporate Affairs and the 
Schedule VII of the Act lists the activities that can be undertaken under CSR. An advisory on 
observance of transparency and due diligence in selection and imp lementation of activities under 
CSR was issued by Department of Public Enterprises to the CPSEs in August, 2016. The 
Department has also issued guidelines on 10.12.2018  to all administrative Ministries & CPSEs 
for adopting a theme -based focused approach ev ery year on CSR expenditure by CPSEs. These 
guidelines inter alia provide that CSR expenditure for such thematic programmes should be 
around 60% of annual CSR expenditure of CPSEs. The aspirational districts identified by NITI 
Aayog may be given preference . The theme for 2019 -20 was School education, Healthcare and 
Nutrition and the theme identified for the FY 2020- 21 is „Health and Nutrition‟.

(Para 55) 
18. The Committee notes that for the Financial Year 2018 -19, both BE & RE allocation 
for RDC & CRR Schemes in the North -Eastern Region stood at Rs. 0.85 c rore.   Since AE 
was also Rs. 0.85 crore, DPE could utilize the entire amount during that year fo r RDC and 
CRR Schemes. 
(Para 56) 
19. However, during 2019 -20, AE was only Rs. 0.53 crore though both the BE & RE 
were Rs. 0.95 crore.  Therefore, DPE could spend only 56% of the allocated amount during 
the Financial Year 2019 -20.  During the Financial Year  2020-21, Rs. 1.05 crore was 
allocated as BE which was reduced to Rs. 0.56 crore at RE stage.  However, no expenditure 
was made under NER for both the Schemes during Financial Year 2020 -21, as no trainings 
could be conducted due to COVID -19 Pandemic.  For the Financial Year 2021 -22, Rs. 0.85 
crore has been allocated under NER for RDC & CRR Schemes.  Therefore, the Committee 
suggests that DPE should resume the trainings and conduct programmes in physical as well 
as virtual mode. The Committee is also of the view that under the CRR Scheme, all efforts 
should be made to identify more number of VRS optees/dependents in NER and  impart 
training to them to enhance their scope for redeployment.  Similarly, under the RDC 
Scheme also, more number of training programmes and workshops should be conducted to 

34 
 
enhance the optimum utilization of allocated budget even though the funds get transferred 
to non-lapsable pool, without getting lapsed. 
(Para 57) 
20. The Committee notes that CAPEX is an important component for moder nization 
expansion and diversification of CPSEs thereby improving the productivity of PSEs as well 
as economic growth of the country.  The Committee therefore suggests that DPE may 
continue to monitor the implementation of CAPEX activities of CPSEs regular ly.  DPE 
may also conduct more number of capacity building workshops on MoU System to sensitise 
CPSEs to leverage their net worth for CAPEX, as CAPEX is included as one of the 
parameters in the MoU evaluation system. 
(Para 62) 
21. The Committee further rec ommends that DPE should take appropriate steps to 
achieve the planned target of capital expenditure by CPSEs to revive the economy from the 
fallout of Covid-19 Pandemic.  
(Para 63) 
22. The purpose of MoU is to measure the performance of management of CPSEs  on 
key selected parameters against the targets agreed upon so as to improve the critical 
performance indicators of the organization.  It is an important tool being used by DPE for 
evaluating and monitoring the performance of CPSEs.  The Committee notes th at the 
number of MoUs signed during the Financial Year 2019 -20 and 2020 -21 was 144 and 124 
respectively. 
 
(Para 70) 
23. The Committee appreciates the new initiative taken by DPE to have a new 
framework for MoU System using a digital platform/dashboard for a self -driven MoU 
mechanism for the target setting and performance evaluation of CPSEs.  The Committee 
hopes that with the implementation of the New Framework and a digital dashboard with a 
centralized portal for entering, signing, monitoring and evaluatin g MoU agreements, the 
MoU System will be more simplified and transparent. 
(Para 71) 
24. The Committee hopes that the I nter Ministerial consultative process, together with 
DPE's role as a mentor and guide would facilitate the incipient sick and weak CPSEs i nto 
taking mid -term course , corrections so that they are saved from entering into the 'sick' 
bandwagon and likewise the 'weak' CPSEs would be able to nurse t hemselves back into 
good financial health.  
(Para 73) 

35 
 
 
 25. The Committee notes with concern that a  sizeable amount of land assets of CPSEs 
under closure are lying unsold/not transferred. The Committee recommends that DPE may 
use its good offices to impress upon the concerned administrative ministries to arrive at 
quick decisions so that these lands  could be utilized for useful purposes so as to become 
gainful and beneficial for the society at large, besides creating employment opportunities 
for the youth.  
(Para 79) 
26. The Committee is of the view that all efforts should be made to infuse the requisite  
capital into the CPSEs, so as to enable them to stand shoulder to shoulder with the private 
entities and offer stiff competition to them. The Committee feels that CPSEs had always 
played a vital role in unbundling privitisation sin ce a long time and thus deserve the much 
needed support. 
(Para 83) 
27. The Committee's view is that CPSEs are the backbone of the economy and there 
should be no stone left unturned to strengthen the CPSEs that are profit making and 
adding their contribution to the nation economic kitty.  The Committee also desires that it 
be apprised of the reasons as to why the reviving and restructuring mechanisms failed to 
have any positive effect on the loss making CPSEs.  
(Para 84) 
28. The Committee is of the opinion that DPE should have a me chanism in place to 
ensure that all their guidelines being issued from time to time are complied with by the 
CPSEs/Administrative Ministries/Departments.  The Committee notes that the 
Government has set a disinvestment target of Rs. 1.75 lakh crores and has decided to divest 
all PSEs which are not in the four strategic sectors identified by DIPAM, M/o Finance.  The 
Committee may be apprised of the revised mechanism for time bound closure of loss 
making CPSEs and disposal of assets, once it is finalized.  Th e Committee also notes that in 
the coming years, more number of CPSEs in the non -strategic sectors and strategic sectors 
(except those in the four areas identified) will be divested/privatized/merged/closed.  
Therefore, the number of employees likely to op t for VRS is also likely to increase.  
Therefore, Department should foresee this scenario and take appropriate measures timely 
to impart their skill training under CRR Scheme to enable them to get re-deployment. 
(Para 85) 
29. The Committee observes that  p rofessionalization of Boards of CPSEs plays a 
significant role in ensuring good corporate governance thereby promoting CPSEs to be 
more dynamic, competitive, performance oriented and viable in the market driven 
economy.  However, it is quite disheartening to note that around 480 positions of Non -
Official Directors (NOD) and 128  posts of senior officers including Functional  Directors 
are lying vacant on the Boards of various CPSEs as on date. The presence of NODs in the 

36 
 
Boards of CPSEs is essential to impro ve the functioning and effectiveness of the Board and 
to safeguard the interests of all stakeholders. 
(Para 91) 
30. The Committee strongly objects to the failure on the part of DPE to furnish the 
information regarding vacant posts of Directors to be filled  by PESB.  DPE brings out the 
Public Enterprises Survey every year & is also the nodal Department of all CPSEs.  Under 
such circumstances, DPE should have been prudent enough to make available  the 
information to the extent available, to the Committee for perusal. 
(Para 92) 
31. The Committee is dismayed by the increase in the number of vacant positions of 
Non-Official Directors of CPSEs from 313 in 2019 -20 to 480 in 2020 -21. Despite repeated 
recommendations made by the Committee in this regard, the Departmen t has not been 
proactive enough to get the huge number of vacant posts  filled up  which has a negative 
bearing on the effective functioning of the CPSEs.   The Committee feels disappointed by  
the inordinate delay in filling up such important positions, putti ng the effective functioning 
of CPSEs at stake.  The Committee therefore, recommends that DPE should play a more 
pro-active role and ensu re that vacancies of Directors o n the Boards of CPSEs do not 
remain vacant for more than six months  as any laid back ap proach in these matters would 
only jeopardize the growth of CPSEs as a whole.  The Department should desist in putting 
the complete onus on the Administrative Ministry/Department of the CPSEs or PESB.  The 
Committee further reiterates its earlier recommenda tion to include this as a parameter for 
MoU evaluation of CPSEs and impose penalty on such CPSEs wh ich do not fill up vacant 
posts of Directors in the Boards of CPSEs timely.  DPE should also chalk out a strategy to 
monitor the status of filling up of vaca ncies by the Search Committee/PESB/Management 
of CPSEs (in respect of below Board level posts). 
(Para 93) 
32. The Committee further recommends that a detailed exercise through a professional 
agency to assess the current status of implementation of various Human Resources (HR) 
development issues /activities in CPSEs should be conducted and best practices be 
identified  and replicated in CPSEs, as had been done by the Department earlier. 
(Para 94) 
33. The Committee is aware that under the Section 135 of the Co mpanies Act, 2013, 
CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility) activities have been made mandatory for all profit 
making CPSEs to allocate 2% of their average net profits (NBT) of the three preceding 
years. Further as per the latest guidelines issued by the DPE vide their OM dated 10 th 
December, 2018, the CPSEs are to utilise the CSR Fund in a focussed manner towards 
national priorities by adopting a theme based approach each year. The Committee  
understands that most of the contributions to the PM CARES Fund by t he CPSEs have 
been made from their CSR Funds diverting a large share that would have otherwise been 
utilised towards theme based CSR activities in the local areas.   The Committee therefore, 

37 
 
desires that the CSR Fund should be utilised to meet the targets s et for the theme based 
activities in the respective area and aspirational districts as spelt out in the guidelines. 
(Para 100) 
34. The Committee notes that CSR expen diture during the year 2019 -20 wa s much 
higher i.e. Rs. 5066.93 crore than the previous Fin ancial Year 2018 -19.  The Committee 
suggests that the CPSEs should utilize the CSR fund mainly for undertaking activities 
related to „Health & Nutrition‟, the theme identified during Financial Year 2020 -21, for 
focused intervention by CPSEs under CSR.  The Committee further recommends that DPE 
should also maintain information regarding the activities undertaken by CPSEs under 
CSR fund as it is a parameter for MoU evaluation & DPE is the nodal Department for all 
CPSEs.  Further, M/o Corporate Affairs may be impressed upon to involve local 
representatives in the project selection.  The Committee further is of the view that a set of 
statutory rules/guidelines may be framed or incorporated in the current rules ibid wherein 
it becomes mandatory to seek the opinion  of the local area representative before 
finalizing/implementing a scheme under CSR. The Committee is also of the view that more 
number of training programmes on CSR should be included in the RDC Scheme to enable 
CPSEs to undertake the right kind of activities under CSR.  
(Para 101) 
35. The Committee further recommends that CPSEs should fulfill the social objective of 
removing regional disparities in the count ry and should, therefore, also focus  their CSR 
expenditure in geographically remote and backward areas of the country, in addition to the 
Aspirational Districts. The Committee further desires that CPSEs should use their 
innovative ideas and management skills for environmental protection.  
(Para 102) 
36. The Committee observes that the Department has tak en initiatives to conduct 
studies in diverse areas. However, the Committee is surprised to note that no study has 
been conducted to assess the impact of Covid -19 on CPSEs, despite the fact that DPE is the 
nodal Department of all the CPSEs.  The Committee, therefore, is of the considered opinion 
that a detailed study needs to be conducted to comprehend at micro-level, the actual impact 
of COVID -19 pandemic and subsequent lockdowns on the performance of the CPSEs so 
that necessary in tervention by the Governme nt could  be made to mitigate the economic 
fallout.  
 (Para 104)
