REPORT
The architecture of mixed economy providing space both to the private and public sectors
served the nation well in reviving our growth and development which suffered irreparable damage
due to colonial rule. The private sector which is the engine of economic growth in 21
st century
India was in no position to invest monetary resources and employ skills and expertise to address
the challenges of our economic growth in the formative stage of our independence. Therefore, the
State relied on public enterprises for the primary purpose of building infrastructure, generating
employment opportunities for people, promoting balanced regional development and above all for
industrializing remote and backward areas of our country. It is due to public sector enterprises that
the nation could create conditions for emergence of private players to shape our economic growth
and take it forward. The first Prime Minister of our country Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru justly
described the public sector enterprises as the commanding heights of our economy.
2. With the onset of economic reforms in the 1990s, a more liberalized and market
environment was created. It is worth noting that the PSEs have proved their worth in the new
environment and successfully met the challenges posed by globalization and competition arising out
of private sector led economic growth. There is now a more expanding market where competition,
innovation, technological upgradation and above all improved customer preferences define the
course of business, trade and commerce. In this age, which is known as the age of geo-
economics, the wide variety of choices available to a customer and the continuously improved
quality of products and accelerated delivery of services become important factors for the
advancement of business practices. In fact, these factors have transformed the way in which
business transactions are maintained, sustained and taken forward. The public sector enterprises
have proved their worth in face of these challenges. While upgrading their performance they have
to improve their productivity and competitive edge and at the same time discharge their obligations
to society at large. Acknowledgement of the good performance of the public sector enterprises by
none less than the Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh is indicative of the positive assessment of
their contributions to our country at the highest political level.
3. It is worthwhile to place in record the statement of Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh
that due to the resilience of the public sector enterprises the country could withstand the challenges
of financial crisis which originated in the United States of America. Therefore, public sector
enterprises are as relevant today as they were relevant in the mixed economy of 1950s.
4. The PSEs have grown from 5 in the First Five Year Plan to 260 by the end of March,
2012. Out of these 220 are in operation. It is heartening to note that 158, which come to 70 per
cent of the operating PSEs, are making profits. In other words, majority of the PSEs are generating
profits. It is found that, “The profit of these 158 CPSEs increased from Rs.895.8 billion in
Financial Year 2007 to Rs.1137.7 billion in Financial Year 2011, led by robust performance by
mining companies, power companies and select oil and gas companies.” Apart from profits, the
PSEs have made important contributions to the nation exchequer by paying excise and custom
duties and dividends and royalties. These also account for over 20 per cent of country’s GDP,
around six per cent of total employment in the organized sector and 20 per cent of direct and
indirect tax collection. In fact, it is worthwhile to observe that the performance of the profit
making PSEs are favorably compared with their private sector counterparts.
5. The genesis of Department of Public Enterprises (DPE), the nodal agency for all CPSEs in
India, can be traced to the formation of a Bureau of Public Enterprises in 1965 pursuant to the
recommendations of the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha.

2
The vision of the DPE is:
“To enable PSEs to become strong, effective and globally competitive”.
While their Mission is:
“To continuously improve the management of public sector enterprises facilitating
better corporate governance, human resource management (HRM), research & development,
innovative efforts and reviving sick PSEs”.
The core functions of the Department are as under:–
• Co-ordination of matters of general policy of non-financial nature relating to public
sector enterprises.
• Formulation of policies, pertaining to public sector enterprises, in areas like board
structures, personnel management, performance improvement, financial management,
wage settlement, etc.
• Conferring and review of Maharatana, Navratana and Miniratna status to CPSEs.
• Policy relating to delegation of powers to Board of Directors.
• Policy relating to deputation of Government officers to public sector enterprises.
• Publication of the annual survey of CPSEs known as Public Enterprises Survey.
• Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between the public sector enterprises and the
administrative Ministries/Departments.
• Policy relating to Counseling, Retraining and Redeployment Scheme (CRR) for
rationalized employees of CPSEs.
• Policy relating to Board for Reconstruction of Public Sector Enterprises (BRPSE).
• Policy relating to reservation of posts in the public sector enterprises for certain
classes of citizens.
• Settlement of disputes through Permanent Machinery of Arbitration (PMA) among
Public Sector Enterprises and between Public Sector Enterprises and government
departments except disputes relating to tax matters.
• Matters relating to International Centre for Promotion of Enterprises (ICPE) and
Standing Conference of Public Enterprises (SCOPE).
• Bringing out annual Survey of CPSEs/SLPEs to create a reliable time series data on
their performance.
• Organizing capacity building programmes for CPSE/SLPE executives.
6. The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department of Public Enterprises has
paid heed to its recommendation to include R&D issue in the mission of the DPE and
accordingly the issue of R&D finds in an appropriate place in the mission statement of the
Department.

10. The DPE stated that the reduction of the BE for 2012-13 at the RE stage was affected due
to budgetary constraints both under Plan and Non-Plan heads. The Committee notes that while the
Non-Plan allocation was slightly increased at the RE stage, the Plan funds were reduced by Rs.3.00
crore. It is further observed by the Committee that the plan funds which stood at Rs.10 crore for
the year 2012-13 were not fully utilized. In fact, the actual amount which was utilized stood at
Rs.5.85 crore which is slightly more than fifty per cent of the plan funds. In other words, half
of the amount was not utilized.

4
11. The Committee regrets that Non-plan funds have been increased and the plan funds
have been reduced. The implication of this trend is that loans, salaries, interest payments,
etc., are  getting more funds and schemes/projects/programmes which are central to the
functioning of the PSEs are not getting adequate budgetary allocation. It is even more
discouraging to find that only 50% of the curtailed allocation under plan funds could be
utilized by the Department with only a month remaining in the financial year. Analyzing the
broader picture, the Committee feels that the utilization figures of DPE under the plan head
could have been done with vision and foresight. In the Demands for Grants Report of the
Committee 2012-13 the Committee had observed that, “The Department must spend the
money put under the plan head to achieve the desired results.” It is rather unfortunate that
the funds earmarked for the plan category have not been utilized in spite of the
recommendation of the Committee to do so.
12. During the course of discussion on Demands for 2013-14, it has been submitted to the
Committee that during 2009-10, 2010-2011 and 2011-12 DPE had surrendered Rs.3.49 crore,
Rs.1.0123 crore and Rs.1.6778 crore, respectively as ‘savings’. While explaining the usage of the
word ‘saving’ the Committee was informed that in certain schemes such as Counselling,
Retraining and Redeployment Scheme for the North Eastern Region the money could not be
utilized and, therefore, that money was treated as saving. With regard to underutilization of Plan
expenditure of CRR the Secretary, DPE stated that “As figures, which you might have seen,
show, there have been shortfalls in all these three. We have not been able to utilize even the
curtailed resources at the RE stage. I take full responsibility for that But I took courage from
the Committee’ s guidance that these schemes are not just to push money and achieve physical
numbers or targets; these schemes are for ensuring qualitative change that for whatever purpose
these monies are to be spent, those changes are achieved, and, therefore, I would like to
enlighten the Committee that in CRR, I received certain complaints on telephone, by mail and
I found that there were problems. Wherever I found these complaints, we verified them and we
found that the monies were not being spent objectively.” The Committee was also informed that
in certain other projects the objectives could be met and the money meant for such projects
could not be fully utilized. For instance, a project like the review of the role and responsibilities
of non-official directors being undertaken by the Institute of Chartered Accountants India could
be completed and whatever money could not be utilized for the completion of such a project was
treated as savings.
13. The Committee notes that the money earmarked for any scheme for the North
Eastern Region does not lapse. However, non-utilization of such money is a poor reflection
on the ability and expertise of the Department. The North Eastern part of India has always
been given special attention for its development and progress. Any scheme meant for that
region has to be seen in this broader context. While it is true that funds meant for schemes
of the North Eastern Region would be carried forward, it is equally important that
determined efforts need to be taken to use the funds allotted for a particular year. The
unutilized funds cannot be treated as ‘saving’. Rather, the Committee feels that such
unutilized funds are indicative of lack of seriousness of purpose and action on the part of
the Department. In case of money which remains unspent after meeting the objectives of
a scheme that money, in true sense of the term, can be called as savings. Accordingly, it
is recommended that that Ministry may use terms which should be exact in terms of
nomenclature and terminology.

Counselling, Retraining and Redeployment Scheme for Separated Employees of CPSEs
(CRR).
16. One of the reasons for which the PSEs could not become viable business units in a market
economy is because of the large number of employees in their rolls. Rightsizing and restructuring,
therefore, received serious attention from both the Government and the management of the PSEs
to make them commercially viable in a globalised and open economy. While doing so, the PSEs
took measures for the future livelihood of those employees who opted for V oluntary Retirement
Scheme. The CRR Scheme essentially aimed at covering those employees for the purpose of
counselling, retraining and redeploying them. It was supposed to prepare them to take up new
vocations or to become self-employed.
17. On these lines, the scheme of CRR was introduced in 2001-02 aiming at benefiting the
employees of CPSEs who took voluntary retirement. The scheme was later modified in 2007, to
widen its scopes and coverage.

(c) Third party inspection and evaluation of nodal agencies, etc.

7
during 2013-14. It has also been informed that the National Institute for Entrepreneurship and Small

22. The Committee notes that the CRR scheme has been an important initiative for the
welfare of the VRS optees of CPSEs. However, it is apparent that the scheme in its present
form is not serving the purpose for which it was created. The Committee is aware that the
DPE intends to reinvent the scheme by identifying fresh pockets of beneficiaries, revision
of course curriculum, national level certification of training, etc. The Committee
recommends an objective assessment of the scheme must be made. The Committee also
recommends that the Department must ensure that the VRS beneficiaries are properly
redeployed. In this context, it is strongly recommends to monitor the scheme and submit
a report to the Committee.
23. The Committee notes that the implementation of the scheme in the North Eastern
part of India is highly unsatisfactory. Year after year, the funds meant for the North
Eastern part are not utilized for implementation of the scheme and these are transferred
to the non lapsable pool without producing any significant results. This is not at all a
heartening development. The Department officials must be more sensitive to the North
Eastern Region. In this context, the Committee recommends that the revamped CRR
Scheme must be more effective in the North Eastern Region of our country.
Scheme for Skill Development/Training programme of Executives of State Level Public
Enterprises (SLPEs)
24. The Standing Committee of Principal Secretaries of Public Enterprises/Departments in State/
Union Territories recommended in 2008 that the Government of India should supplement the effort
of the State Governments for training Executives and employees of State Level Public Enterprises.
Accordingly, DPE submitted a proposal to the Planning Commission in 2009. This submission
resulted in preparation of a scheme called Skill Development/Training programme of Executives of
State Level Public Enterprises (SLPEs) in Bhopal, Delhi and Shimla.
25. Regarding the future progress of the scheme, the Department submitted before the
Committee that the Planning Commission while approving the Annual Plan 2012-13 of DPE
provided a token amount of Rs. 1 crore (in BE) for the Scheme. The Hon’ble Minister of HI&PE
on 29.01.2013 thereafter granted administrative approval for the scheme and Guidelines on the
Training of SLPEs Executives were devised and finalised. Accordingly, five training programmes
under this Scheme were held during 2012-13 resulting in a total expenditure of Rs.10.86 lakhs (out
of which Rs.5.97 lakhs for 3 programmes are still pending for financial approvals). Also a Seminar
on Training Need Assessment in SLPEs was organized by DPE on 16th November, 2012, in New
Delhi. The recommendations of the TNA Workshop have been made the basis for selecting/
designing the training programmes during the FY 2013-14.
26. During the course of examination of Demand for Grants, 2013-14, the Department also
stated that they tried to launch the scheme in 2012-13, but the approval was received only in
December 2012. The Department also took time to give its approval to the scheme. It may be
mentioned that the Committee on Industry had recommended that the Department, instead of
focusing only on the weakness of the beneficiaries must take into account their strength and
experience. Such a recommendation resulted in the formulation of a scheme called Objective
Training Need Assessment under which training programmes would be conducted as per the needs
of the trainees. It was also decided that the resources would be spent for the purpose of training
in requisite areas and building capacity by taking into account the needs and based on SWOT
analysis.

8
27. In this regard, the Committee notes that the scheme which was proposed in 2009
could be finally approved only in January, 2013. It is also being observed that the
Department would do well to avoid any further delay in the implementation of the scheme.
The Committee also observes that the feedback of respective State Governments and State
level Public Sector Enterprises must be taken to further fine tune the scheme. Stress
should be laid for time bound implementation of the scheme and it should be reflected in
the Result-Framework Document of the Ministry/Department.

Utilization of Funds in the North Eastern Region (NER)
31. As per the existing norm, 10% of total Plan funds of the Department have to be earmarked

9
for utilization in the NER. This allocation is provided in the Account Head – NE Areas. As directed
by the Ministry of Finance since this is a non functional head, the expenditure cannot be shown
directly under this head and the same is to be re-appropriated under a functional head before
disbursement.
32. While responding to a query regarding utilisation of Funds in the NER during examination
of Demands, DPE submitted that they have received a proposal from Public Enterprise
Department, Government of Assam in the year 2012-13 for providing funds under Non-lapsable
Central Pool for N.E. region for computerization of the State Level Public Enterprises (SLPEs)
with a view to strengthening I.T. Infrastructure. The Government of Sikkim also sent a similar
request. The proposal received from Government of Assam was examined in DPE and
concurrence of the competent authority (IFW/CCA) was sought for opening a new Sub-head
under Major Head 2552 (NER) and for re-appropriation of the requisite budget allocation from
NER-CRR Head to NER-SLPE Sub-head. Against this submission, the IF Wing/CCA raised certain
queries regarding approval of the Planning Commission on the concept note of the scheme,
outlay and guidelines of the scheme and whether the funds would be released in the form of
grant-in-aid, etc. DPE have resubmitted the proposal to IFW after incorporating above
suggestions for concurrence of the Planning Commission. The Secretary, DPE, further assured
the Committee that if these proposals are approved and implemented, it would lead to effective
utilization of funds for North Eastern Region for the purpose of capacity building of the State
level Public Sector Enterprises.
33. The Committee takes note of the fact that opportunities are now being created for
effective utilization of funds by the North Eastern Region. However, it regrets that the
failure on the part of the DPE to stick to standard budgetary proposal such as placing it
under the proper head, labeling the nature of scheme, etc., led to non-utilization of the
funds for such a long period. In this context, the Committee would like to observe that
development of the North Eastern Region must not suffer due to non adherence to standard
budgetary process. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department must take
measures to comply with such procedures and the proposals received from the States of
Assam and Sikkim must be taken up on a priority basis.
MoU System
34. The MoU regime has served the Public Sector Enterprises very well. The entire architecture
of MoU is based on negotiated agreement between the Government and the management of the
Enterprises specifying clearly the objective of the agreement and obligation of both the parties. The
MoU is used as a tool for assessment of performance of CPSEs against a predecided set of
objectives. The system was introduced in India in 1986 and underwent a major revamp in 2003.
The Committee has been informed by the Department that the MoU system is again set to undergo
a thorough revamp to make it more effective.
35. The Committee while analysis the working of the MoU system understood that
policies followed by the Government impact the performance of CPSEs. For instance, the
import policy of the Government allows import of capital goods and equipment to our
country in a more liberal manner with hardly any duty on the imported item. As a result,
the capacities of the domestic industry which includes the PSEs remain underutilized. Such
policies of the Government adversely affect the MoU performance of the PSEs. As a result,
the concerned PSEs get a poor rating for no fault of theirs. Accordingly, the Committee
recommends that while rating a PSE under a MoU regime the Government policy should be
included as a measurable parameter. In other words, the Government policies must be
factored in the process of evaluation of the MoU performance.

10
36. During the course of the discussion on Demands for Grants many industry associations
submitted the point before the Committee that in our country the level playing field between an
importer and domestic industry is not there. It was suggested that the concept of deemed imports
may be introduced for the goods or equipments supplied by the domestic supplier whose prices
are competitive with the global supplier. The idea that if the prices of the domestic supplier are
competitive of the global supplier, then the equipments supplied by the domestic supplier may be
described as deemed imports sounds good. The Committee is mindful of the fact that this is an
issue which involves the Ministry of Commerce. However, taking into account the larger issue of
Indian market where both the public and private sectors of our country are facing the absence of
a level playing field, the Committee strongly feels that the idea of deemed import may be introduced
for providing opportunities to the domestic industry and strengthening our indigenous industrial and
entrepreneurial base.
Annual Compliance Reports
37. DPE had issue guidelines on 29th July, 2010 that all CPSEs would submit Annual
Compliance Reports within 30 days from the end of the preceding financial year to their concerned
administrative Ministry/Department which in turn would furnish the consolidated Compliance Report
in respect of all CPSEs under their respective administrative control by 30th June every year to
DPE.
38. The Committee regrets to note that only 78 CPSEs have furnished ACRs till now.
The Committee observes that the efforts of DPE till now have failed to generate the desired
response from CPSEs. It is strongly felt that DPE should make all efforts to secure the
Annual Compliance Reports regarding implementation of DPEs guidelines from all CPSEs in
the country.
Revival and Restructuring of CPSEs
39. The CPSEs perform within the rules and guidelines framed by the Government and at the
same time are expected to remain competitive and generate profit in a highly globalised and market
environment. These are also expected to fulfill social objectives. It is not easy to show impressive
performance in such situations. The problem is further compounded by the fact that most of the
CPSEs suffer from structural inefficiencies which arise out of outdated technology, poor
management methods and overstaffing. As a result, many such CPSEs have been incurring huge
losses.
40. Two institutions take care of the revival and restructuring of sick and loss incurring CPSEs,
namely, the Board of Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) and Board for Reconstruction
of Public Sector Enterprises (BRPSE). BIFR is a statutory body and considers those CPSEs whose
accumulated losses are equal to or have exceeded their net worth under the provisions of SICA
(Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Act, 1985. The BRPSE on the other hand considers
CPSEs whose accumulated losses in any financial year is equal to 50% or more of its average net
worth during 4 years immediately preceding such financial year, and/or is a company within the
meaning of Sick Industrial Companies (Special Provisions) Act, 1985 (SICA).
41. During the discussion on Demand for Grants (2013-14), BRPSE submitted that it is
mandatory for them that they submit their recommendations on CPSEs referred to them by the
respective administrative Ministry within a period of two months. But, thereafter, Government takes
a time period ranging from 3 months to 4 years for acting on the recommendations of BRPSE.
However, while going through the materials furnished by BRPSE, it was found that revival of many
CPSEs are still pending with the Government despite the lapse of 6 years after the
recommendations of BRPSE. Regarding the reasons for delay on the part of the Government, the

11
representatives of BRPSE informed that the dichotomous policy prescriptions of successive
Governments are a major reason of delayed approval from the Government. Due to delay, the
revival packages have been rendered infructus and required another review by BRPSE. ( Annexure–I)
42. The Committee while taking note of the mechanism available for revival and
restructuring of CPSEs in India found that there is inordinate delay in getting approval
from the Government which is effecting revival proposals after receipts of recommendations
for revival from the BRPSE. In this regard, the Committee is of the firm opinion that this
state of affairs defeats the very purpose of revival. Delayed action by the Government
exceeding period upto 6 years can render the financial packages redundant. The highly
dynamic market environment in which the CPSEs operate also makes delayed revival
attempts meaningless. Accordingly, the Committee recommends that DPE should take
initiative to establish an appropriate time line for the Government to take action on the
revival recommendations of a sick CPSE.
Boards of PSEs
43. As the nodal Department of the CPSEs, DPE brings out policy guidelines on the Board
structure of CPSEs. Several initiatives had been taken for professionalization of Boards since the
economic liberalization of 1991. A detailed guideline which provided for the induction of Non
Official Directors (NODs) in the Boards of CPSEs was issued in 1992, which was reviewed in
December, 2012.
44. While examining Demands for Grants (2012-13), the Committee has found that there were
250 vacant positions of NODs on the Boards of more than 100 Scheduled CPSEs. Out of this,
the Search Committee recommended candidates to fill up 100 positions. When queried about the
status of implementation of the above recommendations of the Search Committee by the
administrative Ministries concerned, DPE submitted that they do not monitor the status of
vacancies after the recommendations of Search Committee have been conveyed to the
administrative Ministry/Department.
45. In the opinion of the Committee, it is imperative that the status of action taken by
the administrative Ministries or Departments on the recommendations of the Search
Committee needs to be invariably monitored. In this regard, it is recommended that DPE,
being the nodal Department and policy formulator responsible for the general functioning
of CPSEs, should be empowered to follow up the status of implementation of the
recommendation of the Search Committee for the appointment of the NODs. It is also
advisable that in the MoU signed by the concerned CPSEs must reflect the action taken by
the administrative Ministry in appointing NODs. The Government must also be held
accountable in filling up such vacancies.
46. The Public Enterprises Selection Board is part of the Department of Personnel and
Training. Last year the Committee had observed that such a scheme came into existence
when there was no Department of Public Enterprises. Now, that a full-fledged Department
of Public Enterprises is there in the Government it is important that the Public Enterprises
Selection Board must be made part of the Department. The Committee would like to
reiterate the recommendation which it made last year. The Committee strongly recommends
that such a change must take place for the purpose of streamlining and quickening the
process of succession planning in the PSEs. Apart from the PSEs and their administrative
Ministry, the domain knowledge concerning the functioning of the PSEs is with the DPE. It
finalizes their MoUs and sets their targets both in the financial and non-financial sectors.
It is, therefore, appropriate that PESB must function under the jurisdiction of DPE. At a

12
time when the Government of India is emphasizing on the need to reform the functioning
the Central Public Sector Enterprises, it is extremely important to include within the
reform process the decision to transfer the PESB from the control of DoPT to DPE.
Realizing the importance of the recommendation it must be done irrespective of any
resistance from any quarter.
Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)
47. The DPE guidelines on CSR for CPSEs require that all profit making CPSEs mandatorily
earmark, through a Board resolution, budgetary allocation as a percentage of Profit After Tax for
the previous financial year to undertake CSR activities. The administrative Ministries are expected
to monitor the CSR activities of their CPSEs. The MoU system also gives weightage to CSR
initiatives.
48. DPE submitted before the Committee the information furnished by Maharatana and
Navratana CPSEs on the funds allocated and utilized by them for CSR activities during 2010-11 and
2011-12 (Annexure-II).
49. While going through the information it was found that Coal India Ltd, ONGC and BHEL had
not utilized the funds allocated for CSR to a desirable level. The Secretary, DPE stated in this
regard that the new guidelines of DPE on CSR had come into force on 1st April, 2013 wherein
if there is a shortfall in spending the funds set aside for CSR, that amount will get deposited in
a Sustainability Fund operated by an implementation mechanism of the Government of India. The
Department expects this new measure to improve efficient utilization of CSR funds.
50. In the course of examination of DFG it was queried if the CPSEs have any difficulty in
funding good CSR projects and whether that was the reason for CSR funds remaining unspent.
In this regard, the DPE submitted that they were not aware of any such difficulty being faced by
the CPSEs. According to the Department, the CPSEs were free to take the help of the National
CSR Hub located at Tata Institute of Social Science (TISS), Mumbai to select suitable CSR
projects.
51. The Committee regrets to note that some of largest CPSEs in India are not utilizing
the funds earmarked for CSR activities. This can be viewed as an unhealthy trend having
a direct bearing on the commitment of the CPSEs towards India’s social and environmental
wellbeing. It is even more disheartening to note that this situation exists despite 5%
weightage being accorded to CPSEs for CSR activities in their MoU evaluation. Therefore,
the Committee observes that the Department of Public Enterprises has to take cognizance
of this aspect and keep a tab in the future efforts of these CPSEs to improve their
performance in this area. It is expected that the Sustainability Fund would evolve as a
handy tool in this process.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE — AT A GLANCE
1. The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Department of Public Enterprises has
paid heed to its recommendation to include R&D issue in the mission of the DPE and
accordingly the issue of R&D finds in an appropriate place in the mission statement of the
Department. (Para 6)
2. The Committee regrets that non-plan funds have been increased and the plan funds
have been reduced. The implication of this trend is that loans, salaries, interest payments,
etc., are getting more funds and schemes/projects/programmes which are central to the
functioning of the PSEs are not getting adequate budgetary allocation. It is even more
discouraging to find that only 50% of the curtailed allocation under plan funds could be
utilized by the Department with only a month remaining in the financial year. Analyzing the
broader picture, the Committee feels that the utilization figures of DPE under the plan head
could have been done with vision and foresight. In the Demands for Grants Report of the
Committee 2012-13 the Committee had observed that, “The Department must spend the
money put under the plan head to achieve the desired results.” It is rather unfortunate that
the funds earmarked for the plan category have not been utilized in spite of the
recommendation of the Committee to do so. (Para 11)
3. The Committee notes that the money earmarked for any scheme for the North
Eastern Region does not lapse. However, non-utilization of such money is a poor reflection
on the ability and expertise of the Department. The North Eastern part of India has always
been given special attention for its development and progress. Any scheme meant for that
region has to be seen in this broader context. While it is true that funds meant for schemes
of the North Eastern Region would be carried forward, it is equally important that
determined efforts need to be taken to use the funds allotted for a particular year. The
unutilized funds cannot be treated as ‘saving’. Rather, the Committee feels that such
unutilized funds are indicative of lack of seriousness of purpose and action on the part of
the Department. In case of money which remains unspent after meeting the objectives of
a scheme that money, in true sense of the term, can be called as savings. Accordingly, it
is recommended that that Ministry may use terms which should be exact in terms of
nomenclature and terminology. (Para 13)
4. The Committee notes that the Department could get only 68.98% of the Plan fund
sought for the Twelfth Plan period. Other than the flagship schemes of CRR, DPE has
relatively two new schemes such as Skill Development/Training programme of Executives of
State Level Public Enterprises and Scheme for Research, Development and Consultancy
(RDC) on Generic issues concerning CPSEs & SLPEs which have been approved. The
Committee notes that the DPE is going to review the scheme of CRR because of its poor
implementation. The Committee notes that the issue of skill development must be given top
priority. It is, therefore, important that paucity of funds should not come on the way of
effective achievement of targets fixed for the Twelfth Plan period. (Para 15)
5. The Committee notes that the CRR scheme has been an important initiative for the
welfare of the VRS optees of CPSEs. However, it is apparent that the scheme in its present
form is not serving the purpose for which it was created. The Committee is aware that the
DPE intends to reinvent the scheme by identifying fresh pockets of beneficiaries, revision

recommends an objective assessment of the scheme must be made. The Committee also
recommends that the Department must ensure that the VRS beneficiaries are properly

a report to the Committee. (Para 22)
6. The Committee notes that the implementation of the scheme in the North Eastern
part of India is highly unsatisfactory. Year after year, the funds meant for the North
Eastern part are not utilized for implementation of the scheme and these are transferred
to the non lapsable pool without producing any significant results. This is not at all a

The Committee also observes that the feedback of respective State Governments and State

development of the North Eastern Region must not suffer due to non adherence to standard
