1.3.1    The Committee expresses its concern over the provision of inadequate budgetary support to Ministry of ARI as
the projected demand was to the tune of Rs. 1150.10 crore against which Rs. 859.00 crore has been provided which may
affect the progress of the programmes undertaken by the Ministry.  The Committee is of the considered view that since
ARI has potential for generating more employment opportunities in the rural areas and small towns, the Ministry of ARI
must vigorously and affectively take up the issue with the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance urging for
the additional allocation as per requirement at RE stage.
(Para 5.13)
  Action Taken

allocated for the 10
th Plan.  The expenditure during 2002-03 was Rs. 522.42 crore, that during 2003-04 was Rs. 606.13
crore and that during 2004-05 was Rs. 695.98.  Thus, for the first three years, the total expenditure was Rs. 1824.53
crore, leaving a balance outlay of Rs. 1125.47 crore.  The allocation for BE 2005-06 is Rs.859 crore and Rs. 885.50
crore has been proposed for 2005-06 at RE stage. Further for the year 2006-07, a proposal of Rs. 998.61 has been
submitted to Planning Commission (Rs. 592.93 crore for KVIC, Rs. 23.00 crore for Coir Board and Rs.356.71 crore for
PMRY Division).  For the Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI)”, a provision of Rs.
25.97 crore has also been suggested. If the above proposals (RE 2005-06 and BE 2006-07) are accepted, the actual

Recommendation
 
1.4       Utilisation of funds under the PMRY scheme
 
1.4.1    The Committee recommends that the Ministry of ARI and Ministry of Finance should ensure the optimum
utilization of funds under the PMRY Scheme by basing the release/utilization of funds under the Scheme on
performance/achievement of target by the State/UTs.  The funds released to State/UTs should be epent during the same
year.
(Para. 10.2)
Action Taken
 
1.4.2    The Ministry has revised the quarterly schedule for achievement of activities of sponsoring, sanction and
disbursement for 2005-06.  It is expected that with strict adherence to the schedule by the implementing agencies, target
of sanction and disbursement will be achieved by 31.03.2006.  This will also ensure utilization of the funds released
within a financial year.    
 
Recommendation
1.5       Changing the mindset of bankers
 
1.5.1    The Committee desires that public sector banks must yield to the guidelines issued by the Reserve Bank of India
in regard to disbursement of loans on the PMRY.  The managers at the top level should also direct the branch managers
to adopt client-friendly approach and create such atmosphere that the general opinion about the mindset of the bankers
should itself change.  It is possible through making PMRY more popular among the unemployed youth.  The Committee
commends the role of RBI for suitable intervention from time to time whereby the RBI issued guidelines for
consumer/client-friendly working of banks.  As the banking sector is being empowered with functional autonomy, the
onus/lies on the public sector banks and its administrative Ministry to implement the guidelines of RBI in the best
interest of the industries its  objective to serve the nation and not use this functional discretion for denying the
prospective entrepreneurs in harnessing the opportunity for contributing to economic growth and industrial
development. (Para 13.2)
Recommendation
 
1.6       Coordinating and monitoring the working of PMRY
 
1.6.1    The Committee further desires that the financial institutions, respective State Governments and the Ministry of
ARI should coordinate and monitor the working of PMRY in such a way as to enable the process of sponsoring,
sanctioning and disbursement of applications by the States and banks by avoiding the stoppage/delay of disbursement in
PMRY on flimsy grounds and for quick disposal of application on the part of DICs and forwarding the same to the
banks.  Adequate mechanism may be adopted for timely communication of targets to the banks for necessary action on
their part; coverage of SC/STs/OBCs under the scheme as per the norms stipulated by the Government of India;
effective orientation programmes conducted by DIC for the target groups at block/taluka level to
enlighten/popularization of PMRY and giving accordance to the provision of PMRY for advancing loans in the ratio of
50%; 30% and 20% in respect of industry, service and business sector. (Para. 13.3)
 
Action Taken
 
1.6.2    In the review meeting of PMRY held on 16.09.2005 under the chairmanship of Secretary (SSI&ARI),
Government of India, representatives of State Governments, financial institutions, etc., deliberated on improving the
implementation of the Yojana.  Various decisions taken in this meeting to improve the implementation of scheme
include: timely reporting of monthly progress report by the implementing agencies, adherence to the revised quarterly
schedule, convergence of PMRY with other schemes of the Ministry of ARI and Ministry of SSI, regular furnishing of
information by banks on the number of cases rejected by them to the District Industries Centres (DICs) concerned so
that fresh cases could be sponsored in time, etc. States/UTs have also been requested to constitute Task Force
Committees at block level/mandal level.  The recommendations of this meeting have been forwarded to the Reserve

Recommendation
 
1.7       Problems being faced by khadi industries
 
1.7.1    The Committee desires that the Ministry of ARI should take up the issue of requirement of working capital and
budgetary support with the Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission for modernization and renovation of sales
outlets, alongwith the strategy and time frame to execute the same. Adequate steps must also be taken for modernisation
of charkhas and looms, for which a separate fund and scheme i.e. modernization of traditional industries has been made.
(Para 17.1)
Action Taken
 
1.7.2    The recommendation of the Committee is noted.  While making annual budgetary plan proposal for 2006-07 to
the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of ARI has already included allocations necessitated
by the following requirements of implementing NCMP declarations for revamping of KVIC:
 
Replacement of charkhas and Looms that have outlived their utility and scheme to convert khadi fabric into readymade
garments, thus adding value.
Modernisation of sales outlets.
Strengthening of Confederation for Promotion of Khadi and Village Industries CPKVI by providing corpus fund.
Introduction of new scheme for external market development assurance scheme in line with MDA scheme of
Department of Commerce.
Scheme for upgradation of existing training institutes of KVIC/KVIBs and institutions affiliated to KVIC/KVIBs
 
1.7.3    Further, in pursuance of the announcement of Finance Minister in his Budget speech of July 2004 regarding
establishment of a fund for regeneration of traditional industries like coir, handloom handicrafts, sericulture, leather,
pottery and other cottage industries, the Government has approved a scheme titled the "Scheme of Fund for
Regeneration of Traditional Industries” (SFURTI), for the integrated development of traditional clusters of khadi, coir
and village industries, including leather and pottery. It is proposed to take up development of hundred clusters (25
khadi, 50 village industries and 25 coir) over the next five years (beginning 2005-06) at a total cost of Rs. 97.25 crore.
The scheme envisages integrated development of clusters of khadi, coir and traditional village industries and has the
components of replacement of charkhas and looms for increasing the productivity of khadi spinners and weavers, setting
up of common facility centers for various post loom facilities like dyeing, printing, readymade garment manufacturing,
product testing laboratory, etc., and for processing and manufacture of village industry items, development of new
products and designs in khadi and village industries sector, market promotion assistance, capacity building measures
like training, exposure visits, etc.  A provision of Rs. 8.65 crore has been made for this scheme during 2005-06.

1.10     Employment generation under village industries
 
1.10.1  The Committee notes the submission of the Ministry of ARI that KVIC is adopting a strategy to generate large-
scale employment in rural sector by channelising institutional finance. A Working Group constituted by Planning
Commission for the Tenth Five Year Plan has recommended employment target of 89.57 lakh (15 lakh under khadi and
74.57 lakh under V.I.) by the terminal year of the Tenth Five Year Plan.  For this purpose, the Planning Commission has
allocated Rs. 2080 crore to KVIC. Under its REGP scheme, it is proposed to generate 25 lakh employment by the
terminal year of Tenth Five Year Plan. The Committee is of the considered view that an all out efforts should be made
for achieving the target set by the Planning Commission. The Committee wishes to point out that while combating the
problem of unemployment and especially setting the target (that should be determined/fixed time frame) proper and
adequate attention must be paid to population growth rate and the prevalence of level of poverty so that the problem of
unemployment, poverty and population explosion may be solved in a time bound frame with a holistic approach.       
(Para 20.8)
 
Action Taken
 
1.10.2  The Ministry as well as the KVIC is making all out efforts to ensure that employment generation targets are
met.  In fact the Working Group had recommended that the total employment generated (not additional employment)
would reach 89.57 lakh by the terminal year of 10
th Five Year Plan, from the level of 62.64 lakh at the end of the
terminal year of 9th Five Year Plan.  By the end of 2004-05 i.e. 3rd year of 10th Five Year Plan, the employment
generation was to the extent of 76.77 lakh in KVI sector. The target is to reach 82.20 lakh employment generation by
the end of 2005-06 and 88.70 lakh by the end of 2006-07.  In fact during the period 2004-05, as against the target of
generating 5.25 lakh additional job opportunities under REGP, 5.30 lakh additional job opportunities were created. It is
expected that the KVIC would be able to meet the employment generation targets under village industries during the
period 2005-06 and also that of entire10
th Plan period.

3.10.1  The Committee feels that the Ministry of ARI should give serious thought for formulation of National Policy for
Agro and Rural Industries for the growth of rural industries, instead of rejecting the suggestion on the first instance. 
Had the every thing been right with the existing policy measures, India’s Agro and  Rural Industries would have been in
a better position, contributing the national economy and particularly consolidating the rural economy.  The Committee
understands that the Ministry through a dynamic process of policy amendments and positioning is consistently
addressing policy initiatives in response to the changed economic scenario and its challenges, however, the colossal
problem of India is also persisting and that too consistently.  Therefore, it is desirable that the Ministry of ARI should
act and not react on the recommendation of the Committee without giving a proper thought to the ideas behind the
recommendations. (Para. 53.2)
Action Taken
 
3.10.2  Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries appreciates the recommendations of the Committee.   As declared in the
National Common Minimum Programme, the Government dissolved the KVIC and constituted a ten-Member Expert
Committee to review the existing structure, functioning and performance, etc., of the KVIC and recommend suitable
measures for its revamping
 
3.10.3  Based on the recommendation of the Expert Committee, the KVIC (Amendment) Bill, 2005 was introduced in
the Lok Sabha in August 2005 and referred to Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee (DRPSC) on
Industry for examination and report.  Further necessary action is being taken on the Bill in the light of the
recommendations of the DRPSC.  The Expert Committee also made recommendations relating to programmes and
policies of the KVIC.  These are being examined and a proposal relating to new schemes and also revising the existing
schemes in the KVI sector is under active consideration of the Government. These include (i) scheme for making the
khadi industry more productive and competitive by improving the production infrastructure available with the khadi
institutions, in a phased manner and generating sustainable additional employment for khadi artisans, (ii) scheme for
facilitating graduation ofsuccessful PMRY enterprises in rural areas and small towns with population up to 20,000 to
higher levels of investment, operation and employment generationwith assistance under the REGP, (iii) schemes for

strengthening domestic and international efforts in the KVI sector, (iv)  scheme for upgradation of infrastructure,
equipments and other facilities available in the training institutions of KVIC/State/UTs KVIBs and those of institutions
(v)  re-designing of relevant training courses for providing long-term vocational training for skill development and
entrepreneurial capacity to first generation entrepreneurs, etc.
 
3.10.4  Further, in order to bring further improvements to  review the design and implementation practices of the Prime
Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY), Government has set up a Group under the chairmanship of Adviser (VSE),

(SFURTI)) under which 100 traditional industry clusters (of khadi, village industry and coir) would be taken up for

will be the first comprehensive initiative for regeneration of the khadi and village industries sector, based on the cluster
development methodology.
 
3.10.6  It is expected that the above policy initiatives will lead to further growth of agro and rural industries in the
country and contribute substantially to the national economy.

3.10.7  The Committee is of the view that the   amendment  of  KVIC Bill , modification in PMRY, NPRI, REGP,

feels the need for comprehensive policy for the Agro and  Rural industries.

3.11.1  The Committee refers to the concern expressed by Finance Minister in the General Budget 2005-06 and the need

poverty alleviation.  The Committee wishes to highlight that the Agro and Rural Industries has the potential of

REGP and PMRY through higher budgetary support as these programmes are important for creating self-employment
