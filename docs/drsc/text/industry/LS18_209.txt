12.       The Committee observes that the actual expenditure, both Planned and Non-Planned, have been less than the RE for the year. The Committee should be
informed of such a shortfall in the expenditure for the CRR scheme.
 
13.       The Committee noted that the Economic Survey termed the distinction between Planned and Non-Planned expenditures is illogical and dysfunctional.   It has been
recognized that such distinction gives a fragmented view of the resource allocation.   The Survey rather   found the distinction as Capital and Revenue expenditures more
significant.  Yet, in the presentation of Demands for Grants the allocations are indicated in terms of Planned and Non-Planned expenditures.   The Committee sought
Department’s comments. 
 
14.       In its written submission, the Department gave a generic reply that in the presentation of Demands for Grants, the allocations are indicated in terms of Plan and Non-
Plan expenditures as per instructions/guidelines of the Planning Commission in respect of Plan expenditure and Ministry of Finance in respect of Non-Plan expenditure. 
 
15.       However, during the oral witness before the Committee on the 2
nd April 2008, Dr. Renu Parmar, representing the Planning Commission conceded -
 
16.       “Sir, actually, this has been considered over a number of years in various working groups. In fact even as far back as in the Ninth Plan …… and this dichotomy was
considered even at that time. But in fact, there is no resolution to this issue”
 
17.       The Ministry of Finance in written submission on the issue, stated :
 
“Classification of  Government expenditure as Revenue and Capital expenditure is a Constitutional requirement  whereas classification of  expenditure as plan and Non-
plan is   non-statutory; and
 
The distinction between plan and Non-plan expenditure is essentially “ Institution  Driven”.  Plan expenditure generally refers to the expenditure provided for by the
Planning Commission out of the Gross Budgetary Support (GBS).
 
18.       The Committee takes very serious note of inexplicable ambiguities in classification of Demands. The Committee takes cognizance of the reply presented by the
Ministry of Finance which concedes that while classification on the basis of Capital and Revenue expenditure was a constitutional requirement adding to it another
sub classification of Planned and Non-planned expenditure, just because it has been in vogue and institutionalised has led arbitrariness, which even the
representatives of the Department have found difficult to elaborate. In absence of a convincing reply from the Department, the Committee gets an impression that the
Department and its relevant wing too could not comprehend the current format of classification. Absence of any standardised definition of the hybridised categories
obviously has serious consequences and may have resulted in arbitrariness. The Committee in agreement with the observation made in the Economic Survey
regarding Planned and Non-Planned expenditure.  It is the considered opinion of the Committee that once the Budget is approved by the Cabinet for the presentation
to the Parliament, it is superfluous to indicate in the SBE the approval of Planning Commission or any other expert body to individual expenditures. The Committee
therefore feels that if a classification can not be explained even by the representatives of the Department and the Planning Commission, it must be must be done away
with. The Committee recommends that in view of the reply from the Ministry of Finance and the observations made in the Economic Survey, the Financial Advisor to
the Department must peruse with the Ministry of Finance to stop an institutionalised  practice in favour of constitutional requirement.

26.       The Committee has repeatedly commented upon the lack of     financial resources for the BRPSE.
 
27.       The Department in its reply stated that BRPSE was a part-time advisory body and secretarial assistance was provided by Department of Public Enterprises. Till 2006-07,
all the expenses of BRPSE were met from the budget of DPE. However for the year 2007-08, a separate budget of Rs.36.45 lakh was provided within the over all budget of

28.       The Committee further sought the year-wise details of the meetings held by the BRPSE ever since it was set up.
 
29.       It was informed that BRPSE has held 9 meetings in 2004-05 (December 2004 – March 2005), 24 meetings in 2005-06, 13 meetings in 2006-07 and 10 meetings in
2007-08. The Board has considered and recommended 8 cases of CPSEs during the year 2007-08.
 
30.       It was informed that of the 51 cases recommended by BRPSE, 36 cases are to be revived as CPSEs, 4 cases are to be merged with other CPSEs, 2 cases are to be closed
and 9 cases are to be revived through joint venture with either CPSEs/private parties. Out of 9 cases recommended for joint venture, only in respect of 3 cases BRPSE had
recommended cleaning up of Balance Sheet before exploring the options of joint venture
 
31.       The Committee also noted that of the cases recommended for revival so far the maximum 25 PSEs were under the Department of HI. The total budgetary support
required for the revival of all 51 PSEs was Rs 28513.51 crores that involved Rs 24072. 84 crores as Non cash assistance by way of waivers of loans and interests or their
conversions into equity. Rs 4440.67 crores were required for fresh cash infusion through loans, equity or grants.
 
32.       The Committee also noted so far the Government had approved a total of Rs 8430.48 crores to support the revival of 30 PSEs. Of this Rs 1976.13 crores were by the
way of cash infusion and Rs 6454.35 crores by the way of non-cash support.
 
33.       The Committee only wonders that of the cash support approved how much of that is meant for capital investments other than settling the dues of the
employees and other sundry dues.
 
34.       After almost four years of BRPSE’s existence, the Government had approved only 30 cases for revival. Of an expected investment of Rs. 28513.51 crores, so
far only about Rs 8430.48 crores have been approved.  Of the requisite cash infusion of Rs 4440.67 crores, only Rs 1976.13 crores have been approved. The
Committee expresses serious concern as to how could Government generate such resources for the revival. And if the delay in arranging the resources could escalate
the cost of revival.
 
35.       The Committee noted that the NCMP prescribed two recourses for loss making/sick PSEs - revival or closure. It wondered if the BRPSE could create

36.   It was informed that the BRPSE examined the proposals submitted by the administrative ministries/ departments as to the sustainable revival of the PSEs and the amount
of funds required for its revival on case-to-case basis.  While recommending the fresh infusion of funds, the minimum amount required for the sustainable revival of the PSE is

position and the capacity of the sick PSE to pay the loans/interest were taken into account.
 
37.       In view of the reply of the BRPSE, the Committee recommends an objective appraisal of the need for selective revival against the cost of revival. Without such
a selective  criteria the policy as enunciated in the NCMP may become a drag on the exchequer instead of being an enabling provision to revive the PSEs to revitalize
overall industrial sector in the economy. In this context the Committee also notes that the annual performance target set prospectively for next five years for the
revived PSEs are rather conservative. Considering the huge cost of proposed revivals and the limited resources actually committed thus far, it is imperative that some
benchmarks of acceptable cost ought to be prescribed. Such benchmark cost of revival may vary for core/strategic and non-core/ non strategic sectors.

40.       The Committee inquired about the inputs available to the BRPSE for making its recommendations and if it also receives inputs from Operating Agencies and the Banks.
 
41.       It was informed that the concerned administrative ministries/departments, after due consultations with all the stake holders, prepare and submit a comprehensive
proposal in the format prescribed by BRPSE. The format prescribed for submitting proposals to BRPSE seeks detailed information on (i) the actual performance of the CPSE,
(ii)  reasons for sickness/sub-optimal performance such as - organizational/management issues, operational issues, marketing issues, technology and market access issues, taxes
and duty structure issues, etc., (iii) the remedial action taken in the past to address the causes of sickness, (iv) explanations on how the proposed strategies for revival seem to be
different or better than the earlier actions, (v) future prospects of the company indicating the projected profitability as well as projected balance sheet and cash-flow for five
years after the implementation of the proposed  scheme, (vi) funds required for the scheme and means of financing, (vii) competitive advantages of the company after
restructuring, (viii)  sustainability of the profits and responsibility of management and staff in ensuring it, etc. The proposals submitted by the concerned administrative
ministry/department to BRPSE are either prepared or appraised/vetted by independent consultants/operating agencies. The complete proposals received from the administrative
ministry/department are circulated in advance to all the members/permanent invitees and Controller General of Accounts as well as Planning Commission. The Secretary of the
concerned administrative ministry and the Chief Executive of the concerned CPSE make presentation before the Board explaining all aspects of the proposal including
justifications for the concessions sought from Government and sustainability of the revival of the CPSE. The concerned consultants/operating agencies who had prepared/
vetted the proposal are also requested to attend the Board meeting and explain to the Board various aspects of the proposal and answer the queries raised in the meeting.
Chairman and non-official members of the Board are experts in their field. The official members and permanent invitees are from other Government departments/ agencies. The
Board discusses the proposals in detail and makes its recommendations.
 
42.       The Committee observes that with such exhaustive details on the one hand  the Board had considered and recommended 51 cases in its 56 meetings till
February 2008 inter alia it took on an average just about one meeting to consider and recommend the revival or closure of a PSE, on  the other hand the Committee
also observed with reservation the delay in making reference of BRPSE’s recommendations to the competent authority. Considering that the BRPSE includes the
Secretaries of the Ministry of Finance, Department of Public Enterprises and that it relies significantly on the inputs of administrative Departments, the Committee
finds the practice of further referring its recommendations Inter-Ministerial Group or the Committee of Secretaries as superfluous and time consuming. The views of
the Government on the BRPSE’s recommendations are further ratified by the BIFR. Often the revival packages get stuck on the issues with the State Governments
and other agencies. The Committee objects to such a practice that leads to further critical delays. On the one hand BRPSE is not provided with adequate budget to
make its own independent assessment and is made to rely upon the administrative Ministry, on the other hand its recommendations are further referred to a
collateral body of Secretaries.  In view of the Committee such a practice only undermines the recommendations of the BRPSE.  The Committee recommends that the
BRPSE should also ascertain the views of other stakeholders particularly the State/local Governments  independently before making its final recommendations.

49.       The Committee further sought to know if such courses were available in the country.
 
50.       It was informed that India is a founder member of the ICPE, which was set up in the year 1974 at the initiative of United Nations. With the passage of time, a number of
management institutes have been set up in India.  ICPE is an inter-governmental organization and had a linkage with developing countries.   Further, the Department had earlier
received advice from Indian Embassy, Vienna for strengthening ICPE.  On receipt of advice note of Indian Embassy, Vienna the Department examined the proposal with 
Ministry of External Affairs.  Ministry of External Affairs also agreed with the proposal of this Department for enhancing the annual contribution to ICPE. After that, the
concurrence of Department of Expenditure was also obtained.  Programmes conducted by ICPE provide Indian participants’ exposure to latest management techniques and an
exposure to international environment.   It was further conveyed to the Committee that Department of Personnel & Training also deputed central government officials for
undertaking short duration and long duration programmes being conducted by ICPE.  The participants gained exposure by interacting with faculty drawn from various countries
and by having the opportunity to observe the life, institutions and system of developed Europe. They also got opportunity to visit industrial houses and international
organizations in different parts of Europe.
 
51.       Keeping in view the depleting membership of the ICPE and also bearing in view the level of participation during the last three years, when only 28 executives
from 16 PSEs attended the courses at ICPE, the Committee considers that level of annual membership fee to ICPE can not be justified when the similar courses are
available in India at the competitive cost to the company ( CTC). The Committee also observes that most of the PSEs have sent only one executive to a course at
ICPE. It is obviously led to wonder if the training of a single executive could make any worthwhile change in any PSE, except for adding to the qualification of the
individual participant. The Committee finds such an approach of piecemeal training the executives, patently faulty. 
 
52.       Furthermore, the Committee rejects the view of Ministry of External Affairs that membership of a multilateral inter-government academic organization is
required to promote what is essentially bilateral relation between the two countries. The membership of ICPE can not be justified on the basis of an objective cost-
benefit analysis. Exposure of an individual executive to the European experience can hardly be an argument for such a costly burden on the budget. The Committee
therefore recommends that while individual PSE may negotiate price/fee with the ICPE, the membership must not be a burden on the budget.  
 
53.       The Committee also enquired about the status of its recommendation in its 168

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
AT A GLANCE
 
 
            The Committee observes that the actual expenditure, both Planned and Non-Planned, have been less than the RE for the year. The Committee should be
informed of such a shortfall in the expenditure for the CRR scheme.
(Para 12)
 
            The Committee takes very serious note of inexplicable ambiguities in classification of Demands. The Committee takes cognizance of the reply presented by the
Ministry of Finance which concedes that while classification on the basis of Capital and Revenue expenditure was a constitutional requirement adding to it another
sub classification of Planned and Non-planned expenditure, just because it has been in vogue and institutionalised has led arbitrariness, which even the
representatives of the Department have found difficult to elaborate. In absence of a convincing reply from the Department, the Committee gets an impression that the
Department and its relevant wing too could not comprehend the current format of classification. Absence of any standardised definition of the hybridised categories
obviously has serious consequences and may have resulted in arbitrariness. The Committee in agreement with the observation made in the Economic Survey
regarding Planned and Non-Planned expenditure.  It is the considered opinion of the Committee that once the Budget is approved by the Cabinet for the presentation
to the Parliament, it is superfluous to indicate in the SBE the approval of Planning Commission or any other expert body to individual expenditures. The Committee
therefore feels that if a classification can not be explained even by the representatives of the Department and the Planning Commission, it must be must be done away
with. The Committee recommends that in view of the reply from the Ministry of Finance and the observations made in the Economic Survey, the Financial Advisor to
the Department must peruse with the Ministry of Finance to stop an institutionalised  practice in favour of constitutional requirement.
(Para 18)
 
            The Committee only wonders that of the cash support approved how much of that is meant for capital investments other than settling the dues of the
employees and other sundry dues.
(Para 33)
 
            After almost four years of BRPSE’s existence, the Government had approved only 30 cases for revival. Of an expected investment of Rs. 28513.51 crores, so
far only about Rs 8430.48 crores have been approved.  Of the requisite cash infusion of Rs 4440.67 crores, only Rs 1976.13 crores have been approved. The
Committee expresses serious concern as to how could Government generate such resources for the revival. And if the delay in arranging the resources could escalate
the cost of revival.
(Para 34)
            In view of the reply of the BRPSE, the Committee recommends an objective appraisal of the need for selective revival against the cost of revival. Without such
a selective  criteria the policy as enunciated in the NCMP may become a drag on the exchequer instead of being an enabling provision to revive the PSEs to revitalize
overall industrial sector in the economy. In this context the Committee also notes that the annual performance target set prospectively for next five years for the
revived PSEs are rather conservative. Considering the huge cost of proposed revivals and the limited resources actually committed thus far, it is imperative that some
benchmarks of acceptable cost ought to be prescribed. Such benchmark cost of revival may vary for core/strategic and non-core/ non strategic sectors.
