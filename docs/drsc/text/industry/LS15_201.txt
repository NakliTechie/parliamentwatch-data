1.14     The Committee takes note of the cost of  revival of the PSEs. The Committee was also informed that if needed
the PSEs will raise the resources for revival from either internal or extra budgetary sources against the guarantees
provided by the Government.
 
1.15   The Committee notes with concern that more than half of budget support of Rs 4733 crores to the revival will go
into waivers and conversions of outstanding dues. Fresh infusions of about Rs. 1100 crores will be required. Only less
than 10% of the total cost, about Rs 400 crores will go into capital investments. ##
 
1.16     The Committee finds that revival package is largely about restructuring the manpower, waiving or converting
the financial liabilities over the PSEs. It is apparent that not much of the Budgetary support is available to the more
critical issues of technology upgradation and strengthening the marketing of PSEs. PSE are left to generate IEBRs for
capital investments. This is despite the fact that DHI has recognized technological obsolescence as one of the factors of
PSEs’ losing competitive edge.   The Committee therefore observes that the Government should consider this aspect
while preparing  revival  packages for the sick PSEs.
 
1.17     In this regard, the Committee also assessed the previous revival efforts for the same PSEs that have again been
referred to BRPSE. During the period of 1995-2004, an effort to revive these very PSEs was made with an aggregate
cost of some Rs. 6400 crores. Even at that time these PSEs faced similar constraints from global/domestic competition,
technological obsolescence, old machines and over-dependence on Government for marketing and within couple of
years of approval of such costly revival plans, these PSEs relapsed into losses.
 
1.18     From the Demands for Grants of last few years, it appears that no lessons have been learnt from the failures of
the previous revival attempts. The Committee considers that even the current efforts of revival may remain only a costly
window dressing unless the more critical issues of marketing and technological alliances are not effectively addressed.
The Committee however is constrained to note that in successive Budgets this aspect of rehabilitating the PSEs, has
been ignored. It appears from the Demands for Grants that while liabilities are being liquidated with the budgetary
support, for the capital investment the PSEs are being asked to generate IEBRs. The Committee wonders if such an
arrangement could make the revival of PSEs sustainable.  The Committee therefore recommends that Government
should review and re-assess the revival plans of the PSEs.
 
2.0       Impact of Budget proposals on Heavy Industries and Auto Sector

2.1       The Committee enquired about the overall impact of Budget proposals on the Sectors under the purview of DHI. 
The Committee was informed that:
 
1.      Custom duty has been reduced across the board from a peak level of 12.5% to 10%.  This would benefit
PSEs having high level of import content.
 
2.      Increase in the dividend tax (from 12.5% to 15%) and education cess (from 2% to 3%) would erode the
profitability of profit making companies like BHEL, HPC etc.
 
3.      Reduction in custom duty on Dials and movements of Watches from 12.5% to 5% would adversely
affect HMT (Watches).
 
2.2       The Committee was further informed that the positive features of the budget in respect of the auto sector were
the reduction of CST from 4% to 3%, the continuation of the weighted deduction of R&D expenditure under Income
Tax Act for the automobile sector for the next 5 years, and the retention of current customs duty structure on cars and
two wheelers which are likely to encourage local value addition in the domestic economy and generate employment.
 2.3      The Committee observes the divergence in the opinion of DHI and SIAM in respect of reduction of customs
duty on commercial vehicles and auto components from 12.5% to 10%. In the view of DHI, the step is likely to benefit
the PSEs with high import content, however in the view of SIAM   such reduction in duty is likely to affect the industry
adversely, as this would open up imports from low cost economies and deflect investment in the sector.   The
Committee therefore desires the Department of Heavy Industries to take up the issue with Ministry of Finance and
ensure that the interests of Auto sector too are protected.
 
2.4       The Committee was also informed thatincrease in spending on roads both national highways and rural roads will
have some positive impact on the sector. The increase in outlay for the Urban Renewal Mission and its focus on
transport will help increase public transportation in the country.
 
3.0       Plan Outlay for 2007-08
 
3.1       The Committee noted that total Plan Outlay for 2007-08, is estimated at Rs. 2485.22 crores of which Rs. 450
crores will be provided as budgetary support, and remaining Rs. 2035.22 crores are to be generated through internal and
extra budgetary resources. Given the negative growth in engineering and machine tool sector, largest chunk of
Budgetary support has been allocated to this sector.  Only the 14 PSEs are expected to generate IEBR.  
 
3.2            However, the Committee noted that last year in BE 2006-07 too, Rs 1360.22 were allocated for the Plan
outlay, however the estimates were reduced to Rs. 1127.82 crores in RE 2006-07, with both Budgetary support and
IEBR being lowered. Last year too, the larger part of the Plan outlay was allocated to engineering and machine tools
sector that could not utilize the allocations.
 
3.3       The Committee desires DHI to explain the reduction in budgetary support and IEBR and the steps taken to help
PSEs to generate IEBRs in order to reduce their continuous dependence on budgetary support. The Committee observes
that despite Government’s guarantees the PSEs have not been able to meet IEBR  targets which needs to be reviewed
seriously.  
 
3.4       The Committee expresses its serious concern over the constraints faced by the engineering and machine tools
sector in generating the IEBR and even utilizing the allocations, despite the healthy growth of more than 15 % in
successive years 2005-06 and 2006-07. 
 
3.5       The Committee observes with concern that as per the Outcome Budget 2007-08, a substantial part of Rs 1807.84
crores of IEBRs are committed for settling the VRS/statutory dues and salaries, in addition to Rs 150 crores of
Budgetary support. Only Rs. 227.88 crores of IEBR in addition to Rs 5 crores of Budgetary support will be used for
capital investments in PSEs. The Committee considers it as an evidence of a lopsided revival and restructuring of PSEs
where greater stress is being laid on restructuring the manpower instead of capital investments in the PSEs.  ##

4.4       In this context the Committee finds it rather intriguing that the lumpsum allocations of Rs. 400 crores meant for
the loans to the PSEs to settle the VRS and other statutory dues and to pay the salaries to the employees are categorized
as Non-Planned Capital expenditure, whereas  the Salary under the Major Head of Secretariat is placed as a Revenue
expenditure.   The Committee finds the explanation of the Department untenable  that allocations for ‘Loans’ per se are
Capital expenditure even if it is meant for payment of Salaries or VRS dues to the staff. Similarly Rs 25 crores meant
for R&D in Auto Sector, other than NATRIP,, is classified as a Non-Planned Revenue expenditure.  The Committee
directs the Department to take note of the Government Accounting Rules 1990, which states “ the classification of the
transactions in Government Accounts, shall have closer reference to the function, programme and activity of the
Government and the object of revenue or expenditure …”   If that  be the case how can  allocation  meant  for salary  be
classified differently in the  same  Demand.   In this context the Committee also finds it relevant to  mention the recent
pattern  of loans eventually being waived or converted into unsecured equity.    The Committee observes that the 
definition of  capital expenditure  has been contrived and directs the Government to take appropriate  measures to avoid
such inconsistencies.
 
4.5       The Committee notes the definition of Planned and Non-Planned expenditure, as given in GFR 78, which states
“Plan expenditure represents expenditure on Plan outlays approved for each scheme by the Planning Commission and
indicates the extent to which such outlays are met out of budgetary provisions.  Expenditure other than these are
categorised as Non-Plan expenditure.” The obvious corollary to this definition is that the allocations to the tune of Rs
456.30 crores have not been approved by the Planning Commission. However no clarification  has been  offered about
the criteria on which any scheme is approved by the Planning Commission.
 
 4.6      The Committee noted that RE-2006-07 indicated reduced planned revenue and capital expenditure whereas the
non-planned capital and revenue expenditure have exceeded the BE -2006-07.   The Committee was informed that the
Plan expenditure in 2006-07 was reduced due to transfer of one scheme relating to Bharat Earth Movers Ltd.,  having an
allocation of Rs.64 cr. in BE 2006-07  to M/o Defence. Increase in Non-plan allocation has been mainly for revival of
PSEs and VRS/statutory dues etc. Many of the PSEs have been defaulting on payment of wages/salary and statutory
dues. Govt. has provided budgetary support to tide over this situation. An amount of Rs.250 cr. has been sanctioned in
two tranches  of Rs.125 cr. each for payment of wages/salaries upto Dec. 2006 in defaulting PSEs.

7.0  Modernisation of Capital Goods Sector
 
7.1       The Committee wanted to know  if any purpose was served for the Modernization of Capital Goods Sector with
only Rs.1.00 lakh allocated in BE-2006-07, considering no additional resources had been sought  in supplementary
demands during 2006-07, yet the allocation has been enhanced to Rs.20.00 crores in BE-2007-08.
 
7.2       In the written reply Department of Heavy Industries informed that it proposed to launch a Pilot Scheme for
Competitive Enhancement and Modernization of Machine Tools and Textile Machinery Industry.  To start with, the
scheme was to address three core areas of constraints identified in the industry, namely (i) modernization and
technology upgradation (ii) R&D facilities and (iii) Business Development Services to ensure capacity increase and to
enhance competitiveness of the industry in the context of  ongoing globalization. However, as 2006-07 was the last year
of the 10
th five year plan , it was decided to have a pilot scheme for two years covering the sub sectors of machine tools
and textile machinery  at a total cost of Rs. 47.40 crore.  The scheme was under scrutiny of Planning Commission.  In
order to make the scheme operational in the current financial year, a token amount of Rs. 1.00 lakh  was allocated in the
Demand for Grants of Department of Heavy Industries  for the year 2006-07. The Pilot Scheme could not be approved
by the Planning Commission.  However, it was decided that a comprehensive proposal for capital goods sector should
be worked out and presented for the 11
th Plan period.  Accordingly, a presentation has been made to the Planning
Commission and in-principle approval is awaited before finalizing the details and obtaining final approval for the
scheme. The allocation of Rs. 20.00 crore for the financial year 2007-08 is based on the tentative outlay indicated at the
level of Advisor, Planning Commission during plan discussions for DHI.  Activities taken up under the scheme during
2007-08 would either be completed during the same year or may receive additional support during subsequent years
depending upon the gestation period involved.
           
7.3       The Committee was further informed that the proposed scheme was meant to support all capital goods
manufacturers to modernize their technology and to promote setting up of common facilities, R&D centres and business
development services.
 
7.4       The four strands of the scheme include Technology upgradation, Common facility centers, R&D Centers and
Sector specific parks. The Committee was also informed that the project will require Rs. 1328 crore. While the DHI had
sought a Budgetary support of Rs. 150 crores for 2007-08, however Planning Commission recommended  Rs 20 crores
for this year. 
 
7.5       The Committee finds it to be a  typical example of misplaced priorities where a critical project for the revival of
capital goods sector has been delayed due to extraneous considerations,  administrative complexities and layers of
approval. A project that was allocated funds by the Government in the Budget has been over-ridden by the Planning
Commission. The Committee feels that the administrative constraints of the last year of the  Five Year Plan can not be
an alibi for delaying a project for modernizing the capital goods sector. The Committee desires the DHI to inform the
Committee  of the business model, backward and forward linkages and commercial viability of the project. And if DHI
has done a survey of the stakeholders and their stakes in the project. DHI needs to ascertain about the management
structure of the scheme.

COMMITTEE’S OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
            Summary of  Committee’s observation
 
            In view of the reply of the DHI regarding the classification of allocations into four categories, there is a fair
scope of arbitrariness in such classification in absence of specific criterion. The Committee also observed that the
practice of allocating lumpsum had been continuing for last several year since 1998-1999 despite failure of previous
failed revival efforts. No lessons have been learnt to protect Government’s new investments for the revival of PSEs.
 
            The revival and restructuring have remained unspecified in DHI’s response. The two terms have been used as
inter-changeably and to avoid specific details e.g. the ‘lumpsum for revival scheme of PSEs’ is actually meant to pay
salaries to the employees. Similarly in its responses to Committee’s specific queries, the DHI has tried to shift the
responsibility to the BRPSE which in fact considers only the draft revival plan prepared by the concerned PSE. The DHI
appears to have reduced to mere fund raising agency for BRPSE. Considering that the BRPSE’s recommendations are
eventually approved by the Government, the Committee wonders if BRPSE’s recommendations pertain only to the
limited issue of waivers and liquidation of liabilities, or wider problems pertaining more basic issues like marketing,
technological profile of products, consolidation through possible acquisition and merger etc. too have been considered
by the BRPSE and if the Government has accepted the BRPSE’s recommendation in totality. And if such issues require
any Budgetary support?  DHI’s replies lack specific information on these issues.   The Committee recommends that DHI
must apprise it of the broad framework of the strategy for revival of the PSEs. 
 
            In this regard the Committee observed that while Non-Planned allocations have been higher than Planned
expenditure in BE, the mid year Supplementary demands often seek costly waivers that add to the Non-Planned
expenditures. As per the definition provided in GFR it is clear that budgetary resources are being used only to clear the
commitments and liabilities. Such investments by the Government may have added the notional value to the Company

201st report of Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/201streport.htm[04-07-2016 13:12:15]
instead of adding to it worth.  
 
            As the PSEs are required to generate IEBRs to meet their capital expenditures, the Committee observed that
except for a few PSEs, most of the PSEs are not in a position to generate IEBR as indicated from the RE 2006-07, yet
the annual target for IEBR has been raised without explaining the reasons for such optimism. As for the meeting the
requirements of capital expenditure, minimal budgetary support is available by the way of multiple schemes like AMR
or Crucial balancing meant largely for same purpose. The meager money was supposed to be used for minor expenses
towards addition or replacement or modification, however remained largely unutilized. The promise of providing
budgetary support to the capital investments remained only a promise as the token allocations were never enhanced. 
 
            The Committee also noted that capital investments are being generated from the IEBRs whereas the revenue
expenditures like salary and retirement dues are being financed through budget support. Budgetary allocations are being
made to provide loans to liquidate the liabilities however, going by the current practice, the Committee apprehended that
these loans might be waived or converted into unsecured equity in future. Despite Committee’s repeated queries
regarding the steps taken by the DHI to secure these loans as per the OM of Ministry of Finance, the replies were non-
committal. The Committee believed that the amount of such lumpsum allocations should have come down over
successive year with the revival of PSEs and after clearing the VRS and statutory dues of rationalized employees.
However, the lumpsum allocations have remained the same every year.    The Committee would like to know if the
beneficiary PSEs have begun to repay the loans and interest there on or they have been granted moratorium. The DHI
must apprise.
 
            In addition to waivers and conversions, the Government is also loosing money by providing Interest subsidies to
the PSEs. Guarantee fee of which should have accrued to the Government is too has to be waived. And yet the PSEs
have to seek loans from the Government out of budgetary allocations. The Committee considers it to be a clear
indication of credit worthiness of the PSEs.
 
            The Committee also observed the practice that despite retaining the allocations in RE, a significant part of the
allocations are committed only towards last quarter of the FY without actually meeting the proposed outcome. The
Committee takes serious view of this FY-end rush in expenditures. It is for this reason that the Committee has been
asking the DHI to seek Supplementary Demands as and when the BRPSE recommendations are approved by the
competent authority. The Committee found DHI’s reply not convincing particularly in the view of practice where DHI
have been seeking token amounts for certain capital expenditures with an understanding to enhance them through
Supplementary Demands as per the requirement. The Committee found the DHI’s response inconsistent.
 
            On the critical R&D projects like Coal Gassification, the Committee considers  that the avoidable delay in
commissioning the project due to  lack of coordination between the managements of BHEL and NTPC  is patently
unacceptable and  holds the management of the two PSEs accountable for any possible escalation of cost. Given the fact
that power sector in the country is opening up to newer technologies, a delay may affect the commercial prospects of
this project. The Committee feels that the managements of the two Navratna PSEs need to be more alive to the urgent
technology requirements of the country in power sector. The Committee asks the DHI to ensure that the issues between
the two PSEs are resolved and provide the status report to the Committee.
 
            Regarding the NATRIP, it has been Committee’s view that since the Auto Sector in the country has sizable
presence of private players, the project should be modeled as a public-private partnership with an enhanced contribution
from automobile companies and lesser dependence on budgetary support. Considering that the lands for the NATRIP
are being provided by the State Governments at much cheaper rates and the Budgetary support of Rs 1090 crores being
provided as Planned resources, a meager Rs 118 crores  as annual user charges hardly qualifies NATRIP as an ideal
