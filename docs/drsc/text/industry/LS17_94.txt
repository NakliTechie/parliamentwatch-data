MoU SYSTEM IN THE CHEMICAL SECTOR
INTRODUCTION
1. The Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) is an agreement between the Government, being the owner, and specific PSE. The
obligations and mutual responsibilities of both the parties are clearly specified indicating the intensions and the objectives to be achieved.
The main objectives of the MoU system were to improve the performance of the PSE through enhanced autonomy and accountability. It
was also envisaged through the system to provide clarity of goals and objectives to be achieved.   
1.1 The Sub-Committee-III for the Ministry of Heavy Industry and Public Enterprises of Department-related Parliamentry Standing
Committee at its various meetings deliberated upon the MoU system as a performance improvement instrument and examined the
efficacy of the system in the telecom, oil, civil aviation, strategic and allied sectors. In order to have a clear idea of system in chemicals
sector, the Committee decided to have an interactive meeting with the management of the Hindustan Organic Chemicals Ltd. on July 4,
2002 at Mumbai. The Sub-Committee consisted of the following Members :

II. MoU System in HOCL
2.1  The commitments and the mutual responsibilities of the Government and the PSE are clearly defined in the MoU system. The targets
are finalised before the beginning of the financial year and the performance evaluation with respect to these targets is carried out only at
the end of the year. The focus under the MoU system has shifted to achievements of results. Ministries have begun to withdraw from
their tendency to control by procedures. MoU has therefore increased operational autonomy of the enterprise.
2.2  Through the MoU system an effort has been made to strike a balance between the static and dynamic performance parameters. While
the static parameters in the form of financial/physical targets are essential to make the PSEs financially viable, the dynamic parameters in
the form of productivity, marketing efforts, modernization, technology upgradation, quality control, project implementation, R&D,
customer satisfaction and HRD are included keeping in view the growth of the PSEs on a long term basis. By emphasising on the
dynamic parameters and comparing the performance with the private sector, the MoU system made an attempt to help the PSEs to face
competition.
2.3  Recently, the strategic issues and benchmarks have also been introduced to make the MoU system more dynamic. This is a step
towards the direction of helping the PSEs to become global players and to face the competition in a situation of globalisation.
2.4  The performance evaluation under the MoU system is carried out only at the end of the year, however, the Administrative Ministries
undertake quarterly/half-yearly review of performance. With introduction of MOU system, the discussions during these meetings is
reportedly become more focused. This has led to higher quality of debate about the performance of the PSEs.
2.5  By making a distinction between the managerial performance and the performance of the enterprise under the MoU system the
judgements on the performance of managements of the PSEs have become much more fair. This has been very good for the morale of the
employees who know that gross generalisation about public secotor is unfair.
2.6  HOCL signed its first MoU with the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers in the year 1991-92. The effectiveness of signing of MoU
by the HOCL can be gauged from the following facts:
          1.   Several projects (capacity expansion & diversification) implemented with a capital outlay  upto Rs. 50 crores within the Board's
powers under the authority delegated to MoU signing  PSUs.
          2.   The physical performance review/evaluation resulted in continuous growth.
          3.   Inclusion of additional dynamic parameters in MoU ensured sustained overall growth.
          4.   Earlier protective environment in duty structure ensured continuous growth in company's physical and financial parameters.
          5.   Drastic reduction in the duty and consequently withdrawal of duty protection resulted in revision of MOU parameters including
the emphasis on five main core products for performance evaluation criteria.

2.8  It may be seen that HOCL has impressive records in respect of MoU scoring over the years. It has earned good weightage in
efficiency parameters such as specific energy consumption, raw material consumption efficiency, customer satisfaction, R&D, reduction
in outstanding and training of employees. But a most disturbimg fact surfaced during the MoU negotiation meeting in March, 2002 while
finalising the MoU of HOCL. It was noted that the performance of the company was affected due to steep decline in the international
price of its products. The company was suffering from small size of plants, high power and interest rate, low labour productivity and
lower margins. It was mentioned that it was difficult for HOCL to achieve long term economic viability without substantial injection of
new funds to revamp the operations at global scale.
2.9  In view of the drastic reduction of import duty and availability of cheap import, a contrasting situation has emerged. The company
has a good performance record as per MoU rating. If we go by MoU rating it          cannot be said that it has failed in its pursuit of results,
but its operational losses gives an impression that if the losses continue it will be on the road towards its closure. In this regard, the
mention of the mission of signing the MoU and Government's responsibilities is relevant. The mission of signing the MoU by HOCL
with the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers is to retain the leadership in chemical business and emerge as a global player. Moreover,
Part II of the MoU mentioned that Government would provide all possible help to safeguard the interest of the company. Department of
Public Enterprises has also stated that the strategic issues and benchmarks   have also been introduced to make the MoU system more
dynamic and this is a step towards the direction of helping the PSEs to become global Players and to face competition in a situation of
globalisation. In such a situation, the Committee feels that in view of the ground reality, the Government should come out with a scheme
to help the company to come out of the woods. Otherwise, the high sounded words enshrined in the MoU document will remain a pious
desire while the company with a good track record would make its journey towards its doom.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
3.  The Committee after having the interaction session with HOC and going through all reading material supplied by it marks the
following observations/recommendations:-
3.1  The Committee after examining the performance of HOC within the MoU parameters is of the opinion that weightage should be
given to the role played by HOC in developing those chemical derivatives, which are being imported at exorbitant prices.
3.2  The Committee is of the opinion that dumping of cheap chemical products from the countries like China is a major reason for the
deteriorating performance of the indigenous chemical sector industry. The Committee recommends that Anti dumping mechanism be
activated to ensure cheap import and the Government should take up the matter with the designated authorities in this regard.
3.3  The Committee was concerned to note that adequate steps to make the Indian Chemical Industry  globally competitive have not been
taken by the Government. The Committee is of the view that adequate steps to establish world scale capacities for achieving economies
of scale be taken.
3.4  The Committee also observed that having regard to the vital sector in which the unit is operating the structure of the MoU should be
more appropriately geared to this factor. It was observed the focus on financial performance, which is being reiterated in the MoU, should
be altered to shift the focus on more important factors of national interest. Hence the MoU should accord higher weightage to
performance parameters relating R&D Development of Import subsidies etc.
3.5  The Committee recommends that Department of Public Enterprises should support any move made by the Ministry of Chemicals and
fertilizers for securing loan/budgetary support etc. to help the company. The Committee also stresses that the Ministry of Chemicals and
Fertilizers being the administrative Ministry should take appropriate steps in this regard on a priority basis. The Committee hopes that the
Ministry will come forward to lend its helping hand to the company in this hour of need.
3.6  The Committee, after having interactions with the CMD of HOC, advocates the following remedies for making chemical industry
globally competitive:
        1.   WTO safeguard measure to be adopted to protect Indian industry against dumping
        2.   Technology upgradation
`       3.   Amendment of labour laws
        4.   Duty-free import of catalyst
        5.   Establish world scale capacities for economies of scale
        6.   Adequate infrastructure facility of international standard
        7.   Reduce power cost
        8.   Reduce cost of finance
        9.   Amalgamation and merger of units
      10.   Basic Feed Stock to be made available at FOB prices delivered to user industry
3.7
    The Committee feels that necessary financial support may be given to the company so that it becomes viable and may fetch good
price in the event of its disinvestment.

3.3 The Committee, thereafter discussed in details the aims of Auto Policy which aimed at to promote integrated, phased, enduring and
self-sustained growth of the Indian Automobile Industry. The Committee discussed also the objectives of Auto Policy which inter-alia
include promotion of a globally competitive automotive industry; Exalting the sector as a lever of industrial growth and an employment;
Establishment of an international hub for manufacturing small affordable passenger cars and key centre for manufacturing Tractors and
Two wheelers in the world; Ensuring a balanced transition to open trade at a minimal risk to the Indian Economy and local industry;
Modernisation of the industry by facilitating indigenous design, research and development; Steering up India's software industry into
automotive technology; Development of domestic safety and environmental standard at par with international standards; Assisting
development of vehicles propelled by alternative energy sources.
