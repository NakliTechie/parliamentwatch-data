INTRODUCTION
         I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry, having been authorised
by the Committee, hereby present this One Hundred Fifty-first Report on Demands for Grants (2004-05), pertaining to
the Department of Heavy Industries (Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises).

Industries on 9th August, 2004. The Committee also considered the pre-budget expectation and post budget reactions of
the Department of Heavy Industries to have in-depth account of the impact of budget proposals (2004-05) on the Heavy
Industries as a whole.

thankful to the representatives of the Department of Heavy Industries who appeared before the Committee. The
Committee also wishes to thank the Ministry for furnishing the replies to the questionnaire and the points raised during
the course of oral evidence.
4.      The Committee at its meeting held on 25th August, 2004 considered and adopted the Report.

ROLE OF THE DEPARTMENT OF HEAVY INDUSTRIES

151st Report of The Committee on Industry

         The Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) administers 48 Central Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs), a majority of
which are involved in the manufacture of capital goods for power, steel, petro-chemical and fertilizer, transport industry
etc. Apart from the PSEs, the Department of Heavy Industry focuses on promoting the development of automotive
sector, heavy electrical/engineering industry in the country.
2.      The Committee was informed that during the year, the Central PSEs under the Department achieved a turnover of
more than Rs. l2,000 crore The BHEL contributed in the development of the power sector in the country. The company
recorded a turnover of Rs. 8, 653 crore (prov.) during 2003-04. PSEs are being encouraged to take up viable capital
investment programmes for their strengthening.
3.      The Department regularly interacts with various industry Associations and evolves plans for their growth through
policy initiatives, resolution of problems relating to tariff and trade, promotion of technological upgradation and
Research and Development.
4.      Since the beginning of liberalization and opening up of the economy, the Department has been continuously
restructuring Public Enterprises to improve their competitive viability in domestic and global market. Some of the
measures taken in this regard are necessary manpower rationalization, financial restructuring, upgradation of the
technology and improvement in the productivity. During the current year 2004-05, actions are being initiated for
revival/restructuring of PSEs, as per the Government’s policy.
5.      The Committee was informed that periodic review of the performance of PSEs was done at various levels within
the Ministry as well as in the Board. The system of signing MOUs  with PSEs has been effective in enhancing their
performance and ascertaining the accountability. The MOU system is also periodically reviewed and modified.
6.      The proposed plans of the Department include programmes  of renewals and replacement, additions, modifications
in PSEs. These initiatives are expected to improve their productivity.
7.      The Department of Heavy Industry had projected a demand of Rs. 223.24 crore to the Planning Commission for
the year 2004-05. The Planning Commission however finally agreed to an allocation of Rs. 131 crore. Some of the
schemes like Coal Gassification , Auto Testing and programmes  of ILK, Braithwaite etc. have been provided reduced
allocations.
8.      The Committee was informed that any shortfall, as a result, should be projected at RE stage and no project would
be allowed to suffer.
9.      The Committee impresses upon the Government to provide for the requisite funds at the RE stage as the proposed
plan of DHI have a direct bearing on the improvement of the productivity of its PSEs.

AUTOMOBILE SECTOR
39.    The Automobile Sector is highly capital intensive and technology oriented. Consequently, continuous research and
development expenditure is a key to the growth of the industry. The Committee was informed that in 2002-2003, the
total expenditure on R&D by the vehicle manufacturers had been Rs. 483.24 crores, which is almost 12% of gross profit
of the automobile manufacturers. The results are clearly seen in terms of  increasing  number of indigenously developed
light and heavy commercial vehicles.
40.    Moreover, the intensive R&D efforts in the industry has helped in upgradation  of  technology  for achieving
increasingly stringent emission and safety norms. Creation of new Testing Infrastructure for the automotive sector at the
R&D Institutes has enabled the Indian Automotive Industry to be globally more competitive and easy access to the
global market.
41.    The Committee was further informed that the upgradation and creation of Testing Infrastructure for the automotive
sector is a necessity in view of the imminent mandatory safety and emission standards. To realise  the objective .of
making India a hub of global automobile industry as envisaged in Auto Policy, a sound R&D base is essential.
42.    In fact, as recognition to the R&D capabilities developed domestically, the world’s major automotive
manufacturers, have been setting up R&D centres in India to cater to their overseas requirements.
43.    The Committee concurs that in view of emerging requirements of the automotive sector, there is an urgent need
for setting up of a World Class Testing and R&D Infrastructure in the country, which requires huge investment in the
next 3-4 years.
44.    The Committee takes note of the important project - National Automotive  Testing and R&D Infrastructure Project
(NATRIP) that has the potential to contribute towards GDP, exports and employment. The Committee recommends that
requisite budgetary support should be provided to such projects in view of the prospects for the country.
45.    The Committee also feels that the industry should come forward to share the expenditure so as to enable the
Government to set up world-class facilities. The Committee therefore impresses upon DHI to interact with concerned
federations and associations in this regard.
46.    The Committee was informed that the Government of India had extended the benefits of 150% weighted deduction
under Income Tax Act to automobile sector for R&D expenditure in the Union Budget 2004-05. While interacting with
various stakeholders in auto sector viz. SIAM, ACMA, ATMA, ARAI and HMT (Tractor division), the Committee
noted that the auto-industry has generally appreciated the provisions and direction of the budget. Further rationalization
of excise duty regime has been appreciated by the industry. Yet concerns were expressed by the industry pertaining to
rising prices of steel, natural rubber and other inputs. Industry has demanded further rationalization of excise duties.
47.    The Committee takes note of the need for excise duty rationalization in tractors to facilitate the producers to
transfer the benefits of reduced duty to the consumers. The Committee feels that the advantages of duty rationalisation
be such that benefits reaches the farmers.
48.    The Committee, therefore, agrees with this viewpoint and recommends that excise duties on the Tractor
components should be brought under CENVAT.

PSES REFERRED TO BIFR         
51.    The Committee was informed by the DHI that 19 PSEs are sick and referred to BIFR and the cases of 26 PSEs are
in the process of Joint Venture formation.
52.    It was informed that cases of various loss making PSEs shall be reviewed within the           Department as well as
by the proposed Board for Reconstruction of PSEs announced by the Finance Minister in his Budget Speech. Based on
these reviews, necessary revival/restructuring packages shall be formulated for individual PSEs.
53.    The Committee was informed that consultancy services should be sought for          restructuring of PSEs in
accordance with policy under NCMP. This is considered essential as it is a step towards revitalization of PSEs with the
aim of converting them into sustainable entity. A provision of Rs. 2.50 crores is made under the head of Secretariat
Economic Services.   
54.    A provision of Rs.150 crore was made for revival schemes of PSEs in BE 2003-04. These funds are being utilized
whenever BIFR/Government approves a revival scheme. During 2003-04, major amount of this was utilized for
providing working capital support for wages/salaries etc. for some of the needy PSEs like BWEL, HSL, NEPA, TSL,
CCI, HMT (C), TSPL, BWT, AYCL, ILK, HCL, NIDC, HPF, BOGL, BSCL, BPCL.
55. Performance of the PSEs is being reviewed through close monitoring in the Board  and assessment in the Ministry.
Monthly performance reports and discussions with the Management during the periodic review meetings, enables the
assessment of its           improvement in performance or otherwise. It also provides an opportunity to understand the
factors affecting the performance and to consider corrective measures wherever necessary. Further necessary ratings of
MOU performance wherever applicable is also given based on the achievements of the PSEs.
56.    In course of examination of Demands for Grants, while hearing the Secretary, DHI, the Committee was informed
that revival/restructuring packages for the PSEs have been implemented in many cases, for several times. On being
asked why the revival/ restructuring could not be taken at one-time, the Committee was informed that inadequate
funding was also one of the reasons.
57.    The Committee expresses its concern. on the inadequate allocation for the revival of PSEs. The Committee is of
the view that the Units need a comprehensive one­time revival package instead of a package that is staggered over a
longer period of time.
58.    The Committee observes that insufficient infusion of capital for revival is, in fact, a wastage, as it does not meet
the required objectives.
59.    The Committee also impresses upon the Government to introduce a system of accountability on part of
management of the company so as to guarantee the viable revival of the Unit after adequate funds are made available.

CONSTRAINTS  FACED  BY  THE  PSES  UNDER  THE  DEPARTMENT
63.    Committee was informed about the constraints being faced by the PSEs. The PSEs suffer from paucity of orders,
shortage of working , capital, excess manpower and obsolete technology in some cases. With liberalization and WT0
regulations; the tariff barriers are gradually coming down thereby putting pressure on the domestic industry to perform.
The other problems include lack of demand, shortage of working capital, surplus manpower, obsolete plant and
machinery etc. These problems are being addressed by providing funds for manpower rationalization, financial
restructuring, capital investments, and rendering assistance for getting orders from Government Departments/agencies,
on a case to case basis. The Government guarantees for seeking financial assistance from FIs/Banks is also being
provided in some cases. The aim is to improve their viability and make them self-sustaining.

INTERACTION WITH PSES
Heavy Engineering Corporation
69.    Established in 1952, HEC was the largest integrated, complex in the country that catered to the demands of
infrastructure sector, steel plants; railways, ship building; power plants, nuclear and. space research programs; ,defence
and mining industries. However in recent years the capacity and manpower of the Company has remained grossly
unutilized. Lack of assertive marketing, dependence of the protected market of Government and PSEs, inter-union
rivalry, leading to poor work culture, high cost of maintenance, of the equipments, consequent increase in interest
burden and shortage of working capital have collectively resulted in the Company being untenable and was referred to
BIFR in 1992. In July 2004 the BIFR recommended winding up of the Company. An appeal against the order in now
pending before AAIFR.
70.    In recent years however the Company has been able to reduce its operational losses and enhance the productivity.
The Company is now concentrating more on the areas of its core competence, while out sourcing low value addition

activities. It has also been rationalising its manpower. The Company hopes to benefit from the buoyant capital goods
market after a prolonged recession in steel industry.
71.    The Committee observes that the Company is yet to go a long way. The Committee notes with concern that HEC’s
order book position is far from satisfactory. The Committee is amazed to note that the Company does not have any full-

growing market domestically, regionally or even globally. The Committee therefore recommends to the Government to

ANDREW YULE & CO.  LTD.
72.    The Company has been a Government of India enterprise since May 1979. It’s net worth is still negative at Rs.
34.93 crores. Tea is the major area of company’s business. However the recession in tea market has adversely affected
its financial performance. The recent price movement in tea has enhanced the prospects of Company’s tea-product. At
present the company is facing shortage of funds and owes mounting employee’s dues and other statutory dues. In 2003,
the Government approved a restructuring and revival package for the company that entailed Rs 49 crores of fresh equity,
Rs. 20 crores of Bond against Government’s guarantee and ploughing back Rs. 47 crore from the sale of investments in
group companies. The Government also agreed to convert Rs. 41.73 crores of loan into equity and waiver of interest on
its loan.
73.    The Committee was informed by DHI that with the improved tea prices the tea division of the company could be
in position to earn profit from 2006-07 onwards as per the restructuring plan.
74.    The Committee notes that due to NCMP, the group companies like Hoogly Printing Works and Tide Water Oil
could not be disinvested. The Company has submitted alternative proposal for assistance in place of its disinvested
units.
75.    The Committee therefore recommends that the proposal be considered by the Government so as to enable it to turn
around.
HINDUSTAN CABLES LTD.
76.    HCL was set up in 1952 to meet the increasing requirement of telecom cable by the telephone industry. Its main
products are jelly filled and fiber optic cables. However HCL is continuously making losses since 1995-96. Though a
package of assistance was approved by the Government in 1999, HCL was referred to BIFR in 2002: Reduction of
prices and burden of loans to pay VRS dues have reduced the working capital with the company.
77.    The Company requires non-plan assistance to clear salaries and wages and other statutory dues so as to enable it to
operate its bank accounts fully. It also requires assistance to settle its outstanding power-dues of its Rupnarainpur  unit
with Damodar Valley Corporation.
78.    The Committee was informed of lack of coordination between HCL and BSNL, which was the major buyer of its
cables. DHI suggested that ideally the BSNL’s requirement of jelly filled cable should be placed with HCL so that the
later covers 90% of their capacity. However due to fall in cable prices in last years, the price offered by BSNL is unable
to cover the cost of inputs. On the other hand the BSM., is getting cable at a much competitive price from smaller
companies. There is a need on part of HCL to reduce its cost of production. A study to this effect has been entrusted to
IIT Kharagpur. Meanwhile there are exports to obtain orders from BSNL and arrange for working capita.l  loan from
Allahabad Bank.
79.    The Committee observers that HCL was set up to meet the requirements of the predecessor of BSNL i.e. DOT and
presently suffers from lack of orders from them. The Committee recommends to the Government to resolve the issue
amicably at the earliest. The Committee further recommends that the HCL may be merged with BSNL and Government
may provide the Bank Guarantee for raising the working capital from Allahabad Bank.
HMT (BEARINGS) LTD.
80.    HMT (Bearing) Lid. is a Mini-Ratna company. It has been earning marginal though consistent profit till 2001-02.
It incurred loss in 2002-03, however the losses have come down in 2003-04 as rationalisation of manpower is underway.
81.    The Committee was informed that though there is huge domestic market of Rs. 2700 crores for bearings, HMT
(Bearing) Ltd. is only a marginal player with an annual production of Rs 2S-30 crores. Other major players in this field
in the country are SKF, NBC, FAG, Timken and NRB. Primary reason for company’s meager participation is absence
of technology upgradation and lack of funds for R&D. Also the slump in demand from HMT (Tractor), which was the
major buyer of its bearings, too adversely affected the finances of Company. The Company has not been able to find
fresh collaboration to validate its products. Though Company has the potential for a turnaround; it needs to upgrade its
technology.

MEASURES TO MEET THE SOCIAL OBLIGATIONS BY PSES
94.    The information furnished by the Department to the Committee reveals that most of the PSEs are committing less
and less percentage of their turnover for social overheads over the years. .
95.    The Committee observes that the PSEs were envisaged as the temples of modern India. Over the decades the
Public Sector has played a vital role in rapid industrial and technological development of the country. These PSEs have
played a key role in social development. The experience of recent years has shown that financial sickness of companies

151st Report of The Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/151streport.htm[04-07-2016 12:47:20]
has social ramifications too. The Committee therefore desires that social obligations be accorded due priority.
96.    As regards the constraints being faced by the PSEs, the Committee observes as under:
I.     Lack of orders mainly from the Government and other PSEs. In several cases the sickness of one PSE has had
corollary impact on other PSEs too.
II.    Another issue has been the fluctuating duty regime that have made PSEs non-competitive vis-a-vis other
global and domestic players.
III.   Obsolete technology and investment crunch due to reducing capital investment over the years in enhancing
the capacities of the PSEs has adversely affected the PSEs that have not been able to take the advantage of
economies of scale.
IV.   The PSEs at present are reeling under the burden of restructuring and rationalising their manpower. A larger
part of their resources goes into settling the dues often at a higher rate of interest.
V.    The Committee is of the view that in view of new economy and reducing Government support, PSEs shall
have to come to terms with new market realities. With expanding domestic, regional and global market, PSEs
shall have to take benefit of new opportunities.
VI.   In view of the NCMP that envisages a revitalized Public Sector, the Ministry of Heavy Industries should
evolve a Doctrine emphasizing that social obligations of the PSEs is a function of their financial health. The
two are not contradictory but complementary to each other. Therefore to meet its social obligations the Public
Sector must regain their financial health.
97.    Finally the Committee recommends that the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises may formulate its
responses to the observations and suggestions and apprise the Committee of the same periodically.
