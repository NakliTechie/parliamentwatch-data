Report 
 
Government of India has leveraged Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) to achieve 
various socio-economic objectives.  The main objectives for setting up of the PSEs, as 
enumerated in the Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956 were to (i) help in the rapid 
economic growth and industrialisation of the country and create the necessary 
infrastructure for economic development; (ii) earn return on investment and thu s generate 
resources for development; (iii) promote redistribution of income and wealth; (iv)  create 
employment opportunities; (v)  promote balanced regional development;  (vi) assist in the 
development of small -scale and ancillary industries; and (vii) p romote import 
substitutions, save and earn foreign exchange for the economy.  
The Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) in India 
2. The Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) comprise enterprises established 
by the Government of India (GOI) as Gover nment companies under Section 617 of the 
Companies Act, and wherein the equity holding of the GOI is more than 50 per cent. It 
also includes statutory corporations constituted under specific statutes of the Parliament. 
However, the CPSEs do not, include de partmental undertakings, banking institutions and 
enterprises where equity holding of the GOI is 50% or less. 
3. Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) play an important role in the process of 
nation building through their contributions to various aspec ts like economic growth, 
infrastructure development, healthy market competition, balanced regional development 
and price stabilization etc besides fulfilling social objectives.  
4. There were only 5 CPSEs with a total investment of merely 29 crore at the time of 
1st Five Year Plan.  As per the Public Enterprises Survey (2018 -19),  the number of 
CPSEs has increased to 348 with a total investment of Rs. 16,40,628 crore as on 
31.03.2019.  Out of this 348 CPSEs,  249 were operational CPSEs,86 under construction  
and 13 were under closure or liquidation. Out of 249 operational CPSEs, 178 are profit 
making, 70 incurred losses and 1 CPSE(Food Corporation of India) registered no 
profit/no loss. As on 31.3.2019 the overall networth of all CPSEs was Rs 12.09 lakhs 
crores. The overall net profit of operating CPSEs was Rs 1.43 lakh crores during       
2018-19. CPSEs are functioning under various Ministries/Departments and decision with 
regard to major investments, expansion, diversification, joint ventures, modernisation,  
technology upgradation, etc. are taken by the CPSEs in consultation with their respective 
administrative Ministries/Departments. 
Department of Public Enterprises (DPE).  
 5. In their 52 nd Report, the Estimates Committee of 3 rd Lok Sabha (1962-67) stressed 
the need for setting up a centralized c oordinating unit, which could also make continuous 
appraisal of the performance of public enterprises. This led to the setting up of the Bureau 
of Public Enterprises (BPE) in 1965 in the Ministry of Finance. Subsequently, as a result of 
the reorganization of the Ministries/Departments of the Union Government in September, 
1985, BPE was made part of the Ministry of Industry. In May, 1990, BPE was made a full -
fledged Department known as the DPE.  Presently, it is part o f the Ministry of Heavy 
Industries & Public Enterprises (Mo HI &PE). 
1 

6. The DPE is the nodal department for all the CPSEs and formulates policy pertaining 
to them. It lays down, in particular, policy guidelines on performance improvement and 
evaluation, autonomy and financial delegation and personnel management in CPSEs. It 
further, collects and maintains information in the form of a Public Enterprises Survey on 
several areas in respect of CPSEs.  
 7. In fulfilling its role, the Department c oordinates with other Ministries, CPSEs and 
concerned organizations. As per the Allocation of Business Rules of the Government, the 
following subjects have been allocated to the DPE: 
 Residual work relating to erstwhile Bureau of Public Enterprises including 
Industrial Management Pool. 
 Coordination of matters of general policy affecting all Public -Sector 
Enterprises. 
 Evaluation and monitoring the performance of Public Sector Enterprises, 
including the Memorandum of Understanding mechanism. 
 Matters relating to Permanent Machinery of Arbitration for the Public-Sector 
Enterprises. 
 Counselling, training and rehabilitation of employees in Central Public 
Sector Undertakings under Voluntary Retirement Scheme. 
 Review of capital projects and expenditure in Central Public Sector 
Enterprises. 
 Measures aimed at improving performance of Central Public Sector 
Enterprises and other capacity building initiatives of Public Sector Enterprises. 
 Rendering advice relating to revival, restructuring or closure of Public Sector 
Enterprises including the mechanisms therefore. 
 Matters relating to Standing Conference of Public Enterprises. 
 Matters relating to International Center for Public Enterprises. 
 Categorization of Central Public Sector Enterprises including conferring 
‘Ratna’ status. 
 Survey of Public Enterprises. 
8. According to the Annual Report of the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public 
Enterprises the Vision and Mission statements of DPE  are as follows: 
 Vision 
“Effective, Profitable and Globally Competitive Central Public Sector 
Enterprises” 
 Mission 
“To continuously improve management and performance of CPSEs through 
Corporate Governance, performance Evaluation, Human Resource Management, 
Research & Development so as to enhance their global competitiveness” 
 
2 

9. As a part of examinatio n of DFG, the Committee enquired from the Department of 
Public  Enterprises as  to  how  effectively  and through  adoption  of what mechanism does 
DPE as a nodal Department perform its role to achieve its Vision and Mission, apart from 
citing the fact tha t onus for enhancing global competitiveness of CPSEs vests primarily 
with the Administrative Ministry concerned. 
10. DPE clarified that it is primarily a policy formulation Department dealing with 
matters of general policy affecting all CPSEs. In complianc e of its mandate, DPE has 
formulated guidelines on important issues such as (i) performance improvement of CPSEs 
through MoU system, (ii) delegation of powers to CPSEs under ‘Ratna Scheme’ (iii) 
performance appraisal of top management incumbents of CPSEs, (iv) revival, restructuring 
or closure of PSEs, (v) counselling, training and rehabilitation of employees of CPSEs 
under VRS /VSS, (vi) Categorization of CPSEs, (vii) Corporate Governance for CPSEs, 
(viii) time bound closure of sick/loss making CPSEs and di sposal of assets, etc. DPE has 
also taken measures for capacity building of employees of CPSEs through organization of 
regular training programmes in the areas of corporate governance, Companies A ct 
provisions, Ind -As standard, Financial management, Contra ct management CSR etc. in 
association with IIMs, IICA, ICAI, ICSI etc.  
 
11. In order to expand their global operations, the Boards of Maharatna and Navratna 
CPSEs have been empowered to incur capital expenditure without any financial limits, 
make equity I nvestment in JVs/subsidiaries and acquisition of raw material assets abroad 
through merger and acquisition subject to prescribed limits, and enter into Technical 
JV/Strategic Alliances. CPSEs have also been encouraged to formulate action plans in the 
areas of enhancing their global outreach, minimizing the import bill, collaborative research 
with other CPSEs or similar agencies and benchmarking of efficiency/productivity related 
parameters and align their MoU targets with such action plans.  
12. In response  to a query regarding the steps taken by DPE under ‘Research & 
Development’ to formulate policy guidelines which are in tune with the rapid changes 
faced by CPSEs so as to improve their management and performance,  DPE, in the 
written submission informed the Committee that it has not issued any guidelines on the 
subject matter of ‘Research & Development’. However under the DPE plan scheme of 
RDC, DPE has been organizing orientation courses/workshop/ training programme in 
association with reputed Government institutions for capacity building of senior 
management and executives of CPSEs. Further, with a view to promote R&D in CPSEs 
in the MoU system, during target setting of CPSEs, result oriented parameters are given 
for Research & Development (R & D) on the recommendations of Administrative 
Ministries/ Departments. 
13. The Committee is aware that CPSEs in India are undergoing rapid changes 
and have to contribute towards the twin objective of economic development and social 
cause, within the rules and guidelin es framed by Government of India.  CPSEs are 
also expected to remain competitive and generate profit i n a highly globalised and 
market environment.  Under such circumstances, it is very challenging for CPSEs to 
show impressive performances.  The Committee is also aware of the fact that all 
CPSEs give utmost importance to critical performance indicators including R&D, 
technology upgradation etc. in order to improve their performance.  
 3 

The Committee also observes that as the Nodal Department for all CPSEs,  
DPE has been entrusted with a huge responsibility to fulfill.  The Committee , 
therefore, recommends that the guidelines be ing framed by the DPE should be in  
unison with the rapid changes the CPSEs are going through.  Further , the guidelines 
should enable CPSEs to stand on their own and prove their worth in the expanding 
globalised and market economy.  The Committee  further recommends that DPE 
being the formulator of general policy guidelines affecting CPSEs should have a full 
fledged the R&D wing of its ow n to aide and advise the CPSEs to achieve the desired 
goal as explained above.

Categorization of CPSEs 
15. CPSEs are categorized into four schedules namely ‘A’, ‘B’, ‘C’ & ‘D’ based 
on criteria such as quantitative factors like investment, capital employed, net sales, 
profit before tax, number of employees and units, capacity addition, revenue per 
employee, sales/capital employed, capacity utilization, value added per employee 
and qualitative factors such as national importance, complexities of problems being 
faced by the company, level of technology, prospects for expansion and 
diversification of activities and competition from other sectors, etc. 
 
Parameters for categorisation of CPSEs 
 
Quantitative Parameters Investment, Capital employed, Net sales, Profit before Tax, 
Number of employees and units, Capacity Addition, 
Revenue per employee, Sales/Capital Employed, Capacity 
utilization, Value added per employee 
 
Qualitative Parameters  National importance, Complexities of problems faced by 
them, Level of technology, Prospects for expansion and 
diversification of activities, Competition from other sectors 
 
Other Factors Critical strategic importance, Share pric e, MOU ratings, 
ISO certification, Maharatna/Navratna/ Miniratna status

Autonomy of CPSEs: 
 
17. The endeavour of the Government is to make Central Public Sector 
Enterprises (CPSEs) autonomous board managed companies. Government has 
granted enhanced powers to the Boards of the profit making enterprises under 
various schemes like Maharatna, Navratna and Miniratna by delegating powers in 
the areas of (i) capital expenditure, (ii) investment in joint ventures/subsidiaries, (iii) 
mergers & acquisitions, (iv) human resources management, etc. Presently there are 
8Maharatna CPSEs, 16 Navratna CPSEs and 73 Miniratna CPSEs.  
18. Criteria for the grant of Maharatna, Navratna and Min iratna Status to CPSEs 
are as follows:- 
For Maharatna 
• Listed Navratna CPSE 
• Average Annual turnover of more than Rs. 25,000 crore for last 3 years 
• Average net worth of more than 15,000 crore for last 3 years 
• Average net profit of more than Rs. 5,000 crore for last 3 years. 
• Significant global / international operations. 
For Navratna 
• Miniratna category- I, Schedule ‘A’ 
• Excellent/Very good rating in at least 3 out of 5 last MoUs 
• Minimum of 60 marks (out of 100) in 6 performance parameters 
For Miniratna 
• CPSEs wh ich have made profits for 3 years continuously with positive net 
worth. 
• CPSEs which have pre-tax profit of Rs 30 crore or more in at least one of the 
previous three years eligible for Category – I status.

20. The Committee notes that the total no. of CPSE s stood at 348 during 2018 -19, 
whereas the figure for 2017 -18 showed 339 CPSEs only.  Therefore, the no. of CPSEs 
has increased by 9 which shows 2.65% growth over the previous year,.  However, the 
no. of operating CPSEs has reduced from 258 during 2017 -18 to 249 during 2018 -19, 
showing a decrease in growth by 3.49%.  Further most of the indicators such as the 
paid-up Capital, Capital Employed, Networth, Investment, Reserves & Surplus total 
turnover, overall profit, Foreign Exchange Earnings etc. indicate a steady increase 
from 2016-17 to 2018-19. 
 However, indicators such as Dividend paid, regular employment in numbers, 
Market Capitalism etc. have shown a steady decline from the year 2016-17 to 2018-19.  
The committee observes that one of the main objectives  for setting up of PSEs was to 
create employment opportunities in the country.  However, the macro -view of the 249 
operating CPSEs for the last 3 years show  gradual  decline in number of regular 
employees. A s per the information furnished by DPE as part of  examination of 
Demands for Grants 2020 -21, there is a reduction of 55,560 employees from 2017 -18 
to 2018-19.   
 However, as per the Public Enterprises Survey 2018 -19 brought out by DPE, 
CPSEs across Sectors provided employment to 15,14,064 people as on 31 .03.2019, 
including casual workers and contract workers, as against 15,54,967 as on 31.03.2018.  
As per the Survey , there is a reduction of 40,903 employees (2.63%) in total no. of 
people employed by CPSEs including casual and contract employees.  
 The Committee notes that there is a mismatch in the figures regarding number 
of employees in CPSEs  as shown in the written submission made by DPE before the 
committee and the Public Enterprises Survey 2018-19.However,it is a fact that there is 
a gradual decline i n number of regular employees in CPSEs.   The Committee 
therefore recommends that CPSEs should be encouraged to fulfil their Social 
Objective and provide more permanent employment to people, instead of engaging 
personnel on casual and contract basis.  
 The Committee also observed during the course of examining the Demands for 
Grants that a number of PSUs, even the profit making Maharatnas fail to fill up 
vacancies of personnel and many a time, such vacancies are filled by hiring of 
superannuated employees on contractual basis, to fill up the vacancies, which is not a  
6 

healthy trend.  The Committee recommends that DPE should take steps to encourage 
CPSEs and Ministries concerned to desist from this practice and employ young minds 
in the vacancies, on regular  basis, so that CPSEs can gain from the new concepts and 
interests evinced by such young minds and become globally competitive.

MoU System for CPSEs 
 
45. The Government of India introduced the MoU system in the year 1986, based on 
the recommendations given by Arjun S en Gupta Committee Report (1984). The report 
recommended that the CPSEs should enter agreement with their administrative Ministries 
for five years, with progress to be reviewed annually. The MoU system was given broader 
thrust by the Governments after the announcement of the New Industrial Policy of 1991.   
 
46. DPE monitors and evaluates the profitability and efficacy of MoU signing CPSEs 
through their MoU targets & their evaluation to measure the performance of the 
management of the CPSEs on key selected parameters so as to improve the critical 
performance indicators of the organization. 
47. MoU guidelines are reviewed from time to time based on the feedback received 
from Administrative Ministries/ CPSEs and based on the changes occurred in the 
Regulations. 
 
16 

48. Some of the main points of the latest MoU Guideline issued by DPE on 17th 
January, 2019 are as under:- 
 
1. Memorandum of Understanding (MoU): MoU is a negotiated agreement and 
contract between the Administrative Ministry/ Department/ Holding CPSE i.e . 
majority shareholder and the Management of the Central Public Sector Enterprise 
(CPSE) on selected parameters having targets decided normally before the start of a 
new financial year and results evaluated after the end of the year to measure the 
performance.  For carrying out this exercise, CPSEs would provide brief  and trend 
analysis. 
 
2. Purpose of MoU :  The purpose of the MoU is to measure the performance of the 
management of the CPSEs on key selected parameters against the targets agreed 
upon so as to improve the critical performance indicators of the organisation. 
 
3. Scope: All CPSEs (Holding as well as Subsidiaries) are required to sign MoUs. The 
Apex/Holding companies will sign MoUs with their administrative Ministries/ 
Departments, while the Subsidiary  companies will sign MoUs with their respective 
Apex/ Holding companies. 
 
4. Exemption from MoU:    
4.1 High Powered Committee in its meeting held on 8 th March, 2017 had confirmed/ 
approved exemption to following CPSEs from signing of MoU:- 
i.  India Infrastructure Finance Co. Ltd., and its subsidiaries. 
4.2 Following CPSEs may be exempted from MoU system by the Inter -Ministerial 
Committee (IMC): 
i. CPSEs under liquidation where Liquidator has already been appointed. However 
administrative ministry would provide the list of  such CPSEs to DPE along -
with brief write-up. 
ii. CPSE which is not in operation or having no employees or on any other ground 
on the recommendation of administrative Ministry. 
 
5. Parameters:  CPSEs work in various sectors under different conditions.  In view of  
this, the following guidelines are laid down:- 
 
5.1 There would be uniform parameters for measuring financial performances such as 
revenue from operations, operating profit and return on investment (e.g. ratio of 
PAT/Net-worth). This would be applicable to al l CPSEs, except CPSEs which are 
dependent on government grant or performing functions of distribution of grant etc. 
e.g. Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC).  Hence, 3 
financial parameters have been prescribed for all CPSEs with total weightage of 50% 
except for CPSEs like BIRAC.   
5.2 For the remaining 50% weightage, a menu of parameters has been suggested for 
selection depending on the sector in which the CPSE is operating.  Separate Format 
has been given for Finance Sector and another Format for the remaining operating 
CPSEs. The parameters most appropriate and relevant for measuring performance 
shall be suggested by the Pre-Negotiation Committee (PNC) to the Inter-Ministerial  
 
17 

Committee (IMC).  In all the cases IMC shall take appro priate decision on the 
suggestions made by PNC. 
5.3 Chairman, IMC is authorised to modify the parameters or weightages of parameters 
in sector specific cases, if justified. 
 
5.4 For CPSEs under closure/ under construction/ under reconstruction, parameters have 
not been prescribed leaving it to the PNC to suggest the most appropriate parameters 
and targets for IMC to take decisions.  For such CPSEs, the emphasis should be to 
suggest parameters and fix targets for starting commercial operations as early as 
possible.  For CPSEs under closure, the targets would be for ensuring time bound 
closure.   
 
6. MoU Ranking:   
i. With a view to distinguish ‘excellent performance’ from ‘poor performance’, five 
different performance ratings have been fixed in the MoU, i.e., ‘Excellent’, 
‘Very Good’, ‘ Good’, ‘ Fair’ and ‘Poor’. 
ii. Apart from rating of CPSEs, MoU performance of all MoU signing CPSEs, 
there will be category -wise ranking of CPSEs (Maharatna, Navratna, 
Miniratna, others) and sector -wise ranking of CPSEs (Mining, Power, 
Petroleum, Finance, consultancy etc.) only for the purposes of acknowledging 
where they stand in their respective category/ sector.   
 
7. MoU Targets: 
i.  Targets fixed should be realistic, growth oriented and aspirational.Generally 
target for Excellent should not be low er than best achieved in last 5 years and 
Very Good should not be lower than the expected achievement of the current 
year (year immediately preceding the year for which targets are being fixed) 
unless there are specific reasons to fix lower targets and are  duly supported by 
the administrative ministry/ department. 
ii. Once MoUs are signed, any revision of targets would not be permissible. MoU 
targets are unconditional and non-provisional. 
 
8. Pre-negotiation Committee (PNC):   The role of the Pre -negotiation Commit tee 
(earlier known as Standing Committee on MoU) would be to assist IMC in 
determining the most appropriate and relevant parameters for measuring 
improvement in performance and for fixing targets.  Meetings of the Pre -negotiation 
Committee would be held in  each case b efore the meetings of IMC to look at the 
trends, discuss, negotiate and recommend MoU parameters and targets.  The 
composition of Pre -negotiation Committee would remain the same of erstwhile 
Standing Committee.  
 
9. Inter-Ministerial Committee (IM C):MoU targets would be decided by the IMC.  
There is no change in the composition of the Committee. Any change in the 
composition of the committee would be done with the approval of Cabinet Secretary. 
 
10. Time-lines for submission of MoU : The draft MoU with all documents/ Annexures 
should be submitted to administrative Ministry/ Department in respect of all CPSEs 
and their subsidiaries by 21st November of each year for the forthcoming year. The  
18 

draft MoU after the approval of administrative Ministry/ Depa rtment should be sent 
to DPE by 15 th December of each year for the forthcoming year with all 
documents/Annexures. Administrative Ministry/ Department would ensure that the 
targets are realistic, growth -oriented, aspirational and consistent with the latest 
Annual Plan, Budget and Corporate Plan of the CPSE.A copy of draft MoU may also 
be sent to IMC Members other than DPE.  Secretary, DPE is authorized to extend the 
dates.  
 
11. MoU Signing Process :MoUbased on the parameters, targets and weightage 
recommended by IMC without any deviation shall be signed between CMD/ MD of 
CPSE and Secretary of administrative Ministry/ Department in case of holding/ 
independent CPSEs and between CEO/ MD of subsidiary company and CMD/ MD 
of holding CPSE in case of subsidiary by 31 st March (i.e. before start of financial 
year in respect of which targets are fixed) or within 21 days from issue of IMC 
meeting minutes, whichever is later.  In case, deviation is detected, IMC minutes 
would prevail.  
 
12. MoU Evaluation: Evaluation of MoU of the CPSE is done after the end of the year 
on the basis of actual achievements vis -à-vis the MoU targets. CPSEs (Holding as 
well as Subsidiaries) are required to submit performance evaluation reports on the 
basis of audited accounts to Department of Public  Enterprises after approval of the 
Board of CPSE and through the administrative Ministries/Departments on or before 
30th September (in respect of immediately preceding year) or any other date 
communicated by DPE.  Figures and information in the MoU achievement which are 
not verifiable from audited accounts would be relied on the basis of certification by 
way of resolution of the Board given separately for each parameter. 
 
12.1 Pro-rata adjustment may be made in the targets in case of improvement in actual 
performance over the estimated performance in the base year informed at the time of 
target setting. 
 
12.2 If at the time of evaluation, it is observed that any CPSE may have under -pitched 
their projected performance for the year concerned to have soft targets fixed,  DPE/ 
IMC may call the CMD of such CPSE to clarify the matter for enabling the IMC to 
evaluate the performance and assign marks and rating based on justification given by 
the CMD.  
13. MoU Score and Rating :MoU score is an aggregate of score on all parameters w ith 
respect to performance vis-à-vis the targets.  
13.1 The system of rating of CPSEs on the basis of MoU Aggregate Score is as follows:-

Revival/Restructuring/Closure of CPSEs 
51. The Government of India has taken many steps to make the mechanism and process 
for revival/ r estructuring of CPSEs time bound, comprehensive, performance driven and 
efficient. The Government has decided to remove the multiple layers in the decision -
making process to ensure timely revival/ restructuring of CPSEs. It has been decided that 
revival/ r estructuring of sick /incipient sick CPSEs is to be merit based considering 
strategic, national and business concerns of the CPSEs. Accordingly, the Government has 
wound up BRPSE and administrative Ministries/Departments concerned are made 
responsible to m onitor sickness of CPSEs functioning under them and to take timely 
redressal measures with the approval of the competent authority. On these lines DPE first 
issued guidelines on 29 th October, 2015 and thereafter on 7 th September, 2016 for 
‘Streamlining the mechanism for revival and restructuring of sick/ incipient sick and weak 
CPSEs: General principles and mechanism of restructuring’ to be followed by the 
administrative Ministries/ Departments. Further, DPE issued revised guidelines on 14 th 
June, 2018 rega rding ‘T ime bound closure of sick/ loss -making CPSEs and disposal of 
their movable and immovable assets’. These revised guidelines are intended to 
expeditiously complete the procedures for closure of CPSEs and lay down responsibilities

62. As per the provisions on CSR in Section -135 of Compa nies Act, 2013, all

preceding years to undertake CSR activities. Further, in p ursuance to the powers conferred

provide reasons for not spending the amount in its Board’s Report made under Section 134

given in Schedule VII of the Companies Act, 2013.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
The Committee is aware that CPSEs in India are undergoing rapid changes 
and have to contribute towards the twin objective of economic development and social 
cause, within the rules and guidelines framed by Government of India.  CPSEs are 
also expected to remain competitive and generate profit i n a highly globalised and 
market environment.  Under such circumstances, it is very challenging for CPSEs to 
show impr essive performances.  The Committee is also aware of the fact that all 
CPSEs give utmost importance to critical performance indicators including R&D, 
technology upgradation etc. in order to improve their performance.  
 The Committee also observes that as t he Nodal Department for all CPSEs, 
DPE has been entrusted with a huge responsibility to fulfill.  The Committee , 
therefore, recommends that the guidelines being framed by the DPE should be  in 
unison  with the rapid changes the CPSEs are going through.  Fur ther, the guidelines 
should enable CPSEs to stand on their own and prove their worth in the expanding 
globalised and market economy.  The Committee  further recommends that DPE 
being the formulator of general policy guidelines affecting CPSEs should have a full 
fledged the R&D wing of its own to aide and advise the CPSEs to achieve the desired 
goal as explained above. 
(Para 13) 
 The Committee notes that the total no. of CPSEs stood at 348 during 2018 -19, 
whereas the figure for 2017 -18 showed 339 CPSEs only.  Therefore, the no. of CPSEs 
has increased by 9 which shows 2.65% growth over the previous year,.  However, the 
no. of operating CPSEs has reduced from 258 during 2017 -18 to 249 during 2018 -19, 
showing a decrease in growth by 3.49%.  Further most of the in dicators such as the 
paid-up Capital, Capital Employed, Networth, Investment, Reserves & Surplus total 
turnover, overall profit, Foreign Exchange Earnings etc. indicate a steady increase 
from 2016-17 to 2018-19. 
 However, indicators such as Dividend paid, regular employment in numbers, 
Market Capitalism etc. have shown a steady decline from the year 2016-17 to 2018-19.  
The committee observes that one of the main objectives for setting up of PSEs was to 
create employment opportunities in the country.  Howev er, the macro-view of the 249 
operating CPSEs for the last 3 years show  gradual  decline in number of regular 
employees. A s per the information furnished by DPE as part of examination of 
Demands for Grants 2020 -21, there is a reduction of 55,560 employees from 2017-18 
to 2018-19.   
 However, as per the Public Enterprises Survey 2018 -19 brought out by DPE, 
CPSEs across Sectors provided employment to 15,14,064 people as on 31.03.2019, 
including casual workers and contract workers, as against 15,54,967 as on 31.03.2018.  
As per the Survey , there is a reduction of 40,903 employees (2.63%) in total no. of 
people employed by CPSEs including casual and contract employees.  
 The Committee notes that there is a mismatch in the figures regarding number 
of employees in  CPSEs as shown in the written submission made by DPE before the 
committee and the Public Enterprises Survey 2018-19.However,it is a fact that there is 
29 

a gradual decline in number of regular employees in CPSEs.   The Committee 
therefore recommends that C PSEs should be encouraged to fulfil their Social 
Objective and provide more permanent employment to people, instead of engaging 
personnel on casual and contract basis.  
 The Committee also observed during the course of examining the Demands for 
Grants that  a number of PSUs, even the profit making Maharatnas fail to fill up 
vacancies of personnel and many a time, such vacancies are filled by hiring of 
superannuated employees on contractual basis, to fill up the vacancies, which is not a 
healthy trend.  The C ommittee recommends that DPE should take steps to encourage 
CPSEs and Ministries concerned to desist from this practice and employ young minds 
in the vacancies, on regular basis, so that CPSEs can gain from the new concepts and 
interests evinced by such young minds and become globally competitive.

of CPSEs issued by DPE on 10th Dec.2018, a common theme will be identified for each 
year for undertaking CSR by CPSEs, which should cover 60% of annual CSR 
expenditure and Aspirational Districts have to be given preference. 
 
 However, during the recent study visit of the Committee to Hyderabad on 
8/01/2020 the Committee observed that th e CSR expenditure by most of the CPSEs 
were incurred on construction of toilets, contribution to Swachh Bharat Kosh, 
improving existing drinking water facility, health care facilities etc.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that DPE in co -ordination wit h administrative ministries 
should ensure that the CPSEs are aware of the new provisions in the revised 
guidelines such as theme based activity to be undertaken every year, preference  to be 
given to Aspirational Districts, etc. 
 
 The Committee also recomm ends that the CPSEs should desist from using 
CSR fund to supplement Government schemes as the objective of CSR fund is to 
involve corporates in discharging their social responsibility with their innovative ideas 
and management skills.  The Committee is als o of the considered opinion that the 
CSR expenditure in NER should be given more weightage. 
33 

 The Committee feels that the involvement of local representatives such as the 
area MLA/MLC and MP is of paramount importance in CSR related activities.  The 
Committee, accordingly, recommends that area MLA/MLC and MP should be 
consulted both at the time of selection of CSR projects as well as at their 
implementation stage.  This will go a long way to fulfil the aspirations of residents of 
these areas.
