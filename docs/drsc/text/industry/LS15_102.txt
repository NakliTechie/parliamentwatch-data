CHAPTER–I
MEMORANDUM  OF  UNDERSTANDING  BETWEEN  FERTILIZER  PSUS  AND DEPARTMENT  OF 
FERTILIZERS
1.1       The commitments and mutual responsibilities of the Government and the PSUs are clearly defined in the MoU
System. The targets are finalised at the beginning of the year and so are the commitments of the Department of
Fertilizers. The evaluation of the performance of these targets is carried out only at the end of the year. The focus under
the MoU system hence is said to have shifted from mere input orientation to result orientation. This also envisages a
system granting more autonomy to the PSUs with lesser involvement of the Ministries and Departments in the control of
the day-to-day functioning of the PSUs through laid down procedures etc.
1.2       The Sub-Committee-III on Heavy Industry and Public Enterprises of Department-related Parliamentary Standing
Committee on Industry undertook the study of the MoU System of Madras Fertilizers Limited, Chennai and the
Rashtriya Chemicals and Fertilizers, Mumbai. The Sub-Committee consisted of the following members :

CHAPTER–II
MADRAS  FERTILIZER  LTD. – AN  OVERVIEW
2.1       MFL was incorporated in December, 1966 as a public sector undertaking with authorised share capital of Rs. 14
crores in December, 1966 with Government of India and AMOCO India Inc. (a wholly owned subsidiary of Standard
Oil Company of USA) holding 51% and 49% shares respectively. MFL commenced commercial production in
November, 1971. Subsequently, in 1972 as per the Formation Agreement, National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC - a
multi billion state-owned petro-chemical company of Iran) was inducted as shareholder acquiring 50% of AMOCO
shareholding in MFL.
2.2       In 1985, AMOCO, pursuant to their worldwide strategy, disinvested their shares, which were in turn acquired by
Government of India and NIOC. The current shareholding pattern is as follows :

2.3       MFL is engaged in the manufacture of Ammonia, Urea, complex fertilizers and bio-fertilizers. MFL's activities
include marketing of these fertilizers and trading in Agro-chemicals in accordance with the corporate objective. MFL
has its plant facilities and head quarters located on a sprawling 350 acres at Manali, about 20 kms. north of Chennai

CAPTIVE  FERTILIZER  BERTH  AT  ENNORE  PORT  TERMINAL  (EPT)
2.4       MFL is planning to shift its port operations to the newly developed Ennore Port Terminal from Chennai Port,
considering the long-term benefits for the company and to overcome the present problems of port congestion and port-
to-plant logistics.
2.5       In this backdrop, MFL is planning to set up a Captive Fertilizer Berth as a joint venture project to handle 2
million TPA of Dry bulk and Liquid cargo, made up of 1.5 million MT is dry bulk cargo comprising of Urea, MOP,
DAP and finished grades of fertilizer and 0.5 MT liquid cargo comprising of Phosphoric acid and Liquid ammonia.
Modern handling facilities like Unloading Systems, Bagging Systems and Transit Storage form part of the project.
2.6       MFL is in the process of engaging a consultant to select a suitable Joint Venture Partner, among 12 parties who
have evinced interest to participate in this Captive Fertilizer Berth Project.
CAPTIVE  POWER
2.7       MFL consumes around 130 million unit of electrical power per year drawn from TNEB grid and also from
captive generation. 8 Diesel Generators and 2 Gas Turbines generate 32.7 million units (25%) per year as captive power.
2.8       To tide over frequent power failure, under-frequency and voltage fluctuations of TNEB grid, MFL is seeking
alternate power capacity from outside supply sources other than TNEB.
2.9       MFL had preliminary discussion with Chennai Petroleum Corporation Limited (CPCL) for participation in their
proposed Rs. 2837 crore Independent Power Plant (IPP) Project of 500 MW capacity.
2.10     In the meantime, MFL is holding negotiation with CPCL for wheeling 4 to 5 MW of surplus power from their
existing captive Power Plant facility.
2.11     The company did not pay dividend to its shareholders after 1992-93. It is running in losses. The loss was
contributed by shortfall in production of urea and NPK, reduction in contribution for NPK, higher specific consumption
of raw materials, steep exchange rate variation, monsoon failure resulting in lower sales, higher rebates and discounts
and higher interest cost.
2.12     The company's operations continue to be unprofitable. During April and May 2001, Ammonia plant load had to
be restricted due to high Ammonia inventory levels caused by poor off-take by downstream urea/NPL plants. Similarly,
urea plant was also operated at low levels as off-take of NPK for sale has been poor. The annual turnaround was taken
up on May 28, 2001 with a schedule to complete it on June 22, 2001. However, due to the unexpected developments,
the turnaround was extended and the company could come back to production stream in respect of NH
3 only on August
3, 2001. Keeping in view the advice given by urea reactor manufacturers, the company decided to get the urea reactor
inspected which was not envisaged in the original schedule. The inspection of reactor was a difficult job involving
erection of structure above the reactor with hoisting facility. Though an attempt was made to erect the structure while
the plant was in operation, enough progress could not be made due to unfavourable environmental conditions. After the
shut down of the plants, erection work was taken up and hoists were provided at the top level of about 60 mtrs. height to
life the reactor bundles, each weighing 36 T. Reactor B was inspected on July 18 and after attending to some minor
repairs was boxed up.
2.13     In contrast, the inspection of reactor 'A' revealed extensive damages. A quick assessment was made and
necessary action was initiated by the company to rectify the bundles. Notwithstanding all these constraints, the company
decided to start and run the Ammonia and Urea plants at around 50% load. The quality of Urea being produced now is
relatively better with temperature of the Prill hovering around 50
o Celsius, which are the main objectives of Urea Prill
Tower modifications, aimed and achieved by the company. The repair works to Reactor 'A' involve procurement of
imported materials, extensive welding and other specialized jobs, which has been entrusted on a turnkey basis to the
manufacturer M/s Nooter Fabricators Inc. USA to secure Guarantee Test Run of urea plant.

2.14     The acute water scarcity situation affected the Chennai City subsequent to failure of monsoon. The water supply
to the company has been restricted to 0.33 million gallon per day (MGD) as against the requirement of 2.5 MGD. There
is an apprehension that the present supply could be cut down further. This problem is being tackled by the company by
purchasing water at a lighter rate from Metro Water with a view to sustain production at a maximum possible level.
Thus even if there was no problem in Urea Reactor, Urea production had to be curtailed to 45% due to cut in raw water
supply. On the sales front also, the company had been affected due to adverse agro climatic conditions prevailing in the
entire marketing territory of the company viz. Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka. In Andhra Pradesh and
Karnataka, drought like situation is prevailing and posing threat to company's marketing efforts. In these states, rainfall
has been only 73% of normal and sowing has been only 40%.
FINANCIAL  ASSISTANCE  CUM  CAPITAL  RESTRUCTURING  (FACR)
2.15     MFL has submitted original FACR proposal to Government of India during January 1999. Whereas the proposal
relating to FIs have been approved and implemented by them during April 1999, the proposal submitted to Government
of India has undergone several revisions till date. In view of the losses incurred during 2000-01 and continuing during
the current year 2001-02 MFL has requested for a revised FACR packages from Government of India and other
institutions, which have extended long term/short term assistance. In respect of FIs, the revised proposal inter-alia
included downward revision of interest rate with moratorium on principal and interest payment for 2 years. With regard
to Government of India loans, the proposal provides for waiver of interest amounting to Rs. 71 crores and downward
reduction of interest rate. Similarly, the revised FACR envisages lower interest on the working capital assistance
received from commercial banks. With a view to avoid company becoming sick, the FIs and Government of India are
working out a comprehensive package.
2.16     The Company has been taking up with various policy-making bodies about disadvantageous position in which
the company has been operating with costly feedstock like Naphtha on one hand and high capital-related charged on the
other hand (consequent to implementation of Revamp Project at the cost of around Rs. 601 crores). Accordingly, in
order to reduce the yawning gap between cost of production and realization, company has represented to Tariff
Commission to reckon industry related/MFL specific problems in the areas of :-
                  –  Assessment of capacity utilization and normative capacity utilization
                  –  Consumption norms
                  –  Interest on working capital
                  –  Selling and distribution expenses
                  –  Return on capital employed
                  –  PCS based on cost of bought (imported) urea and Naptha-based captive Ammonia used in NPK production
                  –  Compensation to cover cost of 90 days forward cover for exchange rate variation.
            The above, if considered favourable by the Government, will help the company in recovering certain costs
substantially.

2.18     Having regard to the above observation the Committee makes the following recommendations:
                 1.  There is need for restructuring the MoU for the fertilizers industry PSUs.
                 2.  The paramount factor in such energy intensive industry is to ensure the energy efficiency is
accorded higher weightage.
                 3.  R&D should also be accorded higher weightage than the present weight of 1 and this should split in
to process development, product development like products ensuring higher life cycle for soils etc.
and systems improvement like productivity enhancements, ERP, business process re-engineering,
vendor development online packaging cost efficiencies, etc.
                 4.  Environment aspect should also be accorded higher weightage.
                 5.  HRD element should also accord weight for introduction of quality circles, operation of shop
steward systems, remedial provisions for occupational hazards, etc.
                 6.  Safety parameters weight to be enhanced and restructured to include introduction of fail-safe
operational mechanisms.
                 7.  Extension services like farmer support, farmer education linkages with farmers in product
development should also be accorded appropriate weightage.

RASHTRIYA  CHEMICAL  FERTILIZERS – AN  OVERVIEW
3.1       Rashtriya Chemicals & Fertilizers Ltd. (RCFL) was incorporated on 6th March 1978, as a result of
reorganisation of the erstwhile Fertilizer Corporation of India Ltd. At the time of formation, the company had one unit
viz. Trombay (old plants), and two major projects namely, Trombay IV expansion and Trombay V expansion under
implementation, besides the west, south marketing zones and Bombay Purchase and Liaison Office. RCF was the first
fertilizer company in India to commission a green field Mega fertilizer complex at Thalvaishet in the State of
Maharashtra. The objectives of the company are to carry on business relating to fertilizers, industrial chemicals, heavy
water, by products and derivatives. The company has been awarded Mini Ratna status by the Government of India. The
registered and corporate head offices of CRF are located at Mumbai and two units at Chembur and Thal in Raaigad in
the Maharashtra State.

2.       At the outset the Chairman welcomed the Members and apprised them of agenda for the meeting of the day.
3.       The Committee thereafter, took up Auto Policy for discussion. The Chairman apprised the Members that the
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry had recommended in its 59th Report that the Government should
formulate an Auto Policy in view of the ongoing liberalization of the economy and W.T.O. obligations. In response to
the Committee's recommendations the Department of Heavy Industries in its Action Taken Note has informed the
Committee that Government of India has brought out an Auto Policy on 7th March, 2002.
3.1     The Chairman stated that the Committee on Industry took up the Auto Policy for detailed discussion as the policy
laid down certain recommendations for the growth of the Industry. Since most of these recommendations required
actions by the Ministries other than the Department of Heavy Industries and so far these recommendations had not been
translated into real Government Policy.
3.2.    The Chairman apprised the Members that the Committee had already taken up the matter for discussion and in
this regard the Committee had held two meetings on 24th June and 16th July, 2002. Based on the discussion held on
24th June, 2002 a questionnaire was prepared and replies thereon was obtained from Department of Heavy Industries. In
the meeting held on 16th July, 2002 the Committee had interacted with Automotive Research Association of India
(ARAI); Society of India Automobiles Manufacturers (SIAM); Automotive Tyre Manufacturers Association (ATMA)
and Automotive Component Manufacturers Association (ACMA); Prozon Inc. (manufacture of fuel saver). Apprising
the Members the details of discussion held in these two meetings, the Chairman spelt out the scope for discussion in
Auto Policy which inter-alia  included import of second hand imported cars; lack of testing facilities; foreign direct
