II.         Background Of MoU System
 
2.1       The Department of Public Enterprises in their Background Note on the MoU system have stated that as a major
policy initiative, the MoU system was introduced by the Central Government as an important tool to improve the
performance of the Central Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs).  The New Industrial Policy of 1991 laid greater emphasis
on the MoU system requiring all PSEs to be brought under the ambit of the MoU system.  Beginning with 4 PSEs in
1987-88, the MoU system has now been extended to a large number of PSEs during the course of time and during the
year 2000-2001, 108 PSEs have signed the MoUs (Annexure).
 
2.2       It was stated that the main objectives of the MoU system are to improve the performance of the PSEs by
imparting increased autonomy and accountability and to provide a method of evaluation of the performance of the
managements of the PSEs in an objective and transparent manner.  Further, the objective of providing clarity of goals to
be achieved by PSEs is another dimension of the MoU system.
 
2.3       The Committee has noted that there is a High Power Committee (HPC) at the apex level, headed by the Cabinet
Secretary which is basically a policy making body for the MoUs.  This HPC consists of Secretaries of all nodal
Departments of Government such as Finance, Expenditure, Planning Commission and Programme Implementation, etc. 
The HPC undertakes the evaluation of the performance of the PSEs and in addition the HPC also provides a forum to
resolve inter-ministerial issues.
 
2.4       To assist HPC in this task and also to provide the technical inputs and to finalise the MoUs, there is an Advisory
Body called Adhoc Task Force (ATF), it was stated.  The members of the ATF are drawn from various fields having
vast experience in their areas and include retired Secretaries, Chief Secretaries, top experts in different disciplines like
finance, management, science and technology, and prominent academicians.   This body provides the neutrality and
objectivity to the MoU system.
 
2.5       The Department have also informed that under the present set up there is a small MoU Division under the
Department of Public Enterprises, Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises, which co-ordinates the entire
exercise of finalising the MoUs in respect of all the PSEs numbering 108. In addition to finalising the MoUs, the MoU
Division is also responsible for the evaluation of performance of PSEs, both on the basis of the provisional results as
well as the audited results.
 
2.6       Explaining about the process of preparing MoUs, the Department have stated that the MoU process starts with
the issuing of guidelines by the DPE to the PSEs/Administrative Ministries for drafting the MoUs.  The PSEs prepare
their MoUs on the basis of these guidelines and these draft MoUs are then discussed with the Administrative Ministries
and in the Boards of the PSEs and are then submitted to the DPE.  DPE prepares background papers and its comments
on each draft MoU keeping in view the past performance of the PSE, its future potential, the industry scenario and the
economic environment of the PSE, it was added.  It was further stated that these draft MoUs are discussed in the MoU
negotiation meetings where the ATF members, managements of the PSE, officials from the Administrative Ministries
and the representatives from nodal Government Agencies like Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Statistics and
Programme Implementation and Planning Commission are also present.  The MoUs and the targets are finalised in these
meetings through mutual discussions.

India.  The Company has seven manufacturing units located at Bangalore (two units), Naina, Rae Bareli, Srinagar,

4.2       ITI manufactures a wide range of equipments, which include electronic switching equipments, transmission
equipments, VSAT equipments and telephone instruments of various types. ITI is also providing value added services
like VSAT, MRTS, etc.  The Company has been responsible for building around 70% to 80% of the country’s telecom

4.3       Major customer of Company’s products is Department of Telecommunications.  ITI is also supplying telecom
products to Railways, Defence and Corporate Sectors.ITI has exported its products to  foreign  countries  aMoUnting  to

4.4       ITI Ltd. has been signing MoU since 1991 with the Government of India.  The ITI Ltd. has been
manufacturing/supplying various kinds of equipments to the Telecommunication Sector ranging from telephone sets to
small/medium exchanges, large exchanges, digital radio and optical fibre, etc.  The PSE has gone through different
phases in the past especially after the introduction of economic reforms in the country and it has stabilised its operations
during the recent years.  It has been able to develop a very strong infrastructure for supply of products/technologies in
the field of telecommunications. The increased competition due to globalisation has posed a new threat to the ITI Ltd.,
but at the same time with the fast growth of this sector, new avenues of growth are also available for the Company.  It
can become a major player in the sector through diversification of its activities, improving the quality through
introduction of new technologies and by improving the efficiency.  The performance of the Company under the MoU
system is being evaluated under the major parameters details of which are as under:-
 
 
4.4.1
        Financial Performance
 
            The financial performance in respect of this PSE is evaluated against two parameters namely Gross Margin and
the Ratio of Net Profit to Capital employed.  Both the parameters have been assigned significantly higher weight
ranging between 50% to 60% since 1994-95.  Because of competition, the PSE faced the worst situation in 1995-96
when it incurred an operating loss of Rs.88 crores.  In view of this situation, a very modest target of Gross Margin of
Rs.31 crores and Rs.19 crores was kept for the years 1996-97 and 1997-98.  But the performance was much better than
expected.  From 1998-99 onwards it has stabilised its operations and there has been a steady growth in Company’s gross
margin.  The other financial parameter i.e. ratio of net profit to capital employed also indicates that year 1995-96 was
the worst year for the PSE so far as its performance is concerned.  This ratio was negative during 1995-96 and 1996-97. 
But it recovered in the subsequent years and this ratio improved to the level of 3.01 during 1999-2000.
 
 
4.4.2
        Net Sales
 
            Net sales is another parameter to evaluate the performance of the ITI Ltd. under the MoU system.  The weight
assigned to this parameter has varied from 4% to 10% during the period.  The trend in performance of the PSE under
this parameter has been similar to that  under financial parameters.  Its sales were just of the order of Rs.783 crores
during 1995-96.  Since 1997-98, the PSE has shown a turnaround and has been able to exceed the targets under this
parameter.  The sale during 1999-2000 was of the order of Rs.1824.44 crores.
 
 
4.4.3
        Physical Parameter

under :-
 
a)      Supply of small and medium exchanges (KL) – This has been a performance criteria for the evaluation of the
performance of the PSE under the MoU system except during 1995-96 and 1997-98.  The weight assigned to
this parameter used to be 5% and it has been reduced to 2% since 1996-97.

V.        Bharat Electronics Limited
 
5.1       Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL) was established at Bangalore by the Government of India under the Ministry
of Defence in the year 1954.  Presently, it is the country’s premier electronics manufacturing company having 9
operating units.  The authorised and paid up share capital of the Company as on 31.3.99 were Rs.85 crores and Rs.80
crores respectively.
 
5.2       BEL meets the bulk of the electronic equipment needs of the Indian Defence Services, Paramilitary Forces and
other Government users like All India Radio, Doordarshan, Department of Telecommunication, Police Wireless, Airport
Authority of India, Meteorological Department, Railways etc.  BEL is also the premier source in India for Professional
Electronic Components catering to the needs of a variety of customers in the consumer electronics sector viz. the Radio
and TV manufacturers as well as original equipment manufacturers involved in the manufacture of all types of
electronic equipments.  The Company has a very wide product range and makes over 350 different products.
 
5.3       Around 70% of BEL’s turnover today is from products designed in-house/ indigenously.  BEL is also making
export promotional efforts like appointment of overseas agents, participation in foreign exhibitions etc. BEL is also
concentrating on widening the product market in certain sectors.
 
5.4       BEL is signing MoU since 1990-91 and has a good track record of performance.  Amongst the major objectives,
the Company has set for itself include the generation of internal resources for the growth of the Company to achieve
self-reliance by indigenisation of critical material and components and remain a leader in the technology so far as the
requirement of the defence and other selected fields of professional electronics is concerned through Research &

VIII.     General Discussion
 
8.1       During the examination of MoUs signed by various PSUs with their Administrative Ministries, the Sub-
Committee  noticed that no weightage has been given to inter-PSU cooperation in the MoU.  It was felt that in the new
economic scenario, the collaborative approach amongst the PSUs under various ministries should be evolved without
compromising the competitive spirit and quality.
 
8.2       Besides, these PSUs should be given enough elbow room to adapt themselves in accordance with the new
realities.  They need an impetus for sustaining their work order position.  This could be addressed through inter-PSU
collaborative approach.  In this approach it is imperative that the PSUs and State Departments who enjoy client status

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS AT A GLANCE
 
After considering all the issues that have arisen out of the examination of MoU System by the Sub-Committee,
the Committee is emphatic that Government should explore the ways and means for  bringing the element of inter-PSU
cooperation in the MoU system.
 
            The Committee further suggests that:
1.         In the case of domestic projects, where a consortium approach is called for, and one of the telecom PSUs is the
front end, it can invite, in the first instance, other concerned telecom PSUs to be consortium partners, on the basis of
right of first refusal.
 
2.         In the case of foreign contracts where TCIL is the front end and it is to either arrange for consortium partners or
arrange for suppliers, it can invite, in the first instance, the concerned telecom PSUs to participate on the basis of right
of first refusal.
 
3.         With reference to various supplies and contracts, there is an increasing emphasis on suppliers or buyers credit at
competitive rates.  In this context, the Exim Bank be persuaded to review its policies and procedures so that its terms
and conditions, including rates of interest are in line with the competitive best available in the International market.
 
4.         There have been persistent efforts by various Departments with the Finance Ministry to correct the duty
structure.  With the liberalisation, the duties on the finished goods have come down faster than the duties on the raw
materials in electronic and information technology.  If the duty structure is not corrected, the people will find import of
manufactured goods cheaper.  There should be a margin for conversion cost between the finished goods and the raw
materials.  The committee, therefore, suggests that the duty for finished goods should not go down than what is required.
 

46th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/46threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:12:24]
5.         The Sub-Committee in its deliberations gathered that there are instructions that for import purpose services of
agents are to be avoided.  But if one wants to sell something abroad, he needs an agent.  So, while PSUs have been told
to attune themselves with time, terms and conditions are not so dynamic as to make the agenda really interesting in
selling their goods abroad.  The Committee, therefore, suggests that Government should look into this aspect as well. 
Besides, our Missions abroad should have some sort of trade desk to explore the possibilities of having trade etc. with
the foreign countries.
 
6.         Last but not the least, under the present scenario of stiff competition from private and global companies, the
DPE should strengthen the MoU system specially where issues involving inter-ministerial/ inter PSU co-ordination are
concerned. The DPE should play a pro-active role in making the PSUs competitive and strengthening them for sustained
growth.
 
7.         The MoU system has undeniably made a positive contribution to improve the performance of the PSEs.  The
management of PSEs were appreciative of the fact that the MoU system has, for the first time brought about clarity of
the goals to be achieved on the basis of precise targets laid down in the MoU ensuring accountability on an objective
basis.  They were also enthusiastic of the larger delegation of powers available to the MoU signing Companies.  The
managements of the PSEs could act freely to a great extent with clear-cut objectives and goals.  Especially in their day-
to-day operations, the interference by the Ministries had reduced to a considerable extent. This has provided an
opportunity to the managements to take quick decisions and furthermore, evaluation of their performance is now being
done in a very objective and transparent basis.
 
8.         The Committee also feels that through the MoU system, an effort has been made to synthesise different
objectives.  Under the MoU system the parameters selected as performance indicators are a good mix of financial,
dynamic and efficiency indicators.  Thus, it is not only the financial parameters  which are given importance but other
dynamic and efficiency parameters  which are very essential for the future growth of the PSEs, and which are also
incorporated as performance indicators.  Thus, under the present system an attempt has been made to strike a balance
between financial and non-financial parameters.  The Committee is of the firm view that the MoU system has produced
reasonable results as it is understood that during the negotiation meetings an effort is made to set targets, which
represent an improvement over the level of performance in the previous year, and the system has significantly
contributed to a progressive increase in the performance of PSEs.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the MoU
system should continue on a long-term basis.
 
9.         Recently, the Public Sector Enterprises have been exposed to domestic and international competition.  Most of
them were operating in a totally protective environment.  With the policy of liberalisation and globalisation, the PSEs
have to compete in the market for obtaining orders both in products and services.  For a PSE to be competitive it is
essential to have an edge over its competitors, both in cost and quality.  In such a situation, the Committee feels that  up
gradation of technology including modernisation, up gradation of skills of employees and rationalisation of manpower
etc. are key factors to generate necessary competitive strength to PSEs.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
MoU system should be used as an effective instrument to bring about the change in the working environment of the
PSEs by introducing specific strategies through it to make them more competitive.
 
10.       The Committee observes that the role of the Ministries in the target setting exercise is very crucial but the work
relating to MoU system has not got the required attention of some of the Ministries due to various reasons.  The
concerned Ministries, therefore, need to play a greater role in the finalisation of MoU targets.
 
11.       Though the parameters such as preparation and implementation of corporate plan and devising a strategic plan or
vision plan, etc. are being mentioned in the MoU documents, the targets set in the MoUs do not often correspond to the
corporate vision/plans drawn up by the PSEs.  The Committee, therefore, stresses that there should be a better linkage
between the corporate plans of the PSEs and the MoUs.
 
12.       The Committee is constrained to note that the MoU preparation has generally been an annual target setting
exercise.  The Committee feels that the MoU system should focus on strategic issues rather than be a mere annual target
setting exercise.  For that purpose the Administrative Ministries should provide adequate input/support to enable PSEs
to develop future strategies in association with the Ministries.  The parameters under the MoU need to be devised
depending upon the strategy for its future growth in the particular area of its operations.  The performance of the PSEs
needs to be reviewed on the basis of parameters so devised.  This would give a clear picture as to how a particular PSE
has been able to make its products/services cost effective and how they compare with their counter parts in the private
sector.  Such an approach would certainly make the PSEs not only competitive but also provide an opportunity to
become global players.  Therefore, the Committee suggests that the approach under the MoU system needs to be
reviewed in line with the existing competitive economic environment.

file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/46threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:12:24]
 
13.       As PSEs have to operate in a free market, they cannot continue to depend upon Government’s assistance on
matters such as obtaining orders, etc.  In the present situation, negotiating for orders is a managerial function and,
therefore, the Government’s  intervention should be very selective, if at all.  For instance, a number of the PSEs are
working in the same area but they are under the control of different administrative ministries.  In some cases, these PSEs
operate as competitors to each other.  Similarly, PSEs are also involved in research and development projects and some
of the PSEs are spending quite a significant aMoUnt on R&D activities.  Unfortunately, these PSEs are working in
isolation and there is no coordination to provide synergy in R&D, marketing, etc. to enhance their competitive edge over
their counter partners in the private sectors.  Since these PSEs are functioning in watertight compartments, they are not
able to reap the maximum benefits of their investments on R&D and up gradation of technology.  Separate and isolated
efforts on the part of the PSEs in such areas only result in addition to their costs.  Therefore, there is tremendous scope
in bringing together PSEs operating in the same discipline, but under different Ministries, to optimise investments in
marketing, R&D etc. in a collective and coordinated manner.  The Committee, therefore, feels that the Department of
Public Enterprises operating through the MoU system appears to be an appropriate forum where the issues involving
inter-ministries/inter-PSEs coordination can be taken up more effectively and DPE as a nodal Department should play a
more pro-active role in making the PSEs competitive and strengthening them for sustained growth.
 
14.       On the question of reduction of budgetary support to Department of Public Enterprises and its consequent
winding up in pursuance of recommendation of Expenditure Reforms Commission (EFC), it has not considered the
essentially vital role played by the  Department of Public Enterprises in making the Memoranda of Understanding which
the Government of India enters with its Public Sector Units on the annual basis.  The Department is merely not
facilitating but enabling the MoU to be an important tool for performance assessment mutates mutandis to that of Profit
Center Concepts of large Conglomerates in developed countries.  The MoU is an effective and objective tool today only
because of it being facilitated by a non-stakeholder that is Department of Public Enterprises.  Hence, the Committee
strongly feels that the Department of  Public Enterprises should be strengthened with budgetary support. 
 
15.       In the course of evaluation study of several MoUs  the Sub Committee also noted that many of the Public
Sectors Enterprises  are working in isolation.  A better synergetic approach with each Central Public Sector Enterprises
working in closer cooperation with other Public Sectors Enterprises will enable many of the Sick PSEs to come out of
the woods.  The Sub Committee felt that had any such mechanism been in existence the sickness in many Public Sector 
Enterprises could have been avoided especially in respect of those where the sickness can be attributed to lack of 
orders.  Moreover, a synergetic effort must be made in terms of joint bidding in respect of international offers, joint
effort to enable pre-qualification by giving them better access to larger market, cost competitiveness and also a joint
strategy in tackling sickness.  Hence the Committee strongly recommends that an Inter Public Sector Enterprises
Coordination Cell should also be established in the Department of Public Enterprises for providing a mechanism for
better  and cohesive cooperation amongst the Units Central Public Sector Enterprises.
 
 
16.       The Sub Committee during the course of its study of MoUs of many Public Sector Undertakings like Bharat
Electronics Limited, ITI Limited, HTL Limited, TCIL Limited, etc., observed that the products of these units are of high
quality and excellent potential exist for export  of these products.  However, there  is a need to facilitate this export
opportunity through better coordination, contract specific joint ventures etc.  The Department of Public Enterprises
could also be vested with this function of promoting  exports from these Central Public Sectors Enterprises.  The
Committee, therefore, recommends  for setting up of an Export Promotion Cell for Central Public Sectors in the
Department of Public Enterprises.
 
17.       The Committee observes that the strength of the MoU system is that the targets are finalised and the
performance of the PSE is evaluated in an objective and transparent manner that is ensured by the DPE and ATF.  Thus,
in order to maintain and further instil objectivity, the Committee feels that DPE should play a more active role in the
process and the MoU Division should be strengthened so that they can carry out bench marking studies, performance
related studies and other associated work so that the targets given to the PSEs are more realistic.
 
18.       The Committee is, however, constrained to note that the MoU Division of the Department of  Public Enterprises
has been proposed to be abolished.  The Secretary, Ministry of Heavy Industry and Public Enterprises, during the course
of his oral evidence before the Committee in connection with the Demands for Grants, informed the Committee that the
Expenditure Reforms Commission has recommended that the Department of Public Enterprises may be down-sized .  It
was proposed that  the  MoU System  may be transferred to the respective Administrative Ministries of the concerned
PSEs. The Committee, however, does not agree with this.   The Committee feels that the Department of Public
Enterprises provides a neutral forum for finalising MoUs  which is essential to make it an objective systems.  It is also
ensured that the entire MoU exercise is carried out in an objective, transparent and broad based manner.  The

46th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/46threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:12:24]
Committee, therefore, recommends that instead of transferring the MoU System to the concerned Administrative
Departments, it may be retained with the Department of Public Enterprises. In fact the MoU Division needs to be further
strengthened for an effective implementation of the MoU System.

2.         The Convenor welcomed the Members of Sub-Committee-III and Secretaries  and Senior Officials of  the
Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises,  Ministry of Defence, Department of Telecommunications (Ministry
of Communications)  and Bharat Electronics Ltd.  At the outset, the  Convenor briefed the officials about the purpose of
the meeting.   He stated that  as a result of globalisation and privatisation some new problems have come up . Infact , 
there is a need for cooperation amongst various  Telecom. PSUs keeping in view the changed environment in a
libralised and competitive era. By mutual cooperation and interaction  the Central Public Sector Undertakings   would
be in a position to  compete  with the  private sector  and multinationals without  losing competitiveness , quality of
products and price.  He stated that in order to improve their performance and efficiency , the Telecom. PSUs should be
given  more freedom and liberty so that they can take  importance decisions independently. Further, the Convenor
authorized the Director, Shri Tapas Das Gupta, to spell out broad outlines  of   the deliberations which the Sub-
Committee had with the CMDs  of Telecom. Sector PSUs in a meeting held on the  6 th December, 2000 in respect of  
inter-PSU cooperation. Thereafter, he requested the Secretary, Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises to
present his view points   on this  issue.  
 
3.         Shri Ravindra Gupta, Secretary, Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises emphasized the need  to
involve  the Government departments  like Ministry of Information  Technology  in the discussion.  He also narrated 
how the lack of coordination has resulted  in  duplicity  involving parallel  expenditure.  He also   stated that  there is a
need  to rationalize duty structure.  He said that if the duty structure is not corrected the customers will start importing
the finished goods. In such a situation the smaller companies cannot survive before the big multinationals.

4.         Shri Subir Dutta, Secretary, Department of Defence Production and Supplies (Ministry of Defence) informed
that there are 39 Ordnance Factories and 8 Defence Public Sector Undertakings  including   BEL. So far as telecom
sector is concerned it is only BEL which supplies telecom goods. Even within the company,  7 to 8%  of the total
production is accounted by the telecom goods whose total indigenous production is around 2.5%. Therefore, BEL is
smaller player in the telecom.  sector.  Secondly, the DRDO does not carry out any R&D works in the telecom. sector.
Since BEL is a smaller player, it would  like as much of orders and concessions as possible from the other telecom
PSUs  to make inroads in the sector.  He     further    informed     the    Committee   that a council of research and
development (R&D) was set up in 1995 by the Department of Telecommunications which was headed by the Secretary,
DoT, on which various concerned Ministries and the CMDs of all concerned PSUs were represented. At this forum,
which meets every six months, the problems of telecom PSUs relating to R&D, etc., are put up.  Shri Dutta, agreed to
offer his cooperation and collaboration on R&D and other issues to the other telecom PSUs. 
 
5.         Thereafter, the Convenor requested  Shri Shyamal Ghosh, Secretary, Department of Telecommunications
(Ministry of Communication), to offer his view point on this issue  and asked  for  cooperation   and   collaboration  to  
the   telecom  PSUs without  sacrificing   quality, performance and efficiency. Shri Ghosh explained that India is the 9
th
largest network in the world in terms of   subscribers including  28 million subscribers,  a little over three million
cellular subscribers and  28 thousand   digital electronic exchanges.    In spite of  such a large network, our tele-density
is only 3%  which is  one of the lowest  in the world.    He  informed the Committee that DoT is already giving about 25
to 30%  orders  to the Telecom. PSUs.   In addition  to that,  they are also giving  these PSUs advances  to meet this
orders  to the tune of 50% .  He stated that in the telecom. sector things are changing rapidly  due to  the change in 
R&D and technology. He stated that it is in this context that they we would have to look for cooperation amongst the
telecom PSUs. He informed that Central of Development of Telematics (C-DoT) is a major player in respect of R&D
and technological innovations in the telecom. Sector. BEL is supplying 8 to 10% telecom. goods, HCL is supplying
optical fibers and  TCIL have a very sound system of consultation in the telecom. facilities. Keeping into account the
diverse activities of the telecom. PSUs, he welcomed the initiatives that the Sub-Committee had taken in developing
synergies amongst the PSUs under the Telecom. Sector. He informed the Committee that they are in the process of 
formulating their synergies for export marketing.
 
6.         The Convenor, thereafter, thanked the Secretaries  of  the Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises, 
Ministry of Defence, Department of Telecommunications (Ministry of Communications)  and  appreciated  the
suggestions and deliberations given by them  in the  meeting .
 
A verbatim record of the proceedings of the meeting was kept.
 
7.         The Sub-Committee then adjourned at 4.30 P.M..
