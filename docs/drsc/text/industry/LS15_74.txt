REPORT 
 
1          Agro and Rural Industries play an important role in the economy of our country. This sector  has vast potential in
terms of employment and dispersal of industries in rural areas. In view of its growing importance, the Ministry of Agro
& Rural Industries  was carved out of the erstwhile Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Agro & Rural Industries  
w.e.f. 1st September, 2001. The main function of the Ministry is to formulate policy measures for the growth of rural
industries for achieving rapid increase in gainful employment and balanced regional growth  which are among the main
objectives underlying the strategy of industrialization in the country. It also supervises and coordinates the activities of
different promotional agencies operating at the Central and State Level.
1.1       The various policies, programmes/schemes related to agro and rural industries are implemented through various 
organizations under the Ministry, namely, Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), Coir Board and Small
Industries Development Organisation (SIDO) etc.
1.2      The Committee welcomes the formation of the new Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries. It has satisfied the 
long-felt need for the creation of   a new Ministry which would  look  exclusively  after the needs of this sector. This is
expected to  give a boost to the sector,  especially when our  industries  now face competition  from global companies 
and find it hard to survive.
1.3       Due to  the creation of the new Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries,  Demands for Grants for the Agro and
Rural Sector under  the new Ministry  have been dealt with  separately  in the budget for the year 2002-03. Earlier  , they
used to be the  part of the   budget allocation for the erstwhile Ministry of Small Scale Industries & Agro and Rural
Industries.

            KHADI AND VILLAGE INDUSTRIES  COMMISSION (KVIC)
 
3          The Khadi and village Industries Commission (KVIC)  is a statutory organization established in 1957 by an Act
of  Parliament. It plays a pivotal role in the strengthening of the rural economy by promoting and developing Khadi and
Village Industries. The objectives of the KVIC include skill improvement, employment generation in rural areas,
transfer of technology, rural industrialization and promoting self-reliance among the people, and to build up a strong
rural community base.
3.1       The functions of the KVIC are to plan, promote, organize and assist in implementation of the programmes for
the development of khadi and village industries. To achieve this, it undertakes :-
a)      Financing of eligible units and institutes.
b)      Training of  persons employed or desirous of  seeking employment in Khadi and Village industries and training
of personnel.
c)      Building  reserves of raw materials and implements, and supplying them at reasonable rates.
d)      Taking up R&D in Khadi and Village industry products.
e)      Encouraging cooperative efforts among   persons engaged in khadi and village industries
f)        The KVIC operates through 30 State/ UT KVI Boards, 5,599 registered institutions and 30,138 cooperatives,
188 departmental units and 15,441 sales outlets.
3.2       The Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry in its 49 th Report on Demands for
Grants (2001-02) pertaining to Ministry of Small Scale Industries and Agro & Rural Industries had expressed its
concern at  the inordinate delay in reconstituting  the Khadi and Village Industries Commission. The term of the
previous Khadi and Village Industries Commission expired in September, 1999 and since then the Commission had
been non-existent which adversely  affected the working of Khadi and Village Industries.  The Committee has been 
informed  that the Commission has been reconstituted w.e.f. 2
nd October, 2001.  The Committee welcomes the
reconstitution of the Commission and hopes that the new Commission will be able to take care of the requirements of 
the  Khadi sector to make it  self  sustaining and  vibrant.
3.3       The share of production generated under KVI programme, in the total traditional industrial activities of the
country like handloom, handicraft, sericulture etc., comes to around 7 percent. In the overall SSI sector,  the same is
about 1 percent.
3.4       The main objective of  creating KVIC was to generate employment in the rural areas through the artisans
working in cottage industries. KVI activities are providing a large number of employment to SC/STs and women. At
present,  32%and 46% of total KVI employment is from  SC/STs and women artisans,  respectively.
3.5      Keeping in view the importance of  rural industries sector for generating new opportunities of productive self-
employment the Hon’ble Finance Minister in his Budget speech to  2002-03  announced  that :-
“The promotion of rural industrialization would be helped greatly through capacity building and technology upgradation
in Khadi and Village Industries. To help in this effort, I propose to upgrade the Wardha Institute started by Mahatama
Gandhi in 1935 as a National Institute to be called Mahatama Gandhi Institute of  Rural Industrialisation.”
3.6       The Committee has been informed that  with a view to upgrade the Khadi and Village Industries Commission’s 
R&D Centre at Wardha i.e. Jamnalal Bajaj Centre Research Institute (JBCRI),  the KVIC has entered into a
Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Delhi with an investment of Rs.
8.35 crores, over a period of 3 years.
3.7       So far as the execution of work is concerned the IIT, Delhi has already started implementation of the DPR
(Detailed Project Report) as per the implementation schedule submitted to the Government.  Workshops on Quality
Assurance, Organic  and Herbal Foods, Rural Crafts and Pottery & Ceramics have been organized at different places.
Orientation Course for KVIC employees has also been conducted at the campus of JBCRI, Wardha.

3.8       The process of deployment of manpower for the execution of work has also started and a Co-ordinator for the

extension and training.  The institute is also expected to prescribe quality standards for rural products and enable them

3.9      The Committee welcomes this move as it aught to provide a great fillip to  the sector. Simultaneously,  the
Committee notes that there is no specific provision in the Budget  for the required funds to implement  the 
announcement of upgrading the Wardha Institute  as a National Institute  named after Mahatama Gandhi. The

3.10    The Committee observes that R&D is the need of the hour if industries are to compete globally and growth in the
sector is to be stimulated. The Committee, therefore, recommends that allocation for Research and Development for
KVIC  requires  be increased.  If need be,  joint collaboration with foreign firms may be taken up.
3.11     Another important announcement of budget-2002-03 is regarding  Jai Prakash Rozgar Guarantee Yojana
(JPRGY). As a special concern for disadvantaged people in the most distressed districts of the country,  the Finance
Minister  announced that :-
“A Task Force headed by the Minister of Rural Development will be setup to design and implement a
massive programme for employment generation in these districts. KVIC, DC(SSI) and other agencies
will be fully involved in the implementation of this scheme.”
3.12    While appreciating this initiative,   the  Committee expresses its  concern that JPRGY should not meet the fate of
NPRI (National Programme for Rural Industrialisation) as announced in the Budget  for the financial year 1999-2000.
The allocation of Rs. 40 lakhs was kept for the NPRI  scheme in the B. E. 2001-02 which was reduced to nominal
amount of Rs. 1.00 lakh in the R. E. of that  year.  The scheme is yet to take off.  However, in the B. E. 2002-03 , a
provision of Rs 90 lakhs has been made. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon  the Ministry to review the progress
of  NPRI  so that the earlier  mistakes are not repeated by the administration.
3.13    The Committee is also unhappy to note that there is no concrete progress at all on the front of “Task Force on
Employment Opportunities” constituted in 1998. The Committee hopes that the Report of “Special Group on 50 million
new job opportunities” will be given due importance and properly implemented during the  Tenth Five Year Plan
period.
3.14     KVI Sector plays a very important role in the Indian economy. However, the recent lack of growth in  the Khadi
industry is a matter of serious concern,  especially in the light of declining production, sales, rural employment
opportunities and the share of Khadi in the total business of the KVIC. This assumes special significance as population
and per capita consumption of cloth in our country has increased over the years while per capita usage of Khadi has not
improved. One of the reasons for this could be that the khadi sector has not changed its products or even the product mix
in order to meet changing consumer expectations and preferences. It has, thus, not been able to exploit the full potential
of Khadi as a product category in all its forms.
3.15     The Committees feels   that for increasing sales of Khadi and providing greater and better rural employment
opportunities in this sector, it is necessary to provide and offer products that match consumer preferences.

3.21     The Committee welcomes  the brand promotion by KVIC like `Khadi` and `Sarvodaya` The Committee observes
that  although  brand building  has traditionally been the   forte of  consumer products,  high technology companies too
have resorted to aggressive brand building especially the technology  sector like Microsoft, etc. Advances in 
technology, specifically,  the internet, will give further impetus to the creation of  global brands.
3.22     The Committee, therefore, recommends that KVIC should find out  (albeit through research)  the  products that
hold promise of creating value to the customers and should have  focused projection of these brands for better marketing
of KVI products.   The KVIC should propagate these  to reach out to the various customers  throughout the entire
country. The Committee hopes that brand name to various products under KVI sector will bring required  changes in the
marketing area of KVI products in the near  future. Further more,  to make their  produce acceptable, the KVIC should
improve and upgrade packaging particularly bottled products.

3.24     The Committee, therefore, recommends that KVIC should  take steps to generate internal resources on its own so

3.25    As against a provision of Rs.106.30 crores in B.E. and R.E. 2001-02  for Khadi Industries, an allocation of

Khadi Grant & Rs. 1.35 crore under Khadi S&T).  In case there is improvement in expenditure, enhancement will be
requested at RE stage. 
3.26     Additional Secretary and Financial Advisor of the Ministry informed that “there were circumstances under
which the interest accrued  out of allocation for KVIC  was utilized for meeting  administrative expenditure. The present
sum has been because of the previous years, it has been built up over a period of time. This has not impacted on the
outlay for the current year, i.e., 2002-03. In fact, there has been an increase. This balance has been built up in the past
partly on account of the fact that adequate provision was not made for the administrative expenses of the KVIC. Hence,
there was a tendency on the part of the KVIC to hold back some part of the releases and use the interest earned out of
that for its administrative expenses. I would like to submit that we have now made structural rectification and now we
have  provided for the administrative expenses”.
3.27     The Committee views with concern that KVIC had   an unspent balance of Rs.147.51 crore   as on 31
st March,
2001. This is not a healthy trend as non-utilisation of money   earmarked for a specific  purpose does not   augur well for
the premier institution. The money should have been put to  better use.
3.28     The Committee recommends that in  cases where lapses had been continuing over the years,  responsibility
should be fixed and better budget management  undertaken. This would be a major step forward.   The Committee
recommends further that expenditure frame-work needs to be strengthened. This would involve identifying current and
future financial requirements, and deviations from the prescribed procedure should not be allowed under any
circumstances.  
3.29     Asked about increase in Non-plan allocation of Rs.50.57 crores to Rs.55.00 crores of KVIC, the Ministry has
stated that  KVIC has a total strength of  200 Officers and 2712  Non-gazetted staff spread all over India covering
Central Office, Mumbai, 36 State Offices/ Regional Offices and other Departmental Centres, like Training Centres,
S&T Centres, etc.  In addition, there are about 3154 pensioners under KVIC. The actual expenditure to meet the
committed expenditure of pay, allowances and pensions etc., has been Rs. 57.25 crores for the year 2001-02, out of
which Rs. 14.25 crores was for pensions. The estimated requirement for the next financial year 2002-03 towards
meeting the committed expenditure is Rs. 59.45 crores against which an allocation of Rs.55.00 crores has been made. 
Additional funds will be provided for at RE stage.
3.30     The   Committee finds that non-plan allocation for Khadi and Village Industries  shows an increasing trend. It is
also not managed efficiently  while KVIC is  over-staffed. The  Committee recommends that KVIC should undertake
trimming exercise for reducing surplus staff by extending VRS in order to curtail non-plan expenditure and to make the
institution economically viable.
3.31     The Committee has been informed that in view of the changes in  the new economic scenario, it was found
necessary to strengthen the KVIC structurally to enable it to face the challenges of globalisation. Under these

3.33     It appears from  the functioning of KVIC that it  is inefficient,  mismanaged  and over staffed. For example, even
in the last year, the inventory lying at various out-lets was to the tune of Rs.700 crores. The stocks have  piled up,
money is blocked and  sale has come to a stand still.  When asked to comment on this, Secretary M/o A&RI stated that 
that  KVIC  wanted  to dispose of this inventory by giving more and more discounts or through some other method to
generate cash. The Secretary further submitted that there are stocks worth of Rs.600 or Rs.700 crores and he had asked
the CEO of KVIC to give complete details as to  the original value of inventory, the amount of godown charges,
administrative charge, the cost of stock, since how long is it lying, etc. He assured that he would  intervene  and take the
matter  up with the people concerned and take steps to see that the dead stock is  cleared.
3.34     Regarding  the unsold stock, KVIC Chairman stated that inventory had come down by Rs.100 crores or so and
right now, it is Rs.650 crores., this was  spread over 7001 outlets; per outlet share came to Rs.10 lakhs. Stock
requirement for the outlets is Rs.350 crores worth goods, but KVIC had an excess stock of Rs.350 crores more. He was
taking steps with the package given. The Government had given  permission to sell this extra stock at heavy discount
and he would  go ahead with it.
3.35     The Committee expresses serious concern that the stocks of goods worth several hundred crores are lying locked
and unused.  Perhaps this is why, KVIC  has been experiencing working capital difficulties. If this state of affairs 
continues, KVIC  will not only be faced with resource crunch as the money remains locked up and stocks lying unsold
but also lose customer base as there will be no takers for the old stocks.
3.36     The Committee therefore  recommends that KVIC should dispose of the old stock as early as possible even by 
providing heavy discounts and recover money. KVIC may utilize the revenue earned on fresh inventory and recycle the
money. KVIC should keep fresh inventory as  old inventory  would not fetch much money.
3.37    The Committee observes that poor quality and poor designs have resulted in poor sales and accumulation of 
large inventories.The working cycle of production sales-cash-realization for Khadi is long and substantial funds are
blocked as working capital  for Khadi institutions. This has adversely affected performance.
3.38     The KVIC  set   up Central Cotton Purchase Committee (CCPC) for purchase of cotton. The CCPC in turn
constituted Regional Cotton Purchase Committees for actual procurement of cotton at the regional level.  The agreed
programme of the institutions approved by the Standing Finance Committee of KVIC, was the guiding factor in
deciding upon the quantity of the cotton to be procured in a particular year. Though the system was good it was perhaps,
the lack of periodical review by the CCPC that did not take care of the stock build up before approving the quantity to
be purchased in the following years.
3.39     In order to correct the distortion, the KVIC  has already taken corrective action by rationalizing the Cotton
Purchase Policy for year 1999-2000 and also issued the notices to defaulting institutions to pay the penal interest.
3.40     Against non lifting of cotton, KVIC  has charged the penal interest @ 24% per annum and recovery notices have
been issued to 151 institutions spread over 13 States. In addition, the penal interest to the tune of Rs.1,08,993/- has
already been recovered  in installments from rebate outgo or  any other financial outgo.
3.41     The Committee, therefore, recommends that  CCPC  should henceforth undertake  periodical review of the stock
build up before approving the quantities to be purchased. Further, responsibility for the lapses for allocating of funds
and  excess  stocking of cotton on the part of officials/non-officials should be fixed.
3.42     The Committee recommendsthat the following policy support measures should be extended to Khadi and Village
Industries to give it  further boost :-

(i)            Most of the states have given sales tax exemption to KVI products,  however, Value added-tax is to
come into force w.e.f. 1.4.2003.  KVI Sector should be exempted from value added tax, octroi, entry tax etc.
(ii)     Existing labour laws, by and large exit to safeguard the interest of organized labour in large and medium
industries. High Power Committee headed by the then Prime Minister Shri Narasimha Rao recommended
exemption of KVI sector from the existing labour laws as artisans are mostly self-employed. A policy
decision  in the matter should be taken at the earliest.
(iii)    Financial institutions have started playing important role from mid 1990s after introduction of REGP. But
still now the investment is very low compared to other areas in priority sector. Special earmarking for KVI
sector is felt necessary.
(iv)    Preferential purchase support as is available to certain sectors is not there for KVI sector. The policy
decision for state and central governments to purchase a minimum percentage of utility articles from KVI
sector will give boost to the sector.

5          Major activities undertaken by the Coir Board for the development of the Coir Industry are as follows :-
(a)
                Undertaking scientific, technological and economic research and development activities.
(b)               Collection of   statistics relating to exports and internal consumption of Coir and Coir products.
(c)                Development   of new  products and designs.
(d)               Publicity for promotion  of exports and internal sales.
(e)                Marketing of coir and coir products in India and abroad.
(f)                 Preventing unfair competition among producers and exporters.
(g)                Assisting in the setting up of units for the manufacture of products.
(h)                Promoting cooperative organizations among producers of husks, coir fibre, coir yarn and manufacturers
of coir products.
(i)                  Ensuring remunerative returns to producers and manufacturers.
 
5.1       India is the world’s largest producer of coir. The coir industry is labour intensive and provide employment to
more than five lakh persons of which women constitute 80 per cent. The production of coir and coir products has been
rising continuously. The production of coir fibre has risen from 2,96,000 metric tones in 1997-98 to 3,64,000 metric
tones in 1999-2000. The export of coir and coir products has also been increasing from Rs.238.93 crores in the year
1997-98 to Rs.313 crores in 2000-01. Though India is the largest producer of coir, its contribution in world trade is
small due to low technology. Only 30% of the coconut husk is utilized for coir production. Coir Board is taking a
number of steps to improve productivity, export potential and better utilization of the raw material. These are :-
·
  Significant Research and Development activities in areas like Coir Geotextiles for soil and water conservation
and Coirret for speeding up the retting process.
·  Blended fibres are used for more value added products and a wider product range. MoU signed with NID,
Ahmedabad for collaborative research on design and product development  which includes blending of coir with
other natural fibres like sisal, jute, palmirah, banana, pineapple etc.
·  Coir Board is providing increasing support for brown fibre activities. White fibre is restricted to Kerala only
while brown fibre is available in all the other coir producing states.
·  Coir board is promoting increased   mechanization for higher productivity.
·  UNDP project for technology   transfer, modernization and capacity building at a cost of US $ 846,000.
·  Export promotion    activities   like participation  in fairs and festivals are taken up by the Coir Board.
5.2       A new Market Development Assistance (MDA) scheme has been introduced in Coir Sector. This scheme has
replaced the existing "Rebate Scheme" and provides financial assistance @ 10 per cent to the Apex Cooperative

5.3       Coir Industry was traditionally confined to Kerala due to the abundant availability of raw-material and natural
facilities for retting. It is a labour intensive, export oriented cottage industry employing 5.43  lakh people. The rapid

the coir industry spread to non-traditional areas like Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and  Orissa and  West

TINY AND MICRO ENTERPRISES
9          Tiny enterprises are an important component of the SSI sector whose relative share in village and small
industries is increasing. The sector is important since it utilizes local raw material, provides employment to local
manpower and caters broadly to local market. This sector however, suffers from weak financial structure, use of
traditional and obsolete technology resulting into poor quality goods and having no access to the economies of scale.
These are basically one man shows and lack modern and efficient entrepreneurship.
9.1       The tiny sector enjoys distinct advantage of its ability to utilize local resource and skill. They cater to local needs
and generate substantial employment opportunities. However, they suffer from certain inherent weaknesses :-
(i)
                  Very poor capital base and difficulty in raising institutional credit
(ii)                Lack of infrastructure facilities particularly power
(iii)               Inadequate access to appropriate technology
(iv)              Problems in marketing the produce on account of quality, cost and inability to adopt    the required
marketing techniques
(v)                Inadequate competitive abilities
(vi)              Inability to cope up with plethora of laws, rules and regulations
(vii)      Lack of entrepreneurial skills and training
9.2       The Committee observes that tiny and micro enterprises are  the most  vulnerable sector in the era of free
imports and intense  competition.  The sector is most threatened  in the face of challenges thrown from China and cheap
imports from Nepal. Office of Development Commissioner (Small Scale Industries) should see that concern of tiny
sector are addressed  adequately and optimum assistance may be provided to them to tide over hurdles faced by them
like poor capital base, lack of infrastructure, obsolete technology marketing and competitiveness etc.  In view of the
importance of this sector, appropriate measures for promotion of tiny sector by the Govt. may be taken by reorienting its
policies.

of  NPRI  so that the earlier  mistakes are not repeated by the administration.

            The Committee is also unhappy to note that there is no concrete progress at all on the front of “Task Force on
Employment Opportunities” constituted in 1998. The Committee hopes that the Report of “Special Group on 50 million

period.

opportunities in this sector, it is necessary to provide and offer products that match consumer preferences.
(Para 3.15)

concessions offered    in the Khadi Package to it.
(Para 3.20)

that  although  brand building  has traditionally been the   forte of  consumer products,  high technology companies too

technology, specifically,  the internet, will give further impetus to the creation of  global brands.
(Para 3.21)
 
            The Committee, therefore, recommends that KVIC should find out  (albeit through research)  the  products that
hold promise of creating value to the customers and should have  focused projection of these brands for better marketing
of KVI products.   The KVIC should propagate these  to reach out to the various customers  throughout the entire
country. The Committee hopes that brand name to various products under KVI sector will bring required  changes in the
marketing area of KVI products in the near  future. Further more,  to make their  produce acceptable, the KVIC should
improve and upgrade packaging particularly bottled products.

            The Committee finds that target under PMRY set by the Ministry has remained static during the last four years
as shown above.  The Committee had hoped that with the creation of  new Ministry, the target and allocation for the

scheme for employment generation,  target  for PMRY should be increased at the RE  stage and consequently the
allocation may be enhanced for the purpose.
(Para 6.7)
 
            The Committee observes that the crux of the problem lies in the recovery scenario under PMRY which has been
showing a  decreasing trend   If this sort of thing continues,  there will be less  funds available year by year  to
channelise for future schemes as the money will be in short supply. A stage  may come  that the  scheme may have to be
closed down due to non-availability of funds. The Committee, therefore, recommends   that recovery of loans,  which is
a grey area in the present scenario, should be given more attention and loopholes in this regard should be plugged  so
that the scheme may not be scuttled for want of funds in future  and recycling of funds may continue. The
Chairman/Managing Directors – All India Scheduled Commercial Banks may be suitably advised by the RBI to
improve their recovery performance to recycle the funds and constitute `recovery cells’ at Regional/ Zonal  office level.
(Para 6.9)
 
            The Committee further observes that though the Government achieved in some measure quantitative target of the
PMRY,  no efforts were made either by the Govt. or by other agencies to evaluate the qualitative achievement of the
scheme. The beneficiaries experienced difficulties in starting and running the venture. The major problem in starting the
unit was in getting the loan. 50% of the beneficiaries were affected by the delay in disbursement of the loan. This forced
them to take loan from  moneylenders  at a very high rate of interest and this had an adverse effect on their business.
The beneficiaries reported that because of the financial delay they could not reach  their  expectations. The beneficiaries
of the category made default in payment of loan.
(Para 6.10)
 
            The Committee finds that shortage of funds for working capital is hindering the PMRY beneficiaries. They could
not execute  their  orders in time due to shortage of funds and for marketing capital requirements, they resort to private
money lenders at a high rate of interest resulting in the increased cost of production and short fall in profit margin.
(Para 6.11)
 
            The high cost of operation is another problem where 20% of the industries were affected by this and  they could 
not compete in the local market. Increase in the cost of spare parts are the main cost accelerating items and
entrepreneurs could not  repay- the interest in time. The entrepreneurs could not adjust  themselves  to the demand and
supply mechanism of the market.
(Para 6.12)
 
            As regards the undue delay in giving loans by banks, the Committee impresses upon banks to avoid undue
delay.  The Committee recommends that implementing banks should be advised by the RBI to sanction the applications
within the prescribed time frame and speedily dispose off the same to ensure that there is no inordinate gap between the
sanction and disbursement of loan. DICs should play a pro-active role in this regard. RBI`s guidelines for disposing of
applications under PMRY within the time period of thirty days must be adhered to by banks.
