REPORT
The capital goods and engineering industry constitute the building block of our economy.
A strong capital goods sector is indicative of the health and vibrancy of a robust market. Rights
since our independence our leadership accorded priority to build capital goods industry by setting
up key public sector units. In the economic environment of 21
st century India, it is the private
sector which is driving the growth and development. However, it is found that the public sector
units are as relevant in the private sector led economy as they were in the mixed economy of the
1950s. The engineering and capital goods PSEs assume critical role in nation building.
 2. The Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) under the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public
Enterprises is the nodal department of the Heavy Industries Sector in India, which can be generally
classified into two broad segments, Capital Goods and Equipments. The Department deals with
nineteen industrial sub-sectors that comprise of boilers, cement and dairy machinery, electrical
furnace, freight containers, material heading equipments, metallurgical and mining machinery,
machine tools, oil field equipment, printing, pulp and paper machinery, rubber machinery,
switchgear and control gear, locomotive, sugar machinery, turbine and generator set, transformers
and textile machinery. Besides these, the very important sector of automobiles is also under the
oversight of DHI.
3. The Heavy Industries sector in India has quite a few important CPSEs, the number of
which adds up to thirty two under the administrative control of Department of Heavy Industry. Of
these, eighteen are profit making and fourteen incur losses, currently. The performance of the
heavy industries sector is a crucial marker of the total industrial growth of the country. Therefore,
the Government makes continuous efforts to create a facilitative environment for this sector
through initiatives like NATRiP, the Automotive Mission Plan 2006-16, the National Electric
Mortality Plan 2020, Schemes for Modernization of Capital Goods Sector, etc.
4. The growth of the user industries like infrastructure, oil and gas power, mining,
automotives steel and consumer durable are key to the health of the heavy industries sector. The
Government’s thrust on these areas coupled with India being seen as an outsourcing destination by
global companies due to lower labour cost and availability of quality manpower acts as growth
drivers of this important sector.

National Automotive Testing and R&D Infrastructure Project (NATRiP)
17. NATRiP envisages setting up of world –class automotive testing and homologation facilities
in India. Its principal facilities will come up in the three automotive hubs of India, in the south,
north and west. The project aims at (i) creating critically needed automotive testing infrastructure
to enable the Government in ushering in global vehicular safety, emission and performance
standards, (ii) deepening manufacturing in India, promoting larger value addition leading to
significant enhancement of employment potential and facilitating convergence of India’s strengths
in IT and electronics with automotive engineering, (iii) enhancing India’s considerably low global
outreach in this sector by de-bottlenecking exports and (iv) removing the crippling absence of basic
product testing, validation and development infrastructure for automotive industry. The scheme was
introduced in August, 2005, with an investment of Rs. 1718 crore and it was to be completed by
30.09.2011. Thereafter, Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), in its meeting held on
5.4.2011, revised the project completion date to 31.12.2012 along with the revised cost estimate
to Rs. 2288 crore. However, it is understood that the scheduled date of completion of the project
is again proposed to be extended by a couple of years to 31.12.2014. It is not clear whether there
will be also be a further revision of the cost.
18. The BE 2012-2013 for NATRiP was Rs. 488.48 crore which was reduced to Rs. 341.94
crore in RE stage. During 2012-13, an amount of Rs. 341.94 crore has been released to NATRiP
on 28.9.2012 and the actual expenditure made was Rs. 146.54 crore. Representatives of NATRiP
informed the Committee that allocation of Rs. 341.94 crore of 2013-14 is not sufficient and
informed that Rs. 753.19 crore is required for effective achievement of targets for the project for
the year. The Committee notes that a cumulative amount of Rs. 1512.38 crore has been released
for NATRiP till Jan, 2013. Out of this, the Department could utilize Rs. 1246.32 crore till January,
2013. Considering that the total funds earmarked for NATRiP is Rs. 2288.06 crore and that the
Cabinet would grant extension of time completion till Dec., 2014, an amount of Rs. 775.68 crore
remains to be released within 2 years.

Modernization of Capital Goods Sector (CGS)
21. The Capital Good Sector contributes 12% to the total manufacturing Capacity (approximately
15% of GDP) of India. Accordingly, the domestic CG Industry would be having a size of $30 billion.
However, India’s import dependence in CGS is increasing over the years and stands close to $45
billion in a year. In view of this, the CG Sector is identified as a strategic sector by National
Manufacturing Competition Council (NMCC). Despite, such acknowledgments a lack of long term
road map to develop indigenous manufacturing base in this sector is a glaring lacuna.
22. Some of the important reasons isolated by the Department for the laggard performance of
the CG sector in India are:
(1) Lack of world class cutting edge technology and no R&D initiatives;
(2) Increasing trend in import of capital goods including second hand capital goods in
some sub sectors, e.g., shuttle less looms;
(3) Lack of skilled manpower;
(4) Lack of level playing field vis-à-vis global players.
23. Taking these issues on board, DHI has introduced a Scheme for enhancement of
competitiveness of the Indian Capital Goods Industry on the basis of recommendations of the
working group on Capital Goods and Engineering Sector. The components of the scheme include
setting up of infrastructural facilities, e.g., common facility centre, product development centre,
industrial cluster park, etc. The Scheme also envisages setting up of such facilities for several
sectors of capital goods, e.g., Machine Tools sector, Earthmoving and Construction Machinery and
Textile Machinery. The Department has also been raising the issue of second hand import of capital
goods with NMCC and Ministry of Textiles. A meeting of the Committee of Secretaries took place
in May, 2012 which recommended several measures to arrest the growth of second hand imports
in capital goods sector. DHI has also been taking follow up action on this as well as on the issue
of zero import duty on construction machinery which are being made indigenously.
24. Regarding the current status of the scheme, the DHI submitted to the Committee that in
January 2013 Planning Commission communicated in principle approval for the Scheme and a
budgetary allocation of Rs.1081.22 crore for the Scheme has been made during 12
th FYP. DHI also
placed before the Committee that a plan is already in place to efficiently utilize the BE of Rs. 70.00
crore for the year 2013-14 under the scheme, by preparation of Detailed Project Report for
implementing the many components of the scheme. The various industrial associations who were
heard by the Committee during the course of discussion for Demand for Grants 2013-14 also
emphasized the importance of successful implementation of this scheme.

7
25. The Committee finds that though the scheme for Enhancement of the Competitiveness
of the Capital Goods Sector was originally slated to have been in operation during 2012-13,
the inprinciple approval of the Planning Commission was obtained only in January, 2013. Now
that a beginning has been made, it is expected that DHI will strive to attain all the
parameters intended for 2013-14 by effective use of Rs. 70.00 crore provided for the Scheme.
The Committee observes that considering the magnitude of the problem faced by the CG
Sector in India and the enormity of the steps to be taken to counter these problems, the
Twelfth Five Year Plan allocation of Rs. 1081.22 crore looks highly inadequate. Meanwhile, it
is felt that while Government can play the role of facilitator and take the initiatives as it has
already done, the participation of the private sector can also to be roped in to achieve the
modernization of the CG Sector of the country at appropriate juncture. The Committee also
takes into cognizance the apprehensions expressed by various industry associations about the
Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) and Preferential Trade Agreements (PTAs) being inked by the
Government of India with various countries. Therefore, it is recommended that the
Government should take all necessary steps to protect the interests of our Capital Goods
industry as well as the domestic industry as a whole, while entering into such agreements.
26. The problem of capital good industries of our country is compounded by the dumping of
capital goods from many other countries like China and South Korea. Many industry associations
who appeared before the Committee expressed grave concern on this issue. The Committee notes
that the import policy is determined by the Ministry of Commerce and other concerned Ministries.
Import duties on the capital goods imported from abroad is quite less. As a result, the domestic
industry consisting of the private and public sector units are suffering very badly. The Committee
strongly recommends that the Government of India must impose duties on imported capital
goods as per the WTO agreement. It is understood that by adhering to the provisions of
the WTO agreement, we can protect the domestic capital industry which is central to our
industrial activities and technological capability and security. The Committee wants the
Department of Heavy Industry to place the recommendation of the Committee while taking
up this issue with the concerned Ministries.
Scooters India Ltd. (SIL)
27. The Scooters India Ltd. is a company engaged in designing, manufacturing and marketing of
three wheelers, their spares and engineering goods. SIL is the only automobile company in the country
operating in the Public Sector. Incorporated in 1972, it was first declared sick by BIFR in 2010 and
thereafter was referred to BRPSE. In line with the recommendations of BRPSE, the Government
decided to identify a strategic partner for SIL through a resolution in Parliament. However, the resolution
introduced in Monsoon Session 2011 was withdrawn under directions from Prime Minister’s Office and
also on account of several representations. Subsequently, a revised revival proposal for SIL was
prepared and placed before the Cabinet on 19.05.2012, which was approved.
28. The following are the salient features of the Revival Plan:–
1. To infuse fund of Rs. 70.38 crore as equity by GOI for capital expenditure.
2. To provide Rs. 20 crore as interest free plan loan by GOI for working capital. The
plan loan may be recovered in 5 equal annual instalments commencing from
31.03.2015 after moratorium of three years.
3. To convert plan loan of Rs. 1.93 crore, outstanding as on 31
st March, 2012 into equity.
4. To convert non plan loan of Rs. 83.28 crore outstanding as on 31 st March, 2012 into
equity.
5. To waive interest accrued and due of Rs. 22.17 crore as on 31 st March, 2012.

8
6. To waive interest accrued and not due of Rs. 4.20 crore as on 31 st March, 2012.
7. Enhancement of superannuation age from 58 years to 60 years in relaxation of DPE
guidelines.
8. Implementation of 2007 Pay Revision as per DPE guidelines.
29. As part of the examination of Demand for Grants, the Committee interacted with
representatives of various PSEs including, SIL. During the discussion, the representative of SIL
submitted that the business of the Company was severely hampered as the permits for three
wheelers were controlled by State Governments through their Regional Transport Authorities.
30. Regarding the revival of SIL, the Committee feels that along with prompt and time
bound execution of the revival plan, it is important that the matter of three wheeler
permits controlled by the Regional Transport Authorities of State Governments is also given
urgent attention. The Committee recommends that the following two requests of SIL shared
with the Committee shall be examined and given due consideration as these aspects are
important for the future business prospects of SIL.
(a) The permits for three wheelers in passenger categories (6+1) may be released
as per the demand/need of the city/town and in a way should be deregulated and
decontrolled as is the case with four wheelers and two wheelers.
(b) The State Governments and Registration authorities may be advised to release
passenger permits for three wheelers in 6+1 category for outside city limits
areas where Hon’ble Courts has enforced a ban. This assumes significance as
the city buses which occupy more volume/space on roads because of its designs
features could not provide last mile solution to common man and where only
three wheelers can approach because of its design and constructional advantages.

explanation. The Department also, on their part should have noticed the variance in figures
and taken it up with BHEL.
35. Since, the IEBR figures of BHEL are at variance, the Committee refrains from
commenting on it. But the reasons quoted for the shortfall in IEBR figures for 2011-12 and
2012-13 are common. The Committee observes that the BHEL should take measures to
address the problem which has caused shortfall. The Committee feels that the avoidable
delay cited by the BHEL should not be allowed to spill over to another year by a Maharatana
Company like a BHEL.
36. During the discussion of Demands, the DHI as well as the representatives of BHEL
informed the Committee that though the manufacturing capacity of BHEL has gone up from 6000
MW to 20,000 MW, the number of orders received by them did not correspondingly go up.
Further, it was pointed out that the Indian power sector scenario presents a bleak picture as
availability of coal and gas is insufficient and import of coal is expensive. Also, the order book of
BHEL was expected to diminish in the next two to three years. As such, it has come down from
Rs. 60,000 crore to Rs. 32,000 crore currently. The Committee was informed that the BHEL must
get orders from the defence sector and do business in setting up many of our defence projects.
In this context, the statement of the Defence Minister that the country needed indigenization of
defence capability was cited and it was argued that the BHEL can step in to carry forward the
cause of indigenization and thereby improve its own strength and serve the cause of country’s
defence and security. Besides, the BHEL must diversify its activities to new areas such as
alternative sources of energy.
37. In view of the above, the Committee recommends that the DHI, which is the
administrative Department for BHEL should extend all support to the efforts of the
company to convince the Ministry of Defence regarding the relevance and utility of the
BHEL for the defence sector. It is expected that the presentation to be made by BHEL
before the Ministry of Defence, in presence of Hon’ble Minister of HI and PE would be a
beginning in this regard. Besides, the BHEL must further expand its activities for
generating power from renewable sources of energy.
38. The Committee observed that there are many problems involving BHEL, Rashtriya
Ispat Nigam Ltd., Neyveil Lignite Corporation, etc. It is understood that the orders placed by
many PSEs with the BHEL are not being completed in time. As a result, several PSEs are not
able to improve their performance. The Committee is constrained to note that the inter PSE
coordination is missing. However, the Committee was informed by the officials of the DHI
that instead of going to the court the inter-departmental dispute mechanism available in the
Cabinet Secretariat may be used to sort out the issues among Ministries. The Committee got
the assurance from the Secretary, DHI that he would look into it. The Committee strongly
recommends that the matters involving several PSEs must be resolved in a time bound
manner for the completion of the projects and for improving the efficiency and performance
of the PSEs. The Committee would like to get a detailed report in this respect from the DHI.
Heavy Engineering Corporation (HEC)
39. The HEC Ltd. was incorporated on 31
st December, 1958 for the manufacturing of steel

10
plant equipments. The company never performed well financially till the year 2005. Due to the
inability of the company to face challenges of economic liberalization of 1991, the Company was
referred to BIFR in February 1992. The revival plan proposed therein did not take off. However,
another plan was submitted to BRPSE in September, 2005, with the initiatives of DHI. The plan
was approved by the Government in December, 2005 and by the High Court of Jharkhand in
November, 2009.
40. The Committee notes that HEC has been making profit, though small, since 2006-07. The
sales performance of the Company has increased from Rs.178.18 crore in 2005-06 to Rs. 759.75
crore in 2012-13. However, it is given to understand that the achievements of the HEC comes from
operations of outdated plants and machinery and it will not be able to sustain the marginal profits
for a very long time without modernization and upgradation of those plants and machinery.
Meanwhile, a Working Group under the Secretary, DHI has recommended the Planning Commission
to build HEC as a National Champion.
41. Subsequently, the modernisation plan of the HEC to be undertaken at a cost of Rs.1516.13
crore during the 12
th Five Year Plan has been cut down to Rs.763.66 crore, owing to downward
revision of the allocations for DHI. Out of this, budgetary support is for Rs.350 crore and Rs.300
crore is allotted for EBR (loan from FI) and Rs.113.66 crore is to be generated internally. In tune
with this a budgetary support of Rs.200 crore was sought for 2013-14, against which only a token
provision Rs.0.01 crore has been kept aside for HEC in the Budget. Meanwhile, HEC is trying to
complete the Techno Economic Viability (TEV) Report and Detailed Project Report (DPR). If the
required approvals from Planning Commission and Ministry of Finance come through, the Company
hopes to seek more funds during the supplementary demand.
42. The Committee views the case of HEC as a classic example of turn around of a
CPSE, through a revival package. The progress the HEC has achieved despite obsolete
technology and machinery inspires confidence that the PSEs can do much better with
upgradation of plant and machinery. Accordingly, the Committee is of the firm opinion that
HEC has earned the right to seek budgetary support and necessary funds for capital
expenditure without which it would not be possible for the HEC to increase its production.
The Committee strongly recommends that the Government and the Planning Commission
must extend all support to the HEC to emerge as a role model in the public sector. If the
HEC can show impressive results by using obsolete technology then it is quite sure that it
can show much better results by using modern and updated equipments. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry, Ministry of Finance and the
Planning Commission should expedite the requisite approval for timely infusion of funds to
the HEC. Timely support to HEC will send a positive message to all CPSEs. The HEC, on
their part should be ready with the TEV and DPR at the earliest.
43. Many PSUs are incurring losses. Government has plans to revive them. There are many
steps which are taken to revive a sick industry. It involves going to Board for Reconstruction of
Public Sector Enterprises (BRPSC), Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR), the
Cabinet and consultation with experts and other concerned people. The Committee found that such
a long drawn out process takes lot of time and in the process the calculation of the cost for revival
is done several times. As a result, the cost goes up. The Committee strongly recommends that
decision in such matters must be taken on time.
Issues placed before the Committee by the Industry and Auto Associations
44. The Committee heard the views of various associations like ASSOCHAM, FICCI, CII,
ACMA, SIAM, IMTMA,  etc., as part of the examination of Demands for Grants, 2013-14, to get
the views of the industrial units of the private sector.

11
45. The collective grievance of the industry included lack of the level playing field in the
capital good sector which rendered domestic companies uncompetitive. The import of second
hand machinery compounded these problems. The industry was also extremely worried about the
signing of Foreign Trade Agreements (FTAs) and Preferential Trade Agreements (PTAs) with
various countries that would lead to lower or nil import duties and other countries routing their
products through FTA countries. Lack of effective R&D, absence of necessary infrastructure and
shortage of skilled manpower weakened the capability of the Indian industry to compete at the
global level. It was also submitted before the Committee that the educational institutions
particularly the engineering colleges do not produce the required skilled youth who would be
useful to the industry. The Committee suggested that the industry associations should
interact with the educational institutions in developed curricula to minimize the gap
between skill requirement and availability. The Committee also suggested that a core
group of the industry consisting of selected skilled people may draft educational curricula
and send it to all educational institutions for their adoption and incorporation in the
syllabus for engineering students.
46. The industry associations wanted our embassies abroad to inform through their websites
opportunities available in foreign countries to do business. They pointed out that no such
consolidation information is available in the website of the MEA or the website of our
embassies.
47. The industry associations also put their specific grievances before the Committee. The auto
industry associations referred to the lack of standardization in the auto components and demanded
adoption of standards for auto components in the pattern of European countries. Some of the
associations stated that they have been launching a campaign called Asli Naqli  to sensitise the
buyers of auto components about the genuineness of auto parts. The Committee commends such
initiative and recommends that the Government must take measures for standardization of
auto components.  The bottlenecks in the Construction equipment industry included lack of speedy
project implementation, import of second-hand machinery and lack of tariff and non-tariff
incentives. The issues bothering Textile machinery sector included cheap and second-hand Chinese
machinery, duty reduction on shuttle less looms, price disadvantage to local manufactures, lack of
R&D support and absence of FDI, etc.
48. The Auto associations of the country also placed many issues of relevance before the
Committee. These related to demand for reduction in excise duty on vehicles and SUVs, help of
Government to overcome difficulties in valuation of excisable goods, inclusion of automobile CBU
(completely built up units) trade in FTA with EU, etc. The points raised by the Auto component
industry included interest subvention for technology upgradation and development, export
allowances, input credit on diesel, protection while entering into FTAs, encouragement for in-house
R&D, increase in depreciation rate in the cost of plant and machinery, etc.
49. Having heard the grievances of both the industry and auto associations, the Committee
understands that certain problems cut across all sectors of industry be it in the private or in
the public sector. Of the most pressing issues is the low duty on imported goods, in adequate
R&D, import of secondhand Capital Goods, free trade agreements and skill deficit. The
Committee recommends that the domestic industry must be protected and in this regard the
Government can take sufficient measures by following the WTO norms. The Committee is
also of the firm opinion that due consideration should be given to the suggestions put forth
by the Committee of Secretaries in its meeting held on 23
rd May, 2012 for letting NMCC
separately examine the impact of FTAs on domestic industries and thereafter to have an
inter-ministerial consultations on those findings. The Committee, in tune with the industry’s
interests, favours non-tariff trade barriers and effective implementation of the Scheme for
Modernization of the Capital Goods Sector to strengthen the indigenous industry.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERV ATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE — AT A GLANCE
1. The Committee notes that during 2011-12 the Department of Heavy Industry
managed to utilize the funds allocated to them in a relatively efficient manner. However,
during 2012-13, the utilization of the total money allocated has not been up to the mark.
With only a month left of the financial year, the Department could muster only an
utilization of 71.40%. In view of this, the Committee recommends that Department of
Heavy Industry, as the nodal department, having oversight of one of the most important
sectors of the Indian industry, should improve their fund utilization. If the Department lags
in this area, there can be a possible negative cascading effect on the allocation of funds
during the ensuing years and many important initiatives planned by the Department will be
left without sufficient funding. (Para 7)
2. The Committee takes note of the fact that funds earmarked for Lumpsum provision
for the benefits of NER and Sikkim has been going unutilized for the last two years. Taking
cognizance of the submission of Department of Heavy Industry about the revival plan for
NPCC, the Committee recommends that the Department should do everything in its power
to expedite the necessary approvals for the revival package for NPCC and consider utilizing
the funds of Rs. 58.50 crore allocated under the lumpsum scheme for 2013-14, for the
revival of NPCC. The Committee also observes that the trend of funds earmarked for NER
going unutilized is highly worrisome. Considering that there are 4 CPSEs under Department
of Heavy Industry in the NER, it is difficult to conceive that there can be a dearth of
proposals for their modernization, upgradation and other similar requirements. Therefore,
the Committee is of the considered opinion that the Department has to be more proactive
for proper utilization of the funds for NER. It should not be a difficult task to plan the
utilization of the fund as it is a given fact that 10% of Plan funds of the Department has
to be set aside for the NER every year. (Para 11)
3. Though the Committee takes into consideration that funds for 2011-12 (Rs. 18.30
crore) were released in the last month of the financial year, it remains a fact that the
Department could not fully utilize this amount till November, 2012. The Department has
quoted this under utilization as the reason for curtailment of funds for the year 2012-13.
In this context, the Committee is of the opinion that if DHI faced any problem for the
release of funds from the Ministry of Finance that could have been taken up at the
appropriate juncture. Once funds are released there cannot be an excuse for under-
utilization as the attainment of the defined objectives would be affected, especially, in an
area as important as R&D. Further, under this scheme till February, 2013 out of the
Rs. 20 crore (RE), no money has been actually allocated. Accordingly, the Committee is
constrained to note that the R&D has been compromised by not getting the amount
released for the year 2012-13. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Department
should take strong measures to avoid a repetition of this mistake in future and impress
upon the Ministry of Finance for prompt release of funds. Also, the Committee is hopeful
that the review of the ongoing scheme will help the Department to grapple with the
problems that it faced earlier. (Para 16)
4. The Committee notes that DHI’s flagship project of NATRiP has been severely
plagued by delays and cost overruns. The main reasons for the delays as cited by
Department of Heavy Industry are delay in acquisition of land and clearance and shifting

13
of utility. Moreover, the Committee is extremely disturbed to observe that the Actual
expenditure year wise since 2005-06 for NATRiP has been abysmal. Barring one year (2008-09),
the Actual expenditure has been well below 50% of the corresponding RE figures. After an
analysis of this scenario, the Committee observes that the performance of the planning
machinery for NATRiP has not been satisfactory and lack of long term vision has crippled
the implementation of the scheme, which has been one of the most awaited projects of the
automobile sector. The Committee recommends that at least at this late point in time, in
comparison with the original date of completion, NATRiP should strictly adhere to the
revised completion date of December, 2014 and also avoid cost escalation in the attainment
of the remaining targets. (Para 20)
5. The Committee finds that though the scheme for Enhancement of the
Competitiveness of the Capital Goods Sector was originally slated to have been in operation
during 2012-13, the inprinciple approval of the Planning Commission was obtained only in
January, 2013. Now that a beginning has been made, it is expected that DHI will strive to
attain all the parameters intended for 2013-14 by effective use of Rs. 70.00 crore provided
for the Scheme. The Committee observes that considering the magnitude of the problem
faced by the CG Sector in India and the enormity of the steps to be taken to counter these
problems, the 12
th Five Year Plan allocation of Rs. 1081.22 crore looks highly inadequate.
Meanwhile, it is felt that while Government can play the role of facilitator and take the
initiatives as it has already done, the participation of the private sector can also to be roped
in to achieve the modernization of the CG Sector of the country at appropriate juncture.
The Committee also takes into cognizance the apprehensions expressed by various industry
associations about the Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) and Preferential Trade Agreements
(PTAs) being inked by the Government of India with various countries. Therefore, it is
recommended that the Government should take all necessary steps to protect the interests
of our Capital Goods industry as well as the domestic industry as a whole, while entering
into such agreements. (Para 25)
6. The Committee strongly recommends that the Government of India must impose
duties on imported capital goods as per the WTO agreement. It is understood that by
adhering to the provisions of the WTO agreement, we can protect the domestic capital
industry which is central to our industrial activities and technological capability and
security. The Committee wants the Department of Heavy Industry to place the
recommendation of the Committee while taking up this issue with the concerned Ministries.
(Para 26)
7. Regarding the revival of SIL, the Committee feels that along with prompt and time
bound execution of the revival plan, it is important that the matter of three wheeler
permits controlled by the Regional Transport Authorities of State Governments is also given
urgent attention. The Committee recommends that the following two requests of SIL shared
with the Committee shall be examined and given due consideration as these aspects are
important for the future business prospects of SIL.
(a) The permits for three wheelers in passenger categories (6+1) may be released
as per the demand/need of the city/town and in a way should be deregulated and
decontrolled as is the case with four wheelers and two wheelers.
(b) The State Governments and Registration authorities may be advised to release
passenger permits for three wheelers in 6+1 category for outside city limits
areas where Hon’ble Courts has enforced a ban. This assumes significance as
the city buses which occupy more volume/space on roads because of its designs
