Para 2.5
ALLOCATION OF FUNDS
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee noted that many of the Central PSEs are making losses due to their inability to face the competition, because of their obsolete
R&D, high cost of production, shortage of work orders, etc. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Heavy Industry should
infuse capital, state of the art technology and entrepreneurship into potentially viable PSEs so as to make these ‘Modern Temples’ capable to face the
private sector MNCs in the consumer economy.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        The strategies for strengthening and improving the potentially viable PSEs include:---
           1.    Revival of PSEs through BIFR.
           2.    Financial restructuring wherever appropriate.
           3.    Joint Venture formation to have continuous access to technology, finance and marketing.
           4.    Manpower rationalization.
        Out of 26 BIFR referred PSUs under the Department of Heavy Industry revival plans have been sanctioned by BIFR in case of 12 PSUs. These
involve cash infusion of Rs.654 crore by the Government besides financial restructuring of Rs.2106 crores. Apart from BIFR sanctioned revival
plans, Government has on its own, approved the financial restructuring in case of 7 other sick/loss making PSUs involving fresh infusion of funds to
the extent of Rs.531 crores and financial restructuring of Rs.1443 crores.
        In order to rationalize the manpower, Government have provided an amount of about Rs.1700 crores under Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS)
for retiring about 70,000 employees of PSUs.
        Lagan Jute Machinery Co. Ltd. (LJMC), a subsidiary of Bharat Bhari Udyog Nigam Ltd. (BBUNL) and Belting division of Andrew Yule & Co.
Ltd. have already been converted into joint venture companies. Also in case of 30 PSUs of Department of Heavy Industry, the process of JV
formation/disinvestments has been initiated.

Para 2.9
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee recommends that while rationalising the Government equity in non-strategic PSEs, interests of the workers should be protected
fully.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        The Government is fully alive to the need to protect the interests of the workers. Provisions in the Shareholder agreements generally incorporate
the following clauses regarding employee protection.
        1.     The parties envision that all employees of the Company on the date thereof shall continue in the employment of the Company.
        2.     The Company shall not retrench any part of its labour force for a stipulated period from the closing date other than any dismissal or
termination of employees of the Company from their employment in accordance with the standing orders/disciplinary rules of the Company.

Para 6.2
GUARANTEE FEE SUBSIDY
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee is constrained to note that the Department of Heavy Industry has provided for the provision of guarantee fee subsidy to only four
PSEs in 2001-02 leaving most of the needy PSEs to face hardships in getting bank finance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Government should provide guarantee fee subsidy to the needy PSEs for leveraging finance for sustaining their operations.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        It may be mentioned that Government has already given guarantees to number of PSEs under DHI for cash credit facilities from Banks/Financial
Institutions for meeting working capital requirements. The total sum guaranteed by the Government for various PSEs under DHI as on March 31,
2002 is Rs.1333.20 crore. The guarantee fee is only 1% of the guarantee given. The guarantee fee subsidy provided to Hindustan Cables Ltd., Burn
Standard Company Ltd. Hindustan Machine Tools Ltd. And Heavy Engineering Corporation Ltd. Mentioned in the para was as per the approved
revival schemes of these PSEs. A new scheme was also introduced in 2000-01 for providing interest subsidy on bank finance to PSEs, for
implementation of VRS. As per this scheme, 50% of the interest due on funds raised through bonds for VRS is disbursed as interest subsidy to the
PSEs concerned, which is much higher than the guarantee fee subsidy. However, guarantee fee subsidy as approved by the Government in the revival
plans of the PSEs, will continue to be provided.
Para 6.6 ---- 6.7
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee recommends that under this new scheme introduced by DHI, more funds should be given to Richardson & Cruddas (R&C),
Tung Bhadra Steel Products Ltd. (TSPL), Triveni Structurals Ltd, Instrumentation Ltd, Burn Standard Company Ltd (BSCL) and Hindustan Cables
Ltd. (HCL). To so as to meet their demands to finance manpower rationalisation programme undertaken in these PSEs.
        The Committee further recommends that in case the amount earmarked is not sufficient for this purpose, the additional allocations may be
sought at RE stage.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        A Budget provision of Rs.10 crore was made in BE 2000-01 for interest subsidy on Bank Finance to PSEs for raising funds from
Banks/Financial Institutions for VRS in order to reduce the burden on the Companies. The initial response from the PSEs was not quite encouraging
as the scheme represented a marked departure from the prevailing practice of extending full budgetary support for VRS. Hence the provision was
reduced to Rs.1 crore at RE stage. Out of that provision of Rs.1 crore, only Rs.44.37 lakh could be utilized by one PSE viz., Richardson and Cruddas.
A budget provision per Rs. 20 crore was made in 2001-02 for this purpose. There was good response from the PSEs and a total amount of  Rs. 21.28
crore i.e. Rs. 1.28 crore more than the budget provision was released (HMT – Rs. 20.71 crore, Richardson and Cruddas – Rs. 0.25 crore and
Tungabhadra Steel Products – Rs. 0.32 crore). Consequent of this better response, the budget provision has been enhanced to Rs. 25 crore in BE
2002-03. Out of this provision an amount of Rs. 10.03 crore has already been released in the first quarter of the financial year 2002-03 to Richardson
and Cruddas (Rs. 0.25 crore), Bharat Heavy Plates and Vessels Ltd.  (Rs. 0.86 crore) and HMT (Rs. 8.92 crore). Funds will be released to the PSEs as
and when the demand is received. However, if need arises, additional funds will be sought at RE stage or by way of supplementary demands for
grants at the appropriate time.

PLAN INVESTMENT IN PUBLIC SECTOR ENTERPRISES
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee is constrained to note that keeping in view the restructuring/revival schemes going on in various PSEs, the Government has

Para 8.1
NON PLAN LOANS TO PUBLIC SECTOR ENTERPRISES
RECOMMENDATION
        Since a number of PSEs are running under losses, the Committee is of the view that the allocated amount of Rs.150.00 crores for revival
schemes and Rs.250.00 crores for VSS and payment of statutory dues may not be sufficient to meet the requirements of large number of PSEs. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that additional allocations, if required, may be made at the RE stage.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        In case the budget provisions of Rs. 150 crore for revival schemes and Rs. 250 crore for VSS/VRS and payment of statutory dues fall short of
the demands of the PSEs, additional allocation for the purpose will be sought at RE stage through supplementary demands for grants.

Para 10.3 ---- 10.4
HINDUSTAN PAPER CORPORATION LTD. (HPC)
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee admits that the Company is in dire need of financial assistance to meet its working capital requirements and for other projects in
pipeline. The Committee also feels that cut/removal in the budgetary support, interest subsidy, government guarantee, etc. to HPC would adversely
affect functioning of the HPC.
        The Committee, therefore, recommends that the DHI should provide interest subsidy, Government guarantee, bank guarantee, etc. to fill the gap.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        Government has been extending budgetary support to Hindustan Paper Corporation Limited (HPC) for meeting plan outlay. Plan Budgetary
Support of Rs.13.50 crore was released to HPC in 2000-2001 and Rs.4.45 crore in 2001-2002. Government has approved further financial
Restructuring of HPC and conveyed the approval to the Company on June 10,2002 which, inter-alia, includes:-
        (i)    Write off of frozen past interest including penal interest amounting to Rs. 442.38 crore as on March 31.1994.
        (ii)    (a)    Adjustment of part of temporary accommodation of Rs. 181.00 crore extended to MNPM and NPPC by HPC and accrued interest of
Rs. 26.18 crore thereon upto March 31,2000 against HPC’s loan and interest dues to Government of India respectively subject to the
condition that the amount, if any, received out of sale of MNPM over and above the present liability would be payable to the
Government.
               (b)   Till such time as the arrangement proposed in para (iv) and (vii) below materializes, adjustment of the temporary accommodation
provided by HPC to its subsidiaries for payment of salary and wages beyond March 31, 2000 against HPC’s dues to Government of
India with the concurrence of Ministry of Finance.
        (iii)   Redemption of Non-cumulative Redeemable Preference Shares of Rs. 106.68 crore on March 30,2008 and reissue of the same in favour of
Government of India to be redeemed in the next 10 years.
        (iv)   Government of India’s financial support, if any, in future would be provided direct to the subsidiaries of HPC instead of routing the same
through the holding Company.
        (v)   Grant of interest holiday and moratorium on repayment of loan and interest on Government of India loans released to HPC for MNPM since
the Company has gone into liquidation w.e.f. October 20,2000, till such time as the liquidation proceedings are completed. Also ‘in
principle’ approval for irrecoverable loans of Government of India, if any, after completion of the winding up proceedings.
        (vi)   Writing off of loan of Rs. 2.03 crore released by Government of India for IPP through HPC and interest accrued amounting to Rs. 0.80
crore as on March 31, 2000.
        (vii)  Separation for Hindustan Newsprint Limited, currently a subsidiary of Hindustan Paper Corporation (HPC) from the holding company
HPC.

Para 12.2
RAJASTHAN ELECTRONICS AND INSTRUMENTS LTD. (REIL)
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee views REIL as a potentially viable PSE, which needs fresh infusion of funds so as to update its R&D and projects. The
Committee is, therefore, in conformity with the DHI to invite a joint venture partner for the Company.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        REIL, Jaipur, was incorporated on August 12, 1981 as a Joint Venture between Instrumentation Limited, Kota (ILK); a Public Sector
Undertaking (PSU), under the Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) and Rajasthan State Industrial Development and Investment Corporation
Limited, Jaipur (RIICO), with 51% to 49% ratio. Government interest in REIL is through the shareholding in REIL, as the Government holds 100%
equity in ILK. REIL is earning profits since inception.
        In principle decision has since been taken by this Department to sell and transfer 51% of the equity of ILK in REIL to a Joint Venture (JV)
partner and the process of disinvestments already initiated.
Para 13.4
HINDUSTAN CABLES LTD. (HCL)
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee is happy to note that with the concerted efforts of the Government by applying restructuring plan, the Company’s decline has
stopped and its turnover increased from Rs.203.00 crores in 1999-2000 to Rs.975.00 crores in 2000-01. The Committee feels that this record turnover
of the Company has been possible mainly due to ‘reservation of orders’ for the Company by the Government. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Government should continue making provisions of work orders for the Company.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        Following the package of assistance, which inter-alia  included reservation of orders, the Company increased the turnover from Rs.221 crore in
1998-99 to Rs.803 crore in 1999-2000 and to Rs.875 crore in 2000-01. The reservation of orders was initially for a period of 2 years. The Cabinet
Committee on Disinvestment in its meeting held on February 1, 2001, however, decided for continuation of current reservation of 25% of the annual
requirement of telecom cables of BSNL/MTNL along with 50% advance in favour of the Company till disinvestments and reservation for a further
period of two years after disinvestments.

Para 9.8 – 9.10
PRICE PREFERENCE TO DOMESTIC INDUSTRY
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee is concerned to note that the domestic manufacturers are not able to avail price preference of 15% for projects funded by
international agencies like World Bank, United Nations, Asian Development Bank etc. as imports on such projects is allowed at ‘nil’ duty as per
Notification No.85/99-customs dated July 6, 1999. The power projects for 10th and 11th Five Year Plans are being finalised. Some of these projects
may entail multilateral funding. Additional power projects of 60,000 MW during the 11 th plan period are likely to be finalised. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Customs Notification No. 85/99 – custom dated July 6, 1999 should be rescinded to enable domestic manufacturers to
effectively compete against foreign suppliers by taking advantage of 15% price preference provided in ICB tenders.
        The Committee views this issue very seriously since it has effected zero customs duty regime applicable to all goods imported to India for
execution of projects financed by the United Nations/World Bank or any other International Organisation. This amended custom duty structure has
adversely affected competitiveness of the domestic Capital Goods industries.
        The Committee, therefore, recommends that this anomaly needs to be rectified at the earliest possible. The Committee also recommends that if
need arises then the DHI should take up this issue with the Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) to resolve the log jam. The Committee
further recommends that the unresolved matter may be placed before the Cabinet Committee to consider the matter de-novo.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        This matter has been discussed in the Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee. They had constituted a separate Committee headed
by Revenue Secretary for making recommendations on this issue. They have since given their recommendations stating that World Bank guidelines
provide for giving preferential treatment to domestic bidders. Their recommendations were discussed in a meeting on 12.08.2002 by the Standing
Committee (Sub-Committee-III). Further action on these recommendations of the Parliamentary Standing Committee for issue of clarifications is
being taken by the Department of Revenue.
Para 9.11
REFUND OF TERMINAL EXCISE DUTY TO DOMESTIC INDUSTRIES
RECOMMENDATION
        The Committee is concerned to note that the domestic manufacturers continue to suffer cost disadvantages such as sales tax, octroi, and high cost
of finance and infrastructure bottlenecks to the extent of 18-28% while competing with imports. At present custom duty on Coal Mining, Fertilizer
and Power projects is 5% basic custom duty+16% CVD and ‘nil’ SAD. Under this structure the domestic industry is unable to claim refund of excise
duty and become uncompetitive. The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be no duty free import of projects and a minimum of 15%
basic custom duty + ‘nil’ CVD + 4% SAD be imposed to enable the domestic industry to become competitive without increasing the project cost.
ACTION TAKEN BY THE GOVERNMENT
        The issue has already been taken up by DHI with Ministry of Finance vide D.O. letter No. 3(23)/99-PE.XI dated December 29,.2000. Hon’ble
Minister (HI & PE) vide D.O. letter dated December 23, 2000 has also taken up various issues including the above with Hon’ble Finance Minister
(copy enclosed) (Annexure-VII).

Subject : Issues relating to Capital Goods Industry
Dear Shri Yashwant Sinhaji,
        As you are aware the capital goods industry is witnessing an unprecedented demand recession, causing declining market prices and erosion in
domestic market share. Capital goods sector has witnessed a decline of 2.7% in April-Sept.2000 as compared to a growth of 5% in 1999-2000 and
11% in 1998-99.
2.     Some of the factors responsible for deceleration are slow down in investment, reduction in the custom duty on capital goods/project imports
from a high of 85% to 0-5%. Domestic manufacturers continue to suffer cost disadvantages such as sales tax, octroi, high cost of finance and

101st report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/101streport.htm[04-07-2016 12:23:55]
infrastructure bottlenecks to the extent of 18-28% while competing with imports.
3.     This situation is further aggravated by the issue of notification No.85/99 dated 6.7.99 which allows import of goods for the projects financed by
the United Nations, World Bank, Asian Development Bank and other International organizations at “Nil” duty. Due to this notification, domestic
manufacturers are not able to avail the price preference of 15% for projects funded by such agencies.
4.     You are aware that the capital goods sector has very strong backward and forward linkages. Therefore, it is necessary to provide support to give
the desired impetus to this industry for reversing the declining trend and sustain its growth. In this context, I have following suggestions for your
consideration -----
        l     Custom Duty on all capital goods should be brought to bound rate of WTO.
        l     In project imports, restructure this present duty structure by levying minimum basic custom duty of 15% + CVD equal to excise duty @ 8%
or lower and rescind notification No.85/99 dated 6.7.99.
        l     Restoration of Terminal Excise Duty (TED) to domestic suppliers to projects where the end product is not exciseable.
        With regards,
