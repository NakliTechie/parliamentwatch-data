Introduction 
 
In India, the Heavy Industries Sector encompasses H eavy Engineering, Machine 
Tools, Heavy Electrical, Construction Machinery and  the Automotive Industries.  The 
output of these industries permeates almost all sec tors of the economy including basic 
infrastructure like power, rural and road transport s industries and provide heavy 
machinery that are critical to other important sect ors like mining, refineries, 
petrochemicals, fertilizers, shipping, paper, cemen t, textiles etc.  The Department of 
Heavy Industry (DHI) which looks after this industrial sector is the part of the Ministry of 
Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises, which was created by the President’s 
Notification dated the 15 
th  October, 1999. DHI administers 32 operating Centra l Public 
Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), including 12 subsidiary  companies and 1 JV Company, i.e., 
NTPC- BHEL Power projects Pvt. Ltd. The Department also administers the following 
autonomous organizations; 
  
a)  NATRiP 
b)  FCRI 
c)  ARAI 
d)  NAB 
 
2. As per the Economic Survey, 2014-15, the Index o f Industrial Production 
suggests that the Industrial sector is recovering s lowly but steadily with a 2.1% growth in 
April-December 2014-15 against 0.1% during on the same period last year.  The recovery 
is lead by the infrastructure sectors, viz, electrical, coal & cement.  Mining Sector growth 
also turned positive during the year.  From the poi nt of view of Heavy Industries, it is 
further encouraging that Capital Goods sector appears to be on the path of recovery as the 
sector has a multiplier effect on over all economic  growth.  This trend of growth in the 
Industrial Sector coupled with the  ‘Make in India’  programme aimed to facilitate  
investment, foster innovation, enhance skill develo pment, protect intellectual property 
and build best -in -class  manufacturing infrastruc ture rolled out by the Government 
makes the Budget of year 2015-16 , a period of reckoning.

8. An overview of the Budget figures of DHI shows t hat during 2014-15 the Plan BE 
of Rs. 800 crore was reduced by 14.37%  to Rs. 685 crore at the RE stage.  Meanwhile, 
the Non-Plan allocation (BE) of Rs. 443.62 crore wa s raised to Rs. 600 crore at the RE 
stage.  This was an increase of 35.25%.  Further, t he Committee noted that in comparison 
to the total RE for DHI during 2014-15 of Rs. 1285 crore, there is a 26.43% increase in 
total funds allocated to the Department for 2015-16  (BE)  ie., Rs. 1624.73 crore. This 
increase in allocation has been mainly due to a spi ke in the Non Plan funds. The 
Committee also noted that DHI has been allocated mo re Non Plan funds than Plan funds 
for the year 2015-16.  Further the documents mentio ns that large amount of funds (Rs. 
884 crore) have been provided as Lumpsum for  (i) V oluntary Retirement and Separation 

Scheme (VRS/VSS)  and Payment of Statuary Dues and (ii)  Lumpsum for Revival 
Scheme for PSE’s which are Non Plan heads as per th e extant classification of activities/ 
funds into Plan and Non Plan.  As submitted to the Committee, during the year 2015-16 
these Non Plan funds of Rs. 884 crore will be utili zed in respect of the 5 CPSEs under 
DHI which have been decided by the Government to be closed down.  
 
9. Continuing the trend that can be observed over t he last two years, the 
Committee finds that for the year 2015-16 too DHI h as been given lesser Plan funds 
compared to the Non Plan allocations. As per the current classification of funds, this 
trend points towards less asset creation activities  under DHI year after year. 
Accordingly, in view of the Committee, the activiti es of DHI are gradually tilting 
towards non creative activities like winding up of CPSEs, settling dues, managing 
VRS, etc.  Though there might be a contrary view th at winding up of sick CPSEs 
also contributes indirectly to asset creation and e fficient channelisation of available 
resources, as per extant classification of Plan and  Non Plan activities, it does not 
count as a productive activity.   
 
10. Accordingly, the Committee expresses its concer n over the fact that the Plan 
activities of DHI are either stagnating or getting reduced.  This would also mean 
that the Department has largely been unable to come  up with schemes necessary to 
cater to the varying needs of the extremely important Heavy Industries Sector in the 
country.  In view of the above, the Committee obser ves that DHI should strive more 
to come up with Plan schemes in a proactive manner by gauging the needs of the 
Heavy Industries Sector. Such initiatives will faci litate business environment in the 
sector.

Auto Sector 
 
 
18. The Indian Automotive Industry is the 6 th  largest in the world having deep 
forward and backward linkages which serves key segm ents of the economy.  The Auto 
Industry is a key employment generator and currentl y employs more then 29 million 
people both directly and indirectly. Every vehicle,  produced generates secondary and 
tertiery employment, hence, the sector’s multiplier  effect on economic activity is 
considerable. Keeping in view the relevance of the sector to the over all growth of the 
Indian economy, the Committee, during examination o f DFG (2015-16), heard some 
select Auto Associations.    
 
19. During the interaction, the Committee came acro ss the following issues and 
demands: 
 According to the Associations, while considering F TAs, the Government should 
pursue agreements with countries that can provide m arkets for products of Indian 
companies; introduction of single export incentive schemes with adequate rate to 
compensate for logistic and infrastructure disadvan tage; lower excise duty regime for 
vehicles produced in India; one time incentive sche me for retirement of old vehicles and 

to replace them with modern, safe, fuel efficient v ehicles; revisiting of Micro, Small & 
Medium Enterprises (MSME) definition with enhanceme nt of P&M limits; formulation 
of Research & Development (R&D) and technologic upg radation schemes for Auto 
component industry; etc.    
 
20.  The Committee noted that though many of the problem s faced by the auto 
sector have been lingering on, the representatives of the Auto associations were 
appreciative of the initiatives taken and support p rovided by DHI. Accordingly, the 
Committee observes that the Department should furth er their efforts in this 
direction and apprise the Committee on the feasibil ity and practicality of taking 
action on the various demands placed by the associations before it.  
 
NATRiP 
 
 
21. The National Automotive Testing R & D Infrastru cture Project (NATRiP), 
approved by the Government in 2005, envisaged setti ng up of world-class automotive 
testing and homologation facilities in India with a  total investment of Rs.  1,718 crore. 
However, right from its inception, the execution of  the NATRiP has been badly delayed 
and has suffered cost escalation due to several rea sons.  The project got extended to 2012  
and thereafter, the Cabinet again extended the time  line for completion of the project up 
to 31 
st  December, 2014.  The revised cost of the project w hich was to be completed by 
December 2014 was Rs. 2288.06 crore. But the Commit tee notes with extreme concern 
that the completion of NATRiP still needed time.   
 
22. In reply to a question regarding reasons which necessitated further extension of 
time for completion and the cost escalation, DHI su bmitted that initially scheduled date 
of completion of NATRiP project was April, 2011, wh ich was extended to December, 
2012 and further to December, 2014.  The proposal f or further extension till December 
2017 was under process. The project had faced hurdl es in the initial stages on account of 
delays in acquisition of land at various sites, del ays in clearances, shifting of utilities, 
contractual complications, etc. First Revised Cost Estimate (RCE-I) of NATRIP was 
approved by CCEA in April, 2011 at Rs.2288.06 crore . During the implementation of the 
project as per RCE-I approval, it was observed that  the escalation for land cost and the 

increased cost of Civil and Infrastructure works fo r the project were captured only 
partially and there was also foreign exchange variation during the period including delays 
in implementation. The RCE-II proposal of NATRIP wi th proposed cost at Rs.3827.30 
crore with the revised date of completion of the pr oject as 31 st  December, 2017 is under 
the consideration and will be shortly submitted to CCEA for approval. 
  Main reasons for cost overrun were:  
• Increase due to price escalation (Rs.80.80 cr.),  
• Foreign exchange variation (Rs.115.09 cr.),  
• Change in scope (Rs.75.88 cr.),  
• Additions/deletions (Rs.247.23 cr.),  
• Under estimation of certain items in RCE-I (Rs.190.28 cr.),  
• Land acquisition (Rs.145.45 cr.) and  
• Other reasons (Rs.684.51 cr. mainly due to terminat ion of M/s. IVRCL 
contracts for non-performance at risk and cost basis.   
 
23. During 2014-15, an amount of Rs 241.91 crore was released to NATRIP till Oct. 
2014. Since the project completion date expired on 31.12.2014, further fund could not be 
released to NATRIP. As part of the examination of D FG 2015-16, the Department 
submitted before the Committee that Rs 300 Crore ha s been earmarked to NATRiP as 
loan during 2015-16. 
 
24. The Committee notes with apprehension that NATR iP, which was originally 
thought to be completed in 6 years from 2005, after  the expiry of two extensions 
spanning three years from 2012 to 2014, still needs  three more years, i.e., upto 2017, 
for completion.  Having taken cognizance of all sta ted reasons for delays, the 
Committee, still feels that even a flagship project  like NATRiP, which has delivered 
a lot of unprecedented results, could not escape ti me and cost overruns which are 
typical banes of the Indian administrative system.  The Committee is of the 
considered opinion that NATRiP sans the delays woul d have produced more 
spectacular results. Therefore, the Committee recom mends that every effort should 
be made to successfully wind up the project by the current period of extension being 
sought.  The details of the completion of NATRiP sh ould be presented to the 
Committee in the form of a comprehensive Status Report.  
 

Scheme for Testing Infrastructure and R & D Projects for Electric Vehicles  
  
25. The Scheme for Testing Infrastructure and R & D  Projects for Electric Vehicles 
aims to achieve national fuel security by promoting  hybrid and electric vehicles in the 
country with the ambitious target of 6-7 million un it of sales of such vehicles in next 6 
years, i.e, up to 2020. The thrust for the Govt. through this scheme will be to allow hybrid 
and electric vehicles to become the first choice fo r the purchasers so that these vehicles 
can replace the conventional vehicles and thus redu ce liquid fuel consumption in the 
country from the automobile sector. It is expected to save 9500 Million Liters of crude oil 
equivalent to Rs 62000 Crore savings. The scheme co nceptualized by Department of 
Heavy Industry under National Electric Mobility Mission Plan (NEMMP) 2020 approved 
by Ministry of Finance for implementation from 1 
st  April 2015 onwards. The Govt. has 
launched the phase 1 of the Scheme in the Union Bud get for 2015-16, with an initial 
outlay of Rs 75 Crore. 
 
26. This scheme is in alignment with the “Make in I ndia” initiative of the 
Government. It has also been informed by the Depart ment that the Govt. plans to 
sensitize buyers while purchasing these hybrid and electric vehicles by providing 
monetary support. Under this mechanism, the manufac turer will reduce the purchase 
price of a hybrid and electric vehicles at the time  of selling to the buyer, and the same 
will be reimbursed to them by the Government. In it s first Phase, the scheme shall be

35. The Scheme for Enhancement of Competitiveness i n the Capital Goods Sector 
aims to make the Indian Capital Goods sector global ly competitive by addressing the issues of 
lack of technology and innovation, lack of industry  academia connectivity for technology 
development, non-availability of adequate infrastru ctural facility on value chain in 
manufacturing, absence of test and certification ce ntres etc. The Scheme envisages 
infrastructural intervention through Scheme components like setting up of (i) Centre of 
Excellence for technology development, (ii) Integra ted industrial infrastructure centre, 

(iii) Common Engineering Facility centre   (iv) Tes t & Certification centre and (v) 
Technology Acquition Fund.  
 
36. The Scheme, with an outlay of Rs. 2360 crore wa s proposed to Planning 
Commission in 2012- 13, i.e, during the 1 
st  year of the 12 th  Five Year Plan (2012-17). 
DHI proposed a requirement of Rs.10, 600.26 crore a s Plan budgetary support including

Commission had approved the scheme in principle. 
 
37. Regarding the delay in the approval of the sche me, DHI submitted that the 
process for obtaining approval of the Expenditure F inance Committee (EFC) was 
initiated soon after the Planning Commission commun icated it’s ‘in principle approval’.  
In a review meeting chaired by the then Secretary, HI  on 20.3.2013 it was decided to

the exception that funding by GoI for Test and Cert ification Centre should be 100% as

Corporation (UPSIDC) at Industrial Area Utelwa, Jag dishpur, Amethi District, UP. 
Pursuant to the allotment order cited above, JPML h ad paid EMD of Rs.11.8 Crores, 
towards the allotment of 187.59 Acres of Land and a n MoU was signed on  14.12.13 
between  UPSIDC and JPML. 
 
66. Subsequently, it was learnt from UPSIDC that Ka mala Neheru Memorial Trust 
has filed writ petition before the Hon’ble High Cou rt Lucknow Bench against allotment 
of land and the Hon’ble High Court had passed an or der dt.27.11.13 which states as 
follows:- 
           “as a interim measure we direct the part ies to maintain status quo as it exists   
today. The respondents shall ensure no construction  is raised over the land in question by 
fresh allottee. Allotment made shall be subject to further orders passed by this court.”  
 
67. Considering that the matter regarding allotment  of land to JPM is sub- judice and 
was pending before the Hon’ble Supreme court and also the fact that the project itself had 
the CCEA approval in the year 2008, the Committee s ought to know the timeframe 
further required for the commencement of the Greenf ield Pulp and Paper project at 
Jagdishpur under HPC.  The Committee also wanted to  know the possibility of 
considering an alternate location for the Mill.  In  reply, the Department stated that it 
appears the even after the present  legal battle, t here was a likelihood of litigation in the 
future from the farmers cultivating the land and the encroachers of the allotted land as the 
same is not free from encumbrances as per the averm ents made by Kamala Nehru Trust 
(KNT).  
 
68. The present litigating parties i.e. Kamala Nehr u Trust and UPSIDC have been 
fighting the legal battle from the year 2007. Recen tly, UPSIDC has communicated an 
offer to procure land from farmers and allot to JPML, if JPML was agreeable to the offer. 

The said offer of acquisition of land and allotting  the same after acquisition also may not 
be free from litigation and also quite time consumi ng. Under the circumstances, DHI has 
informed that it was not be possible to give a defi nite time frame for settlement of 
litigation and acquisition of land without encumbrances in a case wherein JPML was only 
an impleaded party and as such the settlement of li tigation would much depend on the 
main litigation parties i.e. Kamala Nehru Trust and UPSIDC. Meanwhile, attempts to find 
alternate sites to locate the project duly consider ing the raw material availability, and 
other infrastructure, were being made. 
 
69. The Committee takes note of the fact that the Green field Pulp and Paper 
Mill project at Jagdishpur, estimated at Rs. 3700 crore in 2008, has not taken off the 
ground as on date due to litigation regarding allot ment of land for the project at the 
designated site. The matter being sub judice, the C ommittee refrains from making a 
comment in this regard. Nevertheless, the Committee  recommends that nothing 
should stop the Department from earnestly exploring  the possibility of establishing 
the Mill at an alternate location taking into accou nt factors like raw material 
availability and other linkages. The Committee is o f the considered opinion that it is 
unacceptable to let an important project worth Rs. 3700 crore linger on indefinitely 
without its operationalisation being expected in near future.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE-AT A

1. During the oral evidence in connection with the exa mination of Demands for 
Grants  (DFG) 2015-16, it was submitted before the Committe e that the problems 
related to Coal linkage, delayed environmental clea rness and low demand had a 
negative impact on the performance of CPSEs dealing  in Heavy Industries Sector.  
However, the Committee expects a better performance  from the CPSEs of DHI as 
the teething issues like coal availability which ha s been hampering the growth of the 
power sector is being resolved through successful c oal block allocations.  This 
coupled with the favorable initiatives like the ‘Ma ke in India’ campaign of the 
Government can be presumed to provide the CPSEs of DHI with a conducive 
environment to regain the lost ground. 
(Para No 5) 
2. Further, the Committee observes that NPPC has ha d ‘Nil’ performance for 
the past four years and is not expected to open its  account even in 2015-16.  
However, it has been submitted that BIFR has given the final approval for the 
Modified Draft Rehabilitation Scheme (MDRS) for NPP C and the Revival process 
of the company will be completed by November, 2016.   The Committee wants to be 
informed of the progress in this regard. 
(Para No 6) 
 
3. Continuing the trend that can be observed over t he last two years, the 
Committee finds that for the year 2015-16 too DHI h as been given lesser Plan funds 
compared to the Non Plan allocations. As per the current classification of funds, this 
trend points towards less asset creation activities  under DHI year after year. 
Accordingly, in view of the Committee, the activiti es of DHI are gradually tilting 
towards non creative activities like winding up of CPSEs, settling dues, managing 
VRS, etc.  Though there might be a contrary view th at winding up of sick CPSEs 
also contributes indirectly to asset creation and e fficient channelisation of available 
resources, as per extant classification of Plan and  Non Plan activities, it does not 
count as a productive activity.   
(Para No 9) 

 
4. Accordingly, the Committee expresses its concern  over the fact that the Plan 
activities of DHI are either stagnating or getting reduced.  This would also mean 
that the Department has largely been unable to come  up with schemes necessary to 
cater to the varying needs of the extremely important Heavy Industries Sector in the 
country.  In view of the above, the Committee obser ves that DHI should strive more 
to come up with Plan schemes in a proactive manner by gauging the needs of the 
Heavy Industries Sector. Such initiatives will faci litate business environment in the 
sector.  
(Para No 10) 
 
5. The Committee feels unhappy to note that the Pla n funds allocated for 
crucial input studies that could have benefited the  flagship schemes of DHI could 
not be undertaken due to ‘procedural formalities’.  The study and analysis could 
have shed more light on many major concerns of Capi tal Goods sector and Auto 
Component Industry. Taking the above into account a nd the fact that the Plan 
allocation to DHI has been observed to show a decre asing trend, the Committee 
recommends that the Department should take proactiv e steps to carryout the in-
depth study for critical analysis as well as impact  assessment of Capital Goods 
sector and Auto component Industry. The Committee i s also of the strong view that 
such studies should be done with more frequency, wh ich would in turn help DHI, 
the administrative Department of such crucial secto rs, to come up with innovative 
schemes and thus overcome the hurdles plaguing their sector.       
(Para No 13) 
 
 
6. The Committee observes that there is a persisten t delay in reimbursing the 
claims of CPM of HPC. Considering the fact that the  Mill is already facing financial 
difficulties arising out of logistical challenges, the Committee recommends that DHI 
should augment the process of settling the admissib le transportation cost of CPM in 
a more efficient manner without delay, keeping in m ind that the extra cost in 
transportation is being expected to end by Septembe r, 2016.  The Committee is of 
the view that the timely settlement of the liabilit y would help CPM to start afresh 

from October, 2016 onwards, without financial backl ogs. As a CPSE, with 
considerable presence in the NER, HPC should receiv e special attention of DHI in 
its efforts to operate CPM, effectively and profitably. 
(Para No 17)

(Para No 20)
