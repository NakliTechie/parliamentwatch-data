REPORT 
 
 The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) sector has emerged as a highly 
vibrant and dynamic sector of the Indian economy over the last five decades. It contributes 
significantly in the economic and social development of the country by fostering 
entrepreneurship and generating large employment opportunities at comparatively lower 
capital cost, next only to agriculture. The MSMEs are complementary to large industries a s 
ancillary units and  this sector contributes significantly in the inclusive industrial 
development of the country.  The MSMEs are widening their domain across sectors of the 
economy, producing diverse range of products and services to meet demands of domes tic as 
well as global markets. As informed by the Ministry of MSME, the MSME sector has 
contributed 27 per cent to GDP in the FY2020-21, 36 per cent to Manufacturing Output in 
the FY2020-21, second largest employer after agriculture with 6.34 crore MSMEs providing 
employment to 11.10 crore persons and contributed 45 per cent to Export in the FY2021-22. 
  
2. The MSMEs have been contributing significantly to  the expansion of entrepreneurial 
endeavors through business innovations. The MSMEs in India are also playing a crucial role 
by providing large employment opportunities at comparatively lower capital cost than large 
industries as well as through industrialization of rural & backward  areas, inter alia, reducing 
regional imbalances, assuring more equitable d istribution of  national income and  wealth. 
Besides, the Ministry is working towards an agenda of inclusive development, and has taken 
various initiatives and measures to ensure that demographically as well as geographically 
weaker sections are benefitted for their overall development. 
  
Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
 
3. The Ministry of Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (MSME) envisions a 
progressive MSME sector by promoting growth and development of the Sector, including 
Khadi, Village and  Coir Industries, in cooperation with concerned Ministries/Departments, 
State Governments and other Stakeholders, through providing support to existing 
enterprises, adopting cutting edge technologies and encouraging creation of new 
enterprises. A number of statutory and non-statutory bodies work under the aegis of the 
Ministry of MSME. These include the Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) 
and the Coir Board besides National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC), National 
Institute for  Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (NIMSME) and Mahatma Gandhi 
Institute for Rural Industrialisation (MGIRI).

New Definition of MSME 
 
5. In accordance with the provision of Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises 
Development (MSMED) Act, 2006, the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) are 
classified as below: 
(i) a micro enterprise, where the investment in plan t and machinery or equipment 
does  not exceed one crore rupees and turnover does not exceed five crore 
rupees; 
(ii) a small enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or equipment 
does  not exceed ten crore rupees and turnover does not exceed fifty crore 
rupees; and 
(iii) a medium enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or 
equipment  does not exceed fifty crore rupees and turnover does not exceed 
two hundred and fifty  crore rupees. 
 
6. The new classification has come into effect from 1st July, 2020. The earlier criteria 
of classification of MSMEs under MSMED Act, 2006 were based on investment in plant 
and machinery/equipment. It was different for manufacturing and service units. It was also  
very low in terms of financial limits. Since then , the economy has undergone significant  
changes. A revision in MSME criteria of classification was announced under Atma Nirbhar  
Bharat package on 13th May, 2020. This has been done in order to be realistic with time 

3  
and to establish an objective system of classification and to provide ease of doing business. 
The turnover with respect to exports will not be counted in the limits of turnover for any 
category of MSME units which will offer major relief to the exporters. 
 
7. The primary responsibility of promotion and development of MSMEs is of the State 
Governments. However, the Government of India, supplements efforts of the State 
Governments through various initiatives. The role of the Ministry of MSME and its 
organisations is to assist the States in their efforts to encourage entrepreneurship, 
employment and livelihood opportunities and enhance the competitiveness of MSMEs in  
the changed economic scenario. 
 
8. The Committee is of the view that the new classification of MSMEs would bring 
about positive changes in the MSME Sector including many benefits that will aid 
MSMEs to grow in size  by attracting investments and would generate more 
employment opportunities. In this regard, in the deposition by the Secretary, Ministry 
of MSME on 20.02.2023 before the Committee, it emerged that although the change in 
definition of MSMEs is for their benefit, however,  a study is required for sector-wise 
segregation of  the MSM Es. The Committee  recommends that the Minist ry should 
carry out a study for sector -wise segregation of  the MSMEs in order to ensure 
targeted distribution of budgetary allocation s which w ould facilitate development, 
expansion and growth of the MSME sector.  
 
Dream of Five Trillion Dollar Economy and the MSME Sector 
 
9.      To achieve the set target of becoming the $5 trillion economy by 2024, t he 
Government is implementing various schemes, programmes and initiatives for the 
promotion, development and enhancing the competiveness of MSME sector in the country. 
A number of recent initiatives have been taken to strengthen, support, finance , revive and 
reconstruct MSMEs such as:  
(i) Rs. 5 lakh crore Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (ECLGS), for 
businesses, including MSMEs was announced as a part of Atma Nirbhar Bharat 
Package with initial guarantee coverage of Rs. 3 lakh crore on 13.05.2020 with 
validity of scheme up to 30.11.2020. Subsequently, this was extended with 
infusion of Rs. 1.5 lakh crore on 29.09.2021 with validity of scheme up to 
31.03.2022 and with infusion of Rs. 50,000 crore on 01.02.2022 with validity of 
scheme up to 31.03.2023.  
(ii) Rs. 50,000 crore equity infusion through Self Reliant India (SRI) Fund. This 
scheme has a provision of Rs.10,000 crore from Government of India and Rs. 

4  
40,000 cro re leverage through Private Equity/Venture Capital funds. This 
initiative is aimed at providing growth capital to the deserving and eligible units 
of MSME sector.  
(iii) Credit Guarantee Scheme for Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs), for providing 
collateral free loan up to a limit of Rs. 200 lakh to MSEs with guarantee coverage 
up to 85 per cent  for various categories of loans. Further, Budget 2023 -24, has 
announced the infusion of Rs. 9,000 crore in the corpus of CGTMSE to enable 
additional credit of Rs. 2.00 lakh crore with a reduced cost of credit.   
(iv) New revised criteria for classification of MSMEs. 
(v) No global tenders for procurement up to Rs. 200 crore. 
(vi) “Udyam Registration” for MSMEs, for Ease of Doing Business w.e.f. 1.7.2020 
(vii) Launching of an online Portal “Ch ampions” in June, 2020 to cover many aspects 
of e-governance including grievance redress and handholding of MSMEs. 
(viii) Inclusion of Retail and Wholesale traders as MSMEs w.e.f. 2.7. 2021. 
(ix) Non-tax benefits extended for 3 years in case of an upward change in sta tus of 
MSMEs. 
(x) Launch of Udyam Assist Platform on 11.1.2023 to bring Informal Micro 
Enterprises (IMEs) under the formal ambit for availing the benefits under Priority 
Sector Lending.  
(xi) The Ministry of MSME  vide Notification no. S.O. 5670(E) dated 9.11.2018, had 
made it mandatory for all Central Government Ministries/Departments/ CPSEs to 
procure at least 25  per cent of their annual procurement from MSEs, including 3  
per cent from MSEs owned by women entrepreneurs vis-a-vis the target of 25 per 
cent, Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) procured 34  per cent from Micro 
& Small Enterprises in 2022-23.  
(xii) Field offices of Ministry of MSME organize various awareness campaigns for 
UDYAM registration  and benefits of Go vernment schemes /programmes, in 
coordination with MSME/ Industry Departments of States/UTs concerned and 
other MSME stakeholders through physical workshops, social media, print media 
& electronic media for creating wide publicity for increasing participat ion from 
all stake holders.  
(xiii) To address the issue of delayed payment to MSMEs, RBI has issued guidelines 
for setting up and operating the Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) 
to facilitate the financing of trade receivables of MSMEs from corporate and 
other buyers, includ ing government departments and P ublic Sector Undertakings 
(PSUs) through multiple financiers electronically.

10. The Committee welcomes the goal of the Government for making India a $5 
trillion economy by the Year 2024-25 and opines that the target can be achieved by 
growth and development of the MSME sector. As MSME sector is vital for fostering 
entrepreneurship and generating self -employment opportunities at comparatively 
lower capital cost  and are complementary to large indu stries as ancillary units , 
formulation of targeted policies in the areas of infrastructure development, 
technological upgradation and forward linkage s could help MSMEs to achieve their 
full potential and propel the Indian economy to a higher growth traject ory. The 
Committee recommends that Government need to play a proactive role in promoting 
MSME Sector by handholding the MSME s at the grassroots level and for this , a top 
down approach needs to be implemented right  down to the last tier of the MSME 
sector in letter, spirit and action.  In this regard, a roadmap should be devised to 
achieve the desired results.  
 
Demands for Grants (2023-24) 
 
11. While making presentation on the DFG (2023-24) (Demand No. 68) , the Secretary, 
Ministry of MSME gave an overview about the Ministry and informed the Committee that 
the MSME Sector is providing livelihood to around 11.10  crore people. The Ministry also 
apprised the Committee about the challenges in the MSME Sector viz. (i) Formalization and 
Inclusion; (ii) Access to f inance; (iii) Access to market ; (iv) Access to technology ; (v) 
Access to digitalization  (vi) Infrastructural bottlenecks  and (vii) Inadequate 
skilling/reskilling.  
 
12. The Ministry also highlighted Vision India @ 2047 with regard to the MSME Sector, 
viz. (i) bring in all MSMEs into the formal sector and ensure access to affordable credit; (ii)  
technology for all; (iii) create a sustainable and globally competitive KVI sector for 
generating gainful employment and sustainable livelihoods; (iv) enhance servi ce sector 
through capacity  building/skilling; (v) gender parity in entrepreneurship; and (vi) ensure 
access to  markets and enhance MSMEs  share of exports.

17. The Committee observes that in many sub -heads, nil expenditure was incurred 
and re-appropriation of funds was resorted to  at the revised estimates stage . Although 
the re -appropriation of  funds for some of the schemes were got approved through 
Standing Finance Committee (SFC) at the end of the financial year , but the funds  
remained unutilized.  The Committee is of the view that it is not a healthy sign and 
such practices should be avoided as far as possible in future. 
 
18. It was observed that the allocations for the FY2022-23 of the Ministry were 
either decreased or increased at the revised estimate stage, but the actual expenditure 
incurred is less than the Revised Estimate. The Committee is of the view that scheme-
wise estimation of fund requirement s should be carried out effectively and sincerel y 
well in advance to ensure that budget allocations are utilized optimally. 
 
19. The Committee notes that for the FY2022-23, the Ministry was allocated Rs. 
21422.00 crore which was reduced to  Rs. 15628.71 at the RE Stage and the A ctual 
Expenditure up to the 31st January, 2023  stands at only Rs. 11112.01 crore which is 
71.1 per cent of the RE allocation.  The Committee is of serious concern in this regard 
and would like to be apprised about the reasons for such low utilization of allocations. 
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry should take necessary steps to utilize 
the balance amount of 29 per cent during the remaining period of the financial year in 
consonance with the Financial Rules . The Ministry should henceforth  rationalize its 
expenditure throughout the year to avoid uneven spending during the last quarter of 
the financial year.  
 
20. The Committee notes that an allocation of Rs. 199.88  crore was made at RE 
stage for the SME Division for the FY  2022-23 and the Actual Expenditure up to the 
31st January, 2023 stands at Rs. 118.46 crore, which is only 59.26 per cent of the RE 
allocations. As regards to ARI Division, the  RE for the FY 2022-23 was Rs. 3321.46 
crore whereas the Actual Expenditure up to the 31st January, 2023 is Rs. 2664.31 
crore, which is 80.21 per cent  of the RE allocation. In  case of  Office of DC-
MSME also, the RE was Rs. 12152.37 crore and the Actual Expenditure up to the 31st 
January, 2023 is only Rs. 8329.24 crore which clearly shows that the  DC-MSME could 
utilize only 68 per cent  of the RE for FY 2022 -23. The Committee would like to be 
apprised about the reasons for such low utilization of the allocations made and 
suggests to the Ministry that a systematic expenditure pattern should be devised to 
streamline its expenditure in future.

21. The Committee notes that the BE for FY2023 -24 is Rs. 22137.95 crore, which is 
a slight increase from the BE of Rs. 21,422 crore for the FY2022 -23. Out of this , an 
allocation of Rs.  14,100 crore has been earmarked for  the Guaranteed Emergency 
Credit Line Scheme (GECL) and, only Rs. 8037.95 crore is left for various other 
schemes of the Ministry for the FY2023-24. In absence    of adequate funds, it would be 
difficult for the Ministry to implement its various schemes  effectively in the present 
scenario when the country had undergone the problems of COVID-19 Pandemic which 
hit the MSME Sector badly. The Committee recommends that  the Ministry  should 
ask for enhanced allocation at the RE Stage for proper implementation of its  flagship 
Schemes with a view to strengthening the MSME Sector.

Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) 
 
24. During the course of the discussions on the Demand for FY2023 -24, the Ministry 
informed that the Khadi & Village Industries Commission (KVIC) is a Statutory 
Organization under the aegis  of the Ministry of MSME, engaged in promoting and 
developing Khadi and Village Industries for providing employment opportunities in the rural 
areas, thereby strengthening the rural economy. KVIC has also been identified as one of the 
major organizations in the decentralized  sector for generating sustainable non-farm 
employment opportunities in rural areas at a low per capita investment. It undertakes 
activities like skill improvement, transfers of  technology, research & development, 
marketing, etc., and helps in generating employment/ self-employment opportunities in rural 
areas.  
 
25. The Committee was informed that the main objectives of KVIC include (i) the social 
objective of providing employment in rural areas in general and urban areas  through 
District Industries Center (DIC) particularly; (ii) the economic objective of producing 
saleable articles; and (iii) the wider objective of creating self-reliance amongst people and 
building up a strong   rural community spirit. 
 
26. Further, the Committee was informed that o ver 2935 Khadi Institutions form the vast 
networks which are  implementing the  programmes of KVIC in India. Over 4.97 Lakh 
people are engaged in this activity , o ut of  which, over 80  percent are women  Artisans. 
Khadi is the unique programme of KVIC and a potent tool for creation of employment at  the 
Artisans‟ doorstep being implemente d by Khadi Institutions. The assistance provided

Certificate (ISEC) Scheme is enabling Khadi Institutions to provide employment 
opportunities.

Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) 
  
36. During the course of discussion on the Demand for FY2023 -24, the Ministry 
informed that the PMEGP is a bank appraised and financed credit linked subsidy programme 
for generation of employment opportunities through establishment of non-farm based micro 
enterprises for self-employment in rural as well as urban areas of the country. KVIC is the 

19  
single Nodal Agency at the National level to implement the scheme across the country, and 
at State/District level the implementing agencies are the KVIC and State Khadi and Village 
Industries Boards (State KVIBs) in rural areas, and  District Industries Centers (DICs) in both 
rural and urban areas of the country, and Coir Board for coir related activities. 
 
37. The Ministry also apprised the Committee that the maximum cost of the project under 
the scheme in Manufacturing Sector is Rs. 50.00 Lakh and in Service Sector Rs. 20.00 lakh. 
Margin Money subsidy varies from 15  per cent  to 35  per cent  for different  categories. 
Scheme is available for setting up of new units only. Since inception in 2008-09 and up to 
31.12.2022, a total of about 8.37 lakh micro enterprises have been assisted with a  margin 
money subsidy of Rs. 20,775.00 Crore and provided  employment to about  68.05 lakh 
persons. During the year 2022-23, a target has been fixed for setting up of 101006 
PMEGP units, by disbursing Rs. 2954.58 Crore as margin money subsidy, and generating 
estimated employment    opportunities for 8.08 lakh persons.

Credit Support/Guarantee Schemes 
 
95. The Credit Support/Guarantee Scheme for MSMEs is a Government initiative 
designed to provide financial assistance to MSMEs. To implement the scheme, Credit 
Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) was jointly set up by 
Ministry of MSME and Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) to ensure 
flow of institutional credit to the Micro and Small Enterprises  (MSEs). Credit Guarantee 
Scheme (CGS) was launched to strengthen credit delivery system and to facilitate flow of 
credit to th e MSE sector. Through this scheme, the guarantee cover is provided for 
collateral free credit facility extended by Member Lending Institutions (MLIs) to the new as 
well as existing MS Es. The maximum loan limit has been enhanced from Rs. 100 lakh to 
Rs. 200 lakh and corpus of this fund has also been enhanced from Rs. 2500 crore to 7500 
crore.  
 
96. The Committee was apprised that scheme , which is administered by SIDBI,  provides 
access to credit from banks and other financial institutions to MSEs, and also pr ovides a 
guarantee to the lenders against default  which reduces the risk of lending to MSEs . Under 
the scheme, MSEs can avail of credit up to Rs. 5 crore with a repayment period of up to 5 
years. The scheme also  provides collateral-free loans, which means that the borrower does 
not need to provide any security or collateral to avail of the loan. The scheme is aimed at 
helping MSEs access to credit at competitive interest rates and also to reduce the risk of 
lending to MSEs. The scheme also helps MSEs to exp and their businesses and creation of 
new employment opportunities. The scheme is available to all the MSEs operating in the 
manufacturing, services or agricultural sectors. 
 
97. The CGTMSE has covered more than 67 lakh beneficiaries so far and t he 
beneficiaries have experienced a boost in their turnover as well as employment generation 
a f t e r  approval of CGTMSE funding. I t  h a s a positive impact on six major areas in the 
MSE sector, viz. technology  upgradation, skill upgradation, market development, 
sustainability of scheme, economic impact, and social impact. The scheme has been 
successful in spanning itself geographically across the country with a special focus in  the 
North East. The benefits of the scheme have also reached more than 100  industrial sectors 
in which MSEs are operating. The beneficiaries are not just limited to major industrial 
hubs but are also spread even in tier 3 towns.  CGTMSE has been highly effective in settling 
the claims wherein the first  installment was settled within one month in majority of the 
cases. Following are the eligibility criteria for credit guarantee under CGTMSE: 

40  
• New or existing Micro and Small Enterprises [MSEs] engaged in 
manufacturing, service, retail trade, wholesale trade & educational Institutions 
activities. 
• Collateral security and third party guarantee free credit facility up to Rs. 200 
lakh extended to the MSEs. 
• In the partial collateral security model, the MLIs are allowed to obtain 
collateral security for a part of the credit facility, whereas for the  remaining 
unsecured part of the credit facility, up to a maximum of Rs. 200 lakh, can be 
covered under Credit Guarantee Scheme of CGTMSE. 
• For the credit facilities extended by MLIs and covered under Credit Guarantee 
Scheme of CGTMSE, CGTMSE extends guaran tee coverage which ranges 
from 75% to 85  per cent  as applicable under the scheme depending on the 
category of the borrower and credit limit.  
• As on date, CGTMSE has 150 Member Lending Institutions (MLIs) including 
all Public-Sector Banks, some of Private S ector and Foreign Banks, NBFCs, 
State Financial Institutions, Small Finance Banks, RRBs, Co-operative Banks.
