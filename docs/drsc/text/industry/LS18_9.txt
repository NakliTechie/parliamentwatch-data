While explaining the causes for decline in the production the Performance Budget states:­
"Delay in reconstruction of BF2 under the modernisation scheme by M/s. Tiazpromex­
port Jed consortium due to political disturbances in erstwhile USSR prolonged testing time 
due to subsequent indigenisation of supplies and mishap in BF1 have severely affected the 
production at Durgapur. Low coking coal stocks all through the year leading to frequent 
blend changes and high and widely fluctuating ash indigenous coking coal affected the 
productivity at the blast furnaces. Inconsistent and restricted power supply from the grid, 
particularly from DVC to Bokaro resulted in a severe Joss of production. The major 
disruption in power supply from DVC to Bokaro on 16th September caused considerable 
damage to equipment and its impact continued for nearly a month. Rourkela operations 
suffered a set back due to flooding resulting from unprecedented heavy rains on 14th 
August, 1993. 
However, the production of saleable steel will be as per target." 
The Committee is not convinced that delay in reconstruction of BF2 under the modernisation 
scheme at Durgapur due to political changes in erstwhile USSR was the cause for lower 
production at the plant. After all the political changes in erstwhile USSR had not occurred all of 
a sudden. There were indications that the erstwhile USSR may undergo political changes. The 
SAIL and Government should have prepared alternative plan to meet the eventuality but they 
opted to remain static to the happening in erstwhile USSR without bothering for its impact on 
the production of steel in the plant. 
The committee is surprised to find that low coking coal stocks throughout the year and ash 
contents of the indigenous coking coal also affected the productivity of blast furnaces. It is a 
matter of regret that SAIL which is largest producers of steel not only in the country but also in 
the World did not maintain enougl'l coking coal stocks at its major plants and also could not 
distinguish the ash contents of coal supplied to its plants indigenously. 
Similarly, a strange reason has been given for the less production in Rourkela. It is stated 
that Rourkela operations suffered a set back due to flooding resulting from unprecedented heavy 
rains on 14th August, 1993. The Committee is not convinced with this alibi of SAIL that such a 
prestigious plant which once had the state-of -the-art technology in the field of LD process could 
not withstand a downpour and submerged in the flood waters. 
The Committee, therefore, recommem,Is that SAIL should instil a sense of work culture in its 
plants so that production targets are achieved through concerted e~forts ratl)er than blaming 
natura) calamity and other causes for its decline in production. Of course, inconsistent and 
restricted power supply from the grid; particularly from DVC to Bokaro might have resulted in a 
severe loss of production but the performance budget is silent about the steps taken by SAIL to 
overcome this situation. The Committee, therefore, recommends that matter be taken up with the 
Ministry of Power on priority for the release of enough power for the steel plants in the country or 
as an alternative possibility of private participation for generating power exclusively for steel plants 
may also be explored by the Government.

The objectives of SDF are financing capital schemes, projects and other capital expenditure
