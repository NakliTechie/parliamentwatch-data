CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
 
The Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI)  is crucial in driving industrial growth, fostering 
technological advancements, and promoting self -reliance in the manufacturing sector. It 
formulates policies and implements schemes to strengthen key industries such as the automotive, 
heavy engineering equipment and machine tools, and heavy electrical industries.  
The automotive sector, a key pillar of the economy, contributes 6.8% to India’s GDP and 
accounts for 40% of the manufacturing GDP. The capital goods sector, comprising heavy 
engineering equipment and machine  tools, constitutes 12% of the total manufacturing output, 
translating to 1.8% of GDP. The heavy electrical industry further adds a 1% share to the GDP. 
Given their substantial contributions, these sectors play a vital role in India's economic growth 
and industrial development. 
 
Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI) 
 
2. The Ministry administers three key industry sectors: the Automotive Industry, including 
Tractor and earth -moving equipment; the Heavy Engineering Equipment and Machine Tools 
Industry (HE&MT); a nd the Heavy Electrical Industry (HEI). It also administers 21 central 
public sector enterprises (CPSEs) and four autonomous bodies. Out of the 21 CPSEs, 16 are 
operational. The remaining 4 CPSEs are under closure, and one is non-operational. 
Budget Highlight (2025-26) 
3. The Union Budget 2025 -26 included 35 capital goods in the exemption notification for 
lithium-ion battery manufacturing for electric vehicles (EVs). Th e move is expected to 
significantly impact the automotive industry by promoting the adoption of indigenous advanced 
chemistry cells and strengthening the domestic EV ecosystem. 
4. After the Union Budget 2025 -26 presentation, the Detailed Demands for Grants (2025-
26) of the Ministry of Heavy Indust ries were referred to the Department -related Parliamentary 
Standing Committee on Industry for detailed examination and reporting. The Committee: 
i. Analysed background notes provided by the Ministry, budget documents from the 
Ministry of Finance, and other relevant materials to ensure a comprehensive assessment. 
ii. Examined various documents, including the Annual Report 2024 -25, budget documents 
such as the Details of Demands for Grants and the Output -Outcome Framework, and the 
Ministry’s replies to the questionnaires.  
iii. Deliberated on the Ministry's Demands for Grants in its meeting on 21st February, 2025, 
during which the Secretary, Ministry of Heavy Industries, and other senior officials  
provided a comprehensive PowerPoint presentation.

CHAPTER 3 
AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRY 
Overview of the Automotive Industry 
18. The Indian automotive industry is one of the fastest -growing sectors. Over the years, 
nearly all major global automobile manufacturers have established production facilities in India. 
As a result, vehicle production has surged from 2 million units in 1991 -92 to approximately 28 
million units in FY 2023 -24. India manufactures a wide range of vehicles, including two -
wheelers, three-wheelers, passenger cars, light commercial vehicles, trucks, buses, tractors, and 
heavy commercial vehicles. The production, sale s, and export figures for various vehicle 
categories during financial year 2023-24 are as follows:

19. As per the MHI, the Indian automotive industry has a t otal turnover of ₹20 lakh crore , 
contributing 6.8% to the country's GDP and 40% of manufacturing GDP.  The sector plays a 
crucial role in employment generation, providing direct employment to 42 lakh  individuals and 
indirect employment to approximately 265 lakh people. Regarding global trade, automobile 
exports stand at ₹2.9 lakh crore . Additionally, the industry is a significant contributor to the 
national exchequer, generating ₹2.93 lakh crore in GS T revenue, which accounts for 14% -15% 
of the total GST collection. 
 
20. The Committee observes that India holds a strong global position in automobile 
production, ranking 2 nd in 2-Wheelers, 1st in 3-Wheelers, 4th in Passenger Vehicles, and 5 th  
in Commercial Vehicles. However, despite high production volumes - 214.69 lakh 2 -
wheelers, 9.93 lakh 3 -wheelers, 49.02 lakh passenger vehicles, and 10.66 lakh commercial 
vehicles in FY 2023 -24, exports remain relatively low, particularly in passenger and  
commercial vehicles. 
 
21. The Committee recommends that the Ministry formulate targeted strategies to 
enhance global competitiveness, address export barriers, and strengthen trade agreements 
to boost international market penetration. Additionally, policy interventions should focus 
on improving domestic demand, incentivizing innovation, and ensuring the sustainable 
growth of all vehicle categories to reinforce India's leadership in the global automotive 
industry.

PM Electric Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement (PM E-DRIVE) 
22. The MHI informed that the Government launched a new scheme titled "PM Electric 
Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement (PM E-DRIVE)" from 1st October 2024 for 
two years. The earlier Electric Mobility Promotion Scheme (EMPS -2024), which was 
implemented from 1st April 2024 to 30 th September 2024, has been subsumed under the PM E -
DRIVE scheme. Consequently, the PM E-DRIVE scheme is effective from 1 st April 2024 to 31st 
March 2026 with a total outlay of  Rs. 10,900 crore. For the financial year  2025-26, a budgetary 
allocation of Rs. 4000 crore has been made for this scheme. 
23. The primary objective of the PM E-DRIVE scheme is to expedite the adoption of EVs by 
providing upfront incentives for their purchase  and by facilitating the establishment of essential 
charging infrastructure for EVs. The scheme also promotes mass mobility by supporting mean s 
of public transportation. The scheme aims to promote EVs to reduce the environmental impact of 
transportation, fuel security and improve air quality.

48. Electric vehicles run on batteries; currently, most of the batteries and associated raw 
materials are imported from China. As announced in the Union Budget 2025 -26, the 
Government's recent exemption of customs duties on 35 additional capital goods essential for 
lithium-ion battery manufacturing is poised to significantly impact the automotive industry 
regarding the use of indigenous advan ced chemistry cells.  As Lithium -Ion Cell manufacturing

are required to invest heavily , including on import of the latest Capital Goods required for 
manufacturing of ACCs. Due to lack of indigenous sourcing of capital goods, only way to get a 
competitive edge is by exempting customs duties on such critical  Capital Goods. The duty 
exemptions complement the PLI ACC scheme by incentivising domestic manufacturing of ACC 
technologies. This synergy is anticipated to strengthen India's position in the global EV market, 
making the country's automotive sector more competitive internationally.

competitive edge to domestic manufacturers. Additionally, their alignment with the PLI 
ACC scheme is expected to boost indigenous manufacturing, reduce import dependence, 
and enhance India’s position in the global EV market.

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE 
 
1. The Committee observes with dismay  that the Ministry could not fully utilise its 
allocated budget for the previous two financial years i.e. 2022 -23 and 2023 -24. In 2022-23, 
the Ministry utilized only 84.23% of RE, while in 2023 -24, the utilization further declined 
to 76.87%. In the current financial year 2024-25, only Rs. 1,750.16 crore out of the Revised 
Estimate (RE) allocation of Rs. 4,560.69 crore has been utilised, accounting for just 38.37% 
(as on 31.01.2025).  It recommends that the Ministry ensure the entire RE is fully utilised 
within the financial year. For the next FY 2025 -26, it recommends that the Ministry 
adheres to the Monthly Expenditure Plan (MEP)  and Quarterly Expenditure Plan (QEP) 
to ensure proper and timely utilisation. 
(Para 8) 
2. The Committee observes that u nder the flagship scheme of the Ministry – PM E-
Drive, a sum of Rs. 1870.76 crore was allocated in RE 2024 -25 against which the Ministry 
has only utilised Rs. 366.83 crore ( *under EMPS- subsumed in PM E -Drive) till 31 st 
January, 2025. It expresses serious concern over the underutilisation of funds under PM E-
Drive and the non-utilization under the Payment Security Mechanism. It recommends that 
the Ministry may take necessary steps to ensure utilisation of funds allocated at the RE 
stage. Additionally, the Ministry should avoid accumulation of the unspent funds in the last 
quarter of the financial year and refrain from spending mo re than 33% of the allocated 
amount during the last quarter and 15% in the last month of the Financial Year, as per the 
directions of Ministry of Finance.  
(Para 10) 
 
3. The Committee observes that for the Financial Year 2025 -26, the Ministry of Heavy 
Industries has been allocated a BE of Rs. 7,680.23 crore by the Ministry of Finance, which 
constitutes 85.09% of its projected requirement of Rs. 9,025.83 crore. The Ministry of 
Heavy Industries has informed the Committee that the FAME -II Scheme ended on 31 st 
March 2024, and the budget proposal of Rs. 722.00 crore under BE 2025 -26 was intended 
to meet payments for ongoing committed liabilities. However, the request was not acceded 
to. Given the pending liabilities, the Committee recommends reconsidering the allocation 
for the FAME -II Scheme at the RE stage to ensure the settlement of outstanding 
commitments. It further recommends that the Ministry make every effort to strategize and 
utilise the significant part of the allocations in a timely manner. After tha t, more funds 
should be allocated at the RE stage to implement the key schemes effectively. 
(Para 12) 
 

25 
 
4. The Committee observes that significant funds have been surrendered in key 
schemes such as PLI Scheme for the Automobile and Auto Component Industry a nd the 
Enhancement of Competitiveness in the Indian Capital Goods Sector since FY 2022 -23 to 
2023-24. The Committee urges the Ministry to analyse the underlying reasons for the 
inadequate receipt of proposals under the key schemes. Additionally, the Minist ry should 
identify and address procedural bottlenecks, particularly PFMS validation checks,  to 
expedite fund disbursement. Furthermore, the Ministry should formulate a strategic plan 
to enhance awareness and outreach through targeted campaigns, ensuring optimal use and 
evenly distribution throughout the year  of allocated funds and effectively contributing  to 
the vision of Viksit Bharat @ 2047. 
(Para 16) 
5.  The Committee notes that the Budget 2024 -25 announced the establishment of a 
joint venture between NTPC and BHEL to set up an 800 MW commercial plant utilizing 
Advanced Ultra Super Critical (AUSC) technology, with the govern ment providing 
necessary fiscal support. The Committee is pleased to know  that the site for the 800 MW 
AUSC technology-based power plant has been finalized at Korba, Chhattisgarh, and that 
the technical specifications and supply chain have also been finalized.  
(Para 17) 
6. The Committee observes that India holds a strong global position in automobile 
production, ranking 2 nd in 2-Wheelers, 1st in 3-Wheelers, 4th in Passenger Vehicles, and 5 th  
in Commercial Vehicles. However, despite high production volumes - 214.69 lakh 2 -
wheelers, 9.93 lakh 3 -wheelers, 49.02 lakh passenger vehicles, and 10.66 lakh commercial 
vehicles in FY 2023 -24, exports remain relatively low, particularly in passenger and  
commercial vehicles. 
(Para 20) 
 
7. The Committee recommends that the Ministry formulate targeted strategies to 
enhance global competitiveness, address export barriers, and strengthen trade agreements 
to boost international market penetration. Additionally, policy interventions should focus 
on improving domestic demand, incentivizing innovation, and ensuring the sustainable 
growth of all vehicle categories to reinforce India's leadership in the global automotive 
industry. 
(Para 21) 
 
8. The Committee notes that the budget allocation at the RE stage, amounting to Rs. 
1,870.76 crore, has reached only 19.61% utilisation as o n 12.02.2025. It recommends 

26 
 
therefore t hat the Ministry accelerate the implementation process and take proactive 
measures to ensure optimal budget utilisation in strict adherence to Financial Rules. 
(Para 25) 
 
9. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Heavy Industries reconsider the 
reduction in incentives for registered e -2 wheelers, e -Rickshaws, e-Carts, and e -3 wheelers 
(L5) in FY 2025-26, as the lower subsidy may slow down EV adoption in the country. Given 
the significant increase in the indicative number of vehicles to be supported across these 
segments, maintaining higher incentives would ensure sustained mark et growth, 
affordability, and alignment with the Government's clean mobility goals.  
(Para 28) 
10. The Committee observed that 4,57,840 claims were submitted on the PM E-DRIVE 
Portal. Of these, 2,26,099 claims (49.4%) had been incentivised as of 30 th November 2024. 
The Committee  recommends that the Ministry of Heavy Industries expedite processing 
pending incentive claims. The Committee also observed that while the e -2W and e-3W (L5) 
categories have achiev ed 64.3% and 100.3% of their respective targets, the extremely low 
performance of e -rickshaws & e -carts, meeting only 2.99% of the target, necessitates 
urgent holistic intervention. It recommends that the Ministry implement targeted 
awareness campaigns, facil itate financial access, and enhance infrastructure support to 
improve uptake in these segments.  Additionally, it recommends the establishment of a 
robust monitoring mechanism and regular stakeholder consultations to track progress and 
ensure the timely dis bursement of incentives. These measures will help align the 
implementation of the scheme with the Government’s vision for accelerated adoption of 
electric mobility. 
(Para 30) 
 
11. The Committee also notes that e -4 wheeler passenger vehicles and hybrid vehi cles 
have not been included in the PM E -DRIVE Scheme. Since 4-wheeler passenger vehicles 
constitute a significant share of the vehicle population, particularly in major cities, the 
Committee believes that incentivizing these categories would further accele rate the 
adoption of electric mobility in the country. Therefore, it recommends incorporating these 
vehicle categories into the scheme.  Additionally, it suggests introducing a subsidy 
mechanism linked to the scrapping of old diesel vehicles to encourage th eir phased 
replacement with electric vehicles , thereby promoting cleaner and sustainable 
transportation and ensuring a healthy environment. 
(Para 31) 
 

27 
 
12. The Committee acknowledges the steady growth in electric vehicle (EV) adoption,  
with the overall EV s hare rising from 5.30% in 2022 -23 to 7.32% in 2024 -25 (as on 
February 10). The significant penetration of electric three -wheelers (e -3Ws), surpassing 
57%, reflects a strong shift towards electrification in this segment. However, the growth of 
electric two -wheelers (e-2Ws) and electric four -wheelers (e-4Ws) remain moderate, while 
the adoption of electric buses and electric trucks continues to be limited. To accelerate this 
transition and work towards the country’s ambitious goal of 35% of total vehicle sales  
being electric by 2030, it recommends that the Ministry enhance policy support through 
targeted financial incentives, expansion of charging infrastructure particularly for e -2Ws 
and e-4Ws and promotion of localized manufacturing of key EV components. Addi tionally, 
a strategic focus on high -utilization segments like e -buses and e -trucks is crucial to 
decarbonizing commercial transport. 
(Para 33) 
 
13. The Committee observes that, at the initial stage, the Scheme covers only major 
cities with populations exce eding 40 lakh. Given India’s vast geography and the pressing 
need to reduce fossil fuel imports and vehicular air pollution, limiting e -bus deployment to 
a few large cities will not be sufficient to address these challenges comprehensively. 
Therefore, it recommends that the Ministry expand the scope of the Scheme to include 
smaller cities. If necessary, the Ministry should seek additional budgetary allocations at the 
Revised Estimates (RE) stage from the Ministry of Finance to maximize the Scheme’s 
potential in promoting electric bus adoption and green energy transition.  
(Para 35) 
14.   The Committee emphasises that charging infrastructure is crucial for successfully 
transitioning to electric mobility. A meagre Rs. 180.00 crore was allocated  at the RE stage 
for 2024 -25 for installing EV public charging stations across the country, and the entire 
amount remains unutilised. It recommends that the Ministry proactively seek enhanced 
funding from the Ministry of Finance at the RE stage (2025 -26) to expedite the installation 
of charging infrastructure under the PM E -DRIVE, thereby providing a much -needed 
boost to electric mobility. 
(Para 38) 
15. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Heavy Industries finalize 
guidelines and SOPs to ensure the swift and effective implementation of the PM e-Bus Sewa 
Payment Security Mechanism (PSM) Scheme. It is crucial to expedite coordination with 
States to  facilitate the registration of Direct Debit Mandates (DDMs) with the RBI, 
ensuring wider participation beyond Karnataka and Telangana. Additionally, the Ministry 

28 
 
should establish a robust monitoring mechanism to track implementation progress and 
address any challenges stakeholders face to enhance the scheme’s effectiveness. 
(Para 40) 
 
16.  The Committee was informed that the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme 
for Automobile and Auto Components is expected to attract an  investment of 
approximately Rs. 42,500 crore and generate around 1.48 lakh direct employment 
opportunities over five years. As on 19.02.2025, total 77 variants of Champion Original 
Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) and 39 variant of Component Champion OEMs, both 
overseas and Indian, have been approved under the components of this scheme.  As per 
data provided by the Ministry of Heavy Industries, the actual expenditure under the 
scheme for financial year 2024 -25 up to 31 st January 2025 stands at Rs. 246.21 cror e, 
representing approximately 70.98% of the allocated funds. Given the critical role of this 
scheme in strengthening domestic manufacturing under the Make in India  and 
Aatmanirbhar Bharat initiatives, the Committee recommends that the funds allocated at the 
Revised Estimate (RE) stage be fully utilized by the end of the current financial year.  
(Para 43)

RE, highlighting utilisation challenges. For 2025-26, a budget allo cation of ₹155.76 crore

battery manufacturing.

and enhance India’s position in the global EV market.

Discussion with Representatives of the Ministry of Heavy Industries 
The Chairman welcomed the Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI) officials to discuss the 2025 -26 
Budget. He highlighted/ stated/ asked for 
(i) MHI's role is to develop capital goods, automobile, and heavy electrical equipment sectors 
while overseeing CPSEs and autonomous organisations.  
(ii) MHI's budget allocation for 2025-26 is about 85% of the proposed ₹9,025.83 crores. 
(iii) Action plan for optimally utilising the enhanced budgetary allocation for 2025-26, focusing 
on the PM E-Drive Scheme, which has been allocated more than 50% of the total budget.  
(iv) Information on schemes’ progress, strategies planned for the year, and the targets set.  
(v) Data on performance of CPSEs, steps taken to improve output and productivity, and any 
restructuring or revival plans for sick or loss-making CPSEs.  
2. After the Chair’s permission, through a presentation, the Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI)  
(i) outlined their budget performance from 2020-21 to 2024-25,  
(ii) focused on schemes like the PLI -Automobile Scheme, with an outlay of ₹25,938 crore, 
aimed at boosting the electric vehicle sector,  
(iii) Shared the technological advancements and significant growth of the Heavy Electrical 
Industry and 
(iv) Highlighted the importance of developing Advanced Chemistry Cells to accelerate the 
production of electric vehicles (EVs).  
(v) Provided an overview of the automotive industry, highlighting India’s global position in the 
sector, and discussed the awareness programs launched by the Ministry to educate the 
public about electric vehicles (EVs).  
(vi) Stressed the need to scale up manufacturing capabilities, create jobs, and improve India's 
position in the global automotive market.  
3. The CMDs briefed the Committee about BHEL, Bridge and Roof Company Ltd, and 
Braithwaite Burn and Joseph Construction Co. Ltd.  
4. After the presentation, the Chairman and Committee Members  
(i) Raised underutilisation of the budget in previous years, ranging from 2022-23 to 2024-25 
(ii) Pointed out the reduced allocation under capital heads vis a vis the total allocation; 
(iii) Sought reasons for continuing the legacy name of Braithwaite Burn and Jessop. 
(iv) Inquired about the steps taken to protect domestic players given global players like Tesla 
entering the Indian market 
(v) Sought replies on points like job work, underground coal gasification, coll aboration with 
J&T, Hyderabad for skill development, gravitational storage for large-scale solutions. 

40 
 
(vi) Highlighted that a key challenge is the customs duty on scrap material. When specialized 
material is imported for manufacturing, such as a one -tonne valve, the duty is charged on 
the full weight, including scrap, which adds unnecessary costs. The committee recommends 
addressing this issue with the Ministry of Finance or Commerce. Additionally, large 
companies like BHEL, with their technological capabilities, should focus more on job work 
opportunities. 
(vii) Reminded CMD HEC for the background note, as the Committee intends to recommend 
steps to revive the organisation 
5. The Committee directed that the following be recommended in their DFG report: 
(i) Passenger vehicles (four wheelers) and hybrid vehicles may also be incentivised under the 
PM E-Drive Scheme,  
(ii) Disbursement of incentives under PM E-Drive be streamlined and expedited,  
(iii) Additional funds be allocated to CPSEs, in collaboration with Ministry of Skill 
Development and Entrepreneurship to impart vocational training to locals for enhancing 
their employability 
(iv) Subsidies be offered for scrapping diesel vehicles when purchasing electric vehicles. 
6. The Chairman thanked the Secretary and other Officers for their input and requested that they 
provide written replies to the queries of Hon’ble MP to the Secretariat within 3 days.  
II Discussion with Representatives of Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
(MSME) 
7. The Chairman welcomed the Secretary  and Officers of the Ministry of MSME and its allied 
agencies to the meeting to discuss the Ministry’s budgetary allocations for FY 2025-26, and he 
(i) Acknowledged the increase in the MSME sector's budget allocation for 2025-26 to ₹23,168 
crore, up from ₹22,137.95 crore in the previous financial year,  
(ii) Inquired about the impact of less -allocation(by ₹2,439.08 crore) from the Ministry's 
projected ₹25,607.23 croreon the operation of various schemes, 
(iii) Expressed concern about the capital expenditure allocation, only 3% of the total budget, 
(iv) Sought the strategies for optimal budget utilisation, employment generation for micro and 
small enterprises, technological upgradation and enhanced R&D investment in the sector, 
(v) Asked for the reasons for rise in MSME closures, wit h 32,000 shutting down in FY 2024 -
25, compared to 175 in FY 2020-21  
(vi) Sought sector-wise breakdown of NPAs from RBI; 
(vii) Expressed concern on underutilisation of funds allocated to KVIC. 
8. After the Chair’s permission, the Secretary, M/o MSME apprised the Committee on the 
2025-26 budget highlights, including  
(i) Key measures to strengthen the sector, including increased investment and turnover limits, 
doubling of credit guarantee cover, a ₹5 la kh credit scheme for Udyam -registered micro-
enterprises, and a ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds for startups. 
(ii) Focus and sectoral growth in footwear, leather, toys, and clean tech, with MSMEs 
contributing 30.1% of India’s GDP and ₹12.39 lakh crore in exports.  

41 
 
(iii) Continued support of artisans and MSMEs through schemes like PM Vishwakarma and 
PMEGP, with 25% of public procurement reserved for MSMEs. 
(iv) Reclassification of MSMEs and showcasing significant growth in the MSME sector, with 
registered MSMEs set to rise from 1.65 crore in 2023 to 6 crore by 2025 
(v) Increased (21X) procurement from SC -ST MSEs over the past decade under the National 
SC-ST Hub (NSSH).  
(vi) Emphasizing sustainable development, the Ministry aims to improve access to credit, 
market opportunities, technolog ical support, and infrastructure, ultimately driving the 
MSME sector’s growth as a key engine of the Indian economy, 
(vii) Introducing customised credit cards for micro-enterprises to address the credit gap. 
(viii) Lays a target of supporting 5.55 lakh MSMEs between 20 22 and 2027, with partnerships 
across 34 states.  
(ix) Significant increase in registered MSMEs, projected to reach 6 crore, with continued efforts 
to reduce credit gaps, enhance scalability, and drive investment.  
 
9. The Committee directed that the following be recommended in their DFG report: 
(i) The Government may revise the definition of Micro, Small, and Medium and if required, 
create a new category (nano or any other name) with lowest financial criteria (say, around 
Rs. 10 Lakh) arguing that businesses with a turnover above ₹2.5 crore might not align with 
the core purpose of MSMEs3 
(ii) RBI may regulate a uniform and streamlined interest/ margin rate for MSMEs across banks.  
(iii) The Government may remove the entry barrier of class 8 qualification in PMEGP and focus 
on talent and skill instead of the current support for artisans and handicrafts, which often 
perpetuates family-based traditions and reinforces caste-based systems. 
(iv) The Ministry may improve awareness of UMANG and other MSME IT platforms. 
10. The Chairman thanked the Secretary and other Officers for their input and asked them to 
furnish the written replies to the queries raised by the Hon’ble MPs to the Secretariat within three 
days.  
11. A verbatim record of the proceedings of the meeting was kept. 
The Committee adjourned at 2.10 pm.
