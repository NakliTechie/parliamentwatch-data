PMRY - AN OVERVIEW OF THE SCHEME 
  
1.1    The Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana was launched on 2nd Oct ober, 1993 with a view to providing
self-employment opportunities to educated unemplo yed youth in the country. The scheme was made 
operational with effect from Is t April, 1994. Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojanawas launched on 2nd
October, 1993. The scheme was designed to provide employment to more than  a million persons by
setting up seven lakh micro enterp rises during the Eight Five Year  Plan (1993-97), through industry, 
services and business routes. The scheme was operated in urban ar eas only during 1993-94, and in both 
urban and rural areas of the country from 1994-95 onwards. From 1994-95 the erstwhile-Self-
Employment Scheme for the Educat ed Unemployed Youth (SEEUY) was merged into PMRY. During
the Eight Plan a total of 7.75 lakh cases were sancti oned and out of these, lo ans were reported to have
been disbursed in 6.52 lakh cases as reported by the Reserve Bank of In dia (REI). Until now, nearly 15
educated unemployed youth have benefited from th e scheme. The PMRY is continuing in the Tenth
Plan period.

1.3    The target envisaged for 1999-2000 was 2.20 lakh beneficiaries. Against this target, 1,14,587 cases 
have been sanctioned, and disbursement took plac e in 59,845 cases as reporte d upto the month ending
December, 1999. The Target for 2002-03 remains at the level of Rs.2.20 lakhs, as was the case on 2001-
02. 
  
1.4    The Committee observes that the target (2.20 lakh beneficiaries) set for PMRY is too low to have
any significant impact on the creati on of employment opportunities in the country. This issue weighted
on the Committee's discussion with of ficials of State Government, Mini stry of Agro & Rural Industries
during its visit to Andhra Pradesh, Kamataka, Kerala. The Committee no ted that the target fixed each
year for PMRY has remained st atic during the last three year s i.e. for 2000-01, 2001-02 and 2002-03 
only 2.20 lakh persons were to be c overed under PMRY scheme. This figure, when divided between the
states and thereafter, among districts of each state, is like a drop. Th is figure is too minuscule to have
any noticeable impact on the unemployment scenario in a country of millions. 
  
1.5    The Ministry of Small Scale Industries, with whom the matter was taken up explained that the 
fixing of target was based on two aspects: - 
(a)
    Basic Target which is fixed, and is based on th e State's population as in 1991. This target is 
given by the Planning Commissi on, and is based on the employm ent potential and the numbe r
of unemployed registered with Employment Excha nge. An annual. target of 2.2 lakhs is given
for 2002-03, on an all India basis. This is communicated in March every year, for the next year.
(b)   Additional Target: This is vari able and is fixed on the basis of recovery position during the
previous year, and the performance in the previous year. This is communicated during May. 
         The Committee was also informed that th ere is a Government of Indi a guideline that 125 per cent
of target must also be achieved. 
  
1.6    However, the Committee was of the view that  the overall target for PM RY should be increased so
that states could be given enhanc ed targets, and more and more people can be brought under the ambit
of the scheme each year.

2.1    During the course of the Committee's meeting held at Hyde rabad, Commissioner of Industries
replied that Andhra Pradesh state that the scheme with a high target of around 33,000 and the number of
sanctions have been declining since with accumulation of NPAs. 
  
2.2    The Committee was further informed that th ere are a number of reasons  for under achievement o f
tile target under PMRY which are as under:- 
  
(a)  Banks complained that there is generall y delay in communicating PMRY targets to them,
which results in delay in grounding of the unit. Also, selection of beneficiaries is done only on
specific dates. The delay in comm unicating target of the banks le ads to delay in sanctioning o f
loans and subsequent disbursal of loans to the beneficiaries. Target is communicated in two
installments, which results in delay in its implementation. 
  
(b)  It is generally seen that beneficiaries take up si milar or same lines of  activity, which makes it
unviable. Autorickshaws, photocopy shops, STD booths etc were cite d as examples. Banks
hesitate to sanction loans for activities of simi lar nature as these activities reach a saturation
point which is a major reason to the failure of the scheme. 
  
(c)  Most beneficiaries go in for trade and business activities. 
  
(d)  Percentage of overdues to total loans is 67 per cent, which is a matter of great concern. 
  
(e)  Absconding entrepreneurs who divert funds and close shop are also a matter of concern. 
  
(f)   From the entrepreneurs' side, request for change in activ ity results in delay in getting
approvals. 
  
(g)  Marketing of products is a problem area which entrepreneurs find difficult to surmount. 
  
8.      Mounting NPAs under the PMRY scheme is a problem to be addressed immediately. The
representatives of Government of  Andhra Pradesh apprised the Comm ittee that the following measures
were being taken for overcoming the problems:- 
  
(a)  A list of viable schemes is being identifie d. Project profiles on thes e lines of activities are
being prepared. 
  
(b)  List of eligible beneficiaries in each area, mandal wise, is being maintained. 
  
(c)  Technology link with instit utions is being thought of. A me ntion was made of RUDSETI o f
Karnataka and some districts of Andhra Pradesh, which provide training to beneficiaries. 
  
(d)  Deputy Tahsildars are employe d for recovery of dues. Andhra  Pradesh is the only state doing
so. 
  
(e)  Monitoring is being done by Industry Promotion Officer. 
  
(f)   Awards have been constituted for best Industry Promotion Officer. 
  
(g)  Advertisement campaign for PMRY will be undertaken.

2.3    The Committee observes that there is a great diff erence between applicat ions recommended and
cases sanctioned in Andhra Pradesh. The downtrend in the number of sa nctions is alarming, and the gap
between the number of applications  and disbursals has been increa sing in Andhra Pradesh. As against
the national average, achievement of the target under the scheme be ing 86.3 per cent, the gap of 50 pe r
cent between the target and its achievement in Andhra Pradesh showed a dismal performance. The
progress of the PMRY in Andhra Pradesh depicted a sharp decline in the number of sanctions though the
target remained almost static. (33,600 for 1999-2000, 33,800 for 2000-01 and .33,200 for 2001-02). 
  
2.4    A gap in the PMRY between sanctions and di sbursal of loan was also  seen in Andhra Pradesh,
Against the 16,782 applications sanctioned, only 13,309 cases were disbursed loan during 1999-2000, 
and against 14,857 sanctioned cases during 2000-01, only 12178 aspirants were disbursed loans. 
  
2.5    The Committee observed that timely communication of targets s hould be made and specific dates
for selection of entrepreneurs should be communicated. As regard s large number of beneficiaries
choosing activities which become unviable, the Committee suggests th at counselling of applicants to
choose from agriculture and allied activities should be done. 
  
2.6    The Committee also observ es that coverage of SC/STs unde r the Scheme is not upto the norm
stipulated up Government of India i.e. 22.5 per cen t for SC/ST. Against the all India average of 15.9 pe r
cent, Andhra Pradesh has recorded representation for these categories as 16.9 per cent during 1998-99, 
21.3 'per cent during 1999-2000, 17.5 per cent during 2000-2001 and 15.1 per cent during 2001-02. This 
has to be brought to the stipulated norm. 
  
RECOVERY OF LOANS
 
  
2.7    The recovery scenario in A ndhra Pradesh a dismal picture. Avai lable records indicate that during
the half year ended Marc h, 1999 it was 41.1 per cent; during th e half year ended March, 2000 it was
36.8 per cent and during half year  ended March, 2001 it was 37.9 per cent. During the half-year ende d 
September, 2001 it was 38.8 per cent The representatives of State Government apprised the Committee
that Deputy Tahsildar had been specially deputed  to make recovery of loans from defaulting
entrepreneurs. 
  
2.8    The Committee, during the course of the interaction wished to know if any study on viable projects
in each district was undertaken. The Commissioner of Industries Andhra Pradesh replied that this
exercise was undertaken, and project profiles on identified lines of activities were being prepared. 
2.9    The Committee observes that in order to improve rate of gr ounding of units it was necessary to
rectify the high percen tage of NPAs as mounting NPAs were one of the reas ons for high mortality rate
of PMRY projects. The Committee obs erved that NPAs to large industr ies can not be equated with the
NPAs under the PMRY as the percenta ge. of NPAs in PMRY sector wa s but a small percent of total
NPAs in the country. Out of total NPA of Rs.18000 cr ores, small sector owed only Rs.8,000 crore. NPA
for PMRY, therefore, was only negl igible. The Committee, therefore,  suggests that bankers should be
encouraged to finance self-employment schemes as this would directly increase employment.

         The Committee observes that though th e ceiling on project cost unde r PMRY has been increased
from Rs.l Lakh to Rs.2 Lakhs with effect from  1999-2000; for activities other than business, no 
perceptible increase in the amount of the average loan disbursed is  observed. This may indicate that

routine target-oriented approach in  the implementation of the scheme with little care for the actual
requirement of funds for setting up micro-enterpris es. There is concern that  activities are being unde r 
financed which lead to their una vailability and their misuse ri ght from the very beginning. The
Committee, therefore, advise that Model Project Profiles should be prepar ed in every district on
successful activities and banks could release th e financing, according to  these model profiles.

such sales outlets.                                                            (Para 4.8) 
         The Committee finds that not enough has been done to popularize the scheme. The Committee

in turn, ensure a wider choice during selection of candidates. Fu rther, the Green Channel (Single
Window) system introduced at the dist rict level should be made more effective in assisting more and
more beneficiaries to come forwar d for starting micro-enterprises.
