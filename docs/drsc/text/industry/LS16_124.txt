REPORT 
Agro and Rural Industries play an important role in national economy because of the vast
potential of this sector in terms of sustainable employment generation and dispersal of industries in rural
areas. With the onset of globalizat ion and liberalized econom ic scenario in the country, the Agro and
Rural Industries Sector has been envisaged and acknowledged as one  of the major avenues available for
creation of employment and to en sure balanced economic growth.  Added to the already existing
regional imbalances, opening up of the economy to ma rket   forces with mi nimum control mechanisms,
was a real and potent threat to at taining the avowed objective of balanced economic development. Since
this sector is the major non-farm sector with a vast potential to create and sustain employment across the 
country, specifically the backward and rural areas it is necessary to reinforce and strengthen the
functioning of the sector as a whole, by highlighting its presen t status and ensu ring timely policy
interventions needed. Further streamlining by way of  restructuring field agen cies and the sector’s 
competitive policy positioning has enhanced its inhe rent strength to forge ah ead and ensure balanced
growth.  
1.1       In view of the growing significance of Agro and Rural Industries, the Ministry of Agro & Rural
Industries was carved out of the erstwhile Ministry of  Small Scale Industri es and Agro & Rural
Industries   w.e.f. 1 st September 2001. The need was felt for carving out a Se parate Ministry because 
ofthe increasing criticality of the sector in the libe ralized economic set up needed  to be reinforced. As a
major sector with vast employmen t potential on a sustainable basis exploiting optimally the indigenous
wealth available especiall y by way of traditional craft and t echniques, a separate Ministr y of A gro &

1.2       The Committee understands that Agro and Rural Industries has tremendous scope and potential 
for gainful employment and boosting ba lanced economic growth and development thereby changing the
status of India’s developing econom y to a developed economy keeping in  view the fact that the large
chunk of India’s population resi de in rural areas.   The onus lies on the Ministry of ARI, carved out o f 
the erstwhile Ministry  of SSI and ARI on 1 st September 2001, to translate th is dream into a reality by
undertaking key issues concerning the Agro and Rural Industries synergistically and effectively. 
1.3       The main function of the Ministry is to  formulate policy measures for the growth of rural
industries for achieving rapid increase in gainful employment and balanced re gional growth, which are
among the main objectives underlying th e strategy of industrialization in the country. It also supervises
and coordinates the activities of different promotional agencies operating at the Central and State Level. 
The Ministry acts as a nodal agency for promotion and development of village and cottage industries as
well as tiny and micro enterprises in  both urban and rural areas.  The Charter for the Ministry describes
its objective as one to promote, ai d and foster growth of Agro a nd Rural Industries by providing them
institutional support in the areas  of marketing, export, technol ogy upgradation, training and common
facility services.  The constituents in the Ministry are:- 
(i)                  K&VI sector, 
(ii)                Coir sector,  
(iii)               Tiny and Micro Enterprises. 
1.4       Field agencies that implement policies and programmes relating to this sector are:- 
(i)                  Khadi & Village Industries Commission (KVIC) 
(ii)                Coir Board, 
(iii)               Office of DC (SSI) and 
(iv)              PMRY division.  
1.5       The Committee notes the structural   ar rangement of Ministry of ARI and at the same time
expects that all the field agencies, i.e. KVIC, Coir Board, office  of DC (SSI) and PMRY division would
perform their respective assigned task effectively a nd efficiently.   In this context, the Committee
recommends that all the wings of Ministry of ARI would discharge their functions and responsibilities in
such a manner that Agro and Rural Sector in ge neral may contribute to national economic growth and
development.  
1.6       The Ministry guides and coordinates with the implementing agencies to ensure development and
achievement as per the set mandate.T he Ministry has three-pronged role to play ,all of which aim at 
quality implementation of policies, programmes and sc hemes of the Ministry. In  the role of a policy
maker, the Ministry collects, collates and derives infe rences, which act as critic al input to further policy
formulation/amendments. In the role  of an advocate, the Ministry a dvocates and puts up ‘felt needs’ o f 
the stakeholders involved in the sector  as a whole. In the role of a fa cilitator, the Ministry facilitates,
coordinates and supports its fi eld agencies in implementing the programmes and schemes both
quantitatively and qualitatively. 
1.7       At present, the monitoring mechanism at the Ministry level includes both structured as well as
informal but regular monitori ng. Committee headed by Secretar y (ARI) periodically monitors
implementation of PMRY. A sim ilar Committee is being consti tuted for monitoring programmes
implemented by KVIC. At present, KVIC’s work is monitored based on their monthly progress report as
well as frequent interactions held  between the KVIC and Ministry at various levels.Work of Coir Board
is monitored on similar li nes as well as throu gh its board meetin gs. Measures, which would make the

and structured inputs from local public representatives. 
1.8              The Committee notes that the Ministry of ARI is entruste d with the functions/duties o f 
implementations of policies, prog rammes and schemes and in turn play the role of a policy maker,
advocate and a facilitator.  The Committee also finds that the Ministry monitors the activities undertaken
by its field’s agencies through formal  structure as well as informal meetings.  However, the Committee
is of the view that the Ministry  of ARI would act promptly as a policy maker, advocate and facilitato r
and promote Agro and Rural I ndustries by effective implementat ion of policies programmes and
schemes undertaken by its various field agencies without time a nd cost over run.  The existing
monitoring mechanism must ensure th at targets are achieved within th e stipulated time frame.  The
Committee further recommends that  the Ministry of ARI should have  review meetings  of its field
agencies to take stock of the progress made in the implementation of various schemes and programmes.

it a realistic criteria.  The Co mmittee feels that the family in come of Rs. 40,000 per annum hardly
fulfills the bare needs of a family  consisting of 2-3 persons.  Moreove r, the devaluation /depreciation o f
rupee further over the years has also necessitated the need for raising the limit of family income.  One of
the reasons of low recovery of loans under PMRY may be construed from the fact that loan amount goes
to fulfilling the basic needs of beneficiaries or his family members.  Therefore, it seems reasonable that
suitable amendment in the family income criteria  under PMRY may be brought fourth by raising the
existing limit of Rs. 40,000 to Rs. 1.5 lakh.

11.28   Marketing Strategy of KVIC 
The marketing strategy of KVIC is to focus on its eco friendly and natural product range. Khadi
& Village Industries sector produces  goods and services both. In villa ge industries service based units
basically cover local area needs and marketing is not much of a problem. With rega rd to non-consumer 
goods normally units established have good market as most of such products are intermediary products
used by other industries. Main di fficulty is in consumer goods a nd khadi. For both these products areas
of intervention involves a detail ed marketing strategy for publicit y, branding and brand promotion,
design input, better packaging etc.  Accessing private marketing cha nnel, modernization of existing
channels, organizing large scale e xhibitions are important aspects. Marketing federations have been
formed with various village industries federations to  act as facilitator outsi de the governmental set up.
Design / packaging development scheme and renovation of marketing outlets scheme has been launched.
11.29   For the open consumer market, KVIC has de veloped “Sarvodaya Brand”  consisting of FMCG 
products like Toilet Soap, Pickles, Honey, etc., “K hadi” for exclusive up-m arket and export products

Cereals, Spices Powder, Pulses, Ja ggery, etc. and the same have been  launched at Mumbai and Delhi in
select Outlets.  Based on the experience it is felt that a professional Marketing Agency / Federation is
required, so that KVIC does not get involved in Departmental Trading Ac tivities, since institutions and
marketing professionals can play a better and effect ive role in marketing these products.  A Marketing
Confederation has recently been c onstituted out of existing Khadi & V. I. Federations and has also been
accorded provisional recognition by KVIC.  The Conf ederation is expected to take up aggressive
marketing for KVI products with active support of KVIC.

Reacting to the Committee’s que ry regarding impact of VAT on the village industry products 
Chief Executive Officer, Khadi and Village Commission commented as under:- 
“VAT has replaced the Sales Tax with effect from 01.04.2003; three St ates have already agreed to
implement it.  Most of the States had exempted us from paying Sales Tax.  Unfortunately, in the last two
to three years, Sales Tax has been imposed in cer tain States.  For exampl e, in respect of Khadi
industries, Tamil Nadu has declared rebate only fo r products produced in their States, not for those
which are coming from outside.  Si milarly, some other States have extended a rebate for only those
village industries, which are situated in their States.  In my opinion, zero- based budgeting must be done 
for the village industries sector.  We  have written to all the State Go vernments, to the chief Ministers
and the concerned ministers.  Th e Secretary has also written to the empowered committee on VAT, to
consider this viewpoint.  Also, th e minister has written a letter requesting for ‘across the board’ set-up 
for the khadi and village industries sector.  The ec onomic scale of the vill age and khadi industries is
low.  If this minor advantage of not having to pay Sales Tax is extended, it wi ll help these industries a
lot.” 
11.44   The Committee observes that 16 more states have agreed to  the VAT structure of taxation
system. However, the Committee re commends that the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries should
examine the case of feasibi lity of applying the  Zero- based budgeting for the vill age industry sector and
accordingly advocate the exemptions of tax across  the board for institution or entrepreneur, which is
registered with the Khadi and Village Industries Board or Khadi and Village Industries Commission.

12.33   COOPERATIVISATION SCHEME 
12.33.1            The objectives of the co-operati visation scheme were forma tion of viable coir co-
operatives and revitalisation of the potentially viable dormant societies and bringing the coir workers in
to the co-operative fold so that the quality a nd quantity of production impr oves, workers get bette r
wages and they are freed from e xploitation of middlemen and the i ndustry is put on a sound footing.  It
was aimed to bring 80% of the work-force in the industry under co-operative  fold through of the 
scheme. Coir Board is the m onitoring agency of the Scheme.             The objectives of the scheme are
yet to be achieved due to a mult iplicity of reasons. The Institut e of Rural Management which had
conducted an evaluative study on th e scheme, opined that the “Schem e of Co-operativisation in Coi r 
Industry as it is envisaged and implemented need s to undergo drastic revision to meet the emerging
challenges. The Integrated Coir Development Project is one of the components of the Co-operativisation 
Scheme.  It envisages setting up of new primary co-operatives, modernisation and expansion of existing 
co-operatives, improved handlooms, curling units and automatic spinni ng units etc.   The Project is 
being implemented with the assistance of National Co-operative Development Corporation (NCDC) and 
the coir producing States concerned. Assistance under this component is still being released.   
12.33.2            The Committee is of view that  the objectives of the co- operativisation scheme i.e. 
formation of viable coir co-operatives and revitalizat ion of the potentially viab le dormant societies and 
bringing the coir workers in to the co-operati ve fold  for improving th e quality and quantity o f 
production and putting industry on a sound footing is a unique mission in Coir Industry.    Therefore, the
suggestion of the Institute of Rural Management  that the Scheme of Co-operativisation in Coir Industry 
as it is envisaged and implemen ted needs to undergo drastic revisi on to meet the emerging challenges,
needs attention of the government . The National Co-operative Deve lopment Corporation (NCDC) may 
be entrusted with greater role for implementation of co-operativisation scheme.

            After examining the eligibility criteria for PMRY the Committee ha s arrived at a conclusion that 
while the basis of age, educational qualification, residence and defaulter clause of eligibility criteria are
logical and rational, however, eligibility clause as regards the family income needs modification to make

suitable amendment in the family income criteria  under PMRY may be brought fourth by raising the
existing limit of Rs. 40,000 to Rs. 1.5 lakh.
