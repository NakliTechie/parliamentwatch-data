NATIONAL ALUMINIUM COMPANY LTD. (NALCO)
 
1.         Brief   Background
1.1       The  National Aluminium Company Ltd. (NALCO)  was incorporated on 7
th January, 1981 as the second public
sector company in the Aluminium Sector with a view to exploit a part of the large deposits of bauxite discovered in the
east coast. Nalco operates   bauxite mines  at panchpatmali in district Koraput (Orissa).  It runs a  0.8  million tonnes per
Annum  alumina refinery at   Damanjodi in Koraput  District, and  230000  tonnes per  Annum  Aluminium   Smelter  at
Angul  in Orissa.  The authorised capital of the company  has been reduced    from Rs. 1288.62  crores to  Rs.644.31
crores after the approval  of the  Cabinet.   The Government of India  holds  87.15% of equity  in Nalco, the balance
12.85%  being  held by financial institutions, employees, public etc.Nalco is a zero debt company  and  has been granted
Miniratna status by Government of India and it has also been  classified  as a    star  trading house. Nalco  exports 
Alumina  & Aluminium to  over thirty countries.  It has been  accorded ISO  9002 & ISO  14001 certification.

Expansion programme of  NALCO
Nalco  has undertaken   a  massive   expansion programme of   Rs.3700 crores  of  its       bauxite mines, alumina
refinery, smelter, captive power plant and port facilities.  The resources for the entire  expansion programme  are to be
internally generated.
This includes expansion    of  the capacity of  bauxite mines  from 2.4 millions tonnes  per year  to 4.8 million tonnes 
per year and that of  Alumina Refinery from 0.8  million tonnes  per year  to 1.575 million tonnes per year   at an 
investment  of Rs.1664.60 crores.    The project   was to be implemented within 51 months from the date of government
approval i.e. 18.12.1996.
The Government also approved   expansion of capacity of  Aluminium Smelter and Captive Power Plant of Nalco at 
Angul   so as to  increase the capacity of the Smelter from  230000 TPY  to 345000 TPY  and  that  of the  captive
power plant from the existing  720 MW   to  840 MW at an  investment of Rs.2062 crores .  The project  was also to be
implemented in  51 months from  the date of  Government approval i.e. 3
rd February 1998.

6.    Financial restructuring
During  the course of discussions at Bhubhaneswar,    the Committee    asked  CMD, Nalco  about the    the     reasons 
for reducing the   paid up   share capital of Nalco to 50%  from Rs.1288.62           crore to 644.31 crore in  the year
1998.  The Committee also  asked  about the   impact of  such  capital restructuring   on the  physical and financial
performance of the company.  The CMD of Nalco  informed    that   relative to  (a) Sales turnover, (b) Profits after  tax
and (c) Financial ratios including  paid up capital of competitors like  Hindalco and Indal, the paid up share capital   of 
Rs.1288.62 crore of Nalco  was considered very much on the higher side and  hence the stock          performance ratios
like EPS, PE multiple and Share price etc. were   low  and  was not adequately  reflecting the  intrinsic   strength   of
Nalco. Hence, capital restructuring  was done with the prior permission of the government. Accordingly 50% of paid up
equity capital of 1288.62% was converted to redeemable secured debentures. The paid up equity capital after
restructuring was 644.31 crores out of which Govt of India holding is Rs.561.50 crores(87.15%).  The Committee was
also informed that the original project cost was partly financed by external commercial borrowings and partly by equity
subscribed by govt. of India. The entire foreign currency loan has been repaid by 30.09.98  and Nalco has become a zero
debt company except for debt created out of equity capital through a scheme of capital restructuring with the prior

The Committee commends the zero debt status of Nalco and also feels Nalco has performed exceedingly well as a

7.    Technical know-how
The Committee  was informed       that Aluminium   Pechiney  of France,  a  world   leader in Aluminium  technology,
have supplied  Nalco  the  know how  and the basic engineering technology for  Alumina  and Aluminium Plant.  The
Committee was further informed that there are    many plants  world wide working  with  their technology.  M/s
Engineers India Limited(EIL) were the prime Indian consultants for Mines, Alumina Refinery and Aluminium smelter.
They have also been engaged for the ongoing expansion projects of Bauxite mine, Alumina refinery and smelter plants.
M/s Development Consultants Ltd(DCL) were the consultants for the captive power plant and steam generation plant
and M/s Howe India (P) ltd for the Port facilities. M/s Dasturco have been appointed as consultant for expansion of
Captive Power Plant and M/s. MECON for expansion of Port facilities. The Committee was  apprised that the  quality
of   Alumina and  Aluminium produced  by Nalco meets all international  quality   standards  because of its technology. 
The  cost   of production of  Alumina  of  Nalco is lowest in the world because of appropriate technology and logistics.
Hence Nalco will be in a position to compete in the international market for its quality with cost economics.
It is good to obtain outside technology whenever required. The Committee recommends that Nalco should have a
strong  R&D set up and wherever possible in-house expertise should be utilized instead of always depending on outside
agencies.
8.    Capacity utilization of captive Power Plant
The Committee  was informed that  the captive  power plant is capable  of supplying power  to the Smelter. However,
the power system is connected to Orissa grid, which is unreliable and unstable.   The voltage and frequency variations
are high and at times the  system trips   due to external   fault, causing a total black out of the grid This was  a  frequent
phenomenon earlier .  The situation   at present is  better after NTPC super thermal power plant  came into existence.
The Committee was further  informed that    frequent   power interruption  has detrimental effect on pot operation.  In
1998, the pot  line,  handling of metal at cast house, power failures and excessive heat in summer  were some  of the
major factors  which led to deterioration of  some essential parameters of  pot line and finally some pots were  stopped
to survive. However after  de-bottlenecking of carbon plant, the inputs like  anode and bath are matching to pot line
requirement.  Similarly, addition of billet  casting facility and Strip  Casting plant are  helping to evacuate the hot metal. 
With the improved grid position, it is expected to operate the plant in future without much problem.   With the 
expansion of Smelter  the   capacity  of power plant is also being enhanced from 720MW to 960MW.
The Committee was  informed that the  de-bottlenecking of Smelter was carried  out at a cost of Rs.49.12 crore.  This 
was mainly  in the Carbon area of Smelter Plant, which was proving  to be  a bottleneck for the   Smelter plant to attain
its designed capacity.  The de-bottlenecking of Smelter has helped in attaining 480 pot operation (100% capacity
utilisation)  in  Smelter.  No further de-bottlenecking is  planned in Smelter.  However,   for the third pot line under
Smelter expansion, the above aspect has been taken care of. During the course of oral evidence on 10
th April, 2001, the
CMD informed that Nalco is running at over 100% capacity.
The Committee noted that inadequate checks and maintenance caused the shunting of pots in the smelter in April and
June 1998 resulting in less then 100% utilization.
The Committee recommends that adequate safeguards be built within the system to avoid such recurrence in future.

13. Research and Development
Nalco has two independent R&D centers at both M&R Complex, Damonjodi as well as in S&P Complex at Angul. Both
the centers have been recognized by Ministry of Science & Technology, Govt. of India. Both the centers are equipped
with facilities to take care of plant related process, problems and product development activities for value addition. It
also continues to carry out small experiments to improve the quality of inputs in production process, to improve
productivity and quality of product. The R&D center has successfully implemented projects like Special grade Alumina
pilot and commercial plant and Zeolite "A" commercial plant. Further value-added products from waste materials like
fly ash and red mud have been developed. Some process technologies have been patented. Nalco has taken up some
collaborative R&D project with CSIR laboratory, IIT etc. in areas of process and product development. Nalco's R&D
scientists have received national and international awards like NRDC Award, WIPO Gold Medal, ICI Award etc.
The Committee appreciated several of the value added products made from fly ash. The Committee also noted that the
captive power plant of Nalco generates a considerable amount of fly ash which can be used to make value added
products. During the course of oral evidence on Demands for Grants  the CMD stated  "As far as research and
development wing is concerned, we have developed a special grade alumina and Zeolite.  The special grade alumina
project will be completed in December, 2001. Then we have also undertaken research project in collaboration with
Russia. This research project will help converting iron in the red mud to pig iron".
The Committee recommends that Nalco should explore means of using its R&D output for finding viable means to
commercially dispose of the fly ash. Besides the Committee also feels that  R&D effort should be channelised to explore
ways to improve the quality of alumina and other secondary aluminium products. Efforts may be made to complete the
special grade project as per schedule. The Committee also hopes that the research project in collaboration with Russia to
convert iron from red mud to pig iron would be completed early and would be successful.
14.       Takeover of IAPL
The Committee was informed   that Nalco has taken over IAPL on 16.03.2000 at an estimated project cost of Rs.292
crore. The integration of IAPL operations will benefit Nalco in view of the synergies in several areas. IAPL will remain
as a separate 100% EOU unit even after merger. During the course of the oral evidence on demands for grants (2001-02)
the CMD of Nalco informed that the process of commissioning of the IAPL plant is likely to be completed soon. The
CMD of NALCO  informed during oral evidence that the takeover of IAPL was a strategic move towards value
addition.
The Committee recommends that Nalco should make full use of takeover of IAPL by making a strong thrust in the area
of secondary aluminium products and the result should reflect in Nalco’s overall financial performance  so as to justify
the takeover of IAPL. The commissioning of IAPL may be completed as soon as possible.
15.       Breach of  NALCO’s  fly ash pond at Angul.
The Committee visited  Angul captive power plant on 23
rd January, 2001.  The Committee also visited the fly ash pond
of Nalco and the place where the breach took place. The Committee enquired into the reasons for the breach of the fly
ash pond which took place on 31.12.2000 as a result of which heavy ash with water drained from the pond and a fly ash
flood took place. This had a severe impact on the soil fertility of over twenty-three villages. Acute drinking water
problem arose from  this and around one thousand acres of land got adversely affected. The Committee was informed
that an inquiry Committee had been constituted to inquire into the reasons for the mishap.  During the course of oral

18.   Peripheral area development
The Committee was informed that Nalco plays a catalytic role for improving the infrastructure and general quality of
life of the people living in the periphery of the Company’s plants and facilities through implementation of a systematic
programme of periphery development with particular emphasis on rehabilitation of displaced families and weaker
sections of the society. The Committee was further informed that in both Angul and Damanjodi units of Nalco,
periphery development activities are planned by the Company’s peripheral development  Committees in consultation
with local govt authorities.  These  Committees formulate the annual plans and estimate taking into account the needs of
the Peripheral Villages alongwith the views of State  Agencies and  elected representatives of the  localities.  Works are
mostly  executed  through the placement of funds with the local Government  authorities.  However, depending upon 
the nature of the work some of the jobs are directly executed by the Company.  The Company officials monitor the jobs
for which  funds are  sanctioned and obtain execution/completion certificate from the  concerned agencies from time to
time.
The Committee however was not satisfied with the progress made under the peripheral area development programme.
The Committee considers the peripheral area as an integral part of  Nalco and its development would generate
tremendous goodwill for NALCO.  The CMD of NALCO  stated "Every year, we spend about one crore on the
peripheral development of the two projects i.e. Angul and Damanjodi." The CMD also informed "We have allocated 0.5
per cent of the profit for peripheral development. Now we have definitely planned to increase this. Our profits have
increased substantially. We also want to increase the outlay for peripheral development. I am happy to inform you that
the Ministry of Mines has recently taken a very important decision that it advised us to include the local MPs in the
Committees we have set up for peripheral development". "In case of Angul and Koraput, we will include the Members
of Parliament from these particular regions so that they will get an idea as to what the peripheral development would
be."  As regards ex-servicemen and women, the Secretary Ministry of Mines stated  "As far as ex-servicemen are
concerned, we have not taken up anything. I totally agree with you. In fact, there are two shortcomings. One is regarding
the ex-servicemen and the other is regarding women strength. It is also quite low. These are two important
shortcomings. We will take up these two things and do something."
The Committee recommends that greater stress should be placed upon proper monitoring of the peripheral area
development programmes. The Committee strongly recommends that higher allocation be made for peripheral area
development. Besides the Committee was distressed to note that despite Angul area having a large number of ex-
servicemen,   adequate representation in terms of employment in Nalco has not been given to them. The Committee
hopes that adequate representative be given to Ex-servicemen.
19.       Some problems
The Committee   has been given to understand that  NALCO is facing some problems and         their  solution  is 
required  for increasing Nalco's  market  share and  sales revenue. The     problems are :-
Since lot of scrap aluminium of inferior quality is  being imported into the country and is being used by the aluminium
down stream industries, Nalco as a quality conscious primary producer is facing problems in competing with such
upgraded and low quality scrap metal.
The Committee recommends that  the Government may kindly consider enhancing the import duty on such scraps to the
same level as that of primary aluminium metal.
Due to non-uniform Sales Tax Policy still prevailing in certain states, the company is
facing problems in competing with metal  sold/traded  from  these  areas. This  not  only causes loss to the Government
exchequer, due to less tax  realization   but   also  creates unhealthy competition between different
marketing/distribution channels. It was requested that at least for primary aluminium metal, for which there are limited
number  of producers, uniform Sales Tax structure is followed.

The plant for production of Zeolite-A, used in detergent manufacture is in advanced stage of commissioning. Zeolite-A
has been scientifically proven and commercially implemented as eco-friendly material for this application. However,
most of the detergent  manufacturers are still using STPP (sodium tri poly-phosphate), which is a pollutant and,
therefore, has been banned in, many western countries. When STPP mixes in the water system, it helps in growth of
algae, which extracts dissolved oxygen from water, thereby, the aquatic life perishes.
The Committee takes a serious view of this and recommends  that keeping in view our concern for         environmental
protection, use of STPP may be banned by the Government through an appropriate legislation. Besides, incentives may
kindly be given for use of Zeolite by exempting Excise Duty on this product for a period of at least 5 years.
It was requested that Railways should   accord priority for movement of Nalco's traffic and make available adequate
number of locos to achieve turnaround of Nalco Wagons as planned by RITES.
The Committee recommends that Ministry of Mines may take up the matter with Railways.
