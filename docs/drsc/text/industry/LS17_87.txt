Report
            The Refractory Industry in India is more than century old and the growth of this industry is closely inter-linked with the
development and growth of steel industry as more than 75 per cent of the total refractories produced in the country are consumed by
steel industries. Refractories contribute around 5 per cent   towards the total cost of steel making.  Under the changing scenario of
globalisation and liberalization, steel makers are facing stiff competition in the market, therefore, Steel Industry is  left with no  other
alternative than to produce the best  quality at the least cost.  The Steel Industry has started to demand  superior grade refractories
required for blast furnace, secondary refining and continuous casting units of steel industries which has posed challenges to the
refractory industry.  The emphasis by the industry in the recent years has been towards import substitution and production of special
items like magnesia carbon, slide gate refractories. 
1.1       Bharat Refractories Limited (BRL) was originally incorporated as a subsidiary company of Bokaro Steel Ltd. as the
production unit of (BHRP) which was earlier acquired by Government of India in 1972 and placed under the management of Bokaro
Steel Limited. Consequent upon restructuring of the Public Sector and steel industries on 1
st May 1978, Ranchi Road Refractories
Plant (RRRPL) at Ramgarh, which was under the control of erstwhile Hindustan Steel Limited and Bhilai Refractories Plant was also
transferred to BRL.  India Firebricks and Insulation Co. (IFICO) which was taken over by Steel Authority of India Ltd., with effect
from December, 1976, was also transferred as a subsidiary of BRL with effect from the date of restructurings.  At present, Bharat
Refractories Ltd (BRL) has four units, namely;
(a)    Bhandaridah Refractories Plant, Bhandaridah;
(b)   Ranchi Road Refractories Plant, Ramgarh;
(c)    Bhilai Refractories plant at Bhilai; and
(d)   IFICO Refractories Plant at Ramgarh.
1.2 The company is engaged in the manufacture and supply of various kinds of refractories not only to the integrated steel plants, but
also to the mini and medium steel plants.
1.3       The Committee notes the stages involved in the   formation of Bharat Refractories Ltd.  (BRL)  and hopes   that BRL
management would leave no stone unturned in assessing the   strength of all the four units of BRL and chalk out a short and long
term strategic course of action for early turnaround of company as a whole.  The Committee recommends that BRL management
work to ensure that all four units of BRL are making efforts towards the common mission for achieving common organizational goal
and are contributing to the physical and financial performance of the company.  None of them should    prove a burden on the
company.

3.0       Sickness of BRL and IFICO.
3.1       The Sub-Committee was informed  that  BRL and  India Fire Bricks  and Insulation Company  (IFICO)  were sick due to
under mentioned  reasons  and came under the purview  of Sick Industrial  Companies (Special Provision) Act,  1985 and was
referred to BIFR in 1992:-
 
(a)
         Obsolescence of machinery technological indiscipline and underutilization of capacity.
(b)         Managerial inefficiency and low  man-power   utilization.
(c)         Shortage of working capital  resulting into delay  in the procurement  of raw-material ..
(d)         Erratic power supply.
(e)         Changing   demand pattern of  refractories  etc.
3.2.      The Committee notes the reasons for BRL  and IFICO  becoming sick and coming under the purview of BIFR in  1992.  The
Committee observes that due attention for  revival of BRL was not considered at the appropriate time  and that  is the reason why
BRL continued  to make losses year  after year.  What  other result might be expected, from a loss making company which continued
to operate with  obsolescent machinery;    under utilisation  of  installed capacity;     low labour- productivity and shortage of capital ,
than falling  in the  net of BIFR.  BRL missed the ( the first  opportunity for revival), the Committee concludes.

4.4       The Committee  observes  that since IDBI's revival package had  no cash assistance to BRL as working capital  on the

expenditure.  BRL ended 1997-98 with a cash loss of Rs.14.33 crore and   the same adverse trend persisted during   1998-99.
4.5       The Committee understands that the general recession in steel industry in addition to BRL’s inherent problems i.e. under
utilisation of installed capacity, interruption in power supply, problems in the procurement of raw-material due to lack of working
capital followed by non -implementation of IDBI's   revival scheme led to the continued dwindling in physical and financial
performance of the company.
5.0       Second Revival Package for BRL
5.1       The management explained that   the Continued cash loss incurred by BRL, capital liquidity position, huge accumulation of
outstanding dues coupled with resentment of workers due to non-revision of wages had severely affected production and sale
realisation.  Due to paucity of funds and inadequate assistance from Government, most of the plant and machinery outlived their
effective life and required immediate replacement.  Taking into consideration all these factors the Ministry of steel formulated the
second revival package for BRL. This revival package included: -
i
                Grant of non-plan   loan Rs.400 crore for meeting revised wage bill during 1998-99,
ii               Grant of non--plan interest   free loan of Rs.16.00 crore during 1998-99 for meeting the working capital interest
holiday on existing loan as on 31.3.99 till 2007-2008;
iii             Moratorium on loan repaying for existing loans for another four years i.e. till 2003-2004;
iv             and Government of India  guarantee for  enhancing the  working capital limit  to Rs.24.00 crore. 
5.2       The second revisal package   for BRL was approved by the CCEA in its meeting held on 21.1.99.  Necessary non-plan
provision of Rs.20.50 crore was made   in RE 1998-99.
5.3       Contrary to all hopes the Second revival packages for BRL failed because of the following inherent problems of the company
were not attended to: -
i                Low labour productivity due to excess manpower
ii               High cost of production due to high operating cost and increase in the price of input but decline in the price of
refractory items.
iii             Under utilisation of production capacity due to obsolete machinery, inadequate working capital and low work orders
from steel industry due to decline in demand of conventional refractory items.
iv      Continued monthly loss as a result- of quoting selling price at contribution margin by the company due to stiff
competition.

7.0       Problems being faced by Refectories industry 
7.1       The Sub-Committee was given an understanding that the growth of Refractory Industry is closely inter-linked with the
development and growth of steel industry as more than 75 per cent of the total refractories produced in the country are consumed by
steel industries while 15 per cent are used by Cement & Glass Industries and rest goes for Non-ferrous metal and petrol-chemical
sectors.  Refractories contribute around 5 per cent   towards the total cost of steel making.    During the last several years, the demand
of superior grade refractories required for BOF, secondary refining and continuous casting units of steel industries posed challenges
to the refractory producers.  The emphasis by the industry in the recent years is towards import substitution    and production of
special items like magnesia carbon, slide gate refractories, submerged nozzles, shrouds.  Monoblock stoppers, low –cement castables,
dense silica bricks for tall coke ovens, etc. The choice of refractories for steel making depends on various factors like achieving
reduced fuel consumption, increased furnace capacity, increased furnace life and availability and improved product quality.  The
future of refractory industry lies in continued development of improved bonding in basic and high alumina materials, advances in tar  
pitch bonding and impregnation and development of resion bonding. Monolithics special those incorporating low cement, carbon/.
Graphite emerged and relatively high performance materials.  Besides there is greater emphasis for greater energy efficiency. The
changing attitude of present day consumers is laying more importance on the availability of furnace, cleanliness of steel, ultimate cost
benefit, even if the initial investment were high.  This may be reckoned from the fact that during the last three decades, the
improvement in refractories quality has led to about 50 to 60 per cent decrease in specific consumption of refractories, whereas the
turnover of refractory units has been steadily rising.
 
7.2       The Committee is of the view that, since 75 per cent of the total refractories produced in the country      are consumed by steel
industries and steel industry is demanding superior grade of refractories, the Ministry of steel should facilitate the refractories
industry in general and BRL in particular to     undertake adequate research work for production of such superior grade of refractories
which suits the requirement of steel industry.  The Committee   recommends that research work in refractories industry may take into
consideration the requirement of steel industry which inter-alia include reduction in fuel consumption, incurred furnace capacity,
incurred furnace   life and availability and improved product quality.
 
7.3       The major factors that have led to decline in refractory consumption are increased stringency on the quality of refractory
products and improvement of quality along with improved operational parameters. The operating data of SAIL and their perspective
planning as indicated below for the next decade are indicators of the trend in steel sector.

7.4.      The Sub-Committee wanted to know whether it is a fact that technological improvement in steel making has affected the
entire refractories industry and unhealthy competition has led to price war, if so, the measures that could be undertaken to improve
the physical and financial performance of refractories industry in general and BRL in particular. The BRL management informed the
Sub-Committee that improvement in Steel making Technology alongwith development of superior quality refractories has reduced
the consumption of refractories per ton of steel in the country, which has particularly created a price war situation. Impact of this
during last three years resulted in price reduction to the tune of 20 / 25 per cent every year leading to a situation as on date when
some of the products are priced at almost raw materials cost.
7.5       The Committee observes that technological improvement in steel making has not only led to the increasing demand   of
superior quality refractories and reduction in total consumption of refractories but also generated a price war situation   and unfair
trade price due to excessive competition among refractories producers.  The Committee is of the view that improvement in steel
making technology is a welcome and necessary   development for betterment   of steel industry while reduction in total consumption
of refractories is an unavoidable development and it may be hoped that with the sign of recovery in steel industry the demand of
refractories would increase.  So far as the situation of price was due to excessive competition   is concerned, the Committee
recommends that the Ministry of Steel should take up the matter to ensure that healthy competition should prevail in the Refractories
Industry and competition should not result into unfair trade   prices.  The price of the refractories product be allowed to be determined
by the free market forces of demand and supply.

7.9.      The Committee understands the problems being faced by refractories industry arising due to imbalance between
manufacturing cost and selling price.  The Committee observes that the price of the finished products has been drastically reduced
(ranging from 30 to 44 per cent) whereas there is a trend in increase in the price structure of input materials (ranging from 5 to 17 per
cent).  This is the double loss to the refractory industry and under the   said circumstances any industry is bound to be affected. 
Therefore, the Committee is of the view that refractories industry in general and BRL management   in particular should chalk out a
two pronged strategy i.e. one to arrest the fall in the selling price of refractories and   to reverse the trend and other strategy is
required to minimise the cost of production so that the `margin of profit' for refractories industry may increase.  The Committee
further recommends   that   the Ministry of Steel should act as a facilitator and guide the BRL management to undertake measures for
maximisation of profit.
 
7.10 Cost Component of BRL Products Vis- a-Vis Unit In Private Sectors
7.10.1  Apprising the Sub-Committee of cost component of BRL products vis-a--vis unit in Private sectors and steps undertaken to
reduce the same and out-come of efforts undertaken the management stated that the main difference of cost component between BRL
products vis- a-vis Private sectors products are due to over-head cost mainly Salary & wages and other benefits. This cost is about 30
per cent of   the total cost of production& In case of BRL while in case of private sector it is only 10 per cent. BRL is trying to reduce

proposes to set up the project through joint venture utilising the existing facilities at IFICO. Refractories Plant . In view of the same,
a  provision of Rs.10.90 crore has been made in the BE 2001-2002  with a budgetary support of Rs.0.50 crore.
10.2.3  The Sub-Committee wanted to know the  current status of BRL plan to set up a continuous Casting Refractories Plant in joint

and one Party namely MONNET ISPAT LTD. offered their proposal to become joint venture partner and this has been forwarded to
the Ministry. The proposal is for 60 : 40 percent equity participation by Monnet &BRL and for signing MOU on this basis. BRL
asked permission of Ministry of Steel which is awaited. The BRL management  informed the Sub-Committee that the  original cost of
the project was about Rs. 23 crores as done by MECON in 1992 but later on in 2000- 2001 the cost escalated upto 38 crores when a
separate Company in joint venture was thought.  Replying to the Sub-Committee's query regarding the proportion   of the 
Government equity and the sources to raise the required fund the BRL management stated that BRL's equity part of 40 per cent
would be adjusted by the cost of land and Technology transfer cost alongwith some fund from plan loan to be sanctioned by Govt of
India in 10th 5 year plan .
 
10.2.4  BRL is not  in a position to generate sufficient  internal  resources to meet the cost  of the project.  BRL proposed  to make the
project for a joint venture.  In order to obtain a joint venture partner, BRL floated open  tender  and only one party viz., Monnet Ispat
Limited responded for the same for 60:40 equity participation  by  Monnet and BRL.   The  proposal is under consideration   of the  
Government Chairman-cum-Managing Director, BRL have also  discussed   with Managing  Director, Ferro scrap Nigam Ltd.  for 
their participation in the project which is under scrutiny stage at Ferrro scrap Nigam Ltd.  which is also a public sector undertaking
under the Ministry of Steel.
10.2.5  The   Committee is of the considered view that setting up of Continuous Casting System (RCC)  is long  overdue .The  
Standing  Committee on Industry has  recommended   in its earlier reports for setting up of refractories for  Continuous   Casting 
System at the earliest for ensured survival of the company.  The RCC has got the merit of positive  feasibility report as prepared by
M/s  MECON and the technical know-how as obtained from M/s  Shinagow Refractories  Ltd.,  Japan.  The   technological changes
in steel  making process leaves little scope for demand of conventional refractories and  opens the  avenues    for refractories  for
Continuous Casting System.  All   these factors   necessitate    the setting up of RCC, sooner the better, since  original   estimated
cost  i.e. Rs.23 crore has  already overrun  to the tune of Rs.38.00 crore .  The only   deterring  factor  earlier was the lack of fund for
the project as BRL was  not in a position to generate  sufficient internal   resources to meet the cost of the project.    However, this 
problem seems to be solved  as  M/s Monnet  Ispat Ltd. has offered the  proposal to become a  joint venture partner with BRL  with
the  equity  ratio of  60 : 40 and , not only that , BRL's  equity of 40 per cent  would be  adjusted by  the cost of land and technology
transfer cost alongwith  Government fund from plan loan during the 10
th Five Year Plan.  Would the Ministry of Steel further   allow

13.0     Tax Structure
13.1     Pertaining to the Sub-Committee's query as  to what extent the existing tax structure is beneficial in the interest of refractories
industry  and the steps  which could be undertaken for rationalization of tax structure,  the BRL management stated that most of the
raw materials required for manufacturing of high tech refractory items are not available in the country as such import of huge
quantity of these materials is a must. There has been some rationalization of tax structure during the budget of 2001- 2002 while
custom duties of three items namely D-B-M, Fused Magnesite & Sea Water Magnesia was reduced to 15 per cent. Thereby giving
some relief to Refractory industries. However, there are various other raw materials like B-F-A, W-F-A, Tabular Alumina graphite &
fume Silica required for high Tech products needs the same relief to enable Indian products cost effective & competitive in price
compared to foreign suppliers.
 
13.2     During the budget presentation for 2001-02 relief on  Sea Water Magnesia, Fused Magnesia and Dead Burnt Magnesia  was
considered  by the Government of India and the same has been reduced to  15 per cent   plus 4 per cent surcharge. During the budget  
presentation of 2002-03 , the duty on  Brown fused Alumina, Sintered Alumina, Silicon Carbon,  Graphite etc., was  reduced to 30
per cent .  However, the  refractory bricks  imported from outside is charged 25 per cent   custom duty,   Reduction of custom duty an
input material  to 50 per cent of the finished bricks import duty would be suitable for refractories industry.

time to time, and suggest  measures  for capital restructuring ,in such a way, that BRL  achieves the break -even point and economy
of  scales ensuring maximum   Return on Investment (ROI).
 
( Para : 2.5)
 
Sickness of BRL and IFICO.
 
            The Committee notes the reasons for BRL  and IFICO  becoming sick and coming under the purview of BIFR in  1992.  The
Committee observes that due attention for  revival of BRL was not considered at the appropriate time  and that  is the reasons why
BRL continued  to make losses year  after year.  What  other result might be expected, from a loss making company which continued
to operate with  obsolescent machinery;    under utilisation  of  installed capacity;     low labour- productivity and shortage of capital ,
than falling  in the  net of BIFR.  BRL missed the ( the first  opportunity for revival), the Committee concludes.
 ( Para : 3.2)
 
IDBI's Revival Scheme
            The Committee  observes  that since IDBI's revival package had  no cash assistance to BRL as working capital  on the
assumption that with its revised capital base and net worth position,  BRL   would be in  a position to raise working capital and
sustain higher levels of production during 1995-96 and onwards, the  non-availability of working  capital to BRL  blocked the
chances of increasing its production as well as improving its product  mix  for better   sales realisation . The Committee  understand
that it is because of the slow down in the steel industry .The demand for refractories had  not been of the  level expected. It is but
natural that ,  in the above scenario, the company was  unable to generate  internal resources so as to meet its  plan and non-plan
expenditure.  BRL ended 1997-98 with a cash loss of Rs.14.33 crore and   the same adverse trend persisted  during   1998-99.
( Para : 4.4)
 
            The Committee understands that the general  recession in  steel industry  in addition to BRL's  inherent problems i.e. under
utilisation of  installed capacity, interruption in power supply, problems in  the procurement of  raw-material due to lack of working
capital followed by non -implementation of IDBI's   revival scheme led to the continued  dwindling  in physical and  financial 
performance of the company.
( Para : 4.5)
 
Second Revival  Package for  BRL
The Committee  is constrained to express its anguish over the fact that  the Second Revival  Package for BRL had not 
envisaged the true picture of BRL and ,  henceforth , the Second  revival package  could  not  redress to the actual problems being
faced by  BRL.  Like IDBI's    revival  package, the Second Revival Package for BRL  failed, the committee is perturbed to note.
 
( Para : 5.4)
Physical and Financial Performance of BRL
            The Committee   notes the physical production of BRL and observes  that since  refractory  industry is linked  with the
development and  growth of  Steel Industry and since  steel industry was itself running through   rough whether  it  is but natural that
production performance of BRL is  just good.  However, the   steel industry is now coming out of wood, therefore, the Committee 
hopes  that BRL would, accordingly, streamline the production.
( Para : 6.2)
            The Committee is constrained to express its concern over the financial performance of BRL and observes that   unless and 
until the revival package for BRL is implemented  successfully financial performance would not improve. Therefore, the Committee
recommends that BRL  management would  make all out   efforts to implement the revival package  without time over-run.
( Para : 6.4)
Problems being faced by Refectories industry

            The Committee is of the view that, since 75 per cent of  the  total refractories produced in the country   are consumed by steel
industries and steel industry is  demanding  superior grade of refractories, the Ministry of steel should facilitate the refractories
industry in general and BRL in particular to     undertake adequate  research work for  production of such superior grade of 
refractories which  suits the requirement of  steel industry.  The Committee   recommends that research work in  refractories  industry
may take into  consideration  the requirement of steel industry which inter-alia  include reduction in fuel  consumption, incurred
furnace  capacity, incurred furnace   life and availability  and improved product quality.
( Para : 7.2)
            The Committee observes that  technological  improvement in steel making has  not only led to the  increasing demand   of
superior quality refractories  and reduction in total consumption  of refractories but generated  a price war situation   and unfair trade 
price due to excessive competition  among refractories producers.  The  Committee is of the view that improvement  in steel making 
technology is a welcome and necessary   development for betterment   of steel industry while  reduction in total consumption of
refractories is an unavoidable development and it may be hoped that  with the sign of recovery in steel industry the demand of
refractories would increase.  So far as the situation of price was due to  excessive competition   is concerned, the  Committee 
recommends that the Ministry  of Steel should take up the matter  to ensure that  healthy  competition should prevail in the
Refractories Industry  and competition should not result  into unfair trade   prices.  The price of the  refractories product be  allowed
to be determined  by the free market  forces  of demand and  supply.
 ( Para : 7.5)
            The Committee understands the problems  being faced by refractories industry  arising due to imbalance between
manufacturing  cost and selling price.  The Committee observes that the price of the finished  products has been  drastically reduced
(ranging from  30 to 44 per cent ) whereas there is a  trend in increase in  the price structure of input materials ( ranging from 5 to 17
per cent ).  This is the  double loss  to the refractory  industry  and under the   said  circumstances any  industry is bound to  be
affected.  Therefore, the Committee is of the view that refractories industry in general and BRL  management   in particular should 
chalk out a two pronged strategy  i.e. one  to arrest  the  fall in the selling price of refractories and   to reverse the trend  and other
strategy is required to  minimise the cost of production so that  the `margin of profit' for refractories industry may increase.  The
Committee further recommends   that   the Ministry of Steel should act as a facilitator and guide the BRL management to undertake
measures for maximisation of profit.
(Para: 7.9)

become competitive vis-a-vis units in private s

 (Para: 8.6)
Position of inventories.
            It is but natural that a company which lacks sufficient working capital and there is a low demand of its   products,  would have
little   inventories, and Bharat Refractories Ltd. is no  exception to this rule,  the Committee observes.  However, the Committee feels
that   the BRL management should keep a constant   watch on the inventories and dispose of the    inventory as and when the same
accumulates because even small   inventory, since the volume of production   is confined to the availability of capital and market
demand, would further block the scarce resources of working capital and thereby creating a  `Vicious - circle'  of insufficient
working  capital, low production and no inventories .  Sufficient working, high volume of production and low inventory should be the
motto of the company.
(Para :9.2)

Additions, Modifications & Replacements (AMR)
 
The Committee is of the opinion that modification replacement in existing machineries and addition of new equipment is    
essential    to keep the production process going smoothly   and   effectively.  The Committee feels that since BRL is continuously
making loss and   is not in a position to generate internal revenue, therefore, it is   but natural   that budgetary support   from the
Government is required to carry on the AMR schemes.  The Committee hopes that once BRL is put on the revival track and start
making profit, it would not require budgetary support, any longer.
 (Para : 10.3.3)
 SWOT (Strength, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threat) analysis of BRL
 
The Committee is of the view that BRL   management should keep on reviewing  the SWOT-analysis of the company.  The
Committee further recommends that BRL  management  should take  advantage of its strength  and  avail all opportunities  which 
come its way .  The Committee hopes that with the sanction of revival package by the Government   BRL would  be able to 
overcome  most  of its weaknesses i.e. surplus manpower and  obsolete  technology and non-availability of  working capital etc.  The
Committee   further   hopes that once  the revival package is implemented, properly and in time,  the Company would be able  to
combat all the threats.  As regards the  irregular supply of power by Jharkhand  State  Electricity Board,   the Committee recommends
that the  Ministry of Steel  assist the  BRL management  in taking up the matter with the state government.
(Para: 11.2)
Management By Objectives  ( MBO )
The Committee is happy to note that BRL management is guided by the principle of management by objectives in   letter and
spirit.  The Committee, however, recommends that BRL management should re-orient its objectives as per changing industrial
scenario and make its efforts to achieve the corporate objectives and mission.  The Committee further recommends   that BRL
management may chalk out `Key Areas for Intensive Action' for better performance of the company.
 (Para: 12.3)
Tax Structure
The Committee is pleased to note that Government is giving due consideration to the matter of rationalised tax structure, in
its Annual General Budget.  In General Budget 2001-02 as well as 2002-03 some tax concession have been   given to refractory
industries.   However, the Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry of Steel should keep on studying tax-structure suitable for
refractories Industry and accordingly approaches the Ministry of Finance for suitable action.
 (Para: 13.3)
Foreign collaboration
The Committee is of the view   that the transfer of technical know-how (technology) and its absorption/adaptation in the
system is   vital for carrying out production activities   in the cost effective manner and products of quality production.  In this
context, the Committee is happy to note that BRL   has entered into   collaboration for technical know-how with Central Glass &
Ceramic Research Institute, Kolkata, SAIL, Kawasaki, Refractories   Company, Japan, Singapore Refractory Co., and Plibrico.
Franch and Technology taken from this organisation have effectively been adopted in BRL.  However, the Committee desires that
impact of the `transfer of technology & its absorption in BRL must be reflected in the improved physical and financial performance
of BRL.
 (Para: 14.3)
Manpower in BRL and  VRS

manpower in BRL.  The Committee hopes that BRL would feel no paucity of fund, keeping in view the fact that the revival proposal
