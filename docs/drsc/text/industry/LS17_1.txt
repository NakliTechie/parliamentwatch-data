FIRST REPORT OF THE COMMITTEE 
I, the Chairman, Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry, 
having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this 
First Report of the Committee on the Demands for Grants for 1993-94 of the Ministry of 
Industry. 
2. In pursuance of Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Rajya Sabha 
pertaining to Department Related Standing Committees, Hon-'ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha 
and Hon 'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha nominated 42 members of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha to 
the Committee on Industry for the functions assigned to it under the Rules. Further 
Chairman, Rajya Sabha and Speaker, Lok Sabha decided that all the provisions of Rule 270 
of the Rules relating to the functions of the Committee shall become operative with effer1 
from 30th March, 1993. 
3. The Committee on Industry deals with the subjects falling in the jurisdiction of t11e 
Ministry of Industry, Ministry of Steel and Ministry of Mines. 
4. The Committee accordingly held its first meeting on the 22nd April, 1993 and 
considered relevant documents and papers received from the Ministry of Industry, on the 
Demands for Grants for the year 1993-94. 
5. The Committee also heard the oral evidence of the Secretaries of the various 
Department s of the Ministry of Industry on the performance budget in the context of 
Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Industry. 
6. The Committee considered the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in three sittings and 
considered and adopted the Report at its sitting held on 4th May, 1993.

9.3 (ii) The Committee finds that there is only a marginal increase of Rs. 50 lakh. 'n the 
Budget Estimates compared to last year budget estimates of 32 crores. Rs. 32.5 crores 
sought for in this current year Budget Estimates is to be spent for meeting expenses on two 
items, i.e., transport subsidy and establishment of Growth Centres. From the funds asked 
for under the Scheme, the Committee notes that adequate planning has not been done. The 
implementation is tardy. The backward areas in the country are in difficult terrain and Jack 
of basic infra!'.tructure. Concrete steps have, therefore. to be taken to motivate people to 

avail of the facilities and concessions under the Scheme. State Governments sometimes do 
not keep any provision for the purpose. The infrastructural deVelopment in the area for the 
establishment of Growth Centres is done by the Central Government only when the 
concerned State Government is ready to provide with the required funds. The entrepreneurs 
are caught in the cobweb of Central Government grant, State Government grant, loans from 
financial institutions and market borrowings. The entrepreneur finds extremely difficult and 
feels demotivated when he faces diffculties right from the acquisition of land, power supply, 
availability of water, etc. Many entrepreneurs have left midway their projects due to these 
factors and non-cooperation from different sections of Governmental machinery . The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that for the development of Growth Centres in the 
backward areas 'single window scheme' may be adopted as one of the motivating factors to 
willing entrepreneurs to establish industrial units in backward areas. The Committee would 
further recommend perfect coordination among the Central Government, concerned State 
Government and the financial institution· to make the Scheme successful. The Committee 
would, therefore, like that Government should give re-orientation to the programme and 
ensure its proper implementation.

9.6 (ii) The Committee, therefore, finds that foreign investment and technical collabora­
tion has been approved in Industrial Sector relating to even Food Processing Industries to 
the tune of 8472.2 millions of rupees without any consideration for the growth of indigenous 
industries in this Sector. Food Processing Industry have to a large extent depend on the local 
ancillary small scale and village and cottage industries and thereby there is no safeguard for 
the survival and development of such industries. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that :-
fa) disinvestment by PSUs and the funds accrued on account of the disinvestment 
should be utilised for reviving, and restructuring of the sick PSUs. The demand for 
grants and performance budget are totally silent on this aspect; 
(b) foreign investments should be approved in areas where we are yet to develop the 
technical know-how and depend upon foreign technology, The Government should 
ensure that foreign investment is earmarked for industries in the export sector to 
boost up the export of our industrial production. It should not disturb and dislocate 
the existing industrial growth of the country in the long run. The foreign 
collaboration should be identified for industries where we are lagging behind in 
international market. The Committee regrets to note that the demand for grants for 
the Department of Heavy Largest Industry does not perceive this situation and no 
funds have been allocated to organise and regulate foreign investment in our 
industrial sector particularly in the PSUs which we have developed and grown over 
a period of several decades but for which the present infrastructure for further 
development could not have been there; 
(c) every effort should be made to ensure that all the eight· benches of Board for 
Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) are made functional and the cases of 
sick PSUs pending before them are disposed off expeditiously to minimise the drain 
on the public funds. The Demands for Grants of the Department and the 
Performance Budget do not reflect any concern on the depleted strength of the 
BIFR benches and the expenditure and efforts to be made by the Department to 
make them functional; 
(d) the details of disinvestments made by the Public Sector Undertakings, undertaking­
wise, should be compiled by the Department and the data relating to the sale prices 
of shares, undertaking-wise and the amount of money so collected and spent, 

undertaking-wise may be collected may be furnished to the Committee for further detailed 
examination in order to allay the misgivings in the minds of general publia that the shares 
were sold at throw away prices by some Public Sector Undertakings; and 
(e) concrete steps should be taken by the Department to ensure that foreign investors 
are not allowed to dump their unsold products in India to the detrement of 
indigenous industries. 
9.6 (iii) MUL Incidentally , the Committee finds that the CISF was providing security at 
Maruti Udyog Limited at Gurgaon since 1983. In June 1992, MUL became a non­
government company and it was felt by MUL that the continuance of CISF would probably 
not be possible. MUL looked for alternative arrangements. It was found that Group 4 
Securities India (P) Ltd. was the best. Group 4 Securities is an international security 
company with operations in 40 countries. It started operations in India in February 1990 and 
has six branches in India. After a period of familiarisation and training the Group 4 
contingent has taken over security responsibility for MUL plant w.e.f April 1, 1993. 
The Committee notes with regret that even in the matter of switching over the Security 
network, Maruti Udyog Limited had to opt for an international security company thereby 
opening the door to a multinational company for a job for which trained and adequate 
manpower and resources were available within the country, if it was a necessity to dispense 
with the security cover provided by CISF, local talent and manpower could have been 
engaged by Maruti Udyog.

Comparing the budget figures for an important item like training which is 3.41 crores with 
that of the expenditure on the maintenance of the office of Development Commissioner, it 
may be seen that expenditure priorities are not on sound lines. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that every step should be taken to regulate the expenditure on this item and 
the money thus saved may be earmarked for other developmental item(s) of the Small Scale 
Industries. 
9. 7 (iii) Khadi and Village Industries: Khadi and Village Industries have been earmarked a 
sum of Rs. 525.64 crores in the current year Budget Estimates compared to 352 crores in the 
last years revised estimates. The Khadi and Village Industries cover a vast majority of our 
population in different areas of the country and primarily cater to the needs of our 
economically weaker sections. The Khadi and Village Industries generate vast potential for 
local employment and if given correct direction and infrastructural support, Kha.di and 
Village Industries can be more helpful in our rural economy. The Committee, however, 
finds that inspite of budgetary support, Khadi and Village Industries have not been able to 
compete in the open market and stand on their legs. The reasons , therefore, have to be 
studied and perceptive planning is to be made to make a beginning so that Khadi and 
Village Industries become self-sufficient in their functioning in all respects. The Commit tee 
finds that no thought is given in the Demands for Grants and there is no expenditure on this 
account. The Committee therefore , recommends that the priorities of expenditure may be 

re-fixed and monetary allocation be made. The Committee further recommends that every 
effort should be made to ensure that Khadi and Village Industri es do not suffer from 
shortage of financial resources, lack of infrastructural support and bureaucratic red-tapi sm. It 
is essent ial that the problem of marketing of the finished goods is taken on top priority. The 
Government should issue instructions at least to their Departments that Khadi/Village/Small 
Scale Industries Products are purchased for their requirements on the pattern of encourage­
ment given for Handloom cloth. The other point the Committee emphasises that it should be 
made obligatory on the part of the establishments of such industrie s to pay minimum living 
wages to the employees. The Committee further recommends that concrete steps are to be 
taken to ensure for the general health and welfare of the workers. Co-operatives in the field 
are to be activised and finished goods are marketed for adequate return. The Committee is 
aware of the fact that rural masses are not gainfully employed throughout the year. Some 
programmes for motivating the rural masses to utilise their spare time in engaging 
themselves in such industries are to be devised. The Committee finds that though a sum of 
Rs. 525.54 crores has been earmarked in the Budget Estimates for this item yet no money 
has been allotted for motivating people and for awareness programm es on the role and the 
utility of Khadi and Village Industries to rural masses. The programme for training 
undertaken by the Department also lacks perception. The Committee opines that prog­
ramme for training should be so designed and chalked out so as to give on the spot training 
and guidance by mobile training personnel in the first instance to familiarise the rural masses 
about various projects/schemes under Khadi and Village Industrie s. The initial phase may 
inrer-alia include ground level information about project, equipment, finance and marketing 
facilities for finished products. Once this phase of training is completed and the masses are 
motivated and assured of additional revenue to the person or to the family then additional 
training programmes as envisaged by the Department in the Budget Estimates would yield 
positive results. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the thrust of the training 
programme should be reoriented and grouped into two phases as mentioned above. 
The Committee trusts that its observations and recommendations will receive serious 
considerat ion for implementation.
