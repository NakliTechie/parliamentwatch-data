REPORT
 
I.          INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1       The Department of Heavy Industry is concerned with the development of the heavy engineering industry,
machine tool industry, heavy electrical industry, industrial machinery and auto-industry. The Industries covered by this
Department provide goods and services for almost all sectors of the economy, including power, rail and road transport.
They also cater to the requirements of equipments for basic industries such as steel, non-ferrous metals, fertilizers,
refineries, petrochemicals, shipping, textiles, paper, cement, sugar etc. The Department is responsible for development of
a wide range of intermediate engineering products like castings, forgings, diesel engines, industrial gears and gear boxes.
It is  also  vested with the administrative charge of 48 Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs).
 
1.2       The  PSEs under the administrative control of the Department are engaged in manufacturing of
engineering/capital goods, consultancy and contracting activities. The total investment (Gross Block) in 48 Public Sector
Enterprises under the Department was about Rs.7780.24 crores  till  March, 2000  employing about 1.61 lakh  persons.
 
1.3       The Department holds interactions with various industry councils and evolves plans for their growth. The
Department also assists Public Sector Enterprises through policy initiatives, resolution of problems relating to tariffs and
trade, technological collaboration, up- gradation and Research & Development, etc.

III.       RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT OF AUTOMOTIVE INDUSTRY
 
3.1       The Department stated that R&D and technology development is the backbone of the automotive industry.
Because of this fact, a cess has been imposed on Automobiles with effect from 1.1.1984 and on tractors from 1.10.1985
at the rate of 18% ad-valorem to finance R&D in the industry. The cess is being collected by the Department of Revenue
and forms part of the Consolidated Fund of India. The Ministry of Finance is allocating a Portion of this fund through the
Budget to this Ministry under the head “Cess for the Development Council for Automobile and Allied Industries”.
 
3.2       It was also stated that with the fragmented automobile industry in India, it is not feasible for the individual units
to establish full-fledged R&D set up and testing facilitates. Hence there is a need to establish  R&D testing  facilities
which will cater  to the industry as a whole. With the  introduction of new and stricter emission norms as well as safety
standards , more & more  R&D facilities are needed in the country to meet the demand of the Industry, it was added. The
Department further added that the Development Council for Automotive and Allied Industry (DCAAI) allocates  Cess
funds for research and development to establish R&D institutions through a Cess Committee chaired by Secretary,
Department of  Heavy Industry. 
 
3.3       The Secretary during the course of his oral evidence informed the Committee that  there is tremendous need for
creating the testing facilities in the country as well as having R&D projects. He also informed the Committee that the
DHI have constituted the Development Council of Automotive Sector and  have established a Committee  to  authorize
and recommend use of R&D and testing facilities. The Secretary also mentioned to the Committee that  0.05% cess 

47th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/47threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:12:25]
may  not  be sufficient looking at the requirement of fund that has emerged.
              
3.4          It was further stated by the Secretary that in view of Euro II and Euro III norms,  considerable investment in
testing facilities will have to be generated, and also, DHI  have to provide for testing to see that the imported new and
old cars  conform to CMVR norms. Therefore, there is an added requirement for testing facilities, he felt. He also stated
that  there is a need now to set up at least two testing tracks, one in  the North and another in the South. In view of all
this, the cess needs to be raised to 0.25%, the Secretary felt.  It was revealed by the Secretary,  that the Department had
got Rs.10 crores for utilization from the Cess Fund which  is also insufficient. This will have to be considerably
increased if the DHI wants  to give fillip to R&D and testing facilities, he felt.
 
3.5       The Secretary also informed the Committee that there is need for promoting electric vehicles. He also felt that
there is need for having nominal custom and excise duties for electric vehicles and also for subsidy through the
Department of Non-Conventional Sources of Energy for three wheelers that they were giving for larger vehicles. 
 
3.6       The Committee  feels that the  cess  which  has been used till now for creating facilities for conducting test as per
Central Motor Vehicle Rules needs to be  used for  other areas  of product and process related research with a view to
giving a fillip to   India’s  automobile industries.  It is also necessary to enable the  automobile industries to survive   the 
imports  surge after WTO regime became fully applicable.  The  Committee  recommends that the Ministry should  take
stock of what is being done and what  further is to be done.  R&D  has  to be undertaken for product and process related
research also.
 
3.7       The Committee understands that the cess of 0.05%, which is being charged at  present, needs to be increased to
0.25% for  making   necessary investment in testing  facilities.  The Committee agrees with the view expressed by the
Secretary that the allocation of Rs. 10 crores, that  has been made, is insufficient  as R&D  and testing facilities  have to
be enhanced.  Therefore, Ministry of Finance may consider increasing at RE stage.
 
3.8       The Committee also  agrees  with the views expressed by the Secretary that the need of the hour is electric
vehicles for having pollution free transportation  in the cities, particularly, in the metropolises. The Government   may
consider exemption of customs duty and surcharge on items imported for use in manufacture of electric vehicles and
reduction of excise duty on electric vehicles. The Ministry of Non-Conventional  Sources of  Energy  may  also consider 
providing  subsidies to the extent of 50% to three  wheelers and the Department of Heavy Industry may  pursue the
matter in this regard.
 
3.9       The Department  have also  informed  the Committee that after  India  became  compliant with the WTO regime
with effect from 01-04-2001, quantitative restrictions on imports, regulations regarding local content in manufacture and
export obligations would  not be admissible. Import of automotive vehicles of all categories would  become permissible..
In order to address the following issues, the Ministry is in the process of formulating a comprehensive  holistic policy
compatible with the WTO regime:-
(i)
                  Exalt the sector as a lever of industrial growth and employment.
(ii)                Promote a globally competitive automotive industry and emerge as a global source for auto
components.
(iii)               Enable the development of a domestic safety and environmental regulatory structure for the
sector.
(iv)              Facilitate indigenous design, research and development.
 
3.10     The Committee  feels that the policy compatible with  WTO regime should have been formulated  much   before 
the regime became fully operational.  At least now, there should not be any further delay in formulating the Policy.

agencies. As against total dues of Rs 1189 crores, the total outstanding  dues for payment to BHEL is Rs 929 crores.

XII.  NEPA LTD.
12.1     NEPA Ltd. was floated by a Private Entrepreneur in 1947. The Management was taken over by the then Central
Provinces & Berar Govt. in 1949.  Subsequently, it became  a GoI-PSU in 1959. Commercial production started in 1956
in this pioneering Newsprint manufacturing unit of India.
 
12.2     As part of globalisation, the Newsprint Policy was opened up gradually since Oct'1991 lifting all restrictions and
liberal imports allowed under OGL at zero or negligible duty. NEPA Ltd. was exposed to the international competition
and modern newsprint mills of the country. The company found it ill equipped to face this marketing challenge.
 
 12.3    NEPA suffered the most due to its outdated technology(set up in 1950's) resulting in inferior quality, excess
manpower, higher cost of production, poorer yields and efficiencies, imbalances in pulping capacity etc. Consequently,
NEPA had to close down operations in August 1996 due to unsold finished stock inventory of around I 0,000 MT,
primarily due to fierce competition from imported newsprint which was virtually being dumped in large quantity, as also
acknowledged 'by the Anti Dumping Authority under the Commerce Ministry. Price of Imported newsprint, which was
around $1 000 PMT in 1995, suddenly plunged to a low of $450-550 PMT during 1996 and remained at that level for 4
long years, dragging newsprint industry to recession. This was a departure from the yesteryears when newsprint's
production, price, distribution, sale, stocking was all regulated by the Govt.
 
12.4       Sridharan Committee appointed by Govt. of India to re-start operations recommended one time support of Rs.15
Crores and a subsidy of Rs.7000/- per MT of stock produced, besides wage revision w.e.f. 1.1.1992. However, no such
support was ever available to the Company.
 
12.5       Condition of Plant & Machinery in operation is deteriorating fast for inadequate maintenance leading to
increased down time due to lack of internal generation or any plan fund support. Condition is now very critical which
may result into a stoppage of production if immediate essential/emergency repairs are not undertaken in the Plant &
Machinery.

XIV.  GENERAL PROBLEMS OF PAPER INDUSTRY
 
14.1     Newsprint industry has been traditionally at a disadvantageous position due to lower return on its capital
employed, causing investments to go to higher returnable sectors. The opportunity costs entailed due to the above
scenario could have been obviated by appropriate policy instrument. However, abolition of QRs as a result of WTO
commitment has hit the newsprint harder and the 1:2 procurement condition now will have to be disbanded. Also, other
non-tariff barriers also have caused to be removed leading to inclusion of newsprint under the Open General License. In
this scenario the only ray of light to enable this industry to survive or turn-around is through the systematic tariff regime.
However, it is observed that a progressive and systematic tariff regime has been denied to this industry.  While the bound
rate under the WTO is 35%, the customs duty at present is at 5%. Besides, many of the intermediaries used in the 
manufacture of newsprint also attract very high custom duty rates. This type of customs tariff structure is not
commensurate with the fiscal logic, which accords higher custom duty on end products than the intermediaries. 
 
14.2     Hence, the Committee recommends that the Government may not only consider raising the custom’s duty to the
WTO bound level but also a TARIFF REALIGNMENT exercise may be taken up by the government to enable this
industry  to be accorded a level playing field for realizing higher productivity.
 
14.3     The Committee also observed that the industry has been recently putting a lot of efforts towards replenishment of
critical forests, which are used in newsprint products. This replenishment effort of the industry should be strongly
supported by the government not only to ensure present ecological balance but also as a futuristic measure. The
Committee hence strongly recommends that a proactive support be extended to the industry  in terms of relaxing or
expediting the necessary administrative clearance for this noble exercise of the industry.
 
14.4     During the course of examination of Demands for Grants of Ministry of Heavy Industry,  the performance review
of HPC and NEPA  mills, the Committee observed certain anomalies in the excise/customs tariff classification of papers
and paperboards. The paper and paperboards are classified under the same heading. It is felt that while the end products
are distinctly different, even the manufacturing process involved is also different. The paperboard is made out of waste
recycling through non-conventional source and needs to be classified differently from paper, which, inter-alia, should
cover newsprint papers, etc. In this context it also came to the light that there is an excise duty exemption availed by
paper and newspaper manufacturers as well as paper board manufacturers producing upto 3500 MT annually. During the
investigation,  it came to the light of the committee that the mills are operating at sub-optimal capacity restricting their
production to 3500 MTs, enabling them to avail of this excise duty exemption.
 
14.5     The Committee  recommends that this anomaly needs to be corrected through distinguishing paper from
paperboard by virtue of differently classifying or sub-classifying one product from another. As regards duty exemption,
it is felt that the same should be based on installed capacity of the units and not annual production as such a provision is
vulnerable to abuse resulting in sub-optimal utilization of the capacity by units, which can also, amongst other things,
cause potential shortages of the products resulting in upward trend in prices of boards. 
 
14.6     The CMD, HNL informed the Committee that the quality of the imported newsprint from Canada is definitely
superior to our newsprint in terms of brightness. The price difference is about  Rs. 3000 per tonne. The advantage that
the countries like Canada, Europe, etc., have got is that they are having superior raw materials like spruce, pine, etc.,
where they can go in for lower g.s.m. newsprint. Now, every newspaper  publisher  wants to pay by weight and print by
metre. Newspaper publishing houses want to install machines which can print 1 lakh copies per hour, 40 pages at a time.
They are all going for high speed printing machines. With the kind of raw materials we are having in our country, we
will not be able to catch up to  that level, he added. To that extent, a lot of R&D is required to be done and the R&D
efforts are being done through the Central Pulp and Paper Research Institute,  in a modest way by the newspaper
manufacturers.
 
14.7     The Committee feels that the R&D efforts being made to improve the quality of newsprint are not to the extent it
is necessary. Looking at the present scenario, it  is very essential to catch-up with the best technology. Therefore, the

crores.  Overall the Company achieved turnover of Rs. 803.51 crores during 1999-2000 registering 350% growth over
the actual of Rs. 222.31 crores during 1998-99.
