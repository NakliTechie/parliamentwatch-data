INTRODUCTION
I,  the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee  on Industry,  having   been  
authorised  by the  Committee, hereby present this 100 th Report of  Sub-Committee-I for  Ministry of Steel and Mines (
of Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry)  on  Performance Review  of  National Mineral
Development Corporation Limited (NMDC) which is under the administrative control of  Ministry of Steel .

up the issue of Performance Review of National Mineral Development Corporation Limited (NMDC)). The Sub-
Committee visited Hyderabad  from 28th and 29th June , 2002 and interacted with the  management of National Mineral
Development Corporation Limited (NMDC). 
3.            The Report has been prepared on  the basis of   on the spot  study and  interaction with the    Secretary ,
Ministry of Steel and officials  of National Mineral Development Corporation Limited (NMDC).

REPORT
 
National Mineral Development Corporation Limited (NMDC) , a Government of India Enterprise and  a “Mini
Ratna Category-I” Company was established  in the year 1958, in pursuance of Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 to
promote and undertake development of minerals (other than coal, oil and atomic minerals) in Public Sector. NMDC is
the largest producer and exporter of iron ore in this Country. Its exports are around 7 million tonnes of iron ore per
annum (through Minerals & Metals Trading Corporation Ltd - canalising agency). It has also exported about 1.7 MT of

iron ore directly to China in 2001-02. NMDC has earned approximately Rs.750 Crores during the year 2001-02 by way
of foreign exchange. NMDC has been consistently achieving the rating of “ Excellent” from the very first year of
signing the MoU viz 1991-92.  It was placed in the fourth rank amongst the MOU signing PSEs for the year 1991-92,
from which it moved to third rank in the year 1992-93.  For the years 1993-94, 1994-95 and 1995-96, for NMDC’s
performance of excellence and the achievement of composite score of 1.00 in each of these years under the MOU
performance evaluation, NMDC has been placed as the ‘ Joint Top Rank PSE’ in the country. This unique achievement,
as the Joint Top Rank,   has been achieved continuously for three years in succession.  Again, in 1996-97, 1997-98,
1999-2000, 2000-2001 and 2001-2002 NMDC was adjudged “Excellent”, barring 1998-99 during which period there
was general recession. 
 
1.2.      The Secretary, the Ministry of Steel, during  the course of interaction, held on  4
th April, 02,  on Demands for
Grants (2002-03) informed  the Committee that  the performance of NMDC is of higher order.  NMDC has reported
profit of Rs.160.00 crore for 1999-2000 and upto Rs.234.00 crore for the year (2000-01)  and  in the current year   the
company might improve over the figure  of last year. 
 
1.3       In the same meeting CMD  of NMDC   Shri  P.R. Tripathi  added :-
 
“The Corporation was set up in 1958  with the sole objective of development of minerals resources of the country other
than coal, oil, natural gas and atomic minerals  and  chemicals.  Now, the NMDC’s activities are concentrated on mining
of  Iron Ore, limestone and diamonds.  Due to a very good team spirit, we have an excellent system of management in
the company.  We have been consistently making profits since 1990.   NMDC had been  paying a dividend of 20 per
cent, then it has been increased to 25 per cent on the capital of Rs,132 cores.  We have returned the whole amount that 
has been given to us by the Government.  The profit of the company is the highest in the Steel Ministry.  Last year we
had Rs.314 crores.  This year our profit is very good and in spite of very poor and difficult situation in the steel industry,
we have a very good turnover.  This is mainly because we are very well diversified.  45 per cent of our  products are 
very well  diversified.  45 per cent of our products are being exported and 55 per cent is being consumed by the
domestic market.   ISPAT, ESSAR, VIKRAM are  depending on our product.  VSP is our major customer.  We
maintain very cordial relations with all so  that we can flourish in our business.”
 
1.4       The Committee notes that NMDC had been setting new records of performance in the areas of production,
despatches of iron ore, production and sales of diamonds,  increased supplies to domestic consumers.  Thus, NMDC has
been consistently scaling new heights of achievements from year to year both in physical and financial terms including
foreign exchange earnings to the country.

4.0       MANAGEMENT BY OBJECTIVES (MBO)
 
4.1       Pertaining the sub-committee’s query as  to what extent NMDC management is guided by the principle of
Management by Objectives (MBO) and to what extent the efforts undertaken by the management helped in realizing the
Mission of the company, NMDC management stated that          NMDC is not functioning or guided by terminology of
Management By Objectives but follows a strategy for achieving it’s mission and objectives.  Since mission and
objectives are clear to most of the employees, all efforts are guided to achieve the mission through set objectives.  This
approach is definitely helping the Corporation which can be seen by constant increase of turn over. NMDC had a turn
over of Rs.281.98 crore in 1991-92 which has gone up to Rs.1272.39 crore in 2001-02. This was possible only due to
increase in iron ore production and its sale and also in the production and sale of diamonds.
 
4.2       The Committee is pleased to note that NMDC  follows a strategy for achieving its mission and objectives. By
application of the principle of Management by objective (MBO) the Committee meant  as to what extent the efforts of
management and  workers are  guided to achieve the mission through set objectives. However, the Committee expresses
its satisfaction that NMDC, for all its practical purposes, is guided by ‘management by objective’  (MBO) and not
`management by activities. (MBA).

7.0       FUTURE GROWTH PLANS
 
7.1       Furnishing the details of future business plan of the Corporation, the NMDC management state that in keeping
with the opening of economy, domestic mineral environment and the International scenario, NMDC is planning its
growth by selecting some of the high value minerals and metals, where NMDC by its expertise could play a leading role
in development of open cast mines and processing. NMDC today is producing around 15 million and selling around
18.0 million tonnes of Iron Ore, which is around 22% of the Indian Iron Ore Production.  Because of good quality of the
product, NMDC brand of Bailadila as well as Donimalai has a name in the export market and is also able to satisfy the
special requirement of domestic sponge iron market, specially in the gas based processes. Looking into the future
expansion of steel industry and India being geographically  located close to the steel markets in China, Thailand,
Malaysia, Indonesia and also the new emerging markets in western Asia of Dubai, Bahrain,  India has a very good
chance for establishing its presence because of good quality of Bailadila ore, which could substitute the pellets.  Thus,
NMDC stands a good chance of being leading Export-house in these special markets.  Taking the above into
consideration, NMDC has planned to increase its production capability from the existing 15 million tonnes to 30.7
million tonnes by 2009-10.   This increase is to be achieved by opening of the following Mines:

7.15     PIG IRON STEEL PLANT AT JAGDALPUR, BASTER,
 
The Feasibility Report of setting up of commercial Pig Iron Steel Plant of 0.3 MTPA capacity utilizing iron ore
slime from Bailadila plant based on Romelt technology was approved by NMDC Board in its 322 nd meeting held on
19.12.1998 at an estimated  cost of Rs.298.68 crores including a foreign exchange component of Rs. 34.89 crores. The
Project is to be completed with in two years from the date of land acquisition, required for the plant construction.
Originally the  State Govt.  had offered  land  in  Geedam  village  of  Dantewada  district  of  M.P state.  Accordingly,
N.M.D.C had obtained  various  statutory  clearances  required  for  the  project and  also has  completed the  basic 
engineering  works  by  engaging consultants -  M/s. RSIL and M/s. AMET.  Even  though  the  State  Govt. handed
over the Govt. land, it  could  not  hand over  the  private  land  due  to  administrative  reasons.  In  May  2001,  the 
State  Govt. offered land  at  Nagarnar, and four other villages near by; located on NH-43, approximately 16 kms from
Jagdalpur on Raipur – Vijayanagaram road.  Application to Collector, Jagdalpur for the proposed land  at this site was
filed on 18.06.2001. The Planning & Engg. Works for the plant is completed and tender documents are being prepared
for issue.  The boundary wall construction is in progress.  It is planned to start the operation of plant by March,/April,
2005.

8.0       STATUS OF EQUIPMENTS AND TECHNOLOGY EMPLOYED IN NMDC
 
8.1       Iron ore Mining: NMDC Management is always in search of latest Mining technology and equipment.
Accordingly, higher capacity drills, shovels and dumpers have been deployed in various existing Mines. In new Mines
120 ton capacity Dumpers against 50 T Dumpers and 8 meter cube Shovels against 4.6 meter cube Shovels and single
pass Drills against multi pass Drills are being planned. Latest information technology is being adopted in OCSL Plant
like; PLC, CCTV etc. The modern technology will enhance the productivity of Mines and also will result in reduction in
cost.
 
8.2       Diamond Mining: Mechanised Diamond Mine at Majghawan had a capacity of 24,480 carats per year by
adopting jigging technology. By adopting latest heavy media separation and X-ray sorting machinery, capacity of the
Mine has been enhanced to 84000 carats per year.  During the year 2001-02 the Mine could achieve 81,100 carats of
production. This has resulted in reduction in cost of production.
 
8.3       NMDC has identified the ROMELT technology of Russia which can  make use of low grade iron ore slimes to
convert  into pig iron. The technology has not so far been commercially used and NMDC would be the first Company in

including Rs.20 crores as loan from Technical Development Board, Govt. of India.  The ROMELT SAIL India Ltd
(RSIL) will co-ordinate for transfer of technology from Russia.  Required land has been handed over by the
Government.  All the statutory clearances are obtained  and  construction has started.

equipment capacity so as to increase the production which can result in better financial results.

10.0     COST COMPONENTS OF PRODUCTS PRODUCEDBY NMDC .
 
10.1     The main components of the cost of production are  Fixed Cost and  Variable Cost.
The fixed cost consists of salaries and wages, depreciation and office/ selling expenses. The fixed cost is being reduced
by introduction of multi-skilling concepts and by adoption of CCTV at OCSL Plants. The variable cost consists of
explosives, drill bits, tyre & tubes, spares and consumables. It also consists cost incurred towards royalty and
electricity. 
 
10.2     The cost of production at pit head is comparable with the International Mining Operations. NMDC has to incur
additional Railway freight and Port handling charges  before product reaches the consumer. The port charges and
Railway freight charges are not under the control of NMDC. It may be worth mentioning that the cost incurred in
Railway freight and port charges amounts to 60% of the cost and balance 40% cost is in the control of NMDC. This
situation creates difficulty for NMDC to compete in the  international market for iron ore. Unless efforts are made to
reduce the railway freight and port charges, it will be difficult for NMDC to sustain the progress of the Corporation. 
 
10.3     Continuous efforts are being made towards cost reduction through the various managerial controls such as
 
a.
       Bench Marking
b.      Incentive for maintaining electricity and diesel consumption targets adopted through the Memorandum of
Understanding reached between management and union.
c.       Standard Costing
d.      Continuous review of consumables and improvement in the same
 
10.4     The Committee is pleased to note that NMDC management has introduced the multi-skilling concept to reduced
the fixed cost .    The Committee also notes that various managerial controls measures are at work to reduce the cost of
production .  The Committee  desires that the Ministry of  Steel, in its Action Taken Note of the present Report, would
furnish  the detail as to what extent the cost reduction measure entailed tangible   result.

12.0     MOU RATING OF NMDC 
 
12.1     NMDC is one of the Public Sector Enterprises which has been signing Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)
with the Government of India since 1991. At the beginning of each financial year, an agreement is reached to achieve
certain parameters such as production, sales, gross margin, ratio of net profit to capital employed, energy conservation,
R & D efforts, human resource development, project implementation etc. based on the past performance into account,
the growth need and the potential to achieve such levels of performance.  These achievements at the year end are
evaluated for the said parameters and the company gets ranked based on the evaluation score. NMDC has been
consistently achieving the rating of “ EXCELLENT” from the very first year of signing the MoU viz 1991-92.  It was
placed in the fourth rank amongst the MOU signing PSEs for the year 1991-92, from which it moved to third rank in the
year 1992-93.  For the years 1993-94, 1994-95 and 1995-96, for NMDC’s performance of excellence and the
achievement of composite score of 1.00 in each of these years under the MOU performance evaluation, NMDC has
been placed as the ‘ Joint Top Rank PSE’ in the country. This has been a unique achievement as the Joint Top Rank  
has been achieved continuously for three years in succession.  Again, in 1996-97, 1997-98, 1999-2000, 2000-2001 and
2001-2002 NMDC was adjudged “EXCELLENT”, barring 1998-99 during which period there was general recession. 
NMDC had been setting new records of performance in the areas of production, despatches of iron ore, production and
sales of diamonds, profits, increased supplies to domestic consumers.  Thus, NMDC has been consistently scaling new
heights of achievements from year to year both in physical and financial terms including foreign exchange earnings to
the country.   For the year 2001-2002, the company has surpassed the MOU target of dispatches of 180 lakh tonnes of
iron ore by achieving 182.04 lakh tonnes. The company has achieved an ever highest profit before tax of Rs.320.15
crores during 2001-2002.
 
12.2     NMDC has achieved the "Excellent" rating during the last five years except in the year 1998-99 which was
"Very Good"due to recession in the international steel industry. DPE has awarded NMDC an award for Excellence in
the achievement of MOU Targets for the year 2000-01 and a certificate of Excellence in the achievement of MOU
Targets  for the year 1999-2000. The system of MOU has helped NMDC which is evident from the fact that for the last
11 years i.e. since from the beginning of signing of MOU, it has been rated as Excellentexcept as mentioned above

proposal of NMDC for upgrading to NAVARATNA status is under consideration of the Government.

19.0     LEGAL FRAMEWORK  FOR GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT OF MINING         OPERATION.
 
19.1     To what extent the existing legal framework is conducive to the growth and development of mining operation.
Any suggestion on the part of NMDC management for legal reform in this regard is as follow:-
 
“Mineral industry is governed by certain statutory act and rules such  as  Mines and Mineral Development and
Regulation Act, Mineral Conservation and Development Rules,  Mineral Concession Rule, , Mines Act etc.  With the 
economic liberalization, a new National  Mineral Policy  was announced in 1993 in which 13  major minerals were
dereserved and also foreign investment in the mineral industry was encouraged.  With the existing change in the legal
frame work, State Governments have been empowered to grant and renew mining lease as  minerals are considered to
be property of  the  concerned States.  However, it is observed that State Govts. are imposing their own conditions in
granting of mining lease and this is causing hindrance in the systematic development of mineral industry.  State
Governments should be advised to  form uniform policy and grant mining/prospecting lease on first come first basis,
rather than tendering, asking  for  additional royalty etc.
 
19.2     Ministry of Mines in consultation with GSI & IBM identify mineral zones, which should be developed to meet
the country’s need and such mineral zones, which should be developed to meet the country’s need and such mineral
zones should be taken out from the purview of Forest Conservation Act. “
 
19.3     The Committee is anguished  to  note the  submission of NMDC management  that  State Govts. are imposing
their own conditions in granting of mining lease and this is causing hindrance in the systematic development of mineral
industry and asking  for sweet equity, additional royalty etc.
 
 
 
19.4     The Committee is of the view that  procedural hurdles and bureaucratic rest-redtapism  should not  come onto
the way of systematic development of mineral industry.
 
19.5     The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Steel should   approach the Department of Mines for
organizing  across the  table interaction of representatives of   mineral enterprises and  officials of state  Government  to
form uniform mineral policy as per the canons of National Mineral Policy 1993 and  sort out problems relating  to
granting of  mining/prospecting lease etc.    The Committee  feels that the Department of Mines should  issue direction 
to GSI and IBM for identification of `Exclusive Mineral Zones’ for systematic exploration and exploitation of  Mineral
resources.  Having been give due weightage  to environmental aspects, `exclusive Mineral zones may be taken out from
the  purview of forest   conservation Act.

21.0     'Pricing policy' of NMDC
 
21.1     NMDC is supplying iron ore in the domestic market as well as for export purposes.  The iron ore, which is
surplus to the domestic demand, is exported to countries like Japan, China and South Korea.  The main export of
NMDC’s ore is through MMTC as per the existing EXIM policy.  However, NMDC is also exporting small quantities of
iron ore directly based on the specific export license issued by the Ministry of Commerce / DGFT.
 
21.2     Export Prices ; The prices for export of iron ore are basically determined by the global demand and supply
position on year-to-year basis. The percentage increase/decrease in prices over the previous year is usually finalized
between the major exporters of iron ore like Australia and Brazil and major importers like Japan and European
countries.  The same percentage increase/decrease in prices is made applicable to the exports from India.  Consequently
the prices of NMDC iron ore is also decided on the same basis. 
           
21.3     Domestic Prices: The despatches to domestic market accounts for about 53% of the total dispatches of NMDC’s
iron ore.  The dispatches to domestic market are regulated in terms of long-term agreement signed by various domestic
customers.  The duration of the agreement is for a maximum period of 5 years.  Iron ore market has remained basically a
buyers market due to frequent and prolonged recessionary trends in the steel industry.  Therefore the fixed escalation in
prices is not the acceptable norm in the industry.  In terms of the long-term agreement the prices of iron ore are to be
settled each year by mutual discussions.  The mutual discussions to finalise the prices instills a sense of confidence
among the buyers that NMDC is sensitive to the customer’s needs.
 
21.4     The Board of Directors of NMDC delegated the powers for deciding the pricing through an internal committee
consisting of CMD, Director (Prdn), Director (Fin) and ED (Comml.) and authorizing CMD to take up action as deemed
necessary in this regard.
21.,5    In terms of the above decision of the Board, negotiations are held by NMDC with the parties concerned in the
beginning of the each financial year.  The negotiation are primarily based upon the “what the market can bear” criteria,
and the ground realities of steel sector.  The negotiation also to some extent take into consideration the global
demand/supply position and trends of iron ore prices in the international market. 
 
21.6     The prices so negotiated and finalized with the parties are put up for the information of the Board and adopted
for supplies during the relevant year.

 21.11.0           NEED OF   AUTONOMY FOR  NMDC IN EXPORT PROMOTION I.E.                               
DIRECT EXPORT BY NMDC
 
 
 21.11.1 The export of iron ore produced by NMDC is canalized through MMTC Ltd. in terms of the existing EXIM
policy.  The iron ore which is surplus to the demand of domestic industry is offered for export.  The major chunk of
export is canalised through MMTC and balance uncommitted quantity is permitted for exports directly by NMDC
through special export license.  The inherent drawback of the system are as follows:
 
                        (i)      The licenses issued to NMDC for exports by DGFT do not permit export to major importers of Iron Ore
like Japan and South Korea which together accounts for about 40 - 45% of sea-borne trade.
 
                      (ii)      The licenses are for limited quantity with a validity period of two/three years.  Therefore it is not possible
for NMDC to enter into an agreement/MOU on long-term basis with foreign buyers.
 
                     (iii)      NMDC has to ensure the parity of foreign exchange realization on exports on product-to-product basis with
MMTC.  Thus NMDC is deprived the freedom to adjust the prices etc. depending upon the available
inventory levels vis-à-vis the demand pattern.
 
                    (iv)      The import license imposes the restriction on NMDC to export to Japan and South Korea but for other
countries both MMTC and NMDC will be trading the same product and the brand image of the NMDC
product will never be established.
 
                      (v)      Since NMDC gets permission for Direct Export on piece-meal basis, the foreign steel mills operators have
reservations to consider NMDC as a long-term reliable supplier of iron ore.
 
                    (vi)      Since the Export Licenses are for limited quantities, NMDC cannot offer the whole product mix comprising
of both lump ore and fine ore as required by end users.
 
21.11.2            Therefore in the best interest of exports, NMDC should have complete autonomy and be permitted to
export its iron ore without any restriction of quantity, quality or geographical location.  This will enable NMDC to
operate more like any commercial organization and take business decision, which are in its best interest
 
21.11.3            NMDC is meeting the domestic demand of iron ore to an extent of 55% and the remaining 45% only is
being sold out to international market.  As per the policy of the government iron ore is to be exported through canalising
agency i.e. MMTC. MMTC finds out market to iron ore with 65% Fe content and above, whereas NMDC has got more
quantity of iron ore lying at pit head with less than 65% Fe content for which international market is available, but not
explored by NMDC.   To take care of this product NMDC is requesting Commerce Ministry to allow NMDC to direct
export to the countries other than the purchasers from MMTC i.e. Japan, Pakistan and South Korea. NMDC has already
exported directly to its foreign buyers 1.27 lakh tonnes in 1999-00, 9.65 lakh tonnes in 2000-01and  17.12 lakh tonnes in

25.1  Inflated mileage on Kirandul-Kottavalasa line.
 
25.1.1.Railways have been charging freight on Kirandul-Kottavalasa line (SE Railway) by inflating the distance by 30%
NMDC has been representing for the removal of the same in view of the increased traffic handled on the line. The
inflation in distance on K-K line had been continuing since 1967. Initially it was 65.2% but with the traffic volume
increasing, this was reduced to 30% in 1981. This percentage of inflation has been continuing since then. In the last
eighteen years there has been significant increase in the traffic handled on K-K line originating iron ore movement has
gone up from 56.36 lakh tonnes in 1987-88 to 145 lakh tonnes in 2000-01. In addition, the line has been handling other
traffic particularly on account of the NALCO's alumina plant near Koraput. In addition to the budgetary increase in the
freight charges over the years since 1981, Railways had been revising the classification of iron ore upwards resulting in
further increasing in the freight charges. The classification of iron ore which was 110 in 1989-90 had increased to 120 in
1994-95 and 125 in 1999-2000. For the year 2001-02, the Railway freight in respect of iron ore has been further
increased by 3% over that of the previous year with the classification remaining unchanged at 120.
 
25.1.2  The reduction in inflated mileage would help both NMDC and other domestic customers like VSP, Essar Steel,
Ispat Industries Ltd and Vikram Ispat to improve their economy in the operations.
 
25.1.3  The Sub-Committee was informed  that the Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Ltd. is linked to  Balladilla Mines of NMDC
for sourcing its iron  ore  requirements.  The  Kothavalasa-Kirandul section (  KK Line) under  Waltair Division of S.E.
Railway measures a distance of 475 kms.  Out of this distance, 118 k.m. is  on the Eastern Ghat Section and the balance
distance is in the plains  or  plateau.  This was constructed at a  cost of Rs159 Crore. and  was  opened for traffic in 
November, 1968.  In the initial stages,  Railways levied freight charges by  inflating the distance by 65.2 percent  with
stipulation  that the freight should be levied  on discontinuous  mileage basis (i.e.)  out of  475 k.m., only 35 km (i.e.)
from Kothavalala to VSP  will be charged normal freight.  Upon a further review in  1981, the percentage of  inflation

the inflated freight charge is being levied.  VSP has  transported 463.91 lakh tonnes upto March, 2002 and paid a freight
of Rs.1634.34 crore .Had VSP paid normal freight  (i.e.)  Rs.1284 crore  paid upto 2001-02 , VSP would have saved

Rs.379.36 crore .VSP  on an average is presently paying an extra freight of Rs.48.00 crore on iron Ore  freight . VSP

25.1.4  Railways claim that they use additional locos to the extent of two or  three to move iron ore as well as high
maintenance cost  due to steep gradients  which accounts only for about  1/4th  of the total distance i.e., 118 kms.   But 
the traffic of 6 MT was reached in 1988-89 and it has already  reached 14.5 MT in the year 2000-01  which is almost
two and half times more than what was originally envisaged to have a break-even.
 
25.1.5  Initially the KK line was being used for export of iron ore from, Bailadilla sector only.  However, with domestic
iron and steel   producers  like RINL, Essar  Steel & Ispat Industries started using  the same line and the  haulage of iron
ore on KK line increased rapidly.  It is expected that by  end of the  year 2002, it would be 16.3 million tonnes. 
 
25.1.6  During the last 10 years, VSP has been requesting in various coordination meetings held by Ministry of Steel or
by Ministry of Railways for the withdrawal of  these inflated freight charges.  Since 1995-96, withdrawal of this extra
freight  charge formed a part of the chapter on " assistance from the Government" in MOUs  signed with RINL.
 
25.1.7  In the meeting of  the Sub-Committee  held on 12
th September, 2001 on the subject of "Key Concern Areas of
Steel Industries and promotion of steel consumption in various sectors of economy ", the issue of inflated freight charge 
was  discussed in detailed.  Participating in discussion  Shri R.N. Malhotra, Chairman, Railway Board  expressed his
views :-
 
" On the questions of concessions, I would like to say that we are prepared to consider any request from any
manufacturer for certain concessions which will help them and which in turn would help the Railways.  But Sir, by
reducing our revenue, we cannot help anyone.  If they are prepared to give us  an undertaking that  if we  reduce the
freight on the KK Line by a certain percentage, they will be able to give us so much  of extra  traffic, we can consider
it."
 
25.1.8  Interjecting in the discussion, the Secretary, Ministry of Steel expressed   his opinion in following words:-
 
" The question that has been raised with regard to the KK Line related to movement of iron ore from Bailadilla,
which is used by the Vizag Steel Plant.  It has a very long history.    It is not really a question of incremental traffic.  The
operative issue here is that 65 per cent additional freight charge was put at a certain  point  of time because that line was
specifically created for the movement of iron ore from point A to point  B,  with an understanding that if the level of
freight crossed six million tonnes, this additionality would   be removed.  The  situation today is that, as against the 65
per cent  enhanced rate that was being charged, the  current enhanced rate is 30 per cent, and the traffic  generated on
that line, today,  12 tonnes. We can not have a persistent kind of an incremental situation going on.  We understand that
at a point of time, the line was constructed for captive use, and additional charges were levied in order to make it viable
for the Railways.  Once a certain threshold of traffic generation has been achieved, the incremental rate should not
apply.  That was the original understanding.  One cannot have a situation wherein because  there is an incremental rate,
in order to reduce it, you must have incremental traffic.  The fact  that rate was brought down from 65 per cent  to 35 per
cent and now to 30 per cent is, itself, a recognition of the  understanding on which this 65 per cent  was  charged.  Now,
as per that understanding, that additionally factor should now go.  This  is the issue that has to be examined.
 
25.1.9  Responding to the arguments of Secretary, the Chairman, Railway Board, added :-
 
            " The Additionally which was charged earlier was for several reasons.  The first reason was that because it was a
hilly area, with sharp edges and curves, the construction cost of rails was very high.  The second reason was that we had
put surcharge on  movement of any traffic on that line because of the fact that in place of one locomotive, which is
normally required on a  plain terrain, we had to put three locomotive in place .  So, the cost of operation also was very
high.   Also, the  electricity  bill for the  operational  of  this line is five times higher than what it would cost in the case
of   a line on the plain.  Now, how do we recover that cost ?  We cannot go on reducing our revenue, unless there is a
commitment on the part of the steel plants that they will be able to sell so much of extra steel and thus give us that much
of extra traffic.".

followed the liberalization policy.  While decanalisation  applies with one of PSUs under the Ministry of Steel i.e. Metal
Scrap Trade Corporation  Limited (MSTC) and the same principle has  not been  applied in the case of NMDC.  As per
the existing system, Iron-Ore is to be exported through canalizing agency  i.e.  MMTC.  MMTC finds out market to Iron
ore with 65%  fe content and above whereas NMDC has got  more quantity of Iron Ore lying in  its pit head with less
than fifty-five percent Iron content for which international market  is available but the same is not explored by NMDC.

best – interest of the Company.  The Committee feels NMDC be allowed to direct export to the countries other than the

1.27 lakh tones in 1999-2000, 9.65 lakh tones in 2000-01, and  thus experience  so gained, would proved handy to
NMDC in direct export of Iron-ore.

RECENT RECOVERY IN STEEL INDUSTRY AND FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE OF NMDC
 
The Committee understand that, since the Iron Ore is   an important and major  raw material for production of 
steel, it is  but natural , the existence  of inter-dependence  of steel production  and the quality of Iron –Ore  traded .  The
Committee is pleased to note that NMDC’s sales have a direct relation to production of  steel in the country. That is so,
because  Steel Scenario is gaining  momentum  and is recovering fast, which,  the Committee feels , would lead to
increase in NMDC’s sales.
( Para : 22.4)

precedent  should  be followed by other PSUs and units in private sector , as industrial  peace and workers management
relation has a definite  bearing on the labour productivity.
