HINDUSTAN COPPER LIMITED (HCL).

Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL) was incorporated on 9" November, 1967, under the
Companies Act, 1956. It was established as a Govt. of India Enterprise to take over from national
Mineral Development Corporation Ltd., all plants, projects. schemes and studies pertaining to the
exploration and exploitation of copper deposits, including smelting and refining, for fulfilling
long-term objectives of the nation, viz. development and growth of copper mining industry on
sound lines and to maximise indigenous production of copper metal. This is to be achieved by
developing new copper deposits, known or to be discovered. by adopting appropriate and modern
technologies. The Government of India nationalised the only copper producing Company. Indian
Copper Corporation, Ltd. at Ghatsila in March 1972 and handed over its management and
ownership to HCL.In November, 1982 the prestigious Malanjkhand Copper comprising of a large
and fully mechanised open pit mine and Concentrator plant was dedicated to the nation. The
continuous Cast Copper Rod plant at Taloja Copper Project of Hindustan Copper Ltd. was
commissioned in December, 1989 with an installed capacity of 60,000 tonne.

2.0 Capital Base Of Hindustan Copper Ltd. (HCL)

2.1 As on 31- 000. the authorised capital of the Company stood at Rs. 800 crore ( equity
Rs 600 crores & p capital Rs 200 crore ) and the subscribed capital at Rs 543.41 crore.
The total liability of HCL is Rs 1154.58 crore.

3.0 CAPITAL RESTRUCTURING

3.1 The Government of India approved capital restructuring of the company in December
1998. The features of the restructuring were as follows:

(i) The outstanding Government loan of Rs.180.73 crores with HCL as on 31.3.98
was converted into 7.5% non-cumulative redeemable preference shares to be
redeemed at the end of 10th year from the date of conversion and waiver of the
outstanding interest as on 31.3.1998 amounting to Rs.167.43 crore.

(ii) Raising of working capital term loans from financial Institutions/Banks to the
tune of Rs.75 crores against Government guarantee for meeting its cash losses for
1998-99,

(iii) A non-plan loan of Rs.414 crore was to be given to Hindustan Copper Limited
(HCL) for Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS) at 14.5% rate of interest with a
moratorium of 3 years on repayment of principal and interest and repayment of
principal amount in subsequent twelve years, for separation of 11043 employees
of HCL during the years 1998-99 to 2001-2002. If even after 2 years the London
Metal Exchange (LME) price of copper were lower than $1850 per MT, Ministry
of Finance (Department Of Expenditure) was to review the period of moratorium
extended. However, the moratorium period would not be reviewed if HCL faced
a repayment problem due to its own inefficiency.

IMPLEMENTATION OF CAPITAL RESTRUCTURING

3.2 The Committee was given to understand that in line with the Government decision, the
following steps have been taken :-

(i) Outstanding Government loan of Rs.180.73 crores was converted into preference
shares,


(ii) Outstanding interest on the Government loan amounting to Rs.167.43 crores as
on 313.1998 was waived off.

(iii) A non-plan loan of Rs.414 crore as approved in December, 1998 and an
additional Rs.26 crores) was released to HCL in installments, till 2001-02 with
the result that surplus manpower of 8423 have been separated under VRS.

(iv) HCL has raised a working capital loan of Rs.150 crores (in two tranches of Rs.75
crores each) from IDBI and Rs.100 crores from ICICI against the government
guarantee.

the net worth of HCL has been consistently eroding from 343.45 in 1998-99 to 102.19 in, 2000-

DISINVESTMENT OF HCL

3.4 The Committee has been informed that the Government has recently made an attempt to
disinvest the Khetri unit of HCL along with Taloja plant. by proposing that these two profit
centers may be formed into a separate company. The assets of these units were to be valued
which would form 49% contribution from HCL in a new company, in which 51% equity would
be injected by a strategic partner. The Ministry has further informed that bids were invited, but
the interest of the Qualified Interested Parties (QIP)s declined after their assessment of the
business prospects of the proposed new Company. hence the Government would be required to
make a fresh attempt at disinvestment. It may take till 2002-03 for the disinvestment process to be
completed, it was added. ह

NEED FOR ANOTHER CAPITAL RESTRUCTURING OF HCL

3.5 For facilitating disinvestment of HCL. it is necessary that HCL should not be referred to
the Board of Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR). However, with continued
anticipated loss in 2001-02 and 2002-03, the company would come precariously close to being
referred to BIFR, unless the Government intervenes to save the situation. The Committee was
informed that the Government is at present actively considering the possibility of another
financial restructuring of the company, which Includes Infusion of an additional Rs 85 crores as
plan loan in 2001-02.

3.6 During the course of oral evidence Secretary, Department of Mines stated that HCL is
not doing very well because of the financial problems faced by the company due to sharp
decline in the copper price. The Secretary also stated that the duty protection/tariff on copper
declined gradually depending upon the policy of the Government. Moreover, the company
kept on its operation going in unviable mines consequent to which losses of the company
started to mount high. The Secretary also stated that restructuring programmes of HCL could
not succeed because of continuing decline in the price of the copper. The Secretary informed
the Sub-Committee that financial situation of the company is alarming and the company
might lead to cessation of its operations before March, 2002 unless Government take
concrete steps quickly because net worth of the company is going 100 be in negative before the


close of the present financial year and therefore the company might be referred to BIFR. The
Secretary. stated that the Department of Mines has sought supplementary grant of Rs.85.00
crore loan for carrying out manufacturing operation and —_ Rs.250.00 crore Government
guarantee on 6" December, 2001 under lining the fact that continuation of mining operation is
essential for keeping the card of disinvestment opened.

3.7 The Committee is constrained to express its concern over the adverse business prospects
of Hindustan copper Ltd due to weak financial base of the company which continued to work
long on unviable mines. The company is in deep troubles and is heading towards a point
of no return. Its operation might come to a standstill if concrete steps are not taken in right
direction. The Committee rings the alarming bell . Its really a time to work now or never .

3.8 The Committee is constrained to note that efforts made for capital restructuring could
not prove beneficial to the company. Net worth of the company is dwindling and the company
is almost knocking the door of BIFR. The Committee feels that concrete steps towards capital
restructuring must be taken for ameliorating the company at the earliest not with standing the
process of disinvestment.

3.9 The Committee feels that factor like fluctuating LME price is perhaps beyond reach
and controlling limit of HCL management and the Department of Mines. However, the
Department of Mines in general and HCL management in particular should take steps for
harnessing the further business prospects of HCL by working on those factors viz
identification of viable mines/deposits , reduction in cost of production , interacting with state
government for providing power to the units of HCL at the viable rate, approaching Ministry of
Finance for rationalisation of tax structure.

10.0 छाल Copper Complex (KCC)

10.1 Khetri Copper Compiex is the largest unit of Hindustan Copper situated in the district of
Jhunjhunu of Rajasthan. It is the copper mining-cum-metallurgical unit in the country. The
complex comprises three mines (two large underground mines viz., Khetri and Kolihan, and a
very small Chandmari mine), a concentrator. a flash smelter, an electrolytic refinery, a wirebar
casting plant and an acid-cum-fertilizer plant with all auxiliary facilities. The Atavalli hills from
Singhana to Raghunathgarh over a distance of 80 kms has copper mineralization know as Khetri
Copper Belt. The ore from Khetri, Kolihan and Chandmari mines is treated in the concentrator
plant for the production of copper concentrate of 15-16% Cu . The copper concentrates produced
at KCC are blended to 20% copper in the bedding building and imported for processing in the
smelter plant.The smelter plant has a designed capacity to produce 31,000 t per year of blister
copper (99% copper). The blister copper is ‘fire-refined* in anode furnace. The molten copper
after refining is cast into anodes. The anodes (about 99.5% copper) are sent to the Refinery plant
for electrochemical refining. The anodes, produced in the smelter plant, are electrochemically
refined in the refinery plant and electrolytic grade copper is produced in the form of cathodes
(99.99% copper). The refinery plant has a designed capacity to produce 31,000 tonnes per year of
electrolytic grade copper. Since incorporation of HCL in 1967, copper production has increased
from a meager 7000 t to over 50000 t per year. Production of copper at Khetri has also increased
from about 14000 tonnes in 1980 to 34000 tonnes in 1992. Present production is maintained in
this range. KCC was the first venture of this magnitude to accomplish with indigenous skill and
resources. KCC has been pioneer in technological development. The mines are of the world
class and most advanced in India.

12.0 Problems being faced by khetri unit of HCL

12.1 Apprising the Sub -committee regarding the deteriorating financial performance of HCL
it was stated that the perils of globalization has cast the shadow on financial performance of the
Company. Copper. is being allowed to be imported: the price is governed by market forces.
Khetri Copper Complex, which was a national compulsion had to face the market vagaries. In
international market where for last some years the price has tumbled down from a peak of 2700 $
per t to an all time low of 1500/1600 $ per tonne. Operations at Khetri became unviable and HCL
as a whole has started incurring losses. To tide over the situation Khetri Copper Complex has
prepared a turnaround strategy which includes mining of rich grade ore from Banwas, downsizing
the manpower with introduction of lucrative golden hand shake scheme, reduction in generation
of secondary copper in process, improvement in recoveries besides improving productivity.

12.2. KCC is facing the following problems:-

(0) Low scale of operation: Khetri smelter is of 31,000 t per annum capacity. This is
a very small capacity. Presently World over most of the smelters are of
minimum 1,00,000 t capacity.

(ii) Scale of operations of underground mine, though highest in the country, but it is
very low as compared to world standards. Globally large mines of 2.5 million to
25 million t are in operation.


(iii) | The ore reserves of Khetri deposit are spread over a distance of 4 km unlike
massive ore bodies in other parts of the World within a small range of | km or so.
Further the deposit continues to greater depth as compared to very small depth
for opencast mines.

(iv) Khetri’s proposal of debottlenecking of smelter/refine to improve marginal
capacity by 50% to 45,000 tonnes per year with a marginal cost of Rs. 50 crore
could not be implemented due to severe resource crunch and also the proposed
hive off of KCC under first phase of disinvestments.

(v) Further, recommendations of disinvestment commission, to increase capacity to
1,00,000 t before disinvestments could not be implemented. The Commission
has logically considered that 1,00,000 tonnes capacity smelter could be viable.

(vi) _ Khetri ore contains negligible precious metals like gold, silver which in overseas
mines contributes to 30% ore revenue.

(vii) | Overstaffing: Khetri employed over 8600 persons, which are about 60% more
than normal requirement. Most of the advanced countries manage KCC like
operations within 1500/2000 employees. However, in last three years manpower
strength has been brought down to 5600.

12.3. Replying to the Sub-Committee's query regarding the reasons for not implementing the
recommendations of disinvestment commission to increase production at Khetri to 1,00000
tonne before disinvestment , the Department of Mines clarified that the recommendation of
Disinvestment Commission involved infusion of funds which was not found prudent in view
of the proposed disinvestment of HCL.

12.4. When the Sub-Committee asked as to how long it would take to implement the de-
bottlenecking of smelter to improve marginal capacity, the Department of Mines stated that
implementation of de-bottlenecking of the smelter would take 22 months with 3 month shut-
down. At this stage, it is difficult to raise Rs.50 crore from the market and pending
disinvestment of HCL, it will not be prudent to invest Government funds for de-bottlenecking .

12.5 Regarding the steps taken by the Department of Mines for rationalisation of manpower
at Khetri unit of HCL, the Sub-Committee was informed that the manpower of KCC as on
1.4.98 was 7861 while as on 31.10.01 it was 4528 consisting of 347 class-I officers, 147 class-II
officers and 4034 workman. Thus there has been a total reduction of manpower to the extent of
3333 in the above period.

12.6 The Committee is of the opinion that the problems of KCC are deep-routed therefore,
immediate panacea might not be possible. Since total turn around of KCC involves huge
investment it may not be possible before the restructuring takes place. However, the
Committee is of the view that the process of restructuring should not be procrastinated. In case
it is going to be delayed for more than six months Government should come to rescue of KCC
with necessary fund for early solution of its problems.

14.0 Malanjkhand Copper Project

14.1 Malanjkhand copper deposit is the single largest copper deposit of India with nearly 50%
of country's copper reserves and contributes to around 70% of HCL's total copper production .
The project consists of an open pit mine having initial rated production capacity of 2.0 million
tonnes copper ore per annum with matching concentrator plant, Tailing disposal system and
other auxiliary facilities . MCP has a concentrator plant designed to treat two million tonnes of
copper ore annually. The plant consist of the six sections namely, crushing section; Grinding
Section; Flotation ; Thickening & Filtration; and Tailing disposal and water re-circulation. The
concentration plant has primary crushing system , coarse Ore storage, secondary & Tertiary
crushing system; Grinding system; Flotation system; Thickening and Filtration; and  Tailing
disposal and water reclamation system. Moreover Malanjkhand copper project has infrastructural
facilities which inter-alia include, Repairing facilities, Power supply system, water supply
system; Residential township; Health and Medical facilities' and Educational facilities.

The Committee is constrained to express its concern over the adverse business prospects
of Hindustan copper Ltd due to weak financial base of the company which continued to work
