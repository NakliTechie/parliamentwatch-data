CHAPTER-1 
INTRODUCTION 
 
Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
 
Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) envisions a progressive 
MSME sector by promoting growth and development of small business, including khadi, village 
and coir industries, in cooperation with Ministries/Departments, State Governments and other 
stakeholders, by providing support to existing enterprises, adopting cutting edge technologies 
and encouraging creation of new enterprises. The Ministry is dedicated to fostering inclusive 
growth by ensuring geographic inclusivity of rural and urban areas, demographic inclusivity of 
socially and economically weaker sections such as Scheduled Castes (SCs) / Scheduled Tribes 
(STs)  communities, and promoting women entrepreneurship to boost gender parity. 
 
2. To promote and develop the MSME sector in the country, the Ministry is supported by 
various statutory and non-statutory bodies, including the Khadi and Village Industries 
Commission (KVIC), the Coir Board, National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC), National 
Institute for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (ni-MSME), and the Mahatma Gandhi 
Institute for Rural Industrialization (MGIRI). These organizations play a crucial role in fostering 
growth and providing support to the MSME sector 
 
3. The Ministry of MSME implements more than 25 schemes for credit support, 
technological assistance, infrastructure development, skill development, and training, enhancing 
competitiveness and market assistance to MSME, and creating self-employment opportunities. 
 
4. The Parliamentary Standing Committee, per its mandate, undertook a comprehensive 
assessment of the Ministry's detailed Demand for Grants. The Committee  
i. Reviewed various documents, including the Annual Report (2024-25), Economic Survey, 
Budget documents such as the Details of Demand for Grants, Output-Outcome 
Framework; 
ii. Examined the background note and the answers to the questionnaires; 
iii. Solicited evidence, interacted with and obtained replies from the Ministry. 
iv. Desktop research on Global Data related to MSMEs Sector. 
Public Procurement Policy for Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs ) 
5. The Ministry of MSME implemented the Public Procurement Policy for Micro and Small 
Enterprises (MSEs) under the MSMED Act, 2006, which became mandatory in 2015. The policy 
mandates that Public Sector companies procure 25% of their total purchases from MSEs, with 
4% reserved for SC/ST-owned MSEs and 3% for women-owned MSEs. The Ministry reduced 
the requirements from seven to four in August 2021 to ease compliance. 
6. Additionally, the MSME Sambandh Portal, launched in 2017, tracks procurement data, 
with 40.77% of total procurement reported from MSEs, benefiting over 161,000 MSEs. 
However, the participation of women and SC/ST-owned MSEs remains low at 2.32% and 
1.33%, respectively. The Ministry also encourages MSEs to join the Government e-Marketplace 
(GeM) Portal, with a simplified registration process integrated into the Udyam Registration 
online form. 

2 
 
7. While reviewing the performance of several CPSEs under the central Government, the 
National Hydro Power Corporation (NHPC) has displayed a strong example of successful policy 
implementation. It was observed that the NHPC has effectively engaged vendors, especially from 
remote areas by organizing vendor meetings at the project level to educate them on registration, 
obtaining Udyam certificates, and participating in the tendering process. Further, by uploading 
its annual procurement plan on a web portal, NHPC has ensured vendors have advance 
knowledge of requirements, thereby increasing eligible MSE participation in tenders. 
Recommendations 
8. The Committee, notes that despite efforts to implement sub-quotas within the Public 
Procurement Policy (PPP) for women and SC/ST-owned MSEs, significant gaps remain in 
achieving the quotas, with some regions reporting as low as 70% fulfillment. 
(i) In light of the success of the NHPC, the Committee recommends the MSME 
Ministry to take immediate and decisive action to eliminate barriers preventing the 
fulfillment of sub-quotas. The Ministry must prioritize the implementation of targeted 
measures to ensure full compliance and equitable distribution without delay.  
(ii) Additionally, it is suggested that the Ministry consider including Other Backward 
Classes (OBC)-owned MSEs within the overall PPP quotas, which could enhance their 
participation and further strengthen the policy's inclusivity and effectiveness. 
Budget Speech/Budget Highlights 
9. The Union Budget 2025-26 focuses on strengthening the MSME sector, recognizing it 
as second engine of India’s economic growth alongside agriculture, investment, and exports. 
With 6 crore registered MSMEs employing over 25 crore people and contributing 30% to the 
GDP, 36% to the manufacturing output, 45.73% to India’s total exports in 2023-24, and ₹5 lakh 
crore to GST, the sector plays a crucial role in manufacturing, exports, and employment. The 
new budget introduces a series of measures designed to foster innovation, improve 
competitiveness, and equip MSMEs to expand their reach and boost India’s economic 
development. Following are the highlights of the Union Budget 2025-26 for the MSME Sector:

Credit Cards for Micro Enterprises 
10. A new initiative will be introduced to empower micro enterprises registered on the 
Udyam portal by offering personalized credit cards with a ₹5 lakh limit. This initiative will 

3 
 
provide financial support to help these businesses grow, with 10 lakh cards set to be issued in the 
first year. 
Fund of Funds for Startups 
11. The Alternate Investment Funds (AIFs) for startups have secured commitments exceeding 
₹91,000 crore, supported by a Fund of Funds with a government contribution of ₹10,000 crore. A 
new Fund of Funds, with an expanded scope and an additional ₹10,000 crore contribution, will 
now be established to further support startups. 
Scheme for First-time Entrepreneurs  
12. A new scheme will be launched to support 5 lakh first-time women, Scheduled Castes, 
and Scheduled Tribes entrepreneurs by providing term loans of up to ₹2 crore over the next 5 
years. Drawing from the successful Stand-Up India scheme, it will also include online capacity 
building for entrepreneurship and managerial skills to empower these entrepreneurs. 
Focus Product Scheme for Footwear & Leather Sectors 
13. A focus product scheme will be implemented to enhance India's footwear and leather 
sectors’ productivity, quality, and competitiveness. The scheme will support design capacity, 
component manufacturing, and the necessary machinery for producing non-leather quality 
footwear, along with leather footwear and products. It is expected to generate employment for 22 
lakh people, achieve a turnover of ₹4 lakh crore, and drive exports exceeding ₹1.1 lakh crore..  
Measures for the Toy Sector 
14. Building on the National Action Plan for Toys, a new scheme will be implemented to 
make India a global toy hub. The scheme will focus on developing clusters, enhancing skills, and 
creating a robust manufacturing ecosystem. This will enable the production of high-quality, 
innovative, and sustainable toys that reflect the 'Made in India' brand, boosting India's presence in 
the global toy market. The global toy market is projected to experience significant growth in the 
coming years. Valued at approximately* ₹94,952 crore in 2024, the market is expected to 
generate revenues of around ₹1,10,273.8 crore in 2025. Looking further ahead, forecasts indicate 
the market will expand from US Dollar ₹1,00,015 crore to ₹1,68,573 crore by 2034, reflecting a 
compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6%. This growth highlights the increasing demand and 
evolving trends within the global toy industry. 
Support for Food Processing 
15. In line with the commitment towards 'Purvodaya,' a National Institute of Food 
Technology, Entrepreneurship, and Management will be established in Bihar. This institute aims 
to boost food processing activities in the Eastern region, leading to enhanced income for farmers 
through value addition to their produce. It will also create skilling, entrepreneurship, and 
employment opportunities for the youth, fostering economic growth in the area. 
Manufacturing Mission - Furthering “Make in India” 
16. The Government will establish a National Manufacturing Mission to advance "Make in 
India" by offering policy support, execution roadmaps, and a governance and monitoring 
framework for both central ministries and states, covering small, medium, and large industries.

Export Promotion Mission  
17. An Export Promotion Mission will be established with sectoral and ministerial targets, 
led jointly by the Ministries of Commerce, MSME, and Finance. This mission will provide easy 
access to export credit, cross-border factoring support, and assist MSMEs in overcoming non-
tariff barriers in international markets. 
Global Scenario in MSMEs’ Classification 
18. The International Finance Corporation MSME definition* classifies enterprises as micro, 
small, or medium based on two of three criteria: employees, assets, or sales, or by the MSME 
loan size proxy. Micro enterprises have under 10 employees, assets, and sales below $100,000, 
with loans under $10,000, while small and medium enterprises have higher thresholds. 
Recommendations 
19. The Committee notes that, as of December 31, 2024, out of the 5.77 crore total 
MSMEs, 5.69 crore are micro, 7.3 lakh are small, and 69,000 are medium. Despite this, the 
new revised investment and turnover limits could potentially undermine the purpose of 
MSMEs by elevating micro-enterprises beyond their original scope. It is recommended that 
the Government revise the definition of Micro, Small, and Medium enterprises and create a 
new category –nano with the lowest financial criteria around ₹10 lakh. This would ensure 
that businesses with a turnover above ₹2.5 crore do not deviate from the core purpose of 
MSMEs—driving employment generation and supporting smaller enterprises. 
20. The Committee appreciates the new initiative to issue customized credit cards with a 
₹5 lakh limit for micro enterprises. It recommends that the Ministry expedite the 
distribution of these cards, focusing on micro enterprises that are not entirely digitised. 
Financial literacy programs should also be provided to help these businesses effectively 
utilize the cards, improving access to working capital and financial services.

Recommendations 
24. On Budget Utilization:-  
(i) The Committee recommends that the Ministry prioritize the optimal 
utilization of the allocated ₹23,168.15 crore by focusing on high-impact schemes, 
particularly those supporting technological upgradation, market access, and employment 
generation. To address the ₹2,439.08 crore shortfall, the Ministry may seek additional 
funds at the Revised Estimates (RE) stage or explore alternative funding sources, such as 
public-private partnerships (PPP) and collaborations with financial institutions, to ensure 
the uninterrupted implementation of critical programs. 
 (ii)  The Committee committee observes with concern that there is a significant 
gap between the Revised Estimate and actual expenditure, and a clear under achievement 
in the QEP, with only 41.74% of the allocated budget utilised as of January 20, 2025. This 
shortfall is attributed to challenges posed by the Model Code of Conduct during the general 
elections. The Committee recommends that the Ministry must adopt a more flexible and 
efficient planning approach to mitigate such disruptions in the future. Additionally, it 
should explore methods to accelerate fund utilization in the remaining months of the fiscal 
year to minimize any long-term impact on program outcomes. 
25. With regard to QEP targets set for the Financial Year 2025-26, the Committee 
recommends that the Ministry apply lessons learned from the previous year by 
implementing a clear Quarterly and Monthly Expenditure Plan to ensure efficient fund 
utilization. Additionally, the Ministry should focus on strengthening initiative planning, 
execution, and budget absorption capacity to guarantee that allocated funds are utilized 
effectively, driving tangible outcomes in MSME initiatives. 
26. The Committee notes that 96.9% of the Ministry's budget allocation is allocated to 
revenue expenditure, with only 3.1% earmarked for capital expenditure. To drive 
substantial growth and achieve encouraging outcomes, the Ministry should prioritize 
increasing capital expenditure, as this will give an impetus to infrastructure and 
developmental initiatives. 
27. The Committee acknowledges that most schemes are demand-driven, but it 
recommends that the Ministry set clear, predefined targets for these schemes. IT 
recommends utilizing behavioural science tools to nudge, encourage, and motivate 
organisations, leading to better stakeholder outcomes. 
28. The Committee has observed the following in the explanatory note submitted by the 
Ministry on the proposed Revenue and Capial Outlays – 
i. High Savings in Non-Functional Heads: A large portion of savings was seen in non-
functional heads under the revenue section. Although funds were re-appropriated to 
functional heads for specific regions like the North East and Sikkim, the non-functional 
heads remained underutilized. 
ii. Delayed Scheme Approvals: Several schemes, including MSME Champions and MSME 
Innovative Scheme, were delayed in approval and launch, resulting in zero or minimal 
expenditure since funds couldn’t be utilized until later in the fiscal year. 

8 
 
iii. Lack of Proposals for Demand-Driven Schemes: Demand-driven schemes like Prime 
Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) and Entrepreneur Skill 
Development Programme (ESDP) experienced savings due to the lack of viable 
proposals. Insufficient participation and low proposal submissions led to funds not being 
fully utilized. 
iv. Delayed Sanctioning Process for Infrastructure Development: Infrastructure 
development schemes saw savings due to delays in the sanctioning process and the low 
number of project proposals received, leading to underutilization of allocated funds. 
v. Zero Expenditure under PMEGP and other Credit Schemes & Self Reliant 
Initiative (SRI) Fund Schemes: The SRI Fund of Fund schemes had no expenditure, as 
the funds were dependent on daughter funds raising their own resources, which caused 
delays in deploying the allocated funds. 
vi. Underutilization of Infrastructure Establishment Funds: The infrastructure 
establishment fund also saw savings due to the non-receipt of viable project proposals, 
leading to unspent funds being surrendered. 
vii. High Percentage of Unutilized Funds: Many schemes, such as the Infrastructure 
Development Programme and Research and Evaluation Studies, saw high savings, mainly 
due to delayed approvals and insufficient demand for the schemes. 
And based on these observations the Committee recommends that  
29. The Ministry should establish a structured timeline for the approval and launch of 
schemes to ensure timely operationalization and prevent delays, as seen with the MSME 
Champions and MSME Innovative Schemes. Allocating funds to functional heads at the 
start of the fiscal year will reduce re-appropriations and streamline project 
implementation. To tackle underutilization due to insufficient proposals, the Ministry 
should enhance efforts to engage potential proposers, especially for demand-driven 
schemes like PMEGP, ESDP, and the Infrastructure Development Programme, through 
more targeted awareness campaigns, workshops, webinars, and networking events. A 
robust monitoring system may also be implemented to track proposal submissions, 
ensuring timely follow-ups and support. 
 
30.  The Ministry should prioritize the prompt deployment of capital funds, particularly 
for the SRI Fund of Fund & PMEGP and other credit schemes by collaborating with 
daughter funds to mobilize resources quickly and establishing follow-up mechanisms for 
fund deployment. To improve utilization of infrastructure-related funds, the sanctioning 
process may be expedited, and project proposals should be regularly solicited. An effective 
real-time monitoring system should track fund utilization, enabling quick identification of 
underutilization and corrective actions. Periodic reviews of fund allocation will ensure 
alignment with actual needs. Ministry may focus more towards strengthening coordination 
with implementing agencies and introducing flexibility in fund reallocation will help 
optimize resource usage, reduce savings, and address delays. Additionally, enhancing the 
Ministry's financial management and forecasting capacity may improve fund allocation 
and utilization.

GUARANTEED EMERGENCY CREDIT LINE (GECL) 
34. The Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (ECLGS), launched on 23rd May 2020 as 
part of the Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyaan, aimed to support MSMEs and other businesses 
impacted by the COVID-19 crisis. The scheme provided a 100% guarantee to Member Lending 
Institutions (MLIs) on credit extended to eligible borrowers, enabling businesses to meet 
operational liabilities and resume activities. Implemented by the Department of Financial 
Services (DFS) under the Ministry of Finance, the scheme was operational until 31st March 
2023. According to a research report by SBI dated 23rd January 2023, the scheme protected 
nearly 14.6 lakh MSME accounts, with 98.3% of these being from the micro and small enterprise 
categories, and helped prevent about 12% of outstanding MSME credit from being classified as 
non-performing assets (NPAs). 
35. The allocation for the ECLGS is determined based on the demand raised by the DFS and 
the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (NCGTC). While the scheme was designed to 
act as a safeguard fund in case of NPAs arising from loans extended during the pandemic, there 
has been no shortage of funds for credit guarantee coverage. In fact, Rs. 5 lakh crore worth of 
credit guarantees have been provided over the last two years. As NPAs within MSMEs have 
been declining, the budgetary allocations for the GECL scheme are no longer required to be fully 
utilized. Similarly, for other smaller schemes, the allocations have either remained consistent or 
increased, demonstrating the continued need for support without concerns of unutilized funds.

CREDIT SUPPORT/GUARANTEE SCHEMES 
37. The Credit Guarantee Scheme for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) offers 
collateral-free credit to MSEs through banks, financial institutions, and NBFCs. The scheme's 
loan ceiling was increased from ₹2 crore to ₹5 crore per borrowing unit as of April 1, 2023, with 
up to 90% guarantee coverage. The scheme supports women-led enterprises with enhanced 
coverage and charges a nominal Annual Guarantee Fee (AGF). As of December 2024, the 
scheme has approved 1.01 crore proposals totalling ₹8.07 lakh crore, benefiting MSEs across 
more than 100 sectors, with a significant focus on underserved regions. 
38. In FY 2023-24, the government infused ₹9,000 crore into the CGTMSE corpus, which 
includes ₹8,500 crore from GoI and ₹500 crore from SIDBI (Small Industries Development 
Bank of India). Revamped measures, such as a 50% reduction in guarantee fees and simplified 
claims processing for loans up to ₹10 lakh, are designed to reduce credit costs for MSEs and 
enhance their growth potential. These changes aim to boost credit access and foster the 
development of small businesses, particularly in rural and regional areas. 
39. CGTMSE has leveraged digital platforms to streamline the application process for 
guarantee coverage, with automated approvals and API integration with 13 major banks for 
seamless data flow. These digital advancements led to a significant rise in guarantee approvals, 
reaching ₹2.04 lakh crore by January 31, 2025, surpassing the previous year's record of ₹2.02 
lakh crore. In the upcoming financial year, CGTMSE plans further digital enhancements, 
including complete end-to-end API (Application Programming Interface) integration with MLIs 
(Member Lending Institutions) and improvements to user interfaces and experiences.

58. The RAMP (Raising and Accelerating MSME Performance) scheme is a World Bank-
assisted Central Sector initiative to boost MSME performance by promoting innovation, 
digitisation, technology upgradation, market access, credit, and sustainability initiatives. With a 
total outlay of ₹6062.45 crore from FY 2022-23 to FY 2026-27, the World Bank provides ₹3750 
crore (USD 500 million) in support. 
59. Besides providing gap funding to States under Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs), RAMP 
supports various MSME schemes, including the MSME Champions Scheme, Procurement and 
Marketing Support, and International Cooperation. It also introduces four new sub-schemes: the 
MSE Green Investment and Financing for Transformation (MSE GIFT Scheme), MSE Scheme 
for Circular Economy Promotion (MSE SPICE Scheme), MSE Online Dispute Resolution 
(ODR) for Delayed Payments, and MSME Trade Enablement & Marketing (MSME TEAM) 
Initiative. The programme is expected to benefit over 5.5 lakh MSMEs during its five-year 
duration. 
60. Besides, it is informed that all 36 States and Union Territories (UTs) are participating in 
the RAMP Scheme, with each receiving a grant of ₹5 crore to prepare a Strategic Investment 
Plan (SIP) for the development and promotion of the MSME sector. 34 States/UTs have 
submitted SIPs, and a total of ₹2966.144 crore has been approved for selected projects within 
these plans. The first instalment of grants has been released to these States/UTs to implement the 
approved projects.

RECOMMENDATIONS AT GLANCE 
 
1. The Committee, notes that despite efforts to implement sub-quotas within the Public 
Procurement Policy (PPP) for women and SC/ST-owned MSEs, significant gaps remain in 
achieving the quotas, with some regions reporting as low as 70% fulfillment. 
(i) In light of the success of the NHPC, the Committee recommends the MSME 
Ministry to take immediate and decisive action to eliminate barriers preventing the 
fulfillment of sub-quotas. The Ministry must prioritize the implementation of targeted 
measures to ensure full compliance and equitable distribution without delay.  
(ii) Additionally, it is suggested that the Ministry consider including Other Backward 
Classes (OBC)-owned MSEs within the overall PPP quotas, which could enhance their 
participation and further strengthen the policy's inclusivity and effectiveness. 
(Para-8) 
2. The Committee notes that, as of December 31, 2024, out of the 5.77 crore total 
MSMEs, 5.69 crore are micro, 7.3 lakh are small, and 69,000 are medium. Despite this, the 
new revised investment and turnover limits could potentially undermine the purpose of 
MSMEs by elevating micro-enterprises beyond their original scope. It is recommended that 
the Government revise the definition of Micro, Small, and Medium enterprises and create a 
new category –nano with the lowest financial criteria around ₹10 lakh. This would ensure 
that businesses with a turnover above ₹2.5 crore do not deviate from the core purpose of 
MSMEs—driving employment generation and supporting smaller enterprises. 
(Para-19) 
3. The Committee appreciates the new initiative to issue customized credit cards with a 
₹5 lakh limit for micro enterprises. It recommends that the Ministry expedite the 
distribution of these cards, focusing on micro enterprises that are not entirely digitised. 
Financial literacy programs should also be provided to help these businesses effectively 
utilize the cards, improving access to working capital and financial services. 
(Para-20) 
4. On Budget Utilization:-  
(i) The Committee recommends that the Ministry prioritize the optimal 
utilization of the allocated ₹23,168.15 crore by focusing on high-impact schemes, 
particularly those supporting technological upgradation, market access, and employment 
generation. To address the ₹2,439.08 crore shortfall, the Ministry may seek additional 
funds at the Revised Estimates (RE) stage or explore alternative funding sources, such as 
public-private partnerships (PPP) and collaborations with financial institutions, to ensure 
the uninterrupted implementation of critical programs. 
 (ii)  The Committee committee observes with concern that there is a significant 
gap between the Revised Estimate and actual expenditure, and a clear under achievement 
in the QEP, with only 41.74% of the allocated budget utilised as of January 20, 2025. This 

42 
 
shortfall is attributed to challenges posed by the Model Code of Conduct during the general 
elections. The Committee recommends that the Ministry must adopt a more flexible and 
efficient planning approach to mitigate such disruptions in the future. Additionally, it 
should explore methods to accelerate fund utilization in the remaining months of the fiscal 
year to minimize any long-term impact on program outcomes. 
 (Para-24) 
5. With regard to QEP targets set for the Financial Year 2025-26, the Committee 
recommends that the Ministry apply lessons learned from the previous year by 
implementing a clear Quarterly and Monthly Expenditure Plan to ensure efficient fund

effectively, driving tangible outcomes in MSME initiatives.

increasing capital expenditure, as this will give an impetus to infrastructure and 
developmental initiatives. 
(Para-26)

Discussion with Representatives of the Ministry of Heavy Industries 
The Chairman welcomed the Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI) officials to discuss the 2025-
26 Budget. He highlighted/ stated/ asked for 
(i) MHI's role is to develop capital goods, automobile, and heavy electrical equipment 
sectors while overseeing CPSEs and autonomous organisations.  
(ii) MHI's budget allocation for 2025-26 is about 85% of the proposed ₹9,025.83 crores. 
(iii) Action plan for optimally utilising the enhanced budgetary allocation for 2025-26, 
focusing on the PM E-Drive Scheme, which has been allocated more than 50% of the 
total budget.  
(iv) Information on schemes’ progress, strategies planned for the year, and the targets set.  
(v) Data on performance of CPSEs, steps taken to improve output and productivity, and 
any restructuring or revival plans for sick or loss-making CPSEs.  
2. After the Chair’s permission, through a presentation, the Ministry of Heavy Industries 
(MHI)  
(i) outlined their budget performance from 2020-21 to 2024-25,  
(ii) focused on schemes like the PLI-Automobile Scheme, with an outlay of ₹25,938 crore, 
aimed at boosting the electric vehicle sector,  
(iii) Shared the technological advancements and significant growth of the Heavy Electrical 
Industry and 
(iv) Highlighted the importance of developing Advanced Chemistry Cells to accelerate the 
production of electric vehicles (EVs).  
(v) Provided an overview of the automotive industry, highlighting India’s global position 
in the sector, and discussed the awareness programs launched by the Ministry to 
educate the public about electric vehicles (EVs).  
(vi) Stressed the need to scale up manufacturing capabilities, create jobs, and improve 
India's position in the global automotive market.  
3. The CMDs briefed the Committee about BHEL, Bridge and Roof Company Ltd, and 
Braithwaite Burn and Joseph Construction Co. Ltd.  
4. After the presentation, the Chairman and Committee Members  
(i) Raised underutilisation of the budget in previous years, ranging from 2022-23 to 2024-
25 
(ii) Pointed out the reduced allocation under capital heads vis a vis the total allocation; 
(iii) Sought reasons for continuing the legacy name of Braithwaite Burn and Jessop. 
(iv) Inquired about the steps taken to protect domestic players given global players like 
Tesla entering the Indian market 

58 
 
(v) Sought replies on points like job work, underground coal gasification, collaboration 
with J&T, Hyderabad for skill development, gravitational storage for large-scale 
solutions. 
(vi) Highlighted that a key challenge is the customs duty on scrap material. When 
specialized material is imported for manufacturing, such as a one-tonne valve, the duty

II Discussion with Representatives of Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
(MSME) 
7. The Chairman welcomed the Secretary and Officers of the Ministry of MSME and its 
allied agencies to the meeting to discuss the Ministry’s budgetary allocations for FY 2025-26, 
and he 
(i) Acknowledged the increase in the MSME sector's budget allocation for 2025-26 to 
₹23,168 crore, up from ₹22,137.95 crore in the previous financial year,  
(ii) Inquired about the impact of less-allocation(by ₹2,439.08 crore) from the Ministry's 
projected ₹25,607.23 croreon the operation of various schemes, 
(iii) Expressed concern about the capital expenditure allocation, only 3% of the total budget, 
(iv) Sought the strategies for optimal budget utilisation, employment generation for micro 
and small enterprises, technological upgradation and enhanced R&D investment in the 
sector, 
(v) Asked for the reasons for rise in MSME closures, with 32,000 shutting down in FY 
2024-25, compared to 175 in FY 2020-21  
(vi) Sought sector-wise breakdown of NPAs from RBI; 
(vii) Expressed concern on underutilisation of funds allocated to KVIC. 
8. After the Chair’s permission, the Secretary, M/o MSME apprised the Committee on the 
2025-26 budget highlights, including  

59 
 
(i) Key measures to strengthen the sector, including increased investment and turnover 
limits, doubling of credit guarantee cover, a ₹5 lakh credit scheme for Udyam-
registered micro-enterprises, and a ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds for startups. 
(ii) Focus and sectoral growth in footwear, leather, toys, and clean tech, with MSMEs 
contributing 30.1% of India’s GDP and ₹12.39 lakh crore in exports.  
(iii) Continued support of artisans and MSMEs through schemes like PM Vishwakarma and 
PMEGP, with 25% of public procurement reserved for MSMEs. 
(iv) Reclassification of MSMEs and showcasing significant growth in the MSME sector, 
with registered MSMEs set to rise from 1.65 crore in 2023 to 6 crore by 2025 
(v) Increased (21X) procurement from SC-ST MSEs over the past decade under the 
National SC-ST Hub (NSSH).  
(vi) Emphasizing sustainable development, the Ministry aims to improve access to credit, 
market opportunities, technological support, and infrastructure, ultimately driving the 
MSME sector’s growth as a key engine of the Indian economy, 
(vii) Introducing customised credit cards for micro-enterprises to address the credit gap. 
(viii) Lays a target of supporting 5.55 lakh MSMEs between 2022 and 2027, with 
partnerships across 34 states.  
(ix) Significant increase in registered MSMEs, projected to reach 6 crore, with continued 
efforts to reduce credit gaps, enhance scalability, and drive investment.  
 
9. The Committee directed that the following be recommended in their DFG report: 
(i) The Government may revise the definition of Micro, Small, and Medium and if 
required, create a new category (nano or any other name) with lowest financial criteria 
(say, around Rs. 10 Lakh) arguing that businesses with a turnover above ₹2.5 crore 
might not align with the core purpose of MSMEs1 
(ii) RBI may regulate a uniform and streamlined interest/ margin rate for MSMEs across 
banks.  
(iii) The Government may remove the entry barrier of class 8 qualifications in PMEGP and 
focus on talent and skill instead of the current support for artisans and handicrafts, 
which often perpetuates family-based traditions and reinforces caste-based systems. 
(iv) The Ministry may improve awareness of UMANG and other MSME IT platforms.

furnish the written replies to the queries raised by the Hon’ble MPs to the Secretariat within three 
days.
