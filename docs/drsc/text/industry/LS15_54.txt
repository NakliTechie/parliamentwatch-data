BY THE GOVERNMENT
 
 
RECOMMENDATION  No.3
 
1.         Joint Mechanism for appraising term loans and working capital
 
The Committee came to know that there is no single agency for lending the term loan and working capital to SSI units’
requirement exceeding Rs. 10 lakh which leaves the SSI entrepreneurs to run from pillar to post.  Under the circumstances, the

54th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/54threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:13:29]
Committee feels that situation may improve a lot if there is a mechanism for joint appraisal of term lending and working
capital loan for higher amount (about Rs. 10 lakh) so that there is no gap in the sanction of two types of loans.  The measures,
if undertaken, not only will save the time but also the money for SSI entrepreneurs, the Committee feels.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
With  the enhancement of loan limit under Composite Loan Scheme from Rs.10 lakhs to Rs. 25 lakhs, most of the tiny
units would be covered and the problem of approaching separate institutions for meeting different requirements would be
resolved.
 
 
RECOMMENDATION  No.8
           
2.
         Prime Minister’s announcement of package for SSI sector.
 
(i)                   Presently, the Government has given the capital subsidy in select sectors (under Credit Linked Capital Subsidy
Scheme). The Committee feels that Ministry  of  SSI  & ARI   should  examine  whether  such  capital  subsidy  is 
required in other sectors of SSI for technology upgradation, if so, the Ministry should take up the issue at
appropriate level.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
There is scope for inclusion of more sectors in the scheme.  While formulating the scheme, it has  to be acknowledged that
the identification of technology is a continuous process.  The Scheme envisages that a Technical  Advisory Committee would
be set up under the Chairmanship of Secretary (SSI&ARI) to identify the state-of–the-art technology and benchmark existing
and new technologies.
 
(ii)                 The Committee further observes that since sickness of SSI units  is a chronic problem, a serious thought and
timely action is required for rehabilitation of currently sick but potentially viable SSI units.  The Committee hopes
that RBI will be successful.  In its current efforts to draw a detailed, transparent and non-discretionary guidelines
and same will be issued for effective implementation before the sick but potentially viable SSI units  go beyond the
point of no-return.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
RBI has recently constituted a Committee under the Chairmanship of  Shri S.S. Kohli, Chairman, IBA to draw revised
detailed, transparent and non-   discretionary guidelines for rehabilitation of current sick and potentially viable     SSI units.
 
(iii)                The Committee feels that  policy/package which has been envisaged/ extending to SSI will not suffer the problem
of  the implementation of gap.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
Action on most of the  measures announced in the policy package has been initiated/completed.
 
(iv)               The Committee recommends that Ministry of SSI & ARI while furnishing ATN on the recommendations
contained in the present Report will give detailed note with regard to action taken and its impact on each and every
scheme under the present package for SSI units.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
The action on various points of the comprehensive policy package has been  taken  indicated against each in the
Annexure.  The reaction/impact of certain measures  like doubling excise duty exemption limit, enhancing the coverage of
loans under Credit Guarantee Scheme, raising the project cost limit under NEF, launching of  Credit Linked Capital Subsidy
Scheme for Technology Upgradation, has been positive and overwhelming as many prominent SSI Associations have lauded
the Government efforts to strengthen the SSI sector and enhance its competitiveness both domestically and globally. 
However, with regard to other measures,  it is too early to assess the impact.
 
In regard to point   No.15 in the Annexure submitted by the Ministry on the Recommendation No.8, the Committee felt

RECOMMENDATION NO.1 
 
5.         Meeting of Working Capital Requirement
 
The Committee recommends that steps should be taken to ensure credit facilitation to SSI units upto 20% of SSI
production/turnover.
 
5.1       Comments/Action Taken
 
RBI has advised the banks for enhancement in limit from Rs.4 crores to Rs.5 crores for computation of aggregate
working capital requirements of SSI units on the basis of minimum  20% of their projected annual turnover vide circular
No.PLNFS No.BC7.3/06.02.31/98-99 dated 1.3.1999.Following the discussions at the meeting of Group of Ministers on SSI,
RBI has   reiterated the  instructions contained in its circular dated the 1
st March,1999 to commercial banks for providing
working capital based on this specified norm  vide circular No. RPCD PLNFS. BC.16/06.02.31/2000-01 dated 15 th
Sept.,2000.
 
5.2              Further Observations/ Recommendations
 
            During the course of interaction with the representatives of the Banks in Mumbai on  the Committee was informed by
the representatives of the banks that the assessment of the limit, it is somewhat cumbersome procedure but in case of SSI it has
been agreed that 20% of the turnover should be given as a limit and it enables them to look after about 5 cycles in a year. Now
this 20% is of projected sales. It has ben  seen often there are differences of views between borrower and the banker about
the    projections  given  that  creates many difficulties. Apart from that, the procedure is very simple and normally the bank
should not deduct or deduce once they arrive at the 20% of the sales figure. Generally 20%  in all SSI it is a very clear
situation in the bank branches that they have to abide by the Naik Committee norms which is 20% of the projected turnover. 
Now the projected turnover, sometimes people in their anxiety to get a slightly better proportion of the bank finance put it
unrealistically.
 
5.3       The Committee feels  that proper evaluation of the past trends should be taken into account together with the capacity
of the unit  to increase the turnover depending on availabilities of orders and infrastructure to extend the work orders   by the
Banks so as to arrive at a realistic figure regarding the turnover. The Committee recommends that the Branch Managers
should work out a realistic target so that the 20% is met under all circumstances. The Committee also feels that the Branch
Managers who meet their targets should be suitably rewarded.
 
RECOMMENDATION NO.2
 
6.         Sustainable rate of interest:
The Committee not only hopes that the commercial banks will lend  credit to SSI units at sustainable rate of interest
but expects strongly that Government will chalk out adequate incentive to induce commercial banks for extending loan at
sustainable rate of interest.
 
6.1       Comments/Action Taken
 
The Banks endeavour is to charge the most competitive interest rates on advances to SSI units.  Recently, RBI has cut
down the rate of interest by 1% in two phases.  It is expected that the bank will pass it to their  borrowers.  The recent lowering
down  of bank rate will help to reduce the cost of credit  further.
 
6.2       Further Observations/ Recommendations
 
During the course of interaction with representatives of financial institutions and banks the representative from
Maharashtra State Finance Corporation  stated that the health of SFCs is linked to the health of SSIs, When the SSIs do not do
well, the SFCs don't do well. One of the things that the SFC founds itself as the MSFC because of the narrowing gap between
the borrowing cost and the lending rate of interest that is inter spread. The overall cost of borrowing ranges between 8-10%
while the overall cost of borrowing is 13.50%. The main source of raising funds is the re-finance from SIDBI and IDBI which
charge something like 11.5% and 12.5% respectively. The State Government charges a guarantee fee of 2% so that raising the
cost of borrowing is 13.5%. The interest spread therefore that is available even if they  take only 1.5%, they  can only lend at
15%. In a regime of falling interest rates in the market if  they lend at 15%, and that is the least rate they can lend at, what

54th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/54threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:13:29]
really happens is that good borrowers repay faster and the other problem is that SIDBI and IDBI do not retire high cost funds.
 
6.3       The Committee has noted with concern the narrowing gap between the cost of borrowing and the cost of lending for
financial institutions. The Committee recommends  the possibility  should be explored by SIDBI and IDBI to retire the old
high cost dues so as to facilitate the fresh loan process and give impetus to SSIs. The Committee also recommends that steps
to  evolve necessary mechanism so that the financial health of SFCs is restored.
 
 
RECOMMENDATION NO. 4
 
7.           Wider  Access to Bank Finance
 
The Committee strongly feels that the Govt. should undertake following measures for enabling SSI units to have access to
bank finance-
 
 (i)        Undertaking of orientation course and adequate training for staff/officers of commercial banks for a change in the
attitude for lending to SSI sector.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
(i)             As per the periodical reports received from RBI, regular  training programmes are  organised  by  the Banks for 
staff/officers so as to improve their work performance besides sensitising them towards better service delivery to
SSI sector.
 
Further  Observations/Recommendations
 
The Committee has observed that a change in the mental attitude of the bank officials at the lower levels is required. The
Bank officials should take a proactive role in encouraging entrepreneurship and provide both technological advise and
financial inputs to make the SSI sector strong.
 
 
(ii)                 Simplification of procedures for obtaining credit from Banks.  Arrangement may be made for land mortgage
instead of stamp duty for enabling SSI units to obtain credit from bank against land mortgage.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
As per the periodical  reports received from RBI banks, there is   continuous endeavour to simplify their procedures
with a view to prompt and efficient service to the SSI units.
 
Further  Observations/Recommendations
 
The Committee has noted that an entrepreneur has still to run from pillar to post and completing all procedural
formalities is still a nightmare. The Committee recommends that further simplification of the procedure may be done on an
urgent and priority basis.
 
(iii)                Govt. should create conducive climate for development of factoring services through appropriate policy
prescription/ legislative changes to ease problems relating to stamp duty, registration fee, assignment of Debt
etc.
 
Comments/Action Taken
 
The Ministry of SSI & ARI has constituted a Committee on factoring services  under the Chairmanship of Joint
Secretary.  The Committee has prepared a draft legislation on factoring services which is under consideration of the GOI
 
Further  Observations/Recommendations
 
The Committee would like to know the latest status of the above draft legislation including the steps taken by the
Ministry to expedite the consideration and approval of the legislation.

(iv)               Recapitalisation  and revamping of SFCs with the help of SIDBI and IDBI enabling SFCs to lend fixed capital

Comments/Action Taken

Further  Observations/Recommendations

(v)                 Since RRBs are not widespread with branches even in rural areas so well performing RRBs may be

Under the Credit Guarantee Scheme,  RRBs are eligible for becoming member lending institutions

The Committee has observed that further encouragement is needed for well performing RRBs. The Committee is

RECOMMENDATION NO.5
 
8.         
Popularisation of Venture Capital Fund
 
The committee feels that the Commercial banks should come forward in launching venture capital funds in all its
comprehensiveness so as to effectively integrate and enhance the opportunities for SSIs in domestic and international market.
 
8.1       Comments/Action Taken
 
The matter has been taken up with Department of Economic Affairs (Banking Division). The Ministry is also
discussing with SIDBI, other financial institutions and SSI Associations.
 
8.2       Further  Observations/Recommendations
 
The Committee is constrained  to note that the majority inputs by venture capital has taken place in areas other than SSIs.
The Committee would like to be informed of the output of the discussions initiated with SIDBI and other financial institutions.
The Committee recommends that steps to build up a strong culture of venture capital for SSIs is required to be built up.
 
 
RECOMMENDATION NO.6
 
9.
         Setting up of Technology Upgradation and Development Banks
 
9.1       Although SIDBI has come forward with Technology Development and Modernisation Fund (TDMF) Scheme in order
to assist SSIs at its prime lending rates but the Committee,  taking   into   account   the   magnitude   of  the problem of
technological  obsolescence in SSI sector, feels that Govt. should come forward to set up Technology Upgradation and
Development Banks to take stock of ground realities of sickness of SSI units due to obsolescence of technology and assist the
units by ensuring credit facilitation at appropriate time and thus save the sick but potentially viable SSI units before dying out.
 
 
9.2       Comments/Action Taken
 

54th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/54threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:13:29]
RBI has advised the banks to develop schemes to encourage investment by SSI units in technology upgradation vide
circularNo.RPCD.PLNFS.BC.16/06.02.31/2000-01 dated the 15 th September, 2000.  Besides, Ministry of SSI & ARI  has
launched in October, 2000 a Credit Linked Capital Scheme for new and existing SSI units for technology upgradation.  In
view of these, there is perhaps no case for setting up Technology Upgradation and Development Banks for the present.
 
9.3       Further Observations/ Recommendations
 
The Committee has noted that there has been increase in the NPAs of banks in the recent past. With the increasing
competition arising after WTO and the huge influx of imports from China there is a need to provide technological leadership
to the SSIs. During the course of interaction in Mumbai the representative from the bank stated that Technological leadership
at the district level is the most important thing. Unless the technological leadership is given, the branch manager will not find
the confidence to give money.  The other point is that in the ground level some kind of machinery where investor or self
employed would  get advice to invest. Therefore technological advice is the most important thing.
 
9.4       The Committee recommends that adequate steps to provide technological inputs to SSIs need to be given so as to
enable them to face global competition. The ministry can make a think tank with adequate support so that flow of latest
technology takes place.
 
 
RECOMMENDATION NO.7
 
10.       
Extension of non-financial services
 
The Committee feels that banks need to play the role of a friend, philosopher and guide to their clients.
 
10.1     Comments/Action Taken
 
Banks staff/officers are  being trained and sensitised towards SSI loaning and credit friendly environment.
 
10.2
          Further Observations/ Recommendations
 
The Committee is distressed to note that a lot remains to be done to sensitize the Banks staff/officers at the ground and
cutting edge level. The issue of granting loans without collateral security still poses a big problem.  An entrepreneur has to
really struggle hard  to get a loan sanctioned. The Committee has noted with concern that there are constant complaints that
deposits and collateral are being asked for. During the course of interaction in Mumbai the Committee was informed that some
banks like the Bank of Baroda have done exceedingly well by introducing innovative concepts like PMRY clubs where the
recovery and performance seems to be much better.
 
10.3     The Committee recommends that concrete steps to make the lower level bank staff more responsive  are required to
make a real difference at the  grass root  level are to be given special recognition in the form of annual certificate award etc.
and  the Committee also recommends that cases of banks who have been particularly successful need to be emulated and
followed up by other banks.

RECOMMENDATION NO.11
 
12.       The Committee while expressing serious concern over accumulation of inventory to the tune of Rs.750 crore
recommends that the KVIC should take an all out efforts for clearing all the inventory in the minimum possible time.  The
KVIC should sketch effective marketing strategy for clearing the stocks which has piled up over the years.
 
12.1     Comments/Action Taken
 
The KVIC has started engaging designers so to give a boost for readymade garments and to increase the sale of khadi. 
The KVIC has also participated in various fairs/exhibitions organized by the ITPO, CII, Centre for Bhartiya Marketing
Development (CBMD) in various parts of the country.
 
12.2     Further Observations/ Recommendations
 
The Committee desired to know the details of the response to the fairs. The Committee has noted with serious concern
that KVIC continues to have huge inventories and it reflects on the lack of marketing expertise within KVIC. The Committee
also feels that KVIC has not been able to change according to the market demand and has not been able to develop a suitable a
marketing strategy. The Committee recommends that immediate steps to evolve a effective strategy be taken in this regard.
