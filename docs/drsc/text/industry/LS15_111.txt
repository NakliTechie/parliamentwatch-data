REPORT
Alloy Steel Plant
 
 
 Alloy Steel Plant, a pioneer in the production of alloy and special steel in India, is located at Durgapur, District
Burdwan in the state of West Bengal . Alloy Steels Plant was commissioned in mid 1967 with the objective of
producing and developing various grades of alloy and special steels required by the country.  The plant was designed 
and  engineered by M/s  M.N.  Dastur & Co.  and the production know-how was obtained from M/s Atlas Steel,
Canada.  The integrated production in ASP   commenced from December, 1968 .  The  ingot  steel capacity of the plant
was further augmented by 60.00 tonnes per year by installation of  50 tonnes  arc  furnace with conditioning facilities
and by utilising the second shift of the blooming and  billet mill.  The expansion was completed in March, 1988.  The
capacity of the  plant  has since been expanded from  1,60,000 tpa to 2,60,000  tpa of liquid steel and 1,83,000 tonnes of
saleable steel.    Since its inception, Alloy Steels Plant has not only met the demands of its customers, it has also been
successful in developing many special categories of products required by strategic sectors.  However, despite a much
superior quality edge over its competitors, ASP is facing stiff competition  in the market for selling its products  due to
high cost of production  and  the plant is running loss  on the continuous basis. 
1.2       During the course  of interaction with the Sub-Committee-I,  in the meeting of the Sub-Committee  held  on 14
th
March, 2002,  the Secretary, Ministry of Steel, expressed his concern over  ASP's   performance :-
" The performance of ASP  continues to be disturbing .  It continuous   to make losses.  It is  generally assessed that  in
the prevailing market situation, with the  kind of   technology  and age of equipment that the ASP  has, it would
continue to be a  loss-making unit.  The  disinvestment of ASP is part of the  business   restructuring process of SAIL. 
The Steel Authority has invited bids for  disinvestment. They have invited expressions of interest, but, as of now, the 
assessment is that  there  are no takers.  It is again indicative of the fact that on SAIL's   commercial judgement, ASP is
a loss-making company.  It is something   that the market seems to affirm, because, as  I  said, there has been no 
response to the  advertisement for expressions of interest."
1.3       The Secretary, the Ministry of Steel, further added :-
" There may be  other units which are  on the margin   and with   a change in technology and market conditions,
perhaps, a particular  loss-making unit may turn  into a  profit -making unit.  But  this assessment, unfortunately, is not
right, as far as ASP is concerned."
1.4       The   Committee believes that the Secretary, Ministry of Steel has reasons to express concern over the
operational status of Alloy Steel Plant (ASP).  ASP is facing all odds.  The plant has suffered huge accumulated loss, is
incapable of utilising installed capacity and facilities available, scale of production is unviable, financial performance  is
dwindling,  cost of production is uncompetitive   and it has lost its market.  The disinvestment effect failed as
`Expression of  Interest'  (EOI )  has   evoked  no   response.  Government agencies are not coming forward to help the
plant out.  The performance of plant in the past has been gloomy and at presents the plant is at moribund stage.  The
Committee is of the opinion that the plant may reach to the  point of no return unless a comprehensively considered
revival package is undertaken to improve the operational efficiency   of the plant under  guided   supervision for the
ultimate survival and sustenance of the plant.

3.0
  Facilities available at ASP
3 .1      The Sub-Committee was informed that ASP was  the recipient of ISO:9002 certification for the entire plant  ASP
has an installed capacity of 260,000 tpa of liquid steel and 234,000 tpa of crude steel and 184,000 tpa of saleable steel,
with facilities for producing world class quality alloy and special steels through EAF route. The facilities at ASP include
slab-cum-twin bloom Continuous Casting machine, secondary- refining facilities like Vacuum Arc Degassing (VAD),
Vacuum Oxygen De-carburisation (VOD) and Ladle Furnace. ASP has the capacity to produce slabs, blooms, bars,
plates, and forged items of over 400 grades in a wide range of sizes.

3.3       The Committee, while noting the facilities available at ASP, is constrained to express its concern over the under-
utilisation of facilities available at ASP.  Reasons for under-utilisation may be of different nature, i.e. varying   from
general recession in steel industry to specific and inherent problems of the plant. Some of the   reasons might seems
justified   and might be well defended by   stating that, factors forming the reasons,  were beyond the control of
management, the other reasons  might be pointed out ,could be  dwindling market demand   of  ASP's  product. 
However, the   facts remains that   elaborate facilities is available at ASP and expansion programme   in ASP was
completed as late as March, 1988 and   infrastructure facilities of the plant has   since been expanded from 1,60,000 tpa
to 2,60,000 tpa for producing 1,83,000 tonnes of  liquid Steel.  The Committee is perturbed to observe that investment
on expansion projects in ASP was made without proper techno-economic consideration. Expansion projects in ASP
were bereft of cost-benefits analysis, the Committee feels.

10.0
          Major problems faced by ASP and solution:
 
10.1          The sub-committee was informed that ASP is facing following problems:-
 
i                       Alloy Steels Plant is fundamentally un-competitive because of its  location, low quality of assets and
outdated technology.
ii                     Production at ASP is dependent on the uneconomical low power EAF  route and most of its equipment
is old.
iii                    The market demand for alloy and special steels  in India has stagnated at around 1.4-15 mt over the last
five years and the capacity utilization continues to   be  less than 50 percent  .  The market is high 
fragmented among various small players and ASP'S  market share  is  only 6 percent .
iv                   ASP has a business disadvantage  (vis-à-vis its competitors) in its location in the eastern region, while
nearly 60 percent  of the market demand is from southern and western regions.  ASP has a freight cost
disadvantage vis-à-vis a south based supplier.
v
                     ASP has lost volume steadily, as the requirement of inter-plant transfers have come down, on account of
economic considerations.
vi                   High manpower cost and high overheads.
 
10.2     As a result of inherent problems being faced by ASP, the plant has the trend of decreasing sales realization due
to high variable cost of production   compared to the competitors. SAIL is finding it very difficult to fund the losses of
Alloy Steel Plant, since SAIL'S overall financial performance itself is uncomfortable.
 
10.3     The Committee observes that the problems being faced by ASP are deeply- rooted  and pose the real threat for
the survival and sustenance of the   plant   under the prevailing environment.  There are the   reasons to believe that

i
           Special thrust on reducing manpower; and
ii         To reduce level of operations at ASP, based on market conditions/financial aspects

Operational Status of Alloy Steel Plant (ASP)
 
            The   Committee believes that the Secretary, Ministry of Steel has reasons to express concern over the
operational status of Alloy Steel Plant (ASP)  .  ASP is facing all odds.  The plant has suffered huge accumulated loss, is
incapable of utilising installed capacity and facilities available, scale of production is unviable, financial performance  is
dwindling,  cost of production is uncompetitive   and it has lost its market.  The disinvestment effect failed as `
Expression of  Interest'  (EOI )  has   evoked  no   response.  Government agencies are not coming forward to help the
plant out.  The performance of plant in the past has been gloomy and at presents the plant is at moribund stage.  The
Committee is of the opinion that the plant may reach to the  point of no return unless a comprehensively considered
revival package is undertaken  to improve the operational efficiency   of the plant under  guided   supervision  for the
ultimate survival  and  sustenance of the plant.
( Para : 1.4)

            The Committee is perturbed to note the huge accumulated loss of ASP.  The huge accumulated loss i.e. to the

            The Committee feels that ASP has failed, miserably, in taking advantage   of its strength , at appropriate time . 
ASP is not in a position to avail of its opportunities because of high cost of production as the finishing   facilities are not
cost   effective which   put ACP at disadvantage in matching international   cost and quality in the production of 
stainless steel and  other steel plants  are producing carbon construction steel at a lower cost.  Taking stock of SWOT  -
analysis , the Committee is of the opinion that the plant is heading towards a point of no  return.
( Para :  15.2 )
Disinvestment  of ASP
 
            The Committee understands that, considering ASP's financial performance in the past and no strong possibility
of improvement, in future ASP is proving a nuisance to SAIL's  in general and burden on government's treasure. 
Therefore, efforts were made for divestment of ASP.  However, market proved much more prudent and Expression of
Interest (EOI) evoked   no response for purchasing the ailing plant of SAIL.  ASP is dwindling further, SAIL is not in a
position to invest  , and Government   is not in a position to inject `new lease of life ' to the plant, which seems to be at
the  moribund stage.
( Para : 16.2 )

            Issue  of transfer of Alloy Steels  Plant to Department of Defence Production & Supplies.
( Para :17.0)
 
            In a bid to disown the ailing ASP, the Ministry of Steel, once made efforts for the transfer of ASP to the
Department of Defence (  Production & Supplies) on the logic  that ASP was  conceived  and developed, in early sixties,
to cater to the needs of various sectors of industry, especially Defence and Special scientific  application   like nuclear
power plant.  The Department of Defence production & supplies declined to take over ASP in view of that fact that the
total annual  requirement of  all   kinds  of steel , by ordinance factory  board of Ministry of Defence , is only  a very
small fraction (21 percent )  of the total production of Alloy.  The Committee finds ASP between the devil and the  deep
sea, as it has no taker and the  present  one  is willing to disown it.  The Committee feels that its hardly matters whether 
ASP is under the administrative   control of Ministry of  Steel or the Department of  Defence( Production and Supplies),
and what actually  matters is the question of   the  survival of ASP and the future  prospects of the plant.
( Para :  17.3)

Restriction of Production:

            The Committee firmly believes that the survival  and sustenance of ASP might be guaranteed by   and only by

expenditure is a rationalise  at the earliest .

            The committee welcomes the closure of un-viable  shops in ASP.

The Committee is of the opinion that Alloy Steels Plant is a  pioneer in the production of stainless steel in India,
and   its products are  being  used by the Defence, Railway, Space Research Organisations, Nuclear Power Plants .
When India was debarred to import alloy steels required for Bofors Cannon Shells during Kargil conflict and high
quality special stainless steel required for  Atomic Research Centres ,after the Nuclear Test at Pokhran, it was ASP,
which  came forward to produce all these materials required at the moment of national crisis. Therefore, the Committee
feels that It would  be a national loss if ASP, a holder of ISO-9002 Certificate , is thrown away.
( Para : 19.1)
 
Merger of DSP and ASP
 
The Committee views thatthe close proximity of Alloy Steels Plant and Durgapur Steel Plant, under the same
organisations – SAIL, is a matter of great advantage. ASP is utilizing scrap skull, C. O. gas, electricity,  water, dolomite,
oxygen from DSP.  It also utilises Hot Metal/Liquid Steel, Ingot, Blooms & Billets as input materials. The two sister
plants of SAIL are having common township and Hospital, under the control of same Managing Director. Therefore ,
the Committee feels that the possibility of merger of DSP and ASP may be explored . This plant could be made more
cost effective by utilizing the in-house facilities of the two SAIL Plants, ASP & DSP. This requires  more close
supervision and effective co-ordination. This should not be a difficult job since the two plants are under the control of
same Managing Director.  What is needed, is the will of SAIL to run both the Plants, making best use of the technical
arrangements already prevailing to make both the Plants economically viable.
( Para :  19.3)
 
            The suggestion of R&D, SAIL, to install an AOD at ASP for improving quality of stainless steel and EMS at
concast shop for production of Defence & Automobile requirements, should be examined The other suggestion of
technical experts like Dastur & Co. to install a Billet Caster, Wire Rod Mill and modernize the Forge Shop , may be
taken into  consideration .
( Para :19.3 )
Utilizing inputs from DSP like liquid steel  
 
The Committee is of the view that  to reduce the cost of production of ASP products, a substantial quantity of
ASP customer’s need can be meted out by utilizing inputs from DSP like liquid steel made through BOF route as well as
Ingots, Bloom and Billets.  The Committee hopes production of ASP may be carried on  with close co-ordination with
Durgapur Steel Plant.  With transfer of liquid steel from DSP to the tune of yearly 50,000 MT monthly 4500 MT.  To
facilitate the transfer of this Steel, necessary arrangement may  be made.
 
Installation of two numbers of E.M.S. in the CCS
 
The  installation of two numbers of E.M.S. in the CCS, which is essential for regaining lost market in the
automobile sectors of the Eastern Region , may be examined.
 ( Para :19.9)
Use of DSP-ASP route
Electric Arc Steel making is a costly process which should   be sparingly used  at  ASP.  ASP should utilize
ASP's finishing facilities to produce around 90,000 tonnes of steel from DSP-ASP route per annum to make a cost
effective contribution. 
( Para :19.8)
Nominal   Investment
ASP has a large potential in production of high quality  forgings   upto 12,000  TPA.  A nominal   investment to
augment the Unit in order to bring in high returns can be considered on priority. An  investment  of around Rs.2-3
Crores can enlarge product- mix as per market requirement .
( Para :19.9)

Purchase Preference 
Alloy Steels Plant is a major supplier of steel products to Defence, Railways and almost all the important
Government Engineering establishments like BHEL,NTPC, CIL, GAIL, BARC, DMRL etc. use ASP's product   to the
tune of  50-60.000  TPA  due to it's  superior quality .  Lately these units have also been advised by the Government to
take the material from private Enterprises or on lowest bidders.  The purchase preference should be given to ASP
keeping in view its  high products quality. Since the Department of Public Enterprise has extended the Purchase
Preference Policy upto 31.3.04, the Ministry of Steel should take the opportunity   to bag orders  from the other   PSUs
to  surmount  the present difficult  situation through which Steel PSUs are  passing .
