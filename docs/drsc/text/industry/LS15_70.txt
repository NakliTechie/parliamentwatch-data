REPORT

1. The Ministry of Steel has to play a crucial role in ensuring harmonious and integrated
growth of steel sector. The main functions of Ministry of Steel are as under :-

(a) co-ordination of the growth of the Iron and Steel Industry (including Re-rolling Mills,
Alloy Steel and Ferro Alloy industries, Refractories) both in the Public and Private
Sector.

(b) formulation of policies in respect of production, pricing, distribution, import and
export of iron and steel and Ferro alloys;

(c) planning, development and control of and assistance to the entire iron and steel
industry in the country; and

(d) development of input industries relating to iron ore, manganese ore, refractories etc.,
required mainly by the steel industry.

1.2 Besides the Secretariat, the Ministry of Steel has an attached office viz. the office of the
Development Commissioner for Iron and Steel (DC I & S) located at Calcutta and its four
Regional Offices located in New Delhi, Calcutta, Chennai and Mumbai. Under the administrative
control of Ministry of Steel the following Public Sector Undertakings are functioning :-

i. Steel Authority of India Ltd. (SAIL).

ii. Kudremukh Iron Ore Company Ltd. (KIOCL), Bangalore

iii. National Mineral Development Corporation Ltd. (NMDC), Hyderabad.
iv. Hindustan Steel Works Construction Ltd. (HSCL), Calcutta

v. MECON Ltd., Ranchi.

vi. Manganese Ore (India) Ltd. (MOIL), Nagpur.

vii. Sponge Iron India Ltd. (SIIL), Hyderabad.

viii. Bharat Refractories Ltd. (BRL), Bokaro.

ix. Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Ltd. (RINL), Visakhapatnam.
x. MSTC Ltd. Calcutta.

xi. Ferro Scrap Nigam Ltd. (FSNL), (A subsidiary of MSTC Ltd.).

xii. Kudremukh Iron and Steel Company Ltd., (KISCO), Bangalore (A Joint Venture
Company of KIOCL, MECON and MSTC Ltd.

xiii. Bird Group of Companies, Calcutta, a Government managed company.

1.3 Consequent on acquisition of the shares by the Bird Group of Companies Ltd. 8
companies of the Bird Group related to the steel industry came under the administrative control of
the Ministry of Steel which inter-alia include Eastern investment Ltd. (EIL); Orissa Mineral
Development Co. Ltd. (OMDC); Birsa Stone Lime Co. Ltd. (BSLC); Karanpura Development
Co. Ltd. (KDCL); Scot & Saxby Ltd. (SSL), (a subsidiary of KDCL), Kumardhubi Fireclay &
Silica Works Ltd. (KFSW); Borea Coal Co. Ltd.; and Burraur Coal Co. Ltd. out of which on four
companies (OMDC, BSLC, KDCL and SSL) are now operational.

1.4 The Committee notes the structural and functional aspects of the Ministry of Steel and
PSUs under its administrative control and hopes that all wings of the whole structure would make


their respective/allocated duties and responsibilities with due co- ordination and inter PSUs
cooperation in such a manner that steel sector in general may contribute to economic growth and
national development.

6.1 Hindustan Steelworks Construction Ltd.(HSCL)
(Rs. in crore)
__ Budget 2001-02 Revised 2001-02 Budget 2002-03

6.1.1 The Non-Plan loan of Rs.89.44 crore is to be used for partially clearing arrears of wages
& salaries and statutory dues of the employees of HSCL. It is expected that this entire amount
will be used for this purpose. The large arrears on account of unpaid wages & salaries and
statutory dues have been a source of industrial dissatisfaction among the employees of the
company. This has led to the industrial relations situation being vitiated and has adversely
affected the productivity of the company. Further, the company is also handicapped in
aggressively pursuing the separation of surplus employees through VRS in the face of large
arrears of wages & salaries. The separation of at least 5000 employees through VRS is critical for
the improvement of the operational margins available to the company. It is felt that the non-plan
loan outlay of Rs.89.44 crore will go a long way in solving the above stated problems. However,
it may be noted that the amount of Rs.89.44 crore sanctioned is much below the actual arrears on
account of wages & salaries and statutory dues which, as on 1* March, 2002 stands at Rs.207
crore approximately. To this extent the present non-plan assistance will only be partially
beneficial. As stated above the provision of this loan assistance will be crucial for persuading
employees to accept VRS in large numbers. It has been computed that for the company to
generate positive margins, there is a need to separate at least 5000 employees. The separation of
5000 employees will enable the company to generate positive gross margins. Given the slow
down in the steel sector and low margins in the non-steel sector the prospects of improvement in
physical performance is less. However the Company is striving to diversify its activities in non-
steel sector and is trying to improve its competitiveness by cutting costs.

12.1.17. Progress in implementation of business and financial restructuring of SAIL

Ministry of Steel informed the Committee that while the financial restructuring of SAIL
has been completed, substantial progress has been made in many areas and the assigned tasks
completed. Organisational tasks such as redesigning incentive structures, redesigning key
corporate processes have been completed. All the tasks in the marketing areas such as strategic
marketing, tactical marketing, including introduction of KAM process, improving sales force
effectiveness etc. have also been completed. The capital expenditure has been restricted 10 Rs.426
crore in 2000-01. Till December 2001, the actual capital expenditure has been throttled to Rs.244
crore only-and the year 2001-02 is likely to end with Rs.500 crore. The process of redesigning
the capital expenditure procedures and manuals have made good progress and is more or less
complete. In the area of operation management, Rs.505 crore was saved through cost reduction
in 2000-01. In the current year Rs.306 crore have been saved till January 2002 and by the end of
this financial year, Rs.400 crore is expected to be saved. The progress on purchase cost reduction
and sale of idle assets have been satisfactory. Further efforts in this regard will continue.
However, in a few cases constraints have been faced affecting progress and needing a re-look.
As regards the change from a Functional to Business Unit Structure, a detailed study has been
undertaken with consultation from a reputed management consultant. The basic blueprint for the
restructuring has been made ready and implementation plan is presently under preparation.
Considering the scope of such a major organisation restructuring in a large organisation like
SAIL, and the implications with respect to location of head quarters of SBU, Board Structure etc.,
it has been decided to take up the implementation in two phases. In Phase-I, many critical
changes such as separation of branches and stockyards, enhanced KAM process, revised cash
management system, modified system of MIS, improved reward and punishment system etc. will
be taken up for implementation. Implementation of all these major activities in Phase-I is
expected to start in April 2002 and completed in two month’s time i.e. by end of May 2002. The
2™ Phase will be taken up later for implementation.

17.0 | GENERAL AND CURRENT ISSUES AFFECTING STEEL INDUSTRY
17.1. Impact of the imposition of tariffs by USA

17.1.1 The US administration has selectively imposed duties and quota restrictions on various
categories of imported steel under the safeguard provisions of the World Trade Organization
(WTO). The announcement of safeguard action is disturbing to the extent that it would accentuate
instability in the global steel market. The unilateral closure of the US market to a large number of
exporters will result in diversion of some of the global surplus into the markets of other importing
countries. This would further depress the market price of steel items and encourage dumping of

17.3.2. The data above, however, are to be read with a few observations:

1. Firstly, the production data used to build up the consumption series may not fully
reflect the production of non-flat products as a large number of production unit
operate in the smaller and relatively unorganized secondary sector. This sub-sector
consists of small EAF/IF units and re-rollers. Very often their feed material is derived
from scraps available from different sources including the broken ships. When
estimated from the input side on the basis of total feed material available (i.e., semis
from the organized sector, scrap, DRI, HBI and Pig iron), the total production of non-
flat products in this sub-sector is found to be much higher than the production
reported to the official agency. Therefore, one may conclude that the total production
and hence the total consumption of non-flat products in the economy is somewhat
higher than the reported data.

ii Secondly, in almost a mirror image of the underestimation of non-flat production and
consumption, there is some evidence that the reported production and consumption of
flat products contain an element of overestimation. This arises from the fact that some
of the upstream flat products, which are used for further down-stream processing,

have been double-counted in their value-added stages. Therefore, it is likely that the
total quantum of flat consumption is lower than that reported.

17.3.3. The impact of these enumeration problems may not impact total finished steel
consumption much because the underestimation in non-flats may to some extent neutralize the
overestimation in the flat categories. But it is important to note that this has a significant impact
on the composition of consumption in terms of changing the flat: non-flat ratios. On the basis of
these observations, one may conclude that the profile of Indian steel consumption may be biased
more towards the non-flat consumption as opposed to the pattern shown by the official figures.

17.4 Advanced Licence Scheme

17.4.1. Under the Export- Import Policy in -force, an advance license is issued (by Directorate
General of Foreign Trade) under the Duty Exemption Scheme to allow import of inputs/raw
materials duty free for actual use in the export product. Advance License as the name implies is
issued in advance for import of inputs/ raw materials against specific orders in respect of Physical
Exports, Intermediate Supplies and Deemed Export. It can also be issued on the basis of projected
annual requirement for exports/ supplies in accordance with the specified conditions.

17.4.2.The scheme has been found beneficial by the industry including the Iron and Steel
industry, as they are able to import duty free raw materials/inputs for export production.
However, large-scale import of iron and steel items particularly, seconds and defectives of flat
rolled products at very low prices have been adversely affecting the interest of the iron and steel
industry. The Ministry has noticed this abuse of the scheme. Since the Ministry of Commerce
monitors the scheme, this lacuna has been brought to their notice.

17.5.1. Commenting on the suggestion that the export incentive (WTO compatible) that clearly

17.7.1. Ministry of Steel is proposing enactment of a legislation - “Buy India Act” with the
objective to encourage growth and development of indigenous industry by giving preference to
indigenous steel producers in Government procurement. The legislation would apply to all
projects, industrial or business enterprises set up or to be setup with the grant and aid of public
money. The act stipulates that all such industrial and business enterprises would procure iron &
steel material indigenously for their requirement unless such materials are not available in the
country in the specified quality or within required delivery schedule or contrary to public interest
or detrimental to the project. The use of non- indigenous iron and steel materials for such projects

17.8 Future Outlook of Indian Steel Industry vis-a-vis emerging Global Steel Scenario.

17.8.1. There are tentative signals that the global steel market is on its way up. The prices of
most steel products in the international markets have increased. For example, the export prices of
HR coils, as per information available from World Steel Dynamics and other market intelligence
reports, have increased from about $165 per tonne to about $195 per tonne. The prices for
Japanese mill products are still higher. There are reports that this price increase has been backed
by definite increase in demand/consumption. In response to the increased demand and prices, the
crude steel production globally has also risen.

17.8.2. There are reports that steel consumption in India in the recent months have
increased. As per the latest report of the Joint Plant Committee, the consumption of
finished steel during April- February 2001-02 have increased by 1.9 per cent over the
corresponding period last year. Exports of finished steel have also increased 2.7 percent.
Stock of steel with the main producers has also dropped marginally during February
2002. The steel makers are reportedly planning price hikes in response to the
strengthening of the market within the country as well as outside it.

17.8.3. The demand for steel is expected to increase by 6 per cent in the coming year ( 2002-03 ).
According to the study of the 10" Five Year Plan Working Group on Minerals, carbon steel
demand in the country is expected to maintain an annual growth rate of 6 per cent over the plan
period. Demand for steel will be supported by increased investment on infrastructure, increased
demand for consumer durables and automobiles and residential and industrial construction.

17.8.4. The growth rate of steel consumption in India is expected to remain higher than the global
average in the coming years. Despite increased threat of protectionism worldwide, the Indian
industry will remain dependent on the conditions in the world market. Increased export
opportunities and the emerging competitive strength of the industry will also shape the growth
pattern of the Indian industry

17.8.5. The Committee is pleased to note that global steel scenario is improving and thus
coming out of recession.
