REPORT
1.1    The Small Enterprises sector is a vibrant segment of the Indian economy which contributed over 39 per cent of the industrial sector
output and 34 per cent of the nation’s exports, and provided employment to over 270 lakh persons through 114 lakh units (2003-04)
spread both in rural and urban areas of the country. The major contribution of the small enterprises sector lies in creation of employment
opportunities at lower per capita capital cost. Alongwith agriculture, the small enterprises sector including both SSI and services provides
the largest employment generation potential in the country. During 2003-04, the small enterprises sector registered a growth rate of 8.6
per cent as against a growth rate of 7.0 per cent for the overall industrial sector.
1.2    To enhance their competitiveness both in domestic and global markets by facilitating technological upgradation and adoption of
international quality standards, the investment ceiling in some high-tech and export oriented SSI units has been raised from Rs. 1 crore to
Rs.5 crore in respect of stationery, drugs and Pharmaceuticals, hand tools & knitwear. Various sub-sectors of the SSI are Ancillary
Industrial Undertakings, High-Tech And Export Oriented Units, Tiny Enterprises, Small Scale Service and Business (Industry Related)
Enterprises, etc.
1.3    In the context of globalisation and for facilitating growth with wider employment generation, there is a growing realization that
India should also adopt the SME concept. On 9 th January 2004, the then Finance Minister announced the establishment of a fund of Rs.
10,000 crore for the SME sector. While presenting the budget for 2004-05, the Finance Minister observed that there was need to provide
some space for small-scale industries (SSIs) to grow into medium enterprises.
1.4    The Committee understands that the Small Scale Industries is today on the move world wide, therefore, Small Scale
Industries in India should be given top priority by the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance in plan-allocation so
that small scale units may get adequate space for maturing into medium enterprises to catch the global trend. The Ministry of
SSI should provide the logistic support to the SSI sector. The Ministry of Small Scale Industries must strengthen the SSI sector to
enable it to remain competitive in market-led economy and generate additional employment opportunities. The Ministry should
assist the SSI sector in accessing (or provide) proper and timely inputs like adequate credit from financial institutions banks;
funds for technology up-gradation and modernization; adequate infrastructural facilities; modern testing facilities and quality
certification laboratories; modern management practices and skill up-gradation through suitable training facilities; marketing
assistance; and level playing field at par with the large industries sector.
2.0    ORGANISATIONAL SET-UP AND RETRAINING OF STAFF
2.1    The Ministry of SSI designs policies, programmes, projects and schemes in consultation with its organizations and various
stakeholders and monitors their implementation with a view to assisting the promotion and growth of small scale industries. The Ministry
also performs the function of policy advocacy on behalf of the SSI sector with other Ministries/Departments of the Central Government
and the States and Union Territories.
2.2    The Ministry of Small Scale Industries designs and implements the policies through its field organizations for the promotion and
growth of small scale industries. The implementation of policies and various programmes/schemes for providing infrastructural and
support services to small enterprises is undertaken through its attached offices, namely Small Industries Development Organisation
(SIDO), its field offices, and National Small Industries Corporation Limited (NSIC), a public sector undertaking under the Ministry of
SSI.
2.3    The Committee feels that the organizational set-up of the Ministry must be made effective through reorientation of form
and retraining of staff to enable them to make full use of the opportunities and challenges fast emerging liberalization and
globalization process under post-WTO-regime by undertaking following steps:—

3.1    The Ministry of Small Scale Industries had projected a sum of Rs. 793.94 crore (inclusive of 
Rs. 3.35 crore for works outlay provided in the Demands of Ministry of Urban Development) for the year 2005-06. The Planning
Commission has fixed the outlay for Annual Plan 2005-06 at Rs. 412.26 crore (inclusive of Rs. 3.35 crore for works outlay provided in
the Demands of Ministry of Urban Development). The major reduction is under the Ministry’s contributions to the corpus for the Credit
Guarantee Trust Fund Scheme (76 per cent) has been allocated by the Planning Commission at Rs. 180.00 crore. The other Schemes that
are likely to be affected due to reduced allocation are Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (80 per cent) Technology Upgradation (16
per cent) and Tool Rooms (10 per cent). The Ministry informed that they will take up the matter at the RE stage.
3.2    The Committee is constrained to observe that the Planning Commission has not accorded the financial requirement as
projected by the Ministry of SSI to the tune of Rs. 793.94 crore (inclusive of Rs. 3.35 crore for work outlay provided in the
Demands of M/o Urban Development for the year 2005-06 ) against which a plan outlay of Rs. 412.26 crores (inclusive of Rs. 3.35
crores of Ministry of Urban Development) has been accorded as a  result of which reduced plan allocation would be made to the
schemes like Credit Guarantee Trust Fund Scheme (76 per cent) Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (80 per cent)
Technology Up-gradation (16 per cent) and Tool rooms (10 per cent) of their financial requirement causing a serious implication
to the promotion and consolidation of Small Scale Industries. The reduced allocation may already affect the implementation of
these schemes.
3.4    The Committee however understands the financial crunch with the Government and the consequent problems being faced
by the Planning Commission in judicious and equitably allocation among various Departments/Ministries as per their
requirement.
3.5    The Committee is of the opinion that even the Planning Commission would appreciate the potential of Small Scale Industry
in the employment generation and its contribution to the poverty alleviation. It is not a secret that SSI sector is the fate-fixture of
common man as the SSI units require low investment but effects the lives of millions. Therefore, the question of resurrection of
the SSI sector would mean the improvement in the standard of living of millions. Moreover, the global trend of promoting the
SSI sector is an observable fact. The Finance Minister of India is even tired of stressing the need for providing SSI sector the
adequate space to grow and thrive as medium enterprises therefore, the Committee feels that the Ministry of SSI should get the
sufficient line allocation to implement its schemes/programmes.
3.6    The Committee is also of the opinion that there is a need for better coordination between the Ministry of SSI, the Ministry
of Finance, Planning Commission so that the financial requirement of SSI sector is well communicated to the Planning
Commission as per the actual requirement of SSI sector. The Committee strongly recommends that the Ministry of SSI should
take up the matter with the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance at the RE Stage for plan allocation as per the basic
needs of SSI sector.

4.3    The Committee is in agreement with the revision in the Tenth Five Year Plan target of annual growth rate of Twelve per
cent and additional employment of 44 lakh for the SSI Sector. The Committee is however constrained to note that the revised
growth is yet to be achieved. The Committee therefore desires that resilient monitory mechanism must be set up for timely and
effective implementation of schemes/programmes/Action Plan on whose assumption the revised target has been set up. Since SSI
Sector is emerging as one of the largest employment provider after agriculture, therefore, all Governmental impetus must be
given on the promotion of SSI sector, recognizing and treating SSI as a “real Engine of national growth and development”.
4.4    There is no major policy shift for the year 2004-05 as compared to 2003-04. The endeavor of the Ministry is to strengthen and
facilitate the SSI sector to enable it to remain competitive in the global economy and generate additional employment. The national
economic policy, as enunciated in the National Common Minimum Programme (NCMP) and in the Budget Speeches of the Finance
Minister is, in the context of the SSI sector, focused on employment generation, technology Upgradation and marketing and credit
support to the sector. The budget allocation, accordingly, has been raised to Rs. 408.91 (exclusive of Rs. 3.35 crore for Works Outlay
provided in the Demands of Ministry of Urban Development) crore in 2005-06 compared to Rs. 362.25 crore (exclusive of Rs. 3.75 crore
for Works Outlay provided in the Demands of Ministry of Urban Development) in 2004-05. Efforts are being made to achieve the goals
of NCMP to facilitate the growth of the sector, which in turn will generate employment.
4.5    To address the issues flowing from the process of liberalization and gloablisation for the SSI sector and to assist the sector to
function in a competitive environment, emphasis has been given on technology upgradation of the SSI units. An enhanced budget
provision of Rs.l 8 crore has been provided during 2005-06 under the Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS). A budget
provision of Rs.22.61 crore has also been provided in 2005-06 under the ISO 9000/14000 Reimbursement Scheme. Under the Scheme, a
maximum grant of Rs.75,000 is provided to the SSI units for adopting ISO-9000/14000 quality and environment standards to help them
become globally competitive.
4.6    To help the SSI sector face the challenges posed by liberalisation and globalisation, the Government has been taking several policy
initiatives. These include dereservation of items reserved exclusively for manufacture in the SSI sector in a phased manner, enhancing the
investment limit in plant and machinery from Rs.l crore to Rs.5 crore in 71 select hi-tech and export oriented sectors, etc. The
Government is also in the process of finalizing a Small and Medium Enterprises Development Bill, 2005 to facilitate the promotion and
development of these enterprises and enhance their competitiveness.
4.7    In the Budget 2005-06, the allocations for ‘Promotion of SSI Schemes’ has been enhanced to Rs.l73 crore from Rs.135 crore
provided in 2004-05. Further, SIDBI has established a SME Growth Fund to provide equity support to the small and medium units in the
knowledge- based industries such as pharma, biotech, IT, etc. The above measures would help in promotion and development of small
and medium units and also setting up of new units in the emerging areas of pharma, biotech, IT, etc, leading to generation of more
employment opportunities. In addition a Budget provision of Rs.200 crore (including NE Region) has been made for Credit Guarantee
Fund Trust Scheme, which covers collateral free credit facility (term loan/working capital) extended by eligible lending institutions to
new and existing SSI units. This would help in removing the problem of collaterals  faced by the first generation entrepreneurs,
particularly in the tiny sectors as well as in enhancing the credit flow to the SSI sector.
4.8    The Committee understands that the SSI has to grow and thrive in the global competitive environment unleashed by the
globalization and liberalization process, therefore, concerted efforts should be made for holistic and integrated course of action
for consolidation and promotion of SSI sector by focusing on searching avenues for employment generation on the one hand and

11.0  RE-CHRISTENING OF THE MINISTRY OF SSI MINISTRY OF SMALL AND MEDIUM ESTABLISHMENTS

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/171threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:57:36]
11.1  The inclusion of services and the enlargement of the scope of the Ministry to medium establishment are important in the context of
the emergence of sunrise knowledge-based industries as the harbinger of a new industrial revolution. New knowledge based industries
like Pharmaceuticals and biotechnology industries to be internationally competitive and will require encouragement and support in the
initial period of their development. The emergence of IT and of software in a large way in this country also suggests the need for bringing
the service sector prominently into the ambit of encouragement and support given to small and medium establishments.
11.2  The Committee is of the view that the Government should re-christen the name of Ministry of SSI into the Ministry of Small
and Medium Enterprises (SME) in order to implement the objectives, achievement and targets of Indian SMEs as set by Finance
Minister in General Budget 2005-06. The Finance Minister in his Budget Speech 2005-06 also stated that “In recent years, our
approach to small scale industry has evolved, and now we are inclined to treat the sector as the small and medium enterprises
sector”, the Committee is of the view that creation of two separate Departments, e.g., Department of Small Scale Industries and

that “Small scale industry is, and must be regarded as, an engine of growth. At the same time SSI units must also be given the

enterprises (SME)”. The Committee also endorses the view of the Finance Minister and the scope of Ministry of SSI should be

18.0  THIRD CENSUS OF SSIS (2001-02) AND NEED FOR NATIONAL POLICY FOR SMALL INDUSTRIES
18.1  The Third All India Census of Small Scale Industries for the First time made an estimation of the unregistered sector also, based on
a sample survey. It revealed that out of the total 105.21 lakh units, 13.75 lakh were registered units and the remaining 91.46 lakh
unregistered. Out of the 22.62 lakh registered units, 13.75 (61 per cent) were found to be working. 39 per cent were found to be closed
(though the reasons or periods of such closure were not elicited during the census. 99.5 per cent of the total SSI units comprised tiny
units, viz., with investment upto Rs. 25 lakh. The total employment provided by the sector was 249.32 lakh of which 61.63 lakh were in
the registered sector and 187.69 lakh were in the unregistered sector. The total gross output was Rs. 2,82,26,998 lakh with the share of
the registered sector at 72 per cent and the contribution of the unregistered sector at 28 per cent. The share of the service sector
comprising the SSSBEs constituted nearly 58 per cent of the total number of units. The bulk (92 per cent) of the SSSBEs were in the
unregistered sector. Within the registered sector, 56 per cent of the units were in urban areas and 44 per cent in rural areas. Most of the
units were proprietary concerns, both in the registered and unregistered sectors. While about 89 per cent of the registered units were
proprietary concerns, 97 per cent of the unregistered-sector were in this category.
18.2  The results of the Third Census throw light on the structure and characteristics of SSI sector. The unregistered sector, which
constitutes almost 87 per cent of the total units, has remained largely outside the net of official statistics and also not benefited from the
various policy measures formulated for the support and development of SSI. This would be evident from the fact that the number of units
having outstanding loan with institutional credit within the unregistered sector was only 3.09 per cent. Even within the registered sector,
the extent of accessibility to institutional credit is low. Only 14.26 per cent of the registered units were availing of institutional credit.
Using the definition of sick units given by the Kohli Committee appointed by the RBI, i.e., number of units with erosion of net worth by
more than 50 per cent or delay in repayment of institutional loan by more than 12 months, it was found that 17.8 per cent of the units
having outstanding loan with institutional sources were sick.
18.3  Information was collected from the units about the source from which technical know- how was obtained by them. More than 85
per cent of the registered units indicated that they were dependent on traditional technology/skills. The registered sector showed that 36
per cent of the units had their plant and machinery installed during the seventies and eighties. 26 per cent of the registered units were not
using power as against 42 per cent in the unregistered sector. This is a pointer to the gap in technology upgradation which needs to be
remedied.
18.4  About 63.45 per cent of the registered units were in manufacturing/assembling/processing as compared with 36.12 per cent in the
unregistered sector. The registered sector had only a small share (2.10 per cent) in repairs and maintenance as compared with a higher
share (18.50 per cent) in the unregistered sector. Services constituted an important category with the share of the registered and
unregistered sectors at 34.45% and 45.38% respectively. The emergence of a vibrant service sector (SSSBE) merits special attention.  The
findings of the Third Census have shown that the unregistered sector has overtaken the registered sector in terms of the size (number of
units) and employment. However, the registered sector is significant in terms of contribution to output, which is almost 72% of the total
output.
18.5  The Committee is of the view that the national policy and programmes/schemes should be suitable, and effective for
supporting the promotion and development of small scale industries. The main emphasis should be on easy credit availability,
technological Upgradation and modernization, Infrastructural development, marketing support, etc. Moreover, for uniform and
sustainable growth of small scale industries with an objective for greater employment creation and equitable distribution of
wealth all over the country, there is a need for a national policy for small industries. To address the needs in the changed
economic scenario, the Government must come out with a promotional policy package at the earliest. The findings of the Third
Census can be used for policy formulation as follows:-
            (i)     Liberalise credit policy to make institutional credit more accessible to SSI units, including the unregistered units,
            (ii)    Identify industries requiring technological upgradation which can be assisted either under the on-going
programmes/schemes or new appropriate schemes for the same,
            (iii)   Target sub-sectors which have high employment potential, for assistance under different schemes.
            (iv)   Assistance for improving quality and productivity, especially of registered units which account for bulk of the output.

20.0  EFFORTS OF DC (SSI) ON DEVELOPMENT OF ACCREDITED CLUSTERS
20.1  Cluster can be defined as a sectoral and geographical concentration of enterprises, in particular small and medium facing common
opportunities and threats. 1. Cluster Development approach has been recognized as an effective tool for development of small scale
sector in India and abroad also. There are successful precedents of cluster development in Italy , Taiwan and few other countries. Abid
Hussain Committee and Dr. S.P. Gupta Committee Reports as a development tool in Indian industrial scenario have also advocated this
approach.
20.2  As per the 3
rd All India Census of SSI units conducted in 2001-02, there are 1223 clusters in Manufacturing Registered SSI Sector
having 285150 number of units and 819 clusters in unregistered SSI Sector having 12,50207 number of units. As per the estimates of
UNIDO there are about 388 clusters.
20.3  Cluster Development initiatives in India are being undertaken by various agencies like ”Ministry of Small Scale Industries,
Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion, UNIDO, State Government, financial institutions etc. Under cluster development a
holistic approach is adopted to address identified needs of a particular cluster. To find out needs of the cluster, a diagnostic study is
carried out in the cluster by Cluster Development agent(s). Under this diagnostic study report, the problems of Cluster are identified,
solutions are suggested and action plan is drawn in consultation with various stakeholders including Associations, Representatives of
Industry, financial Institutions etc. Generally the interventions are undertaken in areasTechnology Upgradation, Market
Development,Capacity Building.
20.4  Small Scale Industries contribute significantly in industrial production of the country. They produce a variety of products ranging
from traditional to hi-tech. Although the volume of production from Small Scale Industries is quite large, the quality of the product,
productivity, energy and environmental issues has always been a concern. These concerns have accentuated with the opening of the
economy where productivity and quality play a major role for the survival of the Small Scale Industries. The Office of the Development
Commissioner (Small Scale Industries) has launched a scheme namely the ‘Integrated Technology Upgradation and Management
Programme’ (UPTECH) in 1998, now renamed as ‘Small Industry Cluster Development Program’. The scheme applies to any cluster of
industries where there is a commonality in the method of production, quality control and testing, energy conservation, pollution control
etc. among the units of the cluster. The scheme aims to take care of the modernisation and the technological needs of the cluster. It covers
a comprehensive range of issues related to technology upgradation, improvement of productivity, energy conservation, pollution control,
product diversification and their marketing, training needs etc.
20.5  The Office of DC (SSI) has started a number of Cluster Development initiatives under with holistic approach and active
involvement of beneficiary units through their Associations in their implementation. The activities taken up for development of the
clusters are demand-based. The programmes viz Programme for Development of Rajkot Engineering Cluster; National Programme for
the Development of Indian Machine Tool Industry; National Programme for the Development of Indian Lock Industry; Auto
Components Development Programme, Chennai are being implemented by DC (SSI).

            (ii)    Technology audits and bench marking with competitors to identify gaps.
            (iii)   Establishing world-class product display centres for Small Scale Industries in important cities.
            (iv)   Third party accreditation services. Accreditation is a necessary for buyer comfort. Accreditation indicates
manufacturers ability to supply reliably and effectively. Co-branded accreditation services can be rendered
through established accreditation agencies to enhance acceptance of products made in small industries.
            (v)    Providing reliable market intelligence and trade leads in different industrial sectors. It can also assist SSIs in the
marketing efforts. NSIC Infomediary Services are aimed in this direction.
            (vi)   Brand building and consortia operations are extremely important marketing tools and these call for good degree of
mutual trust among small scale units to maintain uniform quality.

40.0  PROBLEMS BEING FACED BY SIDO IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF SCHEMES/ PROGRAMMES

40.1  SIDO is implementing several schemes/programmes for the promotion and development of the SSI sector in the country. Most of
these schemes/programmes are aimed at augmenting the credit flow to the SSI sector and are linked with the banks/financial institutions.
The banks are, however, not favourably inclined to finance the SSI sector for various reasons like adverse risk perception, lack of credit
rating facilities, higher transaction cost in processing of large number of loan applications, etc. Moreover, some of the schemes are
optional for the banks to participate in their implementation. Furthermore, the implementation of the schemes/programmes is also
dependent on the willingness of the beneficiaries (i.e. the SSI units/ entrepreneurs) and the State Governments. The State Governments
have a major role to play in the implementation of some of the schemes/programmes by way of providing matching financial
contribution, Infrastructural facilities, fiscal incentives/concessions etc.
40.2  The Ministry of SSI has already initiated the process of formulating a scheme on credit rating, which includes providing financial
assistance to the SSI units for obtaining credit rating. The Ministry of SSI has taken up the matter with all the Public Sector Banks for
dissemination of information on schemes/programmes at the branch level and among the SSI entrepreneurs. The SSI Associations have
also been asked to play a pro-active role in increasing awareness about the schemes/programmes/facilities being implemented for the SSI
sector among the SSI entrepreneurs. The Ministry of SSI is in the process of finalizing a Draft Small and Medium Enterprises
Development Bill, 2005, which is aimed at minimizing the plethora of rules, regulations and laws to be complied by the SSI
units/entrepreneurs. The same would be introduced for the consideration of the Parliament shortly. Besides, a promotional package for
the Small scale sector is under preparation.
40.3  The Committee understands that most of the schemes/programmes as implemented by SIDO aims at augmenting the credit
flow to the SSI sector and are linked with the banks/financial institutions. To enhance the credit flow to the SSI sector and at the
same time improve the comfort level of the banks to extend more credit to the SSI units/entrepreneurs, there is a need to

42.1  The Credit Guarantee Scheme was announced on 30 August 2000 and became operational in January 2001.The Objective of the
Credit Guarantee Scheme is to ensure better flow of credit to small and tiny enterprises by minimizing the risk perception of financial
institutions. This will give greater comfort level to banks and also help financing without collateral security. The Mechanism by setting
up a corpus fund with contribution from the Government of India and the Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) in the
ratio of 4:1. At present, the size of the corpus fund is Rs. 931.55 crore. The Operating Agency is the Trust set up by SIDBI/GOI and
serviced by SIDBI. CMD, SIDBI is the Chairman of the Trust; AS & DC (SSI) is the Vice-Chairman of the Trust; two other members are
Chairman, Indian Banks’ Association (IB A) and Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of the Trust. The Modus Operandi is that the lending
institutions can have loans guaranteed by payment of one-time guarantee fee of 2.5 per cent and an annual service fee of 0.75 per cent of
the credit sanctioned to the Trust, the implementing agency. All Commercial Banks - public and private, select regional rural banks
(RRBs), North East Development Finance Corporation (NEDFC), National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC) and SIDBI are eligible
under the scheme. Claim Settlement is done after 2-year lock-in period; settlement up to 75 per cent pending recovery proceedings,
balance 25 percent on completion of recovery proceedings. The extent of guarantee is 75 per cent of loans up to Rs.25 lakh. The
maximum risk cover is 75 per cent of the risk cover subject to a ceiling of Rs. 18.75 lakh. Interest rate payable by borrower not to exceed
3 per cent beyond PLR of lender.
42.2  It is estimated that the loans guaranteed by the Trust were instrumental in creating around 93,200 new job opportunities as at 28
February 2005. The major beneficiaries under the scheme are in the tiny sector comprising of rural artisans, self-employed entrepreneurs,
etc. The progress of the scheme is monitored by the Trust and the Ministry of SSI on monthly basis. The National Advisory Committee
(NAC) of CGTSI, consisting of representatives from the Government, SIDBI, RBI, IBA, banks, experts/professionals and small
enterprises associations, also reviews the implementation of the scheme periodically. IBA Review Forum also assists in implementation
of the scheme through coordination between the banking industry and Trust.
42.3  The Committee understands that one of the main objectives of the scheme is to generate more employment opportunities by
facilitating collateral free loans to small enterprises. However, despite the provision of the loans up to 25.00 lakh without
collateral/third party guarantee and the guidelines of the Reserve Bank of India thereto, Banks are reported to insist on the
collateral security and the third party guarantee. The committee has repeatedly recommended for a change of the mind set of the
Branch Manager, nothing fruitful has come out so far. The Committee directs all the Banks who are Members of this scheme to
implement the scheme so as to make it success.

of FY 2002-03. With the higher plan outlay of Rs. 186.44 crore during 2003-04, the Corpus Fund was enhanced to Rs.686.19 crore at the

49.0  ANALYSIS OF STATUS OF SMALL SCALE INDUSTRIES IN NE & SIKKIM
49.1  The North Eastern Region and Sikkim have abundant natural resources and potential for small and tiny units. These resources can
be utilized for industrial development. The absence of motivated entrepreneurs has hampered the promotion and rapid industrialization.
The growth of small scale industries in the North Eastern States including Sikkim has not kept pace with the growth of SSIs in other parts
of the country. In order to accelerate the setting up of SSI units in this region, several steps have been taken by the Govt. which are
enumerated below:-
            (i)     Small Industries Services Institutes/Branch Institutes(SISIs/BIs) have been opened in all the North Eastern States & Sikkim.
            (ii)     Under the IID Scheme, Central grant (maximum Rs. 4 crore) is provided to the Implementing Agency for setting up IID
Centre in the North Eastern States and Sikkim as against Rs.2 crore to most of the States in India.
            (iii)     Under the Integrated Infrastructural Development Scheme, six IID Centres have been sanctioned in Assam and one each in
Manipur, Mizoram and Tripura.
            (iv)    A Tool Room and Training Centre at Guwahati has been set up to design and manufacture tools, dyes, zigs and fixtures etc.
and to impart training in tool and dye manufacturing, use of computers in design and manufacturing.
            (v)    Indian Institute of Entrepreneurship has been set up at Guwahati.
49.2 The Committee understands that the major areas of concern in the North Eastern Region and Sikkim are lack of
infrastructure like road and rail communication, shortage of power for industrial use, availability of skilled manpower, low
entrepreneurial skills, access to finance, technological obsolescence and lack of large scale manufacturing units which provides
ancillary opportunity to SSI community, therefore, the committee desires that the Ministry of SSI should take initiatives in this
regard by taking the matter with State Governments and DONER. The Committee is of the view that North Eastern Region and
Sikkim should be facilitated with focused entrepreneurship development programmes,technical training & skill up-gradation,
awareness of industrial opportunities for manufacturing in NER & Sikkim and supplying to the rest of the country, relatively
softer credit and marketing assistance programmes.

54.0  INDIAN TINY SECTOR’S COMPETITIVE RESILIENCE TO FACE THE CHALLENGES OF FREE IMPORTS AND

54.1  Small Scale Industries (SSIs) including tiny units in India have enough competitive strength to withstand the impact of free imports

57.0  POLICY OF RESERVATION
57.1  Small scale sector manufactures more than 7500 items. Out of that only 605 items are reserved for exclusive manufacture in small
scale sector. These items mainly are labour oriented with very less investment and low technological requirements. Most of these items
are produced in self employment ventures and do not attract big players in the field. However, some hi-tech export oriented items like
hosiery, hand tools, drugs & pharmaceutical and stationery have been provided with a higher investment limit in plant and machinery, i.e.
upto rupees five crores to enable them to meet the requirements of technology upgradation and face the global and domestic competition.
As such, the reservation policy does not discourage investment. On the contrary the policy is instrumental in employment generation and
meeting the concern of regional imbalances. Where ever it is felt that the reservation of any particular item needs to be dispensed with in
the interest of growth and development of the small scale sector, the issue is discussed with the stake holders and the respective
administrative Ministries, and depending upon the general opinion, the item is de-reserved with the recommendation of the Advisory
Committee constituted under Section 29B of Industries (Development & Regulation) Act, 1951. Accordingly, the list of reserved items
have come down from 873 in 1984 to 605 as on date. There is a proposal to dereserve 108 more items during this year as announced in
the Budget Speech 2005-06, which will further shorten the list of reserved items.
57.2  The Committee hopes that the de-reservation of 108 items, as announced in the Budget (2005-06) would help the small scale
sector to meet the demand for technological upgradation, encourage research and development and make the sector competitive
to face the domestic and global competition. A status report on the practical implication in this regards may be furnished to the
Committee in its Action Taken Notes of the present Report.

62.0  CONCEPT OF FACTORING SERVICES FOR SSI SECTOR

the SSI Sector.

effective role as an alternate mode of working capital finance for the past three decades in some of the countries. The Committee
feels that the Factoring Services Legislation would also help to mitigate the problem of delayed payments to SSI units and
augment the flow of funds to the SSI Sector. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of SSI should also pursue
the proposed Act on “The Factoring of Debts due to Industrial and Commercial Undertakings” with Ministry of Finance
(Banking Division).

            (vii)   Draft of a new “Promotional Package for Small Enterprises” was formulated. This would include measures to improve
credit, incentives for technology upgradation, infrastructural and marketing facilities, etc.
72.3  The Committee recommends that for streamlining and strengthening the contribution of small enterprises sector in the
national economy, the Government should focus the following strategy:
            (i)     Providing full credit, technology upgradation, modernization and marketing support to the SSI sector,
            (ii)    Infrastructure upgradation in general and particularly in the major small enterprise clusters; these would include
road connectivity, regular supply of power, common effluent treatment facility and water supply, etc.
            (iii)   Reducing the rigours of complying with the plethora of rules and regulations applicable in the small enterprises
sector, which is known as Inspector Raj.
            (iv)   Full support to the service industry to fulfill its true growth and employment generation potential,
            (v)    Training of existing and potential entrepreneurs and workers on a large scale in modern management practices and
skill upgradation through advanced training facilities for capacity building in the small enterprises sector.
74.0  ACTION PLAN FOR THE YEAR 2005-06
74.1  The Action Plan for the Ministry for the year 2005-06 is as under : -
            (i)     Formulation of a single legislation, i.e. Small & Medium Enterprises Development Bill to promote the growth of the sector
and free it from ‘Inspector Raj’
            (ii)     Formulation of a Promotional Package for the SSI sector
            (iii)    Examination of problems facing Enterprises in the Unorganised/Informal Sector through the National Commission set up
for this purpose
            (iv)    Facilitate credit flow to the SSI Sector - by implementing the Performance and Credit Rating Scheme for Small Enterprises,
through NSIC
            (v)    Infrastructure upgradation in major industrial clusters
            (vi)    Institutional  reforms in terms of: (i) Decentralisation; (ii) Simplification; (iii) Transparency; (iv) Accountability; and (v) E-
governance.
74.2  The Committee desires that as a precautionary measure to achieve the Action Plan for the year 2005-06, the Ministry of SSI
should review and monitor the progress of the work closely. The functioning of organisations under the Ministry, implementation
of Plan schemes and various developmental activities should be reviewed at various levels regularly. The Monitoring mechanism
to oversee the functioning of various organizations under the Ministry of Small Scale Industries should be made more effective.

CREDIT GUARANTEE SCHEME
         The Committee understands that one of the main objectives of the scheme is to generate more employment opportunities by
facilitating collateral free loans to small enterprises. However, despite the provision of the loans up to 25.00 lakh without
collateral/third party guarantee and the guidelines of the Reserve Bank of India thereto, Banks are reported to insist on the
collateral security and the third party guarantee. The committee has repeatedly recommended for a change of the mind set of the
Branch Manager, nothing fruitful has come out so far. The Committee directs all the Banks who are Members of this scheme to
implement the scheme so as to make it success. (Para 42.3)
         The Committee takes note that under Major Head 2851 Credit Guarantee Scheme the Plan Outlay has been increased to
the tune of Rs. 180.00 crore in BE 2005-06 in comparison to 
Rs. 176.29 crore in RE 2004-05. The provision made under the scheme is for contribution to the Corpus Fund. The envisaged
level of corpus at the end of Tenth Five Year Plan is of Rs.2500 crore. But with a Plan outlay of Rs.900 crore in the Tenth Plan
and Rs.200 crore released during the Ninth Plan, the envisaged level of Rs.2500 crore cannot be achieved. The Committee is
anguished to observe that plan allocation is not made as originally conceived under the scheme. Therefore, in order to enhance
the comfort level of MLIs participating in the scheme, it is desirable that the corpus is enhanced to the envisaged level. With the
building of adequate corpus fund, the fears of MLIs could be allayed as still some of the banks have reservations about the
scheme because of their bad experience in the past with a similar scheme operated by the
RBI.                                                                                                            (Para 42.5)
         The Committee recommends that monitoring mechanism comprising all the Money Lending Institutes (MLIs) at the
regional level and the Directorate of Industries of the respective States, to review the progress made under the Credit Guarantee
Scheme, periodically should be put into place for improving the monitoring mechanism for successful implementation of the
projects undertaken with the aid of credit Guarantee Scheme.          (Para 42.8)

Sikkim should be facilitated with focused entrepreneurship development programmes, technical training & skill up-gradation,
awareness of industrial opportunities for manufacturing in NER & Sikkim and supplying to the rest of the country, relatively
softer credit and marketing assistance programmes. (Para 49.2)

ABID HUSSAIN COMMITTEE REPORT
         The Committee was given an understand that recommendations of Abid Hussain Committee Report are still relevant for
the promotion of small and medium enterprises, as it has provided a road map for making the small enterprise able to grow on
its own and graduate in to medium enterprise. It recommended for single law of small enterprise, limited partnership act and

CONCEPT OF FACTORING SERVICES FOR SSI SECTOR
         The Committee endorses the viewpoint of the Ministry of SSI that factoring services would provide instant liquidity to
industries, particularly to small scale and tiny sectors, prior to the maturity period of various instruments drawn for supply or
purchase of goods or services by assignment of debts or receivables to a factor. Since the Factoring services had played an
effective role as an alternate mode of working capital finance for the past three decades in some of the countries. The Committee
feels that the Factoring Services Legislation would also help to mitigate the problem of delayed payments to SSI units and
augment the flow of funds to the SSI Sector. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of SSI should also pursue
the proposed Act on “The Factoring of Debts due to Industrial and Commercial Undertakings” with Ministry of Finance
(Banking Division).                                                (Para 62.3)

STRENGTHENING OF SMALL INDUSTRIES DEVELOPMENT BANK OF INDIA  (SIDBI) FOR EXTENDING FINANCIAL
AND SUPPORT SERVICES TO SSIS
         The Committee appreciates the role of SIDBI in taking various strategic initiatives to improve the flow of credit to the SSI

         The Committee recommends that for streamlining and strengthening the contribution of small enterprises sector in the
