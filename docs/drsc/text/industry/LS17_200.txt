1.0       INTRODUCTION
 
1.1       The small scale industries (SSI) sector constitute one of the vibrant sectors of the Indian economy in terms of
employment generation; the strong entrepreneurial base it helps to create and; its share in industrial production and
exports.  The Ministry of SSI designs policies, programmes , projects and schemes in consultation with its organizations
and various stakeholders and monitors their implementation with a view to assisting   the promotion and growth of small
scale industries. The Ministry also performs the function of policy advocacy on behalf of the SSI sector with other
Ministries/Departments of the Central Government and the State and Union Territories.  Taking into account the high
potential for growth in the SSI sector in terms of output, employment and exports, the role of the Ministry of Small
Scale Industries is to strengthen the SSI sector, to enable it to remain competitive in market-led economy and generate
additional employment opportunities. For achieving these objectives, the endeavor of the Ministry is to provide the SSI
sector proper and timely inputs like:
 
(i)
               adequate credit from financial institutions/banks;
(ii)             funds for technology upgradation and modernization;
(iii)            adequate infrastructural facilities;
(iv)           modern testing facilities and quality certification laboratories;
(v)             modern management practices and skill upgradation through advanced training    facilities;
(vi)           marketing assistance; and
(vii)          level playing field at par with the large industries sector.
 
1.2       Worldwide, the Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) are recognized as an “engine of growth”. In our country
however, it is necessary to refer to the “Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises” (MSMEs) on account of the significance
of the micro enterprises, both in the total population of MSME and in the economy as a whole. The process of
globalisation has, on the one hand, opened the gateways of opportunities to the MSME: of closer interactions and access
to much larger markets, to name only a few. It has, on the other hand, exposed the segment, particularly the micro and
small enterprises (MSEs) in the country, to the challenges of intense competition, both domestic and global.
 
1.3       The contribution of this segment to the economic sinews of the country is no less significant. Nearly 39 per cent
of the gross manufacturing output and 34 per cent of the exports of the nation arise from these enterprises.  The
estimated number of MSE units in the country is 123.42 lakh of which 18.71 lakh are registered and 104.71 lakh
unregistered. At the end of 2005-06, total production of the MSEs was estimated at Rs. 4, 76,201 crore at current prices
and Rs. 2, 77,668 crore at constant (1993-94) prices.  During the first four years of the Tenth Plan, the output of the
segment has recorded a real growth rate of 8.87 per cent annually. Over six thousand products are manufactured by the
MSEs, they include several sophisticated items used in high technology areas like nuclear power, missile and space
programmes, information technology, biotechnology, etc. The level of exports by this segment also testifies to its overall
competitiveness in the global markets.

1.5       By enacting the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006, one of the needs felt and
articulated by this segment for long has been met. This Act seeks to facilitate promotion and development and
enhancing competitiveness of these enterprises. It provides the first-ever legal framework for recognition of the concept
of “enterprise” (comprising both manufacturing and service entities), definition of medium enterprises and integrating
the three tiers of these enterprises, namely, micro, small and medium.
 
1.6       The Government has announced a Policy Package for stepping up Credit to Small and Medium Enterprises
assuring, inter alia, a 20 per cent year-on-year growth in credit flow. The flow of net bank credit has, as a result,
increased considerably during the first year of implementation of the Package.
 
1.7       The Package for Promotion of Micro and Small Enterprises has also been approved. This Package, in turn,
covers further legislative measures (viz., a law on limited liability partnerships, the bill for which has been introduced in
Parliament), credit support, fiscal support, support for cluster based development, technology and quality upgradation
support, support for entrepreneurial and managerial development, empowerment of women owned enterprises,
strengthening of Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) and strengthening of data base for MSME Sector. The
measures in the Package would run concurrently with the Eleventh Plan.
           
1.8       In the National Manufacturing Strategy adopted by the National Manufacturing Competitiveness Council
(NMCC), it has been concluded that to increase the share of manufacturing in the gross domestic product and to
accordingly enhance competitiveness of the Indian manufacturing sector, particular attention needs to be paid to the
small and medium (manufacturing) enterprises. 
 
1.9       The Tenth Plan recognized the vital role of the SSI segment in the process of industrialization, regional spread of
industry and employment generation. Ministry of SSI informed that  there was no major policy shift for the year 2007-
08 as compared to 2006-   -07.
 
1.10     This trend is expected to continue during the current year (2007-08). In consonance with the past trend, for the
Eleventh Five Year Plan also, a higher growth rate for the SME sector has been projected as compared with both the
industrial sector as a whole and the manufacturing sector.
 
1.11     With the aim of improving manufacturing competitiveness of the segment, the launching of a National
Manufacturing Competitiveness Programme (NMCP) has been announced. The Planning Commission has given in-
principle approval for implementation of the NMCP during the 11
th Five Year Plan. The schemes under this Programme
will be implemented mainly in the Public-Private Partnership mode.
 
1.12     An Empowered Group of Ministers (EGoM) under the chairmanship of the External Affairs Minister has
recently been constituted to lay down the comprehensive policy for cluster development and oversee its implementation.
The Ministry of Small Scale Industries will service the EGoM.
 
1.13     Under the Credit Guarantee Scheme, life insurance cover for Chief Promoters of units provided guarantee cover
by the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Small Industries (CGTSI) has been introduced.  Further, the one-time guarantee
fee under the scheme has been reduced from 2.5 per cent to 1.5 per cent w.e.f.1

1.16     The Committee notes the trend of growth of SSI sector vis-a-vis Industrial sector.  During 2005-06 the Micro
and Small enterprises sector registered a growth rate of 12.3 per cent as against a growth rate of 8.1 per cent for the
overall industrial sector, showing better performance of SSI sector.  The Committee impresses upon the Government to
realize the importance of SSI sector which still has large potential to grow. The Committee solicits all out support, from
all the concerned agencies specially the financial institutions to come forward and provide their best services to this
sector for its overall growth and recommends the Ministry to continue its endeavor to bring together all the agencies so
as to ensure tapping the growth potential of the SSI sector in the country.
 
1.17     Considering that the policy package announced for stepping up of credit to SME sector by providing 20% year
on year growth in credit flow is a positive step, still much remains to be done.  The Committee is constrained to observe
that number of SSI accounts, have drastically gone down from 29.64 lakh in 1998 to 16.33 lakh in 2003.  However, a
slight rise to 18.85 lakh in 2006 is no solace.  Similarly the credit in terms of percentage of Net Bank Credit (NBC) to
SSI has also witnessed the same fate.  It went down from 17.5 % of NBC to 8.1% in 2006.   The Committee is of the
considered opinion that the credit to SSI in terms of percentage of NBC should also not be less than 20% and therefore
calls upon the Ministry of SSI and Ministry of Finance, RBI and the Banks to be more considerate   to SSIs for their
growth and prosperity in the national interest.
 
 
1.18     The Committee desires that the Ministry of SSI should undertake requisite steps  to enable the Micro, Small and
Medium Enterprises  to imbibe the potential to drive  the bases of growth rate, large number of employment-generation
and enhancing to national exchequer, combating the problem of poverty and improving the standard of living hence
contributing to the National Economy.
 
1.19.    The Committee however, endorses the following initiatives of Ministry of SSI :-
 
(i)
                  Providing requisite credit, technology upgradation, modernization and marketing support to the SSI sector;
(ii)                Infrastructure upgradation in general and particularly in the major small enterprises clusters; these would
include road connectivity, regular supply of power, common effluent treatment facility and water supply, etc.
(iii)               Reduction in the plethora of rules and regulations presently applicable to the micro and small enterprises
sector;
(iv)              Providing necessary support to the service industry to fulfill its growth and employment generation potential;
and
(v)                Providing training to existing and potential entrepreneurs and workers on an extensive scale and facilitating
modern management practices and skill upgradation through advanced training facilities for capacity building in
the small enterprises sector.
 
1.20     Ministry of SSI informed the Committee that the Action Plan for the Ministry for the year 2007-08 provides for
implementation of the Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises Development (SMED) Act, 2006 to promote the growth of
the sector; implementation of Promotional Package announced for the SSI sector; examination of problems facing
Enterprises in the Unorganized / Informal Sector through the National Commission set up for this purpose; facilitate
credit flow to the SSI Sector – by implementing the Performance and credit Rating Scheme for Small Enterprises,
through NSIC; and; infrastructure up gradation in major industrial clusters.  As a precautionary measure to achieve the
Action Plan constant review and monitoring of the Action Plan is being done.
 
1.21     The Committee feels these initiatives need to be translated in to result oriented actions.  The Committee hopes

3.0       DEVELOPMENT COMMISSIONER FOR SMALL SCALE INDUSTRIES
 
3.1       The Office of the Development Commissioner, Small Scale Industries, DC(SSI)  is an attached office of the
Ministry of Small Scale Industries and performs a dual role with regard to the promotion and development of small
enterprises. On the one hand, it advised the Government on formulation of appropriate policies, schemes, projects and
programmes and on the other, implements these policies, schemes, etc. for the overall development of the small
enterprises.
 
3.2       The Ministry informed that in the changing scenario of liberalization and globalization, the role of the Office of
DC (SSI) is in the process of being reoriented, broadly on the following lines:
 
(i)         The Office of the DC (SSI) would need to be equipped and strengthened to view the sector as a
composite whole, without losing the focus on the need for generation of employment as well as the
specific developmental/promotional requirements and inter-linkages of each distinct component of the
composite sector and the products and services produced by them.
(ii)        To address the issues of liberalization and globalization, the Ofiice of DC (SSI) need to widen and
continuously update its knowledge base regarding global trends in policies and Government interventions
as well as ascertain ‘best practices’ in this field, within the country and abroad, and appropriately adopt
them.
(iii)       It would need to strengthen its linkages with the State Governments, other Central
Ministries/Departments and associations of SMEs to achieve the objectives more effectively.
(iv)       Finally , it would need to forge stronger links with the relevant international institutions and bilateral
organizations for more effective cooperation so as to derive full advantage of the comparative strengths
of the Indian SME sector.
 
3.3       The Committee also feels that DC’s office needs to  be reoriented in view of changing scenario of the day.  The
Committee desires that a time frame be set up for doing so and ensure that DC’s office successfully implements the
policies and programmes for promotion and development of SSIs.  It’s role remains incomplete if it does not provide

marketing, credit rating, acquisition of technologies, adoption of improved management practices, etc. The budgetary
support through grant is to part-finance its promotional activities and services.

4.6       Market Assistance Scheme is meant for promotion and development of markets for the products of micro and

products.  According to the Outcome budget of the Ministry of SSI, participation in these events would provide micro
and small enterprises an exposure to national and international markets.   The Committee is of the view that organizing
buyer-seller meetings will enhance venture development skills and improve marketing of products and  market
promotion seminars will create awareness of various plans and schemes.  The Committee desires that   intensive
campaigns are required to be organised in both rural and urban areas to have maximum reach. 
 
4.7       The Committee notes with concern that allocation for NE  region towards, the grant meant for credit rating
scheme has been reduced to Rs. 0.50 crore as against Rs. 2.00 crore in 2006-07.  The Committee feels it may discourage
implementation of this scheme.  The Committee desires that Government should accord due importance to it as has been
done in rest of the country.
 
4.8       The Committee notes that NSIC suffers from  surplus manpower at lower levels, which possesses inadequate
skills to operate the schemes of the Corporation that not only lead  to high overheads but also does not allow to employ
/induct professional staff in required areas like marketing, technology and other vital business development areas.   The
Committee feels that Corporation has to shed its manpower particularly in the lower levels so as to make it a
commercial organisation capable enough to withstand the competitive atmosphere. 
 
4.9       The Committee also feels that NSIC has to enlarge its resource base with a view to serve small scale sector and
compete with other institutions and Banks engaged in financing the small sector.  The Committee desires that Ministry
and NSIC may find out the ways to enable the Corporation to compete with other financial institutions.
 
4.10     The Committee observes that though NSIC is expected to play a pivotal role in area of marketing, the bulk
buyers from the Government Departments/Agencies are not responsive in matters of making purchases through the
small scale units registered under NSIC Single Point Registration Scheme.
 
4.11     The Committee therefore, recommends that the Government Departments/Agencies should declare NSIC a nodal
agency for purchases. This will enable NSIC to play an effective role in marketing the products of the micro and  small
enterprises.  The Committee also desires  the Ministry of  SSI to pursue with all the Government Departments and
agencies to declare NSIC nodal agency for purchases.  It will not only make NSIC to play effective role in marketing the
products of micro and small industries but also may generate additional demands for products of SSI units.

9.0       CREDIT LINKED CAPITAL SUBSIDY SCHEME

9.1       The Ministry of Small Scale Industries (SSI) is operating a scheme for technology upgradation of micro, tiny and
small enterprises called the Credit Linked Capital Subsidy Scheme (CLCSS). The Scheme aims at facilitating
technology upgradation by providing   upfront capital subsidy to  small scale units, including tiny, khadi, village and
coir industrial units, on institutional finance (credit) availed of by them for modernisation  of their production equipment
(plant and machinery) and techniques.  The Scheme (pre-revised) provided for 12 percent capital subsidy to SSI units,
including tiny units, on institutional finance availed of by them for induction of well established and improved
technology in selected sub-sectors/products approved under the Scheme. The eligible amount of subsidy calculated
under the pre-revised scheme was based on the actual loan amount not exceeding Rs.40 lakh.  The CLCSS has been
revised with effect from September 29, 2005.  The revised scheme, inter-alia, include raising of ceiling on loans from 
Rs. 40 lakh to Rs. 1 crore and the  rate of subsidy from 12 per cent to 15 per cent.  Presently, the scheme is in operation
upto March 31, 2007 or till the time sanction of aggregate capital subsidy disbursed by the Nodal Agencies/Banks
reaches to Rs. 600 crore, whichever is earlier.  The amount of subsidy disbursed between 2001-02 to 2005-06 is
approximately Rs.36 crore. However, most of it has been disbursed after the revision of the scheme. During the year
2006-07, approximately Rs. 60.0 crore has already been disbursed till end February 2007.  Thus, the scheme has gained
momentum after the revision of the scheme, i.e., from 29.09.2005
           
9.2       The target groups for the scheme are SSI units, including tiny and micro units.  The scope of the scheme has
been widened to include khadi, village and coir industrial products (falling under the purview of the Ministry of ARI).
 
9.3       The basis for the selection of the target group is that the scheme has primarily been conceived for providing
subsidy to the small and micro enterprises in the country.  However, industry  graduating from small scale to medium
scale on account of sanction of additional loan under CLCSS are also eligible for assistance under the scheme. Thus the
scheme will help SMEs in upgrading their technology and remaining competitive in the global market.  
           
9.4       The scheme is implemented through eligible Primary Lending Institutions (PLIs) which include Scheduled  
Commercial  Banks , Scheduled Cooperative Banks [including urban cooperative banks co-opted by the SIDBI under
the TUFS of the Ministry of Textiles], Regional Rural Banks (RRBs),  State Financial Corporations (SFCs) and North
Eastern Development Financial Institution (NEDFI).  The capital subsidy is released to Nodal Banks/Agencies for on-
ward disbursements to the beneficiary units through eligible PLIs.  An advance of Rs. 5 crore and Rs. 3 crore has been
placed with SIDBI and NABARD respectively for operationalising the scheme. Besides, an advance of Rs. 1 crore each
has also been placed with the Public Sector Banks/Government Agencies, which have been inducted as additional nodal
banks/agencies for disbursement of subsidy by them.   The funds utilized by these Agencies towards disbursement of
subsidy under the CLCSS is replenished by the Office of the DC(SSI) from the budgetary  provisions made for the
CLCSS.
 
 
10.0     The monitoring mechanism governing the scheme
 
10.1     The Governing and Technology Approval Board (GTAB) of the CLCSS monitor the scheme. The Secretary (SSI
& ARI) is the Chairperson of the Board and the Additional Secretary & Development Commissioner (SSI) is its
Member-Secretary. The GTAB periodically reviews the functioning of the scheme. There is a Technical Sub-Committee
(TSC) under the GTAB to consider inclusion of new sub-sectors/products and Well Established and Improved
Technologies under the Scheme. With the help of the existing monitoring mechanism, the scheme is implemented
effectively and its progress monitored regularly.  The progress of the implementation of the scheme has been boost up
with the effective working of the GTAB.  The GTAB, in its last meeting, decided to include 9 more Nodal
Banks/Agencies for implementation and release of subsidy directly   through them. The products/sub
sectors/technologies eligible for subsidy under this scheme is demand driven. Initially, the scheme was launched with
only 13 products/sub-sectors. However, over a period of time, 35 new products/sub-sectors have been included, making
it more broad-based for micro and small enterprises.

15.0     CREDIT SUPPORT PROGRAMME
 
15.1     The Credit Guarantee Scheme was launched with a view to alleviate the problems of collateral security faced by
the small enterprises, particularly first generation entrepreneurs, and to remove the impediments in flow of credit to
small enterprises sector.  It provides contribution to the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises
(CGTMSE) for providing Guarantee Cover to Commercial Banks for extending loans to small/tiny Units without any
collateral. Under this head Government will provide assistance to SIDBI to create Portfolio Risk Fund for credit
operations for micro enterprises under this scheme, assistance will be given for empowerment of Women Owned
Enterprises and Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribes and Minorities.  This year the Government provides Rs.190 crores
(including NE region) to provide guarantee cover for 30000 micro and small enterprises with a view to generate higher
confidence level of Banks for lending better credit flow to Small and Micro sector.
 
15.2     The main problem in the implementation of the scheme pertains to low level of awareness about the scheme
amongst the field level officers of MLIs, negative mindset of the MLIs due to greater volume of administrative work in
the small enterprises lending, bankers’ traditional/usual comfort with collateral security and perceived high risk for
providing loans to the small enterprises sector. These problems need to be seriously addressed by the management of the
banks,  by enhancing awareness about the scheme among the field level officers of MLIs through seminars/training,
progress under the scheme could be enhanced. Furthermore, raising of the Corpus Fund up to Rs.2500 crore would
increase the comfort level of participating MLIs and encourage them to avail the scheme on a much wider scale.
 
15.3     The Credit Guarantee Scheme aims to alleviate the problem of collateral security among the small enterprises,
particularly to tiny enterprises.  To cover the entire micro sector, which is defined as units having investment in plant
and machinery upto Rs.25 lakh, the loan limit under the scheme was set at Rs.25 lakh.  As announced in the ‘Package

15.6     The monitoring mechanism
 
15.7     The performance of the Credit Guarantee Scheme, being operated by CGTSI, is regularly monitored by the

Industry.  CGTSI also has interface with the banking industry through IBA Review Forum, which helps in streamlining

progress under the scheme by the banks are reviewed periodically in seminars and conferences organised by industry
associations, etc., which is attended by senior officers of the Ministry of SSI. The performance of MLIs participating in
the Credit Guarantee Scheme also comes up for review by the SLBCs, which are held on regular basis in various States
across the country.

the Scheme are in the tiny sector comprising of rural artisans, self-employed entrepreneurs, etc.  In fact, number-wise,

29.2     The reasons for reducing the plan outlay from Rs.3.30 crore in BE 2006-07 to Rs.1 crore in RE 2006-07 is due to
delayed response from the State Governments in the North East Region for the proposed Growth Poles.
 
 29.3    Presently a proposal for Kamrup region in Assam is under consideration of the Ministry of SSI for DPR for
which these funds are proposed to be utilized.
 
 29.4    The basic strategy to extend assistance in unorganized/ informal sectors will be finalized after the current
deliberations in the NCEUS on credit, marketing, raw materials,  conditions of work and livelihood, technology
upgradation are firmed up.
29.5     The NER and Sikkim have predominantly handlooms and handicrafts based enterprises and activities related to
food processing industries. Through the above interventions of Growth Poles the NCEUS proposes to increase the
productivity of these enterprises and give a fillip to skill formation and growth in these sectors in this region.
 
29.6     While a number of measures would be needed to be put in place for improving the outlook of unorganized/
informal sector in the Northeast region including Sikkim, the NCEUS’ proposal for establishment of Growth Poles
would serve an important function in ameliorating the conditions of the region.

OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS AND CONCLUSIONS  OF THE COMMITTEE-AT A GLANCE
 
 
1.0       INTRODUCTION
 
The Committee notes the trend of growth of SSI sector vis-a-vis Industrial sector.  During 2005-06 the Micro
and Small enterprises sector registered a growth rate of 12.3 per cent as against a growth rate of 8.1 per cent for the
overall industrial sector, showing better performance of SSI sector.  The Committee impresses upon the Government to
realize the importance of SSI sector which still has large potential to grow. The Committee solicits all out support, from
all the concerned agencies specially the financial institutions to come forward and provide their best services to this
sector for its overall growth and recommends the Ministry to continue its endeavor to bring together all the agencies so
as to ensure tapping the growth potential of the SSI sector in the country.
 
                                                                                                                        (Para 1.16)
 
Considering that the policy package announced for stepping up of credit to SME sector by providing 20% year
on year growth in credit flow is a positive step, still much remains to be done.  The Committee is constrained to observe
that number of SSI accounts, have drastically gone down from 29.64 lakh in 1998 to 16.33 lakh in 2003.  However, a
slight rise to 18.85 lakh in 2006 is no solace.  Similarly the credit in terms of percentage of Net Banking Credit (NBC)
to SSI has also witnessed the same fate.  It went down from 17.5 % of NBC to 8.1% in 2006.   The Committee is of the
considered opinion that the credit to SSI in terms of percentage of NBC should also not be less than 20% and therefore
calls upon the Ministry of SSI and Ministry of Finance, RBI and the Banks to be more considerate   to SSIs for their
growth and prosperity in the national interest.
           
(Para 1.17)
 
            The Committee desires that the Ministry of SSI should undertake requisite steps  to enable the Micro, Small and
Medium Enterprises  to imbibe the potential to drive  the bases of growth rate, large number of employment-generation
and enhancing to national exchequer, combating the problem of poverty and improving the standard of living hence
contributing to the National Economy.
    (Para 1.18)
 
            The Committee feels these initiatives need to be translated in to result oriented actions.  The Committee hopes
that action plan prepared by the Ministry of SSI will certainly take care of the fact.  The Committee would however, like
to see the progress in the ATR to be submitted by the Ministry of SSI.
 
 (Para 1.21)

20.0     PROVISION FOR THE PROJECTS/SCHEMES FOR THE BENEFIT OF    THE NORTH EASTERN

            The Committee is of the view that the  North Eastern Region including Sikkim have abundant natural resources
and potential for small and tiny units. These resources needs to be utilized for industrial development.   The absence of
motivated entrepreneurs has hampered the promotion and rapid industrialization. The growth of small scale industries in
the region has not kept pace with the growth of SSIs in other parts of the country. 
(Para 20.4)

(i)         Small Industries Services Institutes/Branch Institutes(SISIs/BIs) have been opened in all the North
Eastern States & Sikkim.
(ii)        Under the IID Scheme, Central Grant (maximum Rs. 4 crore) is provided to the Implementing Agency

States in India. 
(iii)       Under the Integrated Infrastructural Development Scheme, thirteen IID Centres have been sanctioned in
NE Region. 
(iv)       A Tool Room and Training Centre at Guwahati has been set up to design and manufacture tools, dyes,
zigs and fixtures etc. and to impart training in tool and dye manufacturing, use of computers in design
and manufacturing. 
(v)        Indian Institute of Entrepreneurship has been set up at Guwahati.
 
(Para 20.5)

utilization.  The Committee notes with concern the low utilization of funds.  The Committee was told that lack of
proposals from the States of NE Region and Sikkim was mainly responsible for low utilization.  The Committee impress
