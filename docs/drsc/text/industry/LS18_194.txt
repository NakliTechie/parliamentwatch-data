CHAPTER - I
 
RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS, WHICH HAVE BEEN ACCEPTED BY THE GOVERNMENT
 
Recommendation
 
Streamlining the ARI sector
 
The Committee understands that the Agro and Rural Industries need further reinforcing and strengthening through
streamlining the ARI sector and by ensuring timely policy interventions. The committee is of the considered view that
the contribution of this sector can be further consolidated and strengthened by ensuring timely and adequate delivery of
credit, technology upgradation, marketing support in the form of financial support for exhibitions, design intervention
and packaging, etc. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of ARI should make strive in this regard.
The committee foresees that   amendment in the Khadi and Village Industries Commission Act, 1956 would lead further
to streamlining and strengthening the activities in the KVI sector.

Action Taken
 
Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries appreciates the recommendations of the Committee. Most of the credit flow to the
agro & rural industries is through banks. This is further supported by way of margin money subsidy under the credit
linked subsidy schemes of Rural Employment Generation Programme (REGP) and Prime Minister’s Rozgar Yojana
(PMRY). Under these schemes, loans are provided to the prospective entrepreneurs by banks after assessment of the
project reports and the margin money part of the project is borne by the Government through its budgetary allocations.
 
In order to improve credit flow to agro and rural industries sector, KVIC holds regular review meetings with banks to
ensure achievement of targets fixed under REGP. In addition, the following steps have been taken for improving credit
flow under the PMRY:
 
Reiteration of RBI guidelines on keeping interest rates at PLR or less.
Interaction with State Governments, RBI and Banks to reduce gaps  between sanctions and disbursements.
Issue of instructions by RBI to the banks to avoid under-financing of projects.
Similarly, the Ministry of SSI has taken up from time to time the issue of improving credit flow to the SSI sector with
the RBI. These issues include taking up measures like increase in the limit of exemption from collateral security, setting
of targets for growth in advance, opening of specialised SSI banks, time-frame for disposal of loan applications, etc.
These measures have also helped in the improved credit flow to the ARI sector.
Further to above, creation of an independent Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries has reinforced and strengthened the
functioning of the ARI sector as a whole, by highlighting its present status and ensuring timely policy interventions
needed. The contribution of this sector can be further streamlined and strengthened by ensuring timely and adequate
delivery of credit, technology upgradation, marketing support in the form of financial support for exhibitions, design
intervention and packaging, etc. Further, to streamline and strengthen the activities in the KVI sector, the Khadi and
Village Industries Commission Act, 1956 has been amended.
 
Recommendation
 
Monitoring mechanism
 
The Committee finds that the monitoring mechanism at the Ministry level includes both structured as well as informal
but regular monitoring. Secretary (ARI) periodically monitors implementation of PMRY and REGP along with other
schemes being implemented by the Ministry.  Secretary (ARI) also monitors the implementation of the National
Common Minimum Programme. The Committee believes that the ministry of ARI would achieve the targets set in the
Action Plan for the year 2006-07 without any hindrances.(Para : 4.2)
Action Taken
The performance of the schemes being implemented by KVIC, Coir Board and PMRY and the declaration made in
National Common Minimum Programme are reviewed regularly at the level of Secretary in the Ministry. KVIC and
Coir Board are also reviewing the performances of their respective schemes at their level on regular basis. On the basis
of monthly and quarterly performances of progress in various sectors of development, appropriate follow up actions are
taken by the KVIC, the Coir Board and the State Governments.
 
With these efforts, in 2004-05, against the target of generating 5.25 lakh additional job opportunities under Rural
Employment Generation Programme (REGP), 5.30 lakh job opportunities were generated in the country. During 2005-
06, 5.68 lakh jobs opportunities were created against the target of 5.50 lakh. Thus, during 2004-05, and 2005-06, the
achievement under REGP has crossed the annual target. 
 
Similarly under PMRY, as per the data made available by the RBI, there has been significant improvement in the
implementation of the yojana in 2004-05 and 2005-06. As per the data made available by RBI, in 2004-05, against the
target of generating 3.75 lakh additional job opportunities under PMRY, 3.72 lakh additional job opportunities were
generated in the country and in 2005-06, 3.87 lakh jobs opportunities were created against the target of 3.75 lakh.

Recommendation
Economic philosophy behind the budget proposals
 
The Committee is in agreement with the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Ministry of ARI for
the year  2006-07 i.e. the Poverty alleviation and employment generation as  the keystone of the economic policy. The
Committee is of the view that fast growing India must invest in society, therefore, the purpose and policy of budgeting
should be aimed at widening the social safety net, as higher the economic development of a nation the greater the need
for a safety net. (Para : 7.2)
 
Action Taken
 
The economic philosophy behind the budget proposals (2006-07) pertaining to Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries
conform to the needs of   on-going liberalization and globalization process. The liberalization of economy has enhanced
the competitive nature of the whole industrial set up in the country. Open market mechanisms have come in place of
regulated and protected nature of pre-liberalised economy. The challenge posed by globalised economy has enhanced
and enabled nurturing of the inherent competitive and resilient nature of the agro and rural industries sector. This sector
mainly consists of khadi, village industries, cottage industries (handicrafts), and coir, tiny and micro enterprises. The
products of this sector by their very nature are traditional, location specific and exclusively indigenous. This adds to the
specialized and exclusive nature of the sector as such and gives it a dynamic and resilient character. For example, herbal
products, organic manure, organic food, honey production, traditional crafts items, etc are so exclusive by nature that it
offers immense export potential which is almost noncompetitive in the world economy. The emphasis would be to
highlight the exclusivity of ARI products and its suitability to specific local area both for production as well as
marketing as it addresses the felt local needs and to develop quality diversification and aggressively enter the world
market. At the same time steps have been taken to continue and increase the social safety net through schemes such as
Khadi Karigar Janshree Beema Yojana, Shiksha Sahayog Yojana, Artisans Welfare Fund and a Welfare Fund for Coir
Workers.
 
Recommendation
 
Economic Philosophy behind the Budget proposals (2006-07)
 
The Committee is pleased to note that the economic philosophy behind the Budget proposals (2006-07) pertaining to
Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries conform to the logistic of on-going liberalization and globalization process. The
Committee feels that in the euphoria of the rapid globalization humane economic consideration and approach remains at
the backburner, but, in the ideal situation as the growth gathers momentum, sooner or later, enough of it must trickle
down the poor. The Committee hopes that implementation of the budget proposals for the year would lead to enabling
of the poor to access to minimum standard of life. (Para : 7.4)
 
Action Taken

Recommendation
 
Welfare measures and meeting  the social obligation
 
The Committee takes note of the economic philosophy of budgetary provision for this year aim at empowering the
people specially the poor. The Ministry have not yet indicated what special measure they are likely to take to give a
boost to their philosophy.  The increase in allocation for 2006-07 does not however give any indication to this effect as
it remains, more or less the same and it was in 2004-05 & 2005-06.  The Committee desires that the Ministry may
explain about this to the Committee. 
 
(Para : 8.3)
Action Taken
 
Compared to the initially approved outlay for X Plan which was Rs.2950 crore, the additional allocation under Annual
Plan till 2006-07 worked out to Rs.3584.46 crore which represented an increase of Rs.634.46 crore. Among the special
measures contemplated in 2006-07 are
the Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) which is a scheme for integrated development
of clusters in ARI sector.  The scheme would be taken up in selected clusters of traditional industries in various parts of
the country.  Under SFURTI, it is proposed to develop 100 clusters (25 clusters for khadi, 50 clusters for village
industries and 25 clusters for coir industry) over a period of five years commencing 2005-06.  The Scheme would cover
an estimated 50,000 beneficiary families. The objective of SFURTI is to establish a regenerated, holistic sustainable and
replicable model of integrated cluster-based development of traditional industries. The scheme has been sanctioned at a
total cost of Rs.97.25 crore. An amount of Rs.25.97 crore has been allocated in 2006-07 and Rs.13.03 crore has already
been sanctioned to KVIC and Coir Board. The clusters under KVI sector and Coir sector will come up in 2007-08 which
will be developed in an integrated manner and will involve as well as lead to capacity building, training, skill
development, product diversification, quality improvement, common facilities, marketing assistance and increased sales
of the products resulting ultimately in empowering the artisans/weavers/workers in these sectors.
The amount spent/being spent for welfare measures for the artisans/weavers/workers has increased from Rs.171 lakh in
2004-05 to Rs.352 lakh in 2005-06 in schemes such as Khadi Karigar Bima Yojana, Coir Workers Personal Accident
Insurance, Production Enhancement Linked Coir Workers Welfare Scheme, etc.
Ministry has projected a demand of Rs. 1346.14 crore in BE 2007-08 which is being pursued separately with Planning
Commission.

Under the Package for Promotion of Micro and Small Enterprises (MSE) it has been decided to strengthen PMRY and
the following modifications in the existing criteria of PMRY scheme are included:
 
(i)         Enhancement of the eligibility criteria of family income from Rs.40,000 per annum to Rs.1,00,000 per annum.

PMRY.

Plan and non-plan expenditure for the Coir Industries.
     
The Committee approves the provision for implementation of S&T Plan under the Major Head 2851 for the Coir Board,

Action Taken
 
The Board has been undertaking field demonstration on the research outcomes of Central Coir Research Institute
(CCRI)/Central Institute of Coir Technology(CICT) by deputing technical teams to different coir producing centers to
proliferate the technologies to promote the coir industry in the traditional and non-traditional zones.  Recently, the CCRI
has developed a technology for treatment of green husk coir fibre using a vegetable oil emulsion.   It is proposed to
popularise the technology through appropriate field demonstration programmes in both traditional and non-traditional
sectors.  Likewise, demonstration on the application of coir geo-textiles, composting of coir pith etc. are undertaken
regularly as part of the State Specific Action Plan through the regional set up.  For facilitating easy availability of pith
plus in various coir concentrated areas pilot scale laboratories for production of pith plus are being established in the
Regional Offices of the Board at Pollachi in Tamil Nadu, Bhubaneswar in Orissa, Visakhapatnam in Andhra Pradesh
and the Board’s Research Center CICT, Bangalore in Karnataka. Coir Board is also actively considering extension of
indigenously designed ‘Anupam’ and ‘Anugraha’ looms in the field for improving efficiency/productivity and reducing
drudgery of the workers.

Recommendation
 
Export performance of coir and coir products
 
Having gone through the information provided by the Ministry with respect to  export performance of Coir & Coir
products, the Committee is not satisfied on this account. The Committee although finds that   the growth has been
between 10 to 16 percent in terms of value, but the fact remains that majority of coir products are showing declining
trend. The Committee’s worry is how to stop this trend. The Committee impresses  upon the Ministry to apprise the
Committee of the reasons for down trend in products take coir matting, coir geo-textiles, rugs & carpet, Rubberized
Coir, yarn etc., measures taken by the Coir Board & Ministry for export promotion especially when the protected regime
is over. The Committee would like to know whether the allocation for 2005-06 were spent and the expenditure so
incurred achieved the desired result.  The Committee would further like to know the projection for 2006-07 and the
modus-operandi to achieve the target.  (Para : 16.7.7)
 
Action Taken
 
The overall export of coir and coir products during the last 5 years showed an increasing trend both in terms of quantity
and value. During the year 2005-06, 136027 MT of coir and coir products worth Rs. 595 crore  was exported from
India.  It was only Rs.322 crore during 2001-02. The handloom mats, powerloom mat, coir PVC  tufted mat, coir pith,
coir geotextiles, rubberized coir, coir fibre have shown an increasing trend both in quantity and value compared to the
export of previous years. The export of coir yarn, handloom matting, powerloom matting, coir rugs and carpets, etc.
have shown a decreasing trend both in terms of quantity and value.  The coir yarn being the basic raw material for the
value added products, have been showing a decreasing trend during the last 3 years.  In the importing countries, mainly
selected member countries of European Union, coir yarn gets converted to finished products.  Due to the liberalization
policies implemented by the Government of India, the coir industry in India adopted certain imported technology and
machinery for the production of high quality value added coir products  for export purpose (eg. Tufting process).  Due
to various reasons like spiralling wage level and manufacturing cost of imported raw material, the manufacture of coir
products in the importing countries had declined drastically. The machinery used for production of coir products were
transferred to their counter part for setting up export oriented manufacturing units using coir yarn as the basic raw
material.  Sri Lanka is the only coir producing country in the world which is competing with India in the production of
coir yarn.  However, the export of value added products from Sri Lanka is not significant. Therefore, there is no
significant threat from Sri Lanka to the export of finished coir products from India.  However, the threat from China,
Brazil and Mexico in the floor covering market is on the increase particularly from jute, sisal and hemp products
originating from those countries.  In the case of coir geotextiles, the main competitors are Vietnam and Thailand,
besides Sri Lanka. 
 
The export of handloom matting, powerloom matting, coir rugs and carpets has been facing severe threat from other
natural and synthetic floor covering material which are available in the international market at competitive price,
attractive designs and patterns. The change in the concept of interior decoration as a consequence of increased usage of
attractive tiles and flooring materials also contributed to the low off take of such articles of coir.  Production of coir
floor covering blended with other natural fibre materials with attractive patterns and designs at competitive price is now
attempted.  It is also proposed to establish a design clinic exclusively for the coir and coir blended materials as a part of

The Ministry has reviewed the programmes under its purview.  The Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional
Industries (SFURTI) is much more comprehensive scheme for integrated development of clusters in ARI sector.  The
scheme would be taken up in selected clusters of traditional industries in various parts of the country.  Under SFURTI, it
is proposed to develop 100 clusters (25 clusters for khadi, 50 clusters for village industries and 25 clusters for coir
industry) over a period of five years commencing 2005-06.  The Scheme would cover an estimated 50,000 beneficiary
families. The objective of SFURTI is to establish a regenerated, holistic sustainable and replicable model of integrated
cluster-based development of traditional industries. This will primarily mean:
 
more competitive traditional industries with more market-driven, productive, profitable and sustained employment for
the participants;
strengthened local socio-economic governance system of the industry clusters, with the active participation by the  local
stakeholders that can help to continue undertake development initiatives by themselves; and
 
building up innovated and traditional skills, improved technologies, advanced processes, market intelligence and new
models of public-private partnerships, so as to gradually replicate similar models of cluster-based regenerated traditional
industries.
 
            The SFURTI is a Central Sector Scheme to be implemented by the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries, without
any State share.  The various components of the scheme include replacement of charkhas and looms for increasing the
productivity of khadi and weavers, Common Facility Centres (CFC) for ready made warp units for supply of ready-to-
use warps for weaving, post-loom facilities, viz., dyeing and printing, setting up composite ready made garment
manufacturing facilities to convert fabrics into ready-to-wear khadi products, product testing laboratory to enforce
quality standards,service and maintenance, processing and manufacture of village industry items like testing
equipment/laboratory to enforce quality standards specially for processed food items, service and maintenance and other
common infrastructure, etc., Product Development & Design Intervention for fabric designing for upholstery new
designs for high fashion garments, fabric development, embroidery in silk items, use of natural dyes, development of
new products specially for items like hand bags, shoes, new designs for pottery items; improved/new design for
packaging, specially for herbal and cosmetics, confectionery items, etc., Market Promotion Assistance for activities such
as organization of and participation in domestic/international exhibitions, participation in buyer-seller meets, renovation
and upgradation of marketing outlets, computerisation of sales outlets, bar-coding, etc.,  Capacity Building Measures for
undertaking following activities like exposure visits to other clusters and institutions (outside) to understand the self-
help actions, new products, technology, need based training within the clusters (related to global trade issues, skill
development, self-help, credit and other need based issues, support for establishment of cluster level networks (industry
associations) in the form of basic office infrastructure, including books, other need based support as emerge from the
diagnostic study, handholding support by NGOs/institutions of repute who would act as implementing agencies,
appointment of cluster development agents, who would work in the cluster for entire period of five years of cluster
development and technical support and guidance from reputed national level institutes having expertise in cluster
development such as Entrepreneurship Development Institute, Ahmedabad, NISIET, Hyderabad, IIE, Guwahati,
NIESBUD, Noida (UP).  While formulating the scheme of SFURTI, the Ministry of Finance and also the Planning
Commission had advised that “Schemes with overlapping objectives and coverage should be proposed for merger with
the new scheme to avoid duplication of effort, resources and responsibilities” .  Accordingly, considering the
comprehensive scope of SFURTI and the advise of Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission, it was decided to
subsume NPRI under SFURTI. However, a token allocation has been made for NPRI for 2006-07 to meet the

Recommendation
 
NATIONAL PROGRAMME FOR RURAL INDUSTRIALISATION
 
The Committee is of the view that National Programme on Rural Industrialisation was a unique programme aimed at
boosting  up the rural economy by creating more employment   avenues for the rural youth in the non-farm sector, and
arresting  the migration of the rural artisans to towns/cities, however, the performance under the NPRI Scheme has not
been very satisfactory due to meagre financial assistance of Rs 5.0 lakh per cluster for cluster-wise interventions, fill-in-
gaps arrangements, non-availability of credit-linked subsidy component, inadequate synergy with the
Ministries/departments with their own schemes in this area etc and the same has been  subsumed with the new Scheme
of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI). The Committee desires that the National Programme on
Rural Industrialisation should have continued with its set objectives, however, the Committee will wait and watch the
success of the new Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI).(Para : 19.5)
 
 
Action Taken
 
The Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) is a much more comprehensive scheme for
integrated development of clusters in ARI sector.  The scheme would be taken up in selected clusters of traditional
industries in various parts of the country.  Under SFURTI, it is proposed to develop 100 clusters (25 clusters for khadi,
50 clusters for village industries and 25 clusters for coir industry) over a period of five years commencing 2005-06.  The
Scheme would cover an estimated 50,000 beneficiary families. The objective of SFURTI is to establish a regenerated,
holistic sustainable and replicable model of integrated cluster-based development of traditional industries. This will
primarily mean:
 
more competitive traditional industries with more market-driven, productive, profitable and sustained employment for
the participants;
strengthened local socio-economic governance system of the industry clusters, with the active participation by the  local
stakeholders that can help to continue undertake development initiatives by themselves; and
 
building up innovated and traditional skills, improved technologies, advanced processes, market intelligence and new
models of public-private partnerships, so as to gradually replicate similar models of cluster-based regenerated traditional
industries.
 
            The SFURTI is a Central Sector Scheme to be implemented by the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries, without
any State share.  The various components of the scheme include replacement of charkhas and looms for increasing the
productivity of khadi and weavers, Common Facility Centres (CFC) for ready made warp units for supply of ready-to-
use warps for weaving, post-loom facilities, viz., dyeing and printing, setting up composite ready made garment
manufacturing facilities to convert fabrics into ready-to-wear khadi products, product testing laboratory to enforce
quality standards,service and maintenance, processing and manufacture of village industry items like testing
equipment/laboratory to enforce quality standards specially for processed food items, service and maintenance and other
common infrastructure, etc., Product Development & Design Intervention for fabric designing for upholstery new
designs for high fashion garments, fabric development, embroidery in silk items, use of natural dyes, development of
new products specially for items like hand bags, shoes, new designs for pottery items; improved/new design for
packaging, specially for herbal and cosmetics, confectionery items, etc., Market Promotion Assistance for activities such
as organization of and participation in domestic/international exhibitions, participation in buyer-seller meets, renovation
and upgradation of marketing outlets, computerisation of sales outlets, bar-coding, etc.,  Capacity Building Measures for
undertaking following activities like exposure visits to other clusters and institutions (outside) to understand the self-

194th Report of Committee On Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/194threport.htm[04-07-2016 13:06:04]
help actions, new products, technology, need based training within the clusters (related to global trade issues, skill
development, self-help, credit and other need based issues, support for establishment of cluster level networks (industry
associations) in the form of basic office infrastructure, including books, other need based support as emerge from the
diagnostic study, handholding support by NGOs/institutions of repute who would act as implementing agencies,
appointment of cluster development agents, who would work in the cluster for entire period of five years of cluster
development and technical support and guidance from reputed national level institutes having expertise in cluster
development such as Entrepreneurship Development Institute, Ahmedabad, NISIET, Hyderabad, IIE, Guwahati,

sanctioned) i.e. less than half of the target.   The Government is yet to do a lot to achieve targets set up in Tenth Plan. 
With this rate of achievements, The Committee apprehends that the Tenth Plan targets would not be achieved.   The
committee may be apprised as to how the Ministry is going to meet the targets and achieve the much proclaimed
mission of the Ministry to generate employment in the ARI Sector.(Para : 13.6.7)
 
Status of recovery of loans under PMRY

enough to enhance the rate of recovery.  The Committee calls for information, State wise for the last three years clearly
indicating the progress made owing to steps taken by Government in this regard.(Para : 13.7.3)

The Committee suggests that an Action Plan may be envisaged to make KVI as a mega source of employment
generation and poverty alleviation programmes. KVIC can cover larger size of employment in rural areas thereby help
to alleviate poverty provided larger flow of budgetary support in the form of margin money coupled with the

Working of REGP
 
The Committee feels that banks should strictly follow the guidelines issued by RBI and Credit Guarantee Trust Fund
with regards to requirement of collateral security on small loans and credit guarantee cover up to loans of Rs. 25 lakh.
Similarly, banks should charge lesser interst on the loans given under REGP. Further in order to attract the potential
entrepreneurs, workshops, awareness camps, exhibitions should be conducted jointly by KVIC, KVIBs and banks. In
order to overcome the problems faced in implementation of REGP, bankwise/districtwise targets must be communicated
to all field offices of KVIC in advance. Further industry group wise, social sector wise and financial range-wise targets
may be worked out for better effectiveness of the scheme keeping in view the target group.  Model targets should be
fixed for conducting village/taluka level awareness camps, district level workshops, state/national level exhibitions and
EDP training to all field offices.  Regular review meetings (zone-wise) with State Level Banker’s Committee (SLBC),
Lead Banks, KVIC and KVIBs would   ensure better implementation of the scheme.(Para : 18.9)
