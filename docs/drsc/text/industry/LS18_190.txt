Mandate of the Department of Heavy Industries
 
1.      Department of Heavy Industry is concerned with the development of Heavy Engineering/Electrical Industry, Auto Industry and administers 34 operating public sector enterprises.
 
2.      Department also administers two national level Institutes namely Fluid Control Research Institute at Palakkad and Automotive Research Association of India, Pune which cater to the needs of flow and
automotive Industry. This Department is also implementing a project on ‘National Automotive Testing and R&D infrastructure Project (NATRIP) through the NATIS, a society set up for this purpose.
 
3.
      Department of Heavy Industry has total 48 PSEs out of which 14 PSEs have either been closed or operations discontinued. Thus leaving the Department with 34 operating PSEs. Out of 34 operating PSEs,
13 PSEs made profit and remaining 21 made losses during the year 2005-06. The profit making PSEs made a profit of Rs.1578.55 crores and loss making PSEs made a loss of Rs. 1952 crores in 2005-06. The
anticipated overall loss for the year 2005-06 would be Rs. 373.45 crores.
 
4.
      However the Committee observes that of the aggregate profit of Rs. 1578.55 crores, the flagship BHEL alone earned a profit of Rs. 1472 crores. The Committee also recognizes that as per the target set by
DHI for year 2006-07, 9 more PSEs should start earning profit. And that the PSEs under DHI shall earn an overall profit of Rs. 497.86 crores as against the anticipated overall loss of Rs. 373.45 crores  in 2005-
06.

8.      The Committee was informed that the revival plans were being prepared in consultation with the PSEs as well as on recommendations of consultants appointed for this purpose. These revival plans are
further scrutinized by the Department and placed before BRPSE. BRPSE also makes a detailed appraisal and gives its recommendations after examining various projections made in the revival plan. Once the
revival plan is approved by the Government, an MOU is  signed with the management of the company based on the targets and expected revival in a specified time-frame. These MOU targets would be
reviewed regularly for monitoring and mid-course corrections which may be considered necessary.
 
9.
      The Committee observes that greater part of BRPSE approved Revival Plans includes waiver or conversion of previous loans and interest liabilities into equity and Government’s Guarantee for raising fresh
loan. However such Revival Plans do not elaborate other components that would be critical for sustainable revival like technology upgradation and absorption, enhancing marketing network, manpower training
etc. It may be recalled that previous revival efforts too pertained to similar cosmetic restructuring of financial liabilities and fresh assistance without realigning the PSEs to new market realities. As such previous
revival plans could not ensure the sustainability of revival. The Committee doubts whether these packages would be sustainable. The Committee impresses upon the Ministry of HI & PE to examine the
inclusion of these vital components in the revival packages  so that the Companies come out of protected regime and operate in the new market realities.
 
10.
  It however is  the considered view of the Committee that the revival plans pertained largely to financial reconstruction, and technology and marketing aspects are not contained in the revival plan approved
by the Government. As the nodal Department the DHI must monitor with the management of PSEs on these aspects of revival. The Committee would like to be informed of the steps taken by the DHI in this
regard.
 
11.
  The Committee cautions that DHI should bear the previous experiences in mind while assessing and ensuring the sustainability of revival. DHI  should  address the comprehensive restructuring of other
critical factors that have bearing over performance of a PSE. The Committee also suggests that the respective PSEs should be given financial targets to be achieved within a time frame.  
12.
  Moreover, in view of the details furnished by the DHI of the Planned capital expenditure,  the Committee wonders that such meager  infusion as the part of revival plan, would at all be sufficient to carry out
technological  upgradations and to realign  market network. The DHI may offer its comments on the issue.
 
13.
  As regards the cases of revival of such PSEs where Government approval was still pending, the Committee is informed thatlump sum provisions have been made in BE 2006-07 both on Plan as well as on
Non-plan side. The capital expenditure portion is likely to be met from the Plan allocations and other requirements  like loans for VRS, working capital etc. is to be met from the Non-plan side.
 
14.
  Regarding the Committee’s query whether PSEs were encouraged to raise resources by unlocking surplus assets and if Government had formulated any policy in this regard, the Committee was informed
that Government generally encourages PSEs to fund their revival plan from their own resources by either unlocking surplus assets or borrowings from banks/financial institutions. Government is providing
financial support only in those cases that neither have internal resources nor have the capacity to seek funds from external sources. However the Committee was not informed of any policy by the Government to
unlock the surplus assets of the PSEs. Though BRPSE had recommended 11 cases for the unlocking of surplus assets. 
 
15.
  The Committee strongly feels that the Government should come out with a transparent policy for unlocking of surplus assetss by the PSEs. The Committee feels that such a policy need to be uniform in
respect of all the CPSEs which fall under various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. If necessary the concerned State Government may be consulted before the formation of the policy on this

30.  It was further elaborated by the DHI that for the year 2006-07, the major Non-Plan provisions of DHI are of Rs.150 crore for ‘Implementation of revival schemes of PSEs’ and Rs.250 crore for ‘restructuring
of PSEs in Department of Heavy Industry’.  The former provision would be mainly utilized with the approval of the Cabinet/CCEA for payment of salary & wages in sick and loss making PSEs. 
Comments/consent of Planning Commission would be obtained on the proposals.  Similarly in case of the latter provision of Rs.250 crore for VRS etc., the funds for VRS are to be provided for such VRS
schemes which would be/ have been approved by the Cabinet/ CCEA as a part of the revival schemes of PSEs.  In these cases also Planning Commission would be consulted while considering the restructuring
schemes.  Thus in cases of Non-plan expenditures also Planning Commission would be consulted.
31.
  As informed by the DHI the Non-Plan budgetary support of salary & wages and VRS & statutory dues are provided only to sick and loss making undertakings which are unable to meet these expenditures
and are not provided to commercially viable PSEs. Under the NCMP for revival of the sick PSEs, a Board for Reconstruction of PSEs had been set up and a number of revival schemes have been cleared or are
in the process of being cleared by this Board.  Provisions/expenditures would be on the higher side as the budgetary support/ fresh fund infusion would have to be extended under the revival schemes as these
are cleared by BRPSE and approved by the Cabinet/ CCEA.  Once these revival schemes are implemented and the sick PSEs are able to turn around, the budgetary support would consequentially get minimized.
 
32.
  The Committee observes that of the total BE ( 2006-07) of  Rs. 905 crores, Rs 455 crores are Non-Planned expenditures meant for committed expenses. Of this Rs. 400 crores are meant to settle VRS and
statutory dues, salaries and wages. Though these are loans to begin with but the Committee also notes the abysmal record of PSEs in respect of Loan Repayment. The Committee finds it alarming that this
situation has remained for years. Even during the FY 2005-06, it is evident that while the Planned expenditure was enhanced only by Rs. 94 crores, the Non-Planned expenditure was enhanced by Rs. 116
crores. The non-Planned expenditure was increased to meet the salary dues of the PSEs.
 
33.
  The Committee only wonders if the feeding the PSEs out of Budgetary support is sustainable practice.
 
34.
  The Committee is constrained to note that in the several revival packages offering non-planned budgetary support for wages, salaries, VRS and other statutory dues did enable the loss making PSEs to turn
around but only marginally and temporarily. This could have been due to faulty and partial implementation of lop-sided revival plans. The Committee therefore cautions the Government to ensure the viability
before approving the revival plan and also strictly monitor its implementation. The Committee should be apprised of monitoring mechanism and the additional measures for successful implementation.

OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE AT A GLANCE
 
The Committee observes that greater part of BRPSE approved Revival Plans includes waiver or conversion of previous loans and interest liabilities into equity and Government’s Guarantee for raising fresh
loan. However such Revival Plans do not elaborate other components that would be critical for sustainable revival like technology upgradation and absorption, enhancing marketing network, manpower training
etc. It may be recalled that previous revival efforts too pertained to similar cosmetic restructuring of financial liabilities and fresh assistance without realigning the PSEs to new market realities. As such previous
revival plans could not ensure the sustainability of revival. The Committee doubts whether these packages would be sustainable. The Committee impresses upon the Ministry of HI & PE to examine the
inclusion of these vital components in the revival packages so that the Companies come out of protected regime and operate in the new market realities.
(para : 9)
It however is the considered view of the Committee that the revival plans pertained largely to financial reconstruction, and technology and marketing aspects are not contained in the revival plan approved
by the Government. As the nodal Department the DHI must monitor with the management of PSEs on  these aspects of revival. The Committee would like to be informed of the steps taken by the DHI in this
regard.
(para : 10)
The Committee cautions that DHI should bear the previous experiences in mind while assessing and ensuring the sustainability of revival. DHI  should address the comprehensive restructuring of other
critical factors that have bearing over performance of a PSE. The Committee also suggests that the respective PSEs should be given financial targets to be achieved within a time frame.  
(para : 11)
Moreover, in view of the details furnished by the DHI of the Planned capital expenditure,  the Committee wonders that such meager  infusion as the part of revival plan, would at all be sufficient to carry out
technological  upgradations and to realign  market network. The DHI may offer its comments on the issue.
(para : 12)
The Committee strongly feels that the Government should come out with a transparent policy for unlocking of surplus assetss by the PSEs. The Committee feels that such a policy need to be uniform in
respect of all the CPSEs which fall under various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. If necessary the concerned State Government may be consulted before the formation of the policy on this
issue with a view to have broad consensus. The Committee considers that in absence of a transparent policy, it would be difficult for the PSEs to generate resources by unlocking the surplus assets as it may
involve administrative issues and avoidable litigations.
(para : 15)
The Committee observes that considering that the Planned Capital expenditure is vital for a sustainable revival, and survivability of the PSEs, of the total required outlay of Rs. 1350.22 crores, a Budgetary
support of only Rs 450 crores has been given in the BE 2006-07.  The Committee wonders whether the PSEs would be able to generate the remaining Rs. 950.22 crores from non-Budgetary sources. The
Committee also wonders if the BRPSE has identified the potential sources  to generate balance funds from extra Budgetary sources. The Committee would like to have the DHI’s comments on the possibility of
such shortfalls in budgetary allocations which may result in derailing the recommended revival of the PSEs.   
(para : 19)
The Committee observes that of the total BE ( 2006-07) of  Rs. 905 crores, Rs 455 crores are Non-Planned expenditures meant for committed expenses. Of this Rs. 400 crores are meant to settle VRS and
statutory dues, salaries and wages. Though these are loans to begin with but the Committee also notes the abysmal record of PSEs in respect of Loan Repayment. The Committee finds it alarming that this
situation has remained for years. Even during the FY 2005-06, it is evident that while the Planned expenditure was enhanced only by Rs. 94 crores, the Non-Planned expenditure was enhanced by Rs. 116
crores. The non-Planned expenditure was increased to meet the salary dues of the PSEs.
(para : 32)
The Committee only wonders if the feeding the PSEs out of Budgetary support is sustainable practice.
(para : 33)
The Committee is constrained to note that in the several revival packages offering non-planned budgetary support for wages, salaries, VRS and other statutory dues did enable the loss making PSEs to turn
around but only marginally and temporarily. This could have been due to faulty and partial implementation of lop-sided revival plans. The Committee therefore cautions the Government to ensure the viability
before approving the revival plan and also strictly monitor its implementation. The Committee should be apprised of monitoring mechanism and the additional measures for successful implementation.
(para : 34)
 
The Committee observes that the Non-Planned expenditure has been higher than Planned Expenditure for last several years. Meaning thereby that the expenditure is much higher on the committed liabilities
as compared to new developmental and capacity-building initiatives under Government sponsored schemes. Even the committed liabilities reflected under Non-Planned expenditure, the majority of expenditure
has been going to meet on year to year basis the financial obligations incurred by PSEs. In view of the definition of Capital expenditure as provided by the DHI, the Committee only wonders if the lumpsum
allocations of Rs 400 crores to support to clear VRS dues and salary dues to the workers could at all be termed as Capital expenditure. The Committee desires that DHI may clarify the position.
(para : 35)
 
The Committee has not been furnished any information regarding the criteria on which any expenditure is approved by the Planning Commission and is reflected as Planned Expenditure where as other bulk
expenditures are shown as Non-Planned expenditures. The Committee desires to know whether such a division is random or based on any objective criteria.
(para : 36)
 
To consider the Cess as private sector’s contribution would be misrepresentation of fact. The Committee would like to know the initial mandate for which Cess was levied, and if the NATRIP is covered

feels that budgetary constraints should not come in the way. The Committee suggests that DHI may find out the ways to associate industry to meet the funds.(para : 50)
The Committee takes note of elaborate but protracted decision making procedure. However the DPR, as submitted in January 2006, broadly indicates the extent of expenditure to be incurred in the project.
Considering the relevance of the technology demonstrator project, requisite finances should have been earmarked in the BE 2006-07. Here again the Committee finds priorities of DHI flawed. The Committee
accordingly considers that such R&D projects must be catered with long-term committed finances instead of piecemeal allocations based on expeditious requirements.
(para : 51)
The Committee expresses its concern over the delay in sanctioning the project and  would like to know the factors that caused the delay to which the allocation could not be utilized in previous financial
year. Furthermore, the Committee would like to know the basis on which the allocations have been enhanced this year despite the fact the funds remained unutilized last year.(para : 55)
The Committee wonders if the beneficiaries of the previous year would be repeated this year too. The Committee is unable to comprehend the rationale behind having twin policies on VRS dues, one
through the huge lumpsum allocation and also by subsidizing the PSEs for raising resources to settle VRS dues from market.
(para : 59)
The Committee however does not appreciate waiver of loan or interest on the loan given to a PSE. The Committee is not sure whether this aspect is taken into account while extending loans to PSEs. The
Committee desires to know the circumstances under which such loans or interests thereon are waived. The Committee directs the re-examination of the whole issue and DHI should furnish a detailed note on the
issue while submitting the ATN.
(para : 64)
The Committee finds  that in view of the past experience when the Lump Sum allocations in the BE stage have remained unutilized till the last quarter of Financial year. Even in case of lump sum provision
of Rs. 95 crore in RE 2005-06 for restructuring of PSEs, only Rs. 27 crores could be spent till March 2006. The Committee also observed that of this lumpsum amonu a sizable Rs. 44.60 crores were re-
appropriated as non-planned expediture towards CCI.  And that  Rs. 2.70 crores have been surrendered at the advice of Ministry of Finance. The Committee wonders if any worthwhile capacity building of PSEs
been done as part of intended sustainable restructuring. Also observing the smaller amount that have been allocated to individual PSEs for restructuring, the Committee only wonders if the amount could have
raised from the market with requisite subsidies from the Government. The Committee should be apprised if the amounts given out of this lumpsum provision are loans or grants to the individual PSEs.
(para : 69)
The Committee notes with surprise that Rs. 44.60 crores meant for restructuring of CCI as Planned expenditure were re-appropriated to Non-Plan expenditure. The Committee feels that such steps are ad-
hoc and lack any long term strategy for prudent financial management. The Committee asks DHI to clarify under which rule or guideline, such re-appropriation was sought.(para : 70)
Committee observes that the sheer quantum of such non-planned expenditures for revival suggests that revival packages have focused largely on clearing the financial dues which the PSEs owe. Compared
to which the Lumpsum allocations for the restructuring the PSEs under Planned Capital Expenditures has been meager Rs. 87.82 crores this year which has been reduced from Rs. 95 crores.(para : 80)
The Committee also observes that of Rs. 250 crores for VRS/VSS only Rs 201 crores could be spent till the first week of March 2006. Of Rs 150 crores allocated in BE 2005-06 for revival and further
enhanced to Rs 266 crores in Second Supplementary Grant, only Rs 142 crores were spent till the first week of March 2006 of which Rs. 124 crores were spent in the second quarter of the Financial Year 2005-
06. With such uneven appropriation throughout the year and inability to actually spent the allocated money  almost till  the end of the Financial Year, the Committee wonders if there was at all a need to seek an
enhancementof Rs.166 crores in Second Supplementary Demands. The Committee therefore desires that in the ATN, the DHI may furnish the details of the actual expenditure.(para : 81)
The Committee feels strongly of the trend of creating such trench of undefined lumpsum allocations, which are released to clear the financial dues of the PSEs as and when required. Whereas, the capacity
building for sustainable restructuring remains low in priority as is evident from the allocations. (para : 82)
The Committee observes that the Engineering Industries are likely to generate internal and extra budgetary resources (IEBR) to the tune of Rs. 562 crores as compared to Rs. 241.49 crores in RE 2005-06.
Of this Rs. 440 crores will be generated by BHEL and Rs. 97.40 crores by BEML. However in cases of TCIL, REIL and SCI the  IEBR are likely to come down this year as compared to the RE 2005-06. The
Committee however expresses its concern that with most other PSEs are not earning enough IEBR, and their reliance over Budgetray support is likely to increase in coming years.  The Committee holds the
view that PSEs should be able to run on its own with only necessary minimal Budgetary support.
(para : 84)
The Committee feels that with the approval of revival plans the need for such undefined lumpsum allocations should have reduced. Instead the allocation for AMR has been increased. However the
Committee  would like to be apprised of the details of type of expenditures that were met out of the two lumpsum allocations for crucial balancing and AMR and the explanation as to why the allocations for
AMR was increased whereas allocations for crucial balancing were reduced since the revival plans were approved. (para : 95)
The Committee is distressed to note the plight of PSEs who can not pay the regular salaries to their employees and would like to know the PSEs that were given such support. Further, Committee is
bewildered by the complex procedure of decision making, where lumpsum allocations were made in the Budget in advance under presumption and the expenditure is decided later based on expediency. In such
a scenario it is obvious that the expenditure would neither be evenly distributed nor would it cater to any long-term strategy to evolve the PSEs.
 (para : 102)
The Committee is of the view  that the Government has been actually losing money given as  loans to PSEs. In several cases, eventually these loans were either waived or converted into equity, thereby
converting secure debt liability into an insecure liability. The Committee impresses upon the DHI to check this trend and to take appropriate steps to ensure the repayment of loans and interests thereon.
(para : 108)
The Committee observes that against the aggregate Paid up Capital of Government/ holding PSE to the tune of Rs 3911.96 crores, however the PSUs have accumulated loss of Rs 8795.24 crores with an
aggregate  negative net worth of Rs 5097.17 crores. The Committee apprehended that such a financial situation may eat up the modest profit, which PSEs are likely to earn this year.
(para : 111)
The Committee however noted that the efforts in the revival plans is to gradually reduce the losses and turn these companies into viable enterprises. To improve the Net worth of the PSEs the debt/loan
liabilities are being restructured as a part of revival plan. The Committee notes if such a pattern of assistance is sustainable for longer term and if it puts huge pressure on Government’s finances. The Committee
desires the DHI to find the way out.
(para : 112)
The Committee feels that the PSEs to become financially self-relient must harness the export opportunities to earn their own finances for revival.(para : 114)
 
The Committee observes that the order book position of the PSEs under the DHI has grown steadily over past few years. However the Committee expresses its serious apprehensions on the over dependence
on Government for orders.

The Committee observes from SWOT analysis that the PSEs consider increased competition in market as a major threat also the technological drawback and inability to diversify the product profile are
