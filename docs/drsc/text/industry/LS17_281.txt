Report 
Heavy Industries Sector in the country comprises of the heavy engineering, machine tool, 
heavy electrical, industrial machinery and the auto-industry. These industries provide goods and 
services for almost all sectors of the economy, including the infrastructure, power, rail and road 
transport. Lately, the Heavy Industries Sector has been marred by problems like absence of level 
playing field vis-a-vis imports combined with imports of second hand machinery, zero import 
duty under Project Imports, delays in project implementation, non-trade barriers faced in export 
markets, significant technology gaps across sub-sectors, lack of skill availability, cost disabilities 
due to skewed tax and duty structure, cost disadvantage due to duty structure in Free Trade 
Agreements (PTAs), etc. 
2. Indian Auto Industry, a crucial sector under Heavy Industries was at its nascent stage at 
the beginning of the 21st  century which now has become a huge industry that contributes majorly 
to growth and development of Indian Economy. As per the current statistics, the auto Industry's 
turnover is estimated to be equivalent to 7.1% of overall Gross Domestic Product (GDP). The 
auto industry currently employs more than 32 million people both directly and indirectly. As the 
Government's objective is to increase the share of manufacturing in the GDP of the economy 
from an estimated 15 percent to atleast 25 percent, so that employment gets a definite boost, the 
role of auto industry is important and it has already made investments to achieve this objective 
and have increased the capacity to levels that would be needed to achieve the objective. The 
Industry has potential to grow to become a major economic contributor. 
3. The initiatives of the Government to rejuvenate the growth of the Heavy Industries sector 
include the Enhancement of the Competitiveness .in the Indian Capital Goods Sector (CGS) 
Scheme launched in 2014 and the National Goods Policy (NGP), 2016. Likewise, the schemes 
launched by the Government to shore up the Automobile Industry are the National Mission on 
Electric Mobility, 2011 and subsequently, the National Electric Mobility Mission Plan 
(NEMMP), 2020 which was unveiled in 2013. As part of this Mission, Department of Heavy 
Industry (DHI) has notified Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of (Hybrid &) Electric Vehicles 
in India (FAME- India) which is intended to support the hybrid/electric vehicles market 
development and its manufacturing. Further, the Government also finalized Automotive Mission 
Plan (AMP), 2016-26 setting targets for the auto industry for the year 2026.

6. The Committee takes note that the Department has quoted varying figures in its 
Annual Report regarding the number of CPSEs under it. While 31 CPSEs are under the 
`administrative oversight' of DHI, the number of 'operative CPSEs' under it stands at 25. 
Tire Committee assumes that this variance in figures is consequent to the closure of certain 
CPSEs during the preceding year. However, while perusing the status of CPSEs as 
recommended by NITI Aayog placed at Annexure II of the Annual Report (2016-17), the 
number of CPSEs recommended for closure is 7. If this figure is to be considered, the 
number of 'operating CPSEs' should be 24. The Committee observes this as a divergence 
which should have been avoided by more specific description of the status of CPSEs under 
DHI, elucidating the rationale adopted for the same. Accordingly, the Committee 
recommends that the Department should ensure that important documents like the Annual 
Report should be spelt out unambiguously and without deficiencies in future.

13. The Department submitted in this regard that this significant reform initiative in the 
budgeting process has been made to obviate the notion that had widely gained ground among the 
policy- makers and officials across all levels that Plan expenditure is good and Non-Plan is bad. 
This bias in favor of Plan expenditure and against Non-Plan expenditure has led to a situation in 
which even essential Non-Plan expenditure was being neglected. Shift of focus of Plan 
expenditure from Capital to Revenue expenditure and the process of transferring expenditure of 
old schemes to Non-Plan at the end of each five-year Plan meant that a clear correspondence 
could not be drawn between Plan and Development Expenditure. With the removal of Plan and 
Non-Plan distinction, the focus of budgeting and expenditure will shift to Revenue and Capital 
expenditure. It was also submitted that, henceforth, there will be no Five Year Plan, post 12 th  
Plan. 
14. Further, from this year onwards, Plan Finance-H (PF-H) Division; Department of 
Expenditure has been assigned the responsibility of finalizing the consolidated Demand wise 
Outcome Budget Framework in co-ordination with NITI Aayog on the basis of output/outcome 
framework submitted by each Ministry/Department. Therefore, each Ministry/ Department have 
not prepared their separate Outcome Budget. The output/outcome framework of DELI in respect of 
major Central Sector Schemes has been forwarded to NITI Ayog/ PF-II Division, Department of 
Expenditure.

15. 	 The Committee finds that the Plan and Non-Plan segregation of funds has been 
done away, with from the year 2017-18 onwards and the classification of Revenue 
expenditure and Capital expenditure has been put in place. The Committee understands 
that such bunching of funds is in alignment with the classification of funds as mentioned in 
the Constitution of India i.e., 'Revenue and other expenditures'. Also, it is not difficult to 
observe that in the era where budget allocation is being evaluated on the basis of the 
outcomes/outputs, it is only prudent to do away with the prior segmented classification in 
favour of a more holistic approach. The Committee expects that this rational step along with 
the advancement in the date of passing of the Budget will help the Department in utilizing 
the funds provided to it in a more systematic and effective manner. 
16. The Committee also finds that individual Outcome Budgets prepared by Ministries/ 
Departments of the Government has also been done away with from the current year. 
Instead, the Department has sent its output/outcome framework in respect of its major 
Central Sector Schemes to the NITI Ayog/ PF-II Division, Department of Expenditure. 
However, the Committee is of the firm opinion that linking budgetary outlays with 
deliverables/outcomes is an essential feature to ensure public accountability and 
transparency of the Government expenditure. Accordingly, the Committee recommends 
that a structured document clearly bringing out the outcomes in terms of the objectives of 
the schemes/programmes of the Department must be brought out either by the Ministries/ 
Departments concerned or by the Department of Expenditure for the benefit of the public 
and to justify the continued funding of the programmes/schemes.

Development of Automobile Industry  
27. The automotive, industry comprises of automobile and auto component sectors. 
Automotive Industry globally is one of the largest industries and is a key driver of economy. 
Owing to its deep forward and backward linkages with several key segments of industry, 
automotive industry has a strong multiplier effect on the economy. The well-developed Indian 
automotive industry ably fulfills this catalytic role by producing a wide variety of vehicles such 
as passenger cars, light, medium and heavy commercial vehicles, multi-utility vehicles, scooters, 
motor-cycles, mopeds, three wheelers, etc. 
National Automotive Testing and Research & Development Infrastructure Project

• India's emergence as a global outsourcing base for automobiles and auto components in 
furtherance of Auto Mission Plan.

performance after revival. The Committee recommends that DHI shall extend all support
