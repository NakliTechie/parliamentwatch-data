Introduction
The Heavy Industries in India is dominated by the Capital Good Sector (CGS) and the Auto
Sector. The Indian Capital Goods Industry has enough potential but manufacturing capacity is
underutilized to the extent of 40%.  Two major hurdles faced by the sector are cheap imports and
technology gap. The Government has come up with many initiatives/schemes to make the sector
globally competitive. The Department of Heavy Industry (DHI) with a mission to achieve high
growth in Heavy Electricals and Capital Goods Industry have announced the National Capital Goods
Policy (NCGP) on 15
th February 2016 which is a landmark initiative in this regard and has been
reflected in the Budget 2016-17 by way of enhancement of Customs Duty on Heavy Electrical
equipments from 7.5 % to 10%, imposition of Countervailing Duty (CVD) on road construction
machinery and reduction of excise duty from 12.5% to 6% on parts of centrifugal pumps.  All
these measures are expected to help indigenous producers of Capital Goods to be more cost
competitive against imports.
2.. The Auto Sector contributes 45% to the Manufacturing Gross Domestic Product (GDP),
26% to the Industrial GDP and 7.1% to the National GDP hence it is evident that this sector is
critical to the manufacturing sector. The Auto sector provides 29 million job opportunities, both
direct and indirect and has made investments to the tune of  ` 70, 000 crores in the last five
Financial Years (FYs). Considering that the industry had a turnover of USD 124 billion during
2014-15 and assuming a growth rate of 10% during 2015-16, the Government expects it to achieve
around 94% of the target set for the industry in the Automobile Mission Plan (2006-16) by 2016.
An ambitious Scheme for Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of (Hybrid and) Electric Vehicles in
India (FAME-India) is being implemented by the Government to support the hybrid/electrical
vehicles industry and its manufacturing ecosystem to attain self-sustenance. In the Budget
2016-17, the Government announced measures aiming to invest big in infrastructure like outlay of
` 97,000 crore for road infrastructure,  `  200 crore to FAME-India scheme & National Automotive
Testing R&D Infrastructure Project (NATRiP), reduction in customs duty on raw materials for
catalytic converters from 7.5% to 5%, etc that are expected to provide fillip to the Auto sector
in the ensuing years.
Department of Heavy Industry (DHI)
3. The Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises, comprising the Department of
Heavy Industry (DHI) and the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE), was created as per the
Presidential Notification dated the 15
th October, 1999. DHI administers 31 operating Central Public
Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), including 12 subsidiary companies and 1 JV company i.e. NTPC-
BHEL Power Projects Pvt. Ltd. (NBBPL). The Department also administers the following
autonomous organizations:
(a) NATRiP Implementation Society (NATIS) set up in July 2005, for guiding the
1

2
implementation of the National Automotive Testing and R&D Infrastructure Project
(NATRiP);
(b) Fluid Control Research Institute (FCRI), Palakkad, Kerala which caters to the needs
of the flow industry for calibration;
(c) Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI), Pune, Maharashtra; and
(d) National Automotive Board (NAB).
DHI has also been allocated the following subjects/Industrial Sectors:
(a) Heavy Engineering Equipment and Machine Tools Industry.
(b) Heavy Electrical Engineering Industries.
(c) Automotive Industries, including tractors and earth moving equipment.
The 31 CPSEs administered by DHI can be categorized into 3 major groups namely,
Engineering Units, Non- Engineering Units and Consultancy/Contracting Units. The list of the
CPSEs is placed at Annexure I.
4. The main objectives of DHI are: -
(a) Optimum utilization of installed capacity of CPSEs under the Department;
(b) Management Development to ensure professional management and operations in the
CPSEs;
(c) Promotion of profitability, efficiency and productivity through restructuring,
modernization, technology up-gradation and optimal deployment of human and
material resources in the CPSEs;
(d) Closer co-ordination and interaction between the manufacturing and the user sectors;
(e) Development and growth of Sectors looked after by the Department; and
(f) Implementation of a national project in Auto sector for creating state-of-the-art
facilities for testing etc.
5. The enterprises under the Department produce wide range of products ranging from
machine tools, industrial machinery, boiler, gas/steam/hydro turbines, turbo generators, electrical
equipment, and railway traction equipment, pressure vessels, AC locomotives, prime movers,
agricultural tractors and consumer products such as watches, cement, paper, tyres and salts, etc.
The industries provide goods and services for almost all sectors of the economy including power,
rail and road transport. The Department also looks after the Machine Building Industry and caters
to the requirements of equipments for basic industries such as steel, non –ferrous metals,
fertilizers, refineries, petrochemicals, shipping, paper, cement, sugar etc.  Besides, it supports the
development of a wide range of intermediate engineering products like castings forgings, diesel
engines, industrial gears and gear boxes.
(I) Detailed Demand for Grants (DDG) 2016-17
6. The Demand for Grants (DFG) 2016-17 a
ppearing at Sl. No. 44 relating to DHI has been
brought out in a pattern which is a departure from the past years. As part of the Committee’s
examination of Demands of DHI, explanation was sought regarding the changes made for the year
in comparison to the previous years and the rationale behind the changes.

3
7.  In their submission, DHI stated that as per the instructions of Ministry of Finance (MoF),
rationalization of existing plan/ non-plan schemes have been done. The aim is to rationalize all
programmes/schemes by merging the relatively small schemes into a logical large schematic
framework. Rationalized final set of schemes will lead to well defined measurable outcomes which
can be monitored. Schemes/sub-schemes reflected in the Detailed Demand for Grants are also to
be suitably merged /eliminated to yield a more compact framework for schemes/programmes,
which will help the Ministries/Departments gain more flexibility in execution of the schemes and
guard against spreading the resources too thin. MoF have stressed upon the need for rationalizing
Central Sector Schemes so as to ensure that schemes having small outlays could be done away
with or merged with bigger schemes, and the feasibility of achieving the intended objectives
through merger/clubbing could be explored.
8. With this aim, meetings on rationalization of the Central Sector Scheme in respect of DHI
were held in the MoF and accordingly, following three major Umbrella Schemes have been framed
after merging of existing plan/non-plan schemes and accordingly Budget Estimates for the year
2016-17 has been allocated to the tune of  `  300 crore (Plan) and  `  1000 crore (Non Plan).

9. On account of rationalization of existing schemes, only Umbrella Schemes are reflected in
the Statement of Budget Estimates (SBE) 2016-17 and accordingly Budgetary allocations for
2016-17 have been made. Therefore, SBE 2016-17 has been divided into two parts. Part-I with
heading Budget Estimates (BE) 2016-17 has reflected only Budgetary allocation for 2016-17 as per
the Umbrella Scheme. Part-II with heading Revised Estimates (RE) 2015-16  reflects the Actual
2014-15, BE-2015-16 and RE-2015-16 and this part is almost similar to the pattern of SBE 2015-16.
10. The Committee notes that the Demands for Grants (2016-17) for DHI has followed
a new pattern of depiction of allocation of funds when compared to the last year. On a quick
reference of Demands No. 44, the new pattern may appear difficult to comprehend due to
the absence of comparative details upfront. But, in reply to a query on this, the Department
has preferred to an explanation of bunching of smaller schemes under larger umbrella
schemes for the purpose of imparting more flexibility in the execution of schemes and to
guard against spending the resources too thin. The Committee finds reason in the rational
provided by DHI and appreciates the new initiative of the MoF.  It is expected that the new
system of Umbrella Schemes will result in timely implementation of schemes by reallocating
funds between sub schemes as per operational expediencies which should in turn curb
underutilization of funds as well.

13. Token provision has been made under the Major Head 6858 under the Umbrella Scheme
Development of Automobile Industry because funds to NATRiP can be released in the form of
Grant-in-aid (Major Head 2852- Revenue Section) as well as in the form of Loan (Major Head

of Loan, only token provisions have been kept under Capital Section (under 6858).
14. Regarding the allocation of funds to schemes under Development of Automobile
Industry, the Committee observes that funds for the same purpose has been allocated under
two different Heads, with one Head witnessing only a miniscule allocation and the other
reflecting majority of the funds.  The Department’s explanation for differential allocation
is that token allocations in two Heads have been made to cater to the possibility of funds
being allocated to NATRiP in the form of Grants–in-Aid as well as in the form of Loan, even
though the loan possibility is low. The Committee would have found it more appropriate if
the Government would have at least by now decided the manner in which funds to NATRiP
are to be provided, i.e. either as Loan or as Grants. Such uncertainties can affect the
lucidity of the Demand and the transparency with which allocations are made.

18. The Committee takes note that under the Head, Grants to CPSEs, funds have been
reflected under two different Heads – on Depiction Head and on Operational Head. On this
duality of funds, it has been informed that funds under the Depiction Head will be
transferred to the Operational head for executing expenditure.  This arrangement has been
done under the directives of Government of India (GoI). Further, it is encouraging to take
into cognizance that DHI is one of the few Departments of the GoI where all fund transfers
are being made on-line through PFMS and under this system funds can be transferred only
to those Heads which atleast have some token allocation.  The Committee observes that the
PFMS will hasten up the release of funds and eliminate avoidable delays and
mismanagement.
19. However, the Committee also wonders whether it is not ironical that while on the one
hand the Government is moving towards integration of small schemes under larger umbrella
schemes for the benefit of simplification of procedure and facilitating financial flexibility, on
the other hand it is complicating matters by adding more Heads for allocations and
expenditure of funds under a single umbrella scheme.

(ii) R&D Projects – Development of Advanced Ultra Super Critical (Adv+- Ultra
Super Critical – Adv- USC) Technology for Thermal Power Plants ( AUSC).
41. The R&D Project for “Development of Advanced Ultra Supercritical (Adv. USC) Technology
for Thermal Power Plants” is to be carried out jointly by Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd. (BHEL),
Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR) and National Thermal Power Corporation Ltd.
(NTPC). The objective of the First Phase of the project is to undertake R&D on all aspects of
Advanced Ultra Super Critical (Adv-USC) technology for thermal power plants in order to improve
power plant efficiency, reduce carbon-dioxide emissions and reduce coal consumption per unit of
power generated. The proposed project would enable Indian industries to design, manufacture and
commission higher efficiency coal fired power plants with indigenously developed technology and
manufacturing process. The duration of the project is estimated to be of two and a half years from
the date of approval. The Department informed the Committee that the fund of Rs 50 crore
allocated for the project in BE 2015-16 was surrendered because the project has yet not been
approved by the GoI. In the BE 2016-17 a token provision of Rs one lakh has been made for the
project. Further, the Department also updated the Committee on the progress made during 2015-
16 on the project.
42. DHI submitted that pursuant to the decision taken in a meeting held on 20-05-2015 under
the Chairmanship of Secretary, DHI, BHEL had committed to enhance its contribution for the
project from Rs 220 crore to Rs 270 crore. NTPC had confirmed the contribution of Rs 50 crore
for the project and IGCAR was to contribute Rs 234 crore for the project. The balance amount
of Rs 1000 crore was to be funded by GoI. Further, certain pre-project R&D activities were taken
up with funding of approx. Rs 25 Crore provided by the Office of Principal Scientific Advisor
(PSA), GoI, wherein two out of five small projects have been successfully completed and work
on the balance three is underway. However, the work on R&D Phase of the main project would
be taken up based only on the finalization of funding pattern. Final Note was sent to Cabinet
Secretariat on 3-3-2015 for obtaining approval of CCEA. However, the Cabinet Secretariat vide its
O.M dated 16-7-2015 returned the copies of CCEA Note with the direction that DHI may take
necessary action to finalize the note afresh as per Prime Minister’s Office (PMO) direction on the
issue.
43. The Principal Secretary to Prime Minister took a meeting on 20-08-2015 on the above
mentioned subject wherein the following were decided:
(1) NTPC and BHEL need to reconsider their commitment for financial support to the
project. Further, a suggestion was put forward that an amount of Rs 200 crore may
be contributed by the NTPC and Rs 400 Crore each by BHEL and IGCAR. The
remaining amount will be provided as support by the GoI.

19
(2) If the project is to be taken up, the funding of Phase-I & II needs to be finalized
upfront. The sharing pattern for phase-II should also be the same as for Phase-I.
Further, there needs to be a tight Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between
GoI, IGCAR, BHEL & NTPC, delineating their respective responsibilities clearly.
44. As part of their submission DHI also shared with the Committee the comments received on
this from BHEL, NTPC and IGCAR and Ministry of Power (MoP).
IGCAR has expressed its inability to contribute in Phase II and any additional amount in

NTPC has expressed its inability to contribute more than Rs 50 crore in R&D project in

1. The Committee notes that the Demands for Grants (2016-17) for DHI has followed
a new pattern of depiction of allocation of funds when compared to the last year. On a quick
reference of Demands No. 44, the new pattern may appear difficult to comprehend due to

20. As far as CPM is concerned, the Committee notes that the Ministry of DoNER has
given a sanction of  ` ` ` ` ` 20 crore for laying of a railway line and for conversion of metre-gauge
to broad-gauge for six kilometers, which would help CPM to being a profitable unit of HPC.

consideration. (Para No 77)

Government are coming together for reviving a sick unit back to its capacity. At the same

all the good efforts made so far. (Para No 78)

22. The Committee note that though a revival package amounting to  ` ` ` ` `  489 crore was
sanctioned to NPPC in 2013, the approval of BIFR was obtained only by the end of January,
2015.  The Committee observes that it is this sought of delay in the final clearance of
revival/restructuring package of sick PSUs that defeat the very purpose of its revival. Such
delays lead to cost escalation resulting in revised costs which in turn again requires
approval of competent authority and the painful vicious cycle of delays is set forth. Under
such circumstance, the Committee sincerely hopes that the new revival/restructuring
mechanism being put in place by the Government will address such teething problems of the
previous system. (Para No 82)

reduced to zero at RE due to absence of Utilization Certificate in respect of the funds
released in the penultimate year i.e. 2013-14. (Para No 83)
24. The Committee while taking into account the undue delay on the part of the
Government/ BIFR, is equally disappointed that NPPC could not utilize the funds allocated
in 2013-14 timely and also could not produce the Utilization Certificate for the same which
curtailed allocation of funds thereafter. Here, the Committee observes that the funds for
revival of CPSEs are provided by the Government after a long fought approval process.  In
this individual case, after a long drawn process of approval and sanctioning of fund, the
management of NPPC has failed squarely to make best use of the funds. As per the new
guidelines of DPE for revival/restructuring of CPSEs, the administrative ministry has the
primary responsibility regarding the health of its CPSEs. Accordingly, it is recommended
that DHI should take measures to properly guide the management of NPPC in the
implementation of the revival plan and see to that they steer clear of such lapses in the
times ahead. (Para No 84)

revival of the Company. These proactive steps are expected to bring down the production
cost of newsprint and provide HNL a comparative advantage in the market.  The Committee
observes that the steps adopted by Government of Kerala are highly supportive of the cause
of HNL and the management of HNL would do well to make efficient use of it.  Further,
it is recommended that alongwith cost cutting measures, HNL should also revisit its
marketing strategies to be able to fully translate the benefits brought by the expected cost
advantage to tangible financial gains for the company. (Para No 87)

streak of losses.  In this regard, it is expected that DHI will monitor and guide HNL step
by  step towards the turn around and provide all necessary support as expected from an
administrative ministry. The Committee further takes into cognizance the hardships faced
by HNL due to customs and excise duty liabilities. HNL claims that restoration of 10 %
customs duty on imported prints would help the Company immensely and they would be in
a position to substitute imported material. In view of this, the Committee recommends that

NEPA has to secure a bank loan of  ` ` ` ` `  128 crore to complete the funds required for RMDP.

should also ensure the responsibility of the management of NEPA in the timely execution
of RMDP and also in the follow up with the MoF. (Para No 95)
28. The Committee notes that in the Demands for Grants, the IEBR figures of CPSEs
portray a very unrealistic picture.  In most instances, the BE IEBR is greatly reduced or
even comes to naught at the RE stage. The explanation for such reductions provided by the
Government/ CPSEs range from deferred investments, decrease in profit, incurring of loss
by the companies, the companies being or becoming sick, reductions in tune with business
requirements, delay in securing loans, etc.  The Committee feels that though each variation
in IEBR figures are backed up with reasons by Government /CPSEs, it remains a fact that
the overall picture drawn while going through the Demands as such is confusing and borders
on misleading information as a considerable portion of the amount is disappearing at RE
stage. This is also because IEBR of a company comprises of resources generated by
Company concerned through profit, loans, equity and other funds to be secured by the
company, etc and in most cases these figures are assumptive. Accordingly, the Committee
observes that the Demand should contain an explanation to IEBR figures of each of the
CPSEs indicating the nature of the funds projected as IEBR. (Para No 97)

infrastructure, road & highways, satellite cities, etc to generate demands at home in both
these sectors.  For auto industry, the Committee is of the opinion that they should start
looking for export markets in the face of saturating domestic arena while formulating
business expansion plans. Also, they should earnestly try and develop indigenous technology
in the field of electric vehicles. CG associations should come up with a concrete plan in tune
with the NCGP, 2016 objectives of the Government to triple the size of Indian CGS in a
decade. (Para No 100)
