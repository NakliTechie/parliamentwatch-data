Evolution and Mandate  
The Government of India established the Ministry of  Small Scale Industries 
and Agro & Rural Industries (SSI & ARI) in October,  1999 as the nodal Ministry for 
formulation of policies and Central sector programm es/schemes, their 
implementation and related coordination and for sup plementing the efforts of the 
States/Union Territories for promotion and developm ent of small scale, agro and 
rural industries in the country.  Subsequently, in September, 2001 the Ministry of SSI 
and ARI was bifurcated into two separate Ministries, namely, Ministry of Small Scale 
Industries and Ministry of Agro and Rural Industrie s.  In 2006, “Micro, Small and 
Medium Enterprises Development Act (MSMED), 2006” w as enacted by the 
Parliament.  Pursuant to this enactment, the Minist ry of Agro and Rural Industries 
and the Small Scale Industries were merged into a s ingle Ministry, namely, Ministry 
of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises. 
Composition of the Ministry 
 
2. The Ministry of Micro Small & Medium Enterprises  is having two divisions 
called Small & Medium Enterprises (SME) Wing and Ag ro & Rural Industry (ARI) 
Wing.  The SME Wing is allocated the work, inter-al ia, of administration, vigilance 
and administrative supervision of the National Smal l Industries Corporation (NSIC) 
Ltd., a public sector enterprise and NIMESE an auto nomous national level 
entrepreneurship development/training organizations .  The Wing is also responsible 
for implementation of the schemes relating to Perfo rmance and Credit Rating, 
Assistance to Training Institutions and preparation  and monitoring of “Make in 
India” initiative among others.   
3. The ARI Wing looks after the administration of t wo statutory bodies viz. the 
Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), Coi r Board and a society called 

2 
 
Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Rural Industrialisation  (MGIRI).  It also supervises the 
implementation of the Prime Minister’s Employment G eneration Programme 
(PMEGP).  Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) and 
A Scheme for Promoting Innovation, Rural Industry and Entrepreneurship (ASPIRE) 
4. The implementation of policies and various progr ammes/schemes for 
providing infrastructure and support services to MS MEs is undertaken through its 
attached office, namely the Office of the Developme nt Commissioner (O/o DC 
(MSME), National Small Industries Corporation (NSIC ), Khadi and Village 
Industries Commission (KVIC), the Coir Board and tr aining institutes viz., National 
Institute for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises ( NI-MSME), Hyderabad and 
Mahatma Gandhi Institute for Rural Industrialisation (MGIRI), Wardha.

(i) Workshed Scheme for Khadi Artisans and Strengthenin g Infrastructure of Existing Weak

New/Modified Scheme titled “Market Promotion and Development Assistance”. 
(iii) Khadi (S&T), VI (S&T) and Plan (S&T) of Coir have b een clubbed/merged under one 
common budgetary head i.e., Khadi, VI and Coir (S&T). 
(iv) MDA scheme of KVIC has been modified as Market Promotion and Development Assistance 
scheme (MPDA). MPDA scheme is formulated as a unifi ed scheme by merging different 
schemes/sub-schemes/components of different Heads i mplemented in the 11 th  Plan, 
namely, Market Development Assistance, Publicity, M arketing and Market promotion. A 
new component of Infrastructure namely setting up o f Marketing Complexes/ Khadi 
Plazas has been added to expand the marketing network of Khadi & VI products.  
(v) Interest Subsidy (Khadi) & Interest Subsidy (VI) bu dgetary heads have been dropped in BE 
2016-17. 
 
7. The Committee feels that CLCSS and NMCP should b e separated. As per 
the current practice, the CLCSS which draws much la rger budget is clubbed 
with six components of NMCP that draw meager budget . CLCSS is essentially 
an interest subvention scheme like ISEC and should be kept as a credit support 
scheme whereas the NMCP is technology upgradation scheme.

24.  The Committee has observed the background not furnished by the 
Ministry that there was under-utilisation of Planne d Funds allocated under 
SCSP, TSP and NE Region. These funds are earmarked under the stated policy 
of Government of India for the marginalized section s of the society and remote 
areas of NE but could not be utilized due to lack o f proposals/demand not only 
indicates inefficiency on the part of Ministry but alsoshows  insensitivity. These 
Sections have traditional skills which need to be r efined, trained and 
commercialized, and the Ministry has utterly failed  to upscale their enormous   
skill pool. As an indicator in the success of MUDRA  Scheme, it is the Shishu 
loans meant largely for such marginalized sections,  are the biggest contributor. 
Yet the Ministry of MSME has not been able to reach  out to these sections. The 
Committee recommends that utilization of SCSP, TSP and NER should be 
monitored regularly at the highest levels in the Mi nistry and that the Financial 
Advisor must periodically report the Ministry of Fi nance in case of under-
utilisation of these Funds. The Ministry must launch special campaigns for these 
sections.

Trade Receivables Discounting System ( TreDS) 
 

20 
 
42. TReDS was announced in the Budget Speech 2014 t o ease cash flow to MSME 
suppliers. TReDS is an institutional mechanism for facilitating the trade receivables 
of MSMEs from corporate and other buyers including Government Departments and 
PSUs through multiple financers. The RBI had announ ced broad guidelines on 3rd 
December, 2014. In its 271 st  Report, the Committee had asked M/o MSME to obtain  
inputs from M/o Finance regarding status and struct ure of TreDS. The Committee 
was concerned over steep discount rates charged on discounting may harm the 
interests of the MSME suppliers.   
43. The Ministry informed that to solve the problem  of delayed payment to 
MSMEs, RBI has come out with the guidelines for setting up and operating the Trade 
Receivables Discounting System (TReDS). The intenti on is to facilitate Electronic 
Bill Factoring Exchanges in the country, which coul d electronically accept and settle 
MSME bills so that MSMEs could convert their receivables without delay.  
44. Three entities viz. (i) NSE Strategic Investmen t Corporation Limited (NSICL) 
and Small Industries Development Bank of India (SID BI), Mumbai; (ii)  Axis Bank 
Limited, Mumbai; and (iii) Mynd Solutions Pvt. Ltd. , Gurgaon, Haryana have been 
given "in-principle" approval for setting up and op erating TReDS with the approval 
of Board for Payment and Settlement Systems vide  Press Release dated November 
24, 2015. These entities have a time of at least si x months to fulfill the conditions of 
authorisation and need to approach RBI for receivin g final authorization under 
Payment and Settlement System Act. 
 45. The Committee was informed that the discount r ates have not been stipulated. 
TReDS will only act as a facilitator for creating an electronic factoring platform.  The 
bankers joining the platform as financiers can offe r their bids/ discount rates against 
each factoring unit (invoice / bill of exchange) an d the MSME seller is free to accept 
any of the bids. Upon acceptance of the bid, the factoring unit will be financed by the 

21 
 
concerned banker/ financier.  It is expected that t his transparent form of bidding 
would facilitate competitive bidding of MSME receivables by financiers/ banks. 
46. The Committee was informed that the success of Electronic Bill Factoring 
Exchange set up under TReDS  will be measured again st the volume and value of 
trade receivables traded in the Exchange. 
47. The Committee apprehands that such a mechanism though facilitate 
urgent cash flow and liquidity to the MSME supplier s but will also expose them 
to speculative dynamics of money market. The Commit tee wonder afterall what 
will be the incentive for financers to acquire Bill s and invoices? They may  tend 
to seek deeper and predatory discounts to the peril  of hapless MSME supplier.  
The Committee may be informed of  success of this s cheme in terms of 
participation, volumes and value of trade receivabl es traded in the Exchange. 
The Committee also recommends the Ministry to evolv e a mechanism to 
monitor so that discounting rates do not become too  deep and perilously 
predatory and inform the Committee of such mechanism.

53. The Committee noted that under the aegis of Pra dhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana 
(PMMY), MUDRA has already created its initial produ cts / schemes. The 
interventions have been named ‘Shishu’, ‘Kishor’ an d ‘Tarun’ to signify the stage of 
growth / development and funding needs of the benef iciary micro unit / entrepreneur 
and also to provide a reference point for the next phase of graduation / growth to look 
forward to. The financial limit for these schemes are :-  

23 
 
• Shishu : covering loans upto 50,000/-  
• Kishor :  covering loans above 50,000/- and upto 5 lakh 
• Tarun :  covering loans above 5 lakh to 10 lakh 
MUDRA’s delivery channel is conceived to be through  the route of refinance 
primarily to Banks/NBFCs/MFIs. Non–Corporate Small Business Segment (NCSB) 
comprising of millions of proprietorship / partners hip firms running as small 
manufacturing units, service sector units, shopkeep ers, fruits / vegetable vendors, 
truck operators, food-service units, repair shops, machine operators, small industries, 
artisans, food processors and others, in rural and urban areas are the target groups. 
54. The Committee has been monitoring the progress of this important scheme in 
different States and with different Banks. For year  2015-16 a target of Rs 1.22 lakh 
crores was set. Till the first week of February 201 6, loan amounting Rs 97000 crores 
to some 270 lakh borrowers was distributed of which  some 248 lakh were Shishu 
borrowers with Rs 50,000 and less.

found that lending Banks were not coming forward to  avail re-finance from 
MUDRA Bank.  Of the first tranche of Rs 5000 crores  to MUDRA, till the first 
week of February, 2016 only Rs 2184 crores were san ctioned for refinancing of 
which only Rs 1364 crores were disbursed. The Commi ttee found that 
refinancing of MFIs and NBFCs have been rather slug gish as compared to 
Scheduled Banks. The Committee is of the opinion th at MFIs have scarce funds 
and need cheap and prompt refinance and should be n atural partners in 
MUDRA Yojna instead of cash-rich Banks whose RIDF forms the corpus for the 
MUDRA Yojna. Also, the Committee would like to know  the reasons for 
sluggish refinancing under MUDRA Yojna. The Committ ee apprehends that 
perhaps Banks find the interest cap prescribed unde r re-financing conditions, 

24 
 
too restrictive. The Committee would like to know t he assessment of Ministry in 
this regard.  
56. The Committee also finds that while the MFIs ha ve the advantage of 
outreach and minimal paperwork, the actual interest  rate permissible to MFIs 
under MUDRA Yojna was around 19%, which is too high . Whereas the interest 
rate permissible for Scheduled Banks under Mudra Yo jna is at around 8-9% 
which was lower than the prevailing interest rate c harged by Banks. The 
Committee therefore recommends that interest rates for MFIs should be capped 
at a reasonable rate.
