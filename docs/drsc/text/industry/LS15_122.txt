REPORT
         Steel is crucial to the development of any modern economy and is concerned to be the backbone of the human
civilization. Since steel industry is technologically a complex industry having strong forward and backward linkages in
terms of material flow and income generation, all major economies are not invariably characterized by the existence of a
strong steel industry, but also the growth of many of these economies have been largely shaped by the strength of their
steel industry during their early stages of development. Some of the newly industrializing countries like South Korea,
China and Brazil among others have built up substantial capacities not only to support their rapidly growing domestic
economies, but also to export significantly to mark their presence in the international market. India, after independence,
embarked on a massive industrialization programme wherein need was left for self-reliance in steel. Accordingly, large
plants were set up and capacities were created, mostly in the public sector.
1.2    The liberalization of the economy since 1991-1992 has witnessed a spate of new capacity additions in the private
sector because a quantum change took place in the steel industry. Steel Plants in private sector are coming up and
producing two-third of the total finished products. Long-term growth prospect of steel industry is encouraging a large
number of people to set up Greenfield capacities with modern technology in the private sector. These capacities with
state of the art technology involve lower concession costs than the units in the public sector. Under an era of
liberalization and with the lowering of international trade barriers only economic consideration are determining external
trade, both imports as well as exports. Competitiveness of the industry has become crucial not only for increasing exports
but also in competing with imports in the domestic market as tariff barriers are being reduced in a phased manner. As a
matter of fact the steel industry in India has been adversely affected by the rationalization and reduction of duties
resulting in lower tariff protection against imports, South East Asian currency crises, Russian Rouble devaluation, lower
international prices and the consequent meltdown, dumping due to lack of demand. Global Steel Industry for the major
part of 2001 remained a victim of slow down in demand, sliding price levels, low realizations, losses and/or falling
profits. However, from the beginning of 2002, the industry entered a recovery phase, with the gradual firming up of
international prices and demand. These trends largely persist to date. The year 2003 may well see a price rise in the
domestic and the international steel market.
1.3    The Ministry of Steel has to play a crucial role in ensuring harmonious and integrated growth of steel sector. The
main functions of Ministry of Steel are as under:-
         (a)    co-ordination of the growth of the Iron and Steel Industry (including Re-rolling Mills, Alloy Steel and Ferro
Alloy industries, Refractories) both in the Public and Private Sector.
         (b)    formulation of policies in respect of production, pricing, distribution, import and export of iron and steel and
Ferro Alloys;
         (c)    planning, development and control of and assistance to the entire iron and steel industry in the country; and
         (d)    development of input industries relating to iron ore, manganese ore, refractories etc., required mainly by the
steel industry.
1.4    Besides the Secretariat, the Ministry of Steel has an attached office viz. the office of the Development
Commissioner for Iron and Steel (DC I & S) located at Calcutta and its four Regional Offices located in New Delhi,
Calcutta, Chennai and Mumbai. Under the administrative control of Ministry of Steel the following Public Sector

5.0    ECONOMIC PHILOSOPHY BEHIND BUDGET PROPOSALS FOR 2003-04:IMPACT OF
GLOBALISATION AND LIBERALISATION ON THE STEEL INDUSTRY
5.1    Replying to the Committee’s query as regards the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals 
2003-04 and impact of globalisation and liberalisation on Steel Industry the Ministry of Steel explained that the Union
Budget 2003-04 is reform-oriented and its fundamental aim is to create conditions for growth. While globalisation is an
avowed objective of the government policy, the government has also taken note of the possible pitfalls on its way and
has sought to provide, sufficient safeguards to various sectors in the economy especially those considered vulnerable.
The aim of the budget proposals has also been to rationalise certain provisions, bring harmony in fiscal structures related
to various sectors and create a more efficient tax regime.
5.2    The overall objective is visible in the measures for the steel industry. However, given the complexity in the
industry, there are areas where the measures have adversely affected the steel industry. These include raising (with a
view to rationalisation) of import duty rates of raw materials like metallurgical coke and nickel. The reduction of peak
duty rate to 25 per cent from 30 has also reduced the nominal protection rate for the finished steel products in the
country. Further, the elimination of the 5 per cent duty differential as a result of this measure between Hot Rolled Coils
and other downstream products like CR and galvanised sheets, will affect the economics of the stand alone CR and
galvanising mills. Further, although the decision to withdraw the facility given to the integrated steel plants to pay excise
duty on their ex-plant prices, irrespective of where they sell from, is in line with the overall government policy, the
specific conditions in which these plants operate do not appear to have been taken into account, especially the fact that
these plants are close to mines and in backward areas and their markets are located far away.
5.3    The Committee is pleased to note that the Union Budget 2003-04 is reform-oriented and its fundamental aim is to
create conditions for growth. However, it is expected that the Ministry of Steel would seriously and effectively take up
the issue of suitable amendment in the tax structure with the Ministry of Finance so as to rectify some of the tax
rationalisation proposals, which are adversely affecting the steel industry.
5.4    Elaborating the impact of globalisation and liberalisation on the Steel Industry, the Ministry of Steel added that
globalisation is an ongoing process and the steel industry in India has been subject to this steady process of liberalisation
and globalisation since 1991. From 1991, various economic reform measures started changing the contours of regulation
in this important industry. In a series of reforms in 1991, the government removed the steel industry from the list of
industries reserved for the public sector. The sector was opened up further by the removal of the erstwhile licensing
system. Except for certain locational and environmental matters, new capacity development efforts in the country do not
require any clearance of the government.
5.5    The Committee was given an understanding that the Steel industry has also been benefiting from the reforms in the
capital market in tapping investible resources with case and without having to go through various government clearances
that characterised the erstwhile-regulated regime. The simultaneous reforms in the economy, in the form of removal of
the licensing system for imports and exports and reduction in import tariffs and making the country’s currency fully
convertible on trade account brought in a sea change in the overall environment of business. Much of the changes were
brought in to tune the system to the guidelines and requirement of the World Trade Organisation. The industry faced
corresponding liberalisation in their market for raw materials as the domestic and the external frontiers were opened up.
5.6    While the steel consumption in the country continued to grow, new investments started pouring into the industry
through strong initiatives of private entrepreneurs. The public sector also had a major role to play in expanding capacity
mostly through brownfield expansion and modernisation. This strengthened the domestic supply position in the market.
Much of the capacity growth in the industry has been driven by emerging opportunities in the world market. Over the
years, post-reforms, the condition of the market as well as the shape of the industry have changed along with the changes
in the policy environment. The nature of problems has also changed accordingly as the market, earlier driven by
shortages and rationing, came under the pressure of excess production and capacity. The prices dropped in keeping with
international trends, leading to financial distress in the industry. The government, in the face of the emerging crisis in the

industry, took steps to pull the industry out of these recessionary conditions by providing protection through tariff and
non-tariff measures.
5.7    The Indian steel industry’s global entry took place at a time when the international market was in turmoil, the
growth prospects were cut short by the rapidly expanding excess capacity in the industry worldwide, slowdown in
demand, massive supply of steel at cut throat prices from the CIS countries and, more so, large restructuring and cost
cutting efforts all over the major steel producing world. This put pressure on the Indian industry that had just added some
state of the art capacities.
5.8    Although the country’s steel exports grew sharply, estimated to touch 3.5 million tons in 2002-03 from about 0.3
million tons in 1991, the growing protectionism worldwide, especially in the developed countries/regions has come as a
major barrier to Indian industry’s globalisation bid. Anti-dumping and countervailing cases against major Indian steel
products like HR coils, plates and CR sheets in the European Union, USA and Canada have significantly reduced the
global opportunities for the Indian steel makers. On top of this, the spate of safeguard measures has also affected, the
interest of the Indian steel industry. As the Indian steel industry faces challenges, there has also been proportionate
protective response from the government in its defence. The import tariff rates have remained at reasonable levels and
anti-dumping actions have been taken on select products.
5.9    The Committee observes that Indian Steel Industry is marching ahead steadily at a pace determined by the process
and forces of liberalisation and globalisation since 1991. Domestic Economic Reforms i.e. de-regulation and removal of
erstwhile licensing system, capital market reforms, full conversion of currency on trade account etc. led to gearing up of
steel industry for its adherence to the guidelines of WTO regime. The globalisation process opened up new opportunities
for Indian steel industry in the world market. The Committee is pleased to note that Indian Steel Industry is undergoing a
sea change to adapt itself as per the requirement of globalised and liberalised economy. However, the Committee is of
the opinion that the Ministry of Steel should undertake steps to mull out the negative impact of liberalisation and
globalisation of Steel industry and accordingly apprise the Committee in its action taken note on the presentation of this
report. Adequate measures may be taken to safeguard the Indian Steel Industry.

1. With continuous efforts towards improvement
in product quality, expanding global exposure
and niche product development, the Indian steel
industry has full potential to grow significantly
in the global market. The country is also at an
advantage to cater to the markets in the
neighbourhood (Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and
Nepal importantly), Southeast and East Asia
and the Middle East.
1. Globally, the industry has accumulated huge excess
capacity. This will in the first place keep pressure on
prices and then reduce opportunities for growth. An
international low price syndrome in an era of
liberalisation and globalisation is a major threat for
domestic investment towards capacity growth.
2. India is a large and growing economy. The
domestic market advantage is significant
compared to many other steel producing
countries where domestic steel demand has
stopped growing.
2. Given the poor state of health of the steel industry in
India, a sustained period of low international prices
may lead the industry to bankruptcy unless available
policy tools adequately protect them.
3. Adaptability to small batch production will
provide opportunities for niche
products.                           
3. Protectionism has grown worldwide and major Indian
steel products are under trade actions in
countries/regions like the USA, EU, Canada,
Thailand, etc.
  4. Stricter subsidy disciplines may adversely affect the
Indian steel industry’s margins as well as its exports.
  5. Poor infrastructure in the country may add to the costs
of production further making exports unviable in this
strongly competitive world market.
6.2    The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Steel should have the objective assessment of SWOT analysis for
improving the health of Indian Steel industry. Since the SWOT analysis gives the details of the strength, weaknesses,
opportunities and threats to the objects undertaken, it is imperative that the strength is the subject where the strategy-
maker has to bank on, while, weaknesses are to be overcome. Similarly, opportunities are to be availed of and a resilient
strategy has to be sketched up for combating the threats coming across. In this context the Indian Steel Industry policy-
maker has to bank on the low wage rates, low iron cost, superior managerial skill while searching for an adequate
marketing strategy. Further avenues may be opened up for supplying semi-steel products to small scale steel industries
and ancillary steel industries in various parts of country (especially Chennai and Ludhiana where clusters of steel units
are wide spread).

35.0  SETTING UP OF GALVANISING COMPLEX IN NORTH-EASTERN REGION
35.1  Hot Dip Galvanizing Line  (HDGL) Project was approved by SAIL Board at an estimated cost of Rs.42.85 crore in
Jan’97. The work was awarded on a turnkey basis to MECON in Jul’97 with a completion schedule of 18 months. Civil
work was in advance stage of completion and equipment supply had also started. However, due to delay in completion of
the approach road & the bridge work by Govt. of Assam and stoppage of work due to heavy rains & floods in the area,
the project has been delayed.
35.2  In view of the time and cost over run of the project, SAIL requested M/s IFCI to re-appraise the project. M/s IFCI
in its report submitted in Oct’99 indicated that under the existing conditions and without fiscal incentives (viz. exemption
from excise duty and income tax and transport subsidy), the project is not financially viable. It was decided by SAIL
Board in Mar’00 that the project be kept in abeyance and alternate strategies, including indentification of suitable party
who may be interested in owing and executing the project, may be examined.
35.3  Accordingly, the notice of suspension of work was issued in Apr’00 to MECON, the turn-key contractor. In
May’00, M/s PNB Capital Services Ltd.(PNBCSL) were appointed as the adviser for selection of a Joint Venture (JV)
partner, M/s North East Steel (NES), Guwahati were selected as prospective Joint Venture Partner in Jul’01 on
preliminary basis and the required security deposit/bank guarantee was received from them in Dec’01.
35.4  In the meantime, PNBCSL were closed-down by the Board of PNB Caps. In May’02, approval for formation of JV
company and MOU with M/s NE Steel Pvt. Ltd.(NESPL – the newly incorporated company with consortium members
of erstwhile NES as its Directors) has been obtained from SAIL Board. In view of NES consortium have been replaced
by NESLP, M/s. NESPL were required to submit fresh BG in their name and the same was received after repeated
reminders/notices on 11.12.02. Subsequently, on 21.12.02, M/s IFCI Limited has been appointed as the Financial
Adviser for finalisation of transaction & preparation of required documents. M/s Singhania & Partners have also been
appointed as legal advisor for the JV. It is expected that after finalisation of JV, the HDGL Project would be completed
in one year’s time.
35.5  The Committee is of the view that the project of galvanizing complex in North-Eastern Region has been on hold
since long, therefore, the Ministry of Steel should accelerate the process for setting up of a Hot Dip Galvanising Line
(HDGL) project in north-east. The Committee hopes that the project would be completed in one years time after the
finalisation of joint venture.

INDIAN STEEL
36.1  The General Budget for 2003-2004 has provided a thrust to infrastructure projects such as roads, airports, and
seaports through an innovative funding mechanism which prove helpful to the domestic steel industry by boosting the
demand for steel in the country. As regards the impact of rationalization of tax structure, made in budget, on the steel
sector and further tax reforms that may be suggested, a detailed note is given in the subsequent paragraphs.
36.2  Impact of rationalization of tax structure : The Union Budget 2003-2004 has increased Customs Duty on charge
Nickel and Metcoke from 5% to 10% in pursuance of government’s policy to minimize sector-specific and end-use based
customs duty.
36.3  Nickel :- In the case of Nickel, some of the end-users were paying duty at the rate of 15% and others were paying
5%. However, the predominant import of nickel [approx. 30,000 MT out of total imports of 34,000] is for manufacture of
stainless steel and only a minscule proportion of the users (primarily plating industry) were actually paying the higher
duty of 15%. If rationalization had to be done, the duty could have been fixed at 5% for all users keeping in view the
dominant user industry. Moreover, Nickel is not produced in this country at all. Therefore, there is no domestic industry,
which competes with imports. Further, the predominant usage of stainless steel in India is for the utensil sector. The
utensil sector has been encouraged by the Government as reflected in the excise duty exemption to the utensil
manufacturers announced in the Budget. However, the benefit of the excise duty exemption may be eroded by doubling
of customs duty rate on nickel contributes 
50-60% of the total cost of stainless steel and is thus the single most dominant raw material in stainless steel. Moreover,
the international price of nickel has risen from $6000/MT to over $9000/MT over the last one year and the increase in
customs duty adds to the increased cost being borne by manufacturers of Stainless Steel.
36.4  Metcoke :- The customs duty on Metcoke has also been rationalized to a uniform rate of 10%. This too appears to
have been done in pursuance of the policy to minimize sector-specific and end-use based customs duty
variations/exemptions. The steel industry as well as the ferro alloy industry are both going to be adversely affected since
they have been paying a 5% duty on imported Metcoke. The import duty for steel/pig-iron industry using blast furnace
technology was reduced from 15% to 5% in the year 2000 after protracted correspondence with Ministry of Finance. The
benefit was extended to steel manufacturers using COREX Technology in the Budget 2002-2003 and to the Ferro Alloy
Industry as late as in January 2003.
36.4.1  The grounds on which Ministry of Steel had recommended reduction in the import duty on Metcoke for the steel
industry and the Ferro Alloy Industry still holds true. The Mini Blast Furnace Units as well as the Ferro Alloy industry
requires Low Ash Metcoke (LAM), the indigenous availability of which is limited. Therefore, the requirement of LAM
by both these industries is almost entirely met through imports from China.
36.4.2  Though the indigenous Metcoke industry needs to be encouraged, it cannot be given protection at the cost of
Steel/Ferro Alloy industry, especially when the required quality of Metcoke is not available in the country. In fact
Ministry of Steel has been proposing that a different (rate of)’’ in the import duty between Coking Coal-the raw material
for production of Metcoke, and Metcoke should be maintained by reducing the duty on Coking Coal to 0% and retaining
the duty of 5% on Metcoke.
36.4.3  The logic of minimizing sector-specific and end-use based customs duty exemptions/reductions may not be
applicable in this case. Around 29 lac tons of Metcoke is imported every year out of which more than three quarter is
consumed by the steel industry and the remaining mainly be the Ferro Alloy and the Chemical industry. Therefore, in
this case also the rationalization in customs duty could have been done keeping in view the interest of the dominant user
industries i.e. the Steel and the Ferro Alloy industry. Further, as in the case of Nickel, the international price of LAM has
risen by more than 50% over the last one year.
36.5     Excise Duty on stockyard and freight charges :- Ministry of Steel had sought that stand-alone secondary
producers and integrated steel plants (ISP) be treated at par and excise duty be levied on the basis price alone, a facility
available till now only with the ISPs. However, the Budget 2003-2004 has withdrawn the facility given to ISPs and they
would now have to pay excise duty on normal transaction value at depots. This would imply that stockyard price for non-
modvatable items like plates etc. would go up by an average of Rs.150-200 per ton.
36.5.1  ISPs like SAIL established a stockyard network across the length and breadth of the country in order to reach
steel to the common man and also to rural and backward areas. However, owing to levy of excise duty on freight and
distribution charges, the steel will become costlier for deliveries from stockyards. The small-scale
manufacturers/consumers requiring steel in small quantities and who are unable to procure rake-roads directly from the
plants will be put to disadvantage.
36.5.2  In addition, the viability of the stockyards would itself get affected since steel sold through them would get
costlier, vis-a-vis traders who might procure steel directly from ISPs and would not have to pay excise duty on freight,
though final destination for both could be the same. Therefore, this Ministry is of the view that, stand-alone secondary
producers and integrated steel plants (ISP) should be treated at par and excise duty levied on the basic price along.

36.7     Customs Duty
36.7.1  Duty on Coin Blanks :- The import of Coin Blank is duty free. This puts the domestic Coin Blanks manufacturers
at a disadvantage since they have to pay sales tax and other transaction charges. In order to provide an even playing field
to the domestic industry it was proposed by Ministry of Steel that a customs duty of 30%, which is the same as the duty
on Cold Rolled Stainless Steel, (CRSS is the input for the manufacture of Coin Blanks) should be imposed on imported
Coin Blanks alongwith 4% SAD and 16% CVED. Alternately, deemed export benefits should be extended to the supply
of Ferritic Stainless Steel Coil Blanks to the Government of India Mints. In this case, in order to ensure a level playing
field with foreign suppliers, it is necessary that domestic suppliers also be provided drawback facility to offset 4% CST
payable.
36.7.2  Withdrawal of SAD from non-available inputs :- In addition to basic customs duty, all imported items (except old
ships) attract 4% SAD, which has been levied to neutralize 4% sales tax on domestic sales. From 1999, traders have also
been brought under SAD. However, to enable import of essential raw materials, SAD should be waived on items, which
are not available indigenously. A list of such items along with their custom tariff numbers is given in the table below :

36.7.4 Hot Rolled Stainless Steel Coils (HRSS) for Coin-Blanks :- HRSS imported for manufacture of Coin Blanks for
government mints are from customs duty, CVED and SAD. However the usage rate of HRSS Coils for manufacturing 1
metric ton of Coin Blanks as approved by the government is 1.8654 metric on whereas the exemption is available only
for 1 ton of HRSS. This anomaly is required to be removed and custom duty exemption on 1.8654 metric ton of HRSS
should be given against manufacture of every ton of Coin Blank.
36.7.5 Countervailing duty on Ship Breaking :- Restoration of status quo ante i.e. 5% import duty, 4% SAD and CVED
of 16% on import of old ships for breaking so that (a) tax evasion is reduced and revenue collection is increased and (b)
the industry gets the requisite boost in production.
36.8    Excise Duty
36.8.1 Reduction of excise duty on GP/GC sheets for rural/mass consumption :-
           One of the constraints in growth of domestic demand for steel is the high excise duty of 16%. Whereas
intermediate consumers such as machinery manufacturers can adjust the excise duty paid on steel inputs by way of
paying only CENVAT at the time of sales of their products, final consumers such as house builders, construction

122nd Report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/122ndreport.htm[04-07-2016 12:35:49]
industry etc. have to bear the burden of excise duty. The situation is more alarming in the rural sector that consumes
large quantities of GP/GC sheets. In order to boost market demand of steel it is necessary to reduce the cost of
production as well as the market price of iron and steel products. In view of this the excise duty on GP/GC sheets should
be reduced from 16% to 8%. Apparently this may cause loss of revenue, but with production going up such loss will be
compensated.
36.8.2  Notification 345 dated. 23.10.97 regarding clearance of recoveries on ship breaking:-
           A section of the steel industry as well as ship-breakers have drawn our attention to provisions laid down in
Notification No. 345/61/97-CX dated 23.10.97. This notification clarifies that no central excise duty is payable on goods
and materials (recovered during the course of ship-breaking) which are outside the ambit of Section XV of the Schedule
to the CE tariff Act, 1985 and no reversal of MODVAT credit taken on input of entire ship is warranted in respect of
such recovered goods which are considered as not arising out of an activity of manufacture or processing. However, this
provision is being misused by excessive billing of non-excisable goods like generators, motors etc. and temptation for
ship-breakers for selling the same to scrap traders without payment of excise duty, thereby violating the norm of value
addition through breaking of a ship. This notification is giving an undue advantage to unscrupulous ship-breakers. It
would have been logical to charge ED on their sales also after recovery as scrap items from the ships. Therefore the
above CE Notification should be withdrawn. At the same time, non-excisable items such as generators, refrigerators etc.
which are a part of the imported ship, should also be charged customs duty under their respective heading as is being
done in case of bunk oil.
36.8.3 Excise Duty on pre fabricated items of steel used in construction:
           Both steel and cement are used for construction. Items fabricated both out of iron & steel (CH.7308.50) and
cement (CH.38) at the project site continue to be exempt from duty. However, while the prefabricated cement items
(ready mix concrete: CH 3824.20) are exempt from payment of excise duty, prefabricated steel items attract an Excise
Duty of 16%. Since steel and cement both are extensively used in construction prefabricated steel structure (73.08) and
prefabricated buildings (94.06) should be exempted from Excise Duty.
36.9  After having a detailed discussion with the representatives of Indian Steel Alliance, TISCO, Jindal Vijay Nagar the
Committee feels that the existing tax structure needs to be modified to become a source of support for the steel sector.
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Finance should examine the issue of rationalizing the tax structure on
the following lines :-
         (i)     Customs duty on charge nickel and Metcoke be fixed at 5% for all uses keeping in view the dominant user
industry. The Committee agrees with the proposal of the Ministry of Mines that a differential of in the import
duty between Coking Coal – the raw material for production of Metcoke, and Metcoke should be maintained
by reducing the duty on Coking Coal to 0% and retaining the duty of 5% on Metcoke.
         (ii)     Prior to the budget 2003-2004 integrated steel manufacturers were paying Excise duty on the factory gate,
even when goods were sold from the stockyard. It is now proposed that Excise duty has to be paid on
transaction value at depot. Withdrawal of the existing facility would imply an increase in price of construction
steel in particular for supply from stockyard, affecting SAIL and TISCO specifically. Reinstatement of the
existing notification is required.
         (iii)     While there is an exemption of customs duty on Stainless Steel coin blanks, domestic manufacturers such as
SAIL and other have to pay excise duty and local taxes on similar products made in India. This product is
bought by the Ministry of Finance only. Indian companies are loosing tenders to overseas companies. The
Department of Economic affairs must allow exemption of excise duty and sales tax for local producers
supplying to them.
         (iv)    Restoration of status quo ante i.e. 5% import duty, 4% SAD and CVED of 16% on import of old ships for
breaking so that (a) tax evasion is reduced and revenue collection is increased and (b) the industry gets the
requisite boost in production.
         (v)    Reduction of excise duty on GP/GC sheets from 16% to 8% for promoting rural mass consumption.
         (vi)    Withdrawal of Notification 345 dated 23.10.97 regarding clearance of recoveries on ship-breaking and
charging of custom duty on non-excisable items like generators, refrigerators, under their respective heading
as is being done in case of bunk oil.
         (vii)   Exemption of excise duty on prefabricated item of steel used in construction in line with exemption of excise
duty on prefabricated cement items.

38.2  National Mineral Development Corporation (NMDC) : The Disinvestment Commission in its 10 th Report
considered NMDC to be a “Core” organisation in so far as Government of India is concerned. In view of the financial
requirements of NMDC for undertaking Joint Ventures (JVs) overseas, the Commission is of the opinion that NMDC
should get into exploration and mining of metals other than iron ore through JVs with overseas parties so as to enable the
Company to get required financial support besides using its technical expertise so far developed. In order to attract good
global mining companies to join hands with NMDC in overseas ventures, the Commission is of the view that
Government of India could offer upto 20-25% of NMDC shares to a foreign partner. However, the Ministry of Steel is of
the opinion that no disinvestment in NMDC should be immediately resorted to because it is a profit earning company,
paying dividends to the Government. NMDC holds lease for iron ore mines with the best quality iron ore in the world
and it is necessary to scientifically mine it. For this it is necessary that these mines are retained in the Government sector.
It has expertise in mining not only of iron ore but of diamonds, gold and all minerals other than coal and oil.
Disinvestment Commission classified NMDC as a ‘Core’ sector PSU due to its strategic importance in the mineral
sector.

38.8     The Committee was apprised of the opinion of the Disinvestment Commission on the various steel PSUs, status
of divestment process undertaken by the Department of Disinvestment on the basis of advice of the Divestment
Commission. The Committee understands that disinvestment has now become a national economic policy and priority.
However, the basic purpose of disinvestment is to generate operational efficiency of PSUs and withdrawal of the States
role from the business. The Committee is of the considered opinion that the process of the disinvestments should not
dilute the social welfare activities of the government so long the constitutional obligations of the canons of the welfare
state remains intact and keep on making the government aware of its responsibility. India has marched ahead with its

45.0  BUDGET AND THE INDIAN STEEL INDUSTRY
45.1  Following the measures proposed in both the Railway Budget 2003-04 and the Union budget 2003-04, the outlook
in the steel industry is positive. The policy measures proposed in the Railway Budget 2003-04 promise to benefit
industry in general and the iron and steel industry in particular. The proposed rationalization of freight structure,
reduction in the number of classifications, the market orientation introduced through peak and off-peak rates, the
incentives to big shippers in the form of lower rates, if they move more materials, the reduction in freight rates for short

122nd Report of Committee on Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/122ndreport.htm[04-07-2016 12:35:49]
distance ranging up to 100 km as well as the general reductions in the freight rates for finished steel and pig iron---all
carry the potential to boost bottomlines by lowering the overall costs and lead to increased competitiveness. Finally, the
stress on laying of new tracks, broadening of existing ones, addition of new trains and a series of development projects
are all expected to boost demand for steel.
45.2  Union Budget 2003-04 has proposed measures that are meant more for impacting the environment in which the
iron and steel industry operates rather than for the industry itself. Most prominent is the significant thrust provided to
infrastructure development in the country – roads, ports, airports and housing – and this is turn is expected to lead to a
substantial increase in the domestic steel demand. Reduction of excise on passengers cars and appliances like air
conditioner by boosting sales is also expected to indirectly impact steel industry favourably.
45.3  There may be no direct correlation between the International steel production growth rate and the domestic growth
rate. Although a global rise in demand may have similar impact on the industry in India as well as globally, a domestic
rise in demand may, in fact, lead to strong growth in production in the country leaving the global production rate
practically unaffected. Therefore, it may be difficult to estimate whether domestic growth rate will catch up with the
global trends. In fact, while the global annual production growth rate in the last ten years or so has been at less than 2 per
cent, the figure for India has been at about 6 per cent. There are greater chances that the steel industry growth rate in
India will be higher than the global rate in the medium and the long term.
45.4  Major Indian steel companies have already hiked prices of steel items – though, mostly flat products than long
products – from the beginning of the fiscal 2002-03. This is seen in line with international trends where prices have been
rising since beginning of 2002 and is an inevitable fallout of the globalization process. Domestic price rises have
persisted in 2003 in the face of a steel demand that has shown signs of stabilizing.
45.5  Price Volatility :- 2003 opened with rounds of price hikes – of both long and flat – that has given the current
market trends further buoyancy. In January 2003, SAIL, Tata Steel and other flat steel producers increased prices of flat
products by an average Rs. 600-1000 per tonne while SAIL and RINL have hiked long steel prices by Rs. 200-300 per
tonne. This has pushed up hot rolled coil prices in the domestic market, with basic grade price of the commodity being
around Rs. 17,500-18,000 per tonne (average). Similarly, the growth in the construction segment in particular and the
infrastructure segment in general has prompted fresh hikes in long steel prices. Internationally too, trends (W. European
fob levels) remain strong : galvanized coil prices are in the range of $445-485/ tonne; cold rolled coils, $390-430/tonne
and hot rolled coils at around $300-325/tonne. (Source. Metal Bulletin).

47.0  FORMULATION OF NATIONAL STEEL POLICY
47.1  In pursuance of the recommendations contained in the 30 th and 34 th Report of the Department Related
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry, Ministry of Steel is formulating a National Steel Policy. A Drafting
Committee, consisting of members from the Steel Industry, DCI&S as the Chairman and the Chief Economist of
Economic Research Unit (ERU), JPC, as the convenor was constituted. Basic objective of the National Steel Policy is to
prepare a road map for the Indian steel Industry in the context of ongoing reforms, restructuring and globalization.
47.2  The Committee submitted the first draft in April 01. Thereafter a meeting was convened under the Chairmanship of
Secretary (Steel) on 25 th May, 2001 to discuss the draft with all major steel producers, their associations, consultancy
firms in the field of metallurgy and representatives of FICCI, Ministries of Commerce and Finance. The participants
suggested certain changes to be incorporated in the policy document and accordingly it was decided that the draft would
be modified in the light of the suggestions given and the revised document would again be circulated to all concerned
before giving it final shape.
47.3  While further consultations with industry were in progress, it was observed that the industry in general showed
signs of revival. In addition, concerned activity was observed on the trade front. Several developed and developing
countries have started adopting various tariff and non-tariff barriers to protect their domestic steel industry. In addition to
the increasing number of anti-dumping and countervailing duty cases, the strict surveillance mechanism put in place at
the EU and the cumbersome packing and quality standards imposed by Australia and Thailand have affected markets that
are of interest to India. It was felt that the imperatives of the changing scenario should be incorporated in the Steel
Policy. Accordingly, after a round of fresh informal consultations, a revised draft has been prepared.
47.4  The revised draft would be first circulated among major steel producers and their associations and then discussed.
Thereafter, changes would have to be incorporated in the draft on the basis of these discussions and the final draft sent to
Ministries of Commerce, Finance etc. for their comments. Depending upon the comments of other Ministries, the
preparation of National Steel Policy and its presentation to the Cabinet may take a minimum of six months.
47.5  The Committee is of the view that the preparation of National Steel Policy and its presentation to the Cabinet
should not take more than a period of six months as estimated by Ministry of Steel. The Committee is pleased to note
that basic objective of the National Steel Policy is to prepare a road map for the Indian steel Industry in the context of
ongoing reforms, restructuring and globalization.
48.0  DEVELOPMENT OF INFRASTRUCTURE
48.1  Development of infrastructure can very well ensure an increase in steel demand, as steel is a major component of
construction activities. Promotion of steel in the construction sector has already led to the initiation of steel intensive
technology, which offers a better edge over its counterparts in factors like longer lifecycle and time economy. Moreover,
greater thrust given by the Central Government in channelizing resources for infrastructural developments in the country
like: better roads, highways, bridges, ports, power with a special thrust on rural infrastructure and also providing various
incentives for the housing sector (including rural housing), may all ensure a rise in construction activities in the economy
– accompanied by a corresponding rise in the consumption of steel.
48.2  Revival of infrastucture would automatically lead to the growth of the core sector, which has been witnessing
impressive performance over the last few months. Recent data published by the CSO shows that the index for the core
sector – comprising of the six infrastructure industries, rose to 5.2% in April-January ’03 as compared to a low 3.1% in
same period last year. This was achieved largely by a growth in finished steel (8% in April-January ’03 as compared to a
low 3.5% in same period last year) (Source: CSO)
48.3  Investments in infrastructure have a more direct linkage with the iron and steel industry insofar as the construction
of such projects (especially in the power, road and communications sectors) constitutes the single largest source of
demand for steel. Hence government’s infrastructure development programme – both, proposed (as in Union Budget

122nd Report of Committee on Industry

2003-04) and on-going (the mega-projects like the Golden Quadrilateral and the North-East-South-West Corridor)
activities – all carry good potential for reaping benefits for the domestic steel industry in general and the steel PSUs in
particular.
48.4        The Committee is of the view that the domestic steel industry in general and steel PSUs in particular would be able
to reap benefit from the Government’s Infrastructure Development Programme as announced by the finance Minister in
his budget speech (2003-04) that Government would provide a major thrust to infrastructure like road, railways, airport
and seaport through innovative funding mechanism. The Ministry of Steel should chalk out the strategy for increasing
the steel consumption in government’s infrastructure development programme.

IMPACT OF RATIONALIZATION OF TAX STRUCTURE MADE IN BUDGET 2002-2003 ON THE INDIAN
STEEL
         After having a detailed discussion with the representatives of Indian Steel Alliance, TISCO, Jindal Vijay Nagar the
Committee feels that the existing tax structure needs to be modified to become a source of support for the steel sector.
The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Finance should examine the issue of rationalizing the tax structure on
the following lines :-
          (i)    Customs duty on charge nickel and Metcoke be fixed at 5% for all uses keeping in view the dominant user
industry. The Committee agrees with the proposal of the Ministry of Mines that a differential of in the import
duty between Coking Coal – the raw material for production of Metcoke, and Metcoke should be maintained
by reducing the duty on Coking Coal to 0% and retaining the duty of 5% on Metcoke.
          (ii)    Prior to the budget 2003-2004 integrated steel manufacturers were paying Excise duty on the factory gate,
even when goods were sold from the stockyard. It is now proposed that Excise duty has to be paid on
transaction value at depot. Withdrawal of the existing facility would imply an increase in price of construction
steel in particular for supply from stockyard, affecting SAIL and TISCO specifically. Reinstatement of the
existing notification is required.
          (iii)    While there is an exemption of customs duty on Stainless Steel coin blanks, domestic manufacturers such as
SAIL and other have to pay excise duty and local taxes on similar products made in India. This product is
bought by the Ministry of finance only. Indian companies are looking tenders to overseas companies. The
Department of Economic Affairs must allow exemption of excise duty and sales tax for local products
supplying to them.
          (iv)   Restoration of status quo ante i.e. 5% import duty, 4% SAD and CVED of 16% on import of old ships for
breaking so that (a) tax evasion is reduced and revenue collection is increased and (b) the industry gets the
requisite boost in production.
          (v)   Reduction of excise duty on GP/GC sheets from 16% to 8% for promoting rural mass consumption.
          (vi)   Withdrawal of Notification 345 dated 23.10.97 regarding clearance of recoveries on ship-breaking and
charging of custom duty on non-excisable items like generators, refrigerators, under their respective heading
as is being done in case of bunk oil.
          (vii)  Exemption of excise duty on prefabricated item of steel used in construction in line with exemption of excise
duty on prefabricated cement items.                                                                                            (Para : 36.9)
         The Committee has appreciated from time to time the efforts made by the Ministry of Steel for taking up the matter
of rationalization of tax structure with the Ministry of finance and consideration of the Ministry of Finance would be
beneficial to the Steel Industry in general. However, as per the suggestions of the representative of the Steel Alliance and
also that of the Ministry of Steel, the Committee feels that steel industry still requires the due attention of the Ministry of
Finance for rationalization of the tax structure conducive to steel industry keeping in view also the continued
contribution of steel sector to the national exchequer. In this regard the Committee recommends that the Ministry of
