I.          INTRODUCTION
 
1.1       The Department of Public Enterprises acts as a nodal agency for all PSEs and assists in policy formulation pertaining
to the policy guidelines on performance improvement and evaluation, financial accounting, personnel management and in
related areas. It also collects, evaluates and maintains information on several areas in respect of PSEs. In fulfilling its roles  it
associates itself with other Ministries and Organizations.
 
1.2
              The important   role  and  tasks of  the Department  of  Public  Enterprises  are:-
 
(i)        General policy relating to Public Sector Enterprises;
(ii)       Matters relating to issue of Presidential Directives and Guidelines to    Public Sector Enterprises;
(iii)      Formulation of Policy Guidelines pertaining to Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) in areas like performance
improvement and evaluation, financial   management, personnel management, board structures, wage
settlement, training, industrial relation, vigilance, performance appraisal, etc.;
(iv)     All matters relating to Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between the    Public    Sector Enterprises and
the Administrative Ministries / Departments.;
(v)       Matters relating to  the delegation of powers to Board of Directors;
(vi)     To undertake in-depth studies in respect of significant areas of functioning of central PSEs.;
(vii)Matters relating to International Centre for Promotion of Enterprises (ICPE);
(viii)     Matters relating to Standing Conference of Public Enterprises (SCOPE);
(ix)     To monitor and evaluate the performance of PSEs and to act as a repository of data and to bring out an
Annual Survey for the Parliament; and
(x)    Permanent Machinery of Arbitrators for settlement of   disputes among   Public Sector Enterprises and
Government Departments except disputes      relating to tax  matters.
1.3      The Department has five constituent Divisions, viz,  the Financial Policy Division, the Management Policy Division,
the MOU Division, the Administration & Coordination  Division and Permanent Machinery of Arbitration. The Financial
Policy Division comprises the Public Enterprises Survey Unit, the Policy Planning Unit, and the Wage Policy Unit. The
Management Policy Division comprises the personnel policy unit, training unit, performance indicator and work norms unit
(PIWN Unit) and SC & ST Cell. The MOU Division comprises MOU Unit, Data Bank and Computer Cell. The
Administration & Coordination Division comprises the Administration, Library, Parliament and Coordination Wings and
Hindi Cell. The Permanent Machinery of Arbitration (PMA) provides a forum for settlement of commercial disputes
between two or more PSEs as well as between a Government Department and PSEs.

excellence and increasing productivity.

was also stated that even after  the creation of separate Department of Disinvestment, the Department of Public Enterprises
would  continue to be the nodal Department  for formulating general policy  relating to Public Sector Enterprises (PSEs) and
for finalisation of the Memorandum of Understanding signed  between the Public Sector Enterprises and the Administrative
Ministries.
 
2.8       It was also stated  that  the staff strength of DPE at different levels was reduced substantially.  A 10% cut was  effected
in the sanctioned strength as a part of economy measures in 1996. Thus, review and  rationalisation of the working strength
has always been an ongoing  feature process.  It may be mentioned that DPEs working strength was reduced from 262 in 1985

2.9         The Committee is of the view that the present staff strength numbering about 136 of the Department of  Public
Enterprises needs to be increased in view of the wide roles and functions being undertaken by the department. The Department
needs to be strengthened.
 
III.       SEARCH COMMITTEE AND NAVRATNAS &   MINIRATNAS
 
3.1       The Ministry stated that a  Search Committee is set up with the approval of the Cabinet for selecting   non-official part
time Directors for Navratna and Miniratna PSUs.The Search Committee selects non-official part time Directors of impeccable
stature and background for induction of at least 4 non-official Directors in the case of Navratna PSEs and 3 non-official
Directors in the case of Miniratna PSEs.  Non-official part time Directors are drawn from Public men, Technocrats,
Management Experts & Consultants and Professional Managers in Industry & Trade with high degree of proven ability.  They
play a complimentary role in providing professional and managerial advice to the Board and are appointed with the approval
of ACC on part time basis.
 
3.2       Professionalisation of the Boards of Public Enterprises  is one of the thrust areas of Public Sector Reforms as it is vital
to the functioning of the Board and in achieving high standards of performance. Detailed instructions in this regard were

issued vide DPE O.M. No.18(6)/91-GM dated 16.3.1992. Non-official Directors can play an important role in formulation of a
Company’s strategy and in ensuring that the Board sets its sights high enough. Their contribution to strategy is often through
asking well directed questions, critically but constructively.
 
3.3       The Boards of these PSEs have been broad based by appointment of part-time non-official directors. These
enterprises, subject to certain guidelines,  enjoy freedom to make capital expenditure, enter into joint ventures, effect
organizational restructuring, create and wind up of posts below Board level, raise capital from the domestic and international
markets, establish financial joint ventures subject to equity investments with specified limits etc.
3.4        The Government have also granted enhanced financial, managerial and    operational  autonomy to other profit-
making enterprises, called Miniratnas. These enterprises should have earned profits continuously for the three previous years,
have positive net worth, did not seek budgetary support or guarantees from the Government and   have not   defaulted in the  
repayment of   loans/interests to the Government. These enterprises can incur capital expenditure, enter into joint ventures, set
up technological and strategic alliances, formulate schemes of human resources management, etc. subject to certain
stipulations and guidelines.
3.5        The Committee, however, would like to know  how far  such an  enlarged  Board of Directors  consisting of  official
/non-officials/part time Directors has produced the desired  results .  The Committee feels that  the Department of  Public
Enterprises should undertake  a study in the matter. 
 
3.6       It was also stated  that the grant  of Navratna and Miniratna  status was one time affair meaning thereby once the status
of Navratna or Miniratna had been accorded to a PSU it remains with it  forever notwithstanding the subsequent upheaval in
its  performance.
3.7       The Committee has been informed that the Department of Public Enterprises  confers  Navratna status to the identified
central PSEs, which have comparative advantages to become global giants.  The accordance of Navratna status to Central
PSEs is done in consultation with the concerned administrative Ministries after obtaining approval of the Cabinet.
 
3.8       The Department have further informed that there are 11 Navratnas and 46 Miniratnas . It was further informed that the
procedures for accordance of Navratna status to the enterprises and also for divestment of such status have been referred to the
Group of Ministers.  Final decision is yet to be taken.
 
3.9       The Committee, however, feels that  the Department of  Public Enterprises  should review the performance  of such 
companies in order to  know  whether  such companies are maintaining the status for  which there were awarded such
elevated  positions. The Committee feels that the procedure to accord the Navratna status is a tardy procedure which needs to
be revised by the Government. Secondly, the responsibility of  accordance of the status of Miniratna, presently being  held  by
the respective  administrative ministries themselves to the concerned PSE, must be transferred to the Search Committee under
the Department of Public Enterprises.

V.        THE PURCHASE PREFERENCE POLICY
 
5.1       The Government on the 14
th September, 2000 decided to extend the Purchase Preference Policy for products and services of
central PSEs for two years i.e. up to 31.3.2002.
The main rationale of continuing Purchase Preference Policy and its objectives are as under :
 
a.
                   Some of PSEs are still passing through a period of transition;
b.                  Restructuring and adjustment with new economic environment is in process;
c.                   The turnaround of many sick and loss making PSEs and survival of marginally profit making PSEs is dependent on
their securing adequate order book. 
 
5.2       The Committee has been informed that  this policy has been of immense assistance in this endeavour.The Purchase
Preference Policy has greatly assisted PSEs in withstanding the competition in the market.  Central PSEs to a great extent have been
able to avail the benefit of Purchase Preference Policy by utilising their capacities. The Notice Inviting Tender in each case is 
required  to  specify  provisions  of  purchase  preference  as per DPE’s OM dated 14.9.2000 . The Secretary agreed that some Public
Sector Enterprises are not following guidelines and are not specifying  this  provision in their Notice Inviting Tenders.  
 
5.3       The Committee agrees that the policy of Purchase Preference has been of immense assistance for the PSEs and  has greatly
assisted PSEs in withstanding the competition in the present consumer economy. The  Central PSEs to great extent have been able to
avail the benefit of Purchase Preference Policy by utilising their capacities.  The Committee is ,however, constrained to note that the 
Government of India  on the 14
th September, 2000 decided to extend the Purchase Preference Policy for products and services of
Central PSEs for two years  only i.e. up to 31.3.2002.
 
5.4       The Committee recommends that   the Purchase Preference Policy should be extended  for another  3 years , i.e,. upto 
31.3.2004.
 
5.5       The Committee in this connection would like to place on record that the Minister of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises
has recently brought out a Brochure  indicating the highlights and the achievements of the Public Sector Undertakings (Annexture-I).
The Minister has in the brochure  indicated that the Public Sector has played a pivotal role in the industrial and economic
development of the country.  The growth in Public Sector Enterprises has been noteworthy, the Minister indicated.  He has further
stated that the government is committed to reform and restructure the public sector enterprises so that they stand poised with the best
of their kind in the world.
 
5.6       The Committee is very much concerned to note that  certain Central PSEs/Central Govt. Agencies are not including the
Purchase Preference clause in their Notice Inviting Tender (NIT) depriving the other Central PSEs to avail the benefit of the
Government Policy.   This non-cooperative attitude of some of the PSEs does not go well with the overall policy of the government 
to improve the health of  PSEs.  The highlights indicated by the Minister would remain only highlights  without a major shift in their
health status.  If there has to be a major reversal in the health condition of the PSEs, all the PSEs need to have mutual cooperation. 
Purchase Preference Policy is one such gray area which, if followed scrupulously by all the PSUs, would improve the performance of
all the ailing PSEs.
 
5.7       The Committee, therefore, recommends that all the Administrative Ministries/Government Departments should issue the
instructions to the PSEs under their jurisdiction to comply with the policy of the Government on Purchase Preference. 
5.8       The Committee also recommends that the Department of Public Enterprises should ensure and  monitor the compliance of the
Purchase Preference Policy. In this regard all Public Sector Enterprises may be asked to submit documents of NIT to the Department
of Public Enterprises for regular compliance.

VI.    MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
6.1       Under  the present set-up of the Department of Public Enterprises, the MoU Division is one of the five constituent
Divisions of the Department of Public Enterprises which co-ordinates the entire exercise of finalizing the Memorandum of 
Understandings  (MoUs) in respect of all the Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) numbering about 108.  The New
Industrial Policy of 1991 laid greater emphasis of the MoU system requiring all PSEs to be brought under the ambit of the
MoU system.
6.2       With the advent of the privatization and globalisation in Indian Economy mainly after the implementation of  New
Industrial Policy of 1991, the Central PSEs have been exposed to the domestic and international competition. Now, these 
PSEs  have   to   compete  with  the    Private  Sector  in   the   consumer  economy. In such a stiff competitive environment, it
is essential for a PSE to have an edge of both cost and quality over its competitors. MoU system has been accepted by the
PSEs as a panacea  since it has given them more freedom to take decisions independently  and operate more effectively and
proficiently.
 
6.3     The Government of India following the recommendation of the Arjun Sengupta Committee introduced the MOU
system. The MOU system has grown at a steady rate and from 4 MOUs signed in the year 1987-88  it has grown to  108 PSEs
which have  signed MoUs for the year 1999-2000.
 
6.4       The main objective of the MOU system was to improve the performance of the PSEs through the mechanism of
increased autonomy and accountability and also to provide a uniform method of evaluation of the performance of the
managements of the PSEs in an objective and transparent manner. Further, it was also envisaged to provide a clarity of
goals/objectives to be achieved by PSEs.
6.5       The purpose of the MOU system are to :
 
(i)
      Measure the performance of PSEs taking into account the complexity of fusing social and financial objectives
and translating them into measurable parameters:
(ii)    Ensure simultaneous increase in autonomy as well as accountability;
(iii)   Set up new institutions and administrative & personnel systems;
(iv)  Replace `multiple principles with multiple objectives’ with clarity in goals and objectives;
(v)    Break the vicious circle of NOT ME syndrome.
 
6.6       The Department of Public Enterprises provides a neutral forum for finalising the MOUs which is essential to make it
an objective system. The Department provides the entire technical and analytical inputs such as preparation of the common
guidelines for drafting of MOUs, preparation of background notes and critiques on the draft MOUs submitted by the PSEs,
Organisation of MOU negotiation and evaluation meetings, etc., of the expert group called Ad-Hoc Task Force(ATF) in order
to finalise the MOUs and evaluate the performance of the PSEs at the end of the year. Department of Public Enterprises
ensures that the entire MOU exercise is carried out in an objective, transparent and broad based manner.
 
6.7       It acts as the permanent  secretariat to the HPC and ATF. The main functions of the MoU Division are:
·
        To provide logistical support to the Ad-Hoc Task Force.  It is expected to provide not only administrative
support but also technical support to the Ad-Hoc Task Force.
·        To act as buffer between the Ad-Hoc  Task  Force  members and the   signatories to  the MoUs, i e,  PSEs   and
Administrative  Ministries .  It is  expected  that ATF   members will have contacts with signatories to MoU 
through  MoU Division only.
·        To  develop information and data base on various PSEs signing MoU.
·        To  prepare agenda and background papers for the  High Power Committee.
·        To monitor the progress of  the MoUs.  The MoU  Division keeps a watch  on various stages involving in the
preparation of MoUs to ensure that all parties involved in the process adhere to the relevant  guidelines and
deadlines.
·        To  provide advice  and counsel to the MoU signatories on methodological and conceptual aspects  of the
MoU  Policy.
·        To coordinate research  and training on various aspects  of MoU Policy.
 
6.8       During the oral evidence , Secretary, Department of Public Enterprises, informed the Committee that  expenditure 
reforms  commission have recommended that the work relating to the preparation and evaluation of MoU could be left  to the

Administrative Ministries and  MoU Division would be abolished.
 
6.9       On a query as to  whether the object of signing MoUs would be achieved in the absence of a over-seeing agency, i.e.
Department of Public Enterprises, the  Department felt that an overseeing  body like DPE is essential for implementation of
the MoU system to fully achieve the objectives of MoU system .
 
6.10     If the task of preparation and evaluation of MoU is given to the same Administrative Ministry then the main feature of
MoU system, i.e objectivity, neutrality and transparency would suffer. Also there will be no uniform method to cross compare
the performance of the PSEs in other Ministries as being done at present by the Department of Public Enterprises, the
Department felt. Moreover,  objectivity, neutrality and transparency would suffer.
 
6.11        It was agreed that a Ministry can not act as a third neutral party in the process of negotiating and finalising the MoUs
between the Ministry and the PSE under that Ministry.   This will lead to  setting up of  mutual  admiration  club. Under the
present system, all policy issues relating to the MoU system are dealt with by the HPC through DPE in a  centralised manner.
If administrative Ministries are to implement the MoU system, in that case each Ministry will have to approach the HPC
separately.  This will  usher in  an era  of cacophony leaving behind  the era of cohesion.
 
6.12        It was further stated that in case MoU is to be prepared, evaluated and monitored by same Ministry, then separate
unit with some experts will have to be given the task. Such a step  would invite requirement of a large staff strength taking all
the Ministries together as compared to one small single unit of MoU Division in the Department of Public Enterprises.
 
6.13        If preparation/evaluation etc. of MoU is in the same Administrative Ministry, then the influence on concerned public
sector undertakings under that Ministry would obviously make MoU infructuous.
 
6.14       After hearing the  Secretary, Department of Public Enterprises, the Committee expressed its   unhappiness  that the
Expenditure Reforms Commission has recommended that the task of preparing MoUs can be left as well to the concerned
administrative Ministries.  The Committee feels that  the said Commission  in an anxiety  to reduce the  Government
expenditure under mined the role  of   a  nodel agency, i.e. , Department of Public Enterprises without  evaluating  its
performance over the years .
 
6.15        The Committee is also of the view that a unified central agency to implement the MoU system is certainly more cost
effective rather than having separate MoU Units in all the Administrative Ministries. Similarly, pooling expertise  for all the
Ministries from the same ATF as is being done at present by the Department of Public Enterprises is also cost effective.
 
6.16        The Committee  further   feels  that  the  Department  of  Public  Enterprises   provides  a neutral  forum  for 
finalising  the  MoUs, which  is   essential   to make it an objective system, and it should not be abolished.
 
6.17       The Committee is also  of the firm view that  the object of signing MoUs would not be achieved in the absence of an
over-seeing agency, i.e., the Department of Public Enterprises.
 
6.18        The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the proposal of abolishing MoU Division may be shelved. As
the country is now on the threshold of making PSEs  more vibrant, productive and efficient, it is very essential that the MoU
Division should be further strengthened instead of being abolished. Strengthening of MoU Division would go a long way in
making PSEs profitable and responsive to competitiveness.
 
6.19        The Committee, therefore, again recommends that   the Department of Public Enterprises should be strengthened so
as to make it more responsive.

/non-officials/part time Directors has produced the desired  results .  The Committee feels that  the Department of  Public
Enterprises should undertake  a study in the matter. 
( Para 3.5 )
 
 
            The Committee, however, feels that  the Department of  Public Enterprises  should review the performance  of such 
companies in order to  know  whether  such companies are maintaining the status for  which there were awarded such
elevated  positions. The Committee feels that the procedure to accord the Navratna status is a tardy procedure which needs to
be revised by the Government. Secondly, the responsibility of  accordance of the status of Miniratna, presently being  held  by
the respective  administrative ministries themselves to the concerned PSE, must be transferred to the Search Committee under
the Department of Public Enterprises  .  
               ( Para 3.9 )
 
 
             The existing rate of Rs.9000 per head for  non-recurring expenses towards infrastructure, office equipments, prepatary
works, training expenses, follow up services, etc., of nodal agency, may   prove                    insufficient.   The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the existing rate of Rs.9000 per head may be reviewed. If necessary, the allocation may be
increased. 
( Para 4.3 )
 
The Committee feels that the  Department of Public Enterprises  is overburdened after being  assigned the tasks of
counseling, retraining and redeployment of rationalized employees of Central PSEs. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Department of Public Enterprises should involve/appoint new  technocrats, Research Officers, skilled workers etc. 
( Para 4.5 )
 
 
The Committee recommends that   the Purchase Preference Policy     should be extended  for another  3 years , i.e,.
upto  31.3.2004.
     ( Para 5.4 )
 
 
The Committee is very much concerned to note that  certain Central PSEs/Central Govt. Agencies are not including the
Purchase Preference clause in their Notice Inviting Tender (NIT) depriving the other Central PSEs to avail the benefit of the
Government Policy.   This non-cooperative attitude of some of the PSEs does not go well with the overall policy of the
government  to improve the health of  PSEs.  The highlights indicated by the Minister would remain only highlights  without a
major shift in their health status.  If there has to be a major reversal in the health condition of the PSEs, all the PSEs need to
have mutual cooperation.  Purchase Preference Policy is one such gray area which, if followed scrupulously by all the PSUs,
would improve the performance of all the ailing PSEs.
 
    ( Para 5.6 )
           
 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that all the Administrative Ministries/Government Departments should issue
the instructions to the PSEs under their jurisdiction to comply with the policy of the Government on Purchase Preference. 
  ( Para 5.7 )
 
            The Committee also recommends that the Department of Public Enterprises should ensure and  monitor the compliance
of the Purchase Preference Policy. In this regard all Public Sector Enterprises may be asked to submit documents of NIT to the
Department of Public Enterprises for regular compliance.
( Para 5.8 )

              After hearing the  Secretary, Department of Public Enterprises, the Committee expressed its unhappiness  that the
Expenditure Reforms Commission has recommended that the task of preparing MoUs can be left as well to the concerned
administrative Ministries.  The Committee feels that  the said Commission  in an anxiety  to reduce the  Government
expenditure under mined the role  of   a  nodel agency, i.e. , Department of Public Enterprises without  evaluating  its
performance over the years .
               ( Para 6.14 )
 
 
           The Committee is also of the view that a unified central agency to implement the MoU system is certainly more cost
effective rather than having separate MoU Units in all the Administrative Ministries. Similarly, pooling expertise  for all the
Ministries from the same ATF as is being done at present by the  Department of Public Enterprises is also cost effective.
(  Para 6.15 )
 
 
          The Committee  further   feels  that  the  Department  of  Public  Enterprises   provides  a neutral  forum  for  finalising 
the  MoUs, which  is   essential   to make it an objective system, and it should not be abolished.
( Para 6.16 )
 
 
          The Committee is also  of the firm view that  the object of signing MoUs would not be achieved in the absence of an
over-seeing agency, i.e. Department of Public Enterprises.
 
( Para 6.17 )
 
 
          The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the proposal of abolishing MoU Division may be shelved. As the
country is now on the threshold of making PSEs  more vibrant, productive and efficient, it is very essential that the MoU
Division should be further strengthened instead of being abolished. Strengthening of MoU Division would go a long way in
making PSEs profitable and responsive to competitiveness.
( Para 6.18 )
 
 
               The Committee, therefore,  again recommends that   the Department of Public Enterprises should be strengthened so
as to make it more responsive.
( Para 6.19 )

PERFORMANCE OF PUBLIC SECTOR ENTERPRISE – NEW PERSPECTIVE
 
            Department of Public Enterprises present to the Parliament every year an overall view of the fiscal, financial and socio-
economic performance of Central Public Sector Enterprises. The Public Enterprises  Survey 1998-99 compiled by DPE was
laid in the Lok Sabha on 10.3.2000 and in Rajya Sabha on 15.3.2000. The major highlights of the performance of PSEs during
1998-99 were as follows:-
 
·
        Increase in investment in Central public enterprises by Rs.8153 crore i.e. from Rs.2,21,987 crores in 1997-98 to
Rs.2,30,140 crores in 1998-99, an increase of 3.7%.

·        Turnover/operating income has increased upto 12.3%. In absolute terms, the turnover has increased by Rs.33,998
crores i.e. from Rs.2,75,996 crores in 1997-98 to Rs.30.9.994 crores in 1998-99.
 
·        Profit before interest, depreciation and tax has increased to Rs.56,532 crores in 1998-99 from Rs.53,068 crores in
1997-98, thereby registering a growth of 6.5%.
 
·        Profit before interest & tax has registered an increase of 6.9% i.e. from Rs.37.212 crores in 1997-98 to Rs.39.760
crores in 1998-99.
 
·        Overall performance as measured by MoUs – out of 108 MoU signing Public Sector Enterprises evaluated, 42
were rated as excellent and 30 as ‘very good’.
·        Capital employed of public sector enterprises has gone up by 7.9% i.e. from Rs.2,53,751 crores in 1997-98 to
Rs.2,73,793 crores in 1998-99.
 
·
        Public Sector Enterprises as a whole have earned a return on investment (profit before interest and tax to capital
employed) of 14.5%.

99, an increase of 9.9%. Profit before tax to Net Worth for 1998-99 works out to 13.3%.
 
·        Foreign exchange earnings by exports of goods and rendering various types of services amounted to Rs.18,827
crores during 1998-99. Foreign exchange utilization has come down marginally from Rs.60,837 crores in previous
year to Rs.59,549 crores in the current year.
 
·        Central Public Sector Enterprises have declared a dividend of Rs.4932 crores during 1998-99 as against dividend
of Rs.,609 crores in the previous year, an increase of 36.7%. The dividend pay out ratio has gone up to 37.3% as
against 26.3% in the previous year.
·        Inventory in terms of number of days of cost of production/services rendered has come down from 69 days to 64
days.
 
·        Expenditure on social overheads has increased by Rs.256 crores i.e. from Rs.3323 crores in 1998-99.
 
·        The contribution to Central Exchequer by way of excise duty, customs duty, corporate tax, interest on Central
Government loans, dividend and other duties and taxes has gone up by Rs.4633 crores i.e. from Rs.42,292 crores to
Rs.46,925 crores, as increase of 11%.
