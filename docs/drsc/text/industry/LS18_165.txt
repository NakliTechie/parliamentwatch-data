REPORT
The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Heavy Industries 
(Ministry of Heavy Industries & Public Enterprises) on the Recommendations contained in the  151 st      Report (of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry) on  Demands for Grants (2004-05) pertaining to
Department of Heavy Industries. The Report  was presented /laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha on  the
26th August, 2003.
2.         Action Taken Notes have been received from Department of Heavy Industries   in respect of the  
recommendations contained in the Report.   These have been categorized as follows:
Chapter - I-  The recommendations/observations, which have been accepted by the Ministry :   Para 38; 43; 47; 82 and
95
  The Committee is pleased to observe that  the   Ministry  has accepted its  recommendation as  mentioned in Chapter –I
of the  Report. 
Chapter - II- The recommendations/observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the
Government's replies:  Para45; 90 and 97
In respect of these recommendations the Committee is  convinced  with the logic and explanation  advocated by the
Ministry  and therefore   does not want to  pursue  further. 
Chapter - III- The recommendations/observations in respect of which the Committee has not accepted replies of the
Ministry: - Para   9; 21; 23; 26 28; 31; 33; 37;  57;  58 ; 59; 61; 68; 71; 74; 75; 79; 83; 91;  96
 The Committee expresses its concern over the non-implementations  of recommendations contained in  Chapter-III  and
desires that the Ministry should furnish  logical  explanation  in respect of these recommendations.  
Chapter - IV- The recommendations/observations in respect of which  final replies of the  Ministry have not been
received:-  Para 44; 48; 62 ; 87; 92
 In respect  of recommendation Para 4.30  in Chapter-IV, the Ministry has  either   furnished the interim replies or
couched its language in  vague terms like  noted for compliance/guidance, therefore, the Committee desires  that the
Ministry should furnish Action Taken Notes in  respects of recommendations categorized   in Chapter-IV  in detail and
to the point, as per the intention and spirit of the recommendations of the Committee.
3.         The Details of the ATR is being discussed in various Chapters in the succeeding pages.

1.1        The Committee concurs that while releasing the funds for VRS to the PSEs it should be ensured that the dues of

for any such action of diversion. All the PSEs are invariably asked to furnish utilization certificates immediately upon
disbursement of the pending dues to employees.

capacity.

RECOMMENDATION
3.2       Increasing non-plan expenditure
3.2.1    The Committee expresses concern on increasing non-plan expenditure.  The Committee also notes that this
increase in non-plan expenditure is mostly due to writing off outstanding loans and interests, loans to PSEs for VRS
payments, granting interest and guarantee fee subsidies to the PSEs. The Committee expresses its deep concern over this
trend of financing the PSEs and observes such liabilities need to be overcome in a time bound manner.
ACTION TAKEN
3.2.2    The Government provides support for viable revival schemes of PSEs. Generally such revival schemes contain
fresh capital infusion for investments, write off of loans, conversion of loan into equity etc. When all concerned parties
agree to the revival plan then sacrifices have to be made by all including Government to enable revival of the PSEs. 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
3.2.3    The Committee of the view that replies of DHI in regard to the Committee observation on increasing non-plan
expenditure states bare facts only i.e. the Government provides support for viable revival schemes of PSEs. Generally
such revival schemes contain fresh capital infusion for investments, write off of loans, conversion of loan into equity
etc. The Committee's major concern is that the new infusion of fund is going into non-plan expenditure instead of
creating capital and  infrastructure for the company like technological up gradation and capacity enhancement etc.
Government is only shedding the burden but it does not mean that PSEs are no strong enough to face competition. 
Because technological upgradation, marketing, human resource development, research and development are the initial
strength of the company which require adequate investment to make PSEs  viable   and competitive

 3.20  Constraints being faced by the PSEs
3.20 .1 As regards the constraints being faced by the PSEs, the Committee observes as under:
(i)
                  Lack of orders mainly from the Government and other PSEs.  In several cases the sickness of one PSE
has had corollary impact on other PSEs too.
(ii)                Another issue has been the fluctuating duty regime that have made PSEs non-competitive vis-a-vis other
global and domestic players.
(iii)               Obsolete technology and investment crunch due to reducing capital investment over the years in
enhancing the capacities of the PSEs has adversely affected the PSEs that have not been able to take the
advantage of economies of scale.
(iv)
              The PSEs at present are reeling under the burden of restructuring and rationalising their manpower.  A
larger part of their resources goes into settling the dues often at a higher rate of interest.
(v)                The Committee is of the view that in view of new economy and reducing government support, PSEs
shall have to come to terms with new market realities.  With expanding domestic, regional and global
market, PSEs shall have to take benefit of new opportunities.
(vi)             In view of the NCMP that envisages a revitalized Public Sector, the Ministry of Heavy Industries and
Public Sector Enterprises should evolve a Doctrine emphasizing that social obligations of the PSEs is a
function of their financial health, both are not contradictory but complementary to each other.  Therefore,
to meet its social obligations the Public Sector must regain their financial health.(Para 96)
 
ACTION TAKEN
  3.20.2 Government have taken a number of steps for improving the performance of PSEs.  These include manpower
rationalization, temporary financial assistance to sick units, purchase preference, restructuring/asset sale, MOU system
for close monitoring and disinvestment etc.  As per the Public Sector Policy contained in National Common Minimum
Programme, (NCMP), profit making PSEs (9 PSEs)will not be privatized and are to be strengthened by providing
greater autonomy. 27-loss making PSEs will be considered for revival/closure.  This Department is engaged in an
exercise on carefully appraising the fundamentals and capabilities of each of the PSEs and formulating restructuring
plans for each of them with the assistance of professional consulting agencies. As distinct from previous efforts,
restructuring strategies now being undertaken would incorporate comprehensive financial, managerial, technological,
organizational and marketing restructuring to lead to an enterprise whose working would be meaningful and profitability
sustainable.   Such proposals shall be placed before the Board for Reconstruction of Public Sector Enterprises
(BRPSE). 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
3.20.3  The Committee is of the view that PSE which has been envisaged   once as the 'Temple of Modern India' during
the heyday of welfare state when these PSEs met the social obligation and thus plaged pivotal role in economic and
social building of the nation.  These PSEs are now reeling under deep trouble due to lack of orders, fluctuating duty
refine, obsolete  technology and investment crunch and are under the burden of restructuring and retionalising there man
power.  The PSEs are not  competitive and resilient enough to face the stark-realities of fast emerging now market
economy.  All previous steps for improving the financial  health of PSEs fumbled one after another.  Under the
circumstances, the Government has to take careful and cautious efforts to rescuers the PSEs out of red.  The constitution
of Board for Reconstitution of Public Sector Enterprises (BRPSE) under National Common Minimum Programmes is a
new spectrum of rays of hopes which can inject 'Sanjivini booti' (new lease of life)  there PSEs.
