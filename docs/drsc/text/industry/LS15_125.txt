REPORT
 
Steel Authority of India (SAIL), the leading steel making company in India and the World’s thirteenth largest steel
producer in terms of crude steel production, is a Company registered under the Indian Companies Act, 1956 and is an
enterprise of the Government of India. SAIL was incorporated in January, 1973 as a holding company for steel and associated
input industries.  From 1973  to 1978, several steel and alloy  manufacturing companies  and iron ore mines were merged into
and  combined with SAIL’s  operation, while the Government of India (GOI) transferred the ownership  of certain consulting,
construction, mining and fire bricks operation from SAIL to other entities. 
 
  The authorized capital of SAIL is Rs. 5000 crore and the paid-up capital of the Company as on 31
st December 2002
was Rs. 4,130.40 crore of which 85.82% was held by the Government of India and the balance 14.18% by the financial
institutions/GDR-holders banks/employees/individuals etc.  The Company recorded a sales turnover of Rs. 15502 crore in
2001-02, however, due to fall in international prices of steel products,  coupled with continued sluggishness in the economy,
the price realization in the domestic market during 2001-02 declined, resulting into the post-tax net loss to the tune of Rs.
1706.89 crore during the year 2001-02.  As a result of loss, the Company has not declared a dividend for the year ended 31
st
march, 2002.  The gross margin (profit before depreciation and interest) and net loss for the nine-months ended 31 st
December, 2002 was Rs. 1334.40 crore and Rs. 545.96 crore respectively.  The company recorded a sales turnover of Rs.
13097.43 crore during this period. 
 
            Global Steel Industry for the measure part of 2001 remained a victim of slow down in demand, sliding price levels,
low realizations, losses and/or falling profits.  However, from the beginning of 2002, the industry entered a recovery phase,
with the gradual firming up of international prices and demand.  During the  financial year 2002-03, the market is signaling an
upward trend.   The year 2003 may well see a price rise in the domestic and the  international steel market.  There have been
price increases in steel products, which have been absorbed by the market.  There has been improvement in production also as
compared to last year and efforts are being made towards achieving maximum capacity utilization matching with the
increased market demand.  The company is vigorously giving greater thrust on the cost control measures during the current
year as well.  With the improvement in market demand, increase in net sales realization and cost control efforts, the company
expects much better results during the financial year 2002-03.
The turnaround in the market trends is reflected most prominently in the performance of the public sector steel giant,
Steel Authority of India Ltd. The unaudited financial results of SAIL for April-December (Q3) 2002 reveals a sharp
improvement in the performance with losses being slashed to Rs. 79 crore in Q3 ’02 - an improvement of Rs. 507 crore over
the corresponding period of the previous year. SAIL’s loss for April-December ’02 was brought to a level of Rs. 558 crore,
showing a recovery of 57 per cent over the results of the same period in 2001. This helped the company to post a cash profit
of Rs 215 crore in Q3 ’02 as against a loss of Rs 297 crore in Q3 ’01 and a cash profit of Rs. 305 crore in the first nine months
of the current financial year.  SAIL had suffered a cash loss of Rs. 434 crore during the corresponding period in the last fiscal.

SAIL markets and sells most of its products through in house Central Marketing Organisation (CMO) which has a
wide network  of 38 Branch Offices and 47 outlets located in major cities and towns throughout India.  International Trade
Division of SAIL looks after export of steel products to countries worldwide.  Raw materials Division manages operations of
key captive mines and quarries  of SAIL.  The captive mines of iron ore and flux meet most of the iron ore requirement and
half of the flux  requirement   of SAIL.
 
SAIL has undertaken major modernization programmes at Durgapur, Rourkela and Bokaro Steel Plants. Under the
modernization programmes, energy efficient Basic, Oxygen Furnace (BOF) technology for steel-making has been adopted in
place of obsolete Open Hearth Furnaces at Durgapur and Rourkela.  Instead of Ingot Casting, Continuous Casting facilities
have been provided in all the three Plants. The modernization projects are being funded from the internal resource of SAIL
without any budgetary support from the Government.  The total investment in modernization of the three plants is likely to be
about Rs. 12,650 crore.
 
 The main objectives of modernization are:
 
(i)
                  Upgradation of technology
(ii)                Improvement in productivity and yields
(iii)               Reduction in energy consumption
(iv)              Improvement in quality of products
(v)                Reduction in cost of production
(vi)              Reduced environmental pollution

in the quality of raw material, ageing of the plant, obsolete technology, inadequate air pollution control measures and
technological imbalance.  The basic objectives underlying the modernization scheme of DSP were Enabling  the Plant to
attain properly matched steady integrated operation; Improvement in quality and consistency of raw material; Upgradation of
technology; Reduction in cost of production; Pollution Control;  and Improvement in quality of steel produced

STEPSTAKEN FOR TIMELY COMPLETION
 
The Committee was informed that DSP management had taken following steps for timely completion of projects: -
 
(i)
            Close monitoring/follow-up and frequent project reviews at all levels including MD/DSP, Chairman/SAIL and
Secretary (Steel).  Chairman/SAIL frequently reviewed the progress of Modernisation work and took meetings with the
contracting agencies to expedite the work.  Secretary (Steel) reviewed with the contracting agencies once every quarter
and firm commitments were taken from them.  Also, spot decisions were taken to thrash-out the problems.
 
(ii)
            Chasing/follow-up at sub-suppliers’/vendors’ manufacturing works including visits of officials to CIS countries to
expedite strucurals/equipment/refractory supplies.
 
(iii)            Financial assistance/assistance for steel as adjustable advances extended to main contractors in the interest of
expediting progress of supplies/site works and direct payment to some sub-vendors to ensure expeditious delivery of
critical plant and equipment.
 
(iv)            Ensured earliest completion/commissioning of main production units and deployment of DSP’s
operation/maintenance services personnel for erection/cold trial to help expedite hot trial/commissioning.
 
(v)            Rendered continuous help to contractors in planning and monitoring for critical activities.
 
(vi)            Taking over by DSP of a major portion of equipment and part refractory supplied from the scope of TPE Russia for
BF-4 to expedite procurement.
 
The Committee feels that steps taken for timely completion of modernistion package proved inefficient   and   ineffective
as the modernisation projects suffered from time and cost over-run.

Reasons for Delay
(i)
            Disintegration of erstwhile USSR which resulted in delays in supply of imported structurals, equipment, refractories
etc. from TPE, Russia and affected BF, BOF, SP & RMHC packages.
(ii)            Delays in equipment and structurals  supply by indigenous suppliers affected most of the packages.
(iii)            Increase in quantity of work in respect of civil & structurals job for BF, SP and RMHC packages as compared to
original estimates of the consortium resulted in extra time for execution.
(iv)            Inadequate mobilization of resources at site work by contracting agencies resulted in slow pace of work as compared
to targets.
(v)            Funds constraint of contracting agencies and intra-consortium disputes resulted in slow progress in major global
packages.
 
The DSP management stated that prior to disintegration of the erstwhile USSR, all the economic activities in CIS Nations
were controlled from Moscow and the manufacturing units and transporting authorities had to adhere to the delivery schedules
and commitments.  Subsequent to the break-up of USSR into different Nations, these manufacturing units were being
controlled by different authorities and a number of new regulations were introduced.  This resulted in delay in  manufacturing
activities.  Tyazhpromexport (TPE)’s job has suffered seriously, resulting in delays in supply of imported equipment,
structures and refractories from Russia for four global packages viz, Blast Furnace, Sinter Plant, Basic Oxygen Furnaces and
Raw Material Handling Complex.

Rourkela Steel Plant was initially set up with a capacity of 1 million tonne per annum (Mtpa) of ingot steel in 1960-61

operated, more or less, satisfactorily by reaching its rated capacity in 1964-65, the expanded capacity could never be
achieved. The best production level of 1.5 Mtpa of ingot steel was achieved in 1976-77 after which the production capacity
deteriorated. The unsatisfactory performance level was attributable to various factors like deteriorating and inconsistent
quality of raw materials and technological obsolescence. Various studies were carried out to determine the manner in  which
the optimum performance level of the Plant could be achieved. Based on these studies, a scheme for "Renovation &
Technological Upgradation"

  Modernisation Of Bokaro  Steel Plant
 
            Bokaro Steel Plant, conceived in 1959, is the fourth steel plant among the integrated steel plants in the public sector.
The Plant was conceived in 1959 as a 4 MT plant right from the conceptual stage, 1.7 MT being an intermediate stage for the
project. The construction work for the project was started in late Sixties and the Plant went into commercial production in
1972 with the commissioning of its first Blast Furnace. Steel making adopting the BOF route began a year later. The
downstream facilities -Slabbing Mill, Hot Strip Mill and Cold Rolling Mill were put into operation in stages. The. 1.7 MT
stage was completed in 1978 with the commissioning of third Blast Furnace. The work of 4 MT Expansion was. taken ~p
simultaneously and was progressively completed by Nov'90.     The entire steel making process of Bokaro Steel Plant was
based on the Ingot Casting route which was a technology prevalent in late Sixties. Considering the  various advantages of
Continuous Casting as regards to yield improvement,  conservation of energy and improved quality, Govt. of India sanctioned
Stage I  Modernisation of Bokaro Steel Plant in Jul'93 with a commissioning schedule of Jul'97. All major contracts were
awarded in 12 months. Trials for the Steel Refining Unit were started in Sep'97 and the first Continuous Casting Machine
started its trial runs in Nov'97. Subsequently, all units of Stage I Modernisation have been completed by Jan'00. With the
upgradation of the Hot Strip Mill, the quality of the finished products is of international standard and is being well accepted in
both global and domestic market. As a flat product plant, Bokaro's products include a wide range of Hot Rolled and Cold
Rolled Coils, Plates and Sheets, Galvanised Sheets and Black Plates, serving the needs of railways, tube makers and
automobile industry , L.PG cylinder manufacturers, defence and Petro-based  Industry etc. Quality conscious Bokaro is also
concerned with customers' satisfaction.

Given the accumulated losses by ASP and its current performance, the divestment process did not attract interest from any
suitable bidder. Though the last date for receipt of EoI elapsed, no response was received from any bidder. 
 
Attempt was also made to persuade Ministry of Defence for  taking over ASP.  In spite of repeated efforts, the Ministry of
Defence informed MoS that it will not be possible for them to take over ASP.
In view of the above, the divestment option can not be exercised at this juncture.  Alternative options like rationalizing
production units, partial operations, technological efficiency enhancement, manpower reduction etc., are being implemented.
 
3. Divestment Of VISL
The only party left in the divestment of VISL, M/s Sun Flag Iron and Steel Company Ltd have been issued all requisite
informations & documents desired by them. The Party had been asked to submit the price bid by 9
th September 2002.
However, the Party asked for further information / clarification on 9 th September 2002. The issue is being reviewed by IDBI
and their recommendation in this regard is awaited, based on which SAIL will take a view.

Disinvestment of IISCO:
SAIL had invited Expressions of Interest  (EoI) through its Global Advisors IDBI-CASD in April-2000 for Joint Venture
participation in IISCO against which EoIs were received from 5 parties. Out of these, three parties namely,   VO
Tyazpromexport (TPE), BHP Ltd.  and  Mitsui & Co. Ltd. were shortlisted.  After the due-diligence study/visit  by the 
shortlisted parties, BHP-Mitsui consortium gave an offer only for a part of IISCO’s asset, hence the proposal was not
considered further.
The only other party remaining in the fray, M/s TPE submitted a proposal  in November 2001 for joint venture
participation in IISCO.  However, this proposal, on scrutiny, was found to be half-baked, as it did not cover all the operations
of IISCO.The proposal was also dependent on Rupee-Rouble Escrow Fund and grants/concessions from GoI and Government
of West Bengal.   Subsequent to the meeting of IRIGC in February 2002, an expert group visited Russia to hold talks with
TPE and other Russian agencies for working out a viable revival plan for IISCO.  TPE sought time for this but no progress
has been made in the matter since then.
In view of the above, Disinvestment of IISCO may not be possible under present circumstances.

R&D Inputs in SAIL Plants
 
The focus of R&D activities has been to improve steel technologies in SAIL with the aim to reduce cost and improve
product quality and address the market requirements of new value added products.
 
The R&D Centre of SAIL (RDCIS) has contributed significantly in the development of a number of Process
Technologies.  Some of these are:
 
(i)
                  Continuous casting of CRNO steel at RSP.  This has helped RSP to close down ingot casting shop and high
energy consuming slabbing mill.  As a result, RSP became the first plant in SAIL to route 100% of production through
continuous casting.
 
(ii)
                Introduction of innovative technologies, use of higher percentage of imported coal and optimization of process
parameters have significantly improved blast furnace productivity in SAIL plants.

(iii)               A number of energy conservation schemes have been implemented in SAIL plants through design and utilization

(iv)              Quantum improvement in campaign life of converter has been achieved in SAIL plants through introduction of

(v)                Development of a number of sensors and their utilization in SAIL plants has resulted in improvement of product

 Marketing policy of SAIL for improving market share
 
The prime quality Iron & Mild Steel materials produced by SAIL Plants (excluding IISCO) are sold through Central
Marketing Organisation (CMO).
 
            With a view to evolve a sales plan attuned to the market conditions, a detailed exercise is undertaken before the
commencement of each financial year which inter alia include plantwise, productwise, segmentwise sales plan keeping in
view the demand and supply scenario in the country as well as production capability of each of the plants of SAIL.
 
            Simultaneously the order booking schemes and strategies are finalized.
 
            In order to compete in the highly competitive market, marketing strategies are reoriented based on the prevailing
market conditions and requirements and customers’ expectations.  Some of the marketing strategies presently adopted by
SAIL are as under :-
 
(i)
                  Orienting product-mix to suit market requirements.
(ii)                Adoption of Key Account Management Process to provide total service to major / strategic accounts.
(iii)               Special emphasis on marketing value added products.
(iv)              Development and nurturing long term relationships with customers by entering into MOU/ Annual tie ups with
them
(v)                Increasing sales through net work of dealers.
(vi)              Offering full package of steel materials to projects.
(vii)             Increasing  availability of finished steel through conversion.

Help required from the Government
(A)       Coal
i)
                    Steep price increase by CIL over the years has been putting lot of pressure on our competitiveness in the market.
Long-term pricing mechanism is being discussed between SAIL and coal companies, however, it is proposed that
there should be an independent arbitrator to look into the pricing mechanism of domestic coking coal.
ii)                   The availability of indigenous coal is depleting at a very fast rate, thus forcing higher usage of imported coking
coal in the coal blend and consequent pressure on ports. Haldia port is most economical for transportation of
imported coal as three integrated steel plants of SAIL are located in the Eastern Region. Capacity at Haldia  port is
inadequate for handling coal for supply to these steel plants. Moreover, due to low draft at Haldia Port, SAIL is
constrained to part unload the ships at Vizag or Paradip port before bringing it to Haldia port. Help is required for
upgradation of Haldia port.
 
(B)   Power
 
Production in steel plants has been affected in H-1 by inconsistencies in power supply.  This was especially so
in eastern region where four of SAIL’s plants are located.  There were a large number of total power failures,
voltage dips and frequency of power supply remained abnormally high for long durations.
 
High frequency of power supply resulted in loss of captive power generation, equipment damage and erosion
in life of rotating equipment, apart from increased power bill due to higher drawal of power.
 
It is expected that incidence of high frequency will come down after the proposed interconnections of Eastern
grid with North/South in October/ November 2002. However, it is felt that appreciable improvement can be
obtained only by implementation of ‘Availability Based Tariff’.
 
 
(C)             To meet the full compliance of Pollution Control norms in Coke Ovens as per the directives of MOEF, large
scale financial investment is called for.  A provision of Rs. 1000 crores has been made in 10
th Five Year Plan
which will allow us to take up rebuilding of not more than 6 batteries over the next 5 years.  If SAIL has to stick to
the stringent regulatory requirement to make all 21 batteries fully compliant within a tightened time frame, SAIL
will have to seek financial assistance from Government to ensure full compliance in batteries.  Towards this end,
SAIL has proposed Rs. 80 crore assistance from Govt. in 2003-04 BE.
