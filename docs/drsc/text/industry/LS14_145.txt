CHAPTER-I
 
THE STEEL SECTOR
1.1       Steel is crucial to the development of any modern economy. It has  technologically strong forward and backward
linkages in terms of material flow and income generation.   The growth of  many of  advanced economies have been
largely shaped by the strength of their steel industry during their early stages of development.    Some of the newly
industrializing  countries like South Korea, China and Brazil among others have built up substantial capacities not only
to support their rapidly growing domestic economies, but also to export significantly. 
 
1.1.2    The liberalization of the economy since 1991-1992  in India has witnessed a spate of new capacity additions in
the private steel sector.  Steel Plants in private sector are now  producing  two-third of the total finished products.  Long-
term growth prospect of steel industry is encouraging a large number of people to set up greenfield capacities with
modern technology in the private sector.  These units  with state  of-the art technology are more viable than the units in
the public sector.  Under an era of liberalization and with the lowering of international trade barriers competitiveness of
the industry has become crucial not only for increasing exports but also in competing with imports in the domestic
market as  Steel industry in India had been adversely affected in the past by the rationalization and reduction of duties
resulting in lower tariff protection against imports, to South East Asian currency crisis, Russian Rouble devaluation,
lower international prices and the consequent meltdown and dumping.  Global Steel Industry for the major part of 2001
remained a victim of slow down in demand, sliding price levels, low realizations, losses and falling profits.  However,
from the beginning of 2002, the industry entered a recovery phase, with the gradual firming up of international prices
and demand.  These trends largely persist to date.   The year 2003 has  seen a price rise in the domestic and the 
international steel market. 
 
1.1.3    In  India the production, prices and consumption of steel have witnessed a steady rise over the last few months.
Domestic prices have firmed up in the face of strong demand – both domestic and foreign. There has been a robust
growth in exports following a quantum jump in GP/GC exports. The salient trends for April-January 2003 and April ’01

1.1.4    Better price realizations have led to an improvement in financial performance for most of the domestic steel
plants. Coupled with this has come the revival of economic growth and the recovery of the key end-user sectors of
steel.  With the continued focus on infrastructure development and construction activities (including housing) – the
industry mood is upbeat.
 
1.2.0    Industrial output – as measured by the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) –
 
1.2.1    It grew 6.4% in December 2002 as against 2.8% during the correpsonding month last year and for the April ’02 -
January ‘03 period, it reported a 5.5% growth against a mere 2.6% growth in same period last year. For this period,
Manufacturing (5.8%), Electricity (3.8%) and Mining (5.4%) – all recorded impressive growths, as compared with the
same period last year, for which respective sectoral growth rates are 2.8%, 2.9% and 1%. (Source: CSO)
 
1.2.2    Impressive performance of the core sector continued in January 2003 with finished steel and cement continuing
to boost growth in the six infrastructure industries whose index rose to 5.2% in April-January ’03 as compared to a low
3.1 % in same period last year.  (Source: CSO)
 
1.3.0    Budget (2003-04) and the Indian Steel Industry
 
1.3.1    With the measures proposed in both the Railway Budget 2003-04 and the Union budget 2003-04, the outlook in
the steel industry is positive. The policy measures proposed in the Railway Budget 2003-04 promise to benefit industry
in general and the iron and steel industry in particular. The proposed rationalization of freight structure, reduction in the
number of classifications, the market orientation introduced through peak and off-peak rates, the incentives to big
shippers in the form of lower rates, if they move more materials, the reduction in freight rates for short distance ranging
up to 100 km as well as the general reductions in the freight rates for finished steel and pig iron – help in lowering the
overall costs and increasing competitiveness. The stress on laying of new tracks, broadening of existing ones, addition
of new trains and a series of developmental projects are all expected to boost demand for steel.
 
1.3.2    Union Budget 2003-04 has proposed measures that are meant  for improving the environment in which the iron
and steel industry operates. Most prominent is the significant thrust provided to infrastructure development in the
country – roads, ports,  airports and housing - and this in turn is expected to lead to a substantial increase in the domestic
steel demand. Reduction of excise on passenger cars and appliances like air conditioners is also expected to indirectly
impact steel industry favourably. 
 
1.3.3    There may be no direct correlation between the International steel production growth rate and the domestic
growth rate.  Although a  global rise in demand may have similar impact on the industry in India as well as globally, a 
domestic rise in demand may, in fact, lead to strong growth in production in the country leaving the global production
rate practically unaffected.  Therefore, it may be difficult to estimate whether domestic growth rate will catch up with
the global trends.  In fact, while the global annual production growth rate in the last ten years or so has been at less than
2 per cent, the figure for India has been at about 6 per cent.  There are greater chances that the steel industry growth rate
in India will be higher than the global rate in the medium and the long term.
 
1.3.4    Major Indian steel companies have already hiked prices of steel items – though, mostly flat products from the
beginning of the fiscal 2002-03. This is seen in line with international trends where prices have been rising since
beginning of 2002 and is an inevitable fallout of the globalization process. Domestic price rises have persisted in 2003
in the face of a steel demand that has shown signs of stabilizing.
 
1.4.0    Price Volatality
           
1.4.1    The year 2003 opened with rounds of price hikes – of both long and flat which have given the current market

trends further buoyancy. In January 2003, SAIL, Tata Steel and other flat steel producers increased prices of flat
products by an average Rs 600-1000 per tonne while SAIL and RINL have hiked long steel prices by Rs. 200-300
per tonne. This has pushed up hot rolled coil prices in the domestic market, with basic grade price of the commodity
being around Rs 17,500-18,000 per tonne (average). Similarly, the growth in the construction segment in particular
and the infrastructure segment in general has prompted fresh hikes in long steel prices. Internationally too, trends (W.

$390-430/tonne and hot rolled coils at around $300-325/tonne. (Source: Metal Bulletin).
 
1.4.2    The turnaround in the market trends is reflected most prominently in the performance of the public sector steel
giant, Steel Authority of India Ltd. The unaudited financial results of SAIL for April-December (Q3) 2002 reveals a
sharp improvement in the performance with losses being slashed to Rs. 79 crore in Q3 ’02 - an improvement of Rs. 507
crore over the corresponding period of the previous year. SAIL’s loss for April-December ’02 was brought to a level of
Rs. 558 crore, showing a recovery of 57 per cent over the results of the same period in 2001. This helped the company
to post a cash profit of Rs 215 crore in Q3 ’02 as against a loss of Rs 297 crore in Q3 ’01 and a cash profit of Rs. 305
crore in the first nine months of the current financial year.  SAIL had suffered a cash loss of Rs. 434 crore during the
corresponding period in the last fiscal.
 
1.4.3    For the other PSU, namely RINL (VSP) for the period, Q2 (July-September) ’02, net profit has gone up by 119%
and turnover 16% as compared to quarter ending June ’02 (Q1).          The Indian steel companies are free to raise prices
at any time, depending on their commercial judgement.  The conditions for price increases are local in the first place. 
Some such conditions may be created by external developments in the context of the free external trade regime.
Fluctuations in international prices can always have corresponding impact on the pricing decisions of the Indian
companies.       The world prices of almost all steel products have risen significantly since January 2002. The extent of
price rise has been significant for flat products like HR coils, CR sheets and galvanised sheets.  In view of this, the
Indian steel companies have also raised prices to the extent that may be supported by domestic market conditions.
 
1.4.4    In a deregulated and liberalized environment, prices are determined by the free interplay of market forces. 
Government does not intervene in the market to influence prices.  The main reason for the recent upswing in steel price
has been the global increase in the demand for steel.  The domestic prices have followed the general pattern of
international prices.  The increase in demand from the domestic steel consuming industries has further reinforced the
upward movement of prices.  Sharp rise in the cost of metallic and certain other critical inputs like iron ore, coal, coke,
nickel and ferro alloys has also contributed to the rise in prices.
 
1.5.0    Recommendation for change in the existing duty structure for Union Budget 2004-05    
 
1.5.1    Ministry of Steel had sent its recommendation for change in the existing duty structure for Union Budget 2004-
05 to Department of Revenue, keeping in view the interest of both the steel manufacturers and the steel consumers.  The
changes in the duty structure announced recently by Ministry of Finance have addressed concerns in some areas
especially of the steel consuming sector viz.
 
(a)
                The peak rate of import duty has been reduced to 20%.
(b)               4% SAD on all imports has been abolished.
(c)                Import duty on Nickel has been lowered from 10% to 5%.
 
1.5.2    Import Duty on most of the steel items used by the downstream industries like Semis, HR Sheets/Plates and HR
Coils is imposed at the peak rate, which has been lowered by 5%.  This and the abolition of 4% SAD would reduce the
cost of import of steel by more than 10%.  Reduction in duty on Nickel from 10% to 5% would give a boost to the
growth of the domestic Stainless Steel Industry.
 
1.5.3    Earlier, as a measure of import facilitation, Government has also withdrawn steel items from the purview of
DGFT Notification No. 44 and provided the option to downstream steel consumers to meet their requirements through
imports.  The steel consumers have also been able to get a favourable judgement from the Calcutta High Court as the
Honuorable Court has ordered removal of the mechanism of floor price on import of prime grade HR Coil.

CHAPTER-II
 
THE SMALL SCALE SECTOR
 
2.1       There is a very big segment of Small Scale Industries engaged in manufacturing various domestic and industrial
products consuming Iron and Steel in prime and semi-finished form as raw materials for their manufacturing activities. 
This segment includes the forging and casting industries, steel furniture manufacturing, Bicycle parts industries,
Agricultural implements industries etc.  Most of these industries are located in Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Gujarat,
Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh.  Various other SSI and Tiny units spread throughout the length and breadth of the
country also use iron and steel as basic raw material to a great extent.
 
2.1.1    Iron and Steel industry is the main supplier of basic raw material for the light engineering industry such as
Bicycles, Auto Parts, Hard Ware and Hand Tools.  Since last year, the small scale industries are facing problems due to
non-availability of iron and steel, in adequate quantities along with a steep hike in prices.  It is stated that the decontrol
policy has set the cartel of steel manufacturers free in fixing steel price.  The cartel of steel manufacturers has increased
the steel price arbitrarily and the small scale industries are finding it difficult to absorb the rise in prices.

2003.
 
2.1.4    Various Associations of Small Scale Industries, with their member industries consuming Iron and Steel have
represented to check the escalation of price.  These Associations highlighted the unprecedented hike in the prices of Iron
and Steel produced by both Public Sector as well as Private Sector steel units during the last one and a half years.  Prices
of items such as Steel plates, CR steel,  Steel round , Steel pig iron and of various other items of iron and steel have
increased in the range of 12.5% to 42% in the last few months.
 
2.1.5    SSI Associations and entrepreneurs have alleged that the existing policy of the Government has led the steel
plants to export the material to China at a lower rate than the prevailing rate in the Indian market.  China is procuring
raw material at a price lower than the domestic price at which raw material is available to Indian SSI manufacturers and
is exporting finished products at lower rates than our domestic prices.  This has caused extreme hardship for the SSI
manufacturers as the higher domestic price of their raw material renders their finished  products much costlier than the
goods imported from China.
 
2.1.6    Though the policy of the Government has given strength to the iron and steel industry which is witnessing the
sign of revival and growth almost after a decade but, side by side, this situation if allowed to continue would force large
number of SSI units spread across the country to close down which may add to the problem of increasing
unemployment and worsening of law and order in some parts of the country.
 
2.1.7    Amongst the remedial suggestions proposed by the SSI Associations, the following need consideration of the
Government:-
 
(i)                  Export of Iron and Steel at prices lower than those prevailing in the domestic market should not be allowed.

2.2.0    Electronics
 
2.2.1    Representations were also received from Association of UPS and Power Conditioning Systems manufacturers
and also from Indian Transformers Association, wherein, it was stated that the Small Scale Sector is facing the problem
of inconsistent supply of Electrical grade steel from the local steel producing unit of SAIL and other large Steel units.  It
is reported that the SSI Sector contributes the substantial production of transformer laminations as there are
approximately five hundred SSI units engaged in the manufacture of Laminations for transformers. The problems faced
by the small scale industry are the limited availability of CRGO Steel which is used for the manufacture of indigenous
lamination for power and distribution transformers/other transformers and high value of import duty on imported CRGO
Steel. Prime CRGO steel is classified under Chapter 72 of the Customs Act, it is bundled with other steels like mild
steels, alloy steels, stainless steels etc., with a custom duty of 25% which is meant to protect the Indian Steel Industry
against dumping of imported steel products.  The high incidence of basic customs duty @ 25% on an important raw
material (like CRGO steel) for transformer manufacturers is not warranted as there is very limited indigenous
availability.
 
2.2.2    To overcome this problem a lower import duty of 10% on  CRGO steel (electrical steel) will encourage usage of
prime material in the manufacture of power and distribution transformers in the Small Scale Sector.  By using the proper
CRGO steel, the quality of the transformers will be improved resulting in reduction of transmission and distribution
losses thereby effecting considerable savings.

CHAPTER-III
OBSERVATIONS/CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
 
3.1       The Committee observes that after a long recession in the steel industry there is a definite up-beat in the steel
industry so far as the production, consumption and price are concerned.  The steel industry is in the process of
consolidation  and firming up which is but natural as the industry as a whole is coming out of the red.  Being concerned
with the recession phase of the steel industry the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Industry had
undertaken detailed on the spot study of steel units in various parts of the country and suggested remedial measures in
its 34
th Report  on Problems Being Faced by the Steel Industry  (both in Public and Private Sectors) including the
recommendation for the National Steel Policy which is under formulation by the Ministry of Steel .  Therefore, the
Committee understands the problems faced by the steel industry in its rainy days.  However, the adverse situations have
been now overcome.  The Committee is pleased to observe that the demand of steel is growing and steel units in public
as well as the private sectors are undertaking expansion projects to meet the increased domestic and international
demand.
 
   3.2    However, the crucial role of Small Scale Industries in the country’s economy should not be overlooked and
everything possible has to be done to protect this sector. The Committee is of the view that the Government should
come out with a short term strategy to protect and promote the small scale industries and prevent the likely closure of a
large number of small industries because of the non-availability of steel at  a reasonable price.
 
  3.3     During the course of interaction with the representatives of various SSI associations the Committee was
informed that the steel producers are creating artificial scarcity in the country to justify abnormal rise in steel prices. 
Steel producers are reported to be exporting HR Coil at about 20 dollar less than the global price and they are claiming
export incentive of around 20% from the Government exchequer.  They are selling the same product in the country at a
global rate with 31% custom duty and other incidental charges.  The Committee was further informed that the prevailing
situation has gone so bad that finished products made out of steel exported from India are coming back to India at
cheaper rates. Although this was denied by the representatives of the Ministry of Steel, the Committee is of the opinion
that steep rise in the steel prices is  adversely affecting the business in the Small Scale Sector which needs to be
addressed in time as otherwise the growth and development of the small scale industries may be seriously crippled.    
The Committee recommends that a long-term strategy for helping the SSI sector can be considered after a period of six
months taking into account the demand supply pattern during this period and also the trend of prices.  What is called for
is a set of measures to make steel available to the Small Sector and thus prevent the danger of closure of hundreds of
units depending on steel raw materials.
 
3.4       Observing the gravity of the prevailing  situation and its impact on the Small Scale Industries the Committee
would like to  recommend the following strategies and actions to be adopted by the Ministries concerned:-
 
(i)
                  Assured supply of steel as a raw material to the units of small scale industries:- keeping in view the
importance of Small Scale Industries  Steel producers should ensure that SSI mills are not forced to close
down because of non availability of raw materials. The Ministry of SSI must assess the requirement of raw
material for the SSI units through out the country and forward the same in advance to the Ministry of Steel
for ensuring that the supply of these items to the Small units is not arbitrarily reduced.
 
The Government should consider introducing alternative institutional measures to ensure smooth supply
of raw materials-pig iron, coke, scrap iron, aluminium, copper, plastic raw materials, etc., at stable prices to
the Foundry units and SSI units working in the engineering and allied sectors. Even though the Government
has dispensed with the price control it should take note of the severe crisis situation that prevails in the SSI

      The Committee is of the considered view that the Government must ensure the supply of raw materials at

such a mechanism  the duty automatically becomes plus or minus depending on the price.

representatives of producers and consumers.  This Co-ordination Committee may look at the prices although
not with the intention to regulate the prices but keep an eye on the prices. This Committee may also assess

(vi)       Need for Setting up of Competition Commission: - During the course of interaction with SSI associations the
representatives of Indian Micro Small and Medium Enterprises and the Automotive Component Manufacturers
Association of India (ACMA) complained about the phenomenon of cartelisation by steel majors resulting in massive

the price increases in steel items are not included  in the contract.  Moreover, SSI units at present are not in a position to
sign any long-term contract for exports because of uncertainties over the price hike in steel.  The Committee feels that if
the prevailing situation is allowed to continue  for a longer period it may break the backbone of small scale industries
and thus adversely affecting the national economy.  Therefore, the Committee strongly feels that there is an urgent need
to set up the Competition Commission  to pre-empt such cartelisation.  The Committee further recommends that the
Government must also take up the question of international cartelization of steel with the WTO at the earliest.
 
(vii)      Inter-sectoral Co-operation and Collaborative Attitude and Aptitude:- The Committee strongly feels that in

economy with the aid and advice of good governance and regulations under the liberalized and globalised regime. Such

a great need for closer coordination among the  Ministries of Small Scale Industries, Steel, Commerce and the Ministry

(a)  Custom Duty on Prime Steel
 
            The Committee feels that the custom duty on prime steel may be reduced from 20% to 10% as per the
recommendations of Dr. Kelkar Committee, so as to provide level playing field. Even otherwise, the custom duty on
import of Iron and Steel material is to be reduced to 10% by 2005 under the W.T.O. obligations. The removal of non-
tariff barriers and minimum floor rate would be ineffective if the custom duty is not brought down to 10%.
 
(b)  Rationalization of Custom duty on steel raw material
 
            The Committee also agrees that  the customs duty should be lower on raw materials and higher on finished
goods in order to boost the  domestic industries.  Therefore, the duty structure on  steel raw material like HR Coils, CR
Coils, CR Strips, Steel ingots, Steel Flats, MS Rounds, Cutting should be reasonably fixed.   The customs duty on the
Secondary/defective materials  is 40% plus 16% CVD which works out to 56%.  It is therefore high time that the
Customs Duty on these materials should be reasonably brought down.
           
(c)  High Prices of Nickel
 
            Nickel is an 100% imported raw material which bears a Custom Duty of 15%
ad-valorem, 16% CVD.  The London Metal Exchange Rate with reference  to Nickel has gone up to US$ 12800/- which
is unprecedented.  This has almost crushed the tiny / SSI units who use nickel as their basic raw material.  The rates
have gone up to Rs. 850/- per kg.  from Rs. 400/- per kg.             The Committee observes that duty on Nickel should be
drastically brought down and MMTC should canalize the import to ensure the supply at reasonable rates. 
 
(d) Duty Entitlement Passbook Scheme (DEPB)
 
The Committee was apprised that  under the Duty Entitlement Passbook Scheme (DEPB), Indian Steel majors benefit,
from 19 percent duty free imports of goods against exports while  the small manufacturers pay import duty of 40 percent
on H.R. Coils (secondary commercial materials). The Committee recommends that the export benefits given to the steel
producers such as Advance Licence and DEPB should not be misused by the steel producers and the Government
should ensure that the requirements of the domestic industries are met at reasonable rates.
 
(e)        Customs duty Prime CRGO steel
 
The Committee was apprised that the Prime CRGO steel is classified under Chapter 72 of the Customs Act, it is
bundled with other steels like mild steels, alloy steels, stainless steels etc., with a custom duty of 25% which is meant to
protect the Indian Steel Industry against dumping of imported steel products.  The high incidence of basic customs duty
@ 25% on an important raw material (like CRGO steel) for transformer manufacturers is not warranted as there is very
limited indigenous availability.  Therefore, the Committee is of the view that a reasonable import duty on prime CRGO
steel (electrical steel) will encourage usage of prime material in the manufacture of power and distribution transformers
in the Small Scale Sector.   The Committee was assured by the Ministry of Small Scale Industries that by using the
proper CRGO steel, the quality of the transformers will be improved resulting in reduction of transmission and
distribution losses thereby effecting considerable savings.
 
(f)   Custom Duty on Scrap
 
The Committee was apprised by the SSI Associations that at present the Customs Duty on scrap is 5% plus 16%
CVD.  In case, the scrap is imported by traders, the duty rules at 20%.  Thus, the cost of imported scrap has increased to
more than double. Moreover, the scrap is not available in India as China and Bangladesh have started importing scrap
from India.  This trend has adversely affected the secondary producers for whom it is the basic raw material.    Keeping
in view the interests of the domestic industries, the Committee feels that the customs duty structure on scrap may be
suitably  rationalized enabling the tiny and SSI units to compete internationally as well as prohibiting the dumping of
scraps in Indian steel domestic market.  A judicious decision on the part of the Government is required as the steps
taken for rationalization of custom duty on import of scrap must strike a balance in protecting the SSI units as well as

strengthening the anti dumping mechanism for the steel industry.  
(g)        Levying of Central excise duty on ship breaking
 
            The SSI Associations advocated the abolition of the 16% central excise duty recently levied on ship breaking 
which is according to them absolutely uncalled for  as central excise duty can only be levied on the process of
manufacture as per the Act and ship breaking is not  a process of manufacture by any standard.  In their view such a step
would provide scrap for the secondary producers of Iron and Steel at cheaper rates.  However, the Committee was
apprised by the representatives of the Ministry of Steel  that the ship breaking industry has been recognized as the
manufacturing industry.  In fact, the ship breakers have been fighting for being recognized as manufacturing industry. 
Till 1994,  they were not recognized, and after fighting for it, they got the status of manufacturing industry.  Now, they
have gone to the Supreme Court because one of the High Courts has given a ruling that the ship breaking industry is not
a manufacturing industry and therefore, excise is not leviable.    The excise duty paid by the ship breaking
manufacturers are modvatable as they collect it from the customer and pass forward.  The Ministry of Steel has taken up
their demand that the duty should be reduced to 10% .   The Committee is of the view that  pending decision of the
Supreme Court in the matter, the Central Excise Duty may be reduced to the level as low as possible.
 
(h)        Adequate supply of pig-iron to the foundry units in small scale sector
 
            The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry of Steel has directed PSUs especially RINL not to export pig-
iron and the RINL has released about 10,000 metric tonnes of pig iron in the market and tenders have been floated. 
However, the Committee hopes that the steel PSUs will continue to supply adequate quantity of the pig iron to the units
of the small and the medium enterprises.
 
            The Committee has offered several suggestions above to reduce the hardship experienced by the SSI units using
steel items as raw materials.   Some of them particularly those relating to reduction of the duties may be considered very
urgently, so that easier availability of raw materials can be ensured in the next few months.  The Government may
consider extending such a relief as a short-term measure and review the whole situation about raw material availability
after watching the trends of prices for about six months.

3.         The representatives of Federation of Indian Micro and Small and Medium Enterprises advocated for single rate
of custom duty (5-8%).They stated that even though average import tariff  were higher, the Effective Rate of Protection
(ERP) for SSIs was low or even negative because of high duties on their inputs, whereas with low single rate of duty the
ERP  for SSIs should be higher and they would become more competitive. They also suggested  for a uniform single but
low rate of duty on all products in the range of 5-8 per cent and for freeing up import of steel scrap and seconds and to
strengthen the Competition Commission to pre-empt cartelisation.
 
4.         The representatives of Automotive Component Manufacturers Association of India emphasized that the prices of
various grades of steel have undergone a steep increase in the past one-year.  The increase is about 60% from last year’s
prices, approximately Rs. 6000 to 7000 per M.T.  Auto component manufacturers  are finding it extremely difficult to
meet the target prices of their customers, especially Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs).  Due to this price
increase component manufacturers are unable to commit firm prices to their overseas buyers for export. The excessive
price increase which has never been experienced before and has made many SSI units commercially unviable.  They
also informed that the OEMs have not given any proportionate price increase to their component suppliers on account of
the escalation of steel prices. Hence, the Indian automotive industry is passing through a very delicate and sensitive
period. They also suggested reduction of import duty on Steel to10% same as Thailand, lower import duty may be
considered for steel scrap.
