               REPORT
1.         Minerals have  been an important   basis of classification of human  civilization.   A civilization undertakes development in consonance with a
pattern of physical   endowment of valuable minerals like Copper, Iron Ore,  Aluminium,  Zinc, Lead, Gold,   Cobalt, Manganese, Graphite, Lime
Stone, Platinum etc.    Since mineral resources are wealth hidden   beneath the earth-curst, therefore, their conservation and planned   exploitation is
of paramount importance to  national economic development and growth .  As a matter of fact,  mineral resources not only provide the basic inputs to
domestic industry,  but also account for sizeable export and foreign exchange earnings.   The development and regulation of the mineral sector is
concomitant to the optimal utilisation of resources, which ensures all round development, and growth of national economy.
 
1.1.      The Ministry of   Mines is responsible for development and regulation of the mineral sector including  mining and metallurgy     of all non-
ferrous metals both in private and public sectors, but excluding   Natural Gas, Petroleum, Coal and Atomic   Minerals.  As per the Annual Report

(2002-03)   the Ministry of Mines has been   allotted subjects as under: -
i                       Legislation for regulation  of mines and development  of    minerals within the territory of  India, including   mines    and minerals 
underlying the ocean within the territorial waters  or the continental shelf, or the Exclusive Economic Zone and other Maritime Zones of 
India as may be specified from time to  time by or under any law made by Parliament.
ii                     Regulation  of mines and development of minerals   other than coal,  lignite and sand  for  stowing and any minerals declared as prescribed
substances for the  purposes  of the  Atomic Energy Act, 1962 (33 of 1962)  under the control of the Union as declared by law,   including  
questions concerning  regulation and development of minerals in various States and the matter connected therewith with or incidental thereto.
iii                    All other metals and minerals not  specifically allotted to any  other Ministry/ Department  such as aluminium, zinc , copper, gold, diamond
and nickel.
iv                   Planning, development and control of and assistance to all industries dealt with by the Ministry.
1.2.            At the Secretariat level the  Ministry of Mines is  mainly engaged in direction, supervision and coordination of five divisions which inter-alia
include Survey and Exploration,  Mines, Metal, General Administration and Finance and Accounts Division.   Survey and Exploration has two
principal agencies i.e. Geological Survey of India (GSI) and Mineral Exploration Corporation Ltd. (MECL) for the implementation of the
programmes of this division while Mines Division has one subordinate office to implement the programmes of the division, viz. Indian Bureau of
Mines (IBM). Metal division of the Ministry of Mines deals with the planning, exploration, development, monitoring of programmes and research in
respect of non-ferrous metals both in the public and private sectors General Administration Division is mainly concerned with the establishment and
administrative matter of the Department and Finance and Accounts Division is concerned with the budget, financial scrutiny of projects and other
proposals, rendering financial advice and maintenance and compilation of accounts.

1.7       Regarding the exact role of Ministry of Mines in the overall structure of management and working of divested PSUs and whether  the
Ministry would  play the role of a mere co-ordinator or facilitator or can do something more in the functional structure of divested PSUs, the Ministry
of Mines stated that in view of disinvestment of PSUs the Ministry is to act as per Share Holders Agreement /Share Purchase Agreement. The
Government has nominee Directors in the Board who oversee that the new Management is adhering to provisions of the agreement. The Company
like any other legal entity incorporated under company law is accountable as per the provisions of law.
1.8       The Committee notes the structural and functional pattern of the Ministry of   Mines, the Secretariat for the Ministry of Mines and  is of the 
view  that structural aspect must  match the functional requirement and working   of  the  organisation with adequate degree  of co-ordination and
collaboration   among  various units  thus contributing on  their  part    in the overall functioning of the organisation in the interest of national 
economy .
1.9       The Committee, in the light of the fact that Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC)  has been transferred to Department  of North East Region,
Bharat Gold Mines Ltd.,  BGML has been  closed since 1
st March, 2001, Bharat Aluminium Company  Ltd. already  disinvested in March, 2001 and
transferring the management of  Hindustan Zinc Limited (HZL)in new management after disinvestment and   remaining PSUs National Aluminium  
Company Ltd. (NALCO) , Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL) are  on the verge of disinvestments and with the completion of process of
disinvestments of mining  PSUs,  Ministry of Mines would have nothing to direct, supervise and co-ordinate ,therefore, it is the right time for the
Government for pruning its departments.
1.10     Reacting to the Committee’s opinion the Ministry of Mines commented that the Ministry  has the responsibility for overall development of all
metals and minerals not specifically allotted to Ministry of Mines  is enjoined to legislate for regulation of mines and development of minerals within
the territory of India including mines and minerals underlying the ocean within the territorial waters or the continental shelf or the exclusive
economic zone and other maritime zones of India as may be specified from time to time by or under any law made by the Parliament.  Further, the

Ministry is enjoined the task of regulation of mines and development of non-fuel and non-atomic minerals including questions concerning regulating
and development of any other Ministry or Department (i.e. development of aluminium, zinc, lead, copper and diamond, etc.)   The Ministry has been
allocated the work of Geological Survey of India which is the premier agency in earth sciences in the country.  Ministry enforces the regulatory

1.11     The Ministry of Mines reacted that it was  enjoined to look after planning development and control of and assistance to all industries dealt
with by the Ministry including the Public Sector Undertakings.  It is obvious, therefore, the role of Ministry of Mines is not restricted to direct,
supervise and coordinate the PSUs, which is only a part of its overall role and functions in the Government.  Therefore disinvestments of PSUs,
closure of BGML and transfer of SMC to Department of Development of North East Region would  allow the Ministry of Mines to focus on its areas
of core competence, i.e. survey and exploration of all minerals other than natural gas, petroleum and atomic minerals, administer the mining statutes
as well as enter into new areas and develop competence in  the Off Shore Areas Mineral (Development and Regulation) law . 
1.12     Expenditure Reforms Commission was of the opinion that Ministry of Mines be merged with Ministry of Steel into a composite Ministry of
Mines and Metals with three separate wings (not departments) one dealing with public sector companies, another with the PSUs of Ministry of Mines
and the third dealing with policy issues and regulatory aspects including GSI, IBM etc. 
1.13       The Committee understands the core function of Ministry of Mines, however, the real  question arises   whether  a separate Department is
required for discharging the  core function only .  The Committee is of the firm view that the existence of structure of any department   presently the  
Ministry of Mines, must justify itself through discharging   its corresponding duties and functions since this involves substantial  financial
implications. 
1.14     When the Committee asked the Ministry of Mines to comment on the proposal of transferring National Mineral Development  Corporation
Ltd.  (NMDC) , Kudremukh Iron  Ore Company Ltd., Manganese Ore  (India) Ltd. and Metallurgical  &  Engineering Consultants India Ltd.
(MECON)  from Ministry of Steel to Ministry of Mines  in order to rationalise the allocation of business of Government, the Ministry of Mines stated
that since the subject matter relating to legislation, regulation and concessions of manganese and iron ore is allocated to this Ministry,  it is
anomalous that public sector corporations in this area namely; NMDC, MOIL, Kudremukh Iron Ore Company Ltd. are dealt with by the Ministry of
Steel.  A proposal therefore has been submitted to the Cabinet Secretariat for transfer of these public sector undertakings to the Ministry   of Mines.
1.15     Reacting to the proposal of integration / merger of Geological Survey of India, Mineral Exploration Corporation Ltd. and India  Bureau of
Mines  (IBM)  and the same is transferred either to  Ministry of Steel and  Coal or Petroleum with suitable  change in the name of the Ministry, it was
explained that Geological Survey of India, Mineral Exploration Corporation and Indian Bureau of Mines were  mandated to take up different specific
responsibilities, which could not  be merged.  Geological Survey of India’s areas of activity include scientific survey and research for locating
mineral resources and geological studies while IBM is primarily responsible for promotion and conservation of scientific development of mineral
resources of the country and enforce Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act and Rules there under.  The basic aim of MECL is to
bridge the gap between preliminary exploration of mineral prospect and its commercial exploitation.  From this it is clear that these are responsible
for development and regulation of mineral sector including the mining and metallurgy and non-ferrous metals both in private and public sector
excluding natural gas, petroleum and atomic minerals and this cannot be transferred to the Ministry of Steel, Coal and Petroleum as these Ministries
have been allotted specific charters.
 
1.16     The Committee  understands  that GSI, IBM and MECL have their specific charter  of functions but it is also true that MECL  is   an  offshoot
of GSI and later on created a separate body to supplement GSI .Moreover, the submission of the Secretary Ministry of Mines that  GSI, and  MECL 
work  not only for the    Ministry of Mines  but also for  Department of Coal and  Ministry of Petroleum  and even for private sector, the argument of
the Ministry of Mines that GSI, IBM and MECL must remain within the jurisdiction of Ministry of Mines,  remains bereft of substantial and
conclusive logic as these organizations may remain with any of the department for which these organization work.
1.17     The Committee reiterates  its viewpoint as expressed in its 71
st Report that  that possibility of integration of  GSI, IBM and MECL may be
explored and the  efforts may be made for  rationalising  of  non-plan expenditure.  The Committee is of the further view that GSI  MECL and IBM
may  co-exist  under one organic unit and may remain either in the Ministry of  Coal or Petroleum  or Mines discharging  their  roles as mentioned  in
their respective  charter   of function   intact and in original  form.  The Committee opines   that GSI, MECL  and IBM  may be integrated with  their  
differential roles under one  umbrella  and which would not only rationalise   the  non-plan  expenditure  but also  inject synergy  and enhancement
of  brand  endowment.
1.18    The Committee also reiterates its recommendations  as contained in its 71st report on Demands for Grants (2002-03)  that  Government should
immediately constitute  a  Task Force having representatives from Department of Coal, Mines, Petroleum   and Steel   to examine the following
issues:-
(i)
                  Determination of continuation of Ministry of Mines with a view  to discharge its  core function;
(ii)                Feasibility and desirability of integration/merger of GSI, IBM and MECL  and  transferring of  same to either  Department of Coal,
Petroleum or Ministry of  Steel.
(iii)               Transfer of  NMDC, KIOCL, MOIL and MECON from the Ministry of Steel  to Ministry of Mines.
(iv)              Merger of Ministry of Mines, Coal and Steel in view of   the recommendation of Expenditure Reforms Commission . 
1.19     The very fact that the proposal has been submitted  by the Ministry of Mines to the Cabinet Secretariat for transfer of NMDC, MOIL,
Kudremukh Iron Ore Company Ltd to the Ministry  of Mines, Ministry of Coal & Mines, reveals that even  as per view of Ministry of Mines there
are public undertakings which could be under the Ministry of Mines but at present these undertakings are under the Ministry of Steel.  Therefore, 
prima-facie it seems that the Ministry of Steel and Mines may be merged and their roles could be so integrated as to facilitate the discharge of both of
the departments.
1.20     The Committee further reiterates its recommendations contained in 99
th Action Taken Report on Demands for Grants (2002-03) that the
recommendations of the Expenditure Reforms Commission for abolition of just 87 posts is a conclusion arrived at in the pre-disinvestment  era and

2.5       Philosophy behind the budget proposals
2.5.1    The economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Ministry of Mines is broadly based on the principle that extensive survey and
investigation should be carried out in the field of minerals in which the country is deficient or which are of strategic importance.  The need for
advanced exploration and prospecting techniques in the field of geophysical and geochemical methods has been envisaged which requires 
participation of domestic and foreign private explorators  and prospectors. Stress has been laid on recycling of metal scraps and enhancement of
smelting capacities of metals like aluminium, lead,zinc , steel and ferro alloys.  Stress has also been laid on development of dimension and decorative
stones, mining and processing industry, minerals required for advanced ceramic and glasses etc.             States have been encouraged to ensure proper
linkage of raw materials to the industry along with development of necessary basic infrastructure.
 
2.5.2    The Committee is pleased to note that  the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Ministry of Mines is broadly based on the
principle that extensive survey and investigation should be carried out in the field of minerals in which the country is deficient or which are of
strategic importance for the country. The Committee strongly feels that the budget proposals for the year 2003-04 pertaining to the Ministry of Mines
are expected to prove as an instrument to march forward towards the national goal of overall development in the era of liberalized and globalised
economy.   The Committee is of the view that the Ministry of Mines, discharging its core activities as well as its responsibility and role as a facilitator
in the liberalized and globalised economy must guide the organizations and its PSUs to determine the national goals for growth and development of
Mining Industry (i.e. identification of goals, mineral specific industry wise), Chalking out set of programmes, course of Action required and overall
monitoring  for execution of the plans without time and cost over run.  Any effort for shrugging of its responsibility would raise a question mark on 
the raison-de-itre  for the Ministry of Mines.

3.1.14  Though the mineral resources are regionally distributed, the GSI has played the leading role of identifying them to provide a strong base to
the industrial development of the nation.Regional distribution of mineral resources has been  utilized by value addition.  Value addition is possible
broadly in three stages namely mining, mineral based processing industry and export oriented industry which contribute significantly to Gross
National Product.  Minerals are important source of foreign exchange earning.  The policy of export shall keep in view the mineral inventory position
and the long-term need of the country.
3.1.15  Presently GSI is the principal provider of basic earth science information to the government, industry and the general public, as well as
responsive participant in international fora.  After Independence, there has been added emphasis on locating and harnessing the natural resources for
meeting the growing demands of mineral/metal industries, power (thermal, hydro and nuclear), agricultural, irrigation sectors etc.  The thrust areas of
GSI’s activities have evolved with the changing national priorities through out the last nine Five Year Plans and is presently oriented in the light of
the current goals set up for X Plan.
 
3.1.16  Since independence GSI remained in the vanguard of nation building movement particularly in the fields of industrial revolution, power,
irrigation and water resources management.  Major organizations like Indian Bureau of Mines (IBM), Oil and Natural Gas Commission (ONGC),
Atomic Mineral Division (AMD), Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) nucleated from GSI.  Important mining and mineral-based industries like
Hindustan Copper Ltd. (HCL), Hindustan Zinc Ltd. (HZL), National Mineral Development Corporation (NMDC), Raw material Divisions of Steel
Industry, Coal and Lignite Corporations, Bharat Aluminium Company Ltd. (BALCO), National Aluminium Company Ltd. (NALCO) are the spin-
offs.             Vibrant Steel, Coal, Cement, Aluminium, Copper-lead-Zinc, Fertilizer and Chemical industries which expanded phenomenally in post-
independence era, bear eloquent .testimony to GSI’s sustained efforts towards augmentation of mineral resource base
 
3.1.17  The Committee was apprised that an expert  Committee was setup by the Ministry of Mines under the Chairmanship of  Shri Arvind Varma,
former Secretary(Mines), Government of India  on 23.5.2002 to study the role and relevance of Geological Survey of India in the light of
developments in the field earth sciences and recommend changes in the charter of GSI.  The Expert Committee has submitted its report on 2.1.2003. 
It recommended changes in the charter and function of Geological Survey of India.  It suggested that GSI should systematically explore the  shallow
sub-surface domain of the country and develop and maintain national drill core library and documentation center in addition to its other functions. 
World Mining Ministries forum in its meeting held in March 2002 at Toronto discussed the future role of geological surveys and there was a
consensus that building up of fundamental geoscientific data base could not be left to ever-changing market forces.   It was further exphasized that
Geological Surveys should continue to generate geoscientific data for long-term geological understanding.

8.1       Minerals are broadly categorized as fuel minerals, atomic minerals, metallic minerals and non-metallic minerals. Among metallic minerals,
iron and associated minerals are called ferrous minerals (iron, manganese, chrome). All non-fuel, non-atomic and other than ferrous minerals are thus
included in the scope and ambit of non-ferrous minerals (eg.bauxite, copper, gold, zinc etc. metallic minerals as well as limestone, diamond etc.
nonmetallic minerals), for which the Ministry of Mines is primarily responsible. Prior to the formulation of the Statement of Industrial Policy, 1991,
mining of iron ore, manganese ore, chrome ore, gypsum, sulphur, gold and diamond, copper, lead, zinc, tin, molybdenum and wolfram were reserved
for the public sector. Since 1991, the non-ferrous mining and metallurgical industries are mostly in the private sector, for which no direct budgetary
support is needed. The Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) under the administrative control of the Ministry of Mines in the non-ferrous mining and
metallurgical sectors are provided budgetary support to the extent necessary. Of the PSUs, only the loss making PSUs, viz. Hindustan Copper
Limited (HCL) and Bharat Gold Mines Limited (BGML) would require budgetary support during 2003-04 primarily for separating the manpower,
for which budgetary grants have been proposed. Apart from this, for extending support to the mining sector, the Ministry of Mines has proposed

8.3       The Ministry of Mines apprised the Committee that  the Central Government has laid down the broad policy governing the mining sector,
while  the State Governments as owners of minerals grant the mineral concessions for all minerals, including the non-ferrous minerals, within the
framework laid down by the Central Government. The Ministry of Mines has constituted Mineral Advisory Council under the Chairmanship of the
Minister in Charge, in which the State Governments, the Mining and Metallurgical Industry and all other stakeholders are invited. This Council is the
Apex think tank and advises the Government towards desired policy changes for problem solving, and it is the endeavour of the Ministry to hold
meetings of the Council every year. The 27 th meeting of the Mineral Advisory Council was held on 21 st Sept., 2002. From time to time, meetings
with the investors are also held.      As a result of the interaction meetings, changes in the mining statutes have been brought about, inter-departmental
discussions for solving overlapping areas of concern have been held, and State Governments have been sensitized about local level problems of the
investors in the mining sector for corrective action.
 
8.4       The Committee is of the view that the Mineral Advisory Council should meet  at least four times in a year to sort out the problems being
faced by Mining Industry and suggest possible solutions and if required modification in the existing legal framework.  The Committee feels that
interaction of the representatives of Central Government, State Governments and investors in Mining Sector would solve the mutual problems and
thus the consensus-ad-idem and common efforts so evolved would lead to rapid growth and development of Mining Industry in general.
 
8.5       During the course of interaction with the representatives of Federation of Indian Mineral Industries (FIMI) the Committee  was apprised that
while the Government of India has liberalised the policies i.e. planned around the objective of making de-regulation and liberalization of mineral
production and processing industry a ground reality, however, the process of liberalization and dismantling regulatory regimes are yet to percolate to
the State level and to other implementing agencies. 
 
8.6       The Committee feels it imperative that policies that emanate from the Central Government should percolate to the State Government as well
as the implementing agencies in the spirit in which these policies are pronounced.  It is expected that the Mineral Advisory Council in its meeting
should persuade the representative of the State Government as well as the implementing agencies to bring out the necessary amendments in the
existing laws, rules and regulations to harmonize  the policy and practice.
 
8.7       In reply to the Committee’s query as to what extent the private enterprises has entered into non-ferrous Mining and Metallurgical Industries
and the steps  being taken to promote foreign investment, the Ministry of Mines replied that according to the information maintained by the Indian
Bureau of Mines (IBM), about 90% of the total mining leases in the country are held in the private sector, comprising about 65% of the total area
granted under mining lease. The National  Mineral Policy, 1993 promoted induction of private investment including foreign investment in the mining
sector, including in the non-ferrous mining sector. The Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957 (MMDR Act) was amended in
1994 and 1999. The Mineral Concession Rules, 1960 (MCR) and the Mineral Conservation and Development Rules, 1988 (MCDR), framed under
the MMDR Act, 1957 have also been  modified.
 
8.8       In so far as the policy regarding foreign direct investment (FDI) is concerned, in 1994, the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation)
Act, 1957 was amended to remove bottlenecks to induction of foreign equity for mining proposals. While FDI was normally limited to 50% this
limitation did not apply to captive mines of any minerals processing industry, and enhanced equity holdings could also be considered on case to case
basis. In 1997, this policy was further fine-tuned to allow induction of FDI up to 50% on an automatic basis. Since February 2000, all proposals for
foreign direct investment in the mining sector are eligible for automatic approval, subject to ceilings on foreign equity on the mineral concerned for
such approval. Except for precious stones and diamonds, automatic approval of foreign direct investment up to 100% is permissible for gold, silver
and all other non fuel and non atomic minerals. For precious stones and diamonds, automatic approval for foreign direct investment is permissible up
to 74%. Till 31st January 2002, the total number of foreign direct investment proposals approved through the Foreign Investment Promotion Board
(FIPB) with most of the leading global mining companies evincing interest in India was 72, indicating an expected FDI inflow of Rs.4018 crore.   In
October 1996, the Ministry of Mines had issued guidelines to facilitate large area aerial prospecting, in response to which, the entrepreneurs have
taken 65 large area prospecting licenses, covering an area of over 90 thousand square kilometers. After the amendment in the MMDR Act, 1957 in
1999, 119 reconnaissance permits covering an area of over 1,55,000 (approx.) thousand square kilometers have been granted.

16.2     Some of the changes are as follows:-
(i)
                  Custom duty on nickel has been unified at 10% from 5% and 15% irrespective of the class of importer.
(ii)                Custom duty on lead has been reduced from 25% to 20%.
(iii)               Special Additional Duty of Customs (SAD) has been exempted on rock phosphate and sulphur.
(iv)              Excise duty on articles made of mica has been rationalized which will give marginal boost to the crisis ridden mica sector.
(v)                Special Additional duty of Customs (SAD) has been exempted on rock phosphate and sulphur rock phosphate is of low grade and cost of
production and/or beneficiation being higher it will affect the domestic industry by encouraging imports into the country.
(vi)              The major impact on the mining industry would be the rise in fuel cost i.e. diesel as minerals are low value, high volume commodity
where any slight change in the freight would have significant impact.”
16.3              The mining or the mineral industry has not received any boost with the current budget provisions.  The lower custom duty had poised
severe threat on the sustainability of certain minerals like graphite and therefore it was suggested that the customs duty should be revised upwards
to safeguard the Indian industry from cheap imports as the cost of production in India is high because of high power and infrastructure cost. 
However, no relief has been extended to such minerals.
 
16.4                 The representatives of Federation of Indian Mineral Industries in the Committee’s meeting held on 27 th March 2003 and the Ministry
of Mines apprised the Committee through its Pre-budget Papers and expectations of Mining Industry from the budget (2003-04) that the Multi
Disciplinary Committee, constituted by the Ministry of Mines in April 1998 to study the taxation regime in the mining sector,  has given a number of
recommendations for bringing about fiscal priorities between Petroleum and Coal Sectors however, despite the best efforts of the Ministry of Mines
to consider the recommendations for including in the Budget have not received the favorable response of the Ministry of Finance. The major issues
include:-
(i)
      Rate of annual depreciation for mining plant and equipment should be increased to 100 percent.
(ii)    All expenditure incurred prior to commercial production including the expenditure incurred on site and deposit acquisition should be eligible for
amortization over the minimum mining lease period of 20 years or a lesser period at the  option of the lessee.
(iii)   For reclamation of mined out area, the mining companies may be allowed to earmark a percentage of book profits each year to meet
rehabilitation cost in future and set it aside as a special reserve in their books.
(iv)  Import duties on mining equipment may be reduced for equipment used for gold and diamond mining operations nil duty should be applicable
while for to other mining operations the duty should be at par with imports for coal mining.
(v)    Nil levy of excise duty structure for concentrate produced in the leaseholds and low excise duty structure for concentrates produced outside
leaseholds, may be devised.
(vi)  Declaration of ores, minerals and concentrates as “declared goods” under Central Sales Tax Act to limit the Sales Tax rate to 4 percent.
(vii)Abolishing or compounding of local levies, which are not conducive for free flow of goods.
(viii)
           State governments may not insist on equity sharing with investing companies.
16.5                 The Ministry of Mines submitted the recommendations of the Multi Disciplinary Committee to the Ministry of finance for its approval
in July 2000, however, the matter is still pending in the Ministry of Finance

16.6                 The Multi Disciplinary Committee on taxation regime for Mineral Sector in the country in its report submitted in July 2002, reiterated
its recommendations for suitable amendment in the Section 35E of the Income Tax Act for providing that all expenditure incurred prior to
commercial production including the expenditure incurred on site and development acquisition should be eligible for amortization over  the minimum

16.7                 Pertaining to the extension of benefits of Section 33ABA of the Income Tax Act, 1961, inserted w.e.f. 1 st April, 1999 (where a
provision of a site restoration fund has been provided for the petroleum and natural gas sector) for the mining sector in general, with the modification
that for reclamation of mined out areas, the mining companies may be allowed to earmark a percentage of book profits each year to meet the
rehabilitation cost in future and set aside as a special reserve in their books, the Multi Disciplinary Committee opined that:-
“Reclamation of mined out area to its original position involves huge investment which usually takes place at the end of mine life when there is no
come.   The extent of rehabilitation activities is clearly mentioned in the mining plan and environment management plan approved by the IBM and
oEF respectively and is also regulated by these authorities.  Expenditure on site restoration can be as high as 25% of the total project cost.  These are
gitimate expenses and need to be allowed as in the case of petroleum sector which provides the modalities for availing the deduction for exploration
d production for Companies that have concluded Production sharing contracts with the Government of India.  Introduction of similar provisions would
able non-fuel mining companies to provide for rehabilitation costs.  However, the requirement of maintaining a bank accounts as is the case of
troleum sector where the funds are deposited should be discarded as this creates an added cash constraint for the companies.  It is recommended that in
ace of this, the mining companies may be allowed to earmark a percentage of book profits each year to meet rehabilitation costs in future. 
eductibility may be permitted based on the amounts, set aside in a specific reserve created for the purpose in the books of account, instead of any
quirement to set the money abide in a separate bank account.”
16.8                 As regards the exemption of excise duty in the beneficiation of Ores it was stated that Ore and minerals are exempt from excise duty. 
Beneficiation of the ores yields ‘concentrates’ by reduction or removal of impurities to make it suitable as input raw material for subsequent
metallurgical or chemical or extractive operations.  Thus beneficiation of ore is a preparatory process for manufacture, not involving actual
manufacture.  Therefore, it is recommended that concentrates produced through beneficiation of ores within leasehold areas may be exempt from
levy of excise duty in the same manner as ores and minerals.

21.0     Impact of globalisation and liberalization
 
21.1     Since 1994, in a twin strategy, the Government has liberalized the legal framework for the mining regime, as well as progressively opened up
the mining and mineral sector for foreign investment. Statutory amendments have been carried out by which greater powers have been delegated to
the States for streamlining the mining regime, along with  security of tenure for the investors, for globalising the  Indian mineral sector, the policy
regime concerning foreign direct investment has been made more liberal. Since February 2000, all proposals for foreign direct investment in the
mining sector are eligible for automatic approval, subject to ceilings on foreign equity on the mineral concerned for such approval. Except for
precious stones and diamonds, automatic approval of foreign direct investment up to 100% is permissible for gold, silver and all other non-fuel and
non-atomic minerals. For precious stones and diamonds, automatic approval for foreign direct investment is permissible up to 74%.  In response to
the policy initiatives, Government approvals have been granted in respect of 72 applications, involving a proposed foreign direct investment (FDI)
induction worth Rs.4018 crore. Under the mining statutes, the foreign companies bringing in the FDI have to incorporate Indian subsidiary
Companies for being eligible to take mineral concessions, and therefore impact of globalisation and liberalization in the mining sector can be
indicated by the seriousness evinced by the Companies, who were permitted to bring in foreign investment by the Government in taking mineral
concessions. 
 
21.2     In 1997, the Ministry of Mines had issued guidelines to facilitate large area aerial prospecting, in response to which, the entrepreneurs have
taken 65 large area prospecting licenses, covering an area of over 90 thousand square kilometers. After the amendment in the MMDR Act, 1957 in
1999, 119 Reconnaissance Permits covering an area of over 155 thousand square kilometers have been granted as on date.
 
21.3     The Committee is pleased to note that the Government has liberalized the legal framework for the mining regime, as well as progressively
opened up the mining and mineral sector for foreign investment. Statutory amendments have been carried out by which greater powers have been
delegated to the States for streamlining the mining regime, along with  security of tenure for the investors, for globalising the  Indian mineral sector,
the policy regime concerning foreign direct investment has been made more liberal.  The Committee notes that  all proposals for foreign direct
investmentup to 100% in the mining sector, except for precious stones and diamonds, are eligible for automatic approval.   However, the Committee
is of the view that the Ministry of Mines should undertake comparative study of the legal frame work of countries leading in Mining activities and
mull out the relevant provisions for incorporating the same in the Indian Mining legal frame work.  The Committee further recommends that the
Ministry of Mines should also study the negative impact of globalization and liberalization and must  undertake suitable and resilient measure for
over coming the negative impact of globalization and liberalization on the Mining Sector.
 
21.4     The representatives of FIMI pointed out that there is practically no change in the attitude of the State Governments.  No rationalization of
procedures of transparency is evident.  There are inordinate delays in processing of applications and there are no serious efforts to reduce it.  Political
interference and red- tapism in decision making has added another dimension.  There is virtually no infrastructure no good roads, hardly any power

Fiscal Issues Pertaining to Mining Industry
 
                        The Committee after having giving a proper thought to the fiscal related proposals as suggested by FIMI and the Ministry of Mines
has arrived at the conclusion  that the Ministry of Finance should give due consideration to the following items for the rapid growth and development
of mining sector:-
(i)
                  In regard to the Section 35E of the Income Tax Act, 1962, the costs incurred during the pre production period should be amortizable
from the year of commencement of commercial production over the balance period of mining lease, or a lesser period at the option of the
lessee.  Accordingly, therefore, all expenditure incurred prior to commercial production including the expenditure incurred on site and
development acquisition should be eligible for amortization over the minmum lease period of 20 years or a leser period at the option of the
lessee.  The section 35E of the Income Tax Act, 1961 can be suitably amended accordingly without significant and immediate financial
implications.
(ii)
                The Committee is in agreement with the view of the Ministry of Mines for extension of benefits of Section 33ABA of the Income Tax
Act, 1961, inserted, 1st April, 1999 (where a provision of a site restoration fund has been provided  for the petroleum and natural gas sector)
for the mining sector in general, with the modification that for reclamation of minded out areas, the mining companies may be allowed to
earmark a percentage of book profits each year to meet the rehabilitation cost in future and set aside as a special reserve in their books.
(iii)               Concentrates produced through beneficiation of ores within leasehold areas may be exempt from levy of excise duty in the same manner
as ores and minerals.
(iv)              There should be no change in the duty structure on copper concentrate, copper cathodes, copper wire bars and CC rods and copper
scraps.
(v)                The rates of custom duty on aluminum scrap, primary and secondary aluminum may be maintained at the existing rate.

            The Committee is pleased  to  note the contribution  made by Mines companies  to National exchequer  especially  contribution made  by

Exchequer has been declining  over the year.  However, the  Committee  believes  that    once  the  implementation of capital restructuring of HCL
starts exerting  its positive impact on the   physical and  financial performance of  HCL, its contribution  towards    national  exchequer would  
automatically  improve. 
(Para  18.2)
 
            The Committee is of the opinion that since the a large number of private  sectors players   are coming to the mining sector and mines PSUs
are either have already been disinvested  i.e. BALCO  and HZL     or  are on the verge of disinvestment  as in the  case of HCL  and NALCO and  
due  to  on-going  liberalisation  process , the contribution of   mining sector  to national exchequer would improve in the years to come.  The
Committee feels that the on-going  liberalisation process should enhance the capabilities  of making  contribution of mining sector to the nation   and
society in general.  In this regard the  Committee  expects  that the Government would give due consideration towards  the contribution  made by
units of private  sector and PSUs in mining sector.

Impact of globalisation and liberalization
 
 
            The Committee is pleased to note that the Government has liberalized the legal framework for the mining regime, as well as progressively
opened up the mining and mineral sector for foreign investment. Statutory amendments have been carried out by which greater powers have been
delegated to the States for streamlining the mining regime, along with  security of tenure for the investors, for globalising the  Indian mineral sector,
the policy regime concerning foreign direct investment has been made more liberal.  The Committee notes that  all proposals for foreign direct
investmentup to 100% in the mining sector, except for precious stones and diamonds, are eligible for automatic approval.   However, the committee
is of the view that the Ministry of Mines should undertake comparative study of the legal frame work of countries leading in Mining activities and
mull out the relevant provisions for incorporating the same in the Indian Mining legal frame work.  The Committee further recommends that the
Ministry of Mines should also study the negative impact of globalization and liberalization and must  undertake suitable and resilient measure for
over coming the negative impact of globalization and liberalization on the Mining Sector.
(Para  21.3)
            The Committee hopes that the Mineral Advisory Council would take care of  advising the state Government for initiating the liberalization
process at state level by taking appropriate action at the earliest because ultimately states are the owners of minerals and states are likely to be
benefited with the reforms in mining sector.  The Ministry of Mines may chalk out the strategy for convincing states for implementing the
liberalization process in the mining sector.
(Para  21.5)
             “ Key concern Areas of Mining Industry”
 
 
            The Committee while expresses its satisfaction to know that the legal frame-work in mining sector is comparable to the best in the world,
however,  high cost of exploration is a matter of concern.  The Committee is of the view that adequate research and development activities and
employment of modern tools and techniques would certainly bring down the cost for exploration.
(Para  22.4)
             So far as the involvement of a number of agencies of in the grant of mineral concessions before the commencement of  actual operation
acting as a deterrent factor is concerned, it is suggested that the Ministry of Mines should  come forward with the proposal /suggestions for the
absolutely  required structures, stages and process for the grant of Mineral concession vis-à-vis the existing system.   The Committee is of the view
that there should be minimum time gap during the process of  grant of mineral concession, clearance from the Ministry  of Environment and Forest 
and the final commencement of actual operation.
(Para  22.5)
 
            The Committee is constrained to express its deep concern over the fact that the recommendations  of  the Multi Disciplinary Committee
MCD, constituted by the Ministry of Mines in 1998 to look into a taxation regime suitable to the mineral sector, is long pending in the Ministry of
Finance.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Mines should pursue the matter with the Department of Revenue( Ministry of Finance and
Company Affairs).  Since the Ministry of Mines has already examined the and accepted the recommendations of Multi Disciplinary Committee and it
has been found that the recommendations of the Multi Disciplinary Committee contains the potential and strategy for making Mining Sector globally
competitive, it is expected that  one wing of the Government has the co-ordination and consensus-aid-idem with the another wing, the Department of
Revenue would give due consideration to the recommendation of Multi Disciplinary Committee without  further loss of time.
