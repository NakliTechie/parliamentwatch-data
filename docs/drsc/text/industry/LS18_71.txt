REPORT
1.         History  is a witness to the fact  that  minerals  have  been the  basis of classification of human  civilization.   A
civilization undertakes development  in consonance with a pattern of physical  endowment of valuable minerals like Copper,
Iron Ore,  Aluminium,  Zinc, Lead, Gold,   Cobalt, Manganese, Graphite, Lime Stone, platinum etc.    Since minerals resources
are wealth hidden   beneath the earth-curst and are wasting assets    their conservation and planned   exploitation is of
paramount importance to  national economic development and growth .  As a matter of fact,  mineral resources not only provide
the basic inputs to domestic industry,  but also account for sizeable export and foreign exchange earnings.  Henceforth, the
development and regulation of the mineral sector is concomitant to the optimal utilisation of resources which ensures all round
development and growth of national economy.
1.1.      The Department of   Mines is  responsible for development and regulation of the mineral sector including  the mining
and metallurgy     of all non-ferrous metals both in private and public  sectors, but excluding   natural Gas, Petroleum, Coal and
Atomic   minerals.  As per the Annual Report (2001-02) of   the Department of Mines  has been   allotted subjects  as under:-
Legislation for regulation  of mines and development  of    minerals within the territory of  India, including   mines    and
minerals  underlying the ocean within the territorial waters  or the continental shelf, or the Exclusive Economic zone and other
Maritime Zones of  India as may be specified from time to  time by or under any law made by Parliament.
Regulation  of mines and development of minerals   other than coal,  lignite and sand  for  stowing and any minerals declared as
prescribed substances for the  purposes  of the  Atomic Energy Act, 1962 (33 of 1962)  under the control of the Union as
declared by law,   including   questions concerning  regulation and development of minerals in various states and the matter
connected therewith with or incidental thereto.
All other metals and minerals not  specifically allotted to any  other Ministry/ Department  such as aluminium, zinc , copper,
gold, diamond and nickel.
Planning development and control of  and assistance to all industries dealt with by the Department.
At the Secretariat level  the  Department of Mines is  mainly engaged in direction, supervision and coordination of five
divisions which inter-alia include Survey and Exploration,  Mines, Metal, General Administration and Finance and Accounts
Division.   Survey and Exploration has two principle agencies i.e. Geological Survey of India (GSI) and Mineral Exploration
Corporation Ltd. (MECL) for the implementation of the programmes of this division while Mines Division has one  subordinate
office to implement the programmes of the division, viz. Indian Bureau of Mines (IBM). Metal division of the Department of
Mines deals with the planning, exploration, development, monitoring of programmes and research in respect of non-ferrous
metals both in the public and private sectors General Administration Division is mainly concerned with the establishment and
administrative matter of the Department and Finance and Accounts Division is concerned with the budget, financial scrutiny of
projects and other proposals, rendering financial advice and maintenance and compilation of accounts.
Department of Mines has two  subordinate offices, namely :-
Geological  Survey of India ( HQ Kolkata)
Indian Bureau of Mines (HQ Nagpur)
Public Sectors Undertakings (PSUs) - Department of Mines  has four   PSUs
National Aluminium Company Ltd.  (NALCO), Bhubaneshwar
Hindustan Copper Ltd. (HCL), Kolkata
Minerals Exploration Corporation Ltd. (MECL).
Bharat Gold Mines Ltd.  (BGML). (closed since  1
st March, 2001)
1.5.      Joint Venture Undertakings ;  There are  three companies in which the Department of Mines held equity shareholding
during  2001-02
Bharat Aluminium Company Ltd. (BALCO), New  Delhi. ( a company jointly owned by Sterlite Industries Ltd. and
Government of India)
Hindustan Zinc Ltd. (HZL).  ( a company jointly owned by Sterlite Industries Ltd. and Government of India)
Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC) ( a company jointly owned by the State  Government of Sikkim and the Government of
India).  The  allocation of business relating to Sikkim Mining Corporation  (SMC) has been transferred  to the Department of
Development of North  Eastern Region on 21.12.2001.
1.6.      Research Institutions; There are three Research Institutions under the Department of  Mines, namely:
Jawaharlal Nehru Aluminium Research Development and Design Centre, Nagpur;
National Institute of Rock Mechanics, Kolar Gold Fields. Karnataka; and
National Institute  of Miners' health, Kolar  Gold Fields, Karnataka.

1.7.      The Committee notes the structural and functional pattern of the Department of   Mines,   the  Secretariat  for the 
Department of  Mines and  is of the  view  that structural aspect must  match the functional requirements and working   of  the 
organisation with adequate degree  of co-ordination and collaboration   among  various units  thus contributing as their  part    in
the overall functioning of the organisation in the interest of national  economy .
1.8.      The Committee, in view  that Bharat Gold Mines Ltd. has  been closed since 14
th March, 2001,  SMC stands transferred
to Department of  Development of  North Eastern Region,   BALCO since, March, 2001 and Hindustan Zinc Ltd. since April,
2002 has already been    disinvested  and NALCOs  and HCL   are   on the  verge of disinvestment, it is  the right  time  for the
Government  to undertake   the programme for  massive  rationalisation of  manpower not only at the level of  PSUs but also at
the level of Department  of Mines .
1.9.      The Committee was informed that the Department of Mines is enjoined to legislate for regulation of mines and
development of minerals within the territory of India including mines and minerals underlying the ocean within the territorial
waters or the continental shelf or the exclusive economic zone and other maritime zones of India as may be specified from time
to time by or under any law made by the Parliament.   Further, the Department is enjoined the task of regulation of mines and
development of non-fuel and non-atomic minerals including questions concerning regulating and development of minerals in
various States and the matter connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Department further has the responsibility for
overall development of all metals and minerals not specifically allotted to any other Ministry or Department (i.e., development
of aluminium, zinc, lead, copper and diamond, etc.). The Department has been allocated the work of Geological Survey of India
which is the premier agency in earth sciences in the country. The Department enforces the regulatory measures on all mines
pertaining to subjects under its charge including iron ore, through Indian Bureau of Mines.
 
1.10.    The Committee was further informed the Department of Mines is enjoined to look after planning, development and
control of and assistance to all industries dealt with by the Department including the Public Sector Undertakings.  It is obvious,
therefore, the role of the Department of Mines is not restricted to direct, supervise and coordinate the PSUs, which is only a
part  of its overall role and function  in the Government. Therefore disinvestment of PSUs, closure of BGML and transfer of
SMC to the Department of  Development of North Eastern  Region will allow the Department of Mines to focus on its areas of
core competence, i.e.  survey and exploration of all minerals other than natural gas,  petroleum and atomic minerals, administer
the mining statutes as well as enter into new areas and develop competence in, after the Off Shore Areas Mineral (Development
and Regulation) law comes into effect. The Bill stands  already introduced in the Parliament.
1.11.      The Committee understands the  core  function of Department of Mines , however,     the real  question arises  
whether  a separate Department is required for discharging the  core function only .  The Committee is of the  firm view  that
the  existence  of structure of any  department   presently  the   Department of Mines, must  justify itself  through  discharging  
its corresponding duties and function since  this involves substantial  financial implications. 
1.12.    When the Committee  asked the Department  of Mines  to comment on the  proposal of transferring National Mineral
Development  Corporation Ltd.  (NMDC) , Kudremukh Iron  Ore Company Ltd., Manganese Ore  (India) Ltd. and
Metallurgical  &  Engineering Consultants India Ltd. (MECON)  from Ministry of Steel to Department of Mines  in order to
rationalise the allocation of business of Government. The Department of Mines stated that since the subject matter relating to
legislation, regulation and concessions of manganese and iron ore is allocated to the Department of Mines and level playing
field between public and private sector companies are to be ensured therefore it is anomalous that public sector undertakings in
this area namely; NMDC, MOIL, Kudremukh Iron Ore Company Ltd. are dealt with by the Ministry of Steel whereas all the
private sector companies and necessary legislative and regulatory work be dealt with by the Deptt. of Mines.  A proposal
therefore has been submitted to the Cabinet Secretariat for transfer of these public sector undertakings, which are also slated to
be disinvested in due course of time, to the Deptt. of Mines, Ministry of Coal & Mines. 
1.13.    Reacting  to the proposal of  integration / merger of  Geological Survey of India, Mineral Exploration Corporation Ltd.
and India  Bureau of Mines  (IBM)  and the same is transferred either to  Ministry of Steel and  Coal or Petroleum with suitable 
change in the name of the Ministry Department of Mines explained that Geological Survey of India, Mineral Exploration
Corporation and Indian Bureau of Mines are mandated to take up different and specific responsibilities which cannot be
merged. GSI is a scientific organization serving as the principal provider of basic Earth Science information to Government,
Industry and general public.  MECL is a commercial organization for mineral exploration, whereas IBM is an arm of the
Government to enforce the legislations and regulations pertaining to MMRD Act and Rules thereunder.   Geological Survey of
India’s areas of activity include scientific survey and research for locating mineral resources and geological studies while IBM
is primarily responsible for promotion and conservation of scientific development of mineral resources of the country.  The
basic aim of MECL is to bridge the gap between preliminary exploration of mineral prospect and its commercial exploitation. 
From this   it is clear that these are responsible for development and regulation of mineral sector including the mining and
metallurgy of non-ferrous metals both in private and public sector excluding natural gas, petroleum and atomic minerals and
this cannot be transferred to the Ministry of Steel, Coal or Petroleum as these Ministries have been allotted specific charters.
1.14.    The Committee  understands  that GSI, IBM and MECL have their specific charter  of function but it is also true that
MECL  is   an  offshoot of GSI and later on created a separate body to supplement GSI .Moreover, GSI, and  MECL  work  not
only for the    Department of Mines  but also for  Department of Coal and  Ministry of Petroleum  .  The Committee is  of the
view that possibility of integration of  GSI, IBM and MECL may be explored and the  efforts may be made for  rationalising 
of  non-plan expenditure.  The Committee is of the further view that GSI  MECL and IBM may  co-exist  under one organic

unit and may remain either in the Department of  coal or petroleum  or Mines discharging  their  roles as mentioned  in their
respective  charter   of function   intact and in original  form.  The Committee opines   that GSI, MECL  and IBM  may be
integrated with  their   differential roles under one  umbrella  and which would not only rationalise   the  non-plan  expenditure 
but also  inject synergy  and enhancement of  brand  endowment.
1.15.    Under the circumstance, the  Committee recommends that  Government should immediately constitue  a  Task Force
having representatives from Department of Coal, Mines, Petroleum   and Steel   to examine the following issues:-
Determination of continuation of Department of Mines with a view  to discharge its  core function;
Feasibility and desirability of integration/merger of GSI, IBM and MECL  and  transferring of  same to either   of  Department
of Coal, Petroleum or Ministry of  Steel.
Transfer of  NMDC, KIOCL, MOIL and MECON from the Ministry of Steel  to Department of Mines.  
Merger of Department of Mines , coal and steel.
1.16.    The  Committee recommends that the Committee be apprised of the  findings    of Task Force and thereafter  the
Committee would   form its final opinion after examination the  findings of the   task force.  The  Committee recommends that
Task Force  should examine the all issues in  a time bound  programme..

2.2       The Committee is pleased to note that the economic philosophy behind the budget proposals of the Department of
Mines is broadly based on the principle that extensive survey and investigation should be carried out in the field of minerals in
which the country is deficient or which are of strategic importance for the country.  Besides this, the need for advance
exploration and prospecting techniques in the field of geophysical and geo-chemical methods has been envisaged and
participation of domestic and foreign private explorators  and prospectors has been stressed.  Simultaneously, the need for
modernisation and upgradation of technical skills of the organisations like GSI, MECL, IBM and some of the State Department
of Mines and Geology has been planned. In the field of mineral output , stress has been laid on recycling of metal scraps and
enhancement of smelting capacities of metals like aluminium, lead & zinc, copper, steel and Ferro alloys has been taken into
consideration.  Stress has also been laid on development of dimension and decorative stones, mining and processing industry,
minerals required for advanced ceramic and glasses, abrasive minerals and materials, high purity metals alloys and electronic
materials based on minerals. Development of infrastructure is another important issue on which stress has been laid on annual
plan 2002-03.  States are being encouraged to ensure proper linkage of raw material to the industry along with development of
necessary basic infrastructure. In the field of mineral administration, with the desired level of amendments in the Acts and
Rules governing mineral sector, the environment is highly congenial for both domestic and foreign investors. With these
measures, the budget proposals for the year 2002-03 pertaining to the Department of Mines, are expected to prove as an
instrument to march forward towards the national goal of overall development in the era of liberalised and globalised economy.

7.1.1.   The Geological Survey of India (GSI), a premier National Scientific Survey and Research Organisation is the principal
provider of basic earth science information to the government, industry and the general public, as well as responsive participant
in international geo-scientific fora .Set up in 1851 with the sole direction to locate coal for the railways, the GSI over the years
has expanded its role particularly after independence towards elucidation of the geo-scientific set-up of the country, locating
and harnessing the mineral  resources , providing geo-scientific information for the power (thermal, hydro, geothermal and
nuclear), agricultural, irrigation etc. sectors ,management of risks due to natural hazards such as floods, landslides and
earthquakes
defining and delineating areas prone to environmental degradation , providing geological inputs to optimise developments in
the transport and communication sector , geo-scientific studies and inventory of resources in the Exclusive Economic Zone
(EEZ) as well as in extended continental shelf in the surrounding ocean and also the icy continent of Antarctica glaciological
study ,dissemination of Information through map and publication ,training of scientific manpower. The prime activity of GSI
has resulted in coverage of 98.23% of the country (out of total map able area of 3.146 million sq. km.)  by geological mapping
in 1:50,000/63,360 scale 96% of Exclusive Economic Zone in the surrounding ocean (2.02 million sq. km.) 1.5 million sq. km
by aero-geophysical survey .
7.1.2.   GSI has been associated with discovery of almost all major mineral deposits in the country other than oil, gas and
atomic minerals Several Public Sector Undertakings, like HZL, HCL, NALCO, BALCO, Coal & Nevyeli Lignite Corporations,
NMDC etc. were established to exploit the mineral resources discovered by GSI. The vibrant Steel, Coal, Cement, Aluminium,
Copper-Lead-Zinc, Fertilizer, Chemical industries which expanded phenomenally in post-independence era, bear eloquent
testimony to GSI’s sustained efforts towards augmentation of mineral resource base. Value of mineral production increased
from Rs. 7 crore in 1951 to more than Rs. 25,300 crore (excluding petroleum and natural gas) presently, which may mainly be
attributed to the continuous efforts of GSI in augmenting the resource base of the country. Identification of off-shore resources
like heavy mineral placers, lime mud, phosphate rich sediments, micro nodules, calcareous sediments, etc. having mining
potentiality. GSI is conducting site specific environmental impact assessment studies in mining projects. The major mineral
sectors were brought under public control and primary responsibility of mineral exploration (except that of Oil, Gas and Atomic
minerals) was entrusted to GSI. In this context it may be mentioned that the nucleus of the ONGC and the Atomic-Mineral
Division were created in GSI, and later developed into independent organisations .The contribution of GSI in mineral industry
alone runs to enormous magnitude in terms of monetary value. Considering only 10% of resources estimated by GSI, the

selection of S&T projects is to cater to the national requirement and develop technologies which are not available for import
due to either strategic or commercial reasons. The details of major projects are given below:-
Development of technology for extraction of nickel from chromite overburden of Sukinda:- Sukinda Valley in Orissa is the
only known source for nickel in the country. Even in this the nickel content is less than 1% and the ore is oxidic in nature.
Nowhere in the world such an ore is exploited commercially. A process has been developed indigenously and the critical
parameters of the process have been tested in a pilot plant established at RRL, Bhubaneshwar. National Aluminium Company
under the Department of Mines is examining further the commercial viability of the process.

technology used for manufacturing aluminium auto components. The technology is closely guarded and is available for import
only at a very high price, though the components made using this technology are available freely for import at high prices. A
National facility is being set up to develop semi solid forming  technology indigenously. Various auto manufacturers and
research organizations like Indian Space Research Organisation, DMRL have been involved in the development of the project
to ensure gainful use of the facilities.

17.0     Impact of withdrawal of the SSI exemption   on   granite in the budget 2002-03 on the granite industry
17.1.    In the budget 2002-03 Excise Duty exemption available to small scale units upto a  limit of one crore turnover has been
removed and therefore all small scale and cottage scale granite industry come under the net of excise duty @ 16%.  Coupled
with already increased cost, adverse effect of recession in last few years and signing of WTO agreement and dumping of
vitrified ceramic titles and  granite from China had already affected the domestic market.
17.2.The All India Granite and Stone Association has reported that there are approx 5 thousand granite units in small scale and
cottage scale sector/tiny scale entrepreneurs having an investment ranging from Rupees 5 lakhs to Rupees 60 lakhs each.  If
these units have to pay excise duty, many of these would become unviable resulting in unemployment of over 3 lakhs people
working in the industry.   Many of the units in small scale and cottage scale cater to local market directly and export market
indirectly and thus even exports too will suffer.   In larger interest, therefore, restoration of excise  exemption limit up to Rs. 1
crore has been prayed.
17.3.    The Committee observes that the exemption of  excise duty on small scale  and  cottage scale granite  industry upto limit
of Rs.1.00 crore in the  budget 2002-03 has  rendered  5 thousand granite units in  complete  doldrums and employment of  3
lakh workers  is in jeopardy.  The Committee has been given to understand  that SSI  granite industry is already reeling under 
high cost of production, adverse  affect of  recession  and  dumping of  cheaper   product  from China and the  withdrawal of the
SSI exemption  on granite SSI  sector would  further   hit the granite SSI  sector adversely.  Therefore, the Committee
recommends,  that the Government would  take due  note of the provisions  enshrined  in the  budget 2002-03 and  do justice  to
the granite SSI  industry.
