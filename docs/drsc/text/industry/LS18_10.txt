6.01 The Performance Budget of the Ministry of Mines for the year 1994-95 states that the Ministry is 
primarily responsible for the regulation and development of the mineral sector including the mining and 
metallurgy of all non ferrous metals both in private and public sectors but excluding Natural Gas , Petroleum 
coal and Atomic minerals. Mmeral resources are national assets. Conservation and planned exploitation of 
minerals are therefore, of prime importance to the national economy. Besides providing the basic inputs to 
domestic industry the mineral sector accounts for sizeable exports and foreign exchange earni ngs. Having 
regard to the extent and variety of mineral resources in the country there is great potential for rapid growth 
of this sector. It is the onus of the. Ministry of the Mines to develop this sector with utmost care and caution 
to enable this sector to compete with the contemporary globalisation in the internatio nal area. But the 
Committee finds that the entire mines and minerals sector is in doldrums. Large number of mines have either 
closed or at the brink of closure. The situation is very alarming particularl y in the States of Bihar , Andhra 
Pradesh , Rajasthan, Goa, Orissa, Madhya Pradesh etc. where a good number of Mines have closed dov·m. 
There is no provision in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry for the revival of closed Mines in the 
country. The Committee has not been told of any kind of package prepared by the Ministry for the reopening 
of closed mines in the country and make them viable. Rather the Committee has been told that the minerals 
belong to the State Governments. The onus of re-opening of closed mines is therefore the responsibility of 
the States and Ministry has washed its hands off by pointing out the responsibihty on the States ignoring 
the loss being caused to the nation due to the closure of mines throughout the country. The States faced with 
acute resource constraints and are unable to divert their attention towards the closed· mines. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government must also pay attention towards the closed/ 
closing mines throughout the country and prepare a comprehensive package for their revival in 
consultation with the State Governments concerned. There must be a perfect co-ordi nation between

the Governme nt and the Gove rnm ent of the States in thi s •·egard . The Governm ent should constitut e 
a tea m of experts to find out the causes of closure of mines and suggest the ways for their revival within 
a t ime frame. 
6.02 The Committee finds that there is lack of coordination between the Government and the Government 
of the States as far as regulating of mining is concerned. Responding to a query about the foreign investment 
in the mineral sector after the enactment of the Mines and Minerals (Regulation and Development) 
Amendment Act, 1994 the Secretary of Ministry of Mines states that the minerals belong to the State 
Governmen ts. All applicat ions have to be submitted by entrepreneurs to the concerned State Governments 
and it is they who have to give the prospecting licence or the mining lease as the case may be. The 
Government did not have precise information whether any State Government had received any formal 
proposa l and whether any foreign party has applied for any prospecting licence. The Committee finds it not 
convincing. After opening the mineral sector of the country for foreign investment and participation in order 
to accelerating i nflow of domestic and foreign capita l into the mineral sector the Governme nt has left the 
matter entirely to the State Governments. On such a vital issue there should have been a perfect 
understanding and coordination between the Central and State Governments. The Committ ee, ther efor e, 
recommends· that Governm ent must keep a watch on every foreign investment propo sal in the miner al 
sector and advi se the c once rn ed State Government to make best u se of such a propo sal with out 
compr omising nationa l interest and prioritie s and maintain the closest coordin ation with the State 
Govern ment in thi s regar d. If there are stron g sentiment s expr essed by the State Gove rnmen t, as far 
as possib le such sentiments should be respecte d. It is import ant th at compensation money reac hes the 
peo ple allocated to Geological Survey of India (GSI) in Major Head 2853. GS I plays a dominant role for 
the gro\\th of the Mineral sector of the country. According to the Perforn1ance Budget (1994- 95) of the 
Ministry GSI is responsible for collection. collation and dissemination of geological data and providing 
basic geologic~ I information essential for the successful implementation of practically are the developmen­
tal programmes of the count!). The investigation carried out by GSI contribute directly to the discovery and 
evaluation of mineral resources ensuring thereby the continued and steady supply of vital ra\\ materials to 
the core industrial sector . The results of investigation aid in the better understanding of the cmstal 
framework and the geological process with bearing on development in other as vital sectors such as 
agriculture, land use management, irrigation and power development. utilisation of water resources. 
development of transport and communication, educat ion and scientific management of environment. Thus 
survey and mapping and mineral exploration are the most important functions ofGSI for Mineral sector 
and geological process having bearing on other important sectors oft he country but the committee finds that 
out of the total allocation ofRs. 125.74% crores in BE 1994-95 only a sum ofRs. 11.20 crorcs has been 
earmarked for survey and mapping and Rs. 12.90 for mineral exploration. Thus a total sum ofRs. 25.10 
crores will be spent on the vital functions of Survey and mapping and Mineral exploration whereas more 
than Rs. I 00 crores will be spent on other purposes mainly administration etc. The committee, therefore, 
recommends that additional funds be allocated for such primary functio ns as survey and mapping and 
Mineral exploration of the GSI. Such Su rveys and exp lorat ion should conform to a precise ly ind icated 
time frame so that a check would be possib le on tangible res ults achieved. The Committee appreciates 
the gesture of the Government in increasing the resource Budgetary allocation to GSI as per its 
recommendations made in its Thjrd Report on the Demands for Grants of the Ministl) ' of Mines ( 1993-94 ). 
However, the Committee recommends that instead of allocating a substa ntial amou nt for other 
purposes to GSI more thr ust should be given for its basic work. T hough the GSI is achieving its 
ta r gets, additiona l resources will certain ly bring an improveme nt in basic work and the nat ion will 
be benefitted.
