Evolution and Mandate  
The Government of India established the Ministry of  Small Scale Industries 
and Agro & Rural Industries (SSI & ARI) in October,  1999 as the nodal Ministry 
for formulation of policies and Central sector prog rammes/schemes, their 
implementation and related coordination and for sup plementing the efforts of the 
States/Union Territories for promotion and developm ent of small scale, agro and 
rural industries in the country.  Subsequently, in September, 2001 the Ministry of 
SSI and ARI was bifurcated into two separate Minist ries, namely, Ministry of 
Small Scale Industries and Ministry of Agro and Rur al Industries.  In 2006, 
“Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Ac t (MSMED) 2006” were 
enacted by the Parliament.  Pursuant to this enactm ent, the Ministry of Agro and 
Rural Industries and the Small Scale Industries were merged into a single Ministry, 
namely Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises. 
2. The Committee acknowledges that the Micro, Small  and Medium 
Enterprises (MSMEs) including Khadi and Village/Rural Enterprises, constitute an 
important segment of the Indian economy in terms of  their contribution to 
country's industrial production, exports, employmen t and creation of 
entrepreneurship base.  The Committee noted that th e primary responsibility for 
promotion and development of MSMEs lies with State Government.  However, the 
Government of India has always taken active interes t in supplementing the efforts 
of the State Governments.  The role of the Ministry  of MSME is mainly to assist 
the States in their efforts to promote growth and d evelopment of MSMEs.  In 
addition, the Ministry also attempts to address the  common concerns of these 
enterprises.  

-2- 
3.  Instead of assisting the State Government, the Min istry has devised its 
own Schemes and implements them through State agenc ies like KVIBs and 
DICs.  The Committee finds that though State Govern ments are involved in 
consultative process at apex level, and execution l evel, the Central and State 
agencies do not converge institutionally.  For exam ple in the KVIC act, there 
is no institutional arrangement for coordination be tween KVIC, State 
Directorate and KVIBs to execute KVIC programmes.  The Committee 
observes that with greater emphasis of cooperative federalism and setting up 
of Niti Ayog as consultative forum between Centre a nd State Governments,  
the role of the Ministry may be suitably recast with respect of formulation and 
implementation of the various programmes. 
Composition of the Ministry 
 
4. The formulation and implementation of policies a nd 
programmes/projects/schemes for MSME sector is undertaken by the Ministry with 
the assistance of its attached and autonomous organ izations, namely, Micro, Small 
and Medium Enterprises Development Organization (MS ME-DO) or O/o 
Development Commissioner (MSME), the National Small  Industries Corporation 
Limited (NSIC), the Khadi and Village Industries Co mmission (KVIC), the Coir 
Board and three National Level Entrepreneurship Dev elopment Institutes, viz., 
National Institute for Entrepreneurship and Small B usiness Development 
(NIESBUD), NOIDA (U.P.), National Institute for Mic ro, Small and Medium 
Enterprises (NI-MSME), Hyderabad and the Indian Ins titute of Entrepreneurship 
(IIE), Guwahati.  The Ministry of MSME is having tw o Divisions called Small & 
Medium Enterprises (SME) Division and Agro& Rural I ndustry (ARI) Division.  
The  SME Division  is  allocated  the work, inter –alia, of administration, vigilance

(NSIC) Ltd., a public sector enterprise and the thr ee autonomous national level

promotion.  The ARI Division looks after the admini stration of two statutory 
bodies called the Khadi and Village Industries Comm ission (KVIC), Coir Board 
and Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Rural Industrializa tion (MGIRI).  They are also

17. The Secretary, M/o MSME tried to rationalize th e pattern of expenditure: 
“If we take the method of incurring expenditure, we  have the budget 
estimate.  There is a limitation on how much we can spend from quarter to quarter.  
So, in the first quarter, there is a limitation on how much we can spend.  Similarly, 
in the second quarter, third quarter and the fourth  quarter too.  In the existing 
arrangement, while the expenditures may look to be higher, and they are higher, 
compared to the first, second and the third quarter s, in the last quarter.  Actually, 
there is a limitation on how much we can spend with  reference to the Budget 
Estimate and RE in the last quarter.  So, even on c rowding of the expenditure in 
the last quarter, the arrangement  of the Finance M inistry, which is implemented 
through our Financial Advisor, is that there is a l imit up to which we can spend.  
And, that limit is, I think, 33 per cent of the Bud get, which can be spend in the last 
quarter.  So, it cannot be more than that”.

the same in its earlier Reports also. This year too , till 31 
st  December, 2014, by 
Ministry’s own admission it could spent only 55% of   the  allocation with DC-

surrendered at RE stage. Ministry’s plea that expen ditures could not be made

19. The Secretary justified the bulk expenditure in  the bank-linked schemes like 
CLCSS or PMEGP by explaining the structure of schem e where banks raise the 
claims on the Ministry.  His assertion was that the  Ministry disburses the money

20.  The Committee is apprehensive to accept the argume nt. The Committee 
also finds it strange that as per the Ministry’s ow n Annual Report, the Banks 
have been meeting the lending targets for MSE secto r as prescribed by the 
RBI. Yet, banks are not coming forward to support M inistry’s schemes for 
MSME  sector despite budgetary support.  Obviously, the  answer  lies  in  the 
structure of schemes and process of screening by th e Ministry and its 
agencies, that needs to be addressed .   
21.  The Committee wonders if the Banks get the applica tion only towards 
the last months of the Financial Year or they take protracted time to 
scrutinize the application. The Committee will not be inclined to recommend 
to Banks to lower their standards of scrutiny. The applications  are  processed 
by the agencies assigned by the Ministry. Whether t hese agencies scrutinize 
the applications diligently? For example, in case o f PMEGP, the applications 
are processed by DLTFC. Majority of the applications forwarded by DLTFC  

-10- 
are rejected by Banks. The Committee wonders if any  purpose is served by 
DLTFC as the applications forwarded by them are to be rejected on merit. 
The Committee had asked the Ministry to furnish the  information if the 100 
days deadline prescribed by it to settle an applica tion, was at all being 
followed. The Committee too had recommended that th e DLTFC should meet 
at least once in two months to clear the cases. The  Ministry too has launched 
E tracking system. Yet, it was not in position to identify the bottlenecks except 
for pointing at Banks for delay. For example, in ca se of PMEGP the 
Committee has repeatedly asked that the projects sh ould be sanctioned 
throughout the year. Yet, this year, within four da ys from 28 th  February to 4 th  
March, projects worth Rs 400 crores were disbursed.  
22. Another related aspect is targets.  The Committee f inds the targets of 
schemes rather moderate vis-à-vis the scope of MSME s in the country.  The 
Committee  wonders  that  despite cut in RE and further under utilization, the 
Ministry is able to meet majority of its targets.  Obviously either targets were 
kept modest or the demands for allocation were inflated.  
Unorganized Sector  
23. MSMEs are predominantly in unorganized sector.  As per fourth census, out 
of the 360 lakh MSMEs only 15 lakh are registered. 
24. The Committee also took note of the Prime Minis ter’s letter dated 31 
st  
March, 2015 to Small business persons of the countr y. In which he succinctly 
summarized the importance of this sector in the economy: 
"While many think that large companies dominate, in  reality, it is over 5.5 crore 
(55 million) small units like yours, which drive ou r economy through small 
manufacturing, trading and service businesses." 
 

-11- 
25. He sets the priority of the Government to make doing business easier for 
these units: 
“You should be able to run your business easily, no t be harassed by officials, get 
loans for your day-to-day activities, material  for  your operations and markets for 
your product." 
 
26.  On being asked whether the Ministry’s schemes were  reaching to 
remaining unregistered and unorganized sector, the Committee was informed 
in a written reply that the Ministry has not done a ny survey whether its 
Schemes are reaching to unorganized sector nor has it taken steps so far to 
facilitate doing business for the unorganized sector.   
27. The Committee also raised the issue of inaccess ibility of Government 
schemes for the unorganized sector during discussio ns with Industry associations. 
Whether such units are at all aware of the existenc e of such Schemes, it was 
informed: 
“There are some important reasons as to why most of the schemes do not percolate 
down.  Firstly, the MSME Act came into being in 200 6.  Prior to that the units 
which were registered were now supposed to be registered under the EM-Part II of 
the new Act.  The procedure is so cumbersome that  if  you  take  the statistics, only 
35 per cent are registered while 65 per cent cannot  avail any benefit under any 
scheme or even the facilitation council benefits of the recovery of receivables. 
 Sir, we all talk about ease of doing business. Mos t of the micro and small 
units are one man show.  If they have to make sever al trips to the Government 
offices, they lose the interest and then, they just  forget about the rest of the 
procedures.  Since they were already registered ear lier, it should be mandatory to 
give them registration under E.M. Part II, which wo uld solve the problem. This is 
something which needs to be looked into very urgent ly because almost 65 per cent 
of the units are deprived of the benefits.” 
 
 

-12- 
28. When the Committee raised the issue with the Se cretary, M/o MSME, 
during interaction on 25 th  March, 2015, he submitted: 
 
“The question which comes to mind is that why these micro enterprises are, 
actually, not getting registered and there we enter , the issue of ease of doing 
business, the  Inspector Raj and the  laws themselv es, which don't tend to 
distinguish between a big enterprise and a small en terprise when it comes to 
compliance, whether it is taxation laws or labour l aws or local laws, they are 
completely neutral on this; whether they are dealin g with a small enterprise or a 
big enterprise.  So, the compliance burden on the s mall enterprises is such that it 
simply does not make sense for them to register, an d that is why they are 
functioning in such a massive manner in the informa l sector.  This, I myself had 
pointed out in my Report as Chairman of the Inter-M inisterial Committee on 
Manufacturing in the MSME sector in the year 2013 itself, that  something needs to 
be done.  This particular trend is totally unsustai nable,  when the growth of the 
informal sector has been actually, much faster than  that of the formal sector.  
Again, those issues are  connected with  the ease of doing business and in  the ease 
of doing business,  there are, of course, a number of issues which have to be dealt 
with.  They are not be dealt with by the Ministry o f  MSME.  We are doing 
advocacy on behalf of the small enterprises, but these issues have to be really dealt 
with by the other Ministries in the Central Governm ent, mainly the Ministry of 
Finance and the Ministry of Labour.  Some issues, w hich are pertaining to 
pollution control, have to be dealt with by the  Mi nistry of Environment.  But there 
are also a lot of issues relating to land, clearanc es and taxation at the State level, 
by the State Government itself.” 
29. The Committee takes serious note of the situation.  Considering the 
Ministry’s admission that its schemes are mostly ta rgeted to Registered Units, 
and that only a miniscule minority of MSMEs is regi stered, the Committee 
finds the situation unacceptable. The MSMED Act, 20 06 does not make it 
obligatory upon micro enterprises to register. In e xcluding unorganized 
sector, the Ministry has gone against the statuary provisions of the Act in 
respect of registration. Unorganised sector is at t he margins of even 
marginalized MSME sector.  The Ministry should do a  comprehensive survey 
if its Schemes are reaching to unregistered  micro  enterprises  who essentially  

-13- 
seek ease of doing business. The Ministry has highl ighted involvement of 
multiple Ministries and State governments for facil itating conducive business 
environment. The Ministry has conveniently confined  itself to the role of 
advocacy. However, the MSMED Act provides for Natio nal MSME Board 
with representation from all the relevant Ministrie s and the State 
Governments. The Ministry has not informed that thi s issue was ever 
discussed  or  likely to be discussed  in future, in the National Board  so  that a 
comprehensive actionable national policy on “Ease o f Doing Business” 
particularly for the unorganized unregistered sector,  could be evolved.

32. Submitting the status of budget pronouncements made in 2014-15 and this 
year i.e., 2015-16, the Secretary, M/o MSME updated the Committee: 
“Sir, you mentioned about some specific Budget anno uncement -- Rs.10,000 
crore fund for start-up. In the Budget, prior to th at, there was an announcement of 
Rs.5,000 crore for start-up.  Sir, there is no Budg et allocation.  There is no 
allocation  in  the  Ministry’s Budget  or  in  any other Budget for the start-up fund. 
We framed a Scheme.  I have personally sent this sc heme to the relevant 
Departments that if we have this fund, this is how we would like to implement it.  
But there was no money. The Department of Financial  Services informed us that 
this scheme would have to be implemented on using t he funds which are available 
with the Reserve Bank of India coming from the shortfall in priority sector lending.  
Now, those funds are of a short duration, i.e., the y have to be repaid in a period of 
three years. For a start-up equity, you require fun ds which have a long-term 
tenure, at least, for seven-eight years.  So, there  was an asset-liability mismatch 
and, naturally using these short-term funds, no sch eme could be implemented by 
SIDBI effectively for long-term financing, i.e., eq uity support for micro, small and

Credit Guarantee Scheme  
 
53. The Credit Guarantee Scheme covers collateral f ree credit facility (term loan 
and/ or working capital) extended by eligible lendi ng institutions to new and 
existing micro and small enterprises up to Rs. 100 lakh per borrowing unit. The 
guarantee cover provided is up to 75% of the credit facility up to  Rs.50 lakh  (85% 
for loans up to Rs. 5 lakh provided to micro enterp rises, 80% for MSEs owned/ 
operated by women and all loans to NER) with a unif orm guarantee of 50% of the 
credit exposure above Rs. 50 lakh and up to Rs.100 lakh. A composite all-in 
Annual Guarantee fee of 1.0% p.a. of the credit fac ility sanctioned (0.75% for 
credit facility upto Rs. 5 lakh and 0.85% for above Rs. 5 lakh and upto 100 lakh for 
Women, Micro Enterprises and units in NER including  Sikkim) is now being 
charged. The scheme is being operated by the Credit  Guarantee Fund Trust for 
Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) set up jointly by the Government of India 
and SIDBI. The  corpus  of  CGTMSE  is  contributed  by the Government of India 
and SIDBI in the ratio of 4:1. The corpus of CGTMSE  has been enhanced to Rs. 
2389.04 crore with the contribution of Rs. 1911.23 crore from the Govt. of India 
and Rs. 477.8 crore from SIDBI.

Credit Guarantees Scheme  are invoked by Member Len ding Institutions (MLIs) 
and claims are settled by CGTMSE. During FY 2014-15  (April 01, 2014 to 
February 28, 2015), 3,59,081 guarantee proposals ha ve been approved for credit 
amount of Rs.18,995.12 crore.
