1.0            Introduction
 
1.1       The Department of Public Enterprises of the Ministry of Heavy Industries and public Enterprises plays the nodal role in coordinating the matters of general policy of non-financial nature affecting all the public sector industrial and commercial undertakings. Its functions include formation of policy guidelines for PSEs in areas like performance improvement and evaluation, personnel management, Board
structures, pay structures, wage settlement, training, vigilance, performance appraisal   etc. The mandate also includes the matters relating to MoU mechanism to improve the performance of PSUs . The Department is also the mandated with the issues pertaining to the Counselling, Retraining and Redeployment of the rationalized employees of the CPSEs. The DPE is also the administrative Department   for the
Board for Reconstruction of Public Sector Enterprises and the Bureau of Public Enterprises and looks after the issues relating to Permanent Machinery of Arbitration for PSUs..
 
1.2       The Committee sought the views of the Department about the measures to strengthen its role in view of mandate to formulate the non-financial policies for all the PSEs.
1.3       The Committee was informed that Department of Public Enterprises collected, evaluated and maintained information on several areas in respect of CPSEs. The Committee agrees that DPE is the vital interface between the administrative Ministries/Departments and the CPSEs. It appreciated that the DPE has ample scope and potential to encompass in the following areas:
 
                  (i)      Support to training institutes;
                (ii)      Improving  corporate governance
               (iii)      Survey of State Level Public Enterprises
              (iv)      Thematic consultancies and studies
                (v)       Seminars and workshops on generic issues of CPSEs.
 
1.4       In its interaction with the respective Ministries over the compliance of certain guidelines issued by the DPE, the Committee is constrained to observe recurrent violations. The Committee therefore reiterates its recommendation that the DPE must also be proactive and persuasive in collecting, collating and monitoring the performance and progress of the guidelines for PSE. In this regard the Committee
further recommends that the DPE must undertake thematic consultancies and studies, seminars and workshop on the issues concerning PSEs more regularly. And that the Planning Commission should be approached to allocate more funds for this purpose. The DPE must also conduct annual survey of the State PSEs with the help of Institute of Public Enterprises, Hyderabad.  The Committee also recommends
that Institute of Public Enterprises, Central Manufacturing Technology Institute, Bangalore should be brought under the administrative control of Ministry of HI & PE. The Department must also contribute funds to the National Foundation for Corporate Governance. The Committee also recommends that DPE must be represented on the Boards of Governors of the premier management institutes in consonance
with the autonomy of these institutes. The Committee believes that greater interaction with the professional academia will help the DPE to evolve modern management practices for the PSEs. The Committee suggests that DPE should contact these institutions to conduct periodic surveys on the constraints faced by the PSEs and bring out recommendations that could become a useful input for the BRPSE.

4.6       The Committee observes that maximum PSEs for which revival has been recommended by the BRPSE belonged to the capital goods sector under Department of Heavy Industries followed by the chemicals  and fertilizer sector. The Committee expresses serious concern over the costly revivals that largely entail waivers and conversions of the secured loans and interest dues towards Government of India.
The Committee notes that as per the revival plan, the revived units are likely to earn meager profits by the 2009-10. The Committee seeks information from the DPE that of the fresh cash infusions how much would be inform of secured loans, unsecured equity and grants respectively. The Committee also seeks information  regarding the steps taken to secure the government loans and interest there on.
 
4.7       The Committee   also observes among the administrative Ministries, the practice of using the recommendations of the BRPSE as the alibi without actually justifying the revival package. The Committee recommends that once a revival package is approved by the competent authority, the onus of justifying the revival package must be one the administrative Ministries. The NCMP states “While every
effort will be made to modernize and restructure sick public sector companies and  revive sick industry, chronically loss-making companies will either be sold-off, or closed, after all workers have got their legitimate dues and compensation”. However the Committee observes that the revival plans have been largely confined to the manpower rationalization, cutting the wage cost and liquidating the liabilities for
the accounting adjustments. The Committee wonders that after such accounting adjustments the PSEs will be earning only the notional profits or the actual profit. Taking lesson from the earlier failed attempts of reviva  such as HMT etc, it is evident that such window dressing of accounts will result only in notional revival that may not be sustainable in longer run. The Committee wonders if measures to secure
Government investments are built in the revival package. The BRPSE must suggest measures to secure Government’s investments as a substantial part of Government’s investments are in form of unsecured equity. The Committee suggests that instead of waiving the loans and interests dues towards Government, they can be deferred till a prescribed period till such time PSE should attain profitability.
 
5.0       ICPE
 
5.1       The Committee sought to know the reason for increase in the contribution to ICPE from Rs.0.37 crores in BE 2006-07 to Rs.0.73 crore in RE 2006-07 and same amount has been retained this year too. The Committee also required the information regarding the impact of such costly courses.
 
5.2       The Committee was informed that the ICPE was set up at the initiative of United Nations for promoting public enterprises in developing countries. It is an inter-Governmental organization of developing countries for improving the performances of their public enterprise, a strategic instrument of economic and social development. With the passage of time financial position of ICPE has been adversely
affected due to non-payment of membership contributions and inadequate generation of internal resources from programmes  and activities undertaken at the center. After the erstwhile State of Yugoslavia disintegration and winds of economic liberalization swept throughout world, too much emphasis on public sector became somewhat of an anachronism in the changing world scenario. As a result many
member countries have withdrawn there membership and only 19 countries now remain members of ICPE out of 42. Out of these only India and Slovenia  are paying regular membership contributions. The survival of ICPE now critically dependents on India and Slovenia . The hope of survival of ICPE gathered momentum when Slovenia became member of EU and is assuming  presidency of EU by 2008.
Government of India’s contribution to ICPE is paid in advance. During the financial year 2005-06, the membership contribution for the year 2006 could not be paid in advance due to late receipt of demand letter from ICPE.   Therefore, in BE 2006-07, the membership contribution could be paid for the year 2006 only. Thus, contribution for the year 2007 yet remained un-paid, which has been taken care of in
RE 2006-07. And as such in RE 2006-07 an amount of Rs. 72 lakh has been provided. Taking into account these factors and for the revival and revitalization of  ICPE, the annual contribution of Government of India has been enhanced from Rs.36 lakh to Rs.72 lakh per annum from the year 2007-08 on wards. The Ambassador of India, Budapest who was accredited to Slovenia also supported the enhancement
of India’s contribution, as ICPE can provide an intellectual platform for India to project its image in Europe.
 
5.3       In view of absence of Department’s specific reply on the query regarding the impact of costly courses of ICPE and also considering the depleting membership of the ICPE, the Committee feels if the erstwhile members of ICPE did not consider the organization as worthwhile, the rationale for continuing with the costly contribution should be more robust than mere survival  of a moribund  organization.
The Committee, therefore desires DPE to review its stand on ICPE and inform the Committee the outcome thereof.

During 2006-07 ICPE conducted one year MBA programme  in which 4 Executives one each from Hindustan Paper Corporation Ltd., Hindustan Machine Tools and two from Bharat  Heavy Electricals Ltd were nominated. Candidates are

The Committee feels that performance of the Company must justify this expenditure.  However no assessment based on tangible benchmarks regarding the impact of such courses was given to the Committee. The Committee considers such cost-
benefit assessment is essential given the financial stakes involved in such training. The DPE may inform the Committee if such an assessment has at all been formulated. The DPE must initiate a survey of the efficacy of ICPE training.
 
5.6       The Committee in its 168
th Report (2005) underlined the need for organizing similar ED programmes  with Indian Institutions for other member countries of ICPE, so as to enhance and project the academic profile of premier academic and research institutions of the country. In this regard the Committee sought to know that beyond the initial communication of DPE with the relevant institutions, if any
such course for ICPE-member countries has actually been organized by Indian Institutions.
 
5.7       The Committee was informed that in pursuance to its recommendation the matter was taken up with Secretary, Deptt. of Scientific and Industrial Research, Secretary, Secondary and Higher Education and DG, ICPE followed by reminders. DPE received response from DG, CSIR agreeing for imparting training to the participant member countries of ICPE in consultation with ICPE. DG, ICPE has already
been advised in this regard. The response from Secretary, Higher Education was still awaited.
 
5.8       The Committee is constrained to note the tardy progress made after passage of two years. The Secretary, M/O HRD has not even responded to the DPE’s reminder, whereas the DG, ICPE has not followed up on the advise to contact the CSIR. The Committee recommends that India as the prominent contributor and founder member of the ICPE, must persuade the ICPE for such academic cooperation
between  CSIR and ICPE. The Committee strongly disapproves the lackadaisical approach of the M/O HRD. DPE must secure an early and elaborate response from the M/O HRD in this respect.
 
6.0            Training for skill upgradation  
 
6.1       The Committee in its 168
th report had recommended that DPE organize orientation and skill development programmes for shop-floor level technical functionaries, as they are the end users of new material management and production techniques.
 
6.2       In its response the DPE informed that it supplemented the efforts of the public sector enterprises in regard to training and skill development in respect of middle and senior level executives through EDPs in collaboration with premier management and training institutes in the country. The recommendations of the Committee have also been conveyed to all the administrative Ministries/Departments and
Chief Executives of all CPSEs. DPE has since received feedback from a number of CPSEs stating that they have been giving due weightage to skill development of shop-floor technical functionaries by conducting internal programmes as and when required. Training programmes are also being organized by the respective CPSEs for shop-floor employees to enhance their skills.
 
6.3   The Committee finds the reply too general without any specific information. Given the upgradation of tools and machines and increasing computer-based automation it is necessary that the supervisory and lower levels of workers must be trained to align their skill sets with new modifications on floorshops and in production processes. Absence of such skill upgradation will seriously jeopardize the
redeployability of the workers and may cause redundancies leading to possible protest against modernisation , which in itself is essential for retaining competitive edge in the open market. The Committee is constrainted  to note that while DPE and concerned PSEs facilitate expensive trainings  for Middle and Higher management levels, the lower cadre remains vulnerable to changes in company structures and
modernization in technology due to lack of regular training. A fact glaringly borne out by the number of rationalized employees in these cadres and low redeployment rate of employees even after CRR scheme. The Committee finds such large redundancies a telling   example of poor HR management practices and lack of sufficient emphasis on training/skill upgradation and therefore recommends a more
structured approach to this issue. The DPE should  formulate  comprehensive guidelines  in this respect in consultation with the stakeholders and independent resource agencies. The Committee feels that HR management should be held accountable for large redundancies in the PSEs  resulting in large liability towards VRS/VSS cases.

8.0       North Eastern Region
 
8.1       The Committee sought the reasons for reducing the allocations for NE region from Rs.3.15 crores in RE 2006-07 to Rs. 1 crore this year and also about the usage of the money in 2006-07.
 
8.2       The DPE informed the Committee that during 2007-08, Planning commission has reduced the overall budget allocation under CRR Scheme. As per instant norm, 10% of the total allocation is earmarked for NE Region. For the year 2007-08 total allocation is Rs. 10.00 crore. Therefore, allocation for NE region has also been reduced to Rs. 1.00 crore. During 2006-07, Rs. 3.15 crore was earmarked for
NE region under CRR Scheme against which a sum of Rs. 3.95 lakhs  has been released to Indian Council of Small Industry to cover a physical target of 50 VRS optees. Central Institute of Plastics Engineering & Technology, Guwahati has also been assigned a physical target of 50 to be covered against balance fund available with them pertaining to 2005-06. The balance fund of Rs. 3.11 crore pertaining to NE
Region for 2006-07 would be transferred to ‘non-lapsable central resources pool’. The coverage under CRR Scheme in NE Region is lower mainly because of non-availability of sufficient VRS optees in the region. VRS is not very sizeable in the units located in NE Region. Also, a number of VRS optees, immediately after separation, leave NE region to settle in their native place.
 
8.3       The Committee observes that the money is not given as per the requirement of the region but for the sake of a norm that stipulates 10% of funds  be earmarked for North East. A larger part of the funds remain unutilized and transferred to a non lapsable central resource pool. The Committee expresses its reservation over such under utilization of scarce resources and recommends that allocation of funds
should be a function of need and not an administrative fiat.
