Durgapur Steel Plant (DSP), an Integrated Steel Plant of Steel Authority of India Limited
(SAIL), with an initial capacity of 1 million. tonnes of ingots per annum was. set up a the
Consortium of British firms, namely Indian Steel Works Construction Company Ltd (ISCON).
The Contract was signed on October 31,1956 and the plant was commissioned in phases between
1959 and 1962. The plant launched its expansion programme during the Third Plan period for
increasing its ingot capacity to 1.6 MTPA. The various units under expansion were
commissioned by 1969. Salient areas of activities of DSP in terms of production are Base
blending of ores of RMPHP; Coke and sinter for Blast Furnaces; Hot Metal for steel making
shop; Crude steel for long and flat products and Railway items. With the completion of the.
modernization programme, DSP is in possession of state-of-the-art technology for quality steel
making Stabilization of the modernized units has brought about improved productivity,
substantial improvement in energy consumption and better quality products. With the successful
commissioning of the modernized units, DSP is all set to produce 2.088 million tonnes of hot
metal, 1.876-million tonnes of liquid steel and 1.586 million tonnes of saleable steel annually as
per the diversified products-mix .

6.0 TIME OVER-RUN & REASONS

6.1 The Sub-Committee was informed that the commissioning of various packages started
‘progressively as early as Nov’91. Wheel & Axle Plant Commissioned in September, 1992,
Continuous Casting Plant in August, 1994 and BOF in December, 1994 and 2™ (last) Blast
Furnace in April, 1996. The modernization process was completed in March, 1998 with an
overall delay of 60 months.

Reasons for Delay

(i) Disintegration of erstwhile USSR which resulted in delays in supply of imported
structurals, equipment, refractories etc. from TPE, Russia and affected BF, BOF, SP

& RMHC packages.

(ii) Delays in equipment and structurals supply by indigenous suppliers affected most of
the packages.

(iii) Increase in quantity of work in respect of civil & structurals job for BF, SP and
RMHC packages as compared to original estimates of the consortium resulted in
extra time for execution.

(iv) Inadequate mobilization of resources at site work by contracting agencies resulted in
slow pace of work as compared to targets.

(v). Funds constraint of contracting agencies and intra-consortiuin disputes resulted in
slow progress in major global packages.

6.1 The DSP management stated that prior to disintegration of the erstwhile USSR, all the
economic activities in CIS Nations were controlled from Moscow and the manufacturing units
and transporting authorities had to adhere to the delivery schedules and commitments.
Subsequent to the break-up of USSR into different Nations, these manufacturing units were being
controlled by different authorities and a number of new regulations were introduced. This
resulted in delay in manufacturing activities. Tyazhpromexport (TPE)’s job has suffered


seriously, resulting in delays in supply of imported.equipment, structures and refractories from
Russia for four global packages viz, Blast Furnace, Sinter Plant, Basic Oxygen Furnaces and Raw
Material Handling Complex.

6.3 As regards the Under-Estimation by Contracting Agencies, it was stated that the increase
in volume of work, due to under-estimation by package contractors, resulted in increase in time of
execution and also caused funds constraints with consortium members leading to serious intra-
consortium problems, which took time to resolve. There is an overall increase in quantities of
work ranging from. 24 per cent to 71 per cent for various activities. The maximum increase in

volume of work was in Blast Furnace. package wherein, original estimates were based on हे

realizing maces. During — negotiations, _Mannesmann. Demag. Huttentechnik - (MDH)/
Tyazhpromexport (TPE) offered reconstruction of the furnaces resulting in substantial increase in
volume of civil and structural work over those envisaged earlier. ip
6.4 It was further added that cash constraints with the contracting agencies like HSCL, Birla
Technical Services (BTS), Dodsal, Engineering Projects (India) Ltd. (EPI), etc. was one of the
major reasons which affected the execution of work, ‘The main reasons for higher requirement of
funds by consortium members had increased volume of work.and procurement of indigenous
equipment in some cases instead of imported ones as envisaged etc.

6.5 Delay due to Indian Contracting Agencies was caused as the indigenqus agencies like
HSCL, BTS, Dodsal, EPI, etc. were slow in mobilization of resources. There were delays in
erection of structurals and equipment by HSCL. Delays took place in equipment supplies and
erection by BTS in the Raw Materials Handling Complex (RMHC), Sinter Plant (SP), Blast
Furnaces (BF) and Basic Oxygen Furnaces (BOF) packages.

6.6 Delays in Completion of Design Engineering resulted in’ delayed supply of equipment,
structures and refractories, both imported and indigenous. The maximum impact of this was on

the RMHC.

6.7 Lack of intra- consortium co-ordination led to disputes regarding scope of work and
commercial matters, leading to delay and calling for frequent intervention by SAIL/DSP.

6.8 The Committee feels that there may be a variety of reasons for time over run in the
, implementation of modernisation projects. Some of the reasons might be beyond the control of
management, but the fact remains that lack of management by objective also contributed to time
over-run. The Committee feels that the then management failed to act promptly in right
direction. There was a definite lacunae in managerial supervision and direction, the Committee

feels

rates differed from the earlier assumptions due to SAIL’s inability to furid the project through
internal resources, the funding had to be done mostly through commercial borrowings.
