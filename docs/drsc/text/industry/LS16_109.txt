REPORT
The One Hundred-ninth Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Government on the
Recommendations contained in the 84 th Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Industry on implementation of Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana (PMRY) in Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Kerala
pertaining to the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries which was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok
Sabha 19
th December, 2002.
2. Action Taken Notes have been received from the Government in respect of the recommendations contained in the
Report. They have been categorized as follows:
                  (i)    Recommendations/Observations, which have been accepted by the Government:-
Paras : 3.24, 3.27, 3.28, 3.30, 3.32, 3.35, 3.36, 3.41, 4.2, 4.3, 5.5, 5.6, 5.9, 5.11, 6.9, 6.10, 6.12, 6.13,
6.14, 6.15 (Chapter-I).
                 (ii)    Recommendation/Observation which the Committee does not desire to pursue in view of the
Government's replies : - Para : 1.2, 3.2, 3.37, 6.1, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5  (Chapter-II).

3. The Committee is pleased to observe that the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries has accepted its recommendation
as mentioned in chapter - I of the Report.
4. In respect of the recommendations made in Chapter-II the Committee is convinced with the logic and explanation
advocated by the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries and therefore does not want to pursue further. The Committee
also noted the upward change and modifications in the progress report of Prime Minister Rojgar Yojana and the
schemes respectively.
5. The Committee expresses its concern over the non-implementations of recommendations contained in Chapter-III and
therefore the Committee desires that the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries would furnish logical explanation in
respect of these recommendations.
6. In respect of recommendation placed in chpater-IV, the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries has either furnished the
interim replies which leaves the Committee yearning for more; the Committee, therefore, desires that the Ministry of
Agro & Rural Industries would furnish Action Taken Note in respects of recommendations categorized in Chapter-IV in
detail and to the point, as per the intention and spirit of the recommendations of the Committee.

Recovery of Loans in Andhra Pradesh
Para : 2.9
The Committee observes that in order to improve rate of grounding of units it was necessary to rectify the high
percentage of NPAs as mounting NPAs were one of the reasons for high mortality rate of PMRY projects. The
Committee observed that NPAs to large industries can not be equated with the NPAs under the PMRY as the percentage
of NPAs in PMRY sector was but a small per cent of total NPAs in the country. Out of total NPA of Rs. 18000 crores,
small sector owed only Rs. 8,000 crore NPA for PMRY, therefore, was only negligible. The Committee, therefore,
suggests that bankers should be encouraged to finance self-employment schemes as this would directly increase
employment.
Action  Taken  Rerport
The observations of the Committee were forwarded to the RBI. The RBI vide  their letter No. RPCD.PLNFS. No.
606/9.4.01/2002-03 dated 8.10.02 has stated that they agree with the observations of the Committee that NPAs under the
PMRY is very low as compared to the general loans but it is also a fact that the recovery under PMRY is very low
around 40% and therefore banks are often reluctant to sanction/disburse loans under the Scheme. The RBI has informed
that they have advised bank to desist from under financing of projects under PMRY and to sanction loans as per the
requirements, depending upon the projects proposed to be undertaken.

Coverage  of  Projects  Under  Industry  Sector
Para : 4.7
The Committee observes that though the ceiling on project cost under PMRY has been increased from Rs. 1 lakh to
Rs. 2 lakhs with effect from 1999-2000; for activities other than business, no perceptible increase in the amount of the
average loan disbursed is observed. This may indicate that either there is a predominance of business activities in the
cases sanctioned, or banks have taken a routine target-oriented approach in the implementation of the scheme with little
care for the actual requirement of funds for setting up micro-enterprises. There is concern that activities are being under
financed which lead to their unavailability and their misuse right from the very beginning. The Committee, therefore,
advise that Model Project Profiles should be prepared in every district on successful activities and banks could release
the financing, according to these model profiles.
Action  Taken  Rerport
The observation of the Committee have been circulated to the RBI vide  letter No. PMRY-P-PC-10(1)/2002 dated
30.9.2002 and to all State/UTs vide letter No. PMRY-P-C-10(1)/2002 dated 4.10.2002.
The RBI vide their reply placed at Annexure III has replied that the DICs may be in a better position to assess the
ground realities of infrastructure, availability of natural resources, skills of the local people etc. Conducting of
workshops for this purpose can be done at the initiative of the DICs. The average size of the loan has been stagnating at
about Rs. 65,000.00/- loans due to the predominance of activities under the business sector. The project appraisal of the
proposals is the prerogative of the financing banks and the Model Project Profiles can at best serve as a guideline for the
banks. However, they have issued circulars regarding under-financing to all implementing banks vide
RPCD.NO.SP.BC.51/9.4.01/2001-02 dated 31
st December, 2001.

Marketing  of  Products
Paras : 2.13 & 4.8
2.13 The Committee suggests that PMRY beneficiaries can market their products under a common brand name and in
order to improve the quality of goods, testing labs should be identified in all districts. Mobile testing laboratories like

Identification of Viable Activities Under the PMRY
Paras 2.22 & 4.1
2.22 The Committee suggests that a study may be made in this regard by the RBI to find ways and means to overcome
the difficulties of the banks as well as the best possible way to identify viable projects, which may include non-
traditional items like electronics goods.
4.1 Committee recommends that each region, particularly at the District and Block level should identify viable activities
and prepare project profiles to help the borrower and the bank to select viable projects. DIC and banks should meet
regularly in workshops to identify viable and feasible vocations and prepare project profiles. This has to be done at
District and State level at least once a year. The agencies involved in implementation of the programme should
participate in such programmes.
Action  Taken  Rerport
The observation of the Committee was communicated to all State/UTs and the RBI. The RBI has stated that it is not
possible for them to undertake such a task.
Further Recommendations
The Committee notes the evasive reply, that the RBI has stated, that it is not possible for them to undertake such a
study without giving any adequate and valid reasons. The Committee feels that in the interest of the country the RBI
should undertake a study to overcome the difficulties of the banks as well as the best possible way to identify viable
projects.
Coverage of  Projects  Under  Industry  Sector
Para 3.22
The Committee also noted that most of the loans sanctioned under PMRY were for service or business activities.
Coverage of industry under PMRY was extremely poor. Government of India had stipulated the norm of 15 per cent
coverage for Industry under PMRY. The issue needed to be addressed in the larger context. Industry is being neglected,
and in Karnataka, PMRY funding going to business/service sector is not being recovered. This is the pattern all over the
country. Due to non-prioritisation of industry sector, no tangible asset is created, resulting in no employment
opportunity for the people. The Committee is of the view that village industry should be made the principal driver for
economic growth and more emphasis should be laid on the industries sector as it generates greater employment
opportunities.

Action  Taken  Rerport
Modified guidelines of the PMRY Scheme under implementation since 1.4.99 do not provide for any minimum
stipulation for projects under industry sector. Despite the enhancement in the projects cost from Rs. 1 lakh to Rs. 2 lakh
in the revised guidelines, the average loaning under the Scheme continues to be around Rs. 60,000/- per beneficiary,
rendering it difficult to set up any industrial activity out of this. In addition the setting up of projects under industry
sector requires getting power connections, license and NOCs etc. Hence, few people are going into for projects under
industry sector. The self-employment  can mean self employed ventures business and service sectors also. Average
employment generation per unit set up under the PMRY is 1.5% which can either be from industry, service or business
sectors. During the Review Meetings of the PMRY it was deliberated that one of the reasons for rejection of
application/non disbursal of loan was the tendency to adhere the minimum loaning from the industry sector. Since the
aim of the Scheme is to provide self employment, it has been decided by the Government to withdraw the 50% ceiling
on projects from the indsutry sector.
Further Recommendations
The Committee notes the reply given by the Ministry of A & R I. The Committee, however, is still of the opinion
that in the absence of industry, no asset is created. Since PMRY has been brought under the Ministry of A & R I, it
would be appropriate to give a thought as to how to promote Agro and Rural industries under the scheme.
Center-State Forum on Agro & Rrural Industries
Para 4.16
Central Government have laid emphasis on agro and village industries by creating a separate Ministry of Agro and
Rural Industries. To give a meaningful impetus to Agro & Rural Industries, the Committee recommends setting up of
Centre-State forum on A&RI, whereunder the Officers of the Central Govt. would meet the State Industry Deptt.,
District Industries Centre, industry associations, entrepreneurs, and banks from time to time to get feed back about the
credit flow, infrastructural facilities, and other difficulties faced by the Agro and Rural Industries, and formulate
possible solutions. The Committee hope that the Central and State Government should join hands in bringing more and
more people under the ambit of the Agro and Rural Industries.
Action  Taken  Rerport
The implementation of the Scheme is regularly reviewed at different levels. The Central Government has constituted
a High Powered Committee on the PMRY in which members and the Central Ministries, implementating banks and the
State Governments review implementation of the Scheme and take policy decisions. Secretary (SSI & ARI) reviewed
the implementation of the Scheme at five Regional level meetings at Bangalore.
Further Recommendations
While noting the reply given by the Ministry of A&RI the Committee desires that it may be apprised of the outcome
of reviews made by the High Powered Committee about the implementation of Scheme review, meeting-wise.
Coverage  of  SC/STs,  Physically Disable & Weaker Sections in Karnataka
Paras 3.17, 3.21, 3,22
3.17 The Committee observes that PMRY being basically a rural programme, is meant for welfare of the rural people,
especially the weaker sections of society. But the Committee noted with dismay that the SC/ST coverage under the
scheme was not upto expectations.
3.21 The Committee, therefore, recommends that PMRY, being a rural programme, share of SC/STs should be
increased with forward and backward linkages.
3.22 At least 12 per cent coverage should be ensured for SC/STs, making it an All India norm. At least 3 per cent
coverage for the handicapped should also be made mandatory as there is no provision for handicapped candidates under
the scheme. Participation of weaker sections of society should be encouraged.

Action Taken Rerport
The Prime Minister's Rozgar Yojana was launched on 2 nd Oct. 93. During 1993-94, the scheme was in operation
only in urban areas of the country. Subsequently, the scheme is being implemented in both urban as well as rural areas
of the country. The guidelines of the scheme do not prescribe that PMRY is basically a Rural programme only.
Similarly, the guidelines of the scheme envisage 22.5 per cent reservation for SC/STs. It is also stipulated in the
guidelines that in case sufficient SC/ST candidates are not available, the State Governments are competent to consider
the applications from other categories.
Article 40 (Chapter VI) of the persons with the disabilities (equal opportunities and fully participation) Act 1995
provide for not less than 3% reservation for disabled in all poverty alleviation programmes. Since PMRY is an
emloyment generation programme and not a poverty alleviation programme, the provision of 3 per cent reservation for
physically disabled persons does not apply to PMRY Scheme.
Further Recommendations
While noting the reply of the Ministry of A&RI, the Committee is disturbed to know that sufficient number of
applications are not coming from the SC/ST candidates. The Committee recommends that awareness programmes about
the PMRY should be regularly held amongst SC/ST candidates. The Committee also recommends that reservations for
disabled perons in the matter of granting loans under PMRY should be included.
Raising the Age Limit Under the PMRY
Para  4.6
The Committee notes that the beneficiaries are selected from the age group of 18-35 years. The lower age limit
requires to be increased to bring more mature youth to the scheme, the Committee feels.
Action  Taken  Rerport
Under the PMRY, after the modification in the scheme, the educational qualification has been lowered from 10
th
failed to 8th passed. The age limit has been left unchanged. Lowering the educational qualification and enhancing the
eligible age limit are contradictory. Moreover, the Scheme is meant for educated unemployed youth and youth are
defined between the age group of 18-35 years.
Further Recommendations
Since the longevity has increased in general, the Committee feels that the concept of youth as defined  between the
age group of 18 --- 35 years is required to be redefined.
Survival of PMRY Uunits
Para  2.11
The Committee observes that, looking at the high mortality rate under PMRY, Group Approach needs to be
encouraged as group dynamics have more impact on any activity. An activity can be identified and candidates
sponsored. The Central Government can take initiatives in this regard. The Government should concentrate on quality
entrepreneurs, instead of the number of entrepreneurs. As in Korea, Self Help Groups should be formed and they should
concentrate on marketing distribution, etc. Government should monitor the follow up of these activities.
Action  Taken  Rerport
The observations of the Committee were forwarded to the RBI. The guidelines of the Scheme do not provide
implementation through 'Self Help Gorups' (SHG) as there is no legal status or any formal entity in the formation of the
SHG which in most cases, an informal understanding between members of the group. Guidelines of the PMRY Scheme
allow setting up of joint ventures by the eligible applicants costing upto to Rs. 10.00 lakhs. Keeping in view the resource
constraint with the Central Government and federal structure of the country, the activities like identifying an activity
and sponsoring the candidates for the same can be best addressed and tacked by the State Government and the local
