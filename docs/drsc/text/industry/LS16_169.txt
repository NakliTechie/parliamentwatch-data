REPORT
 
1.         THE MANDATE OF DEPARTMENT OF HEAVY INDUSTRY (DHI)
1.1       The Department focuses on promoting the development and growth of capital goods and engineering industry in
the country and administratively dealing with 48 PSEs. The Department of Heavy Industry is also concerned with the
development of the heavy engineering industry, machine tool industry, heavy electrical industry, industrial machinery
and auto-industry and administers 48 Central PSEs.
1.2       The industries covered by this Department meet the requirement of equipments for basic industries such as steel,
non-ferrous metals, fertilizers, refineries, petrochemicals, shipping, paper, cement, sugar etc. The Department is
responsible for development of a wide range of intermediate engineering products like castings, forgings, diesel engine,
industrial gears and gear boxes.They cater to the need of goods and services for almost all sectors of the economy,
including power, rail and road transport etc.
1.3       The Committee takes note that the current structural readjustment and liberalization programme launched in
early 1990s ought to effect public sector reform. At present the public sector enterprises appear to have constraints in
withstanding the global and domestic competition due to financial, structural and technological factors.
1.4       The Committee observes that many of the PSEs, however, have not been able to retain their original position in
the market and withstand the global competition in the new liberalized economy.

2.3       The Committee notes that most of the companies under major conglomerates like BBUNL, BYNL, HMT,
AYCL, with diversified business in heavy engineering, are making losses. These companies rely on major Government
projects for orders to generate working capital. The Committee feels that these engineering conglomerates have critical
potential for the capital goods sector in the country and need to be re-aligned to the larger market. The Committee
would like to know of the measures that are being considered in this respect for these companies.
2.4       The Committee was informed that the losses in the PSEs is mainly attributed to the shortfall in production in
some enterprises owing to poor order book, shortage of working capital, surplus manpower, obsolete plant and
machinery, increase in the cost of inputs etc. PSEs are carrying the conventional baggage of disadvantages like excess
manpower and low level of functional autonomy in addition to disadvantages of location etc. Not only inadequacy of
investment but lack of timely investment have caused significant damage to public sector capabilities. Government
investments and loans including loans for VRS are flowing to PSEs at exorbitant rate of interest further crippling their
financial performance. VRS funds flow to the companies at 17.5% rate of interest largely eclipsing the advantages in
wage bill arising out of manpower rationalization.
2.5       Productivity of PSEs has been generally very low. The Committee has been informed that the ratio of wages and
salaries as a percentage of turnover is very high. In most cases, salary and wages component is unsustainable and eats
up most other advantages that PSEs enjoys.
2.6       With the onset of globalisation, tariff levels are gradually coming down thereby putting pressure on domestic
industries and on PSUs in particular to perform in this environment. While the profit-making PSE are making funds
available from their own internal resources for keeping pace with the technological development, whereas the loss
making PSEs rely heavily on budgetary support. It was informed that some structural measures for improving the
performance of the PSEs should be taken as an alternative to privatization, e.g. :–
            •           Providing greater functional autonomy
            •           Professionalisation of Boards of PSEs
            •           Signing of MoU between PSEs and Government
2.7       However, the Committee observes that most of the PSEs are too heavily dependent on Government for finances
and business orders to exercise the autonomy.
2.8       The Committee observes that the Government has to take necessary steps to reduce the dependence of PSEs on
Government for finances and orders; Professionalise the Boards of the PSEs, in the spirit of Government's policy
leading to more and more autonomy and enter into MoU agreements with more companies, setting out elaborate
objectives for better results.

6.0       SCENARIO OF HEAVY CAPITAL GOODS INDUSTRY
6.1       The Committee was informed that the capital goods sector, has posted a robust growth of 13.6 per cent in 2003-
04 and has maintained its growth momentum during the current year as well. Prospects are encouraging for sustaining
domestic growth in view of the beginnings of an investment cycle.
6.2       Majority of the PSEs under DHI are in the capital goods sector catering to the requirement of sectors like Power,
Petroleum, Railways, Fertilizer, Chemicals etc. The capital goods sector in the country registered a growth of 13.3% in
April-December 2004, compared to which the PSEs under DHI registered a matching growth rate of 13.1%.
6.3       The Committee observes that while the aggregate growth in PSEs under DHI compares well with the capital
goods growth, but this level of growth is mainly contributed by one PSE namely Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd. (BHEL)
which showed a growth of 15% during this period. Most of the PSEs have shown a negative growth mainly due to
financial, marketing and technological constraints.
6.4       The Committee apprehends that despite robust growth in capital goods sector, the PSEs find difficult to secure
orders and in several cases to execute the orders due to lack of working capital and other constraints. The Committee,
accordingly, recommends that efforts should be made by the PSEs to utilize current opportunities in the market. As the
capital goods market is essentially technology-intensive, PSEs must undertake the technology upgradation to secure
high-end market.

8.0       THE MANDATE OF NEW BRPSE
8.1       The mandate of Board for Reconstruction of Public Sector Enterprises (BRPSE) is to undertake strengthening,
modernizing, reviving and restructuring of public sector enterprises. The BRPSE was constituted by Government on
6.12.2004 consisting of a Chairman and three non-official members and three official members.
8.2       The Board will advise the Government on ways and means for strengthening Public Sector Enterprises in
general and making them more autonomous and professional; consider restructuring - financial, organizational and
business (including diversification, joint ventures, seeking strategic partner, merger and acquisition) of CPSEs and
suggest ways and means for funding such schemes; examine the proposals of the Administrative Ministries for
revival/restructuring of sick/loss making of CPSEs for their turnaround and to make suitable recommendations related
thereto; advise the Government on disinvestments/closure/sale, in full or part, in respect of chronically sick/loss making
companies which cannot be revived, in respect of such unviable companies the Board will also advise the Government
about sources of fund including sale of surplus assets of the enterprise for the payment of all legitimate dues and
compensation to workers and other costs of closure; to monitor incipient sickness i.e. PSEs incurring loss for two
consecutive years; and make recommendations and advise the Government on such other matters as may be assigned to
it from time to time.

9.1       The Committee was informed that there was no direct linkage between BRPSE and BIFR. Further, though the
Government proposes to repeal Sick Industries Companies Act (SICA), which would mean disbanding of BIFR and
replace it by National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). As the matter is sub judice, no further action could be taken by
the Government.
9.2       The Committee observes that there is no major qualitative difference in the mandates of BRPSE and BIFR. It
seems a replacement of BIFR, a quasi-judicial body by the BRPSE that is a recommendatory body. The Committee is
yet to understand whose decision will have precedence in case of contradiction?
9.3       The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should decide the on linkage between BIFR and
BRPSE at least, till such time a final decision on BIFR is taken by the competent Court.
9.4       The Committee also observes with concern that multiplicity of agencies even in deciding the revival may and
has resulted in differences in opinion, successive appeals and costly delays. Even after deciding, the revival packages
have failed in several cases. The Committee apprehends that in the process of analyzing the cases of BIFR has assessed
the Techno-Financial feasibilities of individual PSEs prepared by expert agencies. Yet the appeals are filed in AAIFR
and High Courts/Supreme Court and now these may further will be referred to new BRPSE for appraisal before a final
decision is taken by the Cabinet. The entire process is time consuming and costly. The cost of delay has been immense
in terms of rising liabilities that have eaten up the net worth of the PSEs. It may be an effort in duplication.
9.5       The Committee recommends that the Department must take steps to expedite the decision and implementation
for the PSEs' sustainable revival. Accountability must be fixed for the successful and sustained revival once case is
decided by the competent authority.
9.6       The Committee further recommends that the Government should inform the Committee of the revival packages
approved by the Cabinet before approaching the Parliament for Supplementary Demands. The Department must also
give a post-revival SWOT analysis of the PSEs and that must reflect its Technological strengths and market viability.

10.0     CLASSIFICATION OF ALLOCATION
10.1     The Committee was informed about criteria and classification of expenditure in the Budget as follows:
10.2     Revenue expenditure is the expenditure, which does not result in creation of asset. It is for normal running of
the Government Departments and various services, interest on debt incurred by the Government, subsidies etc.
However, all grants given to the State Governments and other parties are also treated as revenue expenditure even
though some of the grants may be for creation of assets.
10.3     Capital payment consists of capital expenditure on acquisition of assets like land, buildings, machinery,
equipment, as also investments in share, etc., and loans and advances granted by Central Government to State and
Union Territory Governments, Government Companies, Corporations and other parties.
10.4     Plan allocation is for development activities specific to the Centrally Sponsored Schemes/Central Plan Schemes
approved by the Planning Commission.
10.5     The Non-Plan allocation is meant for meeting the committed liabilities like establishment expenditure etc.
10.6     The Committee notes that the expenditures are classified into two parallel categories Capital/Revenue
expenditures and Plan/Non-Plan expenditures. These two parallel categories are curiously hybridized into four
supposedly distinct categories of expenditure. The Committee would like to know the rational principles for such
classifications and sub-classifications, instead of description of expenditure under these categories.
10.7     The Committee is of the view that existing classification heads needs further rationalisation and desires that the
Government should review the same.
10.8     Of the aggregate Rs. 861.32 crores allocated for Department of Heavy industry in the Union Budget 2005-06,

29.0     TECHNOLOGICAL CONSTRAINTS BEFORE PSEs
29.1     The Committee appreciates that PSEs face difficulties due to low technological profile, obsolete plants, and swift
changes in product/processes ushered in by a fast globalizing economy. The Committee has held detailed consultations
with major players, resources, agencies, Government Departments and stakeholders in technology upgradation in capital
goods sector.
29.2     The Committee recognizes that with the technology progressing at an unprecedented pace, the technology life
cycle has become shorter and industries around the world are under pressure to develop newer technology to stay ahead
of the competition. The local market in developing countries have become easier to access by foreign competitors while
their competitiveness may not be strong enough to enter the international market. Consequently, development and
commercialization of new technologies has become an activity of focal attention and a force to reckon with.
29.3     With the globalization of the Indian economy the MNCs/Proprietary Technology holders now have direct access
to the Indian market. In many cases, they are unwilling to transfer the high and critical technology to Indian partners, as
they do not perceive a commercial advantage or incentive to do so. Even the existing manufacturers having foreign
collaboration or equity participation in the capital goods sector like machine tools, textile machinery, industrial
machinery, power plant equipment, etc. are finding it extremely difficult to get the contemporary technology in these
fields.
29.4     The Committee observed that the fast pace of technology developments and obsolescence has left very little
choice on technology front for developing countries like India. The Committee, therefore, desires that the Government
should ensure the development and commercialisation of new technologies. The Government should come out with a
policy providing for technology upgradation in general and the PSEs in particular.
30.0     STATE OF TECHNOLOGY IN THE PSEs
30.1     The Committee recognizes that in the past India has been predominantly a technology follower and limited
focused attention has been given for development of a strong R&D base in the country. The poor expenditure on R&D
by the domestic industry is often due to lack of resources. The R&D expenditure as percentage of turnover in 2002-03
was only 0.1% for all PSEs, 1% for the PSEs in the Department of Heavy Industry as compared to the global average of
3-5%.

30.2     Most of the existing manufacturers in the capital goods sector like machine tools, textile machinery, industrial
machinery do not have a viable basic R&D set up to develop a product from design board to commercialization, mainly

32.0     JOINT VENTURES OR TECHNOLOGICAL ALLIANCE TO BRING ABOUT A TECHNOLOGICAL
REVIVAL OF THE PSES
32.1     The Committee was informed that with liberalization of the FDIs, there is no compulsory requirement for the
foreign partner to have a domestic partner. As such the global technology leaders have direct access to the Indian market
and do not need technical collaboration agreements with Indian companies. In recent years, the foreign companies have
been securing large contracts in India.
32.2     The Committee notes that the Chinese model, where the technology transfer to a domestic manufacturer is
mandatory for an MNC to get an order in that country, could be an effective policy in facilitating technology transfer.
China is successfully leveraging its market access to foreign manufacturers, to acquire technology and ensure that a
significant share of the equipment of strategic economic importance is manufactured locally in China. Similarly, the
Bhoomiputra policy of Malaysia has been evolved within the WTO framework to ensure technology transfer in
manufacturing industry and a similar approach would improve the access for the latest technology for the Indian
industry.
32.3     The Committee would like to have the view from Government that in order to bring about the technological
revival of the PSEs one of the important issues is, if the foreign companies securing large contracts in India, could be
obliged to associate competent local companies for technology transfer, as is the policy in some other developing
countries such as China and Malaysia.
32.4     The Committee further suggests that in order to ensure that the PSEs gain from technology alliances, it is
necessary that strong learning capabilities are built up to facilitate skill acquisition, internal training. The receptivity in
the PSEs also would have to be built up to accumulate, retain and transfer of knowledge to take the full advantage of the
Joint Ventures or any other technological alliance.

35.       MAIN ELEMENTS OF RESTRUCTURING PLANS OF THE PSEs
35.1     The Committee observes that so far the restructuring is confined to manpower rationalisation and book clearing.
Committee feels that such a limited restructuring is not sustainable.
35.2     The Committee was informed that in the policy regime of disinvestment of the non-strategic public sector
undertakings pursued in the past, only partial capital restructuring was undertaken to a point of viability so as to make
them attractive for the private entrepreneur. While the new policy environment exhorts to comprehensively modernize
and restructure sick PSEs on a sustainable basis, which necessarily involves being competitive in the globalized
economy.
35.3     The Committee observes that the restructuring exercise should now involve a major strategic repositioning of the
company's competitive focus so as to enhance its value and to strengthen it adequately to compete effectively by
improving its resilience and the earning potential. The Committee recommends that the companies must re-align with
the market, identify and create niche in the market. This will require comprehensive marketing strategy, techno-value
addition of products-profile and diversification of market.
35.4     The Committee observes that the revival effort should aim to optimally allocate resources to ensure the
increasing returns over the long run by improvement in the innovation and implementation capacity and increasing its
reserve capacity to meet the challenges of the market driven globalized economy.
36.0     SUGGESTED STEPS FOR REVIVAL
36.1     The Committee recommends that the objective of the restructuring effort should be :
            (i)         Improvement in the innovation ability of the PSEs by : strengthening the R&D and technology
management and absorption.
            (ii)        Improvement in implementation capacity by : improving the management knowledge and skills,
marketing skills, product quality, and technology familiarity.
            (iii)       Reduction in the vulnerability by improving liquidity and cash flow coverage.
36.2     Furthermore, the restructuring exercise should include :
            (i)         Operational restructuring by modifying production process by bringing economies of scale, improving
the level of the quality and improvement in the process technology. Technological upgradation to target the high-end
market.
            (ii)        Marketing restructuring by repositioning and diversifying the marketing and the distribution channels.
            (iii)       Financial and capital restructuring to increase earning power of the company that may entail increased
reserve capacity, bankability and ability to generate extra-budgetary resources.
            (iv)       Organizational restructuring to motivate performance and formulate a long-term objectives in
accordance with the projected trends of market.
36.3     The Committee would like to know the comments and the concrete, tangible action taken or to be taken by the

43.0     BHARAT HEAVY ELECTRICALS LTD.
43.1     The Committee appreciates the financial performance of the BHEL which is a Nav Ratna Company. The
Committee also appreciates the Company's efforts to become a prominent global player.
43.2     However, the Company has been facing constraints in acquiring technology and uneven competition in the
domestic as well as global market due to certain structural constraints.
43.3     The Committee notes that though initially the Government acquired technologies from Companies in the UK,
USSR and Czechoslovakia for BHEL. Over the years, BHEL has successfully absorbed these technologies and
continually upgraded its technology to contemporary levels through a judicious mix of in-house development and
technology acquisition. A high level of self-reliance with respect to power equipment has thus been achieved in India,
with full support from the Government.
43.4     BHEL is most competitive in its product line within the country and in several export markets. Indian Utilities
have significantly gained in this process by setting up power plants most cost effectively. BHEL has also fully supported
the utilities with spares and services over the entire lifetime of the plants. However, some of the collaborators of BHEL
have now become competitors in an open domestic market. In the last few years, leading power plant equipment
manufacturers in the world have been consolidating through mergers and acquisitions, thereby enabling them to increase
their product offerings and earmarking higher R&D funds to develop technologically advanced products. Indian
manufacturers are not in a position to deploy the enormous resources required to keep abreast to the latest
advancements.
43.5     Further, potential customers are also generally risk-averse and are hesitant to try new products. Technology
transfers from foreign companies will, therefore, continue to play an important role.
43.6     Indian customers are now planning to introduce advanced power plants and transmission systems. While inviting
tenders, customers usually specify qualification requirements such as two years' operating experience and at least two
units of similar advanced systems at two different locations. Indian manufacturers do not qualify due to lack of
operating experience, even when they have already developed, or are in a position to develop the new products and/or
have technology support from OEMs through an ongoing collaboration agreement.
43.7     At present, foreign companies that are technically qualified can participate in tenders and secure orders, on their
own, without any obligation to transfer technology to an Indian Company. Indian manu-facturers are, therefore, finding
it difficult to source technology from foreign Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs).
43.8     This is leading to a situation where certain foreign licensees of OEMs, having their own qualifying references,
may secure orders from Indian customers; whereas, an Indian manufacturer, though having technology support from
OEM and own capability to design and manufacture the equipment, is prevented from even competing in the tender.
43.9     In this scenario, our self-reliance, painstakingly developed over the last few decades, could get eroded.
Therefore, a conscious attempt, at the policy level, needs to be considered to ensure that in selected areas of strategic
economic interest, having a multiplier effect on the Indian economy, a policy of self-reliance is pursued vigorously, so
that foreign OEMs are obliged to share technology with Indian manufacturers especially where the large Indian market
is leveraged for this purpose.
43.10   The Committee recommends that constraints faced by BHEL, need to be addressed by the Government
with a favourable view to support this flag-ship company that is financially self-reliant but facing uneven
competition due to the policy structure.

45.0     NO TANGIBLE LONG TERM OBJECTIVES SET BY DHI FOR ITS PSEs
45.1     The Committee notes that DHI was unable to commit on firm and tangible long-term objectives in quantitative
terms that could be a benchmark against which the performance is assessed. On most of the long-term issues
Department's reply has been elusive. The Committee observed that despite huge financial liabilities all the major
decisions are still left to BRPSE. Pending its recommendations being approved by the competent authority, the
Department is taking measures that are ad-hoc window-dressing of depleted worth of PSEs. The Committee is of the
opinion that scarce resources cannot be lost endlessly amidst huge liabilities of PSEs. A lasting solution has to be found
urgently and accountability must be accordingly fixed for which it is necessary to determine the benchmarks of
performance.
45.2     The Committee recommends that the Department should formulate a road map for the consideration of
Government on the major challenges faced by PSEs like obsolete technology and lack of marketing strategy. The
Committee also suggests to take into account the cumulative cost of delay, previously due to pendency of cases under
BIFR regime and now under BRPSE in finalizing a decision on PSEs.
45.3     The Committee observes that the multiplicity of agencies involved had led to inefficient delays in finalizing
revival. The Committee is of the view that steps must be taken to reduce multiplicity of agencies in preparing, assessing
and revival proposals.
45.4     DHI has the mandate to promote the development and growth of capital goods and engineering industry in the
country, which in turn has critical bearing over the infrastructure development in the country. However, plight of the
PSEs urgently requires an expeditious and consistent action by the DHI instead to postponing the decision on
successive series of multiple bodies.
45.5     The Committee notes that while the PSEs in the heavy engineering and capital goods category are facing the
constraints due to lack of orders mainly from Government and competition from foreign companies due to opening up
of domestic market. Heavy wage bill, inability to generate internal resources or extra budgetary resources due to huge
liabilities and previous NPAs, over reliance on the budgetary support even for working capital and also for the orders,
inability to upgrade the product line according to market needs, technological drawbacks, lack of corporate dynamism,
inability to create new niche in the market or locate new market are the major constraints for any sustainable revival of
the PSEs.
45.6     The Committee, therefore, recommends that technological upgradation, upgrading the product line according to
new niche market and identifying new domestic and export markets have to be the cardinal elements for a sustained
viability of the PSEs.
45.7     The Committee observes that there are some cases where a clear disjoint between Central and State Government,
has seriously imperiled the viability of the PSEs. In order to clear the administrative bottlenecks, the Committee
recommends the Government to consider making respective State Governments a significant shareholder in the PSE.
The representative of the State Government can be a member of the Board of Directors as one of the two Government
Directors at present.
45.8     The Committee recommends that certain Companies in capital goods sector are critical for development of
heavy engineering and infrastructure sectors irrespective of their financial condition. These Companies in any case rely
heavily on the Government's investment in huge infrastructure projects. While they can be encouraged to generate their
internal resources through innovative corporate strategies like global tie-ups and JVs. Government must at the same
time support such facilities.
45.9     The Committee would like to suggest to the Government to consider the critical utility of any Company before
deciding on its sustainable and comprehensive revival/restructuring instead of going only by their precarious financial
condition. The criticality will depend on long-term prospects of the Company in terms of technology spin-offs and self-
reliance in mega-machinery in strategic areas.
45.10   The Committee recommends that DHI should apprise it of the Revised Estimates and the Supplementary
Demands.

            The Committee notes that the Chinese model, where the technology transfer to a domestic manufacturer is
mandatory for an MNC to get an order in that country, could be an effective policy in facilitating technology transfer.
China is successfully leveraging its market access to foreign manufacturers, to acquire technology and ensure that a
significant share of the equipment of strategic economic importance is manufactured locally in China. Similarly, the
Bhoomiputra policy of Malaysia has been evolved within the WTO framework to ensure technology transfer in
manufacturing industry and a similar approach would improve the access for the latest technology for the Indian
Industry.                                 (Para 32.3)
            The Committee would like to have the view from Government that in order to bring about the technological
revival of the PSEs one of the important issues is, if the foreign companies securing large contracts in India, could be
obliged to associate competent local companies for technology transfer, as is the policy in some other developing
countries such as China and Malaysia.                                              (Para 32.3)
            The Committee further suggests that in order to ensure that the PSEs gain from technology alliances, it is
necessary that strong learning capabilities are built up to facilitate skill acquisition, internal training. The receptivity in
the PSEs also would have to be built up to accumulate, retain and transfer of knowledge to take the full advantage of the
Joint Ventures or any other technological alliance.    (Para 32.4)
            The Committee recommends that the PSEs could be effectively used for the creation of infrastructure and
promotion of skills to capitalize on our competitive advantage.            (Para 33.2)
            The Committee recommends that the Government should consider the following policy initiatives to facilitate
technology transfer to capital goods sector :
            (i)         Issue suitable technology transfer policy guidelines so that for all capital goods and services any foreign
vendor desirous of supplying the same in the domestic Indian market must necessarily source at least 30% of the
proposed bid from local companies within India. Moreover, the foreign vendor must also give an option for transferring
technology to the desirous and capable local company for critical equipment and service involved in the project/product.
            (ii)        Enhance bilateral/multilateral co-operation with developed countries for state of art technology transfer
and exchange of technical experts in the areas of emerging technologies.
            (iii)       While granting FDI approval for trading/sale service, wherever there is a provision for setting up a
manufacturing facility in due course with adequate investment, a clause should be incorporated and implementation
monitored meticulously for materialization of such investment on a time bound basis.           (Para 34.1)
            The Committee observes that the restructuring exercise should now involve a major strategic repositioning of the
company's competitive focus so as to enhance its value and to strengthen it adequately to compete effectively by
improving its resilience and the earning potential. The Committee recommends that the Companies must re-align with
the market, identify and create niche in the market. This will require comprehensive marketing strategy, techno-value

            The Committee observes that the Budgetary scheme and explanatory notes on expenditures do not give sufficient
explanation/details of the allocations. There is always a possibility of duplication of expenditures through multiple

parliamentary scrutiny of budget is to know efficacy of the objective and efficiency of expenditure to achieve the

particular objective. The Committee would also like to know the rational necessity and objective criteria of the

            The Committee notes that DHI was unable to commit on firm and tangible long-term objectives in quantitative

Department is taking measures that are ad-hoc window-dressing of depleted worth of PSEs. The Committee is of the
opinion that scarce resources cannot be lost endlessly amidst huge liabilities of PSEs. A lasting solution has to be found
urgently and accountability must be accordingly fixed for which it is necessary to determine the benchmarks of
performance.        (Para 45.1)
            The Committee recommends that the Department should formulate a road map for the consideration of
Government on the major challenges faced by PSEs like obsolete technology and lack of marketing strategy. The
Committee also suggests to take into account the cumulative cost of delay, previously due to pendency of cases under
BIFR regime and now under BRPSE in finalizing a decision on PSEs.            (Para 45.2)
            The Committee observes that the multiplicity of agencies involved had led to inefficient delays in finalizing
revival. The Committee is of the view that steps must be taken to reduce multiplicity of agencies in preparing, assessing
and revival proposals.        (Para 45.3)
            DHI has the mandate to promote the development and growth of capital goods and engineering industry in the
country, which in turn has critical bearing over the infrastructure development in the country. However, plight of the
PSEs urgently requires an expeditious and consistent action by the DHI instead to postponing the decision on successive
series of multiple bodies.(Para 45.4)
            The Committee notes that while the PSEs in the heavy engineering and capital goods category are facing the
constraints due to lack of orders mainly from Government and competition from foreign companies due to opening up
of domestic market. Heavy wage bill, inability to generate internal resources or extra budgetary resources due to huge
liabilities and previous NPAs, over reliance on the budgetary support even for working capital and also for the orders,
inability to upgrade the product line according to market needs, technological drawbacks, lack of corporate dynamism,
inability to create new niche in the market or locate new market are the major constraints for any sustainable revival of
the PSEs.  (Para 45.5)
            The Committee, therefore, recommends that technological upgradation, upgrading the product line according to
new niche market and identifying new domestic and export markets have to be the cardinal elements for a sustained
viability of the PSEs.   (Para 45.6)
            The Committee recommends that certain Companies in capital goods sector are critical for development of
heavy engineering and infrastructure sectors irrespective of their financial condition. These Companies in any case rely
heavily on the Government's investment in huge infrastructure projects. While they can be encouraged to generate their
internal resources through innovative corporate strategies like global tie-ups and JVs. Government must at the same
time support such facilities.            (Para 45.8)
            The Committee would like to suggest to the Government to consider the critical utility of any Company before
deciding on its sustainable and comprehensive revival/restructuring instead of going only by their precarious financial
condition. The criticality will depend on long-term prospects of the Company in terms of technology spin-offs and self-
reliance in mega-machinery in strategic areas.                  (Para 45.9)
            The Committee recommends that DHI should apprise it of the Revised Estimates and the Supplementary
Demands.            (Para 45.10)
