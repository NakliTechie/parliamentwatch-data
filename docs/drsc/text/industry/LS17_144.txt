CHAPTER - I
Recommendations/Observations, which have been accepted by the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
1.1       Jurisdiction of Ministry of ARI
 
The Committee notes that the Ministry of ARI is entrusted with the functions/duties of implementations of
policies, programmes and schemes and in turn play the role of a policy maker, advocate and a facilitator.  The
Committee also finds that the Ministry monitors the activities undertaken by its fields agencies through formal
structure as well as informal meetings.  However, the Committee is of the view that the Ministry of ARI would
act promptly as a policy maker, advocate and facilitator and promote Agro and Rural Industries by effective
implementation of policies, programmes and schemes undertaken by its various field agencies without time and
cost over run.  The existing monitoring mechanism must ensure that targets are achieved within the stipulated
time frames.  The Committee further recommends that the Ministry of ARI should have review meetings of its
field agencies to take stock of the progress made in the implementation of various schemes and programmes.
(Para- 1.8)
 
Action Taken
 
1.1.1    The monitoring mechanism has been strengthened in the Ministry and Priority Agenda for Action has been
evolved for Coir, KVIC and PMRY at the level of implementing agencies and regular interaction is held by the Ministry
officials besides the monitoring mechanism during their official visits to the respective organizations and when the
Chief Executives/Chairman of KVIC/Coir Board visit this Ministry.   A Confederation of Promotion of Khadi and
Village Industries has been set up to give a boost to the marketing effort of KVIC.  This is being done to ensure that the
targets set forth for the year 2003-04 are achieved.    During 2002-03, the implementation of Rural Employment
Generation Programme (REGP) was decentralized. As a result of re-organisation of the scheme, KVIC could give
employment to 3.61 lakh as against the target of 4.00 Lakh i.e. 90%.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
1.2       Objective assessment of the financial requirement of K&VI Sector
 
The Committee is of the opinion that the Ministry of ARI should undertake an objective assessment of the
financial requirement of K&VI Sector, Coir Sector, Tiny and micro Enterprises, in tandem with KVIC, Coir
Board, PMRY division and accordingly take up the case with the Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission
for providing the required fund as per the needs of the Industries, so that no scheme should suffer due to lack of
fund.  The Committee desires that efforts should be taken for avoiding the gap between the projected demand
and the actual fund earmarked for specific purpose in one particular financial year.
(Para-2.5)

1.2.1    The Ministry of ARI while projecting its demand in the plan for 2004-05 has made an objective assessment of

1.3       Non achievement of Targets
 
The Committee further expresses its concern that the targets have not been achieved by KVIC and Coir

effective. The Committee hopes that target would be achieved during 2003-04.

Action Taken
 
1.3.1    During the year 2003-04, the monitoring mechanism at the level of Ministry of ARI has been strengthened and

generation and export promotion are met. In the case of KVIC, the REGP Programme has been decentralized and
release of funds at the Ministry level is done on a regular basis during 2003-04. As a result, achievement under REGP
for 2003-04 (1

RECOMMENDATION
 
1.5       Problem of unemployment and poverty
 
The Committee is of the view that Government should chalk out long term and short-term policy to deal
with the twin problem of unemployment and poverty.  Since the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industry has greater
role to play (with unemployment and poverty being widespread in rural areas) in opening the avenues for gainful
employment.  It is a well known fact that employment generation would enhance the purchasing power in the
economy which in turn would boost the consumption pattern in rural economy and their demand in the market. 
Demand-driven economy would automatically lift the standard of living of people residing in the rural areas
thereby the system so developed would arrest the urban migration as well.

RECOMMENDATION
 
1.9       Secretarial services for the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries
 
Presently, the Secretarial services for the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries are shared with the Ministry of
SSI which as per the opinion adequately addresses the requirements of the Ministry of ARI, the Committee is of the
view that the Government may think for providing a separate secretariat economic services especially for the Ministry
of Agro and Rural Industries because the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries has a tremendous role to play in the
building of rural economy of India which in turn would transform the economic structure of the nation and in case the
separate secretariat economic structure of the nation and in case the separate secretariat economic services for Ministry
of ARI is not feasible the Government should chalk out a scheme for shifting of surplus officer and staff from the
Department/ Ministry where business has decreased and also in light of the fact that Expenditure Reforms Commission
decreased and also in light of the fact that Expenditure Reforms Commission (ERC) has recommended either for closure
or rightsizing of Department/ Ministry.
(Para-6.1)
Action Taken
 
1.9.1    The Ministry of ARI was created in September, 2001. A proposal was sent to the Department of Expenditure for
allocation of additional staff for the Ministry of SSI and the Ministry of ARI to enable them to function as full-fledged
Ministries with separate Secretariat Economic Services. Department of Expenditure has agreed with the proposal in
principle for allocation of additional staff. The Ministry of SSI and Ministry of ARI have now taken up the matter with
Department of IPP, Ministry of Commerce and Industry to post the additional requirement of staff. Once adequate
complement of staff is made available to the Ministry of SSI and Ministry of ARI, it will be possible for creating
separate Secretariat Economic Services for the Ministry of ARI.

RECOMMENDATION
           
1.11     Target under PMRY
 
The Committee is of the opinion that government should fix the target under PMRY keeping in mind the stark
reality of population growth rate, quantum of poverty and absolute number of unemployment (disguised unemployment
as well as seasonal employment).  Need of the hour is formulation of Integrated and Total Planning after having
objective assessment of empirical fact and figures.  The rate of employment generations must be twice/thrice than the
population growth rate so that the problem of unemployment gets out rooted in a time bound manner.  The Committee
would be pleased to observe that the planning is done for exploring employment generation at the geometrical rate and
population growth be limited at the arithmetical rate.  Though at the first instance the suggestion may seem illusive but
to change the fate of India it has to be done and government must strive in this direction.  This is possible only through
coordinated and total Planning and when the problems of Population, Poverty and unemployment are studied and
visualized in a holistic manner and policy maker has to go beyond the cocoon of department/ministries of the
government.
(Para-7.6.3)
RECOMMENDATION
           
1.12     Responsibility of Government to combat the problem of poverty population and unemployment
 
The Committee feels that while the management of assets and prosperity may be left to the private parties and its
management may well be done by private company/individual or government through investment or disinvestments but
the management of poverty, population and unemployment has to be borne by government apparatus.  As no private
company or parties would willingly come forward to address these monstrous problem of Indian economy.  It is the
obligation of state and government to genuinely come forward to address the problems of Indian economy.  It adds to
the duties and responsibility of the government the welfare nature of Indian State and its Constitutional obligation.  The
policy maker of the nation has to keep these fact in mind while fixing the target for employment generation be it PMRY,
on REGP, or Jai Prakash Narayan Rozgar Yojana or any other employment generation Programme.  The rate of
employment must be higher than the population growth rate and the objective of total employment must be a time bound
programme.  The Committee hopes that government in general and the Ministry of ARI in particular would come
forward to take the initiative in this direction and the Planning Commission would lend its helping hand for realistic
allocation of fund for the employment generation programme.
(Para 7.6.4)
Action Taken
           
1.12.1  The Planning Commission had set up a Special Group on Targeting 10 million jobs per year over the 10
th Plan
period under the chairmanship of Dr. S.P. Gupta, Member Planning Commission. This report of the Special Group
which was published in May, 2002 has taken into account the present unemployment scenario in the country and has
chalked out an Action Plan for all the sectors of the government for generating adequate work opportunities for the
growing labour force in the country. The recommendations of this Report have been incorporated in the 10
th Plan
document and form the basis for fixing up targets under all the schemes for all Ministries for creation of job
opportunities. Based on the recommendations of the Special Group, the Ministry of ARI has set targets for REGP,
PMRY and the Coir sector.
 
RECOMMENDATION
 
1.13     PMRY a major Central Sector Scheme for employment generation

The Committee is of the view that the PMRY has emerged as a major Central Sector Scheme for employment
generation among the educated unemployed youth, by providing financial and other entrepreneurial development
assistance in establishing self-employment ventures.  Significant progress has been achieved in the implementation of
the scheme.  The Committee recommends that government should take following steps for the success of PMRY.
 
(a)        Minimise the rejection ratio by banks against no. of application received.

2.12     Merger of the Coconut Development Board and Coir Board
 
The Committee understands that Coconut Development Board and Coir Board have been given the specific
mandate depicting the jurisdiction and areas of functioning. However, the production of coconut and utilisation of the
semi finished products of coconut could be well integrated and comprehensive planning may be undertaken for
enhancing the utilization of husk to 70% to 80% while the current utilization is limited to 37% During the course
interaction of the Sub Committee for Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries (of standing Committee on Industry) with
the representatives of Coir Board and State Governments in Chennai and Pondicherry, it was felt that the merger of the
Coconut Development Board and Coir Board would enhance the potential for optimum utilization of husk. Therefore 
the Committee recommends that the Ministry of ARI should either purpose for merger of Coconut Development Board
and Coir Board for better coordination and Integrated planning or an apex Committee of representative of both the
boards may be constituted for the purpose of coordination and integrated planning. As regards the proposed Hard Fiber
Board , the Ministry of ARI may chalk out the mandate of its functioning. The Committee also suggests that Coir Board
should coordinate with Coconut Development Board and also the proposed hard Fibre Board. The Committee is of the
opinion that Coir Board should act promptly and should not hesitate in undertaking new roles.
(Para 12.37.3)
Action Taken
 
2.12.1   Coir Board is an autonomous organization established by the Central Government under the provision of the
Coir Industries Act 1953 for promoting the overall development of coir industry and upliftment of the living conditions
of workers engaged in this traditional cottage industry. The Coir Board is functioning under the Ministry of Agro and
Rural Industries, and the Coconut Development Board is functioning under the Agricultural Ministry. The Coconut
Development Board concentrates on the overall development of coconut cultivation in India. The areas of thrust are
different for the two organizations. The merger of Coir Board with Coconut Development Board will ultimately result in
loading additional activities on the Coconut Development Board. The focus of attention will vary and the very purpose
of establishment of a Coir Board will remain unfulfilled. In order to make a significant and impressive impact on the
development of coir industry, it is necessary to focus attention to a limited agenda rather than concentrating on
multifarious activities.
 
2.12.2  The coir industry generates additional employment in rural areas through coir processing activities with
minimum investment per job for which focused attention by professional agency like Coir Board is necessary to lead the
industry. The merger of Coir Board with Coconut Development Board is not likely to attract any tangible achievement
in the development of coir industry.  There is already proper co-ordination with the Coconut Development Board. The
Chairman, Coir Board is a member of the Board of Directors of Coconut Development Board.
           
2.12.3  The Government of India reviewed the performance of Coir Board in the wake of recommendations of the
Expenditure Reform Commission by engaging Administrative Staff College of India (ASCI) Hyderabad. Among other
things, they were asked to examine whether similar activities are also being undertaken by any other organizations and
if so, whether there is scope for merging or winding up any of these organizations to avoid unnecessary duplication. The
ASCI have meticulously examined the issue and recommended that as such there is no overlap with the roles of Coir
Board in such activities. The ASCI in their second report on Challenges for the Coir Industry in the New Millennium
have recommended several measures for developing coir industry in India through Coir Board.
 
2.12.4  As suggested by the Standing Committee, the Board has chalked out a scheme for enhancing the utilization of
coconut husk for coir industry. The 10
th Five Year Plan of the Coir Board envisages enhancement of the utilization of
coconut husk from 37% to 50% . The Coir Board has approved a project for manufacturing platform mounted mobile
defibering unit with private participation.

3.25     Implementing the employment programmes
 

144th Report On Industry
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Industry/144threport.htm[04-07-2016 12:45:14]
The facts pointed out by the Ministry of ARI that the activities undertaken by the various agencies implementing
the employment programmes have led to decentralization of programme and lack of uniformity in approach, are of
concern of the committee.  That is why the committee stresses that in order to bring uniformity in approach and
implementation of employment generation programmes, all schemes should be integrated and co-ordinated under one
Ministry and uniformity should be brought in the mode of implementation of schemes.
(Para-18.9)
Action Taken
 
3.25.1  The Ministry is implementing two Employment Generation Programmes namely the PMRY and REGP. Both
these programmes have different clienteles. The PMRY is meant for the educated unemployed in urban and rural areas
between the age group of 18-35 years below a certain income limit. It basically deals with small size projects up to Rs.2
lakhs only. In case of REGP, there is no income or age limit for the entrepreneurs and projects up to Rs.25 lakhs can be
taken up under the Scheme. The only criteria is that it should be set up in rural areas and should generate at least one job
for Rs.50,000 fixed capital investment. It can be seen that the target group for REGP is quite different from that for
PMRY. Thus, the focus is on the entrepreneur in both these schemes. On the other hand, employment generation
schemes by the other Ministries like Ministry of RD are targeted at the people below the poverty line and the main focus
is more on poverty alleviation through self-help groups and less on enterprise building.  Thus, it will be difficult to bring
uniformity in the mode of implementation of all schemes and it is submitted that the effort should be to fine-tune the
existing schemes so as to make them more viable and responsive to the needs of the people.
 
FURTHER RECOMMENDATION
 
3.25.2  It is on the basis of information furnished by the Ministry of Agro and Rural Industries the Committee
recommended that  in order to bring uniformity in approach and implementation of employment generation
programmes, all schemes should be integrated and co-ordinated under one Ministry and uniformity should be brought in
the mode of implementation of schemes.  The Committee while recommending for bringing all the schemes under one
Ministry does not intend to dilute the modalities and target groups of the employment generation programme.  Rather
the  Committee intends that with the administration of all the employment generation programmes under one Ministry
will give the “focused–attention” of the Government to combat the twin problem of unemployment and poverty.  The
Committee, therefore, reiterates its view point and recommendation.
