INTRODUCTION
I, the Chairperson of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home
Affairs having been authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby
present this One-Hundred and Twenty Sixth Report on the Demands for Grants (2007-08) relating
to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
2. While examining Demands for Grants every year it has been the practice in this Committee
to take up one subject/issue for its special focus. It had taken “Disaster Management” and “Delhi
Police and its functioning” for its special focus during 2005-06 and 2006-07, respectively. During
the year 2007-08, the Committee has identified “Union Territories (UTs) without Legislatures” as
the focus area in this report. The Committee has also taken up a separate subject of
“Administration of UTs” for a detailed study and has already presented a report on Administration
of UTs of Chandigarh, Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Pondicherry. It is in the process of
studying the other UTs. During its visit to the UTs without legislature, the Administrators have
brought to the notice of the Committee that though they are contributing substantially by way of
tax collection to the Exchequer, but the budgetary allocation to them is not commensurate to their
contribution. As a result their development/projects are hampered. To facilitate in-depth examination
of the subject by the Committee the officials of the Home Ministry had made an exclusive
presentation in the matter before it. During that presentation, the Chief Secretary/Administrators of
Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu and
Lakshadweep were present.
3. The Committee held in all, three sittings to complete the examination of Demands for Grants
of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The Committee heard the officials of the Ministry of Home Affairs
on 3rd April, 2007 and Administrator/Chief Secretary of the UTs without Legislatures in its meeting
held on 4th April, 2007.
4. The Committee while making its observations/recommendations has mainly relied upon the
following:—
(i) Presentations made by the officials of the MHA and UTs without Legislatures;
(ii) Detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry for the year 2007-08;
(iii) Annual Report of the Ministry for the year 2006-07;
(iv) Outcome Budget of the Ministry for the year 2007-08;
(v) Detailed Explanatory Note on the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs
for the year 2007-08;
(vi) Physical and financial targets fixed and achieved during the Tenth Plan period and the
breakdown of the same for the year 2007-08;
(vii) Projection of outlays for the schemes to be undertaken by the Ministry during the
Eleventh Five Year Plan;
(viii) Details of under-utilization of the allocations made under different heads during the last
three years;
(ix) Written replies furnished by the Ministry to the Questionnaires sent to them by the
Secretariat; and

(x) Written clarifications furnished by the Ministry, on the points/issues raised by the
Members during the deliberations of the Committee.
5. As per the mandate of the Committee, it has been examining the Demands for Grants of
the Ministry of Home Affairs every year since its inception. There are certain programmes/schemes
of the Ministry of Home Affairs which are of on-going matter and the Committee, as part of the
examination of the Demands for Grants, has been making its observations/recommendations on
those programmes/schemes. Though the programmes/schemes have been continuing for quite some
time, the implementation has been very tardy and the targets fixed are not achieved in time. The
Committee has, therefore, decided to make a detailed study on each of the following schemes/
projects:
(i) Border Fencing and Flood lighting;
(ii) Modernisation of Prison Administration;
(iii) Civil Defence;
(iv) Rehabilitation of J&K Migrants;
(v) Rehabilitation of Tsunami-affected persons; and
(vi) Housing for Central Police Forces and Delhi Police.
5.1 The Committee has, accordingly, appointed five Sub-Committees to make a detailed study
of the above mentioned schemes and report to it.
6. The Committee considered and adopted this report in its sitting held on 17 April, 2007.

CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
Maintenance of internal security in the country is the primary responsibility of the Ministry
of Home Affairs. In addition, the Ministry also has the following broad responsibilities:
(i) Issue of Notification in respect of Appointment of President/Vice-President, Prime
Minister and Members of the Union Council of Ministers;
(ii) Appointment of Governors and Lt. Governors;
(iii) Regulation of Pay and Allowances of Ministers;
(iv) Carrying out Census in India once every ten years;
(v) Management of the International Borders;
(vi) Management and Deployment of the Central Police Forces;
(vii) Centre-States Relations/Inter-State Relations;
(viii) Admin istration of Union Territories;
(ix) Implementation of the Provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963;
(x) Maintenance of Communal Harmony and Promotion of National Integration;
(xi) Co-ordination of Policy Relating to Human Rights;
(xii) Providing Relief and Rehabilitation to Refugees/Displaced Persons;
(xiii) Regulation of Entry of Foreigners into India;
(xiv) Grant of Pension and Other Benefits to Freedom Fighters;
(xv) Prevention and Combat of Drug Abuse and Drug Trafficking;
(xvi) Formulation and implementation of National Disaster Management Programme; and
(xvii) Border Area Development Programme.
2. To discharge the above responsibilities, the Ministry has been divided into following six
constituent Departments:
(i) Department of Internal Security;
(ii) Department of States;
(iii) Department of Official Languages;
(iv) Department of Home;
(v) Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs; and
(vi) Department of Border Management.

2
2.1 The Department of Internal Security deals with Police, Central Police Forces, internal
security and law and order, insurgency, terrorism, naxalism, Inter Services Intelligence (ISI)
activities, rehabilitation, grant of visa and other immigration matters, security clearances, etc.;
Department of States deals with Centre-State relations, Inter-State relations, administration of
Union Territories, freedom fighters’ pension, human rights, prison reforms, police reforms, etc.;
Department of Home deals with notification of assumption of office by the President and Vice-
President, notification of appointment/resignation of the Prime Minister, Ministers, Governors,
nomination to Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha, census of population, registration of births and deaths, etc.;
Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs deals with the constitutional provisions in respect
of the State of Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters relating to the State, excluding those with
which the Ministry of External Affairs is concerned; Department of Border Management deals
with management of international borders including coastal borders, strengthening of border
guarding and creation of related infrastructure, border areas development, etc.; Department of
Official Language is concerned with the implementation of the provisions of the Constitution
relating to Official Languages and provisions of the Official Languages Act, 1963.
2.2 The Ministry is under the overall charge of Home Minister assisted by three Ministers of
State. All Departments of the Ministry except the Department of Official Language are interlinked
and are headed by the Home Secretary who is assisted by Secretary (Border Management), one
Special Secretary, three Additional Secretaries and eighteen Joint Secretaries. The Department of
Official Language has a separate Secretary and functions independently. The Ministry is further
divided into nineteen divisions for administrative purposes and are concerned with the tasks
mentioned below for each division:—
(i) Centre-State Division
The Division deals with Centre-State relations, including working of the Constitutional
provisions governing such relations; appointment of Governors; creation of new
States; nominations to Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha; Inter-State Boundary disputes; over-
seeing the crime situation in States, imposition of President’s Rule, etc.
(ii) Internal Security Division
The Division deals with matters relating to internal security and law and order,
including anti-national and subversive activities of various groups/extremist
organizations, policy and operational issues on terrorism, security clearances,
monitoring of ISI activities and Home Secretary level talks with Pakistan on terrorism
and drug trafficking as part of the composite dialogue process.
(iii) Police Division
The Division functions as the cadre controlling authority of IPS officers and also
deals with all matters relating to Central Police Forces, including their deployment,
award of Presidents’ Police Medals for Meritorious/Distinguished Service and
Gallantry.
(iv) Union Territories Division
The Division deals with all legislative and constitutional matters relating to Union
Territories including Delhi and Puducherry. It also functions as the cadre controlling
authority of Arunachal Goa Mizoram Union Territories (AGMU) cadre of  IPS/IAS as
also Delhi Andaman Nicobar Islands Civil Service (DANICS)/ Delhi Andaman Nicobar
Islands Police Service (DANIPS). It is also responsible for overseeing the crime and
law and order situation in UTs.
(v) North-Eastern Division
The Division deals with the law and order situation in North-Eastern States, including

3
matters relating to insurgency and talks with various extremist groups operating in
that region.
(vi) Jammu and Kashmir Division
The Division deals with the Constitutional matters including Article 370 and general
policy matters in respect of J&K and terrorism/militancy in that State. It is also
responsible for implementation of the PM’s Package for J&K.
(vii) Foreigners Division
The Division deals with all matters relating to visa, immigration, citizenship, overseas
citizenship of India, acceptance of foreign contribution and hospitality.
(viii) Freedom Fighters and Rehabilitation Division
The Division frames and implements the Freedom Fighters Pension Scheme and the
schemes for rehabilitation of migrants from former West Pakistan/East Pakistan and
provision of relief to Sri Lankan and Tibetan refugees.
(ix) Human Rights Division
The Division deals with matters relating to the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
and also matters relating to national integration and communal harmony and Ayodhya.
(x) Disaster Management Division
Disaster Management-I Division is responsible for response, relief and preparedness
for natural calamities and man-made disasters (except drought and epidemics).
Disaster Management-II Division is responsible for legislation, policy, capacity
building, prevention, mitigation and long term rehabilitation.
(xi) Border Management Division
The Division deals with all matters relating to coordination and concerted action by
administrative, diplomatic, security, intelligence, legal, regulatory and economic
agencies of the country for the management of international borders, creation of
infrastructure like roads/fencing and floodlighting of borders, border areas
development programme and pilot project on Multi-purpose National Identity Card.
(xii) Police Modernization Division
The Division handles all items of work relating to modernization of State Police
Forces, provisioning/procurement of various items for modernization of Central Police
Forces, police reforms, and security of VIP/vital installations.
(xiii) Policy Planning Division
It deals with matters relating to policy formulation in respect of internal security
issues, international cooperation on counter-terrorism, international covenants, bilateral
assistance treaties and related items of work.
(xiv) Finance Division
The Division is responsible for formulating, operating and controlling the budget of
the Ministry under the Integrated Finance Scheme.
(xv) Security Division
The Division deals with arms and explosives, narcotics, and Narcotics Control Bureau
(NCB), coastal security, National Security Act, 1980 etc. This Division has now been
merged with Internal Security Division except that of coastal security which has been
merged with Border Management Division w.e.f. February 27, 2007.
(xvi) Judicial Division
The Division deals with all matters relating to the legislative aspects of the

4
IPC/Cr PC and the Commissions of Inquiry Act, 1952. It also handles matters relating
to State legislations which require the assent of the President under the Constitution,
political pension to erstwhile rulers before independence, Mercy Petitions under
Article 72 of the Constitution.
(xvii) Administration Division
The Division is responsible for handling all administrative and vigilance matters of the
Ministry, allocation of work among various Divisions of the Ministry of Home Affairs
and monitoring of compliance of furnishing information under RTI Act, 2005, matters
relating to Table of Precedence, Padma Awards, Gallantry Awards, Jeevan Raksha
Padak Awards, National Flag, National Anthem, State Emblem of India and Secretariat
Security Organisation.
(xviii) Coordination Division
The Division deals with intra-Ministry coordination work, Parliamentary matters,
public grievances, publication of Annual Report of the Ministry, Record Retention
Schedule, Annual Action Plan of the Ministry, custody of classified and non-classified
records of the Ministry, Internal Work Study, furnishing of various reports of SCs/
STs and Persons with Disabilities etc.
(xix) Naxal Management Division
This Division was created w.e.f. October 19, 2006 in the Ministry to effectively
tackle the naxalite menace from both security and development angles. It will monitor
the naxal situation and counter-measures being taken by the affected States with the
objective of improving ground-level policing and development response as per the
location specific action plans formulated/to be formulated by the affected States, and
review with the concerned Ministries/Departments to ensure optimum utilization of
funds released and proper implementation of various developmental schemes in the
naxal affected areas.

COASTAL SECURITY SCHEME
10. A Coastal Security Scheme launched in 2005 has been formulated for strengthening
infrastructure, patrolling and surveillance of country’s coastal areas. It aims to check and to
counter illegal cross-border activities and criminal activities using coast or sea. The scheme is being
implemented in all the nine coastal States and four Union Territories from the year 2005-06. Under
the scheme, assistance has been given to the coastal States and UTs to set up 73 coastal police
stations which will be equipped with 204 boats, 149 jeeps and 318 motorcycles for mobility on
coast and in close coastal water.
10.1 As per the presentation made by the AS & FA, MHA, the projection made for
BE 2007-08 is Rs. 4563.79 crore whereas actual allocation is Rs. 2495.35 crore which is half the
amount projected for Coastal Security, IR Battalions; surveillance etc.
10.2 Explaining the present status of the scheme, the Ministry have stated as under:
“(a) Buildings for the police station, checkpost outpost etc.:
Work on construction of buildings has commenced in Gujarat and Orissa. In other
States, it is in various stages like taking over the land and preparation of estimates.
(b) Manpower:
Under the scheme, manpower is to be provided by the States and UTs. Manpower
for the executive posts has been sanctioned by all the States except by Government of
Tamil Nadu. Manpower for the technical posts for boats has been sanctioned by the
Governments of Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and West Bengal.
Proposals for sanction of manpower are under  consideration of the Governments of Tamil
Nadu (both executive and technical posts), Goa and Orissa (technical posts for boats).
In the case of Union Territory Administrations, proposals for sanctioning manpower
are under consideration of Ministry of Home Affairs.
(c) Training:
As an interim arrangement, Coast Guard is imparting training to marine police
personnel in Coast Guard district Headquarters for a duration of 4 weeks including a week
long on job training in Coast Guard ships. 416 marine police personnel have undergone
training so far. More batches are under going training. Coast Guard has also offered to take
trained marine police personnel for further on job training in coastal patrolling in Coast
Guard ships for 15 days.
A Committee under Secretary (Border Management) has been constituted on
28th December, 2005 to examine various aspects of training of marine police including

19
setting up of a marine police training establishment to meet the long term training
requirements.
(d) Procurement of patrol boats:
Boats for coastal patrolling by the coastal police stations will be procured centrally
by Ministry of Home Affairs. Technical specifications of the boats have been finalized.
M/s Goa Shipyard Ltd (GSL) Goa and M/s Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers Ltd.
(GRSE), Kolkata have been nominated to construct and deliver the boats to the States on
the western and eastern coasts respectively. A Price Negotiation Committee (PNC) has been
constituted under Joint Secretary (PM), MHA to arrive at a negotiated price for the
boats. The PNC has held discussions on 15th November, 2006, 4th January, 2007 and
2nd March, 2007. The price negotiations are in progress.
(e) Operationalisation of coastal police stations:
Anticipating delay in procurement of boats and construction of police stations
buildings, the State Governments were advised to make coastal police stations operational
with existing arrangements and trained manpower. So far 27 coastal police stations have
been made operational in Gujarat(10), Goa(3), Karnataka(2), Kerala(1), Andhra Pradesh(6),
West Bengal(4) and Puducherry (1).
Modalities are also being finalized to commence coastal patrolling from the police
stations which have been made operational.
(f) Release of funds:
For construction of buildings and purchase of vehicles, equipment etc.,
Rs. 13.04 crore and Rs. 10.87 crore were released to the States and UTs in 2005-06 and
2006-07, respectively.
Views of State Governments
Governments of Kerala and Tamil Nadu have proposed that the cost of manpower
under the scheme may be met by the Central Government. Governments of Gujarat, Kerala
and Puducherry have proposed that setting up of more coastal police stations and
upgradation of existing police stations in the coastal areas may also be considered under the
scheme.
Modus operandi
The scheme envisages setting up of coastal police stations and providing them with
mobility on the coast and in close coastal waters for effective patrolling and surveillance
of the coastal areas. The police personnel in the coastal police stations are given training
by Coast Guard in maritime activities to enable them to carry out patrolling in the sea upto
2-3 nautical miles from the coast initially. For coastal patrolling, Coastal police stations will
function in close coordination with the Coast Guard Stations.
Institutional arrangements for coordination of intelligence at District level between coastal
police stations and Coast Guard and other agencies will also be made.
Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for the coastal police stations is also being prepared
in consultation with the various agencies.”
10.3 The Home Secretary during the course of the presentation has stated that out of 73 police
stations, 20 have become operational. Wherever, they have not become operational for any reason,
effort was being made to use trawlers in consultation and coordination with the State Governments

20
so that in those particular areas which have been earmarked as police stations, patrolling activities
could go on. He further added that the States would require to bear the manpower cost. In this
regard some States requested that manpower expenses should also be borne by the Ministry of
Home Affairs. He assured the Committee to look into this aspect of manpower expenditure.
10.4 The Committee feels that as there is a greater risk of infiltration from coastal belts,
the scheme needs to be implemented in the right earnest. This requires co-operation of all
the States. However, the Committee is constrained to note that some of the States have not
yet sanctioned the manpower. The Ministry may impress upon the State Governments
concerned to take early action in the matter. The Committee also recommends that the
process of acquiring boats may be completed at the earliest, as they are an essential
component for coastal security surveillance. In regard to the request of some State
Governments to the Centre to meet manpower cost, the Centre may hold consultations and
take a final view in the matter at the earliest.
NATIONAL DISASTER MANAGEMENT AUTHORITY
11. The Parliament had enacted the Disaster Management Act (DMA), 2005 on December 26,
2005 to provide for institutional mechanism by drawing up and monitoring the implementation of
the disaster management plans, ensuring measures by various wings of Government for prevention
and mitigating effects of disasters and for undertaking a holistic, coordinated and prompt response
to any disaster situation. The Act inter alia provides for setting up of a National Disaster
Management Authority (NDMA) under the chairmanship of the Prime Minister, constitution of
National Disaster Response Fund and National Disaster Mitigation Fund and similar funds at the
State and district levels.
11.1 As mandated by the Disaster Management Act 2005, the NDMA has been constituted on
September 27, 2006 with eight members, one of whom has been designated as the Vice-
chairperson. The National Disaster Management Authority has the responsibility, inter alia, of laying
down policies on disaster management and guidelines to be followed by different Ministries/
Departments of the Government of India for the purpose of integrating the measures for prevention
of disaster or mitigation of its effects in their development plans and projects. It has also to lay
down guidelines to be followed by the State Authorities in drawing up State plans and take
measures for the prevention, mitigation, preparedness and capacity building for dealing with the
threatening disaster situations or disaster, as it may consider necessary.
11.2 The responsibility of Disaster Management (except drought and epidemic) was transferred
from the Department of Agriculture and Co-operation to Ministry of Home Affairs alongwith the
Budget provision during 2002-03. Ministry of Home Affairs has been given the nodal responsibility
for management of both natural and man-made disasters; capacity building activities felating to
Nuclear, Biological and Chemical disasters. The Budgetary provision in this regard for the last three

23

INDIA-BANGLADESH BORDER FENCING WORKS
13. The India-Bangladesh border passes through West Bengal (2,216.7 Km.), Assam (263 km.),
Meghalaya (443 km.), Tripura (856 km.) and Mizoram (318 km.). The stretch consists of plains,
hills and jungles with hardly any major obstacles. The area is heavily populated and cultivated
extensively up to the border. The India-Bangladesh border is also marked by a high degree of
porosity and, therefore, checking illegal cross border activities has been a challenging proposition.
The main problem is of illegal migration from Bangladesh into India.
13.1 In order to prevent illegal infiltration and other anti-national activities from across the
border, the Government of India sanctioned erection of fencing in two phases. The Ministry in its
Annual Report has mentioned that total length of Indo-Bangladesh border to be fenced is 3,286.87
km. Out of this 2,455.80 km of fencing has so far been completed. A Pilot Project for flood
lighting along 277 km of Indo-Bangladesh border has also been completed.
13.2 Public Sector Undertakings viz. National Building Construction Corporation (NBCC),
National Projects Construction Corporation Limited (NPCC) and Engineering Projects (India)
Limited (EPIL) have been engaged to complete the work expeditiously in addition to the existing
agencies namely, Central Public Works Department (CPWD), Border Roads Organisation (BRO),
Assam Public Works Department and Tripura Public Works Department.
13.3 The Ministry in its replies to questionnaire has stated that in approximately 300 km long
Indo-Bangladesh border, fencing is required to be constructed within 150 yards from the border
due to constraints of the terrain and habitations. In these stretches, fencing work has not been
undertaken due to objections by Bangladesh Rifles. It was further stated that this matter has been
taken up with the Government of Bangladesh.

constructed under Phase-I in West Bengal, Assam and Meghalaya, as most of the fence has been
damaged due to adverse climatic conditions, repeated submergence etc. The replacement work has
already commenced in the States of Assam and West Bengal.

Committee recommends that the Government may hold discussion with Bangladesh in
consultation with the Ministry of External Affairs for a settlement of the problem. In
consultation with all concerned, the fencing may be taken nearer to the border instead of
150 yards away from the border. In case it is not possible the Government may buy the land
within this 150-yard of the Border so that the people who have properties within the 150
yards may not face problems anymore. The Ministry of Home Affairs may hold discussions
with all concerned, including the Ministry of External Affairs and find a permanent solution
at the earliest.
NAXALISM
14. Naxalism besides being a law and order problem, has deep socio-economic dimensions. The
Central Government has taken measures to address the problem simultaneously on security,

25
development and public perception management fronts. Those measures include deployment of
CPMFs to assist the State Police, sharing of actionable intelligence by central security agencies,
sanction of India Reserve Battalions and financial assistance for modernising and equipping of the
State Police forces.
14.1 The Ministry have stated that Rs. 4173.71 crore has been provided to the naxal affected
States, under the Police Modernisation Fund assistance. In addition, Rs. 210.68 crore has been
given as reimbursement of Security Related Expenditure. On the developmental front, assistance to
the order of Rs. 1597.60 crore has been given under the Backward Districts Initiative (BDI)
Scheme, during last 4 years, Naxal situation in the country is being monitored at various levels in
the Ministry of Home Affairs. The Ministry have also stated that a multi-pronged strategy
essentially of sustained and effective police action coupled with accelerated socio-economic
development and management of public perception is being pursued to effectively tackle the naxalite
menace.
14.2 The Ministry in its written reply to the questionnaire has inter-alia furnished details of
monitoring mechanism which have been set up by the Government for tackling Naxalism which
includes a periodical review by the Cabinet Committee on Security on naxal situation, constitution
of a Standing Committee of the Chief Ministers of the naxal affected States chaired by the Union
Home Minister, Quarterly Coordination Centre meetings chaired by the Union Home Secretary with
the Chief Secretaries and the Directors General of Police of the affected States and the monthly
Task Force meetings of Nodal Officers of naxal affected States and Central agencies taken by
Special Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs. The States have also been asked to hold a monthly
review by the DGP and the bi-monthly review by the Chief Secretary of the naxal situation and
the measures and stratagies to contain the naxal problem on both security and development fronts.
14.3 The Home Secretary during the course of his presentation stated that a number of steps
were being taken by the Ministry for tackling naxal violence effectively. He stated that there had
been a decline in the overall level of violence. In a number of States the decline has been quite
significant. He also explained that the programme of the Government of India, included deployment
of Central Para-Military Forces in the Naxalite areas on a no-cost basis to the States. Special
Security Related Expenditure in the context of the Police Modernisation Schemes, enabling the
States to upgrade their weaponry, additional IR battalions had been sanctioned for the naxal-
affected States. Specialised training in guerilla warfare had also been provided to the police
personnel in the army and the CPMF training centres. Mine-protected vehicles and other
surveillance equipments have been supplied to them. Forty per cent recruitment in the Central
Paramilitary Forces was expected to be from the border areas and areas affected by militancy with
the aim of providing employment.
14.4 The Home Secretary however informed the Committee that some of the naxal affected
States do not found to have sufficient force system. Certain States have a large number of
vacancies in their force. One of the State has about 10,000 vacancies. He further stated that there
is a limit upto which the CPMFs can help the States forces but they can not completely substitute
the State Police Forces and the Ministry was impressing upon the States to fill up the vacancies
on priority basis. The Home Secretary has also stated that the States Police Forces in many cases
do not follow the Standred Operating Procedure (SOP) which results in easy attack by the naxals.
14.5 The Committee notes that the Government are taking steps on different fronts
including the developmental efforts. The Committee has also recommended in successive
reports for a holistic approach in tackling the naxal problem. The Committee however, feels
that there is no perceptible change on the socio-economic front. Though the socio-economic
development cannot be quantified, it can be perceived and if such a perceptible change is
there, undoubtedly it will have a lasting impact. The Committee reiterating the earlier

26
recommendation that an effective developmental approach may be adopted, further
recommends that various schemes under the Ministries of Rural Development and other
Ministries may also be implemented for the socio-economic development in the naxal
affected areas. There needs to be an effective monitoring mechanism on the implementation
of these schemes with the involvement of all the concerned Ministries and agencies.
14.6 The Committee also reiterates its observation made earlier that the naxal problem
is no longer a problem of a few States. It has acquired a national dimension posing gravest
threat to the national security. The naxal activities have spread to more than twelve States
and are reported to be having links with external agencies including ISI. The Committee
is therefore, of the considered view that taking into account the growth and influence of
the Naxal groups in several States and the grave threat posed by it to the internal security
of the country, it is high time that the Ministry constitute a Unified Command to
effectively tackle the menace. Besides the Central Paramilitary Forces, State Police Forces
may also contribute to the unified command. The Committee also takes note of the
observation made by the Home Secretary that naxal attacks on several occasions take place
due to non observance of ‘Standard Operating Procedure’ (SOP) by the State police forces.
The Committee therefore, strongly recommends that Government may consider creating a
unified command common to all the naxal affected States.
14.7 One of the Members of the Committee mentioned that private sector industries are suffering
on many counts due to the naxal threats in affected areas. Public Sector Undertakings are provided
security by the Central Industrial Security Force whereas the private sector industries have to make
their own security arrangements. The private security arrangement by the industries is not adequate
enough to tackle the naxal threats. The Home Secretary replying to this, during the course of the
oral evidence, stated that if any private sector organization in the affected area wants to be armed
sufficiently to protect itself, applications can be made by them for licence or any kind of
protection which can be examined on case-by-case basis. He further assured the Committee to look
into the matter.
14.8 The Committee agrees that naxal threat is a big menace for any industry, including
the private sector. The issue needs to be examined to see that private sector industries are
also protected in the naxal-affected areas. The Ministry of Home Affairs may examine the
matter in consultation with all concerned and take an early decision.
MODERNISATION OF POLICE FORCES
15. The Ministry of Home Affairs has been implementing a Non-plan Scheme for Modernization
of State Police Forces since 1969-70 to supplement the efforts of the State Governments in
modernizing their forces. The Scheme provided assistance to the State Governments, as 50% grant-
in-aid and 50% loans for purchase of vehicles, equipments for forensic science labs, fingerprint
bureau, police training, weaponry, construction of housing for police personnel, police stations,
training buildings etc. Equal matching contributions was expected from the State Governments.
15.1 To face multifarious challenges including controlling law and order, maintaining internal
security, on account of insurgency/extremism/terrorism in some of the States, the Central
Government considered it necessary to modernize the State Police Forces quickly, and therefore,
the Annual Central Assistance under the scheme was enhanced to Rs.1000 crore from year
2000-01, and thereafter to Rs.1400 crore in 2003-04. While enhancing the Annual Central Allocation
during 2003-04, it was also decided to provide the entire Central assistance as grant-in-aid. During
the Financial Year 2005-06, the Government has further enhanced the allocation to RS.1645.00
crore with specific focus on States facing problems of terrorism, left-wing extremism, etc. In a
decision taken in September, 2005, 100% Central funding is provided for NE States and J&K and
75% Central funding for the remaining 20 States.

27
15.2 The Committee in its 119th Report had recommended to extend this important scheme to
UTs also and further recommended to make necessary allocation at RE stage in 2006-07. As a
result, a provision of Rs. 40 crore was made for the modernization of Police Forces in UTs at
RE stage for 2006-07.
15.3 For the year 2007-08, an a mount of Rs. 1190.63 crore has been allocated under this head
against the projection of Rs. 1921.80 crore. Out of which Rs. 1013.83 crore is provided
as assistance to States for modernisation of Police in States whereas Rs. 41.20 crore and
Rs. 13.20 crore are provided to UTs without legislature and UTs with legislature, respectively.
Rs. 122.40, crore have been provided for assistance to Delhi Police for modernization.
15.4 The Ministry have informed the Committee that a mid-term evaluation of the Modernization
of State Police Forces Scheme was conducted by Bureau of Police Research and Development
(BPR&D) in the year 2006. The performance of the States under this scheme has been analyzed
through evaluation of utilization of funds for modernization made available to them. The general
trend in the expenditure of modernization funds in different States varies with the fixation of
priority on different areas of modernization. Overall, it has been observed that more than 50% fund
is utilized in the police housing and police buildings activities; around 20% for police mobility, 1-6%
for police training, 2-13% for police weaponry etc.
15.5 The Ministry have also stated that the State Governments are pursued vigorously for better
and full utilization of funds. Emphasis is also laid on equipping the police forces on a balanced
scale, for which all State plans formulated by them are considered and approved by the High
Powered Committee constituted by the Ministry. The plans are formulated by the respective States
on the basis of their urgent local needs.
15.6 The Committee takes note of the Mid-Term evaluation of the Modernisation of State
Police Forces Scheme by BPR&D. One of the findings made by the BPR&D in the study
which causes concern to the Committee is that more than 50 per cent of the fund is utilized
in the police housing and police building activities. The Committee is of the view that with
the new technological advances taking place in a feverish pitch and in view of the fact that
the Police Forces in India function with age-old equipments and gadgets, much of the
scheme of modernization should concentrate on providing modern gadgets, improving
technology, providing IT facilities, etc. Spending more than 50 per cent on building activities
defeats the very purpose of the scheme. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends
that the Ministry of Home Affairs may review the scheme in the light of pattern of
expenditure incurred by the States and prepare a list of activities which should lay thrust
on improving technology and providing modern gadgets, etc.
15.7 The Committee is constrained to note that there was an unspent balance of
Rs. 47.81 crore under the Scheme of Modernisation of Police forces as on 31.12.2006.
Though the Ministry have stated that they are pursuing vigorously with the States for better
and full utilization of funds, the Committee desires that they may lay a condition stating
that further funds would be released only after the full expenditure of the previous balance.
It should be insisted upon all the States to send Utilisation Certificates and receipt thereof
may also be put as one of the conditions for further release of funds. These are necessary
to ensure that States spend the funds meant for Modernization of Police Forces in a better
manner and as per the schedule.
15.8 In regard to the Scheme for Modemisation of Police Forces in Union Territories; the
Ministry have stated that each UT is required to prepare an Annual Action Plan and get it approved
by the Committee constituted in the Ministry of Home Affairs for this purpose. Accordingly, the
UTs had submitted their Annual Action Plans for 2006-07, which were considered and approved

28
by the Committee with certain modifications, and they were advised to initiate action for
implementation thereof.
15.9 The Ministry have also stated that while communicating approval for implementation of
Annual Action Plans, the Union Territories were also advised that expenditure on the Plan for
2006-07 would be met by locating savings within the sanctioned budget grant. In case it was not
found possible to adjust the amount within the sanctioned grant, they would submit proposals for
sanction of additional funds at RE stage. Since most of the UTs could not find savings, additional
funds to the tune of Rs. 40 crores had been provided in RE 2006-07 through re-appropriation.
15.10 The Committee notes with satisfaction launching of the scheme of Modernisation of
Police Forces in UTs. Since it was initiated in 2006-07 only, it would be too early to assess
the scheme. The Committee notes that UTs were asked in 2006-07 to implement this
scheme effectively by having savings. Though ultimately additional allocation had to be
made as UTs could not effect savings, such a situation could have been avoided by making
allocation initially which would have enabled the UTs to start implementation immediately.
The Ministry may therefore from time-to-time monitor the implementation of the scheme
and ensure that the physical as well as financial target are achieved in time by the State
and Union Territories.
15.11 The Committee is given to understand that Special Branches in States/UTs Police
Forces play an important role in collection of intelligence through technical surveillance and
the real and actionable intelligence at the micro-level comes through human assets.
However, the Committee is of the view that the Special Branches neither have sufficient
infrastructure nor modern equipment and gadgets, etc. In view of the current internal
security situation and the growing naxalite and terrorist activities, strengthening and
modernization of Special Branches acquire significance. The Committee, therefore, feels
that the Special Branches in the States/Union Territories Police Forces need to be revamped
by enhancing their manpower by way of filling up of vacancies; posting of competent officers
with reasonable tenure, dedicated staff for intelligence work right up to the police station
level and reactivating beat constable system and above all making available modern
equipment and gadgets for interception and special training for personnel posted therein.
15.12 The Committee therefore recommends that the Ministry of Home Affairs may
impress upon the State/Union Territories Governments/Administrations to strengthen their
Special Branches. The Ministry may also allocate some of the funds under Modernization of
States Police Forces Scheme for strengthening and modernization of Special Branches.
MODERNISATION OF PRISON ADMINISTRATION
16. The Central Government in 2002-03 launched a non-plan scheme for construction of
additional jails to reduce overcrowding, repair and renovation of existing jails, improvement in
sanitation and water supply and construction of living accommodation for prisons staff. The
scheme known as the Modernization of Prisons is being implemented in 27 States over a period
of five years (2002-07) with an outlay of Rs. 1800 crore. The cost is being shared between Central
Government and State Governments in the ratio of 75:25 respectively.
16.1 The Ministry have stated that though this scheme was launched in 2002-03, the Central
share was released to the States only in March 2003. The State Governments were granted eighteen
months from the date of release to utilize the amounts released to them. Further, there were several
teething problems in the States at the beginning of the scheme and many of them could not utilize
the amount promptly. Due to this, the Scheme has since been extended by a period of two years
w.e.f. 1.4.2007 without affecting the total outlay of Rs. 1800 crore.

29
16.2 This scheme has four components. The physical progress of each component for 2006-07
is as under, as submitted by the Ministry:
“Construction of New Jail:  The Central Empowered Committee has so far approved
the construction of 163 jails of different categories in various States. Out of this, 99 jails
are expected to be completed by 31.3.2007. Targets for 2007-08 and 2008-09 are
construction of 50 jails and 8 jails respectively.
Repairs and Renovations and expansion of jails:  Under this head State Governments
are taking up work for repairs and renovations of the existing jails, which are in dilapidated
condition. The Central Empowered Committee has approved construction of 1360 additional
barracks in the existing jails to augment the capacity in jails. Out of this, 935 additional
barracks are expected to be completed by 31.3.2007. Targets for 2007-08 and 2008-09
are fixed at construction of 305 and 120 barrels respectively.
Construction of Staff Quarters: Construction of 8573 quarters of different categories
for prison personnel has so far been approved. Out of this, 6363 staff quarters are expected
to be completed by 31.3.2007. Targets for 2007-08 and 2008-09 are fixed at construction
of 1735 and 3210 staff quarters respectively.
Water and Sanitation:  Under this head, State Governments were required to take up
various works for providing water and sanitation facilities for prisoners and prison
personnel in various jails. It was required to construct overhead tanks for drinking water,
construction of toilets, drainage and sanitation work. All these works are under various
stages of completion.”
16.3 The Committee was informed that the release to the State Governments were made on the
basis of their performances. Due to this reason, the full central share could not be released against
the annual plans of 2003-04, 2004-05, 2005-06 and 2006-07 to all States. However, majority of
States were now performing well and the total amount spent by the State Government upto
31.12.2006 was Rs. 967.89 crore against the central assistance of Rs. 1016.17 crore. The
percentage of utilisation vis a vis  the central share is 95%.
16.4 On a query whether there is any perceptible change in the condition of inmates in the Indian
prisons after the scheme has been launched and whether any study has been conducted to evaluate
the same the Ministry have stated as under:
“The scheme would definitely help in improving the physical condition in prison as
creation of additional accommodation would help in reducing overcrowding. Repairs,
renovation and water and sanitation works will help in improving the living condition of
prisoners. The construction of staff quarters for prisons personnel will boost the morale of
the prison staff which will thereby help them in performing their duty more efficiently.
A study/evaluation is normally carried out after the completion of the scheme.
However the scheme is closely monitored and review meetings are held regularly at senior
levels. The feedback in their meetings from the States has been positive. The officials of
the Ministry of Home Affairs have been visiting the States regularly to monitor the progress
of the scheme and the feedback received is that the scheme is proving to be extremely
useful and will go a long way in improving the condition of prisoners in jails”
16.5 The Committee reiterates its observation made in its 119th Report that the Scheme,
if implemented in the right earnest, would go a long way in improving the conditions in the
prisons and the inmates thereof. The Committee is, however, constrained to note that more
emphasis is laid under the Scheme on the construction work, repairs and renovation than
improving the actual conditions inside the jails for the prisoners. The Committee, therefore,

30
strongly recommends that the Government may redesign the guidelines for the scheme
laying more emphasis on improving the facilities/conditions of the prisoners.
16.6 The Committee also expresses its concern that even though the Scheme started in

a study evaluation is normally carried out after completion of the scheme shows a casual
approach of dealing with the matter. Whenever any scheme is launched a mid-term
evaluation is carried out. Since the scheme has been extended by two more years with effect
from 01.04.2007, the Government may conduct a mid-term evaluation immediately to assess
its efficacy.
16.7 The Committee is presently examining the Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment)
Bill, 2006. One of the provisions in the Bill seeks to amend Section 167 of the Code for
allowing video conferencing between the Jail and the Court for trial of an accused to avoid
transportation of the accused to the court. To make this provision workable it is essential
to modernise the jails, especially enabling those with IT facilities, computers and all related
equipments. Most of the jails in the country do not have this facility at present. In case,
the above-mentioned amendment is enacted, it would practically be of no use unless the
required infrastructure is available in the jails. The Committee therefore, recommends that
creation of infrastructure in the jails for enabling video linkages between the jails and the
courts, may be made part of the scheme of Modernisation of Prisons and if necessary a
separate allocation may be made for that purpose.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
CIVIC ACTION PROGRAMME OF BORDER GUARDING FORCES
The Committee feels that Civic Action Programme in the border areas is a very
important task. If undertaken well, it will infuse confidence among the border area
populace, develop infrastructure there and promote the feeling of national integration. For
all these to happen, the people should have confidence in the persons who are undertaking
such activities. However, people are sceptical about the uniformed personnel. The
Committee therefore recommends that the implementation of Civic Action Programme by
the BGFs need to be re-looked in its entirety. (Para 8.7)
RELIEF AND REHABILITATION MEASURES FOR J&K MIGRANTS
The Ministry of Home Affairs stated before the Committee that as it is a
reimbursement scheme, as soon as proposals for rehabilitation are received from the State
Government, the grants are provided accordingly. The Ministry further stated that the
schemes were not forthcoming from the State of J&K. As a result of this, though migrants
are living in sub-human conditions in rehabilitation camps, funds allocated for that purpose
are not utilised fully. In view of this, the Committee in its 119th Report had recommended
that Government should impress upon the J&K Government to come forward with schemes
in time and implement them without delay. But inspite of this, the Committee is disturbed
to learn that against the BE 2006-07 provision of Rs. 120 crore the expenditure during 2006-
07 is expected to be only Rs. 70 crore. The Committee once again strongly urges upon the
Ministry to further impress upon the J&K Government about the necessity of sending
schemes expeditiously and implement them in time so that all the J&K migrants are
rehabilitated without further delay. (Para 9.2)
COASTAL SECURITY SCHEME
The Committee feels that as there is a greater risk of infiltration from coastal belts,
the scheme needs to be implemented in the right earnest. This requires co-operation of all
the States. However, the Committee is constrained to note that some of the States have not
yet sanctioned the manpower. The Ministry may impress upon the State Governments
concerned to take early action in the matter. The Committee also recommends that the
process of acquiring boats may be completed at the earliest, as they are an essential
component for coastal security surveillance. In regard to the request of some State
Governments to the Centre to meet manpower cost, the Centre may hold consultations and
take a final view in the matter at the earliest. (Para 10.4)
NATIONAL DISASTER MANAGEMENT AUTHORITY
The Committee is constrained to note that the Rules relating to National Disaster
Response Force (NDRF) have not yet been notified. These rules are very essential for the
functioning of the Force. The Committee desires that the Rules may be finalised and
notified at the earliest. (Para 11.7)

INDIA-BANGLADESH BORDER FENCING WORKS
The Committee takes a serious note of the fact that the allocations are not being
spent. Besides the Ministry are also likely to take up Phase-ill of the fencing work. The
Committee strongly recommends that the physical and financial targets may be achieved
well in time and Phase-II may be completed at the earliest so that Phase-III may be
commenced as soon as possible. (Para 13.7)
The Committee is very much concerned about the problems being faced by the people
residing at the Border areas. It is a fact that there are properties of the Border people
where agricultural activities are carried out and in some areas, people are also residing in
this 150-yard of area. The Committee is anguished to note that people face lot of problems
under the current dispensation and no solution has been found so far for this problem. The
Committee recommends that the Government may hold discussion with Bangladesh in
consultation with the Ministry of External Affairs for a settlement of the problem. In
consultation with all concerned, the fencing may be taken nearer to the border instead of
150 yards away from the border. In case it is not possible the Government may buy the land
within this 150-yard of the Border so that the people who have properties within the 150
yards may not face problems any more. The Ministry of Home Affairs may hold discussions
with all concerned, including the Ministry of External Affairs and find a permanent solution
at the earliest. (para 13.9)
NAXALISM
The Committee notes that the Government are taking steps on different fronts

52
including the developmental efforts. The Committee has also recommended in successive
reports for a holistic approach in tackling the naxal problem. The Committee however, feels
that there is no perceptible change on the socio-economic front. Though the socio-economic
development cannot be quantified, it can be perceived and if such a perceptible change is
there, undoubtedly it will have a lasting impact. The Committee reiterating the earlier
recommendation that an effective developmental approach may be adopted, further
recommends that various schemes under the Ministries of Rural Development and other
Ministries may also be implemented for the socio-economic development in the naxal
affected areas. There needs to be an effective monitoring mechanism on the implementation
of these schemes with the involvement of all the concerned Ministries and agencies.
(Para 14.5)
The Committee also reiterates its observation made earlier that the naxal problem
is no longer a problem of a few States. It has acquired a national dimension posing gravest
threat to the national security. The naxal activities have spread to more than twelve States
and are reported to be having links with external agencies including ISI. The Committee
is therefore, of the considered view that taking into account the growth and influence of
the Naxal groups in several States and the grave threat posed by it to the internal security
of the country, it is high time that the Ministry constitute a Unified Command to effectively
tackle the menace. Besides the Central Paramilitary Forces, State Police Forces may also
contribute to the unified command. The Committee also takes note of the observation made
by the Home Secretary that naxal attacks on several occasions take place due to non
observance of ‘Standard Operating Procedure’ (SOP) by the state police forces. The
Committee therefore, strongly recommends that Government may consider creating a
unified command common to all the naxal affected States. (Para 14.6)
The Committee agrees that naxal threat is a big menace for any industry, including
the private sector. The issue needs to be examined to see that private sector industries are
also protected in the naxal-affected areas. The Ministry of Home Affairs may examine the
matter in consultation with all concerned and take an early decision. (Para 14.8)
MODERNISATION OF POLICE FORCES
The Committee takes note of the Mid-Term evaluation of the Modernisation of State
Police Forces Scheme by BPR&D. One of the findings made by the BPR&D in the Study
which causes concern to the Committee is that more than 50 per cent of the fund is utilized
in the police housing and police building activities. The Committee is of the view that with
the new technological advances taking place in a feverish pitch and in view of the fact that
the Police Forces in India function with age-old equipments and gadgets, much of the
scheme of modernization should concentrate on providing modern gadgets, improving
technology, providing IT facilities, etc. Spending more than 50 per cent on building activities
defeats the very purpose of the scheme. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends
that the Ministry of Home Affairs may review the scheme in the light of pattern of
expenditure incurred by the States and prepare a list of activities which should lay thrust
on improving technology and providing modern gadgets, etc. (Para 15.6)
The Committee is constrained to note that there was an unspent balance of Rs. 47.81
crore under the Scheme of Modernisation of Police forces as on 31.12.2006. Though the
Ministry have stated that they are pursuing vigorously with the States for better and full
utilization of funds, the Committee desires that they may lay a condition stating that
further funds would be released only after the full expenditure of the previous balance. It
should be insisted upon all the States to send Utilisation Certificates and receipt thereof

53
may also be put as one of the conditions for further release of funds. These are necessary
to ensure that States spend the funds meant for Modernization of Police Forces in a better
manner and as per the schedule. (Para 15.7)
The Committee notes with satisfaction launching of the scheme of Modernisation of
Police Forces in UTs. Since it was initiated in 2006-07 only, it would be too early to assess
the scheme. The Committee notes that UTs were asked in 2006-07 to implement this
scheme effectively by having savings. Though ultimately additional allocation had to be
made as UTs could not effect savings, such a situation could have been avoided by making
allocation initially which would have enabled the UTs to start implementation immediately.
The Ministry may therefore from time-to-time monitor the implementation of the scheme
and ensure that the physical as well as financial target are achieved in time by the State
and Union Territories. (Para 15.10)
The Committee is given to understand that Special Branches in States/UTs Police
Forces play an important role in collection of intelligence through technical surveillance and
the real and actionable intelligence at the micro-level comes through human assets.
However, the Committee is of the view that the Special Branches neither have sufficient
infrastructure nor modern equipment and gadgets, etc. In view of the current internal
security situation and the growing naxalite and terrorist activities, strengthening and
modernization of Special Branches acquire significance. The Committee, therefore, feels
that the Special Branches in the States/Union Territories Police Forces need to be revamped
by enhancing their manpower by way of filling up of vacancies; posting of competent officers

The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Home Affairs may

(Para 15.12)
MODERNISATION OF PRISON ADMINISTRATION

Demand No. 96 – Dadra and Nagar Haveli; and
Demand No. 97 – Daman and Diu
The Committee is constrained to note that both the Union Territories of Daman and
Diu and Dadra and Nagar Haveli have been getting very less grant as compared to the
projections made by them. In fact, Dadra and Nagar Haveli has been given about one-third
of the projected amount whereas Daman and Diu has been given less than 50 per cent of
the projected amount. With this reduction in the allocations, the activities proposed to be
taken up by the Union Territories would be considerably affected. The Committee,
therefore, strongly recommends that the allocations for both the Union Territories may be
increased appropriately at RE level. The Ministry of Home Affairs may also hold discussions
with the Planning Commission about the necessity of the projects proposed to be taken up
by the Union Territories. The Committee also recommends that the priority areas of
activities proposed to be taken up during 2007-08 may be completed as per the targets fixed.
Energy sector is an important area in which both the Union Territories proposed to set up
new sub-stations. Dadra and Nagar Hayeli, also proposes to augment the capacity of a sub-
station and Daman and Diu proposes to convert overhead lines to underground lines. These
activities may be undertaken on a priority basis and completed as per the targets fixed.
(Para 23.6)
Demand No. 98 – Lakshadweep
The Administrator, Lakshadweep, during the course of his oral evidence before the
Committee, stated that though the Plan Allocation has increased from Rs.209 crore
at RE 2006-07 to Rs.222.11 crore in BE 2007-08, it was way below the projection of
Rs. 402 crore made by the Lakshadweep Administration before the Planning Commission.
The main reason for projecting Rs.402 crore was that the growth rate during the Tenth Five
Year Plan had become very fast and they wanted to maintain that tempo. He further stated
that discussions were held with the Planning Commission but they were told that the outer
limit of the allocation would be Rs.222 crore only though they appreciated the presentation
made by the Administration. (Para 24.3)
The Committee feels that the allocation made is too meagre as compared to the
projections made by the Lakshadweep Administration in view of the necessity of sustaining
the tempo of the growth rate. The Planning Commission may consider increasing the
allocation to a reasonable extent at RE level so that Lakshadweep may go ahead with its
planned activities. This is necessary because Lakshadweep has a large potential for tourism
and unless adequate infrastructure is created, tourism cannot improve. (Para 24.4)
Among the priority sectors proposed to be taken up during the current financial
year, implementation of perspective plan for shipping sector is an important one. Ships,
ferries, landing barges, oil barges, etc., are proposed to be acquired besides two helicopters.
The Committee endorses the proposal for taking up shipping sector as a priority area during
2007-08 and all the proposed activities may be completed as targeted. (Para 24.5)
Necessary steps may be taken to implement the proposal for opening all islands
for tourism and open up public-private participation in tourism, so that tourism may develop
as a major activity in the islands which has the potential to provide employment.
(Para 24.6)
