CHAPTER -I 
OVERVIEW 
1.1 Introduction 
1.1.1 India‟s North Eastern Region (NER) comprises of eight States - Arunachal Pradesh, 
Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Miz oram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura. The NER is unique in 
terms of growth opportunities as the region possesses the potential to develop into an economic 
powerhouse, being a vibrant source of energy, oil, natural gas, coal, and limestone. Besides being 
endowed with India‟s largest perennial water system in the river Brahmaputra and its tributaries , 
the NER is also home to a great variety of horticultural products, plantation crops, vegetables, 
spices, rare herbs and medicinal plants. The region also offers v ast tourism opportunities, rare 
flora and fauna, breathtaking natural beauty, a rich culture of performing arts besides varied 
cuisine and handicrafts. 
1.1.2 To coordinate the developmental efforts being taken by the Central and the State 
Governments in th e NER, the Department of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) 
was set up in 2001. Subsequently, the Department was upgraded to a full -fledged Ministry in 
May 2004 . I t is the only Ministry with a territorial jurisdiction.   The Ministry of DoNER 
implements the schemes of Non -Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) that have been 
restructured as North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS) and Special 
Packages through State Governments of NER and some Central Ministries . The North E astern 
Council (NEC), Shillong, which was set up in 1971, is under the administrative control of 
Ministry of DoNER.  There are two Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), namely, North 
Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd. (NEHH DC) and North 
Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Ltd. (NERAMAC), under M/o DoNER. 
 
1.2 Coordination with Central Ministries and Departments  
1.2.1 In October 1996, it was decided that Central Ministries/ Departments, unless specifically 
exempted, will set apart 10 per cent of their Plan Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the NER to 
ensure a quantum jump in budgetary resource flow to the region and to fill the backlog and gaps 
in basic minimum services and infrastructure.  Subsequently, funds  for the NER are being 
allocated annually on a lump sum basis by the non -exempted Central Ministries/Departments at 
the BE/RE stage, under the Major Heads 2552 (for revenue expenditure), 4552 (for capital 
expenditure) and 6552 (for loan expenditure). These  funds are expended under their respective 
functional schemes. These schemes may either be in the form of direct intervention by the 
Ministries/Departments or through their institutions/agencies or may even be through the 
concerned State Governments. 
1.2.2 The Ministry of DoNER, being the nodal Ministry, coordinates with the various 
Ministries/ Departments primarily concerned with development and welfare activities in the 
NER and keeps track of expenditure under the mandatory 10 per cent GBS by the non -exempted 
Ministries/ Departments since its inception. This exercise is being undertaken on an annual basis

1.5 Central Pool of Resources for NER 
1.5.1 In 1997-98, when it was found that the actual expenditure of Ministries/Departments for 
NER was less than the mandatory c eiling of 10 per cent, the Government took the decision to 
create a “Central Resource Pool” into which the unutilized portion of the 10 per cent earmarked 
GBS would accrue at the end of the financial year.  Since 1998 -99, the annual surrendered 
amounts out of the earmarked outlays of all the non -exempted Central Ministries/Departments 
for NER have been accounted as accruals to this Pool and the annual expenditure of the Ministry 
under the NLCPR scheme is being accounted as withdrawal from the Pool. However,  this is a 
notional pool, maintained on a proforma basis by the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry of 
DoNER monitors Ministry -wise expenditure/ surrendered amounts for the purpose of 
ascertaining the accrual to the NLCPR pool in consultation with the Ministry of Finance. 
1.6 Re-appropriation Mechanism 
1.6.1 The Ministry of DoNER has been conferred with the powers to review the quarterly 
expenditure of the non -exempted Ministries and recommend reallocation of likely surrendered 
amount to those Ministries/Depa rtments, which have the absorptive capacity to implement 
additional approved schemes/programmes within the financial year.  
 
INSTITUTIONS UNDER MINISTRY OF DONER 
1.7 North Eastern Council (NEC) 
1.7.1 The NEC was constituted as a statutory advisory body und er the NEC Act 1971 (84 of 
1971) marking the beginning of a new chapter of concerted and planned endeavor for rapid 
socio-economic development of the North Eastern Region (NER) and is under the administrative 
control of the Ministry of DoNER.  It is a statu tory Regional Planning Body, which provides 
financial assistance for a variety of projects including infrastructure projects like construction of 
roads, improving air connectivity, etc., through State Governments.  
1.8 Cane and Bamboo Technology Centre (CBTC) 
1.8.1 Cane and Bamboo Technology Centre (CBTC) is an ISO 9001:2008 certified 
organization and was established in the year 2000 as part of Cane and Bamboo Technological 
Upgradation and Networking Programme of United Nations Development Programme (UNDP),  
where the Department of Science & Technology (DST), Govt. of India and United Nations 
Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO) were the main stakeholder s. CBTC has been 
registered as a Society on 21 st July, 2004 with Secretary NEC as Chairman. It works  for the 
development of the cane and bamboo sector in the country especially in the NE Region.  

5 
 
1.9 North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Limited 
(NEHHDC) 
1.9.1 NEHHDC was set up in 1977 at Shillong, Meghalaya, with the mandate of promotion and 
development of handicrafts and handlooms sectors in the NER of the country.  NEHHDC plays a 
key role in the development and promotion of the Handicrafts and Handlooms sectors of the 
North Eastern Region. NEHHDC has been providing Marketing s upport through procurement of 
Handicrafts and Handlooms products directly from the Artisans, Weavers, Self -Help Groups at 
clusters and selling the same through its Sales Emporia located at Shillong, Guwahati, Kolkata, 
New Delhi and Gujarat and through Sale s Promotion Offices located at Chennai and Bangalore 
and also through conduct of various exhibition and marketing events all over the country. 
1.10 North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC)  
1.10.1 North Eastern Regional Agricultu ral Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC) Limited was 
incorporated in the year 1982 as a Government of India Enterprise under the administrative 
control of the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER).  
1.10.2 NERAMAC has been envisaged and functioning as a marketing organization dedicated to 
the agricultural sector of the North-eastern region and is lending a supporting hand to the farmers 
right from the fields and up to the markets to the end consumers for their produces. NERAMAC 
procures produces from the farmers at remunerative prices and maintains the supply chain for 
proper distribution and placing of produces in the markets throughout the country.

1.11 North East Rural Livelihood project (NERLP) 
1.11.1 North East Rural Livelihood Project (NERLP), a World Bank aided project was launched 
in 2012. It was implemented by Ministry of Development o f North Eastern Region (MDoNER)  
to improve rural livelihoods especially that of wo men, unemployed youth and the most 
disadvantaged people by building community based organizations and developing partnership 
with Government and other institutions for knowledge, skill, credit, insurance, extension and 
market. 
1.12 North Eastern Region Community Resource Management Project (NERCORMP) 
1.12.1 NEC supports Community Based Sustainable Livelihood Projects i.e., North Eastern 
Region Community Resource Management Project (NERCORMP), which was launched in May, 
1999. 
1.13 North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Limited (NEDFi) 
1.13.1 The North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Limited (NEDFi) was incorporated 
as a public limited company under the Companies Act, 1956, in 1995. It is notified as a Public 
Financial Institution under Section 4A of the said Act and was registered as an NBFC [Non -

6 
 
Depository-Systemically Important (ND -SI)] in 2002 with RBI. The core business of the 
Corporation comprises of lending activities (fund based) and fee based activities. The lending 
activities are undert aken through the project finance schemes and microfinance schemes. The 
Corporation also provides fee based Advisory & Consultancy Services. NEDFi plays an 
important role in facilitating setting up of new industrial and service sector projects in the North 
Eastern States. 
1.14 Science & Technology Intervention North Eastern Region (STINER) 
1.14.1 The new initiative for Science & Technology Intervention in North Eastern Region 
(STINER) was envisaged by M/o DoNER in close collaboration with the Office of Princ ipal 
Scientific adviser (PSA) to Govt. of India with an aim to improve the livelihood of vulnerable 
groups in a sustainable manner especially that of women, unemployed youth and the poor 
farmers and artisans of the North Eastern Region of the country. The Ministry‟s initiative in the 
form of STINER Project was officially transferred to North Eastern Council, Shillong along with 
a budget Provision of Rs. 40 crore (Rupees Forty crore) in June, 2016 for sanctioning funds to 
implement the project by CSIR-NEIST, Jorhat.

CHAPTER III 
NORTH EAST COUNCIL (NEC) AND RELATED SCHEMES 
3.1.1 The North Eastern Council which was set up as a Regional Advisory Body under the 
North Eastern Council Act, 1971 (Act No. 84 of 1971) was transformed into a Regional Planning 
Body by the 2002 amendment.  The relevant provision of the amended NEC Act is quoted 
below: - 
Section 4 of the original Act: 
 
“(a) for sub-sections (1) and (2), the following sub-sections shall be substituted, namely:- 
1. The Council shall function as a regional planning body for the North eastern area. 
2. While formulating the regional plans for the North eastern area, the Council shall give 
priority to schemes and projects, which will benefit two or more States: 
Provided that in case of Sikkim, the Council shall formulate specific projects and schemes for 
that State including the review of implementation of such project and schemes”. 
 
3.1.2 The Committee was informed that the above provision of the amended NEC Act 
mandated the North Eastern Council (NEC) to function as a “regional planning body for the 
North eastern area” with the tasks of formulating “regional plans for the North eastern area” that 
benefit two or more States. Given the smallness of majority of the State s in the region, in terms 
of landmass and population, it is advantageous for such States that regional plans are formulated 
in a coordinated manner for cohesive and integrated economic development for the region as a 
whole.

3.6 North East Special Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS)

3.6.2 The broad objective of NESIDS is to ensure focused development of North East Region 
by providing financial assistance for the projects of:

primary and secondary sectors.

India and State Governments of the NE Region. Under the scheme, only t hose projects would be 
taken up which are not covered under any scheme of Government of India or the State 
Government. The NLCPR saving s of  Rs. 402.47 crore has been carried forward to NESIDS. 
Since approval of NESIDS on 15.12.2017, projects worth Rs.1904. 94 crore have already been 
selected for funding out of which 57 projects worth Rs. 1799.28 crore have been sanctioned so

5.2 North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Limited 
(NEHHDC) 
5.2.1 The North Eastern Handicrafts and handlooms Development Corporation Ltd(NEHHDC) 
was established in M arch 1977 with the mandate of promotion and development of handicrafts 
and handlooms of the North Eastern Region and to provide the needed marketing support to the 
artisans and weavers of the Region. 
 
5.2.2 The Ministry informed that in order to achieve th ese broad objectives, the Corporation 
has been facing several challenges. The challenges faced by the Corporation are as under- 
 
(i) Lack of infrastructure/ Showroom/ Warehouse : NEHHDC has 11 small and bigger 
showrooms/ Emporium in the country located  in Shill ong, Ambari, Guwahati , 

41 
 
Kolkata, New Delhi, Bangalore, Chennai and Emporium at Kevadia, Gujarat. But in 
order to penetrate the market, NEHHDC requires a wider retail sales base in the form 
of showrooms in high footfall areas with warehouse facilities 
(ii) Inadequate profit margin out of promotional events : At times the administrative 
expenses incurred in organizing/ participating in such promotional events exceeds the 
profit earned out of such events; 
(iii)Inadequate Publicity and Branding :   In order to make the wide  range of North East 
handicrafts and handlooms products popular, adequate publicity and branding is 
required. NEHHDC has not been able to undertake these activities on a larger way 
which results in sluggish turnover. 
(iv) High Cost of sourcing and procurement:   The artisans and weavers are located in far 
flung interior land locked areas leading to higher cost of souring and procurement. 
This results in high Selling price leading to stiff competition in the market. 
(v) Transportation bottlenecks : As Northeast is main ly in the hilly terrain the 
transportation costs of products sourced and procured from the artisans/weavers is 
high, leading to comparatively high Selling price of the products.  
(vi) Constraints of quality Raw material : The main raw materials for the handicraf ts and 
handlooms sectors in the region are bamboo, cane, sitalpati, jute, kauna, water 
hyacinth, cotton and silk yarns and other natural fibbers. The cane reserves are fast 
dwindling and are expensive.  Cotton   yarns in the region come from the mills from  
Maharashtra, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu. Dyes and chemicals manufacturers are also 
not available in the North Eastern Region. This has resulted in making the handlooms 
and handicraft products expensive and NEHHDC faces challenges for marketing 
North East handlooms and handicrafts products. 
(vii) Lack of Mass production:  NEHHDC receives bulk orders for certain products. The 
artisans and weavers produce a large variety of products but there has always been a 
constraint in mass production which hampers bulk supply orders. 
(viii) Lack of design Intervention/ Standardization/Packaging : NEHHDC organises 
design workshops and projects for product development, Awareness for 
standardization of products and packaging at cluster level for handlooms and 
handicrafts products. Due to fund constraints NEHHDC is unable to provide adequate 
Training and conduct Awareness Programme.

handloom and handicraft products from the artisans and weavers of the region and to 
provide them the needed marketing platform.  
(ii) The Ministry has also been supporting by providing funds for implementation of 
workshops, seminars and   Training programmes for the benefits of the artisans and 
weavers of the region. 
(iii)The ministry has been supporting NEHHDC in publicity and ma rketing of the handloom 
and handicraft products by   conducting Exhibitions/Expos/ fairs etc in the country.

funds to participate in overseas exhibitions and business meets. 
(v) In order to boost the market of indigenous products the Ministry provides funds for

(GI).

6.1 Vision 2020 
 
6.1.1 The Committee was apprised about the details of the Vision 2020 Document which 
provides an overarching framework for the development of the North Eastern Region to bring  it 
at par with other developed regions under which different Ministries, including Ministry of 
Development of North Eastern Region have undertaken various initiatives. 
 
6.1.2 The Ministry informed that strengthening infrastructure and connectivity is a ma jor thrust

(SARDP-NE), in which a total of 3029 Km (2101 km SARD -NE P hase - A and 928 Km 
Arunachal Pradesh Packages) have been constructed up to 2018 -19 with expenditure of Rs.

entire North East region were converted in to Broad Gauge by 2017. In the last three years

Rail Network in the last five years . Agartala -Akhaura Rail -Link will connect the existing

operational airports in the region providing connectivity to the North Eastern States with the help

6.7 Waterways 
 
6.7.1 The Ministry stated that the Government of India through various policies  has taken a big 
leap in developing Inland Waterways (IWs) in North Eastern Region. There are 20 National 
Waterways (NWs) in North Eastern Region (NER) including River Brahmaputra (NW -2) and 
River Barak (NW -16).  River Brahmaputra was declared as NW in the  year 1988, while River 
Barak and other 18 rivers of NER were declared as NWs in April 2016 vide National Waterways 
Act, 2016. 
6.7.2 During the Committee's meeting held on 19th Februray 2020, the Committee sought to 
know the s tatus of Water ways in Brahmap utra  river from Kolkata/Chittagong ports. The 
Ministry replied that  Inland Water Transit and Trade Protocol exists between India and 
Bangladesh under which the two Governments agreed to make mutually beneficial arrangement 
for the use of their waterways f or trade and for transit cargo from Indian mainland (Kolkata) to 
NE region. Under the Indo -Bangladesh inland water transit and trade protocol routes valid for 7 
years, the   development of fairway in Ashuganj -Zakiganj of River Kushiyara in Bangladesh for 
connecting NW16 (River Barak)and Sirajganj-Daikhowa of River Jamuna in Bangladesh stretch, 
the dredging and allied work is under progress and both the routes from Kolkata/Haldia Ports to 
NER via Brahmaputra and via Barak through Bangladesh would be operational by 2020.  
6.7.3 The Ministry further stated that the Ministry of Shipping, Govt. of India has signed an 
agreement with Govt. of Bangladesh regarding use of Chattogram (Chittagong) and Mongla 
Ports in Bangladesh for movement of goods to and from India w hich will bring revolutionary 
cost-cutting in  shipments to and from the North Eastern States. In this regard, a Standard 
Operating Procedure (SOP) wherein Bangladesh allowed the use of its Chattogram and Mongla 
Ports for movement of goods to and from India  through its territory was signed on 05.10.2019. 
The agreement and SOP permit the movement of goods in Bangladesh through waterways, rail, 
road or multi-modal transport. Eight (08) routes are provided under the Agreement which would 
enable access of North East Region (NER) via Bangladesh. The routes are: 
(i) Chattogram/Mongla Port to Agartala (Tripura) via Akhura 
(ii) Chattogram/Mongla Port to Dawki (Meghalaya) via Tamabil 
(iii)Chattogram/Mongla Port to Sutarkandi (Assam) via Sheola 
(iv) Chattogram/Mongla Port to Srimantpur (Tripura) via Bibirbazar (and vice versa)  
6.7.4 The Committee was informed that the conclusion of the SOP on Chattogram and  Mongla 
ports  would reduce distance, time and logistic cost for transport of goods. Three landlocked 
states of India viz. Assam, Me ghalaya and Tripura will get access to open sea trade routes from 
Chattogram and Mongla ports via Indian ports. Cargo transportation through IBP waterway route 
from Kolkata/Haldia to North East is limited to 2000 ton vessels. Now, larger ships carrying 
cargo destined for North East can call at Chattogram and Mongla ports thereby increasing trade 
volumes and reducing logistic costs.

6.9 Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport (KMTT) Project 
 
6.9.1 The Committee was informed that in accordance with the Act East Policy of the 
Government, the Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport (KMTT) Project was conceptualized by 
the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) to provide an alternative connectivity to Mizoram with 
Haldia/Kolkata ports through the Kaladan River in Myanmar. Kaladan Multi Mo dal Transit 
Transport Project (KMMTTP) will create a multi -modal transport corridor for shipment of cargo 
from the eastern ports of India to Myanmar as well as North -Eastern part of India via Myanmar. 
The project includes a waterway component of 158 km on Kaladan river from Sittwe to Paletwa 
in Myanmar and a road component of 109 km from Paletwa to Zorinpui on the India -Myanmar 
border in Mizoram State. A Framework Agreement between GOI and Myanmar in this was 
signed in 2008. 
6.9.2 The Ministry informed that  Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) is the Project 
Development Consultant for the waterways component of the project and M/s. Ircon 
Infrastructure Services Ltd. is Project Management Consultant for the road component. Work of 
construction of waterw ays component was awarded to M/s. Essar Project (India) Limited in 
2010. The Estimated cost of the project is Rs. 2904.04 crore (Rs 982.99 crore for waterways and 
Rs 1921.05 crore for road component) as approved by the Cabinet in October 2015. Work for 
construction of road has been awarded to M/s. EPI -C&C JV in March 2017. Expenditure 
incurred so far on Kaladan project is Rs. 639.93 crore (Rs. 519.88 crore towards waterways 
component and Rs. 120.05 crore towards road component).The originally approved works  under 
waterways have been completed in May 2017 and a port operator has been appointed for 
operation and maintenance of the waterways component on 1 February 2020.Work for 
construction of road was to take 3 years to complete. 
6.9.3 The Ministry further informed that the physical progress of road construction work is not 
at all satisfactory. Acute financial crisis faced by JV partner M/s. C&C, delayed action by JV 
partner M/s. EPIL, non -availability of access to the project site from India -Myanmar border in  
Mizoram and adverse security situation in the project area are the main reasons for delays in the 
project. 
6.9.4 The Committee takes note of the progress of the Kaladan Multimodal Transit 
Transport (KMTT) project that is being piloted and funded by the Mi nistry of External 
Affairs. The Committee is displeased to note that the physical progress of road construction 
work is not at all satisfactory. The Committee understands that this project is an integral 
part of the Act East Policy of India and  will contribute to the economic development in the 

56 
 
whole Region. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the reasons for delay must be 
addressed soon and the project should be closely monitored. The Committee further 
recommends that the progress should be reviewed regularly to complete the project at the 
earliest so that the connectivity woes of the North Eastern Region are resolved.

& bridges, power, agriculture, art and culture. The Committee, therefore, strongly 
recommends that M/o DoNER must take a lead in coordinating with t he State Government 
of Assam and BTC for formulating infrastructure projects so that all proposals of BTC are 
submitted in time for final approval. The Committee further recommends that the 
Ministry must have appropriate monitoring mechanism in place to ensure speedy execution 
of projects undertaken including release of funds and furnishing of UCs so that projects 
are completed without any time and cost overrun. 
(Para 4.1.11) 
Karbi Anglong Autonomous Territorial Council (KAATC) 
 The Committee hopes that the  amount of Rs. 57.00 crore allocated under BE 2020 -
21 will be utilized for meeting the liabilities towards ongoing projects. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry must expedite its efforts to meet the entire committed 
liability and undertake new proje cts for the development of Karbi Anglong Autonomous 
Territorial Council (KAATC). 
(Para  4.2.5) 
Dima Hasao Autonomous Territorial Council (DHATC) 
The Committee observes that Dima Hasao Territorial Council (DHATC) is not able to 
absorb the allocated fund and  only 4.25 % funds have been utilized in the year 2019 -20. 
The Committee recommends that DHATC must expedite the utilization process by 
executing all undertaken projects in a time bound manner. 
(Para 4.3.8) 
 
Social and Infrastructure Development Fund (SIDF) 
The Committee notes the reasons for delay in utilisation/slow progress of projects but takes 
strong exception to the low utilisation of allocation i.e. 44.66 % in 2018 -19 and 26.05 % 
upto 31.01.2020 in 2019-20 under the Social and Infrastructure Development Fund (SIDF). 
Therefore, the Committee recommends that efforts need to be made by the Ministry of

recommends that M/o DoNER must take a lead in coordinating with the State 
Governments for formulating infrastructure projects so that all project proposals are 
submitted in time for final approval and the entire allocation is utilized. 
(Para 4.4.9) 
 
 North East Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC) 
The Committee e xpresses the urgent need for the revival of the North East Regional 
Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC) so that the Corporation is capable of 
carrying out its core activities i.e. creation of a world class infrastructure for processing, 
value-addition and marketing of agricultural and allied goods in the NER. Since the revival

must expedite the process of revival plan of NERAMAC.

North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Limited (NEHHDC) 
 The Committee notes that Prag Jyoti textile Park, Darrang, Assam has been

projects in the North Eastern Region.

The Committee takes note of the progress of the Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport

Committee is displeased to note that the physical progress of road construction work is not 
at all satisfactory. The Committee understands that this project is an integral part of the 
Act East Policy of India and  will contribute to the economic development in the whole  
Region. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the reasons for delay must be 
addressed soon and the project should be closely monitored. The Committee further 
recommends that the progress should be reviewed regularly to complete the project at the 
earliest so that the connectivity woes of the North Eastern Region are resolved.  
(Para 6.9.4)

 The Committee takes note of the projects related to tourism promotion that are 
being implemented under the NESIDS Scheme and hopes that with concerted eff orts to 
implement these projects, niche and high end tourism will be developed in the NER.  The

explored in an organized manner by NE States. The Committee therefore recommends that 
Ministry must take steps to explore educational tourism with an aim to make education 
more accessible and providing qualified Human Resource for Tourism and Allied services 
for North Eastern Region States. This will help in making North East India a preferred 
destination for visitors and tourists and generate a pool of qualified youth for handling 
tourism activities there. 
(Para 6.10.7) 
 
North East Industrial Development Scheme(NEIDS) 
 
 
The Committee is displeased to note that no fund has been sanct ioned under NEIDS and 
respective State Level Committees of NEIDS have not recommended release of funds to

to implement the programme/action plan to boost integrated and b alanced 
industrialization in NER and to promote employment in the North East States. 
(Para 6.11.4)

the physical progress of road construction work of Kaladan Multi Modal Transit
