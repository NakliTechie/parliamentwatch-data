1.3 INTRODUCTION OF EXPENDITURE MANAGEMENT
Recommendation
1.3.1 The Committee appreciates introduction of expenditure management and feedback
mechanism in the Ministry to monitor the projects undertaken. The Committee recommends that the
Ministry should further enhance this mechanism to check leakages. The Committee feels that the
officials of the Ministry should be provided training on internal audit, information system audit
and fraud examination in acclaimed institutions. While giving enhanced financial delegation to
field organizations, efforts should also be made to ensure that there are no leakages in the
expenditure and delays in implementation. (Para 2.21.8 ibid)
Action Taken
1.3.2 The recommendation of the Committee is to have a functional and efficient internal audit

6
regime in the Ministry and to build upon the initiatives already taken in this area. There has been
a concerted effort for capacity building and skill up-gradation in this area. Additional Secretary and
FA (Home) has given in principle approval for taking professional certifications in the area of
internal audit and fraud investigations. Even the expenditure incurred in taking these courses is
being reimbursed. Training programs are being organized to up-grade skills in the area of risk based
auditing, report writing and Computer Assisted Auditing Techniques (CAATs). As a step towards
decentralization, enhanced powers are being delegated to field offices. Internal audit universe has
been planned to ensure coverage to a large number of these field offices to ensure that financial
procedures are being followed and there are no leakages in the system.
1.3.3 Also, to further strengthen and modernize the internal auditing systems in the Ministry
training programs would be organized and attended both at the national as well as international
levels. The skill upgradation of officers/officials at the cutting edge level would be the primary
focus of the organization.
1.4 LEFT WING EXTREMISM
Recommendation
1.4.1 The Committee observes that Left Wing Extremism (LWE) has emerged as the biggest threat
to the internal security of our country. Though, the casualities have come down in 2011 the figure
was showing upward trend till 2010. The Committee notes that the Government has approved
extension of Security Related Expenditure (SRE) Scheme for LWE-affected States for another 5
years beyond 31
st March, 2011. The Government should regularly watch implementation of the
scheme. (Para 3.2.15 ibid)
Action Taken
1.4.2 ‘Police’ and ‘Public Order’ being State subjects, action with respect to maintenance of law
and order lies primarily in the domain of the State Governments concerned, who deal with the
various issues related to Left Wing Extremist (LWE) activities in the States. The Central
Government monitors the situation closely and supplements and coordinates their efforts in several
ways, inter-alia, including reimbursement of expenditure incurred by the State Governments on
Anti-naxal Operations under the SRE Scheme.
1.4.3 Considering the usefulness of the SRE Scheme, the Central Government has approved
extension of the Scheme for a further period of five years beyond 31.3.2011. Also, the Scheme
has now been extended to 20 more LWE affected districts, taking the total coverage to 103
districts.
1.4.4 The SRE Scheme, being a reimbursement scheme, the expenditure is first incurred by the
State Governments and thereafter reimbursed by the Central Government after conducting audit in
the respective States. However, 25% advance of the approved Annual Work Plan outlay is provided
to the State Governments concerned in the beginning of the financial year for smooth
implementation of the Scheme.
1.5 SOCIAL SCHEMES OF NAXAL AFFECTED AREAS
Recommendation
1.5.1 The Committee is aware of the fact that the naxal affected regions are underdeveloped areas
with less infrastructure facilities. The Committee is of considered view that schemes such as
Integrated Action Plan (IAP) for Selected Tribal and Backward Districts for accelerated

7
development and Security Related Expenditure (SRE) Scheme should be implemented at a fast pace
in the naxal affected areas. The Committees desires that the Government should make efforts for
implementing the flagship programmes in a proper manner to ensure that the targeted groups are
benefited. The Committee believes that providing employment opportunities to disgruntled youths,
good governance and proper implementation of major development schemes in the areas dominated
by the Maoists, can check the growth of Left Wing Extremism (LWE). Development is the panacea
for the ills of LWE areas. All actions should be directed towards development. The Government,
therefore, should work in tandem with NGOs, local bodies, peoples representatives to spread
awareness among the masses against the outdated ideology of Maoists which is undemocratic and
which believes that power comes through barrel of the gun. (Para 3.2.16 ibid)
Action Taken
1.5.2 The Government’s approach is to deal with Left Wing Extremism (LWE) in a holistic
manner, in the areas of security, development, enforcing rights of local communities, good
governance and public perception management. However, ‘Police’ and ‘Public Order’ being State
subjects, action with regard to maintenance of law and order lies primarily in the domain of the
State Governments concerned. The Central Government monitors the situation closely and
supplements the efforts of the State Governments over a wide range of schemes on both, security
and development fronts. The underlying philosophy is to enhance the capacity of the State
Governments to tackle the LWE menace in a concerted manner.
1.5.3 The Planning Commission is implementing the Integrated Action Plan (IAP) in 82 Selected
Tribal and Backward Districts, inter alia , including 71 LWE affected districts, for accelerated
development of the affected areas. The nature of works taken up under the IAP includes public
infrastructure and services which create a perceptible impact on the ground and among the local
communities. An amount of Rs.3840 crore has been released for financial years 2010-11 and 2011-
12 against which expenditure of Rs.2522.06 crore (65.68%) has been reported (up to 15.6.2012).
1.5.4 The Planning Commission, through a Management Information System (MIS), has been
monitoring the implementation of important development programmes/flagship schemes in the IAP
districts and also reviewing the progress through Video Conferences.
1.5.5 The Central Government has approved extension of the Security Related Expenditure (SRE)
Scheme for a further period of five years beyond 31.3.2011, to continue the support provided to
the LWE affected States by way of reimbursing Anti-Naxal Operations related expenditure incurred
in the covered 103 districts.
1.5.6 In order to wean away the potential youth from the path of militancy or Left Wing
Extremism, recruitment guidelines have been revised to permit 40% recruitment in Central Armed
Police Forces from the border areas and areas affected by militancy or Left Wing Extremism.
1.5.7 It is the belief of Government of India that through a combination of development, security
and Forest Rights related interventions, the LWE problem can be successfully tackled. However,
it is clear that the Maoists do not want root causes like underdevelopment addressed in a
meaningful manner since they resort to targeting school buildings, roads, railways, bridges, health
infrastructure, communication facilities etc in a major way. They wish to keep the population in
their areas of influence marginalized to perpetuate their outdated ideology.
1.6 STRENGTHENING SECURITY FORCES
Recommendation
1.6.1 The Government should also explore the channels/agencies such as NGOs/Social Activists

8
who can build pressure on the Maoists to eschew violence and come to the talking table and join
the mainstream. The Committee feels that the schemes implemented by the Government are not
yet yielding the desired results. Government may consider strengthening security forces of States
on the lines of greyhounds in Andhra Pradesh. Joint elite forces can also be an alternative.
(Para 3.2.17 ibid)
Action Taken
1.6.2 ‘Police’ and ‘Public Order’ are State subjects and action with respect to maintenance of law
and order primarily lies in the domain of the State Governments concerned, who deal with specific
issues related to Left Wing Extremism (LWE) activities in the States. The Central Government
closely monitors the situation and supplements and coordinates the efforts of the State
Governments over a wide range of schemes/issues .
1.6.3 The State Governments have from time to time appealed to the Left Wing Extremists,
especially the CPI (Maoist), the most violent group, to abjure violence and hold talks on any issue
of concern to them. But, no concrete results have emerged so far. The CPI (Maoist) Party believes
in overthrowing the present system of Parliamentary democracy through “Protracted People’s War.”
The Central Government would welcome talks between the concerned State Governments and the
Left Wing Extremists, provided the latter abjure violence and give up their so called armed struggle
against the Indian State.
1.6.4 The Government of India has urged the State Governments time and again to raise Special
Forces on the pattern of Greyhounds in Andhra Pradesh, to deal with the LWE menace. Most of
the LWE affected States have taken action and raised such forces to effectively deal with the LWE
problem.
1.7 MODERNISATION OF CENTRAL AND STATE FORCES
Recommendation
1.7.1 The Committee views that although the multi-pronged development strategy is required to
check spread of Left Wing Extremism, the Government should also provide required training to the
Central Police Forces as well as state police forces and modernize them. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the police personnel should be trained properly before sending them to the naxal
affected areas. The Committee also recommends that these personnel should be equipped with all
necessary modern weapons and gadgets. (Para 3.2.18 ibid)
Action Taken
1.7.2 The Ministry of Home Affairs has been implementing the Scheme for Modernisation of State
Police Forces (MPF Scheme) which is aimed at modernizing the State Police Forces to meet the
challenges to internal security in the form of terrorism and naxalism, law and order problems etc.
1.7.3 ‘Police’ being a State subject as per the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution of India, it
is the responsibility of the concerned State Governments to provide adequate training to the State
Police personnel in keeping with the changing needs. However, the Ministry of Home Affairs has
extended financial assistance to the State Governments under the MPF Scheme, inter-alia , to
upgrade their training infrastructure facilities such as for setting up of police training schools,
police training academies, district training centres etc., setting up of firing ranges, for acquisition
of police training aids/equipment such as grenade simulator, firing simulators, basic infrastructure
required for training schools such as lecture halls, classrooms, projectors etc.

9
1.7.4 Under the Scheme, funds have also been provided to States for acquiring modern arms/
weaponry such as Glock pistols, AK 47 rifles, Insas Rifles, 9 mm pistols, Under Barrel Grenade
Launchers etc.
1.7.5 ‘Police’ and ‘Public Order’ being State subjects, action with respect to maintenance of law
and order lies primarily in the domain of the State Governments concerned, who deal with the
various issues related to Left Wing Extremist (LWE) activities in the States. The Central
Government monitors the situation closely and supplements and coordinates their efforts in several
ways, inter-alia, including providing Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs) to assist the State
Police Forces to deal with the LWE problem. The Central Government also provides assistance in
training of State Police Forces through the Ministry of Defence, Central Police Organisations and
Bureau of Police Research and Development.
1.7.6 The CAPF battalions, prior to their induction in the LWE affected States, undergo Counter
Insurgency/pre-induction training at two stages. At the first stage, they undergo pre-induction
training of four weeks with the Army Infantry units and thereafter familiarization/ orientation
training of 15 days under the respective State Police arrangements.
1.7.7 Besides, in order to strengthen the training inputs of the State Police Forces, Government
has approved setting up of 21 Counter Insurgency and Anti-Terrorist (CIAT) Schools in the States
affected by insurgency and terrorism including Left Wing Extremism. Fifteen such CIAT Schools
have been established in the LWE affected States of Bihar (3), Chhattisgarh (4), Jharkhand (4),
Odisha (3) and West Bengal (1).
Further Observation
1.7.8 The Committee observes that the frequent casualties of the Central Police Forces
while combating the menace of Naxalism in the LWE affected areas reveal the need for
extensive training of forces and equipping them with latest weapons and gadgets before
sending them for actual field operation. The remaining six CIAT Schools may be set up on
priority basis. Adherence to Standard Operating Procedures must be impressed invariably
and non-observance of the same should be viewed seriously.
1.8 COUNTERING NORTH EAST INSURGENCY AND MILITANCY
Recommendation
1.8.1 The Committee notes that the Government of India has initiated talks with some erstwhile
underground insurgent groups which could help establish peace in the region. The Committee also
takes note of the fact that the Government of India has initiated talks with some friendly
neighbouring countries to check these insurgent groups which had earlier become safe haven for
them. But the steps taken have not yet yielded the desired results. Though, there is a separate
Ministry for development of North East region, the insurgent activities are unabated. The
Committee feels that there needs to be a change in the strategy adopted to deal with North East
insurgency and militancy. Steps are required to be taken to bring the people into the mainstream
of the society. (Para 3.3.8 ibid)

and strategy of the Government to deal with insurgency and militancy in North-East region.

undertaken.

security.

The Committee is however given to understand that the frequent transfer/posting and posting of the
CRPF personnel to such areas where they are not able to communicate due to language problem
add to their woes and adversely affect their working proficiency. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that a proper policy should be formulated on transfer/posting of the CRPF . The
Committee further recommends that in order to give adequate relief to the personnel of all Central
Police Forces a posting/transfer policy should be reflected on ground. The Committee is of the view

20
that personnel working in CRP should be stationed in a particular place for, at least, three years
without disturbance unless they are hard-pressed to go to some other area because moving from one
place to other in a short span of time causes difficulties not only to them but also to their families.
The Committee is also of the view that these personnel should be posted to the extent possible in
the areas where they know the local language and culture so that there is no communication gap
between the locals and the CRPF personnel. (Para 4.1.20 ibid)
Action Taken
1.22.2 In order to give adequate rest and relief to the CRPF personnel, a proper posting/ transfer
policy has been laid out which ensures the movement of Force personnel between the hard and
static/soft postings. The policy has been drawn keeping in mind the family obligations of the
jawans in addition to the Force requirements. Since CRPF is not a border guarding force, there
is no fixed location/point of duty, but the same changes as per the internal security situation in the
various parts of the country as well as the request made by various State Governments/UT
administrations as per developing security scenarios.
1.22.3 In order to provide adequate rest and recuperation to the CRPF personnel it has recently
been decided that, as far as possible, the training companies shall not be deployed for active duties
as the main motive of keeping one Coy out of every 7 Coys in a Battalion as a training Coy was
to give proper rest and recuperation to the jawans on rotation basis in addition to honing their skills
in various fields.
1.22.4 Besides, the jawans in CRPF and other CAPFs are eligible for static posting subject to
vacancy and administrative convenience as per the following guidelines:–
(i) CRPF formations/institutions/units have been categorized as “Peace” and “Field”
stations for regulating the transfer of the Force personnel.
(ii) After completion of 8 years field service in case of male; and 4 years field service in
case of female, after basic training, the force personnel are eligible for first peace
station/static posting. Subsequent peace station/static posting can be/is done after
completion of a total of 8 years field service from the completion of last static posting.
(iii) For posting to a peace station/static location, preference is given to those having
longest service in the field Battalions.
(iv) Normal stay of posting in a Group Centre/static institution/peace station is 4 years.
This may be extended upto 5 years only in case of training staff posted in training
institution as a special case.
(v) After deployment of the Force personnel in the hard areas, like Left Wing Extremism
affected areas, they are generally posted to static postings. Such jawans are also asked
to give their preference of posting on leaving the LWE areas out of the five options,
namely Rapid Action Force/ Duty Battalions/ Group Centre/ Training Institution/
Deputation.
(vi) Personnel having good record of service and free from disciplinary and vigilance angle,
may be posted to near their home towns, 2 years before attaining the age of
superannuation, subject to availability of vacancies.
(vii) In order to maintain continuity at a place, a maximum of 25% of the strength is
moved every year from one operational theatre to another within a sector.
1.22.5 In order to keep the Force personnel in their cultural milieu as also for effective and better
utilization, a maximum of 50% of the total personnel posted in a place are from the local area. In

21
case of other Forces like BSF, as far as possible, recruit Constables on completion of their basic
training are posted to their Frontiers located near their home states, depending upon the vacancy
position.
1.22.6 Therefore, all efforts are made to help the Force personnel live with their families
intermittently during the static postings /peace stations. The tenure of such peace postings has been
earmarked as 4 years after due consultation with the Force personnel as also keeping in mind the
general good of the maximum number of Force personnel in CRPF.

1.30 SECURITY IN DIPLOMATIC AREAS
Recommendation
1.30.1 The Committee takes serious view of the bomb blast in an Israeli Embassy car in a high-
security area which occurred in Delhi using sticky bomb. The Committee recommends that proper
security arrangements should be made by Delhi Police in Diplomatic areas so that this kind of
incident does not happen in future. (Para 4.2.23 ibid)
Action Taken
1.30.2 Security arrangements for diplomatic missions and diplomats of foreign countries located in
India are reviewed periodically on the basis of inputs given by the Central Security Agencies and
appropriate advisories issued to Delhi Police/State Police. Based on the inputs given by the Central
Security Agencies, Delhi Police/State Police take suitable measure to strengthen security. Further,
for the security arrangement of Diplomatic Mission located in Delhi, Government has constituted
Diplomatic Security Force (DSF). A sizable number of Police personnel/Central Armed Police
Forces personnel have been deployed under the operational control of DSF on static guard duties
at various Embassies and diplomatic premises.
1.30.3 Delhi Police has taken a large number of anti-terrorist measures in the city to combat
terrorism. These measures can be grouped under the following heads:
1. Armed Response Teams
2. Terrorist related intelligence collection
3. Security audit of Vital Installations
4. Eyes and Ears scheme and Tenant verification

25
5. Checking of Public Places
6. Installation of CCTV System in Markets and Border Check Posts
Their details are as follows:–
Armed Response Teams:
1.30.4 Delhi Police has deployed more than 200 Armed Response Teams across the city. A large
number of them are mobile, whereas some of the teams are on foot to cover a smaller area or
a specific location. Besides we have also (630+139) PCR vans/motorcycles across Delhi. These
Armed Response Teams and PCR Vans/Motor Cycles, have been deployed to deter any terrorist
attack as well as respond suitably. The details of the armed response teams are as follows:–
(i) Quick Reaction Teams  include Cobra and Vajra teams. They are all in vehicles, round
the clock, armed with semi-automatic weapons, wireless sets and protective gears.
Each QRT is headed by an officer or a head Constable, ready to respond to any armed
attack.
(ii) Mobile Striking Parties : Mobile striking parties deployed at strategic locations is
headed by an officer/Head Constable with 1-2 Constables. Officer/HC is equipped with
small weapon and Constable with INSAS/AK-47 rifle and suitable wireless
communication. Each mobile party patrols their assigned area in two shifts from early
morning to late evening depending upon the operational requirement of the area.
(iii) Foot/Static Armed Parties: Foot/Static armed parties, each consisting of 2-4 HC/Cts
armed with SAF/INSAS rifles/pistols/revolvers have been deployed at sensitive
locations like entry/exit of Metro Stations, Five Stars Hotels, Schools etc. They are
on foot and cover the specific areas given to them to counter any terrorist attack. The
timings of deployment are as per the operational requirement of the location.
(iv) CP’s Reserves: CP’s Reserves, with armed component, have been positioned round
the clock at several locations in Delhi. They will be rushed to the spot for assisting
the local police in forming cordons and also respond to the situation, as required by
circumstances.
(v) Mobile Patrol Vans (MPVs): Keeping in view the vulnerability of certain targets
within their beat areas, the bases of some of the MPVs in all the PCR zones have
been re-located so that these vans are located nearest to the vulnerable target area
such as a market place, metro station, hospital, etc. They are also in a position of
operational readiness to respond to exigencies. The checking/patrolling by MPVs is
also being coordinated regularly by the CPCR (Central Police Control Room) with
specific instructions to vulnerable target areas, such as schools, colleges, metro
stations, temples, mosques etc.
(vi) PCR Vans and PCR Motor Cycles: There are 630 bases of PCR Vans and 139
Motor Cycle PCR bases in Delhi. Besides responding to any incident in their respective
beats, they will also rush to any place of terrorist attack and respond according to
the requirement of the situation. The response time of PCR van to reach any place
of occurrence in urban areas is about 10 minutes and rural areas about 20 minutes.
(vii) Emergency Response Vehicles (ERVs): ERVs have been provided one each in 155
police stations of Delhi. They are manned by one upper subordinate and 3 other ranks
and equipped with at least two long range weapons and small arms and wireless sets.
They act as the first responder to all crime and major law and order calls. They are

26
deployed in two shifts from 0800 hrs to 2400 hrs. midnight. Inspector Anti Terrorist
Officer (ATO) also spend at least two to three hours in a day in ERVs to supervise
the anti terrorist tasks allotted to him in his jurisdiction.
(viii) Special Weapon and Tactics Teams (SWAT): The Delhi Police has set up a SWAT
team consisting of 3 officers and 70 commandos well equipped to respond to any
armed attack with a capacity to neutralize armed terrorists attack. SWAT teams has
been stationed at Special Cell Lodhi Colony, New Delhi. The team is well armed with
semi-automatic weapons and protective gears. The SWAT teams have been subjected
to specialized training in counter terrorism at NSG , Manesar. SWAT teams have also
been provided with three Light Armoured T roop Carriers (LATC) for operational
requirements. The strength of the SWAT team is being increased to 100.
Terrorist related Intelligence collection :
1.30.5 Entire system of terrorist related intelligence collection system has been reviewed. The
details are as follows:–
(i) Human Intelligence Collection: The entire system for collection of terrorist related
intelligence has been revamped. The Special Cell is a dedicated unit for anti-terrorist
operations. The officers/staff of this Cell functions primarily on the basis of
intelligence gathered either by them or other agencies. Within the Special Cell
additional ten teams of officers have been constituted and deployed in various sensitive
areas of Delhi to collect terrorist related information. They are not assigned any other
job except collection of intelligence. Analysis teams of special cell, analyse the
intelligence, pursues it and mount operation at appropriate time.
(ii) T echnical Intelligence:  System for technical intelligence collection is also under
upgradation.
(iii) Revamping of Beat Patrolling system : Beat patrolling system has been revamped
and oriented towards collection of criminal/terrorist related intelligence, implementation
of anti-terrorist measures etc. A revised Standing Order No.331/2007 has been issued
to redefine the duties of Beat Officers.
(iv) Inspector (Anti terrorist operations) and Intelligence Collection by Police
Station Staff : One of the Inspectors posted in every police station in Delhi has been
designated as Inspector (Anti terrorist operations) and assigned the task of
implementation of anti terrorist measures including intelligence collection, in police
station area. Besides, special responsibility has been imposed upon the local police
towards collection of intelligence relating to terrorists by closely studying their modus
operandi in past cases, and interaction with Community Leaders, Residential Welfare
Associations, Market Associations, Hotel/Guest House workers/employees etc. A
separate S.O. No.78/08 has been issued in this regard.
Security Audit of Vital Installations
1.30.6 475 important and sensitive establishments including Iconic Monuments, Government
buildings, Pvt. Buildings, Malls, Cinema Halls, Hotels/Guest Houses, Markets and other public
places have been identified as Vital Installations in Delhi. Most of the owner/Estate Managers have
been issued the security guidelines/advisories suggesting them to take specific security measures to
prevent any terrorist attack in their establishments. Meetings are also held with them individually
or with associations to convince them to implement security measures. Their security status is

27
being monitored and efforts are being continually made to convince them to comply with the
guidelines and make their establishments more secure. The Vital Installations have been classified
in five colour codes i.e. green, purple, yellow, orange and red to define the satisfaction level of
various security parameters with respect to perimeter security, access control, Anti-Sabotage check
and parking control etc. The security status of all the 464 vital installations is reviewed on regular
basis by Delhi Police. Market/Traders Associations are also being persuaded to install CCTVs in
front of their shops.
Eyes and ears scheme and Tenant verification:
(i) Eyes and ears scheme : Delhi Police has launched a scheme captioned ‘Public As
Eyes and Ears of the Police’ with special emphasis to sensitize all the stake-holders
such as Rehri Patriwalas, Chowkidars, guards, vendors, parking attendants TSR
drivers, residents/traders welfare associations etc. to keep a watch and look for
suspicious persons and share the information with local police immediately. Regular
meetings are held by all the officers to sensitize and motivate them. The scheme is
catching up with the public and they are coming forward to help the police. 87
criminal cases in the year 2011 were worked out with the help of stake holders of

to recognize their help in solving crime in Delhi.
(ii) T enant Verification:  In order to detect any terrorist or any person with a dubious

1.30.7 All kinds of public places in Delhi are being checked regularly. The details are as follows:–
(i) Checking of Cyber Café:  Division Officers and beat officers of local police and
selected teams of Special Cell regularly check the Cyber Cafes all over Delhi to ensure
that Cyber Café owners maintain a record of proof of identity carrying the
photographs and addresses of Cyber Café users. This record will be useful in tracing

(ii) Checking of Guest Houses and hotels:  Officers of Local Police and Special Cell
regularly check the hotels and Guest Houses all over Delhi to ensure that they maintain

(iii) C hecking hideouts, Cinema Halls, Airport, Railway Stations, ISBTs, Taxi stands,
Chemical shops, Public offices, Gurudwaras, Masjids, Mandirs and STD booths:

announcements over the public address system advising public to be on the look out

(iv) Checking of vehicles and buses: Local Police checks buses and other vehicles
regularly by placing barricades on the road at selected places. DTC Depot Managers

28
and private bus operators are also briefed about precautions to be taken by them and

(vi) Second hand Car/Scooter/Motorcycles dealers: Local Police keeps a liaison with

persons buying them. They have also been instructed to inform local police, in case
of any suspicious person or transaction.
(vii) Wir eless Integrated Public Address System: Delhi Police has installed a centrally
operated Wireless Integrated Public Address System at 31 markets and 11 Metro
Stations in Delhi. General instructions are issued to the public from the central
location at CPCR regarding security precautions and alert them from time to time.

3.1 FAKE INDIAN CURRENCY NOTES
Recommendation
3.1.1 The Committee takes serious view of the fact that the country is flooded with fake currency
notes and large amount of Fake Indian Currency Notes (FICN) are being seized. The Committee
is concerned to note that despite the fact that the Ministry is working in tandem with various other
government agencies to thwart the circulation of FICNs, the circulation of FICN continues
unabated. The Committee recommends that all porous borders should be plugged to prevent
smuggling of FICNs. (Para 3.5.3 of 161
st Report )
3.1.2 The Committee understands that all border checkposts and major international airports
should be modernized to check the smugglers of FICNs. The Committee in this connection,
recommends that the Reserve Bank of India should constantly upgrade security features of Indian
currency and the people should be made aware on these security features to enable them to easily
distinguish fake from real notes. The Committee recommends that note sorting machines should be
installed at all bank branches to detect fake currency notes as soon as they enter the banking
channel. Since, Nepal and Bangladesh are main transit points for smuggling of FICNs into India,
the Committee feels that India should help train Nepalese and Bangladeshi customs officials in
identifying and keeping watch over FICNs. The Committee also recommends that India should
pursue these countries for the installation of hi-tech luggage scanners at their major airports and
land check posts so that the baggages of passengers are thoroughly checked for counterfeit
currency. (Para 3.5.4 ibid)
Action Taken
3.1.3 To address the multi-dimensional aspects of the FICN menace, several agencies such as the
RBI, the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Home Affairs, Security and Intelligence Agencies of the
Centre and States, Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) etc., are working in tandem to thwart the
illegal activities related to FICNs.
3.1.4 Further, a special FICN Coordination Group (FCORD) has been formed in MHA to share
the intelligence/information amongst the different security agencies of State/Centre to counter the
menace of circulation of Fake Currency within the country.
3.1.5 NIA has been empowered by the National Investigation Agency Act to investigate and
prosecute such offences. The Government has also constituted a Terror Funding and Fake
Currency Cell in National Investigation Agency (NIA) to focus on investigation of Terror Funding
and Fake Currency Cases. The security features in the High V alue currency notes are being
constantly upgraded. RBI has also strengthened the mechanism for detection of counterfeit notes
by the Banks.
3.1.6 Regarding training of Nepalese and Bangladeshi customs officials in identifying and keeping
watch over FICNs and installation of hi-tech luggage scanner at their major airports land check

44
posts so that the baggages of passengers are thoroughly checked for counterfeit currency, the said
concerns are also being captured and being dealt with in the HS level bilateral talks.
3.1.7 Border Security Force (BSF) is deployed on Indo-Bangladesh and Indo-Pakistan Borders. All
possible efforts are being made to check/contain the illegal activities, including smuggling of FICN,
across these borders which includes construction of fencing and floodlighting. The BSF also keeps
vigil along the borders by way of patrols, deployment of high tech equipments etc. In addition,
there are also bilateral institutional mechanism/forums, in which these issues are discussed and
addressed suitably with mutual cooperation.
3.1.8 Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB) has been deployed at Indo-Nepal Border as a Border Guarding
Force. Regular presence, round-the-clock patrolling, random checking and surveillance are carried
out by SSB to stop cross border criminal activities. Besides, State Police concerned is also keeping
a vigil. There are other institutional mechanism like Home Secretary level talks between two
countries, periodic Border District Coordination Committee (BDCC) meeting, real-time exchange of
information/intelligence etc. between the two countries to effectively deal with such issues.
Further Observation
3.1.9 The Committee is constrained to observe that despite various efforts made by the
Government, the problems of FICN still persists in the country. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that immediate steps are required for constitution of Terror Funding Fake
Currency Cell in National Investigation Agency (NIA). Efforts of Reserve Bank of India by
constantly upgrading security features in the High Value Currency Notes and strengthening
of mechanism for detection of counterfeit notes should be made more effective in
eradicating the menace of FICN.
3.2 INFILTRATION FROM ACROSS THE BORDER
Recommendation
3.2.1 The Committee feels that though cases of infiltration from across the border and number
of persons apprehended have decreased in recent period, the Government should not feel
complacent. India is already burdened with a large number of infiltrators. The Committee

of the trans-border crimes and has spilled over to the hinterland. This also has links with other
trans-border crimes like smuggling of FICN, narcotics etc.
3.2.3 In its efforts to check the cattle smuggling, BSF has identified in South Bengal, Malda,

people especially poor and illiterate ones might have returned without lodging their complaints
