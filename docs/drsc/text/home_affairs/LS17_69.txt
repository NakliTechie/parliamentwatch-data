A.        DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
 
APPOINTMENT OF NOTARIES
Recommendation (para 11.1)
 
5.0       The Committee does not find the pending position with regard to the applications for appointment of Notaries
satisfactory.  It reiterates its earlier  recommendation  and hopes that the Government would ensure  quick disposal of
applications for appointment of Notaries and feels that if a definite time frame is fixed for disposal of applications, it
will considerably reduce the pendency. The Committee further recommends that Government should ensure due
representation of Schedule Caste, Schedule Tribe and Other Backward Classes while appointing Notaries.

Action Taken/Proposed to be taken
5.1.0    Notaries are appointed under the Notaries Act, 1952  and the Notaries  Rules, 1956.   The Government of India
is empowered to appoint  Notaries for the whole or any part of India, whereas, a State Government is empowered to
appoint Notaries for the whole or any part of that State.  On an application made by eligible person,  by submitting a
memorial to the Competent Authority appointed  by the Government,  comments of the Bar Council, where the
applicant  is enrolled  are called for.  After considering various factors mentioned in the Notaries Rules, appropriate 
decision to appoint a person as Notary or otherwise  is taken by the Competent Authority.  Thus the procedure of
appointment of a Notary involves a number of statutory steps.  However, the requirement  of holding   of interviews has
been dispensed with since June, 1999 to quicken the process of appointment.
5.1.1    Of late,  a large number of applications are being received by the Government , which are being  processed on a
day to day basis, but the actual  appointments take some time due to the earlier  backlog  and the statutory procedure
explained  above.  As such, it may not  at present be  possible  to fix a time frame  for disposal of applications.
5.1.2    No data as to the number   of SC,ST and OBC candidates is available at present, as the basic  document  for
processing of the application, i.e.,  the ‘Memorial’ does not contain a column of the candidate’s caste and status.
5.1.3    The Notaries Rules, 1956 have been amended  and the eligibility criteria in respect of persons belonging  to the
Scheduled Castes/Tribes and Other Backward Classes and women have been  relaxed.   According to the amended 
provision, a person belonging to these classes would require  only seven years of legal practice instead of ten years as
required for general category candidates, for becoming eligible for appointment as a Notary.
Observations/Recommendations of the Committee
5.2.0    From the perusal of replies, it  appears  that the Ministry is taking considerably  long  time to complete the
process of appointment of Notaries as the persons interviewed  for the post  way back in June, 1999 were not appointed
till the submission of replies to the Committee i.e. 21 August 2000.  Further, the Ministry is not in a position to indicate
the time-frame  by which the pending applications for Notaries would be disposed.
5.2.1     The Committee expresses its displeasure over the casual approach of the Ministry to the pending applications
for Notaries and desires that the task should be completed within a given time-frame. If  required, a special cell may be
constituted for the purpose.
5.2.2      The Committee also points out that neither has the Ministry taken any action nor has it prepared any action plan
to assess  the requirement of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes candidates for the post of
Notaries so as to give them their due  representations.  The reply that   data  in respect of  SC, ST & OBCs in Notaries
are not available, is a routine  approach and directs that the data may be compiled, which would ensure due
representation of SC,ST and OBCs candidates  in Notaries.
5.2.3    The Committee also desires that the feasibility of  introducing additional column for the category in the
‘Memorial’ prescribed for appointment of Notaries may be examined.

NATIONAL COMMISSION TO REVIEW THE WORKING OF THE CONSTITUTION
Recommendation (para 16)
 
9.0       The Committee is of the view that the Government should allay fears, if any, about the setting up of the
Commission.  Further the Committee is also of the opinion that the State Governments should have  been consulted in
the process and recommends that the public should be informed about the  subjects on which the Commission will work
so that the basic structure of the Constitution remains unchanged.
Action Taken/Proposed to be taken
 
9.1.0
    The National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution has been constituted by the Government
of India  to examine, in the light of the experience of past 50 years, as to how best the Constitution can respond the
changing needs of efficient, smooth  and effective system of goverance  and socio-economic development  of modern
India  within the framework of Parliamentary  Democracy and to recommend  changes, if any, that are required in the
provisions of the Constitution without interfering with its basic structure or features.  Its function is to review  the
working of the Constitution not to  rewrite the Constitution.  It emphasized  that its function is essentially advisory in
nature.
9.1.1    Broadly the Commission will examine the working of the present provisions in the constitution and the
applicable laws and practice  and consider how to better achieve  these objectives.  The Commission has identified the
following areas of immediate contemporary concern for its examination:-

(i)                  Strengthening the institutions of Parliamentary democracy and their accountability; the grave and
persisting menace of un-principled defection;
(ii)                Constitution and pace of socio-economic change and development, promoting, literacy, generating
employment, ensuring social security and removal of poverty;
(iii)               Union-State  relations: Working of Article 356, appointment and removal of Governor;
(iv)              Decentralization and devolution of powers strengthening of Panchayati Raj Institutions;
(v)                Fundamental rights; Enlargement of the Fundamental Right in Part III of the Constitution by specific
incorporation of the Freedom of the Media, Right to Compulsory Elementary Education, Right to Privacy
and Right  to Information;
(vi)              Effective enforcement of the Directive Principles of State Policy in Part IV  of the Constitution in order
to achieve  the goals enshrined   in the  Preamble and for good governance;
(vii)             Fundamental duties under  Part-IV – A  of the Constitution; and
(viii)           Fiscal and Monetary Policies, size of government expenditure,  Efficiency of Public audit mechanism.
 
9.1.2    The Commission proposes to prepare a consultation paper on each of these areas along with a questionnaire,
formulated in an appropriate format.  This shall be circulated to political parties, Constitutional and Statutory
Commissions such as, National Commission for  Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, National Commission for
other Backward Classes, National Commission for Minorities, National Human Rights  Commission, National
Commission for Women and other similar bodies;  Trade Unions; Representative Bodies of Trade and Industry; Bar
Associations;  Universities and Colleges; Academicians; Research Institutes, Media  and NGOs  and other concerned
organizations.  This consultation paper and the questionnaire will be placed on the Commissions’ website portal on the
Internet for access to the general public and for eliciting its response and suggestions.
 
9.1.3    Thereafter, the recommendations of the Commission shall be put up to public debate.  After  considering the
various points and responses elicited and received the Commission would formulate its recommendations to the
Government of India.
 
Observations/Recommendations of the Committee
 
9.2       The Committee  recommends  that the Commission should ensure that the consultation papers and
questionnaires be made easily accessible  to general public at the earliest.  Also, to elicit maximum response and
suggestions from general public,  insertions in major dailies , in English  and regional languages, is recommended.

LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT
 
ELECTORAL REFORMS
Recommendation (para 17.1)
 
10.0     The  Committee is of the view that Electoral  Reforms are overdue and  needs utmost attention of the
Government since it has a far   reaching impact on the political system of the country and as such it reiterate its earlier
recommendations made in its 56th Report that the  Government should consider the matter as early as possible.
Action Taken/Proposed to be taken
 
10.1.0  The Government  is committed to  bring necessary reforms in electoral laws  pertaining to elections to
Parliament and Legislatures of the States.  The President, in his Address to the Parliament, at the commencement of the
first session after constitution of the 13
th Lok Sabha, has also stressed the necessity of bringing   comprehensive 
reforms in the Electoral Laws of the country as a priority subject for the present Government.
10.1.1  The National Agenda for Governance of the previous Government had also mandated introduction of necessary
electoral reforms on the basis of recommendations of the Dinesh  Goswami  Committee  and, accordingly,  that
Government  took up on priority basis consideration of unimplemented  recommendations of the Dinesh Goswami
Committee.  In this connection, a meeting  was held with political parties on 22 nd May, 1998 to discuss  various

69th Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/69threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:42]
proposals  on electoral  reforms which mainly contained  unimplemented recommendations of the Dinesh Goswami
Committee.  Pursuant  to a decision taken in that  meeting,  a High Powered  Committee under the  Chairmanship of
Shri Indrajit Gupta  was constituted   to suggest concrete measures for providing State funding of elections  to
recognized political parties.  The Committee  was also to consider  the related proposals  pertaining to maintenance  of
accounts by political parties and audit thereof, ban on donations by companies to political parties, inclusion of
expenses   of political parties in the election expenses of candidates for the purposes of ceiling on election expenses and
empowering the Election Commission to fix ceiling on election expenses before every general election.  The
Committee, which submitted its report to the Government on 14 th  January, 1999 , has recommended, inter alia,  partial
State funding of elections, in kind only, to recognized political parties and candidates set up by them.  The various
recommendations of the Committee are being examined by the Government in consultation with the  Election
Commission and State Governments and may be discussed along with  other pending proposals on electoral  reforms
with political parties at an appropriate time.
10.1.2  Further, with a view to finding ways of expediting disposal of election petitions pending in courts, the Law
Commission which was requested by the Government  in November, 1995 to study  the problem of delay in disposal of
election petitions and to suggest   remedial  measures, took up, suo motu,  the subject of reform of electoral laws, in
addition, and made a number  of important  recommendations which are available in its 170 th report.  The
recommendations of the Law Commission are being examined by the Government  and may be discussed with political
parties  at an appropriate time.
10.1.3  Recently, the Government, keeping in view the fact that the members of the armed forces  at present have the
option to vote in  person or through the system of postal ballot at elections and that the minimum campaign period for
elections has been reduced from 20 days to 14 days  as also the logistic  constraints involved in dispatching postal
ballots to members of the armed forces through  their record offices and receiving these ballot papers back before
 counting process commences, on the recommendation of the Election Commission, decided  to provide  an additional
option to the members of the Armed Forces of the Union and of Forces to which the provisions of Army Act, 1950 have
been made applicable, whether with or without modifications, to vote through the system of proxy at elections.  A Bill,
namely, the Election Laws (Amendment) Bill, 1999 was accordingly introduced in the Lok Sabha on 9.12.99.  The Bill
has been  referred to the Department related Parliamentary Standing  Committee on Home Affairs for examination and
report.  The Committee met on the 31
st May and 14th June 2000 wherein it heard the oral presentation  of Secretaries of
different  concerned Departments including the  Legislative Department.  The Committee is again scheduled to meet on
10th July 2000 to undertake  clause-by-clause  consideration of the Bill.
10.1.4  The Government, as part of the National Population Policy strategy, has recently decided to extend the current
constitutional embargo on undertaking fresh delimitation of electoral constituencies up to the year 2026 as a
motivational measure  to enable state governments to fearlessly pursue the agenda for population   stabilization.  The 
embargo was laid through the Constitution  (Forty-second Amendment) Act, 1976  with a view to boost  family
planning norms  so that the States, which do not control their population, are not rewarded through  the delimitation
process. It was thought that by the year 2000, the progress of implementation of family  planning programmes would,
more or less, be uniform  throughout the country.  In this regard, the States in the South may be said to have achieved 
some comparative  success  in this direction vis-à-vis  the States in the North  and this situation  has prompted  the
former  to raise  the demand  for extending  the aforesaid  constitutional embargo.  The embargo would end after 
publication of figures of the first census to be held after the year 2000.  Recently, the Government convened a meeting
of leaders of political parties, on 13
th May, 2000, to discuss  the aforesaid  issue as well as some  other connected issues
viz. (i) delimitation of constituencies contained  within a  State, without altering the number of seats in legislatures 
presently allotted, so as to remove the imbalances caused due to uneven growth of population/electorate in different 
constituencies (ii) rotation of reserved constituencies  and (iii) entrusting the job of delimitation along with all
associated issues to the Election Commission of India on permanent  basis.  There was consensus in favour of 
extending the constitutional embargo on undertaking fresh delimitation of constituencies till 2026  AD.
10.1.5  In view of the political instability and repeated General Elections, however, no concrete action  could be possible
after the last batch of Electoral  Reforms in 1996  {The Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1996}, except
for some  piecemeal measures, like expanding   the category of employees to the enrolled for election duties {The
Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1998} and making of provision for postal ballot facility for migrant
voters {The Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1999}.  The  Government is, however, seriously engaged

Observations/Recommendations of the Committee
10.2     The Committee is of the view that the Government should come forward with comprehensive Electoral Reforms
at the earliest.

11.1.4   The Official Languages Wing of the Legislative Department, which includes officers both from the Hindi side and
regional languages, is primarily concerned with the over-all aspects of work relating to translation  in Hindi and other
languages.  The main duties of the Hindi side officers in the Official Languages Wing are as under:-

Recommendation (para 27.1)
 
20.0     The Committee notes that there has been inordinate delay in filling in vacancies. Therefore an urgent step is
necessary to deal with it  forthwith before long term decision  is taken in the form of setting up a new forum for judicial
appointments. The vacancy occurs on elevation, retirement or death of a judge in position. Death  is such an uncertain
event that one cannot foresee it and rationally deal with it in advance.  But, retirement is known in advance.  In the past
the Committee has time and again   observed that the  process for filling up the vacancy must start considerably in
advance before the actual date of retirement of a Judge which would cause the vacancy.  The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the process of filling up the vacancy should be initiated in such a manner that the vacancy is filled on
the day it occurs.
Action Taken/Proposed to be taken
20.1.0  According to the judgement of October 6, 1993, read with the Advisory opinion of  October 28, 1998, of the
Supreme Court of India the initiation  of a proposal for appointment  of a  Judge in the Supreme Court must be by the
Chief Justice of India, and in the case of a High Court by the Chief Justice  of that High Court.  For transfer of a
Judge/Chief  Justice of a High Court to another   High Court, the proposal has to  be  initiated by the Chief Justice  of
India.  This is the manner in which proposals for appointments to the Supreme Court and the High Courts as well as for
the transfers of Judges/Chief Justices of the High Courts must invariably be made.
20.1.1  According to the Memorandum of Procedure  for appointment of Judges of High Courts the Chief Justice of the
concerned High Court will, as early as  possible but at least 6 months before the date of occurrence of the vacancy,
forward the recommendation in consultation with his two senior-most colleagues to the Chief Minister of the State.  It
also provides   that the Governor, as advised by the Chief  Minister, should forward  his recommendation along with the
entire set of papers to the Union Minister of Law, Justice and Company Affairs as early as possible but not later than six
weeks from the date of receipt of the proposal from the Chief  Justice of the High Court.  The Minister of Law and
Justice  would consider  the recommendations in the light of such other reports as may be available to the Government
in respect of the names under consideration.  The complete material would then be referred to the Chief Justice of India
who, in consultation with his colleagues in the Supreme Court, and other Judges who are likely to be conversant  with
the affairs  of the concerned High Court, would in the course of four weeks, send his recommendation to the Union Law
Minister.  The Minister  of Law and Justice would then put up as early as  possible, preferably  within three  weeks, the
recommendation of the Chief Justice of India to the Prime Minister who would advise  the President in the matter of
appointment.
 
20.1.2  For filling up the vacancies of Judges in the High Courts, the Minister  of Law, Justice and Company Affairs had
in his letter dated 19

20.1.3  The Chief Justice of India in his letter  dated 30 th March, 2000 has also requested the Chief Justices of all the
