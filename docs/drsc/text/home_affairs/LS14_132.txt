PREFACE
I, the Chairperson of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home
Affairs having been authorised by the Committee to submit the report on its behalf, do hereby
present this One Hundred and Thirty second Report on Demands for Grants (2008-09) relating to
the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).
2. While examining Demands for Grants every year, it has been the practice in this Committee
to take up one subject/issue for special focus. It had earlier taken up “Disaster Management”,
“Delhi Police and its functioning” and “Union Territories without Legislature” focused study during
2005-06, 2006-07 and 2007-08, respectively. In the current year, the Committee had decided to
highlight “Naxalism” in its report. To facilitate in-depth examination of the subject by the
Committee, the officials of the Home Ministry made an exclusive presentation in the matter before
it. However, keeping in view the wide ranging implications of the subject, the Committee decided
to make a detailed study of the subject and present a separate report after taking into account the
views of the affected State Governments.
3. The Committee held in all three sittings to complete the examination of Demands for Grants
of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The Committee heard the Home Secretary on Demands for Grants
(2008-09) of Ministry of Home Affairs and Naxalism in its meetings held on 28th March and 5th
April, 2008, respectively.
4. The Committee while making its observations/recommendations has mainly relied upon the
following:—
(i) Presentations made by the Home Secretary and other concerned officers;
(ii) Detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry for the year 2008-09;
(iii) Annual Report of the Ministry of Home Affairs for the year 2007-08;
(iv) Outcome Budget of the Ministry for the year 2008-09;
(v) Detailed Explanatory Note on the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs
for the year 2008-09;
(vi) Physical and financial targets fixed and achieved during the Tenth Plan period and the
breakdown of the same for the year 2008-09 of the Eleventh Plan;
(vii) Projection of outlays for the schemes to be undertaken by the Ministry during the
Eleventh Five Year Plan;
(viii) Details of under-utilization of the allocations made under different heads during the last
three years;
(ix) Written replies furnished by the Ministry to the Questionnaires sent to them by the
Secretariat; and
(x) Written clarifications furnished by the Ministry, on the points/issues raised by the
Members during the deliberations of the Committee.
5. The Committee considered and adopted this report in its sitting held on 11th April, 2008.

6. For facility of reference and convenience, observations and recommendations of the
Committee have been printed in bold letters in the body of the Report.

CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
Maintenance of internal security, management of para-military forces, border management,
Centre-State relations, administration of Union Territories, disaster management are the major
responsibilities of the Ministry of Home Affairs. In addition, the Ministry also has the following
broad responsibilities:
(i) Issue of Notification in respect of Appointment of President/Vice-President, Prime
Minister and Members of the Union Council of Ministers;
(ii) Appointment of Governors and Lt. Governors;
(iii) Regulation of Pay and Allowances of the Ministers;
(iv) Carrying out Census in India once every ten years;
(v) Implementation of the Provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963;
(vi) Maintenance of Communal Harmony and Promotion of National Integration;
(vii) Co-ordination of Policy relating to Human Rights;
(viii) Providing Relief and Rehabilitation to Refugees/Displaced Persons;
(ix) Regulation of Entry of Foreigners into India;
(x) Grant of Pension and Other Benefits to Freedom Fighters; and
(xi) Prevention and Combat of Drug Abuse and Drug Trafficking.
2. To discharge the above responsibilities, the Ministry has been divided into following six
constituent Departments:
(i) Department of Internal Security;
(ii) Department of States;
(iii) Department of Home;
(iv) Department of Jammu and Kashmir (J and K) Affairs;
(v) Department of Border Management; and
(vi) Department of Official Language.
2.1 The Department of Internal Security deals with Indian Police Service, Central Police
Forces, internal security and law and order, insurgency, terrorism, naxalism, activities of inimical
foreign agencies, rehabilitation, grant of visa and other immigration matters, security clearances, etc.
2.2 Department of States deals with Centre-State relations, Inter-State relations, administration
of Union Territories, freedom fighters’ pension, human rights, prison reforms, police reforms, etc.
2.3 Department of Home deals with notification of assumption of office by the President and
Vice-President, notification of appointment/resignation of the Prime Minister, Ministers, Governors,
nomination to Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha, census of population, registration of births and deaths, etc.

2
2.4 Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs deals with the constitutional provisions in
respect of the State of Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters relating to the State, excluding
those with which the Ministry of External Affairs is concerned.
2.5 Department of Border Management deals with management of international borders
including coastal borders, strengthening of border guarding and creation of related infrastructure,
border areas development, etc.
2.6 Department of Official Language is concerned with the implementation of the provisions
of the Constitution relating to official languages and the provisions of the Official Language
Act, 1963.
3. The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Minister of Home Affairs assisted by three
Ministers of State. All Departments of the Ministry except the Department of Official Language are
interlinked and are headed by the Home Secretary who is assisted by Secretary (Border
Management), two Special Secretaries, three Additional Secretaries and nineteen Joint Secretaries.
The Department of Official Language has a separate Secretary and functions independently. The
Ministry is further divided into eighteen divisions for administrative purposes and is concerned with
the tasks mentioned below for each division:-
(i) Centre State Division
The Division deals with Central-State relations, including working of the Constitutional
provisions governing such relations; appointment of Governors; creation of new
States; nominations to Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha; Inter-State boundary disputes; over-
seeing the crime situation in States; imposition of President’s Rule; etc.
(ii) Internal Security Division
The Division deals with matters relating to internal security, including anti-national and
subversive activities of various groups/extremist organizations; policy and operational
issues on terrorism; Narcotics Control Bureau; National Security Act; etc.
(iii) Police Division
The Division functions as the cadre controlling authority of IPS officers and also
deals with all matters relating to Central Police Forces, including their deployment.
(iv) Union Territories Division
The Division deals with all legislative and constitutional matters relating to Union
Territories including Delhi and Puducherry. It also functions as the cadre controlling
authority of Arunachal Pradesh-Goa-Mizoram and Union Territories (AGMUT) Cadre
of IAS/IPS as also Delhi-Andaman Nicobar Islands Civil Service (DANICS)/Delhi-
Andaman Nicobar Islands Police Service (DANIPS). It is also responsible for
overseeing the crime and law and order situation in UTs.
(v) North-Eastern Division
The Division deals with the law and order situation in North-Eastern States, including
matters relating to insurgency and talks with various extremist groups operating in
that region.
(vi) Jammu and Kashmir Division
The Division deals with the Constitutional matters including Article 370 and general
policy matters in respect of J and K and terrorism/militancy in that State; it is also
responsible for implementation of the PM’s Package for J and K.
(vii) Foreigners Division
The Division deals with all matters relating to visa, Protected Area Permit/Restricted

3
Area Permit regime, immigration, citizenship, overseas citizenship of India, acceptance
of foreign contribution and hospitality.
(viii) Freedom Fighters and Rehabilitation Division
The Division frames and implements the Freedom Fighters Pension Scheme and the
schemes for rehabilitation of migrants from former West Pakistan/East Pakistan and
provision of relief to Srilankan and Tibetan refugees.
(ix) Human Rights Division
The Division deals with matters relating to the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
and also matters relating to national integration, communal harmony and Ayodhya
affairs.
(x) Disaster Management Division
This division is bifurcated into two parts: Disaster Management-I Division is
responsible for response, relief and preparedness for natural calamities and man-made
disasters (except drought and epidemics), whereas Disaster Management-II Division
is responsible for legislation, policy, capacity building, prevention, mitigation and long
term rehabilitation.
(xi) Border Management Division
This Division deals with all matters relating to management of international borders,
including coastal borders, creation of infrastructure like integrated check posts, road/
fencing and floodlighting of borders and the Border Area Development Programme.
(xii) Police Modernization Division
The Division handles all items of work relating to modernisation of State Police
Forces, provisioning/procurement of various items for modernisation of Central
Police Forces, police reforms and security of VIPs/vitals installation.
(xiii) Policy Planning Division
It deals with meetings of the SAARC Interior/Home Ministers, matters relating to
policy formulation in respect of internal security issues, international cooperation on
counter-terrorism, international covenants, bilateral assistance treaties and related
items of work.
(xiv) Finance Division
The Division is responsible for formulating, operating and controlling the budget of
the Ministry under the Integrated Finance Scheme.
(xv) Judicial Division
The Division deals with all matters relating to the legislative aspects of the Indian
Penal Code, 1860, Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 and the Commissions of Inquiry
Act, 1952. It also handles matters relating to the State legislations to the extent such
legislation require the assent of the President under the Constitution; political pension
to erstwhile rulers before independence and mercy petitions under Article 72 of the
Constitution.
(xvi) Administration Division
The Division is responsible for handling all administrative and vigilance matters of the
Ministry of Home Affairs, monitoring of compliance of furnishing information under
the Right to Information Act, 2005 and it also deals with matters relating to Table of
Precedence, Bharat Ratna and Padma Awards, Gallantry Awards, Jeevan Raksha
Padak, National Flag, National Anthem, State Emblem of India and Secretariat Security
Organisation.

4
(xvii) Coordination Division
The division deals with intra-Ministry coordination work, Parliamentary matters,
public grievances, publication of Annual Report of the Ministry, Record Retention
Schedule, custody of classified and non-classified records of the Ministry, Internal
Work Study, furnishing of various reports relating to employment of SCs/STs and
persons with Disabilities to Department of Personnel and Training, etc.
(xviii) Naxal Management Division
This Division was created w.e.f. October, 29, 2006 in the Ministry to effectively
tackle the naxalite menace from both security and development angles. It monitors the
naxal situation and counter-measures being taken by the affected States with the
objective of improving ground-level policing and development response as per the
location specific action plans formulated/to be formulated by the affected States and
review with the concerned Ministries/Departments to ensure optimum utilization of
funds released and proper implementation of various developmental schemes in the
naxal affected areas.

CHAPTER–III
SCHEMES, POLICIES AND PROGRAMMES OF THE MINISTRY
Multipurpose National Identity Cards Scheme
9.0 The Committee notes that the Government has taken up implementation of a pilot project
for preparing a National Register of Indian Citizens and issue of Multipurpose National Identity
Cards (MNIC) to all citizens of the country. The main purpose of this is to provide a credible
individual identification system and simultaneous use of this card for several multifarious socio-
economic benefits and transactions within and outside the country.
9.1 An amount of Rs.44.36 crore has been sanctioned for MNIC pilot project. Of this,
Rs.38.36 crore is for the implementation of the project and another Rs.6.00 crore for testing and
running the project for one year from the date of completion. As stated by the Ministry of Home
Affairs, twenty one lakh ninety four thousand persons have been verified for their status as citizens
out of the total targeted population of twenty eight lakh sixty two thousand persons. Three lakh
seventy thousand persons are in the process of being verified and two lakh ninety eight thousand
could not be verified due to various reasons. Of the twenty one lakh ninety four thousand verified
persons, thirteen lakh forty seven are of the age of 18 years and above and nearly twelve lakh
persons have been issued identity cards as on date and another fifty thousand persons are expected
to be issued the card additionally. The Committee has also been given to understand that the pilot
project would be completed by 31.03.2008.
9.2 It was further stated by the Ministry that the pilot project would be continued for one year
to record the changes in the citizen database as well as issue of identity cards to those who had
been either left out in the past or those who attained the age of 18 years in the next one year or
those who come to reside in the pilot areas thereafter. Accordingly to the Ministry, nearly two to
three lakh cards are expected to be additionally personalized during 2008-09.
9.3 On being asked about the next course of action of the Ministry, after this pilot project, the
Home Secretary stated as under:—
“A scheme is going on which is called the Unique Identity Number Scheme, which is
being implemented by the Department of Information and Technology under the overall
supervision of the Planning Commission. The main aim is for better administration in
programmes like Public Distribution System, issue of Below Poverty Line (BPL) cards.
Under this scheme, a primary database is created by the collation of Election
Commission, computerized database based on the voter list and whatever database is
available relating to the BPL Scheme, and they have already been able to, according
to our last discussion, get the records of sixty eight crore people. A Group of Ministers
(GoM) has been constituted to see how the MNIC scheme and UID scheme can be
converged.”
9.4 The Committee expresses its strong displeasure on the adhocism adopted by the
Government. The MNIC scheme was launched on pilot basis prior to the announcement of
the UID scheme. The Planning Commission should have obtained status as well as feedback
on the MNIC Scheme and then taken a decision on any other scheme, let alone UID
21

22
scheme. Running of two parallel schemes appear to be overlapping in objectives and may
be perceived to be wastage of time and resource. Implementation of the pilot project for
MNIC has taken unduly long time and yet it is not known how long it would take to
complete the Scheme in the entire country and on top of that another scheme has been
launched. The Committee recommends that the Group of Ministers may critically review the
MNIC and UID Schemes and examine the feasibility of merging the two into a single
integrated programme, so as to avoid overlapping of objectives and likely wastage of
resource and manpower. Whatever may be the recommendation of the Committee,
Government must ensure that such a programme should be made feasible for
implementation within a reasonable and strictive time-frame.
Modernization of Police Forces
10.0 The law and order situation and crime scenario have made it imperative to modernise the
State Police Forces quickly. The State Police, being directly concerned with the law and order,
there is a need to improve their functioning and to equip them with the latest technology so that
they are able to successfully meet emerging challenges. To supplement the efforts of the State
Government/UT administration in modernizing their police forces, the Ministry of Home Affairs has
been implementing a Non-Plan Scheme of Modernization of Police Force (MPF). It is also
necessary for capacity building of the State Police Forces (SPF), especially for meeting the
emerging challenges to internal security in the form of terrorism, naxalism, etc. The main objective
of the scheme is to reduce the dependence of the State Governments on the Army and Central
Police Forces to control internal security and law and order situation by way of equipping the State
Police Forces adequately. The scheme also aims at a balanced development of the State Police
Forces.
10.1 The Committee in its 119th Report had recommended for extension of this important
scheme to UTs and further recommended for necessary allocation at RE stage in 2006-07. As a
result, provision was made for the Modernization of Police Forces in UTs at RE stage for the year.
For the year 2008-09, an amount of Rs.1340.63 crore has been allocated under this head. Out of
this Rs.1163.83 crore is for assistance to States for modernization of their police. Whereas
Rs.41.20 crore and Rs.13.20 crore are for UTs without legislature and UTs with legislature,
respectively. Rs.122.40 crore has been provided for assistance to Delhi Police for modernization.
10.2 The Ministry in its reply to a question has stated that the main components included under
the Scheme are construction of secure police stations, outposts, police lines, mobility, modern
weaponry, security, Surveillance, communication, forensic equipment, upgradation of training
infrastructure, police housing, computerization etc. The States have formulated and submitted their
annual action plans broadly covering these aspects and also on the assessment of their needs taking
into consideration the prevailing ground situation.
10.3 Explaining about the importance of the scheme, the Ministry in the Annual Report stated as
under:
“The scheme has made perceptible impact on all the States and has provided much-
needed assistance and impetus to police modernization. For instance, proper buildings
for police stations/outposts with required facilities have provided a safe, secure and
comfortable environment, construction of houses for police personnel and provision of
modern weapons have boosted their morale, particularly in extremist-affected areas.
The increased availability of vehicles at cutting edge level has improved mobility and
reduced response time.”

23
10.4 On being asked about whether the Government has any monitoring mechanism to ensure
the optimum utilization of funds, the Ministry has stated that the State Governments are periodically
persuaded to ensure optimum utilization of funds and furnishing utilization certificates in regard to
the central funds released during previous years. It was further stated that visits by central teams
are also undertaken for monitoring the implementation of the Scheme in the States and the matter
regarding optimum utilization of funds under the MPF is reviewed from time to time, in
consultation with concerned States.
10.5 The Committee observes that the role of the Ministry is not confined to merely
allocating funds to the States but it has the responsibility to ensure that each and every
rupee is spent in the furtherance of the objective of the scheme. The Committee reiterates
its recommendations made in the earlier reports that it should be insisted upon all the
States to send utilization certificates at the earliest in order to ensure that States spend the
funds meant for modernization of their police forces in an efficient manner.
10.6 The Committee observed that the major component of the Scheme is construction of
residential and non-residential buildings for police forces. It is the view of the Committee
that though allocation of funds for construction of police housing and other buildings are
equally important and definitely enhance the morale of the police forces, yet it should not
be done at the cost of modernization of police forces. The Committee is constrained to note
that after nearly eight years of commencement of this Scheme, there are hardly any
perceptible changes in the functioning of police stations. The Committee feels that housing
for police is the basic necessity and it should not be clubbed with modernization. If
necessary, Government may separate the component of housing from modernization and
provide separate grant for the former. The Committee recommends that ‘modernization’
should appropriately include modern technological advancements, modern gadgets and
weaponry and such other facilities.
10.7 The Ministry in its written reply has stated that the improvement in police performance due
to modernization, to a considerable extent, will depend on the provision of basic necessary
infrastructure in all the police stations as well as in the district police headquarters. Emphasis is
also being laid on providing IT facilities. A Project called “Common Integrated Police Application”
(CIPA) is being implemented in all the States to ensure a uniform framework and mechanism
for computerization of crime registration, investigation, etc. at the police station level. NIC is
the implementing agency for the project, which is being implemented in all the States in a phased
manner.
10.8 The Ministry giving details about the scheme stated that:—
“CIP A was launched on a pilot basis in 6 police stations in Delhi in the year 2005
and subsequently it was decided to implement the project in the entire country in a
phased manner. Till 2007, out of total of 14000 police stations in the country nearly
1300 police stations were covered under CIP A. The Project is being implemented by

24

(i) General information to public about police station, District area, jurisdiction, names

(ii) Registration of online complaints;
(iii) Providing information relating to status of case registered at police station;
(iv) Providing information relating to an application, petition or request;
(v) Provide copies of permissible documents like FIR, Post Mortem Report, etc.;
(vi) Coordination of stolen, recovered and seized property;
(vii) Coordination of missing, arrested, unidentified persons, and dead bodies; and
(viii) V erification of persons for employment, domestic help, passport, etc.
10.11 The Scheme is scheduled to be completed in a period of four years commencing from

the year 2008-09.
10.12 The CIPA scheme is a path breaking initiative in networking the entire policing

Bureau of Immigration (BoI)
13.0 The major role of Bureau of Immigration is to control 11 Immigration Check Posts (ICP)
out of the total 77 ICPs to regulate entry into and exit from the country. The remaining 66 ICPs
are controlled by the State Governments/UT Administration on agency basis on behalf of the
Government of India. Modernisation of 33 ICPs which cater to around 99% of passenger traffic
was initiated in the year 2004-05 for improving the immigration services in the country. The
modernization programme, inter alia, includes upgradation of computer systems; installation of
Immigration Control System (ICS) software; installation of Passport Reading Machines (PRMs);
installation of Questionable Document Examiner (QDE) Machines; and networking of ICPs with
Central Foreigners Bureau (CFB).
13.1 The Ministry stated that with these modern facilities put into place under the modernization
programme, there has been a qualitative improvement in the immigration services in terms of
enhanced security screening of the passengers coupled with expeditious immigration clearance.
13.2 The Ministry further stated that Bureau of Immigration had submitted a proposal for taking
over of 38 ICPs (including the 11 controlled by it) in phases during the FY 2008-09. An enhanced
amount of Rs.57.90 crores was provided to BoI in the BE 2008-09 as compared to an amount of
Rs.28.65 crores in the BE 2007-08 to Complete the modernization programme of 33 ICPs and to
extend the modernization facilities to 5 more ICPs i.e. from 33 to 38.
13.3 The Committee notes the progress made in the modernization of BOIs but feels that
latest equipments are required for upgradation of system like equipments which could be
laser compatible so that it could identify counterfeit currencies, foreign countries passports,
etc. The Committee feels that these days the equipments are becoming more advanced and
the credentials of the individuals could be checked with thumb impression, colour of eye,
blood report, etc. The modernization should be aimed at keeping in view the requirement
for next ten years. The Committee also recommends that all ICPs should be covered under
modernization in a phased manner.

Illegal Bangladeshi Migrants
15.0 The porosity of the Indo-Bangladesh border and the practical difficulties due to
topographical reasons acting as impediments in the timely completion of border works’ projects,
are amongst the contributory factors towards influx of illegal Bangladeshi migrants. It has also
been reported that these illegal migrants have been able to secure ration cards, driving licences,
voter identity cards and even PAN cards. Media reports have attributed claims to the intelligence
sources that terrorist groups have been recruiting Bangladeshis in India. It has also been reported
that certain Bangladeshi insurgent groups have been involved in the terrorist incidents in our
country.
15.1 The Ministry in its written response stated that the Government was aware of reports, from
time to time, about illegal immigration into India from Bangladesh. Various measures have been
taken by the Government to check and contain such illegal immigration, which inter alia , includes
the construction of border fencing/flood lighting and roads to enable more effective patrolling, etc.,
assignment of the border guarding duties on the Indo-Bangladesh border to a single force, viz., the
BSF, augmentation of the strength of BSF through additional battalions, reduction of gaps between
border outposts, provision of modern surveillance and other equipment and gadgetry to the BSF,
etc.
15.2 While the above measures are aimed at reducing the possibilities of illegal migration into the
country from across the border, instructions have also been issued to the State Governments and
the UT administration, from time to time, to take sustained action to detect foreign nationals,
including Bangladeshi nationals, staying illegally in the country. 32 Foreigners Tribunals under the
provisions of the Foreigners (Tribunals) Order, 1964 have also been constituted in the State of
Assam for detention of illegal migrants/foreigners.
15.3 The Ministry has also stated that involvement of terrorist groups based in Bangladesh in
some incidents of terrorist violence had come to notice, but there were no reports of recruitment
of Bangladeshi nationals in India for these purposes. Apart from the various measures being taken
within the country for preventing terrorist incidents which, inter alia , include continuous sharing
of information and intelligence with the State Governments, strengthening and upgradation of the
State Police Forces under Police Modernisation Scheme, etc., the issue of involvement of
Bangladesh based terrorist groups in such activities has also been regularly taken up with the
Government of Bangladesh in various bilateral discussions, during which they have been assuring
that the territory of Bangladesh will not be allowed to be used for activities inimical to India.
15.4 The Committee feels that a large presence of illegal Bangladeshi immigrants poses
a grave threat to the internal security and it should be viewed seriously. Reports have also
appeared that counterfeit notes are in large circulation along Indo-Bangladesh border. The
Committee strongly recommends that movement along the border may be strictly
monitored.

MULTIPURPOSE NATIONAL IDENTITY CARDS SCHEME
The Committee expresses its strong displeasure on the adhocism adopted by the
Government. The MNIC scheme was launched on pilot basis prior to the announcement of
the DID scheme. The Planning Commission should have obtained status as well as feedback
on the MNIC Scheme and then taken a decision on any other scheme, let alone UID
scheme. Running of two parallel schemes appear to be overlapping in objectives and may
be perceived to be wastage of time and resource. Implementation of the pilot project for
MNIC has taken unduly long time and yet it is not known how long it would take to
complete the Scheme in the entire country and on top of that another scheme has been
launched. The Committee recommends that the Group of Ministers may critically review the
MNIC and UID Schemes and examine the feasibility of merging the two into a single
integrated programme, so as to avoid overlapping of objectives and likely wastage of
resource and manpower. Whatever may be the recommendation of the Committee;
Government must ensure that such a programme should be made feasible for
implementation within a reasonable and strictive time frame. (Para 9.4)

involved in road rage cases and wherever necessary laws may be amended. (Para 12.7)
BUREAU OF IMMIGRATION (BoI)
The Committee notes the progress made in the modernization of BoIs but feels that
latest equipments are required for upgradation of system like equipments which could be
laser compatible so that it could identify counterfeit currencies, foreign countries passports,
etc. The Committee feels that these days the equipments are becoming more advanced and
the credentials of the individuals could be checked with thumb impression, colour of eye,
blood report, etc. The modernization should be aimed at keeping in view the requirement
for next ten years. The Committee also recommends that all ICPs should be covered under
modernization in a phased manner. (Para 13.3)

35
SHIFTING OF BOUNDARIES TO THE ZERO LEVEL
The Committee is of the view that the shifting of the fencing to the zero line is the
only solution left for the convenience of the people residing in nearby villages to carry out
their agricultural activities. The Committee therefore, recommends that suitable action
should be initiated at the earliest for shifting of the fence to zero line. The Committee also
desires that recruitment of female constables be done at the earliest. (Para 14.2)
ILLEGAL BANGLADESHI MIGRANTS
The Committee feels that a large presence of illegal Bangladeshi immigrants poses
a grave threat to the internal security and it should be viewed seriously. Reports have also
appeared that counterfeit notes are in large circulation along Indo-Bangladesh border. The
Committee strongly recommends that movement along the border may be strictly
monitored. (Para 15.4)
BORDER AREA DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (BADP)
The Committee in its One Hundred thirteenth Report on Demands for Grants
(2005-06) pertaining to the Ministry of DoNER had recommended that it should specify the
developmental work under the Border Area Development Scheme which should be
undertaken around 60-75 Km of the actual border. But the ground realities in this context
are different as the Committee came to know about the fact that these funds are spent on
civilian gardens and parks depriving the people in the border areas where there is hardly
any communication. The Committee recommends that a proper monitoring mechanism
should be put in place to monitor the utilization of these funds as the people living in the
border areas are suffering due to lack of infrastructure viz. schools and other medical
facilities. (Para 16.2)
The Committee feels that border areas in the North-Eastern part of the country have
been neglected for a long time. Even today some border areas are not easily accessible. The
Committee is constraint to note that in spite of its recommendation made in earlier reports,
the Cabinet Secretariat has not taken any decision on the issue of transferring of BADP
scheme to Ministry of DoNER. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the issue be
resolved immediately in the interest of the nation. (Para 16.8)
INSURGENCY IN NORTH-EASTERN STATES
The Committee feels that for the last four or five decades, this region is facing the
problem of insurgency and people are frustrated with the prevailing situation. The
Government has to take on the responsibility of resolving the problem. The Committee is
of the considered view that inspite of decrease in number of the causalities; reported
instances of extortion activities and collection of levies by the outlawed groups are
increasing. The Committee calls upon the Ministry to keep a close watch on the
developments in this regard and all efforts be made to foil the nefarious design of the
secessionists. (Para 17.2)
