R E P O R T
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having been authorised  by the
Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Fifty-seventh Report on the Foreigners (Amendment) Bill, 1998.
2.         In pursuance of the rule relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha in
consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha referred* the Foreigners (Amendment) Bill, 1998 (Annexure-I) as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 
8 July 1998 and pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.
3.         The Bill seeks to amend the relevant provisions of the Foreigners Act, 1946, by classifying offences and providing for punishment
depending on the gravity of  the offence committed under the Act.  It also seeks to enhance the maximum punishment under the Foreigners
Act  upto eight years and fine upto Rs.50,000.  The enhancement of the punishment will have the following effect, namely;
(i)
                 serious  offences will become triable by the Court of Sessions; and
(ii)               the Court will be required to give notice to the State Government thereby affording it an opportunity to oppose the bail
application under proviso to sub-Section (1) of the Section 439 of the Court of Criminal Procedure, 1973.
4.         The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill explains that contravention of the provisions of the Foreigners Act, 1946, or of any
order made thereunder, or of any direction given in pursuance of that Act or such order,  is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to
five years and fine. 
4.1       The cases under the Foreigners Act are cognisable, non-bailable and triable by the First Class Magistrate.  However, the accused person
arrested under the said Act very often manages to obtain bail under Section 437 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.  Further, the Act also does
not classify the violation of the various provisions of the Act or order made thereunder or of any direction given in pursuance of the Act or
such order, and only provides for punishment which may extend to five years and fine.  The quantum of punishment is left to the discretion of
the Court.   Accordingly, it is proposed to classify offences under the Foreigners Act and to provide for punishment depending on the gravity
of offence committed.  It is also proposed to provide for punishment under the Foreigners Act,  upto eight years and fine upto Rs. 50,000.
5.         The Ministry of Home Affairs in its Background Note on the Bill has stated that  entry, stay, movement, etc. of foreigners  in India  is
governed by the Foreigners Act, 1946.  It gives wide powers to the Central Government to make orders for prohibiting, regulating and
restricting the entry of foreigners into India or their departure from India or their continued presence therein.   The power to make orders

Act, 1946,  on the ground that although offences  under the said Act were cognisable , non-bailable    and    triable     by    First  Class

As per  Section 437 of Cr. P.C.,  any person accused of or suspected of the commission of any non-bailable  offence was arrested or detained
without  warrant  by an officer in charge of a police station or appeared or was brought before a Court, other than the High Court or Court of
Session, he might be released on bail, unless  there appeared   reasonable grounds for believing that he had been guilty of  an offence 
punishable with death or imprisonment for life, or if he has previously been convicted for offence punishable with death or imprisonment for
life or imprisonment for seven  years or more, or if he had  previously been convicted on two or more occasions   of a non-bailable  or
cognizable offence. 
5.2       According to sub-section (6) of Section 437 Cr.P.C., if, in any case triable by a Magistrate, the trial of person accused of any
non-bailable offence was not concluded   within  a period  of 60  days from the first date fixed for taking   evidence in the case, such person
shall, if he was   in custody  during the whole of the said  period, be    released     on   bail    to    the  satisfaction     of   the    Magistrate,    
unless    for   reasons to be recorded in writing, the   Magistrate otherwise directed.  The Rajasthan Government had stated that even if bail
applications were opposed by the Public  Prosecutor/ State   Government and the accused persons were initially denied bail, they were able to
get bail, as it was impossible to conclude  the proceedings within a period of 60 days.
5.3       The Background Note goes on to state that the matter was examined by the Ministry of Home Affairs in consultation with the
Department of Legal Affairs.  That Department opined that even if the offences under the Foreigners Act were  made  triable  by the Court of
Sessions, still the Judge, in his  discretion, might grant bail to the accused person under the provisions  of Section   437 Cr. P.C.   The Ministry
of Law felt that by making the offences under the Foreigners Act triable by the Court of Sessions, the objective of ensuring that the bail was
not easily granted by the Courts, was not likely to be achieved.   The Department of Legal Affairs also opined  that  the proper  course of action
would be that the Public  Prosecutor should oppose the bail application on the ground  that the concerned foreigner, if released on bail,   was
likely to go underground.  This opinion was, accordingly, communicated to the State Government on  22 January 1993.
5.4       It has further been stated that the Rajasthan Government, after passage of some time, reiterated its earlier request and advanced the
following reasons for enhancement of punishment under the Foreigners Act and for making offence under the Act triable by the Court of
Sessions:
(i)
                   Even if bail applications were opposed by the Public Prosecutor/State Government and the accused persons were initially
denied bail, they were able  to get bail as it was impossible to conclude the proceedings  within a period of 60 days.
(ii)                 After sealing of Punjab border, the entire pressure of infiltration and clandestine activities from across the border had shifted
to the Rajasthan border.
(iii)               Specific cases of smuggling of arms, ammunition, narcotics and movements of persons from across the border having links
with terrorist organisations had come to the notice of the State Government.  In some cases, although recoveries

of arms, ammunition, narcotics and other incriminating material were made, the cases under the Official Secrets Act and other
Central enactments could not be sustained due to lack of evidence.
(iv)               Enhancement of punishment would have deterrent effect and, if the cases were made triable  by the Sessions Court, the level
of discretion for grant of bail would lie with the higher judicial authority.
5.5       The Background Note also refers to the view of the Estimates Committee of the Tenth Lok Sabha, which while reviewing the
functioning of Consular, Passport and Visa  Division of the Ministry of External Affairs, had, in its 5 th Report,  presented on 27 February
1992,  recommended  inter alia that the punishment under the Foreigners Act be made more deterrent.
5.6       Accordingly, a Note for consideration of the Cabinet was prepared by the Ministry proposing enhancement of the existing punishment
from five years imprisonment and fine to eight years imprisonment and fine.  The Cabinet, in its meeting held on  29 October 1996, felt that
simpler offences should have simple punishment  and   more   severe   offences  must   have   more      severe    punishment.      It  wanted   the
classification of various offences to be made and to indicate different types of punishments.  The Cabinet, therefore, deferred the consideration
of the proposals contained in that Note.
5.7       As per the wishes of the Cabinet, the matter was examined. Upon examination, it was realised  that two fold classification of offences
under the Foreigners Act, 1946, was  possible. A higher punishment viz., of imprisonment for a term which should not be less than two years
but which might extend to eight years and fine which should not be less than ten thousand rupees but which might extend to fifty thousand

or on forged travel documents.  The present scale of punishment of a maximum of five years and fine might continue for other offences under

6.         The representatives of the Ministry of Home Affairs led by the then Home Secretary and the concerned   Special Secretary made a
presentation vis-à-vis the Foreigners (Amendment) Bill, 1998, before the Committee in its meeting held on  10 September 1998. The
concerned Special Secretary of the Ministry while making a presentation, gave an overview on the Bill which inter alia   encompassed various
aspects of the influx of foreigners  into the country.  He informed the Committee that  in the year 1997, 24,91,946   passengers     arrived   and  
24,67,092   passengers   departed   through   various  exist points of India. Of the total passengers who arrived and departed, 82.91%
passengers came and departed by air, 15.65% passengers used the land route   while 1.44% relied on the sea
route.                                                                          
6.1       The Special Secretary stated that overstay after entry on valid passport and visa; entry and stay on forged or fake passport and visa; and
illegal entry and stay without any documents i.e. infiltration, unauthorised entry and stay in restricted and protected areas were some of the
major contraventions of the Foreigners  Act, 1946.  He informed the Committee that the number of cases pertaining to the forgery of passport
and visa by foreigners, detected at entry and exit points, was 550 in 1997 as against 431 in 1996, 510 in 1995 and 421 in 1994.  These figures
excluded the cases detected in the country other than the entry and exit points.
 
4
 
6.2       Speaking on the salient features of the Foreigners Act, 1946, the Special   Secretary  stated that   Section 3 of the Act provided  for
making orders for prohibiting, regulating  and restricting the entry, stay, movement and departure   of foreigners.  Those powers of the Central
Government had been entrusted to the State Governments and the UT Administrations under articles 258 and 239, respectively, with their prior
consent. Orders were issued under the Act from time to time to deal with  (1) movement of foreigners in restricted and protected areas; (2)
foreigners coming for  mountaineering expeditions, for  making  feature films  and documentary films, etc.; (3) restrictions on the    residence  
of   foreigners in certain areas, for example,  premises of areas   belonging to foreign diplomatic   mission, consular post and trade mission; and
(4) restrictions    on    taking    up    employment     by    foreigners    in    certain       sensitive  sectors such as undertakings supplying basic
services to Government  like power, water, petroleum, etc.
6.3       Thereafter, the Special Secretary dwelt on the various categories of visas issued by Indian authorities.  He said that in all, there were 15
types of visas. Diplomatic, Official, UN Diplomat and UN Official visas were governed by the Ministry of External Affairs.  The remaining
types  were governed by the Ministry of  Home Affairs.  The visas were, in fact, issued by the Indian embassies and missions located abroad. 
The policy etc., was decided at the MEA level or at the MHA level, which ever was the concerned    Ministry.   He further said that Section 14
of the Act, as it stood at present, provided for only maximum punishment up to five years imprisonment and fine for contravention of any
provision of the Act  or orders or directions  issued thereunder.  There was no provision   today for minimum punishment as also there was  no
classification of offences.
6.4       The Special Secretary, emphasising the need to amend the Foreigners Act, 1946, said that the same was a Central Act but its
implementation  was entrusted to the State  Governments and the UT Administrations.  He informed that during the Ministry’s interaction with
them, the representatives of the States and the UTs had been mentioning     about    infiltration     and    other   border-related    offences  and 
had  been recommending that keeping in view the spiral increase in the incidents of infiltration and clandestine   activities including smuggling
of arms, narcotics, etc., foreigners entry on forged  and fake travel documents, violation of  Foreigners  Act and Orders, the punishment be
made more deterrent.   The Rajasthan Government based on the experience of trans-border infiltration, smuggling of arms and ammunition,
etc., requested that the Act be made more deterrent by  providing for the offence  to be triable by the Court  of Sessions.  The intention was that
it would imply application of mind at a higher judicial level.
6.5       The Special Secretary also referred to the Fifth Report of the Estimates Committee of the 10th Lok Sabha and said that the latter while
reviewing the functioning of Consular, Passports and Visa Division of Ministry of External Affairs, had recommended that (a) punishment for
violation under Foreigners Act be made more deterrent; (b) penalties under Foreigners Act and Indian Passport Act be similar;   (c) offences be
made non-bailable with the provision for imprisonment and fine of higher deterrence, and (d) deportation on completion of sentence be made
mandatory.
6.6       The Special Secretary stated that it was in this backdrop that it was felt necessary to provide for  (a) minimum punishment; (b)
classification of punishment on the basis of gravity of contravention ; and (c) enhancement   of punishment for more serious  contraventions  
under the Act.

documents, and (2)  For all other contraventions of the provisions of the Act, penalty to remain same as  at  present  i.e. maximum  
imprisonment upto  five  years  and fine.  Such contraventions included:

6.7       Enlightening the Committee on the effects of the proposed amendment, the Special Secretary said that they would be as follows:
(a)        cases mentioned in proposed Section 14A to become triable  by Sessions        Court.  This would ensure application of mind by
higher level of judicial  authority;
(b)        the Court would be required to give notice to the State  Government  thereby affording  it an opportunity   at the time   of

 (d)       in the final analysis, the enhanced penalties   would make the Act more effective and deterrent in checking problems of
infiltration, overstay and other violations/contraventions of the provisions of the Foreigners Act.
7.         After the presentation of the Special Secretary, the then Home Secretary made a brief presentation before the Committee.   Underlying
the objectives behind the amendment to Section 14 of the Foreigners Act, 1946, he said that the aim was to make that Section more rigorous
than it was in its existing form.  Tracing the genesis of the Foreigners Act, he stated that the scheme of the law which was formulated a little
before Independence or on the eve of Independence  in 1946 made it clear that this law  was going to empower the executive  for prescribing, 
regulating and restricting  amongst  others the presence  and continued presence of foreigners in India. 
7.1       This law was amended six times.   Two amendments were made in 1947 and one amendment was made in 1951.  Thereafter, it was
amended in 1957 and again it was amended in 1962.  It was amended four times but six changes were made in total. 
7.2              The 1947 amendment was more substantial because of the transfer of power.
7.3       The 1951 amendment was because   of the incorporation of Hyderabad. 
7.4       It was in 1957 that the rigour of the law was slightly decreased to facilitate the free entry of nationals of Commonwealth countries.

7.5       In 1962 because of relations with China the law was made more stringent.  The law was again proposed to be made more rigorous  
largely on account of the fact that situation on borders was such which called for this kind of a provision. 
7.6       He further stated that the response to the situation on borders particularly with regard   to   Pakistan   which   had     been    sending   
trained   militants    inside   India to create difficulties was  manifold. One of those difficulties was the fencing and floodlighting on Indo-Pak
border. The total length of the border with Punjab was 554 kilometers  and  almost    the entire boundary i.e. 452 kilometers  had been fenced.  
The rest could not be fenced because of geographical factors.  So far as floodlighting was concerned, 460 kilometers of area had been covered. 
One of the achievements of this fencing on the Punjab area was rapid decline in movement of militants and also there was a more effective
control on smuggling activities.   This led to the shifting of focus to other borders, namely of Rajasthan , Jammu and Kashmir and Gujarat.  In
case of Rajasthan, there was a boundary of 1,035 kms.  Of this 865 kms. had been fenced and floodlit and the work  was in progress for the
other areas.  It was felt during the last three or four years that the Rajasthan sector was very soft compared to the Punjab  sector   for
encroachment by militants.  Therefore, it was decided to study that matter in depth in consultation with the Rajasthan Government and it was
made clear by the latter that unless those operations were made triable by the Sessions Court, it would continue to be easier for the offenders to
get bail under Section 437 of the Criminal Procedure Code.
7.7       Taking up the questions from the Questionnaire prepared by the Secretariat and sent to the Ministry of Home Affairs for replies, the
Home Secretary referred to the question as to why it was Rajasthan and not other States and UTs, which came forward with the suggestion for
amendment to the Foreigners Act, 1946, he said that the matter had been engaging the attention of the  Central Government and   that in    early
1992, at a Conference of the Chief Ministers and Chief Secretaries to discuss this issue,  it was felt that a special law should be made to take
care of several of the problems that were being faced on the border.  Meanwhile, the Estimates Committee of the Parliament had also made this
recommendation in 1992 to make the provisions more stringent.   He further stated that in a different context, in November 1997, comments
were also received from the Governments of Maharashtra, Gujarat, Punjab and Tripura.    Referring to the second question that had been raised
with regard to the experience of the Union Government in tackling the problem, he said that its experience had been that despite the provisions
of the Foreigners Act, it had not been possible for State Governments, Governments of the Union Territories and the Central Government   to
strictly enforce that law.  This had been on account of a variety of factors.  One of those factors was that there was a large scale influx of
infiltrators from Bangladesh, Tibet, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and a number of other places.   Another experience of the Union Government had been
that the Courts had been very lenient and that they had been granting light punishments like imprisonment for fifteen   days to one or two
months and at times only till the rising of the Court, and the fine was also largely between Rs.500 to Rs.1,000.  The experience had been that
bails too were granted.
7.8       Referring to the scheme of things obtaining  in Pakistan vis-à-vis such cases, the Home     Secretary said that Pakistan had the same
Cr.P.C.  but  when it came to the question       of   things    happening    on    their    borders, they had tightened their legal arrangement.   What
they had done was that whoever entered their country illegally would not be released on bail till the trial was over.  But it was not so in case of
India.  Therefore, the position was that in Pakistan bail was not granted at all whereas in India it was not denied.     The other point which our
law itself made clear  was   that  a  foreigner  could  not  be given  full  rights of citizenship but at the same time a

8.         The Chairman and the Members of the Committee raised a  number of points relating to the various aspects of the Bill in its sitting held
on 10 September 1998.  The important points raised were  as follows:
8.1       During the course of the discussion, the Committee desired that the Government should undertake an in-depth study on the
efficacy of the proposed amendment in checking infiltration, smuggling of narcotics, arms, ammunition etc. from across the porous
borders  in NE, Rajasthan, J&K,  Punjab and the Indo-Nepal border.  The Committee favoured a holistic approach in dealing with
violations of  borders and aliens getting caught in the acts of violation and considering in this context, the desirability   of a
comprehensive legislation on the subject to arm the Government  to effectively deal with the problem.
8.2       It was  suggested in the Committee that the focus of the study should be on review of various provisions of the Act so as to make
it more purposeful and deterrent, if necessary, in concert with other statutes.
8.3       Some of the Members of the Committee also desired to know whether the proposed amendments would automatically have the
effect of serious offences becoming triable by Sessions Court and making grant of bail more difficult.
8.4       Another suggestion emerging  from the discussions was the desirability of summary trial for an offence endangering the
security of the country since the existing provisions of section 439 Cr.PC are not of much help in dealing with offenders under the
Foreigners Act, 1946.  Therefore, a procedure has to be formulated which is effective and at the same time reasonable and fair in law;
and in case of an alien  found  guilty under the Act, as also indulging in the acts of terrorism, the burden of proof should be on him.
8.5       The Committee desired to know the details of provisions in the enactments of other countries, especially western countries,
with  reference to the  penal   provisions and emphasis on human rights to deal with such problems.
9.         The Home Secretary briefly responded  to  some of the points raised.  He stated that on some queries he would have to seek  the
opinion of the Law Ministry again and also the opinion of the Attorney General   and only then the Home Ministry    would be in a position to
formulate its responses. The Chairman of the Committee then directed the Secretary to send detailed written responses of the Ministry to all
the points raised by the members. 
9.1       Replying to some of the points raised by the Members, the Home Secretary   concurred  with  the  view   for   strengthening     the

of proviso to Section 439(1) of the Code of Criminal   Procedure, 1973, the Court of Sessions would have to give notice of the application for
bail to Public Prosecutor unless for reasons to be recorded in writing, if it was of the opinion  that it was not practicable   to give such notice.

14.1     Clause 2 of the Bill seeks to amend Section 14 of the Foreigners Act, 1946.  This section inter alia   provides for punishment   which
may extend to five  years and fine for contravention of provisions of the Act or of any order made thereunder, or of any direction given in
pursuance of that Act or such order.  The amendment proposes to classify offences under the Foreigners Act, 1946, and to provide for
punishment depending on the gravity of offence committed.   It is also proposed that the maximum punishment under the Act may be upto
eight  years and fine upto  Rs.50,000.  As stated in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill, the enhancement of the punishment will
result in the serious offences  becoming  triable by Court  of  Sessions and the Court will be required to give notice to the State Government
thereby affording it an opportunity to oppose the  bail application under proviso to sub-section (1) of Section 439 of Cr.P.C, 1973.
14.2     During the examination of Clause 2 of the Bill by the Committee, the following suggestions/views were put forward:
14.2.1  It was observed that as per the proposed Section 14 any person caught with a forged passport was liable to get a punishment of
only upto five  years which was not sufficient keeping in mind the gravity of the offence. In that context, a  view was expressed that the
words “with or without valid   passport” as appearing in sub-section (b) of proposed Section 14,  should be deleted because if a person
had obtained a visa on a forged passport then it was a serious offence and hence, should not be considered a minor  infringement of the
law.
 
 
11
14.2.2  A point was raised regarding the difference in the connotation of the words “remains in India” and “stay in India” as used in
sub-sections (b) and (c) of Section 14, respectively.   It was sought to be known if there was any difference between the expressions or
they conveyed the same meaning.
14.2.3  Having noted the opinion of the Law Ministry that the proposed amendment would have the effect of making serious offences
triable by the Court of Sessions and obtaining of bail would become difficult under Section 437(3) read with Section 439(1) of  Cr.P.C.,
the Committee was not convinced with their view and was of the opinion  that there was a need to incorporate explicit provisions in the
Bill to put the matter  beyond any shadow of doubt.  In that context, it was suggested that the following may be considered for being
added at the end of proposed Section 14A:
(i)
        “Notwithstanding any provisions in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, the offences punishable under this Section
shall be triable by the Court of Sessions”. 
(ii) “No court shall grant bail to the accused without first affording an      opportunity to the Public Prosecutor to oppose the
bail, as provided in sub-section (1) of the Section 439, Cr.P.C., 1973”.
14.2.4  In the same context, another suggestion was put forward that a suitable provision may be incorporated in the Bill so as to
ensure that the accused persons were not able to secure bail before the trial was over.
14.2.5  Again in the same context, it was suggested that in addition to the punishment provided  in proposed Sections 14 and 14A, it
may be considered whether a ‘deportation’ clause could be added.  The idea was that the Court could, while announcing the
punishment, issue orders for deportation on completion of jail term as it was felt that such a provision could effectively tackle the
problem of large scale infiltration of foreigners into the country.
14.2.6  It was then suggested that a few Courts of Sessions should be designated as “Special Courts” for the speedy and exclusive trial
of the cases registered under the Foreigners Act, 1946.
14.2.7  A suggestion was made for laying down a specific time frame for the disposal of the cases registered under the Foreigners Act,
1946 so as to ensure that trials under the Act did not drag on indefinitely.
14.2.8  So far as ‘burden of proof’ as envisaged in Section 9 of the principal Act was concerned, it was suggested that the said Section
may be broadened and made more elaborate so as to specifically provide that the onus of burden of proof would lie, in all cases of
violation/contravention of order, rules, etc. incorporated in  Section 3 of the principal Act, on the accused persons.
14.2.9  A suggestion was made that in view of the proposed Section 14B relating to penalty for abetment  of any offence punishable
under the proposed Sections 14 and 14A, it should be examined whether any consequential amendments would be required in Section
13 of the principal Act relating to attempts, abetment, etc. to contravene the provisions of the Act. 
14.2.10  Subject to the above the  clause was adopted.

12.0     Then the Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill.
Clause 2
12.1        Clause 2 of the Bill seeks to amend Section 14 of Foreigners Act, 1946.  This section inter alia  provides for punishment which may extend to 5 years  and
fine for contravention of provisions of the Act or of any order made thereunder, or of any direction given in pursuance of that Act or such order.  The amendment
proposes to classify offences under the Foreigners Act, 1946 and to provide for punishment depending on the gravity of offence committed.   It is also proposed that
the maximum punishment under the Foreigners Act, 1946 may be upto 8 years and fine upto  Rs.50,000.  As stated in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the
Bill, the enhancement of the punishment will result in the serious offences  becoming triable by Court  of  Sessions and the Court will be required to give notice to
the State Government thereby affording it an opportunity to oppose the  bail application under proviso to sub-section (1) of Section 439 of the Cr.P.C, 1973.
12.2     During the examination of clause 2 of the Bill by the  Committee, the following suggestions/views were put forward.
 
25
 
12.2.1
    It was observed that as per the proposed Section 14 of  the Bill, any person caught with a forged passport was liable to get a
punishment of only upto 5 years which was not sufficient keeping in mind the gravity of the offence. In that context, a  view was proposed that
Section 14,  should be deleted because if a person had obtained a visa on a forged passport then it was a serious offence and hence, should not
be considered a minor  infringement of the law.
12.2.2  Another view expressed was regarding the difference in the connotation of the words “remains in India” and “stay in India” as used in
sub-sections (b) and (c) of Section 14, respectively.   It was sought to be known if there was any difference between the expressions  or they
conveyed the same meaning.
12.2.3  Having noted the opinion of the Law Ministry that the proposed amendment would have the effect of making serious offences triable
by the Court of Sessions and obtaining of bail would become difficult under Section 437(3) read with Section 439(1) of the Cr.P.C., the
Committee was not convinced with the view of the Law Ministry and  was of the opinion  that there was a need to incorporate explicit
provisions in the Bill to put the thing beyond any shadow of doubt.  In that context, it was suggested that the following may be considered for
being added at the end of proposed Section 14A:
 
(i)
                 “Notwithstanding any provisions in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, the offences punishable under this Section shall be triable
by the Court of Sessions”. 
(ii)                “No court shall grant bail to the accused without first affording an opportunity to the Public Prosecutor to oppose the bail, as
provided in sub-section (1) of the Section 439, Cr.P.C., 1973”.
12.2.4  In the same context, another suggestion was put forward that  a suitable provision may be incorporated in the Bill so as to ensure that
the accused persons were not able to secure bail before the trial was over.
12.2.5  Again in the same context, it was suggested that in addition to the punishment proposed in amended Sections 14 and 14A, it may be
considered whether a ‘deportation’ clause could be added.  The idea was that the Court could, while announcing the punishment, issue orders
for deportation on completion of jail term as it was felt that such a provision could effectively tackle  the problem of large scale infiltration of
foreigners into the country.
12.2.6  It was then  suggested that  a few  Courts of Sessions should be designated as “Special Courts” for the speedy and exclusive trial of the
cases registered under the Foreigners Act, 1946.
12.2.7  Another suggestion was for laying down a specific time frame for the disposal of the cases registered under the Foreigners Act, 1946 so
as to ensure that trials under the Act did not drag on indefinitely.
12.2.8  So far as ‘burden of proof’ as envisaged in Section 9 of the principal Act was concerned, it was suggested that the said Section may be
broadened and made more elaborate so as to specifically provide that the onus of burden of proof would lie, in all cases of
violation/contravention of order, rules, etc. incorporated in  Section 3 of the principal Act, on the accused persons.
12.2.9  A suggestion was made that in view of the proposed Section 14B relating to penalty for abetment   of any offence punishable under the
proposed Sections 14 and 14A, it should be examined whether any consequential amendments would be required in Section 13 of  the
principal Act relating to attempts, abetment, etc. to contravene the provisions of the Act.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
 
 
            Contravention of the provisions of the Foreigners Act, 1946, or of any order made thereunder, or of any direction given in pursuance  of that Act 
or such order, is punishable with imprisonment which may extend   to five  years and fine.  The cases under the Foreigners  Act are cognizable,
non-bailable  and triable by the  first class Magistrate.  However, the accused persons  arrested  under the said Act  very often manage to obtain bail under
section  437 of the Code of Criminal   Procedure.   Further, the  Act also not classify the violation   of the various provisions of the Act   or order  made
thereunder or of any direction given in pursuance of that Act or such order and only provides for punishment   which may extend  to  five years and fine. 
The quantum of punishment for various offences is left to the discretion of the court.  It is proposed  to classify offences committed.   It is also proposed 
that the maximum  punishment under the Foreigners Act may be  upto eight years  and fine upto Rs. 50,000.  The enhancement of punishment will have
the following effects, namely:-
 
(i)
                 serious  offences will become triable by the Court of Sessions; and
 
(ii)        the court will be required to give notice to the State Government thereby affording it an opportunity to oppose the bail application under
proviso to sub-section (l) of section 439 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.
 
(iii)       The Bill seeks to achieve the aforesaid  objects.
