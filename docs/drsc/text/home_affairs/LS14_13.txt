6. In its background note supplied to the Committee the Ministry of. Law, Justice 
and Company Affairs explained the necessity for amending section 28 of the Indian 
Contract Act, 1872 as under :-
(i) The Law Commission of India has recommended, in its 97th Repon, 
amendment in section 28 of the Indian Contract Act. 1872. The subject 
was taken up by the Law Commission on its own having regard to its 
imponan ce and relevance. The Report addresses itself to an anomalous 
situation that prevails under the present law in regard to prescriptive 
clauses in contracts. Such clauses .tim at extinguishing rights under a 
contraCt on the expiry of a specified period. The subject is of great 
importance from the point of view of economic justice, avoidance of 
hardship to consumers and symm etry of the law. 
(ii) Under section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, an agreement which 
limits the time within which a party to an agreement may enforce his 
rights in a court is void to that extent. But the Section does not 
invalidate an agreement which provides that if the rights are not enforced 
at the end of a specified period, such rights shall cease to exist. lt may 
be stated that such stipulatio ns are frequently found in agreements entered 
into in the course of business. 
(iii) The relevant part of section 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 reads as 
under 
"Every agreement, by which any party thereto is restricted 
absolutely from enforcing his rights under or in respect of any 
contract by the usual legal proceedings in the ordinary tribunals, 
or which limits the time within which he may thus enforce his 
rights, is void to that extent." 
\ 
(iv) In such a case, which went upto the Supreme Court, a clause in an 
insurance policy provided that all benefits under the insurance policy shall 
be forfeited if a suit was not brought within a specified period. The 
clause was held to be valid (AIR 1976 SC 287). This judgement 
expressly approved the decisions of the High Courts taking a similar view 
(AIR 1914 Born 223, AIR 1924 Cal. 186 and AIR 1974 Bom. 228). 
These cases hold that it is only when a period of limitation is curtailed 
that section 28 of the Contract Act comes into operation. As was 
observed in a Bombay case, 'lf (Section 28) does not come into 
operation when the (contractu al) term spells ou t an extinction of the right 
of the plaintiff to sue or spells out the discharge of the defendants from 
all liability in respect of the claim.' (AIR 1951 Born. 347).

The Law Commission has, while discussing the subject, observed that 
what is hit by section 28 is :!In ugrecmcnt relinquishing the remedy only 
i.e. where the time-limit specified in the agreemcm is shorter than the 
period of limitation provided by the Limitation Act. A distinction is 
assumed to ex.ist between "remedy" and "right" and this distinction is the 
basis of the present position under which a clause barring a remedy is 
void, but a clause extinguishing the rights is valid. This approach may 
be sound in theory but, in pmeticc, it causes serious hardship and might 
even be abused. 
The Report of the Law Corrunission has been studied and it is felt that 
present legal position with respect 10 prescriptive clauses in contract 
cannot be defended as a ma11cr of justice, logic and convenience. It 
harms the interest of the consumer dealing with big Corporations and 
causes serious hardship to those who are economically disadvantaged. 
(vii) By the Indian Contract (Amendment) Bill 1992, which has been 
introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 27th July, 1992, it is proposed 10 
amend Section 28 of the Jmlian Contract J\ct, 1872 so as to r~ndcr 
invalid such contractual clauses in agreement which purport to extinguish 
the rights of a party on the expiry of a specified tem1 or which 
discharge any party from any liability under the contract on such expiry.
