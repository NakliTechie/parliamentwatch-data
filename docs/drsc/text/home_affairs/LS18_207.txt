INTRODUCTION
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having
been authorized by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Two Hundred
Seventh Report on Action Taken by Government on the Recommendations/Observations contained in the
Two Hundred Second Report on Demands for Grants (2017-18) of the Ministry of Development of North
Eastern Region (DoNER).
2. The Two Hundred Second Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Home Affairs was presented to the Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of the Lok Sabha on 21st March,
2017. After presentation of the Report, the copies thereof were sent to the Ministry of DoNER requesting
them to furnish Action Taken Notes on the recommendations of the Committee contained in the Report.
The Ministry of DoNER furnished the English Version of the Action Taken Notes on the above mentioned
202nd Report to the Committee on 9th October, 2017 and the Hindi Version on 16th October, 2017.
3. The Committee has noted that the Statement on the status of implementation of the recommendations
contained in the said Report of the Committee has been laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha on 28th
December, 2017 and in Lok Sabha on 27th December, 2017 in pursuant to the direction of Chairman,
Rajya Sabha dated 24th September, 2004.
4. The Committee considered the draft Two Hundred Seventh Report and adopted the same at its
meeting held on 8th February, 2018.
5. The Recommendations/Observations of the Committee are printed in bold letters.

REPORT
The Action Taken Report of the Committee deals with the action taken by the Ministry of
Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred
Second Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2017-18) of the Ministry of DoNER. The Two
Hundred Second Report was presented to Rajya Sabha /laid on the Table of Lok Sabha, on 21st March,
2017.
2. Action Taken Notes as received from the Ministry of DoNER in respect of the recommendations
contained in the Two Hundred Second Report have been categorized as follows:
Chapter I: The Recommendations/Observations, which have been accepted by the Government:
Para Nos. 2.1.7, 2.1.10, 2.3.5, 2.4.10, 2.7.2, 2.8.2, 2.9.7, 2.10.7, 2.11.12, 2.12.5,
2.18.6, 3.1.4, 3.11.5 and 3.13.4 (of Two Hundred Second Report)
Total recommendations - 14
The Committee is pleased to note that the Ministry has accepted its forteen recommendations
as mentioned in Chapter-I of the Report.
Chapter II: The Recommendations/Observations on which the Committee does not desire to
pursue the matter in view of the Government's reply: Para Nos. 2.2.3, 2.4.3, 2.13.3,
2.16.5 and 3.14.3 (ibid)
Total recommendations - 5
The Committee is convinced with the explanations furnished by the Ministry and, therefore,
does not want to pursue the recommendations further.
Chapter III: The Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the Government
have not been accepted by the Committee: Para Nos. 2.1.4, 2.4.9, 2.5.4, 2.7.9,
2.11.11, 2.11.13, 2.14.3, 2.17.4, 3.1.3, 3.1.5, 3.2.5, 3.3.10, 3.12.4, 3.15.4, 3.15.7
and 3.16.2 (ibid)
Total recommendations - 16
The Committee expresses its concern over the non-implementation of its recommendations
contained in Chapter-III and desires that the Ministry should furnish convincing action taken steps
in respect of further comments thereon.
Chapter IV: The Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the Government
have not been received: Para Nos. 1.3.4, 2.2.2, 2.7.4, 2.7.6, 2.15.6, 3.15.6 and
3.17.2 (ibid)
Total Recommendations - 7.
In respect of .......... recommendations in Chapter-IV , the Ministry has either furnished interim
reply or couched its response in vague terms. Therefore, the Committee desires that the Ministry should
furnish pointed and detailed Action Taken Notes in respect of the comments categorized in Chapters III
and IV in keeping with the spirit of the recommendation.
3. The details of the ATNs have been analyzed in the succeeding chapters.

1.1 OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF DEMANDS FOR GRANTS
Recommendation
1.1.1 The Committee takes strong exception to the low utilisation of allocation under Social and
Infrastructure Development Fund (SIDF) i.e. ` 41.57 crore in 2014-15 and ` 49.88 crore in 2015-16
against allocation of ` 170.00 crore. Against the total allocation of ` 170.00 crore in BE 2016-17 and
` 100.00 crore in RE, there is utilisation of ` 1.32 only. The Committee is constrained to express its
anguish over the dismal utilization of the fund which must have affected the performance of the schemes.
The Committee, therefore, desires that the implementing line Ministry should ensure better implementation
of the schemes. (Para 2.1.7)
Action Taken
1.1.2 The expenditure under the scheme increased towards the end of Financial Year 2016-17 from
`1.32 crore (as on 13th February, 2017) to ` 47.70 crore (by 31st March, 2017). The main reason for
slow pace of utilization of funds in the scheme was non-submission of alternative projects by the State
Government after scrapping of earlier identified projects.
1.1.3 The State Government submitted proposals against scrapped projects and these were referred back
with a request to submit big proposals having envelope size of more than ` 20.00 crore in conformity
with the recommendations of DRPSC. The State Governments revisited its proposals and resubmitted
projects of higher estimated cost with envelope size of `15 crore and more, except few ones and
requested to sanction these projects, considering their strategic locations and importance. Accordingly,
PAC meeting was convened and projects were identified against the balance available fund. Now, no
balance fund is available to sanction any projects under the SIDF. The scheme shall be closed with
completion of identified/ongoing projects. All the projects have been identified against the available
amount of `586.20 crore under the SIDF.
Recommendation
1.1.4 The Committee is constrained to observe that the overall utilisation in 2016-17 as on 13.2.2017
is 77.99 % of the total outlay of 
` 2491.21 crore at RE stage despite the fact that the Ministry of DoNER
has taken several measures for speeding up the implementation of projects/schemes. This is a sorry state
of affair which needs to be corrected to ensure timely implementation of projects.
(Para 2.1.10 ibid)
Action Taken
1.1.5 It may be observed that as a departure from previous year, this Ministry got enhanced allocation
in RE stage. Even though 77.99 % of the total outlay was spent till 13.02.2017, this Ministry spent 99.57
% of the Plan RE by the end of the year as on 31.03.2017.

1.1.6 As far as the NLCPR Scheme is concerned, 100% funds have been utilized against the funds
earmarked under the Scheme in 2016-17.
1.2 TOTAL REVENUE AND CAPITAL OUTLAY
Recommendation
1.2.1 The Committee, while noting the utilization of overall funds allocated in RE 2016-17 under
Revenue Head, is constrained to express its displeasure that under Major Head No. 2250 pertaining to
Capacity Building & Publicity only 23.8% of the fund allocated has been utilized till December, 2016
which is quite low. The Committee feels that the inability of the Ministry to utilize the Revenue funds
is unjustifiable. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministry must ensure that no
funds should go unspent under the Revenue Head during Financial Year 2016-17 and also during the
next fiscal year. (Para 2.3.5 ibid)
Action Taken
1.2.2 The recommendations of the Committee are noted. It is submitted that the expenditure under the
Capacity Building & Publicity Scheme was initially less. In this regard, it is also submitted that the
Capacity Building/Skilling of unemployed youths component of the erstwhile CB&TA scheme along with
all related project proposals received by Ministry of DoNER was transferred to the Ministry of Skill
Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE). After due processes, a budget of ` 25.00 crore was also
transferred to MSDE in the month of December by LoA. It took some time for the MSDE to sanction
projects and release funds from the transferred funds. The Ministry of DoNER continued to fund
projects for training/capacity building of in-service government functionaries of the State Governments
only.
1.2.3 During Financial Year 2016-17, under the Advocacy & Publicity Scheme, ` 25.30 crore was
released to State Governments of the North Eastern Region, various Departments of the Central Ministries/
PSUs/Universities and Colleges for purchase of prototypes under STINER, organizing Craft Bazaars and
Exhibitions, Seminars and Workshops etc.
1.2.4 However, at the end of the Financial Year 2016-17, the total expenditure was ` 59.23 crore out
of total allocation of ` 70.19 crore at RE Stage (after re-appropriation). Similarly, the total allocation in
RE for Revenue Section (after re-appropriation) was ` 2050.42 crore out of which Rs 2034.88 crore
was spent in 2016-17.
Recommendation
1.2.5 The Committee notes that there is a cumulative shortfall of 
` 758.62 crore or 26.6% under the
Revenue Head and that of ` 413 crore or 36.8% under the Capital Head as compared to the projected
amount. The Committee is surprised to observe such huge shortfalls and wonders how the Ministry would
be able to implement its projects and activities with this deficit. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Ministry of DoNER should streamline its expenditure as per the amounts allocated and pursue
the Ministry of Finance for granting additional allocations, wherever necessary, at RE Stage of
2017-18. (Para 2.4.10 ibid)

4
Action Taken
1.2.6 The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. The expenditure of the Ministry of
DoNER was streamlined as per the allocations in the Revised Estimates 2016-17. Ministry of Finance
will be requested to grant additional allocations, wherever necessary, at RE Stage of 2017-18. The
recommendations of the Parliamentary Standing Committee have been forwarded to Ministry of Finance
for appropriate action.

Recommendation
1.3.1 The Committee notes the capital expenditure during 2015-16 to the tune of 
` 149.96 crore and

1.5 CAPACITY BUILDING AND PUBLICITY
Recommendation
1.5.1 The Committee takes note that the guidelines of the scheme have been revised to widen the sphere
and scope of the scheme. However, the allocated budget to the tune of 
` 52.00 crore in BE 2017-18 has
been reduced from BE and RE of 2016-17. The Committee agrees with the contention of the Ministry
that this allocation would not be sufficient for the various initiatives that the Ministry intends to

undertake. Therefore, the Committee recommends that the Government should enhance budgetary allocation
for the scheme at the RE stage so that the set target under the scheme is achieved without any financial
hindrance. The Committee also recommends that the projects sanctioned for 2017-18 should be taken up
in right earnest and the physical targets should be achieved. All the initiatives proposed for 2017-18 may
be taken up with meticulous planning and efforts should be made to avoid time and cost overruns.
(Para 2.9.7 ibid)
Action Taken
1.5.2 Capacity Building & Technical Assistance: During year 2016-17, the Capacity Building/Skilling of
unemployed youths component of the erstwhile CB&TA scheme along with all related project proposals
received by Ministry of DoNER and related budget was transferred to the Ministry of Skill Development
and Entrepreneurship (MSDE). The Ministry of DoNER continued to fund projects for training/capacity
building only for in-service government functionaries of the State Governments and upto the end of
August, 2017 a sum of ` 1.37 crore only has been released for Capacity Building projects of the Ministry.
1.5.3 During the Financial Year 2017-18, upto August, 2017 under the Advocacy & Publicity Scheme
` 11.05 crore has been released to various Departments of the Central Ministries/ PSUs/ Universities and
Colleges for organizing Film Bonanza, Craft Bazaars and Exhibitions, Seminars and Workshops etc.
1.5.4 In this context the BE allocation under sub-head Livelihood, Skill and Capacity Building for
Capacity Building & Publicity in the BE of FY 2017-18 is reasonable and may be enhanced in the RE
as per actual requirement. New projects already sanctioned for the year 2017-18 are being taken up in
right earnest so that the physical targets are fully achieved, without time and cost overruns.
Further Recommendation
1.5.5 The Committee observes the paltry release of funds of ````` 1.37 crore for Training/Capacity
Building projects for in-service government functionaries of the State Governments upto the end
of August, 2017 and also notes that no reason/rationale for the same has been provided. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry must impress upon the State Governments
to prepare and undertake more projects for training/capacity building of government functionaries.
1.6 NER LIVELIHOOD (EAP) PROJECT (NERLP)
Recommendation
1.6.1 The Committee is constrained to express its displeasure that against the estimated expenditure of
` 220.00 crore only ` 150.00 crore was earmarked in BE 2016-17, thus leaving a fund gap of ` 70.00
crore. Against this, fund to tune of ` 24.00 crore only was allocated at RE stage. The resultant is reflected
in the outcome of the scheme. As against the target of three lakh households 2.80 lakh rural households
were covered and against the target of 28,000 SHGs, 24,139 SHGs were formed and out of that 19,787
SHGs received either revolving fund or livelihood fund. The Committee feels that had the scheme been
infused with sufficient fund, the set targets would have been achieved. The Committee is constrained to
point out that against the target of 8400 SHGs only 300 SHGs received bank credit due to insufficient
network of bank branches in Nagaland and their operational limitations. The Committee feels that such

6
a disturbing trend in the implementation of the scheme is not likely to be reversed in the financial year
2017-18 with an allocation of ` 150.00 crore against the projected demand of ` 280.00 crore for
2017-18. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the impediments in achieving the cherished objectives
of NER Livelihood (EAP) Project (NERLP) must be removed and the government must consider allocating
fund as per the requirement of the scheme. Concerted efforts must be taken to enhance the credibility
of SHGs and to create a conducive banking environment. (Para 2.10.7 ibid)
Action Taken
1.6.2 T otal project cost for the programme is `827.00 crore as per prevailing exchange rate. Taking into
account the release of `144.95 crore, out of the budgetary allocation of `150.00 crore during the current
financial year i.e. 2017-18, the total amount released so far in the project is ` 557.94 crore. As of now,
the released amount comensurates with the expenditure estimated till September, 2017 by the North East
Livelihood Promotion Society (NELPS). Additional requirement of funds, if any, felt during the year
would be arranged at the stage of Revised Estimates through re-appropriation.
1.6.3 So far the formation of SHGs are concerned, the society has outperformed by creating 26186
SHGs by March, 2017 against the project target of 26000 SHGs.
1.6.4 For improving bank linkages, efforts are being taken to improve financial discipline and investment
capacity of the SHGs thereby increasing their credibility. While working on creating conducive banking
environment and increasing confidence of the banks on SHGs, steps are also being taken up to build
rapport with banks by engaging bank facilitators under the project.

is also planning to conduct such auction in collaboration with Spices Board in other NER
states like Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland so that the farmers' income can be enhanced.
(ii) Recently, NERAMAC in collaboration with MSTC (Metal Scrap Trading Corporation- a PSU
under the Ministry of Steel) and; CRWC (Central Railways Warehousing Corporation- a PSU

better price for their produce.

3.10 INFRASTRUCTURE CIVIL  AVIATION
Recommendation
3.10.1 The Committee understands that long-term measures like operationalization of non-operational

26
operational airports is a continuous process depending on the traffic demand and other operational
