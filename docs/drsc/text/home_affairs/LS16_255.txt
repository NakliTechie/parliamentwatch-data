INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home 
Affairs, having been authorized by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby 
present this Two Hundred Fifty Fifth Report on Action Taken by Government on the 
Recommendations/Observations Contained in the Two Hundred Fifty Second Report on 
Demands for Grants (2025-2026) of  the Ministry  of Home Affairs.

1.1 Overall Assessment of Demand for Grants (2025-2 6) of the MHA 
Recommendation/Observation 
1.1.1 The Committee observes, based on the above-me ntioned information, that the actual 
Budget Estimate (BE) for 2025-26 is lower than the projected BE for 2025-26 across several key 
areas, reflecting a more conservative allocation th an initially anticipated. The total budget has 
been reduced from ₹184,236.94 crores (projected BE 2025-26) to ₹160391.06 crores (actual BE 
2025-26), marking a 12.94% decrease. Within the Cen tral Armed Police Forces (CAPFs), actual 
allocations fall short of projections, with reducti ons observed across the CRPF, NSG, BSF, 
ITBP, CISF, Assam Rifles, and SSB. This indicates a  recalibration of expenditure priorities. 
Similarly, significant downward revisions have been  made to allocations for the Intelligence 
Bureau (IB), NATGRID, Central Police Organizations (CPOs) and Delhi Police. Police 
infrastructure projects, particularly CAPF building  projects, have experienced a substantial 
reduction from ₹6,403.02 crores to ₹4,038.70 crores , which may potentially impact construction 
of new buildings. The Committee notes that such red uctions may adversely affect critical 
schemes under these heads and may slow down moderni zation programs as well as hinder the 
execution of developmental projects, particularly i n border areas. Hence, the Committee 
recommends that the MHA may take up the matter with  the Ministry of Finance to seek 
increased allocations under these heads in alignmen t with their original projections. This will 
ensure that the essential developmental schemes are  not affected due to insufficient funding. The 
Committee hopes that funds may be demanded for various developmental and expansion projects 
at the RE stage depending on the utilization pattern. 
(Para 2.5.3 of   252
nd   Report)  
Action Taken  
1.1.2  Recommendations of the committee have been noted. The matter will be taken up with the 
Ministry of Finance at RE 2025-26 Stage, if the additional funds will be required.  
Recommendation/Observation 
1.1.3 The Committee observes that the significant r eduction in the Grant-in-Aid to States for 
Home Guards from ₹566.01 crores in RE 2024-25 to ₹3 0.01 crores in BE 2025-26 is based on 
the expectation that all backlog claims will be cle ared in FY 2024-25. However, the Committee 
emphasizes the need to reassess the funding require ments for new claims and ensure that States 
are adequately supported to maintain the operationa l efficiency of Home Guards. Similarly, with 
respect to the Holistic Development of Islands in U nion Territories, where the allocation has 
been reduced from ₹88 crores in RE 2024-25 to a nom inal ₹0.01 crore in BE 2025-26, the 
Committee recommends expediting the preparation of EFC papers and project reports to ensure 
timely approval and funding for developmental activ ities in the UTs. The Committee further 
recommends closely monitoring expenditure trends an d engaging with stakeholders to ensure 
that reductions across various components do not ad versely impact service delivery or 
developmental goals.

linguistic inclusivity in governance. The Committee  appreciates that the Budget under the head 
“official language” has been increased to the tune of more than 20% which may enable the 
Ministry to prioritize engaging technical expertise  and ensuring seamless integration with 
Government communication systems.

Demand  No. 57 – Transfers to Delhi 
Further Recommendation 
The Committee notes with concern that neither NDMC nor MCD regulates or 
licenses private hostels and coaching centres in De lhi, despite the fact that a large number 
of students from across the country rely on such fa cilities. The absence of any survey, 
licensing framework, or regulatory oversight raises  serious issues relating to safety, 
adequacy of living conditions, and well being of st udents. The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Ministry of Home Affairs, in co ordination with the GNCTD and local
