by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Ninty-fourth Report on the Supreme Court Judges
(Salaries and Conditions of Service) Amendment Bill, 2002·.

Sabha in consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha referred ¨¨ theSupreme Court Judges (Salaries and Conditions of Service)

REPORT
 
            The Supreme Court Judges (Salaries and Conditions of Service) Amendment Bill, 2002 (See Annexure-I) seeks to
provide for addition of five years of service to the pensionable service in the case of those Judges of the Supreme Court who are
elevated directly from the Bar, to enable them to get the full pension, to the extent of the maximum ceiling of Rs.1,80,000 per
year.
 
2.0       The Supreme Court Judges (Salaries and Conditions of Service) Act, 1958 (41 of 1958) and the rules made thereunder
govern and regulate the salaries and other conditions of service including pension of the Judges of the Supreme Court.
 
2.1       Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts are placed in three categories for the purpose of computation of basic
pension1
:-
           
(i)            Part I       :   These Judges are elevated directly from the Bar.
           
(ii)            Part II     : These Judges are elevated from the Indian Civil Service 2.                                                        
           
(iii)            Part III   :   These Judges are elevated from the State Judicial Services.
 
3.         The Statement of Objects and Reasons appended to the Bill states -
“An advocate having practice of at least ten years in a High Court or in two or more such Courts in succession can
directly be appointed as a judge of the Supreme Court under the provisions of sub-clause (b) of clause (3) of article 124
of the Constitution.  As per the convention, no such advocate below the age of 55 years is normally considered for
appointment as a Judge of the Supreme Court.  The pension to a Part I Judge (who is elevated from the Bar) is payable in
accordance with the provisions of section 13 read with Part I of the Schedule to the Supreme Court Judges (Salaries and
Conditions of Service) Act, 1958 and accordingly, the maximum pension of Rs.1,80,000 per annum is payable to a Judge

of the Supreme Court after he renders a minimum service of 13 years (at the rate of Rs.14,630 for each completed year
of service on his retirement).  It is felt that Part I Judges can never have enough completed years of service entitling them
to earn full pension.  In the case of Judges not completing 7 years of minimum service, a fixed amount of Rs.64,030 per
annum is payable as pension.
 
A former Chief Justice of India, while forwarding a representation from one such Judge, suggested that provision be
made for grant of pension to such Judges by adding, about 7 to 10 years period to the tenure of service rendered in the
Supreme Court.
 
To remove anomaly between Part I and Part III Judges of the Supreme Court as regards their entitlement to pension and
to give incentive to the eminent advocates who are meritorious and talented to come forward to be appointed as Supreme
Court Judges, it is proposed to amend the said Act to provide five years added benefit of service to a Judge of the
Supreme Court for the purpose of his pension”.
 
4.         The Committee heard the Additional Secretary, Department of Justice, Ministry of Law and Justice in its meeting held
on 3 July 2002 wherein he inter-alia stated that in September 2000, the then Chief Justice of India forwarded an application from
Mr. Justice Kuldip Singh dated 8 August 2000 complaining of inequity in the matter of similarly placed Judges of the Supreme
Court in regard to pension (SeeAnnexure II).  He further stated that the matter was considered by the then Law Minister and
examined in consultation with related Ministries and found that there were parallel provisions in the Central Civil Service
(Pensions) Rules, 1972.  He cited Rule 30 of CCS (Pensions) Rules, which authorizes the Government to add a period, not
exceeding five years, to the pensionable service of an officer in the government whose service conditions require special
qualifications or professional experience.  It was noticed that members of the Bar, who are appointed under Article 124 (3)(b) of
the Constitution also come with specialized qualifications and professional experience and thus can be given benefit of adding
five years service for the purpose of pension.
 
5.0       The witness further stated, that in the States such as Punjab there is a provision which authorizes the Government to add
a period, not exceeding ten years, to the pensionable service of a Judicial Officer in view of his practice at the Bar.  This
provision coupled with Rule 30 of the Central Civil Service (Pension) Rules, 1972 led the Government to introduce the Bill.
 
5.1       He also informed that the proposed legislation will benefit three retired Judges, namely, Mr. Justice S.C. Roy, Mr.
Justice S.M. Sikri and Mr. Justice Kuldip Singh, a sitting Judge, Mr. Justice Hegde and all other such future appointees who
would be   appointed from the Bar. 
 
6.         It may be mentioned here that besides writing to the Law Minister, Mr. Justice Kuldip Singh petitioned the Supreme
Court in this regard which is still pending at the time of writing this report.
 
7.0       The Committee wanted to know as to what was the rationale behind the practice being followed that advocates of less
than 55 years of age are not appointed directly from the Bar to the Bench of the Supreme Court when the Constitution under
Article 124 provides for appointment of advocates with at least 10 years of practice at the Bar to the Supreme Court.
 
7.1       The Ministry, in its written reply, justified that keeping in view the guidelines prescribing the age limit of 40-45 years for
appointment as a Judge of the High Court and qualifications prescribed for a Judge of Supreme Court in Article 124 (3) (a)  & 
(b) of the Constitution, this convention helped in maintaining a sort of age parity between the Judges of the Supreme Court
appointed from the Bench and the Bar. 
 
8.0       The Committee observes that the judiciary itself has initiated the practice of appointing only those advocates who are not
less than 55 years of age to be a Judge of the Supreme Court whereas the Constitution has prescribed 10 years’ practice at the
Bar.  It, therefore, fails to comprehend as to why the Government now requires a legislation to wriggle out of the situation which
has been created due to the said practice.
 
8.1       After having heard the presentation of the Additional Secretary, Department of Justice on 3 July 2002, having gone
through the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill and background notes received from the same Department and also
holding  detailed discussion, the Committee has come to the conclusion that there is no principle involved in the proposed
legislative measure as it is meant to benefit a few Judges only. Besides, the Committee feels that when the petition is already
before the Supreme Court, legislative recourse should not be taken to legitimize the measure.  The Committee, therefore, feels
that the Government may explore other possibilities to achieve the objective of the Bill.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
 
            The Supreme Court Judges (Salaries and Conditions of Service) Act, 1958 (41 of 1958) was enacted to regulate the
salaries and certain conditions of service of the Judges of the Supreme Court.  An advocate having practice of at least ten years
in a High Court or in two or more such Courts in succession can directly be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court under
the provisions of sub-clause (b) of clause (3) of article 124 of the Constitution. As per the convention, no such advocate below
the age of 55 years is normally considered for appointment as a Judge of the Supreme Court.  The pension to a Part I Judge (who
is elevated from the Bar) is payable in accordance with the provisions of section 13 read with Part I of the Schedule to the said
Act, and accordingly, the maximum pension of Rs. 1,80,000 per annum is payable to a Judge of the Supreme Court after he

Service) Amendment Bill, 2002, recommends the introduction and consideration of the Bill in Lok sabha under article 117(1)
and (3) of the Constitution of India.
 
 
FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM
 
            Clause 2 of the Bill seeks to insert a new section 14A, after section 14 of the Supreme Court Judges (Salaries and
Conditions of Service) Act, 1958 so as to provide benefit of adding a period of 5 years to the service for pension of a Part I
Judge of the Supreme Court appointed from the Bar under article 124(3)(b) of the Constitution.  The proposal does not involve
any substantial financial implication as the number of Part I Judges of the Supreme Court is very few.  The expenditure in
respect of the Judges of the Supreme Court is to be borne by the Central Government and is charged on the Consolidated Fund
of India.  The approximate recurring expenditure of the Central Government would not be more than rupees 3 lakhs per annum.
 
            2. The Bill does not involve any other expenditure of either recurring or non-recurring nature.

2.         My appointment was in terms of Article 124 (3) (b) above.  I retired from the office on January 1, 1997.  My pension has
been fixed in terms of the Presidential Order dated January 13, 1999 issued by the Ministry of Law, Government of India. 
Under the said Order a Judge of the Supreme Court retiring with seven or more completed years of service, his pension is fixed
by applying the multiplier “Rs.14,630 X Number of years served” subject to the maximum of “Rs.1,80,000”.  I served as a
Judge for 8 years and 18 days.  My pension per annum has been fixed by multiplying Rs. 14,630/-by 8.
 
3.         The pension-fixation formula is on the face of it causing hardship to the appointees under Article 124 (3) (b).  It is
almost an established convention that no Advocate below the age of 55 years is considered for appointment to the Supreme
Court.  As such the Judges appointed under the Article 124 (3) (b)  can never have enough completed years  of service to claim
full pension.  On the other hand the appointees to the Supreme Court from the High Courts have invariably more than 10 years
of service as Judges of the High Courts to their credit and while fixing pension, their entire service as a High Court Judges is
also taken into consideration.  Almost all of them enjoy the maximum pension. 
 
4.         There are more than twenty brother Judges (retired) who were junior to me in the Supreme Court and have served as
Supreme Court Judge for lesser years than me but all of them are drawing more pension than me.  A rule which permits a higher
pension to a person who has served 3 years as a Supreme Court judge and a lesser pension to another who has served 8 years as
a Supreme Court judge is on the face of it arbitrary.
 
5.         I am the only retired Supreme Court Judge as at present, who was appointed direct from the Bar.  One way of doing
justice to the direct appointees to the Supreme Court is to add the eligibility period of 10 years as an advocate to the actual years
spent in the Supreme Court and pension fixed on that basis.
