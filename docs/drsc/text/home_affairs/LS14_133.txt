CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
1.0 The North Eastern Region of our country consists of eight States of Assam, Arunachal
Pradesh, Meghalaya, Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura and Sikkim which despite having hilly
and inhospitable terrains, porous borders, highly inadequate road links, extremely poor transport
connectivity, limited air services, inadequate rail links and weak telecommunication infrastructure,
has potential to become India’s economic power house. The panoramic setting of the lofty ranges
of the eastern Himalaya, snow peaked mountains, gurgling rivers from Tibet, exotic flora and
fauna, dense forests criss-crossed with network of trekking routes, diverse ethnic cultures and
heritage, the colorful tribal festivals, evergreen forests of Namdapha in Arunachal Pradesh, lush
green grasslands of Kaziranga, the perfect eco-system of Manas in Assam, make this region an
ideal place for tourists particularly for eco and adventurous tourists. Besides, being rich in natural
resources and an energy plus region in terms of hydropower, this region has immense potential to
develop provided certain infrastructure bottlenecks are removed.
1.1 With an objective to convert the untapped potential of North East Region into an opportunity,
the Department of North East Region was formed in September, 2001 which was upgraded to the
status of Ministry of DoNER on 27th May, 2004 by the Government of India for faster socio-
economic development of the region. This Ministry rather than an administrative unit acts as a
facilitator between the Central Ministers/Departments and the State Governments of the North Eastern
Region including Sikkim in the economic development including removal of infrastructural
bottlenecks, provision of basic minimum services, creating an environment for private investment and
to remove impediments to lasting peace and security in the North Eastern Region.
1.2 The Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region seeks to achieve the following goals:
(i) Accelerating the pace of socio-economic development in the North Eastern Region;
(ii) Synergizing the efforts of Central/State Governments/Other Stakeholders for
balanced development of the Region;
(iii) Integrated and holistic planning for the region, taking into account the heightened
consciousness of the people of the region, their aspiration, needs and sensitivities;
(iv) Evolving a c ommon approach for all the agencies working for the development of
the region; and
(v) Formulating appropriate policies for the rapid development of the region.
2.0 The Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, mandate the Ministry of
DoNER with the following functions:—
(i) Matters relating to the planning, execution and monitoring of developmental
schemes and projects of North Eastern Region including those in the sectors of
Power, Irrigation, Roads and Communications;
(ii) Hill Area Development Programme and Border Area Development Programme in
North Eastern Region;

(iii) Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR);

2.1 The Ministry implements the work mandated in the Allocation of Business Rules, 1961,
through the formulation of appropriate policies, and implementation of specific Plan Programmes.
The North Eastern Council, Shillong also implements various Plan Schemes in the region.
2.2 The North Eastern Council (NEC), Shillong, an organization under the Ministry, is the
Regional Planning Body for the region. It was set up under the NEC Act, 1971 for securing the
balanced development of the North East. The Minister in charge of the Ministry of Development
of North Eastern Region is the ex-officio Chairman of NEC. Its Members include the Governors
and the Chief Ministers of the eight Member States and three Members nominated by the President
of India.
3.0 In the Tenth Plan period, the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER)
had been implementing the following Central Plan Schemes:
(a) Advocacy and Publicity,
(b) Technical Assistance and Capacity Building,
(c) Assistance to Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC),
(d) Assistance to North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi).
3.1 Besides the above, the following Schemes are also under implementation in the form of
Assistance to the State Plans:
(i) Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR)
(ii) Plan Schemes of the North Eastern Council (NEC)
(iii) Infrastructure development projects under the special package for Bodoland
Territorial Council
(iv) Development of Cities and Roads in the North Eastern States (Externally Aided
Projects)
3.2 Since 1998-99, all non-exempted Ministries/Departments of Government of India are
required to earmark at least 10% of their Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the development of
North Eastern States for creation of both economic and social infrastructure such as Power, Roads
and Bridges, Education, Health, Water Supply and Sanitation, etc. in the region. The unutilized
portion of the 10% Plan allocation made by Ministries/Departments is credited at the end of each
financial year in a reserve fund in the Public Account titled ‘Central Resource Pool for Development
of North Eastern Region’ and in administrative parlance known as the Non-Lapsable Central Pool
of Resources (NLCPR). The funds from this Pool are budgeted for financing new infrastructure
projects/schemes in the region under the NLCPR Plan Scheme.

3
3.3 The NLCPR is meant for infrastructure development in almost all the major socio-economic
development sectors. For this purpose, projects are sanctioned for State sector as well as Central
sector. However, the funds available under the Central Pool are not meant to supplement the normal
Plan programmes either of the State Governments or Union Ministries/Departments/Agencies.
4.0 The Ministry also supervises the work of its three enterprises, viz, North Eastern
Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd. (NEHHDC), North Eastern Regional
Agricultural Marketing Corporation Ltd. (NERAMAC) and North East Development Finance
Corporation (NEDFi).
5.0 While the respective Ministries/Departments are responsible in respect of subjects allocated
to them, the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region coordinates with various Ministries/
Departments of Government of India and the State Governments for various development and
welfare programmes/schemes/activities in North Eastern Region.
6.0 Following the decision of the NEC, eight Special Sectoral Summits have been organized in
different sectors. These sector specific summits have helped in sensitizing both the Central
Government and the States to improve delivery systems and focus attention on the Region and its
issues. The Ministry is actively pursuing the progress of implementation of the recommendations
of these Summits.

7.4 On being asked as to why the development is not visible in North-East Region in spite of
funds being given through different sources, the Secretary, NEC stated as under:
“The whole question of the visibility of what is not taking place in the North-East, I think,
the dimensions are basically two. When we talk of development visibility, I think, there has
been some change. But the requirement of funds that we have done through and we are doing
the Vision-2020 analysis; it came to a staggering amount of Rs. 25,000 crore even to catch
up with the present national per capita income. So, if we have to catch up with this, we
require an investment of Rs. 25,000 crore. But, why is the North-East not getting the visible
sign is that whereas other States are able to attract substantial funds from the private sector,
in the North-East, the only source of funding is the Government source of funding.”
7.5 The Committee is concerned that even though the Ministry of DoNER made a
projection of Rs. 2922 crore for the current year, the actual allocation made was Rs. 1455
crore under plan scheme. The Committee has been given to understand that due to the
reduction in the allocation by almost 50%, many projects would suffer. The Ministry will not
be able to implement the schemes for the development of the region. The Committee,
therefore, strongly recommends that the allocation to the Ministry be made as per its
requirement as it would be in the interest of the overall development of the North-East
Region.
7.6 The Committee is not convinced by the reasoning given by the Secretary, NEC on the
visibility aspect of the development. The component of private investment is a totally
different matter to be viewed in the proper context and perspective. First of all, the
outcome of the public investments should be visible and transparent. Then only a positive
message will go for private investment. The Committee appreciates that there are basic and
inherent problems in converting public investment into tangible results. That can only be
achieved if there is a sense of commitment among implementing agencies and the State
Governments. The Committee recommends that sincere efforts should be made to ensure
that whatever investment is made for the development of NER should yield concrete result
so much so that the outcome should manifest itself.

8.1 NLCPR which was created in 1998, is meant for accrual of the unspent balances of the
10 per cent allocation earmarked in their budget by various Ministries/Departments for North-
eastern region. The Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) is provided
budgetary allocation for sanction of infrastructure development projects in the North Eastern States
out of the NLCPR. The broad objective of the NLCPR scheme is to ensure speedy development
of infrastructure in the North Eastern Region by increasing the flow of budgetary financing for
specific infrastructure projects/schemes in the region.
8.2 There are 17 Ministries/Departments, particularly Petroleum and Natural Gas, Social Justice
and Empowerment which have been exempted from allocating the stipulated ten per cent of Gross
Budgetary Support (GBS) for the NE States. According to the Ministry of DoNER the following
criteria are followed for exempting a Ministry/Department from earmarking 10% of their GBS for
the North East:—
(a) Highly scientific Ministries /Departments with hardly any scope for work in the North
East;
(b) The Ministries /Departments having no direct involvement in the North East including
Sikkim such as the erstwhile Department of Ocean Development; and
(c) The Ministries in which case the expenditure in the North Eastern Region was ‘Nil’
during the last three years.
8.3 The Committee was also informed that Government of India does not provide any budgetary
Support to Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, as the Ministry generates its own resources.
The Ministry is therefore exempted from making provisions for North East. As regards Ministry
of Social Justice and Empowerment, all Divisions of the Ministry other than the Scheduled Castes
Division (SCD) do earmark 10% of their Plan allocations to the North Eastern region. In the case
of the Scheduled Castes Division, only 2% of its GBS is allocated to the North East (NE),
considering the fact that the Scheduled Castes constitute less than 2% of the NE population.
8.4 The Committee in its reports on Demands for Grants for 2006-07 and 2007-08
recommended that the other Ministries may also be taken out from the List of Exempted Ministries/
Departments. The Ministry in its ATR on the recommendation of the Committee contained in its
127th Report stated as below:
“After a thorough review with the Ministries concerned, the exemption of Department of
Bio-technology has been withdrawn and some of the Ministries which have some scope
available for expenditure in the North Eastern Region were asked to provide maximum
possible percentage of GBS of the North Eastern Region. The Ministries which are
surrendering large sums of money have been requested to revise the norms according to the
conditions of North Eastern States so that they are able to utilize the money allocated in
the year of allocation.”

9
8.5 The Ministry in its written reply further stated as under:
“The Ministry is in constant touch with other line Ministries and Departments to revise the
norms meeting the needs of North Eastern States so that allocated funds could be utilized
suitably. While some of the Ministries like Agriculture, Coal, Health and F . W. and Power
are yet to respond, the Ministries of Rural Development and Human Resource Development
have already changed the guidelines in favour of North Eastern States in respect of their
major projects namely Pradhan Mantri G rameen Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) and Sarva Shiksha
Abhiyaan (SSA).”
8.6 The Committee feels that the efforts made by Ministry of DoNER to persuade
Ministries/Departments to change their norms according to the conditions of the NER have
not yielded the desired results. The Committee recommends that more vigorous efforts
need to be made by the Ministry of DoNER so that more and more Ministries/Departments
change their norms in respect of their flagship programmes and spend the allocated amount
in the NER.
8.7 A glance at the allocation to NLCPR shows that the hike in BE 2008-09 over BE 2007-08
is Rs. 50 crore. Explaining about the projects proposed to be undertaken in 2008-09 under the
NLCPR scheme the Ministry stated as under:
“There is a net increase of Rs. 50 crore in the NLCPR Budget in BE 2008-09 which is less
than 10% of the BE 2007-08. It will help in partially meeting (the funds under NLCPR
are released in instalments) committed liability of about Rs. 1358.92 crore of ongoing
projects and some new projects in pipeline which will be sanctioned in 2008-09 and they
would need first instalment. The Ministry so far received 2008-09 priority lists of projects
only from two States. The priority lists are yet to be considered by the NLCPR Committee.
Therefore, at this stage projects to be taken up in 2008-09 are not known.”
8.8 Explaining about the projects that have not achieved targets, the reasons therefore and the
steps being taken in this regard, the Ministry have stated as under:
“There are 233  projects which State Governments could not complete in time schedule. The
delay in utilization of funds by the State Governments and limited working season are the
main causes for non completion of the projects in time schedule.”
8.9 Giving the details of the infrastructure projects taken up and completed in the last financial
year, the Ministry stated as under:
“Up to 29.02.2008 a total of 925  infrastructure projects at an estimated cost of Rs.6637.09
crore to NE States have been sanctioned which includes projects in Power  sector, Roads and
Bridges, Irrigation, Education, Health, Water Supply, etc. and Rs.5048.41 crore released for
their implementation. Out of this, State Governments reported completion of 426  projects.”

In order that the Scheme can address the legitimate demands from the States, the allocation
must be at least doubled. Keeping in view the requirement of funds for various projects

8.13 The Committee views with concern the fact that even though DoNER had projected
Rs. 1200 crore for NLCPR, only Rs.650 crore has been allocated for the current financial
year. The Committee also takes note of the fact that for NLCPR the projection was made
on the basis of performance during the last two years. The Committee further notes the

16.0 CONNECTIVITY
16.1 The biggest problem being faced by the North Eastern Region in realizing its economic
potential is the problem of connectivity. The poor connectivity through road, rail, air and water has
been one of the biggest stumbling blocks in the way of the fast development of the region. It is
mainly due to hilly terrain in most of the North Eastern States and high cost of aircraft operation
in the region. The road connectivity in the North-east region is much below the national average
on most parameters. The Railway network in the north-eastern part of the country is far more

25
deficient than the required level which is only 4% of the country’s railway network. Even some
of the states have zero level of rail network. The intra region air connectivity as well as
connectivity with the rest of the country is of great concern from the point of view of
development of the region. In the northeast region and other remote areas, the management of
airport infrastructure as well as air services is not considered commercially viable because of low
utilization and low fare structures, etc. Given the topography and inaccessibility of the region, the
need for air services is much greater.
16.2 The Ministry in its Annual Report has mentioned that the Union Government’s intention is
to invest about Rs.50,000 crore on the road sector in the north-east region over the Eleventh Five

programmes initiated by Ministry of DoNER and NEC. Regarding railway network, it has been
decided that the projects of the Railways designated as National Projects will be supported by
Government of lndia in the ratio of 75:25 where 25% will be given from the GBS of Railways and
75% from Government of India. It has also been decided to create a Fund for development of rail
infrastructure in the North East. In the case of air-connectivity, greenfield Airports are proposed
to be developed at Itanagar, Cheithu and Pakyong and two new Greenfield airports have been

21.0 POWER SECTOR

cater to the needs of the region as well as the other parts of lndia. But, still this potential of the
region has remained untapped.

“In the area of Hydro power, few new projects have been taken up but the thrust is on

improvement, Small Hydro Projects, Support for conduct of Load Flow Study through CEA

21.3 During the oral evidence before the Committee, the Secretary stated as under:

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE DEMANDS FOR GRANTS
The Committee is concerned that even though the Ministry of DoNER made a
projection of Rs.2922 crore for the current year, the actual allocation made was Rs.1455
crore under plan scheme. The Committee has been given to understand that due to the
reduction in the allocation by almost 50%, many projects would suffer. The Ministry will not
be able to implement the schemes for the development of the region. The Committee,
therefore, strongly recommends that the allocation to the Ministry be made as per its
requirement as it would be in the interest of the overall development of the North-East
Region. (Para 7.5)
The Committee is not convinced by the reasoning given by the Secretary, NEC on the
visibility aspect of the development. The component of private investment is a totally
different matter to be viewed in the proper context and perspective. First of all, the
outcome of the public investments should be visible and transparent. Then only a positive
message will go for private investment. The Committee appreciates that there are basic and
inherent problems in converting public investment into tangible results. That can only be
achieved if there is a sense of commitment among implementing agencies and the State
Governments. The Committee recommends that sincere efforts should be made to ensure
that whatever investment is made for the development of NER should yield concrete result
so much so that the outcome should manifest itself. (Para 7.6)
NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES (NLCPR)
The Committee feels that the efforts made by Ministry of DoNER to persuade
Ministries/Departments to change their norms according to the conditions of the NER have
not yielded the desired results. The Committee recommends that more vigorous efforts
need to be made by the Ministry of DoNER so that more and more Ministries/Departments
change their norms in respect of their flagship programmes and spend the allocated amount
in the NER. (Para 8.6)
The Committee views with concern the fact that even though DoNER had projected
Rs. 1200 crore for NLCPR, only Rs. 650 crore has been allocated for the current financial
year. The Committee also takes note of the fact that for NLCPR the projection was made
on the basis of performance during the last two years. The Committee further notes the
unspent balance lying with other Ministries. The Committee therefore recommends that the
allocation for NLCPR may be increased appropriately at RE stage to meet the requirement
of various projects proposed to be taken up by the Ministry. The Committee also
recommends that allocation may also be made from out of the unspent balance of various
Ministries. The Committee desires that the Ministry of DoNER may pursue the matter at
highest level for early release of the funds. (Para 8.13)
The Committee is constrained to observe that the State Governments could not
complete 233 projects within the time schedule mainly due to the delays in utilization of
funds and limited working seasons. The Committee is however not convinced by the reasons
35

36
for the backlog in completion of projects. The Committee strongly recommends that the
State Governments should strictly adhere to the time schedule in this regard. (Para 8.14)
NORTH-EASTERN COUNCIL
The Committee takes a critical view of the allocations made for NEC. The allocation
of Rs. 624 crore in BE 2008-09 as compared to the allocation of Rs. 600 crore in the
previous year, is in no way commensurate with the number of projects to be taken up by
NEC. Besides the new projects, the NEC has also several spilled over projects. The
Committee is in agreement with the view that it is difficult for NEC to fulfill its role as
a regional planning body due to financial constraints. The Committee therefore recommends
that the NEC may be provided with suitably enhanced allocation at RE stage so that
NEC projects which are facing difficulties due to shortage of funds do not get affected.
(Para 9.10)
ADVOCACY AND PUBLICITY
The Committee takes note of the reply given by the Ministry that media campaign
will be operational soon. The Committee would like to be apprised of the activities to be
undertaken under the media plan. The Committee recommends that efforts should be made
to achieve the objectives of the strategy in right earnest. The Committee is also of the view
that whatever steps that are being taken under ‘Advocacy and Publicity’ and its results are
still not visible. It feels that there must be inherent weaknesses and problems in the plans
being made. In the present age of globlisation, efforts should be made to bring the North-
East Region into the mainstream of the country at the earliest and for that Advocacy and
Publicity is the most effective medium. The Committee therefore recommends that renewed
thrust may be given on media campaign and it should be effectively used. (Para 10.9)
TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE AND CAPACITY BUILDING
The Committee is happy to note that 36 students have been selected for training in
Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL) and Air craft Maintenance Engineering (AME) and some
young persons have also been selected for airhostess training. The Committee however,
wishes to observe that these trainings are being held outside the North-Eastern region. If
the North-Eastern region has to be strengthened and the capacity for the region has to be
upgraded, such Institutes should be opened there instead of sending people outside for
training. The Committee, therefore, recommends that an early decision may be taken on
starting the Pilot Training Institute at Lilabari. Whatever problems that are coming in the
way be sorted out at the earliest and the Central Ministry may take initiative in this
regard. The Committee is of the view that there is huge potential for fashion designing and
fashion technology in the North East which is still to be tapped. The Committee reiterates
that a National Institute of Fashion Technology may be opened in the North East at the
earliest. The Committee is also of the view that there is a huge potential for sports persons
in the North East and this potential has not yet been tapped. To tap this potential, a
separate Sports Institute or Sports Academy is required in the North East. The Committee,
therefore, reiterates that a Sports Academy may be opened in the North Eastern Region.
(Para 11.7)
EXTERNALLY AIDED PROJECTS

With the implementation of this project, road connectivity in the North-east region will

improve significantly. The Committee, therefore, desir es that EFC/CCEA approval for the
project may be obtained at the earliest. The Committee feels that the project must take off
during 2008-09 and there should not be any delay in this regard. The Committee
recommends that follow up action on the report of the consultants on North Eastern States
Trade Investment Creation Initiative Project (NESTICI) may be taken immediately. Efforts
should be made to ensure that the implementation of the project commences during the
current year. The Committee observes that even though NER Livelihood Project (NERLP)

consultants for preparing the main project reports. The project is taking a very long time
to take off and by the time it is implemented, there will be cost overrun which may

made to ensure that NERLP is implemented during 2008-09. (Para 12.10)
NORTH EASTERN REGIONAL AGRICULTURAL MARKETING CORPORATION LTD. (NERAMAC)
The Committee recognizes the fact that the North East Region, though industrially
backward due to poor infrastructure, has great potential in terms of agriculture,
horticulture, floriculture. This potential of the region has remained unharnessed and here
the NERAMAC comes into the picture which could accelerate the growth in these sectors.
The Committee expresses its disquiet over the fact that the Ministry in general and
NERAMAC in particular are not able to tap the potential available in abundance and this

DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMME (BADP)
The Committee expresses its deep concern that even though the two programmes
i.e., Hill Area Development Programme (HADP) and Border Area Development Programme

Seventh report, the Cabinet Secretariat has not taken any steps in this direction. The issue

issue should be resolved on priority basis. (Para 14.4)
PROMOTION OF TOURISM AND HOSPITALITY
The Committee is of the considered view that the NE region has a great potential to
become an attractive tourist destination in the country particularly for eco and adventure
tourism. If the tourism potential of the region is to be fully tapped, sufficient infrastructure
and peaceful environment should be in place at the earliest. Moreover, tourism in NE
provides a large number of employment opportunities. The eco-tourism not only provides
livelihood for local communities and adds importance to local traditions and cultures,
but also generates substantial revenue for environmental conservation and management.
The Committee is pleased to note that in the proposed BE 2008-09, an amount of
Rs.9 crore has been allocated for Tourism which is an increase of 79.64% from the last
year. (Para 15.9)
The Committee appreciates the several initiatives including Media Campaign and
involving the private sector for development of the tourism sector, taken by the Ministry.
