R E P O R T
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having been
authorised by it, present this Sixty-second Report of the Committee.  The Report pertains to the Salaries, Allowances,
Leave and Pensions of  Officers and Servants of the Supreme Court Bill, 1994 *.  The Bill,  as per its Statement of
Objects and Reasons, seeks to rectify the serious anomalies that had crept into the pay scales of the employees  of  the
Supreme Court on account of various judgements and orders passed by the Courts  in  various Writ  Petitions filed by
the employees.
2.         Pursuant to the provisions of the Rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
Home Affairs, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha, referred§
 the Bill, as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 19 August, 1994 and
pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.
3.0       The Committee (1994-95) considered the Bill in its meeting held on 29 November, 1994 and decided to invite
views/suggestions of Employees’ Welfare Associations of the Supreme Court on the provisions contained in the Bill.
The Supreme Court Employees, Welfare Associations had submitted vide
 communication dated 28 November, 1994
views/suggestions on the various provisions of the Bill for consideration of the Committee and requested for providing
an opportunity to give oral evidence on the provisions of the Bill  before it. It was decided to hear the representatives of
the Associations at an appropriate stage.
3.1       While the Bill was still under consideration of the Committee, the Tenth Lok Sabha got dissolved and so did the
previous Committee.  
3.2       Meanwhile, the matter relating to service conditions of the employees of the Court was referred to the Fifth
Central Pay Commission.   
3.3       The Committee (1996-97), which was constituted on 1 August, 1996,   further  considered  the Bill in its meeting
held on 23 September 1996 and decided to ascertain the views of the Government regarding enactment of the Bill in its
present form in view of the fact that one of the terms of  reference of the Fifth Central  Pay Commission was to examine
and recommend scales of pay in respect of the employees of the Supreme Court.  In response thereto the Committee was
informed by the Department of Justice vide
 its communication dated 23 December, 1996 that since the employees of the
Supreme Court had been included in the terms of reference of the Fifth Central Pay Commission and the report was
expected shortly, the Committee may wait till the presentation of the report by the Commission to the Government, 
before taking further action on the Bill.

3.4       In the meantime, the Committee’s tenure came to an abrupt end consequent upon the dissolution of Eleventh
Lok Sabha on 4 December, .1997.
3.5              In 1997, the Fifth Central Pay Commission, in its report  (para 103.32), recommended as
follows :-
(a)        The entire question of conditions of service, including pay scales, allowances, etc. of the officers and
employees of the Supreme Court of India and the High Court of Delhi has to be decided in the manner
laid down in the Constitution of India, as interpreted in the various judgements of the highest court in the
land.
 
(b)     The Fifth  Central Pay Commission finds itself unable to make any  recommendations whatsoever for the
officers and employees of the Supreme Court of India and High Court of Delhi.
(c )    However, we have made our detailed recommendations on pay scales, allowances,
pensions, etc. with regard to different categories of Central Government employees.  We would be happy if
these recommendations are of any assistance to the Hon’ble Chief Justice of India and the Hon’ble Chief
Justice of the Delhi High Court, as background materials for basing their own decisions.
Before we close this discussion, we would like to conclude with one last respectful submission.  In case
the Hon’ble Courts decide to grant pay scales, allowances, etc. to their own employees that are at variance with
the pay scales, allowances, etc. of Central Government employees on the ground that their qualifications, duties
and responsibilities, are different, they may, if they so desire, modify the designations of such posts to make
these different from the designations used in the Central Government so as to avoid any confusion in the future.
3.6       The Committee (1998-99) was reconstituted on 5 June, 1998.  In the light of the recommendations of the Fifth
Central Pay Commission as contained in para 103.32 of their report, and also in view of the passage of a considerable
period of time since the introduction of the Bill, under reference, it was felt that the views of the Government with
regard to the enactment of the legislation may be ascertained so as to enable the Committee to proceed further. 
Accordingly, a reference was made to the Department of Justice on 21 July, 1998 to furnish the views of the
Government on the enactment of the Bill in its present form.  On 11 November, 1998 the Department informed that the
matter was under submission to the Cabinet and as and when a decision of the Cabinet was received, the same would be
conveyed to the Committee.
3.7       Finally, the Department vide
 their O.M. dated 21 December, 1998 informed that the Government had decided to
pursue the Bill further in the Parliament with necessary formal amendments as might be required due to passage of time.
3.8       In response thereto, the Department was requested vide
 O.M. dated 24 December 1998 to furnish a separate note
giving details of the proposed formal amendments to the Bill so that the Committee may be in a position to proceed
further with their examination.  The Department, vide their O.M. dated 4 February 1999, forwarded a note giving details
of the formal amendments (appendix)  to the Bill for consideration by the Committee.
3.9       The Committee’s tenure again came to an end consequent upon the dissolution of the Twelfth Lok Sabha on 26
April 1999 and the present Committee (1999-2000) was re-constituted on 31 December 1999.
3.10          The Department of Justice vide  Secretariat’s communication dated  13 January 2000 was requested to furnish a
detailed Background Note on the Bill, indicating therein inter alia in chronological order the various developments
which had taken place in the matter, including summation of the main contentions in the Writ Petitions filed by the
employees of the Court, judgements thereon, the present structure of salaries and allowances of the employees and
officers of the  Court and the formal amendments which the Government proposed to  make in the Bill, so that a
complete information was before the Committee while considering the Bill.
3.11     In the meantime, the Supreme Court Employees Welfare Associations were requested vide
 communication dated
20 January, 2000 to submit their updated memoranda on the provisions of the Bill for consideration of the Committee. 
 
3.12     The Department of Justice vide communication dated 24 January, 2000 forwarded the detailed Background Note
along with a statement containing inter alia the details of Writ Petitions filed by the Court employees, recommendations
of the Fifth Central  Pay Commission, salary structure of employees proposed to be given through the Bill.

4.0         The Committee heard Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs on the provisions of the Bill,  in the meeting held on
27 January, 2000.   The Committee in its meeting held  on 8 Febuary, 2000 decided to invite the representatives of  the
Supreme Court Employees Welfare Associations to hear their views in person before making recommendations on their
salaries, allowances, leave, pensions, etc.  
4.1       The Supreme Court Employees Welfare Association, the Supreme Court Shorthand Knowing Staff Welfare
Association and the Supreme Court Class IV Employees Welfare Association, submitted their updated memoranda  on 4
& 7 February, 2000, respectively.  
4.2       The Department of Justice was requested vide communication 14 February 2000 to furnish the para-wise
comments of the Government on the memoranda of the Associations.   The Department submitted their response vide
communication dated 23 March, 2000.
4.3         The representatives of the Supreme Court Employees’ Welfare Association, the Supreme Court Shorthand
Knowing Staff Welfare Association and the Supreme Court Class IV Employees Welfare Association appeared before
the Committee on 29 May, 2000 to tender their oral evidence on the various provisions of the Bill.  The Associations
also furnished their written responses to the points raised by the Members of the Committee during the course of their
evidence.
5.         The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill reads as under :-
Serious anomalies have crept into the pay scales of  the employees of Supreme Court of India on account
of  various judgements and orders passed by the Court in Writ Petitions filed by its employees.  These
distortions and anomalies in the pay structures of the staff  of Supreme Court are likely to cause serious
agitation amongst similarly placed Central Government staff. 
This might ultimately lead to large scale distortions in the pay structure of the Central Government
employees with attendant serious consequential financial implications.  The Government examined the
implications of the emerging distortions in the pay structures of Court employees and considered it appropriate
to enact suitable legislation to rectify the situation as that was the only remedy available to the Government.
6.         Article 146(2) of the Constitution provides as under :-
Subject to the provisions of any law made by Parliament
, the conditions of service of officers and
servants of the Supreme Court shall be such as may be prescribed by rules made by the Chief Justice of
India or by some other Judge or officer of the Court authorised by the Chief Justice of India to make
rules for the purpose:
Provided that the rules made under this clause shall, so far as they relate to the salaries,
allowances, leave or pensions, require the approval of the President
.
 
7.         In this connection  the observations  of  Shri T.T. Krishnamachari and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, made  in  the  course 
of  the   debates   in   the  Constituent  Assembly  on  Articles 146  and  229 of the Constitution, are relevant. Shri T.T.
Krishnamachari  observed as follows :-
“At the same time, Sir, I think it should be made clear that it is not the intention of the House or of the framers of
this Constitution that they want to create specially favoured bodies which in themselves become an Imperium in
Imperio, completely independent of the Executive and Legislature and operate as a sort of superior body to the
general body politic.”   ( C.A. Deb. Vol.VIII, 16 May – 16 June, 1949;p.389)
The submission of Dr. Ambedkar  is also extracted below :-
But it seems to me that there is another consideration which goes to support the proposition that we should retain
the phrase with the approval of the President and it is this.  It  is undoubtedly a desirable thing that salaries,
allowances and pensions payable to the servants of the State should be uniform and there ought not to be
material variations in these matters with regard to the civil service.  It is likely to create a good deal of heart
burning and might impose on the treasury an unnecessary burden.  Now if you leave the matter to the Chief
Justice to decide, it is quite conceivable – I do not say that it will happen – but it is quite conceivable that the
Chief Justice might fix the scales of allowances, pensions and salaries very different from those fixed for civil
servants who are working in other departments besides the Judiciary, and I do not think that such a state of
things is desirable thing.”      (ibid)

8.0       The Department of Justice has stated in their Background Note that the Chief Justice of India framed the
Supreme Court Officers and Servants (Conditions of Service and Conduct) Rules, 1961.  Under these Rules, the officers
and servants of the Supreme Court were placed at par with corresponding employees of the Central Government and the
pay scales were accordingly fixed on the standard Central Government pattern. 
8.1       The Department further stated that under the Delhi High Court Officers and Servants (Salaries, Allowances,
Leave and Pension) Rules, 1970, the employees of the Delhi High Court were getting the pay scales at par with the
corresponding staff of the Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi, which was following the standard Central
Government pattern.  However, the employees of the Delhi High Court got higher scales of pay by  taking matters to the
Courts which allowed their petitions.  This resulted in serious anomalies in the pay scales of the employees of the Delhi
High Court, the Department stated. 
8.2       The Department further informed that, in early, 1986, the Supreme Court employees decided to take advantage
of the anomalous higher scales of pay of the employees of the Delhi High Court and filed two Writ Petitions in the
Supreme Court praying that :-
(i)
                  They should be given higher pay scales than admissible to corresponding categories of employees in
Central Government because of special nature of work in the Supreme Court, and
(ii)                As an interim measure, parity should be accorded with Delhi High Court staff wherever the latter was
getting emoluments higher than the Supreme Court employees.
 
8.3       The Supreme Court passed a number of interim orders from 25 July, 1986 onwards and granted parity to
different categories of its employees with employees of the Delhi High Court, wherever the latter was getting higher pay
and allowances, the Committee was informed by the Department of Justice.
 
8.4       The Department then added that in February, 1989, a Group of Ministers was constituted to examine the
implications of the emerging distortions in the pay structures of Court employees due to Court decisions.  The Group of
Ministers recommended  that the only remedy available to the Government was to enact suitable legislation to rectify
the situation.  The Department stated that as per the basic scheme of the Constitution (Article 146),   Parliament is
competent to enact a law regulating the service conditions including those relating to pay, allowances, leave and
pensions of the officers and servants of the Supreme Court, to ensure that no anomalies in respect of these matters are
created vis-à-vis corresponding levels of other Government servants.  Accordingly, the Salaries, Allowances, Leave and
Pensions of Officers and Servants of the Supreme Court Bill, 1994 was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 19 August,
1994.                   
8.5       The Government, in the Background Note, further stated that one of the terms of reference of the Fifth Central
Pay Commission was to examine and recommend the scales of pay in respect of officers and servants of the Supreme
Court.  However, the Commission did not give any recommendation in respect of the employees of the Supreme Court,
as the Commission was of the view that the appointments and conditions of  service, including matters relating to
salaries, allowances, leave and pensions of the officers and  servants of the Supreme Court   were to be decided in the
manner laid down  in the Constitution of India.  The Government further stated  that it is proposed that the officers and
servants of the Supreme Court may be given scales   of pay on the analogy of the Fifth Central Pay Commission’s

5.         The Home Secretary in his presentation, informed that the two Bills sought to rectify the anomalies and
discrepancies that had crept into the pay-scales of the employees of the Supreme Court and the Delhi High Court since
1979 on account of various judgements and orders of these two Courts which had granted higher scales of pay to their
employees compared to the corresponding categories of employees in the Central Government.  He stated that the Chief
Justice of India and Chief Justice of Delhi High Court were empowered to make rules regarding the conditions of
service of their Officers and Servants under Articles 146(2) & 229(2) of the Constitution, respectively, subject to the
provisions of any law made by Parliament.  In pursuance of Article 146(2), the Chief Justice of India had framed rules
with regard to the Officers and Staff of Supreme Court in 1961 and in pursuance of Article 229(2) the Chief Justice of
Delhi High Court had made rules for the Officers and Employees of the Delhi High Court in 1970.  At that time their
pay-scales were at par with corresponding categories of Central Government employees.  However, the rules under the
proviso to Clause (2) of Articles 146 and 229, so far as they related to the salaries, allowances, leave or pensions,
required the approval of the President in case of Supreme Court and the Lt. Governor of Delhi, in case of Delhi High
Court.  Since Parliament had not made any law in that regard, the officers and employees of the Supreme Court & Delhi
High Court were enjoying higher scales of pay due to judgements of the Courts despite the Government’s opposition to
it.  The Home Secretary stated that the Government of India through these Bills would like to clear the existing
distortions in the pay-scales of employees of the two Courts by reverting to the original principle of parity in their pay
scales with those corresponding to the Central Government employees.  However, he submitted that the pay of
employees of the two Courts who were drawing higher scales after the enactment of law, would be protected.  The
Home Secretary then sought the considered recommendations of the Committee so that the law could be enacted.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
 
 
      Serious  anomalies have crept  into the pay-scales of the employees of the Supreme Court of India on account of
various judgments and orders passed by the Court in Writ Petitions filed by its employees.  These distortions  and
anomalies in the pay-structure  of the staff of Supreme Court are likely to cause serious agitation amongst similarly
placed  Central Government staff.
 
      2.   This might   ultimately lead to large-scale distortions in the pay-structure of the Central Government employees
with attendant serious consequential financial  implications.  The Government  examined the implications of the
emerging distortions in the pay-structures of Court employees and considered  it  appropriate to enact suitable
legislation  to rectify the situation as that was the only remedy available to the Government.
 
      3.   The Bill seeks to achieve the above object.

      Sub:           The Salaries, Allowances, Leave and Pensions of the Officers and Servants of the Supreme Court and
Delhi High Court  Bills, 1994 – further  pursuing thereof by the Department-related Parliamentary
Standing Committee on Home Affairs. – proposed amendments thereof.
 
 
      Under the Supreme Court Officers and Servants (Conditions of Service and Conduct) Rules, 1961, the  officers and
servants of the Supreme Court have been  placed at par with  corresponding  employees of the Central  Government  and
their  pay scales  had accordingly  been  fixed  on the standard  Central Government  pattern.  Similarly, in the Delhi
High Court  officers and Servants  (Salaries, Allowances, Leave and Pension) Rules, 1970,  the employees  of the Delhi
High Court were getting the pay scales  at par with the corresponding staff of Delhi  Administration which is following
the Standard Central  Government pattern.  However, the Court’s  employees  got higher scales of pay by taking
matters  to the Courts  which allowed their petitions.  This resulted in serious anomalies in the pay scales of the
employees of the Delhi High Court as well as those of the Supreme Court of India.
 
2.   With a view to rectify the anomalies which had crept into the pay scales of the Courts employees, the aforesaid 
Bills of 1994 were introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 19.8.1994.  It was proposed through the Bills that the Officers and
Servants of the Delhi High Court and the Supreme Court be given pay scales on the analogy of the IVth Pay
Commission’s recommendations and the Schedule to the Bills were accordingly prepared.
 
3.   The recommendations of the Vth Pay Commission have become available and the scales of pay  of the Central
Government  Employees  have already   been revised  accordingly.  The pay scales  indicated in the Schedules to the
1994  Bills will be replaced  by the revised  pay scales approved by the Government in respect of corresponding  Central
Government employees on the basis of the recommendations of the  Fifth Central Pay Commission.  Accordingly, the
Schedules to the 1994  Bills  indicating  the revised pay scales based on the  recommendations of the Vth Pay
Commission are prepared and annexed to this NOTe at Annexe-I.
 
4.   In the revised Schedule to the Supreme Court Bill, 1994, the posts from Sl.Nos.1 to 14, 20 and 22 to 46 have been
granted normal replacement scales of those proposed in the 1994 Bill.  For the posts  15 to 19  the proposed  scale in the
1994 Bill was Rs.2300-3700/-which is not a standard pay scale, for this scale the replacement of the pre-revised  scales 
of Rs.2375-3500/-  and Rs.2375-3750/-, i.e. Rs.7450-11500/-  has been indicated.  The post of Assistant Accounts
Officer in the  Scales of Rs.6,500-200-10,500/- has been added at Sl.No. 21, this is a standard scale for the AAOs in the
Central Government.
 
5.   In the revised Schedule to the Delhi High Court  Bill,  1994, normal replacement scales have been indicated for all
the posts.
