Commiuee for Implementing Legal Aid Scheme (CILAS) 
12. From the information furnished by the Ministry of Law. Justice a nd Company 
Affairs and deposition made by its representativ es, the Committee notes that the minimum 
income ceiling prescribed by CILAS for providing free legal aid to lhe poor and needy has 
been revised by some States viz. Assam (Rs. 10,000), Jammu and Kashmir (Rs. 7,200), 
Karnataka (Rs. 8,000), Punjab (Rs. 18,000), Sikkim (Rs. 7 ,800) and West Bengal (Rs. 
7,000). Maharashtra and Haryana had already revised the income ceiling for the purpose. 
The Committee has also been apprised that the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 
contains a provision for enhancement of income ceiling from Rs. 6,000 to Rs. 9,000 or such 
higher amount as the State Governments may prescribe if the case is before a court other 
than the Supreme Court and from Rs. 9,000 to Rs. 12,000 or such higher amount as the 
Central Government may prescribe if the case is before the Supreme Court. The Committee 
is of the view that if benefits are to reach to a larger number of people across the country, 
examples set by the above States should be followed by the remaining States . 
13. The Committee has been informed that the Partial Legal Aid Scheme for middle 
income group is optional and is on self-financing basis with no additional cost to the public 
exchequer. It is initially applicable for cases pending or intended to be filed in the High 
Courts and Supreme Court. In the beginning, the scheme was adopted by Delhi, Haryana 
and Punjab. Later, Maharashtra also adopted the scheme on 5.1.1995. The maximum 
income ceiling prescribed for eligibility under the scheme by these States is Rs. 75,0001-, Rs. 
1,00,0001-, Rs. 75,0001- and Rs. 1,20,0001-, respectively. The Supreme Court Legal Aid 
(middle income group) Committee has also adopled the scheme and the maximum income 
ceiling prescribed by it is Rs. 1,20,000. 
14. The Commit!~ had, in its 11th Report on the Demands for Grants (1994-95) of the 
Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs recommended that the P~rtial Legal Aid 
Scheme should be a<!opted by more and more States and extended to the subordinate courts 
also as done in the case of Haryana. The Committee, in this context, finds that th~ response 
Is not very encouraging as only one State I.e. Maharashtra has so far, come forward to adopt 
the scheme. The Committee, therefore, reiterates Its earlier recommendations that the scheme 
should be Implemented by more and more States . and extended to subordinate courts also.
