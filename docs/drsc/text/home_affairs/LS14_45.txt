OVERVIEW 
1. The Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs comprises the following 
Departments:-
(i) Department of Legal Affairs, 
(ii) Legislative Department, 
(iii) Department of Justice, and 
(iv) Department of Company Affairs. 
2. The main functions of the Department of Legal Affairs are:-
(i) To render advice to the various Ministnes!Departments of the 
Government of India on legal matters; 
(ii) To carry out the conveyancing work of the Central Government; 
(iii) To attend the litigation work in the Supreme Court, High Courts and 
some of the subordinate courts; 
(iv) To handle the treaties and agreements with foreign countries in matters 
of Civil Law; 
(v) To authorise officers to execute contracts and assurances of property on 
behalf of the President under Article 299 (1) of the Constitution; 
(vi) To sanction grants-in-aids to certain institutions engaged in research in the 
field of law. 
3. The Department of Legal Affairs in further concerned with appointment of the 
Law Officers, narr.~ly the Attorney-General of India, the Solicitor-General of India 
and the Additional Solicitors-General of India, etc. It is also administratively incharge 
of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Appellate Board. 
4. The Legal Affairs Department has a two-tier organisational set-up, namely, the 
main Secretariat at New Delhi and the Branch Secretanats at Mumbai, Calcutta, 
Madras and Bangalore. 
5. The Legislative Department is concerned mainly with the following matters:­
(i) Scrutiny of Notes for the Cabinet in relation to all Legislative proposals 
from drafting and constitutional points of view; 
(ii) Drafting of all Government Bill including Constitution (Amendment) Bills 
before introduction in Parliament; translation of all such Bills into Hindi 
and forwarding both English and Hindi versions of the Bills to the 
Parliament Secretariat; drafting of non-official amendments and rendering 
of assistance to Administrative Ministries to decide on the acceptability or 
otherwise of non-official amendments; 
(iii) Rendering assistance to Parliament and Joint, Select or Standing 
Committees of Parliament at all stages through which a Bill passes before 
enactment. This includes scrutiny of and/or preparation of reports of such 
Committees, and Hindi Translations of such reports ar.d minutes of 
dissent; 
(iv) Drafting of Ordinances to be promulgated by the President;

DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS 
Central Agency Section (CAS) 
13. The Central Agency Section (CAS) was set up in the year 1950. It is responsible for 
the conduct of litigation work in the Supreme Court on behalf of all Ministries/Departments 
of the Union Government and Administration of the Union Territories and performs 
functions of an Advocate-on-record. Government Advocates in the Central Agency Section 
are Advocates-on-record of the Supreme Court. They appear before the Supreme Court in 
all matters as per the Supreme Court Rules and whenever necessary, engage the Law 
Officers and other Advocates from the panel of Central Government Counsels. The 
Counsels are paid fees prescribed by the Union Government. The fee bills of the Counsels 
are scrutinised and the fees payable is certified by the Government Advocates of the Central 
Agency Sections. As per Annual Report (1997-98) of the Ministry of Law, Justice and 
Company Affairs, during the period from 1st April, 1997 to 31st March, 1998, the Central 
Agency Sections received 2929 new cases from various Departments of the Government of 
India as well as the Union Territories in which the Union of India or Union Territories were 

4 
either petitioners or respondents. The Department of Legal Affairs, in its written replies to 
the queries of the Committee stated that, "As on date 3,300 cases are pending and from 
1997 till date 4,176 cases were disposed of by the Central Agency Section". While 
appreciating the rate of disposal of cases by the Central Agency Section of the Department of 
Legal Affairs, the Committee urges upo'n the Departm~nt to chalk out a time bound 
programme for arranging litigation work in the pending cases in the Supreme Court on behalf 
of the Ministries/Departments of the Central Government and Union Territory 
Administrations, so that the litigation work of the Central Government and Union Territory 
Administrations in the Apex Court gets accelerated. 
LITIGATION (HIGH COURT) SECTION 
14. The litigation work in Delhi High Court and Central Administrative Tribunal 
(CAT), Principal Bench at New Delhi on behalf of all Ministries/Departments of the 
Government of India except Railways and Income Tax Departments is handled by the 
Litigation (High Court) Section. The cases dealt with, generally relate to Civil and Criminal 
Writ Petitions under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India, letters patent 
Appeals, Suits, Appeals, Reviews, Division Bench Appeals, Company Applications, 
Execution Applications and Criminal Miscellaneous and Service matters. There is a panel of 
Central Government Standing Counsels and Government Counsel who are nominated for 
contesting the cases. In most important matters, Senior Counsels are also engaged at the 
request of the Ministries/Departments concerned. In some matters of public importance and 
also in complicated matters one of the Law Officers, namely, Attorney-General of India, 
Solicitor-General of India or Additional Solicitors-General of India is also engaged. On 
receipt of requests from the Ministries/Departments, action is taken by the Department of 
Legal Affairs for engaging a suitable Counsel to appear on their behalf in the cases. Close 
liaison is maintained with various Departments by the counsels to safeguard interest of the 
Government in the litigation in the High Court at Delhi and Central Administrative 
Tribunal. Fee bills received from the Counsels are scrutinised having regard to the terms and 
conditions of their appointments before certifying for payments at prescribed scales. As per 
the Annual Report (1997-98) of the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs, during 
the period from 1st January, 1997 to 31st March, 1998, Litigation (High Court) Section 
engaged Counsel in 4922 cases to safeguard the interest of the Government litigation before 
Delhi High Court and the Central Administrative Tribunal. The Department of Legal 
Affairs informed that at the end of the year 1997-98 in the Litigation (High Court) Section 
21453 cases were pending while 602 cases were disposed of during that period. The 
Committee impresses upon the Department to initiate steps in coordinating the litigation work 
in the pending cases in the Delhi High Court and CAT, Principal Bench, New Delhi, on behalf 
of all Ministries/Departments of Government of India, except Railways and Income Tax 
Department. As the number of cases pending in the Section is very high, a time schedule may 
be prepared and strictly adhered to.

the Government; and

NATIONAL LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITY (NALSA) 
16. Since the year 1952, the Government of India has been taking steps for providing 
legal aid to the poor in as much as equality before law is a fundamental constitutional right. 
Article 39A of the Constitution of India enjoins upon the Government to provide legal aid 
to the poor and weaker sections of society to ensure equal justice for all , the cardinal 
principle on which the Administration of justice is based . The Legal Services Authorities 
Act. 1987 (as amended by the Act of 1994) aims at establishing a nationwide network for 
providing free and comprehensive legal services to the weaker sections of the society on 
uniform basis. National Legal Services Authorities, a statutory body constituted under the 
Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to monitor and oversee the legal aid programmes 
throughout the country, has become fully functional in January, 1998. The various functions 
to be performed by the Authority, inter-alia include organising Lok Adalats and legal aid 
to the persons declared eligible under Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities 
Act , 1987. 
17. From a perusal of the papers, it is seen that NALSA has become fully functional 
only in January/February, 1998 and prior to that it was functioning with skeleton staff. The 
working of NALSA is being supervised by the Chief Justice of India who is its Patron-in­
chief and by Dr. Justice A.S. Anand, Judge, Supreme Court of India who is the Executive 
Chairman of the Authority. The Committee recommends that the Annual Report and 
Audited Statement of Accounts of NALSA should be laid on the Table of the Houses of 
Parliament. 
The income criteria for imparting Legal Aid to the poor was fixed way back in 1987 by 
the Committee for Implementing Legal Aid Scheme (CILAS). It is high time that the income 
criteria is reviewed by the Government without delay. 
A budgetary provision of Rs. 5 crores was made in 1997-98 B.E. stage for carrying on 
the activities of NALSA. The Committee in its 39th Report on the Demands for Grants 
(1997-98) of the Ministry of Law & Justice had observed that the said amount allocated for 
the Scheme for free Legal Aid to the poor was quite inadequate. Despite this observation of 
the Committee, the grant was reduced at the R.E. stage to Rs. 3.38 crores. A provision of 
Rs. 4 crores has been made in B.E. 1998-99. The Committee, accordingly, recommends that 
the Government should at least increase the allocation to Rs. 5 crores so as to restore it to the 
1997-98 B.E. stage and thereafter review the allocation with a view to enhancing it even 
beyond 5 crores.

LAW COMMISSION 
20. The Law Commission of India is a non-statutory body constituted by the 
Government from time to time. The Commission was originally constituted in 1955. It is 
reconstituted every three years. The present Law Commission (15th Law Commission) was 
constituted with effect from 1st September, 1997 for a period of three years. The Law 
O;>mmission has so far submitted 158 reports on different subjects, out of which 80 reports 
have been fully implemented and 17 reports were not accepted. The remaining reports are 
under consideration of the Government. The 14th Law Commission had submitted two 
reports viz. 155th and 156th. The Commission had submitted 154th Report on "the Code of 
Criminal Procedure, 1973" and 156th Report on the "the Indian Penal Code"wherein various 
important amendments have been recommended. Both the 154th and 156th Reports have 
been forwarded to the Ministry of Home Affairs for implementation. The Committee has 
been informed that the Department of Legal Affairs has a cell dealing with implementation 
of the recommendations of the Law Commission, legal profession and the administration of 
the Advocates Act, 1961. 
21. From the facts made available by the Government, the Committee is constrained to 
note that the special cell of the Department of Legal Affairs dealing with the implementation 
of the recommendations of the Law Commission has yielded the least result since 
approximately 60 reports of the Commission are yet to be implemented and that Government 
has failed to ensure the accountability of the administrative Ministries of the Government for 
implementation of the accepted recommendations. The Committee also recommends for 
containing the growth of expenditure of the Commission under the head 'Foreign Travel'. The

INCOME-TAX APPELLATE TRIBUNAL (ITAT) 
22. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, constituted under the Income Tax Act, deals 
with second appeals in all matter s relating to direct taxes, and with appeals against orders in 
revision of commissions as well as orders of acquisition of properties under Chapter XXA of 
the Income Tax Act. The Tribunal is headed by the President who is assisted by one senior 
Vice President and three Vice Presidents. As per Annual Report (1997-98) of the Ministry of 
Law, Justice and Company Affairs, there are 106 Members for 53 Benches spread over 24 
cities. Earlier there were 38 benches of the Tribunal spread over 19 cities. The Government 
created 15 additional benches on 1.4.1997. The Committee was informed that out of 15 
additional benches, only one bench in Rajkot had started functioning and efforts were bejng 
made to locate suitable office premises for housing the remaining 14 benches. The 
Government informed the Committee that there were 10 vacancies of Juditial Members and 
I4 vac<mcies of Accountant Members in the Tribunal. The Committee was also informed that 
there were more than three lakh pending cases before the Tribunal. Regarding the growing 
trend in the expenditure under the head 'Domestic Travel Expenses' for the Tribunal, the 
Government informed the Committee that the increase was due to the necessity to send more 
Members on tours to places where there were no regular bench of the Tribunal and also due 
to general rise in travel costs. The (Jovernment further stated that the substantial increase in 
BE for 1998-99 under the head 'Travel Expenses' was due to the fact that 15 additional 
benches were likely to become functional during the current financial year. 
23. The Committee is astonished to note that out of 15 benches of Income Tax Appellate 
Tribunal , 14 benches are yet to start tunctioning while more than three lakh cases are pending. 
The Committee is also not convinced with the explanation of the Government in this regard and 
recommends that all the benches should be made functional without any further delay. The 
Committee also recommends that all the vacancies in the Tribunal should be filled up with 
utmost expediency. The Committee would also urge the Government to contain the expenditure 
of IT AT particularly under the heads 'Domestic Travel', and 'Other Administrative expenses 
(Hospitality)'. 
INTERNATIONAL CENTRE FOR ALTERNATIVE DISPUTE RESOLUTION (ICADR) 
24. In India number of cases filed in the Courts has mushroomed in recent times for 
variety of reasons thereby indicating the urgency for alternative dispute resolution. It is in this 
context. that a resolution was adopted by all the Chief Ministers and the Chief Justices of the 
High Courts in a meeting held on 4th December, 1993 under the Chairmanship of the then 
Prime Minister and presided over by the then Chief Justice of India, emphasising the 
desirability of disputants taking advantage of alternative dispute resolution which provided 
procedural flexibility, saved valuable time and money and avoided the stress of a conventional 
trial. The first major steps in the direction of settlement of dispute by arbitration and 
conciliation was the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 which came into force w.e.f. 22nd 
August, 1996. This Act has showed the way for the growth of the Alternative Dispute 
Resolution (ADR) movement in India on scientific lines. It is in this backdrop that "the 
International Centre for Alternative Dispute Resolution" (ICADR)-an autonomous 
organisation under the aegis of the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs was 
registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 on 31.5.1995 with the following main 

8 
objective~:-
(i) To propagate, promote and popularise the settlement of domestic and 
international disputes by different. modes of ADR; 
(ii) To provide facilities and administrative lind other support services for holding 
conciliation, mediation arbitration proceedings; 
(iii) To promote reform in the system of settlement of disputes and its healthy 
development suitable to the social, economic lind other needs of the community; 
(iv) To appoint conciliators, mediators, arbitrators, etc., when so requested by the 
parties; 
(v) To undertake teaching in ADR and related matters and to award diplomas, 
certificates and other academic or professional distinction; 
(vi) To develop infrastructure for education, research and training in the field of 
ADR; and 
(vii) To impart training in ADR and related matters and to arrange for fellowships, 
scholarships, stipends and prizes. 
25. The Committee is optimistic that the ICADR would be able to successfully translate 
its charter of duties into reality. The Committee in its 39th Report on the Demands for Grants 
(1997-98) had urged upon the Government to ensure timely submission of Annual Report and 
Statement of Accounts of ICADR to Parliament. The Committee is happy to note that the 
First Annual Report and Accounts (1995-96) of ICADR have since been laid. However, the 
Committee urges upon the Department to make sincere efforts also to lay on the Table of the 
Houses, the remaining Annual Reports and Accounts at the earliest.

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE 
31. The Department of Justice was created in the year 1971. The subjects handled by 
the Department are constitution and organisation of the Supreme Court and the High 
Courts. appointment of Judges in the High Courts and the Supreme Court and their service 
conditions. Judges strength, administration of justice in Union Territories and monitoring of 
pendem:y of cases in courts. Courts in the country are at three levels. At the district level, 
there is subordinate judiciary, at the Stitte level, High Courts and finally the apex court at 
the Union level. As per the provisions of Articles 233, 234 and 235 of the Constitution, the 
supervision. control and appointment of subordinate judiciary is done at the level of tl)e 
State Governments and the concerned High Court. The Union Government is concerned

NATIONAL JUDICIAL ACADEMY 
37. The Eleventh Law Commission in its 117th Report in Nov. 1986 had recommended 
for the setting up of a Central Academy for providing formal training to judicial officers of 
various States. In August-September, 1985, in a joint conference of Chief Justice, Chief 
Ministers and Law Ministers held in New Delhi had also adopted the resolution for setting 
up an institute or an academy for Judicial Officers. The Judgment of Supreme Court dated 
the 13th November 1991 in the matter of All India Judges' Association and Others versus 
Union of India and other State Governments was inter-alia related to setting up of an All 
India in-service training Institute for Judicial Officers. Keeping in view these facts and 
pursuant to the decision 9f the Cabinet, the National Judicial Academy was registered under 
the Societies Registration Act, 1860 with the Registrar of Societies, Delhi on 17th August, 
.. l993 which is being funded as a central project. For 1998-99, Rs. 18 crores have been 
allocated for the Academy. The Registered Office of the Academy is located in the Supreme 
Court premises at New Delhi. The Government of Madhya Pradesh gave 60 acres of land 
free of cost in Bhopal and the Academy will be located at Bhopal where the building 
complex is under constrcution since Jan., 1997 and is expected to be completed in 1999. 
38. The Committee while appreciating the purpose of th~ National Judicial Academy 
recommends that the expenditure for the academy should not form part of the Centrally 
Sponsored Scheme relating to the infrastructural facility for judiciary; rather it should be 
!JtV&rately allocated funds.

41. The Committee has been informed that the total sanctioned strength of Judges in the 
High Courts as on 1st June, 1998 is 587 against which 474 judges are in position, thus leaving 113 
posts vacant. The Committee finds that the maximum number of vacancy exists in the 
Allahabad Hogh Court. As against the sanctioned strength of 71 judges, 48 judges are in

High Courts have 10, 11, 11 and 9 vacancies of Judges, respectively (see Annexure A). The 
Department has informed that they have received proposals of appointment of only 60 Judges 
from the Cheif Justices of High Courts. The Committee feels concerned over the number of the 
vacancy of Judges in High Courts which has a direct co-relation with the increasing pendency of 
cases in the Courts. 
42. The <lommittee has been informed that the pendencies at the High Court level are 
increasing. As on 31st December, 1993, there were 26.51lakh cases pending and it increased to 
31.74 lakhs as on 31st December, 1997 (see Annexure B). The Deapartment further informed 
that they had taken various steps in that direction. The Law Minister had written to the Chief 
Justice of India to place the matter before the Annual Conference of Chief Justices of High 
Courts and suggest measures to reduce the pendencies. The Department added that it was also 
trying to streamline the Civil Procedure Code and the Criminal Procedure Code. The 
Government had also written to the High Courts not to grant adjournments on frivolous 
grounds. 
43. The Committee views with serious concern the disturbing phenomenon of pendency of 
cases at the High Court level. It notes the various steps taken by the Department with a view to 
reducing the pendencies. In this context, it recommends to the Government to suggest the High 
Courts to follow the steps taken by the Supreme Court in bringing down the pendencies, such as 
grouping and clubbing of cases having the same question of law and listing of cases by compuiers 
etc. 
44. The Government informed the Committee that the pendency in the subordinate courts 
was 2.18 crores as on 31st December, 1995 and it decreased to 1.99 crores as on 31 December 
1996 following the judgement of the ·Supreme Court on 1st May, 1996 in which it d~rected the 
subordinate courts to close the cases of minor offences pending for more than two years and if 
the proceeding have not been started. The Union Government has also written to all the State 
Governments to appoint Special Magistrates under Section 13 and 18 of the Criminal Procedure 
Code for minor/petty offences and most of State Governments have appointed the special 
magistrates. The Committee while expressing its consternation over the staggering number of 
pending cases in the subordinate judiciary, notes the steps taken by the Government in this 
regard which should help sustain in the decreasing tendency in the pendency figures.
