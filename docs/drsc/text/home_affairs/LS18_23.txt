r the Chairman of the Committee on Home Affairs, having been authorised by the 
Com~ittee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Twenty-third Report 
· of the Committee relating to the Arb itration and Conciliation Bill*, 1995. 
2. In pursuance of the Rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha referred** the Arbitration and Conciliation Bill, 
l995, as introduced in the Ra jya Sabha on the 16th May, 1995 and pending therein , to the 
·Committee for examination and report. 
· · 3. The Committee, in its meeting held on the 17th August, 1995 considered the Bill and 
. . decided to invite the Law Secretary before it to make a presentation on the subject. 
Accordingly, the Law Secretary made a presentation on the Arbitration and Conciliation 
• Bill, 1995 before the Committee on the 4th September, 1995. During his presentation he 
explained the salient features of the Bill and the circumstances necessitating this 
comprehensive legislation. He infoni1ed the Committee that the present Legislations on the 
- subject, i.e. the Arbitration Act , 1940, the Arbitration (Protocol and Conventions) Act, 1937 
and the Foreign Awards (Recognition and Enforcement) Act, 1961 have become outdated. 
He stressed that economic reforms in the country may not become effective if the law 
dealing with settlement of domestic and international commercial disputes remains out of 
tune with such reforms. The Law Commission and several representative bodies of trade and 
industry have proposed amendments to the Arbitration Act of 1940. Further, the Law 
Secretaries and Law Ministers of all the States and Union Territories, at a meeting held at 
Calcutta on November 15- 17, 1994, also adopted a resolution that a comprehensive 
legislation on Arbitration and Conciliation be made, based largely on UNCITRAL Model 
Law. As explained in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill, the General 
Assembly of the U.N. Commission on International Trade Law has also recommended that 
all the countries should give due consideration to the Model Law adopted by the U.N.O. 
The Law Secretary informed members that the proposed Bill seeks to consolidate and 
amend the Arbitration Law , taking into account the UNCITRAL Model Law and Rules . He 
also stated that a number of bodies/persons were consulted on the subject matter of the Bill, 
before it was drafted. The Committee heard the Law Secretary again on the 18th October , 
1995 and sought certain clarifications on the Bill from him. 
4. After hearing the Law Secretary and considering the other materiaVinformation 
furnished by the Ministry, the Committee took up clause-by -clause considerat! cn of the Bill 
in its meeting held on the 9th November, 1995. 
5. The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its mee ting heid on the lOth 
November , 1 995.

therefor are set out hereinafter.

Clause 9 •• 
This clause empowers a party to an arbitration proceedings, to apply to court, duri ng 
the proceedings, to obtain certain interim measures to protection before or during the 
proceedings in respect of the property involved in the arbitration proceedings. The 
Committee finds that the Second Schedule to the Arbitration Act, 1940 contains a provision 
for appointment of a guardian for a minor or a person of unsound mind . But there is an 
omission of a similar provision in the present BilL The Committee feels that it will be useful 
to provide a similar provision in the Bill. It, therefore, recommends that clause 9 of the 
Arbitration and Conciliation Bill, 1995 be amended so as to make a provision for 
appointment of a guardian for a minor or a person of unsound mind before or during the 
arbi tr;•l proceedings in respect of the property involved in the arbitration proceedings. 
Clause 11 
This clause provides for the procedure for appointment of Arbitrators. The Committee 
finds that the construction of Clause 11(12)(b) is incomplete. It, therefore, recommends that 
in line 2 of c!ause 11 (12)(b), after 'other arbitration' the words 'the reference to "Chief 
Justice " in those sub-sections shall be construed as a reference to', be inserted. The 
amendment proposed is darificatory in nature , intended to make the sentence complete and 
brings out the intention of clause 11 more clear. Similarly, in line 15, after the words 'that 
clause', the word 'to' should be 'inserted'. This amendment is only consequential. 
Clause 34 
This clause deals with applications for setting aside an arbitral award. The Committee 
finds that Explanation to clause 34(2)(b)(ii) explains the meaning of 'public policy of India'. 
Clause 75 of the Bill requir es the Conciliator and the parties to conciliation proceedings to 
keep confidentiality of all mat ten; relating to conciliation proceedings. Similarly, clause 81 of 
the Bill deals with admissibility of evidence in other proceedings and prohibits parties from 
introducing as evidence, in any subsequent arbitral or judicial proceedings, the views 
expressed during such proceed ings, admissions made in the course of the proceedings, etc. 
Reference to these matters appears to be missing in the Explanation to Clause 34(2)(b)(ii). 
The Committee feels that a further clarificatory addition to the Explana~ion needs to be 
made by incorporating a _reference to clauses 75 and 81 of the Bill. It, therefore, 
recommends that Explana tion to Clause 34(2)(b)(ii) be amended by inserting the words 'or 
was in violation of Section 75 or Section 81', after the word 'corruption' in the last line of 
the Explanation. 
Clause 36 
This clause provides for enforcement of an arbitral award when the same becomes final , 
in the same manner as if it were a decree of the court. The Committee feels that a 
clarificatory amendment to the clause is needed, by way of an explicit reference to the Code 
of Civil Procedure, 1908, laying down a provision that the award will be enforced like a 
decree of the court under the code of Civil Procedure, 1908. It, therefore, recommends that 
clause 36 be amended by inserting the words 'under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, after 
the word 'en forced' in the last line of the clause. 
Clause 37 
This clause contains provisions for making appeals to the courts from certain orders in 
respect of arbitral awards. Clause 37 (1) says that an appeal shall lie to the higher court from 
orders made under clause 9 of the Bill . With the amendm~nt proposed by the Committee in 
clause 9, for including the power of appointment of a guardian of a minor-or of a person of 
unsound mind . the Committee feels that the wording of clause 37(1)(a) needs a slight 
consequential chcnge. lt , therefore, recommends that the word 'an interim' in clause 
37(1)(a) be subsriuaed by the word ' any'.
