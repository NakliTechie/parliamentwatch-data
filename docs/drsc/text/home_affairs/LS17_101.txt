allocation of Delimitation Commission. The parameters for the working of the Delimitation
Commission are contained in the Delimitation Act, 2002 and have also been detailed in the guidelines
framed by the Commission. Pursuant to the enactment of the Constitution (Eighty-fourth
Amendment) Act, 2001, the Delimitation Act, 2002 was enacted. The Delimitation Commission is
a three member body constituted on 12.7.2002, under the provisions of the Delimitation Act, 2002

with Shri Justice Kuldip Singh, a retired Judge of the Supreme Court, as its Chairperson and
Shri B.B. Tandon, one of the Election Commissioners and the third Member of the Commission is

Commission is to readjust the division of territorial constituencies of the seats in the House of the

4.5 For the remaining seven States of Bihar , Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Pondicherry, Tamil

DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
6.0 This Department has a two-tier organisational set up i.e. the Main Secretariat at New Delhi
and the Branch Secretariats at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Bangalore.
6.1 The main functions of the Department of Legal Affairs are:
(i) to extend advice to the various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India
on legal matters;
(ii) to carry out the conveyancing work of the Central Government;
(iii) to attend to the litigation work in the Supreme Court, High Courts and some of the
Sub-ordinate Courts;
(iv) to deal with the treaties and agreements with foreign countries in matters of Civil Laws;
(v) to authorise officers to execute contracts and assurances of property on behalf of
the President under Article 299 (1) of the Constitution;
(vi) to appoint law officers such as Attorney General of India, Solicitor General of India
and the Additional Solicitors-General of India and
(vii) to sanction grants-in-aid to certain institutions engaged in research in the field of law,
constitutional studies and Parliamentary institutions.
6.2 The Department of Legal Affairs is further concerned with administering the Advocates
Act, 1961, the Notaries Act, 1952 and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. The Department
is also administratively in-charge of the Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange (ATFE), Income
Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITA T), Indian Legal Service and Law Commission of India.

AFFAIRS
Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)
7.0 The Secretary, Department of Legal Affairs in his deposition before the Committee stated
that the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal which decides appeals against the orders of the
Departmental authorities in respect of all direct taxes, has a pendency of 1,82,625 cases at the end
of February, 2003. Number of measures have been adopted to reduce pendency of cases such as
clubbing of cases having similar issues, identification of small cases for quick disposal,
identification of cases involving issues already covered by the decisions of the Supreme Court,
raising the limit from Rs.l lakh to Rs.5 lakh for appeals to be heard by a single Member, formation
of mobile Benches etc. As a result of these measures the increase in pendency has been arrested
and it is going down. Most of the Benches of ITAT are housed in private accommodation taken
on rent. There is also a problem of providing suitable residential accommodation to Members of
ITAT. On these aspects a public interest litigation is pending in the Supreme Court and the Supreme
Court has been issuing directions from time to time regarding providing office accommodation as
well as accommodation for residential purposes for the Members of ITAT. The Department has
been trying to meet the directions of the Supreme Court within the available financial resources and
there is a genuine need to construct office accommodation for various Benches of ITAT as also
to provide residential accommodation to Members of ITAT. The Department sought the support of
the this Committee for the construction of infrastructure for the Tribunal so as to ensure smooth
and efficient discharge of its functions.
7.1 The Committee recommends that the Government should explore the possibility of
including the financial proposals for the requirement of accommodation for official as well as
residential purposes for the ITAT under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for the Development
of Infrastructural Facilities for the Judiciary through hundred per cent central funding.
National Legal Services Authority
8.0 The Secretary in his deposition before the Committee stated that the National Legal Services
Authority is engaged in providing legal services, legal aid and speedy justice through Lok Adalats.

8
The Authority having its office at New Delhi is headed by the Chief Justice of India who is the
ex-officio  Patron-in-Chief. Similarly, State Legal Services Authorities and District Legal Services
Authorities have been constituted in every State. Supreme Court Legal Services Committee, High
Court Legal Services Committees and Taluk Legal Services Committees have also been constituted
in every State. As per the information available with NALSA, as on 31 December, 2002 about
47.43 lakh persons have benefited through court-oriented legal aid programmes. Till date about
1 lakh 55 thousand Lok Adalats have been organised wherein about 1 crore 46 lakhs cases have
been settled. It was felt that a major drawback in the existing scheme of organisation of Lok
Adalats under Chapter VI of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 was that the system of Lok
Adalats was mainly based on conciliation/compromise or settlement between the parties. If the
parties do not arrive at any compromise or settlement, the case is either returned to the court of
law or the parties are advised to seek remedy in a court of law. The Legal Services Authorities
Act, 1987, was, therefore, amended in 1995 and provisions for Pre-Litigation Conciliation and
Settlement have been inserted in the Act. With this amendment, Lok Adalats have been given power
to decide the cases involving public utility services like transport service, posts, telephones,
electricity, sanitation, hospitals, insurance services, etc., involving an amount of not more than
Rs. 10 lakh, on merits, in case the parties fail to arrive at any compromise or settlement. The cases
involving these public utility services need to be settled urgently. So most of the cases which ought
not to go in the regular courts would be settled at pre-litigation stage itself which would result in
reducing the work load of the regular courts to a great extent.
8.1 The Committee appreciates the establishment of permanent Lok Adalats which would
go a long way in bringing down the pendency of cases before the courts.
Law Commission
9.0 The Law Commission of India is a non-statutory body constituted by the Government once
in three years. The Commission was originally constituted in 1955. The present 16th Law
Commission was constituted on 1 September, 2000. It consists of a  Chairman, three full-time
Members, one Member-Secretary and three or more Part-Time Members depending upon the nature
of work. At present, besides the Chairman and the Member-Secretary, one Full-Time Member are
in position. The Law Commission has submitted 185 Reports ( Annexure-I).
9.1 Law Commission Reports are considered to be primary source of law for Parliament as well
as judiciary. Law Commission present its Report to the Government, the Government then lay those
Reports on the Tables of both Houses of Parliament. Out of 185 Reports, only one Report is yet
to be presented to Parliament.
9.2 Department of Legal Affairs forwards Law Commission Reports for examination/
implementation to the concerned administrative Ministries/Departments. Final decision on a law
Commission Report is taken by the concerned Ministries/Departments and not by the Department
of Legal Affairs. Out of 185 Reports, 45 Reports were pending for implementation/consideration
with different concerned Ministries/Departments as on 28 February, 2003. As per the
recommendation of this Committee in its 56th Report on Demands for Grants (1999-2000) and 61st
Report on the Demands for Grants (2000-01) of this Ministry, the Department has submitted in
written reply that the concerned Ministries/Departments must be pursued and periodical meetings
with them be called once in a month to look into the progress in regard to the implementation of
the pending Reports, they were pursued through correspondence as well as 49 meetings were held
on various dates in this Department and as a result, the process of consideration/implementation
of the pending Reports has been accelerated. Consultations with State Governments/Union Territory
Administration with a view to implement reports on subjects falling under List-III of Seventh
Schedule to the Constitution are carried on by the concerned administrative Ministries/Departments.

9
9.3 The Committee recommends that the concerned Ministries/Departments may be
prescribed a time bound action plan for implementation of recommendation contained in the
Law Commission Reports pending with them.
LEGISLATIVE DEPARTMENT
10.0 Legislative Department is one of the oldest Departments, which was set up in the
pre-independence period in 1883 and at one time it was part of the Department of Home. The main
task of the Legislative Department is to draft Bills for enactment of new laws or amendment of
the existing laws and to advise and assist the Administrative Ministries and Departments on
legislative matters at all stages and also to process the Bills at all stages till President’s assent is
received and the Act is published in the Official Gazette.
10.1 Apart from preparing the Bills in the light of the legislative proposals, the Department also
finalises the Statements of Objects and Reasons, Financial Memoranda and the Notes on Clauses,
in connection with introduction of Bills. It also prepares the Memorandum regarding Delegated
Legislation, where required.
10.2 The Department mainly performs the following functions:
(i) It handles the legislative business of the Government of India;
(ii) Administratively handles election laws;

Fast Track Courts
18. The Eleventh Finance Commission, recommended a Scheme for creation of 1734 additional
courts in the country for disposal of long pending sessions and other cases. The Ministry of
Finance sanctioned an amount of Rs.502.90 crore as a special package and upgradation grant for
Judicial Administration. This amount is to be spent over a period of five years, i.e. 2000-05. An
amount of Rs.328.26 crore has been released to various States so far by the Ministry of Finance
for proper implementation of the Scheme. The overall functioning of the Fast Track Courts is the
primary responsibility of the respective State Governments and the concerned High Court. The
cases are transferred to Fast Track Courts under the directions of the respective High Courts.
18.1 Fast Track Courts are taking up sessions cases pending for two years or more and the
cases of under-trials in jails on priority. At present there are about two lakh under-trials in jails on
whose maintenance State Governments are spending about Rs.400 crore per annum at the rate of
Rs.55 per day per person in jail. About 18 per cent of the under-trials are in jail for more than
one year. Such cases are likely to be disposed of by the Fast Track Courts soon. The Scheme
will effect substantial saving in expenditure on jails besides addressing a serious human rights
problem. It will also expedite the disposal of sessions and other cases pending for a long time,
which will have a salutary effect on law and order and crime control. As on 21st February, 2003,
1308 courts have been established out of which 991 courts have already been functioning and they
had disposed of 77,891 cases out of 1,84,207 cases transferred to them.
18.2 The Committee recommends that all out efforts should be made to make all the Fast
Track Courts already notified, functional at the earliest. The Committee is of the view that
the Department of Justice, concerned State Governments and High Courts should evolve an
effective monitoring mechanism to rationalise the pace of disposal of cases by those courts
so that the purpose for which they were created is fulfilled.

20.4 The Judges of the High Courts were appointed by the President after consultation with the
Chief Justice of India, the Governor of the State and in the case of appointment of a Judge other
than the Chief Justice, the Chief Justice of the High Court vide article 217 (1) of the Constitution.
Before 1993, the process of appointment of Judges of High Courts and Supreme Court was
required to be initiated by the Executive i.e. Department of Justice, as far as possible 6 months
prior to the date of vacancy. There were delays in filling the vacancies during that period also
but the Government was answerable and accountable to Parliament. After the Judgement of
the Supreme Court in Advocates-on-Record Association vs. Union of India and others on
October 6,  1993, a proposal for appointment of a  Judge cannot be initiated by the  Government.
According to the Judgement of October 6, 1993, read with the Advisory Opinion of
October 28, 1998, of the Supreme Court of India, the initiation of the proposal for appointment
of a Judge of the Supreme Court must be by the Chief Justice of India and in the case of a Judge
of the High  Court by the Chief Justice of that High Court. For transfer of a Judge/Chief Justice
of a High Court to another High Court, the proposal has to be initiated by the Chief Justice of
India.
20.5 The Government is now committed to setting up of a National Judicial Commission, which
would make recommendations for appointment of Judges of the Supreme Court and the High
Courts, as also draw up a Code of Ethics for Judges. The National Commission to Review the
Working of the Constitution has recommended (Para 7.3.7) for setting up the National Judicial
Commission for the Supreme Court only.

position of appointment of judges of Supreme Court and High Courts be introduced The
Government feels that the conflicting opinions on the merit of the 1993 judgement would get

to the relevant articles of the Constitution of India.
20.7 The Government has taken steps to build up a consensus on the proposed establishment of
the National Judicial Commission. Political parties have been requested to communicate their views

20.8 The Committee would like to impress upon the Government to take suitable action
at the earliest to restore the pre-1993 position in the matter of appointment of Judges in

Benches of Supreme Court
21. The Committee in its meeting held on 21st March, 2003 was of the view that the reply of
the Supreme Court regarding setting up of Benches of Supreme Court in far-flung areas with a
view to provide justice to poor people for whom it is impossible to go to the National Capital, was
not convincing and felt that setting up of Benches outside Delhi would neither impair the unity and
integrity nor undermine the importance of the Supreme Court. Rather on the other hand, it would
send a message of integration and a sense of belonging in the country.
The Department in its written reply, has informed the Committee as under:
“Representations have been received from time to time from various Bar Associations etc.
for establishment of Benches of Supreme Court in various parts of the country. Suggestions
have also been made by the Governments of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka,
Tripura and West Bengal for establishment of Benches of the Supreme Court for Southern/
Eastern and North-Eastern parts of the country. The Law Ministers of Eastern and North-
Eastern States adopted a resolution unanimously in their meeting held on October 4, 1996
in Calcutta that it is essential for the Supreme Court to sit at a suitable place in their region.
The matter has been referred to the Chief Justice of India from time to time. The Supreme
Court has been opposed to setting up of a Bench of the Supreme Court at any place outside
Delhi. In its communication dated April 26, 1997 received from the Registry of the Supreme
Court, it was stated that the suggestions for establishment of Benches of the Supreme Court
outside Delhi had been considered and disapproved by the Full Court in a meeting presided
over by the Chief Justice of India.
The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, while discussing the Demands for
Grants of the Ministry of Law and Justice for 1999-2000 in its 56th Report recommended
that Government should think of setting up Benches of Supreme Court in Southern, Western
and North-Eastern parts of the country.
The recommendation was conveyed to the Chief Justice of India for his views in terms of
article 130 of the Constitution of India. The Chief Justice of India intimated vide his letter
dated 3rd February, 2000 that having regard to the importance of the matter the subject was
discussed in a Full Court meeting of the Hon’ble Judges of Supreme Court held on
15th December, 1999. The Full Court after due deliberations, unanimously reiterated its
stand already taken on the subject in its Resolutions dated 18th August, 1984, 11th May, 1994
and 18th March, 1997 opposing the setting up of the Benches and Filing Section of the
Supreme Court outside Delhi.

19
The views of the Full Court opposing the setting up of the Benches of the Supreme Court
outside Delhi were brought to the notice of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
Committee. The Committee in its 61st and 72nd report reiterated its recommendations made
in its 56th Report for setting up of Benches in the Southern, Western and North-Eastern
parts of the country so that the people of far-flung areas get easy access to the Apex
Court. The Committee also urged upon the Government to again take up the matter with
the Chief Justice of India.
The above recommendation of the Parliamentary Committee was communicated to the Chief
Justice of India on 29th June, 2000.
The Chief Justice of India has conveyed vide his letter dated 2nd May, 2001 that the matter
was discussed in a Full Court meeting of Hon’ble Judges on 30th April, 2001 which after
thorough reconsideration of the issue unanimously found no justification in deviating from
their earlier resolutions on the subject. Accordingly, the Full Court did not accept the
demand of the Department related Parliamentary Committee regarding setting up of Benches
of the Supreme Court in Southern, Western and North-Eastern parts of the country”.
22. The Committee having noted the written replies of the Department of Justice once
again reiterates its recommendation for setting up of Benches of Supreme Court outside
Delhi and request the Government to approach the Chief Justice of India for
reconsideration by the Supreme Court of its earlier decision in the matter, as the
Committee strongly feels that establishment of Benches of Supreme Court in Southern,
Western and North-Eastern parts of the country would facilitate the litigants to avail justice
from the apex court without travelling to Delhi from far away places.

etc., for speedy disposal of cases. A Pilot project has been started for Networking and
Computerisation of City Courts in the four major metro cities of Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata and
Mumbai to serve as a model. The purpose of this project is to augment the capacities of courts
and speed up adjudication through better court management with the use of information technology.
23.4 This year the Department of Justice is initiating a pilot project of upgrading the computer
network of the four metropolitan High Courts of Delhi, Bombay, Calcutta and Madras to the level
of the Supreme Court so as to bring about the same speed and efficiency in disposal of cases in
these High Courts as exists in the Supreme Court.
23.5 Civil Procedure Code has been amended and the amendments have been brought into force
with effect from 1.07.2002 which, inter-alia , limit the number of adjournments which can be
granted to a party to three, provide for speedy process service by speed post, courier services,
fax or e-mail, authorise the court to fix time limit for oral arguments and provide for a number
of other measures aimed at ensuring expeditious disposal of cases. Permanent Lok Adalats are being
set up for expeditious resolution of disputes relating to public utilities.
23.6 Huge arrears of cases in the High Court and Sub-ordinate courts is a matter of
serious concern. The Committee having noted the various steps taken to reduce the arrears
in courts, urges upon the Government to consider initiating other new measures to arrest
pendency of cases.

Income-Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)
The Committee recommends that the Government should explore the possibility of
including the financial proposals for the requirement of accommodation for official as well
as residential purposes for the ITAT under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme for the
Development of Infrastructural Facilities for the Judiciary through hundred per cent central
funding. (Para 7.1)
National Legal Services Authority
The Committee appreciates the establishment of permanent Lok Adalats which would
go a long way in bringing down the pendency of cases before the courts. (Para 8.1)
Law Commission
The Committee recommends that the concerned Ministries/Departments may be
prescribed a time bound action plan for implementation of recommendations contained in
the Law Commission Reports pending with them. (Para 9.3)
The Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research (ILDR)
The Committee recommends that the necessary infrastructure to enable the Institute
of Legislative Drafting and Research to function independently should be provided at the
earliest. (Para 11.3)
Electors Photo Identity Cards
The Committee having taken note of the progress of work of Electors’ Photo Identity
Cards (EPIC) and the road map for the future recommends that the EPIC may be made
available to all the citizens in the country before the forthcoming Assembly and General
elections. (Para 12.3)
Electronic Voting Machine (EVM)
13.7 The Committee appreciates the intention of Election Commission to use EVMs in
forthcoming General as well as Assembly elections. The Committee now urges upon the
Election Commission to examine the feasibility of use of EVMs in the elections to local
bodies. This will help the people to familiarize themselves with EVMs faster. (Para 13.7)
National Judicial Academy (NJA)
The Committee, therefore, recommends that all requisite steps may be taken by the
concerned authorities so that full-fledged functioning of the National Judicial Academy in
the newly constructed building may commence without further delay. (Para 17.3)
21

22
Fast Track Courts
The Committee recommends that all out efforts should be made to make all the Fast
Track Courts already notified, functional at the earliest. The Committee is of the view that
the Department of Justice, concerned State Governments and High Courts should evolve an
effective monitoring mechanism to rationalise the pace of disposal of cases by those courts
so that the purpose for which they were created is fulfilled. (Para 18.3)
Family Courts
The Committee recommends that the Government should ensure that Family Courts
are established in each district. The Committee is also of the view that the public at large
are not aware of the existence of such courts in the country like Consumer Courts. Thus,
the Committee recommends that serious efforts may be made to bring awareness about the
existence of the Family Courts through advertisement in television and other mass media.
(Para 19.9)
Vacancies of Judges
The Committee would like to impress upon the Government to take suitable
action at the earliest to restore the pre-1993 position in the matter of appointment of
Judges in Supreme Court and High Courts, by bringing in, if necessary a suitable
legislation. (Para 20.7)
Benches of Supreme Court
The Committee having noted the written replies of the Department of Justice once
again reiterates its recommendation for setting up of Benches of Supreme Court outside
Delhi and request the Government to approach the Chief Justice of India for
reconsideration by the Supreme Court of its earlier decision in the matter, as the
Committee strongly feels that establishment of Benches of Supreme Court in Southern,
Western and North-Eastern parts of the country would facilitate the litigants to avail justice
from the apex court without travelling to Delhi from far away places.
Pendency of Cases
Huge arrears of cases in the High Court and Sub-ordinate courts is a matter of
serious concern. The Committee having noted the various steps taken to reduce the arrears
in courts, urges upon the Government to consider initiating other new measures to arrest
pendency of cases. (Para 23.6)
