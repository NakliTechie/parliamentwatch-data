Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Seventy-sixth Report of the Committee on the Repealing and
Amending Bill, 1999*.

REPORT
 
            The Repealing and Amending Bill, 1999 (Annexure-1) seeks to repeal altogether 357 amending Acts either wholly or partially.   The
Bill also seeks to rectify the patent error and inadvertent mistakes in Indian Succession Act, 1925 and the Code of Criminal Procedure,
1973, respectively, by way of making amendments to those Acts.
 
2.0       All those 357 Acts to be repealed either wholly or partially are from the period spanning from 1985 to 1998 and are listed in the First
Schedule to the Bill.
 
2.1       Out of the listed 357 Acts, those amending Acts, which stand incorporated in the parent legislation would be repealed wholly as
those are no longer required on the Statute Book.  But some of the provisions of certain amending Acts have got independent existence and
retention thereof are necessary, and are, thus, to be repealed partially.  Thus, the basic purpose of the Bill is to    cleanse the Statute Book.
 
3.0       As mentioned in the Statement of Objects and Reasons, the Bill is one of those periodical measures by which enactments which
have ceased to be in force or have become obsolete or the retention whereof as separate Acts is unnecessary are repealed or by which the
formal defects detected in enactments are corrected.
 
3.1       It is, thus, in the nature of routine exercise by which Parliament weeds out ‘unnecessary’ enactments which clutter the Statute Book
and is often described as ‘legislative scavenging’ for it neither enacts any new law nor cancels any existing law.
 
3.2       In England, a provision called “Statute Law Revision Acts” which is akin to the Repealing and Amending Act in India exist there to
take care of legislative scavenging.
 
4.0       The Legislative Department being responsible for drafting of the laws and also keep reviewing the amendment Acts and other Acts
enacted by Parliament from time to time.
 
4.1       The Legislative Department has stated that since the Repealing and Amending Bill is a routine exercise, no prior approval of Cabinet
is taken before it is introduced in Parliament which has been followed as a practice since 1952.  The Bill also does not attract any provision
of the Constitution which requires prior recommendation of the President before introducing the  same in either House of Parliament. 
However, before proposing any repeal/amendment, the Legislative Department of the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs
consults the concerned administrative Ministries/Departments in consultation with the Department of Legal Affairs and obtains the approval
of the Law Minister.
 
4.2       The Committee is not clear as to whether prior approval of the Cabinet for the Repealing and Amending  Bill before it is introduced
in Parliament is required or not.  The Department in their interim reply has stated that since this is a routine exercise, no prior approval of
Cabinet is taken before it is introduced in Parliament which has been followed as practice since 1952.  However, in their written submission
to the Committee, it is seen that the Department of Legal Affairs to whom the Legislative Department had forwarded a copy of the draft
Repealing and Amending Bill, 1999, for their comments had inter-alia advised that they concur in the draft note for Cabinet.   Here again,

5.0       The repealing would not affect the substantive provisions of the parent legislation   by virtue of  section 6A of the General Clauses
Act, 1897, which reads as under:
 
“Repeal of Act making textual amendment in Act or Regulation where any Central Act or Regulation made after the
commencement of this Act repeals any enactment by which the text of any Central Act or Regulation was amended by the
express omission, insertion, or substitution of any matter, then, unless a different intention appears, the repeal shall not affect
the continuance of any such amendment made by the enactment so repealed and in operation at the time of such repeal.”
 
Besides the provision of the General Clauses Act, a saving clause has been provided in the Bill itself.   Hence, the repeal of the Act will not
affect the continuance of any such amendment made by the enactment so repealed.
 
6.0       The Secretary (Legislative) in his presentation has outlined three types of amendment Acts listed in First Schedule to the Bill which
are to be repealed completely by the proposed legislation.
 
(i)
                  Acts which got incorporated in the parent Act and its independent existence is not required i.e. the Foreign Contribution
(Regulation) Amendment Act, 1985;
(ii)                Acts which are temporary in nature, renewed from time to time and lapsed by now i.e. the Terrorist and Disruptive
Activities (Prevention) Act, 1985; and
(iii)               Acts which were enacted as a one time measure and has outlived its purposes i.e. the cancellation of General Elections in
Punjab Act, 1991.
 
The other category of amendment Acts enlisted contain some provisions which are otherwise of independent nature and cannot get
incorporated in the provisions of the principal Act, would be repealed partially to the extent mentioned in column 4 of the First Schedule to
the Bill shown against the particular amending Act. 
 
6.1.0    He has mentioned that the Second Schedule to the Bill   lists two Acts, namely, the Indian Succession  Act, 1925 and the Code of
Criminal Procedure, 1973 for the purpose of making amendments so as to correct patent   errors and inadvertent   mistakes pointed out at a
subsequent  phase.
 
6.1.1    In the Indian Succession Act, 1925, certain amendments would be made in entry 6 in Part II of Second Schedule to replace the
words “grand parents’ children” by the words grandparents’ parents’ children” to rectify the printing error.  At the time of enactment of the
said Act the word “parents” got omitted.  The provision is relatable to section 55 of the Act, which deals with the division of property where
a Parsi dies intestate  and leaves neither  lineal descendants nor a widow or  widower nor a widow of any lineal descendant.
 
6.1.2    The Bill also  proposes to replace all entries ( extent of punishment, nature of offence and the trial court) against   section 377 of the
Indian Penal Code in the First Schedule to the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.  The Secretary has explained that the error with respect to
section 377 of the Indian Penal Code occurred when the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 was amended in 1983.  In the Code of Criminal
Procedure, 1973 as it stood in 1974, section 377 is followed by section 376 which relates to rape, (a cognizable offence) and the punishment
and the Court for which is imprisonment for life or imprisonment for the ten years and fine, and the court of sessions, respectively is
mentioned.  Section 377 relates to unnatural offences and the punishment and nature of offence are the same as in the case of Section 376,
thus, the word ‘ditto’ was used under the above heading against Section 377.  But when the Criminal Procedure Code was amended in 1983
sections 376A,376B and 376C and 376D were inserted.  Punishment, nature of offence and trial courts mentioned against Section 376B is

same as in Section Nos. 376 C and 376D.  Since word ditto is used against Section Nos. 376C & 376D and 377 under the aforesaid heading,
it is interpreted that the punishment, nature of offence and trial courts mentioned against 376B is applicable to Section 377 but the actual
intention is not so.  The punishment, nature of offence and trial Courts as mentioned against Section 376 is applicable to Section 377 and is
to be mentioned categorically in the place of word ‘ditto’.
 
6.1.3    The Legislative Department has clarified that clubbing of repealing and amending of Acts together in the present legislation would
not create any problem as no change of substantive nature is carried out in the Law by the Bill.
 
6.1.4    While scrutinizing First Schedule to the Bill, the Committee felt that there was no indication about the reasons for repeal of
amending Acts and details of their enforcements in the Schedule.  The Committee, hence, directed the Legislative Department to mention
aforesaid details against each amending Acts by adding another two columns to the existing one in the First Schedule.  Accordingly, the
Ministry had submitted a statement explaining details of enforcement and reasons for repeal of each Act enlisted in the First Schedule,
which is placed as Annexure-II.
 
6.1.5    The Department in its written replies has not given any reply to the query made by Shri Sahu as to why some of the Acts amended
earlier which are now being proposed to be repealed were never implemented.  The Ministry’s representatives at the meeting held on May
28, 2001, stated that it was for the concerned administrative Ministry to clarify the position.
 
6.1.6    the Committee is not satisfied with this type of clarification.   The Committee feels that since the Legislative Department is the
Department piloting the Bill, it is for this Department to collect the necessary and requisite information/data in respect of the contents of the
Bill and clarifications sought by the Members of the Committee.  To that extent Department’s reply is not complete and satisfactory.   The
Committee hopes that the Ministry will take this point into account while taking up the Bill for consideration in the House.
 
7.0       It was pointed out in the meeting of the Committee that the Commission on Review of Administrative Laws under the Chairmanship
of Shri P.C. Jain inter-alia  had recommended for repeal of 315 amending Acts.   In that connection, the Committee sought clarifications
whether any of the Acts out of the 315 amending Acts have been included in the Bill.
 
7.1       The Legislative Department has replied that the aforesaid Commission has not recommended repeal of any Acts included in the Bill
and Acts included in the Bill were also not considered by the Commission.
 
7.2       The Committee is not convinced with the reply of the Legislative Department.   It, therefore, fails to understand as to why the Jain
Commission did not consider 357 amending Acts included in the First Schedule to the Bill.
 
8.0       The Committee took note of the fact that the last Repealing and Amending Act was enacted in the year 1988 and wanted to know the
reasons for not including the amendment Acts of the year 1985 in that Act, which are sought to be repealed by the present Bill.
 
8.1       The Legislative Department in its written reply has submitted that the procedure regarding repealing of Acts is a lengthy one as the
concerned Ministries/Departments have to be consulted before finalizing the Bill for introduction in Parliament.  Only those Acts are to be
included in the Bill which are cleared by the concerned Ministries/Departments for repealing.   Other Acts are included in the subsequent
legislation.
 
8.2       In the same context the Department has mentioned that the last Repealing Bill was initiated in the year 1985 and consultation
process took considerable time after which the Bill was introduced in the year 1986 and enacted in the year 1988.  The process of drafting
the present legislation was initiated in the year 1997 and the Bill was introduced in Parliament in  December, 1999 after consulting the
concerned Ministries/Departments.
 
9.0       The Committee pointed out why laws struck down by the Supreme Court had not been included in the Bill.
 
9.1       The Legislative Department had replied that the Bill in question did not include any provision which had been struck down by the
Supreme Court.  Such provisions remained on the Statute Book and did not stand automatically repealed.  Such provisions were not covered
by the General Clauses Act and fell in a different category.
 
10.0     Besides the points/queries stated in the preceding paragraphs, Members raised a number of other points/queries in the meeting of the
Committee held on 23 January, 2001.  the Legislative Department had sent written replies to all to points/queries   (See Annexure-III).  
Subsequently, a member of the Committee   (Shri Anadi Sahu) had raised a number of additional queries (See Annexure-IV) to which the
same Department had also responded (Annexure-V).
 
11.0     The Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sitting held on 28 May, 2001, wherein the representatives of
the Legislative Department and Department of Legal Affairs were present.

11.1.0  The clause repeals 357 amendment Acts to the extent mentioned against the particular Act in the fourth column of the First Schedule
to the Bill.
 
11.1.1  The Clause was adopted without any change.
 
Clause – 3
 
11.1.2  The clause amends two Acts to the extent and in the manner mentioned in the fourth column against the particular Act in the Second
Schedule to the Bill to rectify the patent error and inadvertent mistakes in those Acts.
 
11.1.3  The Ministry was asked to clarify as to why the Indian Penal Code and Criminal Procedure Code were not being amended.   In
response to that the Ministry in their written reply has stated that the Department has not proposed amendment in Section 377 of the Indian
Penal Code on merits.  The Section is being modified with a view to rectifying a patent error.  Suggestions given by the Hon’ble Member
will be referred to the Ministry of Home Affairs for their consideration as they are administratively concerned with the Indian Penal Code
and Criminal Procedure Code after the Committee submits its report to Parliament.
 
11.1.4  The Committee expresses its deep anguish at the manner in which Ministry has chosen to respond to this patent error in the present
Act.  Section 377 provides for imprisonment for life or imprisonment for ten years and fine.  The existing provision under the Criminal
Procedure Code provides that this offence is to be tried by a Magistrate of the First Class.  This is against the Criminal Procedure Code
provisions as offences attracting punishment more than three years are to be tried by Court of Session.  Therefore, the Legislative
Department itself should have taken cognizance of this anomaly in the Criminal Procedure Code and taken corrective action.   On the
contrary, it has decided to take the line of least resistance and passed on this responsibility to the Ministry of Home Affairs which
administers the Indian Penal Code and Criminal Procedure Code.  The Committee hopes that while taking up the Bill in the House,
necessary remedial action would be taken to correct the mistake in the Section 377 of the Criminal Procedure Code.
 
11.1.5  The Clause was adopted without any change.
 
Clause – 4
 
11.1.6  The clause is a saving clause which states that the repeal of any amending Acts will not  affect  the parent Acts in which the repealed
enactment have been applied, incorporated or referred to.  The clause also provides saving of right, title, obligation, liability acquired,
accrued or incurred or any remedy in respect thereof, etc. and also provides that the Bill shall not affect the principle of rule of law and also
any enactment revived, restored, any jurisdiction, office, custom, title, exemption, usage, etc. which are not now existing or in force.
 
11.1.7  The Clause was adopted without any change.
 
Clause – I, The Enacting Formula and Title
 
11.2     Clause I, Enacting Formula and Title were adopted with some changes which are of consequential nature, namely, ‘1999’ and
‘Fiftieth’ to be substituted by ‘2001’ and ‘Fifty-second’ respectively.
 
11.3     The Committee recommends that the Bill be passed in the present form.

4.         The Secretary, Legislative Department   gave a brief introduction of the Repealing and Amending Bill, 1999. He  stated that  the
Government  of India periodically undertook measures to repeal Acts which were no longer required on the statute book as they had already
been  incorporated in the parent legislation, such as, Sections 2,3 & 4 of the General Insurance Business Nationalisation (Amendment) Act,
1985 or the whole of Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Act, 1985, or Acts which were  of temporary nature such as the
Terrorist and Disruptive Activities (Prevention) Act, 1985, and also legislations which were enacted as a one-time exercise such as the
Cancellation  of General Elections in Punjab Act, 1991 or the Gold (Control) Repeal Act, 1990 as contained in the First Schedule of the
present Bill.
5.         The Secretary  apprised the Members that the Second Schedule consisted of two provisions; the first being the Indian Succession
Act, 1925 and the second provision relating to the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973.  While justifying the Second Schedule of the Bill, he 
stated that with the insertion of 376A, 376B and 376C in the 1983 amendment of the Criminal Procedure Code, and 376D followed by
Section 377 which related to unnatural offences, the words “ditto” which was in the original amendment got incorporated which was not
intended, resulting in the alteration of the nature of imprisonment and also offence and, therefore, called for an amendment.
