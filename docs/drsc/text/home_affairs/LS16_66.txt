REPORT
 
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs having been
authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Sixty-sixth Report on the Constitution (Eighty-
eighth Amendment) Bill, 1999*.
2.         In pursuance of the rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees the Chairman, Rajya
Sabha referred** the Constitution (Eighty-eighth Amendment) Bill, 1999 (Annexure-I), as introduced in the Rajya Sabha
on 23 December 1999 and pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.
3.         The Bill seeks to  insert a proviso at the end of  Article 335 of the Constitution  with a view to enabling the State to
restore the relaxations of qualifying marks and standards of evaluation in the matters of reservation in  promotion for
Schedule Castes and Scheduled Tribes, which has been withdrawn w.e.f. 22 July, 1997.
4.0       The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill explains that the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes had
been enjoying the facility of relaxation of qualifying marks and standards of evaluation in matters of reservation in
promotion. Such relaxations were withdrawn by the Government of India through Department of Personnel & Training
Office Memorandum dated 22 July 1997 (Annexure II) with a view to giving effect to the contentions of the Supreme
Court’s judgements in the case of  S. Vinod Kumar Vs. Union of India and Indra Sawhney and Others Vs. Union of
India.  The Supreme Court in both the judgements has held  that    relaxations    in matter of  reservations in promotion were
not permissible under  Article 16(4) in view of the command contained  in Article 335 of the Constitution.
4.1       It is further mentioned in the Statement of Objects and Reasons that Government has received representations from
several quarters including the Members of  Parliament to restore the relaxations existing prior to the issuance of Office
Memorandum dated 22 July 1997 for which the Bill has been introduced for restoration of such facilities to the Scheduled
Castes/Tribes.
5.0       Reservation in matters of direct appointment/promotion for Scheduled  Castes/Scheduled  Tribes  is provided 
through executive  instructions issued by the  Government  for which the Government derives its authority from Articles
16(4) & 16 (4A) of the Constitution.
5.1       Reservation to Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes  were  provided  in direct  recruitment   as well as promotion
w.e.f. 21 September, 1947  and 4 January, 1957, respectively, in accordance with the reservation policy of the Government
of India.
5.2       The percentage of reservation for Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes in direct recruitment  as well as  promotion is
limited to 15%   and 7.5%, respectively, which is fixed on the basis  of their population.  The percentage  of   population 
vis-à-vis   representation  of
SCs/STs  in Government  Services  as  submitted by the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions  is
reproduced  below :-

5.3       Special dispensations in the   form of relaxation/concession in the standards of evaluation in initial appointment as
well as promotion have been provided to SCs/STs  to ensure reservation envisaged  under Article 16(4) and 16(4A) of the
Constitution.
5.4       In the Background Note on the Bill, it has been mentioned that relaxation in direct appointment  as well as promotion
were permissible in favour of the Scheduled Castes/Tribes  prior to the issuance of DOPT, OM dated 22 July 1997. 
Relaxation in matters of promotion included more number of chances to appear in qualifying promotional tests, extended
zone of consideration, relaxation in  maximum age limit wherever permissible,  qualifying marks and standard of evaluation.
Following are the main relaxations/concessions permissible   to SCs/STs:-
(i)                  Relaxation in upper age limit by 5 years in Direct Recruitment and also in promotions  where  prescribed
age is not exceeding 50 years.
(ii)                No fee to be paid by SCs/STs at recruitment stage.
(iii)               Relaxation of experience in direct recruitment.
(iv)              Relaxation in standards of suitability in Direct Recruitment.
(v)                When the promotion is by non-selection method, in the event of non-availability  in order  of seniority,
eligible SC/ST candidates could be picked up till the bottom of the Seniority List for filling up the reserved
vacancies.
(vi)              When the promotion is by Selection Method, the normal zone of consideration is extended up to five times
the number of vacancies proposed to be filled up by promotion in order to make up the short-fall of suitable
SCs/STs in the normal zone of consideration.
(vii)
             When recruitment is by promotion as well as Direct Recruitment and no SC/ST  candidate satisfying the
eligibility criteria is available, such a vacancy is temporarily  diverted from promotion method to Direct
Recruitment method in order to ensure that the reserved vacancy is filled up only by persons of the reserved
category.
(viii)
           In case of non-availability of  a SC or ST for a continuous period of 3 years, the vacancy is exchanged  with
the other category.  However, in case of promotion by Selection to Group `B’  posts and above  carry forward
is not allowed  but exchange is allowed in same year.
(ix)              Separate interview is held  for the SCs/STs in Direct  Recruitment.  SC/ST candidates called for interview by
the UPSC/SSC etc. are granted T.A.
(x)        Relaxation in qualifying marks in Limited Department Competitive Examination wherever held  for
promotion.
(xi)       Relaxation in qualifying examination for promotion wherever prescribed.  SC/ST candidates not upto the
general qualifying standard  could be considered for promotion provided they were not found unfit.
(xii)             Relaxation in standards of evaluation viz. SCs/STs not meeting the prescribed benchmark  could be
promoted provided they were not found unfit for  promotion.
 
6.0       The Office Memorandum under reference inter alia states that the Supreme Court in the case of S. Vinod Kumar Vs.
Union of India (JT 1996(8) S.C. 6431) has held that the provisions for lowering qualifying marks/lesser level of evaluation,
in the matter of promotion, provided for candidates belonging to the Scheduled Castes/Tribes under the Government’s
instruction is not permissible under Article 16(4) in view of the command contained in Article 335 of the Constitution.
6.1       The said OM  withdrew the relaxations/concessions enumerated  at Sr. Nos.(x), (xi) and (xii) of  para No. 5.4 supra
in pursuance of the  Supreme Court judgement in Vinod Kumar case which has reiterated the contentions of nine-judge
bench judgement in the case of Indra Sawhney  Vs. Union of  India,  popularly known as Mandal case.
7.0       The Indra Sawhney Vs. Union of India case basically relates to the issue  of making provision of reservation  to the 
Other Backward  Classes, wherein Article 16 (4)  was interpreted.  The Supreme Court  while interpreting Artilce 16(4) has
held that   reservation in promotion for the Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes is not sustainable  under Article 16(4)  in
view of provision laid down in Article 335.
7.1.0    Para 831 of the Supreme Court judgement in Indra Sawhney case reads as under :-
“We must also make it clear that it would not be impermissible for the State to extend concessions  and relaxations to
members of reserved categories in the matter of promotion without compromising the efficiency of the
administration.  The relaxation concerned in State of Kerala Vs. N.M. Thomas (1976) 2 SCC 310, and the
concessions namely carrying forward of vacancies and provisions for in-service coaching/training  in Akhil
Bharatiya Soshit karamchari Sangh V. Union of India. (1981) 1 SCC 246, are instances of such concessions and
relaxations.  However, it would not be permissible to   prescribe lower qualifying marks or a lesser level of
evaluation for the members of reserved categories since that would compromise the efficiency of administration.  We

66th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/66threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:40]
reiterate that while it may be permissible to prescribe a reasonably lesser qualifying marks or evaluation for the
OBCs, SCs and STs – consistent with the efficiency of administration and the nature of duties attaching to the office
concerned – in the matter of direct recruitment, such a course would not be permissible in the matter of promotions
for the reasons recorded hereinabove.”
 
7.1.1    Further,  has  para 829 of the Supreme Court judgement in Indra Sawhney’s case protection  has been given for
reservation in promotion for five years without permitting lower qualifying marks/lesser level of evaluations in the
examination for promotion.
7.1.2        In this context, the Supreme Court has also in the same case observed that:
“…We see no justification to multiply `the risk’, which would be the consequence of holding that reservation can be 
provided even in the matter of promotion.  While  it is certainly just to say that a handicap should be given to 
backward class of citizens at the stage  of initial appointment, it  would be a serious  and unacceptable inroad  into 
the  rule  of   equality of  opportunity   to say that such a    handicap   should   be
 provided  at every stage of promotion throughout their career.    That  would  mean creation of a permanent  separate
category apart from the mainstream – a vertical  division of the administrative  apparatus.  The members of reserved
categories need not have to compete  with others but only among themseleves.   There would  be no  will to work, compete
and excel  among  them.  Whether  they  work  or not, they tend to think, their  promotion is assured.  This in turn is bound
to generate a feeling of despondence  and “heart-burning” among open competition  members.   All this is bound  to affect
the efficiency of administration.  Putting the members of backward  classed on a fast-track would necessarily result in leap-
frogging and the deleterious  effects of “leap-frogging” need no illustration at our hands.  At  the initial stage of recruitment 
reservation can be made in favour of  backward  class  of  citizens  but once  they enter the service, efficiency  of
administration  demands that these members too compete  with others and earn promotion like  all others; no further
distinction can be made  thereafter with reference  to their “birth-mark”, as one of the learned Judges  of the Court has said
in another connection.  They are expected to operate  on equal footing with others.  Crutches cannot be provided throughout 
one’s career.  That would not be in the interest of efficiency  of administration nor in the larger  interest of the nation"”
 
7.2       Salient rulings/observations of the Supreme Court having bearing on various aspects of reservation policy in matter
of promotion as submitted by the Ministry are reproduced under:-
 
(i)
                  Nine-Judge Constitution  Bench Judgement dated 16.11.1992 in the case of Indra Sawhney Vs. Union of
India:-
 
(a)        In providing for the reservation of appointments or posts under Article  16(4), the State has to take
into consideration the claims of the members  of the backward    classes consistently  with the
maintenance  of the efficiency  of administration.
(b)
               It must not be forgotten that efficiency of administration is of such paramount importance that it
would be unwise and impermissible  to make any reservation at the cost of efficiency of
administration.  That undoubtedly  is the effect of Article 335.
(c)                It would not be permissible to prescribe lower qualifying marks or a lesser level of evaluation for the
members of reserved categories since that would compromise the efficiency of administration.
 
(ii)                Two-Judge Bench Judgement dated 1.10.96 of the Supreme Court in the case of  S. Vinod Kumar  Vs.
Union of India
 
(a)    The provision for lower qualifying marks or lesser standards of
evaluation, in matters of reservation in promotion is not permissible under Article  16(4) in view of
the command contained in Article 335 of the Constitution.
(b)   The protection for five years given to reservation in promotion by the judgement in Indra Sawhney case
did not apply  to such  concessions/relaxations.
 
(iii)               Five-judge Constitution bench judgement dated 16.9.1999 in the case of Ajit Singh and Ors  Versus the
State of Punjab  and others.
 
 
(a)        Article 335 takes care to make this an express Constitutional limitation upon the discretion vested in
the State while making provision for adequate  representation for the Scheduled Castes/Tribes.  Thus,
in the matter   of due  representation in service for Backward Classes and Scheduled Castes and

Tribes, maintenance of efficiency  of administration  is of paramount importance.  As pointed in Indra
Sawhney, the provisions of the Constitution must be interpreted in such a manner that a sense of
competition is cultivated among all service personnel, including the reserved categories.
 
8.0       The Government in order to overcome  the judgement of Supreme Court in Indra Sawhney case (1992)  had inserted
Article 16(4A)  making explicit  provisions of reservation for SCs/STs  in promotion   through  Constitution (Seventy-
seventh Amendment) Act, 1995. 
8.1       The said Article was again challenged in Vinod Kumar case (1996) which reiterated the judgement in Indra Sawhney
case  as result of which Para 829 of latter judgement became valid.  In short, both judgements of the Supreme Court lay
emphasis on the point that relaxations of  qualifying marks  and  standards  of  evaluation  in  matters  of  reservation  in
promotion are not permissible in view of the provisions laid down in Article 335 of the Constitution.
9.         In order to implement the above said judgement and also some other judgements, the Department of Personnel and
Training issued four Office Memoranda during 1997 (January 30, July 2, July 22, August 29), the impugned one being the
OM No.36012/23/96-Estt (Res) dated  22 July 1997 which withdrew the instructions in regard promotion for the SCs/STs
contained in a host of DOPT’s OM having Nos. i.e. (i) 8/12/69-Estt. (SCT) dated 23 December 1970; (ii) 36021/10/76-Estt.
(SCT) dated 21 January 1997 and (iii) para 6.3.2 of the DPC guidelines contained in OM No.22011/5/86-Estt. dated 10 April
1989, which prescribed lesser qualifying marks for the Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes in departmental
qualifying/competitive examination for promotion.  Fifth OM dated 13 August 1997 was issued in pursuance of Article
16(4A) of the Constitution to ensure that reservation in promotion for the Scheduled Castes/Tribes continues after 15
November, 1997.
10.       The Bill under consideration takes care of only one Office Memorandum dated 22.7.1997. In respect of another
Office Memorandum dated 29.8.1997, Constitution (Eighty-First Amendment) Act, 2000  has already come into  force. 
Office Memorandum  dated 2.7.1997 is still under examination.  As  regards the Office Memoranda  dated 30.1.1997 and
13.8.1997, it has been  decided to await the outcome of certain  Writ  Petitions which stand filed in the Supreme Court on
the content and constitutionality of Article 16 (4A) of the Constitution.
11.       The proceedings of the Lok Sabha were disrupted on 17 & 18 March, 1999 due to persistent demand from some of
the Members that the five OM issued by DOPT adversely affected the interests of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled
Tribes and should
be withdrawn.  Subsequently, an unscheduled debate took place on the reservation issue relating to these OM and the Prime
Minister made a statement in the Lok Sabha in which he stated as under :-
 
“….. In view of the various representations that have been received from several quarters and the debate in the
House, the Government has already  started  the process of    reviewing   of these memoranda. Two of  these
memoranda are currently
under  adjudication of the Supreme Court.  We would welcome the formation of a Committee of Members of 
Parliament who could go into the subject and assist the Government in this regard.  The Government is committed to
maintain the system of reservations that has been followed so far and is prepared to bring forward a legislation even
to amend the Constitution, if required to do so.”
12.       The Government  sought the legal opinion of Attorney-General on the subject matter of the office memorandum
dated 22 July 1997.  The opinion of Attorney-General as quoted in the Background Note  of the Ministry is produced as
under :-
(i)
                  the OM dated 22.7.1997 has correctly understood the judgement of the Supreme Court in Vinod  Kumar’s
case and implemented it.
 
(ii)
                the Supreme Court in Vinod Kumar’s case has taken the view that the relaxation in qualifying marks and
lesser standard of evaluation does affect the efficiency of the  administration.  There can be two opinions on
whether the understanding of the Supreme Court in Indra Sawhney’s case on the scope and effect of the
earlier 7-Judge Bench case in Thomas’ case is correct.  It is also arguable that this issue did not directly arise
for consideration in Indra Sawhney’s case.  It is equally arguable that the observations of the Supreme Court
in Indra Sawhney are obiter dicta.  Be that as it may, the Government is bound by the judgement in Vinod
Kumar’s case unless it is re-considered or over-ruled by a larger Bench.
(iii)               neither the judgement in Indra Sawhney’s case nor the judgement in Vinod Kumar’s case has been over-
ruled in a subsequent judgement and they constitute the correct law of the land and are binding under Article
141 of the Constitution.

(iv)              if the intention is to get over the judgement in S. Vinod Kumar’s case, it can be done, if at all, only by a
constitutional amendment by the Parliament exercising its constituent power and not by legislation .  I must
point out that such an amendment would also be subject to judicial review in view of the judgement in
Keshavananda Bharti’s case (1973) 3 SCC 225 for examining whether the Constitutional amendment is
violative of the  basic structure of the Constitution.   It is not, however, possible to predict with any certainty
the result of such judicial scrutiny.
(v)
                an amendment of the Constitution may be considered by way of a proviso to Article 335 rather than as a
sub-Article or proviso  to Article 16.  This could conceivably enable the Government to contend  that the
Right to Equality, which is a basic structure of the Constitution, is not, in any manner, being affected by the
amendment, and that the Article requiring  maintenance of efficiency of the administration is being suitably
amended and thus no part of the basic structure of the Constitution is damaged or destroyed.
(vi)
              the Government would be in a position to restore the relaxation withdrawn in OM dated 22.7.1997 if a
proviso is inserted to Article 335 to the following effect :-
“Provided, however, that nothing in this Article shall prevent the State from making any provision in
favour of the members of the SCs and STs for relaxation in qualifying marks or lowering the
standards of evaluation in a qualifying examination in matters of promotion to any class or classes of
posts in the services under the State.”
13.0     The Ministry has mentioned in the Background Note that the incorporation of a proviso on the lines exactly as
suggested by the Attorney-General would not enable the Government to restore the relaxation withdrawn by OM dated 22
July, 1997.  Such restoration could be possible if  the words “in  a qualifying examination”  appearing  between
the words “standards of evaluation” and “in matters of promotion” in the suggestion of the Attorney-General were not added
in the amending  Bill.
13.1     To give effect to the intention of the Government, the Legislative Department of Ministry of Law, Justice  and
Company Affairs drafted the  Bill in question  to insert proviso  to   Article 335 of the Constitution.
14.0     The Committee heard  the presentation of the Secretary (Personnel)  in its sitting held on 26 June 2000.  His
presentation revolves around the circumstances and opinion of Attorney-General on the Bill as explained in preceding
paragraphs, which led the Government to come forward with Constitution amendment Bill. 
14.1     The Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sittings held on 10 July 2000
14.2     The Committee in its sitting held on 10 July 2000,  pointed out the difference between the amendment suggested by
the  Attorney-General and the proposed amendment drafted by Legislative Department, and wanted to know the reasons
behind omissions of certain words from the proviso to Article 335 as  suggested by the Attorney-General. 
14.3
          The representative of the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances  and Pensions as well as Legislative Department 
enumerated   the following  existing methods through which a candidate  gets promotion :-
 
(i)
                  qualifying examination
(ii)                evaluation of standards
 
14.3.1  The latter is mainly assessed through  the Annual Confidential Report.   In case of adhering  to the scheme as
suggested by Attorney-General, the Government would not have been able to been ensure the position which existed prior to
22.7.1997, in the matters of promotion in favour of SCs/STs because of the fact that holding of limited examination in case
of evaluation  of standards  (which is normally done through ACR) would have become mandatory.  Thus, the purpose
behind the amendment of Constitution in  the present form is to restore staus-quo ante.
14.4     The Ministries apprised  ( see Annexure-III) the Committee that the proposed amendment has been agreed to by
Attorney-General and prepared in consultation with the Law Secretary, Secretary,  Department of  Personnel and  Training
and Secretary, Legislative Department.
15        The Committee is of the view  that the proposed amendment will be open to judicial review .  The Ministry has
also submitted that  the proposed amendment to the Constitution as any other constitutional issue, is amenable to judicial
scrutiny in the Supreme Court.  It is also been submitted by the Ministry that the Attorney-General has not indicated in his
opinion as to whether  the Indra Sawhney and Vinod Kumar judgements will remain operative or not after the proposed
amendment.
16.       The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its sitting held on 12 July, 2000.

Clause 2
 
17.0     Clause 2, is a proviso to Article 335 of the Constitution which makes provision of relaxation in qualifying  marks in
any examination or lowering standards of evaluation for reservation in matters of promotion to any class or classes of
services or posts in favour of Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes.
17.1     The Clause was adopted without any change.
Clause 1, the Enacting Formula and Title
18.       Clause 1, the Enacting Formula and Title  were  adopted with some changes which were of consequential or drafting
nature, namely, “1999’ and “Fiftieth” to be substituted by “2000” and “Fifty-first”, respectively.
19.       The Committee is in agreement with the provisions of the Bill and recommends that the Bill in its present form be
passed.

THE CONSTITUTION (EIGHTY-EIGHTH AMENDMENT)
 BILL, 1999
 
A
 
BILL
 
Further to amend the Constitution of India
 
BE it enacted by Parliament in the Fiftieth Year of the Republic of India as follows :-
 
 
 
5
 
 
 
10
 
 
 
1.This Act may be called the Constitution (Eighty-eighth Amendment) Act, 1999.
2. In article 335 of the Constitution, the following proviso shall be inserted at the end. Namely
:-
 
     “Provided that nothing in this article shall prevent in making of any provision in favour of
the members of the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes for relaxation in qualifying
marks in any examination or lowering the standards of evaluation, for reservation in matters of
promotion to any class or classes of services or posts in connection with the affairs of the Union
or of a State”.
 
Short title
Amendment
of article 335
 
 
 
STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
     
The Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes had been enjoying the facility of relaxation of qualifying marks and
standards of evaluation in matters of reservation in promotion.  The Supreme Court in its judgment dated 1.10.1996 in
the case of S. Vinod Kumar Vs. Union of India held that such relaxations in matters of reservation in promotion were not
permissible under article 16(4) of the Constitution in view of the command contained in article 335 of the Constitution. 
The Apex Court also held that the law on the subject of relaxations of qualifying marks and standards of evaluation in
matters of reservation in promotion is one laid down by the nine-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court in the
case of Indira Sawhney and others Vs. Union of India others.  Para 831 of Indira Sawhney judgment also held such
relaxations as being not permissible under article 16(4) in view of the command contained in article 335 of the

66th Report
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/66threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:40]
Constitution.  In order to implement the judgment of the Supreme Court such relaxations had to be withdrawn with effect
from 22.07.1997.
 
2.   In view of the adverse effect of the order dated 22.07.1997 on the interests of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled
Tribes, representations had been received by the Government from several quarters including the Members of
Parliament.  Considering the various representations, the Government has reviewed the position and decided to move for
constitutional amendment with a view to restore the relaxations which were withdrawn vide instructions issued by the
Department of Personnel and Training on 22.07.1997.
 
3.
      The Bill seeks to achieve the aforesaid object.

2.2    Salient rulings/observations  of the Supreme Court having bearing on various aspects of reservation policy in matters
of promotion are as under:
 
(i) Nine-Judge Constitution Bench Judgment dated 16.11.1992 in the case of lndra Sawhney Vs. Union of India: -
 
*          In providing for the reservation of appointments or posts under Article 16(4), the State has to take into consideration
the claims of the members of the backward classes consistently with the maintenance of the efficiency of
administration.
 
*          It must not be forgotten that efficiency of administration is of such paramount importance that it would be
unwise and impermissible to make any reservation at the cost of efficiency of administration. That
undoubtedly is the effect of Article 335.
 
*           It would not be permissible to prescribe lower qualifying marks or a lesser level of evaluation for the members of
reserved categories since that would compromise the efficiency of administration.
 
(ii)  Two-Judge Bench Judgment (dated 1.10.1996) of the Supreme Court in the case of S. Vinod Kumar Vs. Union of
India reiterating the principles enunciated in the Nine-Judge Constitution Bench Judgment (dated 16.11.1992) of the

*          The provision for lower qualifying marks or lesser standards of evaluation, in matters of reservation in promotion is
not permissible under Article 16(4) in view of the command contained in Article 335 of the Constitution
.
*          The protection for five years given to reservation in promotion by the judgment in Indra Sawhney case did not apply
to such concessions/relaxations.
 
(iii)       Five-Judge Constitution Bench Judgment dated 16.9.1999 in the case of Ajit Singh and Ors Versus the State of
Punjab and ors. (to be called Ajit Singh-11):-
 
*          Article 335 takes care to make this an express Constitutional limitation upon the discretion vested in the State while

administration is of paramount importance. As pointed in lndra Sawhney, the provisions of the Constitution must be
interpreted in such a manner that a sense of competition is cultivated among all service personnel, including the
reserved categories.

Query raised
*          Reservation is justifiable at the initial stage of recruitment. However, after that to continue providing
reservation for SCs/STs in promotion also is not justifiable.
Reply
 
Supreme Court in lndra Sawhney case held that reservation in matter of promotion is not sustainable under Article
16(4) of the Constitution. Supreme Court also held that it would not be permissible to prescribe lower qualifying marks or a
lesser level of evaluation for the members of reserved categories since that would compromise the efficiency of
administration. Following observations of the Supreme Court in the Indra Sawhney case are relevant in this regard:-
 
“…We see no justification to multiply ‘the risk’, which would be the consequent of holding that reservation can be
provided even in the matter of promotion. While it is certainly just to say that a handicap should be given to
backward class of citizens at the stage of initial appointment, it would be a serious and    unacceptable inroad into the
rule of equality of opportunity to say that such a handicap should be provided at every stage of promotion throughout
their career.      That would mean creation of a permanent separate category  apart from the mainstream - a
vertical division of the administrative apparatus. The members of reserved categories need not have to compete
with others but only among themselves. There would be no will to work, compete and excel among them.
Whether they work or not, they tend to think, their promotion is assured. This in turn is bound to generate a
feeling of despondence and "heart-burning" among open competition members. All this is bound to affect the
efficiency of administration. Putting the members of backward classes on a fast-track would necessarily result in
leapfrogging and the deleterious effects of "leapfrogging" need no illustration at our hands. At the initial stage of
recruitment reservation can be     made in favor of backward class of citizens but once they enter the service ,
efficiency of administration demands that these members too compete with others and earn promotion like all
others; no further distinction can be made thereafter  with reference to their "birth-mark", as one of the learned
Judges of this Court has said in another connection. They are expected to operate on equal footing with others.
Crutches cannot be provided throughout one’s career.     That would not be in the interest of efficiency of
administration nor in the larger interest of the nation".

reservation in promotions.   In pursuance thereof, necessary orders were issued on 13.8.1997 to continue reservation in
promotion beyond 15.11.1997 when the five year period allowed by the Supreme Court for reservation in promotion to
remain operative would have come to an end.
