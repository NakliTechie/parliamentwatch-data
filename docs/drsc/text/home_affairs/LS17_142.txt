CHAPTER-I
THE SCHEME OF MODERNISATION OF PRISON ADMINISTRATION
1.1 Background
1.1.1 Explaining about the historical background and the genesis of the scheme of Modernization
of Prison Administration, the Ministry of Home Affairs in the background note submitted in April,
2007, stated that keeping in view the pathetic, unhygienic and awful condition of the average Indian
Prisons primarily because of overcrowding, Mulla Committee (1983) ♣, Kapoor Committee
(1986) ♦, Iyer Committee (1987) ♥ and Parliamentary Committee on Empowerment of Women
(2001)♠ recommended for replacement of dilapidated prisons; improvement of living conditions;
provision for medical and psychiatric services; vocational training and gainful employment;
diversification of educational programme; improvement in the condition of women prisoners;
segregation of prisoners; provisions for free legal aid and speedy trials including humane and
dignified treatment with the prisoners. In compliance with the recommendations of these
Committees, the Government of India released a fund to the tune of Rs. 125.24 crore during 1987-
2000. However, conditions of the prisoners did not improve as per expectations nor was there any
significant change in the general conditions of prisons or in the attitude of the jail authorities.
1.1.2 The M inistry further stated that ‘prison’ being a State Subject as per Entry 4 of List II (State
List) to the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution, the upkeep of prisons was within the jurisdiction
of the State Governments. Due to paucity of funds with the State Governments and keeping in view
the awful condition of the prisons in the States, the Kapoor Committee, especially, emphasized the
need to provide Central Assistance to the States under the scheme called the Modernisation of Prisons
for improving the conditions of the prisons, prisoners and prison personnel.
1.1.3 Considering the appalling condition of the prisons in the States, paucity of funds with the
State Governments and the dire need for improving the conditions of prisons, prisoners and prison
staff, a much larger investment in this sector was required. Based on an assessment conducted by
the Bureau of Police Research and Development (BPR&D), the Central Government launched the
scheme of Modernisation of Prison, Administration. The Ministry of Home Affairs in the
background note stated that the non-plan scheme “Modernisation of Prison Administration” with a
total outlay of Rs. 1800 crore was launched by the Central  Government. The scheme involved
the contribution from the Central Government as well as the State Governments on a cost sharing
basis of 75:25 and was proposed to be implemented over a period of five years from 2002-03 to
2006-07. The scheme is applicable to 27 States except Arunachal Pradesh to whom Eleventh
Finance Commission had granted Rs. 10 crore separately for building a jail.
♣ All India Jail Reforms Committee of 1980-83 under the Chairmanship of Justice Anand Narain popularly known as the
Mulla Committee, to study all aspects of prison administration in the country and to advise measures for its effective
improvement.
♦ A Group of Officers on Prison Administration, headed by Shri R.K. Kapoor (1986) popularly known as Kapoor
Committee, was constituted to examine and review various aspects of administration and management of prisons, espe-
cially in the context of security and discipline in prisons and suggest measures for their improvement.
♥ National Expert Committee on Women Prisoners of 1987 chaired by Justice V .R. Krishan Iyer.
♠ Third Report of the Parliamentary Committee on Empowerment of Women on “Women in Detention” prsented to the
Parliament on 24th August, 2001.

2
1.1.4 According to Model Prison Manual for the Superintendence and Management of Prisons in
India, formulated by BPR&D, Ministry of Home Affairs, closed prisons are classified into three
categories, as under:—
(a) Central Prison: authorized population not to exceed 1000.
(b) District Prison: authorized population not to exceed 500.
(c) Sub-Jail: authorized population not to exceed 300.
1.1.5 The Ministry further stated that the scheme envisaged preparation of five years perspective
plan and annual action plans for each year by the States and their approval by Central Empowered
Committee. The Five Years Perspective Plan and Annual Action Plan 2002-03 were obtained from
the State Governments and approved by the Central Empowered Committee. As the scheme was
launched in the middle of the year 2002-03, no budget provision was earlier made and the
requirement of Rs. 270 crore as Central share for the year was made in March, 2003 through a
supplementary demand at a much later stage. Accordingly, the release of Central share to States
aggregating Rs. 270 crore in the first year was released in March, 2003.
1.1.6 The Ministry further stated that due to delayed start, the Scheme virtually started in the
third year, and was therefore extended by a further period of two years w.e.f. 1.4.2007 without
affecting the total outlay of Rs. 1800 crore. The State Governments had also been allowed to utilize
10% of their entitlement under the Annual Action Plan 2006-07 on purchasing equipment for
modernization of jails and building/improving infrastructure for undertaking correctional
programmes.
1.1.7 The Ministry stated that the primary objective of the scheme was to assist the States in
enhancing the prisons’ capacity for accommodating prisoners and to improve the living conditions
of inmates and prison staff. The scheme aims at extending basic amenities, medical and psychiatric
services, better security, vocational training programmes, rehabilitation of prisoners, improving the
conditions of women prisoners and facility for the care and primary education for their children.
1.1.8 Under the scheme, financial assistance is given to State Governments for undertaking the
following items of works:—
(i) Construction of additional jails to reduce overcrowding;
(ii) Repair and renovation of existing jails;
(iii) Improvement in sanitation and water supply; and
(iv) Living accommodation for prisons staff.

CHAPTER-II
OFFICIAL PRESENTATION BY THE MINISTRY OF HOME AFFAIRS
2.1.1 During the course of presentation made before the Sub-Committee, the Joint Secretary,
MHA stated that although the primary responsibility was with the State Governments to take
necessary action for modernizing infrastructure of prisons, with a view to bringing the prison
management in consonance with the philosophy of reformation and rehabilitation in place of
deterrence and retribution, the Scheme for Modernization of Prison Administration was introduced
by the Central Government due to paucity of funds available with the State Governments. It was
also stated that elaborate measures had been taken to monitor the implementation of the scheme
which inter-alia included submission of quarterly progress reports by the State Governments,
extensive review meeting periodically with each State Government and regular visits by the senior
officers of MHA to States.
2.1.2 Explaining about the Scheme, the Joint Secretary, MHA informed the Sub-Committee that
the following measures were taken for an overall prison reforms:—
(i) Insertion of new section 436A  in the Code of Criminal Procedure which provides
that where an undertrial prisoner, other than the one accused of an offence for
which death has been prescribed as one of the punishments, has been under
detention for a period extending to one half of the maximum period of imprisonment
provided for the alleged offence, should be released on a personal bond with or
without sureties;
(ii) Section 436(1) in the Code of Criminal Procedure has been amended to provide that
if a person is accused of a bailable offence and cannot furnish surety, the Court shall
release him on a personal bond without sureties;
(iii) A system of plea bargaining has been introduced where an accused will be entitled
to a reduced sentence if he admits to the crime and agrees to compensate the victim;
(iv) Proposal for amendment in the Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Bill, 2006,
whereby in Section 167, following sub-Section(2) is to be inserted
♣:—
“no Magistrate shall authorize detention of the accused in custody of the police
under this section unless the accused is produced before him in person for the first
time and subsequently every time till the accused remains in the custody of the
police, but the Magistrate may extend further detention in judicial custody on
production of the accused either in person or through the medium of electronic video
linkage.
Provided further that in case of woman under eighteen years of age, the detention
shall be authorized to be in the custody of a remand home or recognized social
institution.”
(v) Approval has been given for creation of 1562 Fast Track Courts upto 31 March, 2010.
So far 10.4 lakh cases have beet disposed off;

2.1.3 During the course of presentation, the DG (BPR&D), made the following submissions
before the Committee:
(i) Prisons do not have budget of their own.
(ii) The Prison Act, 1894, which is still in use, needed to be amended.
(iii) The Annual Conference of DGs (Prisons) should be held at fixed intervals on the
lines of the Director Generals of Police conference.
(iv) The P rison Act, 1894 does not come in the way of implementing ‘Model Prison Manual’.
(v) The adoption of the ‘Model Prison Manual’ by states would solve many problems
related to human rights, staff constraints, etc.
(vi) States like Punjab are going at a very slow pace in regard to modernization of
prisons even though sufficient funds are available.
(vii) A Monitoring Group should be formed to ensure quality in terms of utilization of
money for prison modernization.
(viii) A Board of Visitors should be formed in every State to inspect the prisons.
(ix) Use of latest technology in modernisation scheme should be incorporated.
(x) NCRB records should be checked to know the performance of State Governments.
(xi) The Committee should ask the states to clarify as to whether they were abiding by
the judgments of Supreme Court on prison systems.
(xii) Alternative sources of energy should be used in the newly constructed police-stations
and jails.
(xiii) Garbage should be converted into manure in the jails.
2.2 Further comments of the Ministry of Home Affairs
2.2.1 The Ministry vide its note submitted in January, 2009 stated that the scheme had been
extended for a period of two years without additional funds to enable the State Governments to
complete their activities by 31st March, 2009. Out of total Central share of Rs. 1347.40 crore
allocated to all the States over a period of five years, an amount of Rs. 1314 crore had already
been released to the State Governments.

5
2.2.2 The Ministry also stated that the scheme was being monitored regularly by way of obtaining
quarterly progress report from all the State Governments and regular review meetings with the
State Governments officials. The senior officers of the level of Joint Secretary of the Ministry had
been assigned to make on the spot inspection and review the scheme with the State Governments.
All the States who were slow in the progress of implementation had assured that they would
complete all the activities undertaken under the scheme timely.
2.2.3 The Ministry further stated that with a view to evaluate the scheme of Modernization of
Prisons through an independent agency, Request for Proposals (RFPs) from reputed firms were
invited. Three firms had been shortlisted at the pre-qualification stage. On the basis of evaluation
of financial and technical proposals submitted by these firms, the work would be awarded to one
of these companies. It was also stated that a Committee under the chairmanship of Additional
Secretary (CS), Ministry of Home Affairs had been constituted to finalise the firm.
2.3 Second phase of scheme of modernization of prisons
2.3.1 The Ministry in their further comments also informed that considering the demands of
various States for granting further financial assistance for prison infrastructure and correctional
administration, the Ministry of Home Affairs had initiated the process of formulating the second
phase of the scheme of modernization of prisons. It was also stated that necessary steps were
being taken in this regard in consultation with the Ministry of Finance.

CHAPTER-III
VISITS TO JAILS
3.1 The Sub-Committee visited some selected jails in the country to make on the spot study
of the implementation of the scheme. The Sub-Committee visited Tihar Jails in Delhi on
21st September, 2007. The Sub-Committee also visited Haryana, Punjab, Chhattisgarh, Orissa,
Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu. The Sub-Committee, during the study visits, also
held discussions with the senior officers of the jails and the State Governments. The details of the
discussions and the findings of the Sub-Committee are given in the succeeding paragraphs.
3.2 Visit to Tihar Jails, Delhi
3.2.1 The Sub-Committee visited Tihar Jails, Delhi on 21st September, 2007. The sub-Committee
visited Central Public Relation Office at Gate No. 3, Model Interview Room, IGNOU Study Centre,
Jail Hospital and Drug de-addiction Centre in Jail No. 3, Jail Factory in Jail No. 2. The Sub-
Committee also visited Meditation Room, TIP Room in Jail No. 4 besides a creche in Jail No. 6.
3.2.2 It was apprised that the erstwhile Delhi Jail, located in Delhi Gate area, was shifted to Tihar
Village in the Western part of Delhi in 1958. In the beginning, only one Central jail was
commissioned, but as on date, there were ten functional jails in Delhi-nine Central Jails in Tihar
Campus and one District Jail at Rohini.
3.2.3 During the course of the visit and the interaction with the inmates and the officials of the
prison, the Sub-Committee came across following facts:—
(i) There was overcrowding of inmates in various jails. The total lodging capacity of all
the Nine Central Prisons and District Jail, Rohini is 6250 prisoners whereas there
were 12884 prisoners lodged in the jails as on 17th September, 2007;
(ii) A large number of under-trials, almost 81% of prisons population, are lodged in the
jails. There were undertrials who are unable to furnish surety after the grant of bail;
(iii) The lodging of high security prisoners including dreaded terrorists, pose serious
problems to the jail administration and its management;
(iv) About 8 to l0 per cent of the prisoners are drug addicts and their management
through Drug Detoxification and Rehabilitation Centers was itself a big task. There
should be compulsory testing of HIV/AIDS of all inmates;
(v) A drug De addiction Centre with a capacity of 120 beds is not fully functional due
to shortage of medical officers and other para-medical staff. A proposal for additional
requirement of 54 medical and para-medical staff has been forwarded to Government
of NCT of Delhi;
(vi) The jail administration is facing acute problem due to shortage of staff in various
ranks. Around 25% of posts of the total sanctioned strength of 1154 are vacant. The
ideal requirement of the prison staff as per the Model Prison Manual should be in
the ratio of 1:6. A proposal has been sent to Delhi Government for creation of 16
posts of Deputy Superintendents and 78 posts of Assistant Superintendents in Delhi-
Jails for their smooth functioning;

7
(vii) There is no training institute to train the prison personnel in various aspects of prison
management. The present system of training being imparted to the staff for three
months at Patiala is inadequate;
(viii) Adequate rehabilitative schemes should be introduced for the convicts who are
released from the prisons after undergoing imprisonment for five years or more.
Provision of assistance of Rs.10,000/- to the life convicts by the Social Welfare
Department is totally inadequate;
(ix) A proposal was sent to GNCT of Delhi for upward revision of wages paid to the
prisoners on the scale of Rs.52/- for the skilled, Rs.44/- for the semi-skilled and
Rs.40/- for the unskilled per day;
(x) The process of acquisition of land and its allotment to the Prison Department by
DDA at Narela and Baprola has not been completed;

4.4 Lodging of inmates as per their classification
4.4.1 The Committee has been given to understand that the inmates in the prison are classified
and lodged on the basis of sex, age, gravity of crime, undertrial/convicts. During its study visits
to various States, the Sub-Committee found that the first time offenders were being kept with
hardened criminals in the prisons and it could lead to their involvement in further criminal activities.
When this was brought to the notice of the Ministry, they replied as under:
“Prison is a State subject as per List II of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution and
the responsibility of prison administration lies primarily with the respective State
Governments. Therefore lodging of inmates in the jails as per their classification is done
by the respective State Governments. However, the Model Prison Manual  prepared by the
Government of India also prescribes segregation of inmates on the basis of their age, sex,
gravity of crime etc.”
4.4.2 The Committee is disappointed with the routine reply of the Ministry. Although
‘Prison’ is a State subject, the Committee feels that the Ministry should take the initiative
for ensuring that the State Governments comply with the Model Prison Manual. The
Committee is of the considered view that the young offenders/undertrials need to be kept
separately as it has been observed that, when they are kept with other hardened criminals,
they often commit more serious crimes after release from Jails. The Committee desires
that serious action may be taken to address this issue.
4.5 Section 436A of Cr. P.C. and Plea Bargaining
4.5.1 Section 436A of the Criminal Procedure Code provides that where an undertrial prisoner
other than the one accused of an offence for which death has been prescribed as one of the
punishments, has been under detention for a period extending to one half of the maximum period
of imprisonment provided for the alleged offence, should be released on a personal bond with or
without sureties. It also provides that in no case will an undertrial prisoner be detained beyond the
maximum period of imprisonment for which he can be convicted.
4.5.2 Section 436(1) of Cr. P.C. has been amended to the provide that if a person is accused of
a bailable offence and cannot furnish surety, the Court shall release him on a personal bond without
sureties. There is also a mechanism of plea bargaining in which an accused will be entitled to a
reduced sentence if he admits to the crime and agrees to compensate the victim.
4.5.3 When asked about the status report on implementation of the provisions for mandatory

4.7 Conditions of women prisoners
4.7.1 There are instances when women inmates had been harassed sexually by the prison staff.
It has also been seen that women prisoners face social ostracism after coming out of the jail and
the stigma of jail term sometimes ruins a women’s life because her family usually refuses to take
her back after her release. Separate prisons for women are not available in all the States. They are
generally housed in segregated wings of prisons for men. The situation of children accompanying
their mothers inside the jail is also a cause of concern. The sub-Committee during its study visit
found that the facility of creche for these children was not available in some of the jails.
4.7.2 The Supreme Court has expressed its deep concern on the plight of children who are lodged
in prisons with their mothers and has issued thirteen guidelines in the case of R.D. Upadhayay Vs.
State of Andhra Pradesh (Writ Petition Civil 559 of 1994) for implementation by all the States/UTs
and State Legal Authorities ensuring the extension of certain facilities to the women prisoners and
their accompanying children, with the direction to comply with these directives within four months
from the date of issue of the order and file compliance report by the individual State. The
Parliamentary Committee on Empowerment of Women after undertaking a study on the subject
“Condition of Women Prisoners” had in its third report emphasized the need to provide facilities such
as creches for children of women prisoners kept in jails, hygienic living conditions and provisions
for separate kitchens for women prisoners, etc. Responding to the issue, the Ministry stated that:—
“the Scheme for Modernisation of Prisons includes construction of new jails, repair and
renovation of existing jails, construction of staff quarters and improving the water and
sanitation in jails. The State Governments had the flexibility of building creches, and
separate kitchens for women in the new prisons to be constructed and under the repair and
renovation component. It was also stated that depending on the requirement States like
Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab etc. had used the funds for the above purpose.”
4.7.3 T he Committee takes note of the disappointing reply of the Ministry. The guidelines
for the preparation of action plan by the individual States lay down specifically that all the
States will have at least one prison exclusively for women. The States which do not have
prison exclusively for women have to plan for the construction of at least one such prison
with the capacity depending upon the average number of women prisoners in the respective
State. The guidelines also lays down the requirement of creation of infrastructural facilities
like creches for the accompanying children of women prisoners, hygienic living conditions
and provision of separate kitchen for women prisoners. The Committee feels that the State
Governments should be advised to give priority to construction of separate women jails,
repair and renovation of existing women jails and provision of facilities such as creche for
minor children lodged with their mothers; and hygienic living conditions for the children
and separate kitchen for women prisoners.
4.8 Sanitation, water supply, health and hygiene in prisons
4.8.1 The prisons in India face the problem of water supply, sanitation and hygiene and lack of
health care facilities. The overcrowding in the jails has further aggravated these problems and in
many jails, living conditions are deplorable.

26
4.8.2 The Sub-Committee during its study visit, found that the jails at Karnal (Haryana), Ludhiana
(Punjab) and Raipur (Chhattisgarh) were lacking in proper toilets and bathroom and were
inadequate to cater to the daily needs of the prisoners. The prisoners at Raipur jail complained of
low supply of water. According to the guidelines for the preparation of action plan by the individual
States, there should be one toilet per 10 prisoners, 100-150 litres of water per prisoner per day
and adequate arrangement for storage of water in each ward. The guidelines also say that water
harvesting may be included as an essential component of all prison buildings. The Sub-Committee
during its study visit also found that many jails were lacking proper medical facilities and many
prisoners complained of lack of proper attention by the doctors and medical staff. The Sub-
Committee was also given to understand that there were instances when sick prisoners needing
emergency medical attention were not provided the same due to unavailability of police escort.
4.8.3 The Sub-Committee also observed that the inmates were being shaved by the barber using
same set of blades which could lead to spread of HIV/AIDS and other infection among the prisoners.
4.8.4 The Committee strongly recommends that concern of health and hygiene in the
prisons should be urgently addressed for which adequate supply of water and proper
sanitation facilities were a must. It also recommends that the existing medical facilities in
the prisons should be overhauled and one police escort party should always be provided to
accompany a prisoner requiring emergency medical attention. In the second phase of the
scheme, proper emphasis may be laid on this aspect. The Committee feels that at least in
the newly constructed additional barracks and jails, facilities for water harvesting,
alternative sources of energy, converting garbage into bio-gas etc. must be provided. The
Committee is also of the firm opinion that the old system of shaving should be dispensed
with and each prisoner should be provided a separate disposable rajor to check the spread
of HIV/AIDS among the prisoners.
4.9 Visitors' Room
4.9.1 The Sub-Committee was informed that chances of malpractices were very high when
visitors (Mulaqaatis) (friends and relatives) come  to meet the inmates. At that time they happen to
be emotionally charged and their emotions/sentiments which are exploited by the staff/members of
the prisons posted there. During its study visits, the Sub-Committee observed that the visitors’
rooms and associated areas of activity were neglected by the authorities and due to presence of
a large number of persons in that area, the inmates were not able to talk to their visitors-properly.
4.9.2 The Committee recommends that steps should be taken to make the procedure of
visits to jail inmates transparent in all the prisons of the country and there should be a
mechanism to get the feedback from the relatives of the inmates. The Committee strongly
recommends that modern visitors’ chamber should be constructed in place of ramshackled
ones in which there would be transparent glass facade and intercom facilities so that the
prisoners’ visitors can talk while looking at each other.
4.10 Other related issues
4.10.1 Prison, as per the modern concept, is a correctional centre where undertrials and individuals
convicted for a crime are confined and usually deprived of a range of personal freedom. Keeping
an individual in isolation from society in itself is a major punishment for any crime which he/she
has allegedly committed. So, the modern sociological consideration demands that the condition of
life in the prison should not be miserable and its environment should be humanized. The idea of
“humanization” does not mean making prisons a comfortable place to which every prisoner would
like to return after release but only treating inmates as human beings. The condition of prisons in
our country is widely viewed as extremely wretched.

27
4.10.2 It is the considered opinion of the Committee that the conditions in an average
Indian Prison are awful, which present a very depressing picture. Being overcrowded,
unhygienic and gloomy, these incarceration centers are presumed to be places far from
being any kind of correctional centre. They often breed hardened criminals who practically
become a menace to the society. A mindless adherence to centuries old jail manuals leaves
very little scope for any innovative approach in the matter of dealing with people who end
up in prisons for various reasons and under various circumstances. With revolutionary
changes taking place in every field around us, it is high time that our mindset towards
prisoners also undergoes a change, that a prison should truly reflect the spirit of correction
and reformation by treating the inmates as human beings.
4.11 Telephone facility for inmates
4.11.1 The Committee recommends that telephone facility should be provided in some
selected prisons of the country on a trial basis. The inmates could use this facility in the
presence of a jail staff. The telephonic conversation could also be taped which would help
in tracking the connections of the hardened criminals. In the opinion of the Committee,
telephone facility for inmates could check illegal use of mobile phones in the prisons.
4.12 Prison Workshops
4.12.1 The Committee observed that barring a few prisons, the prison workshops were still
of primitive type. In these workshops activities like carpet making, carpentry, etc. were still
being done. The Committee recommends that prison workshops need to be urgently
modernized and they should function like small modern industrial units with latest
equipments. The Ministry may look into this aspect in the second phase of the scheme.
4.13 Wages of Prisoners
4.13.1 The Committee is of the firm opinion that daily wages of the convicted prisoners
working in the prison workshops need to be increased. Simultaneously, these prisoners
should also be given the option of working extra hours to earn more money. The Committee
also desires that undertrials should be given an opportunity to work in the prison workshop,
if they willingly agree to do so.
4.14 Vocational Training
4.14.1 The Committee desires that there should be an effort to achieve total literacy among
prisoners across the country. The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be
facility of correspondence courses in vocational disciplines in the prisons and degrees/
diplomas should be awarded to the prisoners after successful completion of their course so
that they could lead a normal life in the mainstream of society, after their release.
4.14.2 The Government may consider opening of ITI-like institutions within the jail
premises to impart industrial training in various disciplines to the inmates, which would go
a long way in rehabilitating them after their release. In this context, the State authorities
can also consider converting existing prison workshops into ITIs.
4.15 Grievance Redressal System
4.15.1 The Committee is of the opinion that a grievance redressal mechanism should
properly function in every jail in the country and there should be transparency in its
functioning. The Committee, therefore, suggests that every State Government should
introduce “Mobile Petition Box” for prisoners to give suggestions and complaints.

28
4.16 Vacancies
4.16.1 The Committee is of the considered opinion that the existing vacancies of prison staff must
be filled up expeditiously and new posts for which proposals have already been sent, be created
at the earliest. The Committee also feels that the duties and responsibilities of Jail Superintendents
are of a very sensitive nature requiring high degree of commitment, discipline and good physical
fitness. Under the Jail Manual the Superintendent of a Jail is the back-bone of the entire supervision
and governance of a particular jail. Most of the present incumbents are promotees, in the age group
of 56-59 years and not capable of withstanding the high-stress assignment and unable to exercise
effective supervision and control in managing the Prison affairs. The Committee recommends that
State Governments should be advised to post young and dynamic officers as Jail Superintendents
for effective and meaningful management of Jails.
4.17 Open Jail
4.17.1 During its study visits the Sub-Committee observed that ‘Open Jail’ was a prison of a new
kind where prisoners having good conduct and who had spent satisfactorily a certain portion of
their sentence, were kept. In this prison, the prisoners were allowed to live a community life and
enjoy much freedom during their stay in the jail. The Sub-Committee also visited open jails near
Bhubaneshwar and Hyderabad. The inmates of the prisons were very well behaved. In the open
prison near Bhubaneshwar, the land was being utilized properly for growing medicinal and aromatic
plants by using biological farming practices. The Superintendent of this prison informed that the
vegetables and other eatables produced in the jail were consumed by the prisoners and the surplus
was sold in outside market. The factory workshop in the prison was functioning properly.
4.17.2 The Committee feels that every State should have such open jails where the
prisoners having good conduct and who have spent considerable years of their sentence,
could be sent.
4.18 Release of Prisoners Sentenced for Life
4.18.1 D uring the course of their study visits, the Sub-Committee found that there were many
poor convicts who have completed fourteen years of term with a good conduct but as they were
unable to pay bribe, the officer in-charge of the respective police station, the latter would give
negative reports and consequently the State Sentence Revisory Board would not consider their
cases for release.
4.18.2 The Committee recommends that the power of giving character certificate entrusted
to be respective Jail Superintendents as they are the authorities who had been closely
watching the activities of the inmates for a long time.
4.19 Horticulture in Jail Premises
4.19.1 The Sub-Committee came across the large tracts of land in the jails which remained
unutilized. The Committee feels that planting of medicinal plants, horticulture and kitchen
garden etc. should be encouraged in such land as it would not only increase the income of
the prisons but would also lead to improving the physical environment in the jail premises.
4.20 Drugs in Jail Premises
4.20.1 During the study visits to various jails, the inmates complained to the
Sub-Committee about the nexus between officials of the jails and some inmates resulting
in the supply and use of drugs inside the jails. The Committee takes a serious view of this

29
and strongly recommends that necessary steps should be taken by each of the State
Governments and the prison officials to prevent such a nexus and use of drugs in jails.
4.21 General
4.21.1 The Sub-Committee noticed and came across several problems in the jails visited by
it. All the problems have been explained in the preceding chapters. The Committee desires
that the concerned jails and the State Governments and also the Union Ministry of Home
Affairs may look into those problems and solve them at the earliest.
4.21.2 As stated elsewhere in this Report, the condition of most of the jails in the country
leave much to be desired and require a complete change of mindset of those providing
administration to these prisons. The ideals of reformation and resettlement should be a
predominant feature of reforming the inmates in a democratic society. The purpose of the
present Report would be more than achieved if it attracts the attention of the concerned
quarters in the administrative machinery and this modest effort is continued further to
ensure that our country has prisons of global standards with all basic and primary facilities
for the inmates.

UTILIZATION OF FUNDS UNDER SCHEME
The Committee, while taking note of the fact that the scheme got a slow start due
to delay in preparation and approval of estimates and release of matching share by the State
Governments, expresses its concern that against the total released amount of Rs.1756.56
crore (with Central share of Rs. 1315.14 crore and the States share of Rs. 441.42 crore upto
December, 2008), the funds utilized by States remained at Rs. 1375.53 crore which is 78.3%
of the total amount. The Committee also takes a note that due to a variety of reasons, the
implementation of the scheme has been behind schedule by almost two years and the
scheme has therefore been extended by two years w.e.f. 1.4.2007 without affecting the total
outlay to enable all the State Governments to complete the entire work by 2008-09.
However, the Committee is unhappy to note that even with the extended tenure of the
scheme, the States have not taken advantage of the same and the works are yet to be
completed. (Para 4.1.4)
The extended tenure of the Scheme will come to an end on 31.03.2009. The
Committee is of the view that given the fact that barely a month has been left before the
scheme would come to a close, it would be unrealistic to expect that all pending works would
be completed. That being the scenario, the Committee urges upon the Union Home
Secretary to urgently convene a meeting of Chief Secretaries and DGPs and IG (Prisons)
of all States so that brainstorming sessions could take place to introspect on the
achievement and shortcomings of the Central Scheme. Such a conference would also provide
opportunities to find out the reasons for the unsatisfactory performance of some State
Governments and also to acknowledge and encourage the good work done by other States.
The Committee is firmly of the view that such an exercise would be a good prelude before
giving concrete shape to the second phase of the Scheme. In this context, the Committee
underscores the need for getting an evaluation of the Scheme done by an expert body. The
Committee does not appreciate the idea of the Ministry to engage an external body for the
purpose. The Committee does not understand why MHA should look for an external agency
when an organization under its control, viz., Bureau of Police Research and Development is
very much competent to take up such a task. The Committee strongly recommends that
MHA should entrust the study of evaluation of the implementation of the Scheme to
BPR&D. (Para 4.1.5)
The Committee supports the idea of increase in allocation to the better performing
States like Chhattisgarh, Haryana, Gujarat, J&K, Manipur, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh
etc. which would not only lead to improving the conditions of the prisons, prisoners and
prison staff but would also encourage other States not performing well, to catch up with the
better performing States in the country. (Para 4.1.6)
The Committee has noted that the States of Mizoram, Nagaland, Orissa, and
Uttaranchal, have utilized hundred per cent funds released to them under this Scheme
and the States of Haryana, Karnataka, Manipur, U.P and West Bengal are nearing the
target in terms of utilization of funds allocated to them. However, Bihar, Goa, Himachal
Pradesh, Jharkhand and Kerala have performed badly. The Committee desires that special
30

31
attention may be given to those States lagging behind in the second phase of the
Scheme. (Para 4.1.7)
PHYSICAL PROGRESS OF V ARIOUS COMPONENTS OF THE SCHEME
From the above table, it could be seen that there is substantial shortfall in

its repetition before starting the second phase of the Scheme. (Para 4.2.2)
OVERCROWDING IN PRISONS
The sub-Committee during its visits to various jails has found that one of the most
important hurdles coming in the way of prison reforms is the problem of overcrowding of
prisoners which is the root cause of most of the ills besetting the prisons. This problem
could be solved only by construction of additional barracks and new jails on priority basis.
The Ministry of Home Affairs should seriously introspect and discuss this aspect with all the
State Governments before launching the second phase of the Scheme. (Para 4.3.1)
LODGING OF INMATES AS PER THEIR CLASSIFICATION
The Committee is disappointed with the routine reply of the Ministry. Although
‘Prison’ is a State subject, the Committee feels that the Ministry should take the initiative
for ensuring that the State Governments comply with the Model Prison Manual. The
Committee is of the considered view that the young offenders/undertrials need to be kept
separately as it has been observed that, when they are kept with other hardened criminals,
they often commit more serious crimes after release from Jails. The Committee desires
that serious action may be taken to address this issue. (Para 4.4.2)
SECTION 436A OF Cr. P.C. AND PLEA BARGAINING
During its study tour to various jails, the sub-Committee found that many poor
prisoners were languishing in jails despite being granted bail due to their inability to provide
surety. The Committee feels that the State Governments have not been able to effectively
invoke Sections 436A, 436(i) and 265 to 265 L of the Cr.P.C., 1973 with the objective to
reduce the overcrowding and languishing of undertrials in jails. The Committee urges upon
all the concerned authorities to extensively use these legal provisions which would help in
reducing the burden on the prison infrastructure. (Para 4.5.4)
MODEL PRISON MANUAL
Contrary to the claim of the Ministry of Home Affairs, the sub-Committee, during
its study visits to the various States, found that the Model Prison Manual was not being
implemented properly. The Committee, therefore, desires that the Ministry should impress
upon the State Governments to implement the Model Prison Manual in its letter and spirit
so as to maintain uniformity in the prison administration throughout the country which will
go a long way in effecting prison reforms. (Para 4.6.2)
CONDITIONS OF WOMEN PRISONERS
The Committee takes note of the disappointing reply of the Ministry. The guidelines

32
for the preparation of action plan by the individual States lay down specifically that all the
States will have at least one prison exclusively for women. The States which do not have
prison exclusively for women have to plan for the construction of at least one such prison
with the capacity depending upon the average number of women prisoners in the respective
State. The guidelines also lays down the requirement of creation of infrastructural facilities
like creches for the accompanying children of women prisoners, hygienic living conditions
and provision of separate kitchen for women prisoners. The Committee feels that the State
Governments should be advised to give priority to construction of separate women jails,
repair and renovation of existing women jails and provision of facilities such as creche for
minor children lodged with their mothers; and hygienic living conditions for the children
and separate kitchen for women prisoners. (Para 4.7.3)
SANITATION, WATER SUPPLY, HEALTH AND HYGIENE IN PRISONs
The Committee strongly recommends that concern of health and hygiene in the
prisons should be urgently addressed for which adequate supply of water and proper
sanitation facilities were a must. It also recommends that the existing medical facilities in
the prisons should be overhauled and one police escort party should always be provided to
accompany a prisoner requiring emergency medical attention. In the second phase of the
scheme, proper emphasis may be laid on this aspect. The Committee feels that at least in
the newly constructed additional barracks and jails, facilities for water harvesting,
alternative sources of energy, converting garbage into bio-gas etc. must be provided. The
Committee is also of the firm opinion that the old system of shaving should be dispensed
with and each prisoner should be provided a separate disposable rajor to check the spread
of HIV/AIDS among the prisoners. (Para 4.8.4)
VISITORS’ ROOM
The Committee recommends that steps should be taken to make the procedure of
visits to jail inmates transparent in all the prisons of the country and there should be a
mechanism to get the feedback from the relatives of the inmates. The Committee strongly
recommends that modern visitors’ chamber should be constructed in place of ramshackled
ones in which there would be transparent glass facade and intercom facilities so that the
prisoners’ visitors can talk while looking at each other. (Para 4.9.2)
OTHER RELATED ISSUES
It is the considered opinion of the Committee that the conditions in an average
Indian Prison are awful, which present a very depressing picture. Being overcrowded,
unhygienic and gloomy, these incarceration centers are presumed to be places far from
being any kind of correctional centre. They often breed hardened criminals who practically
become a menace to the society. A mindless adherence to centuries old jail manuals leaves
very little scope for any innovative approach in the matter of dealing with people who end
up in prisons for various reasons and under various circumstances. With revolutionary
changes taking place in every field around us, it is high time that our mindset towards
prisoners also undergoes a change, that a prison should truly reflect the spirit of correction
and reformation by treating the inmates as human beings. (Para 4.10.2)
TELEPHONE FACILITY FOR INMATES
The Committee recommends that telephone facility should be provided in some
selected prisons of the country on a trial basis. The inmates could use this facility in the

33
presence of a jail staff. The telephonic conversation could also be taped which would help
in tracking the connections of the hardened criminals. In the opinion of the Committee,
telephone facility for inmates could check illegal use of mobile phones in the prisons.
(Para 4.11.1)
PRISON WORKSHOPS
The Committee observed that barring a few prisons, the prison workshops were still
of primitive type. In these workshops activities like carpet making, carpentry, etc. were still
being done. The Committee recommends that prison workshops need to be urgently
modernized and they should function like small modern industrial units with latest
equipments. The Ministry may look into this aspect in the second phase of the scheme.
(Para 4.12.1)
WAGES OF PRISONERS
The Committee is of the firm opinion that daily wages of the convicted prisoners
working in the prison workshops need to be increased. Simultaneously, these prisoners
should also be given the option of working extra hours to earn more money. The Committee
also desires that undertrials should be given an opportunity to work in the prison workshop,
if they willingly agree to do so. (Para 4.13.1)
VOCATIONAL TRAINING
The Committee desires that there should be an effort to achieve total literacy among
prisoners across the country. The Committee, therefore, recommends that there should be
facility of correspondence courses in vocational disciplines in the prisons and degrees/
diplomas should be awarded to the prisoners after successful completion of their course so
that they could lead a normal life in the mainstream of society, after their release.
(Para 4.14.1)
The Government may consider opening of ITI-like institutions within the jail
premises to impart industrial training in various disciplines to the inmates, which would go
a long way in rehabilitating them after their release. In this context, the State authorities
can also consider converting existing prison workshops into ITIs. (Para 4.14.2)
GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL SYSTEM
The Committee is of the opinion that a grievance redressal mechanism should
properly function in every jail in the country and there should be transparency in its
functioning. The Committee, therefore, suggests that every State Government should
introduce “Mobile Petition Box” for prisoners to give suggestions and complaints.
(Para 4.15.1)
