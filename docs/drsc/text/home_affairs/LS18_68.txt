CITIZENS’ CHARTERS
 
Recommendation (para 10.4)
 
5.0       Committee feels that the initiative  taken by the Ministry to formulate  Citizens’ Charters on the pattern of the
UK Government is commendable one.  The Committee suggests that widest possible consultation should be undertaken
in formulating the Charters.  The Committee desires  that the Citizens’ Charters  so far formulated should be made
available to the public including representatives of people for information and review. The Committee strongly
recommends that all those States/UTs/Organisations who have not formulated the Citizens’ Charters be pursued to do so
at an early date.
 
 
Action Taken/Proposed to be taken
 
5.1       Ministries/Departments/Organisations and States/UTs have been addressed demi-officially vide d.o. letters on
25.5.2000 and 17.5.2000 emphasising the need for (a) consultation (b)  putting the Charter on Website and making it
available  to public including representatives of people, and (c) expediting formulation of Charters.
 
Observations/Recommendations  of the Committee
 
5.2.0    The Committee notes that the approach of Ministry of Personnel, Public  Grievances and Pensions for making
Citizens Charters a viable instrument has been confined merely to writing couple of d.o. letters to
Ministries/organizations for framing  their Citizens’ Charters and putting those on their respective websites for which no
time frame has been  indicated.  Further no clear cut instructions have been issued to the individual Ministry /Department
to give wide publicity to their respective websites, which could enable public to evaluate their Citizens’ Charters.
 
5.2.1    The Ministry has not clarified  how Citizens’ Charters framed so far  would be made available to public including
representatives  of the people for review.    The  Ministry is also silent  as to the  question of the mode of  receipt of
feedback from public and representatives  of the people  for their suitable  modifications by the concerned Ministry,  so
as to make  those citizen-friendly.
 
5.2.2    The Committee, therefore, desires  that Citizens Charters and websites  of the Ministries/Departments may be

 ADMINISTRATIVE COURTS
 
Recommendation (para 14.4)
 
9.0       The Committee suggests that the Lok Adalat in Delhi may be put on experimental  basis.  In  case it serves the
purpose, similar Court may be established in other parts of the country. The Committee also advocates the enactment of
an administrative procedure code whereby if any administrative officer fails in discharging his/her responsibility,  he/she
would be made punishable for dereliction of duty.
Action Taken/Proposed to be Taken
 
9.1.0    As already  stated in our reply to the Questionnaire on the Demands for Grants (2000-2001), Lok Adalat has
already  been set up in Delhi  under the Delhi Legal Services  Authorities Act, 1987  under  the aegis  of Ministry of
Law.  It has jurisdiction over all Central Ministries/Departments located in Delhi and falling within the jurisdiction  of
the  Delhi High Court.  It is seen that the Lok Adalat  Scheme  set up in Delhi  has been specifically  formulated  for
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India.  As   Lok Adalats are the concern  of Ministry of Law, the
recommendation of Committee to extend it to other parts of the country has been forwarded  to Ministry of Law vide a
d.o. letter dated 02.08.2000.
 
9.1.1    As regards enactment of an administrative procedure code, it may be mentioned that at present, recruitment  to
various Central Services  and posts is regulated  by separate  recruitment rules framed by the President in exercise   of
the powers  conferred  by the proviso  to Article 309.  No law has yet been enacted to regulate  the recruitment  and the
conditions  of service   of Central Government  employees  as contemplated   in Article 309,  excepting to a  limited
extent.  The question of enactment  of Act of Parliament under Article  309 was considered in 1950,  soon after the
Constitution  came into force.  After a protracted  discussion,  it was   decided in 1954 to continue  to regulate service 
conditions  by a “regimen of rules”, as it was found to be more convenient in practice.
 
9.1.2    The recommendation  of the Committee was examined in great detail and it was found not acceptable mainly for
following reasons:-
 
(i)
                  The number of posts and the  categories  under various Ministries  and Departments are too many 
having different conditions of service.  It would be an almost  impossible task  to codify all these different
rules  into a comprehensive legislation.
(ii)                The administrative machinery of the Government  has grown up so much in volume  and complexity.
(iii)               The existing  system also requires thorough scrutiny by the  various  agencies like   the Department of
Personnel, Ministry of Law, Ministry of Finance, C&AG, and  UPSC etc.   After the rules are framed,
these are also subject to scrutiny  by the Committee on Subordinate Legislation.
 
(iv)
              Even if a comprehensive Bill is drafted and passed into an Act of Parliament, in each and every
subsequent amendment, the matter will have to be processed in Parliament, which will burden both the

68th Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/68threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:36:41]
Houses further.
(v)                The existing system of framing  rules has  got flexibility.  Rules are less formal and easier to operate  and
amend, compared to the Acts of Legislature, which involve a complicated process.
 
As regards the objective of the recommendation that if any administrative officer fails in discharging his/her
responsibility, he/she  would be made  punishable for dereliction of duty, it may be stated  that the existing  disciplinary
rules provide for imposition of any penalties (ranging  from ‘censure’ to ‘dismissal’)  for ‘good and sufficient  reasons’. 
Under the provisions of the Conduct  Rules, every  Government  Servant  is required to maintain  devotion to duty and
shall do nothing , which is unbecoming of a Government  Servant.  It has  been prescribed  in the Conduct Rules that a
Government  Servant who habitually fails to perform the task assigned to him  within  the time set for the purpose and
with the quality of performance  expected of  him shall be deemed to be lacking  in devotion  to duty.  Thus any case of
dereliction of duty can be dealt with under the existing rules itself.
 
Observations/Recommendations  of the Committee
 
9.2       Explanations offered for not enacting the administrative procedure code is noted.
 
 
LAW OF ADMINISTRATION
Recommendation (para 15.5)
 
10.0     The Committee appreciates the endeavour made by the Government to implement the recommendations of the
Commission on Review of Administrative Law and suggests that the implementation should be completed within a
specific time period.  The Committee desires that the Ministries/Deptts which have not given the rationale of retaining
laws recommended by the Commission for repeal should furnish the same to the Ministry of Personnel.
Action Taken/Proposed to be Taken
 
10.1.0  Copies of the Report of the Commission have been sent to all the Ministries/Departments of the Government  of
India  and Chief Secretaries  of State  Governments/U.Ts for examination  of the various  recommendations and for
devising  the suitable Action Plan  for implementation of the recommendations.  Most of the Ministries  have initiated 
action to bring about suitable  amendments/modifications in the Acts  and Laws, etc. being  administered  by them.  They
have also  initiated action to repeal the dysfunctional  laws.  The implementation of the  recommendations of the
Commission is an on-going process.  It is being monitored on a regular basis by the Department of Administrative 
Reforms &  Public Grievances and the Legislative  Department (Ministry of Law, Justice & Company Affairs).  The
Government has also set up a Standing Committee under the Chairmanship of Secretary(Personnel) to monitor the
follow-up action on the recommendations of the Commission.
 
10.1.1  The concerned Ministries/Departments have been addressed to indicate the reasons for their decision to retain the
Acts recommended to be repealed by the Commission.  Of the 18 Acts in this category, reasons have been received in
respect of 17 Acts.  A list of these 18 Acts indicating the present status is enclosed. Annex - V.
 
Observations/Recommendations  of the Committee
 
10.2     The Committee observes that monitoring of the follow up action on the recommendations of the Commission
may be taken up with utmost sincerity and in a time bound manner.

23.1.0  The Committee has, inter alia, observed that the various Committees of Parliament including Standing

23.1.2  The Government has once more gone through the Thirty-eighth Report of the Committee.  In the  Thirty-eighth

(1)        Prior to 1995, a person moving from  Central Government  Departments to any of the central autonomous

(i)         The concept of an overriding consequence of the inclusionary  part in he definition of an autonomous
body, as given in para 4 of OM dt. 29.8.84, has been  negatived.  The CAT has clearly held that for counting of
past service in an autonomous body as per the extant rule i.e. OM dt. 29.8.84, the body  should be such institution
whose more than 50% expenditure is met with by Cess or Government grant.
 
(ii)        The CAT has clearly upheld the validity of the clarification provided vide OM dt.30.5.95, subsequent to
the definition of autonomous body indicated in OM dt. 29.8.84.
 
(iii)       The CAT has also gone into the question  of a body being considered as “the State”  and its
consequences, vide para 8 of the judgement.  The Hon’ble  CAT  has referred to the Supreme Court judgement in
this regard and held that the issue is not with regard to functions of the autonomous  body but the admissibility of
counting  the past service of working in the autonomous body when an employee joins  the Government service.

entitled for counting past service if it is wholly or substantially financed from Cess or Central Government
grants.
 (iv)      The CAT has gone into  the  question of an institution  being considered as autonomous body for the

the CAT, Mumbai and Mumbai High Court, the Government feels that the question of interpretation of existing order is
no longer open for discussion.
 
23.1.5  As regards desirability of extending the benefits of counting of past service  to cover cases of mobility between
LIC/Banks, etc. and Government and vice-versa,  the matter  does not  seem to have been considered by the
Parliamentary  Committee. However, in deference to wishes of the Committee,  the Government  has considered  the
desirability of extending  the provision of order dt 29.8.84 to LIC, Banks etc.  The Government feels that these
organisations are commercial institutions and they do not follow  the pattern  of  Central  Government rules including
CCS(P) Rules  for their employees.  The pension  structure  in these organisations is based   on a separate Pension Fund,
unlike  in the  Government.  These bodies  re-employ a large number of retired  employees from Defence/Civil
departments and counting  of their past  service would tremendously  increase the liability of their Pension  Fund. 
Besides,  in the  emerging  scenario of economic  liberalisation, the Banking and Insurance sectors are likely to open up 
in a rather  big way to private  equity participation.  In  such an eventuality  drastic  changes are likely in the institutional
structures affecting inter alia  the conditions of service in these organisations.  It would not be appropriate  to encourage 
mobility between these sectors and Government  and/or increase  financial liability in these sectors  at this juncture. 
These are intricate issues  that may have substantial impact on Government  finances and policy.  The Government is,
therefore, of the opinion that it would not be appropriate at this stage to initiate fresh action  to extend benefits of
counting past service in case of mobility between Government & LIC/Banks etc. and vice-versa.
 
Observations/Recommendations  of the Committee
 
23.2.0  It is understood that the Ministry of  Personnel, Public Grievances and  Pensions is the first respondent  in case
No. 591/98 (Manohar Burde vs. Union of India) in CAT Mumbai Bench. 
           
23.2.1  A perusal of the ATRs of the Ministry, does not reveal that the recommendations made by the two  Parliamentary
Committees (This Committee and  Committee on Petitions) were brought to the notice of the Tribunal by the Ministry. 
We in India have adversarial  system of adjudication taken from the English Legal System in contradistinction  to the
inquisitorial  legal system followed in the Continents.  In the adversarial  system, the judge is not  supposed  to search 
for the truth.  He has to decide the case based upon the material  produced before him.  In the instant case, out of all the
parties before the Tribunal, the Ministry of Personnel was surely in the know of the recommendations made by the two
Parliamentary Committees.  Therefore, it was incumbent upon the Ministry to bring the same to the notice of the
Tribunal along with other material facts.  Be that as it may, the Committee urges upon the Ministry to reconsider its
decision and intimate the outcome thereof.
