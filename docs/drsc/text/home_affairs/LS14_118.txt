INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairperson of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having been
authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this One Hundred and Eighteenth
Report of the Committee on the Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Bill, 2005.* 
 
2.         In pursuance of sub rule (a) of Rule 273 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Rajya Sabha
relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha referred** The
Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Bill, 2005 (Annexure-I), as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 13 December
2005 and pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.  The Chairman directed the Committee, under
sub-rule (b) of Rule 273 of the Rules of Procedure to present the Report on the Bill within three months from the date of
reference.
 
3.0       The Committee held three sittings on the Bill. In its meeting held on 19 January 2006, the Committee heard the
presentation of the Home Secretary on the Bill and held preliminary discussion thereon.
 
3.1       In its sitting held on 31 January 2006, the Committee held discussion on the Bill.  The Committee thereafter
considered the Bill clause-by-clause on 3 March 2006.
 
4.         The Committee also considered the draft Report in its sitting held on 3 March 2006 and adopted the same.
 
5.         In the course of its deliberations, the Committee has made use of the background note on the Bill and other
material  received from the Ministry of Home Affairs and replies of the Ministry to the queries raised by the Members
on the Bill in the meetings (Annexure-III).
 
6.         For facility of reference and convenience, observations and recommendations of the Committee have been
printed in bold letters in the body of the Report.

REPORT
The Protection of Human Rights (Amendment) Bill, 2005 seeks to amend as many as seventeen Sections
(2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,13,18,21,22,23,24,26,40 and 41) of the Protection of Human Rights (PHR) Act, 1993 (Annexure-II)
and insert a new Section 40 B to that Act.
 
1.1       Amendments to twelve Sections (2,3,4,5,12,13,18,21,22,23,26 and 40B) of the Act are substantial in nature;
amendments to two Sections (10 and 40) are clarificatory in nature and the amendments to rest four Sections (6,8,24 and
41) are consequential in nature 
 
   Some of the amendments are based on the recommendations of the Advisory Committee set-up by the National
Human Rights Commission (NHRC)1
, under the Chairmanship of Justice Shri A. M. Ahmadi, former Chief Justice of
India and others have been recommended by the Inter-Ministerial Committee comprising  Ministries of Home Affairs,
Defence, External Affairs and Law.
 
1.3       Amendments proposed in the Bill may be broadly categorized into three heads (i) amendments pertaining to
National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) (ii) amendments pertaining to State Human Rights Commission (SHRC)
& (iii) amendments to the definition clause:
Amendments pertaining to NHRC
 
1.3.0    Amendment to Section 3(2)(a) proposes modification in the eligibility criteria for the appointment of
Chairperson of NHRC to enable the Union Government to consider a retired Judge of Supreme Court having a
minimum of three years of service/experience for appointment to the post. Presently only retired Chief Justice of India is
considered eligible for the post of Chairperson of NHRC as the post enjoys the status of Chief Justice of India. The
amendment has been recommended by the Inter-Ministerial Committee to make available ample choice with the Union
Government in the appointment.
 
1.3.1    Increase in deemed members of NHRC from three to four has been proposed in the amendment to Section 3(3)
in view of bifurcation of National Commission for Schedule Castes and Schedule Tribes into two separate
Commissions, one for Schedule Castes & other for Schedule Tribes.
 
1.3.2    Amendment to Section 3 (4) enables the Chairperson, in addition to the Commission, to delegate some of the
powers and function of the Commission to its Secretary-General.
 
1.3.3    Amendment is proposed to Section 12(a) to enable the NHRC to inquire into the cases of human rights violation
and abetment thereof, referred by the Court in addition to  the present system of making inquiry suo motu or on the
petition by the victim or on behalf of the victim. 
1.3.4    Section 12(c) is proposed to be amended to enable NHRC to make surprise visits to jails, which is now possible
only after giving due intimation to the concerned State Government.
 
1.3.5    A new sub-section (6) is proposed to be added to Section 13 to enable the NHRC to transfer cases to SHRC
when NHRC feels that the complaint falls within the jurisdiction of SHRC.
 
1.3.6    Section 18 is proposed to be amended to enable NHRC to recommend award of compensation or interim relief to
the victim(s) even during the inquiry which at present can be done only after the completion of inquiry.
 
1.3.7    Tendering of resignation by  Chairperson and Members of NHRC to the President is proposed in the amendment
to Section 5 of the Act as existing provisions in the Act mention about removal only and are silent about resignation.
 
Amendments pertaining to SHRC

1.3.8    Eligibility criteria for the appointment to the post of Chairperson and Members of SHRC have been proposed to
be modified in the amendment to Section 21 (2). Only retired Chief Justice of a High Court of that State and also having
knowledge of language of that state is considered for appointment to the post of Chairperson  of SHRC. A retired Judge
of a High Court having minimum five years experience would now be considered for the post.   The amendment is
proposed as a number of States are finding difficulty to appoint Chairperson of SHRC due to lack of Chief Justice of
High Court having knowledge of vernacular of the State.  As per the proposed amendment, a Judge of High Court or
District Judge having seven years experience would be considered for appointment to the post of member of SHRC.

1.3.9    Section 21 is proposed to be amended also to provide for the appointment of common Chairperson/Member of
SHRC for two or more States (particularly smaller and financially weaker States).  This would be useful to the smaller
states particularly in the North East who for financial or other reasons may not be in a position to establish a separate
commission.
 
1.3.10  Section 23 is proposed to be amended to provide for the resignation of Chairperson and Members to be tendered
to the Governor, as the present provision mentions removal only and is silent about resignation.
Amendment to definition clause.
 
1.3.11  The definition of the term “International Covenants” is proposed to be broadened so as to include other
conventions/covenants of General Assembly of United Nations, which the Union Government may specify by
notification, in addition to Covenants on Civil and Political Rights and Economic, Social and Cultural Rights adopted by
General Assembly.  
 
Views of Members of the Committee and Response of the Ministry of Home Affairs thereon.
 
2.0       The Home Secretary in his deposition inter alia stated that only a few recommendations of the Advisory
Committee, Chaired by Justice Ahmadi, for amendments to the principal Act, have been accepted by the Government. 
In this context, the Committee sought reasons for non-acceptance of the other recommendations of the Advisory
Committee.  It was then clarified that the Advisory Committee constituted by the NHRC had submitted its report to the
latter and not to the Government of India.  A statement (Annexure-III) showing the recommendations of the NHRC
which have not been accepted by the Government along with reasons thereof was then furnished by the Ministry and
circulated amongst the Members of the Committee. 
2.1       As per the Principal Act, only the Commission is empowered to delegate some of its powers to its Chief
Executive Officer having the designation of Secretary-General.  One of the proposed amendments is going to empower
the Chairman of the Commission also to delegate the power of the Commission to its Secretary-General.  The
Committee understood that essential functions of the Commission which were quasi-Judicial in nature could not be
delegated even by the Commission to its Secretary General.  It felt that there may be scope of misuse of power by the
Chairperson in making excessive delegation of powers to its Secretary-General by-passing other Members of the
Commission. However, the Ministry of Home Affairs in its written submission has stated that the proposed amendment

is necessary to facilitate smooth functioning of the Commission. The representatives of the Ministry in the oral
submission also clarified in the meeting of the Committee held on 3 March, 2006 that the NHRC does not perform 
judicial function and the intention was only to delegate  the administrative functions and powers. The Committee,
however, desired that some safeguards may be provided in the Bill.
  The Principal Act provides for Selection Committee2
 for appointment of Chairperson and Members of NHRC and
SHRC under Sections 4 (1) and 22 (1), respectively. As per the provision of Clause 4 of the Bill, the appointment of
Chairperson/Members of the Commission would not be invalid due to vacancy or absence in the Selection Committee. 
The Committee observed that absence due to illness, out of station engagement and other such unavoidable reasons may
be considered as valid grounds. But the Bill was silent on absence of Members due to protest or dissent on the
appointment of a Chairperson or a Member. 
 
2.3       Clause 12 of the Bill seeks to amend Section 21(6) of the Principal Act to provide for common
Chairperson/Members in the State Human Rights Commission, for two or more States.  The Committee agreed that it
would be useful to smaller states, particularly NE states which may not be in a position to establish a separate
Commission.  The Committee, however, felt that the power to opt for common SHRC (Chairperson/Members(s) may be
left to the Selection Committee rather than the State Government. 
 
Clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill
 
3.0       The Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sitting held on 3 March 2006. 
Clause 2
 
3.1.0    The clause seeks to redefine International Covenants, Members of National Human Rights Commission & State
Human Rights Commission and define National Commissions for the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
           
3.1.1    The clause is adopted without any change.        
Clause  3
 
3.2.0    Sub-clause (a) provides for amendment to sub-section (2) of Section 3 of the principal Act.  In sub-section (2)
the words “Chief Justice of the Supreme Court’ are proposed to be substituted by the words “a Chief Justice of the
Supreme Court or a Judge of the Supreme Court for at least three years”. The Committee noted that the Constitution
uses  the expression “Chief Justice of India”.  The Committee therefore, recommends that in the proposed amendment,
the words “Chief Justice of the Supreme Court” may be substituted by the words “Chief Justice of India”. 
 
3.2.1    The clause further proposes to amend Section 3 (3) of the principal Act in order to enable the Chairpersons of
the National Commission for Scheduled Castes and National Commission for Scheduled Tribes to be deemed Members
of the NHRC. The clause furthermore seeks to amend Section 3(4) of the principal Act so as to empower the
Chairperson of the NHRC to delegate some powers and functions of the Commission to its Secretary General, who is
the Chief Executive Officer of the Commission, in addition to NHRC.
           
3.2.2    The Committee is of the view that an express provision needs to be incorporated in the amendment to clear the
apprehensions and doubts that the Chairperson may tend to misuse the power in making excessive delegation.  The
Committee, therefore, recommends that in sub-section (4) of Section 3 of the Principal Act, the words “except judicial
functions” may be added after the words “such powers and discharge such functions of the Commission”.
 
3.2.3    Subject to the above, the clause is adopted.
 
Clause 4
 
3.3.0    The clause seeks to amend Section 4 (2) of the principal Act to facilitate the appointment of a Chairperson or a
Member of a Commission even in the case of absence of any Member in the Selection Committee in addition to any
vacancy in the Selection Committee.

Clause 5
 
3.4.0    The clause seeks to amend Section 5 of the principal Act to provide for the  resignation of the Chairperson or
any Member of the NHRC and the authority to whom such resignation to be submitted. 
           
3.4.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause  6
 
3.5.0    The clause amends Section 6 of the principal Act to include the term “Chairperson” in the marginal heading,
‘Term of office of Members’ consequent to the proposed amendment in Sections 2(1) (b) and 2(1) (g) of the principal
Act.
 
3.5.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 7
 
3.6.0    The clause seeks to amend Section 8 of the principal Act to include the term ‘Chairperson’ in the body of the
clause as well as marginal heading consequent to the changes proposed in the definition clause.  
 
3.6.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 8
3.7.0    The clause seeks to amend Section 10 (2) of the principal Act to empower the Commission to lay down by
regulations its own procedure subject to the provisions of this Act and the rules made thereunder.
 
3.7.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 9
3.8.0    The clause seeks to amend Section 12 of the principal Act to empower the NHRC to inquire into violation of
human rights on the direction/order of any courts, and also to visit  any jail or institution for the study of the living
condition of the inmates thereof without prior intimation to the concerned State Government.
 
3.8.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 10
3.9.0    The clause seeks to add new sub-section 6 in Section 13 of the principal Act which empowers the NHRC to
transfer complaints relating to a particular state received by it to the concerned State Human Rights Commissions for
disposal by the latter.
 
3.9.1    The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 11
3.10.0  The clause seeks to amend Section 18 of the principal Act to give power to the NHRC to recommend award of
compensation or damages to the victim or his relatives in the case of violation of human rights or negligence or
prevention or abetment thereof by public servant, even during the course of enquiry.
 
3.10.1  The clause is adopted without any change.

Clause 12
3.11.0  The clause seeks to amend Section 21 (2) of the principal Act to  reduce the number of Members from five to
three (Chairperson and two Members) in the State Human Rights Commission. This clause further seeks to amend
Section 21 by adding sub-section (6) in the principal Act to provide for a Common State Human Rights Commission for
two or more Sates.                               
           
3.11.1  The Committee is of the view that the State Governments while so opting for the Chairperson and/ or Member
(s), may tend to by-pass the Selection Committee.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that the following proviso
may be added after the proposed amendment i.e., insertion of a new sub-section (6) :
           
“Provided that every appointment made under this sub-section shall be made after obtaining the recommendation of the
Committee referred to in sub-section (1) of Section 22 in respect of the State for which common Chairperson or
Member or both, as the case may be, is  proposed to be appointed.” 
 
3.11.2  Subject to the above, the clause is adopted. 
 
Clause 13
3.12.0  Like clause 4 of the Bill, the clause seeks to amend Section 22 (2) of the principal Act to facilitate the
appointment of a Chairperson or  Members of SHRC even in the case of absence of any Member in the Selection
Committee.
           
3.12.1  The Committee while reiterating its observation made in respect of the proposed amendment to sub-section (2)
of Section 4 of the principal Act, recommends that the amendment proposed to sub-section (2) of Section 22 of the
principal Act may be dropped.
 
3.12.2  Subject to the above, the clause is adopted.
 
Clause 14
3.13.0  The clause  is a corresponding clause to clause 5 which seeks to amend Section 23 of the principal Act to reflect
the aspect of  resignation of Chairperson or a Member of the SHRC. 
 
3.13.1  The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 15
3.14.0  Like clause 6, the clause seeks to amend Section 24 of the principal Act to include the term  “Chairperson” in the
marginal heading in view of distinction shown between Chairperson and Member of the Committee in the definition
clause.
 
3.14.1  The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 16
 
3.15.0  The clause is a corresponding clause to clause 8 and seeks to amend Section 26 of the principal Act to provide
for ‘terms and conditions of service’ of the Chairperson and Members of the State Commission by including the term
“Chairperson” in the body of the clause and marginal heading. 
 
3.15.1  The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 17
3.16.0  This clause seeks to propose consequential amendment in Section 40 (2) (a) of the principal Act  by substituting
the words “Members” by the words “Chairperson and Members”.
 
3.16.1  The clause is adopted without any change.
