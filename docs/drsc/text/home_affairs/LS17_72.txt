DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
 
 
6.         The main functions of the Department of Legal Affairs are:
 
(i)
                 to extend advice to the various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India on legal matters;
(ii)                to carryout the conveyancing work of the Central Government;
(iii)              to attend to the litigation work in the Supreme Court, High Courts and some of the Sub-ordinate Courts;
(iv)              to deal with the treaties and agreements with foreign countries in matters of Civil Laws;
(v)               to authorize officers to execute contracts and assurances of property on behalf of the President under
Article 299 (1) of the Constitution; and
(vi)              to sanction grants-in-aid to certain institutions engaged in research in the field of law, constitutional 
studies  and Parliamentary  institutions .
 
7.         The Department of Legal Affairs is further concerned with appointment of the Law Officers, namely the
Attorney-General for India, the Solicitor-General for India and the Additional Solicitors-General for India, etc.  It
administers the Advocates Act, 1961, the Notaries Act, 1952 and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.  The
Department is also administratively in-charge of the Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange, Income Tax Appellate
Tribunal, Indian Legal Service and Law Commission of India.
           
8.         The Department has a two-tier organizational set-up, namely, the main Secretariat at New Delhi and the Branch
Secretariats at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Bangalore.
 
G
rants-in-aid to Institutes
 
9.         The Secretary in his evidence before the Committee apprised that with a view to promoting studies and research
in matters of law, constitutional studies and Parliamentary institutions, the Department of Legal Affairs sanctions
grants-in-aid to certain institutions engaged in research in the above fields.  He informed the Committee that the Indian
Law Institute, New Delhi, the Institute of Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies (ICPS), New Delhi, and the Bar
Council of India are the institutions receiving grants from the Department.  The Indian Law Institute receive a grant of
Rs. 40 lakhs for conducting research in the field of law.  The ICPS, receives an annual grants-in-aid of Rs. 15 lakhs for
promoting studies in the constitutional systems of different countries, constitutional law and Parliamentary procedure. 
The Secretary informed the Committee that the Bar Council of India has recently been included in the list of Institutions
receiving grants from the Department with a one-time grant of Rs. 50 lakhs during the year 2000-2001 and from 2001-
2002 onwards, it will get a recurring grant of Rs. 10 lakhs for the purpose of improvement of legal education in the
Country.
 
9.1       A point was raised in the Committee that the ICPS has become a defunct institution as no worthwhile
contribution is being made by it. The Indian Law Institute(ILI) has also declined. The ILI has not produced any work
worth the name. Its journal is hopelessly in arrears for many years.
 
10.       The Committee regrets that the Institute of Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies has virtually become
defunct and the Indian Law Institute has also declined. However, it felt that grant amount received by them should be
utilized judiciously.  The Committee emphasizes that every penny spent on the legal and parliamentary education is an
investment into future.
 
Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)
 
11.       The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, constituted under the Income Tax Act, deals with second appeals in all
matters relating to direct taxes, and appeals against orders of revision of Commissioners as well as orders of acquisition
of properties under Chapter XXA of the Income Tax Act.

12.       The Tribunal is headed by a President, assisted by one Senior Vice-President and seven Vice-Presidents.  The
present strength of Members of Tribunal is 106 for 53 Benches spread over 25 cities.  There are normally two members
in a Bench known as Judicial member and Accountant member having substantial experience in their respective fields.
 
13.       The matters which the Appellate Tribunal disposes of are of vital importance involving revenue to the tune of
millions of rupees. The Tribunal is entrusted with the responsible task of deciding intricate questions of law and facts. 
The presence of both the Judicial and Accountant members ensure that questions of facts which arise for their
consideration are properly enquired into and that the accountancy points, as also the legal angle, have been weighed
properly.

15.       To reduce the pendency a decision was taken by the Government to create 15 additional Benches, of the tribunal
in addition to the 38 existing Benches with effect from 1
st April, 1997.  The Committee in its 45 th Report and 61 st
Report had expressed its displeasure over the delay in making all the additional Benches functional.  The Committee has
been informed that the Appellate has a pendency of approximately 2,44,320 cases as on March 1, 2001.  The Ministry
informed that with the exception of one Bench at Delhi, all the additional Benches have since been made functional. 
And for the remaining one Bench at Delhi, efforts are being made to locate suitable accommodation as to make the
Bench functional as early as possible.  The Committee was also informed that a number of steps are being taken by the
government to reduce the pendency of cases such as identification of cases involving issues already settled by High
Courts/Supreme Courts; grouping of cases involving common issues; organizing camp Benches at places where there is
no Bench; sending of Members on tours to stations where there is shortage of Members; conferment of powers on
Members who have completed   five years of service to dispose of cases where the assessed income is up to   Rs. 5 lakhs
in a Single Member Bench.  As a result of these measures, pendency of cases in the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has
already come down and is expected to further decline substantially.
 
 
16.       The Committee is not convinced with the argument put forward by the Ministry that the pendency has come
down substantially owing to the various measures taken and making all the Benches functional except one Bench at
Delhi. The facts speak otherwise.  The pendency is still very high as the average pendency per Bench is about 5000
appeals.  The Committee recommends to the Government to put the various measures mentioned by it into practice and
see to it that pendency is actually reduced substantially.

Ministry for the year 1999-2000 and 2000-2001 respectively had recommended to avoid inordinate delays in the

19.       The Committee appreciates the amendment to the  Notaries Rules, 1956 being carried out in favour of SCs, STs,
OBCs and Women for appointment as Notaries.  However, the speed at which the applications are disposed of is not
satisfactory.  The Government should, therefore, direct the Notary Cell to devise means to quicken the disposal at a
faster pace and also see that there is no backlog in respect of the aforesaid categories.
 
La
w Commission
 
20.       The Law Commission of India is a non-statutory body constituted by the Government from time to time.  The 1st
Commission was constituted in 1955 and is reconstituted every three years. The present (16 th) Law Commission has
been constituted with effect from 1st September, 2000 for a period of three year consisting of a Chairman, Two full-time
Members, one Member-Secretary and three or more Part-time Members depending upon the nature of work.  The
Committee was informed that at present, besides the Chairman and the Member-Secretary, two full-time Members one
of whom is a vice-Chairman are in position.  The Commission has so far submitted a total of 175 Reports and all except
one have since been laid before the Parliament. Giving further details the Government informed the Committee that the
remaining one Report will also be laid before Parliament as soon as its Hindi translation becomes available. 91 Reports
have been implemented and 33 Reports were not accepted.  51 Reports are presently under
consideration/implementation.
 
21.       On the Committee’s query as to how many Reports out of these 51 Reports exclusively relate to subjects falling
under List I & III of the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution, the Government informed that there are 16 and 35
Reports whose subjects are relatable to List I and III , respectively, of the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution.
 
22.       The Committee in its 45
th, 56th and 61st Reports on   Demands for Grants of the Ministry had recommended to
ensure the accountability of the administrative Ministries of the Government for implementation of the accepted
recommendations.  As regards reports to the subject falling under List III of the Seventh Schedule the Committee in its
61st Report had recommended that the matter may be taken up on priority basis with the State Government/UTs
Administrations in order to get these Reports implemented in a time-bound manner.
 
23.       The Committee has been informed that in accordance with its recommendations in 56 th and 61 st Reports,

correspondence as well as periodical meetings.  During the period from January 2000 till March 2001, as many as 19
such meetings were held for the purpose.  As regards the implementation of Reports relating to the subject falling under
the List III of the Seventh Schedule, consultations with the State Government/UT Administration, with a view to
implement these Reports, are carried on by the concerned administrative ministries/departments.

National Legal Services Authority  (NALSA)
 
26.       The National Legal Services Authority,  a statutory  body constituted  under the National Legal Services
Authorities Act, 1987 as amended by the Act of 1994, is responsible  for providing  free legal assistance to poor  and
weaker sections of the society on the basis of equal opportunity.  NALSA is engaged in  providing legal services, legal
aid and speedy justice through Lok Adalats.  The Authority having its office at New Delhi is headed by the Chief Justice
of India who is the ex-officio  Patron-in-Chief.  Similarly,  State Legal Services Authorities  have been constituted in
every State  Capital.  Supreme  Court Legal Services Committee, High Court  Legal  Services Committees, District
Legal Services  Authorities and Taluk  Legal Services Committees have also been constituted in every State.
 
27.       The Committee was informed that as per the information available with NALSA, as on 31
st December, 2000
about 34 lakh persons have benefited through court-oriented legal  aid  programmes.  Of them  about 5 lakh persons
belonged to  the Scheduled Castes, about 2.5 lakh to the Scheduled Tribes, more than 3.5 lakh  were women and about
30 thousand  children.  Till date about 97 thousand Lok Adalats have been organized wherein about 1 crore 25 lakh
cases have been settled.  Maximum members of Lok Adalats have been organized in Gujarat  (28,534), followed  by
Rajasthan (21,941), Andhra Pradesh (11,139)  and U.P. (5,102).  But the maximum no. of cases are settled in U.P. 
(39,74,861), followed by Gujarat (23,68,622), Orissa (18,26,995) and Rajasthan (11,61,922).
 
28.       The Ministry further apprised the Committee that there is a large number of pendency cases in various courts,
candidly admitting that even the establishment of a number of Quasi-judicial Tribunals has not helped  in significantly

Election Commission
 
54.       The Election Commission is entrusted by the Constitution to superintend conducting of general elections and the
Legislative Department is accountable to the Parliament so far as the functioning of Election Commission is concerned. 
But the entire electoral process is looked after by the Election Commission.
 
55.       Today the responsibilities of the Election Commission have increased tremendously because of many factors. 
Most important of these is frequent elections and separate elections for State assemblies and Lok Sabha elections unlike
before when both elections were held simultaneously resulting into less expenditure and less pressure on the Election

56.       The Committee appreciates the work done by the Election Commission and feels that it needs its own
administrative infrastructure.  Recognizing the fact that elections are going to be a frequent feature of our polity and
Election Commission has to discharge its Constitutional responsibilities, it is necessary that it has its own minimum
requisite supportive staff and officers and administrative set up instead of depending totally upon the State Governments
and other authorities.

to reduce the pendency of cases:
 
(a)
               More practical categorization & grouping of cases. 
(b)               Stabilization of Benches.
(c)               As far as possible cases scheduled to be taken up for hearing on a day listed for that day.
(d)               No accumulation of defective matters.
(e)               Reservation of more and sufficient slot of time for old pending Miscellaneous and SLP maters, so that
they are listed in chronological order in sufficient numbers.
(f)                 Better utilization of available computer network and streamlining of administration and manpower of
the Registry staff.
 
Pendency in Supreme Court
 
90.       The Committee was informed that as a result of the above measures the pendency of cases in the Supreme Court
has shown a downward trend.  The pendency of cases which was 1,04,936 as on 31
st December 1991 has come down to
21,936 as on 1 st February 2001.  This decrease is due to a number of steps taken by the Supreme Court like a practical
categorization and grouping of cases; stabilization of Benches; non-accumulation of defective matters; streamlining and
better utilization of available computer network; and streamlining of administration and manpower of the Registry Staff.
 
 
 
Pendency in High Courts
 
91.       The Ministry informed the Committee that the main reason for increase in pendency of cases in High Courts is
increased institution of cases as compared to the disposal of cases.  The pendency of cases in the High Courts which was
26.51 lakhs as on 31
st December 1993 increased to 29.81 lakhs as on 31 st December 1995 and further to about 31.81
lakhs as on 31st December 1997.  The pendency of cases as on 30th June 2000 was 34.33 lakhs.
 
Pendency in Subordinate Courts
 
92.       The Government further informed the Committee that the pendency of cases which was to the tune of 1.65 crore
in 1989, rose to 1.69 crore in 1991 and further to 2.18 crore in 1994, came down marginally to 2.02 crore in 1998.  Non-
filling up of the posts by the State Governments and High Courts in the Subordinate Courts is one of the main reasons
for pendency of cases.
 
Creation of Fast Track Courts

93.       The Committee was informed that the Government has been deeply concerned about pendency of cases in
Courts.  At the initiative of the Department of Justice, the XI Finance Commission recommended a scheme for creation
of 1734 additional courts in the country for disposal of long pending sessions and other cases.  The scheme has since
been sanctioned and an amount of Rs. 502.90 crore has been allocated as special problems and upgradation grant for
judicial Administration.  This amount is to be spent over a period of five years i.e. upto 2000-2005.  For the current
year, 2000-2001, an amount of Rs.202.27 crore has been allocated, from out of which Rs.50.56 crore has been released
to the States in November 2000.
 
94.       The Ministry also informed that these courts will start functioning from 1
st April 2001.  They will first take up
session cases  pending for two years or more and the cases of under trials lodged in different jails.  At present, there are
about 1.8 lakh under trials in jails on whose maintenance State Governments are spending about Rs.361 crore per
annum.  Cases of all under trials are likely to be disposed of in the very first year of the scheme, namely 2001-2002.  It
is expected that two million cases will be disposed of by these courts by the year 2005.  The scheme will effect
substantial savings in expenditure on jails besides addressing a serious human rights problem.  It will also expedite the
disposal of sessions and other cases pending for a long time, which will have salutary effect on law and order and crime
control.
 
New Plan Schemes
 
95.       The Department of Justice is going to launch two new plan schemes during the year 2001-2002.  One of these
pertains to networking of the Department of Justice with the Supreme Court and High Courts.  An amount of Rs10 lakh
has been provided in BE 2001-2002 for this scheme.  When the project is completed, it will give to the Department of
Justice online access to information of pendency and other related matters in the Supreme Court and High Courts,
reducing thereby the time lag in the flow of information to a great extent.
 
96.       The other scheme envisages computerization of courts in four metropolitan cities of Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and
Mumbai.  Courts in the country, particularly District and Subordinate courts, which constitute the cutting edge of the
justice system are in dire need of modernization.  It is proposed to computerize city courts in the four metropolises of
Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai at an approximate cost of Rs.10.60 crores, on the basis of estimates prepared by
the National Informatics Centre.  These courts would serve as models of computerized modern courts.  Planning
Commission has allocated       Rs.8.53 crore for this scheme in the year 2001-2002.  The district courts in Delhi would
be the first to be taken up for computerization under the scheme, during 2001-2002.  Once the courts are on a
computerized network, people would be able to file their petitions and complaints addressed to the courts at a central
filing and facilitation counter.  Notices and cause-lists would be made available by the computer.  Thus, the litigant
would know the date of hearing of his case from the computerized enquiry and facilitation center or through e-mail on
Internet.  Copies of orders, including interim orders, would be available through the computer to the interested party on
payment of fee.  The entire proceedings of a case would also be on the computer network.  It is expected that
computerization and networking of courts would expand the capacities of the courts substantially and thereby speed up
the delivery of justice.
 
97.       The Committee, as regards pendency of cases, is of the opinion that there is no doubt that the extension of
facilities like the infrastructural development upto sub-ordinate Courts, networking of the Department of Justice with
the Supreme Court and High Courts, computerization etc. will go a long way in early disposal of cases.  The Committee
notes with serious concern that out of about 28 lakh cases in the High Courts, almost 50 percent of the cases are pending
in Allahabad High Court.  The Committee feels that Allahabad High Court requires special attention and whatever
problems they may have, those must be resolved in consultation with the State Government.

THE DEPARTMENT OF COMPANY AFFAIRS
 
105.     The Department of Company Affairs, as a key systemic bridge between the Government and the corporate
sector, is charged with the responsibility of maintaining close and continuing dialogue between the Government and the
Management process of the corporate sector to the overall societal goals and also undertakes to protect the rights of
investors and shareholders.
 
106.            Functions of the Department include administration of the Companies Act, 1956, and the Monopolies and
Restrictive Trade Practices Act, 1969 (MRTP Act)  and to exercise  supervision over the activities of the Institute of
Chartered Accountants, the  Institute of Company Secretaries and the Institute of Costs and Works Accountants, which
are financially self-supporting.  The administration of the Indian  Partnership Act, 1932, and the Societies  Registration
Act, 1960 (21 of 1860) also rests with this Department.
 
107.     There are four Regional Directors’ offices at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Kanpur.  The main functions of
these officers are to advise and guide the Registrars of Companies on technical and administrative matters, to report to
the Government particularly on the activities and operations of companies  and also to function as a link  between the
Central Government  and the State   Governments  in the regions.  The Regional Directors supervise the offices of the
Registrars of Companies and Official Liquidators  in their respective  regions.  Some of the powers under the
Companies Act, 1956, have also been delegated to the Regional Directors for direct disposal of the applications.

72nd Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/72ndreport.htm[30-06-2016 17:39:48]
 
108.     The offices of the Registrars of Companies are located throughout  the country.  There are in all twenty offices
of the Registrars  of Companies.  Their main functions  are registration of Companies  and to scrutinize  the Annual
Returns, Balance Sheets  and other  documents filed by the public and private  companies located in their  respective 
States under the  provisions  of the Companies  Act, 1956, and to take necessary  action on the irregularities  noticed as
a result of such scrutiny.  Legal action is also  taken  by them against defaulting Companies for contravention of the
provisions of the Companies Act, 1956.
 
109.     Under the Companies Act, 1956, the Official Liquidators  are appointed by the Central Government and are
attached to each High Court and are in charge of all the companies  under compulsory  liquidation.  Some of the
companies  in voluntary liquidation are also placed under the Official   Liquidators to realize the assets of the companies
in liquidation and to pay off  the creditors of the company.  If there is any surplus, it is distributed among the
contributories  (shareholders).
 
MONOPOLIES AND RESTRICTIVE TRADE PRACTICES COMMISSION
 
110.     It is a statutory body set up under the MRTP Act, 1969.  The main functions of the Commission are:
(i)         to inquire into monopolistic  trade practices on a reference  made to the Commission by the Central
Government or on the basis of its own knowledge  or information; and
(ii)                to inquire into any restrictive/unfair trade practice on receipt of a complaint from any trader or any trade
association or from any consumer or from a registered  consumers’ association or on a reference from 
the Director General  of Investigation & Registration or from the Central or State Governments or on the
basis of its own knowledge or information.  The Commission has the power to punish for its own
contempt. Besides passing its own order the Commission has the power to award compensation  and
grant temporary injunction.
 
111.     The budget of the Department provides for the expenditure  on the Secretariat  of  the Department of Company
Affairs, offices of the Registrars of Companies, Official Liquidators and Regional  Directors and also for expenditure 
on offices of Monopolies and Restrictive  Trade Practices Commission, Director General of Investigation &
Registration and Company Law Board.  The total  Budget Demand for the year 2001-2002 amounts to Rs.46.25 crores 
(i.e. Rs.45.25 Crores under the Revenue Section and Rs.1.00 Crore under the Capital Section ) .
 
112.     The Department has no plan outlay.  The entire budget of the Department is non-plan and establishment-
oriented.  About 63.98% of the budget is for salaries, 23.98% for office expenses, 6.76% for rents and the balance
5.28% is for small sub-heads  like OTA, travel expenses,  publication, etc.  The budget  for office expenses includes  Rs.
5.00 crore for implementation of the scheme for computerization of the offices of Registrars  of Companies.  For the
purpose of purchase of land/building/construction of Office premises /residential accommodation for staff a sum of 
Rs.1.00 crores has been provided.
 
113.     Under  the Companies Act, 1956, fees are realized from the companies under various  heads such as for
registration of companies, for filing of returns/documents by companies and for making applications etc.  There are also
receipts by way of costs awarded  by courts in prosecution cases under the Companies Act.   Thus, Department of
Company Affairs is a revenue earning  Department.  Revenue  by the Department during 2000-2001 amounts  to
Rs.395.22 Crores upto December, 2000  as  against  Rs. 139.73 Crores earned during the year 1999-2000.

recommendations.  Regarding the winding up of the Companies the Eradi Committee has said that revival and

Co-operative Business
 
116.     The Committee was informed that the Central Government  had constituted a high level Committee on 1.11.99

5.
                  On the Constitution (Ninety-first Amendment) Bill, 2000, the Secretary informed the Committee that the Bill
had three components; firstly, freeze on the allocation of seats to States in Lok Sabha till 2026; secondly, readjustmentof
territorial constituencies on the basis of figures of 1991 census and thirdly, refixation of number of SC/ST seats on the
basis of 1991 census.
 
5.1       Further, the Secretary stated that the Bill proposed to extend the constitutional embargo on undertaking
delimitation of territorial constituencies  from 2000 to 2026.  However, a proposal had been made for refixing the
number of seats reserved for the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in Lok Sabha and Legislative Assemblies.  In
other words, the overall number of seats for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies would remain unchanged but the number
of Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe seats might undergo change within the overall limit of seats, as these will be
fixed on the basis of figures of the 1991 census. 
 
 
6.
                  The members were by and large in agreement with the proposed freeze on undertaking delimitation of territorial
constituencies from 2000 to 2026, but expressed their viewpoints  over the proposed increase/decrease of the number of
