            The management of personnel policies in Government of India, to begin with, was the joint responsibility of the Ministries of Home Affairs (Services and Establishment Wing) and Finance (Establishment Division) with Union Public Service Commission playing an important advisory
role. The Establishment Officer to the Government of India, functioning under the Cabinet Secretary had an important role to play in the senior appointments under the Government of India. All this made for improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified central direction.
 
2.         To plug this organisational loophole, the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha in its 93rd Report (April 1966) observed that “the ever expanding role of the government in a welfare state with its natural concomitant of a large civil service calls for effective personnel control
through a single agency”. The Administrative Reforms Commission too urged upon the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel, to be placed directly under the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal agency for the personnel functions of the Government.
 
3.         The Union Government accepted this recommendation and as a consequence the ‘Department of Personnel’ was set up in August, 1970 and placed within the Cabinet Secretariat. In February, 1973, the Department of Administrative Reforms, till then with the Ministry of Home Affairs,
was amalgamated with the Department of Personnel and the newly created department was christened, the ‘Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms’. In 1977, this department was transferred to the Ministry of Home Affairs. In March, 1985 a full fledged ‘Ministry of Personnel,
Public Grievances and Pensions’ was created.
 
4.0       The Ministry is the coordinating agency of the Central Government in personnel matters especially issues concerning recruitment, training, career development, staff welfare as well as the process of responsive people–oriented modern administration.  The Ministry comprises three
Departments, namely: -
 
(i)         Department of Personnel and Training;
(ii)        Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare; and
(iii)
               Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.
 
4.1       The Ministry has under its administrative control  four attached offices and four other offices, namely: -
 
            (i)         Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI);
(ii)
                Staff Selection Commission (SSC);
(iii)               Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM); and
(iv)              Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA).
 
4.1.1    The other offices under the administrative control of the Ministry are: -
 
(i)
                  Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), a constitutional body;
(ii)                Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), proposed to be converted into a statutory body;
(iii)               Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), a statutory quasi-judicial body; and
(iv)              Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a body set up by the resolution of Government.
 
5.         The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister, assisted by a Minister of State.  All the three Departments are functioning under the charge of the Secretary (Personnel) assisted by one Special  Secretary, three Additional Secretaries and three Joint Secretaries and other
supporting officers and staff.
 
 
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING AND THE VARIOUS AGENCIES UNDER ITS ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
 
 
6.0       The role of the Department of Personnel and Training can be conceptually divided into two parts.  In its larger nodal role, it acts as the formulator of policy and the watch dog of the Government ensuring that certain accepted standards and norms, as laid down by it, are followed by all
Ministries/Departments in the recruitment, regulation of service conditions and deputation of personnel as well as other related issues.  Towards this end, guidelines are issued by it for the benefit of all Ministries/Departments and it monitors the implementation of these guidelines.  It also advises
all organisations of the Central Government  on issues of personnel management.  At a more immediate level, the Department has the direct responsibility of being the cadre controlling authority for the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) and the Central Secretariat Service (CSS).  The
Department also operates the Central Staffing Scheme under which suitable officers from  All India Services and Group ‘A’ Central Services are selected and then placed in posts at the level of Deputy Secretary, Director and Joint Secretary on the basis of tenure deputation.  The Department also
deals with cases of appointment to posts of Chairmen, Managing Directors, full-time functional Directors/Members of the Board of Management of various public sector undertakings/enterprises, corporations, banks and financial institutions.  It also deals with the assignment of Indian experts to
various developing countries.
 
Recruitment Agencies
6.1       The two organisations through which the Department ensures recruitment of personnel for the Government are the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and the Staff Selection Commission (SSC). The former is constituted under a provision of the Constitution and is responsible for
conducting examinations for appointment to the higher civil services and civil posts under the Union Government, including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a mandatory provision for consulting the Commission on all matters relating to methods of  recruitment, principles to be
followed in making promotions and transfers from one service to another and all the disciplinary matters. The SSC is responsible for making recruitment for Group ‘B’ non-gazetted posts of Assistants, Stenographers, etc.
 
Training Institutions
6.2       Two major training institutions directly under the administrative control of the Department are the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM), New Delhi. The
former   is   mainly   responsible   for   providing   induction   training   to  recruits   to  the Indian Administrative Service and other All India Services and Central Services. The Institute of Training and Management provides induction as well as in-service training to members of
the Central Secretariat Service.
 
Senior Appointments under the Government of India
6.3       All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the approval of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary to
the ACC.  These include board level appointments to Central Public Sector Undertakings and appointments of Joint Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the Ministries/Departments. In addition, all appointments by promotion, which require the approval of the ACC are also processed
through the Establishment Officer.
 
 
Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB)
 
6.4       The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making recommendations for appointment to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executives and functional Directors and part-time Chairmen in the Central Public Undertakings.
 
 
Administrative Vigilance
 
6.5       The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for the maintenance of integrity of the public services and eradication of corruption and coordinates the activities of various Ministries/Departments in that area.
 
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
6.6       Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Central Vigilance Commission. It has jurisdiction and power in respect of all matters to which the executive power of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted with the supervision of the Central Bureau of
Investigation (CBI). The Commission enjoys the same measure of independence and autonomy as the Union Public Service Commission.
 
 
Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)
 
6.7       The Central Bureau of Investigation is the premier investigating agency of the country to investigate complex and important cases particularly relating to internal security, espionage, sabotage, narcotics, economic offences, cybercrimes and cases  registered under

Joint Consultative Machinery
6.8       The Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM) is three-tier machinery consisting of the National Council, the Departmental Council and the Regional/Office Council. Service matters pertaining to the interests of the generality of employees or specific groups of them are dealt
with by this machinery.
 
Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
6.9       In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to employees aggrieved by Government decisions, the Government has set up the Central Administrative Tribunal  (CAT) in 1985, which now dealswith all cases relating to service matters which were previously dealt with by Courts upto
and including the High Courts. 
 
 
Staff Welfare
 
6.10     The Department supports various programmes, which are provided in various Ministries/Departments, Central Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in Central Government offices located outside Delhi, Residents’ Welfare Associations/Area Welfare
Officers; Benevolent Fund and Departmental Canteens.  Department of Personnel and Training is the nodal agency for three registered societies set up  for the welfare of the Government employees and their families.  These societies are the Central Civil Services Cultural and
Sports Board, Grih Kalyan Kendra and Kendriya Bhandar. 
 
DEPARTMENT OF PENSIONS AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE
7.         The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare is the nodal agency of the Government of India for formulation of general policy on pension and other retirement benefits, as also for redressal of grievances on retirement benefits.  The Department was set up in 1985
as a part of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions and has been rendering service for the benefit of pensioners since then.  The Department has taken a number of steps over the years for streamlining the pension administration system, for greater convenience
and welfare of the pensioners.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEVANCES
8.         The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of the Government of India for administrative reforms as well as redressal of public grievances relating to the State in general and grievances pertaining to  Central Government agencies in particular.
The Department disseminates information on important activities of the Government relating to administrative reforms and public grievance redressal through Management Publications and Documentation.  This Department also acts as the nodal agency in regard to Organisation and Methods
activities and offers advice to the Ministries/Departments. It also undertakes activities in the field of international co-operation and exchanges to promote public service reforms.

10.7.    The trend of decrease in the projection of Demands of the Ministry for the year 2002-2003 is perceivable in both Plan and Non-plan outlays.
 
Non-Plan outlay/Expenditure
 
10.8.0.The Non-plan budget of the Ministry has slightly decreased from Rs 179.61 crore in BE (2001-2002) to Rs 178.69 crore in BE (2002-2003) showing a sum of Rs 92 lakh reduction (i.e. 0.5 percent).  The Ministry in its explanatory note has stated that out of the Non-plan budget provision of
Rs 178.69 crore, Rs 90.23 crore (i.e. 50.49 % of the Non-plan budget) is for the Central Bureau of Investigation alone. Five percent of the Non-plan budget i.e. 8 crore is towards making payment of House Building Advance to All India Service Officers.  The remaining portions of the Budget
(45.03%) is meant for meeting the expenditure on administrative expenditure in respect of organizations and schemes mentioned at para 10.0 of the Report.
 
10.8.1.It may be observed from the analysis of BE and RE (2001-2002) that perceptible reduction has been effected for the provisions for Secretariat General Services (Ministry proper) and the CBI under Non-plan outlay.  The Ministry has stated in their explanatory note that the reduction is due
to the implementation of guidelines issued by Ministry of Finance for mandatory 10 per cent cut in the budgetary allocation for Non-plan and Non-salary expenditures.  The guidelines as outlined in the OM No.7(4)/E-Coord/2000 dated 24 September, 2000 of the Ministry of Finance are as
follows:-

(i)                  There shall be a mandatory 10% cut in the Budgetary allocation for non-plan, non-salary expenditure of all Ministries/Departments and Autonomous Institutions.
(ii)                Utmost economy should be exercised in use of staff cars and official vehicles.  There shall be a 10% cut in the consumption and allocation of funds for expenditure on POL for staff cars.
(iii)               There will be a complete ban on purchase of new vehicles for one year.
(iv)              Implementation of existing instructions concerning 10% cut in posts and abolition of posts lying vacant for more than one year should be ensured.  There shall be a ban on creation on new posts in all Ministries/Departments/Autonomous institutions for one year.
(v)                Foreign travel should be restricted to unavoidable official engagements and there shall be a ban on foreign travel for Study Tours, Seminars etc.  Size of official delegations, where Foreign Travel is unavoidable, shall be restricted to the bare minimum.
(vi)              The permissible per diem allowance for travel abroad shall stand reduced by 25% for all countries and for all categories, officials/non officials.  These instructions shall also apply to autonomous institutions and PSUs. 
(vii)             Austerity should be reflected in organizing Conferences/Seminars/Workshops. Existing guidelines for holding such events and expenditure ceilings thereon should be enforced.
 
10.8.2.Keeping in view the above guidelines, the budget under Non-plan outlay at RE stage of 2001-2002 was reduced to Rs 174.08 crore (the provisional actual given in the explanatory note being the same) from Rs 179.61 crore (a net reduction of Rs 5.53 crores), which is approximately three
percent and is far behind the ten percent cut stipulated by the Ministry of Finance.  A comparison between BE (2001-2002) and BE (2002-2003) under the Non-plan outlay shows the decrease of a meagre amount of Rs 0.92 crore.  (0.5 percent)
 
10.8.3.While examining the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Ministry, the Committee has enquired whether 10 percent job cut, as announced by the Finance Minister in his budget speech in 2001, during the next five years had been reflected in the BE (2001-2002).  The Ministry in its
reply had intimated that since the provision had been made before the announcement of budget speech, the proposed curtailment of posts had not been reflected in BE for the year 2001-2002 and would be reflected in RE stage of the same year.
 
10.8.4.A glance over the minor heads –Salaries of all three Departments of the Ministry under the Major Head  “2052”- Secretariat- General Services at BE & RE (2001-2002) and BE (2002-2003) lends credence to the reply given by the Ministry.  The minor head Salaries of the Central Bureau
of Investigation under the Major Head “2055”- Police does not show the tendency commensurating this reflection in other Departments of the Ministry as the BE & RE (2001-2002) for Salaries of the CBI being the same (Rs 61.7 crore). Further a glance over office expenses of Departments of the

CITIZEN’S CHARTER
 
12.0     The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances coordinates the efforts to formulate and operationalise citizen’s/user’s charter by the Central Government Ministries/Departments and their PSUs/autonomous/statutory bodies in areas in which they provide services to the
public. The Department also coordinates the efforts of formulating and operationalising Citizen’s Charter in various States/Union Territories.
 
12.1.0  The Committee has been informed that 15 State Governments/UT Administrations have informed of the mechanisms evolved by them to ensure proper implementation of Charters, namely, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Pondicherry, Jammu & Kashmir, Andaman and Nicobar
Administration, NCT of Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Rajasthan, Haryana, Tripura, Gujarat and Union Territories of Lakshadweep.
 
12.1.1  On examination of the steps taken by those States/UTs, the Committee notes that certain measures taken by them to popularise Citizen’s Charter such as seminars, regular inspections by an appointed official for the purpose, ‘Reader Forum’ incorporated in the Secretariat newsletter, panel
discussion with members of consumer fora, workshops and constitution of Administrative Reforms Advisory Committees are commendable and desires that these may be used as guidelines for the States/UTs who have not furnished the steps taken by them to the Ministry. The Committee, while
reiterating its earlier view that publicity of Citizens’ Charters is very essential to make the general public aware about its existence and utility, which would help in soliciting their feedback for its evaluation, recommends that model guidelines may be framed by the Ministry of Personnel and given
to all the Ministries/Departments/Organisations so that the mode of receiving feedback would be uniform.
 
12.1.2  The Committee, thus, desires that the States which has not informed the steps taken by them in this regard to the Ministry, may be required to do so in a time- bound manner.
 
12.2     In the Action Taken Replies to the observations/recommendations contained in the   Seventy-first Report of the Committee, the Ministry had simply stated that the observations/recommendations of the Committee has been communicated to the Nodal Officers for Citizens’ Charters of all
the Ministries/Departments of Central Government and also to the Chief Secretaries/Administrators of all States/UT Governments on 25.05.01 for compliance.  The Ministry has further stated, that they have been requested to ensure that the Charter movement in their organisation is given
adequate publicity and is properly implemented.  They have been requested to evolve a feed back mechanism with participation from the representatives of the people.  This Committee is not happy the way the Ministry intends to comply with the observations/recommendations of the
Committee.  The Ministry has not stated clearly its own monitoring of the instructions/guidelines issued to different agencies under Central and State/ UT Governments.  The Ministry should have evolved a time-frame within which these agencies should have given feed back to the Ministry in
compliance of its instructions/guidelines.  As stated by the Ministry, the instructions/guidelines were issued in May 2001.  But nothing has been stated in the ATR as to what actual action has been taken by these agencies till the time of submission of the ATR to the Committee.  It appears that the
Ministry intends to avoid its responsibility.  It intends to confine its activity by merely issuing guidelines/instructions without caring for their proper compliance.  This attitude of the Ministry is bound to defeat the spirit of the observations/recommendations of the Committee.  This Committee
therefore urges upon the Ministry to submit to it the results of their monitoring of Citizen’s Charter.

PUBLIC GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL
 
14.0     The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances  issues  various  guidelines  from  time to time  to reduce number of public grievances.  During the last year the Department has published  a booklet  titled  “Guidelines for Grievances Redress Mechanism in Government
of India” in English which contains  detailed guidelines on Redressal  of public grievances. 
 
14.1     In the Action Taken Replies on the recommendations of the Committee as contained in its Seventy-first Report, the Ministry has taken the plea of discharging their responsibilities by merely reiterating  the earlier guidelines issued by the Department of AR & PG on 27 July 1989 for
institutionalising  mechanism for picking up grievances appearing in newspapers’ columns and for taking expeditious  action for their time-bound disposal.  But the Ministry has not furnished full facts as to how many such cases have been received and how many of those have been disposed of
each year during the last ten years.  This Committee does not understand why the Ministry is trying to evade its responsibility of monitoring its guidelines/instructions in compliance of the observations/recommendations of this Committee.  It is the duty and responsibility of the Ministry to ask
different agencies under both Central and State/UT Governments of the periodic reports of the achievements in quantified terms and to further submit its report to this Committee.  The Ministry is, therefore, directed to do the needful.
 
14.2.0  The organizational set up for handling public grievances  in Government of India are as follows:-
 
(i)
                  The Public Wing of the Prime Minister’s Office (PMO)
(ii)                The Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions
(iii)               The Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions
(iv)              Directorate of Public Grievances, Cabinet Secretariat
 
14.2.1  The Committee was apprised of the intention of the Government to institute “Central Grievance Commission” (CGC) as the apex body for the redressal of public grievance.  The Committee had observed in its Seventy-first Report, that proliferation of grievance redressal bodies and
apparent over-lapping of functions between these may entail confusion in the minds of complainants and would also have direct bearing on the Government exchequer.
 
14.3     The Ministry, in the Action Taken Replies on the recommendations of the Committee as contained in its Seventy-first Report, has simply stated that the observations of the Committee with regard to the policy formulators for public grievance redressal have been communicated to the
Cabinet Secretariat with the request that the Committee of Secretaries  may be asked to take note of the observation of this Committee while considering the proposal.  But the Ministry has not stated the further developments in the matter.  It is not known if the Cabinet Secretariat has, thereafter,
considered the observations/recommendations of this Committee, or if the matter has been presented before the Committee of Secretaries for their consideration, or if it has been considered, what are their views.  If nothing has been done by the Cabinet Secretariat after the communication was
sent by the Ministry, what are the reasons for the delay.  The Committee regards the Action Taken Replies as incomplete and therefore desires that details of all the developments along with the reasons if nothing has been done in this regard in compliance of the observations/recommendations of
this Committee, may be furnished to it.
 
14.4     Redressal of grievances has been decentralized as a result of which each Ministry/Department has its own internal grievance redressal mechanism for settling grievances pertaining to it.   An officer of the rank Joint Secretary or above has been designed as Director of Grievances, who has
been vested with the powers to call for files/papers relating to public grievances in the cases in which a decision is pending for more than three months and take a decision, with the approval of the Secretary/head of the Department of the Ministry/ Department concerned.
 
14.5.0  The Department of AR&PG acts as an external monitoring agency and review periodically the performance of Ministries/Departments with respect to redressal of public grievances.  The grievances received in the Department are gone through and sent to the concerned
Ministry/Department for appropriate action.  An acknowledgement is sent to the complainant and he is also informed that his grievances has been sent to the specifically mentioned Department which would send him a reply about the complaint.  Even in cases where the petition does not contain a
grievance and no action is warranted by this Department, a reply is sent to the petitioner.  Quarterly review meetings are held with Directors of Public Grievance/Staff Grievances Officers.  The Department also carries out periodic evaluation of grievance redressal machinery set up in selected
Ministries/Department having large interface with the public.  In the process of dealings with public grievances the Department also identifies the systemic deficiencies and takes up studies of grievance-prone areas with a view to suggesting reform.
 
14.5.1  A mechanism for redressal of the grievances of the Central Government employees is available under the  scheme of Joint Consultative Machinary and Compulsory Arbitration which provides for setting up of Joint Councils at National, Department and Regional/Office levels.
 
14.5.2  Under the Joint Consultative Machinery Scheme (JCM), there exists a Board of Arbitration (BoA).  In the event of disagreement in the Joint Council, grievances are compulsorily referred to the BoA whose recommendations are binding subject to overriding authority of the Parliament. 
The BoA may be referred grievances relating to:
 
(i)
                  Pay and allowances;
(ii)                Weekly hours of work; and
(iii)               Leave of a class or grade of employees.
 
14.5.3  The Committee was informed that during 1999-2001, 13 items out of the 32 new items taken up by the JCM, were finalised and 4 items were referred to the BoA in the fag end of 2000 which finalised 2 cases by early part of 2002.
 
14.5.4  The Committee, while appreciating the role of the JCM and BoA in solving the cases relating to grievances of Government servants, reiterates its earlier view that these mechanisms may be strengthened for timely redressal of grievances.
 
14.6.0  The Committee drew the attention of the Ministry to the necessity of instituting Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) as the Administrative Reforms Commission was set up way back in 1966.  The Committee feels that in the context of the changed environment and the need to
reduce the role of Government as well as for greater involvement of non-officials and NGOs, an overhaul of the administrative system is due to look into the range of issues.
 
14.6.1  The Government, in response to the observation of the Committee stated that improvement of governance is an ongoing process.  It has taken certain specific measures to promote responsive and effective administration including the introduction of two Bills in Parliament on Freedom of
Information and institution of Lokpal, granting statutory status to Central Vigilance Commission, formulation of Citizen’s Charters and setting up of IFCs.  It, therefore, observes that setting up of another Administrative Reforms Commission is not necessary.
 
14.6.2  The Committee notes the reply and urges upon the Government to continuously strive to identify ways to tackle the whole range of issues for good governance.
 
14.7.0  The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievance has also developed an online computerized system over NICNET for speedy redress and effective monitoring of complaints received from public on 5.9.2001 called Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System

CENTRAL ADMINISTRATIVE TRIBUNAL
 
16.0.    Pursuant to the provision of Article 323A of the Indian Constitution, the Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985 was enacted by Parliament to set up Administrative Tribunals for adjudication of disputes and complaints with regard to recruitment and condition of services of persons appointed
to public services and posts in connection with the affairs of the Union and the States.
 
16.1.    Administrative adjudication, which is quasi-judicial in nature, is the main function of Administrative Tribunals.  The basic objective of enacting the Administrative Tribunal Act was:
 
(i)
                  to relieve congestion in the ordinary set of courts; and
(ii)                to provide for speedy disposal of disputes relating to service matters.
 
16.2.    The Central Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 provides that the Tribunals shall not be bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 but shall be guided by the principle of natural justice and subject to the other provisions of Administrative Tribunals Act and
rules made by the Central Government.  The Tribunal shall have power to regulate its own procedure including the fixing of places and times of enquiry.  As a result of the judgement of the Supreme Court in the case of L. Chandra Kumar dated 18 March 1997, the appeals against the orders of an
Administrative Tribunals lie before the Division Bench of the concerned High Court.
 
16.3.    Administrative Tribunals are distinguishable from the ordinary courts with regard to their jurisdiction and procedure.  They are free from the shackles of many of the technicalities of the ordinary courts.  The litigant(s) has to submit an application in paper book form prescribed by rule 4 of
Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1985.  It means that every application should be accompanied by the following:-
 
(i)
                  Certified copy of the order against which the application has been filed;
(ii)                All documents relied upon by the applicants and mentioned in the application;
(iii)               Details of the crossed Demand Draft or crossed Indian Postal Order representing the application fee of Rs. 50/-; and
(iv)              Index of documents.
 
16.3.1  The aggrieved person can appear before the court personally.  Government can also present its case through its departmental officers or legal practitioners.
 
16.4.    At present CAT has 17 regular benches, 15 of which operate at the principal seats of High Courts and the remaining two at Jaipur and Lucknow.  These Benches also hold circuit sittings at other seats of High Courts. 
 
16.5.    The Tribunal consists of a Chairman, Vice-Chairmen and Members.  The Members are drawn from both judicial as well as administrative streams so to give the Tribunal the benefit of expertise both in legal and administrative spheres.  The present sanctioned strength of Chairman, Vice-

17.11.2            In three cases referred by the Government of Sikkim, in which the consent accorded by the State Government under Section 6 of the DSPE was subsequently withdrawn, the matter regarding withdrawal of consent came up before the Supreme Court in Kazi Lhendup Dorji vs. CBI. 
The CBI contended that the consent under Section 6 of the DSPE Act fell in the category of conditional legislation and question of its withdrawal could not arise.  Once consent of the State Government in regard to investigation of a case has been granted, it should not be withdrawn under any
circumstances.  The Supreme Court after hearing the matter, in its order dated 29.3.1994 held that once a consent was given by a State Government empowering the CBI to investigate into an offence, the former could not withdraw the same.
 
17.11.3            The Government has also stated that such withdrawal of consent obstructs the functioning of CBI.  However, in view of the judgement of the Supreme Court on the point the legal position has been clarified and the consent once accorded by the State Government cannot be
withdrawn.

reiterating its earlier view that publicity of Citizens’ Charters is very essential to make the general public aware about its existence and utility, which would help in soliciting their feedback for its evaluation, recommends that model guidelines may be framed by the Ministry of Personnel and given
to all the Ministries/Departments/Organisations so that the mode of receiving feedback would be uniform.
(para 12.1.1)
 
The Committee, thus, desires that the States which has not informed the steps taken by them in this regard to the Ministry, may be required to do so in a time- bound manner.
(para 12.1.2)
 
In the Action Taken Replies to the observations/recommendations contained in the   Seventy-first Report of the Committee, the Ministry had simply stated that the observations/recommendations of the Committee has been communicated to the Nodal Officers for Citizens’ Charters of all the
Ministries/Departments of Central Government and also to the Chief Secretaries/Administrators of all States/UT Governments on 25.05.01 for compliance.  The Ministry has further stated, that they have been requested to ensure that the Charter movement in their organisation is given adequate
publicity and is properly implemented.  They have been requested to evolve a feed back mechanism with participation from the representatives of the people.  This Committee is not happy the way the Ministry intends to comply with the observations/recommendations of the Committee.  The
Ministry has not stated clearly its own monitoring of the instructions/guidelines issued to different agencies under Central and State/ UT Governments.  The Ministry should have evolved a time-frame within which these agencies should have given feed back to the Ministry in compliance of its
instructions/guidelines.  As stated by the Ministry, the instructions/guidelines were issued in May 2001.  But nothing has been stated in the ATR as to what actual action has been taken by these agencies till the time of submission of the ATR to the Committee.  It appears that the Ministry intends
to avoid its responsibility.  It intends to confine its activity by merely issuing guidelines/instructions without caring for their proper compliance.  This attitude of the Ministry is bound to defeat the spirit of the observations/recommendations of the Committee.  This Committee therefore urges upon
the Ministry to submit to it the results of their monitoring of Citizen’s Charter.
(para 12.2)
 
INFORMATION AND FACILITATION COUNTERS
 
During the examination of Demands for Grants (2001-2002), it was intimated to the Committee that only three organisations having Citizen’s Charter had not set up IFCs.  The increase in number of organisations for setting up IFCs from 3 to 21 shows that the Department of Administrative
Reforms is not pursuing the matter vigorously with the latter.  The Committee fails to comprehend as to how the number of Ministries/Departments/Organisations having Citizens’ Charters but have not set up IFCs has increased in the face of tall claim of the Ministry for proper monitoring and
coordination with the Departments/Organisations in the direction of setting up IFCs.  The Committee, therefore, is of the considered view that the Government needs to take a serious stand in this regard and that such Ministries/Departments/Organisations may be requested for setting up of IFCs,
as Citizens’ Charter  is meaningless without its physical manifestation.  The Committee further urges upon the Government to monitor each of the Ministries/Departments/Organisations who have not set up IFCs in setting up of their IFCs and render necessary assistance.
(para 13.2.1)
 
In the Action Taken Replies to the observations/recommendations of the Committee as contained in its Seventy-first Report, the Ministry has stated that such Ministries/Departments/Organisations which have Citizens’ Charters but do not have IFCs have been asked to set up IFCs in their
respective organisations throughd.o. letter dated 18June 2001.  However, the Ministry has not stipulated clearly as to what happened to compliance of their D.O. letter.  It gives the impression that either the Ministry does not intend to furnish full facts or is trying to avoid its responsibility of
monitoring the developments in compliance of their instructions/guidelines.  The Committee, therefore, recommends to the Ministry to submit a detailed report of monitoring the developments in compliance of their instructions/guidelines every year.
(para 13.2.2)
