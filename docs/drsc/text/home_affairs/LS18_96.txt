 The Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension Sc heme, 1980 has been under consideration of 
a sub-Committee of the Department-related Par liamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs

AN OVERVIEW OF THE SWATANTRATA SAINIK SAMMAN PENSION SCHEME, 
1980  
 
2. In 1969, a Scheme, known as “Ex-Andaman Political Prisoners Pension Scheme” was 
launched.  Under the Scheme, pension was sanctioned to those freedom fighters who had suffered 
imprisonment in the Cellular Jail, Port Blair (Andaman Islands) for a minimum period of five 
years.  In 1972, on the occasion of 25
th Anniversary (Silver Jublilee) of Independence, a regular 
Freedom Fighters’ Pension Scheme was introduced covering other categories of freedom fighters.  
In 1980, the scheme was liberalized and renamed as “Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension 
Scheme”.  Liberalisation was done in the following areas: 
 
(a) The income limit of Rs.5000/- per annum under the 1972 scheme was removed 
and pension was viewed as a token of ‘Samman’. 
(b) Minimum period of imprisonment was reduced from six months to three months 
for women and SC/ST freedom fighters. 
(c) Under the 1972 scheme, evidence from official record was required to prove 
underground/externment/internment sufferings.  Under the new scheme, if the 
records were not available, corrobora tive evidence by way of certificates from 
prominent freedom fighters, with proven ja il sufferings of two years or more and 
belonging to the district of the applicant, was accepted. 
 
A list of recognized movements/mutinies for Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension (SSSP) 
is at Annexure-I. 
 
ELIGIBILITY CONDITIONS 
 
3. Every person who took part in the freedom struggle is not eligible for pension under the 
scheme.  Only those freedom fighters, who ha d undergone the prescribed minimum sufferings, 
are eligible.  The minimum sufferings which make a freedom fighter eligible for pension are: 
 
(a) A person who had suffered a minimum im prisonment of six months in the 
mainland jails before Independence.  Ex-INA personnel are also eligible for 
pension if the imprisonment/detention suffe red by them was outside India for six 
months or more.  In case of women and SC/ST freedom fighters, the minimum 
period of actual imprisonment for eligibility of pension is three months. 
(b) A person who remained underground for six months or more provided he was:- 
(i) a proclaimed offender; 
(ii) one on whom an award for arrest/head was announced; or 
(iii) one whose detention order was issued but not served. 
 7

(c ) A person who was interned in his home or externed from his district under the 
orders of the competent authority for six months or more. 
(d) A person whose property was confiscat ed or attached and sold due to 
participation in the National Freedom Struggle. 
(e) A person who was permanently incapac itated during firing or lathi charge. 
(f) A person who lost his Government job (including job in a local body) for 
participation in the National Freedom Struggle. 
(g) A person who was awarded the punishment of 10 strokes of 
caning/flogging/whipping. 
 
4. To become eligible for pension an app licant has to submit documentary evidence duly 
verified by the State Government along w ith their recommendation as required under the SSSP 
Scheme.

Facilities being provided to the Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pensioners 
 
(i) Free railway pass (1 st Class/AC-2 tier Sleeper) for freedom fighter/widow with an 
attendant for life. 
(ii) Free medical facilities in all the Central Government hospitals and also in the hospitals 
run by the PSUs under the control of Bureau of Public Enterprises.  C.G.H.S. facilities 
have also been extended to freedom fighters and their dependents. 
(iii) Telephone connection, subject to feasibilit y, on payment of half of the rentals and no 
registration and installation charges. 
(iv) General Pool residential accommodation to  freedom fighters in Delhi in the national 
interest/international obligations.  Widow of freedom fighter is permitted to retain the 
accommodation for a period of six months after the death of the freedom fighter. 
(v) Accommodation in the Freedom Fighters’ Home, set up at Baba Kharak Singh Marg, 
New Delhi for the freedom fighters who have none to look after them. 
(vi) Two per cent dealership/distributorship of petroleum products allotted through Dealer 
Selection Boards are reserved for freedom fighters. 
(vii) Facility of financial assistance from Home Minister’s Discretionary Grant on the 
occasion of marriage of daughter(s) of freedom fighter pensioners. 
 
5.1 In addition to the above facilities, ex-A ndaman Political Prisoners and their widows are 
entitled to the following facilities:- 
 
(a) Free voyage facility to visit Port Blair once a year along with companion. 
(b) Free air travel facility to visit Port Blair once a year from Chennai/Calcutta along 
with companion. 
(c) Facility to travel in Shatabadi/Rajdhani Express trains along with companion.

7. As many as 2,39,290 applications have been received after the cut-off date for grant of 
Samman Pension.  The primary reason for such a deluge of applications was relaxation made in 
the 1980 Scheme with regard to production of secondary evidence i.e. Co-prisoner Certificate 
 9

(CPC) and Personal Knowledge Certificate (P KC) in the absence of primary documentary 
evidence from the official records.   
 
7.1 In view of the large number of applicati ons received after the cut-off date, and following 
the recommendations of the Committee on Petitions , Rajya Sabha, as contained in its Ninety-
seventh Report presented to Rajya Sabha on 9 August 1989, a proposal was submitted for 
consideration of the Cabinet, for the closure of the Scheme vide Note dated February 27, 1992.  
In the Note, approval of the Cabinet was solicited to the proposals – (a) the existing system of 
considering and deciding the cases, with referenc e to the terms and conditions of the Schemes 
already approved, may continue; (b) cases already rejected, at the level of Joint Secretary and 
above, should not ordinarily be re-opened for fresh consideration; (c) where however, new strong 
evidence was adduced and it prima facie justifies re-opening of the case, an exception may be 
made and the case reconsidered once only with the approval of the Minister; (d) in specific cases 
of genuine difficulty, relaxation may be given allowing consideration of application even after the 
prescribed last date; and (e) except for the exceptional circumstances as above, the scheme be 
treated as closed. 
 
7.2 The Cabinet in its meeting held on March 23, 1992, considered the said Note dated 
February 27, 1992 and approved the same.  It was also decided that genuine cases of hardship 
may be looked into by the Home Minister.  However, the Supreme Court of India, in its 
judgement dated May 14, 1993, in the case of Mukund Lal Bhandari vs. Union of India (AIR 
1993 SC 2127), inter alia , directed the Government that “…the respondents should accept the 
applications of the petitioners irrespective of the date on which they are made.  The applications 
received hereafter should also be entertained w ithout raising the plea that they are beyond the 
prescribed date.”  As a result of the said decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court, the process of 
receipt and disposal of applications, review pe titions and filing of court cases has been unabated. 
Moreover, the closure of the Scheme had not become possible due to certain exceptions made. 
 
7.3 Many State Governments and Union Territo ry Administrations have their own schemes 
for sanction of pension to freedom fighters.  There are quite a large number of freedom fighters 
who are drawing both the State Government and Central Government pensions.  In many cases, 
where the Central Government pension has not been sanctioned, freedom fighters are receiving 
pension from the State Government, as eligibility co nditions for sanction of pension by the State 
Governments are more liberal.  Thus, eligible genuine freedom fighters are already receiving 
pension from the State Government or both from the State Government and the Central 
Government. 
 
7.4 Several instances of manipulation or forgery of documents to obtain pension have come 
to the notice of the Ministry.  In several cases,  pension was obtained on furnishing incorrect 
information or concealing material information.  Consequently, pension has been suspended in 
about 3,076 cases and cancelled in about 1,551 cases so far.  Instructions have been issued by the 
Ministry to the concerned authorities for recovery  of pension so obtained, and to prosecute the 
guilty in suitable cases. 
 
7.5 The scheme has been in operation for more than 29 years since 1972.  At present, the 
Ministry is primarily handling review petitions/M P/VIP references and court cases.  From April, 
2000 to August, 2002, out of about 23,681 review applications, including some fresh applications, 
pension has been sanctioned in only 241 cases, that  too by giving benefit of doubt in many cases.  
These exclude about 13,500 cases recommended by the Hyderabad Special Screening Committee 
from January 1997 to May 1998 and referred to the State Governments of Andhra Pradesh, 
Karnataka and Maharashtra for verification.

PENSION TO FREEDOM FIGHTERS OF GOA LIBERATION MOVEMENT 
 
13. The Status Note received from the Ministry  of Home Affairs further states that the 
Swantantrata Sainik Samman Pension Scheme recognizes 37 movements, mutinies, 
strikes, and conspiracies for the purpose of grant of pension.  This includes Goa 
Liberation Movement. There were three phase s of the Goa Liberation Movement – (i) 
Phase-I (1946); (ii) Phase-II (1954-55) and (iii) Phase-III (1960-61). 
 
 11

13.1 Pension under the Scheme was granted to all the living batch leaders who had led these 
three phases of the movement and the participant Satyagrahis of the first and third phases of the 
movement who had been arrested, tried and pu nished with 10 to 28 years of rigorous 
imprisonment. Grant of pension to all such pe ople became possible because records of their 
sufferings were available with the Central Jail, Aguada, and with the Judicial Lock up in Panaji.  
As per records, as many as 911 persons have be en granted pension on this account by this 
Ministry, and, as on date, no application, duly recommended and forwarded by the State 
Government, is pending with the Ministry. 
 
13.2 Satyagrahis of the Second Phase of the M ovement had never undergone the required six 
months of imprisonment/externment/internment et c., as required under the Scheme.  They were 
never arrested, tried and punished by the Port uguese Government and/or by the Martial Law 
Court.  They were physically thrown back from Goa into the adjoining Indian Territories in 1954-
55.  There is absolutely no record of as to how many such Satyagrahis suffered this insult of 
being thrown back primarily because no such records were ever maintained.  The Goa 
Vimochana Sahayak Samiti, Gulbarga, which has been championing the cause of freedom 
fighters of the Goa Liberation Movement and who had organized the second phase of the Goa 
Liberation Movement, has not been able to throw any light on the actual identity and number or 
the present whereabouts of such category of Satyagrahis. 
 
13.3 Representations from individuals, Freedom Fighters Associations and through other 
quarters have been received in the Ministry of Home Affairs from time to time for grant of 
pension to all participants of the Goa Libera tion Movement, particularly to those who had 
participated in Phase-II of the movement.  Thei r representations also contained a demand for 
constitution of a Screening Committee for th e purpose of recommending the cases of the 
participants for grant of pension by the Central Government.  These requests were considered and 
rejected at the level of the Prime Minister in 1991 and at the level of Minister of State/Home 
Minister in 1992, 1993 and 1995. 
 
13.4 Discussions were held at the level of Ho me Minister in 1996 and 1997 with prominent 
leaders of the movement, namely, Shri Bhagwant Rao Gaikwad of Maharashtra and Professor 
Madhu Dandavate.  It was decided to call for a list of the participant Satyagrahis from the State 
Governments of West Bengal, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh, as there were 
participants from these States in this moveme nt.  Professor Madhu Dandavate was also requested 
to furnish the information, if any available with him.

(a) Government of West Bengal

PENSION  TO FREEDOM FIGHTERS OF HYDERABAD LIBERATION MOVEMENT 
(1947-48) 
 
14. As regards pension to freedom fighters of Hyderabad Liberation Movement (1947-48), 
the Status Note of the Ministry of Home Affairs mentions that the SSSP Scheme,1980 has 
recognized so far 37 movements, mutinies, strikes, conspiracies etc., including participation in the 
Border Camps in the erstwhile State of Hyde rabad for carrying out struggle for merger of 
Hyderabad State in the Indian Union during 1947-48.  The movement lasted for 18 months from 
March 1947 to September 1948.  The Nizam of Hyderabad had issued a general Farman to detain 
the participants of the Hyderabad Liberation Movement.  Initially the number of such camps was 
estimated at 43.  Subsequently, the number of such camps was reported to be 98 and about 11,000 
persons having participated in the movement. 
 
14.1 In the beginning, the applications of th e persons who applied for pension pertaining to 
this movement were not accepted as they did not fulfil the eligibility criteria under the SSSP 
Scheme.  Accordingly, the Central Government  constituted a Special Screening Committee in 
 13

1983 under the Chairmanship of Shri Govind Bhai Shroff to scrutinize applications of persons 
who claimed sufferings in the border camps during the Hyderabad Liberation Movement.  In June 
1985, the sufferings of the participants in border camps were  recognized for the purpose of grant 
of Swantantrata Sainik Samman Pension Sche me.  During the period from July 1985 to 
December 1997, the Committee was reconstitute d from time to time.  The Committee was 
constituted initially under the Chairmanship of Shri Govind Bhai Shroff, who remained Chairman 
upto December 1996.  In December 1996, the Committee was reconstituted under the 
Chairmanship of Shri N. Giri Prasad, MP (RS).  On his death in April 1997, Shri Ch. Rajeshwar 
Rao, MLA, Andhra pradesh was appointed as Chai rman.  In December 1997, prior to the expiry 
of its term, the Committee resolved that all the cases found to be deserving had been considered 
and it was of the strong opinion that the purpose for which the Committee was set up was served.  
However, in view of the orders of the Andhra Pradesh High Court in over 1500 writ petitions to 
place the cases of over 25,000 petitioners before the Committee, its term was extended upto 
31.5.1998.

 THE SPECIAL AUDIT TEAM (SAT)  
 
21. On August 12, 1996, during the consideration of the Demands for Grants (1996-97) of 
the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), the Home Secretary had stated before the Committee on 
Home Affairs that many cases received from State Governments were rejected by the Ministry 
because they lacked necessary supporting docum ents and expressed readiness to set up a 
Committee of non-officials to review, as a one time measure, all the cases which had been 
rejected by the Ministry for want of supporting documents. 
 
21.1 Accordingly, a Special Audit Team (SAT ) was constituted, as one time measure, on 
October 28, 1996 under the Chairmanship of the Joint Secretary incharge of the Freedom Fighters 
Division, initially for a period of three months, to review the rejected cases in which review 
applications were pending.  Two non-official s, viz., Shri Ranbir Singh, ex-MP and Working 
President of the All India Freedom Fighters Organisation, New Delhi, and Capt. S.S. Yadava, Ex-
INA and General Secretary of the All India INA Committee, New Delhi were nominated as 
members of SAT.  As per the procedure laid down, rejected cases in bundles of 50 were to be 
referred to the members for advice, and final decision was to be taken by the Joint Secretary.   
 16

 
WORK DONE BY SAT  
 
22. SAT started functioning from the month of December, 1996 and reviewed about 2000 
cases which had been rejected earlier.  A bout 800 of these cases were recommended by the 
members of SAT.  Out of the 800 cases recommended by the members of SAT, 175 cases related 
to ex-INA personnel while most of the remaining ca ses related to the State of Bihar.  Most of 
these cases were requisitioned by the members of SAT.  Some cases were also referred to the 
members, duly recommended by the Freedom Fighters’ Division of the Ministry on receipt of 
fresh information/clarification/evidence from the applicants or fresh verification/recommendation 
from the State Governments. 
  
22.1 The members of SAT recommended sanction of pension in about 700 cases straightaway, 
and in about 100 cases their recommendation was subject  to further verification or clarification 
from the concerned State Governments/applicants.  Pension has so far been sanctioned in 666 
cases.  In about 50 cases recommended by the members of SAT, it has not been found possible to 
accept their recommendations due to reasons, such as, (i) insufficiency of documentary evidence 
submitted in support of claimed suffering, (ii) lack of vital information in the documentary 
evidence regarding nature and period of claimed suffering (iii) contradiction/changes made by the 
applicants in the claimed suffering, (iv) submissi on of documents of doubtful nature, etc. and the 
same have been rejected.  
 
22.2 In the Ministry, the documentary evidence furnished by the applicants is examined 
strictly in accordance with the provisions of the Scheme and policy guidelines issued thereunder.  
However, the members of SAT adopted a more liberal approach, such as, (i) recommending cases 
on the basis of personal knowledge, (ii) giving  benefit of doubt to the applicant, (iii) 
recommending that verification/back references to  the State Government be dispensed with in 
view of advanced age, infirmity, helplessness, pove rty of the applicants, and possible delay, (iv) 
recommending the cases on the basis of personal a ffidavits of the applicants in spite of 
discrepancies in claims/records, etc.  The Mini stry alleged that most of the cases recommended 
by the members of SAT were requisitioned by th em which would indicate that they had personal 
knowledge of these cases.  The recommendation of the members of SAT was honoured in most of 
the cases. 
 
22.3 Although no formal orders were issued ex tending its tenure beyond 31.12.1996, SAT was 
allowed to function till March 1998.  
 
WORK DONE IN THE ABSENCE OF SAT   
 
23. The Ministry in its Status Note has further mentioned that in 1996-97 and 1997-98, when 
SAT was functioning, pension was sanctioned in 466 and 488 cases,  respectively.  Even during

sanctioned in 254 cases.  Thus, the review of reje cted cases is being carried out even in the

PROBLEMS IN THE FUNCTIONING OF SAT 
 
24. The basic idea of constituting the SAT was th at Ministry of Home Affairs would refer the 
rejected cases for their advice wherever considered necessary.  However, with the passage of time 
the SAT ceased to function as a team as was origina lly conceived.  The Under Secretary was also 
not associated in the proceedings.  The problem was more intensified with the association of Shri 
 17

Satyanand Yajee, son of late Shri Sheel Bhadra  Yajee.  Shri Yajee issued a circular dated 
31.10.1996 on his own inviting fresh applications.  He also started a parallel office for settling the 
pension cases of freedom fighters.  The Bihar State Freedom Fighters Association also filed a 
complaint alleging the ‘unauthorised’ intervention of  Shri Satyanand Yajee.  The Leader of the 
Staff Side, Ministry of Home Affairs also made a complaint to the Home Minister in the matter.   
 
24.1 To sum up, SAT had reviewed 2000 cases out of which they recommended sanction of 
pension in 800 cases (i.e. 40%).  SAT agreed with  the earlier decisions taken in the Ministry in 
1,200 cases (i.e. 60%).  Out of 800 cases recommended by SAT, pension has so far been 
sanctioned in 666 cases.  In 50 cases, the recommendation of the non-official members of SAT 
was not accepted.  Thus disagreement exists be tween the Ministry and the non-official members 
of SAT only in 6% of the recommended cases (i.e. 50 out of 2000).  Even in these cases, if fresh 
evidences/clarifications are received, they may be reconsidered by the Ministry as is being done 
in other review cases. 
 
25. While the information contained in the Status Note on SSSP Scheme received from 
Ministry of Home Affairs as reproduced in the pr eceding paragraphs was under consideration of 
the sub-Committee, a communication dated 8 June 2000 was received from the Ministry of Home 
Affairs regarding the proposal for the closure of the Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension Scheme, 
1980.  The said communication delineated the following points:  
 
(i) That the scheme of political pension to freedom fighters and their eligible dependents 
known as “Freedom Fighters Pension Scheme” was introduced on 15 August, 1972 
on the occasion of 25
th anniversary of Independence; 
 
(ii) That the scheme has been in operation for almost 28 years now and pension has been 
sanctioned to 1,64,329 applicants; 
 
(iii) That out of 13,547 applications, most of which are review petitions, processed during 
the year 1999-2000, pension could be sanctioned in only 85 cases; 
 
(iv) That there are very few fresh cases; 
 
(v) That the long duration of the scheme has gi ven rise to many malpractices such as 
submission of false/forged documents, concealment of material facts, etc.   69 cases 
had to be cancelled on this account; 
 
(vi) That the scheme is a token of respect and not a quid pro quo  for the contributions 
made by the freedom fighters; 
 
(vii) That the Joint Committee of Freedom Fighter s and officials constituted to look into 
the problems of freedom fighters under the Chai rmanship of former Minister of State 
(Home) in 1997 had recommended the closure of the scheme by 31 December, 1998; 
and 
 
(viii) That many of the freedom fighters who are not eligible   for pension under the 
Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension Scheme, are already receiving   pension   from   
the State Governments under their respective schemes. 
 
 18

26. In view of the aforementioned points, the Ministry proposed to discontinue the 
Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension Scheme from a prospective date after due publicity.  The 
salient features of the proposal are as follows: 
 
(a) All the State Governments will be asked to send all eligible cases pending with 
them before the said date.  The reports received by the said date in regard to 
pending cases will be duly considered. 
 
(b) Freedom fighters and their eligible dependents, who are already in receipt of 
pension, shall continue to get the same along with other facilities provided by the 
Government after closure of the scheme. 
 
(c)  Pending Court cases will also be considered after closure of the same in 
accordance with the provisions of the exis ting scheme in the light of the Court’s 
directions. 
 
27. Keeping in view the far-reaching implicati ons of the proposal of the Ministry, the sub-
Committee placed the matter before the main Committee for its consideration. 
 
27.1 The main Committee, in its meeting held on 12 September 2000, discussed at length the 
proposal of the Ministry and strongly disappr oved of the same.  The Committee unanimously 
expressed the view that the Government should continue with the scheme and asked the sub-
Committee to go ahead with the examination of th e subject assigned to it and submit its report 
thereon to it for its consideration. 
 
27.2 The sub-Committee, in its meeting held on  19 December 2001 c onsidered an updated 
Status Note received from the Ministry of Home Affairs.  The sub-Committee while recalling the 
view expressed by the main Committee in its meeting held on 12 September 2000, opined that the 
Ministry of Home Affairs should continue to operate the Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension 
Scheme.  It, accordingly, decided to send its vi ews on the matter to the main Committee for its 
consideration. 
 
28. The main Committee, on the basis of the views expressed by its sub-Committee on SSSP 
Scheme, makes the following recommendations/observations vis-à-vis SSSP Scheme, 1980: 
 
(i) While strongly disapproving the proposal  of the Ministry of Home Affairs for 
closure of the Swatantrata Sainik Samman Pension Scheme, 1980, the Committee is 
of the considered view that the Government should continue with the operation of 
the Scheme.  The Government, however, sh ould exercise utmost care and caution so 
that no pension is sanctioned in bogus ca ses.  All applications for grant of pension 
under the scheme should be thoroughly scrutinised to ensure that pension is granted 
only in genuine cases. 
 
(ii) The Committee is also of the considered view that the Government should not close 
its doors for receiving applications from genuine freedom fighters.  It, therefore, 
urges upon the Government to dispose of  all fresh claims, if any, and review 
applications in a time bound manner as per the parameters of the Scheme.  The 
Government should also ensure that applications of genuine freedom fighters are 
processed and pension is sanctioned to them expeditiously.
