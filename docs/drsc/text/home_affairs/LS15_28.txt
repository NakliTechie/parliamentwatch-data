REPORT 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Home Affairs, having been authorised by the 
Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Twenty-Eighth Report of the 
Committee, relating to the Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Bill, 1994•. 
2. In pursuance of the Rules relating to Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha referred•• the Code of Criminal Procedure 
(Amendment) Bill, 1994, as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on the 9th May, 1994 and 
pending therein, to the Committee ' for examination and report. 
' 3. The Bill seeks to amend various sections of the Code of Criminal Procedure , 1973, 
with a view to removing certain difficulties and lacunae felt in its working, taking into 
account the recommendations made by the Law Commission, the National Police 
Commission, the observations made by courts and the suggestions received from State 
Governments, etc. 
4. The Committee held 13 sittings in all. In its meeting held on the 26th May. 1994, the 
Committee held preliminary discussions on the Bill and heard the presentation of th~ 
representative of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The Committee, then, decided that a press 
communique, inviting the views of the individuals, institutions, organisations, etc. interested 
in the subject matter of the Bill, be issued. A copy of the press communique issued is plared 
at Annexure-!. The Committee also decided to request the Statutory Bodi.e~, State 
Governments, etc. to send their views on the Bill. Again , on the 20th July, 1994. the 
Committee heard the presentation of the Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs, on the Bill. 
. 5. In response to the press communique issued on the 27th May, 1994, 70 memoranda 
containing the views/suggestions of various individuals, institutions , organisations, statutory 
bodies, State Governments. etc. on the various provisions of the Bill, were received. A list 
of individuals/bodies from whom memoranda were received is placed at Annexur e-H. 
6. The Committee heard the views of eminent personalities and the representativ es of 
various bodies. A list of such individuals, etc. is placed at Annexure-III. Besides , the 
.. Committee considered the comments received from the Ministry of Home Affairs on the 
memoranda received on various provisions of the Bill. The Committee wishes to express its 
· thanks to the Ministry of Home Affairs for placing before it the material required for its 
examination of the subject. The Committee also wishes to express its thanks to the 
individuals, 'the representatives of various institutions/organisations , etc.) who appeared 
before it and gave it the benefit of their valuable views and suggestions on the subject. 
7 . . In its sittings held on the 20th, 21st September , 6th October , 1995 and 30th January, 
1996, the .. Com~ittee held clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill.

9. The principal changes suggested by the Committee m the Bill and the reasons 
therefor are set out hereinafter :-
Clause 2 
This Clause seeks to amend Section 20 of the Code by inserting a new Sub section (4A), 
to enable the State Governments to delegate their powers to the District Magistrates for the 
purppse of placing the Executive Magistrates in charge of a sub division. The Committee is 
of the view that powers of State Governments should not be delegated to the District 
Magistrates. Hence, the Committee disapproves the proposed amendment. 
Clause 4 
This Clause seeks to insert a new Section 25A, empowering the State Governments to 
establish Directorates of Prosecution. While agreeing with the general principle of this 
Clause, the Committee recommends that for appointment as Director of Prosecution and 
Deputy Director of Prose cution , a person should have been in practice as an Advocate of 
Supreme Court or of a High Court for not less than ten years and seven years, respectively. 
such appointments should be made in concurrence with the Chief Justices of the concerned 
High Courts. 
Clause 5 
This Clause seeks to amend Section 29 of the Code to enhance the sentencing powers of 
the Magistrates of the First Class to impose fine, from five thousand rupees to twenty five 
thousand rupees and, Magistrate of the second class from one thousand rupees to five 
thousand rupees. The Committee is of the view that the maximum fine imposing powers of 
the Magistrates of the First Class and Second Class should be rupees ten thousand and five 
thousand, respectively. 
Clause 6 
This Clause seeks to amend Sub-section (2) of Section 45 of the Code so as to extend 
the protection from arrest to such other public servants, besides members of the Armed · 
Fotces,as are charged with maintenance of public order. The Committee is. of the view that 
this provision is likely to be misused and, therefore, disapproves the proposed amendment. 
Cla~e 7 
This Clause seeks to amend Section 46 of the Code to empower the police officers 
concerned to use all means necessary to effect the arrest, including causing death, in the case 
of proclaimed offenders under Sub-section (4) of Section 82 of the Code. The Committee is 
of the ,view that this provision is likely to be misused and, therefore, does not approve the 
proposed amendment. 
This Clause also seeks to add a new Sub section (4) to Section 46 of the Code to 
prohibit c·arrest of a woman after sunset and before sunrise, except in unavoidable 
circumstances. The Committee is of the view that (i) after sunset and before sunrise, a 
woman should be arrested by a woman police officer only; and (ii) prior permission of the 
First Class Judicial Magistrate, within whose local jurisdiction the offence is committed or 
the arrest is to be effected, should be mandatory. 
Clause 8 
This Clause seeks to insert a new section 50A in the Code so as to require the police to 
give information about the arrest of the person as well as the place where he is being held,

Clause 9 
This Clause seeks to substitute the Explanation to Section 5:1 of the Code. by new 
Explanation, so as to expla in the meaning of the expressions "examination" and "registered 
medical practi oner". appearing in Section 52. 53A and 54 of the Code. The Committe e is of 
the view that the expression "sexual assault" occurring in the proposed expla nation (a) has 
nowher e been defined in the Indian Penal Code. 1860 or the Code of Criminal Procedure . 
1973. It should, therefore. he substituted by the expression "sexual offence" which is the 
most common express ion and has been used in respect of the category of offences under 
Section s 375 to 376-D of the Indian Penal Code. 1860. 
Clause 10 
Thi s Clause seeks to insert a new Section 53A in the Code to provide for a detailed 
medical exa mination of a person accused of an offence of rape or of an attemp t to commit 
rape , by the registered medical practitioner employed in a hosp ital run by the Government 
ur a local auth ority and. in the absence of such a pract~ner. by any other Registered f. 
Medical Practition er. The Committee is of the view that the medical exam ination of such an 
ac~used person by a private Registered Medical Practitioner should be valid. only if no 
hosp ital run by Government or a local authority is located within a periphery of sixteen 
kilometers from the place of crime. 
Clause 11 
This Clause seeks to amend Section 54 of the Code so as to provide that a copy of the 
report of the medical examination of the arrested person should also be furnished hy the 
registered med ical practitioner to the arrested person or his nominee. on request, after his 
medical examination has been conduc ted. The Committee is of the view that the provision 
for furnishing a copy of the medical repo rt to the arrested person or to his nomin ee, by the 
Registered Medical Practitioner. should be mandatory . 
Clause 18 
This Clause seeks to insert a new section 144A in the Code to enable the District 
Magistrates to prohibit mass drills (or training) with arms in public places. The Committee is 
of the view that enough provisions already exist in Section 1~ of the Code to deal with 
such situations and there is no need to insert a new Section to the Code. The Committee 
therefore, doe s not approve the proposed amendment. 

4 
Clause 19 
This Clause seeks to insert a new Section 164A in the Code to provide for rnedi<:al 
examination of the victim of a rape by a Registered Medical Practitioner employed in a 
hospital run by the Governm~nt or a local authority and, in the absence of such a 
Practitioner, by any other Registered Medical Practitioner. The Comr:nittee is Of the view 
that the medical examination of such a victim should be held within twenty-four hours of 
commission of the offence. Further, the word, 'forwarded' in sub-clause (2) of this Clause 
should be substituted by the word 'sent'. 
Clause 20 
This Clause seeks to add a new Sub-section (3A) to Section 173 of the Code, to enable 
the police to take note of the desire of the parties to compound offences compoundable 
under Section 320, even at the stage of investigation. The Committee is of the view that this 
provision could be misused. by forcing the parties to agree to a compromise and. therefore, 
disapproves the proposed amendment. 
Clause 21 
This Clause seeks to amend Section 176 of the Code to provide that in the case of death 
of disapperance of a person. or rape of a woman while in the custody of the police, there 
shall be a mandatory judicial enquiry and, in case of death, examination of the dead body 
shall be conducted within twenty-four hours of the death. The Committee is of the view that 
incidents of death, disappearance and rape, occurring in judicial custody, should also be 
brought within the purview of this Clause. · 
Furiher, in case of death in the police or judicial custody, compensation to the bereaved 
family in the form of either employment to one of its eligible members or adequate family 
pension, say rupees fifteen hundred per mensum, should be given. For this, relevant 
provision should be incorporated in the Code. 
Clause 22 
This Clause seeks to insert a Proviso to Sub-section (1) of Section 190 of the Code so as 
to enable a Magistrate to pass an order of remand for further detention of an accus'ed person 
in custody for a period not exceeding .a week, after recording the reasons therefor, after 
submission of police report under Section 173 (2) of the Code and before taking cognizance 
of an offence disclosed by the police report under Section 190. The Committee is of the view 
that sufficient powers are vested with the Magistrates under Section 167 of the Code. The 
Committee, therefore, does not approve the proposed amendment. 
Clause 23 
This Clause seeks to amend Sub-section (3) of Section 197 of the Code, with a view to 
extending protec tion to_such other public servants, besides members of the Armed Forces, 
as are charged with' mainterHince of public order, in the matter of prosecution in respect of 
offences al!eged to have been committed while acting or purporting to act in the discharge of 
their duties. The Committee is of the view that this provision is likely to be misused ~tnd, 
therefore, disapproves the same. 
Clauses 47 & 49 
The Committee is of the view that amendments proposed in Sub-clauses (a) and (b) of 
Clause 47 and Sub-clause (a) of Clause 49 are only consequential in nature. In view of the 
Committee's recommendation in respect of Clause 18 of the Bill, the Committee disapproves 
the amendments proposed to be made through these Sub-clauses.
