DEPARTMENT OF LEGAL AFFAIRS
 
4.         This Department has a two-tier organizational set up i.e. the Main Secretariat at New Delhi and the Branch
Secretariats at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Bangalore
 
4.1       The main functions of the Department of Legal Affairs are:
(i)                 to extend advice to the various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India on legal matters;
(ii)                to carry out the conveyancing work of the Central Government;
(iii)              to attend to the litigation work in the Supreme Court, High Courts and some of the Sub-ordinate Courts;
(iv)              to deal with the treaties and agreements with foreign countries in matters of Civil Laws;
(v)               to authorize officers to execute contracts and assurances of property on behalf of the President under
Article 299 (1) of the Constitution;
(vi)              to appoint law officers such as Attorney General of India, Solicitor General of India and the Additional
Solicitors-General of India and
(vii)            to sanction grants-in-aid to certain institutions engaged in research in the field of law, constitutional 
studies  and Parliamentary  institutions .
 
4.2       The Department of Legal Affairs is further concerned with administering the Advocates Act, 1961, the Notaries
Act, 1952 and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.  The Department is also administratively in-charge of the
Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange, Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Indian Legal Service and Law Commission
of India.

5.6.1   Regarding providing the corpus grants, the Ministry in its reply said that in terms of the provisions of Rule 18 of
the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules, 1978 and orders thereunder, any proposal on expansion of existing service or
any new service involving expenditure exceeding Rs.5 crore will have to be submitted for consideration/approval of the
Committee for Non-plan Expenditure.  For any proposal involving expenditure below the said limit can be sanctioned
by the Secretary of the concerned Department with approval of Integrated Finance, though it will still be at the
discretion of the Department to refer the matter for consideration of the Committee for Non-plan Expenditure.  The
Grant-in-aids are generally open to revision depending on the need of the institution, whereas after the creation of
corpus, these institutions will have to manage their activities solely on the basis of the income arising out of interest on
the corpus and as interest rates keeps on fluctuating it is difficult to envisage at this juncture whether the future income
arising out of corpus would be sufficient to meet their requirements.  In fact, creation of corpus may not be in the

5.7   Keeping in view the decline in the Institute of Constitution of Parliamentary Studies (ICPS) and the Indian Law

view to restore their original glory.
 
5.8       A further point was raised in the Committee that the lease of the land allotted to ICPS for constructing the
building has not been extended which is going to have an adverse effect on the functioning of the Institute and the
matter should be looked into. 
 
5.8.1    In this regard the Ministry has informed the Committee that the Institute of Constitutional and Parliamentary
Studies was allotted land  measuring 1.136 acres (4597.27 sq. meters) by the Ministry of Urban Affairs  (L&DO) in
1976 at 3, Institutional Area Nyaya Marg, (Dharam Marg), New Delhi-110021.  The Institute paid a sum of Rs.
1,13,600/- as lease amount on 24.4.1976 and had been paying the ground rent regularly and the land was in possession
of the Institute  since then.    The Institute  has been obtaining extension  for putting up construction on the said plot
from  time to time and the last extension granted to it was upto 31
st October, 1999.   The Institute is in constant touch
with the Ministry of Urban Affairs for further extension of lease.  In the meantime, the Institute has been approaching
different Ministries for grant-in-aid for construction of a building.  However, in partial  modification of the L&DO
office letter  No.L-H-I(203)/67 dated 12.4.71, L&DO issued a sanction for the resumption of land measuring 2415.18
sq.mtrs. in 3-Institutional area Nyaya Marg, New Delhi as per L&DO  plan No.1730-D/I vide letter dated 12.4.71.  The
allotment of the land in favour of Institute of Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies so resumed was cancelled
consequently to that extent.  The Ministry of Law, Justice & Company Affairs, Department of Legal Affairs is not
administratively concerned with the question of allotment and extension of term of lease. The administrative Ministry in
this regard is Ministry of Urban Development.  Hence , the ICPS may take up the matter with concerned administrative
Ministry.
 
5.8.2    As per the facts, a piece of land was allotted to ICPS to construct its own building in 1976 and even after a lapse
of 25 years the Ministry has not been  able to secure  grants for construction of building of the Institute. Added to this,

extended any further after 1999.  Surprising is the fact that about half of the same land has been resumed to Legislative
Department for establishment of the Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research.  Incidentally the Legislative
Department is a sister Department of the Law Ministry.  The Ministry has also made a provision of Rs.1.55 crore in the
BE (2002-2003) for construction of building of Institute of Legislative Drafting and Research on  the said land.
 
5.9       The Committee takes serious note of the apathy of the Department of Legal Affairs towards the Institute of
Constitutional and Parliamentary Studies.  ICPS was designed to be the premier institute for research on constitutional
and parliamentary studies.  The Committee, did not expect, to say the least, scramble for a plot  of  land by the sister 
Department of the Ministry.  The Committee, therefore, strongly recommends to the  Government to resume the land to
ICPS and make a provision for construction  of  a building at the earliest.  If  need be the provision can be made in the
supplementary grants.
 
Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)

6.         The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, constituted under the Income Tax Act, 1961 deals with second appeals in
all matters relating to direct taxes, and appeals against orders of revision of Commissioners as well as orders of
acquisition of properties under Chapter XXA of the Income Tax Act, 1961. 
 
6.1       The Tribunal is headed by a President, who is  assisted by one Senior Vice-President and seven Vice-Presidents. 
The present strength of Members of Tribunal is 106 for 53 Benches spread over 25 cities.  There are normally two
Members in a Bench known as Judicial Member and Accountant Member having substantial experience in their
respective fields.
 
6.2       The matters which the Appellate Tribunal disposes of are of vital importance involving revenue to the tune of
millions of rupees. The Tribunal is entrusted with the task of deciding intricate questions of law and facts.  The presence
of both the Judicial and Accountant Members ensure that questions of facts which arise for their consideration are
properly enquired into and that the accountancy points, as also the legal angle, have been weighed properly.
 
6.3    The pendency of appeals with ITAT has had  been a matter of serious concern for  the Committee. It has been
urging upon the Government to reduce the huge pendency of cases. The Committee in its 45
th  ( 1998) and 61st Reports
( 2000) had expressed its displeasure over the delay in making all the additional Benches (To reduce the pendency a
decision was taken by the Government to create 15 additional Benches, of the Tribunal in addition to the 38 existing
Benches with effect from 1
st April, 1997.) functional which had a direct bearing on the unrelenting  pendency.
 
6.4       In its 72
nd  Report ( 2001) on the Demands for Grants ( 2001-02) of Ministry of Law, Justice and Company
Affairs,  the Committee had observed that the pendency was still very high as the average pendency per Bench was
about 5000 appeals.  The Committee also recommended to the Government to initiate all necessary measures  and see to
it that pendency was  reduced substantially.
 
6.5   The Secretary informed the Committee that ITAT has a pendency of approximately 2,07,000 cases as on March 1,
2002 as against 2,44,320 in the corresponding month during the previous year. He further elaborated that all the
additional benches have since been made functional and due to various efforts made to reduce pendency of cases, the
pendency which had once crossed the mark of three lakh has now come down to 2,07,000 clearly indicating an arrest of
the increase in pendency.
 
6.6 The Committee notes the state of pendency of appeals which has  gone down from about 5000  pending cases per
bench in March 2001 to about 4000 pending cases per bench at the end of February, 2002. However, it feels it is still
very high by any standard and the Government should continue it efforts in the right direction and hopes that the
pendency would come down substantially by the end of the current financial year.
 
Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange
 
7.  In the Demands for Grants (2002-03) there is a budget provision of Rs. 40 lakh for new minor head “31” Appellate
Tribunal for Foreign Exchange. The Committee wanted to know about the functioning of the  Appellate Tribunal for
Foreign Exchange.
7.1  The Ministry informed the Committee that the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 was enacted with a view
to consolidate and amend the law relating to foreign exchange with the objective of facilitating external trade and
payments and for promoting the orderly development  and maintenance of Foreign Exchange in market in India.  The
said Act came into force w.e.f. 1.6.2000.
 
7.2  With the coming into force of the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, the Foreign Exchange Regulation
Act,  1973 ( FERA) stood repealed and as a result the Foreign Exchange Regulation Appellate Board (FERA Board) set
up under the 1973 Act also stood dissolved.
 
7.3 In exercise of powers conferred by section 18 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (42 of 1999), the
Central Government established the Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange w.e.f. 1.6.2000.

7.3   The functions of Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange is to hear appeals against the orders of the Adjudicating
Authorities  and the Special Director (Appeals) under Sections 16 & 17 of the Act of 1999. The Appellate Tribunal shall
consist of a Chairperson and such number of Members as the Central Government may deem fit.
 
7.4  In exercise of powers conferred by Section 46 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999 (42 of 1999) the
Central Government have made the rules regulating the recruitment, salary and allowances and other conditions of
services of the person appointed as Chairperson or Members of the Tribunal. In accordance with said rules, the
Appellate Tribunal shall have one Chairperson and members not exceeding four (two of whom will be part-time
Members).
 
7.5  The Secretary in his deposition before the Committee said the present Chairperson, a retired Judge of the High
Court is in position with effect from 12 February 2002. Two more Members to whom offers have  been sent are yet to
join.
 
7.6 The Committee takes note of the status of the Appellate Tribunal for Foreign Exchange.  It is, however,  too early 
for it to make any observation on the functioning of the Tribunal. However, the Committee hopes that the Tribunal
would work efficiently and effectively and would not let arrears of cases accumulate.
 
Appointment of Notaries
 
8.         Notary Cell  of the Department is responsible for the administration of the Notaries Act, 1952 and the
appointment of Notaries. This Cell also conducts enquiries into the allegations of professional or other misconduct on
the part of the Notaries. 
 
8.1       In the light of  vacancies, the Committee in its 56
th, 61st  and 72nd Reports on the Demands for Grants of the
Ministry for the years 1999-2000, 2000-2001 and 2001-2002, respectively, had desired that  inordinate delays in the
appointment of Notaries be avoided.  In its 61
st Report, the Committee had recommended to the Government to take
measures for quick disposal of applications for appointment of Notaries.  It had also recommended for due
representation of SCs, STs and OBCs in the appointment of Notaries, as the ‘Memorial’, the basic document for
processing of the application did not contain a column to ascertain candidates’ caste and status.
 
8. 2      The Government has informed the Committee that in response to the Committee’s recommendation, the Notaries
Rules have been amended to prescribe a ‘revised Memorial’.  The revised Memorial contains a column indicating
whether the applicant belongs to SC/ST/OBC or General.  The Notaries Rules, 1956 have also been amended to relax
the eligibility criteria in respect of persons belonging to SCs/STs/OBCs and Women.  According to amended provision,
a person belonging to these classes would require only seven years of legal practice instead of ten years as required for
general category candidates, for becoming eligible for appointment as Notary. 
 
8.3   The Government have  also informed the Committee that about 1200 Memorials and files are pending in the
Notary Cell for appointment as Notary. In response to a query as to how many candidates belonging to weaker sections
have been appointed, the Government informed the Committee that till date 2116 Notaries have been appointed by the
Central Government, out of which 130 are women. As necessary amendment in the formats of Memorials for indicating
caste status was made with effect from 12.3.2001 only, the Notaries appointed thereafter include 4 from the SCs and 3
from OBCs.
 
8.4       The Committee in its 72
nd Report on the Demands for Grants ( 2001-02)  had appreciated the amendment to the 
Notaries Rules, 1956 being carried out in favour of SCs, STs, OBCs and Women for appointment as Notaries.  
However, the number of candidates belonging to these categories appointed as Notaries is  low even after a lapse of one
year since the Memorials were revised. The speed at which the applications are disposed of has also not improved. The
Government should, therefore, devise means to quicken the disposal at a faster pace and also launch a special drive to
fill the vacancies  in respect of the aforesaid categories.

National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)
           
9.      The National Legal Services Authority (NALSA), a statutory body constituted under the National Legal Services
Authorities Act, 1987, as amended by the Act of 1994, is responsible for providing free legal assistance to poor and
weaker sections of the society on the basis of equal opportunity. NALSA is engaged in providing legal services, legal
aid and speedy justice through Lok Adalats.  The Authority has its office at New Delhi and is headed by the Chief
Justice of India who is the ex-officio Patron-in-Chief.  Similarly, State Legal Services Authorities have been constituted
in every State Capital.  Supreme Court Legal Services Committee, High Court Legal Services Committees, District
Legal Services Authorities and Taluk Legal Services Committees have also been constituted in every State.
 
9.1   During  2001 about 4.16 lakh persons had benefited through court-oriented legal aid programmes . Of them, about
86,650 persons belonged to the Scheduled Castes, about 14,850 to the Scheduled Tribes, more than 60,900 were women
and about 14,600 were children. It  was expected that legal aid would  be dispensed to more than  2.10 lakh persons
during the last four months of the financial year, i.e. from December 2001 to March 2002.
 
9.2   About 2025 Lok Adalats had been organised during 2001, wherein more than 6.93 lakh cases had been settled.  In
about 40,650 Motor Vehicles Accident Claims Cases compensation amounting to over Rs.221 crore had been awarded.

20.  According to Annual Report of Ministry of Law, Justice & Company Affairs  2001-2002, as on 26.2.2002 , 64.25
percent of electors have been provided with photo identity cards in the country.  The States like West Bengal and Kerala
have equipped more than 80% of their voters with the photo identity cards.  However, States like Bihar (and Jharkhand)
have been able to give photo identity cards to only 37.10 percent  of their voters.

of Jharkhand was created in 2000 by bifurcating the State of Bihar.  It was also  stated  that in May, 2000, the Election
Commission had issued revised and detailed guidelines for the preparation of the photo identity cards.  According to
these guidelines, the computerisation of the electoral rolls is a pre-requisite for the preparation of the photo identity

Committee urges the Government to take up the matter with the Election Commission so that Electors’ Photo Identity

the administrative association of the Department of Justice with the Home Secretary as the Secretary, Justice, while

Judge-Population Ratio
 
33.       The Committee in its 85th Report (2001)on Law’s Delays : Arrears in Courts had urged upon  the Government to
reconsider the recommendation of the 120th Report of Law Commission and take steps to increase the strength of judges
per million population, at the earliest.  The Law Commission had stipulated that the judges’ strength should be increased
from 10.5  (1987) judges per million population to 50 judges per million.
 
33.1     The Secretary informed the Committee, that Committee had submitted its 85
th report to the Rajya Sabha on 31 st
December 2001, regarding tackling arrears in Courts.  The Committee had endorsed the 120 th Report of the Law
Commission regarding judges-population ratio at the rate of 50 Judges per million population in a phased manner.  The
recommendation of the Committee had been taken note of by the Hon’ble Supreme Court during the hearing of the All
India Judges cases (All India Judges Association Vs. Union of India).  Hon’ble Supreme Court had desired that there
should be at least 10 per cent increase in the strength of the judicial officers each year.  Thus, in three years, about 3800
posts of judicial officers would have to be created i.e. 1267 new judicial officers would have to be added each year.  The
construction of Courts and quarters for 1267 judicial officers would require an investment at the rate of about Rs.127
crore per year.  It implies that the Government would have to provide additional funds for creation of infrastructural
facilities under the Centrally Sponsored Scheme to the tune of about Rs.127 crore per year.  The State Governments
would correspondingly be required to increase the strength of judges, place them in position and provide their matching
contribution of funds.
 
33.2     The Committee is happy to note that the Supreme Court has taken cognizance of the recommendations of this
Committee and recommends that the Government may take necessary action for providing additional funds for creation
of infrastructural facilities for new judges to be added to the existing strength per year.

36.       The Department of Company Affairs, as a key systemic bridge between the Government and the corporate

for judicial appointments now rests in the domain of the judiciary.  The Union Law Minister is accountable to
Parliament for the delay in filling up of the vacancies of judges but  he has functionally no contribution to make. 
The Supreme Court read into the Constitution a power to appoint judges that was not conferred upon it by the
text or the context.  The underlying purpose of securing judicial independence was salutary but the method of
acquiring for the Court the exclusive power to appoint judges by the process of judicial interpretation is open to
question.  Against this backdrop the Committee recalls a recent discussion in the Rajya Sabha in which the
Government was asked regarding alternate arrangements to fill up the vacancies and whether there was any
scope for having a freshreview of the Supreme Court’s judgement.
 
The Committee is of the view that the delays in judicial appointments is a matter of serious public concern.  The
Committee is duty bound to point out that the failure to fill judicial vacancies promptly and punctually cannot be
shielded or defended in the name of judicial independence.”

Annexure-II
Reasons for enhancement of fund for Indian Law Institute
 
 
(a)        The pay scales of staff members of the Institute, revised on the basis of the 5 th Pay Commission’s
recommendations, have increased the financial burden of the Institute.  The existing grant-in-aid and
income generated from their resources, viz, the diploma courses & training programmes etc.,  do not
suffice to meet the various expenses of the Institute.  As a result, the Institute is unable to function to its
full capacity, and is constrained to realise it goals.  Some specific matters which could not be concretised,
due to paucity of funds, are according to the Institute, as indicated in the following sub-paras.
(b)        During the last ten years, over 15 employees retired, but their posts could not be filled up.  As against 25
positions of research staff, there are only 9 persons on the rolls at present.  The shortage of staff has
telling effect on the research output of the Institute.  Hence, there is a dire and urgent need to fill up the
vacant posts.
(c)At one time, the Institute’s library was one of the best law Libraries in the country.  During the last 15
years, the position of the library has deteriorated considerably.  The new editions of books have not been
purchased .  No new titles have been added.  Many valuable periodicals and journals have been
discontinued.  Out of 273 periodicals subscribed earlier, the Institute is getting only 173 at present. 
Further, the Library needs modernisation with the state-of-art facilities, e.g. computers, internet, CD
ROMs and other related facilities.
            The student section of ILI Library, which caters to the needs of diploma students of different courses (
about 400 in number), too requires replenishing with the latest editions of the prescribed book/readings.
(d)        The Institute needs sufficient funds to conduct doctrinal and non-doctrinal research.  The help and
involvement of outside experts/institutions, required for the purpose, remains unavailable for want  of
sufficient funds.
(e)        The Institute’s building is now more than 40 years old and requires massive renovation and repairs.
(f)         Facilities such as medical allowances, education allowance for children of the employees and other
facilities as provide to the Central Government employees are not available to employees of the Institute.
