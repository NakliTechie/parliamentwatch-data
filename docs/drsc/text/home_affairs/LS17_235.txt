OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF DEMANDS FOR GRANTS 
 
1.1 Overview 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.1.1 The Committee takes note of the ongoing vaccination process for COVID-19 and observes 
that, till now, less than 1% of the Indian population has been vaccinated and at this rate it will take 
many years to vaccinate whole population. The Committee also expresses its concern that a large 
number of people are missing the second dose of the vaccine. The Committee believes that it is 
very serious issue as the virus is mutating and new variants of COVID-19 are being reported in 
different parts of the world. The Committee therefore, recommends that all frontline health workers 
and the Corona Warriors including CAPFs and State/UT Police personnel receive the 
recommended doses of vaccine.  An attempt should be made to cover maximum people as early as 
possible.  
(Para 2.1.15 of 231st Report) 
 
Action Taken 
 
1.1.2 The COVID-19 vaccination drive was started on 16.01.2021 and around 38.76 crore 
beneficiaries across the country were successfully vaccinating within 180 days. All persons above 
the age of 18 years are eligible for COVID-19 vaccination since 1 st May 2021. The Revised 
guidelines for Implementation of National COVID vaccination Programme were announced on 7 th 
June, 2021 which has come in effect from 21 st June, 2021. Under the revised guidelines, 
Government of India is procuring 75% of the vaccines being produced by the manufacturers in the 
country which are being provided free of cost to States/UTs based on criteria such as population, 
disease burden and the progress of vaccination. These doses are to be administered by the 
States/UTs free of cost to all citizens aged 18 years and above as per priority through Government 
Vaccination Centres. Nearly 22% of the eligible population have received at least a single dose of 
COVID-19 vaccine, so far. 
1.1.3 The Ministry of Health & Family Welfare has time and again issued advisories and 
emphasized at various platforms for States/UTs to ensure timely dose to the due beneficiaries as 
per the vaccination schedule for adequate protection. As per the coverage statistics on Co-WIN 
portal on 13 th July 2021, 90.9% of the Front-Line-Workers, which includes CAPFs, have been 
vaccinated with the 1st dose of COVID-19 vaccine and 51.4% have received the 2 nd dose. Similarly, 
among the Health Care Workers (HCWs), the 1st dose coverage is 87.5% and 2nd dose is 63.4%. 
1.1.4 The Ministry of Health & Family Welfare is holding regular meetings with States/UTs for 
increasing the pace of COVID-19 vaccination. As vaccine availability is likely to increase in the 
coming months, the coverage would increase significantly. 
1.1.5 The vaccination of all NDRF personnel including Medical & Para-Medical Staff has been 
done except some personnel who are suffering from various chronic diseases like TB, hypertension, 
asthma and allergy etc. A close monitoring is being taken to complete 100% vaccination in respect 
of all NDRF personnel as early as possible.

Action Taken 
 
1.2.12 MHA has been continuously monitoring the expenditure by UT of Ladakh. However, the 
reason for less expenditure has been due to following reasons: 
i) Short working seasons in a year. 
ii) Stalling of activities due to Covid-19 Pandemic lockdown. 
iii) Working season lost in lockdown and unlock started in off season. 
iv) Ladakh depends on labour coming from outside. Due to lock down and pandemic 
related restrictions on movement of labour, the execution of works was affected. As a 
result of this, the responses to the tenders were also affected. 
v) Not having enough technical fields‘ staff such as draftsmen, JEs, AEs and AEEs with 
various State and Central executing agencies such as CPWD, NHIDCL and 
WAPCOS posted in Ladakh during this period. The said agencies also took some time 
to set up their offices in Ladakh during this period.  
vi) Inadequacy of local contractors in terms of men and machinery in UT of Ladakh. 
vii) A number of new big-ticket infrastructural projects are proposed to be taken up 
which requires comprehensive planning and specialist consultants for preparing 
DPRs. There is also a requirement of designing the projects keeping in view energy 
efficiency and various other aspects related to the region. The same required 
thorough consultation with stakeholders and expert agencies took due time during 
this period. 
1.2.13 In order to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness in execution of the projects and thereby 
maximise the utilisation of budgetary resources, several steps were taken: 
(i) Requisite Contract Committees and Local Purchase Committees have been 
constituted. 
(ii) Project Implementing Units (PIUs), Project Management Units (PMUs) and 
Consultants have been engaged for timely execution of various projects.  
(iii) Training of officers/staff conducted in GeM, PFMS, E-Office etc. 
(iv) Allotted 90 works to NHIDCL, 01 work to NBCC, 02 Works to WAPCOS and 14 
works to CPWD. 
(v) Ministry of Jal Shakti and CPWD have been approached for providing senior 
engineers on deputation. 
(vi) Government of J&K have been approached for deputation of officers viz. 
KAS/Planning/Finance and Engineers etc., for increasing the efficiency and 
effectiveness in execution of the projects.  
(vii)  Requisite staff/field functionaries at subordinate level have been engaged on 
outsourcing basis.

Demand No. 48 - Ministry of Home Affairs 
4.1 Glacial Outbursts 
 
Recommendation 
4.1.1 The Committee observes that excessive cutting of trees and use of dynamites on 
Hills/Mountains of the Himalayan region for roads, tunnels, dams and other 
developmental/infrastructural projects is significantly weakening the surrounding hills and may be 
causing an avalanche, landslides, flash floods and other catastrophes. The Committee understands 
that the Himalayas are fragile mountains and geologically and environmentally sensitive. 
Therefore, the developmental projects taking place in the region should be considered from the 
environmental and ecological point of view. The Committee recommends that the MHA may 
coordinate with the concerned agencies including NDMA, Ministry of Road, Transport & 
Highways, etc. to immediately ban the use of dynamites for developmental projects in the 
Himalayan region and make judicious use of technology particularly in high altitude Himalayan 
region to minimize the damage to ecology and environment. The Committee further recommends to 
explore technological solutions including use of cutting-edge technology and best practices 
followed by the other countries for cutting of hills/mountains for various developmental projects. 
(Para 3.8.11 ibid) 
Action Taken 
4.1.2 Defence Research Geoinformatics Establishment (Ministry of Defence) has informed that 
the development of these areas will require roads, dams, tunnels etc. Hence use of explosives (used 
in mining) is very common for these developmental projects. To avoid any damage to mountains, 
controlled blasting, using specific quantity of explosive and delay detonators is a viable technology 
being used by advanced countries. The same is being used for mining, tunnelling etc. in our 
country. The commonly used material is gel explosive which is efficient and is being manufactured 
in our country. It is suggested that a careful use of explosives be done to achieve controlled 
blasting and avoid undue damage to hills. Also, techniques like hydro-seeding be used to achieve 
sustainable vegetation cover along with slope stabilization measures. M/o Road Transport & 
Highways (MoRTH) has also been asked to look into the matter and come up with a viable solution 
to the problem by using modern technology. MoRTH has sought views/ inputs from its executing 
agencies. 
4.1.3 As recommended by the Committee, the Government will make judicious use of modern 
technologies in this regard. 
Further Recommendation/Observation 
4.1.4 The Committee notes the reply and would like to be informed about the progress made 
by the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH) in finding a viable solution by 
using modern technology for developmental/infrastructural projects in Himalayan Region .

Demand No. 55 - Lakshadweep 
4.11 Fisheries  
 
Recommendation 
 
4.11.1 The Committee observes that the fishing potential of the UT of Lakshdweep is not being 
harnessed properly. The fishermen are not catching enough fish  due to lack of infrastructure like 
small crafts, ice plant, cold storage, processing units, etc. to catch, preserve and process fishes 
before they are sold in the market. The Committee is of the view that proper storage and processing 
unit will give fishermen the confidence that if the catch is not sold, at least it can be preserved, and 
can be sold in the market the next day. The Committee, therefore, recommends that investments 
should be made for creation of basic infrastructure to boost fishing industry in the UT to provide 
income security to the fishermen. The Committee is of the view that inland fisheries too have vast 
scope for the development of fisheries sector in the UT. The Committee, therefore,  recommends to 
utilize the available inland resources like ponds, swamps and low lying rain fed areas,  for fresh 
water fish farming through mariculture, cage culture and sea weed culture The Committee also 
recommends to increase budgetary allocation to develop fishery industry in the UT. 
(Para 5.6.28 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
 
4.11.2 The local fishermen of Lakshadweep islands use the most sustainable fishing method i.e. 
‗Pole & line‘. Now most of the fishing crafts have b een modernized in Maldivian style with craft 
size of 50 ft and above. Financial Assistance for construction / renovation of fishing small crafts 
and deep sea fishing vessels is also provided under UT Scheme.  In the FY 2021-22, UT of 
Lakshadweep Administration is taking action to develop post- harvest fish processing 
infrastructure facilities in various islands and  64% of the total Grant (GIA) of this current year 

71 
 
budget is earmarked for development of Fishery Infrastructure Development (FID). In addition to 
this, Rs.1.5 crore has been earmarked for cold chain development and establishment of fish 
handling centres in various islands. Similarly, five ammonia based ice plants have already been 
established at: Kavaratti, Kalpeni, Androth, Kadmath and Bitra. Besides, 3 numbers of 5 ton 
capacity Modern Containerized Ice Plants and 5 ton capacity cold storage have been installed at 
Agatti, Amini and Minicoy. Proposal for establishment of Cold storages, chilled rooms, ice plants 
and fish handling centres in various islands with the participation of private sector is also under 
consideration. As regards, quality insulated ice boxes, 65 boxes have been procured for free 
distribution to the fishermen and procurement of 235 boxes is also under process. Procurement and 
installation of Slurry Ice machines is also underway as a pilot project in Agatti.  
4.11.3 The Seaweed culture has been taken up as a pilot project with the technical support from 
Krishi Vigyan Kendra, Kavaratti. The Dept of Fisheries, UTL is implementing Marine Ornamental 
Fish culture project at Agatti with the support of National Cooperative Development Corporation
