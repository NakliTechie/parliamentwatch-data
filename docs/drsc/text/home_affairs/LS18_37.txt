REPORT 
The Ministry of Home Affairs has been entrusted broadly with the following functions: 
(a) To deal with all matters relating to internal security; 
(b) To deal with all matters relating to Centre-State relations and Inter-State 
relations; 
(c) Implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating to Official 
Language and the provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963; 
(d) To discharge certain basiC'-functions like notification of assumption of office by 
the President and the Vice-PreSident; notification of appointment of the Prime 
Minister and other Ministers; notification of appointment, resignation and 
removal of Governors and Lt. Governors, appointment of Lt. Governors, Chief 
Commissioners and Administrative Officers in Union Territories; and 
(e) To deal with matters like citizenship and naturalisation, census of population·, 
National Anthem, National Flag of India, etc. 
2. To discharge the above functions,. the Ministry has been divided into four 
Departments:-
(i) Department of Internal Security-to deal with Police, Law and Order and 
rehabilitation; 
(ii) Department of States-to deal with Centre-State relations, inter-State relations, 
Union Territories and Freedom Fighters' Pension; 
(iii) Department of Official Language-to deal with the implementation of the 
provisions of the Constitution relating to Official Language and the provisions 
of the Official Language Act, 1963; and 
(iv) Department of Home-to deal with the notification of assumption of office by 
the President and Vice-President, notification of appointment of the Prime 
Minister and other Ministers, etc.

12. The Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs made a detailed presentation before the 
o1 Committee on 26th March, 1997 in the cours~ of which he explained the broad thrust of the 
demands relating to his Ministry, the reasons for variation in these demands as compared to 
the Revised Estimates of the previous year (1996-97), the nature of allocation for important 
sector such as housing needs of CPMF personnel, modernisation of police forces and dwelt 
at some length on the general internal security situation obtaining in the country. The 
Committee brought to his notice that as against total outlay of Rs. 7547.36 crores provided 
for the Ministry at the RE stage during the year 1996-97, budgetary provision of 
Rs. 7440.72 crores had been made for the Ministry for the year 1997-98 which was 
comparatively Rs. 107 crores lesser than the Revised Estimates of 1996-97. Similarly 
Demand Nos. 45 and 46 reflected allocations as proposed for 1997-98 on a considerably 
lower side. On the contrary, the budgetary outlay as proposed for the year 1997-98 for the· 
Union Territories (without Legislature) namely Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Dadra & 
Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu and Lakshadweep showed an upward trend. The Secretary was 
asked to clarify this phenomenon underlying the Demands of this Ministry. The Secretary 
pointed out that the reasons for lesser allocations as proposed for 1997-98 vis-o-vis the 
Revised Estimates of 1996-97 were manifold in that parliamentary elections coupled with 
assembly elections in some States necessitated movement of para military personnel from 
one State to another involving additional cost; two additional battalions-one each in BSF 
and CRPF-had to be raised specially for recruitment of surrendered militants in Jammu & 
Kashmir a sum of Rs. 85 crores was earmarked, pursuant to Delhi High Court decision, to 
pay compensation to the victims of 1984 anti-Sikh riots; and additional provision to meet the 
expenditure on providing relief/succour to the victims of riots between the Bodos and 
Santhals had also to be made. This was one time expenditure involving substantial amount 
of additionality at the RE stage in 1996-97 and if this is excluded, then the outlay for 
1997-98, is higher by Rs. 355.35 crores (5%) over the Budget Estimates of 1996-97. 
Responding to a query from the Committee about the higher allocations for most of the 
Union Territories (without Legislature), it was explained by the Secretary that more 
attention was being given to Andaman & Nicobar Islands and enhanced allocat~ons were 
also made to take care of the normal growth. 
13. The attention of the Secretary was also drawn to the lesser allocations to the tune of

of Rs. 353.30 crores for non-plan sector in 1997-98 as against RE of Rs. 368.66 crores for 
1996-97. The Committee does not find any convincing reason for effecting a reduction in the 
budgetary outlay of UT of Clrandigarh for 1997-98 when for all the other UTs without

20. The Secretary stated before the Committee that there were difficulties, in 
accounting for all the foreigners particularly those infiltrating into the country illegally and it 
is only on the basis of their interceptions at the borders that figures can be given. He, 
however, expressed the view that the Electoral Registration Cards (Photo Identity Cards) 
which were being issued by the Election Commission would be of immense help if issued to 
the whole population. This would help in the identification and the detection of foreigners or 
illegal entrants in the country . If necessary, two sets of Cards one for the foreigners and 
another for the electorate can be issued. 
21. The Committee has noted that the figures oi 1994, 1995, 1996 in respect of 
foreigners detected in the country are on the lower side whereas their figure might be much 
more. In some of the States people feel aggrieved on account of incessant flow of foreigners 
from across the borders and agitate for their detection and deportation. One of the reasons 
for their feeling agitated is· that the phenomenon of influx of foreigners on a large scale has a 
bearing on the demographic character of the State as also their cultural ethos. It is, 
therefore, very important that their illegal entry into the country is thoroughly checked on 
immediate time bound basis and those already having entered illegally should be detected 

9 
and deported. The Committee, in its 32nd Report had touched upon this issue and.:had 
observed that the Photo Identity Cards if issued on a national plan will be of immense :use to 
the citizens in many ways particularly from the point ofview of internal security. They will 
also help in identification of the illegal foreign migrants who could be deported back to the 
countries of their origin. A look in its entirety had, therefore, to be had on this issue and a 
time-frame could be laid down for issuance of Photo Identity Cards to all the people. It is 
doubtfull whether providing Photo Identity Cards to the people would solve the problem of 
illegal e'ntry of foreigners into our country in toto but they will definitely go a long way in

REHABILITATION OF 1947 REFUGEES IN J&K 
61. In the course of the meeting of the Committee on March, 26 and April 9, 1997 
points were raised to the effect that in the wake of Pakistani aggression in J&K in the year 
1947, thousands of families from former West Pakistan and areas now under the illegal 
occupation of Pakistan migrated to J&K, but they have yet not be properly rehabilitated. 
People who came from Sialkot have no right to vote for the State Legislature and are not 
even considered as citizens of the State. 
62. The Ministry has clarified that displaced persons from the Pak Occupied Kashmir 
have not been given any compensation under the Displaced Persons (Compensation & 
Rehabilitation) Act, as the whole of Jammu and Kashmir has always been considered an 
integral part of India and the areas presently under the occupation of Pakistan are 
considered to be under illegal occupation. The claims in respect of the properties left behind 
by these displaced persons could not be verified. Pak occupied areas are part of India and 
the 'Evacuee Property Law' as such does not apply to that State. In view of this, 
Government decided that these families may be given ex-gratia payment which had nothing 

23 
to do with the value/quantum of properties )eft by these displaced persons in PAK. 
Accordingly; · Ex-gratia payment was made as per the following scale:-
(i) Urban families · Rs. 3,5(}()1- per family 
(ii) Agricultural families Rs. 1 ,000'- plus 2 to 3 acres of irrigated or 4 to 6 
acres of unirrigated land, depending upon the 
strength of each family.

(ii) Removal of deficiency in their land allotments; and 
(iii) Improvement in living conditions in their colonies.

(i) the amount of Rs. 350(¥- paid to these families in 1960 would compare favounibly with

65. With regard to the displaced persons from former West Pakistan, who had been

compensation under the Displac~d Persons (Compensation & Rehabilitation) Act/Rules 
1954/1955 for the properties left behind by them in West Pakistan. These displaced persons

appropriate if the , amendment to the State Laws is left to the Jammu and Kashmir State 
Assembly.

some incentives, particularly concessional tax structures are being offered to the entrep­
reneurs to set up industries there. The Committee feels that over-industrialisation of these

AIR FIELD IN DIU 
81. In the course of the meeting of the Committee on 26th March, 1997, a point was 
raised about the proposed extension of the existing runway in Diu for use by Indian Air 
Force and· whether it would not have an adverse impact on the ecology and tourism factors. 
The Committee was informed that Diu has an old airfield with a runway of 1825.60 meters 
in length and in view of revised threat perceptions and for reasons of national security, 
Indian Air Force has proposed to extend. this existing runway from the present length of 
1825.60 meters by another 600 meters. The airfield is proposed to be developed by Indian 
Air Force jointly with the Ministry of Civil Aviation and the UT administration. The Indian 
Air Force do not intend to operate from Diu on a permanent basis. On the contrary the 
airfield will be used for civilian purposes as well and the extension of the airfield would 
support the development of tourism in the area as Boeing 737 class of aircraft will be able to 
operate from there. However, since the extension of the airfield would inter-alia involve 
trimming/ removal of about 300 Hooka Palm trees which are considered to be endangered 
and endemic species, the advice of the Ministry of Environment and Forests on the proposal 
of the Indian Air Force has been· sought by the UT administration. 
82. The Committee feels that while security of the country has to be given the priority it 
deserves, at the same time care has to be taken to ensure that environment is duly protected 
to keep the tourist potential intact.
