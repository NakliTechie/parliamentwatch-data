CHAPTER -I
OVERVIEW
1.1 Introduction
1.1.1 The North Eastern Region comprising eight States of the North East  viz . Arunachal
Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura is a region of
myriad challenges and opportunities. The development challenges for these eight States require
special attention due to following special features:-
· The seven States of the North Eastern Region barring Sikkim are linked to the
rest of India through the 26 km long Siliguri Corridor commonly known as
Chicken’s neck. They therefore form an integrated geographical unit.
· 98% of NER’s border is bounded by India’s international neighbors.
· The region offers great diversity - of topography, climatic conditions, language,
religion, ethnicity and yet has common developmental challenges.
· Most of the region is hilly and of difficult terrain.
· While Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram are overwhelmingly
tribal, the other four States also have substantial tribal population.
· Infrastructure deficit in the region, particularly connectivity in all forms is
acute.
· The economy of the region is overwhelmingly agricultural.
For the above reasons, the Department of Development of North Eastern Region was
set up in September, 2001 under the Ministry of Home Affairs and subsequently upgraded to
a Ministry in May, 2004 underscoring its complete commitment to ensure development with
equity for the NER to unleash the potential of its human and natural resources.
1.2 Mandate of  the Ministry
1.2.1 Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region or DONER, as it is commonly
known, is a unique Ministry in the Union Government as its activities are regional and more
importantly advocating the special needs of the region to the other Ministries/Departments and
policy makers.
1.3 Goals of the Ministry
1.3.1 Goals of Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region inter-alia  include the
following:-
i) Accelerating the pace of socio-economic development in the North Eastern
Region.

2
ii) Synergizing the efforts of Central/State Governments/Other Stakeholders for
balanced development of the Region.
iii) Integrated & holistic planning for the Region, taking into account the heightened
consciousness of the people of the Region, their aspirations, needs and
sensitivities.
iv) Evolving a common approach for all the agencies working for the development
of the Region.
v) Formulating appropriate policies for the rapid development of the Region.
1.4 Functions of the Ministry
1.4.1 To fulfill its role the Ministry of DoNER undertakes the following functions and
activities:
i) Matters relating to planning, execution and monitoring development schemes/
projects of the North-East Region including projects in roads, power, irrigation
and communication sectors.
ii) Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR).
iii) Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) Scheme (State and Central).
iv)  All matters relating to the North Eastern Council (NEC).
v) Providing support and guidance to:
(a) North Eastern Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi)
(b) North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC)
and
(c) North Eastern Handlooms & Handicrafts Development Corporation
(NEHHDC).
vi) Building capacities and competencies in critical areas.
vii) Showcasing strength and potential of the region.
1.5 Policy Framework
1.5.1 The Ministry implements work mandated in para 1.1.1 through formulations of
appropriate policies, and implementation of specific Plan programmes. North Eastern Council,
Shillong also implements various Plan Schemes in the Region.
1.5.2 All non-exempted Ministries/Departments of Government of India are required to
earmark at least 10% of Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) towards development of North
Eastern States for creation of both economic and social infrastructure. Since 1998-99, annual
unspent amount out of outlays earmarked for NE Region by non-exempted Central Ministries/
Departments is being accounted as accrual to Central Resource Pool  for Development of North

3
Eastern Region which is maintained on proforma basis by Ministry of Finance and is known
as Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources  (NLCPR). Accruals from this Pool are utilized for
financing new infrastructure projects/schemes in the Region under NLCPR Plan Scheme
implemented by Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region.
1.5.3 The Ministry also supervises work of Public Sector Enterprises, North Eastern
Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd.  (NEHHDC) and North Eastern
Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Ltd.  (NERAMAC). The Government provides
interest free loan to the North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi), a
financial institution which,  inter alia  provides credit and other facilities to industrial
enterprises, infrastructure projects, agro-horticulture units etc. in the Region for promotion,
expansion and modernisation.
1.5.4 While respective Ministries/Departments are responsible in respect of subjects allocated
to them, Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region coordinates with various Ministries/
Departments of Government of India and State Governments for various development and
welfare programmes/schemes/activities in North Eastern Region.

1.7 Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources For North Eastern Region (NLCPR)
1.7.1 All the States in the NER are Special Category States, whose development plans are
centrally financed on the basis of 90% Grant and 10% Loan. Further, the Special Category
States are allowed to use up to 20% of the central assistance for Non-plan expenditure. In
October 1996, the then Prime Minister announced “New Initiatives for North Eastern Region”
and stipulated that at least 10% of the Budget( s) of the Central Ministries/Departments will
be earmarked for the development of North Eastern States. A preliminary exercise undertaken
by the Planning Commission revealed that the expenditure on the North East by some Union
Ministries during 1997-98 fell short of the stipulated 10% of the Gross Budgetary Support
(GBS) for that year. Planning Commission thereafter set up a Central Pool of Resources for
the North East out of the unspent amount of stipulated 10% of GBS to support infrastructure
development projects in the North East. Since 1998-99, all non-exempted Ministries/
Departments of Government of India are required to earmark at least 10% of their GBS for
the development of North Eastern States for creation of both economic and social
infrastructure such as power, roads and bridges, education, health, water supply and sanitation,
etc. in the region.
1.7.2 The unutilized portion of the 10% Plan allocations of the concerned Ministries/
Departments is credited at the end of each financial year in a reserve fund known as the
Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR). The funds from this Pool are used for
financing new infrastructure projects/schemes in the region to ensure speedy development of
infrastructure in the North Eastern Region by increasing the flow of budgetary financing for
such projects/schemes. Both physical and social infrastructure sectors are considered for
providing support under the Central Pool and projects in physical infrastructure sector
receive priority. For this purpose, projects are sanctioned for State as well as Central
sectors. However, the funds available under the Central Pool are not meant to supplement the
normal Plan programmes either of the State Governments or Union Ministries/Departments/
Agencies.
1.8 Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region - Central
(NLCPR-Central)
1.8.1 The Union Cabinet, on 11
th August 2011, approved for introduction, of a new Central
Sector Plan Scheme titled “Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources-Central (NLCPR-
Central)”. This plan scheme has been formulated with the objective of enhancing effective and

5
optimal utilization of funds in the NLCPR Pool towards development of the North Eastern
States. The scheme is not meant to supplement the existing schemes, but to fill critical gaps
in the existing developmental programmes or schemes. As a Central sector plan scheme,
projects of Union Ministries/Departments pertaining to subjects listed in the Union and
Concurrent Lists of Constitution of India can be proposed for funding under the scheme.
Expenditure incurred under the NLCPR-Central scheme will be debited to the NLCPR Pool. The
guidelines for the scheme have been circulated to concerned Union Ministries/Departments,
inviting them to propose appropriate projects. The Governments of North Eastern States and
North Eastern Council have also been informed about the procedure by which they may
propose projects under the scheme.

2.1.4 The Committee sought to know the bottlenecks faced by the Ministry and to what
extent the Ministry had been able to overcome them in regard to implementation of NER
projects during the last three years. The Ministry in response informed that it has been
focusing on expeditious development of physical infrastructure and time bound completion with
adequate funding provisions. Some of the major bottlenecks faced by NEC are:
(i) Low availability of funds during 12 th FYP
(ii) Requirement of AG’s Audit Certificate for release of last installment,
(iii) A vailability of officers to serve in NEC,
(iv) Need for streamlining the procedures in NEC for smooth implementation of
projects,
(v) Management Information System,
(vi) Scrutiny and appraisal of projects etc.
2.1.5 It was also informed that all the concerned Central Ministries and agencies are now
regularly monitoring progress of the ongoing projects and have been pursuing with the State
Governments for issues like land acquisition, forest clearance, law & order problems, etc.
Ministry of DoNER as a coordinating and facilitating Ministry also participates in the
discussions and give its suggestions about the ground level situations and if necessary, takes
up with the State Governments to resolve the issues affecting progress of the projects. As a
part of the commitment, Ministers from the various Central Ministries have been regularly
visiting the region to review progress of programmes in their respective sectors. Government
has also pro actively taken initiatives for promoting linkages with other parts of the country
and for close bilateral relations by developing connectivity with the neighboring countries and
other South East Asian countries in pursuance of the’ Act East Policy’. Establishing closer ties
between India and Bangladesh will also go a long way in promoting peace and development in
the NE Region.

2.3 North Eastern Council
2.3.1 North Eastern Council (NEC) was set up under NEC Act 1971, far securing
balanced development of North Eastern Area with seven constituent members viz . Arunachal
Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura. Through an
amendment of NEC Act in 2002, Sikkim was added as eighth member. By virtue of
Amendment, Council has been made regional planning body for North Eastern Area. It
functions as a regional planning body for its constituent States. It is also mandated to
maintain security and public order. The Council plays an advisory role in areas of interest
that are common to all its constituent States such as economic and social planning,
transport, communications, power and flood control. Besides ensuring effective
coordination, it is also mandated to put forward proposals for developmental schemes and
projects submitted by the States to the Centre.

15
2.3.2 NEC funds Inter-State and State-specific roads in the region. So far, it has funded
construction of more than 10,000 km of road in the region. Apart from roads in the region,
77 temporary bridges have been converted to permanent bridges in the region. Also 3
ISTTs and 9 ISBTs have been constructed. The NEC has undertaken development of
airports in the region and 12 airports are being upgraded regularly under its funding of
60:40 ratio with the Airports Authority of India (AAI). Apart from putting in its own funds
into the improvement of the infrastructure in the region, the NEC has also been
instrumental in providing necessary inputs to the line ministries i.e.  Ministry of Road
Transport & Highways, Railways, Inland Waterways and Ministry of Civil Aviation on
projects undertaken by them from time to time. Although, the resources with the NEC are
limited but it monitors the projects being executed by Central Ministries. The Rural
Development Ministry is doing the PMGSY programme for linking villages upto 250
population. The development of the Barak River as National Waterway No.6 will usher in
long term development in the Cachar Valley of Assam.

2.8.8 North East India is a region which has been starved of development primarily due
to lack of critical infrastructure. The Committee has time and again stressed on
increasing pace of infrastructure creation in the Region. The latest status of
infrastructure projects submitted by the Ministry of DoNER has left the Committee
disappointed. A huge number of projects, more than one-third of the total numbers of

delay, whatever the reasons may be, is incomprehensible and unacceptable. The

taken for expediting such projects to this Committee. The Committee also recommends

2.8.9 The Committee also takes note of the report of the Comptroller and Auditor General
of India (CAG) on the performance audit of roads and bridges projects funded by Non-
Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) and the North-Eastern Council (NEC) in Audit
Report of Arunachal Pradesh for the year ended 31 March 2013. It reflects the financial
mismanagement as well as losses due to ill planning, lack of internal audit and inability of

30
the Departments to come up with utilization certificates. Some major audit findings are
highlighted below:
· There were delays ranging from 7 to 13 months in submission of Priority Lists.
· Records of 25 test-checked NLCPR projects revealed that Detailed Project
Reports were prepared without proper survey and investigation.
· There were delays ranging from 3 to 48 months in release of funds by the State
Government to executing agencies.
· The State Government did not contribute its share aggregating to Rs. 12.15
crore (48.41 percent) towards implementation of 25 test-checked NLCPR
projects.
· Against the total amount of Rs. 403.15 released up to 2012-13 for
implementation of NLCPR, utilization certificates (UCs) for Rs. 111.50 crare (30
per cent) were pending as of March 2013.
· In 22 test-checked projects, executing agencies incurred inadmissible
expenditure of Rs.10.16 crore against NLCPR funds.
· In three projects, executing agencies diverted Rs. 5.26 crore from NLCPR/NEC
funds to other projects.
· The completion rate of projects under NLCPR/NEC was far from satisfactory.
Out of 49 projects due for completion by March 2013 or earlier, only 11
projects (23 percent), involving an expenditure of Rs. 105.44 crore, were
completed. Even completed projects had huge time overruns ranging from 12 to
24 months.
· No Evaluation Study was conducted to assess the impact of projects created.
· Adequate transparency and publicity/dissemination of information relating to
NLCPR/NEC projects was not ensured through the Local media and Display
Boards.
2.8.10 The Committee has a similar feeling that such audit objections in the CAG
reports of performance audits of may also be there in respect of some other NE States.
The Committee notes the inconsistency between the Ministry’s submission and the
existing NLCPR Guidelines. Moreover, the States must not be allowed to withhold funds
beyond reasonable period of time. The Committee recommends that the Ministry must
effect suitable amendments to incorporate strict penal provisions in the NLCPR
Guidelines if the States withhold release of funds for a time greater than a maximum
acceptable limit. If the States fail to release funds for more than three months then the
funds may be reverted back to the Ministry of DONER and NEC and the payment may
be made directly to the executing agencies bypassing the States altogether in case of the
project where funds are not timely released by the States. The Committee feels that
such a strong measure is necessary to prevent huge delays in projects due to non-
release of funds by the States. Accordingly the ministry must apprise the Committee at
the earliest on the action taken on this recommendation.

2.15 NER Livelihood Project ( NERLP) - Eap
2.15.1 North East Rural Livelihood Project (NERLP) is a multi-State central sector
externally aided project funded by World Bank which has become effective from March
2012. It covers four States of Mizoram, Sikkim, Tripura and Nagaland. It has four
components namely Social Empowerment, Economic Empowerment, Partnerships
Development and Management and Project Management, to be implemented in two districts
each of the four NE States.
2.15.2 The objective of the project is to improve rural livelihoods, especially that of women,
unemployed youth and the most disadvantaged in the four project states. The key outcomes
to be achieved through its various project activities are:
(i) Creating sustainable Community Based Organisations (CBOs)- women Self-Help
Groups (SHGs), SHG Federations, Community Development Groups (CDGs),
and Producer Organizations (POs);
(ii) Building capacity of the CBOs for self-governance, bottom up development
planning and democratic functioning with transparency and accountability;
Creating livelihood opportunities by managing natural resources, developing
employable skills of youths, providing complete value chain, providing bank
linkage and creating critical infrastructures;
(iii) Developing technical partnerships for community institutions.
2.15.3 The Ministry apprised the Committee with the review of past performance of the
Scheme which is as under:

village Entry Point Activities (EPAs) and identify 32 Community Service

recruiting all the vacant positions and recruitments for all the vacant posts in

3.6 Extinction of Many Tribal Languages of North East Region
3.6.1 The Committee was concerned about the issue of extinction of many tribal languages
of North East Region especially in Arunachal Pradesh. Therefore it wanted to know the steps
being taken to address this issue in particular and preservation of tribal cultures in general. The
Ministry informed that the issue of preservation of tribal culture is being addressed under the
Research Scheme of the Ministry of Tribal Affairs under which Grant-in-Aid is provided to
Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs) situated in 18 States and the UT of Andaman & Nicobar
Islands for activities which includes, documentation of intangible heritage/arts and crafts of
tribal communities, developing database for Scheduled Tribes (STs) and Particularly Vulnerable
Tribal Groups (PVTGs) including collection of documentation, translation and publication on
the tribal heritage, festivals, oral and visual folklore, arts, local games, sports, songs, literature
etc; development and printing of primers in tribal languages and local official language,
publication of books/dictionaries of tribal languages and translating policy provisions in major
tribal languages for dissemination of the same amongst tribals etc; and organization of tribal
festival and exchange of visits by tribals. Conservation and preservation of tribal culture and
heritage is one of the key components of recently launched approach “Van Bandhu
KalyanYojana”.
3.6.2 Committee is dismayed to note the routine and evasive reply of the Government
on such a specific and important issue. The Committee has been given to understand
that according to UNESCO Atlas of the World’s Languages in Danger data published in
the year 2009, 26 languages of Arunachal have been identified as endangered and it is
even more worrying that even languages of largely populated tribal groups like Adi,
Galo, Nyishi too have been categorized as unsafe by UNESCO due to the heavy influence
of Hindi language. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should
look into this matter seriously and take concrete steps for preservation of tribal
cultures and their languages in NER. Separate budgetary provision may be made for this
purpose.
3.7 Bru Tribal Families’ Repatriation And Rehabilitation in Mizoram:
3.7.1 The Committee took cognizance of the issue of Bru tribal families’ repatriation and
rehabilitation in Mizoram as there were reports of inadequacy of funds provided for their
rehabilitation and enquired about the details of the issue and how the Government intends to

59
resolve this issue. The Ministry, in this regard submitted that due to ethnic violence in Mizoram
in October, 1997, approximately 30,000 (5,000 families) Bru migrants were migrated from
Mizoram and sheltered in relief camps set-up in Kanchanpur district of North Tripura. The
Ministry of Home Affairs has been extending following assistance/Grants-in-Aid to Govt. of
Tripura since 1997-98 for maintenance of Bru migrants sheltered in relief camps in Tripura and
to Govt. of Mizoram w.e.f. 2004-05 for rehabilitation & resettlement of Brus in Mizoram:
i. Housing assistance to each family of Rs.38,500.
ii. Cash assistance to each family of Rs.41,500.
iii. Free ration to each adult and minor member for one year.
iv. Reimbursement of transportation cost incurred by Govt. of Mizoram.
v. Blankets and utensils to each Bru family.
3.7.2 The Bru migrants are being repatriated from Tripura to Mizoram in a phased manner.
As envisaged, the repatriation process was disrupted/stopped due to protests by certain Mizo
NGOs in 2011 and 2012. Hence, Grant-in-Aid to Government of Mizoram could not be released
in 2012-13 and 2013-14 as no repatriation took place. Though, steps have been taken to
complete the repatriation process and as a result of regular follow up, 173 families have been
repatriated in the 6th Batch including self-repatriation, making a total repatriation of about
1,210 Bru families (approx. 5,000 people) as on 28.2.2015. During the financial year 2014- 15,
Rs. 35 crore and Rs. 5 crore were provided to the Government of Tripura and Mizoram
respectively under the scheme. As per Action Plan submitted by the Govt. of Mizoram, the Bru
repatriation process is expected to be completed by August/September, 2015. Now more funds
have to be released to the Government of Mizoram in 2015-16 for rehabilitation of Bru
migrants with the corresponding decrease of funds for the Government of Tripura.
3.7.3 The Committee is overwhelmingly concerned that even after almost two decades
the Bru tribe is suffering from the aftermath of ethnic violence. It is disappointed with
the efforts of the Governments of Tripura and Mizoram and is worried about the fact
that till now the complete repatriation and rehabilitation of Bru migrants has not taken
place. The Committee recommends that Bru migrants must be accorded all support and
protection and their repatriation and rehabilitation be completed at the earliest. The
Committee also recommends that Ministry must ensure that shortage of funds must not
come in the way of full and final repatriation and complete rehabilitation of Bru
migrants.
