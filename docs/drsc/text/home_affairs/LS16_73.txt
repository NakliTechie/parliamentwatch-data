INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs having been authorised by the
Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Seventy-third Report on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) relating to
the Ministry of Home Affairs.
2.         The Committee held in all four sittings to complete the examination of the Demands for Grants of the Ministry. In its meeting held on
27 March 2001, the Committee considered the relevant documents and papers received from the Ministry of Home Affairs and heard the
Home Secretary. In its meeting held on 28 March 2001, the Committee heard the representatives of North-Eastern Council (NEC).  On 30
March 2001, the Committee heard the Commissioner of Police, Delhi and two experts on Traffic Management in Delhi. 
3.         The Committee while making its observations/recommendations has mainly relied upon the following:-
 
(i)         Presentations made and clarifications given by the Secretary and other senior representatives of the Ministry; Secretary, NEC,
Commissioner of Police, Delhi and two experts on Traffic Management in Delhi;
(ii)        Detailed Demands for Grants of the Ministry for the year 2001-2002;
            (iii)       Annual Report of the Ministry for the year 2000-2001;
            (iv)       Performance Budget of the Ministry for the year 2001-2002;
(v)        Detailed Explanatory Note on the Demands for Grants for the year 2001-2002;
(vi)       Statement showing approved outlays for and the expenditure incurred on Plan Schemes of the Ministry during the Ninth Plan
period (1997-2002);
(vii)      Written replies furnished by the Ministry, NEC, Delhi Police and two experts on Traffic Management in Delhi to the
Questionnaires sent to them by the Secretariat;
(viii)      Written clarifications furnished by the Ministry, NEC, Delhi Police and two experts on Traffic Management in Delhi on
points/issues raised by the Members in the meetings of the Committee; and
(ix)
              Action Taken Notes furnished by the Ministry on the observations/recommendations of the Committee contained in its Fifty-
ninth Report on the Demands for Grants (2000-2001) of the Ministry.

REPORT
 
The Ministry of Home Affairs has been entrusted broadly with the following functions:
            (a)        To deal with all matters relating to internal security;
(b)        To deal with all matters relating to Centre-State relations and Inter-State relations;
(c )       Implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating to Official Language and the provisions of the Official Language
Act, 1963;
(d)        To discharge certain basic functions like notification of assumption of office by the President and the Vice-President;
notification of appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers; notification of appointment, resignation and removal of
Governors and Lt. Governors, appointment of Lt. Governors, Chief Commissioners and Administrative Officers in Union
Territories;
(e)        To deal with matters like citizenship and naturalisation, census of population, National Anthem, National Flag, etc.; and
(f)         To deal with all constitutional provisions with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters relating to the
State of Jammu and Kashmir excluding those with which the Ministry of External Affairs is concerned.
2.         To discharge the above functions, the Ministry has been divided into five Departments:
(i)         Department of Internal Security – to deal with Police, Law and Order and rehabilitation;
(ii)        Department of States – to deal with Centre-State relations, inter-State relations, Union Territories and Freedom Fighters’
Pension;
(iii)       Department of Official Language – to deal with the implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating to Official
Language and provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963;

PRESENTATION ON DEMANDS FOR GRANTS (2001-2002)
13.       The Committee began consideration of the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in its meeting held on 27 March 2001.  The Home
Secretary made a presentation before the Committee with the help of audio-visual means.
 14.      The Home Secretary began his presentation with an overview of internal security situation in the country.  He apprised the Committee
about the progress made on various fronts and the steps and measures taken last year.  He stated that the year 2000 witnessed two significant
peace efforts in Jammu and Kashmir as also two mass killings to further widen the communal and sectarian divide in the trouble-torn state of
Jammu and Kashmir- Chittisinghpora massacre in March and killings of Amarnath pilgrims at Pahalgam in August.  The Hizbul Mujahideen
declared a cease-fire in July 2000 to which Government of India reacted positively.  However, the Hizb leadership withdrew the ceasefire
within two weeks under pressure of Pakistan establishment causing wide spread disappointment among the people of Jammu and Kashmir. 
He further stated that Government of India took a peace initiative in November 2000 which coincided with the holy month of Ramzan and
had since been extended twice upto May 2001.  The people of Jammu and Kashmir had welcomed the peace initiative of Government of
India.  This initiative had also provided reprieve to the border population in that the cross-border shelling had almost ceased. 
14.1     Referring to the developments in the North-East, he stated that National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN-Khaplang) and the
Bodo Liberation Tigers (BLT) had responded positively to the peace initiatives of Government of India.  He informed that a mutual
suspension of operations with BLT had been agreed and ground rules had been finalized.  Talks to evolve a package to resolve the Bodo
issues were progressing satisfactorily and it was hoped that a final accord would be mutually agreed during the present year itself.  He further
informed that a formal ceasefire with NSCN (Khaplang) might materialize as soon as the ground rules were finalized in that regard.  The
ceasefire with NSCN (IM) continued and talks were on to revise the ground rules. 
14.2     The Home Secretary stated that grant of statehood to Uttranchal, Jharkand and Chhatisgarh was a landmark development which raised
the number of the States of the Union from 25 to 28 and set at rest the agitational activities over the creation of these States.
14.3     Touching upon the Census of India, he stated that the census had been completed in the whole country except some areas of Gujarat
and Kinnaur district of Himachal Pradesh which were affected by natural calamities.
14.4     He went on to state that a general impression was being created by some interested elements that there existed a communal tension
between Hindus and Christians. The attacks on Churches were being used to prove that point. However, the involvement of a Muslim sect
called Deendar Anjuman in the attacks proved that they were carried out at the behest of Pak-ISI.    
14.5     While referring to the review of national security system, the Home Secretary mentioned that a Group of Ministers headed by the
Home Minister was constituted in April 2000.  The Group of Ministers constituted four Task Forces of which two were serviced by the
Ministry of Home Affairs.  The Group of Ministers submitted its report to the Prime Minister which was now under the consideration of the
Government.  He stated that it was for the first time that various issues and questions relating to national security both internal and external
had been so comprehensively deliberated upon. 
15.       Thereafter the Home Secretary touched upon the internal security trends in different parts of the country with special emphasis on the
known disturbed areas.
15.1     In relation to the situation in Jammu and Kashmir, he stated that the number of violent incidents in the year 2000 was 3074 which was
almost equal to the figure of the preceding year. However, the number of killings of security forces and civilians registered a slight increase
from 1229 in 1999 to 1244 in 2000.  Similarly the number of militants killed by the security forces also went up from 1082 in 1999 to 1520 in
2000.  As compared to Kashmir valley, Jammu region witnessed a spurt in violence.  Ever since Kargil intrusions, the militants had started
targeting security forces’ camps and pickets by using suicide squads which resulted in a disturbing increase in the casualties of security
forces.  He informed that greater involvement of foreign mercenaries in the State proved that the local support to the militancy had been
waning.  Referring to the post- ceasefire developments, he stated that activities of foreign militant outfits had intensified not only in Jammu
and Kashmir but also in other parts of the country.  He drew the attention to the attack on Red Fort and the killings of Sikhs at Mehjoor Nagar
near Srinagar. 
15.1.1  He stated that during the 105 days preceding the declaration of Ramzan ceasefire, there were 1062 violent incidents in which 560
militants had been killed.  During 105 days after the declaration of ceasefire, the number of violent incidents came down to 1023 and the
number of militants killed during this period dropped to 258.  He apprised the Committee that the security forces had been asked to remain in
combat readiness to meet any challenge keeping in view the post-ceasefire security assessment.  A strict vigil was also being kept to check
infiltration and exfiltration of militants.  He informed that while the security forces would not initiate operations against militants, there was
no ban on their taking action against militants found moving with arms and ammunition.  He further informed that census operation in Jammu
and Kashmir was undertaken and completed successfully despite serious threats from militants.  Another notable development was the
holding of panchayat elections in Poonch, Rajouri, Kathua, Doda, Jammu and Udhampur districts of Jammu region and some blocks of
Kupwara and Baramullah districts of Kashmir region.
16.       The Home Secretary then apprised the Committee of the situation in the North-East.  He stated that the intensity of violence continued

to  remain high in the North-East in the year 2000.  In all, 1960 incidents of violence were recorded which resulted into 1657 deaths of which
165 were security force personnel and 585 were militants.  He commented that the causalities among the security force personnel were lower
as compared to the previous year.  However, there was increase in the number of incidents of violence and deaths of civilians.  He informed
that States of Assam, Tripura and Manipur were worst affected while Nagaland, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh were comparatively less
affected. 
16.1     Referring to the attacks on Hindi speaking Assamese and non-Assamese people, he stated that ULFA killed 89 people in 12 incidents
of violence against these  people.  He informed that the creation of United People’s Democratic Solidarity (UPDS) had added a new
dimension to the violence profile in Assam.  The UPDS was responsible for 34 incidents of violence in which 72 persons were killed. 
16.2     In regard to the situation in Manipur, he stated that there was a severe crisis on account of subversion of the Government machinery
and a political stalemate.  The situation required intervention of the Ministry of Home Affairs to restore a semblance of civil governance.  A
26- point Action Plan was formulated in consultation with the State Government which was now under implementation.  As regards the State
of Tripura, he stated that a number of suggestions and action points were being closely monitored.
16.3     The activities of NSCN (IM) in Tirap and Changlang districts of Arunachal Pradesh were also a matter of concern for the Government
which had since declared them as disturbed areas under the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1967.  The situation in Meghalaya also took
a turn for the worse during the year 2000.  Two outfits namely, ANVC and HNLC were declared unlawful associations by the Union
Government at the request of the State Government.
17.       With regard to the security situation in Punjab, the Home Secretary stated that Punjab remained generally peaceful and did not witness
any major incident of violence.  However, Pakistan based Sikh militant groups remained under pressure of Pak-ISI to resume violent activities
in the State.  Recovery of explosives and sophisticated weapons from militants in Punjab, Rajasthan and Delhi confirmed that serious attempts
were being made to resurrect militancy in the State.  The security forces arrested 35 militants during the year and recovered 10 AK series
rifles, 62 handguns, 22 grenades, 12 bombs, 63.6 kgs of explosives and 1465 cartridges.  He stated that there had been no dilution in
Pakistan’s efforts to revive militancy in Punjab.  The formation of Pakistan Gurudwara Prabandhak Committee (PGPC) had provided a
platform to the pro-Khalistan elements all over the world.  Pakistan had also reportedly constituted an International Gurudwara Kar Sewa
Committee in which top ranking militants and pro-Khalistani elements had been given prominence. 
18.       Drawing the attention to the situation in Delhi, the Home Secretary stated that it continued to be a major focus of Home Ministry’s
attention.  He informed that a 4.2% decline in the number of IPC crimes was registered during the year.  Efforts were being made to dispel the
general feeling of Delhi being an unsafe metropolis from law and order angle.  During the year 2000, four cases of bomb blasts occurred in
the capital.  A total of 43 militants including 17 Pak nationals were arrested.  The shootout in the Red Fort on 22 December 2000 had worst
impact both on public and security forces.  He further stated that  Delhi continued to be perceived as a convenient hide-out for the members of
Mumbai under-world.  Referring to the study on manpower and modernization requirement of Delhi Police by Bureau of Police Research &
Development (BPR&D), he informed that Bureau submitted its report on 19 October 2000 which was referred to the Lt. Governor of Delhi
for examination as he exercised direct control of Delhi Police on behalf of the Union Government.  He further stated that while examining the
report in the Ministry it was noticed that a number of critical issued had not been addressed adequately and accordingly additional
information and data was called for from Delhi Police.  He informed that a Committee headed by Joint Secretary (Union Territories) had been
constituted to examine the report in association with the BPR&D and Delhi Police. 
19.       Speaking on the left-wing extremism, the Home Secretary stated that left-wing extremist violence in the country registered a marginal
decline both in terms of number of incidents and casualties due to sustained efforts of the security forces.  Compared to 1246 incidents and
598 deaths in 1999, the year 2000 registered 1177 incidents and 550 deaths.  While Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra
witnessed a decline in the number of incidents as well as deaths, Bihar registered an increase in the number of incidents but a decrease in the
number of deaths.  Jharkhand, Chhatisgarh and Orissa saw an increase in both incidents as well as deaths.  He stated that CPML-People’s War
continued to dominate the left-wing extremists scene and was responsible for 51.4% of such violence in the country.  It was followed by
Maoist Communist Centre and CPML-Liberation which was responsible for 31.61% and 5.86% of violence, respectively.  He further
informed that in a meeting of the Chief Ministers of the States affected by left-wing extremism convened by the Union Home Minister on 4
April 2000, it was agreed that while the Centre would continue to extend all possible assistance to the States in intelligence sharing, security
related expenditure, training needs of police personnel, etc., the States concerned on their part would concentrate their efforts on inter-State
coordinated operations, ground level intelligence gathering, greater thrust on responsive and sincere administration of trouble-prone areas and
redressal of people’s grievances.  He informed that the coordination centre headed by the Union Home  Secretary had met 8 times since then. 
20.       Touching upon the communal violence involving Hindus and Muslims, the Home Secretary stated that it continued to show downward
trend for the ninth consecutive year.  As compared to 598 incidents during 1999 there were 561 such incidents in 2000.  However, the number
of deaths registered an increase from 160 in 1999 to 224 in 2000.  The number of incidents of violence involving Christians and their
institutions showed an increase from 120 in 1999 to 216 in 2000.  Inquiries revealed that out of 216 incidents only 114 resulted from Hindu-
Christian tension.  While 93 incidents were purely criminal acts having no communal angle. 
20.1     He further informed that Bihar and Karnataka witnessed an increase in caste related incidents while Orissa, Andhra Pradesh,
Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu registered a decrease in such clashes.  The agrarian sector remained
by and large peaceful during 2000.  Similarly, there was no large-scale violence or protracted agitation involving students and youth.   On the

SITUATION IN JAMMU & KASHMIR
28.       The situation in Jammu & Kashmir has taken a positive turnaround with the declaration of the latest peace move of
Government of India. The most heartening development has been the positive response of the people of the State to the peace
initiative which has sprung from their strong yearning for restoration of peace and normalcy. The people of Jammu & Kashmir need
to be commended for their strong desire for return of peace and tranquility but at the same time some foreign militant organizations
deserve to be condemned for their unabated efforts to derail the peace initiative. Hopefully, people at large will disapprove of such
anti-peace efforts of foreign mercenaries operating in the State.
29.       Although people at large have welcomed the peace initiative, they are skeptical of the role of security forces. Of late, the incidents of
custodial deaths and firing have left a sense of distrust among the people towards security forces. The role of Special Operation Groups
(SOGs) of Jammu & Kashmir Police has come in for severest criticism.
30.       Given the delicate nature of the situation in the State, the Committee urges upon the security forces to exercise utmost
restraint while dealing with scourge of militancy. This, however, should not be construed as a reflection on the security forces. The
Committee is fully aware of the adverse circumstances in which security personnel are discharging their duty.
31.       The role of Village Defence Committees (VDCs) has engaged the attention of the Committee. It was informed that VDC system was
evolved by the State Government with a view to involving local population in the fight against terrorism as well as to enable remote hamlets
and villages to defend themselves and with a view to inculcating a sense of security in far flung areas. The VDC consists of volunteers from
the village and till 1988, there used to be one Special Police Officer (SPO) attached to each VDC. However, in pursuance to the 1998 Action
Plan to tackle militancy, each VDC was provided with a nucleus of 3 SPOs. The number of VDCs was enhanced from 1455 to 2486 and the
number of SPOs was also increased to 7458. In addition to this, State Government has other SPOs for other operational purposes. This apart
greater integration of VDCs with counter-insurgency grid has been ensured by imparting training from time to time to VDC members and
SPOs through Army, BSF and J&K Police in handling weapons, wireless sets, tactics and other defensive/operational measures. The number
of SPOs has further been enhanced from 18,000 to 21,000 w.e.f. 15 February 2001. Another notable point is the fact that the concept of VDC
has been successful in Jammu region whereas it has not met with success in Kashmir Valley.
32.       Keeping in view the important role being played by VDCs in tackling militancy in Jammu & Kashmir, the Committee feels that more
emphasis is needed on their organizational set up. It is noteworthy that steps have been taken to increase the number of VDCs and SPOs. Yet,
it feels that lot more is desired to be done to strengthen the network of VDCs. Their strengthening becomes all the more imperative in view of
the fact that more and more VDC volunteers are being targeted by the militants and the villages where such Committees have been set up
continue to be struck at will by the militants. In this backdrop, the Committee is of the opinion that at least two or three fully trained security
force personnel should be attached with each VDC. The attachment of security personnel will add a touch of professionalism to their
functioning. Moreover, the presence of security personnel amongst VDC volunteers will help establish a better contact between   VDCs and
security forces participating in counter-insurgency operations. This apart, the attached security personnel could impart training to VDC
members in using sophisticated weapons and communication devices like wireless sets etc. The Committee, therefore, urges upon both the

73rd Report of Committee on Home affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/73rdreport.htm[30-06-2016 17:40:49]
Union Government as well as the State Government to consider its opinion and find ways and means to make it happen as soon as possible.
32.1     The Committee is of the view that VDCs/SPOs are not getting adequate financial compensation for their services in counter-
insurgency operations. While strengthening the VDCs/SPOs, the Government should also keep in mind the need for providing adequate 
financial package to them so as to encourage the participation of even greater number of people.    
33.       The ongoing turmoil in Jammu & Kashmir has forced thousands of Kashmiri Pandits to migrate to safer places in Jammu region and
elsewhere in the country. Their return to their ancestral places in the valley has not materialized despite tall claims by the Government. In this
regard the Committee has been informed that in order to enable safe and honourable return of migrants to their native places in the Valley, the
State Government had constituted an apex level Committee under the Chairmanship of Minister for Revenue, Relief and Rehabilitation to
look into all aspects of this problem and suggest solutions. A sub-Committee headed by Financial Commissioner (Planning & Development)
was asked to prepare a plan for the return of the migrants. The sub-Committee submitted an Action Plan for return of Kashmiri migrants to
the Valley, which was duly approved by the State Cabinet. The Action Plan was received from the Government of J&K in October 2000 with
a request to release necessary funds for its implementation.
33.1     The Action Plan envisages an expenditure of Rs. 2589.73 crore for rehabilitation of 52,689 Kashmiri displaced families. The Action
Plan is proposed to be implemented in a phased manner. The State Government has for the present approved first phase of the Action Plan for
rehabilitation of 2122 families at an estimated cost of Rs. 43.94 crore. The package broadly consists of case incentives, repairs/reconstruction
of existing houses, interest subsidy on new business loans and loan for orchards etc. for returning families. The Plan envisages settlement of
2122 families in 1065 houses in 68 identified clusters in six districts in the Kashmir Valley. The clusters are located in villages/mohallas with
sizeable Kashmiri Pandit population and where security is already provided.
33.2     The Action Plan was discussed in a meeting with the State Government officials on 16.11.2000 in which the following views
emerged:-
The foremost requirement for implementation of the Action Plan is the willingness of the migrants to return to the Valley and their
perception of improvement of security scenario in the Valley as a whole. Secondly, the houses of the migrants and their orchards, etc.
should not be under anybody’s occupation so that there is possible backlash to their return from those in occupation of their property.
The Action Plan should be implemented in phases starting with the places where adequate security could be ensured.
The initiative for return to the Valley should come from the migrants. Those who intend to return should first of all interact with their
neighbours in the Valley so that their security is reasonably assured. Once the rehabilitation scheme takes off, the scheme may be
offered to rest of the migrants who have come from other places and are willing to go back.
 
33.3     After discussions, it was decided that those migrants who came from Srinagar, Budgam and other areas where there was less security
problem should be rehabilitated. Migrants who were owners of the houses in the identified clusters would be approached first. To begin with,
about 200 families who came from Srinagar could be rehabilitated. For this purpose, a provision of Rs. 2 crore could be kept in RE 2000-2001
and Rs. 10 crore could be kept under BE 2001-2002. The State Government was accordingly requested to identify the people who were to be
rehabilitated in the clusters and offer them the scheme so as to find out their willingness to return back to their homes. Funds would be made
available as reimbursement of expenditure incurred by the State Government under this Plan on a quarterly basis. Ministry of Home Affairs
would be making necessary budgetary  provisions for this purpose.
33.4     The progress of implementation of Action Plan was subsequently discussed with State Government officials on several occasions. It
has been learnt from the State Government that efforts are on at the political level to persuade migrant families to accept the rehabilitation
plan and actual work could begin only after the winter season was over. Accordingly, the State Government also did not request for release of
funds in the current year.
33.5     The recent massacre of 6 members of the Sikh community in Srinagar could make the task of persuading the migrant families to return
to their homes in the Valley very difficult.
33.6     The rehabilitation plan is being submitted to Cabinet Committee on Security for  approval of expenditure to be incurred on the
rehabilitation of all the Kashmiri Pandit migrants in the Valley in phases.
34.       In view of the position explained by the Ministry of Home Affairs as given in the preceding paragraphs, the Committee recommends
that consistent and persistent efforts should continue to be made by  State as well as Union Government for safe and early return of Kashmiri
Pandits to their homes in Kashmir Valley so that the nefarious designs of some foreign elements to engineer ethnic cleansing in the valley are
defeated.  Till such time the return of these migrants to their homes in the valley is materialized, the Government should ensure adequate
financial assistance to them for their sustenance.
 
SITUATION IN NAGALAND
35.       The ceasefire between Government of India and National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN-IM Group) is now over four years

appreciable decline in the level of violence ever since ceasefire became effective. The Ministry of Home Affairs has informed the Committee
that all sections of the society in Nagaland have by and large welcomed the ceasefire. As a result of the ceasefire, there has been a palpable
decrease in the atmosphere of fear. The civilian population is now able to freely exercise their opinions without fear of being threatened or
killed. A large number of peace rallies have also been held in the State signifying the desire of the people for the continuation of ceasefire and

36.       In this backdrop, the Committee is concerned to note that a well-organised extortion business is continuing unabated despite there

DEPUTATION QUOTA FOR IPS IN CPMFs
37.       The issue of making amendments to IPS (Cadre) Rules, 1954 so as to make posting of IPS officers to the CPMFs mandatory has been
hanging fire for long. The proposal in this regard was put forward by Ministry of Home Affairs. However, the Ministry failed to carry out the
requisite amendments. In its Action Taken Notes on the recommendation of the Committee as contained in its 59
th Report in this regard, the
Ministry has replied that the proposal for making posting of IPS officers on Central deputation mandatory was well considered. This proposal
came up because of shortage of IPS officers for manning posts under the Government of India. It was felt that if such provision was made in
the IPS (Cadre) Rules, it would not only ensure availability of officers but would also provide sufficient exposure to the IPS officers to enable
them later to occupy senior posts under the CPMFs. This matter was debated on several occasions at inter and intra ministerial levels. The
matter was examined threadbare both in this Ministry and in the DOP&T. The opinion of the Ministry of Law was also obtained. In the
absence of any foolproof mechanism for enforcing the orders of the Central Government, it was felt that the proposal would have to be
dropped. There was no lack of commitment in pursuing the proposal. This Ministry have again taken up the matter with DOP&T and will
follow up the same vigorously.
38.       The Committee notes the first part of the Action Taken Notes of the Ministry with regard to the proposed amendments to the IPS
(Cadre) Rules, 1954, in pursuance of the recommendations of the Committee as contained in its 59
th Report.  But the second part which inter
alia states that in the absence of a foolproof mechanism for enforcing the orders of the Central Government, the proposal had to be dropped,
does not convince the Committee because of the sheer irrationality of the proposition propounded therein. It fails to comprehend as to what
precludes the Government from evolving a suitable mechanism in this regard. It appears from the reply of the Ministry that not enough
thought has been applied in the matter. If the concerned Ministries/Departments had shown seriousness to make this proposal a reality, a
suitable mechanism could have been evolved. Whether it is the lack of commitment or something else which is a stumbling block, remains an
enigma for the Committee. It, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendations in this regard as contained in its 54 th and 59 th Reports and
hopes that this time a brainstorming exercise will be undertaken by the concerned Ministries/Departments to evolve a suitable mechanism to
make the posting of IPS officers at the Centre mandatory.
38.1     The Committee, however, recommends that the Government should formulate rules in such a manner that an IPS Officer should not be
considered eligible for Central deputation in the ranking of IGP/DGP unless the officer has had experience of working in a junior level in the
Central Government.  
 
MODERNISATION OF CENTRAL PARA MILITARY FORCES
39.       Central Para-military Forces are playing a crucial role both in the maintenance of internal security as well as guarding of country’s
borders.  Some of these forces such as CRPF have as high as 95% deployment level.  Needless to say that our forces are doing commendable
work under most trying and taxing conditions.  They deserve to be appreciated for their courage to withstand all kinds of odds.  It, therefore,
is incumbent upon the Government to rid them of their all handicaps.   This can be done by equipping them with sophisticated weaponry and
state-of- the art equipment.  Augmentation of manpower is also one of the areas which should receive attention so that enough reserves are
maintained.  It was precisely with these views in mind that the Committee, in its 59
th Report, had hoped that the modernization plans of
CPMFs would soon be implemented with fullest sincerity and commitment.
40.       In response to its recommendation, the Ministry of Home Affairs, in its Action Taken Notes on 59 th Report of the Committee has
stated that the modernization Plan initially submitted by the CPMFs, for the five-year period from 2000-01 to 2004-05, a tentative projection
of Rs.4025 crore had been made.  The basis premise on which these plans have been prepared is that the deployment pattern of the forces will
remain largely the same during the next five years, i.e. the future raisings during the period have not been brought under the purview of these
plans.  The original plans submitted by the respective forces have been discussed Force-by-Force keeping in view the following main
considerations:
(i)
                  To increase striking power of forces with superior arms & ammunition and strategic equipment and effective transport
vehicles.
(ii)                To provide state-of-the art communication and surveillance system which is the mainstay of developing operational strategy.
(iii)               To continuously upgrade the skill of the troops by providing training facilities.
(iv)              To keep the troops operationally fit by providing personal comfort through modern outfits.
40.1     After discussions certain modifications have been made in the plans which have resulted in reduction in the overall projection of the
plan of all the Forces together to Rs.3740.71 crore.  These modifications have reduced the projections of Assam Rifles, BSF and ITBP by

about 11%, 8% and 14%, respectively and marginally for CRPF and NSG.  In case of CISF the projection has been increased by about 11%.
40.2     The fund required for implementation of the Modernisation Plan cannot be met from the normal budget grant until and unless
budgetary support to CPMFs is augmented substantially.  With an assumed 10% notional growth in annual allocation, the availability of funds
to the CPMFs is expected to be of the order Rs.4508 crore over a five year period which could absorb about Rs.1117 crore towards

41.       On the basis of Action Taken Notes of the Ministry on 59
th Report of the Committee and the replies submitted by it to the
Committee during this year’s examination of Demands for Grants, it becomes amply clear that it is still a long time before the
proposed plan for modernization of CPMFs can see the light of the day.  The initial projection for this five-year plan from 2000-01 to
2004-05 was Rs.4025 crore which now stands scaled down to Rs.3740.71 crore.  But the notable point is that even this reduced amount
of funds is not going to be available during the period for which the plan is actually meant.  There will be a huge shortfall of Rs.2624

Home Affairs, not enough urgency is being shown by it to put its implementation in place with right earnest.  It is, indeed, pathetic to
note that the spectre of shortage of funds is looming large over the proposed modernization plan even before it has taken off.  The
imminent shortage of funds raises doubt about the time bound implementation of the plan.  And, when there is delay in the
implementation of a plan within a specified timeframe, it leads to cost over-runs as well.  Both time as well as cost over-runs, in effect,
make a mockery of a plan.  They are also a drag on the economy.  In this backdrop, the Committee recommends that the Government
should accord topmost priority to the modernization plan of CPMFs.  It hopes that the Cabinet will approve the plan sooner than
later.  As regards, the bridging of the anticipated shortfall of funds to the tune of Rs.2624 crore, the Committee strongly recommends
that suitable ways and means should be found by the Government to meet this shortfall so that the proposed modernization plan is
taken to its logical conclusion without any time and cost over-runs.
41.1     In this context, the Committee notes with concern the ever-increasing dependence of the States on CPMFs even for routine
maintenance of general law and order. This over-dependence of the States on CPMFs puts greater pressure on the latter as they have
to perform even those duties which are normally not part of their primary role.   Recognising the need for augmenting and
strengthening the CPMFs, the Committee is of the view that if additional strength of the CPMFs is used to supplement the
requirements of the States then the purpose of augmenting the strength of CPMFs will not be fulfilled.   Therefore, the need of the
hour is to strengthen and augment the State police forces so that the over-dependence of the States on central forces is reduced to the
maximum possible extent. 
41.2     Incidentally, this is also the case with investigating agencies. Whenever, there is a serious crime or a crime of high magnitude,
it is found that State investigating agencies are not at all equipped to investigate such crimes.  In such a situation, States have to turn
to central agencies such as CBI.  The Committee is concerned at this growing trend of more and more reliance of States on CBI. 
Therefore, it is of the considered view that in addition to the modernisation and strengthening of CPMFs and CBI, it is also equally
important to augment and modernise State police forces and investigating agencies so that their over-dependence on the former is
minimised.

TRAFFIC MANAGEMENT IN DELHI
56.       While examining the Demands for Grants of Delhi Police, the Committee took up the subject of traffic management in Delhi for

73rd Report of Committee on Home affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/73rdreport.htm[30-06-2016 17:40:49]
consideration.  It heard the views of Prof. A.K. Sharma, Head of Department, Department of Transport and Centre for Transport Studies and
Traffic Laboratory, School of Planning and Architecture, New Delhi and Dr. T.S. Reddy, Deputy Director (Traffic and Transportation),
Central Road Research Institute, New Delhi, on the subject in its meeting held on 30 March 2001.
57.       Prof. A.K. Sharma, in his presentation focused on major issues concerning the problems of traffic in Delhi.  The most important was
the functional distribution of work related to the traffic management in Delhi.  These functions were planning, engineering and management. 
The role of traffic police came under the ambit of management especially in terms of enforcement and regulation of traffic.  So far as
engineering and planning inputs was concerned, Delhi Police had no mechanism in their cadres.  He stated that there existed multiplicity of
authorities to take care of traffic management. Agencies like DDA, MCD, NDMC, State Transport Authority, etc. were responsible for traffic
management in Delhi. This multiplicity of authority had resulted into the disintegration of transport planning functions. He suggested that
there should be one nodal agency for performing the planning functions which should be under the jurisdiction of
 
Government of Delhi. This agency should have an expert group who could sensitise other agencies regarding the future policies, programme
implementation and execution. The enforcement, however, should remain with Delhi Police.  The next major issue was that of road safety
which was intrinsically linked to licensing of drivers and the use of road space within the city. He suggested for stringent procedures for issue
of licenses which should inter alia have a mandatory requirement of minimum level of literacy, six months compulsory training   from a
driver training institute, etc. Referring to the safety of school children of the roads, he stated that a proper system of school transportation
needed to be evolved. He felt that there was an urgent need to have a dedicated transport system for school children. Touching upon the issue
of enforcement, he stated that a high-end technology and traffic surveillance system should be put in place. He opined that dedicated
technologies could help in controlling traffic. Another important aspect was training of traffic personnel. In his view, most of the traffic
personnel were not trained in traffic management, which was a specialized subject of study. He felt that rotation of traffic personnel was not
going to produce any results. Sustained involvement of traffic personnel  was key to the success. He was of the view that improvement in the
functioning of traffic police relied on the inputs of local agencies responsible for traffic management.
58.       Another traffic expert, Dr. T.S. Reddy stated that the worsening traffic situation was largely due to the total negligence of planning.
The transport system had not been planned properly. He opined that three Es-engineering, education and enforcement were the most
important aspects of traffic management.  Of these, engineering was the most neglected as a result of which traffic was being controlled
manually by traffic personnel. He felt that there was need for a selective and strategic enforcement drive to discipline the behaviour of
motorists on the roads. This apart, a tremendous thrust should be given for bringing about an attitudinal change in the traffic police. In fact,
there was need for systematic traffic training school. He further stated that a comprehensive parking policy was also needed. As for the
training aspect, he felt that it was the most important thing for personnel managing the traffic.
59.       The worsening traffic scenario of the capital has attracted the attention of the Committee primarily because of its negligence by the
authorities concerned.  There is no gainsaying the fact that it is not too easy a problem to be dealt with.  A city with close to four million
vehicles with an almost static road network definitely poses huge challenges for its traffic managers.  However, it is interesting to note that in
spite of having so many challenges to grapple with, there is no unified authority to take them on.  There seem to be as many authorities as
there are challenges.  This is probably the reason why things are pulling in different directions.  There is no unity of command nor there is any
coordination among the agencies responsible for the management of overall traffic scenario.  At a time when situation warrants concerted,
well-coordinated and unified measures to be taken, the system lacks the required mechanism to see it through.  Though a slew of other
agencies such as DDA, MCD, NDMC, STA etc. are also responsible for the traffic management of the city either this way or the other, the
onus of receiving the brickbats for the traffic woes lies purely with the Delhi Traffic Police.  To put the whole blame on Traffic Police will
mean closing eyes to the reality.  Delhi has acquired the dimensions of being billed as one of the fastest expanding megapolises of the world. 
The deterioration of traffic scenario is nothing but the reflection of an over-stretched civic infrastructure.  Therefore, the need of the hour is to
see how best can the limited civic infrastructure of this over-populated and over-crowded city be managed. 
59.1     In the opinion of the Committee there is an urgent need for setting up a Unified Traffic Management Authority, which should be
responsible for the overall planning, and management of traffic scenario of Delhi.  This body may consist of noted experts with proven
expertise on traffic management, senior officers of traffic police, representatives of local civic agencies and public representatives.  It should
be vested with adequate powers.  Besides, there should be clear division of work.  Ideally, there should be three functional units one each for
enforcement, education and training and engineering.  Enforcement should remain with Delhi Traffic Police whereas for education and
training and engineering two separate units should be created.  These three functional units should be placed within the jurisdiction of the
proposed Authority.  In such a set up it should be the sole responsibility of the Authority to plan, implement and monitor a comprehensive
traffic management plan based on present as well future requirements.
60.       Enforcement plays a crucial role in managing traffic on the roads.  For a city like Delhi, which has the largest number of vehicles in
the country, enforcement of traffic rules is an extremely difficult task.  Manual enforcement cannot match with the scale at which
enforcement is required to be done.  Therefore, state-of-the art technological devices should be pressed into service for the rescue of traffic
enforcement personnel.  The growing number of traffic violations in Delhi makes it abundantly clear that enforcement through primitive
methods has lost its efficacy.  The use of latest gadgets and technology alone can reduce the number of violations.  This can be illustrated
with the successful use of red light camera by Delhi Traffic Police which helped to reduce the rate of traffic violations quite considerably.  In
the light of the experience of successful use of red light camera by Delhi Traffic Police, it is imperative that such need-based technological
devices are made available so as to restore sanity on the roads.  The Committee, therefore, recommends that Delhi Traffic Police should be
provided with adequate number of state-of-the art technological devices such as Red Light Speed Cameras, CCTV Cameras, Radar Guns,
Alco meters and Interceptors so as to enable it to discipline the behaviour of errant motorists.  Top most priority should be accorded to the
acquisition of these devices by finding suitable financial ways and means.

67.       North Eastern Council came into being as an inter-state structural entity to provide a functional framework for ensuring all-round and
balanced development of the North Eastern region.  The concept of having a regional organization for the seven sister States was aimed at
providing an institutional platform to discuss the matters of common interest in the field of economic and social planning, transport &
communication and power and flood control.  It was also aimed at infusing a sense of cooperation and coordination among all the States of
the region so as to enable them to be part of national mainstream.  However, a look at the over-all performance of the NEC ever since it
started functioning, reveals that it has not been able to live up to the high expectations.  It is now in the 29
th year of its existence but its
performance record is not as impressive as it should have been.  Its contribution lies in adding a mere 247.50 MW of power to the installed
capacity, 1887 kms. of power transmission lines and 4800 kms. of roads.  This apart, it has built two major bridges across the Brahmaputra at
Tejpur and Jogigopa.  In addition to this, it has established 5 regional institutes for development of manpower and improvement of health
care    facilities.  The   achievements    of  the Council have belied the high expectations with
which it was set up.  Almost all projects of NEC have suffered from cost and time over-runs.  For instance, 31 road projects have experienced
cost over-run to the tune of Rs.840.84 crore and are still going on.  Similarly, Ranganadi Hydro-Electricity Project (405 MW) is already
delayed by 7 years and has experienced cost over-run to the tune of Rs.1133.31 crore.  Besides, Ranganadi Transmission Lines project will be
late by 9 years with a cost over-run of Rs.113.09 crore.  Given the way NEC has performed, it raises doubts about its efficacy and puts a
question mark on the continuation of its very existence in its present form.
68.       The Governors and Chief Ministers of all the seven States of North Eastern region are members of North Eastern Council (NEC).  The
Committee is of the considered view that it should be made more broad based so as to enable it to have a much wider perspective vis-à-vis
over-all development of the member States.  The involvement of elected representatives of the people in the decision making process of the
Council can help it generate a sense of belonging among the people of the region towards it.  For, it is very significant to take the people
along in the developmental process.  People, in general, should not feel alienated and isolated from the process, which in effect, is aimed at
their welfare.  The association of their representatives with a body which exists for the all-round development of their States, will definitely
lead to the identification of a common man’s aspirations with the actions of that body.  Therefore, the Committee is of the opinion that at least
one MP from each North Eastern State should be member of NEC so that he can present the people’s viewpoint before the Council vis-à-vis
its various projects/plans. 
69.       As mentioned in the preceding paragraphs, almost all projects of NEC have had cost and time over-runs.  Although these projects are
sponsored by NEC, their execution remains with the concerned States.  Since most of these projects have an inter-State character which
means the involvement of various agencies, the progress of work would always remain tardy especially in the absence of a single monitoring
agency.  Therefore, in the view of the Committee, the execution of the projects/plans sponsored by NEC should also be included in its
mandate.  The recommended widening of the mandate of the NEC, the Committee hopes, will help it reduce cost and time over-runs to a large
extent.
70.       Construction of inter-State roads is part of NEC’s mandate.  Ever since its inception, it has added 4800 kms. of road length to the
network of roads in the North Eastern Region.  Despite that, the condition of roads in almost all States of the region remains pathetic. 
Apparently, the quality of construction and maintenance of roads is very poor due to which roads have a very short span of life.  In the
opinion of the Committee, road projects of NEC should be executed by an agency with a creditable performance record having strict quality
controls.  Border Roads Organisation (BRO) is one such agency which could deliver the desired results in this regard.  Therefore, the
Committee recommends that NEC projects involving construction and maintenance of roads should be entrusted with the Border Roads
Organisation (BRO).

proposed restructuring of NEC should be done expeditiously keeping in view present day ground realities and requirements.

SITUATION IN JAMMU & KASHMIR
*          The situation in Jammu & Kashmir has taken a positive turnaround with the declaration of the latest peace move of Government of
India. The most heartening development has been the positive response of the people of the State to the peace initiative which has
sprung from their strong yearning for restoration of peace and normalcy. The people of Jammu & Kashmir need to be commended for
their strong desire for return of peace and tranquility but at the same time some foreign militant organizations deserve to be
condemned for their unabated efforts to derail the peace initiative. Hopefully, people at large will disapprove of such anti-peace efforts
of foreign mercenaries operating in the State.
(Para 28)
*          Given the delicate nature of the situation in the State, the Committee urges upon the security forces to exercise utmost restraint while
dealing with scourge of militancy. This, however, should not be construed as a reflection on the security forces. The Committee is
fully aware of the adverse circumstances in which security personnel are discharging their duty.
(Para 30)
*          Keeping in view the important role being played by VDCs in tackling militancy in Jammu & Kashmir, the Committee feels that more
emphasis is needed on their organizational set up. It is noteworthy that steps have been taken to increase the number of VDCs and
SPOs. Yet, it feels that lot more is desired to be done to strengthen the network of VDCs. Their strengthening becomes all the more
imperative in view of the fact that more and more VDC volunteers are being targeted by the militants and the villages where such
Committees have been set up continue to be struck at will by the militants. In this backdrop, the Committee is of the opinion that at
least two or three fully trained security force personnel should be attached with each VDC. The attachment of security personnel will
add a touch of professionalism to their functioning. Moreover, the presence of security personnel amongst VDC volunteers will help
establish a better contact between   VDCs and security forces participating in counter-insurgency operations. This apart, the attached
security personnel could impart training to VDC members in using sophisticated weapons and communication devices like wireless
sets etc. The Committee, therefore, urges upon both the Union Government as well as the State Government to consider its opinion
and find ways and means to make it happen as soon as possible.
(Para 32)
*          The Committee is of the view that VDCs/SPOs are not getting adequate financial compensation for their services in counter-
insurgency operations. While strengthening the VDCs/SPOs, the Government should also keep in mind the need for providing
adequate financial package to them so as to encourage the participation of even greater number of people.        
(Para 32.1)
*          In view of the position explained by the Ministry of Home Affairs as given in the preceding paragraphs, the Committee recommends
that consistent and persistent efforts should continue to be made by  State as well as Union Government for safe and early return of
Kashmiri Pandits to their homes in Kashmir Valley so that the nefarious designs of some foreign elements to engineer ethnic cleansing
in the valley are defeated.  Till such time the return of these migrants to their homes in the valley is materialized, the Government
should ensure adequate financial assistance to them for their sustenance.

being a palpable decrease in the atmosphere of fear. There are reports that extortion is going on in such a manner that it is very hard to

TRAFFIC MANAGEMENT IN DELHI
*          The worsening traffic scenario of the capital has attracted the attention of the Committee primarily because of its negligence by the
authorities concerned.  There is no gainsaying the fact that it is not too easy a problem to be dealt with.  A city with close to four
million vehicles with an almost static road network definitely poses huge challenges for its traffic managers.  However, it is interesting
to note that in spite of having so many challenges to grapple with, there is no unified authority to take them on.  There seem to be as
many authorities as there are challenges.  This is probably the reason why things are pulling in different directions.  There is no unity
of command nor there is any coordination among the agencies responsible for the management of overall traffic scenario.  At a time
when situation warrants concerted, well-coordinated and unified measures to be taken, the system lacks the required mechanism to see
it through.  Though a slew of other agencies such as DDA, MCD, NDMC, STA etc. are also responsible for the traffic management of
the city either this way or the other, the onus of receiving the brickbats for the traffic woes lies purely with the Delhi Traffic Police. 
To put the whole blame on Traffic Police will mean closing eyes to the reality.  Delhi has acquired the dimensions of being billed as
one of the fastest expanding megapolises of the world.  The deterioration of traffic scenario is nothing but the reflection of an over-
stretched civic infrastructure.  Therefore, the need of the hour is to see how best can the limited civic infrastructure of this over-
populated and over-crowded city be managed. 
(Para 59)
*          In the opinion of the Committee there is an urgent need for setting up a Unified Traffic Management Authority, which should be
responsible for the overall planning, and management of traffic scenario of Delhi.  This body may consist of noted experts with proven

73rd Report of Committee on Home affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/73rdreport.htm[30-06-2016 17:40:49]
expertise on traffic management, senior officers of traffic police, representatives of local civic agencies and public representatives.  It
should be vested with adequate powers.  Besides, there should be clear division of work.  Ideally, there should be three functional
units one each for enforcement, education and training and engineering.  Enforcement should remain with Delhi Traffic Police
whereas for education and training and engineering two separate units should be created.  These three functional units should be
placed within the jurisdiction of the proposed Authority.  In such a set up it should be the sole responsibility of the Authority to plan,
implement and monitor a comprehensive traffic management plan based on present as well future requirements.
(Para 59.1)
*          Enforcement plays a crucial role in managing traffic on the roads.  For a city like Delhi, which has the largest number of vehicles in
the country, enforcement of traffic rules is an extremely difficult task.  Manual enforcement cannot match with the scale at which
enforcement is required to be done.  Therefore, state-of-the art technological devices should be pressed into service for the rescue of
traffic enforcement personnel.  The growing number of traffic violations in Delhi makes it abundantly clear that enforcement through
primitive methods has lost its efficacy.  The use of latest gadgets and technology alone can reduce the number of violations.  This can
be illustrated with the successful use of red light camera by Delhi Traffic Police which helped to reduce the rate of traffic violations
quite considerably.  In the light of the experience of successful use of red light camera by Delhi Traffic Police, it is imperative that
such need-based technological devices are made available so as to restore sanity on the roads.  The Committee, therefore, recommends
that Delhi Traffic Police should be provided with adequate number of state-of-the art technological devices such as Red Light Speed
Cameras, CCTV Cameras, Radar Guns, Alco meters and Interceptors so as to enable it to discipline the behaviour of errant motorists. 
Top most priority should be accorded to the acquisition of these devices by finding suitable financial ways and means.
(Para 60)
*          Apart from enforcement, another key area which requires attention is the need for educating the public about traffic rules and
regulations. The Delhi Traffic Police should lay special emphasis on creating awareness among people for behaving in a disciplined
manner on the roads as it was in their own interest. The awareness campaign in this regard should be a constant exercise instead of
being an occasional one. Traffic personnel, while enforcing traffic rules, should also focus on educating the public about the dangers
of not adhering to traffic rules.  
(Para 60.1)
*          Another important aspect that requires immediate attention is the training of traffic personnel.  Any number of technological devices
will fail to produce desired results unless the persons responsible for traffic management have requisite professional skills to make use
of them.  Traffic personnel should have a flair for their professional duties.  Adequate orientation towards the calling of their job is
essential.  Enforcement of traffic rules should not be treated as a mere policing job.
(Para 61)

recommends that a comprehensive curriculum covering all the aspects of traffic management should be prepared for being imparted to
the personnel of Delhi Traffic Police.  Besides, the Committee is also of the view the recruitment policy needs a re-look.  Recruitment
of personnel on conventional pattern may no longer be in tune with present day requirements.  People with adequate professional
background and a natural flair for the job, alone should be recruited.

(Para 63)

the Border Roads Organisation (BRO).

PERSONNEL POLICIES FOLLOWED IN CISF
*          The grievances of the direct recruit Group ‘A’ officers of CISF have been engaging the attention of the Committee for quite some time
now, as part of its larger study of personnel policies being followed in respect of the Central Para-military Forces.  In its successive
reports on Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs, the Committee has made detailed observations/recommendations on
the subject.  In the sitting of the Committee held on 28 March, 2001, members raised issues relating to the grievances of the direct
recruit Group ‘A’ CISF Officers, when the Secretary (Personnel) was present in connection with the examination of the Demands for
Grants (2001-2002) of his Ministry.  In the next sitting of the Committee held on 29 March 2001, members raised the matter again. 
After some discussion, the Committee decided to have a separate sitting to discuss at length all the aspects relating to the grievances of
the said officers.  It was also decided that Home Secretary and Secretary (Personnel) would be requested to be present in that sitting to
respond to the questions/queries of the members.  Accordingly, the Committee in its sitting held on 10 April 2001 discussed the
subject in detail.  The Home Secretary and Secretary (Personnel) made brief presentations.  They also replied to some of the
points/queries raised in the meeting.  As a large number of points/suggestions were raised/made, the Home Secretary was requested to
submit at an early date written responses of the Government thereto for further consideration of the Committee.  Keeping in view the
deliberations held in the Committee, it has decided to present a separate report in the matter to Parliament.
