CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
1.1 INTRODUCTION
1.1.1 The North Eastern Region (NER) collectively represents eight states of the North East viz.
Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura. The region
possesses enormous potential and opportunities and also inherent challenges. The Department of
Development of North Eastern Region was set up in September, 2001 under the Ministry of Home Affairs
and subsequently upgraded to a full-fledged Ministry in May 2004 underscoring its complete commitment
to ensure development with equity for the NER to unleash the potential of its human and natural
resources. The Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region implements schemes and programmes
through State Governments and some Central Ministries for which Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources
(NLCPR) and funds for Special Packages are allocated. The Ministry aims to give focused attention to
address the special needs of the region. It mainly coordinates with various Ministries/Departments
primarily concerned with development activities in the NER whereas, the respective Ministries/Departments
remain responsible in implementation of programmes in their respective fields. North Eastern Council
(NEC) is a statutory Regional Planning Body which provides financial assistance for a variety of projects
including infrastructure projects like construction of roads, improving air connectivity etc. through State
Governments.
1.2 MANDATE AND RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE MINISTRY
1.2.1 As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, the Second Schedule (Rule
3), subjects allotted to the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (Uttar Poorvi Kshetra Vikas
Mantralaya) are as follows:
(a) Matters relating to the planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes and
projects of North Eastern Region including those in the sectors of Power, Irrigation, Roads
and Communications;
(b) Hill Area Development Programme in North Eastern Region;
(c) Non-lapsable Fund for the North Eastern Region;
(d) North Eastern Council;
(e) North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi);
(f) North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited (NERAMAC);
(g) The Sikkim Mining Corporation Limited;

2
(h) North Eastern Handloom and Handicrafts Development Corporation (NEHHDC), Shillong;
(i) Road works financed in whole or in part by the Central Government in the North Eastern
Region; and
(j) Planning of Road and Inland Waterways Transport in the North Eastern Region.
1.2.2 While the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) would coordinate with
various Ministries/Departments concerned with development and welfare activities in the North Eastern
Region, respective Ministries/Departments would be responsible in respect of subjects earmarked for
them. The projects under different schemes of the Ministry are planned and executed through the State
Governments. According to the Annual Report (2016-17) of the Ministry, its main mandate is to coordinate
with various Ministries/ Departments primarily concerned with development and welfare activities in the
region. It also monitors their expenditure under 10% GBS, as surrendered amounts are transferred to the
NLCPR (pool) which is notionally maintained by Ministry of Finance. However, it is a subject matter of
M/DoNER as per Allocation of Business rules. This exercise is being undertaken on annual basis and the
allocation and actual expenditure figures given by the respective Ministries/Departments are collected,
compiled and forwarded annually to Ministry of Finance for vetting.
1.3 TRANSFER OF SCHEMES TO LINE MINISTRIES
1.3.1 The Committee, taking note of the transferring of the scheme of organic farming from the
Ministry of DoNER to the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare and Capacity Building & Technical
Assistance scheme to the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), desired to know
the reasons for such transfer of jurisdiction and whether such arrangement has encroached upon the
original mandate of the Ministry of DoNER and the residual role of the Ministry of DoNER in respect
of these schemes. In response to that, the Ministry of DoNER replied that in compliance with the
decision taken in the Committee of Secretaries meeting held on 16th September, 2016 on convergence
of Central Skill Development Schemes/Programmes/Institutes under the Umbrella of M/o Skill Development
and Entrepreneurship (MSDE) and on proposal by the MSDE, Capacity Building component of Capacity
Building and Technical Assistance (CB&TA) scheme of Ministry of DoNER has been transferred to
Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE) so as to avoid increase in administrative
expenses, duplication of effort, non-uniformity of standardization, etc. It was also stated that the Ministry
of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare implements the organic farming scheme because of its domain
expertise.
1.3.2 It was further added by the Ministry of DoNER that it is closely coordinating with the Ministry
of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare through various committees constituted for the scheme not only in
respect of the scheme of organic farming but also in respect of other schemes for which joint workshops
have been organized in the North Eas?t. The Ministry of DoNER is a part of the Committee of MSDE
that reviews and approves the proposals made under the scheme. It has been mentioned in the Allocation
of Business Rules that "While the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region would coordinate
with various Ministries/Departments primarily concerned with development and welfare activities in

3
North Eastern Region, respective Ministries/Departments would be responsible in respect of subjects
allocated to them". As such, transfer of the schemes has not encroached upon the original mandate of
the Ministry of DoNER.
1.3.3 The Annual Report of the Ministry stipulates further that the coordinating role of the Ministry
emanated from monitoring development projects for socio-economic development of the region. The
Ministry coordinates with various Central Ministries/Departments in respect of the development and
welfare schemes/programmes implemented by them in the North Eastern Region. Besides, calling the
progress of expenditure out of the earmarked outlay for NER, a set of Ministries/Departments has been
assigned to each senior officer of the Ministry for regular and continuous interactions with respective
Ministries/Departments. In addition to the routine interactions, the Ministry also convenes sectoral review
meetings with key Ministries such as Railways, Road Transport & Highways, Civil Aviation, Power,
Environment, Forest & Climate Change, Tourism etc. for effective implementation of the schemes and
time-bound completion of major infrastructure projects. The Ministry of DoNER is represented in Inter-
Ministerial Groups of major Ministries such as MoRTH, Railways, MSME, Home, etc. and is also
consulted at policy formulation and project implementation stage through EFC, Cabinet note circulation.
To ensure co-ordination among the Central Ministries, Ministry of DoNER has constituted several Inter-
Ministerial Committees (IMC). Currently there are four IMCs functioning in Road sector, Civil Aviation
sector, Livelihood Programmes and Tourism sector. Meetings of the IMCs are held regularly.
1.3.4 The Committee understands that the Ministry of DoNER has the primary responsibility for
accelerating the overall economic development in NER and, in order to boost fast track
development, a greater synergy is required amongst the Ministry of DoNER and implementing
agencies of schemes. In this regard, the Committee reiterates its view expressed in its 173rd
report on strengthening of the working of the Ministry of DoNER for effective implementation
of Policies, Programmes, schemes and projects meant for NER that the Ministry of DoNER can
play a larger and effective role in shaping the regional developmental priorities and implementing
the regional or State-specific programmes and projects. Active involvement of the Ministry of
DoNER is a precondition for successful implementation of the policies, programmes, schemes,
and projects meant for the NE region. The Committee strongly reiterates its recommendation
that there is urgent need to review and redefine the role of the Ministry of DONER in coordinating
and liaising with the line Ministries. There should be a clear cut defined relationship between the
NITI Aayog, the Ministry of DoNER, and the NEC for dovetailing and synergizing developmental
plans, projects, and schemes and a mechanism for such coordination should be evolved.
1.3.5 The allocation of funds for the North Eastern Region is being done annually on lump sum basis
by the non-exempted Central Ministries/Departments at the BE/RE stage under Major Heads 2552 (for
revenue expenditure), 4552 (for capital expenditure) and 6552 (for loan expenditure) expended under
their respective functional schemes. In the Budget 2017-18 several changes were introduced, for example
the distinction between Plan and Non-Plan has been done away with, revenue/capital classification has
been adopted and Railway Budget merged with Union Budget. Besides, earmarking for North Eastern

4
Region has been reflected through Statement 11 of the Union Budget, Expenditure V olume. As per the
Statement 11, a total of ` 32,180.08 crore was allocated by 55 Central Ministries/Departments for NE
Region during 2016-17 (RE), including the Ministry of DoNER, but excluding the allocation by the
Ministry of Railways of ` 5050 crore for the region under the Rail Budget 2016-17. Similarly, for the
current Financial Year 2017-18, the total budgetary allocation for the NE Region by non-exempted
Ministries/Departments is ` 43,244.64 crore.

2.2 ADV ANCE/PERSPECTIVE PLANNING IN THE POST FIVE YEAR PLAN PHASE
2.2.1 When the 12th Five Year Plan ends in 2016-17 it will mark the end of traditional Five Year
development planning. This Ministry has identified key focus areas in medium and long term horizon.
A 3-year Action document for 2017-18 to 2019-20 aligned to the predictability of financial resources
during the 14th Finance Commission Award period has been prepared. The elements of an Inter-
Ministerial Coordinated Strategy for the period 2017-18 to 2023-24 have been identified. Connectivity
being the key focus area, the Ministry is in its coordinating role-tracking initiatives in road, rail and
telecom sector in close coordination with the implementing Ministries/Departments and also identifying
and bridging gaps in social and physical infrastructure through the DoNER/NEC schemes. It was
informed by the Ministry that efforts would be made to accelerate sustainable growth of the region
particularly keeping in view its comparative advantage in organic farming and potential of processed
products; handlooms and handicrafts, tourism, bamboo, medicinal plants as well as imparting newer
employable skill for promoting livelihood creation & entrepreneurship and scope for economic linkages
offered by the Act East Policy initiatives.
2.2.2 The Committee understands that the Five Year development planning approach is coming
to an end with the conclusion of the last financial year of the 12th Five Year Plan i.e. 2016-17.
The Committee takes note of the key focus areas identified by the Ministry to give impetus
during the medium and the long term period. The Committee desires that the Ministry of DoNER
should expedite the process of identification of the projects meant for being implemented in the
key focus areas so that the development process in those areas will be accelerated. The Committee
further desires that specific monitoring mechanism must be put in place to ensure timely
implementation of economic infrastructure projects to be undertaken during medium and long
term period. The Committee is of the considered view that when strict adherence to the timeline
and the stipulated cost are ensured, while implementing projects/schemes by the executing
agencies under the three year action plan, perceptible change can be achieved.
2.2.3 The Committee also takes note of the Inter Ministerial Coordinated Strategy chalked out
for the period 2017-18 to 2023-24. The Committee is of the considered view that the Ministry of
DoNER must be assigned the pivotal position in the whole arrangement so as to steer economic
development in NER. As indicated in the mandate of the Ministry, coordinating role of the
Ministry in tracking initiatives in the key focus areas like connectivity (i.e. rail, road, air) and,
telecom sector and the monitoring mechanism of implementing projects/schemes of the Ministry
as well as NEC, must be given top priority. The Ministry should also take maximum advantage
of the scope for economic linkages offered by the Act East Policy Initiative.

2.11.1 North East Road Sector Development Scheme (NERSDS) was formulated by the Ministry of
DoNER to take up such interstate roads as identified by NEC in consultation with the State Governments
of the Region that were critical for connectivity but were not maintained by the State Governments. The
objective of the scheme is to develop road transport Sector in the NER by extending financial support
for construction/up-gradation of strategic roads and corridors (ODR, MDR, Single lane and double lane
roads etc.). NEC as a Regional Planning body has supported the Infrastructure Development of the
Region since its inception. As a part of its initiative towards the development of the Region during FY

State Governments of the Region, which are orphan in nature, as these roads are not maintained by either

Committee which would be responsible for screening, appraisal, approval and monitoring of project
proposals. Sanctioned projects would be implemented/executed by Central Ministry/Agency or State Govt

3.1.1 During the course of the meeting held on 1st March 2017, some Members raised the issue about
the factors impeding optimum utilisation of funds earmarked for development projects/schemes implemented
by NEC and under NLCPR and steps taken to remove those obstacles while implementing the projects
undertaken during last five years. The Committee wanted to know the problems being faced by the
Ministry of DoNER while coordinating with state governments and line ministries and monitoring of
implementation of projects and steps taken to address the delays and ineffectiveness on the part of the
implementing departments or agencies. Similar issues were raised in the Committee's meeting held on
31st January 2017 wherein the Committee had expressed its concern over the relevance of the Ministry
of DoNER in building capacity in the North East observing that not enough capacity has been built in
the NER despite DoNER being in existence for 15 years.
3.1.2 The Committee further took note of the fact that the small value projects under special development
package for three councils are under implementation for periods beyond 12 months and 24 months. The
Secretary, the Ministry of DoNER in the Committee's meeting held on 31st January 2016 submitted
before the Committee that there are projects which are pending since the last 7-8 years. He informed
the Committee that the Minister for DoNER after taking a review of the old schemes suggested to the
State Governments to close five year old schemes at the earliest. The Secretary also apprised the
Committee of the status of projects under NLCPR. The Secretary, NEC explained in the Committee's
meeting held on 1st March 2017 that subsequent to the meeting of DRSC on Home Affairs on 31
st January
2017, a meeting with all the eight States was held on 23rd February, 2017 and, in consultation with the
State Governments it was decided to close 59 projects which were beyond five years on an "as is where
is" basis. It was concluded that out of 285 projects, 154 are actually not fit to be called projects as they
are actually seminars, support festivals and are just one-time events and the remaining 131 projects are
physically completed but awaiting either a completion certificate or there is the lack of State Matching
Share. The Committee was informed that 59 projects are beyond five years, 140 projects are 3-5 years,
174 projects are 1-3 years old and 89 projects are less than one year old. When the committee enquired
why it takes 3-5 years to complete a project with average value of about ` 8-15 crore, it was explained
by NEC that in North East Region there is a very limited working season. Under the existing mechanism,
40 per cent is released as first instalment and then after a series of inspections at various levels by
consultants and regional level monitoring by officers another 40 per cent is released followed by a
mandatory inspection before the release of remaining 20 per cent. The system, of course, takes time.
On the issue, the Secretary, Ministry of Doner further added as under:-
"We had a similar problem in the NLCPR Schemes. We found that on an average the State
Government would take between one and one and a half years to release the funds...our schemes
were skewed in a manner that we were incentivising new projects. The moment the project is
43

44
sanctioned, we would give 40 per cent as first instalment. So, the money goes to the State
Government and it is lying there. Next year again, we have money. So, we give money for new
projects. So, this situation was there for over a number of years...... We will give only an amount
of `10 lakh as a token sanction. You do the tendering work; issue the work order. The moment
the work order is issued, we will give 40 per cent. So, that has now put the pressure back on the
State Governments to finish the ongoing schemes. Now, NEC has decided to put 80 per cent on
old schemes. So, there is a disincentive for the States to go in for new schemes. Otherwise, if I
were in State Government, it would make a lot of sense for me to have new schemes because on
sanction of the project, I get 40 per cent advance and that 40 per cent lingers around. .. look
at NLCPR. They have the same problem. But, for the last two years, we have been working on
that and we have almost stopped and curtailed new projects and the focus is on ongoing projects."
3.1.3 The Committee is of the considered view that the Ministry must focus on bigger projects

number, bigger in envelope size and there should be tighter timeframe for implementation and
