INTRODUCfiON 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Home Affairs having been authorised by the 
Committee , present this Thirty-First Report of the Committee on the Action Taken by 
Government on the recommendations/observations of the Committee, contained in its 
Seventeenth Report on the Demands for Grants (1995-96) of the Ministry of Law, Justice 
and Company Affairs. 
2. The Seventeenth Report was presented to the Rajya Sabha on the 8th May , 1995. 
The Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs furnished replies indicating Action Taken 
on the recommendations contained in that Report, on the 25th October, 1995, 12th January 
and 1st March , 1996. The recommendations of the Committee were contained in Paras 12 to 
15, 20 to 22, 25 to 33, 35, 39 and 40 of the Report. 
3. The Committee in its meeting held on the 16th January, 1996 decided to take up 
consideration of the Action Taken Notes furnished by the Ministry of Law , Justice and 
Company Affairs (Departments of Legal Affairs; Justice; Company Affairs and Legislative 
Department) and seek clarifications thereon from the Secretaries of the respective 
Departments of the Ministry. Accordingly, the Committee considered the Action Taken 
Notes and sought clarifications from the Secretaries/representatives in respect of Action 
Taken replies on the recommendations relating to their respective Departments in its 
meeting held on the 7th March , 1996. 
4. The recommendations/observations contained in this Report are based on the Action 
Taken Notes furnished by the Ministry and the presentations made by the Secretaries/ 
representatives of the Ministry before the Committee on the 7th March, 1996. 
5. The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on the 29th 
March, 1996.

Recommendation: 
From the information furnished by the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs

ENFORCEMENT OF LEGAL SERVICES AUTHORITIES ACT 
Recommendation: 
7. On a query raised regarding the present status of the enforcement of the Legal 
Services Authorities Act, the Committee has been apprised that the Act is yet to come into 
force. The Committee has also been apprised that the Draft Model Rules for the State Legal 
Services Authorities Act drafted by the CILAS have been forwarded to the State Legal Aid 
and Advice Boards and the Legislative Department for finalisation/vetting. The Act will be 
brought into force only after the rules have been framed and approved by the competent 
authorities. The Committee feels that the Legal Services Authorities Act, if brought into 
force, will assume special significance as it is intended to provide statutory basis to the 
CILAS. The Committee therefore, strongly urges upon the Government to expedite its 
enforcement. 
8. In its Action Taken Note, the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs has 
stated:-
"The proposal to enforce the Act is under process in consultation with the Legislative 
Department." 
9. The Additional Secretary, Department of Legal Affairs, in his deposition clarified as 
under:-
"The Legal Service Authorities Act has now been brought into force on 
9th November, 1995 except Chapter 3. Chapter 3 deals with the constitution of the 
Legal Services Authority for the States. The States are being requested to frame the 
rules as early as possible." 
10. From the explanation of the representative of the Ministry, the Committee notes 
that notwithstanding the Legal Services Authorities Act assuming special significance, the 
Ministry has not made serious efforts to enforce it in letter and spirit. As of now, the Act 
has been brought into force partially with the Chapter 3 yet remaining to be enforced subject 
to framing or fules by the States. The Committee, therefore, stresses the need for early 
enforcement of the Act and calls upon the Ministry to take necessary steps in this direction.

DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE - RATIONALE BEIDND ITS BEING UNDER ADMINIS­
TRATIVE CONTROL OF MINISTRY OF HOME AFFAIRS 
Recommendation: 
29. The Committee notes that the Department of Justice is a part of the Ministry of 
Law, Justice and Company Affairs and Demands for Grants of the Department are also 
accounted for on behalf of the Ministry. However, its administrative control lies with the 
Ministry of Home Affairs. On the question of rationale behind keeping the Department 
under the administrative control of the Ministry of Home Affairs, the Department of Justice 
has explained that the Department was brought under the administrative control of the 
Ministry of Home Affairs in the year 1971 following a Presidential Order. Initially, seven 
items of work being handled by the Ministry of Home Affairs were transferred and the staff 
was also provided by the Ministry to the Department of Justice. Subsequently, ten items of 
work were transferred to the Department. At present, the Home Secretary and one 
Additional Secretary in the Home Ministry function concurrently as Secretary and 
Additional Secretary in the Department of Justice. 
30. All work of the Department of Justice relating to the administration, cash and 
accounts and other general housekeeping functions like stationery, furniture, typewriters, 
accommodation, receipt and issue etc. continue to be handled by the Ministry of Home 
Affairs. The parliamentary work of the Department is handled by the Parliament Section of 
the Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs. 
31. The Department of Justice has also stated that the inter-linkage of the Department 
with the Ministry of Home Affairs ensures close coordination between the Ministry and the 
Department in the matter of appointment of Judges of High Courts and the Supreme Court. 
It also ensures better coordination for the administration of criminal justice with the States. 
Any change in the existing arrangements with a view to making the Department of Justice 
entirely self-contained would entail the creation of large number of additional posts involving 
financial implications. It would also upset the close coordination that exists now between the 
Department of Justice and the Ministry of Home Affairs. 
32. The Committee is not convinced with the arguments advanced by the Ministry. The 
Committee feels that the existing set-up of the Department wherein some of the its functions 
are being handled by the Ministry of Law and some others by the Ministry of Home Affairs, 
gives an anomalous picture which needs to be set right. In this context, the Committee finds 
that the apprehension of the Department of Justice is misplaced as the idea of the 
Committee is not to create an independent Department but to place it under the Ministry of 
Law for all purposes, thereby dispensing with the administrative control of the Ministry of 
Home Affairs. This can be done by transferring back the items of work relating to the 
Ministry of Home Affairs to the Ministry of Law which· are presently being handled by the 
Department of Justice. 

7 
33. The Action Taken Note furnished by the Department of Justice stated:-
"ln this context it may again be pointed out that a considerable part of the work of the 
Department of Justice deals with appointments, transfers of Judges for which close 
cOordination with \'arious agencies under the Ministry of Home Affairs is very essential. 
Further, the Judicial administration of the UTs is also with the Department of Justice 
which requires close coordination with the various Divisions of the Ministry of Home 
Affairs. Further in most of the States, the administration of Justice Department is- with 
the Home Department of the State Government!UT administration. In view of this, it 
is felt that there are some advantages in the present stipulation that the Department of 
Justice would be under the administrative control of Ministry of Home Affairs. In view 
of this position, the Government has re-considered the question of the administrative 
control of the Department of Justice and has with the approval of Minister of State for 
Law, Justice and Company Affairs decided to continue with the present arrangement." 
34. The Committee is not convinced with the reply given by the Ministry as it appears to 
be a mere repetition of what the Ministry had stated earlier at the stage of consider~tion of 
the issue by the Committee. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its earlier recommendation 
that the Department of Justice should appropriately be placed under the Ministry of Law for 
aiJ purposes, thereby dispensing with the administrative control of the Ministry of Home 
Affairs. Alternatively, in case it is deemed convenient from coordination point of view, the 
Department of Justice can be merged with the Ministry of Home Affairs for all purposes.

DEPARTMENT OF COMPANY AFFAIRS- RATIONALE BEHIND ITS BEING PART OF 
LAW MINISTRY 
Recommendation: 
39. On a pointed query as to why t he Department of Company Affairs is part of the 
Ministry of Law, Jus tice and Company Affa irs and why it should not be the part of the 
Ministry of Industry, the .Ministry stated as under: -
"The Department of Company Affairs is primari ly concerned with orderly growth and 
functioning of Joint Stock Companies. It seeks to prov ide appropriate basis for their_ 
working without unnecessary restr ictions and irritants. The Department also seeb to 
provide adequate protection to shareholders, creditors and members of public through 
adm inistration of Company Law. Through, operation of the Monopolies and Restrictive 
Trade Practices, it is responsible for ensuring competition in the domestic market by 
control of monopolies restrictive and unfair trade practices. The Monopolies and 
Restrictive Trade Practi ces Commission, an organ of the Department, enquires into and 
takes appropriate actions in respect of these trade practices. The work of the 
Department , therefore. mainly relates to administration of Corporate Laws." 
40. The Committee is of the view that the matters handled by the Ministry of Industry 
and the Department of Company Affairs are related to each other. Moreso, both the 
Ministry of Industry and the Department of Company Affairs seek to achieve similar 
objectives. lt will, therefore, be appropriate if the Department of Company Affairs is made 
a part of the Ministry of Industry. The Committee, therefore, recommends that this issue be 
examined by the Government. . 
41. In its Action Taken Note, the Department of Company Affairs has stated the 
following position: -
"The Department of Company Affairs has at times been part of Ministry of Finance, 
Ministry of Industry and Ministry of Law, Justice & Company Affairs. At present, 
though it is under the Ministry of Law, Justice & Company Affairs, the Secretary, 
Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion , is also the Secretary, Department of 
Company Affairs and thus close coordination is achieved between the two Depart­
ment s." 
42. The Committee notes that the repl y of the Department is too general and doe~ not 
satisfactorily explain the position as to why the Department of Company Affairs should 
continue to be part of the Ministry of Law, Ju~tice & Company Affair s. The Committee is of 
the view that the function s of the Department of Company Affair s, as enumerated above , 
can be discharged in a better way, if it forms part of the Ministry of Industry. The
