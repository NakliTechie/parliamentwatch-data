OVERVIEW 
 
1.1 Introduction 
 
1.1.1 North Eastern Region (NER) comprises eight States namely Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, 
Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura, with a total area of almost 8 per 
cent of India. The region shares International Borders with several neighbouring countries - 
Nepal, Bhutan, China, Myanmar and Bangladesh. As per the India State of Forest Report, 2021, 
the forest resources in the North Eastern States account for 23.75% of the total forest cover of the 
country. The region is characterized by its rich bio -diversity and heavy precipitation and has 
immense potential for development.  As per Census 2011, the population of the region is about 
4.5 crores, constituting 3.78% of the total population of India. The rural population makes up 
81.64% of the total population of the region, while the urban population accounts for 18.36%.  
 
1.1.2 The Department of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER) was set up in 2001 to 
give focused attention to address the special needs of the NER. It was constituted, first as a 
Department in 2001 and then converted into a full -fledged Ministry in 2004 for better 
coordination between State Governments of NER and Central Mi nistries/Departments of the 
Government of India. It is the only Ministry with territorial jurisdiction. The vision of the 
Ministry is to accelerate the pace of socio -economic development of the NER so that it may 
enjoy growth parity with the rest of the co untry. For this, it coordinates with various 
Ministries/Departments primarily concerned with development activities in NER. However, 
respective Ministries/Departments remain responsible for implementing programmes in their 
respective fields.  
 
1.2 Mandates and Responsibilities of the Ministry of DoNER 
 
1.2.1 The Ministry is mandated to deal with the following matters: - 
 
(i) Matters relating to planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes and 
projects of NER including those in the sectors of Power, Irrigation, Roads and 
Communications.

1.4 Coordination with Central Ministries and Departments  
 
1.4.1 Coordinating role of the Ministry of DoNER emanates from monitoring development 
projects for socio -economic development of the NER. The Ministry coordinates with various 
Central Ministries/De partments regarding the development and welfare schemes/programmes 
they implement in the North Eastern Region. Besides, calling the progress of expenditure out of 
the earmarked outlay for NER, a set of Ministries/Departments has been assigned to senior 
officers of the Ministry for regular and continuous interactions with respective 
Ministries/Departments. Moreover, all non -exempted Ministries/ Departments have appointed 
nodal officers in their respective Ministries to coordinate over 10% GBS with the Minist ry of 
DoNER and to apprise them about the major initiatives undertaken in NER.  
 
1.4.2 In addition to the routine interactions, the Ministry also convenes sectoral review 
meetings with key Ministries such as Railways, Road Transport & Highways, Civil Aviat ion, 
Power, Environment, Forest & Climate Change, Tourism etc. for effective implementation of 
schemes and time -bound completion of major infrastructure projects in NER. Ministry of 
DoNER is represented in Inter -Ministerial Groups of major Ministries such as the Ministries of 
Road Transport and Highways ( MoRTH), Railways, Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises  
(MSME), Home, etc. and is also consulted at policy formation and project implementation stage 
through EFC/ SFC and Cabinet notes.  
 
1.4.3 The Ministry has five Inter-Ministerial Committees (IMC) viz. IMC on Roads, IMC on 
Health & Nutrition, IMC on Medicinal Aromatic Plants, IMC on HADP and IMC on STINER 
wherein issues of importance in these domains are discussed with the Stakeholder 
Ministries/Departments.

1.5 Gross Budgetary Support (GBS): Earmarking of Union Budget by Central 
Ministries/ Departments for North Eastern Region 
 
1.5.1 In October 1996, the Central Government announced ‘New initiatives for the North 
Eastern Region (NER)’, one of these measures announced was the policy decision to earmark at 
least 10% of Plan Budgets of the Central Ministries/Departments for the development of NE 
States. Accordingly, it was decided that Central Ministries/Departments, unless specifically 
exempted, will set apart 10% of their Plan Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the NER to 
ensure a quantum jump in budgetary resource flows to the region and to fill the backlog and gaps 
in basic minimum service and infrastructure. Subsequently, allocation of funds under 10% GBS 
for the NER introduced in 2014 -15 (Statement-23), is being done annually on a lump sum basis 
by the non -exempted Central Ministries/Departments at the BE/RE stage under Major Heads 
2552 (for revenue expenditure), 4552 (for capital expen diture) and 6552 (for loan expenditure) 
under their respective functional schemes.  
 
1.5.2 In the Budget 2017 -18, several changes were introduced, e.g. dispensing with the 
distinction between Plan and Non -Plan expenditure, adoption of revenue/capital classification, 
the merger of the Railway Budget with Union Budget, discontinuation of block gra nts, etc. In 
addition, the earmarking for NER was reflected through Statement -11 of the Expenditure Profile 
of the Union Budget, as against Statement -23. To mitigate dislocation, the Ministry of DoNER 
has a special dispensation until year 2022 to carry out their schemes under Budget Head 3601.  
 
 1.5.3 As per Statement -11, a total of ₹ 68440.26 crores was allocated by 54 non -exempted 
Central Ministries/Departments for NE Region during 2021-2022 (RE), including the Ministry of 
DoNER. Similarly, for the Financial Year 2022-23, the total budgetary allocation for the NER by 
54 non-exempted Ministries/Departments is ₹ 76040.07 crores.

1.7 Central Pool of Resources for NER 
 
1.7.1 In 1997-98, when it was found that the actual expenditure of Ministries/Departments for 
NER was less than the mandatory ceiling of 10 per cent, the Government decided to create a 
"Central Resource Pool" into which the unutilized portion of the 10 per cent earmarked GBS 
would accrue at the end of the financial year.  Since 1998-99, the annual surrendered amounts 
out of the earmarked outlays of all the non -exempted Central Ministries/Departments for NER 
have been accounted as accruals to this Pool and the annual expenditure of the Ministry under 
the NLCPR scheme is bei ng accounted as withdrawal from the Pool. However, this is a notional 
pool, maintained on a proforma basis by the Ministry of Finance. The Ministry of DoNER 
monitors Ministry -wise expenditure/ surrendered amounts for the purpose of ascertaining the 
accrual to the NLCPR pool in consultation with the Ministry of Finance. 
 
INSTITUTIONS UNDER MINISTRY OF DoNER 
 
1.8 North Eastern Council (NEC) 
 
1.8.1 The North Eastern Council (NEC) was constituted as a statutory advisory body by an Act 
of Parliament under the NE C Act, 1971 (84 of 1971) marking the beginning of a new chapter of 
concerted and planned endeavour for rapid socio -economic development of the North Easter n 
Region (NER). Over the last 48 years, the Council has played a significant role in ushering 
development into the Region at a time when most States were in their formative stages. Road and 
air connectivity were supported, foundations for important regional Institutions were established, 
and various socio -economic infrastructures were created across the length and breadth of the 
Region, making a difference in the lives of the people and thereby earning goodwill in the local 
community. Presently, NEC is looking to continue its efforts, especially in terms of gap-filling in 
areas which have escaped the attention of various development agencies and creating 
convergence at different levels. NEC is also looking at building capacities, sharing best practices 
and introducing cutting-edge technologies among the NE States. 
 
1.9 North East Cane and Bamboo Development Council (NECBDC)  
 
1.9.1 Cane and Bamboo Technology Centre (CBTC) now re -named as North East Cane and 
Bamboo Development Council (NECBDC) is an ISO 9001:20 08 certified organization and was 
established in the year 2000 as part of Cane and Bamboo Technological Upgradation and

Networking Programme of United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), where the

1.10 North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Limited  
(NEHHDC) 
 
1.10.1 The North Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd 
(NEHHDC) was established in the year 1977 at Shillong, Meghalaya with the mandate of 
promotion and development of Handlooms and Handicrafts Sector in the North Eastern Region. 
NEHHDC h as been providing marketing support through procurement of Handicrafts and 
Handlooms products directly from the Artisans, Weavers and Self -Help Groups at clusters and 
selling the same through its Sales Emporia located at Shillong, Guwahati, Kolkata, New De lhi 
and Gujarat and through Sales Promotion Offices located at Chennai and Bangalore and also 
through the conduct of various exhibitions and marketing events all over the country. 
 
1.11 North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited  
(NERAMAC)  
 
1.11.1 North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation (NERAMAC) Limited was 
incorporated in 1982 as a Central Public Sector Enterprise under the administrative control of the 
Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER). NERAMAC has been envisaged 
and is functioning as not just a marketing agency for farmers and entrepreneurs, but also 
undertakes agri-horti projects, skill curriculum supplies inputs, GI produ ces promotion activities, 
agri-events and assists in setting up of horticulture value chain (food processing) etc. to approach 
the aspect of Agri -Horti development holistically. A revival package for NERAMAC was 
approved in August 2021. The Corporation is on track to achieve a turnover target of ₹ 64 crores 
set at the time of approval.

1.12 North Eastern Region Community Resource Management Society (NERCRMS) 
 
1.12.1 The North Eastern Region Community Resource Management Society (NERCRMS) is a 
society under the North Eastern Council (NEC), Ministry of DoNER, Government of India for 
the implementation of development projects in the North Eastern Region of India. The Soc iety 
successfully implemented 3 (three) phases of the NERCORMP project  i.e. (a) NERCORMP -I 
from 1999 to 2008 jointly funded by Govt. of India and IFAD’ (b) NERCORMP -II from 2010 to 
2017 jointly funded by Govt. of India and IFAD and (c) NERCORMP -III from 2 014 to 2021 
with funding from Govt. of India only.  
 
1.12.2 The NERCORMP-I & II was implemented in 6 districts in States i.e. Karbi Anglong and  
Dima Hasao districts of Assam; Ukhrul and Senapati districts of Manipur;  West Garo Hills and 
West Khasi Hills districts of Meghalaya. The NERCORMP -III was implemented in Changlang 

7 
 
and Tirap (including Logding) districts of Arunachal Pradesh, Chandel and Churachandpur 
districts of Manipur. The society developed community -based institutions for implementation of 
NERCORMP-I, II & III.  During three phases of NERCORMP , the society formed 2889 Natural 
Resource Management Groups (NaRM -Gs), 8403 Self Help Groups (SHGs), 290 NaRM -G 
Cluster Associations, 300 SHG Federations, 4 Community -Based Micro Credit Institutions and 
50 Farmer Producer Groups. A total 1,18,843 Household in 2532 villages benefited from 
NERCORMP I, II & III with a total cost to Govt. of India ₹ 731.28 crores (Total investment 
including funds from other agencies, support from financial institutions and com munity 
contribution was ₹ 970.94 crores).  
 
1.12.3 The NERCRMS continue implementing development interventions beyond NERCORMP 
covering all 8 (eight) States of NER of India with sanction/allotment of additional 
projects/schemes of North Eastern Council beyond 2021. 
 
1.13 North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi) 
 
1.13.1 North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd (NEDFi) is a public limited 
company established in 1995 under the Companies Act 1956. It is notified as a Public Financial 
Institution under Section 4A of the said Act and is registered as a Non -Banking Financial 
Company (NBFC) in 2002 with RBI. The core business of the Corporation comprises lending 
activities (fund -based) and fee -based activities. The lending activities are u ndertaken through  
project finance schemes and microfinance schemes. The Corporation also provides fee -based 
Advisory & Consultancy Services to the Central and State Governments. 
 
1.13.2 NEDFi has been playing an important role in facilitating the setting u p of new industrial 
and service sector projects in the North Eastern States. During 1st January to 31 st December, 
2022, NEDFi extended financial assistance to 3263 projects with total sanctions & disbursements 
of ₹  1035.34 crores and ₹  725.34 crores respectively, facilitating the capital formation of ₹  
2119.64 crores. Various sectors financed were hotels & tourism, healthcare, education & training 
institute, food processing, handlooms & handicrafts, microfinance etc.  
 
1.14 Science & Technology Intervention North Eastern Region (STINER) 
 
1.14.1 Ministry of DoNER with the close collaboration of the office of the Principal Scientific 
Adviser (PSA) to the Government of India initiated STINER ( Science & Technology 
Interventions in the North East Region ) in 2017 -18 as a mechanism for synergy between 
technology and users. STINER supports appropriate technology dissemination of field -tested 
technologies from S&T institutions to the farmers /artisans of NER which helps augment 
economic development/livelihood in the NER. The STINER Project was officially transferred to 
North Eastern Council, Shillong from Ministry along with a budget Provision of ₹ 40 crores in 
June, 2016 for sanctioning funds to implement the project by CSIR -NEIST, Jorhat. STINER has 
been proposed to be implemented in Hub & Spoke mode, where STINER-hub is started at CSIR-

3.1 Schemes of North East Council  
 
3.1.1 The North Eastern Council, which was constituted as a statutory advisory body by an Act 
of Parliament under the North Eastern Council Act, 1971 (Act No. 84 of 1971) , works under the 
administrative control of the Ministry of DoNER. It provides financial assistance for various 
projects, including infrastructure projects like the construction of roads, improving air 
connectivity, etc., through State Governments.   
 
3.1.2 The "Schemes of the NEC" is a Central Sector Scheme with a 100% Grant from the 
Government of India. The projects under the scheme are implemented in focused areas through 
designated agencies of the States/Central Government with a stipulation that in the case of State 
agencies, the funds will be transferred to implementing agencies.  
 
3.1.3 The Committee was informed that the Union Cabinet, in its meeting held in January 
2020, approved earmarking of 30 per cent of the North E astern Council's (NEC's) allocation for 
new projects under the existing "Schemes of North Eastern Council" for focused development of 
deprived areas; deprived/neglected sections of society and emerging priority sectors in the North 
Eastern States. The bala nce allocation shall be bifurcated into the existing two components viz. 
State component-60 per cent and Central component-40 per cent.

3.2 Central Pool of Resources for North East and Sikkim /North East Special 
Infrastructure Development Scheme (NESIDS) 
 
3.2.1 The Scheme of ‘Central Pool of Resources for North East and Sikkim’ is conventionally 
also called the Non Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) Scheme. NLCPR Scheme 
aims to fill the gap in the infrastructure sector of the North Eastern Region by providing financial 
assistance to the projects prioritized by the Central and State Government. The Ministry gets 
annual budgetary allocation for funding projects under NLCPR Scheme. Funding of projects 
under the NLCPR scheme is on a 90:10 sharing pattern betwe en Central and State Governments.

3.7.1 PM-DevINE was announced in the Union Budget 2022 -23 as a new Scheme to address

development gaps as per the NER District Sustainable D evelopment Goal (SDG) Index 2021 -22 
prepared by NITI Aayog, UNDP and MDoNER.  PM-DevINE was announced to address these

25 
 
15th Finance Commission period). It is in addition to the quantum of resources available for the 
development of the NER. It will not be a substitute for existing Central and State Schemes.

(i) Fund infrastructure convergently, in the spirit of PM GatiShakti;  
(ii) Support social development projects based on the felt needs of the NER;  
(iii) Enable livelihood activities for youth and women;  
(iv) Fill the development gaps in various sectors.

infrastructure, support industries, and social development projects and create livelihood activities 
for youth and women, thus leading to  income and employment generation . It will provide

also provide an end–to-end development solution instead of isolated projects.

4.1 Agriculture and Allied Sectors  
 
4.1.1 The Committee was informed by the Ministry that the Department of Agriculture and 
Farmers welfare ( DA&FW) is organized into 28 Divisions for coordination with State-level 
agencies and implementation of schemes in their respective fields. Further, one Public Sector 
Undertakings (National Seeds Corporation), nine autonomous bodies including Small Farmers’ 
Agri Business Consortium, National Horticulture Board, Natio nal Centre for Cold Chain 
Development etc., ten national level cooperative organizations and two authorities are 
functioning under the administrative control of the Department. Most of the central sector’s 
schemes related to Agriculture are implemented by DA&FW including Agriculture Infrastructure 
Fund, Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North Eastern Region (MOVCDNER) etc.       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               
4.1.2 The major Schemes implemented by DA&FW to develop agriculture and farmers’ 
welfare in NER include:  
 
a) Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi  
The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM Kisan) is a central sector scheme under 
which financial support o f ₹ 6,000 per year in three equal instalments is transferred directly into 
the bank accounts of eligible farmers under Direct Benefit Transfer. The scheme became 
operational in December 2018. Under the scheme, State Government and UT administration 
identify the farmer families which are eligible for financial support as per the scheme guidelines.  
 
b) Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana 
The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) provides insurance coverage and 
financial support to the farmers in the event of fa ilure of any of the notified crops due to natural 
calamities, pests & diseases. The scheme also encourages farmers to adopt innovative and 
modern agricultural practices and stabilizes the income of farmers to ensure their continuance in 
farming activity. T he scheme is implemented by a multiagency framework by a few selected 
insurance companies under the supervision of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare 
(MoA&FW), Govt of India. 
 
c) Agriculture Infrastructure Fund 
The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund ( AIF) is a central sector scheme to facilitate the 
financing of agricultural infrastructure at the post -harvest stage i.e., farm gates and aggregation 
points.  The scheme was launched in 2020 and has a total outlay of ₹ 1 Lac crore. The scheme 
provides Interest subvention of 3% p.a., limited to ₹ 2 crores per project in one location, though 
the loan amount can be higher. The scheme also has a cap on lending rate, so that benefit of 
interest subsidy reaches the beneficiary and services to farmers remain affordable. 
 
d) Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North – Eastern Region 
The Mission Organic Value Chain Development for North Eas tern Region 
(MOVCDNER) is a sub mission under National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA). It 
is a central sector scheme and aims at the development of certified organic production in a value 

31 
 
chain mode to link growers with consumers and to support the development of the entire value 
chain starting from inputs, seeds, and certification, to the creation of facilities for collection, 
aggregation, processing marketing and brand building initiative. 
 
e) Modified Interest Subvention Scheme 
The Government implements a modified interest subvention scheme to ensure that 
nominal interest rates are charged to farmers for short -term crop loans for agriculture and allied 
sectors. Under the scheme for FY 2021 -22, the lending rate to farmers and the rate of interest 
subvention has been kept at 7% and 2% respectively.  
 
f) Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana - Remunerative Approaches for Agriculture and 
Allied Sector Rejuvenation 
The scheme was originally initiated as RKVY in 2007 to ensure the holistic development 
of agriculture and allied sectors by allowing States to choose their own agriculture and allied 
sector activities as per the State Agriculture Plan. It is a central sponsored scheme with a funding 
pattern ratio of 60:40 between the Centre and States and 90:10 for North Eastern States and 
Himalayan States. RKVY -RAFTAAR aims to make farming a remunerative economic activity 
through the strength ening of farmer’s efforts, promoting Agri -business entrepreneurship, focus 
on pre & post-harvest infrastructure.  
 
g) Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture 
Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture (MIDH) is a Centrally Sponsored 
Scheme for the holistic growth of the horticulture sector covering fruits, vegetables, root & tuber 
crops, mushrooms, spices, flowers, aromatic plants, coconut, cashew, cocoa, and bamboo. Under 
MIDH, the Government of India (GOI) contributes 60% of the total outl ay for developmental 
programmes in all the States except States in North East and the Himalayas, 40% share is 
contributed by State Governments.  
 
4.1.3 The Committee, in its meeting held on 27th February, 2023, discussed about various 
indigenous crop varieties in the NER. The Committee discussed about Boka Saul; an indigenous 
variety of rice produced exclusively in lower regions of Assam. It was discussed that this rice is 
not cooked conventional ly, as it can be soaked in warm or cold water, and after around 30 -40 
minutes, it is ready for consumption. It does not need any kind of fuel to cook. The Committee 
believes that the potential of this variety of rice can be tapped as it is not only nutriti ous but can 
be used as an alternate source of food in emergencies like flood, earthquake and disaster 
management. It could also be used for army and armed forces personnel posted in high altitude 
or remote areas.  
 
4.1.4 Further, the abundance of Bamboo in  NER and the viability of bamboo for commercial 
purpose was also discussed in the meeting of the Committee. Bamboo is not covered in the 
category of a tree after the amendment made to Indian Forest Act, 1927, and there is no 
prohibition on growing or cutti ng bamboo even outside the forest areas. This makes it easier to 
use Bamboo commercially.

journey and experience of tourists more comfortable and pleasurable so that more people 
want to visit these festivals and other attractions in th e North Eastern States. This implies 
that a seamless transportation infrastructure for tourists is required. There is also a need 
to develop a structure that allows tourists to experience this rich culture while also 

52 
 
preserving the autonomy of these tribal  areas. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry of DoNER, in collaboration with the Ministry of Tourism, focus on developing a 
mechanism to foster healthy and holistic growth in the tourism sector, thereby boosting the 
NER economy. The Committee would a lso like to be apprised about the details of 80 
infrastructure projects worth ₹ 2598.15 crores relating to water supply, power and 
connectivity which may support the tourism sector in NER. 
 (Para 4.4.2) 
 
TRANSPORT AND CONNECTIVITY 
Roadways 
The Committee believes that completing road projects on time is critical to the 
economic development of NER. The Committee takes note of various road projects carried 
out in NER under various schemes. The Committee notes with satisfaction that all the 12 
road projects sanctioned under North Eastern States Road Inves tment Programme 
(NESRIP) have been completed. The Committee notes that physical progress on many road 
projects (>₹ 300 crores) expected to be completed between 2023 and 2024 is slow. Similarly, 
the Committee also notes that, few projects under the Schemes of NEC are yet to be 
completed. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry, in collaboration with 
the NEC and MoRTH, make efforts to identify and resolve bottlenecks that are delaying 
projects so that the benefits intended for the North East can be extended as soon as 
possible. 
 
 (Para 4.5.4) 
Railways 
The Committee takes note of the progress made towards completing various railway 
projects in the NER. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of DONER in 
coordination with the M/o Railways ensure that all ongoing projects are completed within 
the time frame specified to provide NER residents with railway c onnectivity. The 
Committee would also like to know the status of the currently sanctioned project to connect 
all State capitals in the North Eastern Region, as well as the status of all other projects 
undertaken by the Northeast Frontier Railway (NFR).  
 (Para 4.6.5) 
 The Committee also notes that the delay in completing the Agartala -Akhaura rail 
link project is due to issues with the contractual agency in Bangladesh and that the Indian 
side will be completed by June 2023. The Committee recommends that all efforts be made 
to complete the Indian side work by the projected date. Furthermore, the Committee 
recommends that the matter may be taken up with the Ministry of External Affairs for 
further discussion at a higher level, so that project work gets underway  on the Bangladesh 
side. 
 (Para 4.6.6) 
Waterways 

53 
 
The Committee notes the work carried out by the Ministry for the development of 
National Waterways in NER. It may be stated that North Eastern region has a large 
potential for regional connection through wat erways and this may be optimally reaped by 
the Ministry of DoNER and the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways. 
 (Para 4.7.2) 
Civil Aviation  
The Committee notes the efforts of the Ministry of DoNER and the Ministry of Civil 
Aviation in constructing an d operationalizing a number of new airports in NER. North 
Eastern Region connectivity is a critical attribute for the region's overall development. The 
Committee also suggests that a similar impetus may be given and efforts made to 
operationalize the remai ning airports. This will help to promote tourism and last -mile 
connectivity in NER. The Committee would like to be updated on the status of the UDAN 
scheme's implementation in NER, as well as the frequency of flight operations and aircraft 
types used at these newly identified/operational airports. 
 (Para 4.8.6) 
Kaladan Multi Modal Transit Transport (KMMTT) 
 
The Committee appreciates the efforts made towards the completion of the 
waterways component of the Kaladan Multi Modal Transit Transport (KMMTT), a 
strategic connectivity project to connect the eastern ports of India with Myanmar, and all 
the works that have been done for the operationalization of the port. The Committee notes 
and hopes that since a new Project Executing Agency has been appointed for exe cution of 
the road component of KMMTT, similar commitment may be shown to realise the 
completion of road component of the project in a time bound manner.   
 
 (Para 4.9.2)
