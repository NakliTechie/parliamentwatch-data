REPORT
 
The Ministry of Home Affairs has been entrusted broadly with the following functions:
 
            (a)        To deal with all matters relating to internal security;
(b)        To deal with all matters relating to Centre-State relations and Inter-State relations;
(c )       Implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating to Official Language and the provisions of the
Official Language Act, 1963;
(d)        To discharge certain basic functions like notification of assumption of office by the President and the Vice-
President; notification of appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers; notification of appointment,
resignation and removal of Governors and Lt. Governors, appointment of Lt. Governors, Chief Commissioners
and Administrative Officers in Union Territories;
(e)        To deal with matters like citizenship and naturalisation, census of population, National Anthem, National Flag,
etc.; and
(f)        To deal with all constitutional provisions with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters
relating to the State of Jammu and Kashmir excluding those with which the Ministry of External Affairs is
concerned.
2.         To discharge the above functions, the Ministry has been divided into five Departments:
(i)         Department of Internal Security – to deal with Police, Law and Order and rehabilitation;
(ii)        Department of States – to deal with Centre-State relations, inter-State relations, Union Territories and Freedom
Fighters’ Pension;
(iii)       Department of Official Language – to deal with the implementation of the provisions of the Constitution relating
to Official Language and provisions of the Official Language Act, 1963;
(iv)       Department of Home – to deal with notification of assumption of office by the President and Vice-President,
notification of appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers, etc.; and
(v)        Department  of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs – to deal with all constitutional provisions with respect to the State of
Jammu and Kashmir and all other matters relating to the State of Jammu and Kashmir excluding those with which
the Ministry of External Affairs is concerned.
3.         On behalf of the Ministry, ten Demands have been presented to Parliament.
4.         In Vol. I of the Demands for Grants, there are five Demands namely, (I) Demand No. 45 – Ministry of Home Affairs; (ii)
Demand No 46 – Cabinet; (iii) Demand No. 47 – Police; (iv) Demand No. 48 – Other Expenditure of the Ministry of Home
Affairs; and (v) Demand No. 49 – Transfers to Union Territory Governments.
5.         Vol. II of the Demands for Grants relates to Union Territories (without Lagislature), namely, Demand No. 99 – Andaman
and Nicobar Islands; Demand No. 100 – Chandigarh; Demand No. 101 – Dadra and Nagar Haveli; Demand No. 102 – Daman and
Diu and Demand No. 103 – Lakshadweep. The entire Budget of the Union Territories  (without Legislature) on the Plan as well as
Non-Plan side is reflected in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs.

12.       Demand Nos. 99, 100, 101, 102 and 103 which are given in Volume-II of the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of
Home Affairs, relate to Union Territories (without Legislature), namely Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh, Dadra &
Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu and Lakshadweep, respectively. The entire budget of the Union Territories (without Legislature) on
the Plan as well as Non-Plan side is reflected in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry. Though the budget of these Union
Territories forms part of the Ministry of Home Affairs, being the nodal Ministry, it, however, does not dilute the responsibilities
of the Ministries of the Central Government from scrutinising and sanctioning the schemes under their purview. The factual
position regarding budgetary procedure in this regard is that the entire budget requirement for Plan and Non-Plan, revised
estimates for current year and Non-Plan budget estimates for the ensuing year is discussed by the Ministry of Finance in the pre-
budget meetings with the representatives of the concerned Union Territories and Ministry of Home Affairs. An overall ceiling is
fixed in respect of each Union Territory . Annual Plan allocations for the ensuing year are decided by the Planning Commission.
The Union Territory Administrations, prepare budgetary   break-up showing budget provisions according to their respective
budgetary heads and also showing the allocation against Ministries/Departments of the Central Government administratively
concerned. This budgetary break-up is included in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs.

13.       The Committee began consideration of the Demands for Grants of the Ministry in its meeting   held on 23 March 2000. 
The Home Secretary, while making a presentation before the Committee vis-à-vis Demands of the Ministry, stated that the total
budget for the year 2000-2001 was estimated at Rs.13,326.98 crores with a very meagre plan    component of Rs.1,702.29 crores
and a much larger non-plan component of Rs.11,624.69 crores.  The Home Secretary informed the Committee that the largest
chunk of the budget of Ministry of Home Affairs went to the Police at it was the mainstay of the Ministry’s functioning.  The next
largest share was that of the seven Union Territories which were directly administered by the Ministry.
14.       The Home Secretary, while comparing the figures of the last five years’ (1996-2001) budget estimates in respect of each
grant of the Ministry, stated that Grant No.45 – Ministry of Home Affairs registered a quantum jump of Rs.615.50 crores at B.E.
level of 2000-2001 mainly on account of the forthcoming census.  Regarding Grant No.46 – Cabinet, the Ministry had neither any
role nor any control (an amount of  Rs.145 crores has been projected in BE 2000-2001).  In respect of Grant No.47 – Police, the
Home Secretary stated that it pertained to the most substantial functional area of the Ministry as it covered the Central Para
Military Forces and other Central Police Organisations.  He submitted that more funds would be required under that head during
the next five to seven years.  In so far as Demand No.48 – Other   Expenditure of    Ministry  of  Home Affairs was concerned, he
stated that it had stayed nearly at the R.E. level of the outgoing financial year.  Moving on to the Demand No.49 – Transfer to
Union Territories, the Home Secretary stated that Union Territories (with legislatures) were allocated Rs.728.96 crores in B.E.
1999-2000 out of which Rs.483.96 crores were under Plan and Rs.245 crores under Non-Plan.  The allocation was increased to
Rs.828.99 crores with Rs.516.49 crores for Plan and Rs.312.50 crores for Non-Plan at the R.E. stage.  He submitted that Delhi
and Pondicherry might feel starved  in the financial year 2000-2001, especially with regard to their plan allocation which was
pegged at Rs.483.96 crores same as was the B.E. 1999-2000.  As for Union Territories (without legislatures) Grants Nos.99-103,
Rs.2,069.16 crores were allocated in B.E. 1999-2000 with a Plan component of Rs.597.63 crores which was enhanced to
Rs.691.60 crores at R.E. stage.  However, B.E. 2000-2001 for Union Territories (without legislatures) was kept at the level of
B.E. 1999-2000.  The Home Secretary submitted that it happened despite strident demands of the Union Territories for a higher
Plan allocation and Ministry’s unreserved support to their demands.  He urged upon the Committee to consider recommending
higher Plan allocations for Union Territories.
15.       The Home Secretary further elaborated that of the total budget of Ministry of Home Affairs, 53% went into salaries; 17%
into other establishment cost; 9% went into grants-in-aid to State Governments; 3% to arms and ammunitions; 4% to machinery,
equipment and motor vehicles; and 6% to works.  He submitted before the Committee that most of the manpower with the
Ministry of Home Affairs consisted of combatants who needed weapons, mobility, communication equipment and training for the
maximisation of their outputs.  They also needed houses to live in and other infrastructure for their operations.   He felt that those
critical aspects were not adequately addressed in the budget of the Ministry of Home Affairs.  He admitted that it was true that at
times the funds allotted to the Ministry for various provisions, procurements as also for upgradation and acquisition of  new
technologies, could not be utilised.    The reasons for non-utilisation of allocated funds were many including cumbersome official
procedures and regulations.  He stated that a time had come when the Ministry would have to act according to a comprehensive
plan spread over the next five years, to upgrade the men, the materials and the skills comprising Ministry’s forces.  It had become
necessary because the threats to the national security had not only increased spatially and in terms of numerical incidents but also
in terms of focus, intensity, virulence and commitment.   The Home Secretary  further  stated   that  the  proportion of mercenaries
and foreigners among the militants, who were equipped with state–of-the-art weaponry and technology,   had increased
substantially during the last couple of years and as a result the complexion of militancy and terrorism in the country had
undergone a drastic change.   The answer to those developments lied in constantly upgrading the equipment and skills available  
with    the   forces   so that they were able to keep a step ahead of their adversaries.  He
conceded that at the moment forces did not possess that supremacy over the militants.   It was primarily due to this that the
militants were able to strike at will at the security forces as well as the soft targets.   He informed the Committee that the Ministry
was working on plans to strengthen and modernise security forces, and very soon  proposals would be brought for Government’s
decision to raise new battalions.  He further informed that as per the initial estimates, 261 new battalions would be required to be
raised to meet the demands of border security and internal security.   He also informed that as per the decision of the Cabinet
Committee on Security, an inter-ministerial Group had been set up to examine the futuristic assessment made by the Ministry. 
Elucidating the financial implications of the futuristic assessment, the Home Secretary stated that an effort had been made to cut
down costs by proposing restructuring of the existing battalions.   The augmentation plan would require Rs.2083 crores as non-
recurring expenditure, Rs.2730 crores as capital cost and Rs.1600 crores as annual recurring cost.  The funds needed to back up
the futuristic assessment would be required over the next five years.  The Home Secretary urged upon the Committee to support
the augmentation plan to strengthen Ministry’s hands.
16.       Thereafter, the Home Secretary informed that BSF, CRPF, ITBP, CISF, NSG and Assam Rifles had since prepared their
modernisation plans for implementation over a period of five  years and were being processed by the Ministry.  The financial
implication of the plan was to the tune of Rs.4025 crores which included Rs.2530 crores for BSF, Rs.546 crores for CRPF and
Rs.218 crores for ITBP, Rs.101 crores for CISF, Rs.84 crores for NSG and Rs.546 crores for Assam Rifles.  The proposals to
strengthen BSF’s Air Wing and Water Wing by acquiring new aircrafts and watercrafts had been accepted in principle and were

17.       Thereafter, the Home Secretary made a brief mention of the important activities of the Ministry during the outgoing year
and also the initiatives reserved for the next financial year.
18.       With regard to the situation prevailing in Jammu and Kashmir, the Home Secretary stated that the modifications in the
Action Plan, chalked out to tackle militancy, had been implemented on ground.  A sum of Rs.272.57 crores had so far been
released to the Government of Jammu & Kashmir on account of security related expenditure.   Rs.25 crores had been given as
advance for raising two India Reserve Battalions, Rs.35.61 crores had been released directly to the Ordnance Factories   for the
supply of arms and ammunitions to the Jammu & Kashmir Police and Rs.2.39 crores had been released to various automobile
companies for the supply of vehicles to Jammu & Kashmir Police.   A sum of Rs.2.25 crores had been given for civic action
programme.  In addition to that, an amount of Rs.100 crores had been released during the outgoing financial year under the Debt
Relief Scheme announced by the Prime Minister in 1996.
19.       In relation to the North-East, he stated that an understanding had been reached with the Bodo Liberation Tigers and as a
result of that there was a mutual cessation of hostilities.  As regards Nagaland, he informed that there was a temporary impasse in

SITUATION IN JAMMU & KASHMIR
28.       The State of Jammu and Kashmir has been reeling under terrorism for over a decade.   Though our security forces have
persistently been making efforts to stamp out militancy from the troubled State, the aid in the form of men and material, being
extended to the misguided youth of the State from across the border has not allowed the spirits of militants to die.  At a time,
when our forces were beginning to gain a perceptible upper hand over the militants, there came a solid boost from across the
border in the form of large scale infiltration in Kargil.  Post-Kargil phase of militancy in the State has been marked by a subtle
upswing in the violent activities of the militants.  There have been numerous attacks on security forces and establishments located
in the State.  While on the one hand, attacks have been masterminded and targeted at the security forces, on the other, several soft
targets have, very meticulously, been identified such as the Sikh community of the State.   The brutal massacre of  thirty-five  
innocent persons belonging to the Sikh community in the Chatti Singhpura village of Anantnag District of Jammu & Kashmir, has
not only  outraged the entire nation but has also evoked worldwide condemnation. The Chairman of the Committee raised this
issue in the beginning of the meeting of the Committee held on 23 March 2000.  He stated that as per newspaper reports, thirty-
five members of the Sikh community were butchered by heavily-armed gunmen  dressed in military-style fatigues who raided this
typical Kashmiri roadside village along the Anantnag-Pahalgam Highway.  This incident which was the latest in the long series of

HOUSING FOR CPMFS
53.       The provision of housing facilities to the personnel of Central Para Military Force has an important   bearing  on  their  
morale.   Unfortunately,  there  is  an acute shortage of dwelling units for these forces.  This is a major cause of worry keeping in
mind the harsh nature of duties these forces  have to discharge.    This is precisely the reason that this issue has been engaging the
attention of this Committee for the last several years.  It has highlighted its concern in this regard in its various Reports urging
upon the Government to pay immediate attention to the woeful availability of residential accommodation to the forces.  The
Committee in its 54
th Report also, had dwelt at length on the issue.  From the papers submitted before the Committee, it came to
the conclusion that the problem had two aspects; one was the scarce availability of financial resources and the other was under-
utilisation of allocated funds for this purpose.  As regards the first aspect, the Committee was given to understand that the
satisfaction level in respect of residential accommodation as laid down for the non-gazetted personnel of the CPMFs   was 14%. 
The major constraint in reaching the targeted figure of 14% was the non-availability of funds.  The total anticipated requirement
of funds for achieving the 14% target was around Rs.1,000 crore at the current price index.  As regards the second   aspect i.e.
under-utilisation of allocated funds, the Committee was given to understand that it happened   because of the logistical problems

VISA REGIME FOR PAKISTANI NATIONALS
87.       The Home Secretary in his presentation before the Committee spoke at length on the existing visa regime for the visits of
Pakistani nationals to India.  He stated   that grant of visa to Pak nationals was governed  by the  provisions of the Indo-Pak Visa
Agreement, 1974 as amended from time to time.  Initially, under this agreement, the different categories of visa that could be
granted to Pak nationals for visiting India included   diplomatic, non-diplomatic, officials, visitor and transit visa.  The first   three
categories of visa were the concern of the Ministry of External Affairs and the latter two, that of Ministry of Home Affairs.  The
main category of visa i.e. visitor visa was meant for Pak nationals desiring to visit their relatives, friends, etc. in India.  It also
covered bona fide Pak businessmen.  Initially, only four places were permitted during each visit and the initial visa grant by the
High Commission of India, Islamabad  was for a period up to three months.  Later, the number of places were increased to eight
and then  to twelve.  Originally, the Indo-Pak Visa regime was based on reciprocity.   However, in order to encourage   people-to-
people contact between the citizens of both the countries, the Government of India had allowed, during the period from 1997 to
1999, many relaxations in the  visa regime for Pak nationals at the behest of the Ministry of External Affairs without demanding
similar facilities from the Pakistan Government for Indian nationals desirous  of visiting Pakistan. These included the following:
i)          Chennai    Airport had been designated as check post for entry and exit of Pakistani nationals in addition   to Delhi and
Mumbai airports.
ii)
                  Reputed Pakistani artists had been allowed to visit India for giving commercial performances.
iii)                One year multi-entry facility with stipulation of maximum  stay of 3 months during each visit   and EPR (Exemption
from Police  Reporting) facility to bona fide Pak businessmen  recommended   by the SAARC Chamber of Commerce of
Pakistan.
iv)                Visa with EPR facility to the youth  up to the age of 12 years, the elderly aged 70 years and above, very well known and
reputed journalists, media personalities, women artists and writers.
v)                  The facility  of one year visa with EPR facility and without restriction on places of visit (except restricted/protected
areas) had been extended to the Judges of Supreme  Court and High Courts of Pakistan, Attorney General, Solicitor
General, Chairperson of the Supreme Court Bar Association, Members of  the National Assembly and the Senate, Leader
and Deputy leader of Provincial   Assemblies as well as Leader and Deputy Leader of Opposition in these Assemblies,
Editors-in-Chief, owners and principal functionaries   of national newspapers, Vice-Chancellors of Universities, and the
like.
The Home Secretary then observed as follows :
“It was hoped that the Government of  Pakistan would reciprocate these gestures by extending similar facilities to the
Indian dignitaries and other Indian nationals desirous of visiting   Pakistan.  This has not happened so far.  The only

Are You suprised ?
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/59th%20Report.htm[30-06-2016 17:34:30]
reciprocal arrangement between the two countries has been the recent entry and exit facility to the nationals of each other
through Wagha Road Check Post while travelling by Delhi-Lahore bus service introduced   in  February 1999.  In the
aftermath  of Kargil, it is strongly felt that the unilateral relaxation in the visa regime extended to Pak nationals should be
withdrawn since these are likely to be misused and could prove to be detrimental to our security interests.   It is also felt
that a system of 100% pre-verification of  Pak nationals desirous of visiting India  coupled with the system of
sponsorship  certificate   with or without liability of Indian sponsor should be introduced.   Such a proposal had been
mooted in 1996-97 but had been deferred at that time.   Again, in  1998, this proposal was revived but did not make much
headway  because the Ministry of External Affairs did not  respond to the proposal.  In the present highly charged security
environment  in the sub-continent, it is strongly recommended that the visa regime regulating the visit of Pak nationals   to
India should be made stringent, restrictive, based on 100% pre-verification and based on the principal of reciprocity.   We
seek the Hon’ble  Committee’s  guidance and advice in the matter before pursuing these proposals  vigorously.”
88.       The Committee has taken note of the unilateral relaxations extended to the Pakistani nationals in order to facilitate
greater people-to-people contact  between the citizens of both India and Pakistan.  It is, however, really unfortunate that
the Government of Pakistan has not  made any reciprocal   gestures  in response to the unilateral   friendly initiatives of 
Government of India.  In this context, it is also pertinent to mention that such relaxations  have often been misused.  This
is precisely  the reason why a large number of Pakistani nationals after having entered  the Indian  territory  mysteriously 
disappear.  These Pakistani nationals are very difficult to be traced because of several biological and racial similarities  
which they share with their Indian counterparts.   As a result of that they freely   indulge   in anti-India  activities    right 
on   the Indian soil.  A large number of  them
are even alleged to be  the trained agents of ISI.
89.       It is in this backdrop that the Committee has come to the conclusion that in the effort  to befriend  a hostile 
neighbour of   ours, we should not put our own security in danger.  Care should be taken to see that in the name of 
people-to-people  exchange, people coming from across  the border  do not become a potent threat  to the security and
integrity of our country.  The Committee is, therefore, of the considered  opinion that the visa regime regulating  the visit
of Pakistani nationals  to India  should be made stringent, restrictive and based on hundred per cent pre-verification and
on the principle of reciprocity.

SALES TAX IN PONDICHERRY
 
166.     A point was raised that the implementation of  uniform floor rate of sales  tax  in Pondicherry shall result in heavy revenue
loss and there should be a mechanism to provide a ‘permanent subsidy’ to compensate Pondicherry for this loss.
167.     The Ministry of Home Affairs in reply to the query stated that it was decided in the State Chief Ministers  and Finance
Ministers Conference held on 16 November 1999 to introduce uniform floor rates of sales tax throughout the country in all the
States and Union Territories.  This constituted a landmark decision in the field of tax reforms as it will ensure a balanced growth
of industry and trade throughout the country.  The Ministry further stated that the Government of Pondicherry raised certain
apprehensions about the implementation of the new policy decision on the ground that the imposition of the uniform floor rates of
sales tax in Pondicherry would result in loss of revenue and employment .  A team of experts constituted by the Ministry of
Finance to look into these apprehensions has, however, reported that the difficulties apprehended by the Government of
Pondicherry are not related to the introduction of uniform floor rates of sales tax and that the uniform rates would not have any
detrimental effect on industry in Pondicherry.  The team has suggested that the problem could be sorted out by, at best, amending
the Pondicherry Sales Tax Law in order to extend concessional   treatment for raw materials used by the industrial units; reducing
the existing central sales tax rates to as low as one per cent for encouraging inter-state trade; and providing subsidies unrelated to
sales tax (viz. subsidies related to power and infrastructure) to new industries.
168.     The Ministry further stated that it is also important to point out that the budget of the Union Territory of Pondicherry is
reflected in the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs and any gap between the revenue and expenditure on the
non-plan side is met by the Ministry of Home Affairs.  Therefore, if there is any loss of revenue on account of introduction of
uniform floor rates of sales tax in Pondicherry, the gap to that extent will be filled by the Central Government.
169.     The Committee notes the factual position and recommends that the new policy decision vis-à-vis uniform floor rate
of sales tax may be implemented in Pondicherry after persuading the  Government of  Pondicherry , keeping in view the
apprehensions and sentiments expressed by the Legislature of the Union Territory .   In this context, the Committee notes
the assurance of the Government that if there is any loss of revenue on account of introduction of uniform floor rates of
sales tax in Pondicherry, the gap to that extend will be filled by the Central Government.

             VISA REGIME FOR PAKISTANI NATIONALS
*          The Committee has taken note of the unilateral relaxations extended to the Pakistani nationals in order to facilitate
greater people-to-people contact  between the citizens of both India and Pakistan.  It is, however, really
unfortunate that the Government of Pakistan has not  made any reciprocal   gestures  in response to the unilateral  
friendly initiatives of  Government of India.  In this context, it is also pertinent to mention that such relaxations 
have often been misused.  This is precisely   the reason why a large number of Pakistani nationals after having
entered  the Indian  territory  mysteriously  disappear.  These Pakistani nationals are very difficult to be traced
because of several biological and racial similarities   which they share with their Indian counterparts.   As a result
of that they freely   indulge in anti-India  activities right  on the Indian soil.  A large number of  them are even
alleged to be  the trained agents of ISI.
(Para  88)
*          It is in this backdrop that the Committee has come to the conclusion that in the effort  to befriend  a hostile 
neighbour of   ours, we should not put our own security in danger.  Care should be taken to see that in the name of 
people-to-people  exchange, people coming from across  the border  do not become a potent threat  to the security
and integrity of our country.  The Committee is, therefore, of the considered  opinion that the visa regime
regulating  the visit of Pakistani nationals  to India  should be made stringent, restrictive and based on hundred
per cent pre-verification and on the principle of reciprocity.

detailed discussion on the Demands for Grants of the related Ministries/Departments in a free, frank and objective
manner.  To achieve that objective, the Committees have been closely interacting with the Secretaries and other
senior officers of the related Ministries/Departments and have also been calling for relevant papers and
documents.  The focus of the Committees has been to consider relevant issues in their right perspective with the aid
of necessary inputs furnished by the Ministries/Departments and help the Ministries/Departments in arriving at
appropriate decisions.  Many a time it has been   clarified   that   these Committees are not fault-finding in nature;
they are not
64
courts of inquisition.  On the contrary the aim of the Committees is to work in close cooperation with the executive
in the discharge of its duties.
(Para 178)
*          However, it has been observed over the years that the related Ministries/Departments and their Secretaries and
other senior officers are not frank and forthcoming with the Committees in sharing information/facts with them. 
Rather the tendency has been to be evasive and not to reply to specific points and queries.  This makes the work of
the Committee difficult.  
(Para 179)
 
·
                     A special reference may be made about the quality of the Action Taken Replies furnished by the Ministry of
Home Affairs on the recommendations/observations of the Committee as contained in its 54 th  Report (1999)  on
the Demands for Grants of the Ministry of Home Affairs.  The Committee is constrained to observe that in
response after response the Ministry has been sketchy, evasive and has sought to bye-pass the main issues
involved.  In respect of certain matters particularly in relation to the grievances of the directly recruited Group
‘A’ Officers of CISF, the Ministry has repeatedly given factually incorrect and misleading information to the
Committee. Here, it may be relevant to point out that in the meeting of the Committee held on 31.3.2000, the Home
Secretary assured that he shall look into this matter and find out  if there had been any misreporting or
intentional  withholding of information.  The Home Secretary then informed the Committee that he shall report to
it the outcome of his enquiries.  It is astonishing  that despite the specific and clear assurance of the Home
Secretary on the floor of the Committee, the Ministry has chosen to send to it the same reply to which the
Committee had taken serious exception.  Then, in a bid  to quickly retrace its steps, the Ministry of Home Affairs
after two days, sent a revised reply and regretted to inconvenience caused.   This speaks volumes of the fact that
the Ministry of Home Affairs  has been taking the Committee very lightly and paying scant regard to its
observations/recommendations.
(Para 180)

towards it.  The matter will be placed before the Committee at its next meeting to consider the matter further in
