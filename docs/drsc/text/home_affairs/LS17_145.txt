Chapter - I 
 
OVERVIEW 
 
1.1.1 The North-Eastern part of India comprise s of the States of Arunachal Pradesh, 
Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Naga land, Sikkim and Tripur a. Flanked by lofty 
hills, the mighty Brahmaputra river slashes a central path between its north and south.  
The western side of the North East is conn ected to the eastern part of the Indian 
subcontinent by a narrow land corridor, sometim es referred to as the Siliguri Neck or 
"Chicken's Neck". The NER of India covers an area of 2.62 lakh s q. km. It accounts for 
7.9% of total geographical area of the c ountry. With a total population of 39 million 
(2001), it accounts for 3.8% of total population of India.  The entire North-East of India 
is marked by diversity in customs, culture s, traditions and languages and a home to 
multifarious social, ethnic and linguistic groups.   
 
1.1.2 Richly endowed with natural resources, India's North Eastern Region is a rainbow 
area; extraordinarily diverse and colourful.  The region is one of the biodiversity hotspots 
in the world, and it has the highest rainfall in the country, with large and small river 
systems nesting the land and is a treasur e house of flora and fauna.  Another unique 
strength of the region in the present era of globalization, and in th e context of India's 
Look East Policy, is its long international bor ders.  Despite having hilly and inhospitable 
terrains, porous borders, highl y inadequate connectivity, limited air services, inadequate 
rail links and weak telecommunication infrastr ucture, the NE Region has the potential to 
become India’s most attractive place and most sought after tourist destination.   
 
1.1.3 According to the Ministry of DoNER, rapid development of the NER is among 
the foremost priorities of the Central Government.  In view of their strategic significance, 
the States of the region have been accorded th e status of Special Category States.  With 
an objective to fast track economic growth of  the region, the Department of North East 
Region was formed in September 2001 which was upgraded to the status of a Ministry in 
2004 to push further socio-economic developmen t of the region. This Ministry, rather 
than an administrative unit, acts as  a facilitator be tween the Central 
Minisries/Departments and the State Govern ments of the North Eastern Region for 
economic development including removal of in frastructural bottlen ecks, provision of 
basic minimum services, creating an environm ent for private investment and to remove 
impediments to lasting peace and security in the region. 
 
1.1.4 The Ministry of Development of Nort h Eastern Region seeks to achieve the 
following goals: 
 
(i) Accelerating the pace of socio-economic development in the  
 North Eastern Region; 
 
(ii) Synergizing the efforts of Central/State Governments/Other

1.1.6 The North Eastern Council (NEC) also im plements various Plan Schemes in the 
region. The NEC is an organization set up under the Ministry which functions as a 
Regional Planning Body for the region.  It was constituted under the NEC Act, 1971 for 
securing the balanced development of the No rth East.  The Minister in charge of the 
Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region is the   ex -officio Chairman of NEC.   
Its members include the Governors and the Chief Ministers of the eight Member States of 
the region and three Members nominated by the President of India.

1.2 NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL PO OL OF RESOURCES FOR NORTH 
EASTERN REGION (NLCPR) 
1.2.1 The North East has essentially b een dependent on central funding for 
development works. All the States in th e NER are Special Category States, whose 
development plans are centrally financed on the basis of 90% Grant and 10% Loan. 
Further, the Special Category States are allowed to use up to 20% of the central 
assistance for Non-plan expenditure. 
1.2.2  As the benefits of economic developm ent have yet to steadily accrue to the 
region, efforts have been ini tiated in this dire ction through various supportive measures. 
In October 1996, the then Prime Minister anno unced "New Initiatives for North Eastern 
 8

Region" and stipulated that at least 10% of the Budget(s) of the Central 
Ministries/Departments will be earmarked for the development of North Eastern States. A 
preliminary exercise undertaken by the Planning Commission revealed that the 
expenditure on the North East by some Uni on Ministries during 1997-98 fell short of the 
stipulated 10% of the Gross Budgetary  Support (GBS) for that year. Planning 
Commission thereafter explored the possibility of creating a Central Pool of Resources 
for the North East out of the unspent amount of stipulated 10% of GBS to support 
infrastructure development projects in the North East.   
1.2.3 Since 1998-99, all non-exempted Ministries/D epartments of Government of India 
are required to earmark at least 10% of th eir GBS for the development of North Eastern 
States for creation of both economic and social  infrastructure such as power, roads and 
bridges, education, health, water supply and sanitation, etc. in the region.  The unutilized 
portion of the 10% Plan allocation made by Mini stries/Departments is credited at the end 
of each financial year in a re serve fund in the Public Acco unt titled ‘Central Resource 
Pool for Development of North Eastern Re gion’ which in administrative parlance is 
known as the Non-Lapsable Cent ral Pool of Resources (NLCPR ).  The funds from this 
Pool are budgeted for financing new infrastruc ture projects/schemes in the region under 
the NLCPR Plan Scheme. 
1.2.4 The broad objective of the NLCPR scheme  is to ensure speedy development of 
infrastructure in the North Eastern Region by increasing the flow of budgetary financing 
for new infrastructure projects/schemes. Both physical and social infrastructure sectors 
are considered for providing support under the Central Pool, with projects in physical 
infrastructure sector receiving priority.  
 
1.2.5 The NLCPR is meant for infrastructure development in almost all the major 
socio-economic development sectors.  For this  purpose, projects are sanctioned for State 
as well as Central sectors.  However, the f unds available under the Central Pool are not 
meant to supplement the normal Plan program mes either of the State Governments or 
Union Ministries/Departments/Agencies.

2.1.10 The Ministry has used the refrain that the main reason for the slow pace of 
utilization of fund is the prolonged monsoon season in the North Eastern Region, which 
severely affects almost all the construction and other economic activities. Besides, the 
 13

Ministry also pleaded that in surgency hampers the utilisatio n of funds.  The expenditure 
normally picks up after the month of Novemb er with a flurry of activities in the 
remaining months of the financial year, i. e., during December-March every year.  The 
Ministry hoped that by the end of financia l year, almost 100% utilisation of allocated 
funds would be achieved. 
 
2.1.11     Human beings have little or no control over the vagaries of weather. 
Likewise, the climatic peculia rities of the North-East are not new and cannot and   
should not be used as a pretext for the sl ow pace of utilization of funds and delayed 
completions of projects in the NER. Theref ore, given the climate conditions of that 
region, it would be a prudent step on the part of administrative Ministry and the 
State Governments, to organize the activities in a rational way to offset the adverse 
impact of the nature. Accordingly, the projects in NER should be implemented 
during the seasons when conditions are favou rable at a grater pace to compensate 
the loss of time during the monsoon season , so that projects are implemented in 
time. In this context, the Committee would li ke to advise the Government to step up 
vigil in monitoring the expenditure as in the flurry of work, there is every likelihood 
of siphoning of funds meant for development.  
 
2.1.12     The Committee is unhappy to note  that only 56.8 % of the allocated fund 
under plan scheme has been utilized upto February end.  The Committee is 
disappointed to note that during the financial year 2008-09, utilization had been low 
in Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC) (6 7%), Advocacy & Publicity (52.8%), 
North Eastern Region Livelihood Projec t (73%) and North Eastern State Road 
Project (Nil). During the financial year upto 28 February, 2010, under utilization of 
funds has been reported in sectors like Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources 
(41.1%), North Eastern Council (62.5%), Advocacy & Publicit y (32.5%),  North 
Eastern Region Livelihood Project (19.2%).   The Committee desires to highlight 
that in the case of projects like NERLP, where the vagaries of weather do not play 
any role, only 19.2% of allocated fund has been utilized. That being the case, the 
Committee is not optimistic that the allo cated funds would be fully utilized by 
March 2010. The Committee observes that  under- utilization of funds by the 
Ministry, makes its case weak before the Planning Commission for higher 
projection for allocation.  The Committee fe els that outlays made should give the 
desired results as the Planning Commiss ion would consider the demand for higher 
allocation only when allocated  funds meant for development  of  NE Region are 
fully and optimally utilized and show the results.

3.5    SARVA SHIKSHA ABHIYAN IN THE REGION 
3.5.1 Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA ) is Government of India' s flagship programme for 
achievement of Universalization of El ementary Education (UEE) in a time bound 
manner, as mandated by eighty-sixth Amendmen t to the Constitution of India, making 
free and compulsory education to the children of 6-14 years age group, a Fundamental 
Right. The programme seeks to open new schools in those habitations which do not have 
schooling facilities and strengthen existing sc hool infrastructure through provision of 
additional teachers, class rooms, toilets, dr inking water, maintenance grant and school 
improvement grants. SSA has a special focus on girl's education and children with special 
needs. SSA also seeks to provide computer education to bridge the digital divide.  
3.5.2 The North-Eastern States ar e required to meet 10 per cen t of the expenditure from 
their resources as their share for SSA. However,  as per report of th e Thirteenth Finance 
Commission, several of these States have not  been able to provide even this amount, 
leading to slowdown in implementation of SSA.  The Ministry explaining about the status 
stated as under:-  
 “The Ministry of Human Resource De velopment had taken up the case for 
assistance to elementary education sector of NE States by the Thirteenth 
Finance Commission during 2010-2015 to help  these States to fill gaps in 
financing elementary education and en able them to provide their State 
share towards implementation of SSA  programme. Presently, State share 
is outstanding against Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Mizoram and 
Sikkim. The matter for realizing necessa ry State share has been taken up 
with these States.”  
3.5.3 The Committee observes  that  despit e the literacy rate in the NE region 
standing at 68.5 per cent, with a female literacy rate at 61.5 per cent, which are 
higher than the country’s average of 64.8 per cent and 53.7 per cent, respectively, 
there are significant variations  in the literacy rates am ong different States, with 
Assam, Arunachal Pradesh and Meghalaya below the national average. Moreover, 
there are concerns about the quality of ed ucation. More importantly, the literacy 
rate has not been translated into higher employability or productivity. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government   should popularize the 
Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan in the region, for equitable outcome of the scheme in all the 
States of the region. 
 
3.5.4 The Committee takes strong exception to  the fact that some of the  North-
Eastern States, namely, Arunachal Prades h, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Sikkim have 
not  provided their 10% share for Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan which has led to 
slowdown in implementation of this fl agship scheme of the Government. The 
Committee recommends that these State Governments should be persuaded to 
 43

provide their share as soon as possible to en able the   implementation of the Scheme 
which is meant to ensure that the fundament al right of the child ren of the country 
becomes a reality.

3.8.1  One of the major assets of the north eastern region is its massive potential of 
hydro-power. Though identified in the early  years after independence, the large 
hydropower potential of the region has remained mostly unexploited so far. Although the 
region has tremendous potential for generating hydroelectric power which is estimated at 
about 60,000 MW, the actual generation is very less.  
  
3.8.2  Regarding progress made to tap the hydr o-power potential available in North East, 
the Ministry has furnished information which is as follows: 
 
“Based on the studies for re-assessment of hydro-electric potential of the 
country, completed by Central Electri city Authority in 1987, assessed 
hydropower potential in the country is about 1,48,701 MW (1,45,320 MW 
for projects with capacity above 25 MW). This includes 62604 MW of 
potential in North Eastern (NE) Region including 4248 MW in the state of 
Sikkim. The assessed potential in NE  Region including Sikkim together 
constitutes about 43% of the total a ssessed hydro power potential in the 
country. Presently, 1.91 % of the assessed potential (for projects above 25 
MW) has already been developed in N. E. region other than Sikkim while 
13.42 % of the assessed potential (for  projects above 25 MW) in Sikkim 
has been developed so far.” 
 
3.8.3 The Ministry further informed that  at present 11 Hydro Electric Projects (above 25 
MW) with an aggregate installed capacity of 1686 MW are in operation in North Eastern 
region and Sikkim.  Besides, 10 H.E. Project s with an aggregate installed capacity of 
4891 MW are under construction both in private a nd public sectors.  In the public sector, 
North Eastern Electric Power Corporat ion (NEEPCO) and National Hydel Power 
Corporation Ltd. (NHPC) are constructing some of the Power Plants.

IMPLEMENTATION OF NER PROJECTS 
 
Human beings have little or no co ntrol over the vagaries of weather. 
Likewise, the climatic peculia rities of the North-East are not new and cannot and   
should not be used as a pretext for the sl ow pace of utilization of funds and delayed 
completions of projects in the NER. Therefo re, given the climate conditions of that 
region, it would be a prudent step on the part of administrative Ministry and the 
State Governments, to organize the activities in a rational way to offset the adverse 
impact of the nature. Accordingly, the projects in NER should be implemented 
during the seasons when conditions are f avourable at a grater pace to compensate 
the loss of time during the monsoon season , so that projects are implemented in 
time. In this context, the Committee would li ke to advise the Government to step up 
vigil in monitoring the expenditure as in th e flurry of work, there is every likelihood 
of siphoning of funds meant for development.  
(Para 2.1.11)

Committee is not optimistic that the allo cated funds would be fully utilized by
