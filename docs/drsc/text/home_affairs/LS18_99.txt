CHAPTER -- I
Introductory
        The management of personnel policies in Government of India, to begin with, was the joint responsibility of the
Ministry of Home Affairs (Services and Establishment Wing) and Finance (Establishment Division) with Union Public
Service Commission playing an important advisory role. The Establishment Officer to the Government of India,
functioning under the Cabinet Secretary had an important role to play in the senior appointments under the Government
of India. All this made for improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified Central direction.
2.     To plug this organisation loophole, the Estimates Committee of the Third Lok Sabha in its 93 rd Report   (April
1966) observed that “the ever expanding role of the government in a welfare state with its natural concomitant of a large
civil service calls for effective personnel control through a single agency”.  The Administrative Reform Commission
(ARC) too urged upon the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel with a full Secretary in charge, to
be placed directly under the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal agency for the personnel functions of the Government.
3.     The Union Government accepted this recommendation and as a consequence led to the reorganization of the
Ministry of Home Affairs as it then stood, resulting eventually in the formation of Ministry of Personnel, Public
Grievances and Pensions as it stands today. This process was initiated on June 26, 1970 when the Department of
Personnel was notified as a Department in the Cabinet Secretariat. The process was taken further on August 1, 1970
with the notification of the items of work to be dealt with by the Department of Personnel. Eventually, on March 15,
1985 a full fledged ‘Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions’ was notified.
4.0   The Ministry is the coordinating agency of the Central Government in personnel matters specially issues
concerning recruitment, promotion and morale of services, training, vigilance and discipline, career development and
service conditions of the senior middle management, grievances and staff welfare as well as the process of responsive
people-oriented modern administration. The Ministry comprises three Departments, namely: -
(i) Department of Personnel and Training.

(ii) Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare.
(iii) Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances

4.1   The Ministry has under its administrative control three attached offices, namely: -
(i) Staff Selection Commission (SSC);
(ii) Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM); and
(iii) Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA).
4.2   The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) was also an attached office under the administrative control of the
Ministry until the amendment in the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, affected on 30 January
2003 thereby delinking the CBI from the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions. The CBI is now under
the administrative control of Cabinet Secretariat.
4.3   The other offices under the administrative control of the Ministry are:-
(i) Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), a constitutional body;
(ii) Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), proposed to be converted into a statutory body;
(iii) Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), a statutory quasi-judicial body; and
(iv) Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB), a body set up by the resolution of Government.
5.     The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister, assisted by a Minister of State. All the three
Departments are functioning under the charge of the Secretary (Personnel) assisted by one Special Secretary, three
Additional Secretaries and three Joint Secretaries and other supporting officers and staff.
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING AND THE VARIOUS AGENCIES UNDER ITS
ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
6.0   The role of the Department of Personnel and Training can be conceptually divided into two parts. In its larger
nodal role, it acts as the formulator of policy and the watchdog of the Government ensuring that certain accepted
standards and norms, as laid down by it, are followed by all Ministries/Departments in the recruitment, regulation of
service conditions and deputation of personnel as well as other related issues. Towards this end, guidelines are issued by
it for the benefit of all Ministries/Departments and it monitors the implementation of these guidelines. It also advises all
organisations of the Central Government on issues of personnel management. At a more immediate level, the
Department has the direct responsibility of being the cadre controlling authority for the Indian Administrative Service
(IAS) and the Central Secretariat Service (CSS). The Department also operates the Central Staffing Scheme under
which suitable officers from all India Services and Group ‘A’ Central Services are selected and then placed in posts at
the level of Deputy Secretary, Director and Joint Secretary on the basis of tenure deputation. The Department also deals
with cases of appointment to posts of Chairmen, Managing Directors, full-time functional Directors/Members of the
Board of Management of Various public sector undertakings/enterprises, corporations, banks and financial institutions.
It also deals with the assignment of Indian experts to various developing countries.
Recruitment Agencies
6.1   The two organisations through which the Department ensures recruitment of Personnel for the Government are the
Union Public Commission (UPSC) and the Staff Selection Commission (SSC). The former is constituted under a
provision of the Constitution and is responsible for conducting examinations for appointment to the higher civil services
and civil posts under the Union Government, including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a mandatory
provision for consulting the Commission on all matters relating to methods of recruitment, principles to be followed in
making promotions and transfers from one service to another and all the disciplinary matters. The SSC is responsible for
making recruitment for Group ‘B’ non-gazetted posts of Assistants, Stenographers, etc.
Training Institutions
6.2   Two major training institutions directly under the administrative control of the Department are the Lal Bahadur
Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the Institute of Secretariat Training the
Management (ISTM), New Delhi. The former is mainly responsible for providing induction training to recruits to the
Indian Administrative Service and other All India Services and Central Services. The Institute of Training and
Management provides induction as well as in-service training to members of the Central Secretariat Service.
Senior Appointments under the Government of India
6.3   All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the approval of the
Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961

are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary to the ACC. These include board level appointments to
Central Public Sector Undertakings and appointments of Joint Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the
Ministries/Departments. In addition, all appointments by promotion, which require the approval of the ACC are also
processed through the Establishment Officer.
Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB)
6.4   The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making recommendations for
appointment to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executive and functional Directors and part-time Chairman in
the Central Public Undertakings.
Administrative Vigilance
6.5   The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for the maintenance of integrity of the
public services and eradication of corruption and coordinates the activities of various Ministries/Departments in that
area.
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
6.6   Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Central Vigilance Commission. It has jurisdiction and power in
respect of all matters to which the executive power of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted
with the supervision of the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). The Commission enjoys the same measure of
independence and autonomy as the Union Public Service Commission.
Joint Consultative Machinery
6.7   The Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM) is three-tier machinery consisting of the National Council, the
Departmental Council and the Regional/Office Council. Service matters pertaining to the interests of the generality of
employees or specific groups of them are dealt with by this machinery.
Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
6.8   In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to employees aggrieved by Government decisions, the
Government has set up the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) in 1985, which now deals with all cases relating to
service matters which were previously dealt with by Courts upto and including the High Courts.
Staff Welfare
6.9   The Department of Personnel and Training is intimately involved in several staff welfare and similar activities.
Mention in this connection can be made of the institution of Grih Kalyan Kendra (a society registered under the
Societies Registration Act, 1860), the Kendriya Bhandar (a registered cooperative society set up in 1963) and the
Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board (a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860).
6.10 The Department also supports various programmes, which are provided in various Ministries/Departments, Central
Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in Central Government offices located outside Delhi,
Residents’ Welfare Associations/Area Welfare Officers, Benevolent Fund and Departmental Canteens.
DEPARTMENT OF PENSIONS AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE
7.0   The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare is the nodal agency of the Government of India for
formulation of general policy on pension and other retirement benefits, as also for redressal of grievances on retirement
benefits. The Department was set up in 1985 as a part of Ministry of personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions and has
been rendering service for the benefit of pensioners since then. The Department has taken a number of steps over the
years for streamlining the pension administration system, for greater convenience and welfare of the pensioners.
7.1   This Department, does not deal directly with pensioners but is the focal point for dealing with issues concerning
pensioners and for issue of instructions and guidelines regarding matters pertaining to pensioners.
7.2   The main responsibilities entrusted to this Department are:
          (i)    Formulation of policies and coordination of matters relating to retirement benefits of Central Govt.
employees including Defence and Railways;
          (ii)    Administration of
                 (a)   Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972;
                 (b)   Central Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1981;
                 (c)   Central Civil Services (Extraordinary Pensions) Rules;
                 (d)   General Provident Fund (Civil Services) Rules, 1960;
                 (e)   Deposit Linked Insurance Revised Scheme; and

DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEVANCES
8.0   The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of the Government of India
for administrative reforms as well as redressal of public grievances relating to the State in general and grievances
pertaining to Central Government agencies in particular. The Department disseminates information on important
activities of the Government relating to administrative reforms and public grievance redressal through Management
Publications and Documentation. This Department also acts as the nodal agency in regard to Organisation an Methods
activities and offers advice to the Ministries/Departments. It also undertakes activities in the field of international co-
operation and exchanges to promote public service reforms.
8.1   This Department, has, in the last few years, taken several initiatives with a view to bring about improvement in the
functioning and effectiveness of Government Departments. To mention a few, the following are important ones:
          (i)    drawing up an agenda for reforms to be carried out in the Central Government as well as in States; and
          (ii)    undertaking through a Commission, a review of administrative laws which have become obsolete and no
longer need to be in the statute book.
8.2   The Department also initiated an exercise on the part of Departments having public interface to draw up their
individual Citizen’s charter to indicate the commitments of that organization for quality and the time-frame in which
services performed by that organization would be completed for members of the public.

DEMAND NO.-74 (UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION)
21.0 The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional body, created under Article 315 of the Constitution. The
Commission’s charter of duties is given in Article 320  and 321 of the Constitution. In brief, it is the duty of the
Commission to conduct examinations for appointment to services of the Union and to advise Government on all matter
relating to framing of recruitment rules, method of recruitment and the principle to be followed in making promotions
and transfers from one service to another. The Commission is also required to be consulted in respect of disciplinary
matters affecting a civil servant including appeals and memoranda filed by individual Government servants. The
Commission’s advice is also sought regarding any claim for reimbursement of legal proceedings instituted against him
for acts done in execution of his duties and on the question of award of pension for injury sustained by a Government
servant in a civil capacity. By virtue of the provisions made in the relevant Acts, the Commission is required to be
consulted in respect of appointments to certain categories of posts under the Municipal Corporation of Delhi, the
Employees’ State Insurance Corporation and the Employees’ Provident Fund Organization.

CHAPTER – III
POLICY, PROGRAMME AND SCHEMES OF THE MINISTRY
LAL BAHADUR SHASTRI NATIONAL ACADEMY OF ADMINISTRATION
22.0 The Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA) located at Mussoori in Uttaranchal is
primarily an institution for training of entrants to Government service through the annual Civil Service Examination
conducted by the UPSC. The responsibility is discharged by the Academy by means of a Foundational Course
conducted for the bulk of the different services covered by the Civil Services Examination, besides imparting intensive
and in-depth training to entrants to the Indian Administrative Service. The Academy also conducts programmes for in-
service training of IAS officers, including officers appointed to the IAS from State Civil Services. For other services
like the Indian Police Service (IPS), the Indian Revenue Service as well as the different Railway Services, the training

99th report of Committee on HOME AFFAIRS

institutions are the National Police Academy at Hyderabad, the National Academy for Direct Taxes at Nagpur and the
Railway Staff College at Vadodara.
22.1 In so far as the Foundational Course for the All India and the Central Services is concerned, the objectives of this
course is given below:
           (a)    to promote esprit de corps among the probationers of different services, emphasizing the inter-dependence
of various services as part of the higher civil services;
           (b)   to motivate probationers towards developing certain basic professional, administrative and human values;
and
           (c)    to impart a basic understading of the constitutional, political, social, economic, legal, historical, cultural and
administrative framework within which the services function.
22.2 In addition to the induction and professional courses exclusively for IAS officers, the Academy also runs a large
number of short duration courses, from 2-3 days to two weeks period.
22.3 On being asked as to the number of short duration courses conducted by the Academy during the last three years,
the Government informed that in 2000, 19 courses were conducted where 542 participated, 17 courses in 2001 with 350
participation and 8 courses in 2002 with 172 participation. From a perusal of the details of cadre/service for whom it
was intended, it has been observed that out of the 44 courses 3 catered to services other than IAS and 20 courses catered
exclusively to the IAS while no mention was made with regard to 21 courses.
23.   The Committee taking note of the contribution made by the Lal Bahadur National Academy of Administration in
catering to the training needs of services other than the IAS, desires that the Academy should devise short-term in-house
departmental capsule courses for the new entrant officers in respective departments on a quarterly basis. Attending these
courses for all newly posted officers from the level of Under Secretary and above should be made mandatory. Similar
quarterly capsule courses should be devised for new entrants below rank of Under Secretary to impart complete
knowledge about the Department. The course content of the LBSNAA for the junior civil servants should include
subjects like agriculture. By incorporating the detailed knowledge of various departments to the departmental training
programmes, the LBSNAA could increase the time in the inputs for improving the agricultural contents in the
probationers course.
24.   The Committee has also been informed that due to non-clearance of sponsoring authority or for other reasons, the
number of participants actually attending the programme is less than the number of participants who have confirmed
their participation in the course. Besides, there is a sense of declining seriousness on part of the participants, perhaps
because their performance in the training programmes do not count for their promotion or find entry in the Annual
Confidential Reports (ACRs).
25.   The Committee taking note of the declining seriousness of the participants in the training programmes, feel that
this decline is perhaps more due to the lack of relevance to the actual job and as such the course contents should be
made relevant to his/her posting. Further, the training programmes for junior level officers should include components
on self-awareness and human values and inputs that would enable them to improve their basic attitude and have a better
understanding of ground level conditions. The Committee opines that such training programme should include
awareness of the Freedom of Information Act, 2002 for bringing in transparency in the administrative function of the
Government.
26.0 To a question with regard to the objectives and concerns of the training activities of the Academy, the Committee
has been informed that in addition to the objectives of imparting knowledge, developing skills and enhancing policy
formulation capabilities, the Academy also lays stress on bringing about right attitudinal orientation.
26.1 The Committee wanted to know whether training programme on ethics and values are conducted by the Academy.
The Government informed that the Academy conducts two courses of one-week duration each, every year on the theme
“Ethical values in Today’s Administration”. So far, the Academy has conducted seven such courses. The basic issues
dealt with the regarding ethical dilemmas in the Government. The Academy shares the concern that there is an erosion
of the humane aspects of administration. The Academy exposes the trainees to persons of eminence who can serve as
positive role models. Modules on “Self Awareness” and other training inputs like Team Building, Responsive
Administration and Sensitisation to the problems and needs of the people, especially those who belong to the poorer and
backward sections of the society, and Village visit are also conducted aimed at bringing about right attitudinal
orientation in the officer trainees.
26.2 The Ministry further stated that the objective of the programme is to expose the participants to the basic principles

PUBLIC ENTERPRISES SELECTION BOARD
28.0 Public Sector Enterprises (PSE) under the control of the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions

28.1 The PESB functions as a professional body with a large measure of autonomy. Its function are:
          (i)    to be responsible for the selection and placement of personnel in the posts of Chairman, Managing Director

          (ii)   to advise the Government on matters relating to appointment, confirmation or extension of tenure an

          (iv)  to advise the Government on a suitable performance appraisal system for both the PSEs and the managerial

51.0 Pursuant to the provision of Article 323A of the Indian Constitution, the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 was
enacted by Parliament to set up Administrative Tribunal for adjudication of disputes and complaints with regard to
recruitment and condition of services of persons appointed to public services and posts in connection with the affairs of
the Union and the States.
51.1 Administrative adjudication, which is quasi-judicial in nature, is the main function of Administrative Tribunals.
The basic objective of enacting the Administrative Tribunal Act was:
          (i)    to relieve congestion in the ordinary set of courts; and
          (ii)    to provide for speedy disposal of disputes relating to service matters.
51.2 The Central Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 provides that the Tribunal shall not be bound by the procedure laid
down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 but shall be guided by the principle of natural justice and subject to the other
provisions of Administrative Tribunals Act and rules made by the Central Government. The Tribunal shall have power
to regulate its own procedure including the fixing of places and times of enquiry. As a result of the judgement of the
Supreme Court in the case of L. Chandra Kumar dated 18 March 1997, the appeals against the orders of an
Administrative Tribunals lie before the Division Bench of the concerned High Court.
51.3 Administrative Tribunals are distinguishable from the ordinary Courts with regard to their jurisdiction and
procedure. They are free from the shackles of many of the technicalities of the ordinary Courts. The litigant(s) has to
submit an application in paper book form prescribed by rule 4 of Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules,
1985. It means that every application should be accompanied by a proper containing the following:-
          (i)    Certified copy of the order against which the application has been filed;
          (ii)    All documents relied upon by the applicants and mentioned in the application;
          (iii)   Details of the crossed Demand Draft or crossed Indian Postal Order representing the application fee of Rs.
50/-; and
          (iv)   Index of documents.
        The aggrieved person can appear before the Court personally. Government can also present its case through its
Departmental officers or legal practitioners.
51.4 The Tribunal consists of a sanctioned strength of one Chairman, 16 Vice-Chairmen and 49 Members. The
Members are drawn from both judicial as well as administrative streams so to give the Tribunal the benefit of expertise
both in legal and administrative spheres.
51.5 The Committee, in its 87th Report on Demands for Grants (2002-2003) of the Ministry had recommended that the
process of selection of persons to fill up vacancies in the CAT should be initiated at least three months before its

conducted in advance. However, due to litigation of some other unavoidable norms, the selection process is held up.

additional Secretaries of the rank of Joint Secretary to the Government of India and other supporting officers and staff.

60.0 The Committee takes serious note of the high pendency of cases for implementation of advice of the CVC in the
Ministries/Departments. In administration the objective of rewards and punishment gets completely defeated unless they
are awarded with utmost promptness. This is specially true about punishments. As promptness in punishments has a
salutary effect on the tone of administration and in the light of the fact that the CVC is directly responsible to the
Parliament, there appears no escape for the Committee but to directly involve itself through an in-depth study of the
problem of delays of more than six months in thousands of cases. The Ministry has enumerated six reasons for the
delays. The Committee therefore would like to have break up of all the delayed cases of the past five years that are
respectively attributable to the six stated reasons in para 59.2. It would further like to know the specific remedial
measures that Government intends to adopt to tackle each of the six categories of reasons respectively. Additionally, the
Committee would like to know how the Government intend to implement each of the measures it so suggests for
example, in the measures already suggested by the Government in Para 59.3; how exactly will it be possible to
implement the measures given at (a) & (b) in Para 59.3. Further, in what way are the delays going to be reduced by
resorting to action proposed in (c), (d) & (e) of Para 59.3 ? Of the measures contemplated to curb delays, barring (a) of
Para 59.3, none of the other measures contemplated seem to have any direct bearing on the ‘causes of delay’ as
enumerated in clauses (a), (c), (d), (e) & (f) of Para 59.2. That being the case, the Committee has grave doubts if the
measures contemplated will at all make any dent on the problem of delays.
60.1 Till an in-depth study is made by the Committee to effectively deal with this problem in a systematic and sustained
basis, the Committee has the following to suggest:
As each case of punishment has its own unique complexities, it is therefore suggested that in the light of the
complexities of each case which would be apparent to the CVC before it tenders the first stage advise, the CVC
must prescribe specific time frames with dates by which Ministries/Departments/Organisations must report to the
CVC about the implementation of CVC’s first stage advice. The same should be done in respect of the second
stage advice. If the Ministries/Departments/Organisations find that the time frame prescribed by the CVC is
impractical, they themselves must inform the CVC within seven days of the receipt of the advice as to the date by
which they shall be able to report the said implementation. If, thus the Ministries/Departments/Organisations fail to
keep up to their own suggested dates, the CVC should report to the head of the Department for taking action
against the dealing officer for inability to abide by his own date. Initially, warnings to such officers should be
placed on their personal files but if his performance does not improve even after three warnings, major punishment
proceedings should be initiated against that officer for incompetent handling of his duties. The Committee believes
that the root of the problem of delays lies in the absence of accountability of the dealing officers of the CVC, and
the dealing officers of the Ministries/Departments/Organisations and it is here where the action should commence
forthwith.
60.2 In the Meanwhile, the Committee, in consultation with the Ministry and the CVC shall in due course evolve a
permanent operational procedure.
SCHEME FOR COMPASSIONATE APPOINTMENT
61.0 The Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions devises welfare measures for the Central Government
employees. The scheme for compassionate appointment is one of the welfare measures.

99th report of Committee on HOME AFFAIRS

61.1 The object of the scheme is to grant appointment on compassionate ground to a member of the dependent family
(spouse, son, daughter, brother or sister) of a Government employee dying in harness or who takes retirement on
medical grounds, thereby leaving his family in penury and without any means of livelihood, to relieve the family of the
Government employee concerned from financial destitution and to help it get over the emergency.
61.2 The Scheme for compassionate appointment has been last revised/simplified and consolidated in the light of
various Court judgements. Fifth Central Pay Commission Report and the Study Reports of 1990 and 1994 prepared by
the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances in October 1998.
61.3 To a questions as to how far the scheme has served the welfare of the Government employees whose near relations
have opted for compassionate appointment, the Ministry has stated that the scheme for compassionate appointment
provides for compassionate appointments in Group ‘C’ and ‘D’ posts to the extent of 5% of the vacancies to be filled by
direct recruitment in Group ‘C’ and ‘D’ posts. It is further stated that the compassionate appointment is to be granted
only in most deserving cases to save the dependent family of a deceased/medically retired Government employees from
financial destitution. The Ministry has stated that the Scheme has its utility as a welfare measure.
62.0 Certain other specific questions had been put to the Ministry Viz.
          (a)   How far has the scheme served the welfare of Government employees ?

Government employees have been benefited and what are the details of such assessment?
          (c)   Whether the Ministry has ever collected data as to the number of deserving families that could not be helped
under this scheme ?
          (d)   Whether any Court Judgement/Order has defined what is “a family in penury”?
          (e)   Whether any supplementary guidelines defining “indigent conditions” were issued?
          (f)    Did the original scheme envisage the maintaining of a waiting list? What is the period of validity of such
waiting lists?
          (g)   As per the scheme, if the entire 5% vacancies are full in one Ministry/Department, whether appointment on
compassionate grounds could be made in any other Government of India Ministry/Department? Accordingly,
was any waiting list monitored to enable their accommodation in other Departments?
          (h)   How many cases on the waiting list of one Department were accommodated in other Departments during the
preceding 10 years?
          (i)    Whether calculation of 5% vacancies was affected by the ban on fresh recruitments? Has it meant a reduction
or is the 5% being calculated on the basis of the original sanctioned strength?
          (j)    Was there any time bar for compassionate appointments under this scheme?
          (k)   Whether the dependants of the deceased employees were permitted to retain Government accommodation in
anticipation of compassionate appointments?
          (l)    Whether the Ministry deems fit to consider giving a Group ‘D’ post to an applicant of Group ‘C’ post to

          (m)  Whether DPOT or the Directorate of Estate prescribes any time limit regarding retention of the Government
