INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs having been
authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Seventieth Report on the Election Laws
(Amendment) Bill, 1999·
.
 
2.         In pursuance of the rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees, the Chairman,
Rajya Sabha in consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha  referred¨¨ the Election Laws (Amendment) Bill, 1999 as
introduced in the Lok Sabha on 9th December, 1999 and pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.
 
3.0.      The Committee considered the Bill in ten sittings held on 31 May, 14 and 29 June, 13 July, 16 August, 11 and 12
September and 7 November, 2000, 9 February and  10  April, 2001 spread over twelve hours.
 
3.1.      The Committee took the oral evidence of Secretary, Legislative Department, Defence Secretary, representatives
of the Armed Forces Headquarters, Secretary, Department of Posts, Home Secretary and representative of Election
Commission individually as well as jointly.  The Committee also held detailed discussions amongst its members.
 
3.2.      In the course of its deliberations, the Committee made use of the detailed background notes supplied by the
Legislative Department which is the nodal Ministry for the Bill and replies sent by the concerned
Ministries/Departments to the questionnaires.  The Committee also made use of the replies received from the Legislative
Department, Ministry of Defence, Department of Posts, Ministry of Home Affairs and Election Commission on the

REPORT
 
1.         The Election Laws (Amendment) Bill, 1999 (see Annexure-I) seeks to amend Sections 59,60 and 62 of the 
Representation of the People Act, 1951  and  Section 171-D of  the Indian Penal Code in order to provide the system of
proxy voting  as an  additional  option to some  categories  of service voters i.e. Armed Forces of the Union and the
forces to which the provisions of Army Act, 1950 have been  made applicable, with/without  modifications, to enable
them to exercise  their   franchise  effectively.
1.1.      Section 60 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, provides for  the scheme  of postal ballot  and rule 18
of Conduct of Election Rules, 1961, details the following  categories of voters, who are entitled to vote by post at an
election in a Parliamentary or Assembly constituency:
Special voters;
Service voters;
Voters on election duty;
Electors subjected to preventive detention;
 
1.1.1    The category of Service Voters are further divided into the following sub-categories:-
            (a)        A Member of Armed Forces of the Union;
A Member of the Force to which the provisions of the Army Act, 1950
have  been  made applicable  whether with or without modifications;
(c )       Members of an Armed Police Force of a State which is serving outside that State; and
(d)        A person who is employed  under the Government of India in a post outside India.
 
Out of these sub-categories of service voters, the present Bill proposes to give  right  of proxy voting only to (a) and (b) 
above.
2.         From a perusal of the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill, it appears that the scheme of proxy voting to
a few categories  of service voters was recommended   by  the Election  Commission   keeping  in view the logistic 
constraints  involved in despatching  postal ballots to the  members of Armed Forces through  their Record Offices and 
receiving those ballot papers  back before counting process  commences and the reduction of  maximum campaign
period from 21 to 14 days with the enactment of the Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 1996.
2.1       The proposal  to extend  the facility of voting by proxy to the Armed Forces of the Union and other Service
Voters flows from the D.O. letter No.3/ER/97-JSII dated 19  December 1997 of  the  Chief  Election Commissioner,
addressed to the then Prime Minister (see Annexure-VI).
2.1.1    The Chief Election Commissioner in the said D.O. letter had proposed to provide proxy  voting   in  place of
existing system of postal ballot for service voters, namely, Armed Forces personnel, Central Para-Military Forces
personnel, Diplomatic and Consulate  representatives of India abroad and other officers and staff working in the Indian

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
embassies,  mainly due to inadequacies  in the existing postal ballot system caused by the  logistic constraints  of
sending and receiving back postal ballots before the commencement of counting process and reduction of  the maximum
campaign period from 21 to 14 days.  The Chief Election Commissioner in his said D.O. letter had    mentioned that  the
facility for them to vote by post remains only a facility on paper as hardly 10 to 15% postal  ballots were reaching back
to the   Returning Officers in time, when a comparatively larger interval  was available for the purpose before the
recent  reduction of campaign  period.
2.1.2    The Chief Election Commissioner had cited a few examples of  advanced  democracies  such as the United
Kingdom and France and of Algeria having less developed democratic  traditions, where the system of proxy voting was
working smoothly.
3.         The system of proxy  voting as proposed by the Election Commission basically allows a person categorized  as
service voter to register as elector  in the service  category to exercise his right for choosing a proxy.  The service voter
would be permitted to authorise any adult  member of his family, namely, wife or any  of his sons or daughters or father 
or mother  to vote for him in his native place for which the service voter  would be required to send a standing letter of
authority  to the Electoral   Registration  Officer  and the authority will be valid so long as the same  is not withdrawn
or modified by the service voter.
4.         The   system of proxy voting, as proposed in the Bill, covers only two categories  of service voters, namely, 
Armed Forces personnel and the personnel to whom the Army Act, 1950 is applicable with/without  modifications. 
Further, the system of proxy voting would be made available to them not in lieu of postal ballot; rather the same is an
additional option   to the existing system of voting in person or voting through postal ballot.
5.         The Committee heard the Secretary, Legislative Department in its sitting  held on 31 May 2000. 
5.1       He  inter alia informed the Committee that the Bill was  introduced in deference to the long standing demand for
the same from the Armed Forces.
5.2       As far as the secrecy  part of the system of proxy voting was concerned, he replied that it was a question of faith
between  the voter and his/her proxy.    He further added that even in family one knew to whom the other  had voted. 
Thus, it would not be a breach of secrecy  in the real sense.
6.         The Committee also heard the Defence Secretary together with the representatives of  Army Headquarters  on
the provisions of the Bill  in the same sitting held on 31 May 2000.
6.1       The Defence Secretary was supportive of the proposal of the  Election Commission   that only 10 to 15 per  cent
of postal ballots were coming back to the Returning Officers for the purpose of counting  because of the fact that the
Armed forces personnel deployed in high altitude,  forward posts, inhospitable  hilly terrain and battalions  on patrol 
duty on the border were not able to exercise their voting rights  because  of the delay in delivery of postal ballots within
the stipulated period of time.  Therefore, in his opinion, proxy would be the correct solution.
6.2.      The Defence  Secretary was supported by the representative of  the  Army Head- quarters,  who gave a detailed 
account  of the to and fro  movement of postal ballot  in the Army establishment  in particular.  Though the return 
journey  of the ballot  paper was quite direct, the onward journey of postal ballot was indirect and thereby time
consuming.
6.2.1    The representative  of the  Army Headquarters, recounting  the procedure  being followed  in respect of postal
ballots explained  that during the onward journey of postal ballot, the Returning  Officer  sends the postal  ballots   at
his  disposal to a central place called ‘Record Office’, which maintains  records of persons ranging  from 3000 to
1,00,000.  Out of 53 Record Offices, 47  were effective with regard to the electoral process.  When a Record Office
received postal ballot from Returning Officer, it segregated the ballot papers on the basis of the unit in which the person
was then posted.  Thereafter, it sent those to the Army Post Office  (APO), which delivered the same  to the concerned
army personnel.  However, in case of Navy and Air Force, the ballot papers are not sent through the APO; rather they
are sent directly to the concerned individual through the Record Office only.  But, the return journey of the postal ballot
in case of the personnel of all the three forces  is the same, i.e. the individual after exercising his franchise sent the same
directly to the Returning  Officer.
6.3       The Committee raised several queries during  the course of the presentations of the Defence Secretary and the
representative  of the Army Headquarters.  The Committee specifically  intended  to know which of   the forces other
than the   Army, Navy and  Air Force had  been brought   under   Section 4 of the Army Act uptil then.
6.4       The Defence Secretary in a written reply submitted to the Committee that Section  4 of the Army  Act, 1950, 
stipulated: “The Central Government  may by notification, apply, with or without  modifications, all or any   of the
provisions of this Act, to   any force raised and  maintained  in India under the authority of that Government….”. 
Besides, Central Para-Military Forces,  the following forces have been  brought under the application of the Army Act, 
with or without modifications:-

file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
Assam Rifles;
Civil General Transport  Companies;
General Reserve Engineering Force (GREF); and
Rashtriya Rifles.
 
7.         The Committee took oral evidence of the Secretary, Department of Posts on 14 June, 2000 and ascertained his
views on the Bill and sought to know from him the ways and means contemplated by his Department to improve upon
the existing postal ballot system to enhance the percentage of participation of the Armed Forces and Para-military
Forces personnel in the country’s electoral process.  The Secretary, Department of Posts apprised the Committee of the
detailed procedure followed in the Post Offices in the movement of postal ballot. He also stated that the alternative
method to improve the postal ballot system was speed post which would be an expensive proposition.
8.         The Committee in its sitting held on 29 June, 2000 heard the Home Secretary on the Bill.
9.         The Committee held further deliberations on the Bill in its sitting held on 13 July, 2000.
10.       The Committee resumed discussion on the Bill in its sitting held on 16 August, 2000. 
11.       In its sitting held on 11 September, 2000 the Committee jointly heard the Home Secretary, Defence Secretary,
Secretary, Department of Posts, Secretary, Legislative Department, representatives of the Armed Forces Headquarters
and the Deputy Election Commissioner.
12.       In the course of the deliberations in the Committee and on scrutiny of the replies to the questionnaires and the
statistics furnished by the Legislative Department, Election Commission, Department of Posts and Ministry of Home
Affairs, serious discrepancies were noticed in relation to the total number of service voters entered in the electoral rolls,
total number of postal ballot papers sent by the Returning Officers to the service voters and the number of postal ballot
papers received back from the service voters before the commencement of the counting, during the last two general
elections held in 1998 and 1999.   The Election Commission, Ministry of Defence and Legislative Department
maintained that only 10 to 15 percent of the postal ballot papers were received back by the Returning Officers  from the
service voters.  The Department of Posts on the other hand claimed that on an average 60 percent  of postal ballot papers
were reaching back  to the Returning Officers from the service voters.
12.1     Several attempts were made in the Committee with a view to reconciling the divergent statistics.  The
Committee directed the Home Secretary to use his good offices so that correct figures of service voters, total number of
ballot papers sent by the Returning Officers and those received back from the service voters could be made available to
the Committee.  Number of attempts were also made by the Legislative Department, Election Commission and the
Department of Posts to furnish authentic and accurate data to the Committee.  An Inter Departmental Meeting was held
at the behest of the Legislative Department in the office of the Election Commission on 22 August, 2000 (see Annexure-
XIII for minutes of the Meeting).  However, in spite of the attempts made in the Committee and the endeavour of the
concerned Ministries/Departments and the Election Commission, the discrepant figures remained un reconciled,
unresolved and inconsistent. 
13.       While scrutinizing the various replies received from the Legislative Department and the Election Commission
from time to time, the Committee also came across a number of misleading, vacillating, self-contradictory statements. 
The Committee took serious note of  them .
14.       In the backdrop of contradictory and un reconciled facts and figures relating to service voters, the Defence
Secretary in his deposition before the Committee on 11 September, 2000 inter alia maintained that non-reconciliation of
figures of service voters need not be held against the validity of the objective of empowering the service voters
belonging to Armed Forces to exercise their franchise.
15.       In the course of deliberations on various aspects of proxy voting, a large number of issues were raised by
members.  Some of the major ones have been summarized below: -
Time constraint may come in the way of communication between the service voter and his/her proxy about the change
in former’s choice of party/candidate, on the eve of General Election.  In the era of coalition politics, if a service voter
has communicated his choice to the proxy to vote for a particular party and that party instead of fielding a candidate due
to seat adjustment is supporting some other party/candidate, then how the proxy will be in a position to vote for a
suitable candidate of service voter’s choice?
The provision of proxy voting as an additional option to the two sub-categories of service voters to the exclusion of the
remaining categories of service voters may tantamount to discrimination and violative of the principle of equality
enshrined in Article 14 of the Constitution extending proxy voting to a group of service voters may invite similar
demand from other categories of service voters as well as other categories of voters, in future.
It would be undemocratic if one is to vote according to the wishes of other voter and not of his own.  The like and

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
dislike of husband and wife, parents and children, etc. in choosing the candidates may not be the same.
The performance and effectiveness of the system of postal ballot may be reviewed by the Election Commission and
made efficient.
Election period may be extended by four/five days to overcome the feared bottlenecks.
The gap between the election date and counting date, if possible, may be extended.
Ballot papers can either be faxed/e-mailed to various headquarters of Armed Forces.
16.       The Committee has deliberated on the Bill in great detail.  However, on the question of providing the option of
proxy voting at an election to the Armed Forces of the Union and of forces to which the provisions of the Army Act,
1950 have been made applicable, the Committee is sharply divided.  There is no consensus in the Committee either in
favour of or against the Bill.  There is no agreement in the Committee on proxy voting even in principle. 
16.1     A section of members  is strongly in favour of granting the option of proxy voting to the Armed  Forces as they
feel that the postal ballot system has been grossly deficient thereby depriving the majority of the members of the Armed
Forces from exercising their suffrage.  They strongly feel as the members of Armed Forces have been asking for proxy
voting since long, there should be no hesitation in acceding to their long standing request.  As regards the statistics of
service voters and the postal ballots, they feel that it may not be possible to obtain correct figures as the same do not
seem to have been maintained by the concerned Ministries/Departments/Election Commission in a methodical manner. 
This section of members has contended that the figures furnished to the Committee may not be corroborative of the
stand taken by the Election Commission and Ministry of Defence that only 10 to 15 per cent of the postal ballots were
reaching back to the Returning Officers in time from the Armed Forces personnel, but they are sufficiently indicative of
the deficiency of the postal ballot system and the only way to overcome the same is to introduce proxy voting.  They are
also of the view that the Committee need not be bogged down by the rigmarole of figures and data and that it should
straightway recommend introduction of proxy voting.
16.2     The other section of members strongly feels that introducing the system of proxy voting would militate against
the whole concept of historically evolved electoral process in our country, the bedrock of which is the secret ballot. 
Proxy voting will seriously jeopardize the basis of the electoral process in the country.  It will strike at the very root of
inalienability of the citizens’ statutory right to vote personally and will violate the principle of voting by secret ballot. 
This section of members is not in  favour of diluting the individual citizen’s right to vote.  However, they are in
complete agreement over the fact that everything possible should be done, all avenues, short of proxy voting, should be
explored including reforming the existing postal ballot system, to ensure that the Armed Forces of the Union and those
forces to whom the provisions of the Army Act, 1950 have been made applicable, are able to exercise their franchise in
an effective manner, so that  the member of the force would feel to be part of the mainstream of the country’s electoral
process.  They have also suggested that the Government should convene an all-party meeting for arriving at a consensus
before proceeding with the Bill in Parliament.
16.3     Notwithstanding the sharp division there is,  however,  complete unanimity in the Committee that all-out efforts
need  be made to ensure fullest participation by the members of Armed Forces of the Union in the country’s electoral
process and towards that end the bottlenecks in the way of effective exercise of franchise by the Armed Forces in large
number should be removed.  But, on the question of introduction of proxy voting, there are differences in the Committee
on principles and perceptions.
17.       Meanwhile on 15 February 2001 the Defence Secretary along with senior officers of the Ministry of Defence and
the Director-General (MP&PS), Armed Forces Headquarters, met the Chairman of the Committee in connection with
the Bill.  The Defence Secretary apprised the Chairman of certain inputs as a result of the former’s recent visit to
Siachen and interaction with the army personnel posted there.  The Defence Secretary and the representative of the
Armed Forces Headquarters  reiterated the difficulties of logistics and constraints of time in dispatching the postal
ballots to the army personnel posted in forward, high altitude and difficult terrains and sending them back to the
Returning Officers before counting begins. 
17.1     The Defence Secretary on behalf of the Ministry of Defence and also on behalf of the Chiefs of Staff  of the
Armed Forces pleaded that the Committee may kindly consider their latest submissions before taking a final view on the
Bill.
17.2     In this connection the Chairman mentioned to them that a suggestion has been put forward in the Committee for
use of computers and e-mail for the purpose of voting by the Armed Forces personnel posted in the far-flung forward
areas.  The Defence Secretary expressed the difficulties in the introduction of such a system in view of lack of
infrastructure  especially electricity in those areas.  He also stated that resorting to voting by electronic means would
result in large scale breach of secrecy of ballot compared to the proxy voting.
18.       Be that as it may, in view of the sharply divergent perceptions in the Committee on the subject matter of the Bill,

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
it is of the considered view that the Government should take up the issue at a larger level with major political parties to
explore the possibilities of evolving a consensus on this issue.

the whole concept of historically evolved electoral process in our country, the bedrock of which is the secret ballot. 
Proxy voting will seriously jeopardize the basis of the electoral process in the country.  It will strike at the very root of
inalienability of the citizens’ statutory right to vote personally and will violate the principle of voting by secret ballot. 
This section of members is not in  favour of diluting the individual citizen’s right to vote.  However, they are in
complete agreement over the fact that everything possible should be done, all avenues, short of proxy voting, should be
explored including reforming the existing postal ballot system, to ensure that the Armed Forces of the Union and those
forces to whom the provisions of the Army Act, 1950 have been made applicable, are able to exercise their franchise in
an effective manner, so that the  member of the force would feel to be part of the mainstream of the country’s electoral
process.  They have also suggested that the Government should convene an all-party meeting for arriving at a consensus
before proceeding with the Bill in Parliament.
(Para 16.2)
·          Notwithstanding the sharp division there is,  however,  complete unanimity in the Committee that all-out efforts
need  be made to ensure fullest participation by the members of Armed Forces of the Union in the country’s electoral
process and towards that end the bottlenecks in the way of effective exercise of franchise by the Armed Forces in large
number should be removed.  But, on the question of introduction of proxy voting, there are differences in the Committee
on principles and perceptions.
(Para 16.3)
·          Be that as it may, in view of the sharply divergent perceptions in the Committee on the subject matter of the Bill,
it is of the considered view that the Government should take up the issue at a larger level with major political parties to
explore the possibilities of evolving a consensus on this issue.
(Para 18)

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
            Section 60 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 permits any person with “service qualification” to give
his vote by postal ballot. “Service qualification” as defined in sub-section (8) of section 20 of the Representation of the
People Act, 1950, inter alia, includes members of the armed forces of the Union and of the forces to which the
provisions of the Army Act, 1950 have been made applicable with or without modifications.
            2.         The Election Commission of India, keeping in view the fact that with the enactment of the Representation
of the People (Amendment) Act 1996, the minimum campaign period has been reduced from twenty days to fourteen
days and keeping in view the logistic constraints involved in despatching postal ballots to members of the armed forces
through their record offices and receiving those ballot papers back before counting process commences, recommended
that the members of armed forces, in order to ensure their full participation in the electoral exercise, may be allowed to
vote by proxy instead of postal ballot.
            3.         The Government has considered the matter and has decided to provide an option to the members of the
armed forces of the Union and of forces to which the provisions of the Army Act, 1950 have been made applicable,
whether with or without modifications, to vote either in person or through the system of postal ballot or through the
system of proxy, at an election in the manner to be specified in the rules.  This requires amendments to the
Representation of the People Act, 1951 and the Indian Penal Code.
            4.         The Election Laws (Amendment) Bill, 1998 introduced in the 12
th Lok Sabha seeking to achieve the
aforesaid objective lapsed with the dissolution of that House.  Hence this Bill.

CHAPTER IX
ARREST AND PROCEEDINGS BEFORE TRIAL
 
101.(1) Any person subject to this Act who is charged with an offence may be taken into
military custody.
      (2) Any such person may be ordered into military custody by any superior officer.
     (3) An officer may order into military custody any officer, though he may be of higher
rank, engaged in a quarrel, affray or disorder.
 
102. (1) It shall be the duty of every commanding officer to take care that a person under
his command when charged with an offence is not detained in custody for more than forty-
eight hours after the committal of such person into custody is reported to him, without the
charge being investigated, unless investigation within that period seems to him to be
impracticable having regard to the public service.
     (2) The case of every person being detained in custody beyond a period of forty-eight
hours, and the reason thereof, shall be reported by the commanding officer to the general or
other officer to whom application would be made to convene a general or district court-
martial, for the trial of the person charged.
    (3) In reckoning the period of forty-eight hours specified in sub-section (1), Sundays and
other public holidays shall be excluded.
   (4) Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Central Government may make rules
providing for the manner in which and the period for which any person subject to this Act
may be taken into and detained in military custody, pending the trial by any competent
authority for any offence committed by him.
 
103.  In every case where any such person as is mentioned in section 101 and as is not on
active service  remains  in  such  custody for a longer period than eight days, without a
court-martial for his trial being ordered to assemble, a special report giving reasons for the
delay shall be made by his commanding officer in the manner prescribed, and a similar
report shall be forwarded at interval of every eight days until a court-martial is assembled
or such person is released from custody.
 
104.  Whenever any person subject to this Act, who is accused of any offence under this
Act, is within the jurisdiction of any magistrate or police officer, such magistrate or police
officer shall aid in the apprehension and delivery to military custody of such person upon
receipt of a written application to that effect signed by his commanding officer.

has held a commission for not less than three whole years and of whom not less than
four are of a rank not below that of captain.
 
114. A district court-martial shall consist of not less than three officers, each of whom
has held a commission for not less than two whole years.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
115. A summary general court-martial shall consist of not less than three officers.
 
116.(1) A summary court-martial may be held by the commanding officer of any corps,
department or detachment of the regular Army, and he shall alone constitute the court.
   (2) The proceedings shall be attended throughout by two other persons who shall be
officers or junior commissioned officers or one of either, and who shall not as such, he
sworn or affirmed.
 
117.(1) If court-martial after the commencement of a trial is reduced below the minimum

130.(1) At all trials by general, district or summary general court-martial, as soon as the
court is assembled, the names of the presiding officer and members shall be read over to
the accused, who shall there upon be asked whether he objects to being tried by any officer
sitting on the court.
(2) If the accused objects to any such officer, his objection, and also the reply thereto of
the officer objected to, shall be heard and recorded, and the remaining officers of the court
shall, in the absence of the challenged officer decide on the objection.
  (3) If the objection is allowed by one-half or more of the votes of the officers entitled to
vote, the objection shall be allowed, and the member objected to shall retire, and his
vacancy may be filled in the prescribed manner any another officer subject to the same
right of the accused to object.
 (4) When no challenge is made, or when challenge has been made and disallowed, or the
place of every officer successfully challenged has been filled by another officer to whom
no objection is made or allowed, the court shall proceed with the trial.
 
131.(1) An oath or affirmation in the prescribed manner shall be administered to every
member of every court-martial and to the judge advocate before the commencement of the
trial.
   (2) Every person giving evidence before a court-martial shall be examined after being
duly sworn or affirmed in the prescribed form.
   (3) The provisions of sub-section (2) shall not apply where the witness is a child under
twelve years of age and the court-martial is of opinion that though the witness understands
the duty of speaking the truth, he does not understand the nature of an oath or affirmation.
 
  132.(1) Subject to the provisions of sub-sections (2) and (3), every decision of a court-
martial shall be passed by an absolute majority of votes; and where there is an equality of
votes on either the finding or the sentence, the decision shall be in favour of the accused.
   (2) No sentence of death shall be passed by a general court-martial without the
concurrence of at least two-thirds of the members of the court.
   (3) No sentence of death shall be passed by a summary general court-martial without the
concurrence of al the members.
   (4) In matters, other than a challenge or the finding or sentence, the presiding officer
shall have a casting vote.

Dear Mr. Prime Minister,
            I wish to  draw your kind attention to a serious issue, which has been agitating, since a long time, the minds of
the most disciplined and devoted sections of our electorate, namely, armed forces  personnel, para-military  forces
personnel, diplomatic and consular  representatives of India  abroad and other officers and staff  working  in Indian
Embassies, etc.,  as they have a grievance that they are being denied opportunity to participate in electoral exercises
which elect the Government of the day.
 
2.         Under the existing law, the above electors form a separate class and are known as ‘service  voters’.  They are
allowed the facility of getting themselves  enrolled in their native places  and of voting by post.  But you know well the
logistic constraints of dispatching postal ballots  to them through their record offices, etc., and receiving those ballots
back, before the counting process  commences.  These postal ballot papers can be dispatched to  them only after the lists
of final contestants  in each constituency are prepared after the last date for withdrawal of candidatures, and the period 
available for their dispatch and receipt  back  has now been further curtailed, because of the reduction of the minimum 
campaign period from 20 to 14 days by  recent amendment to the law.  Thus, the facility to them to vote by post
remains  only a facility on paper, as hardly 10 to 15% postal ballots were reaching back the Returning Officers in time,
even when a comparatively larger interval was available for the purpose before the recent reduction of compaign
period.  That seems to be the reason, even for their lethargy in getting themselves enrolled.
3.         The Commission has given its anxious consideration to the above issue, and has discussed it thoroughly with the
Chief of the Army Staff and other authorities concerned.
4.         After such extensive discussions, the Commission is, unanimously, of the considered view, that the only
effective method of ensuring full participation by the above mentioned service voters in the electoral exercise, is to
enable them to vote through the system of ‘Proxy’ instead of the present system of postal ballots.  Under this system, the
service voter would be permitted to authorise any adult member of his family, namely, his wife or any of his sons or
daughters or father or mother, to vote for him in his native place.  In appropriate cases, where such authorised person so
desires, he can even consult the voter concerned, through post or any telecommunication link, about his choice of the
candidate for whom he wishes to vote.
5.         It may be interesting to know that such facility of proxy voting is very much prevalent in advanced democracies,
like, the United Kingdom and France, and even in those countries whose democratic traditions are perhaps less
developed than ours, like, Algeria.  I was told by the British High Commissioner in India that he exercised his vote at
the recent general election in his country.  Through his mother.
6.         The above idea of proxy voting has been whole-heartedly welcomed, not only by the Defence personnel, but also
by several major political parties, like, Bhartiya Janata Party.
7.         The Commission would urge the Government to give immediate consideration to the above issue and, if
possible, undertake necessary legislation through Presidential Ordinance to amend the relevant law, so that the same can
be made effective for the ensuing general elections to the Lok Sabha and certain State Legislative Assemblies.  The
relevant provisions of the existing law which would require to under-go change to give effect to the above proposal are
indicated, for convenient reference, in the enclosed annexure.  While undertaking required amendments to the law, the

and prescribed by the Commission.

(1)        Data regarding number of service voters to be covered under the provisions of the Election Laws (Amendment)
Bill, 1999: The electoral rolls in all the States and the Union territories of Delhi and Pondicherry are prepared assembly
constituency-wise, and for the remaining Union territories having no Legislative Assembly, the same are prepared
parliamentary constituency-wise.  In each electoral roll for an assembly or, as the case may be, parliamentary
constituency, the last part of the electoral roll contains the names of service voters as defined in section 20(8) of the
Representation of the People Act, 1950.  In this part, the names of all service voters, including the foreign service voters
working outside India in a post under the Government of India, are included,  Further he wives of services voters
ordinarily resident with them are also included in the said last part.  At the  time of the last general election to the House
of people held in 199, the total number of service voters as included in the aforesaid past part of the electoral rolls in all
constituencies was 5,46,144.  It is not possible to say how many of them were armed service personnel or the persons to
whom the Army Act, 1950 applies.
(2)        Data regarding votes cast by service voters: At the time of the counting, all votes cast by the electors on election
duty by post, are counted together.  In order that the secrecy of votes cast by service voters which are small in number is
maintained, these votes are not counted separately but are counted along with other postal ballot papers and, therefore,
no exact figures are available in respect of the votes cast by service voters separately.  But is is well known fact that
very few postal ballot papers are received back from service voters in time, and the majority of the postal ballots are cast
by voters on election duty.
(3)        Data regarding votes cast by service voters belonging to armed forces residing in cantonment areas: The
electoral rolls pertaining to the cantonment areas are general electoral rolls in which the names of all persons ordinarily
resident in those areas, whether belonging to the armed forces or civilians, are registered as general electors.,  There is
no separate indication in those electoral rolls in respect of voters belonging to armed services. Many of the service
voters resident I the cantonment areas might have got themselves registered as electors in their native places a service
voters in the last part of the electoral rolls of their home constituencies.  The names of such persons will not be found in
the electoral rolls of cantonment areas.  Therefore, no statistics have been collected at any time to make any comparison

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
in respect of voting in the cantonment areas vis-à-vis other civilian areas of the constituency.
(4)        Use of internet/websites and digital signatures for the purpose of casting votes: It is not possible to make use of
internet/website and digital signatures for the purpose of casting votes.  Firstly, internet/website facilities may not be
available in the remote areas where the service voters are stationed.  Even, most of the Returning Officers, particularly
of the assembly  constituencies are not having such facilities available in their offices.  Secondly and more importantly,
the votes cast by means of internet/website and containing signatures will not remain secret at all.
(5)        Segregation of voters opting to vote through proxy system of voting: It would not be difficult to segregate voters
who opt to appoint their proxies to voter on their behalf, as their names are contained in the last part of the electoral roll
and an additional column can be provided in that part to indicate the names of proxies so appointed, by the service
voters.
(6)        Secrecy of voting under the proposed proxy system of voting: The votes will be cast by proxies on behalf of the
service voters at the polling stations in the same manner in which the other votes are cast.  These votes will remain
secret in the same manner in which their own voters cast by those proxies are kept secret under the existing law.
(7)        Measures against possible misuse of the proposed provision of voting through proxy: To ensure that the right to
vote to proxy is not misused, it is hoped that the Election Commission of India which is the field agency in the conduct
of elections will be able to exercise proper control/monitoring over its use.
(8)        Whether there was demand for proxy voting from persons other than the army personnel: No such demand has
come to the Government’s notice except from non-resident Indians.
(9)        Success of the proxy voting system in other Countries: IPU was contacted in the matter but no information has
been received till date.

Procedure of dispatch of Postal ballot papers as informed by the Department of Posts
 
            According to Department of Post, as and when General Elections to Lok Sabha or Legislative Assemblies are
notified by the Election Commission, detailed guidelines for speedy and safe handling of ballot papers are
communicated to all concerned through special circular known as DG Special Circular.  The manner of handling of
ballot papers in Civil Post Offices as well as Army Postal Services is detailed here under.
 
            Handling of Ballot Paper (outward)
 
(i)         In the Civil Post Offices
 
The concerned Divisional Superintendents are directed to contact the Returning Officers and identify the post office for
acceptance of ballot papers under Certificate of posting .  These ballot papers are not allowed to mix up with the other
mails and handled on priority basis.
 
These ballot papers are then sent to nearest mail office for detailed sorting and for onward dispatch to the Army Postal
Service authorities for further processing of the same.
 
(ii)        In Army Postal Service
 
Immediately on receipt of instructions about the General Election the following offices are instructed about the
procedure for handling of ballot papers.
 
Adjutant General’s Branch (Org.3 (C), Army HQ – in respect of officers of Indian Army.
 
Record offices of Indian Army – in respect of personnel below Officer Rank (PBOR).
 
 
All Army Post Offices – for speedy handling of the Postal Ballot papers in respect of Defence Service personnel and
submission of timely statistics through departmental channel.
 
(iii)       Initiation of Postal Ballot Papers
 
Postal Ballot papers are initiated in respect of each person serving in Armed Forces by the respective Returning Officers
based on the information available with them about service voters.
The envelope containing the ballot papers are addressed by Returning Arrangement made by the Civil Postal authorities
in consultation with the Concerned Returning Officer.

Procedure of Dispatch of Postal Ballot Papers  As Informed By The
Election Commission Of India
 
            When postal ballot papers are sent  by the Returning Officers to  the service  voters  belonging to armed forces,
all the ballot  papers meant for such service  voters registered with a particular Record Office, are  sent in one cover
addressed to the Record Office concerned.  These ballot papers are then sent to the service voters cocerned  by the
 Record Offices through their  own channels.  Therefore when a Returning Officer dispatches the postal ballot papers in
one cover addressed to the Record Office, it is just one communication as far as the postal department is concerned,
although postal ballot papers for more than one voter might have been sent in the cover.  Subsequently, the service
voters send their marked postal ballot papers to the Returning Officers individually.  The Returning Officers maintain
separate registers regarding the number of postal ballot papers sent by them category-wise and the number of those
received back in time, after the prescribed time, and those returned undelivered.

MINUTES OF THE MEETING HELD ON 22.8.2000  TO DISCUSS THE SYSTEM OF POSTAL BALLOT
PAPERS FOR  ARMY PERSONNEL
 
            Following were present in the meeting:
 
1.         Shri Sayan Chatterjee, Deputy Election Commissioner
2.         Shri K J Rao, Secretary, Election Commission
3.         Shri Sharan Pal Singh, Secretary, Election Commission
4.         Shri N.L. Meena, Joint Secretary  & Legislative Counsel (Admn), M/Law
5.         Shri Surender Kumar, Under Secretary, M/Law
6.         Shri R.P. Bagai, Joint Secretary , Ministry of Defence
7.         Lt. General H.S. Bagga, Army Headquarters
8.         Shri S.P. Singh, DDG (MM&TS), Department of Posts
 
            Shri  N.L. Meena, JS M/Law explained that the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Ministry of Home Affairs
had a detailed discussion on the subject of proxy voting by Army and paramilitary personnel and wanted to have
authentic data on the efficacy of postal ballot  papers for army personnel as the figures furnished by the Commission
and Postal Authorities differ.
 
            Shri Chatterjee, DEC explained that as per the information available with the Commission, the postal ballot
papers are issued to all service voters by the Returning Officers within 48 hours from the last date for the withdrawal of
candidatures.   Lt. Gen.  Bagga of AHQ explained that the postal ballots are sent by the Returning Officers to the Record
Offfices.  The Record Offices then sort out the postal ballot paper covers APO wise  and despatch the same to the units
of army personnel.  The army personnel after exercising the franchise return the same direct to the Returning officers.
 
            As per information available with the Commission, the number of service electors and their wives during the
General Elections to Lok Sabha held in 1999 were 546144.  Besides this, simultaneous elections were held to the State 
Legislative Assemblies of Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal  Pradesh,  Karnataka, Maharashtra and Sikkim, in which  there

PARLIAMENT OF INDIA
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/70threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:37:43]
were 62,900 service voters.
 
Joint Secretary, Ministry of Defence stated that as the campaign period of the most of the constituencies is 14 days, in
many cases it is operationally not feasible to receive back the postal ballot papers from the army personnel posted in
remote areas before the counting process is started.  The Election Commission officials endorsed this view.
 
            The details of postal ballot papers issued to service voters and their wives and the postal ballot papers received
back during the General Election to Lok Sabha held in 1999 in respect of Arunachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Manipur,
Meghalaya, Orissa and Punjab were brought to the notice of participants.  A constituency wise statement showing the
details of postal ballot papers issued to service voters and the number of ballot papers received back was prepared by the
Chief Electoral officer, Punjab and this too was brought to the notice of the participants.  These figures support the stand
of the Ministry of Defence and the Election Commission that only 10-15% ballot papers are received back from army
personnel in time for the counting by the Returning Officers.  The details are given in Annexure-I and Annexure-II.
 
            Shri S.P. Singh, DDG (MM&TS), explained that as per their record 12,95, 662 ballot papers were posted by the
Returning Officers during the General Elections to Lok Sabha held in 1999.  The details in this respect were as follows:
            a)         Postal ballot papers received back from voters              8,33,460
            b)         Postal ballot received back by postal authorities and      8,05,305
                        handed over to Returning Officers within time
            c)         Postal ballot papers delivered to Returning Officers         26,955
                        after the due date.
 
            He also stated that there was a discrepancy in the figures compiled by their Department on account of double
entry in two circles.  The Postal Department has issued instructions to verify the figures again with reference to the
record  to the kept by them and report back on the same.  These figures are likely to be received within a week or so.  He
further stated that a Committee could across check the figures furnished by their Department as soon as these are
compiled.
 
            It was pointed out by the ECI officials, that as the total number of service voters in the electoral rolls of 1999
was 5,46,144 and for simultaneously held elections to five State Assemblies was 62,900 it was just not possible for
more postal ballots to be issued to service voters higher than this figure.  It was further clarified that apart from service
voters postal ballots were also issued to some of the staff on election duty.  It was opined that perhaps the postal
department had mixed these two categories, namely, the service voters and those on election duty, who had been issued
postal ballots.  This would perhaps explain both the high figure of postal ballot issued as well as the high percentage of
receipt of the issued postal ballots prior to counting.  For postal ballots, that were issued to officials engaged in the
conduct of elections, there was little difficulty in obtaining these back before counting, as the distances involved were
not large.  However, it was decided to await the outcome of the revised exercise being carried out by the postal
department.

Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961 would ensure maintenance of secrecy of voting by a proxy on behalf of a service
