INTRODUCTION

Affairs, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby
present this One Hundred and Twenty-Seventh Report on the Demands for Grants (2007-08)
relating to the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER).

Committees are mandated to consider the Demands for Grants of the related Ministries during the
said recess period of Parliament and make reports thereon to the Houses within the specific period.

CHAPTER-I
OVERVIEW
The States of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura
and Sikkim having rich natural resources with fertile land, dense forests and substantive mineral
and hydrocarbon deposits form part of North-Eastern Region of the country. With a view to
convert the untapped potential of North Eastern Region into an opportunity, the policy of the
Central Government inter alia focuses on special assistance to upgrade and expand infrastructure
and accords priority to the development and expansion of physical infrastructure like roads,
highways, power, water supply etc. In this direction the Ministry of Development of North-Eastern
Region (DoNER) was formed as a Department in September 2001 and then was upgraded to
the status of a Ministry in May, 2004. As per the Government of India (Allocation of Business)
Rules, 1961, the Ministry of DoNER is concerned with the following:
(i) Matters relating to the planning, execution and monitoring of developmental schemes
and projects of North Eastern Region including those in the sectors of Power,
Irrigation, Roads and Communications;
(ii) Hill Area Development Programme and Border Area Development Programme in North
Eastern Region;*
(iii) Non-lapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR);
(iv) North Eastern Council (NEC);
(v) North East Development Finance Corporation (NEDFi);
(vi) North Eastern Regional Agricultural Marketing Corporation Limited (NERAMAC);
(vii) Sikkim Mining Corporation Limited (SMC);
(viii) North Eastern Handloom and Handicrafts Development Corporation (NEHHDC);
(ix) Road works fina nced in whole or in part by the Central Government in the North
Eastern Region; and
(x) Planning of Road and Inland Waterways Transport in the North Eastern Region.
2.0 The Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region seeks to achieve the following goals:
* Accelerating the pace of socio-economic development in the North Eastern Region.
* Synergizing the efforts of Central/State Governments/Other Stakeholders for balanced
development of the Region.
* Integrated and holistic planning for the region, taking into account the heightened
consciousness of the people of the reigon, their aspiration, needs and sensitivities.
* Evolving a common approach for all the agencies working for the development of the
region.
* Included in the original mandate of the Ministry of DoNER but has not been transferred from Planning
Commission and MHA, respectively.

2
* Formulating appropriate policies for the rapid development of the region.
3.0 The North Eastern Council (NEC) , Shillong, an organization under the Ministry, is the
Regional Planning Body for the region. It was set up under the NEC Act, 1971 for securing the
balanced development of the North East. The Minister in charge of the Ministry of Development of
North Eastern Region is ex-officio Chairman of NEC. Its Members include the Governors and the
Chief Ministers of the eight Member States and three Members nominated by the President of India.
4.0 In the Tenth Plan period, the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (DoNER)
had been implementing the following Central Plan Schemes:
* Advocacy and Publicity,
* Technical Assistance and Capacity Building,
* Assistance to Sikkim Mining Corporation (SMC),
* Assistance to North Eastern Development Finance Corporation Ltd. (NEDFi).
4.1 However, under the Plan allocation for 2007-08, Planning Commission has transferred a
scheme, Development of Inland Waterways” in North East, to this Ministry under the Central Plan
Scheme from Department of Shipping, Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways. On
the other hand, SMC has been dropped from the Central Plan scheme w.e.f. 2007-08 as a decision
has been taken to wind up the Corporation.
5.0 The Ministry implements the work mandated in Allocation of Business Rules, 1961, through
the formulation of appropriate policies, and implementation of specific Plan Programmes. The North
Eastern Council, Shillong also implements various Plan Schemes in the region.
6.0 Besides the above, the following Schemes are also under implementation in the form of
Assistance to the State Plans:
* Non-Iapsable Central Pool of Resources for North Eastern Region (NLCPR)
* Plan Schemes of the North Eastern Council (NEC)
* Infrastructure development projects under the special package for Bodoland Territorial
Council
* Development of Cities and Roads in the North Eastern States (Externally Aided
Projects)
6.1 Since 1998-99, all non-exempted Ministries/Departments of Government of India are
required to earmark at least 10% of their Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the development of
North Eastern States for creation of both economic and social infrastructure such as Power, Roads
and Bridges, Education, Health, Water Supply and Sanitation etc. in the region. The unutilized
portion of the 10% Plan allocation made by Ministries/Departments is credited at the end of each
financial year in a Reserve fund in the Public Account titled ‘Central Resource Pool for
Development of North Eastern Region’ and is known as the Non-Lapsable Central Pool of
Resources (NLCPR). The funds from this Pool are budgeted for financing new infrastructure
projects/schemes in the region under the NLCPR Plan Scheme.
6.2 The NLCPR is run for infrastructure development in almost all the major socio-economic
development sectors. For this purpose, projects are sanctioned for State sector as well as Central
sector projects/schemes. However, the funds available under the Central Pool are not meant to
supplement the normal Plan programmes either of the State Governments or Union Ministries/
Departments/Agencies.

3
7.0 The Ministry also supervises the work of two Public Sector Undertakings, viz., North
Eastern Handicrafts and Handlooms Development Corporation Ltd. (NEHHDC), North Eastern
Regiona1 Agricultural Marketing Corporation Ltd. (NERAMAC).
8.0 While the respective Ministries/Departments are responsible in respect of subjects allocated
to them, the Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region coordinates with various Ministries/
Departments of Government of India and the State Governments for various development and
welfare programmes/schemes/activities in North Eastern Region. In that direction, every week,
Secretary holds meetings with the officers of one of the Ministries and the concerned officers of
the State Governments.
9.0 To review the progress of Central Sector/Centrally Sponsored Schemes being implemented
by Central Ministries/Departments and also with a view to evolve policy framework for each sector
and also to ensure that maximum development takes place in the North-East Region, 16 sector-
specific high level meetings chaired by Minister, DoNER have been fixed for every month by
rotation in the Capitals and some other important towns of the eight North-East States.

NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES (NLCPR)
12.0 Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources (NLCPR) has been created by the Government of
India for the States of North Eastern Region out of the unspent amount of stipulated ten percent
of Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) of individual Ministries/Departments of Government of India for
financing infrastructural development projects in the North-East. However, at present, eighteen
Ministries/Departments have been exempted for allocating the stipulated ten percent of GBS for NE
States.
12.1 The Ministry in their written reply on the grounds on which eighteen Ministries/
Departments, particularly Petroleum and Natural Gas, Social Justice and Empowerment have been
exempted from allocating the stipulated ten percent of Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) for the NE
States stated that the following criteria are being followed for exempting a Ministry/Department
from earmarking 10% of their GBS for the North East:—

7
(a) Highly scientific Ministries/Departments with hardly any scope for work in the
North East;
(b) The Ministries/Departments having no direct involvement in the North East including
Sikkim such as the erstwhile Department of Ocean Development;
(c) The Ministries in which case the expenditure in the North Eastern Region was
‘Nil’ during the last three years; and
Government of India does not provide any Budgetary Support to Ministry of Petroleum and
Natural Gas, as the Ministry generates its own resources. The Ministry is therefore
exempted from making provisions to North East. As regards Ministry of Social Justice and
Empowerment, all Divisions of the Ministry other than the Scheduled Castes Division (SCD)
do earmark 10% of their Plan allocations to the North Eastern region. In the case of the
Scheduled Castes Division, only 2% of its GBS is allocated to the North East (NE),
considering the fact that the Scheduled Castes constitute less than 2% of the NE population.
12.2 The Secretary, Ministry of DoNER in her presentation informed the Committee that
consequent to the recommendation of the Committee in its 120th Report to review the exempted
Ministries, a review of exempted Ministries was carried out and exemption to Department of
Biotechnology was withdrawn. She added that guidelines to administer NLCPR were formulated in
December, 2001 and revised in July, 2004 to make them more comprehensive and that further
additional provisions were made in February 2007. An Inter ministerial Committee headed by
Secretary DONER has been constituted to administer NLCPR funded schemes. States are required
to submit priority list of socio-economic infrastructure projects with concept papers for such
projects by 31st December. Thereafter the Committee considers the list and selects appropriate
projects and after approval of Minister, DoNER the States are requested to submit Detailed Project
Reports (DPRs) of the selected projects. DPRs are examined in consultation with the Ministries of
Government of India and the proposals are put up before the Committee whose recommendations
are submitted for approval of Minister. She mentioned that eight hundred and forty two projects
worth Rs. 5,967.97 crore have been approved and Rs. 4,358.77 crore released to the States up
to 31 March, 2007.
12.2.1 Elaborating upon the monitoring of projects, the Secretary stated that rigorous monitoring
through Quarterly Progress Reports is done and funds are released only after certified utilization
of earlier releases. Progress is reviewed with the state authorities periodically. She further
mentioned that recently a system of online data entry has been introduced and monthly reviews
through video conference have started. Concerned officers of the State Governments are also
required to inspect projects regularly.

12.3 Replying to the query of the Chairperson about the large surrender of funds by certain
Ministries particularly the Ministry of HRD and Ministry of Health and Family Welfare from the
funds allocated for NE region, the Secretary during her oral evidence stated that she had reviewed
the position with the Ministry of Human Resource Development to find out the reasons for
surrender of funds last year. The Schemes under the Ministry of Human Resource Development
which had surrendered funds were Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan and Mid-day Meal Scheme. Sarva
Shiksha Abhiyan is a National Programme with norms applicable to all over the country. She further
stated that with these norms for the population of North-Eastern Region, which is four per cent
of the population of the country, it is difficult to utilize 10 per cent of the allocation in that Region,
unless the norms are modified to suit the Region. The Secretary pointed out another anomaly that
75 per cent of the grant under this Scheme goes from the Centre and 25 per cent is to be raised
by the States. The States could not raise even that 25 per cent and the Ministry of DoNER were
asked to give 15 per cent and, accordingly, Rs. 100 crore were given. Yet, the Ministry of Human
Resource Development had surrendered Rs. 475 crore.
12.4 Explaining about the position in regard to surrender of funds by the Ministry of Health and
Family Welfare, the Secretary, DoNER stated that spending in health was improving. The Joint
Secretary from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare informed the Committee that they were
trying to break away from the national norms and effect certain relaxations for programmes in the
North-Eastern Region. He further informed the Committee that since the beginning of allocation of
10 per cent, the Ministry had been surrendering 40 per cent to 45 per cent, primarily because of
the poor absorption capacity, lack of adequate manpower and lack of additional infrastructure in
the North-Eastern Region.
12.5 Due to this, North-East Division was formed in the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
and it came out with a special strategy for all the States of North-East. But, they were not
approved by the Planning Commission because they were outside the National Rural Health Mission.
The representative of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare further stated that they informed
the Planning Commission that NRHM alone cannot address the issue adequately and they have to
adopt certain NRHM norms such as strengthening district hospitals, State civil hospitals, medical
colleges, training, etc. On this, the Planning Commission informed the Ministry that an umbrella
programme had been brought and the Ministry may try to implement that first. He further stated
that with the increase in the budget in the Ministry in a sizeable manner, the allocation to North-
East also goes up considerably. It has gone up from Rs. 350 crore earlier to Rs. 1100 crore last

9
year. Out of Rs. 1100 crore, they were able to spend about Rs. 1000 crore and the surrender was
very less.
12.6 The Ministry have also informed that as per the recommendation made by the Committee,
allocations for Bodo Territorial Council and Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan have been made separately
instead of from NLCPR funds.
12.7 The Committee is happy to note that this year, no allocation has been made out of
NLCPR funds, for Bodo Territorial Council and Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan Schemes, as
recommended by the Committee last year. The Committee is also happy to note that the
Ministry has reviewed the Exempted List of Ministries/Departments in respect to allocation
of 10 per cent to the budget allocation to North-Eastern States. However, the Committee
expresses its concern that even after the review, only the Department of Biotechnology has
been taken out of the List of exempted Ministries. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its
recommendation made in the 120th Report on Demands for Grants for 2006-07 that the
other Ministries also may be taken out from the List of Exempted Ministries/Departments.
12.8 The Committee expresses its concern over the surrender of large sums of money by
different Ministries, which ultimately go into NLCPR. Even the Ministries of social sector
are also not able to spend 10 per cent of their budget in the Region. If the national norms
for any particular programme are found to be unsuitable for the North-Eastern Region, the
Ministry concerned should relax the norms according to the conditions of the North-Eastern
States so that the allocation made is spent in the year in which it was allocated.
NORTH-EASTERN COUNCIL
13.0 The NEC, which was created in 1971 and revitalized in 2002 as a Regional Planning Body,
during its three-decades of existence has been instrumental in releasing funds for the projects/
schemes beneficial to two or more states of the NER for speedier development of that region.
13.1 The Committee was informed that against the allocation of Rs. 3500 crore for Tenth Plan
for NEC, the actual allocation was Rs. 2511.50 crore only. Hence many projects of regional
importance identified by the States and the NEC could not be taken up. During the first four years,
against the total allocation of Rs. 1911.50 crore, the expenditure made was Rs. 1897.36 crore,
which is 99.26% For 2006-07, an amount of Rs. 600 crore has been provided for NEC Plan
schemes against which the expenditure during April, 2006 to February, 2007 is Rs. 401.00 crore.
13.2 While making presentation on Demands for Grants of the Ministry, the Secretary, Ministry
of DoNER informed the Committee that NEC in its 53rd meeting considered the draft Annual
Plan, 2007-08 document. The Member (NE), Planning Commission advised to review the
document. Based on the review, the NEC re-cast its Annual Plan, 2007-08 document. The revised
document was referred to the two full-time Members with request to screen and ensure that the
ongoing and proposed schemes did not overlap with Central and State Sector programmes and
suggest thrust areas and select pilot projects. Accordingly, two Committees chaired by two full time
Members of Planning Commission examined NEC’s ongoing and proposed schemes in consultation
with the Central Ministries and the Planning Commission. A Task Force chaired by Secretary,
Ministry of DoNER assessed committed liabilities on ongoing road projects and recommended
priority funding for their completion. She mentioned that NEC’s Annual Plan for 2007-08 restricted
to Rs. 600 crore as per outlay conveyed by the Planning Commission with the following thrust
Sectors:
Completion of ongoing projects;
Connectivity by Road and Air;

10
Power-Transmission/sub transmission viability gap funding for Small Hydro Power Projects
(SHPs);
Institution development; and
Capacity building and skill development
13.2.1 Secretary, Ministry of DoNER informed the Committee that maintenance of roads
constructed by NEC is the responsibility of State Governments as funds come out of their non-plan
funds. But, if the roads are in bad shape and the renovation is to be done, NEC can take up the
proposals. On the issue of new projects including small power projects the Committee was
informed that plan size of NEC has been pegged at the last year’s level i.e. Rs. 600 crore. NEC
is concentrating more on completion of its ongoing projects before taking up new projects. There
was a liability of Rs. 1277 crore. However, Planning Commission has approved the transfer of
North-East Police Academy (NEPA) from the Ministry of DoNER to Ministry of Home Af fairs and
Regional Institute of Medical Sciences, Imphal, Regional Institute of Para Medical and Nursing
Sciences (RIPM and NS), Aizwal and Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi Regional Institute of Medical
Health (LGBIMH), Tezpur from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare to the Ministry of
DoNER as a result of which there is a net additionality of about Rs. 55 crore.
13.2.2 The Secretary, Ministry of DoNER stated that though the Plan size of the NEC has been
pegged at the last year’s level, i.e., Rs. 600 crore, they have a huge liability of ongoing schemes.
They have not been able to provide assistance for any new road projects. NEC has a liability of
Rs. 1277 crore for the ongoing road projects only. The Ministry was not able even to provide one-
third of the liability. Out of the total allocation, only Rs. 285 crore has been given for the ongoing
projects against the committed liability of Rs. 1277 crore. It is going to take years to complete

GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ( ALLOCATION OF BUSINESS) RULES, 1961
17.0 The Go vernment of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 allocates the work of Hill
Area Development Programme and Border Area Development Programme in North-Eastern Region
to the Ministry of DoNER. However these two programmes have yet not been transferred to the
Ministry and they continue to be with the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Home Affairs,
respectively.
17.1 The S ecretary, Ministry of DoNER justifying the demand of the Ministry to transfer these
subjects stated that it being the only Ministry which is a territorial Ministry for the North-East and
responsible for the whole gamut of developmental work in the North-Eastern region and the border
area development programme in the North-Eastern region be transferred to it.
17.2 The Additional Secretary and Financial Advisor to both the Ministry of Home Affairs and
Ministry of DoNER stated that for the Border Area Development Programme the Ministry of
Finance provides extra support for the State plans. It is like the Gadgil Formula support to other
States. Even the Ministry of Home Affairs is not disbursing any fund. They merely recommend to
the Ministry of Finance to disburse a certain amount of extra support to State plans, as per the
plans, prepared by the State Governments. He further stated that while in the North-Eastern region
there are Indo-Bangladesh and Indo-China borders, there are borders with other countries on other
sides. He also stated that after the  recommendations of the GoM on National Security, the
Government of India had set up a Department of Border Management in the Ministry of Home
Affairs to focus on the problems of border areas. At that time, it was felt that the Border Area
Development Programme should be transferred to the Ministry of Home Affairs. However a part
of the work also falls within the domain of the Ministry of DoNER. He stated that it was being
tried to work out how these could work in mutual concert. The Ministry has implemented major
programmes of border fencing which also contribute to the development in some form. He further
stated that interaction of the border guarding forces with the local population was required so that
they enjoy mutual trust. The fact is that whether it is border development, which is the concern
of certain segments. All along the border most of the States are about the borders; but not all
the States are really contiguous to the border for which set policies are there in terms of ingress,
in terms of protection, etc. He felt that an integrated view of the programme in terms of
security perception and incidental development concerns would be a better way to approach the
problem in the national interest as the country has a large international border, land as well as
coastal.
17.3 T he Committee is concerned to note that even though the two programmes i.e., Hill
Area Development Programme (HADP) and Border Area Development Programme (BADP)
are shown in the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 to be with the
Ministry of DoNER but the programmes are yet to be transferred to it. The Committee feels
that it is not right on the part of the Government to show the programmes against a
Ministry without transferring them. The Committee recommends that the issue may be
resolved expeditiously.

19.0 The North Eastern Region has the distinct air connectivity constraint due to hilly terrain in
most of the North Eastern States, high cost of aircraft operation in the region and low fares. Intra

for those looking towards development of the region. In the Northeast region and other remote
areas, the management of airport infrastructure as well as air services are not commercially viable
because of low utilization and low fare structures etc. Given the topography and inaccessibility of
the region, the need for air services is much greater. Considering these aspects there have been
various proposals to enhance and develop Civil Aviation sector in NER. An agency-FAST

15 per cent and in annual terms it was Rs. 30 lakhs. Now, the norm is a cap of 30 per cent and
the amount is Rs. 1.50 crore.
21.1 Elaborating on the New Policy it was stated that capital investment subsidy has been
enhanced from 15 per cent of investment in plant and machinery to 30 per cent and the limit for
automatic approval of subsidy has been pegged at Rs. 1.5 crore per unit. For grant of capital
investment subsidy higher than Rs. 1.5 crore and upto the maximum limit of Rs. 30 crore, the
empowered Committee under the Chairmanship of Secretary, Department of Industrial Policy and
Promotion has been enforced, Cabinet is empowered to approve proposals beyond Rs. 30 crore.
21.2 It was mentioned that 100 per cent excise duty subsidy, 100 per cent income tax
exemption, interest subsidy at 3 per cent on working capital and transport subsidy will also
continue. Another feature of the new Policy is that the service sector has been brought in. Earlier,

20
it was given to the manufacturing sector only. Now, service sector like hotels, nursing homes, etc.
are also covered. The Power generation up to 10 MW is also covered under the new Policy.
21.3 The Ministry in its written submissions stated as under:
“The Government of India has approved a package of fiscal incentives and other
concessions for the North Eastern Region namely the ‘North East Industrial and Investment
Promotion Policy (NEIIPP), 2007’ . This package has come into effect from 1.4.2007 and
includes Sikkim as a part of the region.
It may be mentioned that the North East Industrial Policy (NEIP), 1997 announced earlier
on 24.12.1997 covered the States of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya,
Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura. A similar package of incentives known as “New Industrial
Policy and other concessions for the State of Sikkim” was announced separetely for the
State of Sikkim on 23.12.2002, when it became a part of the Norih East Council (NEC).
NEIIPP, 2007 is more generous than the earlier North East Industrial Policy, 1997 (NEIP,
1997) in terms of incentives and subsidies made available to various industries, both new
and earlier units wanting to undergo expansion NEIPP, 2007 also includes Sikkim in
addition to the other seven States of North East. Consequently, the “New Industrial Policy
and other concessions for the State of Sikkim” announced on 23.12.2002 and the schemes
thereunder i.e. Central Capital Investment Subsidy Scheme, 2002, Central Interest Subsidy
Scheme, 2002 and Central Comprehensive Insurance Scheme, 2002 are discontinued with
effect from 1.04.2007.”

AGRICULTURE SECTOR
22.0 The North Eastern Region has rich natural resources with fertile land, thick forests and vast
potential in the agriculture and allied sectors. The economy of the region continues to be
predominantly agrarian. A large number of the people in the hills still follow the traditional practice
of shifting cultivation. Hence agriculture and allied sector activities should aim to introduce better
agricultural practices with a view to improve production and productivity. Keeping this in view, the
NEC has supported schemes like introduction of new technology for cultivation of fruits, etc.
However, due to small plan allocation, their impact is very limited. The Ministry of Agriculture has
also undertaken several schemes for the region.
22.1 The Secretary, Ministry of DoNER, during her oral submission, stated that the North East
Region has a natural advantage in agriculture and agro products. There is a Horticulture Mission
which is dedicated to the North-East, i.e., North-East Horticulture Mission and the National

21
Bamboo Mission was initiated in November last year. The National Bamboo Mission (NBM) has
been approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) on 27.10.2006 at a cost
of Rs. 568.23 crore. This is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme with 100% Central Assistance which
will be implemented during 2006-07 and first four years of the Eleventh Five Year Plan. The
scheme is being implemented by Ministry of Agriculture. National Fisheries Development
Programme is proposed to be launched this year. She, therefore, stated that in view of these
programmes being run by various Central Ministries, NEC and the Ministry of DoNER would not
like to duplicate these efforts. But, both the Ministry of DoNER and the NEC have a role in seeing
how these national programmes get implemented. The Ministry constantly tries to do that, because
the NEC is an additional planning body and the Ministry of DoNER, a coordinating Ministry, which
is supposed to see how programmes are implemented and to influence policies with the Central
Ministries. Recently, the Ministry had a review of various Commodity Boards in the North-Eastern
region. One of the problems with the tea industry, according to the Secretary, is that there are
about 50,000 small tea growers in Assam alone who do not have pattas. If they don’t have pattas,
it would be difficult for them to carry on improvements, and they cannot take loans because
ownership is not with them. The Chief Minister of Assam assured that very scon pattas would be
issued to the small tea growers. She agreed that Tripura is second only to Kerala in rubber
production. They can overtake Kerala because Kerala is already saturated, whereas in Tripura there
is a lot of land where cultivation of rubber can be expanded. She said that Mizoram, Nagaland were
also very highly suited for rubber. A Committee has been set up in the NEC under one of the full-
time members, to go into the whole issue.
22.2 In regard to ginger processing and marketing, the representative of the Ministry and
Chairman, NERAMAC stated that last year only a novel experiment from Meghalaya for ginger
procurement and marketing was started on a nominal commission basis so as to meet the
expenditure. Ginger is procured by farmers’ societies, taken to different markets at Kolkata, Delhi
and other places and a nominal commission charged for the expenditure on transport, etc. They
get the market price. He felt that the experiment has been very successful. He further stated that
a ginger processing unit has been established in Burnihat which will be fully functional from the
next year. He stated that ginger powder and ginger oil are being processed there and in Tripura,
ginger paste and the other materials will be processed.
22.3 In regard to the problem of Shifting/Jhoom cultivators, the Secretary stated that the
Livelihood Project is being implemented among shifting cultivators. It is shifting cultivators who
have been brought under this Livelihood Project. The community has been sensitised; organised to
take care of the environment and to take sustainable activities. She also stated that the project is
so successful that now it is being upscaled under the World Bank aided project, which is much
larger, and the livelihood issue, in particular the issue of shifting cultivation, is being addressed
under that project.
22.4 So far as horticulture is concerned, the Secretary stated that there are funds under the
National Horticulture Mission and there is a programme dedicated to the North-Eastern Region.
However, Manipur is not able to access funds, and it would be taken up with the Commissioner
of Horticulture.
22.5 She also stated that NER has a huge potential in herbal, medicinal and aromatic plants, due
to which exemption given to the Department of Biotechnology from investment of 10% of its GBS
has been withdrawn.
22.6 The Committee agrees that agriculture sector plays a predominant role in the North-
Eastern Region. There is a huge potential for increasing the revenue and the allied
industries in the North-Eastern Region by which employment opportunities can be increased
and there can be more income-generating activities. However, the investment being made

22
by NEC under agriculture and allied sector is only 1.74 per cent of the total Plan allocation
which is very meagre and it will not have any significant impact. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that allocation may be increased for agricultural activities under the Plan
allocation of NEC. Similarly, the Ministry of Agriculture may also increase the agricultural
and allied activities in the North-Eastern Region. The Committee feels happy to learn that
Bamboo Mission was initiated during 2006-07 with a budget of Rs. 568.23 crore. If the
Mission is implemented in the right earnest, it is going to have a great impact on the
economy of the States.
22.7 The Committee takes a serious note of the fact that the small tea growers in Assam,
who are about 50,000 in number, do not have pattas due to which they are not able to carry
out any improvements on the land nor are they able to get loans. The Committee makes
a strong recommendation that immediate steps may be taken to ensure that the small tea
growers are issued pattas at the earliest. The Committee also agrees that the North-Eastern
States have a great potential for rubber production. The Committee expresses its concern
that production of rubber in the North-Eastern States has not been taken up very seriously
so far. The Report of the Committee set up by NEC under one of the full-time Members,
when submitted, may be examined thoroughly and immediate steps may be taken to
introduce rubber plantation in the North-Eastern States wherever it has potential. If
necessary, separate technology mission may be initiated for this purpose exclusively for
North-Eastern States with separate budgetary provision. The Committee takes note of the
fact that North-Eastern States have a great potential for ginger production. However, there
is no proper system for marketing and for processing of the ginger produced to enable the
farmers to sell their produce at a profitable rate. The Committee is happy to learn that the
initiative already taken by the Government has been very successful but the Government
should not be complacent with this. This is only an initial effort and a lot more has to be
done for marketing of the produces such as ginger of the North-Eastern States. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that processing facilities may be established for all the
agricultural produce along with marketing facilities.

VISION NER 2020
24.0 The NEC had initiated a process of preparing a 15-year perspective plan for integrated and
holistic development of NER through a process of participatory planning. The vision document was
sought to be perceived as a people’s plan and the people must develop faith therein and see
constructive avenues being made available to them. During the year 2006-07, NEC initiated a mass
exercise for formulation of the vision document introducing for the first time a paradigm shift in
the planning process with a bottom up approach. Chairman, NEC, had in 2005, constituted a
Committee consisting of the nominated Members and Secretary, NEC, to formulate the “North East
Vision 2020” document as a road map for the rapid economic and social development of the North
Eastern Region. More recently, NEC has engaged the National Institute of Public Finance and
Policy (NIPFP), New Delhi to prepare the Vision document. To guide preparation of an
implementable Vision document a high level Steering Committee chaired by Shri B.G. Verghese has
also been constituted. It is expected that the document, when finalized, will enable NEC to play
its role as a regional planning body more effectively. The Ministry also stated that the National
Institute of Public Finance and Policy (NIPFP), New Delhi, which has been entrusted the task to
prepare vision NER 2020, has agreed to submit it by November, 2007.
24.1 The Vision document is undoubtedly the most ambitious project of NEC since its
inception. To achieve the developmental goals of a region, which has lagged far behind from
the mainstream, such measures are a necessity. The final version of the document was
scheduled to be in place by June, 2006. The Committee is of the view that of the fifteen
years period, two more years would be lost in finalizing the document. Hence, it feels that
efforts be taken to complete such an ambitious document on time, without further delay.
24.2 The Committee also reiterates its earlier recommendation for including the
establishment of national level sports schools and institutes like National Institute of
Fashion Technology in the region in the Vision 2020. Similarly, it also recommends that
Road and Power sectors should form an important part of the document.

the takeover of three Institutions; viz., RIM in Imphal, Manipur, Tejpur Mental Health Hospital and
the RIPANS, Aizwal the expenditure of the Ministry will also go up. These would be developed as
Centers of Excellence. He also stated that there is a proposal for setting up one medical college
in each State. But Arunachal is going to be a problem going by the MCI norms, which are very
stringent. He further stated that taking into account the special needs of region and special

recommended by the Committee earlier.
TRADE PROMOTION
26.0 A bout the steps being taken for promotion of trade and industries in the North Eastern
Region, the Ministry in its written reply stated that according to the Department of Commerce,
assistance is provided to the NE States for creation of infrastructure under the Assistance to States
for Development of Export Infrastructure and Allied Activities (ASIDE) Scheme. 10% of ASIDE
Scheme outlay is allocated to the North Eastern States including Sikkim. An allocation of
Rs. 45 crore has been made for the North Eastern Region under ASIDE Scheme during 2006-2007.
26.1 The Ministry has informed that the major infrastructure projects taken up by the
Departrnent of Commerce under ASIDE Scheme in the North Eastern Region are as follows:—
Setting up of a Trade Centre at Guwahati at a cost of Rs. 17 crore:
The entire amount of Rs. 17 crore has been released to the India Trade Promotion
Organisation. Demand of additional fund of Rs. 3.38 crore has been received on account
of cost escalation and increase in area of construction and the same has been approved by
Empowered Committee (EC) on Assistance to States for Infrastructure Development and
Allied Activities (ASIDE) Scheme on 07.11.2006. In addition Rs. 42.5 lakh had been
requested by ITPO for essential furnishing of the centre. However, Rs. 36.067 lakh have
been approved for the purpose. On 19.2.2007 sanction for release of Rs. 2.067 crore
including Rs. 36.067 lakh for furnishing has been issued. The work is under progress.
Development of infrastructure at Land Customs Station Zokhawthar in Mizoram:
An amount of Rs. 4.22 crore has been released to the implementing agency Border Road
Organisation (BRO) for the construction of a composite building at Zokhawthar, which is
in progress.
Development of infrastructure at the Border Trade Mart at Sherathang in Sikkim to
operationalise border trade with China through Nathula Pass:
A sum of Rs. 6.36 crore has been released to the Government of Sikkim for this purpose
so far.
Infrastractural facilities like a warehouse and trade center with a conference hall
have been provided at LCS Moreh. in Manipur.
Besides, an Export Development Fund has been created in the Department for promoting
activities which have a linkage with exports from the region. So far an amount of Rs. 40.82
crore has been sanctioned for 48 projects under the scheme.

26.2 The Ministry further informed that with the Technical Assistance (TA) of ADB a project for
the North Eastern States Trade and Investment Creative Initiative (NES-TICI) has been undertaken
which seeks to build on the locational advantage of the NER, which borders five Countries, viz.,
Bangladesh, Myanmar, China, Bhutan and Nepal. Those products in which NER has a comparative
advantage and trading points through which trade can be promoted are to be identified. The TA
also seeks to identify policies, legal and constitutional constraints hampering trade, and to develop

all NE States except Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim were requested to furnish comments on the
draft final report prepared by the ADB Consultants on the North Eastern Trade and Investment
Creation Initiatives (NES-YICI). A Secretary level meeting will be convened after receipt of
comments from all the NE States, the Ministry stated.
26.4 The Secretary, while replying to the issue of trade points, stated that the Ministry is trying
to take up their concerns with the Department of Border Management. There are a lot of land
custom stations operating in the North-Eastern region. There are demands for opening more,
because Nagaland does not have any, and all its borders are with Myanmar; which are international.
They don’t have a land custom station. Arunachal Pradesh also does not have any. Other States
want more stations. Further these stations are not fully equipped. The infrastructure is not
satisfactory. Although the Ministry of Commerce, through one of its programmes, has taken up
infrastructure development, many of the roads are in poor shape. The roads leading to these
borders and the land custom stations are in poor shape.
26.5 Trade promotion is an important aspect for the development of any region and the
North-Eastern Region is no exception to it. The existing infrastructure is very inadequate to
give sufficient avenues to the North-Eastern Region for trade promotion. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry may make an overall assessment of the requirement of trade
promotion activities for the North-Eastern Region and necessary steps may be taken in this
regard. The trade centre proposed to be set up at Guwahati is a good proposal for promoting
trade and industry not only in Assam but in the entire North-Eastern Region. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that the Centre may be commissioned as early as possible so that it
may commence functioning soon. The project for the North-Eastern States’ trade and
investment, creative initiative undertaken with the technical assistance of Asian Development
Bank (ADB) is an important project for the overall growth of trade and industry in the North-
Eastern States. The Draft Report submitted by the ADB Consultants may be examined at the
earliest and a final decision may be taken soon to make the project functional. The
Committee also learned that the North-Eastern States are asking for more land custom
stations and development of infrastructure in the existing stations. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry may examine these issues and take up with the concerned
Ministries and take an early decision. Development of infrastructure to the custom stations
is very essential because without proper infrastructure, the custom stations will not be
successful. The Ministry of DoNER may consider this aspect and take up the infrastructure
development for custom stations on a priority basis with separate allocation, if required.

OBSERV ATIONS/CONCLUSIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS — AT A GLANCE
NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES (NLCPR)
The Committee is happy to note that this year, no allocation has been made out of
NLCPR funds, for Bodo Territorial Council and Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan Schemes, as
recommended by the Committee last year. The Committee is also happy to note that the
Ministry has reviewed the Exempted List of Ministries/Departments in respect to allocation
of 10 per cent to the budget allocation to North-Eastern States. However, the Committee
expresses its concern that even after the review, only the Department of Biotechnology has
been taken out of the List of exempted Ministries. The Committee, therefore, reiterates its
recommendation made in the 120th Report on Demands for Grants for 2006-07 that the
other Ministries also may be taken out from the List of Exempted Ministries/Departments.
(Para 12.7)
The Committee expresses its concern over the surrender of large sums of money by
different Ministries, which ultimately go into NLCPR. Even the Ministries of social sector
are also not able to spend 10 per cent of their budget in the Region. If the national norms
for any particular programme are found to be unsuitable for the North-Eastern Region,
the Ministry concerned should relax the norms according to the conditions of the
North-Eastern States so that the allocation made is spent in the year in which it was
allocated. (Para 12.8)
NORTH-EASTERN COUNCIL
The Committee wishes to recall its recommendation made in its One Hundred
Twentieth Report wherein it had expressed its concern that Tenth Plan outlay for NEC had
not been adhered to till 2006-07 which was the terminal year for the Tenth Plan. The
Committee is now informed that the actual allocation to NEC during Tenth Plan has been
Rs. 2511.50 crores against Rs. 3500 crores. The Committee while reiterating its earlier
recommendations feels that NEC is entitled to additional allocation of Rs. 988.50 crores
during Eleventh Plan as carry over of un allocated outlay from Tenth Plan. (Para 13.3)
The Committee expresses its concern that though NEC is in a position to absorb the
funds, the Plan schemes of NEC are languishing due to lack of it. The Committee is
surprised to note that there is a committed liability of about Rs. 1277 crore for various on
going projects/schemes. With the present allocation, the projects will take very long for
completion. In addition, there will be escalation of costs year after year. The better solution
for this could be to transfer funds from NLCPR for the projects of NEC. The funds that had
accrued for a particular sector in NLCPR may be transferred to the same sector under any
NEC projects. The Committee desires that a mechanism may be evolved for such a transfer
of funds to enable the early completion of the Plan projects under NEC. The Committee also
takes note of the fact that certain Plan schemes under NEC would be transferred to other
Ministries. The Committee desires that this may be done at the earliest so that other Plan
schemes may get sufficient funds during the current year. (Para 13.4)
ADVOCACY, PUBLICITY, CAPACITY BUILDI NG AND TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE
The Committee do recognise the importance of showcasing North-East through Plan
32

33
Schemes such as Advocacy and Publicity and appreciates the efforts of the Ministry
whereunder it had taken such programmes in capital cities and other cities of various States
during last year. The Committee however wants to impress upon the Ministry to adopt some
innovative methods. (Para 14.5)
The Committee expresses its concern over the undue and uncalled for delay in pilot
and airhostess training. It is surprising to note that in the entire year the process of
identifying the institute for training could not be completed. The Committee takes strong
exception to such avoidable delays which also result in cost overrun. The Committee
recommends that the process of identifying the institute may be completed within next
three months and the training may be conducted at the earliest. (Para 14.6)
EXTERNALLY AIDED PROJECTS
The Committee while appreciating the efforts of the Ministry in coming out with
several projects under the Externally Aided Projects scheme reiterates its earlier
recommendation that the faster such schemes are processed the better it would be. The
Planning Commission may give ‘in principle’ approval for the North Eastern State Roads
Project early so that the remaining processes may be completed during the current financial
year and the project started. The Committee also strongly recommends that atleast the first
phase of North Eastern Urban Development Project may be put in place during the current
financial year. The Committee cautions the Ministry about the user charges to be recovered
in six years. This aspect should be carefully considered before taking a final view. The
Committee is also of the view that the 10% loan component, which has been assigned to
the State Governments to be raised on their own, should be re-examined in light of the
difficulties being faced by the North East States. (Para 15.4)
NERAMAC
The Committee was informed that the allocation of Rs. 2 crore as loan to NERAMAC
was received at RE 2006-07 stage and this year also the Ministry is hopeful to get the same
at RE stage. The Committee does not appreciate this ad-hoc approach to such an
organisation like NERAMAC which has one fruit processing unit for the whole of North
East. The Committee also recommends that necessary steps may be taken to strengthen the
organisation. More units of this organisation may be started in various other States in
North-East. (Para 16.1)
GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ( ALLOCATION OF BUSINESS) RULES, 1961
The Committee is concerned to note that even though the two programmes i.e., Hill
Area Development Programme (HADP) and Border Area Development Programme (BADP)
are shown in the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961 to be with the
Ministry of DoNER but the programmes are yet to be transferred to it. The Committee feels
that it is not right on the part of the Government to show the programmes against a
Ministry without transferring them. The Committee recommends that the issue may be
resolved expeditiously. (Para 17.3)
PROMOTION OF TOURISM AND HOSPITALITY
18.3 The Committee feels that the NE region has an excellent untapped tourist potential.
If the potential is fully tapped with sufficient infrastructure, there would be all sided
development of the region including a large number of employment opportunities. The

34
Committee is happy to note that several private parties are showing interest in the hotel
and tourism sector in North-East. However, there is a long way to go and a lot of investment
is required to develop tourist infrastructure in the NER to make it one of the best tourist
attractions not just of the country but of the world as well. The Committee desires that a
long-term plan and a roadmap may be prepared for creation of tourism infrastructure on
a fast track basis. (Para 18.3)
CIVIL A VIATION
The Committee while noting the reply of the Ministry is of the opinion that civil
aviation should be declared a priority sector for NER. It also feels that the Government
should be more proactive in implementing its announcement for the region and sectors like
Civil Aviation should get priority and special impetus. The Committee’s considered view is
that a boost to Civil Aviation in the region and improving air connectivity within the region
as well as from the rest of the country is of paramount importance if the region has to be
brought to the mainstream. The Committee recommends that all the airports in the NER
may be upgraded at the earliest. The Committee is dismayed over the fact that Planning
Commission has yet not given the ‘in principle’ approval for ‘Teju’ airport, despite the
recommendation made by it in its 120th Report on Demands for Grants (2006-07) of
Ministry of DoNER. The Committee is also of the view that the offer of the Ministry of
Defence for providing trained pilots on permanent absorption basis should be gladly accepted
by the Ministry of Civil Aviation, if it is really serious about development of civil aviation
in the region and that Ministry should take early decisions on such important proposals.
They should also expedite the matter relating to technical assistance for the Pilot Training
Institute at Lilabari. (Para 19.6)
ROPEWAY AT TAWANG
The Committee is happy to note that funds had been released for Tawang Ropeway
in July 2006 and construction has already started. The Committee recalls that the
foundation for the Ropeway was laid by the President of India way back in 2002. Since there
was no progress in the work, the Committee in its 120th Report of Demands for Grants
(2006-07) expressed its dismay over the extraordinary delay in the matter. Now that the
funds have been released and work has already commenced, the Committee urges upon the
Ministry to ensure that the work is finished as per the target fixed. (Para 20.1)
PROMOTION OF INDUSTRIES
The Committee notes the contents of the North-East Industrial and Investment
Promotion Policy (NEIIPP), 2007 vis-a-vis NE Industrial Policy, 1997 and also the negative list
whereunder industries like Tobacco, Pan Masala, Plastic Carry bags of less than 20 microns
and some specified goods produced by petroleum oil and gas refineries are not eligible for
benefits under NEIIPP, 2007. The Committee feels that the incentives and subsidies
mentioned in the new policy if implemented in the right earnest would go a long way in giving
a fillip to the tourism, hospitality and power sectors. The Committee is also of the considered
view that considering the region’s diversity, the Government should ensure that the small
scale and agro based industries get support in the new Industrial Policy. (Para 21.4)
AGRICULTURE SECTOR
The Committee agrees that agriculture sector plays a predominant role in the North-
Eastern Region. There is a huge potential for increasing the revenue and the allied

35
industries in the North-Eastern Region by which employment opportunities can be increased
and there can be more income-generating activities. However, the investment being made
by NEC under agriculture and allied sector is only 1.74 per cent of the total Plan allocation
which is very meagre and it will not have any significant impact. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that allocation may be increased for agricultural activities under the Plan
allocation of NEC. Similarly, the Ministry of Agriculture may also increase the agricultural
and allied activities in the North-Eastern Region. The Committee feels happy to learn that
Bamboo Mission was initiated during 2006-07 with a budget of Rs. 568.23 crore. If the
Mission is implemented in the right earnest, it is going to have a great impact on the
economy of the States. (Para 22.6)
The Committee takes a serious note of the fact that the small tea growers in Assam,
who are about 50,000 in number, do not have pattas due to which they are not able to carry
out any improvements on the land nor are they able to get loans. The Committee makes
a strong recommendation that immediate steps may be taken to ensure that the small tea
growers are issued pattas at the earliest. The Committee also agrees that the North-Eastern
States have a great potential for rubber production. The Committee expresses its concern
that production of rubber in the North-Eastern States has not been taken up very seriously
so far. The Report of the Committee set up by NEC under one of the full-time Members,
if submitted, may be examined thoroughly and immediate steps may be taken to introduce
rubber plantation in the North-Eastern States wherever it has potential. If necessary,
separate technology mission may be initiated for this purpose exclusively for North-Eastern
States with separate budgetary provision. The Committee takes note of the fact that North-
Eastern States have a great potential for ginger production. However, there is no proper
system for marketing and for processing of the ginger produced to enable the farmers to
sell their produce at a profitable rate. The Committee is happy to learn that the initiative
already taken by the Government has been very successful but the Government should not
be complacent with this. This is only an initial effort and a lot more has to be done for
marketing of the produces such as ginger of the North-Eastern States. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that processing facilities may be established for all the agricultural
produce along with marketing facilities. (Para 22.7)
MEDICINAL PLANTS
The Committee is happy to note that emphasis is being given on utilizing the
potential of medicinal plants in the country by the National Medicinal Plants Board. Out of
the total allocation of Rs. 80 crore for current financial year, 10% will be spent on NER
and similarly about Rs. 100 crore would be available in Eleventh Plan. The Committee
recommends that the fund proposed to be earmarked for the purpose be utilized fully in the
region. Appropriate plans may be drawn for better utilization of the funds. The Committee
feels that more positive initiatives need to be taken to develop the sector in the naturally
rich North-East Region. (Para 23.2)
VISION NER 2020
The Vision document is undoubtedly the most ambitious project of NEC since its
inception. To achieve the developmental goals of a region, which has lagged far behind from
the mainstream, such measures are a necessity. The final version of the document was
scheduled to be in place by June, 2006. The Committee is of the view that of the fifteen
years period, two more years would be lost in finalizing the document. Hence, it feels that
efforts be taken to complete such an ambitious document on time, without further delay.
(Para 24.1)

36
The Committee also reiterates its earlier recommendation for including the
establishment of national level sports schools and institutes like National Institute of
Fashion Technology in the region in the Vision 2020. Similarly, it also recommends that
Road and Power sectors should form an important part of the document. (Para 24.2)
HEALTH SYSTEM
Considering the different demography, topography and the needs of the region,
national norms of MCI for setting up medical colleges cannot be applied there. The
Committee feels that the norms should not come in the way of providing health
infrastructure in the NE Region. The Committee recommends that the norms may be
revised for NER. Steps may also be taken to start medical colleges in every State as
recommended by the Committee earlier. (Para 25.1)
TRADE PROMOTION
Trade promotion is an important aspect for the development of any region and the
North-Eastern Region is no exception to it. The existing infrastructure is very inadequate
to give sufficient avenues to the North-Eastern Region for trade promotion. The Committee
recommends that the Ministry may make an overall assessment of the requirement of trade
promotion activities for the North-Eastern Region and necessary steps may be taken in this
regard. The trade centre proposed to be set up at Guwahati is a good proposal for promoting
trade and industry not only in Assam but in the entire North-Eastern Region. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Centre may be commissioned as early as
possible so that it may commence functioning soon. The project for the North-Eastern
States’ trade and investment, creative initiative undertaken with the technical assistance of
Asian Development Bank (ADB) is an important project for the overall growth of trade and
industry in the North-Eastern States. The Draft Report submitted by the ADB Consultants
may be examined at the earliest and a final decision may be taken soon to make the project
functional. The Committee also learned that the North-Eastern States are asking for more
land custom stations and development of infrastructure in the existing stations. The
Committee recommends that the Ministry may examine these issues and take up with the
concerned Ministries and take an early decision. Development of infrastructure to the
custom stations is very essential because without proper infrastructure, the custom stations
will not be successful. The Ministry of DoNER may consider this aspect and take up the
infrastructure development for custom stations on a priority basis with separate allocation,
if required. (Para 26.5)
SPECIAL INITIATIVES
The Committee is happy to note that in the meeting chaired by the Prime Minister
on 16.10.2006, decisions had been taken inter-alia that the line Ministries would consult
Ministry of DoNER before finalizing any scheme to be implemented in North East. The
Committee recommends that this decision should be formalized by incorporating the same
in the Allocation of Business Rules, 1961 and by effectively communicating the same to all
Ministries/Departments so that there is no confusion in any quarter in this regard.
(Para 27.1)
