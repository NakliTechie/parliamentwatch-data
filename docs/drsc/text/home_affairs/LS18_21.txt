REPORT 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Home Affairs, having been authorised by the 
Committee, present this Twenty-first Report of the Committee relating to the Hire-Purchase 
(Amendment) Bill•, 1989. The Bill seeks to amend the Hire-Purchase Act , 1972. 
2. In pursuance of the rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha referred•• the Bill, as introduced in the Rajya 
Sabha on the 5th May, 1989 and pending therein, to the Committee, for examination and 
report. 
3. The Committee considered the Bill and heard the representative of the Ministry of 
Law, Justice and Company Affairs in its meeting held on the 29th November, 1994. 
4. While considering the Bill, the Committee took note of the following documents/ 
information placed before it:-
(i) Background note on the Bill; 
(ii) Note explaining in chronological order the development from the enactment of 
the Hire Purchase Act, 1972 to the introduction of the Hire Purchase 
(Amendment) Bill, 1989; and 
(iii) Ninety-Second Report of the Committee on Petitions, Rajya Sabha relating to 
the question of bringing into force the Hire-Purchase Act , 1972. 
5. The Committee adopted the Report in its meeting held on the 19th July, 1995. 
6. From the information made available to the Committee and .the submission made by 
the representative of the Ministry, the Committee notes that the Hire-Purchase Act, 1972 
was enacted pursuant to the recommendations of the Law Commission contained in its 
Twentieth Report and the recommendations made by the Joint Committee of Parliament on 
the Hire Purchase Bill, 1968, mainly to regulate the hire-purchase transactions. befbre the 
Act could be enforced, several representations were received from various quarters against 
bringing the Act into force on account of certain difficulties/anomalies pointed out in its 
implementation. As a result of these representations, notifications giving effect to the Act 
had to be rescinded twice by the Government. The Committee on Petitions, Rajya Sabha, 
which had an occasion to examine the hire-Purchase Act, 1972, with particular reference to 
its non-enforcement, recommended that the Act be brought into force forthwith. It is in this 
back-drop that the Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Bill, 1989 was introduced in the Rajya 
Sabha on the 5th May, 1989 to overcome the main difficulties noticed. 
7. The Committee notes with serious concern that one of the reasons for non­
enforcement of the Hire Purchase Act, 1972 even after a lapse of 23 years was that no 
decision could be taken regarding the Ministry which would administer it. it is reall)' 
surprising that such a vital issue remained undecided for so long. 
8. As a result of intervention of the Chairman of the Committee, the Ministry of Law, 
Justice and Company Affairs, subsequently informed- "the question as to whether the 
administration of the Hire-Purchase Act, 1972 needs to be transferred to some other 
Ministry has been reconsidered and in view of the fact that the Act needs no separate

9. The Committee af~er considering the Bill and other aspects related thereto makes the 
following recommendationslobservations:-
(i) The Hire Purchase Act since its passage in 1972 has remained in-operative till now. 
In between, great many socio-economic changes, particularly during the last couple 
of years, have taken place. As the legislation intends to govern the rights and 
duties of both the business and consumer communities, it becomes imperative to 
have these changes reflected in the legislation, otherwise the entire exercise in this 
regard may prove to be futile. 
(ii) The Bill which seeks to amend comprehensively nearly half of the provisions of the 
Hire Purchase Act, 1972, in some cases substituting the entire provisions of the 
Act, has been brought forward pending enforcement of the Principal Act. It, 
therefore, necessitates the desirability of enacting a fresh legislation of the subject 
instead of doing complicated patch-work here and there in the Act. 
(iii) Some of the provisions of the Bill are too technical and intricate to be 
comprehended by a common man, who is concerned with the subject matter of the 
Bill. Those provisions, therefore, need to be simplified. 
10. In view of the above, the Committee is of the considered view that the present Bill 
may not suffice in achieving the desired objectives. The Committee, therefore, recommends 
that Government may consider referring the whole issue of hire-purchase to the Law 
Commission for an in-depth examination, keeping in view the above observations and 
thereafter bring in a fresh comprehensive legislation on the subject before Parliament, as 
expeditiously as possible.
