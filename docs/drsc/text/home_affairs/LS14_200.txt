1.1 TOTAL REVENUE AND CAPITAL OUTLAY 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.1.1 The Committee found that the Ministry had ask ed for around Rs. 3850 crore as grants but was 
given only Rs. 2400 crore and this deduction was do ne because the Ministry could not spend the money 
allotted in the last financial year. The Committee is also given to understand that there is a change in 
the method of fund allocation to the Ministries. No w the budget allocation is done on realistic 
expenditure and not on the notional expenditure. However, the case with the NER is different from other 
Ministries, as Utilisation Certificates (UCs), one of the most important criteria for further release of 
grants, remain pending with the NE states. As per t he admission of the Secretary, DoNER, and also 
mentioned in the Outcome Budget, UCs worth Rs. 961 crore are pending. This means delay in 
submission of the UCs is affecting the budget alloc ation and in turn, it is hampering the growth and 
development in the NER. The Committee in its 186 
th  Report on the Demands for Grants (2015-16) had 
suggested that the MoDONER in consultation with Nor th Eastern States, NITI Aayog and Ministry of 
Finance may explore a different mechanism like cons idering a different time period (when there was 
normal weather in NER) for assessment of utilisatio n of funds and consequent increase/decrease in 
allocation at RE stage. The Committee had also advi sed that the Ministry must continually exhort state s 
to ensure timely submission of UC/requisite documen ts for timely release of funds for the projects. Th e 
Committee reiterates its recommendations on the issue of UCs given in the 186 th  Report. 
 
(Para 2.2.7 of 196 th  Report) 
Action Taken 
 
1.1.2 The Ministry of DoNER has taken a number of s teps to ensure the quicker processing of the 
proposals under the NLCPR scheme of the Ministry.  Two-pronged action has been taken on this front.  
One, as a part of Business Process Re-engineering ( BPR), the guidelines have been revised which 
incentivize faster completion of the projects taken  up under the scheme.  Second, the entire process 
starting from conceptualization of the project till  completion of the project has been automated.  
Automation has been enabled by launching NLCPR Proj ect Portal through which all the required 
documents are received online in the Ministry.  Thi s facilitates greater accountability as well as 
transparency in the entire process.   
 
1.1.3 Not only this, the process of monitoring and control has been strengthened substantially.  This 
has been achieved by means of constant review of th e progress of utilisation of funds with the State 
Governments at all levels. The officials of the Min istry are regularly visiting and camping in the Sta te 
Capitals for expediting the Utilisation Certificate s. Apart from visit by the senior officials to the States 
for review of the projects, the Ministry is in cons tant touch with the officials concerned of the Stat e 
Governments through video-conferencing and other IT tools like Whatsapp, SMSes and E-mails.    
 
1.1.4 The aforesaid steps have resulted in progress  in the utilisation of funds, completion of project s 
and quality of projects.   
 
1.1.5 Due to all these efforts made by the Ministry , the pending UCs under the NLCPR Scheme has 
come down drastically to the level of Rs. 322.30 cr ore by the end of October, 2016 from its earlier 
figure of Rs.1158.95 crore as on 01.04.2016.  The number of completed projects has risen from 30 in the

year 2015-16 to 76 so far in this fiscal year. The fund utilisation has risen by almost 90% this year

relatives/friends and traders/money-lenders, for bo th consumption and productive purposes. Interest 
rates are very high. Therefore, NEDFi should earmar k certain funds for helping the agro-horti sector 
and also help to revive defunct food processing uni ts. The Ministry should provide all the support 
needed by the NEDFi in carrying out its mandate suc cessfully and help in the growth and development

1.4 NON-LAPSABLE CENTRAL POOL OF RESOURCES FOR NER 
 
Recommendation 
 
1.4.1 The Committee has noted that out of total pro jects sanctioned of Rs. 14,287.82 crores, 56.87 
 
percent have been completed and the remaining 43.13  percent are ongoing. But in the ongoing projects 
36.04  percent projects have got delayed. Since, in NLCPR scheme the project cost is not allowed to be 
escalated due to delay, the quality of project is b ound to suffer. Therefore, the Committee recommends  
that any delay must not be encouraged except in cases where it is beyond control. 
(Para 3.1.12 ibid) 
Action Taken 
 
1.4.2 The Ministry shares the concerns expressed by  the Committee. Procedures for sanction of 
projects have been revamped so that DPRs would henc eforth get approved by the State level 
Empowered Committee chaired by the Chief Secretary.  With the operationalisation of the NLCPR 
project portal, documents are being received and pr ocessed much faster. The status of ongoing projects  
is reviewed regularly with concerned State Governme nt officers and also through field visits by office rs 
of the Ministry. To expedite completion of ongoing delayed projects, the Ministry has been utilising 
80% of budget provision for ongoing projects since last financial year 2015-16. This has led to more 
funds being available for such projects. 
 
1.4.3 States are being urged to transfer funds to i mplementing agencies as this is a major cause of 
delay. New proposals are being considered only afte r confirmation that funds have been transferred by 
the State’s Finance Department and substantial fund s are not lying unutilised.   Review of progress in  
respect of delayed projects is undertaken at variou s levels. With the new procedure of sanction and 
release of funds under the revised NLCPR guidelines , it is expected that the time over runs would be 
substantially reduced.

2.1 OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF DEMANDS FOR GRANTS 
 
Recommendation 
 
2.1.1 The Committee examined the figures of nationa l socio-economic development parameters vis-a-
vis socio-economic development and growth comprisin g details of indicators like health, education, 
poverty, income, unemployment, population etc. in r espect of the North Eastern States and found that 
health indicators in respect of a few states viz, A ssam and Meghalaya are very dismal. The Infant 
Mortality Rate (IMR) in these two States are higher  than the national average and most shocking is the  
fact that no data is available on account of Matern al Mortality Rate (MMR) in North Eastern States 
except Assam which records very high MMR, almost do uble of the national average. The Committee 
understands that high IMR and high MMR reflect extr emely poor availability of health facilities in the  
area and therefore strongly recommends that the Min istry must procure MMR data in respect of other 
North Eastern States and make all out efforts to im prove the situation on the ground in collaboration 
with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. 
(Para 2.1.5 of 196 
th  Report) 
Action Taken 
 
2.1.2 The recommendation has been noted. This issue  would be taken up both by the Ministry and 
NEC, in consultation with the Ministry of Health an d Family Welfare. This Ministry along with North 
Eastern Council would regularly monitor the situati on of Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) and Maternal 
Mortality Rate (MMR) with the concerned North Eastern States. 
 
2.1.3 However, as per the recent Sample Registratio n System-2014 (July, 2016) of Registrar General 
of India, the Infant Mortality Rate is 39 per 1000 live births, whereas Assam and Meghalaya has 49 and  
46 respectively. As per the SRS 2014 report Assam h as shown 5 points decline whereas Meghalaya has 
shown 1 point decline as compared to SRS 2013. 
 
2.1.4 In order to reduce IMR in the country includi ng NE States, the following interventions are being  
made under National Health Mission: 
i) Promotion of Institutional deliveries through cash incentive under Janani Suraksha 
Yojana (JSY) and Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakaram (JSSK) which entitles all pregnant 
women delivering in public health institutions to a bsolutely free delivery including Caesarean 
section, post-natal care and treatment of sick infants till one year of age. 
ii) Home Based Newborn Care (HBNC) through ASHAs for pr omotion of essential 
newborn care including breastfeeding practices, ear ly identification and referral of neonatal 
illnesses. 
iii) Facility Based Newborn Care (FBNC) is being scaled up for care of small or sick 
newborns. Special Newborn Care Units (SNCUs) have b een setup in district hospitals and 
medical colleges to provide round the clock services for sick newborns. 
iv) Newer interventions to reduce newborn mortality have also been implemented, including- 
Vitamin K injection at birth, Antenatal corticosteroids in pre-term labour, Kangaroo Mother Care 
and empowering ANMs to provide Injection Gentamycin  to young infants for possible serious 
bacterial infection. 

15 
 
v) Promotion of Infant and Young Child feeding practic es (IYCF)-Exclusive breastfeeding for 
first six months, complementary feeding beginning a t six months and appropriate infant and 
young child feeding practices (IYCF) are being prom oted in convergence with Ministry of 
Woman and Child Development. Ministry has launched MAA-Mothers’ Absolute Affection 
Programme on 5th August 2016 to improve breastfeedi ng practices in the country. Village 
Health and Nutrition Days (VHNDs) are observed for provision of maternal and child health 
services and creating awareness on maternal and chi ld care including health and nutrition 
education. 
vi) Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) is being sup ported to provide vaccination to 
children against many life threatening diseases suc h as Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus,
