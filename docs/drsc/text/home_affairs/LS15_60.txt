REPORT
 
            The management of personnel policies in Government of India, to begin with, was the joint responsibility of the Ministries of Home Affairs (Services and Establishment Wing) and Finance (Establishment Division) with Union Public Service 
Commission playing an important advisory role.  The Establishment Officer to the Government of India, functioning under the Cabinet   Secretary had an important role to play in the senior appointments under the Government of India.  All this made for
improper division of responsibility and lack of a unified Central direction.
2.         To plug this organisational loophole, the Estimates Committee of the third Lok Sabha in its 93rd Report (April 1966) observed that “the ever expanding role of the government in a welfare state with its natural concomitant of a large civil service calls
for effective personnel control through a single agency”. The Administrative Reforms Commission too urged upon the Government to set up a separate Department of Personnel, to be placed directly under the Prime Minister to serve as a nodal agency for the
personnel functions of the Government.
3.         The Union Government accepted this recommendation and as a consequence the ‘Department of Personnel’ was set up in August, 1970 and placed within the Cabinet Secretariat. In February 1973, the Department of Administrative Reforms, till then
with the Ministry of Home Affairs, was amalgamated with the Department of Personnel and the newly created department was christened, the ‘Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms’. In 1977, this department was transferred to the Ministry of
Home Affairs. In March, 1985 a full fledged ‘Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions’ was created. The Ministry comprises three departments, namely:-
 
1.                  Department of Personnel and Training.
2.                  Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances.
3.                  Department of Pensions and Pensioners’ Welfare.
 
4.         The Ministry is under the overall charge of the Prime Minister, assisted by two Ministers of State – one for the Department of Personnel and Training and Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare and the other for the Department of
Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. All the three Department are functioning under the charge of Secretary (Personnel) assisted by seven additional Secretaries/Joint Secretaries and other officers and staff.
 
DEPARTMENT OF PERSONNEL AND TRAINING AND THE VARIOUS AGENCIES UNDER ITS ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
 
5.0       The role of the Department of Personnel and Training can be conceptually divided into two parts.  In its larger nodal role, it acts as the formulator of policy and the watch dog of the Government ensuring that certain accepted standards and norms, as
laid down by it, are followed by all Ministries/Departments in the recruitment, regulation of service conditions and deputation of personnel as well as other related issues.  Towards this end, guidelines are issued by it for the benefit of all
Ministries/Departments and it monitors the implementation of these guidelines.  It also advises all organisations of the Central Government  on issues of personnel management.  At a more immediate level, the Department has the direct responsibility of being
the cadre controlling authority for the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) and the Central Secretariat Service (CSS).  The Department also operates the Central Staffing Scheme under which suitable officers from  All India Services and Group ‘A’ Central
Services are selected and then placed in posts at the level of Under Secretary and above on the basis of tenure deputation.   The Department also deals with cases of appointment to posts of Chairmen, Managing Directors, full-time functional
Directors/Members of the Board of Management of various public sector undertakings/enterprises, corporations, banks and
financial institutions.  It also deals with the assignment of Indian experts to various developing countries.
Recruitment Agencies
5.1       The two organisations through which the Department ensures recruitment of personnel for the Government are the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and the Staff Selection Commission (SSC). The former is constituted under a provision of
the Constitution and is responsible for conducting examinations for appointment to the higher civil services and civil posts under the Union Government, including recruitment to the All India Services. There is a mandatory provision for consulting the
Commission on all matters relating to methods of  recruitment, principles to be followed in making promotions and transfers from one service to another and all the disciplinary matters. The SSC is responsible for making recruitment for Group ‘B’ non-
gazetted posts of Assistants, Stenographers, etc.
Training Institutions
5.2       Two major training institutions directly under the administrative control of the Department are the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie and the Institute of Secretariat Training and Management (ISTM),
New Delhi. The former   is   mainly    responsible   for   providing   induction    training   to  recruits    to  the Indian Administrative Service and other All India Services and Central Services. The Institute  of Training and Management provides induction as
well as in-service training to members of the Central Secretariat Service.
Senior Appointments under the Government of India
5.3       All proposals for senior appointments under the Government of India, which require the approval of the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) are processed through the Establishment Officer as Secretary to the ACC.  These include board
level appointments to Central Public Sector Undertakings and appointments of Joint Secretaries, Directors and Deputy Secretaries in the Ministries/Departments. In addition, all appointments by promotion, which require the approval of the ACC are also
processed through the Establishment Officer.

Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB)
 
5.4       The Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB) is entrusted with the responsibility of making recommendations for appointment to the full-time Board level posts of Chief Executive and functional Directors and part-time Chairman in the Central
Public Undertakings.
 
Administrative Vigilance
 
5.5       The Department of Personnel and Training determines Government policy for the maintenance of integrity of the public services and eradication of corruption and coordinates the activities of various Ministries/Departments in that area.
Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
5.6       Advice on all vigilance matters is provided by the Central Vigilance Commission. It has jurisdiction and power in respect of  all matters to which the executive power of the Union Government extends. The Commission is also entrusted with the
supervision of the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). The Commission enjoys the same measure of independence and autonomy as the Union Public Service Commission.
Joint Consultative Machinery
5.7       The Joint Consultative Machinery (JCM)  is a three-tier machinery consisting of the National Council, the Departmental Council and the Regional/Office Council. Service matters pertaining to the interests of the generality of employees or specific
groups of them are dealt with by this machinery.
Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT)
5.8       In order to provide speedy and inexpensive justice to employees aggrieved by Government decisions, the Government has set up the Central Administrative Tribunal  (CAT) in 1985, which now deals with all cases relating to service matters which
were previously dealt with by Courts upto and including the High Courts.  There are now 17 regular Benches of the CAT functioning in various parts of the Country, including its Principal Bench in Delhi.
 
Staff  Welfare
 
5.9       The Department supports various programmes, which are provided in various Ministries/Departments, Central Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in Central Government offices located outside Delhi, Residents’ Welfare
Associations/Area Welfare Officers; Benevolent Fund  and Departmental Canteens.  Department of Personnel and Training is the nodal agency for three registered societies set up  for the welfare of the Government employees and their families.   These
societies are the Central Civil Services Cultural and Sports Board, Grih Kalyan Kendra and Kendriya Bhandar. 
 
DEPARTMENT OF PENSION AND PENSIONERS’ WELFARE
6.         The Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare is the nodal agency of the Government of India for formulation of general policy on pension and other retirement benefits, as also for redressal of grievances on retirement benefits.  The Department
was set up in 1985 as a part of Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions and has been rendering service for the benefit of pensioners since then.  Department has taken a number of steps over the years for streamlining the pension administration
system, for greater convenience and welfare of the pensioners.
DEPARTMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE REFORMS AND PUBLIC GRIEVANCES
7.         The Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances is the nodal agency of the Government of India for administrative reforms as well as redressal of public grievances relating to the State in general and grievances pertaining to  Central
Government agencies in particular. The Department disseminates information on important activities of the Government relating to administrative reforms and public grievance redressal through Management Publications and Documentation.   This
Department also acts as the nodal agency in regard to Organisation and Methods activities and offers advice to the Ministries/Departments. It also undertakes activities in the field of international co-operation and exchanges to promote public service reforms.

9.0       Demand No. 97 pertains to Union Public Service Commission (UPSC). The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional body, created under Article 315 of the Constitution. The Commission’s charter of duties is given in Article 320 and 321
of the Constitution. In brief, it is the duty of the Commission to conduct examinations for appointment to services of the Union and to advise Government on all matters relating to framing of recruitment rules, method of recruitment and the principle to be
followed in making promotions and transfers from one service to another. The Commission is also required to be  consulted in respect of disciplinary matters affecting a civil servant including appeals and memoranda filed by individual Government servants.
The Commission’s advice is also sought regarding any claim for reimbursement of legal proceedings instituted against him for acts done in execution of his duties and on the question of award of pension for injury sustained by a Government servant in a civil
capacity. By virtue of the provisions made in the relevant Acts, the Commission is required to be consulted in respect of appointments to certain categories of posts under the Municipal Corporation of Delhi, the Employees’ State Insurance Corporation and
the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation.

CITIZENS’ CHARTER
 
10.0     Citizens’ Charters contain the commitments of the concerned organisation alongwith expected  standard of service delivery, grievance   system etc., Of  late the Ministry has intimated   that 61 charters have been formulated which include 27 charters
for Public Sector Banks and four charters for hospitals, which are  given at Annexure-A.
 
10.1     In the replies to the questionnaire, the Ministry has also submitted a list of 200 Charters formulated  by organisations of different States and UTs which is placed at Annexure–B. The Department of ARandPG has also requested the other States/UTs to
formulate  their  Charters  and keep the Department informed as and when they do so.
 
10.2     The Ministry in its ATR has informed that a  Core Group on Citizens’ Charter has since been constituted under the Chairmanship of Secretary, Personnel, which has evaluated   59 Charters out of 61 and given feed-back to the respective   Ministries/
Departments for modifying  their Charters so as to cater to sector specific needs.  The Department of ARandPG has also reviewed Citizens’ Charter of the Union Territory  of Pondichery.
 
10.3     The formulation   of Citizens’ Charter by the remaining Ministries/Departments of the Government of India is being  pursued by the Department of ARandPG, particularly during the meetings of the Standing Committee of Secretaries for Public
Grievance  Redress.
 
10.4     The Committee feels that the initiative  taken by the Ministry to formulate  Citizens’ Charters on the pattern of the UK Government is commendable one.  The Committee suggests that widest possible consultation should be undertaken
in formulating the Charters.  The Committee desires  that the Citizens’ Charter  so far formulated should be made available to the public including representatives of people for information and review. The Committee strongly recommends
that all those States/UTs/Organisations who have not formulated the Citizens’ Charters be pursued to do so at an early date.
 
INFORMATION FACILITATION COUNTER
 
11.0     Information Facilitation Counters (IFCs) are set up outside the security zone of offices  with   a  view to   making     information   on  schemes,   rules,  procedures  etc.   of  the  concerned organisation    easily accessible  to the  user and  to accept  
applications/grievances  too.  In brief,  IFCs  are the physical   manifestation  of  Citizens’  Charters formulated by the concerned Ministries /Departments.
 
11.1     The Ministry in its ATR has mentioned that only 45 Ministries/Departments have IFCs.  The list of Ministries/Departments having IFCs are at Annexure–C
 
11.2     The Committee points out that 16 odd Ministries/ Departments have Citizens’ Charters without its physical manifestations (IFCs).  The Committee in that backdrop wanted to know how those Ministries/Departments would be able to be  citizen-
friendly  and grievances-free without IFCs.
 
11.3     The Ministry in its replies has acknowledged the fact that IFC and Citizen Charter in many ways reinforce each other but the initiatives of setting up IFCs and the formulation of Citizens’ Charters are taken up separately.  The Ministry maintains that
there is no one-to-one correspondence between the IFCs and Citizens’ Charter.
 
11.4     The Committee disagrees with the view offered  by the Ministry.  The Charters are meaningless unless and until the commitment enshrined therein is implemented.  The Committee  feels that IFCs are better way to implement the
Charters and hence the organisations/Ministries/Departments  having Citizen Charters but not IFCs may be advised to make endeavour to set up IFCs in their respective organisations.
 
11.5     The Ministry in the ATR has stated that the Department of ARandPG has undertaken external   survey by an NGO, namely,   the ‘Consumer Coordination Council’ from  a  customer’s point of view for the sake of getting adequate   independent  
feedback on the functioning of IFCs.  The Report of the survey has not yet been received from the  Consumer Coordination Council.  The Department has also prepared a calender of internal survey to be carried out during March – April, 2000.
 
11.6     The Committee desires that the findings of both internal as well as external survey conducted on IFCs alongwith Department of ARandPG’s comments thereon may be furnished to it.
 
PUBLIC GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL
 
12.0     In response to the recommendation   of the Committee in its 55th Report for restructuring  existing internal decentralised grievances mechanism   of the Ministries/Departments, the  Ministry  has constituted   a Standing Committee   of Secretaries   on
Grievance   Redressal  under the Chairmanship of Cabinet Secretary  for review of grievance  machinery of the Ministries, which has so far reviewed  23 Ministries/Departments upto March, 2000.
12.1     The recommendations/observations   of the  said  Committee are based  on the deficiencies   noticed in the implementation of the guidelines   issued by the Department of AR and PG on public grievance redressal mechanism and its monitoring.   The
observations/recommendations  of the Committee are generally implemented by the Department of ARandPG within a month of each meetings of the Committee.
 
12.2     The Department of AR and PG has issued various guidelines aimed    at reduction in the number of public grievances, such as the nomination of a senior officer for handling the grievances in the Ministry/Department; the installation of a uniform
software to monitor the grievances received/disposed;  prioritisation of grievance handling by inclusion  of the grievance redressal mechanism and statistics in the Annual Action Plan and the Annual Report of Ministries/Departments; review of grievances by
the Secretary  and  Joint Secretary of each Ministry/Department on a weekly/monthly basis; monitoring of the pendency of grievances in the organisations under it by the  concerned Ministry/Department; analysis of grievances   and identification of

CENTRAL ADMINISTRATIVE TRIBUNAL
 
17.0     Pursuant to the provision of Article 323A of the Indian Constitution,   the  Administrative Tribunal Act, 1985 was enacted  by Parliament  to  set  up Administrative Tribunal for  adjudication  of disputes  and complaints with regard to recruitment and
condition  of services  of  persons  appointed  to public  services  and  posts  in connection with the affairs of the Union and the States.
 
17.1     Administrative adjudication, which is quasi-judicial in nature, is the main function of Administrative Tribunals. The basic objective of enacting the Administrative Tribunal Act was:
 
        (i)   to relieve congestion in the ordinary set of courts; and
 
        (ii)  to provide for speedy disposal of disputes relating to service matters.
 
17.2     The Central Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 provides that the Tribunal shall not be bound by the procedure laid down in  the Code of  Civil Procedure, 1908 but  shall be  guided by
the principle of natural justice and subject to the other provisions of Administrative Tribunals  Act and  rules made by the Central Government. The Tribunal shall have power to regulate its own procedure including the fixing of places and times of enquiry.
 
17.3     Section 14(2) of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 empowers the Central government to apply the provisions of the Act to local or other authorities within the territory of India or under the control of Government of India and to Corporations or
Societies owned or controlled by Government of India. In exercise of these powers, the Central Government has so far extended the provisions of the Act to 38 organisations. 
 
17.4     In  brief, the objective of the Administrative Tribunal is to provide for speedy and inexpensive justice to the litigants for which the  procedure followed by Tribunal is made flexible and technically free from the ordinary courts.
 
17.5     At present  it has 17 regular benches,  15  of which operate at the principal seats of High Courts and the remaining two  at  Jaipur and Lucknow.  The present sanctioned strength of  the Chairman, Vice-Chairmen and Members of the CAT is as given
below:

                        Vice-Chairmen            -           16
                        Members                      -           49

Rs.48 lakhs under BE (2000-2001) for holding  more circuit sittings which will contribute to increase in the disposal of cases.
 
17.8     The Government of India has set up Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT) in order to decide the service-related cases of the Central Government employees expeditiously.  Accordingly, Rule 14(2) of the Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure)
Rules, 1987 envisages  that, as far as possible, every application   shall be heard  and decided within a period of six months from the date of registration.   However, the objectives   so far have not been achieved  at all.  As per the information furnished in the
Annual Report  for the year 1999-2000 of the Ministry, as on 31.12.1999, the number of pending cases are 47,899. The break-up  of bench wise pendency of cases in the CAT is at Annexure-F and G.

Section 35 and clause (C) of Section 36 of the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985.
 
17.10   The Ministry has also mentioned that although Rule 14(2) of the Central Administrative Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1987 provides that every application should be heard and decided as far as possible within six months from the date of its
registration.  In practice, it is not always possible to adhere to this schedule for the following reasons:-
 
(a)
                Sometimes, Government Departments require more time to file counter replies as the service matters are required to be examined in detail in consultation with various other Departments. At times, the applicant also seeks adjournments for
filing further submissions and rejoinders and there is no restriction as per the Rule on the number of adjournments which are to be given in a case.
(b)               Sometimes, the case is of a complicated nature and requires detailed examination/consideration. The Ministry in its reply has further clarified that instructions have been issued from time to time to all the Ministries/Departments to ensure
prompt and proper handling of cases before the Tribunal with a view to help in speedy disposal of cases. Instruction have also been issued that where more than one Department is involved, a single affidavit on behalf of all the Departments
(after mutual consultations) should be filed so that procedures are not unnecessarily lingered on by filing separate affidavits on behalf of various respondents in the same case.
(c)                Efforts are also made to fill up the vacancies of Chairman and Vice-Chairmen and Members of the Tribunal as expeditiously as possible.
(d)               The Law Commission in their 162 nd Report have also made certain recommendations relating to the functioning of CAT, some of which also relate to the aspect of selection of judicial functionaries. These recommendations are being
considered by the Department in consultation with the Ministry of Law.
(e)                Chairman, CAT has also recently written to the Prime Minister for setting up some additional Benches of the CAT. This proposal is also under examination. The Ministry hopes that with the implementation of the above steps, it should be
possible to further expedite disposal of cases.

20.0     The Central Staffing Scheme was introduced through Government of India Resolution No. F-34(3)-E.O./57 dated the 17 th October, 1957, which was subsequently published in Gazette of India on 26 th October, 1957.   The   Scheme   was   introduced  
with   clear-cut  intention  to provide  systematic
arrangement for manning senior administrative posts at the Centre of and above the rank of Deputy Secretary.
20.1     The Central Staffing Scheme which is currently in operation is the amended version of original    Central   Staffing Scheme  adopted  in 1957.  In  the  latest Government   Orders  on   the matter being Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and
Pensions’ O.M. No. 36/77/94-E.O.(SM-I), dated the 5th January, 1996, it is mentioned  that the scope of the Central Staffing Scheme is bound by the following parameters:-
(i)                 All posts of the rank of Under Secretary and above in the Government of India may be filled on tenure deputation from the All India Services and the participating Group ‘A’ services of the Central Government, excluding such posts of Under
Secretary and Deputy Secretary as are filled by CSS Officers; and
 
(ii)               In so far as the Officers from the Central Secretariat Service (CSS) are concerned, a specified number of posts at the level of Under Secretary will be treated as part of their cadre and posts over and above these will be filled under the Central
Staffing Scheme.
 
20.2     From the perusal of   both the versions (the original and the latest), it appears that the post of Deputy Secretary and above fall within the purview of “senior administrative post”. But the connotation of ‘senior administrative post’ has been diluted by
the amended version of the scheme which includes the post of Under Secretary under the scheme. The Committee understands that the post of Under Secretary, which by any stretch cannot be construed as a senior administrative grade post rather than it is a

Are You suprised ?
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/ha/Report%2060.htm[01-07-2016 11:59:03]
lower level administrative post playing supporting role in decision making process in the Secretariat. Further, the CSS being one of the participating services in Central Staffing Scheme does not participate for the post of Under Secretary, which was viewed
by the Committee  as discriminatory and the amendment was designed  to  bring officers  of  All India   Services  and Group ‘A’  Services at   the  post  of  Under Secretary so as to stifle the career progression of  Section Officer (Group-B Gazetted) within
Central Secretariat Service. Thus, the Scheme in operation is not  in consonance with the basic objective of the original scheme. The Committee, therefore, had recommended in its 55 th Report to honour the original scheme of 1957 by keeping the post of
Under Secretary in Central Secretariat for the Central Secretariat Service exclusively.
 
20.3     The Ministry in its Action Taken Replies to 55 th Report of the Committee has stated that  though the post of Under Secretary is included in the Central Staffing Scheme, officers belonging to All India Services and other Group ‘A’ Services are not
generally appointed at that level. Rarely are the posts of Under Secretaries filled by All India Services/Group ‘A’ Services officers. The Ministry advanced the logic that if the post of  Under Secretary is taken out of the Central Staffing Scheme, it may
become difficult to fill such posts located at places other than Delhi, where CSS Officers may be reluctant to go, while it may be possible to post officers of other services participating in the Central Staffing Scheme at those places.
 
20.4     The Committee fails to understand how come Section Officers of Central Secretariat Service, who are experiencing stagnation in his/her grade and having housing problem will be  reluctant to accept promotion to the post of Under Secretary located at
places other than Delhi. In this context, the Committee intended to know whether the  Section Officers of CSS have ever been offered the post of Under Secretary located outside Delhi.
 
20.5     The Ministry in its reply  stated that posts of Under Secretary level located outside Delhi have been offered to Central Secretariat Service e.g., a post at US level in SSC Bangalore Regional Office,
a CSS cadre post, could not be filled from CSS as there were no optees from CSS. It was proposed to ex-cadre the post for other services, but when it got upgraded to DS level a CSS officer joined the post; one post of US in Department of Agriculture and
Cooperation at Nagpur remained vacant, despite being a CSS cadre post, as it could not be filled for absence of optees. Department of Personnel and Training had to advise that Department to make its own arrangements for filling the post; two posts of Under
Secretary in the Department of Atomic Energy at Mumbai when circulated had no optees from amongst the CSS and were filled by an IAS officer of Bihar cadre and one UTCS officer; one post each of Under Secretary at SSC, Chennai, in Department of
Agriculture and Cooperation at Faridabad and in the Department of Ocean Development at Cochin have been filled by CSS officers.
20.6     The Committee understands that the vacancies of the posts might not have been given wide publicity among the Section Officers of CSS. In that context, the Committee wanted to know whether any post of Under Secretary located in Delhi have ever
been filled up by the officers of All India Services and Group ‘A’ Services and sought details thereof for the period form 1993 to 1999.
20.7     The Ministry has supplied Ministry wise information regarding appointment from AIS and Group ‘A’ Services  to the post  of  Under Secretary  located at Delhi  under Central Staffing Scheme.
The Ministry has also mentioned that an on 1.3.2000 out of 552 Under Secretary level officers working at the Centre, 533 belong to the CSS and the rest is occupied by the officers belonging to AIS/Group ‘A’ Service.
20.8     The Ministry has mentioned, in this context, that it invites nominations from State Governments/Centre Authorities for Central deputation each year for filling the posts of Deputy Secretary level and above upto Joint Secretary/equivalent under the
Central Staffing Scheme. However, considering cadre management requirements whenever the State Governments or Group ‘A’ Cadre Authorities suo motu forward the names of officers for the appointment at Under Secretary level, they are considered for
placement at that levels. Hence, posts of Under Secretaries other than those held by CSS Officers are considered to be well within the scope of the Central Staffing Scheme.
20.9     To     the    another   question   on   the   Central   Staffing   Scheme   –   What   are      the
reasons/circumstances prompting the Ministry to include the post of Under Secretary under Central Staffing Scheme by the way of supersession of the resolution of 1957 by an O.M. of the Ministry dated 15.7.1992, the Ministry has replied that the pay scale
attached to the post of Under Secretary is Rs. 10,000-15,200 and hence it is a Group ‘A’ post and therefore brought within the purview of the Central Staffing Scheme. The Committee was  informed that the post of Under Secretary was included in the
Scheme way back in 1970 by O.M. No. 33(1)-E.O./70 dated 27
th October, 1970.
20.10   In this context, it may be mentioned that the O.M. under reference of the Ministry speaks less about the Central Staffing Scheme and more about constitution and functions of ‘Senior Selection Board’, ‘Central Establishment Board’ and
‘Establishment Officer’. The O.M. does not reflect the picture of Central Staffing Scheme as has been depicted in the Original Resolution of 1957 and in the latest Office Memoranda of 1992 and 1996.
20.11   The Committee takes serious note of the flippant replies submitted by the Ministry on the Central Staffing Scheme. The replies are also ambiguous and misleading. The Committee does not appreciate the logic that the post of Under Secretary is Group
‘A’, thus included in the Central Staffing Scheme. The Committee understands the post of Under Secretary is Group ‘A’ all along  even before the formulation of Central Secretariat Service Rule, 1962. Why the post was included in the Central Staffing
Scheme in 1970, why not at the time of giving shape to original version of the Central Staffing Scheme.
20.12
      The Committee understands  that  the concept of the Central Staffing Scheme was tailored to keep hold on the post of Under Secretary in Central Secretariat by the Officers of AIS and Group ‘A’ Services which is totally against the
objective of the Central Staffing Scheme and reiterates  its stand taken in its 55th Report to keep the post of Under Secretary in Central Secretariat out of the purview of the Central Staffing Scheme.
20.13   As per the CSS Rules, 1962 the composition of Central Secretariat Services is from Assistant (Group B) to Deputy Secretary (Group A). The Committee feels that the post of Deputy Secretary should be reserved for the CSS Officers.
20.13   The Ministry in its reply has mentioned that not less than 25% of the posts of Deputy Secretaries in the various Ministries/Departments are, as far the extant policy, filled by CSS Officers only. The rest 75% of the posts of Deputy Secretaries are filled
by officers of 37 other services participating in the Central Staffing Scheme. If Officers of these services are excluded from manning posts of Deputy Secretaries, that would mean exclusion of these officers below 14 years of service from appointment to post
under the Central Staffing Scheme. Such an exclusion has not been intended or provided in the Central Staffing Scheme or even in the CSS Rules. Further, the Government will be deprived of the benefit of the expertise and experience of the Officers of the
various services at the level of Deputy Secretaries if appointment at that level is made of only CSS officers. It is also pertinent to point out that there is a specific Central Deputation Reserve (CDR) built in the cadres of the various States, in respect of the
three All India Services, namely, the IAS, the IPS and the Indian Forest Service. The CDR level is based on the need of the Central Government in respect of the officers of the three All India Services for manning its posts at various levels. The CDR of IAS
alone stood at 1111 as on 1.3.2000. Officers to the extent   of the number covered in the CDR of the three All India Services have been recruited for the affairs of the Central Government on tenure deputation basis. If AIS officers are to be considered for
placement at the level of Director and above only, their appointments will be confined to posts at Director level and above, and since the number of posts available at those levels as on 1.3.2000 was only 1220, it may not be feasible or desirable to fill 1111
posts out of the total 1220 posts  at Director level as it stood on 1.3.2000, by IAS officers only.
20.15   The objective of the Central Staffing Scheme that has been in existence for over four decades is the Centre’s need for fresh inputs at senior levels in policy planning, formulation of policy and implementation of programmes from diverse sources,
namely the All India Services and the Group ‘A’ Central Services participating in the Central Staffing Scheme, as also the scientific and technical personnel and professionals, all borrowed on tenure deputation basis. The objectives of the Central Staffing
Scheme would be defeated if the posts at certain levels are exclusively reserved for the offices of any particular service.
 
 
20.16
      The Committee agrees with the basic objectives enumerated in the Central Staffing Scheme. The Committee also understands that when the scheme was originally introduced the post of Deputy Secretary was at the bottom level for
decision making process or otherwise treated as one of the senior administrative posts. But, subsequently, when the post of Director was introduced, it took the place of Deputy Secretary in the decision making process making the post of
Deputy Secretary middle management level rendering supporting role in the decision making process. The other 37 participating services have career progressions in their respective cadres whereas the CSS which has career progression only
in the Central Secretariat is affected by the Central Staffing Scheme. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the post of Deputy Secretary should also be part of CSS cadre as per the statutory provision of CSS Rules, 1962. But keeping in
view of the reasons stated by the Ministry, in the preceeding paras, the Committee recommends that 50% of the post of Deputy Secretary instead of extant 25% should be reserved for the officers of CSS.

 22.4    In view of the analysis made in the preceding paras, the Committee suggests the Ministry  to ensure that all recruitment notices of  Government are published in local newspapers also.

CENTRAL BUREAU OF INVESTIGATION
28.0     The Committee had recommended in its 55th Report to ensure functional autonomy of the CBI under the Central Vigilance Commission and also a fixed tenure for the Director of CBI.
28.1     The Committee has been informed that in accordance with the directions of the Supreme Court,  the CBI Director has been given minimum tenure of two years regardless of the date   of his superannuation, which also finds a place in the Central
Vigilance Commission, Bill, 1999. This would ensure that an officer suitable in all respects is not ignored merely because he has less than two years to superannuate from the date of his appointment.   The transfer of an incumbent Director, CBI in an
extraordinary situation, including the need for him to take up a more important assignment, should have the approval of the Selection Committee.
 
28.2     The Committee has also been further informed that in pursuance of Supreme Court judgement in the Vineet Narain case,  CBI Selection Board with Central Vigilance Commissioner as Chairman and the Home Secretary   and the Secretary
(Personnel) as Members have been constituted for inter alia  making recommendations for the appointment of Director, CBI. While making recommendation, the Board draws up a panel of IPS officers on the basis of their seniority, integrity, experience in
investigation and anti-corruption work.  The incumbent Director, CBI is also consulted in the matter of suggesting a list of suitable persons for preparation of such panels by the Board. However, the consideration of suitable persons is not restricted to the list
suggested by the incumbent Director.
 
28.3     The Selection Board suggests a panel of three names in descending order of preference for consideration by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC). The final selection is made by the ACC from the panel recommended by the Selection
Board. If none among the panel is found suitable, the reasons thereof shall be recorded and the Board shall be asked to draw up a fresh panel.
 
28.4     For making the functioning of the CBI efficient and impartial, Independent Review Committee setup in 1997 to examine the structure and functioning of the CBI has inter-alia given the following main recommendations:-
 
(i)
                 The CVC to overview CBI’s functioning and CBI’s reporting to Government to be streamlined without diluting its functional autonomy.
(ii)               Constitution of Selection Committee for identifying a panel of names for selection of Director, CBI; final selection to be made by ACC from such panel.
(iii)             The Director, CBI to have minimum tenure of two years. Transfer of incumbent Director, CBI should have endorsement of the Selection Committee.
(iv)             The Director, CBI to be ensured full freedom for allocation of work within the agency, including constitution of investigation teams.
(v)        Selection/extension of tenure of officers upto the level of Joint Director (JD) shall be decided by a Board comprising  the Central Vigilance Commissioner, Joint Director and above would need the approval of ACC.
(vi)       Director, CBI to be responsible for ensuring time limits for filing chargesheets in courts.
(vii)      Secretary of Administrative Ministry to convey a decision regarding registration of Preliminary Enquiry (PE)/Regular Case (RC) within 2 months of receipt of request. If not satisfied with decision, Director, CBI free to make fresh reference to the 
Committee headed by Cabinet Secretary within a period of four weeks and the latter to decide thereon within a period of four weeks.
 (viii)    Time limit of three months for according sanction for prosecution. Where consultation is required with the Attorney General or the Solicitor General, additional time of one month could be allowed.
(ix)       Cases falling within the jurisdiction of the State Police which do not have inter-state or inter-national ramifications should not be handed over to CBI by States/Courts.
(x)        Government to establish special Courts for the trial of CBI cases.
(xi)       Director, CBI to conduct regular appraisal of personnel to weed out the corrupt and inefficient, and maintain strict discipline within the organization.
 
28.5     The Supreme Court in the Vineet Narain case had directed the Government that Central Vigilance Commission shall be responsible for the efficient functioning of the CBI, while the Government shall remain answerable for the functioning of CBI
primarily to introduce visible objectivity for over viewing the CBI’s working. Following the directions of the Supreme Court the provision of exercising superintendence over the functioning of the CBI, in so far as it relates to  the  investigations  of  offences 
alleged  to  have  been  committed  under  the  Prevention   of Corruption Act, 1988 has been given to CVC. The Central Vigilance Commission Bill, 1999, is currently under the consideration of Joint Parliamentary Committee. The CBI shall report to the
CVC about cases taken up by it for investigation; progress of investigation; cases in which charge sheets are filed and progress thereof. The Central Vigilance Commission shall review the progress of all cases moved by the CBI for sanction of prosecution of
public servants which are pending with the competent authorities especially those in which sanction has been delayed or refused. However  Clause 19 of the Bill, provides that the power of superintendence of the CVC shall not extend to its giving directions
to the CBI to investigate or dispose off a particular case in a particular manner.
 
28.6     Thus, the superintendence of the CVC over the CBI is limited to the investigation of offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act only. The superintendence of CVC shall not extend to investigation of cases relating to general crime and economic
offences. As a result, a clear cut demarcation of the area of superintendence over the functioning of the CBI  has been drawn to ensure
impartial, and efficient functioning of the CBI. However, the stability of the tenure of the Director, CBI  will help infuse full freedom in  its functioning.
 
28.7     Further, the CBI shall have full freedom for constituting team for investigations. Any change made by the Director, CBI in the Head of investigative team should be for cogent reasons and for improvement in investigation, the reasons being recorded
which has also been communicated to the CBI through letter No. 142/1/98-AVDI dated 3.2.1998 for necessary action.
 
28.8     The extension of tenure or premature repatriation of officers upto the level of Joint Director shall be with the final approval of Board comprising Central Vigilance Commissioner, Home Secretary and Secretary (Personnel), which is also approved by
the ACC.
28.9     Keeping in view the ever increasing cases pertaining to  economic offences being handed over to CBI for investigation and the Supreme Court’s direction to strengthen CBI’s in house expertise, professionals from the revenue, banking and security
sectors shall be inducted into the CBI. The Government has sanctioned a total number of 183 posts including technical posts for augmenting the Special Crime division and the Economic Offences Wing of the CBI. The experts/professionals so inducted will
play an advisory role and give expert advice in the investigation of complicated cases of specialised nature pertaining to economic offences.
28.10   CBI has also recently set up an investigating unit designed as Cyber Crime Investigation Cell (CCIC) at New Delhi under the Economic Offences Division (EOD) of CBI for taking up investigation of offences involving computer crimes, crimes
committed in computerized environment and other high-tech crimes in areas like telecom etc.
28.11   In accordance with the CBI Crime Manual, in case of adverse remarks or strictures passed by the courts, it will be incumbent upon the CBI to take action   against the officers found guilty, after obtaining explanation from the affected officers. Reports
in every such case where adverse remark has been made is sent to the Head Office of the CBI for taking strict action against the officer found guilty of dereliction of duty.
28.12   CBI  has  also  prepared  a  panel  of  competent  lawyers  having  experience  and  impeccable reputation to be appointed as prosecuting counsel in significant cases of CBI.

Amendment Act, 1952 (for P.C. Act, 1947) and under section 3 of the P.C. Act, 1988. While under the Criminal Law Amendment Act, 1952 only the State Governments had the power to appoint such special judges, under the prevention of Corruption Act,

28.23   From the perusal of table titled  “Disposal of Court Cases” it appears that No. of cases ended  in acquittal   in  1999  is  41.7%  whereas  the  same  was  33%  during the year 1997. The reasons attributed for such acquittal are that the trial proceedings
take a long time during which some of the important/vital witnesses either expire or are not available for deposition in the court. Further, some of the witnesses become disinterested over a period of time or won over by the accused and turn hostile. Some of
the cases also result in acquittal as the courts take a view that the evidence adduced was insufficient to prove the allegation beyond doubt.
 
28.24   The Committee is of the view that CBI prosecutors should be impressed upon to prepare a strong case and lacunae, if any, should be pointed out  to the CBI before filing the chargesheet so that CBI can collect the required evidence in
this regard and the accused is not acquitted on the ground of insufficient evidence.
 
28.25   The Committee understands that the CBI does not have its own cadre of officers and the officers on deputation have their loyalty to their parent cadre. The question of CBI’s own cadre comes to lime light in  the Priyadarshini Mattoo Case wherein 
the court has reprimanded the CBI for biased investigation as a son  of DIG police was involved in the case.
 
28.26   The Ministry has stated that the CBI is the premier investigating agency of the country and is handling various types of cases of very complicated and technical nature. Investigation of these cases requires specialised knowledge and expertise in
various fields. There are experienced officers of the State Police Forces and other enforcement agencies who have wealth of experience in investigation of various kinds of cases, particularly cases relating to conventional crime. Hence it is considered
necessary to draw  officers from  the State Police Forces  and  other enforcement agencies so that their experience is utilized by the CBI. The officers coming on deputation to CBI acquire experience in investigating cases relating to corruption, while collar
crimes and other cases having national/international ramifications during their tenure in CBI. After returning to their parent cadre, they share the knowledge/experience acquired in CBI with their colleagues in their parent Department which is beneficial to

28.27   It is, therefore, a well thought out policy of the Government to have a homogenous mix of departmental officers as well as officers on deputation not only from the State Police Forces but also from other enforcement agencies. During investigation of
a case, care is taken to ensure that the case is investigated impartially and that the concerned IOs/supervisory officers are not biased in any manner.
 
28.28   The  Ministry has informed that the CBI has also filed a writ Petition in the High Court of Delhi for expunging certain adverse observations regarding the investigation carried out by CBI officers.
 
28.29   The Committee suggests that the officer held responsible for biased investigation must be punished at once in order to restore faith in the premier investigating agency of the Country. The Committee also recommends to maintain
transparency in the investigation conducted by the CBI. The Ministry may also  take measures to infuse professionalism in the existing cadre of the CBI and create a permanent cadre in the long run.

29.1     There is no provision in the existing  Recruitment Rules for direct recruitment of Inspectors in the CBI. Posts in the grade of Inspector are either filled by promotion or by deputation. The feeder grade for promotion to the rank of Inspector is that of
Sub-Inspector.
 
29.2     Sub-Inspectors in the CBI recruited through Staff Selection Commission (SSC) as well as through the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination (LDCE) from within the CBI. Sub-Inspectors recruited through SSC are required to be Graduates
whereas those recruited through the LDCE are required to be Graduates with three years of service or Matriculations with 8 years of service. But there is no minimum qualifications prescribed as such for deputation to the grade of Inspector.
 
29.3     The Ministry   further  informed  that  the  Recruitment  Rules for the  posts  of  Inspector and
Sub-Inspector in the CBI are under revision subsequent to the recommendations of V Central Pay Commission. It has been proposed that for deputation to the post of Inspector in the CBI a minimum qualification of graduation should be made compulsory. It
is also proposed that Sub-Inspectors recruited through LDCE should be graduates. The proposed Recruitment Rules are under the consideration of the UPSC whose concurrence  has been sought.
 
29.4     As the Sub-Inspector and Inspector are required to go to the field for investigation, the minimum educational qualification of graduation is very much essential for them. The Committee recommends  to amend the Recruitment Rules of
the CBI for introducing minimum qualification of graduation for the deputationist to the both Sub-Inspector and Inspector. Inspector recruited through LDCE should also be graduate. The Committee also recommends that officers who are
on deputation to the post of Sub-Inspector and above in the CBI and have no graduation degree should be repatriated forthwith.
