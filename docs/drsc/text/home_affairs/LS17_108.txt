INTRODUCTION
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home
Affairs, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby
present this One Hundred-eighth Report of the Committee on the Assam Rifles Bill, 2003*.
2. In pursuance of the Rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
Committees, the Chairman, Rajya Sabha in consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha referred** the
Bill, as introduced in the Lok Sabha on 8 May 2003 and pending therein, to the Committee for
examination and report.
3. The Committee considered the Bill in four sittings held on 2 September, 28 October,
19 November and 17 December, 2003.
3.1 In its sitting held on 2 September, 2003 the Committee heard the oral evidence of the Home
Secretary and a senior officer of the Home Ministry on the Bill.
3.2. The Committee held in-house discussion on the Bill in its sitting held on 28 October, 2003.
3.3 The Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill in its sitting held on
19 November, 2003.
3.4 The Committee considered and adopted the draft Report on the Bill in its sitting held on
17 December, 2003.
4. In the course of its deliberations, the Committee has made use of the following papers/
documents:—
(i) Background note on the Bill received from the Ministry of Home Affairs;
(ii) Assam Rifles Act, 1941;
(iii) Army Act, 1950; and
(iv) Comments of Ministry of Home Affairs on the points raised by the Chairman and
Members in the sittings of the Committee held on 2 September, 28 October and
19 November, 2003.
5. For facility of reference and convenience, observations and recommendations of the
Committee have been printed in bold letters in the body of the Report.

REPORT
The Assam Rifles Bill, 2003 ( Annexure-I ) seeks to repeal the existing Assam Rifles
Act, 1941 and to replace it by a new Act with the following salient features:
(i) the constitution of the Assam Rifles as an Armed Force of the Union, with provisions
for its control and other provisions relating to its establishment, etc.;
(ii) establishment and procedure of the Assam Rifles Courts, offences committed by the
Force members, punishment therefor and trial of such offences by the Assam Rifles
Courts;
(iii) provisions relating to retention, custody and trial of the offenders under the proposed
legislation;
(iv) confirmation, revision and execution of sentences, pardons and remissions, etc., and
miscellaneous provisions;
(v) deductions to be made from the pay and allowances of the members of the Force
for certain offences;
(vi) manner of execution of sentences of death as well as imprisonment and provisions
relating to powers and duties of members of the Assam Rifles; and
(vii) statutory protection for acts done by members of the Assam Rifles in discharging
their official duty.
2. The history of the force, as has been stated in the Statement of Objects and Reasons of
the Bill, dates back to 1835 when it was raised as Cachar Levy and was deployed on frontier
watch and ward duty to assist the civil administration in the maintenance of law and order in the
tribal areas of the erstwhile composite State of Assam particularly in Lushai and Mikir Hills, Naga
Hills, Khasi-Jantia and Garo Hills as also in the erstwhile North-East Frontier Agency.
2.1 The Force came under the control of the Central Government in 1941 after the passage of
the Assam Rifles Act, 1941. It was administered by the Governor of Assam through the North-
East Frontier Agency (NEFA) under the overall control of the Ministry of External Affairs and was
later on transferred to the Ministry of Home Affairs in 1965. Certain Assam Rifles battalions were
placed under the operational control of the Army. The strength of the Assam Rifles was 5 battalions
till 1950 and was later on increased to take care of border security and internal security duties.
The Assam Rifles is now comprised of 39 battalions.
3. This force has been charged with the responsibility of ensuring security of the borders of
India, to carry out counter insurgency operations in the specified areas and to act in aid of civil
authorities for the maintenance of law and order and for matters connected therewith. The
personnel of Assam Rifles are governed by the Assam Rifles Act, 1941 except for the personnel
of those battalions of the Assam Rifles which are at present deployed under the operational control
of the Army in whose case the Army Act, 1950 is applicable with certain exceptions and
modifications. The Assam Rifles Act, 1941 is however not applicable to the Cadre officers of the
force, who are governed under Central Civil Services (Control, Classification and Appeal) Rules,
1965 which are basically meant for the civilian personnel, thus there is a vacuum so far as Cadre
officers are concerned. Considering the nature and purpose of the force and to bring uniformity
in the application of the legal provisions, it is felt that a separate enactment, which will provide
for its special needs, should regulate the Force.

2
4. The Ministry has stated in the Background Note that the Assam Rifles is presently
performing the following duties:
(a) To conduct counter insurgency operations in the North East and other areas
wherever necessary under the overall control of the Army;
(b) To guard the Indo-China and Indo-Myanmar borders;
(c) To ensure security of rear areas in the Tactical Battle Area (TBA) during war;
(d) To assist the Army in other operational tasks as and when required during war and
peace; and
(e) To act as a para-military force of the Central Government in internal security
management.
5. It has further stated that the organisational structure, sphere of responsibility and role of
this force have undergone a considerable change over a period of time. The force has also
expanded both in terms of size and responsibility. In view of the changes that have taken place
and the experience gathered over the years, it was felt that the Assam Rifles Act of 1941 does
not adequately meet the requirements of the force and therefore, needs to be modified and updated.
The problems/lacunae in the existing Act as mentioned in the background note are as follows:
(a) The Assam Rifles Act, 1941 is applicable only to the personnel upto the rank of
Subedar Major and their terms and conditions of service are regulated under the
provisions of this Act. The officers on deputation from the Army are governed under
the Army Act. The cadre officers i.e. those promoted from the ranks of the force
above the rank of Subedar Major are not covered under the Assam Rifles Act and
their disciplinary matters etc. have to be dealt with under Central Civil Services
(Control, Classification and Appeal) Rules, 1965 which are basically meant for the
civilian personnel, and are not designed to deal with the matters peculiar to a
combatised force. This set of officers needs to be brought under the purview of the
Assam Rifles Act.
(b) There is no provision of court martial in the Assam Rifles Act, 1941; as a result the
cases of the personnel of the force have to be handed over to civil police for
investigation and trial, whereas in other Armed Forces there is a provision for trial
of offences committed by the force personnel by General Security Force Courts
(GSFC).
(c) The Director General, Deputy Director General, Inspector General, Deputy
Inspectors General, Deputy Commandants of the Assam Rifles do not figure in the
Assam Rifles Act, 1941 and therefore, have no powers either to try any offence or
act as appellate authorities under the present Act. The appeal or revision against any
sentence/order passed by any Commandant or Assistant Commandant in exercise of
the magisterial powers vested in them under the said Act can be filed only in the
Civil Courts. In all other Para Military Forces, the superior officer of the force
concerned exercises these powers.
(d) The Assam Rifles Act, 1941 does not specify the force as an Armed Force of the
Union, whereas Acts of other central forces like Border Security Force, National
Security Guards, ITBP etc. have explicit provisions to this effect.
(e) There are no provisions in the Act for penalties like, (i) dismissal, removal or
compulsory retirement from service; (ii) reduction in rank; (iii) forfeiture of seniority/

3
service for the purpose of promotion; (iv) severe reprimand/reprimand; (v) fine; and
(vi) summary punishments.
(f) There are also certain offences, which are not covered under the Assam Rifles Act,
1941, like (i) conduct unbecoming of the personnel of the force; (ii) absence without
leave; (iii) insubordination; (iv) irregularity in connection with arrest; (v) offences in
relation to property; (vi) escape from custody; and (vii) penal offences under the
Army Act, 1950 which are provided in sections 56 to 70 except section 63.
6. In order to overcome the difficulties enumerated above and to make the pr oposed Assam
Rifles Act, similar to the Acts of other Para Military Forces, it is proposed to replace the existing
Assam Rifles Act, 1941 by a new legislation. Hence, the Assam Rifles Bill, 2003 has been
introduced in the Lok Sabha on 8 May, 2003.
6.1 After its introduction in the Lok Sabha and while pending therein, the Bill was referred to
the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs on 22 May, 2003 for
examination and report.
7. Accordingly, the Committee took up consideration of the Bill in its meeting held on
2 September, 2003. It heard the oral evidence of the Home Secretary in the same meeting.
7.1 The Home Secretary briefly mentioned the history of the force, its growth and present
composition. The concerned Joint Secretary in the Ministry of Home Affairs made detailed
presentation on the provisions of the Bill and other related matters.
7.2 Highlighting the achievements and activities of Assam Rifles, the Joint Secretary mentioned
about its role in World War II, checking insurgency in Nagaland, its role in high altitude areas of
Sikkim, assistance to civil population; its climbing the Mount Everest, etc. He then dwelt upon the
Assam Rifles Act, 1941, by which the force was being governed. His presentation comprised the
salient features of the Act such as its extent, offences, their trials, punishments, etc. He highlighted
the shortcomings of the Act and stated that the need for the new Bill was to obviate the existing
shortcomings in the light of the changed role and composition of the force.
7.3 As regards the Assam Rifles Bill, he stated that it was a Bill to consolidate and amend the
law relating to the governance of the Assam Rifles. The Bill contained 12 chapters and 168
sections, which mainly dealt with its constitution, conditions of service, offences, punishments,
arrest and proceedings, Assam Rifles Courts, execution of sentence et al. He apprised the
Committee of the salient features of the Bill. He also made a comparison of Assam Rifles Bill with
the BSF Act and Army Act.
8. During the course of deliberations in the Committee, Members raised several points/queries,
which were responded to by the Ministry of Home Affairs.
8.1 Replying to an observation of a Member that the provisions of Criminal Procedure
Code, 1973, should be made applicable to Assam Rifles Courts, the Ministry has stated that the
proposed Bill is a special legislation applicable only to a class of people who are subject to the Bill.
The Ministry has also stated that this Bill provides for arrest of offenders, investigation of offences,
pre-trial hearing of the case, trial of offenders by Assam Rifles Court, Departmental remedy against
the findings and sentences of Assam Rifles Courts, confirmation of findings and sentences awarded
by an Assam Rifles Courts, revision and review of findings, mitigation, remission of sentences,
execution of sentences, grant of parole etc. Since this Bill is in the nature of a special enactment,
there is a requirement of special procedure for the Courts convened under the proposed Bill.
Moreover, most of the provisions of the Criminal Procedure Code, 1973, may not be applicable in
the case of an Assam Rifles Court in view of the differences in the hierarchy and system of courts
as provided for in the Bill and under the Criminal Procedure Code, 1973. There are also differences

4
in procedures with regard to investigation and other pre-trial proceedings, appeal, confirmation of
findings and sentences, execution of sentences, etc. Further, in the case of similar other
enactments like Army Act, 1950, BSF Act, 1968, NSG Act, 1986 and made applicable to the Courts
convened under the said enactments, separate procedures have been laid down. However, in the
case of Courts convened under the proposed Bill, the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 has been made
applicable under clause 109.
8.2 A Member made an observation that clause 21 (j) of the Bill stipulates punishment of death
for offences like sentry sleeping in post or is intoxicated in the post, etc. has no rationale and that
the offence has again been mentioned under clause 23(c). To this the Ministry has replied that the
offences mentioned in clause 21 are offences which are in relation to enemy. Some of these acts
are acts of cowardice like shamefully abandoning the post, place or guard while in action against
enemy, or induce or compel a member of the armed force from acting against the enemy,
shamefully cast away arms, ammunition, etc. in presence of enemy, or communicate intelligence
with enemy, spreading false alarm at the time of active operation, while being a prisoner of war
voluntarily aid the enemy, harbour or protect the enemy, being a sentry in time of active operation
against enemy sleeps or is intoxicated in the post, or knowingly does any act calculated to imperil
the success of the armed forces against the enemy. It has further stated that the clause has been
framed in the Bill mainly based on the existing provisions of the Army Act, 1950 (Section 34), BSF
Act, 1968 (Section 14), ITBP Act, 1992 (Section 16) and NSG Act, 1968 (Section 15). The
Ministry has emphasised that severe punishments upto death sentence are provided for these
offences only as they are committed while on active operation against enemy.
8.2.1 It has further stated that in clause 23 (c) the offence of sentry sleeping or is intoxicated
on the post has been mentioned as being committed while on ‘active duty’ or otherwise. The
punishment for those while on ‘active duty’ are maximum upto fourteen years and while not on
‘active duty’ is maximum upto seven years. These are considered to be serious offences as any
lapse on part of the sentry may result in large casualty to the force. This Clause has been framed
in the Bill in accordance with existing provisions of Army Act, 1950 (Section 36(c)), BSF Act,
1968 (Section 16 (c), ITBP Act, 1992 (Section 18(c) and NSG-Act, 1986 (Section 16(c).
8.3 To an observation of a Member that punishments should be commensurate to the offences
in accordance with the principle of ‘proportionality of punishments’, the Ministry has replied that
the maximum  punishment that can be awarded by a court for each offence has been mentioned
in the Bill and considering the nature and circumstances of each case courts can award such lesser
punishments as deemed fit.
8.3.1 The Min istry has further mentioned that the offences of lesser gravity like absence without
leave for a short period, minor acts prejudicial to good order and discipline etc. can be disposed
of by awarding minor punishments by way of summary trial by force authorities as in clauses 62,
64, 65 and 66. These provisions, according to the Ministry have been framed in the Bill in
accordance with existing provisions in other related enactments like Army Act, 1950 (Sections 80,
83, 84 and 85), BSF Act, 1968 (Sections 53, 54 and 55), ITBP Act, 1992 (Sections 56 and 58)
and National Security Guard Act, 1986 (Sections 51, 53 and 54). The Ministry has stressed that
discipline is the bedrock of any armed force and various punishments have been prescribed in the
Bill to ensure discipline in the force. These are in consonance with the punishments provided for
in the other similar enactm ents like Army Act, 1950, BSF Act, 1968 and ITBP Act, 1992.
8.4 Replying to a query of a Member that there is no provision for appeal in the case of a
person convicted to ‘death sentence’ by an Assam Rifles Court, the Ministry has stated that the
Bill provides for various Departmental remedies to a person convicted by an Assam Rifles Court.
As per clause 139(1) of the Bill, a person convicted by an Assam Rifles Court can make an appeal
to the confirming authority before confirmation of the finding and sentence passed by the Court.

5
Further, as per Clause 139(2) of the Bill such a person can also make an appeal to the Central
Government, the Director General or any prescribed officer superior in command to the confirming
authority, after the confirmation of finding and sentence. Thus, at two stages viz. pre-confirmation
and post-confirmation stages a convicted person can make an appeal. These provisions are
applicable to all findings and sentences including ‘death sentence’ passed by an Assam Rifles Court.
8.4.1 The Ministry has further stated that while considering such an appeal, the confirming
authority, the Central Government, the Director General or the prescribed authority can mitigate,
remit or commute the findings and sentence or annul the proceedings. The Bill, in clause 137, also
provides for mandatory review of proceedings of Summary Assam Rifles Court by a superior
authority, which can set aside the proceedings or reduce the sentence awarded by the Court.
8.5 Replying to an observation of a Member that the Bill should provide for reasonable and fair
trial, the Ministry has stated that the clauses in the Bill relating to Assam Rifles Courts and
procedure for trial are framed in accordance with the existing related provisions in Army Act, 1950,
BSF Act, 1968 and ITBP Act, 1992 and National Security Guard Act, 1986. The Ministry has
enumerated the provisions under the Bill to ensure an opportunity for reasonable and fair trial to
the accused such as:
(i) Every trial by a General and Petty Assam Rifles Courts consist of more than one
officer who constitute the Court. In the case of a General Assam Rifles Court the
minimum number of members is five and in the case of Petty Assam Rifles Court
the minimum number of members is three.
(ii) At the commencement of a trial an accused person has a right to object to being
tried by any member sitting in the Court. If the objections raised are found to be
valid by majority of the members of the Court, then the member objected to is
removed and a new member is appointed in his place who is also liable to be
objected by the accused.
(iii) On arrest of an offender, the Commandant of a unit is bound to investigate the case
within 48 hours.
(iv) In case an offender is detained for more than eight days without his trial by an
Assam Rifles Court being ordered, then a special report is to be made by the
Commandant to the superior authorities. Provisions will be made in the delegated
legislation regarding competent authority to grant sanction for detention without trial
as provided for in Rule 27 of Army Rules, 1954. Under this rule, the Chief of Army
Staff can sanction detention-upto three months, without a trial being ordered to
assemble and for detention for a period beyond this, approval of the Central
Government is mandatory.
(v) The provisions of Indian Evidence Act, 1872 have been made applicable to an Assam
Rifles Court. Hence, all the rights and privileges available to an accused person
before a Criminal Court under Indian Evidence Act, 1872, like right to cross
examine, right to call for documents, burden of proof etc. are available to an
accused before Assam Rifles Courts convened under this Bill.
(vi) Every decision by an Assam Rifles Court has to be passed by a majority and in the
case of death sentence it has to be passed with the concurrence of at least two
thirds of the members of the Court.
(vii) All other provisions regarding the pre-trial hearing, rights of accused like right to
documents relating to the case, engaging defence counsel, objection to the charge,
objection to the jurisdiction of the Court, appointment of friend of accused etc. will

6
be included in the delegated legislations as they are pr esently available under Army
Rules, 1954, BSF Rules, 1969 and ITBP Rules, 1994.
(viii) Every trial by a General Assam Rifles Court has to be and petty Assam Rifles Court
may be attended by a Law Officer to assist the Court. The duties of these officers
in Assam Rifles Courts are similar to those of Judge Advocate in a Court Martial
under Army Act,  1950 or Force Courts under BSF Act, 1968.
(ix) The accused will have right to seek advice from the Law Officers in matters relating
to Law as have been provided under Rule 105 of Army Rules, 1954. This will be
provided under subordinate legislation.

Clause 55
9.54 Clause 55 seeks to provide the trial of a person subject to this legislation for commission
of a civil offence, that is offences triable by courts of ordinary criminal justice, subject to the
provisions of clause 56 of this Bill.
9.54.1 Replying to an observation of a Member that there should be specific provision in the Bill
distinguishing offences which are triable by the Assam Rifles Courts and Civil Courts, the Ministry
of Home Affairs has stated that clause 55 of the Bill prescribes the civil offences that can be tried
by an Assam Rifles Court subject to restrictions placed under clause 56 of the Bill. The term ‘civil
offence’ is defined in clause 2(e) of the Bill and it means an offence which is triable by a Criminal
Court. As per the restriction placed by clause 56, offences of murder, culpable homicide not
amounting to murder and rape, when committed against a person not subject to the Bill, are triable
by an Assam Rifles Court only when committed on active duty or at any place outside India or
at any place specified by the Central Government by notification in this behalf.
9.54.2 The Ministry has further stated that clause 102 of the Bill provides for choice between
Criminal Court and Assam Rifles Court in the case of offences where Criminal Court and Assam
Rifles Court are having concurrent jurisdiction and in such cases the first choice will be with the
force authorities. Clause 103 of the Bill provides that if a Criminal Court is of the opinion that
proceedings should be instituted before it, then a written notice should be given to the force
authorities to deliver the offender and the force authorities shall either deliver the offender or in
case of difference of opinion shall forthwith refer the matter to the Central Government, whose
decision in the matter shall be final.
9.54.3 The Ministry feels that clause 55 deals with the civil offences and clause 56 prescribes the
restrictions placed on force authorities to deal with certain civil offences. Both these clauses are
included in the chapter dealing with ‘offences’ in the Bill. Clause 102 and 103 of the Bill provides
for choice between Criminal Court and Assam Rifles Court in the case of offences where both the
Courts are having concurrent jurisdiction and procedure for the same. Therefore, these clauses are
part of chapter on ‘Assam Rifles Court’. In view of this, the provisions of clauses 102 and 103
cannot be made proviso to the clauses 55 and 56.
9.54.4 It has further stated that clauses 102 and 103 of the Bill are framed in accordance with
provisions of Criminal Courts and Court-martial (Adjustment of Jurisdiction) Rules, 1978. These
Rules are made in exercise of powers conferred by sub-section (1) of section 475 of Code of
Criminal Procedure, 1973. The ibid section prescribes that when any person who is liable to be
tried either by a Court-martial or by a Criminal Court and brought before a Magistrate, he will
deliver such person to the Commanding Officer of that person for the purpose of being tried by
a Court-martial.
9.54.5 The Ministry has given some instances existing in the laws governing other Armed Forces
of the Union such as Sections 125 and 126 of Army Act, 1950, sections 80 and 81 of BSF Act,
1968, Sections 92 and 93 of ITBP Act, 1992, sections 77 and 78 of NSG Act, 1986.
9.54.6 Moreover, the Ministry feels that trial of force personnel in a Civil Court is not desirable
in all cases particularly considering the fact that the Units of the Force are liable to move to various
locations/places and pendency of court cases in old station may adversely affect the functioning
of the Unit because of the delay caused in Criminal Courts. It is pertinent to mention that in the
case of trial by an Assam Rifles Court, a Court is constituted to try a particular offender and the
trial is proceeded on a day-to-day basis. In most of the cases a trial is concluded within a period
of 1-2 months. Further, distinguishing offences for the purposes of jurisdiction based on offences

14
committed an duty and those committed while not on duty may not be practicable. In this
connection it may be noted that there are past instances of Armed Forces personnel involved in
espionage activities while on leave. In the Armed Forces, an individual is considered to be on duty
unless he is not on leave.
9.54.7 In the light of the fact cited the Ministry has clarified that a civilian who is a co-accused
along with Force personnel cannot be tried by an Assam Rifles Court. However, it feels that
necessary provisions will be made in the delegated legislation in order to enable the force authorities
to exercise the discretion vested in them by virtue of clause 102 on the line of provision existing
in other similar enactments. (Rule 41 of BSF Rules, 1969 and Rule 43 of ITBP Rules, 1994).
9.54.8 Subject to above the Committee adopts the clause.

Clause 57
9.56 Clause 57 seeks to provide for various punishments which may be awarded by Assam Rifles

Clause 62
9.61 Clause 62 seeks to authorise Commandants and other officers specified by the Director
General with the consent of the Central Government to try in the prescribed manner any person
lower in rank than an officer or subordinate officer for an offence and award one or more of the
punishments specified therein.
9.61.1 Replying to a Member’s observations that the offences should be classified as ‘major’ and
‘minor’ for the purpose of trial and punishment, the Ministry has stated that the Bill provides for
two types of trial – one, trial by an Assam Rifles Court (Clause 86) and two, summary trial under
clauses 62, 64, 65 and 66. The summary trial powers under clauses 62 and 66 are vested in the
Commandant of a Unit, whereas the powers under clauses 64 and 65 are vested in the DIG and
IG, respectively. Further, only the maximum punishment that can be awarded for an offence has
been mentioned in the Bill and considering the nature and circumstances of each case such lesser
punishment as deemed fit may be awarded. The list of punishments that can be awarded is
mentioned in clause 57 of the Bill.
9.61.2 The Ministry has further clarified that under the provisions of clause 61 of the Bill, persons
subject to the Bill can also be awarded punishments under clauses 62, 64, 65 and 66 without the
intervention of an Assam Rifles Court. Thus, the offences of lesser gravity like absenting without
leave for a short period, minor acts prejudicial to good order and discipline and other delinquencies
may be dealt with summarily without convening an Assam Rifles Court. It has also stated that
categorisation of offences as “major’ and ‘minor’ will curtail the power of force authorities to deal
with habitual offenders and thereby adversely affect the discipline of the force. For example, if
absent without leave for a short period is classified as a ‘minor offence entailing a minor
punishment’ it may lead to award of same punishment to a first time offender as well as to a

16
habitual offender. This is not desirable for the maintenance of discipline and good order in the
force. It has stated that under the present formulation of the Bill, such habitual offenders may be
dealt by the force authorities by convening an Assam Rifles Court. The discretion regarding mode
of the trial will be vested with the Commandant of the Unit in the delegated legislation as has been
done in BSF Rules, 1969 (Rule 51) ITBP Rules, 1994 (Rule 53) and Army Rules, 1954 (Rule 24).
Thus, the Ministry is of the view that classification of offences as ‘minor’ and ‘major’ is not
desirable in view of maintenance of discipline and good order in the force.
9.61.3 In view of the reply of the Ministry, the Committee observes that delinquencies of
minor nature should not be considered as an offence for awarding severe punishment. In
case previous misdemeanour is taken into account, the very essence of Section 324 of the
Criminal Procedure Code, 1973 should be kept in mind.
9.61.4 Subject to above, the Committee adopts the clause.

GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS
10. In regard to clauses 21 and 22 which prescribe death sentence and imprisonment for
a term which may extend to fourteen years, respectively, or such less punishment as is
mentioned in the Act, for offences in relation to the enemy, a view emerged in the
Committee that in case of such offences minimum punishment should also be prescribed so
as to ensure that a person who commits such an offence does not get away with punishment
which may not be commensurate with the offence. Accordingly, it was felt that the words
“or such less punishment as is in this Act mentioned” appearing in clauses 21 and 22 should
be suitably substituted by providing for minimum punishment in addition to the maximum
punishment already provided in the Bill.
10.1 Replying to the point raised, the Ministry of Home Affairs has stated that clauses 21 to 55
of the Bill mention the offences triable and punishable under the Bill and only the maximum
punishment that can be awarded for each offence has been specified therein. In each of these
clauses the words ‘or such less punishment as is in this Act mentioned’ has also been added in
order to enable the Court to award lesser punishment for offences of similar nature but of lesser
gravity. Thus the said provision is made in the clauses with a view to enable the Court to exercise
its discretion while awarding the punishment(s) having due regard to mitigating circumstances in
each case.
10.2 The Ministry has stated that if these words ‘or such...Act mentioned’ are deleted from
clauses 22 and 23, then the same may entail deletion of these words from other clauses as well.
It has justified the unacceptability of the proposition for deletion of the said words from clauses
21 and 22 on the following grounds:
(i) By deletion of the words ‘or such...Act mentioned’ discretionary powers of the
Courts will be curtailed and they will have no choice but to award punishment of
imprisonment upto the prescribed limit in each clause. The Bill prescribes a scale of
punishments .in clause 57 which ranges from ‘death sentence’ to ‘stoppage of pay
and allowances’. This scale lays down punishments as have been provided in Indian
Penal Code as well as penalties specified under CCS (CCA) Rules, 1965 which are
specific to those in Government Services. With inclusion of these penalties in the list
of punishments, Courts will have the discretion to award such punishments which
may vary from dismissal to reduction in payor pension etc. having due regard to
gravity of the offence proved against the accused, apart from or including
imprisonment upto the prescribed limit under each clause.
(ii) Assam Rifles consists of ‘cadre personnel’ and ‘deputationists from the Army’. At
present 80% of the officer cadre of the Force are deputationists from Army . Under
the provisions of clause 3(3) of the Bill the deputationists from Army are not subject
to the proposed Bill and will be  governed by the pr ovisions of Army Act, 1950. It
may be noted that clauses 21 to 55 of the Bill are framed in accordance with

33
sections 34 to 69 of Army Act, 1950 and the punishments prescribed for each
corresponding offence are also similar. In case the proposed amendment is made in
clause 22 and 23 of the bill, it will result in an Assam Rifles cadre personnel being
punished more severely vis-a-vis an army deputationist(s) for the same offence
committed under similar circumstances. This may result in an anomaly which is
against the ‘principles of equality before law’.
(iii) Clauses 21 to 55 have been framed on the precedence of different sections of the
Army Act, 1950, BSF Act, 1968 and ITBP  Act, 1992. Provisions under these sections
have stood the test of time as well as law and have not been reviewed by any
judicial pronouncements. Therefore, Assam Rifles being an Armed Force of the Union
like other Forces must have similar provisions regarding punishments.
10.2.1 In the light of the position as explained above, the Ministry is of the view that the words
“or such less punishment as is in this Act mentioned” should be retained in all the clauses in
Chapter IV .
10.3 In view of the reply of the Ministry of Home Affairs, the Committee is of the
considered view that the Government should undertake a comprehensive exercise to make
necessary amendments in the Army Act, 1950, BSF Act, 1968, ITBP Act, 1992 and similar
such Acts including the present Assam Rifles Bill after its enactment with a view to provide
for a minimum punishment in case of offences in relation to enemy.

— AT A GLANCE
NO NECESSITY FOR DEFINING ‘HOSTILE TRIBES’
The Committee pointed out that since all the tribes of the country are being brought
to the mainstream, there is no necessity for defining ‘hostile tribes’. In view of the
observation of the Committee, the Government has agreed to delete the term “hostile
tribes” from clause 2(1)(a)(i). (Para 9.1.1)
INSTITUTIONAL ARRANGEMENT FOR REDRESSAL OF GRIEVANCES
Regarding remedy to the grievances of aggrieved persons, the Committee felt that
there must be some institutional arrangement for redressal of grievances because a major
problem in all the forces has been that the superior officers sometimes tend to get very
oppressive, which should not be allowed to happen. It also felt that a time period for
redressal of grievances should be set out, failing which one could directly approach the
Commandant or the Director-General, as the case may be, for redressal of his grievances.
(Para 9.14.1)
MINOR OFFENCE SHOULD NOT BE AWARDING SEVERE PUNISHMENT
In view of the reply of the Ministry, the Committee observes that delinquencies of
minor nature should not be considered as an offence for awarding severe punishment. In
case previous misdemeanour is taken into account, the very essence of Section 324 of the
Criminal’ Procedure Code, 1973 should be kept in mind. (Para 9.61.3)
DELETION OF THE EXPRESSION ABSOLUTE FROM THE WORD  ‘ABSOLUTE
MAJORITY IN SUB-CLAUSE (1) OF THE CLAUSE 108
The Committee feels that the expression “absolute majority” in the clause is vague
and unnecessary in the context of sub clause (1) of the clause 108. It appears that the
intention is to provide for simple majority. Accordingly, the Committee suggested that the
word ‘absolute’ appearing before the word ‘majority should be omitted. (Para 9.107.1)
EXAMINATION OF  CLAUSES 121 TO 125
The Committee was of the view that the administrative Ministry and Law Ministry
may examine clauses 121 to 125 so that these are not inconsistent with the Lunacy
Act, 1912. (Para 9.120.1)
During the deliberations in the Committee on the Bill, a view was expressed that
powers to make regulations should be vested with the Director-General, Assam Rifles and
not with the Central Government as provided in the Bill. (Para 9.161.1)

Clause 55
6.5 Clause 55 seeks to provide for the trial of a person subject to this legislation for
commission of a civil offence, that is offences triable by courts of ordinary criminal justice, subject
to the provisions of clause 56 of this Bill.
6.5.1 Replying to an observation of a Member that there should be specific provision in the Bill
distinguishing offences which were triable by the Assam Rifles Courts and the Civil Courts, the
Ministry of Home Affairs had stated that clause 55 of the Bill prescribed the civil offences that
could be tried by an Assam Rifles Court subject to restrictions placed under clause 56 of the Bill.
The term ‘civil offence’ was defined in clause 2(e) of the Bill and it meant an offence which was
triable by a Criminal Court. As per the restrictions placed by clause 56, offences of murder,
culpable homicide not amounting to murder and rape, when committed against a person not subject
to the Bill, were triable by an Assam Rifles Court only when committed on active duty or at any
place outside India or at any place specified by the Central Government by notification in this
behalf.
6.5.2 The Ministry further stated that clause 102 of the Bill provided for choice between Criminal
Court and Assam Rifles Court in the case of offences where Criminal Court and Assam Rifles
Court were having concurrent jurisdiction and in such cases the first choice would be with the
force authorities. Clause 103 of the Bill provided that if a Criminal Court was of the opinion that
proceedings should be instituted before it, then a written notice should be given to the force
authorities to deliver the offender and the force authorities should either deliver the offender or in

49
case of difference of opinion, should forthwith refer the matter to the Central Government, whose
decision in the matter should be final.
6.5.3 The Ministry felt that clause 55 dealt with the civil offences and clause 56 prescribed the
restrictions placed on force authorities to deal with certain civil offences. Both these clauses were
included in the chapter dealing with ‘offences’ in the Bill. Clauses 102 and 103 of the Bill provided
for choice between Criminal Court and Assam Rifles Court in the case of offences where both the
Courts were having concurrent jurisdiction and procedure for the same. Therefore, these clauses
were part of chapter on ‘Assam Rifles Court’. In view of this, the provisions of clauses 102 and
103 could not be made proviso to the clauses 55 and 56.
6.5.4 It further stated that clauses 102 and 103 of the Bill were framed in accordance with
provisions of Criminal Courts and Court-martial (Adjustment of Jurisdiction) Rules, 1978. These
Rules were made in exercise of powers conferred by sub-section (1) of section 475 of Code of
Criminal Procedure, 1973. The ibid section prescribed that when any person who was liable to be
tried either by a Court-martial or by a Criminal Court and brought before a Magistrate, he would
deliver such person to the Commanding Officer of that person for the purpose of being tried by
a Court-martial.
6.5.5 The Ministry also quoted some identical provisions existing in the laws governing other
Armed Forces of the Union such as sections 125 and 126 of Army Act, 1950, sections 80 and
81 of BSF Act, 1968, sections 92 and 93 of ITBP Act, 1992, sections 77 and 78 of NSG Act,
1986.
6.5.6 Moreover, the Ministry felt that trial of force personnel in a Civil Court was not desirable
in all cases particularly considering the fact that the Units of the Force were liable to move to
various locations/places and pendency of court cases in old station might adversely affect the
functioning of the Unit because of the delay caused in Criminal Courts. It was pertinent to mention
that in the case of trial by an Assam Rifles Court, a Court was constituted to try a particular
offender and the trial proceeded on a day-to-day basis. In most of the cases a trial was concluded
within a period of 1-2 months. Further, distinguishing offences for the purposes of jurisdiction
based on offences committed on duty and those committed while not on duty might not be
practicable. In this connection, the Ministry pointed out that there were past instances of Armed
Forces personnel involved in espionage activities while on leave. In the Armed Forces, an individual
was considered to be on duty unless he was not on leave.
6.5.7 In the light of the fact cited the Min istry clarified that a civilian who was a co-accused
along with Force personnel could not be tried by an Assam Rifles Court. However, the Ministry
informed that necessary provisions would be made in the delegated legislation in order to enable
the force authorities to exercise the discretion vested in them by virtue of clause 102 on the lines
of provisions existing in other similar enactments. (Rule 41 of BSF Rules, 1969 and Rule 41 of
ITBP Rules, 1994).
6.5.8 Subject to above, the Committee adopted the clause.

Clause 62
6.9 Clause 62 seeks to authorise Commandants and other officers specified by the Director
General with the consent of the Central Government to try in the prescribed manner any person
lower in rank than an officer or subordinate officer for an offence and award one or more of the
punishments specified therein.
6.9.1 Replying to a Member’s observations that the offences should be classified as ‘major’ and
‘minor’ for the purpose of trial and punishment, the Ministry stated that the Bill provided for two
types of trial – one, trial by an Assam Rifles Court (Clause 86) and two, summary trial under
clauses 62, 64, 65 and 66. The summary trial powers under clauses 62 and 66 were vested in the
Commandant of a Unit, whereas the powers under clauses 64 and 65 were vested in the DIG and
IG, respectively. Further, only the maximum punishment that could be awarded for an offence had
been mentioned in the Bill and considering the nature and circumstances of each case such lesser
punishment as deemed fit might be awarded. The list of punishments that could be awarded was
mentioned in clause 57 of the Bill.
6.9.2 The Ministry further clarified that under the provisions of clause 61 of the Bill, persons
subject to the Bill could also be awarded punishments under clauses 62, 64, 65 and 66 without
the intervention of an Assam Rifles Court. Thus, the offences of lesser gravity like absenting
without leave for a short period, minor acts prejudicial to good order and discipline and other
delinquencies might be dealt with summarily without convening an Assam Rifles Court. It also
stated that categorisation of offences as “major’ and ‘minor’ would curtail the power of force
authorities to deal with habitual offenders and thereby adversely affect the discipline of the force.

51
For example, if absent without leave for a short period was classified as a ‘minor offence entailing
a minor punishment’ it might lead to award of same punishment to a first time offender as well
as to a habitual offender. This was not desirable for the maintenance of discipline and good order
in the force. It had stated that under the present formulation of the Bill, such habitual offenders
might be dealt with by the force authorities by convening an Assam Rifles Court. The discretion
regarding mode of the trial would be vested with the Commandant of the Unit in the delegated
legislation as had been done in BSF Rules, 1969 (Rule 51) ITBP Rules, 1994 (Rule 53) and Army
Rules, 1954 (Rule 24). Thus, the Ministry was of the view that classification of offences as
‘minor’ and ‘major’ was not desirable in view of maintenance of discipline and good order in the
force.

6.14.2 In response to the views expressed by the Committee, the Legislative Department, Ministry

the law relating to the governance of the Assam Rifles, an Armed Force of the Union for ensuring
the security of the border of India, to carry out counter–insurgency operations in the specified
areas and to act in aid of civil authorities for the maintenance of law and order and for matters

6.14.2.1 The Legislative Department further stated that the expression ‘lunatic’ was defined in

the said Act as “(1) “Asylum” means any asylum or mental hospital or lunatic establishment,
established or licensed by the Central Government or any State Government”. Further, the Mental
Health Act, 1987, was enacted to consolidate and amend the law relating to the treatment and care
of the mentally ill persons to make better provisions with respect to their property and affairs and
for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The expression ‘mentally ill person’ had been
defined in clause (1) of section 2 of the said Act which reads as “(1) ‘mentally ill person’ means
a person who was in need of treatment by reason of any mental disorder other than mental
retardation”. It stated that the concept of asylum which was defined under the Indian Lunacy
Act, 1912 was similar to the expressions “psychiatric nursing home” which had been defined in
clause (q) of section 2 of the Mental Health Act as follows:—
‘(q) “psychiatric hospital” or “psychiatric nursing home” means a hospital or, as the case
may be, a nursing home established or maintained by the Government or any other person for the
treatment and care of mentally ill persons and includes a convalescent home established or
maintained by the Central Government or any other person for such mentally ill persons; but does
not include any general hospital or general nursing home established or maintained by the
Government and which provides also for psychiatric services.
6.14.2.2 Further mentioning Section 5 of the Mental Health Act, 1987 the Legislative Department
stated that it empowered the Central Government and the State Governments to establish and
maintain the psychiatric hospitals or psychiatric nursing homes for the admission, treatment and
care of mentally ill persons.
6.14.2.3 Lastly, it had been stated that sections 111 to 115 of the Indo-Tibetan Border Police Force

Rifles Court shall be deemed to be a court within the meaning of Sections 345 and 346 of the Code
of Criminal Procedure, 1973.
6.16.1 In response to an observation of a Member that an explanation should be provided in the
Rules for clause 128 of the Bill, the Ministry stated that under the provisions of clause 128 the
proceedings before an Assam Rifles Court were deemed as judicial proceedings within the meaning
of Sections 193 and 228 of Indian Penal Code, 1860 and a Court within the meaning of Sections
345 and 346 of Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. These provisions had been made in the Bill to
allow initiation of proceedings in Civil Courts against personnel who were not subject to the Bill
for giving false evidence before an Assam Rifles Court or committing contempt against an Assam
Rifles Court. Thus, the Ministry was of the view that there was no need for further elaboration
of this clause under subordinate legislation.
6.16.2 The Committee adopted the clause without any change.
Clauses 129-134
6.17 The Committee adopted the clauses without any change.
Clause 135
6.18 Clause 135 seeks to provide for revision of a finding or sentence of an Assam Rifles Court,
which requires confirmation, once on the order of confirming authority by the same court.
6.18.1 Replying to a query of a Member that there was no provision in the Bill for review of
finding or sentence by the Court itself, the Ministry stated that clause 135 of the Bill provided for
revision of finding or sentence awarded by a general and petty Assam Rifles Courts, by the same
Court on the orders of confirming authority. It further stated that this clause had been framed in
the Bill based on Section 160 of Army Act, 1950, Section 105 of BSF Act, 1968, Section 109 of
NSG Act, 1986 and Section 127 of ITBP Act, 1992. The Ministry also stated that the procedure
for revision would be included in the delegated legislation which would be framed based on rules
of similar enactments.
6.18.2 Subject to above, the Committee adopted the clause.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
The Assam Rifles was initially raised in 1835 as Cachar Lavy and was
employed on frontier watch and ward duty to assist the civil administration in
the maintenance of law and order in the tribal areas of the erstwhile composite
State of Assam particularly in Lushai and Mikir Hills, Naga Hills, Khansi-Jantia
and Garo Hills as also in the erstwhile North-East Frontier Agency (Now
Arunachal Pradesh).
2. The Force came under the control of the Central Government in 1941
after the passing of the Assam Rifles Act, 1941 (5 of 1941). It was administered
by the Governor of Assam through the North-East Frontier Agency (NEFA)
under the overall control of the Ministry of External Affairs, and was later on
transferred to the Ministry of Home Affairs in 1965. Certain Assam Rifles
battalions were placed under the operational control of the Army. The strength
of the Assam Rifles battalions, was 5 battalions till 1950 and was increased to
take care of border security and internal security duties. The Assam Rifles is
now comprised of 39 battalions.
3. This Force has been charged with the responsibility of ensuring security
of the borders of India, to carry out counter insurgency operations in the
specified areas and to act in aid of civil authorities for the maintenance of law
and order and for matters connected therewith. The personnel of Assam Rifles
are governed by the Assam Rifles Act, 1941 except for the personnel of those
battalions of the Assam Rifles which are at present deployed under the
operational control of the Army in whose case the Army Act, 1950 is applicable
with certain exceptions and modifications. The gradual change in the complexion
of the Force has made the existing Assam Rifles Act, 1941 outdated. The Assam
Rifles Act, 1941 is not applicable to the officers of the Force and thus there is
a vacuum. Considering the nature and purpose of the Force and to bring
uniformity in application of the legal provisions, it is felt that a separate
enactment, which will provide for its special needs, should regulate the Force.
4. In view of above, it is proposed to repeal the existing Assam Rifles Act,
1941 and to bring the present legislation, The salient features of the proposed
legislation are as under:—
(i) the constitution of the Assam Rifles as an Armed Force of the
union, with provisions for its control and other provisions relating to its
establishment, etc.;
(ii) establishment and procedure of the Assam Rifles Courts,
offences committed by the Force members, punishment therefor and trial of
such offences by the Assam Rifles Courts;
(iii) provisions relating to retention, custody and trial of the
offenders under the proposed legislation;
(iv) confirmation, revision and execution of sentences, pardons and
remissions, etc., and miscellaneous provisions;
121

122
(v) deductions to be made from the pay and allowances of the
members of the Force for certain offences;
(vi) manner of execution of sentences of death as well as
imprisonment and provisions relating to powers and duties of members of
the Assam Rifles;
(vii) statutory protection for acts done by members of the Assam
Rifles in discharging their official duty.
5. The Bill seeks to achieve the above objects.

Government, Director General and an officer not below the rank of Deputy
Inspector General to pardon, mitigate, commute and grant parole to any person
subject to the proposed legislation convicted by an Assam Rifles Court.
Clause 151.—This clause seeks to provide for cancellation of conditional
pardon, parole and remission in certain cases.
Clause 152.—This clause seeks to provide the power of Central
Government, Director General or any officer empowered to convene a General

Clause 154.— This clause seeks to provide for release of an offender
when a sentence is suspended under clause 152.
Clause 155.—This clause seeks to provide that the period of sentence
spend under suspension shall be reckoned as part of term of such sentence.

Clause 156.—This clause seeks to provide that authority specified under
clause 152 may order that the offender shall undergo the un-expired portion of

Clause 157.—This clause seeks to provide for reconsideration of orders
of suspension of sentence in intervals of not more than four months.
Clause 158.—This clause seeks to provide that an offender, whose

Clause 162.—This clause seeks to empower the Central Government to
confer on members of the Force, the powers exercisable by other Government
officers under Central Acts in regard to the prevention of any offence against
such Acts or the apprehending of any person who has committed any such
offence. In the case of Central Acts expressly mentioned in the clause, the
powers can be conferred in respect of all offences because such offices are
primarily of a Tran-frontier nature. In the case of other Central Acts, the powers
can be conferred only in respect of cognizable offences. Further this clause
seeks to empower the Central Government to confer upon members of the Force
powers exercisable under State Acts, by police officers holding corresponding
ranks and makes it clear that such powers may be conferred on with the
concurrence of  the State Government concerned.
Clause 163.—This clause seeks to provide protection for acts of the
members of the Force who exercise or discharge any power or duty under sub
clauses (1) and (2) of clause 162 of the proposed legislation.
Clause 164.—This clause seeks to provide the power of Central

Clause 165.—This clause seeks to provide for the power of the Central

Clause 166.—This clause seeks to provide for the power of the Central

Clause 167.—This clause seeks to provide that the rules and regulations
made under the proposed legislation shall be laid before each House of Parliament.
Clause 168.—This clause seeks to provide for the repeal of Assam Rifles
Act, 1941 and saving of all acts, appointment and enrolment done under the
repealed Act.

Clause 2(1)(d) read with clause 105 of the Bill empowers the Central
Government to appoint Chief Law Officer and Law Officer to attend the General

to carry out counter insurgency operation in the specified areas and to act in aid
of civil authorities for the maintenance of law and order and the matters
connected therewith.

of the Force shall be vested and other officers to assist the Director-General.

6. The Assam Rifles was constituted in 1835 as Cachar Levy and was
employed on frontier watch and ward duty to assist the civil administration in
the maintenance of law and order in the tribal areas of the erstwhile composite
State of Assam particularly in Lushai Hills, Mikir Hills, Naga Hills, Khasi, Janitia

Arunachal Pradesh) will be deemed to have been constituted under the proposed
legislation ( vide Clause 168). All expenses incurred concerning the administration
of the Force would be met from the Consolidated Fund of India, As the Force
has already been constituted, no additional expenditure of a non recurring nature
is likely to be involved when the Bill is enacted and brought into Force. The
recurring expenditure on the administration of the force during 2002-2003, was
of the order of Rs. 718.17 crores.
