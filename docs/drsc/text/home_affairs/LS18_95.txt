INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs, having been
authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Ninety-fifth Report of the
Committee on the Repatriation of Prisoners Bill, 2002.
2.         In pursuance of the Rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees, the Chairman,
Rajya Sabha in consultation with the Speaker, Lok Sabha referred ** the Repatriation of Prisoners Bill, 2002 as
introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 9 May 2002 and pending therein, to the Committee for examination and report.
 
3.         The Committee considered the Bill in its sittings held on 3 July, 9 September and 9 October 2002.
 
3.1       The Committee took oral evidence of the Special Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs in its sitting held on 3
July 2002.
 
3.2       In its sitting held on 9 September 2002, the Committee took up clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill.
 
3.3       The Committee considered the draft Report on the Bill and adopted the same in its sitting held on 9 October
2002.
 
4.         In the course of its deliberations, the Committee has made use of the background note on the Bill received from
the Ministry of Home Affairs and replies of the Ministry to the queries raised by the Members in its sittings.
 
5.
                  For facility of reference and convenience, observations and recommendations of the Committee have been
printed in bold letters in the body of the Report.

REPORT
            The Statement of Objects and Reasons appended to the Repatriation of Prisoners Bill, 2002 (Annexure-I) states
that in the absence of any legal provision either in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 or any other law under which a
foreign prisoner could be transferred outside the country, there has been long detention of foreign prisoners in the jails
causing thereby considerable concern to the Government of India as well as the concerned foreign Governments.  It
was, therefore, felt that if foreign convicted nationals were transferred to their home countries to serve their remaining
jail terms, it would take care of the human aspect inasmuch as the convicts would be near their families and have better
chances of social rehabilitation.  In addition, it would also take care of the problems which the Indian jail authorities are
facing in keeping these prisoners. Thus, the Bill proposes legislation, in conjunction with bilateral treaties to enable the
Central Government to transfer foreign convicted persons to their country and vice versa.
 
2.         The salient features of the proposed legislation inter alia are as follows:
(a)
                a request for transfer will have to be made by the convicted prisoner or a person entitled to act on his
behalf in view of his age or physical or mental condition;
(b)               the request for transfer will have to be agreed upon by the transferring State and the receiving State;
(c)                the transfer will be effected if the judgement awarding the sentence is final in India i.e. no appeal or

95th Report of Committee on Home Affairs
file:///D/Committee%20on%20Home%20Affairs/95threport.htm[30-06-2016 17:55:46]
revision against the judgement is pending in any court;
(d)               the transfer will be made if the convicted prisoner is a citizen of the receiving State notwithstanding that
he may also be citizen of any other foreign state;
(e)                the enforcement of the sentence shall be governed by the law of the receiving State.  However, the
receiving State shall be bound by the legal nature and duration of the sentence as determined by the
transferring State;
(f)                 the enforcement of the sentence in the receiving State, to the extent that it has been enforced in the
transferring State, shall have the effect of discharing the sentence;
(g)                the transferring State alone shall be competent to decide any application for review of the judgement
and grant pardon, amnesty or commutation in accordance with its constitution or other laws;
(h)                The convicted person shall not be transferred if-
 
(i)         the transferring State is of the opinion that the request would prejudice its sovereignty, security,
national interest or other essential interests;
(ii)                any other case is pending in the transferring State against the convicted prisoner;
(iii)               he is convicted of an offence under military law not being an offence under the ordinary
criminal law of the transferring State; and
(iv)              the death penalty has been awarded to the convicted prisoner in the transferring State.
 
3.         In its background note on the Bill, the Ministry of Home Affairs has informed the Committee that there is
presently no legal provision under which foreign prisoners can be transferred to the country of their origin to serve their
sentence there nor is there any provision for the transfer of the persons of Indian origin convicted by a foreign court, to
serve their sentence here.  In the absence of legislative provision, it is not possible to execute treaties/agreements with
foreign Governments for transfer of convicted prisoners.  This apart, the sentences awarded by foreign courts cannot be
acted upon under the existing laws.
 
3.1       It has been further stated that the complaints have been received from time to time regarding long detention of
foreign prisoners in jails pending investigation and trial, and alleged unsatisfactory living conditions in which they are
kept.  Though the total number of foreign nationals in jails has never been very large yet they have been causing
considerable concern to the Government.  At the end of the year 1999, there were 665 foreign convicted and 1486
undertrial prisoners in different jails of the country.
 
3.2       The background note further states that those and other related issues concerning foreigners in jails were
discussed at a very high level meeting where it was inter alia decided to amend the Code of Criminal Procedure and/or
the transfer of Prisoners Act, 1950 for making an enabling provision for transfer of convicted prisoners from one
country to another.  The Ministry of Law and Justice was, however, of the view that a new legislation  may be enacted
for the transfer of foreign prisoners to the country of their origin on the lines of the Commonwealth Scheme for the
transfer of convicted offenders.
 
3.3       The background note goes on to state that in the recent past, the Government has negotiated agreements with the
Governments of United Kingdom, France and Norway in regard to the transfer of convicted offenders.  Similar
agreements with the Governments of other countries are under discussion.  However, in the absence of an enabling
legislation, these agreements cannot be acted upon.
 
3.4       The background note adds that accordingly a note dated 30 October 1996  was  submitted seeking approval of
the Cabinet for enacting legislation to enable the transfer of convicted foreign prisoners to the countries of their origin
and vice versa.  In its meeting held on 12 November 1996, the Cabinet considered the said proposal and approved the
same.  However, before the Bill in this regard could be proposed and introduced in Parliament, the Eleventh Lok Sabha
was dissolved on 4 December 1997.  In order to proceed further in the matter, a note dated 1 July 1998 was placed
before the Cabinet.  In its meeting held on 7 July 1998, the Cabinet considered the note, but did not approve the
proposal.  Considering the necessity of the proposed legislation, the Cabinet note was again submitted and was approved

3.5       The background note concludes that the proposed legislation in conjunction with bilateral treaties would enable
the Government to transfer the foreign convicted offenders to the country of their origin and receive Indian citizens
serving sentences in a country or place outside India.  Its provisions shall apply to the enforcement of sentences imposed
before as well as after the enactment of the law.
 
4.         The Bill consists of 16 clauses which were considered and adopted by the Committee as mentioned in the
succeeding paragraphs.
 
Clause 2
 
4.1       The clause contains the definitions of various terminology required to be used in the enactment.
 
4.2       A point was raised by the Committee regarding the definition of ‘prisoner’ as prescribed in clause 2(c)  as to
whether the prisoners sent to the jail by the Civil Court were also included in the definition.  The written response of the
Ministry of Home Affairs in this regard is reproduced below:
 
“The definition of prisoner under clause 2 (c ) provides that prisoner means a person undergoing a sentence of
imprisonment under an order passed by Criminal Court including a Court established under the law for the time
being in force in contracting State.  The definition of the word “prisoner” does not include a prisoner sent to jail
by a Civil Court.  There is, therefore, no ambiguity in the definition of prisoner who are covered under the
proposed law”.
 
4.3       Regarding the word “warrant”, the Committee wanted to know whether the ‘warrant’ as stipulated in the clause,
can be issued by a  Joint Secretary to the Government.
 
4.4       The  Ministry of Home Affairs  furnished the comments of the Ministry of Law and Justice as  given below:
 
“It may be noted that warrant contemplated under section 105 and 105B, Cr.P.C. is a warrant for arrest of a
person, who is free, but the warrant conceived under clauses 7(1) and 8 of the Bill is not a warrant for arrest, but
a warrant only to deliver the custody of a prisoner to the person authorized by the contracting State to which the
prisoner is to be transferred.  There seems to be no conflict between the provisions of the Cr.P.C. and the Bill, as
the nature of the warrants under the two are different”.
 
4.5       The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 3
 
4.6       The clause deals with the application of the Act.
 
4.7       Regarding sub-clause (3) of the clause,  the Committee wanted to know as to whether the sub-clause proposed to
give power to the Central Government to circumvent and modify legal/judicial processes on the strength of a simple
notification in the Official Gazette.  The clarification  of Ministry of Home Affairs is reproduced below:
 
“Clause 3(3) of the Bill provides that if  the Central Government is of the opinion that with respect to a country
or place outside India, provisions of this Act require to be modified to give effect to a treaty in relation to such
country, it may, by notification in the Official Gazette, direct that the application of this Act to such country
shall be subject to such conditions, exceptions and modifications specified in the notification.  This clause does
not empower the Central Government to alter the mandatory legislative intentions.  This provision only
authorizes the Central Government to make required modifications to give effect to a treaty with another country
subject to certain conditions and exceptions to be specified in the Official notification.  Such notification may be
complementary and not in conflict with the substantive provisions.  In this connection kind attention is also
invited to Section 105L, Cr.P.C. as well as Section 3 (3) (c ) of the Extradition Act, 1962, which are similar to

State”.
 
4.8       The clause is adopted without any change.
Clause 4
 
4.9       The clause deals with the application to be made to the Government by a foreign prisoner who is the citizen of
the contracting State for transfer of his custody to that contracting State.  If the prisoner is unable to make an application
himself because of his health, mental condition, old age or being a minor, then any other person entitled to act on this
behalf may make the application.
 
4.10     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 5
 
4.11     The clause provides for power of the Union Government for consideration of request  made under clause 4.
 
4.12     The Committee wanted to know from the Government whether any request of the foreign prisoner belonging to
the country with which no treaty existed could be considered.  The representative of the Ministry of Home Affairs
replied that as mentioned in clause 3, the legislation aimed at the transfer of prisoner between the contracting States.
 
4.13     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 6
 
4.14     The clause deals with the comments to be sought from the contracting States on the application made under the
provision of clause 3.
 
4.15     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 7
 
4.16     The clause seeks to make a provision for the procedure for consideration of the request and action to be taken
thereon by the Union Government regarding the transfer of foreign prisoners.
 
4.17     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 8
 
4.18     This clause aims to provide for issue of a warrant on behalf of the Union Government directing the officer-in-
charge of the prison to deliver the custody of the prisoner to the person authorised by the contracting State to which the
prisoner is to be transferred.
 
4.19     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 9
 
4.20     The clause provides for the operation of warrant and retaking of foreign prisoner.
 
4.21     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 10
 
4.22     The clause refers to the transfer of record of any proceedings including judicial proceedings relating to that

4.23     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 11
 
4.24     The clause makes a provision for safeguarding the power of the court  to review its judgement  and  the Central
Government or State Government to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of  the prisoners to be transferred in
accordance with any law for the time being in force.
 
4.25     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 12
 
4.26     The clause provides for acceptance of the transfer of the Indian prisoner.
 
4.27     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 13
 
4.28     The clause provides for the procedure to be followed in respect of determination of prison and issue of warrant
for receiving transfer in India.
 
4.29     The Committee expressed its apprehension regarding sub-clause (6) of this clause giving the executive powers to
the Government, which appears to be in conflict with the prevailing judicial system.
 
4.30     The Ministry of Home Affairs, in its reply, stated that if the sentence of imprisonment passed against the
prisoner in the contracting State is incompatible with the Indian law as to its nature, duration or both, the Central
Government may, by order, adapt  the sentence of such punishment as to the nature, duration or both as the case  may
be, as is compatible to the sentence of imprisonment provided for a similar offence had that offence been committed in
India.  The sentence so adapted shall as far as possible correspond with the sentence imposed by the judgement of the
contracting  State to the prisoner and the adapted sentence shall not aggravate the punishment by its nature, duration or
both.
 
4.31     The Committee notes the reply and adopts the clause without any change.
 
Clause 14
 
4.32     This clause prescribes for making rules under the Act.
 
4.33     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 15
 
4.34     The clause makes a provision for the Government for laying of rules etc. before Parliament as prescribed under
Clause 14.
 
4.35     The clause is adopted without any change.
 
Clause 16
 
4.36     The clause aims at making such provisions not inconsistent with the provisions of this Act as may appear
necessary for removing the difficulties that might arise in  giving effect to the provisions of this Act.

Clause 1, Enacting Formula and the Title
 
4.38     Clause 1, Enacting formula and the Title are adopted without any change.
 
4.39     The Committee is of the view that there should be a provision in the Bill itself for laying of Annual Report on

arrangements signed or agreed upon between India and other countries as also the number of prisoners repatriated from

4.40     The Committee recommends that the Bill be passed after incorporating its suggestions.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS
           
Long detention of foreign prisoners in Indian jails has been causing considerable concern to the Government of
India and the foreign Governments concerned. It was, therefore, felt that if foreign convicted nationals were transferred
to their home countries to serve their remaining jail terms, it would take care of the human aspect inasmuch as the said
convicts would be near their families and have better chances of social rehabilitation.  Further, it would also take care of
the problems which the Indian jail authorities are facing in keeping these prisoners.  There is presently no legal
provision either in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 or any other law under which a foreign prisoner could be
transferred outside India.  The Bill proposes legislation, in conjunction with bilateral treaties to enable the Central
Government to transfer foreign convicted persons to their country and vice versa.
2.         The salient features of the proposed legislation inter alia, are as follows:-
            (a)        a request for transfer will have to be made by the convicted prisoner or a person entitled to act on his
behalf in view of his age or physical or mental condition;
            (b)        the request for transfer will have to be agreed upon by the transferring State and the receiving State;
            (c )       the transfer will be effected if the judgement awarding the sentence is final in India i.e. no appeal or
revision against the judgement is pending in any court;
            (d)        the transfer will be made if the convicted prisoner is a citizen of the receiving State notwithstanding that
he may also be citizen of any other foreign State;
            (e)        the enforcement of the sentence shall be governed by the law of the receiving State.  However, the
receiving State shall be bound by the legal nature and duration of the sentence as determined by the transferring State;
            (f)        the enforcement of the sentence in the receiving State, to the extent that it has been enforced in the
transferring State, shall have the effect of discharging the sentence;
            (g)        the transferring State alone shall be competent to decide any application for review of the judgement and
grant pardon, amnesty or commutation in accordance with its constitution or other laws;
(h)        the convicted person shall not be transferred if-
(i)         the transferring State is of the opinion that the request would prejudice its sovereignty, security,
national interest or other essential interests;
(ii)        any other case is pending in the transferring State against the convicted prioner;
(iii)
             he is convicted of an offence under military law not being an offence under the ordinary criminal
law of the transferring State; and
(iv)       the death penalty has been awarded to the convicted prisoner in the transferring State.
 
3.         The Bill seeks to achieve the above objects.

FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM
 
 
            The proposed legislation aims at providing for the transfer of certain prisoners from  India to a country or place
outside India and reception in India of certain prisoners from a country or place outside India.  This legislation would be
implemented in conjunction with bilaterial treaties as provided in clause 3 of the Bill.
 
            2.  The treaty to be entered into with the country outside India may incorporate therein the provision for
expenses on account of transportation of prisoners.  At this stage, it is not possible to calculate the probable expenditure
on this account.  Hence, it is not possible to provide exact amount of the recurring and non-recurring expenditure in
details.
MEMORANDUM  REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION
 
            Sub-clause (1) of  clause 3 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to direct by notification in the Official
Gazette, a country or place outside India to which the provisions of the proposed legislation shall apply.
 
            2.  Clause 14 of the Bill empowers the Central Government  to make rules and such rules, in particular, provide
for the means through which an application of the prisoner may be forwarded under sub-section (1)  of section 6, the
forms in which a warrant may be issued under sub-section  (1)  of section  7 and sub-section (2) of section 12 and any
other matter which may be prescribed under clause (d) of sub-section (4)  of section 13 of the proposed legislation.
 
            3.  The matters in respect of which rules may be made are matters of procedure and administrative detail. The
delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal character.
