INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairperson, Standing Committee on Chemicals and Fertilizers 
 (2025-26) having been authorized by the Committee, do present on their behalf this  
Thirty-fourth Report on Action taken by the Government on the Observations/Recommendations 
of the Committee contained in their Twenty-third Report (Eighteenth Lok Sabha) on ‘Demands for

Pharmaceuticals.

March, 2026. The Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, Department of Pharmaceuticals furnished

contained in the Twenty-third Report. The Committee considered and adopted this Report at their

contained in the Twenty-third Report (Eighteenth Lok Sabha) of the Committee is given in 
Appendix-II.

Observation/Recommendation No. 10 
 
PROMOTION OF RESEARCH AND INNOVATION IN PHARMA MEDTECH SECTOR (PRIP): 
Fund utilization in FY 2025-26 
 
1.5.   The Committee made the following recommendation regarding financial performance of 
PRIP scheme and need for enhanced utilisation of funds: 
 
“The Committee note that for the Financial 2025-26, while there has been no revision of 
funds allotted at BE stage to RE stage, the utilized amount stands at ₹ 107.23 crore (as on 
20.02.2026) out of ₹ 245 crore i.e. 44 percent fund utilization upto 31st January, 2026. The 
Committee take note of the Department’s submission that initial low utilization is typical in 
research and infrastructure-oriented schemes and that the PRIP Scheme has since been 
restructured with revised guidelines (issued on 01.10.2025), extended timeline up to FY 
2029–30 and strengthened monitoring mechanisms. In this regard, the Committee hope that 
with the revised framework and strengthened oversight in respect of PRIP , substantial 
improvement in fund utilization in FY 2025-26 and the coming financial years may be 
achieved by the Department to ensure that the Scheme is successful in materializing its 
intended objectives.” 
 
 
1.6    In their Action Taken Notes, the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (Department of 
Pharmaceuticals) submitted as under: 
 
“Pursuant to restructuring of the PRIP Scheme and issuance of revised guidelines, 
monitoring and review mechanisms have been strengthened to ensure timely 
implementation of approved activities and improved utilisation of funds under the Scheme. 
The Department is making continuous efforts towards effective implementation of the 
Scheme for achievement of its intended objectives. 
       Under the Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma MedTech Sector (PRIP) 
Scheme, applications under Component-B were invited through the online portal and a total 
of 710 applications were received from startups, MSMEs and industry by the last date of 
submission i.e. 19.11.2025. The Technical Committee (TC) held a series of meetings to 
review the screening progress, guide the evaluation framework and facilitate timely 
completion of the assessment process. 
    Based on the recommendations of the TC, Project Appraisal and Approval Committee 
(PAAC) recommended 9 projects for funding support of Rs.324 Crore under this scheme.   
Accordingly, an amount of ₹67.99 crore has been released to the selected applicants for 
undertaking R&D activities. 
Further, 12 additional projects recommended by the TC have been considered by PAAC in 
its 3rd meeting held on 21.5.2026 of which 11 projects were approved for funding support of 
Rs. 393 Cr under this scheme. 

3 
 
    Under Component-A support is provided to NIPERs for Centres of Excellence (CoEs) set 
up under the scheme. The institutes have been advised to ensure industry participation while 
undertaking projects under CoEs, so as to enhance industry relevance and facilitate 
translational research outcomes under the scheme. Under this component 118 research 
projects have been approved, 81 research papers were published and 9 patents filed. 
Further an amount of ₹ 181.72 crore was utilised in the financial year 2025-26 under this 
scheme.” 
 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
1.7 The Committee, in their earlier recommendation, had hoped that with the revised 
framework and strengthened oversight in respect of Promotion of Research and Innovation 
in Pharma-MedTech Sector (PRIP) Scheme, substantial improvement in fund utilization in 
Financial Year 2025-26 and the coming financial years would be achieved by the Department 
to ensure that the Scheme is successful in materializing its intended objectives. The 
Department, in their Action Taken Reply, have submitted that continuous efforts are being 
made towards effective implementation of the Scheme for achievement of its intended 
objectives. The Department have also submitted under Component A of the PRIP Scheme, 
118 research projects have been approved, 81 research papers were published and 9 
patents were filed and under Component-B, 9 projects for funding support of ₹ 324 crore, 
have been recommended and 11 projects were approved for funding support of ₹ 393 crore 
under this scheme by the Project Appraisal and Approval Committee (PAAC). However, the 
Committee note that despite restructuring of the PRIP Scheme, issuance of revised 
guidelines, strengthening of monitoring and review mechanisms, an amount of ₹ 181.72 
crore only out of ₹ 245 crore allocated at Revised Estimates (RE) stage i.e. 74 percent of RE 
allocation could be utilised in the Financial Year 2025-26. The Committee, therefore, 
recommend that the Department should initiate concrete steps to ensure optimal utilisation 
of allocated funds in the ongoing and coming Financial Years.

Observation/Recommendation No. 18 
 
REVAMPED PHARMACEUTICALS TECHNOLOGY UPGRADATION ASSISTANCE SCHEME 
(RPTUAS): Expenditure incurred under the Scheme 
 
1.14.   The Committee made the following recommendation regarding non-utilisation of funds 
under RPTUAS and need for time-bound disbursement of funds: 
 
“The Committee learn that Revamped Pharmaceutical Technology Upgradation Assistance 
Scheme (RPTUAS), a sub-scheme under Scheme for Strengthening of Pharmaceutical 
Industry (SPI)/ Development of Pharmaceuticals Industry, aims to facilitate technology and 
processes upgradation of existing pharmaceutical units to Revised Schedule-M and World 
Health Organization - Good Manufacturing Practices (WHO-GMP) certification. The scheme 
guidelines were revised in March 2024 to enhance its uptake and support the technological 
advancement of the Indian pharmaceutical industry, ensuring alignment with global 
standards. The Financial outlay is ₹ 300.10 crore for the period FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26. 
However, the Committee regret to note that despite Budget Estimates allocation of ₹ 95 
crore in 2023–24, ₹ 5 crore in 2024–25 and ₹ 100 crore in 2025–26, no actual expenditure 
has been incurred under the Scheme so far.  
The Committee have been given to understand that release and utilization of financial 
assistance under the scheme are contingent upon the completion of procurement of plant 
and machinery, installation, technology upgradation and the receipt of certification and 
verification from the concerned drug regulatory authority/authorities confirming compliance 
with the revised Schedule M of the Drugs Rules, 1945, and the World Health Organization 
– Good Manufacturing Practices. The approved projects are at different stages of 
implementation. However, approval of ₹ 18.24 crore in favour of 18 applicants, who had 
applied during the initial phase of the scheme, is under process and the amount will be 

5 
 
released shortly. Therefore, funds have not been disbursed under the scheme so far, and to 
that extent the corresponding financial outlay is committed expenditure. During oral 
evidence, it was also submitted by the representative of the Department that interaction with 
units on the ground was not envisioned as part of this Scheme. In this regard, the Committee 
observe that sole reliance on post-certification reimbursement model and lack of field-level 
engagement with beneficiary MSME units have rendered the funds allotted under the 
Scheme completely unspent. Therefore, the Committee recommend that urgent measures 
to ensure that funds under the RPTUAS earmarked for technological upgradation of eligible 
MSMEs are disbursed in a time-bound manner in the final Financial Year i.e. 2025-26 may 
be taken by the Department and the Committee be apprised accordingly.” 
 
 
1.15 In their Action Taken Notes, the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (Department of 
Pharmaceuticals) submitted as under: 
 
“The observations and recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee have been noted. 
It is submitted that under the RPTUAS, the Scheme Steering Committee (SSC) has 
approved 301 applications/pharmaceutical units for incentives aggregating to approximately 
₹300.48 crore, which is nearly equivalent to the total outlay of the sub-scheme, as on 31st 
March, 2026. 
Further, out of the 301 approved applicants, claims of 64 pharmaceutical units amounting to 
₹45.80 crore have been duly approved. Of this, an amount of ₹41.70 crore has already been 
disbursed for 55 pharmaceutical units during FY 2025–26. 
The implementing agency of the Department is working in close coordination with the 
pharmaceutical units that have applied under the scheme to monitor the progress of their 
projects. However, it has been observed that the operations of pharmaceutical units are 
adversely affected during the upgradation period. 
Accordingly, micro and small pharmaceutical units tend to undertake upgradation activities 
in a phased manner over a period of time rather than executing them at once, which results 
in delays in project completion. In contrast, medium-sized units may have backup production 
facilities to manage such transitions. Further, pharmaceutical units generally prioritize 
meeting their existing market commitments and demand obligations, which also contributes 
to the slower pace of implementation. 
The status of the remaining approved applicants/pharmaceutical units is as under: 
No. of claims under process 29 for Rs.54.07 crore 
No. of pharma units who have indicated completion of 
projects, but yet to submit certificates 
56 for Rs.53.65 crore 
No. of pharma units whose projects are under various stages 
of implementation

” 
COMMENTS OF THE COMMITTEE 
1.16 The Committee had recommended that urgent measures may be taken by the 
Department to ensure that funds under the Revamped Pharmaceutical Technology 

6 
 
Upgradation Assistance Scheme (RPTUAS) earmarked for technological upgradation of 
eligible Micro, Small and Medium Pharma Enterprises (MSMEs) be disbursed in a time-
bound manner in the final Financial Year i.e. 2025-26. The Department, in its Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted that under the Scheme, 301 applications/pharmaceutical units for 
incentives aggregating to approximately ₹ 300.48 crore have been approved, which is nearly 
equivalent to the total outlay of the sub-scheme, as on 31st March, 2026. Further, out of the 
301 approved applicants, claims of 64 pharmaceutical units amounting to ₹ 45.80 crore have 
been duly approved and of this, an amount of ₹ 41.70 crore has already been disbursed for 
55 pharmaceutical units during FY 2025–26. The remaining number of approved 
applicants/pharmaceutical units i.e. 237 out of 301 approved applicants/pharmaceutical 
units are stated to be at various stages, including claims under process, projects completed 
but yet to submit certificates and projects under implementation. The Committee note that 
despite approvals nearing the total scheme outlay of ₹ 300.10 crore and some progress in 
disbursement during FY 2025–26, a substantial number of approved units remain at various 
stages of implementation, with a significant portion of funds yet to be released. The reasons 
impacting timely fund disbursement and utilisation under the Scheme, as cited by the 
Department, include adverse effect of upgradation period on operations of pharmaceutical 
units, phased upgradation by MSME units and prioritization of market commitments and 
demand obligations by the units. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Department should institute urgent facilitation mechanisms for handholding and guiding 
the 237 remaining approved applicants/pharmaceutical units in achieving timely and 
smooth upgradation, so that funds earmarked under the RPTUAS are disbursed in a time-
bound manner. The Committee may be apprised of the action taken in the matter.

operationalization of the three Bulk Drug Parks at Nakkapalli (Andhra Pradesh), Bharuch

furnished to the Committee at the earliest.

Observation/Recommendation No. 1 
 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION VIS-A-VIS UTILISATION 
2.1 Projection vs allocation of funds to the Department of Pharmaceuticals for                            
BE 2026-27  
The Committee note that against the projected requirement of ₹ 6,642.54 crore at the Budget 
Estimates (BE) stage for FY 2026–27, the Department has been allocated ₹ 4,831.22 crore, 
on the basis of utilisation levels of the Department of Pharmaceutical’s schemes during the 
ongoing financial year. Taking into account this significant gap of ₹ 1800 crore between the 
projection and the final allocation to the Department of Pharmaceuticals based on utilisation 
trends, the Committee would like to be apprised on the course of action of the Department 
to run its schemes with reduced allocation. 
2.2 In their Action Taken Notes, the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (Department of 
Pharmaceuticals) submitted as under: 
The scheme wise details of steps initiated are as under: 
 
(1) Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Promoting Domestic Manufacturing of 
Medical Devices 
 
The PLI Scheme for Medical Devices, with a total outlay of ₹3,420 crore and a five-year 
incentive period from FY 2022–23 to FY 2026–27, has made significant progress towards 
strengthening domestic manufacturing capacity and reducing import dependence. 
A total of 77 applications were received across five rounds, out of which 27 applicants have been 
approved, with a committed investment of ₹1,161.53 crore and expected employment generation 
of approximately 7,399 persons. 
 
The key outcomes under the scheme are as follows: 
 
 23 projects have already been commissioned, resulting in the manufacturing of 57 medical 
devices.  
 Investments worth ₹1,138.95 crore have been made against the committed investment of 
₹1,161.53 crore.  
 Domestic manufacturing of high-end medical devices has commenced, including Linear 
Accelerator (LINAC), CT-Scan, MRI Scan, Mammogram, C-Arm, X-Ray machines, MRI 
Coils and Ultrasound machines.  
 As of December 2025, cumulative eligible sales of ₹13,624.52 crore have been achieved, 
including export sales of ₹6,425.48 crore.  
 
These developments have contributed significantly towards reducing import dependence and 
enhancing domestic value addition in the sector. 

9 
 
 
(2) Scheme for Promotion of Medical Devices Parks 
 
The Scheme for Promotion of Medical Devices Parks aims to provide world-class common 
infrastructure facilities to manufacturers, thereby enhancing competitiveness and reducing 
production costs through economies of scale. 
Under the scheme, three Medical Device Parks are being developed in Greater Noida (Uttar 
Pradesh), Ujjain (Madhya Pradesh), and Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu), with a total project cost of 
₹871.11 crore, including Central assistance of ₹100 crore for each park. 
 
The outcomes achieved so far include: 
 Civil construction in all three parks is at an advanced/final stage.  
 As of March 2026, land has been allotted to 210 medical device manufacturers across a 
total area of 355 acres.  
 42 units have commenced construction of their manufacturing facilities.  
 5 companies have already started production.  
 
The scheme is expected to significantly strengthen the manufacturing ecosystem by enabling 
access to shared infrastructure and fostering cluster-based development. 
 
(3) Scheme for Strengthening of Medical Device Industry 
 
The Scheme for Strengthening of Medical Device Industry, launched on 08.11.2024 with a 
financial outlay of ₹500 crore (FY 2024–25 to 2027–28), is aimed at addressing critical gaps across 
the value chain through targeted interventions. The progress under its sub-schemes is as follows: 
 
(a) Common Facilities for Medical Device Clusters: Nine projects have been approved, 
including three for creation of common manufacturing facilities and six for establishment of testing 
facilities, aimed at strengthening infrastructure and improving cluster competitiveness. 
 
(b) Marginal Investment Scheme for Reducing Import Dependence (MIS-RID): Against a 
budgetary outlay of ₹180 crore, 10 proposals have been approved with a total grant-in-aid of ₹44.16 
crore, leveraging a total project cost of ₹277.39 crore. The scheme is facilitating domestic 
manufacturing of critical components and reducing reliance on imports. 
 
(c) Capacity Building and Skill Development in the Medical Device Sector: With an outlay of 
₹100 crore, a total of 13 proposals for Master’s programmes and 5 proposals for PG Diploma 
programmes have been approved. An amount of approximately ₹18.12 crore has been reimbursed 
to 10 beneficiary institutions during FY 2025–26, contributing to the development of a skilled 
workforce aligned with industry needs. 
 
(d) Medical Device Clinical Studies Support Scheme: Under this sub-scheme (outlay ₹100 
crore), 18 proposals have been approved with a recommended grant-in-aid of ₹7.25 crore against 
a total project cost of ₹37.25 crore. The scheme is supporting clinical validation and facilitating 
regulatory approvals of indigenous devices. 

10 
 
 
(e) Medical Device Promotion Scheme: With an outlay of ₹10 crore, initiatives such as seminars, 
workshops, and awareness programmes are being conducted to promote the sector. The India 
MedTech Expo 2025 was successfully organized from 4–6 September 2025 at Bharat Mandapam, 
New Delhi, in collaboration with the Export Promotion Council for Medical Devices (EPCMD), 
enhancing industry outreach and stakeholder engagement.

Observation/Recommendation No. 2 
 
Reduction of funds allocated at RE stage  
2.3 The Committee note that the budgetary allocations of the Department of 
Pharmaceuticals have shown a consistently increasing trend from ₹ 3,160.06 crore (BE 
2023-24) to ₹ 4,089.95 crore (BE 2024-25) and further to ₹ 5,268.12 crore (BE 2025-26). 
However, Budget Estimates (BE) allocation of the Department for the year 2023-24 was ₹ 
3160.06 crore which was reduced to ₹ 2697.9 crore at Revised Estimates (RE) stage.

viability of Jan Aushadhi Kendras, thereby encouraging greater participation and ensuring 
sustained operations by Kendra owners.  
 
2. Extension of Credit Facility: The credit facility extended to distributors has been increased 
from the existing 45 days to 90 days. Correspondingly, distributors shall extend the credit 
period to JAKs from 30 to 75 days. This measure is expected to significantly reduce the 
working capital burden on Kendra owners, improve their cash flow position, and motivate 
them to maintain a wider range of Jan Aushadhi medicines and products for the benefit of 
consumers.  
 
3. Product Rationalisation and Inventory Support Measures:  
The Department has also rationalized and categorized the products offered under the 
PMBJP Scheme as fast- and slow-moving products. The slow-moving products shall be 
supplied to JAKs on a returnable basis in case of expiry. These measures are aimed at 
reducing the apprehension among Kendra owners regarding expiry-related losses and 
inventory risks associated with slow-moving products. The initiative is also expected to 

22 
 
improve the overall availability and range of Jan Aushadhi medicines across all Kendras 
under the Scheme.  
The above measures collectively aim to strengthen the operational sustainability of Jan 
Aushadhi Kendras, enhance retailer confidence, improve medicine availability, and further the 
objectives of the PMBJP Scheme in providing affordable and quality medicines to the public. 
Further, a one-time special incentive of ₹2 lakh for setting up JAK is given for JAKs opened 
in the North-Eastern States, Himalayan areas, island territories and backward areas notified as 
Aspirational Districts by NITI Aayog, and to those JAKs that are opened by entrepreneurs of certain 
eligible categories, including Women, ex-servicemen, divyangjan and members of the Scheduled 
Castes and Scheduled Tribes. 
Recent trends indicate a clear movement towards financial self-reliance among JAKs, driven 
by increased public awareness and trust in generic medicines, higher acceptance of Jan Aushadhi 
products, expansion of the product basket (including surgical items, nutraceuticals, and 
consumables), improved supply chain management and digital monitoring systems and enhanced 
margin realization through the introduction of high-MRP, high-demand Jan Aushadhi products. 
The product basket under the Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana has 2,110 
medicines and 315 medical devices and consumables, covering all major therapeutic groups, such 
as cardiovascular, anti-cancer, anti-diabetic, anti-infectives, anti-allergic and gastro-intestinal 
medicines and nutraceuticals.  The Department acknowledges the recommendation of the 
Committee to enhance the financial viability of Jan Aushadhi Kendras. Presently, the scheme is 
focused on its core objective, which is to provide affordable medicine to common man. Further, 
matters such as insurance on health is covered under other Government schemes such as 
Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana.

along with an additional financial incentive of 20% linked to stock maintenance and monthly 
purchases of Jan Aushadhi medicines, subject to a maximum ceiling of Rs. 20,000 per month.

effective return of 40%. 
In order to further strengthen the financial viability and self-sustainability of Jan Aushadhi Kendras 
as well as reducing the dependency of Kendra owners on incentives, the incentive structure and 
product margin have now been revised. Under the revised framework, the trade margin provided

to JAKs has been enhanced from 20% to 30%, while the financial incentive component has been 
reduced from 20% to 12%.

generation of Rs. 42,000, equivalent to an effective return of 42%. Similarly, on a monthly purchase 
of Rs. 2,00,000, a Kendra shall now receive Rs. 60,000 as trade margin and Rs. 12,000 as financial 
incentive, resulting in a total revenue generation of Rs. 72,000, which used to be Rs.60,000 with 
the previous model. 
The revised structure is expected to significantly improve the operational sustainability of Jan 
Aushadhi Kendras by increasing their direct trade earnings rather than relying predominantly on 
incentives. Further, Kendras will continue to derive additional earnings from incremental sales 
without any upper ceiling, thereby encouraging them to enhance the sale and outreach of Jan 
Aushadhi medicines across the country.

PRODUCTION LINKED INCENTIVE SCHEME FOR PROMOTION OF DOMESTIC 
MANUFACTURING OF CRITICAL KEY STARTING MATERIALS (KSMS)/ DRUG

Reduction in excessive dependence on a single source of critical Key Starting Materials 
(KSMs)/ Drug Intermediates (DIs)/ Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) in India. 
2.7 With a view to avoid disruption in supply of critical APIs (used to make critical drugs 
for which there are no alternatives) by reducing excessive dependence on a single source, 
‘Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for promotion of domestic manufacturing of 
critical Key Starting Materials (KSMs) / Drug Intermediates (DIs) and Active Pharmaceutical 
Ingredients (APIs) in India’ (commonly known as PLI scheme for Bulk Drugs) was approved 
by the Government of India on 20th March, 2020. The scheme intends to boost domestic 
manufacturing of identified KSMs, DIs and APIs by attracting large investments in the sector 
and thereby reduce India's import dependence in critical APIs. The primary objective of the 
Scheme is to boost domestic manufacturing of 41 identified critical products and thereby 
attain self-reliance in key APIs/ KSMs which were largely imported prior to implementation 
of the Scheme. The tenure of the scheme is till the financial year 2029-30. Regarding the 
extent to which the scheme has been able to reduce dependence on a single source for 
production of Critical Drugs and boost domestic manufacturing of identified KSMs etc., the 
Committee have been apprised that out of the 41 identified products, 33 have been 
subscribed. Among these, production has commenced for 17 products and manufacturing 
capacity has been established for 11 additional products. Thus, capacity creation has been 
completed for 28 notified products. Domestic manufacturing capacity of approximately 
56,800 MT per annum has been developed for these 28 critical products. The scheme has 
resulted in cumulative sales of ₹ 2,720 crore reported till December 2025, including exports 
of ₹ 527.96 crore, thereby avoiding imports worth ₹ 2,192.04 crore. The Committee note that 
8 out of the 41 identified products are yet to be subscribed and production has commenced 
only for 17 out of the 41 targeted products, indicating that the intended level of domestic 
manufacturing is yet to be fully realized. Given that the scheme will remain operational until 
FY 2029-30, the Committee recommend that the Department may intensify efforts to ensure 
boosting of domestic manufacturing of the remaining products under the Scheme.

Drug procurement manual 
2.39  The Committee note that the Department of Pharmaceuticals (DoP) does not

Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order, 2017 and related clarifications. 
Drug procurement involves specific technical parameters such as statutory compliance 
under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules, adherence to Good Manufacturing Practices 
(GMP), quality testing, shelf-life requirements and traceability. These requirements are

Observation/Recommendation No. 18 
 
REVAMPED PHARMACEUTICALS TECHNOLOGY UPGRADATION ASSISTANCE SCHEME 
(RPTUAS)  
Expenditure incurred under the Scheme  
4.5 The Committee learn that Revamped Pharmaceutical Technology Upgradation 
Assistance Scheme (RPTUAS), a sub-scheme under Scheme for Strengthening of 
Pharmaceutical Industry (SPI)/ Development of Pharmaceuticals Industry, aims to facilitate 
technology and processes upgradation of existing pharmaceutical units to Revised 
Schedule-M and World Health Organization - Good Manufacturing Practices (WHO-GMP) 
certification. The scheme guidelines were revised in March 2024 to enhance its uptake and 
support the technological advancement of the Indian pharmaceutical industry, ensuring 
alignment with global standards. The Financial outlay is ₹ 300.10 crore for the period FY 

37 
 
2021-22 to FY 2025-26. However, the Committee regret to note that despite Budget Estimates 
allocation of ₹ 95 crore in 2023–24, ₹ 5 crore in 2024–25 and ₹ 100 crore in 2025–26, no actual 
expenditure has been incurred under the Scheme so far. The Committee have been given to 
understand that release and utilization of financial assistance under the scheme are 
contingent upon the completion of procurement of plant and machinery, installation, 
technology upgradation and the receipt of certification and verification from the concerned 
drug regulatory authority/authorities confirming compliance with the revised Schedule M of 
the Drugs Rules, 1945, and the World Health Organization – Good Manufacturing Practices. 
The approved projects are at different stages of implementation. However, approval of ₹ 
18.24 crore in favour of 18 applicants, who had applied during the initial phase of the 
scheme, is under process and the amount will be released shortly. Therefore, funds have 
not been disbursed under the scheme so far, and to that extent the corresponding financial 
outlay is committed expenditure. During oral evidence, it was also submitted by the 
representative of the Department that interaction with units on the ground was not 
envisioned as part of this Scheme. In this regard, the Committee observe that sole reliance 
on post-certification reimbursement model and lack of field-level engagement with 
beneficiary MSME units have rendered the funds allotted under the Scheme completely 
unspent. Therefore, the Committee recommend that urgent measures to ensure that funds 
under the RPTUAS earmarked for technological upgradation of eligible MSMEs are 
disbursed in a time-bound manner in the final Financial Year i.e. 2025-26 may be taken by 
the Department and the Committee be apprised accordingly.

Pharmaceuticals) submitted as under: 
  
The observations and recommendations of the Hon’ble Committee have been noted.

approved 301 applications/pharmaceutical units for incentives aggregating to approximately

2026. 
Further, out of the 301 approved applicants, claims of 64 pharmaceutical units amounting to

disbursed for 55 pharmaceutical units during FY 2025–26.

However, it has been observed that the operations of pharmaceutical units are adversely affected 
during the upgradation period.

in a phased manner over a period of time rather than executing them at once, which results in 
delays in project completion. In contrast, medium-sized units may have backup production facilities 
to manage such transitions. Further, pharmaceutical units generally prioritize meeting their existing
