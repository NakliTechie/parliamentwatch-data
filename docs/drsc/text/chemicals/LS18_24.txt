Recommendation Sl. No. 1 
 
Revitalization and Strengthening of Fertilizer PSUs for Food Security and Self-
Reliance 
 
1.5 Expressing  concern over the classification of the fertilizer sector as non -strategic 
under the New PSE Policy despite its critical role in food security, farmer welfare and 
national self -reliance, and emphasising the need for a balanced policy approach to 
strengthen domestic production and reduce import dependence, the Committee 
recommended as under: – 
 
“The Committee have been apprised of the decision of the Ministry of Finance to 
classify the fertilizer sector as non-strategic under the New Public Sector Enterprise (PSE) 
Policy, despite multiple submissions made by the Department of Fertilizers —including 
D.O. letters dated 24 March 2021 and 9 April 2022. These submissions highlighted the 
sector’s critical role in ensuring food security, sustaining rural livelihoods, and advancing 
national self-reliance— factors that warrant its inclusion in the strategic sector. However, 
the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM) turned down these 
requests, arguing that Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) contribute only 25% 
and 11% of the country’s urea and non -urea fertilizer production respectively; that many 
operate at a loss; and that their continued existence is inconsistent with fiscal prudence 
and the criteria for strategic classification, as approved by the Cabinet Committee on 
Economic Affairs (CCEA) under the New PSE Policy. The Committee find this reasoning 
incongruent with the Government’s Atmanirbhar Bharat agenda. Contrary to DIPAM’s 
claim, several fertilizer PSUs have shown remarkable turnaround, notably FACT, which 
has transitioned from a loss -making entity to a consistently prof itable enterprise. 
Additionally, the revival of closed units at Gorakhpur, Sindri, Barauni (under HURL) and 
Ramagundam (under RFCL) through CPSE-led joint ventures, has added over 76.2 Lakh 
Metric Tonnes (LMT) to India’s annual urea production capacity. Th is clearly 
demonstrates the strategic value of leveraging PSU assets to meet national goals. In light 
of rising global fertilizer prices and India’s continued reliance on imports for over 90% of 
its potash and phosphate needs (procured under the Open Gener al License), the 
sustained operation and strengthening of fertilizer PSUs is crucial—not only for domestic 
production but also for price stabilization, disaster resilience, and long -term food 
sovereignty.  The Committee urge that the Government continue to  make efforts to 
revitalize the fertilizer sector by ensuring adequate support for modernization, technology 
upgradation, and capacity expansion of fertilizer PSUs. Further, the Committee 
emphasize the need for a balanced policy approach that strengthens domestic production 
capabilities while reducing over -dependence on imports of vital raw materials such as 
potash and phosphates. The Committee are of the considered view that sustained 
investment in fertilizer PSUs will not only enhance self -reliance but al so safeguard 

3 
 
national interests in times of global supply disruptions, thereby reinforcing India’s long -
term food and nutritional security.”

recommendations made in  the Ninth report of  the Standing Committee on action taken 
on recommendations of the Committee in their 3rd Report on Demands for Grants 2024-
25 pertaining to Department of Fertilizers a request has been made  for reconsidering the 
placement of "Fertilizer" sector under the Strategic sector in view of the sectors' evolving 
strategic relevance in light of global supply chain disruptions and escalating input costs to 
DIPAM vide  letters, dated 24.10.2025 & 10.11.2025.  
2. Further, recently, the Department of Fertilizers while furnishing its comments on 
the Revised New PSE policy for CPSEs in non -strategic sector vide OM dated 
24.10.2025, has reiterated its request to Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) to 
consider the classification of "Fertilizer" in the strategic sector. Additionally, an OM, dated 
19.11.2025 has been sent to DIPAM for necessary action on this particular 
recommendation. 
3. The Department of Fertilizers under the existing framework of fertilizer subsidy 
policies has continued its efforts towards promotion and strengthening of domestic 
production capacities. 
4. Under Nutrient Based Subsidy Policy (NBS) policy, P&K fertilizers are covered 
under Open General License (OGL) and free to import/manufacture   fertilizers as per 
their business dynamics. To boost fertilizer production and make country Atmanirbhar, 
the following measures have been taken: 
(i) Based on the requests, the new manufacturing units or increase in 
manufacturing capacity of existing units have been recognized / taken on record under 
the NBS subsidy scheme. 
(ii) The number of P&K fertilizers covered under NBS policy has increased from 22 
grades in 2021 to 28 grades.  
(iii) Freight Subsidy on SSP, which is an indigenously manufactured fertilizer, has 
been approved since Kharif, 2022 to promote SSP usage for providing Phosphatic or 'P' 
nutrient to the soil. PDM which is the indigenously available source of K nutrient is 
included under NBS Policy.  
(iv)  DoF has also issued revised Reasonability Guidelines on 18.01.2024 
(applicable w.e.f. 01.04.2023) to ensure reasonableness of MRP, encourage domestic 
production, enhance new domestic production capacity and   encourage balanced 
fertilization of soil, self-reliance in the P&K fertilizers. Some of the prominent features of 
new reasonability guidelines to enhance domestic capacity utilization are are Differential 

4 
 
profit margin  to encourage domestic production, evaluation of reasonability has been 
shifted from product basis to segment basis across all fertilizer grades, discount or 
promotional expenditure has been dis -allowed to discourage promotion of chemical 
fertilizers and to promote PM-PRANAM initiative and regulate the sector, Dealer’s margin 
which was uncapped earlier has been allowed to the extent of 2% of MRP for DAP & MOP 
and 4% of MRP for all other grades of fertilizers under NBS (TSP, MAP, SSP, NPKs &

(v) Special focus is given on domestically produced alternate fertilizers to replace

SSP. 
 
5.  In Urea sector, Government had announced New Investment Policy (NIP) – 
2012 on 2nd January, 2013 and its amendment on 7th October, 2014 to facilitate fresh 
investment in the urea sector and to make India self -sufficient in the urea sector. Total 6 
new urea units have been set up under NIP -2012 which includes 4 urea units set up 
through Joint Venture Companies (JVC) of nominated PSUs and 2 urea units set up by 
the private companies. these units have added urea production capacity of 76.2 LMTPA, 
thereby increasing total indigenous urea production capacity from 207.54 LMTPA during

1.7 The Committee appreciate that the Department has taken steps to seek strategic

are noted as a constructive step. However, as the decision on reclassification is yet

safeguard the interests of fertilizer PSUs pending a final decision on

reclassification. The Committee also desire to be apprised of the progress made in

Recommendation SL. No. 2 
 
Restructure Legacy Government Debt and Rationalize Debt Structures 
 
1.8 Highlighting the mounting debt burden and unsustainable interest liabilities of key 
Public Sector Undertakings under the Department of Fertilizers, particularly FACT and 
MFL, and emphasising the need for urgent financial restructuring to restore their viability 
and creditworthiness, the Committee recommended as under: – 
 
“The Committee are deeply concerned about the unsustainable debt burden and 
high-interest liabilities afflicting key Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) under the 
Department of Fertilizers, particularly Fertilizers and Chemicals Travancore Limited 
(FACT) and Madras Fertilizers Limited (MFL), where legacy Government of India (GoI) 
loans have accrued interest far exceeding the principal, severely undermining their 
financial viability and creditworthiness. For instance, FACT faces an interest liability of 
₹1,912.13 crore on a principal loan of ₹1,770.49 crore, while MFL’s outstanding dues to 
the GoI total ₹1,499.94 crore—including ₹945.70 crore in accrued interest on a principal 
of ₹554.24 crore. Despite long -pending proposals from both companies for financial 
restructuring—including waiver of interest and conversion of principal into zero -interest 
loans repayable in instalments —no concrete relief has been granted. Although the 
Department of Fertilizers has forwarded these proposals, the Department of Expenditure 
has dismissed them for being inconsistent with the New PSE Policy (2021), even though 
these liabilities originated long before the policy’s adoption and continue to obstruct the 
companies’ revival. Notably, both FACT and MFL have taken significant steps to improve 
their balance sheets and have undertaken expansion projects aimed at enhancing 
production capacity, diversifying product portfolios, and upgrading critical infrastructure. 
These efforts require complementary financial restructuring and government support to 
ensure long-term sustainability. The Committee, therefore, strongly recommend that the 
Government convert high-interest GoI loans into equity or interest-free loans and write off 
unserviceable interest obligations to reduce the debt overhang, restore financial stability, 
and enhance these PSUs’ ability to raise resources from the market for modernization 
and expansion. The Committee also recommend encouraging the refinancing of high-cost 
debt into low -interest, long -term instruments through Gover nment-backed bonds, 
concessional finance, or other market -based mechanisms, especially in view of the 
improved credit profile and recent operational turnaround of certain PSUs. Such 
measures would enable public financial institutions to extend more favoura ble lending 
terms and support the sustained revival of these strategic enterprises.” 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT 
 
1.9  In their action taken reply to the afore -mentioned recommendation of the 
Committee, the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, Department of Fertilizers has 
stated as under: - 

6 
 
“The recommendations of the Committee have been noted for further action.” 
 
OBSERVATION/RECOMMENDATION OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
1.10 The Committee note that the Department’s reply does not adequately 
address the recommendation made regarding the debt burden of FACT and MFL. 
The Committee had highlighted the issue of significant interest liabilities in both 
cases and had suggested possible measures such as restructuring of Government 
of India loans, including options like equity conversion, interest relief and 
concessional refinancing. The reply indicating that the recommendations have 
been noted does not provide clarity on whether a ny concrete proposal has been 
examined or placed before the concerned authorities. The Committee observe that 
the issue of legacy debt continues to have implications for the financial position of 
these PSUs. In this context, the Committee reiterate their earlier recommendation 
that the Department, in consultation with the Ministry of Finance and the 
Department of Expenditure, examine feasible options for addressing the legacy 
debt of FACT and MFL, including ap propriate restructuring measures. The 
Committee recommend that the status of proposals considered in this regard, 
including the outcome of earlier deliberations, be furnished and that the progress 
made towards resolution of the issue be reported. 
 
Recommendation Sl. No. 3 
 
Modernize Ageing Infrastructure  
 
1.11  Expressing serious concern over the ageing and technologically obsolete state of 
fertilizer production infrastructure, particularly in Public Sector Undertakings, and 
emphasising the urgent need for modernization to improve efficiency, reduce costs and 
ensure environmental compliance, the Committee recommended as under: – 
“The Committee express serious concern over the ageing and technologically 
obsolete state of a large portion of India’s fertilizer production infrastructure, particularly 
within Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs). Of the 33 operational urea units in the country, 
27 are over 25 years old, and 7 have crossed the 50 -year mark—making them highly 
susceptible to inefficiencies, excessive energy consumption, and increased operating 
costs. Notable examples include Rashtriya Chemicals and Fertilizers Ltd.’s Suphala plant 
at Trombay (57 years old), FACT’s NP plants at Udyogamandal (52+ years old), and 
BVFCL’s Namrup -II unit (48 years old), which remains shut due to major operational 
failures. These vintage facilities are prone to frequent breakdowns and incur high 
maintenance costs, thereby undermining production stability and overall competitiveness. 
Despite commendable production outcomes —such as National Fertilizers Limited’s five 
units collectively producing over 36.88 Lakh Metric Tonnes (LMT) of urea in 2023 – 24—
these achievements are being maintained under increasing strain. The lack of significant 
technological upgrades, rising production costs, and volatility in international natural gas 
prices further exacerbate the situation. Compounding the challenge is India’ s heavy 
dependence on imported raw materials like natural gas, phosphate, and potash, which 

7 
 
heightens the Country’s strategic vulnerabilities in ensuring long -term food security. 
Recognizing current challenges in the fertilizer sector, the Committee strongly 
recommend launching a centrally-supported "Revamp and Upgrade Scheme" for fertilizer 
PSUs. This scheme should provide for phased capital investment to replace obsolete 
plants with modern, energy efficient, and environmentally compliant facilities. To ensure 
its success, the initiative must be backed by long -term, low-cost financing and aligned  
with key national missions such as Atmanirbhar Bharat and Green Growth. A structured 
modernization plan —encompassing technology upgrades, capacity enhancement, or 
complete replacement —should be pursued through capital investment sourced via 
budgetary allocations, public-private partnerships, and internal accruals.” 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT 
 
1.12 In their action taken reply to the afore -mentioned recommendation of the 
Committee, the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, Department of Fertilizers has stated 
as under: - 
“The Union Cabinet, in its meeting held on 19.03.2025, approved the establishment 
of a new Brownfield Ammonia-Urea Complex (12.7 LMTPA) at Namrup IV within BVFCL 
premises, with a project cost of ₹10,601.40 Crores and a debt -equity ratio of 70:30, 
through a Joint Venture. 
The matter related to the decommissioning of Namrup-II has been referred to DIPAM for 
obtaining in-principle approval of Alternative Mechanism. Further, the recommendation 
has been noted for further action.” 
 
OBSERVATION/RECOMMENDATION OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
1.13 The Committee note that the reply is of an interim nature. The Cabinet 
approval for the Namrup -IV Brownfield complex on 19.03.2025 is a concrete and 
welcome step that addresses one specific ageing plant situation, namely the 
shutdown Namrup -II at BVFCL. Ho wever, the broader recommendation for a 
centrally supported “Revamp and Upgrade Scheme” covering the 27 urea plants 
over 25 years old and the 7 over 50 years old across all fertilizer PSUs has not been 
addressed. The reply does not indicate whether the Dep artment has assessed the 
total capital requirement for a sector wide revamp programme, identified the 
financing mechanism such as long-term low-cost loans, budgetary support or PPP, 
or initiated consultations with NITI Aayog and the Ministry of Finance on scheme 
design. The Committee, therefore, reiterate their earlier recommendation that the 
Department furnish the status of the assessment of ageing urea and P&K fertilizer 
facilities, including their modernization requirements, residual economic life, and 
the indicative capital investment involved. The Committee also recommend that 
details of any policy framework under consideration and the current status of the 
Namrup-II decommissioning proposal, including timelines for decision and 
implementation, be furni shed, and desire to be apprised of the progress made in 
this regard.

Recommendation SL. No. 4 
 
Reclaiming Idle Potential: HFCL's Durgapur and Haldia Units as Pillars of 
Regional Fertilizer Security 
 
1.14  Highlighting the need for early revival of strategically located fertilizer units at 
Haldia and Durgapur, with substantial existing infrastructure and significant potential to 
enhance domestic production, reduce import dependence and promote regional 
development, the Committee recommended as under:– 
 
“The Committee are of the considered view that the revival of strategically located 
fertilizer units at Haldia and Durgapur; both formerly under the Hindustan Fertilizer 
Corporation Limited (HFCL) —is long overdue. These sites with huge land parcels on 
nominal rates possess all essential infrastructure —such as natural gas, water, coking 
coal, land, and industrial support systems—within a 10- kilometre radius. The Committee 
appreciate the decision taken to revive Barauni Fertilizer unit which is fruitfully contributing 
in fulfilment of the Fertilizer needs of the nation. The Committee find that the objectives of 
the Atmanirbhar Bharat initiative can be achieved if such ready-to-operate assets capable 
of strengthening domestic fertilizer self -sufficiency, reduc e import dependence, and 
generate employment in eastern India are revived based on viability and economic 
parameters. The Committee further note that the Department of Fertilizers has linked the 
revival of these units to the successful commissioning of the  Talcher plant under the 
National Mission on Coal Gasification, expected to become operational next year. While 
the Committee appreciate the technological ambition of this approach, they firmly believe 
that the revival of Durgapur and Haldia units must not be made contingent upon a single 
project. Accordingly, the Committee urge that the Government consider the early revival 
of HFCL’s Durgapur and Haldia units through the joint venture (JV) route, as successfully 
implemented in the case of Ramagundam Fertil izers and Hindustan Urvarak & Rasayan 
Limited (HURL). The revival may factor in the high strategic value of the land assets 
involved and the urgent fertilizer needs of the region’s farmers, thereby aligning economic 
development with national agricultural priorities.” 
 
REPLY OF THE GOVERNMENT 
 
1.15  In their action taken reply to the afore -mentioned recommendation of the 
Committee, the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, Department of Fertilizers has stated 
as under: - 
“It is mentioned that the previous submissions to the Standing Committee included 
land details and revival possibilities for Haldia and Durgapur plants vide DoF's letter dated 
18.02.2025 and 05.06.2025. Revival decisions are contingent to urea supply deman d 
dynamics in the country; however, the recommendations of the Committee have been 
noted. Towards this direction, a VC meeting was held on 04.09.2025 between CMD 
HFCL-cum-Addl. Secretary, DoF and CEO, ADDA confirming payment position on part of 

9 
 
HFCL with the request to execute the lease deed in favour of HFCL in Durgapur. This has 
been followed by a request dated 04.11.2025 to the State Government of West Bengal 
for early finalization of the matter.” 
 
OBSERVATION/RECOMMENDATION OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
1.16 The Committee note that the reply furnished by the Department does not fully 
address the recommendation regarding the revival of HFCL’s Durgapur and Haldia 
units through the joint venture route. The response places the matter within the 
broader context of urea supply demand considerations and does not indicate any 
specific timeline, feasibility assessment, or approach towards revival in line with 
the model adopted in other cases. While certain administrative steps have been 
taken in respect of the Durg apur lease deed, these appear to be preliminary in 
nature. The Haldia unit has also not been specifically addressed in the reply. The 
Committee further note that the joint venture model has yielded encouraging 
outcomes in comparable projects. The Committee, therefore, reiterate their 
recommendation that the Department examine the feasibility of revival of the 
Durgapur and Haldia units, including through the joint venture route, taking into 
account the relevant technical and economic considerati ons. The Comm ittee 
recommend that the current status of the Durgapur lease deed with the Government 
of West Bengal, including any constraints being faced, be indicated, and that 
details of the approach envisaged for the Haldia unit be furnished. The Committee 
also desire to be apprised of the progress made in this regard. 
 
Recommendation Sl. No. 6 
 
Revival of Underutilized Land Assets of Fertilizer PSUs 
 
1.17 Expressing concern over the underutilisation of valuable land and asset holdings of 
fertilizer PSUs due to encroachments, litigation and lack of updated valuations, and 
emphasising the need for their optimal utilisation and monetization to support sectoral

Recommendation Sl. No. 13 
 
Unlocking The Fertilizers and Chemicals Travancore Limited -FACT’s Potential 
Through Timely Financial Restructuring 
 
5.1 Expressing  concern over the persistent financial stress faced by FACT due to an 
unsustainable interest burden despite its operational turnaround and strong market 
performance, and emphasising the need for urgent financial restructuring prior to any 
disinvestment, the Committee recommended as under: – 
“The Committee note with concern that despite FACT’s significant operational 
turnaround and strong market performance—including a market capitalization of ₹57,400 
crore as of December 2024, the highest among fertilizer PSUs —its financial health 
remains seve rely constrained by an unsustainable interest burden. As of 30 January 
2025, FACT’s total liabilities stood at ₹4,610.69 crore, of which ₹3,682.62 crore were dues 
to the Government of India, comprising ₹1,770.49 crore in loan principal and ₹1,912.13 
crore in accrued interest at a punitive 13.5% rate. Alarmingly, the interest now exceeds 
the principal, undermining the viability of a PSU with strategic and social responsibilities. 
While FACT has made notable progress in production, marketing, and asset utili zation, 
these gains are overshadowed by unresolved debt restructuring. The Committee find the 
Ministry of Finance’s rejections of FACT’s restructuring proposals — endorsed by NITI 
Aayog and involving partial conversion of loans to equity and interest -free instruments—
somewhat short-sighted and not fully aligned with long-term fiscal prudence. The rationale 
of maintaining high interest rates to preserve disinvestment value is skewed, as it erodes 
long-term value creation and discourages investor confidence. G iven FACT’s strategic 
importance, especially in southern India, the Committee strongly recommend urgent 
financial restructuring, including interest waiver, equity conversion, and soft loan options. 
The Committee further urge the Government to recognize FAC T as a strategic public 
asset and retain it under public ownership until modernization is complete. Infrastructure 
upgrades to replace ageing plants must be prioritized through dedicated funding for green 
and energy -efficient technologies. Finally, any dis investment should follow —not 
precede—financial rehabilitation, to ensure robust valuation and equitable asset 
monetization.”
