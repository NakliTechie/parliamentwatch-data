THIRTY-SIXTH REPORT 
“SURVEY OF VILLAGES ABADI AND MAPPING WITH IMPROVISED 
TECHNOLOGY IN VILLAGE AREAS (SVAMITVA) SCHEME” 
PART – I  
NARRATION ANALYSIS  
I INTRODUCTION 
1.1 The Government of India has decided to implement the Central Sector scheme 
“SVAMITVA”. The Scheme aims to provide an integrated property validation solution 
for rural India. The demarcation of abadi areas (the abadi area includes inhabitant 
land, inhabited areas contiguous to Abadi and wadis/basties in rural areas) would be 
done using Drone Surveying technology, with the collaborative efforts of the Ministry 
of Panchayati Raj, State Panchayati Raj Department, State Revenue Departments 
and Survey of India. 
1.2 This would provide the ‘Record of Rights’ to village household owners 
possessing houses in inhabited rural areas in villages which, in turn, would enable 
them to use their property as a financial asset for taking loans and other financial 
benefits from Banks. Further, this would also enable updation of property and asset 
register to strengthen tax collection and demand assessment process of Gram 
Panchayats in States where Property tax is devolved to the Gram Panchayats. 
1.3 Apart from demarcation of individual rural property, other Gram Panchayat and 
community assets like village roads, ponds, canals, open spaces, school, Anganwadi, 
Health sub-centres, etc. would also be surveyed and GIS maps would be created. 
Further, these GIS maps and spatial database would also help in preparation of 
accurate work estimates for various works undertaken by Gram Panchayats and other 
Departments of State Government. These can also be used to prepare better-quality 
Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP). 
1.4 SVAMITVA Scheme is a Central Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Panchayati 
Raj that aims to provide ‘Record of rights’ to village household owners possessing 
houses in inhabited rural areas in villages with the issuance of Property Cards to 
property owners. As on date, Drone Survey has been completed for about 3.30 lakh 
village abadi areas out of targeted 3.38 lakh villages. 
II RATIONALE AND NEED FOR THE SCHEME 
1.5 Survey of rural land in India for Settlement and record of rights had last been 
completed many decades back and moreover, ABADI (inhabited) area of villages were 
not surveyed/mapped in most States. Hence, in the absence of a legal document, the 
owner of the property in the rural habitations are not able to leverage their own property 
as a financial asset acceptable by the banks for the purpose of providing loans and 
other financial assistance.  In order to provide the legal right of the property to the 

2 
 
household owner in a transparent and cost-effective manner, there is a need of latest 
Drone Technology and Continuously Operating Reference Station (CORS) technology 
for capturing images. 
1.6 The Survey of India has evolved standard operating procedures for acquiring 
very high-resolution aerial images and preparing very large-scale maps at 1:500 scale 
using Drones. The high resolution and accurate image base maps have facilitated 
creation of the most durable record of property holdings in these areas with no legacy 
revenue records. Such accurate image base maps provide a clear demarcation of land 
holdings in a very short frame of time compared to on ground physical measurement 
and mapping of the land parcels. Further, these maps are free from measurement 
errors to a very large extent, which is not the case with physical on ground 
measurements. 
1.7 Such maps provide a visual aid to the landowners as well as to the revenue 
officials for identifying and resolving any property dispute and are also an invaluable 
tool for local level planning. 
1.8 In rural India, a large number of properties in abadi (inhabited village) areas 
lack clear ownership records leading to disputes, limited access to institutional credit 
and challenges in property taxation and planning. To address this long-standing issue, 
the Government of India launched the SVAMITVA Scheme. 
III OBJECTIVES OF THE SCHEME 
1.9 The Scheme seeks to achieve the following objectives: 
(i)  Creation of accurate land records for rural planning and reduce property 
related disputes; 
(ii)  To bring financial stability to the citizens in rural India by enabling them to 
use their property as a financial asset for taking loans and other financial 
benefits; 
(iii) Determination of property tax, which would accrue to the GPs directly in 
States where it is devolved or else, add to the State exchequer; 
(iv) Creation of survey infrastructure and GIS maps that can be leveraged by 
any department for their use; 
(v) To support in preparation of better-quality Gram Panchayat Development 
Plan (GPDP) by making use of GIS maps. 
IV COVERAGE OF THE SCHEME 
1.10 All villages in the country will be eventually covered under this Scheme. The 
entire work is likely to be spread over a period of five years from April 2020 to March 
2025. 
1.11 Pilot Phase of the Scheme implemented in FY 2020-21 covered States of 
Haryana, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand, 
Punjab, Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh and CORS network establishment in States 
of Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. 

3 
 
1.12 SVAMITVA aims to provide the ‘Record of Rights’ to village household owners 
in rural abadi areas and issuance of Property Cards. The Scheme covers all eligible 
inhabited villages across the Country and envisages establishment of 567 
Continuously Operating Reference Station (CORS) network. The financial outlay of 
the Scheme is ₹566.23 crore including ₹79.65 crore for the Pilot Phase of the Scheme 
during FY 2020-21. 
1.13 SVAMITVA Scheme was launched as a pilot scheme in six States namely 
Haryana, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Karnataka 
and was further launched nationwide on 24 April 2021. The Scheme is being 
implemented in 30 States and Union Territories and drone flying has been completed 
in more than 3.3 lakh villages against a target of approximately 3.38 lakh villages. 
V TECHNOLOGICAL ARCHITECTURE OF THE SCHEME 
1.14 The SVAMITVA Scheme represents one of the largest technology-enabled 
property mapping exercises undertaken in rural India. The Scheme leverages Drone 
Survey Technology, Continuously Operating Reference Station (CORS) Network, 
Geographic Information System (GIS), high-resolution ortho imagery, spatial 
databases and digital property records for creation of an integrated property validation 
system for rural inhabited areas. 
1.15 When asked about the developed standardized APls for data exchange 
between SVAMITVA, DILRMP, Gram Panchayat systems and State databases, the 
Ministry of Information and Technology in their written reply have submitted the 
following: 
“NIC has enabled Integration of geospatial and ownership data through 
SVAMITVA Portal. Information exchange among Ministry of Panchayati Raj, 
Survey of India, State/UTs Revenue Departments, and Panchayati Raj 
Institutions. APIs Integration is also enabled for sharing data to Gram 
Manchitra, Panchayat Enterprise Suite (PES).” 
VI ESTABLISHMENT OF CONTINUOUS OPERATING REFERENCE SYSTEM 
(CORS) NETWORK 
1.16 Continuously Operating Reference Stations is a network of reference stations 
that provide a virtual base station that allows access long-range high-accuracy 
Network RTK corrections. The RTK networks extend the use of RTK to a larger area 
containing a network of reference stations. Accuracy is increased in a CORS network, 
because more than one station helps ensure correct positioning and guards against a 
false initialization of a single base station. 
1.17 Real-time kinematic (RTK) positioning is a satellite navigation technique used 
to enhance the precision of position data derived from satellite-based positioning 
systems. The Survey of India shall perform the establishment of CORS Network, 
preparation of Geoid Model and ensure operation and maintenance of CORS network 
stations and provision of CORS network services for five years. 

4 
 
1.18 The Scheme envisages establishment of 567 Continuously Operating 
Reference Station (CORS) network across the country. 
1.19 The CORS network supports in establishing Ground Control Points, which is an 
important activity for accurate Geo-referencing, ground truthing and demarcation of 
lands. Once the CORS network is established, it could be used by any State 
agency/Department viz. Revenue Department, Gram Panchayat, Public Works 
Department, Rural Development Department, Agriculture, Drainage & Canal, 
Education, Electricity, Water, Health etc. for the survey and implementing/using GIS 
based applications. 
1.20 This would facilitate use of CORS network for any survey related activities like 
road construction, irrigation works and infrastructure works. The CORS infrastructure 
created under the Scheme is therefore expected to serve as a long-term national 
geospatial asset.

VII LARGE SCALE MAPPING USING DRONE TECHNOLOGY 
1.22 Rural inhabited (Abadi) area would be mapped by Survey of India in 
collaboration with State Revenue Department and State Panchayati Raj Department 
to generate revenue maps to confer ownership property rights. Based on these maps 
or data, property cards would be issued to the rural household owners in Abadi area. 
The high resolution and accurate image base maps would facilitate creation of the 
most durable record of property holdings in areas with no legacy revenue records. 
1.23 Large scale mapping of rural inhabited area (abadi) using Drone or UAV 
(Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) would be carried out using Professional Survey Grade 
UAVs as these UAVs are RTK (Real Time Kinematic)/PPK (Post Point Kinematic) 
enabled and use high resolution RGB sensor on 1:500 scale with image capturing of 
better than 5 cm GSD ±12.5 cm or better planimetry accuracy to provide accurate 
projection centre and orientation of the images captured during the flying. 
1.24 The process involves acquisition of images, establishment of Ground Control 
Points, aerial triangulation, generation of Digital Elevation Models (DEM), Digital 
Terrain Models (DTM), ortho-rectified images and creation of GIS databases. 
VIII Feature Extraction and GIS Database Creation 
1.25 Ortho-rectified Images (ORI) shall be used for extraction of all topographical 
features by Survey of India including property boundaries marked in ground jointly by 
Gram Panchayat and State Revenue Department. 
1.26 State Revenue Department and State Panchayati Raj Department will provide 
requisite support to the Survey of India feature extraction team in identification of 
features or assets viz. open plots, Government owned land, Gram Sabha owned 
properties and individual properties. 
1.27 The GIS database created under the Scheme shall be Open Geospatial 
Consortium (OGC) compliant and shall include attribute information, land parcel maps, 
village maps and digital spatial databases. 
IX SPATIAL PLANNING AND DIGITAL PLATFORMS 
1.28 A spatial planning application “Gram Manchitra” has been implemented by the 
Ministry of Panchayati Raj for leveraging GIS maps and technology for the preparation 
of Gram Panchayat Development Plans. As the drone survey progresses, the digital 
spatial data/maps created under drone survey shall be leveraged for creation of spatial 
analytical tools to support preparation of GPDP. 
1.29 Online Monitoring and reporting dashboard would be centrally hosted for real 
time monitoring of the SVAMITVA Scheme. It will monitor progress of activities under 
the Scheme and facilitate online reporting by States and implementing agencies.

X IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK 
1.30 The brief/broad level implementation process flow of the Scheme comprises 
preliminary activities, pre-survey activities, survey activities and post-survey activities. 
 Pre-Survey Activities 
(i) Establishment of CORS network 
 
(ii) The regional revenue office takes permission from the Collector’s office to 
conduct the survey. The Collector notifies the survey area through a public 
notice. 
 
(iii) The Panchayati Raj Department invites the residents of the village for 
Gram Sabha to inform them about the schedule of the survey and to 
sensitize about the surveying methodology and its benefits. 
 
(iv) Identification of individual properties, Government assets, Gram Sabha 
land parcels, roads, open plots etc. and Ground marking of property areas 
to be surveyed will be done by State Government.  
 
(v) SoI takes requisite permission for flying Drones from Directorate General 
of Civil Aviation/ Ministry of Defence. 
 
(vi) Mission planning for drone flying using available maps/satellite imagery 
 
 Survey Activities 
(i) The regional revenue office would provide scanned maps to SoI; 
 
(ii) SoI should provision for Ground Control Points or Check points before 
flying; 
 
(iii) Acquisition of Aerial images using Professional Survey Grade 
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle/ Drone for Large Scale Mapping of rural abadi 
area, inhabited areas contiguous to Abadi and wadis/basties in rural 
areas; 
 
(iv) The images captured through Drone would be processed by Survey of 
India in its Geographic Information System lab. 
 
(v) Data Processing: Basemap Generation and 2D Feature Extraction 
a. Generation of DEM & Ortho Rectified Images 
b. Extraction of 2D topographical features from Ortho-rectified images 
c. Generation of Land Parcel maps at a scale of 1:500 

7 
 
d. Linking of Attributes with extracted topographical features 
e. Creation of boundaries of villages 
f. Numbering of properties/structures within village abadi 
area /Lal dora area as per the numbering system 
provided by State Government 
ii. Creation of GIS database 
iii. Ground validation of the processed images for better than 10 cm 
accuracy. 
 
 Post -Survey Activities 
(i) Legal Notification for joint verification of ownership by State Revenue 
Department and Gram Panchayat. 
(ii) Inquiry Process for Ownership adjudication: The process is initiated, 
where survey officials verify ownership of the land parcels, with the help 
of gram sabha, land owners, and review the existing documents 
(iii) Issue of notification for providing adjudication of final results. The Enquiry 
Officer (from the Survey Department) re-verifies the ownership and 
resolves any post survey objections received from property owners. 
These may be related to correction in owner name, property boundaries, 
joint holding etc. For unresolved objections / disputes, the final decision 
shall rest with the District Magistrate / Collector/ or State may indicate a 
resolution mechanism. 
(iv) After Ground truthing, inquiry process and dispute resolution, final Digital 
maps/ LPM /DEM/ GIS datasets would be generated and handed over to 
MoPR/Authorised agency as per the standard procedure for use. 
(v) Printing and distribution of property cards to village household owners 
(legal document of property ownership). 
(vi) Provisions should be made for the proper storage and regular update of 
records, 
(vii) Gram Panchayat shall update the property tax and asset register of GP, 
wherever applicable. 
(viii) State Revenue shall perform and responsible for regular updation of 
maps. 
(ix) SoI shall provide training and Capacity building of Revenue Department 
Government Officials

1.31 Post validation and completion of inquiry process, property owners are provided 
an opportunity to raise claims and objections. Disputes are resolved before issuance 
of Property Cards by the State Authorities/Revenue Department. 
1.32 On being asked about how many States have integrated SVAMITVA records 
with State Revenue Department database and registration systems, the Ministry of 
Information and Technology in their written reply submitted the following: 
 
“SVAMITVA Portal maintains drone data of all habitations covered under the 
scheme. The field verifications and corrections are applied by the States/UTs. 
The finalized and partial data is shared using APIs with the SVAMITVA Portal 
for centralized access and seamless data integration. The data for 17 States 
and Union Territories—Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal

Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tripura, Uttarakhand, 
and Uttar Pradesh—is available on SVAMITVA Portal. This is being integrated 
by the States with DILRMP scheme for seamless access.”

XII INSTITUTIONAL MECHANISM 
1.34 The SVAMITVA Scheme is implemented through a multi-tier institutional 
framework involving the Ministry of Panchayati Raj, Survey of India, State 
Governments, Gram Panchayats and other stakeholders. The Scheme provides for 
institutional mechanisms at National, State, District and Panchayat levels for 
implementation, monitoring and management. 
1.35 The following stakeholders are involved in carrying out the Scheme: 
(i) Nodal Ministry (Ministry of Panchayati Raj), Government of India; 
(ii) Survey of India (Technology Implementation Agency); 
(iii) State Revenue Department; 
(iv) State Panchayati Raj Department; 
(v) Local District Authorities; 
(vi) Property Owner; 
(vii) Gram Panchayat; 
(viii) National Informatics Centre (NIC-GIS Division); and 
(ix) Other line departments having assets in rural inhabited areas for preparing 
comprehensive database. 
1.36 When asked is there a common national data architecture for SVAMITVA, or 
are States maintaining separate platforms and what challenges have been 
encountered, the Ministry of Information and Technology in their written reply 
submitted the following: 
“States maintain their own SVAMITVA Portals to support survey management, 
ownership verification, workflow coordination, and the generation and 
distribution of Property Cards. In some States, the software has been 
developed and is being maintained by the National Informatics Centre (NIC) in 
coordination with the respective Revenue Departments. In other States, the 
software has been developed and is being maintained independently by the 
respective State Revenue Departments. After data is finalized the same is 
shared with SVAMITVA Portal for centralized management and access.”

XIX STAKEHOLDERS: ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES 
1.44 Ministry of Panchayati Raj: The Ministry of Panchayati Raj is the nodal 
Ministry for implementation of the Scheme. It is responsible for overall policy direction, 
coordination with States and Survey of India, release of funds, monitoring of 
implementation and facilitation of convergence with other developmental initiatives. 
The Ministry is also responsible for enhancement of the Gram Manchitra platform, 
establishment of monitoring systems, dissemination of best practices and review of 
implementation progress. 
1.45 Survey of India: Survey of India functions as the Technology Implementation 
Agency under the Scheme. 
Survey of India shall perform the establishment of CORS Network, preparation of 
Geoid Model and ensure operation and maintenance of CORS network stations and 
provision of CORS network services. It is also responsible for drone surveys, image 

13 
 
processing, feature extraction, map generation, GIS database creation, capacity 
building and technical support. 
1.46 State Revenue Department: State Revenue Department is responsible for 
legal and administrative aspects of implementation. It provides support in demarcation 
of property parcels, validation of ownership details, ground verification, inquiry 
process, dispute resolution and issuance of Property Cards. 
1.47 State Panchayati Raj Department: State Panchayati Raj Department 
supports implementation through community mobilisation, awareness generation, 
Gram Sabha consultations and facilitation of survey activities. It also assists in 
utilisation of GIS datasets for local planning purposes. 
1.48 Gram Panchayat: Gram Panchayat plays a key role in identification of 
properties, mobilisation of citizens, support during survey operations, validation of 
maps and maintenance of local records. 
1.49  Property Owners:  Property owners participate in demarcation, verification, 
inquiry and objection processes and provide necessary information for preparation of 
property records. 
1.50 NIC-GIS Division: The National Informatics Centre (NIC-GIS Division) 
supports integration, hosting and management of spatial datasets generated under 
the Scheme and facilitates utilisation of GIS-based planning applications. 
1.51 State Department of Land Records:  State Department of Land Records shall 
facilitate utilisation of the Data Centre infrastructure available at the State, District and 
Tehsil levels under the Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme 
(DILRMP) for hosting and storage of digital data created under the Scheme and 
ensure seamless integration of Bhu-Naksha with SVAMITVA maps. 
1.52 Joint Responsibility (State and Survey of India):  State Governments and 
Survey of India share certain responsibilities relating to execution of the Scheme. 
These include signing of Memoranda of Understanding for implementation of the 
project, establishment and densification of Ground Control Points using the respective 
Ground Control Point libraries and reconciliation of survey data with available records 
for finalisation of dimensions of individual land parcels.

XXIII OUTCOMES AND ACHIEVEMENTS 
1.65 SVAMITVA Scheme is a reformative step towards establishment of clear 
ownership of property in rural inhabited areas through use of drone technology and 
creation of accurate land records. The Scheme has facilitated creation of a digital 
inventory of rural properties and assets and has strengthened the foundation for 
evidence-based governance and planning. 
1.66 As on date, Property Cards have been generated for more than 3.18 crore rural 
property owners and distributed to beneficiaries across the country. The Property 
Cards provide documentary evidence of ownership and facilitate access to financial 
services and institutional credit. 
1.67 Approximately 70,000 square kilometres of rural abadi area have been 
surveyed under the Scheme. The digital maps generated through drone surveys have 
enabled creation of accurate property records and GIS databases which can be 
utilised by multiple departments for planning and implementation of developmental 
activities. 
1.68 The Scheme has enabled creation of one of the largest geospatial databases 
of rural inhabited areas in the country. The GIS datasets generated under the Scheme 
include property boundaries, public infrastructure, roads, ponds, canals, schools, 
Anganwadi Centres, health facilities and other community assets. 
1.69 The Scheme has also contributed to development of the drone ecosystem in 
the country by promoting adoption of advanced geospatial technologies, creating 
demand for skilled manpower and facilitating growth of drone-based survey 
operations. 
1.70 The Property Cards generated under the Scheme have emerged as a tool for 
financial inclusion. According to the Ministry, 11,147 loans amounting to ₹1,713 crore 
have been sanctioned using SVAMITVA Property Cards as collateral security. 
Several States have also taken initiatives for recognising women as co-owners in 
Property Cards, thereby contributing to strengthening women’s economic 
empowerment and ownership rights.

XXIV IMPACT EVALUATION 
1.74 The Ministry of Panchayati Raj commissioned an independent impact 
assessment of the SVAMITVA Scheme through the Indian Institute of Management 
Ahmedabad to assess the socio-economic and governance outcomes of the Scheme. 


19 
 
1.75 The impact evaluation study found that the Scheme has contributed 
significantly towards formalisation of property ownership, enhancement of access to 
institutional credit and strengthening of local governance systems. 
1.76 The study observed that in Madhya Pradesh, annual loan amounts increased 
by approximately ₹22,129 per parcel following implementation of the Scheme, 
indicating increased utilisation of residential properties for accessing formal credit. 
1.77 The study further found that registered residential mutations increased by 
approximately 6.2 per cent, reflecting greater formalisation of ownership records and 
property transactions. 
1.78 In Maharashtra, the impact assessment indicated a 4.71 per cent increase in 
Gram Panchayat property tax revenue and a 7.57 per cent increase in other local taxes 
following issuance of Property Cards. 
1.79 Analysis based on Reserve Bank of India data showed an increase of 
approximately 6.5 per cent in credit uptake in States where the Scheme was 
implemented, with Maharashtra recording the highest increase among the study 
States. 
1.80 The findings of the impact evaluation suggest that the Scheme has contributed 
to unlocking rural capital, enhancing financial inclusion, improving property 
governance and strengthening the fiscal capacity of local governments.

PART – II 
 
OBSERVATIONS / RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE 
 
1. Need for Expeditious Completion of SVAMITVA Coverage Across All 
Target Villages 
The Committee note that the SVAMITVA Scheme was launched with the 
objective to resolve the lack of formal property rights in rural India and 
accordingly creating accurate property records in rural inhabited areas through 
drone-based surveys and issuance of Property Cards. The Committee further 
note that against the target of 3.32 lakh villages, drone survey operations have 
been completed in about 3.29 lakh villages. However, progress remains uneven 
across States and Union Territories. The Committee observe that while several 
States and UTs have achieved saturation, substantial gaps continue to exist in 
States such as Manipur, Tripura, Odisha, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh and 
Karnataka. The Committee are of the considered view that unless uniform 
progress is achieved across all States and Union Territories, the objective of 
creating a comprehensive national rural property database may remain only 
partially realised. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of 
Panchayati Raj prepare State-specific action plans with clearly defined timelines 
for completion of pending surveys and periodically review progress at the 
highest level so that saturation of the Scheme is achieved within the stipulated 
timeframe.  
2. Need to Review the Scope of Coverage of the Scheme 
The Committee note that the SVAMITVA Scheme was conceived with the 
objective of providing records of rights to village household owners possessing 
houses in inhabited (abadi) areas through drone-based surveys and issuance 
of Property Cards. Scheme is presently confined to abadi areas and has made 
significant progress in creation of accurate property records and geospatial 
databases in such areas. However, the Committee are of the view that with the 
near completion of survey operations in a majority of villages, it would be 
appropriate to review the existing scope of the Scheme in the light of emerging 
requirements and practical experience gained during implementation. The 
Committee observe that certain residential structures, habitation clusters, 
extensions of villages and long-settled households located outside the 
traditional abadi limits may remain outside the coverage of the Scheme, thereby 
limiting the objective of providing comprehensive and inclusive property 
records in rural areas. The Committee are of the considered view that exclusion 
of such households may impede equitable access to the benefits of formal 
property rights, institutional credit and improved governance. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj, in consultation with 
States/UT Governments and other stakeholders, undertake a comprehensive 

21 
 
review of the existing coverage of the Scheme and examine the feasibility of 
extending its benefits to other eligible residential structures and habitation 
clusters presently lying outside the traditional abadi areas. The Committee also 
recommend that special attention be paid to ensuring that vulnerable and 
historically disadvantaged sections are not deprived of the benefits of formal 
property records on account of technical or jurisdictional limitations and that 
suitable guidelines be evolved to ensure more inclusive coverage while 
preserving the objectives and integrity of the Scheme. 
3. Need to Ensure Timely Distribution, Legal Validity and Effective Utilisation 
of Property Cards 
The Committee are informed that more than 3 crore Property Cards have 
been generated under the Scheme. The Committee also note that the ultimate 
success of the Scheme depends not merely on generation of Property Cards but 
on their widespread acceptance as legally recognised documents for ownership 
verification, financial transactions and governance purposes. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR), in 
consultation with State/UT Governments, ensure seamless integration of 
Property Cards with revenue administration systems, registration processes, 
mutation mechanisms, DigiLocker and banking institutions. The Committee 
further recommend that Property Cards be integrated in a time-bound manner 
with DigiLocker, BhuNaksha and other citizen-centric digital service platforms 
across all States and Union Territories so as to facilitate secure online access, 
verification and utilisation of Property Cards by beneficiaries. The Committee 
note that data relating to 17 States and Union Territories is already available on 
the SVAMITVA Portal and is being integrated with the Digital India Land Records 
Modernization Programme (DILRMP). The Committee, therefore, recommend 
that the remaining States and Union Territories also complete such integration 
in a time-bound manner so as to ensure seamless verification, wider legal 
acceptability and uniform utilisation of Property Cards across the country. The 
Committee further note that the utility of Property Cards for facilitating 
institutional credit would depend upon their legal recognition and acceptability 
across States. Accordingly, the Committee further recommend that State 
Governments undertake suitable amendments in their revenue laws, rules and 
administrative procedures so as to confer adequate legal sanctity upon Property 
Cards and ensure their universal acceptance by financial institutions. The 
Committee also recommend that MoPR periodically review the status of such 
reforms and undertake studies to assess the extent of utilisation of Property 
Cards for institutional credit and other citizen services. 
4. Integration of SVAMITVA Data with PM GatiShakti Platform 
The Committee note that cadastral maps prepared under the Digital India 
Land Records Modernization Programme have already been integrated with the 
PM GatiShakti National Master Plan platform and are being utilised extensively 

22 
 
for infrastructure planning and project implementation. The Committee, 
however, note with concern that despite creation of a vast repository of high-
accuracy geospatial data under SVAMITVA, the datasets generated under the 
Scheme have not yet been integrated with the PM GatiShakti platform. The 
Committee are of the view that integration of SVAMITVA datasets with PM 
GatiShakti would create a seamless geospatial framework for village-level 
planning and improve convergence among infrastructure and citizen welfare 
programmes/projects relating to drinking water, roads and other developmental 
activities. The said integration of data of both the schemes will bridge rural 
ground level realities with national development. The Committee, therefore, 
strongly recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj coordinate with the 
concerned Ministries and ensure onboarding of datasets relating to all surveyed 
villages onto the PM GatiShakti platform in a time-bound manner. The 
Committee note that cadastral maps of nearly 20 States and Union Territories 
are already available on the PM GatiShakti platform and are being utilised for 
infrastructure planning. The Committee are, therefore, of the view that 
integration of SVAMITVA datasets would provide a comprehensive geospatial 
framework for village-level planning, convergence of Government schemes and 
evidence-based decision-making. 
5. Leveraging SVAMITVA Data for Gram Panchayat Development Plans 
The Committee note that one of the principal objectives of the Scheme is 
to facilitate preparation of better-quality Gram Panchayat Development Plans 
(GDPDs) through GIS-based planning. The Committee, however, find that 
utilisation of the rich spatial database generated under the Scheme for local 
planning remains limited in many States. The Committee also note that the 
Scheme envisages integration of the spatial database with Gram Manchitra for 
creation of analytical tools to support preparation of Gram Panchayat 
Development Plans. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of 
Panchayati Raj (MoPR) formulate a comprehensive framework for integration of 
SVAMITVA datasets with Gram Manchitra and other planning applications and 
ensure that adequate training and technical support are provided to Gram 
Panchayats and field-level officials so that the geospatial assets created under 
the Scheme are effectively utilised for evidence-based planning and 
implementation of development programmes. The Committee further 
recommend that the MoPR periodically monitor the extent of utilisation of 
SVAMITVA datasets by Gram Panchayats and ensure availability of adequate 
digital infrastructure and broadband connectivity for effective use of GIS-based 
planning tools. The Committee are of the view that effective utilisation of these 
datasets would significantly improve the quality of Gram Panchayat 
Development Plans and promote scientific and data-driven planning at the 
grassroots level. 
 

23 
 
6. Need for a Sustainable Mechanism for Updating Property Records 
The Committee appreciate the efforts made in creating an extensive 
database of rural properties. However, the Committee are concerned that in the 
absence of a robust updation mechanism, the utility of the database may 
gradually diminish due to transfers, inheritance, partition and other changes in 
ownership. A sustainable mechanism for the SVAMITVA scheme is critical to 
permanently transform rural India from a landscape of ambiguous land tenure 
into one of formalized property rights and self-reliance. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR), in 
consultation with State/UT Governments, evolve a uniform framework for 
continuous updation of property records and GIS maps and ensure seamless 
integration of SVAMITVA records with State land administration systems. The 
Committee further emphasise the need for institutionalising structured and 
time-bound mechanisms for disposal of objections and post-survey disputes. 
Draft maps should invariably be placed before Gram Sabhas for community 
verification so as to enhance transparency, minimise litigation and safeguard 
the interests of vulnerable sections. The Committee also recommend that 
suitable grievance redressal mechanisms with clearly defined escalation 
procedures from Gram Panchayat to District level be established for expeditious 
resolution of disputes arising from mapping errors or omissions. The existing 
objection, verification and correction mechanisms adopted prior to issuance of 
Property Cards may be institutionalised for regular updation of records so that 
changes arising from inheritance, partition, transfer or mutation are reflected in 
a timely manner. Particular attention should be paid to protecting the interests 
of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and economically weaker sections 
residing on community lands or lacking formal documentation so that the 
benefits of the Scheme are equitably shared and no deserving households are 
excluded. 
7. Enhancing Access to Institutional Credit through Property Cards 
The Committee note with satisfaction that Property Cards issued under 
the Scheme have facilitated sanction of loans amounting to over ₹1,700 crore, 
demonstrating the transformative potential of the Scheme in unlocking rural 
capital and promoting financial inclusion. The Committee, therefore, 
recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj, in consultation with the 
Department of Financial Services, NABARD and banking institutions, evolve a 
dedicated framework for wider acceptance of Property Cards as collateral 
security and undertake awareness campaigns among banks and rural 
households to maximise utilisation of this facility. The Committee also note that 
online verification of Property Cards has already been enabled in States such 
as Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka and Maharashtra. Similar digital verification 
facilities may be extended across all States and Union Territories to facilitate 
faster acceptance of Property Cards by financial institutions and improve 

24 
 
citizens' access to institutional credit. The Committee further recommend that 
State Level Bankers' Committees (SLBCs) may be actively associated with the 
process so as to ensure wider acceptance of Property Cards and alignment of 
their format and features with the requirements of financial institutions. 
8. Strengthening and Leveraging the Geospatial Infrastructure Created 
under the Scheme 
The Committee note that establishment of the CORS network and creation 
of high-accuracy GIS databases constitute valuable national geospatial assets. 
The Committee further note that the Scheme guidelines envisage utilisation of 
these assets by departments dealing with roads, irrigation, agriculture, water 
resources, electricity, education and other infrastructure sectors. The 
Committee are of the view that these assets should be leveraged to the fullest 
extent for developmental purposes. The Committee, therefore, recommend that 
the Ministry of Panchayati Raj prepare a roadmap for inter-departmental 
utilisation of the geospatial infrastructure created under the Scheme and 
establish mechanisms for its long-term maintenance, periodic upgradation and 
capacity building of user departments. 
9. Institutionalisation of Impact Assessment and Outcome Monitoring 
The Committee note with satisfaction the encouraging findings of the 
impact assessment undertaken by the Indian Institute of Management, 
Ahmedabad, which indicate positive outcomes in terms of financial inclusion, 
formalisation of property rights and enhancement of revenue mobilisation. The 
Committee, however, are of the view that the long-term success of the Scheme 
would depend not merely on completion of surveys and issuance of Property 
Cards but also on sustained monitoring of its socio-economic and governance 
outcomes. For the long-term success of the scheme there will be a need to 
prioritize three core elements i.e. ensuring financial institutions accept rural 
property cards as primary collateral, building local digital literacy to maintain 
dynamic land records and robust dispute resolution mechanism. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR) 
institutionalise a robust monitoring and evaluation framework with clearly 
defined and measurable outcome indicators so as to periodically assess the 
long-term impact of the Scheme. Such indicators may, inter alia, include 
reduction in property-related disputes, uptake of institutional credit against 
Property Cards, mobilisation of property tax revenues and strengthening of 
own-source revenues of Gram Panchayats, enhancement of women's 
ownership and joint ownership of properties, utilisation of GIS databases and 
spatial tools for evidence-based planning, and improvement in the quality and 
effectiveness of Gram Panchayat Development Plans. The Committee also 
recommend that indicators relating to utilisation of GIS-based planning tools by 
Gram Panchayats and the degree of interoperability with other digital platforms 
may be incorporated into the monitoring framework so as to assess the 

25 
 
effectiveness of integration and utilisation of the digital assets created under 
the Scheme. The Committee further recommend that periodic third-party impact 
assessments may be undertaken to evaluate the effectiveness of the Scheme 
and facilitate evidence-based policy interventions and course corrections, 
wherever necessary. The Committee desires that the MoPR document and 
disseminate best practices emerging from such assessments for wider 
adoption across States and Union Territories. 
10. Promoting Women's Property Ownership and Financial Empowerment 
The Committee note with satisfaction that the SVAMITVA Scheme has 
contributed to strengthening women's economic empowerment through 
formalisation of property rights. The Committee further note that, as highlighted 
by the Ministry, formalisation of property records under the Scheme has 
resulted in a 24.6 per cent increase in the credit amount sanctioned to first-time 
women borrowers. The Committee also note that Madhya Pradesh has made 
provision under the Madhya Pradesh Land Revenue Code for inclusion of 
women as co-owners in Abadi areas, thereby promoting greater participation of 
women in ownership of assets and access to institutional credit. The Committee 
are of the considered view that the Scheme presents a unique opportunity to 
advance gender equity and strengthen women's ownership of productive assets 
in rural areas. The Committee, therefore, recommend that the Ministry of 
Panchayati Raj issue suitable guidelines to encourage States and Union 
Territories to promote joint ownership of properties and record women as co-
owners wherever feasible, with particular emphasis on single, widowed and 
vulnerable women. The Committee further recommend that the best practices 
adopted by States such as Madhya Pradesh may be suitably documented and 
disseminated for wider replication across the country. The Committee also 
desire that progress achieved in respect of women's ownership and joint 
ownership of properties may be periodically monitored and documented as an 
important outcome indicator of the Scheme. 
11. Strengthening Gram Panchayat Finances through Property Tax Reforms 
The Committee note that determination of property tax and strengthening 
of Gram Panchayat finances constitute one of the important objectives of the 
Scheme. The Committee further note with satisfaction that the impact 
assessment undertaken by the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad has 
indicated positive outcomes in this regard, with Gram Panchayat property tax 
revenues in Maharashtra increasing by about 4.71 per cent and other local taxes 
by about 7.57 per cent following implementation of the Scheme. The Committee 
are of the view that accurate and updated property databases created under 
SVAMITVA have considerable potential to improve assessment and collection of 
property taxes and thereby enhance the financial autonomy and fiscal 
sustainability of Panchayats.  The Committee, therefore, recommend that the 
Ministry of Panchayati Raj, in consultation with the States/UTs, encourage 

26 
 
effective utilisation of SVAMITVA databases for rationalisation of property tax 
assessment and strengthening of own-source revenues of Gram Panchayats 
wherever taxation powers have been devolved. The Committee further 
recommend that Gram Panchayats may be provided adequate technical support 
and capacity building for maintenance of Property Tax Registers, Asset 
Registers based on the spatial database generated under the Scheme so as to 
improve assessment, transparency and efficiency in revenue collection.  The 
Committee also desire that best practices and successful experiences emerging 
from States demonstrating improvements in local revenue mobilisation may be 
suitably documented and disseminated for wider replication across the country. 
12. Ensuring Post-2026 Sustainability and Capacity Building 
The Committee note that while substantial progress has been achieved in 
completion of drone surveys, considerable post-survey activities relating to 
ground verification, disposal of objections, preparation of final maps and 
distribution of Property Cards remain to be completed in certain States. The 
Committee also note that the technical progress under the Scheme reveals a 
gap between completion of drone surveys and generation of final Property 
Cards. The Committee are of the considered view that sustained institutional 
support would be necessary even after the formal completion of the Scheme 
period in September, 2026 to ensure that the assets created under the Scheme 
continue to deliver long-term benefits. The Committee, therefore, recommend 
that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR) deploy focused technical support for 
expeditious completion of pending verification and Property Card generation 
activities in lagging States. The Committee further recommend that adequate 
resources be provided by the MoPR for continuous capacity building of 
Revenue officials and Gram Panchayats so as to enable local bodies to maintain 
and periodically update GIS databases and utilise them for preparation of Gram 
Panchayat Development Plans and strengthening their own-source revenues. 
The Committee further recommend that continuous capacity building 
programmes, workshops and knowledge-sharing mechanisms may be 
institutionalised to equip Gram Panchayats and field functionaries for effective 
use and maintenance of geospatial databases and related digital tools. The 
Committee also recommend that institutionalised technical support and training 
mechanisms be continued beyond the Scheme period to facilitate maintenance, 
updation and effective utilisation of the digital assets created under SVAMITVA. 
Suitable arrangements may also be put in place for long-term maintenance, 
periodic upgradation and wider utilisation of the geospatial assets created 
under the Scheme so that the benefits accruing from the Scheme are sustained 
beyond its present tenure. 
13. Positioning SVAMITVA as a Global Best Practice in Digital Land 
Governance 

27 
 
The Committee note with satisfaction that the Scheme has received 
international recognition and has emerged as a model for technology-enabled 
rural property governance. The Committee are of the view that India's 
experience under SVAMITVA offers valuable lessons for other developing 
countries facing challenges relating to land administration and property rights. 
The Committee recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj systematically 
document best practices and lessons learnt from implementation of the Scheme 
and disseminate the same through national and international workshops, 
exposure visits and global forums so as to strengthen India's leadership role in 
digital land governance and geospatial planning. The Scheme guidelines 
themselves envisage documentation of best practices and organisation of 
workshops and exposure visits. 
14. Need for Strengthening Data Governance and Cyber Security Framework 
The Committee note that the SVAMITVA Scheme has resulted in creation 
of one of the largest repositories of rural geospatial and property-related data in 
the country. The Committee are of the view that protection of such data assumes 
critical importance in view of increasing concerns relating to cyber security, 
unauthorised access and misuse of digital information. The Committee, 
therefore, recommend that the Ministry of Ministry of Panchayati Raj, in 
coordination and collaboration with other concerned ministries and other 
agencies, put in place a robust data governance and cyber security framework 
covering storage, transmission, access protocols, privacy safeguards and 
mechanisms to prevent unauthorised commercial use and data breaches. The 
Committee also desire that appropriate grievance redressal mechanisms be 
established to address complaints relating to misuse of geospatial property 
data. 
15. Need for Enhancing Interoperability and Digital Integration 
The Committee note that the utility of the Scheme would be greatly 
enhanced through seamless integration with other digital governance 
platforms. The Committee are of the view that interoperability among SVAMITVA, 
DILRMP, Aadhaar-enabled services and other e-Governance platforms is 
essential for maximising the benefits of the Scheme. The Committee, therefore, 
recommend that the Ministry of Panchayati Raj prepare a roadmap for achieving 
end-to-end interoperability with other relevant digital platforms and periodically 
review the State-wise status of such integration so as to facilitate seamless 
delivery of citizen-centric services. The Committee note that APIs have already 
been developed for integration of SVAMITVA with Gram Manchitra and the 
Panchayat Enterprise Suite (PES). The Committee recommend that such 
interoperability be extended in a time-bound manner to all relevant land 
administration, registration and citizen service platforms to maximise utilisation 
of the geospatial database created under the Scheme. 

28 
 
16. Need for Strengthening Technical Quality Assurance and Independent 
Audit Mechanisms 
The Committee note that accuracy and reliability of maps constitute the 
foundation of the Scheme. Strengthening the technical quality assurance and 
independent audit mechanism of the SVAMITVA scheme is critical to ensuring 
the legal validity, financial reliability and long-term utility of the generated land 
records. The Committee are of the view that continuous quality assurance and 
validation are essential to ensure public confidence in the records created under 
the Scheme. The Committee, therefore, recommend that robust quality 
assurance mechanisms and periodic independent technical audits of geospatial 
datasets be institutionalised. The Committee further recommend that the 
Ministry of Panchayati Raj periodically review instances requiring re-survey and 
examine the feasibility of adopting emerging technologies such as Artificial 
Intelligence, LiDAR, satellite imagery and machine learning to further improve 
mapping accuracy and minimise technical disputes.
