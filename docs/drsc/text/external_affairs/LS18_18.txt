Budgetary Allocations  
(Recommendation Nos. 1&2) 
5.        The Committee in their original report observed/recommended as under:
  
(Recommendation No. 1) 
 
“The Committee note that the Ministry of External Affairs has been allocated Rs. 
22,118.97 crore in Budget Estimates (BE) 2026-27. This represents an increase of 
7.81% over BE 2025-26 of Rs. 20,516.61 crore and 1.73% over Revised Estimates 
(RE) 2025-26 of Rs. 21,742.74 crore. As a proportion of the total Union Budget 2026-
27, the MEA's allocation stands at 0.41%, which is the same as in 2025-26. The 
Committee observe that while the absolute allocation has increased in nominal terms, 
the proportional share has remained relatively low, even as India's global ambitions, 
diplomatic footprint, and development partnership commitments have expanded 
substantially. 
The Committee are concerned to note that the budgetary allocation for the Ministry 
of External Affairs as a percentage of the total Union Budget has declined consistently 
from 0.69% in 2017-18 to 0.41% in 2026-27. In absolute terms, India allocates 
approximately 0.06% of its GDP to foreign affairs, which compares unfavourably with 
countries such as the United States (approximately 0.15-0.20% of GDP) and United 
Kingdom (approximately 0.25-0.30% of GDP). The Committee further note that MEA 
is ranked 23 rd among Union Ministries in terms of budgetary allocation at Rs. 
22,118.97 crore in BE 2026-27, despite being entrusted with managing India's entire 
external engagement at a time when India occupies an increasingly prominent 
position in global affairs. 
In its Fifth Report on DFG 2025-26, the Committee had specifically recommended a 
budgetary enhancement of at least 20% in allocation to MEA. The Ministry has 
conveyed that this recommendation was formally communicated to the Ministry of 
Finance by the Foreign Secretary ahead of the pre-budget consultations. Despite 
this, the eventual increase has been limited to 7.81% - well short of what the 
Committee had recommended. The Committee are constrained to observe that this 
level of increase is inadequate to keep pace with India's expanding international 
engagements and the rising costs of diplomacy. The Committee therefore, strongly 

3 | P a g e 
 
recommend that due cognizance should to be given to the strategic imperatives 
driving MEA's expenditure and accord the Ministry a budgetary allocation 
commensurate with India's aspirations as a leading global power. The Committee 
reiterate their earlier recommendation for a budgetary enhancement of at least 20% 
and urge that the MEA's allocation be progressively raised to a level closer to the 
global benchmark for comparable major economies. The concern of the Committee 
may be conveyed to the Ministry of the Finance for further necessary action. 
 
(Recommendation No. 2) 
 
The Committee note that MEA currently ranks 23rd among all Ministries and 
Departments in terms of budgetary allocation, at 0.41% of the Union Budget. 
Ministries such as Finance (35.93%), Defence (14.29%), Road Transport and 
Highways (5.64%), and Railways (5.12%) understandably command larger shares 
given their domestic infrastructure mandates. However, the Committee are of the 
view that MEA's allocation must be compared not merely in terms of rank but in terms 
of the strategic multiplier it provides: effective diplomatic engagement, secures trade, 
investment, Diaspora welfare, neighbourhood stability, and India's standing in 
multilateral institutions - dividends that accrue across sectors and are not fully 
captured in any single Ministry's budget. 
The Committee had persistently recommended that the Ministry should commission 
an independent empirical study to assess MEA's budgetary trends in a comparative 
framework. Pursuant to this recommendation, the Research and Information System 
for Developing Countries (RIS) has prepared a Report titled 'Analysis of Trends of 
MEA Budgetary Allocation', examining MEA's budget both through an inter-Ministerial 
comparison within the Government of India and a cross-country comparison with 
major economies.  
The Committee recommend that MEA should utilize the RIS Report and update it 
annually with the latest figures on both inter-Ministerial allocation trends and cross-
country comparisons. The updated Report should be formally annexed to MEA's 
budget demand submission to the Ministry of Finance and should be shared with the 
Committee alongside the Demands for Grants. The Committee are of the view that a 
data-driven, rigorously benchmarked case before the Ministry of Finance will be far 
more persuasive than incremental administrative representations, and the RIS 
Report provides exactly the kind of credible evidence base that is required.” 
 
6.  In its action-taken reply, the Ministry of External Affairs has stated as under: 
(Reply to Recommendation No. 1) 
“The Hon’ble Committee’s recommendation for budgetary enhancement of at least 
20% is noted. The Ministry has been following the Hon’ble Committee’s advice to 
take up the requirement of optimum allocation of funds with the Ministry of Finance. 
The Ministry continues to engage with the Ministry of Finance at all levels to

emphasize the importance of providing adequate budgetary allocations so that there 
is no shortage of funds for the Ministry in the pursuit of India’s international 
commitments. This sustained engagement has resulted in an increased budgetary 
allocation of Rs. 22,118.97 crore to the Ministry in BE 2026-27. This is an increase of 
Rs. 1602.36 crore i.e. 7.81% from productive BE of 2025-26. The Ministry will make 
a strong case to the Ministry of Finance for a further increase in budgetary allocation 
at 2026-27 RE stage.  
 
(Reply to Recommendation No. 2) 
Ministry has noted Hon’ble Committee’s recommendation to update the RIS report 
and use it in the budget process. In line with the Hon’ble Committee’s 
recommendations, the Ministry had made a strong case for increased funding to the 
Ministry of Finance for the FY 2026-27. As a result, Ministry has received BE 2026-
27 of Rs. 22118.97 crore, which is an increase of Rs. 1602.36 crore from productive 
BE 2025-26. The Ministry had also received an augmentation of Rs 1235.63 crore in 
the first Supplementary for FY 2025-26.  
Ministry accords utmost priority to effective budgetary allocation and resource 
prioritization. The Ministry continues to hold extensive discussions with Ministry of 
Finance at all stages of the budgetary cycle to impress upon them the need for 
adequate budgetary allocation to MEA, within the overall constraints of Government 
finances.” 
 
7.  Noting that the budgetary allocation for the Ministry as a percentage 
of the total Union Budget has declined consistently, the Committee in their 
original Report had reiterated their earlier recommendation for a budgetary 
enhancement of at least 20%. The Committee had also recommended the 
Ministry to commission an independent empirical study to assess MEA’s 
budgetary trends in a comparative framework and make use of the Report 
prepared by Research & Information System for Developing Countries (RIS) 
which have examined MEA’s budget both through an inter-Ministerial  
comparison with the Government of India (GoI)  and a   cross country 
comparison with major economies.  The Committee note that there has been a 
marginal increase of 7.81% in budgetary allocations of the Ministry of External 
Affairs in BE 2026-27.  The Ministry has assured to make a strong case to the 
Ministry of Finance for a further increase in budgetary allocation in the current 
financial year at RE stage. While appreciating the Ministry’s efforts for optimum 
allocation, so that there is no shortage of funds for fulfilling India’s 
international commitments, the Committee believe that these efforts need to be 

5 | P a g e 
 
supported by relevant data to compare the trend at both inter-Ministerial and 
cross-country level. Since the information is being captured by Research and 
Information System for Developing Countries (RIS) in their report on 'Analysis 
of Trends of MEA Budgetary Allocation’, the Committee are of the view that the 
updated report of RIS should be used to project the requirements and 
appropriate levels of higher allocations be pursued with the Ministry of 
Finance. The Committee  would further like to be apprised of the allocations 
made to the Ministry at RE 2026-27. The Committee desire that the updated RIS 
report along with the Demands for Grants be placed before them during their 
examination of the next Demands for Grants to enable them to make a realistic 
analysis of budgetary allocation. The Committee are of the considered view 
that MEA’s budgetary allocation needs to be enhanced significantly 
considering India’s global ambitions, diplomatic footprints and development 
partnerships commitments which are expanding at a faster pace.  
 
Non-inclusion of Line of Credit Provisioning 
(Recommendation No.3) 
 
8.  The Committee in their original report observed/recommended as under: 
“The Committee had consistently held that Line of Credit provisioning, which 
represents a notional expenditure and an adjustment of accounts between different 
branches of Government, charged to the Guaranteed Redemption Fund, gives a 
disproportionate and misleading picture of MEA's actual expenditure. The Committee 
are satisfied to note that BE 2026-27 allocations do not include any provisioning for 
Lines of Credit (LoC) through the EXIM Bank. The Foreign Secretary, during evidence 
confirmed that the Ministry remains engaged with the Reserve Bank of India and the 
Ministry of Finance on this structural issue.  
The Committee reiterate that LoC provisioning must not be included in MEA's regular 
budget in future years. The Committee suggest that this position be formalized 
through a permanent institutional arrangement, whereby such provisioning is charged 
directly to an appropriate dedicated instrument managed by the Ministry of Finance 
and the Reserve Bank of India, entirely outside the MEA's Demand for Grants. The 
Ministry should formally propose this structural arrangement to the concerned 
authorities and report back to the Committee on the outcome in the next year.” 
 

6 | P a g e 
 
 
9.  In its action-taken reply, the Ministry of External Affairs has stated as under: 
“The provisioning burden for FY 2025-26 is around USD 276.29 million i.e. about 
Rs. 2615 crore. Since no amount was allocated towards provisioning in MEA’s 
budget for FY 2025-26 and in BE 2026-27, an amount of Rs. 2615 crore was drawn 
as Contingency Advance with the approval of Department of Economic Affairs for 
provisioning. The Committee’s recommendation that provisioning must not be 
included in MEA’s regular budget in future and should be charged separately and 
directly to an appropriate dedicated instrument managed by DEA/RBI outside of 
MEA’s Demands for Grants, is noted. MEA is engaged with Department of 
Economic Affairs, M/o Finance and RBI on the matter of provisioning.” 
 
10. While stressing that Line of Credit provisioning gives a 
disproportionate and misleading picture of MEA’s actual expenditure, the 
Committee had recommended  that LOC provisioning must not be included in 
MEA’s regular budget and this position be formalized through a permanent 
institutional arrangement, whereby such provisioning is charged directly to an 
appropriate dedicated instrument managed by the Ministry of Finance and the 
Reserve Bank of India, entirely outside the MEA's Demands for Grants. The 
Committee had also suggested that the Ministry institute a structured 
arrangement for this purpose. The Committee note from the action taken reply 
that since no amount was allocated towards provisioning in MEA’s budget for 
FY 2025-26, an amount of Rs. 2615 crore has been drawn as Contingency 
Advance in BE 2026-27 to meet the provisioning burden of 2025-26, with the 
approval of Department of Economic Affairs. The Committee find that despite 
their repeated highlighting of the issue of LOC provisioning in MEA’s budget in 
previous Reports on Demands for Grants, the issue has not got the attention 
of the Ministry as it warrants.  MEA is still engaged with Department of 
Economic Affairs, M/o Finance and RBI on the matter of provisioning.  The 
Committee are keen to know the underlying challenges due to which Ministry 
of External Affairs, Ministry of Finance and RBI have not been able to come on 
board on a formal institutional arrangement and this structural issue of 
budgeting has not yet been addressed. Since LoC provisioning does not reflect 
the actual utilisation of funds, the Committee would once again reiterate that a 

7 | P a g e 
 
permanent institutional arrangement be created urgently and preferably before 
the Demands for Grants 2027-28, whereby provisioning is charged directly to 
an appropriate dedicated instrument managed by the Ministry of Finance and 
the Reserve Bank of India, entirely outside MEA's Demands for Grants. 
 
Budgetary Allocation for Diaspora Welfare and Engagement 
(Recommendation No. 9) 
 
11.  The Committee, in their original report observed/recommended as under: 
 “The Committee have examined with considerable care the budgetary allocations 
and institutional framework governing India’s engagement with its diaspora. India’s 
diaspora of over 32 million persons, spread across more than 180 countries, remits 
over US$ 100 billion annually to the country and constitutes India’s most effective 
instrument of cultural diplomacy and soft power. The Committee note that the 
budgetary allocations for diaspora welfare and engagement are spread across 
multiple sub-heads such as ‘Welfare of Overseas Indians’, ‘Engagement with Indian 
Diaspora’, ‘Scheme for Welfare of Indian Women Abroad’, and ‘Protector of 
Emigrants’. Individual scheme allocations tell their own story. The allocation for 
repatriation of Indian destitutes has remained frozen at Rs. one lakh rupees for six 
consecutive years. Legal assistance to women facing problems in NRI marriages has 
been frozen at 5 lakh for four consecutive years. The Awareness Campaign and 
Media Plan has been slashed by over 90%, from Rs. 11 crore in 2020-21 to Rs. 1 
crore in 2025-26, precisely when recruitment fraud, illegal migration, and trafficking 
are on the rise. The Pre-Departure Orientation Training programme, designed to 
protect the most vulnerable emigrant workers before they depart, recorded nil 
utilization in 2022-23 and expenditure of barely Rs. 0.29 crore in 2024-25 against a 
grant of Rs. 1 crore. From the above observation, the Committee note that the 
budgetary allocations for their welfare and engagement are not reflective of their 
strategic significance in our foreign policy objectives.  
The Committee strongly recommend that the Ministry should undertake a 
fundamental review of the budgetary framework for diaspora welfare and 
engagement with the explicit objective of aligning allocations with the strategic 
significance of our diaspora. The Committee also recommend that the Government 
should examine, as a matter of urgency, the case for establishing a dedicated 
Department of Overseas India Affairs within MEA, headed by a Secretary-level officer 
with an exclusive and undivided mandate for diaspora engagement and welfare.” 
 
12. In its action-taken reply, the Ministry of External Affairs has stated as under: 
 “Secretary (CPV & Overseas Indian Affairs) of the Ministry of External Affairs is in 
charge of all matters related to diaspora and overseas Indian affairs covering 
consular, passport and visa matters, diaspora engagement, overseas emigration and 

8 | P a g e 
 
mobility, welfare of vulnerable Indians etc. The following Divisions of the Ministry, 
headed by Joint Secretary/Additional Secretary level officers, handling overseas 
Indian affairs report to Secretary (CPV & OIA): (I) Diaspora Engagement esides in 
that region. Through coordinated efforts of all the above-mentioned Divisions, the 
Ministry is able to meet the expectations of the Indian diaspora in the most optimal 
fashion.  
The budget allocation for diaspora engagement and welfare has also steadily 
increased over time to address the need to promote more efficient and effective 
mechanisms to connect with the Indian diaspora community overseas. The Ministry 
of External Affairs implements several programmes for the welfare of the Indian 
diaspora and has made significant progress in engaging and supporting the Indian 
diaspora through its key programmes. The Pravasi Bharatiya Divas (PBD) held 
biennially, continues to serve as the flagship event connecting overseas Indians with 
the Government of India. The Scholarship Programme for Diaspora Children (SPDC) 
provides opportunities for diaspora youth to pursue higher education in India, 
strengthening their academic and cultural ties with the country. The Know India 
Programme (KIP) engages young members of the diaspora, enhancing their 
understanding of India’s heritage, culture and contemporary socio-economic 
developments. The Promotion of Cultural Ties with Diaspora (PCTD) supports 
initiatives by Indian Missions/Posts abroad and Indian organizations to preserve and 
promote Indian culture and foster deeper cultural connections with overseas 
communities. Together, these programmes have strengthened diaspora 
engagement, promoted cultural diplomacy and reinforced India’s bonds with its global 
community. Through the Indian Community Welfare Fund, support is provided to 
Indian diaspora by way of emergency medical care, legal counselling, boarding and 
lodging to distressed members of diaspora including women. Ministry is also in the 
process of establishing ‘One Stop Centres’ overseas to assist Indian women in 
distress. Government of India has thus not only expanded the scope and ambit of its 
diaspora engagement activities, but has also intensified activities directed towards 
protecting their interests and welfare in foreign lands. 
The safety, security and welfare of Indian diaspora remains one of the highest 
priorities of the Government of India. All possible efforts are made to promote legal 
migration, prevent human trafficking and fraudulent recruitment of Indian nationals 
for employment overseas. India is also a signatory to the ‘Global Compact of 
Migration’ and safe, orderly and regular migration has been one of its key objectives. 
The Ministry has optimally used the potential of social media platforms to promote its 
objective of spreading awareness, including safe and legal migration. 
 The Government has issued regular advisories cautioning Indian nationals against 
fraudulent job offers and unauthorized recruiting agents and emphasized the need to 
verify employment credentials through Indian Missions/Posts abroad in the 
respective countries. A list of 3505 (till January 2026) unregistered agents in the 
country has also been notified on the e-Migrate portal to caution prospective 
emigrants. Special emigration awareness campaigns are organised, including 
workshop/events and distribution of leaflets/publicity material. This Ministry is also 
commissioning multiple videos in different languages to educate potential Indian 
emigrants in line with the Government’s motto of “Surakshit Jayen, Prashikshit 
Jayen”. Video spots and PSA videos are proposed to be created in 12 languages 

9 | P a g e 
 
(Hindi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Marathi, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Odia, Bengali, 
Assamese and English) featuring journalists, influencers or experts having good 
appeal/followership on social media in their respective languages.  
The awareness campaign on safe and legal migration through e-Migrate portal is also 
being carried out by this Ministry and 16 Protector of Emigrants (PoE) offices across 
the country by conducting workshops, trainings, information sessions, digital 
campaigns for media groups, police officials and law enforcement agencies, local 
administration, aspiring recruiting agents, entrepreneurs and general public. During 
these sessions awareness on emigration regulations, schemes beneficial for 
emigrants like Pravasi Bharatiya Bima Yojana (PBBY), Pre-Departure Orientation 
Training (PDOT), eMigrate portal and various advisories issued by Indian Embassies 
are brought to the notice of all stakeholders and job seekers.  
 Indian Missions/Posts encourage Indian diaspora members to register with them, 
join WhatsApp Group created for the Indian community and also to lodge their 
grievances on the MADAD Portal and CPGRAMS of this Ministry. The grievances are 
being responded almost on a real time basis through telephone calls, walk-ins, 
emails, social media, 24x7 emergency helplines, Consular Camps, Open Houses, 
the MADAD Portal and CPGRAMS. Indian Missions/Posts also proactively reach out 
to distressed Indian nationals, either directly or through the local Indian Diaspora 
Associations within their jurisdiction to provide them consular services/assistance. 
Indian Missions/Posts in countries with instability remain vigilant and closely monitor 
developments that may adversely affect the Indian diaspora in such regions. From 
time to time, they issue advisories to caution Indian nationals to avoid unnecessary 
travel, remain in touch with them through published helpline numbers and follow safety 
guidelines and protocols issued by local authorities. Any unfortunate incident or 
challenges faced by Indian diaspora are immediately taken up with the host country 
to suitably resolve them. Such issues are also raised during bilateral meetings, 
including at the highest levels, as appropriate. During emergencies, Indian 
Missions/Posts help distressed/stranded Indians by providing them food, shelter, 
medicines and evacuate them to India, if necessary. Notable recent evacuation 
operations undertaken by the Government of India include Vande Bharat, Operation 
Devi Shakti (Afghanistan), Operation Ganga (Ukraine), Operation Kaveri (Sudan), 
Operation Ajay (Israel) and Operation Sindhu (Israel and Iran). These efforts have 
demonstrated this Ministry’s commitment to ensuring the safety and well-being of 
Indian diaspora in foreign lands. 
 It may also be noted that in recent years, the aforementioned Awareness 
Campaign and Media Plan budget head highlighted by the Committee is being used 
only for incurring expenditure related to promotion of the Know India Programmes and 
other Diaspora Engagement activities. The nature of these campaigns has also 
evolved over the years, with increasing focus on social media and digital platforms 
which has reflected on the budget allocation as well.”

13. Taking note of the fact that the budgetary allocation for diaspora 
welfare and engagement is scattered over multiple sub-heads and the meagre 
allocation of funds under the schemes relating to repatriation of Indian 
destitutes and legal assistance to women facing problems in NRI marriages etc., 
the Committee had recommended for fundamental review of the budgetary 
framework for diaspora welfare and engagement and examine the case for 
establishing a dedicated Department of Overseas India Affairs within MEA, with 
an exclusive and undivided mandate. Although, from the replies, it is noted that 
Secretary (CPV&OIA) has been overseeing all the divisions entrusted with the 
management of diaspora and overseas affairs related matters, the allocation 
and utilisation of funds under various programmes/schemes related to welfare 
of diaspora and handling of overseas affairs present a different picture as noted 
by the Committee during their examination of the Demands for Grants (2026-
27). The Committee also find that while the details of the existing programmes 
and schemes relating to welfare of the diaspora have been furnished, the 
reasons for lower allocations and lesser utilisation of budgetary resources for 
the activities relating to Indian diaspora have not been explained by the Ministry. 
Further, the Ministry has also not addressed the specific recommendation 
regarding creation of a dedicated Department of Overseas India Affairs within 
the Ministry of External Affairs.  The Committee feel that a cohesive approach 
is required for better coordination at ground level which can only be achieved 
under a separate department dealing exclusively with diaspora affairs. The 
Committee are of the considered view that creation of a department by merging 
all the related divisions dealing with matters relating to the diaspora under its 
ambit will result in better focus and utilisation of resources for the welfare of 
Indian diaspora making them an important link in advancing our foreign policy 
goals. The Committee, therefore, reiterate that a dedicated Department of 
Overseas India Affairs may be created within MEA with an exclusive and 
undivided mandate for diaspora engagement and welfare .

“The Indian Community Welfare Fund (ICWF) has been set up in all Indian Missions 
and Posts abroad to assist Overseas Indian nationals in times of distress and 
emergency. ICWF is a self-sustaining fund raised by levying a service charge on 
Consular, Passport and Visa services provided by the Mission. The fund is used to 
provide legal assistance, boarding & lodging assistance, emergency medical care, 
air passage to stranded Indians, transportation of mortal remains of Indian nationals, 
payment of small fines and penalties for release of Indian Nationals from jail/

Consular officer and a three-member committee appointed in every Mission and Post.

circumstances of the case and financial capability of the concerned Indian national.

(Recommendation No. 15)

local police are aware of and act against the blacklisted agents operating in their

Recommendation No. 15 
 
Combating Illegal Recruitment 
The Committee note that despite the eMigrate portal, the mPassport Police 
App, and the network of PoE offices, illegal recruitment agencies and 
unauthorized intermediaries continue to operate, exploiting vulnerable Indian 
workers seeking employment abroad. The Committee observe that the 
Ministry has acknowledged the challenge of combating fraud in the 
recruitment process and the need for coordination with local police and cyber-
crime units, but has not provided the Committee with specific data on the scale 
of illegal recruitment activity, the number of agents prosecuted, or the 
outcomes of enforcement actions. The Committee are of the firm view that the 
protection of Indian workers from exploitation, trafficking, and fraudulent 
recruitment is a fundamental obligation of the State, and that the current 
enforcement architecture - characterized by a small PoE network, limited 
financial resources, and dependence on State police coordination - appear 
insufficient to meet this obligation at the required scale. 

59 | P a g e 
 
The Committee, therefore, recommend that MEA should develop and 
implement, in coordination with concerned Ministries (such as Home Affairs 
and Labour and Employment) and state governments, a comprehensive 
strategy to counter illegal recruitment, encompassing a strengthened

online recruitment advertising, coordination with social media platforms to 
detect and remove fraudulent job advertisements, and a dedicated rapid 
response mechanism for distress cases involving illegal recruitment. The 
Committee further recommend that the Ministry institute a publicly accessible, 
real-time blacklisted illegal and deregistered recruiting agents, accessible to 
potential emigrants and their families through the eMigrate portal, MADAD,

ensure that local police are aware of and act against the blacklisted agents

The Ministry takes appropriate measures and issues guidelines from time to time to 
regulate the registered Recruitment Agents (RA) in the country. As per Section 10 of 
the Emigration Act, 1983, no person/agency can function as RA without a valid 
Registration Certificate (RC) issued by the Registering Authority i.e. Protector

incorporate current as well as futuristic aspects to effectively meet the modern-day 
challenges in the emigration sphere.

provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) and other legislations in place.

SUMMARY OF FINDINGS OF REPORT ON ‘MID-TERM IMPACT 
ASSESSMENT OF TELE-EDUCATION COMPONENT OF E-VBAB 
NETWORK PROJECT SUBMITTED BY RIS 
 
Background: 
1. Pan Africa e-Network Project (PAeNP) was conceived by former 
President of India late Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam in 2004. Cabinet had 
approved Phase-1 of PAeNP in July 2007 and it was formally launched 
on February 26, 2009. The  phase-I of PAeNP was implemented from 
2009-2014 but extended up to 2017. Under Phase-1 of PAeNP, India had 
set up a fibre-optic network to provide satellite connectivity, tele-medicine 
and tele-education to African countries. The project was aimed to create 
significant linkages for tele-education and telemedicine, internet, video-
conferencing and VoIP services, making available the facilities and 
expertise of some of the best universities and super-speciality hospitals 
in India to the people of Africa.  
2. During the 3 rd India Africa Forum Summit held in October 2015 in 
Delhi, the Hon’ble Prime Minister had announced 50,000 scholarships for 
African students, out of which 15,000 scholarships were to be offered in 
on-line mode. To fulfil this commitment, the Cabinet in November 2015, 
approved Phase 2 of Pan Africa e-Network Project (PAeNP) for a period 
of 5 years from 2016-21, at a cost of Rs. 951.22 Cr. to be implemented 
by M/s TCIL. 
 
3.     Phase 2 of the PAeNP is rechristened as e-VidyaBharati and e-
AarogyaBharati (e-VBAB) Network Project. It is technologically upgraded 
version of Phase 1 of PAeNP as in e-VBAB,  the course delivery has been 
through internet based web portals, instead of Satellite based as was in 
PAeNP .  The project was launched in October 2019 and the actual 
implementation began in January 2020. The implementation modality 
was changed from satellite to internet- based service delivery; and UGC 
approved the first set of 7 Universities eligible to offer online 
UG/PG/Diploma courses in January 2020.  
 
 

154 | P a g e 
 
 
4.  Under Tele-Education component of e-VBAB project, a total of 
15,116 students from  22 African countries enrolled to pursue online 
UG/PG/ Diploma courses/ programmes. 27 Indian Universities (10 
Government & 17 Private) participated in the e-VBAB project to offer 
more than 250  UG/PG/ PG Diploma courses/programmes. The target of 
15,000 Tele-Education scholarships was achieved in July 2023 
academic session. 
 
5. The Mid-Term Impact assessment of the Tele-Education 
component of the e-VBAB Network project  was undertaken by Research 
and Information System for Developing Countries (RIS). RIS submitted 
its report in April 2025. The report evaluates only the e-VidyaBharati 
component as the tele-medicine component was dropped in November, 
2023 and covers first three completed sessions only (Jan 2020, Jul 2020, 
Jan 2021; Number of students = 3,556). 
 
6.  Key findings of the Report on Mid-Term Impact assessment of the 
Tele-Education component of the e-VBAB Network project is presented 
below:  
 
 Strategic Impact: e-VBAB aligns with India’s South-South Cooperation 
goals, Africa centric policy , and Agenda 2063 of African Union , by 
promoting affordable, accessible, and flexible education.  
 Reach: 
 Over 15,116 students enrolled  (2020–2023), with a focus on 
postgraduate programmes (especially MBA). 
 63.8% pass rate  among early beneficiaries(2020–21) and many 
students are still completing courses. 
 High interest among young aged 26–35,  with around 22.1% 
females, indicating gender disparity. 
 Employment Outcomes : Among 1,070 alumni surveyed, 84.3% are 
employed, especially in education, ICT, finance , and health-care. 
Many reported improved job responsibilities or promotions post e-VBAB 
course. 
 Cost effectiveness: Indian courses are free and competitive in quality 
versus expensive regional/global alternatives (e.g., $2,000–$50,000 for 
other online MBAs).

 Relevance for Africa’s developmental goals:  The tel-education 
component of the e-VBAB is relevant to Africa’s developmental goals 
because: 
i) By 2050, more than half of Africa's population will be under the 
age of 25; 
ii) 1/5th of African youth can be classified under the category of Not 
in Education, Employment, or Training (NEET); 
iii) Existing challenges in education sector in Africa impeding 
human resource development. 
 Comparative advantage of courses in e-Viya Bharati: A comparison

• Affordability, as scholarships are provided free of 
 cost  
 
 Role in enhancing human capital in Africa: e-Vidya Bharati is playing

i. India offers better-quality and more affordable education than most

ii. The programme supports the internationalisation of Indian 
universities by building their global presence.  
iii. The eVidyaBharati model is cost-effective – students incur no 
tuition, admission, or administrative fees, improving skill 
development and employability. 
 
 Challenges: 
 Connectivity issues, lack of laptops, and power outages.  
 Limited enrolment from Francophone and Lusophone countries. 
 Feedback indicates need for better university support, platform

 Recommendations: 
 Introduce screening tests , strengthen the iLearn platform , and
