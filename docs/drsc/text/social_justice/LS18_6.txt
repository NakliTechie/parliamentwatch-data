CHAPTER I 
The Department of Disability Affairs was carved out of the Ministry of Social 
Justice and Empowerment on May 12, 2012 in order to give focused attention to policy 
issues and meaningful thrust to the activities aimed at welfare and empowerment of the 
Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). It was subsequently renamed as Department of 
Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities on 8 December, 2014 and acts as a nodal 
agency for matters pertaining to disability and persons with disabilities including 
effecting closer coordination among different stakeholders. 
1.2 Empowerment of persons with disabilities is an inter-disciplinary process, 
covering various aspects namely, prevention, early detection, intervention, education, 
health, vocational training, rehabilitation and social integration. Thus the vision of the 
Department is to build an inclusive society in which equal opportunities are provided for 
the growth and development of persons with disabilities so that they can lead 
productive, safe and dignified lives. 
1.3 The Objectives of the Department are enumerated as under : 
(i) Physical rehabilitation, which includes early detection and intervention, 
counseling and medical rehabilitation and assistance in procuring 
appropriate aids and appliances for reducing the effect of disabilities; 
(ii) Educational rehabilitation including vocational education; 
(iii) Economic empowerment; 
(iv) Developing rehabilitation professionals/personnel; 
(v) Improving internal efficiency/responsiveness/service delivery; and 
(vi) Advocating empowerment of persons with disabilities through awareness 
generation among different sections of the society such as cultural, 
recreational and sports activities. 
1.4 The scope of the Department is enumerated as under:- 

2 
 
(i) Special schemes aimed at rehabilitation and social, educational and economic 
empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, e.g. supply of aids and appliances, 
scholarships, residential schools, skill training, concessional loans and subsidy for 
self-employment, etc.  
(ii) Education and Training of Rehabilitation Professionals. 
(iii) International Conventions and Agreements on matters dealt within the Department 
e.g. United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. 
(iv) Awareness generation, research, evaluation and training in regard to subjects 
allocated to the Department. 
(v) Charitable and Religious Endowments and promotion and development of 
Voluntary Efforts pertaining to subjects, allocated to the Department. 
1.5 The Department deals with the following Legislations governing different aspects 
of disability, welfare and empowerment of the Persons with Disabilities: -  
1. The Rehabilitation Council of India Act, 1992 (34 of 1992). 
2. The National Trust for the Welfare of Persons with Autism, Cerebral Palsy, Mental 
Retardation and Multiple Disabilities Act, 1999 (44 of 1999).  
3. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (49 of 2016). 
1.6 The following Statutory Bodies/PSUs Autonomous Bodies come under The 
purview of the Department :- 
(i) The Rehabilitation Council of India. 
(ii) The Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities. 
(iii) The National Trust for the Welfare of Persons with Autism, Cerebral 
Palsy, Mental Retardation and Multiple Disabilities. 
1.7 The CPSUs/Autonomous Bodies under the DEPwD are as under:- 
(i) The National Handicapped Finance and Development Corporation (NHFDC).  
(ii) Artificial Limbs Manufacturing Corporation of India (ALIMCO), Kanpur.

 Hearing Impairment: Deaf, Hard of Hearing 
 Speech and Language Disability 
(ii) Intellectual Disability 
 Intellectual Disability 
 Specific Learning Disability 
 Autism Spectrum Disorder 
(iii) Mental Behavior  Mental Illness 
(iv) Disability caused due to  Chronic Neurological Conditions: Parkinson’s Disease, Multiple Sclerosis

(v) Multiple Disabilities  More than one of the above specified disabilities

1.14 During the course of evidence, the Secretary, Department of Empowerment of 
Persons with Disabilities also apprised the Committee of the new initiatives aimed at 
improving the lot of Persons with disabilities in India. 
“One is that a lot of State Governments are actually setting up independent 
departments. They are also appointing independent Ministers forDisability. 
Several States, such as Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, and Rajasthan, have 
increased their budgets from zero to approximately ₹1,000 crore or ₹1,500 crore. 
So, State Budgets are really starting to look very good”.  
“The Karnataka Government is the only one that has village-level workers in 
disability, with about 7,000 workers serving approximately 30,000 villages for the 
past 10 to 15 years. We are retraining these workers and creating a new breed 
of community-based professionals, which we are also funding”.  
“In terms of accessibility within the SIPDA scheme, our funding for building 
accessibility is decreasing. We are undergoing a paradigm shift. We have 
initiated a Sugamya Yatra across all States, where a team of 10 to 15 individuals, 
including people with various disabilities, visits collector offices, SP offices, 
district judges, main shopping malls, cinemas, colleges, and tourist attractions in 
their districts. They are raising awareness and requesting corrections for 
identified shortfalls. By including Divyang community, we will probably achieve 
what even ₹2,000 crore of funding would not achieve. We are making significant 
progress, and the response from the public has been very good. Everyone is 
welcoming them and providing free entry into their offices and shopping malls. 
Although they mention that it may take three to six months to construct the 
ramp”. 
1.15 The Committee appreciate the proactive initiatives taken by 
various State Governments in establishing dedicated Department for 
disability affairs and significantly increasing budgetary allocations. 
They also acknowledgethe innovative approach of community-based 
interventions, such as Karnataka’s village-level disability workers and 
the nationwide SugamyaYatra initiative, which fosters grassroots 

7 
 
accessibility improvements. To build on this momentum, the 
Committee recommend that the Department actively collaborate with 
States to institutionalize similar models across the country, ensuring 
sustainable and community-driven disability inclusion. Additionally, a 
structured framework should be developed to document and monitor 
accessibility improvements under SugamyaYatra, enabling the 
replication of best practices nationwide.

OVERALL BUDGETARY ANALYSIS AND DEMANDS FOR GRANTS FOR 2025-26 
Demand No. 94 covers the expenditure of the Department of Empowerment of 
Persons with Disabilities (Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment). The detailed 
Demands for Grants of the Department were laid on the Table of the LokSabha on 11 
February, 2025. 
2.2 To a query on the proposed allocation of the Department, the Committee was 
informed as under:- 
“Department had submitted a projected BE requirement of ₹1704.87 crore for 
the financial year 2025-26 with the objective of maintaining momentum of the 
ongoing activities and also to initiate new activities. After assessment by 
Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance, ₹1275.00 crore has been 
allocated to the Department in BE 2025-26”. 
2.3 The Committee further enquired as to how the Department would be managing 
their programs and ensure effective implementation of key schemes particularly when 
the projections for 2025-26 was much higher than allocation. In reply, the Department 
have stated as under:- 
“The Budget Estimates in respect of the Central Sector Schemes namely ADIP, 
SIPDA, DDRS and Scholarship were recommended by the EFC in its meeting held 
on 11.08.2021 for continuation for next five years. All the four schemes are 
appraised and approved with financial allocation and physical targets for the 
entire period of the 15th Finance Commission’s recommendations. Therefore, the 
budget allocation of FY 2025-26 of the schemes will be utilized as per the 
approved activities to maintain the ongoing activities as approved in EFC outlay 
to meet out the desired targets”. 
2.4 They have also assured the Committee that in case of additional requirement of 
funds under any particular Schemes or Non Schemes, the additional demand for grants 
would be raised with the Ministry of Finance at the RE stage .

2.6 It has been observed from the tables above that for the year 2025-26, an 
amount of ₹1,275.00 crore has been fixed for the Department at the BE stage, which is 
approximately 9.23 percent  more  than the BE of the previous financial year (2024-25) 
which was ₹1,225.27crore. The RE for the year 2024-25 was ₹1,167.27 crore and 
actual utilisation was ₹892.68crore (till 03.03.2025) which was 76.45 percent w.r.t RE 
allocation. Given this slow expenditure trend, the Department was requested to clarify 
the key factors behind this low utilization. In Reply the Department has stated as 
under:- 
 “In 2024-25, the major reason for slow pace of expenditure is due to 
implementation of CNA Model-1(TSA)/1A (Hybrid TSA). Earlier the 
funds were being released from CNA Model-2. However, as per 
revised guidelines of Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance 
the Central Sector Schemes whose annual outlay is more than 100 
Crore are now required to on-board on the Model- 1/1A/TSA Hybrid 
Model.  
Due to change in procedure of release of funds, no funds could be 
released in 3rd quarter of 2024-25 as the process had technical issues 

11 
 
such as return of CNA balances to CFI, opening of new RBI Account 
and Mapping of all the agencies in TSA Model.”  
 
2.7 Further, it can be observed from the table above that while BE has remained 
almost static over the past 3 years , a pattern of reduction at the Revised Estimate (RE) 
stage and under spending in actual expenditure is evident. In 2022-23, the BE was 
₹1,212.42 crore, but the RE was reduced to ₹1,015.98 crore, whereas the actual 
expenditure stood at only ₹989.35 crore. This pattern has been repeated in 2023-24 
where BE/RE has remained stable at ₹1,225.15/1,225.01 crore but actuals were to the 
tune of ₹1,143.89 crore only. Similarly, in 2024-25, the RE was lowered from BE of       
₹1,225.27 crore to ₹1,167.27 crore, reflecting a downward adjustment. 
2.8 On being asked to explain the underlying reasons for these reductions at the RE 
stage and the persistent shortfall in actual expenditure, the Department has submitted 
as under:- 
“This Department is implementing various Central Sector schemes namely SIPDA, 
DDRS, ADIP, Scholarship which are demand driven and funds are released to 
implementing agencies based on the proposals received from 
States/UTs/Institutions/NGOs, individuals etc. The specific reasons for the slow 
trend/under-utilization of expenditure seen in preceding financial years are: 
 Skill Training was undergoing a rigorous overhaul to enable streamlining 
of the process, check financial irregularities by Empanelled Training 
Partners (ETPs) and to ensure a robust and secure fund flow mechanism. 
This caused some delay in release of funds to ETPs.  
 UCs from State Governments was not received on time under SIPDA 
Scheme. 
 Non-receipt of adequate proposals under NER categories of various 
schemes”. 
2.9 The Secretary, DEPwD during the course of evidence admitted as under:- 
“Sir, very broadly, you are right, every year we end up not spending the BE 
and even the RE. But in the last two years, we were also stuck with a lot of 
changes in the expenditure system, like the CNA and TSA were introduced. 
So that caused delays and a lot of bunching of expenditure in March. This 

12 
 
year, we will not have too much bunching. We have already spent 70 per 
cent. Hopefully, by February end, we should touch 80 percent. The 
bunching in March will not be 40 per cent; it will be just 20 or 22 percent” 
2.10 The Committee were however assured by the Department that the Schemes of 
the Department had now been on-boarded on TSA Model and funds were being 
released to all the agencies and therefore, it is expected that they will be able to fully 
utilize the entire RE before the end of the current financial year. 
2.11 The representative of the Department also expressed an optimistic outlook and 
stated as under before the Committee:- 
“***the budget estimate over the last three years have remained constant at 
around ₹ 1225 crore But the good news is that we have got a slight 
enhancement in 2025-26. That is a reflection of the efforts being made by this 
Department. I will give a short background to this. The ADIP scheme, which is a 
flagship scheme of the Department, we were given a budget allocation of ₹315 
crore in 2024-25. But because we made very good progress in this scheme, and 
we were running short of funds because we had a large number of proposals in 
the pipeline and we were ready to do so much more, we moved the Department 
of Expenditure, requesting for more funds. We wanted ₹ 500 crore enhancement 
over what had been approved by the Expenditure Finance Committee for five 
years. So, we made a very strong pitch to the Ministry of Finance, as a result of 
which we got an enhancement at the RE stage. It is a rare thing for a 
Department to get more money at RE stage. Normally, we see a cut. But 
because we did very good work, the enhancement was around ₹35 crores, and 
that was within the 20 percent which the DOE can do at its level. As a result of 
that, we have a higher allocation under this ADIP scheme. As far as the 
expenditure progress is concerned, as on date, we have spent ₹811 crore, which 
works out to around 68 percent. But we have proposals in the pipeline and we 
have finances already concurred. So, taking that into account, we are at 73 per 
cent as on date, which I think is not a very bad situation given that we have a 
little more than a month to go”. 

13 
 
2.12 On being asked what corrective measures have been adopted to improve fund 
utilization, the Department has informed as under:- 
“The Department has taken up the matter with IAs/States/NGOs and 
conducts regular meetings for sensitizing them on the need for providing 
UCs and complete documents so that funds under various schemes are 
released on time”. 
2.13 To a query on the specific corrective measures or new initiatives undertaken by 
the  Department in response to  the challenges, inefficiencies, and bottlenecks observed 
in the previous year’s performance, the Committee were informed as under :- 
“The Schemes of the Department are implemented through various portals 
namely ARJUN Portal, PM-DAKSH Portal, NSP Portal, E-anudaan Portal for NGOs 
and UDID Portal for proper implementation and monitoring the schemes so that 
benefits of the schemes reach the intended beneficiaries in a transparent 
manner. 
ARJUN Portal has been developed under ADIP Scheme. All ADIP beneficiaries 
data since, 2014 has been uploaded on the portal. It helps in checking duplicity 
of beneficiaries, online submission of beneficiaries data, and reduced time lag in 
providing devices and digitization of records of beneficiary data. It also facilitates 
beneficiaries to apply for aids and appliances through online mode. 
PM-DAKSH Portal- The Skill Training Scheme is being monitored through PM-
DAKSH Portal. This portal is a one-stop digital destination for PwDs, who need 
skilling and employment, and for Training organizations and Employers/job 
aggregators of PwDs. Monitoring the bio-metric attendance of PwDs, getting skill 
training from NGOs are also being done through this portal. 
NSP Portal- The Scholarship Schemes are being monitored/implemented through 
NSP Portal. 
E-anudaan Portal for NGOs- The Department has developed centralized on-line 
application software with the assistance of National Informatics Centre

 The main objective of the Scheme is to provide grants-in-aid to the various 
implementing agencies (National Institutes/ Composite Regional Centres /Artificial 
Limbs Manufacturing Corporation of India (ALIMCO)/District Disability Rehabilitation 
Centres/State Handicapped Development Corporations/other local bodies/ NGOs)   to

of Persons with Disabilities through reducing the effects of disabilities and at the same 
time enhancing their economic potential.  Assistive devices are given to Persons with 
Disabilities with the aim of improving their independent functioning, and arresting the 
extent of disability and occurrence of secondary disability. The Scheme also envisages 
conduct of corrective surgeries, whenever required, before providing an assistive 
device. The Scheme has been revised/modified w.e.f  26.09.2024.

DEENDAYAL DIVYANGJAN  REHABILITATION SCHEME (DDRS) 
  Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme is a Central Sector scheme of the 
Department to provide grant-in-aid to non-governmental organizations (NGOs) for 
projects relating to rehabilitation of persons with disabilities aimed at enabling them to 
reach and maintain their optimal, physical, sensory, intellectual, psychiatric or socio-
functional levels. The Scheme is being implemented since 1999 with the objective of 
ensuring effective implementation of the Right of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. 
Activities/components admissible for grant under the scheme to the organisations 
include honorarium to staff, transportation of beneficiaries, stipend for 
beneficiaries/hostel maintenance, cost of raw materials and contingencies to meet office 
expenses, electricity, water charges and rent. 
4.2      The Grant-in-aid is provided to Implementing agencies (IAs) which are 
registered with NGO- Darpan Portal of NITI Aayog and have obtained unique ID of NITI 
Aayog. The Grant-in-Aid (GIA) is provided through e-Anudaan portal of the Department. 
All financial assistance is provided to the Implementing agencies through Public 
Financial Management System (PFMS). 
 4.3 The eligibility conditions for Grants under DDRS are as under:- 
Organizations registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 (XXI of 1860), or 
any relevant Act of the State/Union Territory; or a Trust registered under the Indian 
Trust Act, 1882 or any other similar Act for the time being in force; or a Not-for-Profit 
company registered under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 or any relevant Act for 
the time being in force.    
a. Registration should have been in force for at least 2 years at the time of applying 
for grant under this scheme. 
b.  Must be registered in the NITI Aayog portal (NGO-Darpan) portal and obtain 
Unique  ID of NGO-Darpan before applying for Grant under the scheme. 
c. Must be registered either under erstwhile Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995 or 
Rights   of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.

(i) It should have a managing body with its powers, duties and responsibilities

(iii) It should not be run for profit to any individual or a body of individuals.

5.24 National Action Plan for Skill Training under SIPDA 
The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities launched 
the National Action Plan for Skill Development of Persons with Disabilities (NAP-
SDP) in March 2015 with the objective to enhance skills of Persons with 
Disabilities, enable them to have gainful employment/ become self-employed and 
to make them self-reliant and independent members of society. Training under 
the NAP-SDP scheme is being provided through various Government and Non-
Government Organizations. The scheme is being implemented on the common 
norms prescribed by the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship 
(MSDE). PwDs with at least 40per cent disability in the age group between the 
age of 15-59 years are eligible to avail skill training programme. This is being 
implemented through PM-DAKSH-DEPwD Portal which is providing opportunity to 
PwDs to apply for the skill training as per their choice of training partner, place 
of training centre and desired job roles. Any of PwDs can register himself on the 
basis of valid UDID Number. Thereafter, PwD is eligible to check the course and 
training centers as per his disability and locality. PwD also can enroll in area of 
interest on PM-DAKSH-DEPwD portal.  
NAP-SDP Scheme is a Central Sector Scheme, it is a demand driven scheme. 
Funds under the scheme are released to empanelled organizations from across 
the Country on the basis of their demands/proposals.  Since 2022, an amount of 
Rs.23.54 crore has been released for conducting skill training under NAP-SDP as 
till date.  
 
5.25 There are two modules on PM DAKSH-DEPwD portal to provide skill training and 
employment to PwDs:- 
 
a. DivyangjanKaushalVikas: The National Action Plan for Skill Development of 
PwDs (NAP-SDP) is being fully implemented through the PM DAKSH- DEPwD 
portal. The portal provides end-to-end facilities for PwDs interested in skill 
training. The facilities include Unique Disability Identity (UDID) based seamless 
registration, enrolling in skill training out of a list of 300+ courses, e-learning 

51 
 
digital resources, exploring ETPs in their state/District providing skill training, 
access to study materials, as well as detailed information on the trainers/ETPs. 
 
b. DivyangjanRozgarSetu: For employment of PwDs, the Department has 
developed Divyagnjan Rogar Setu, which is a unique digital platform dedicated to 
providing information about the vacancies for PwDs in various private 
organizations. The platform is a bridge between PwDs and employers hiring 
PwDs. The platform provides geo-tagged-based information on 
employment/earning opportunities within private companies as well as details 
about PwDs from all over India. Further, the Department has also signed 20 
MoUs with various private sector companies like Amazon, Youth4Jobs, Atypical 
Advantage, Volvo, Zomato among others that employ PwDs.

5.34 Unique Disability ID Project (UDID) 
 
The UDID Card or Unique Disability Identity card is an initiative by 
Government of India with a view of creating a National Database for PwDs and 
to issue a Unique Disability Identity Card to each person with disabilities. The 
Department has instituted and online portal (www.swavalambancard.gov.in) 
where the medical authorities notified by the respective State Government have 
been on-boarded for issuance of disability certificate/Unique Disability Identity 
Card (UDID) to Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). The disability certificate/UDID 
card is issued by the notified medical authority of State/UT. 
 
The main objectives of UDID are :   
a. To create a National Database for PwDs by identifying and issuing a 
Unique Disability ID Card to every Person with Disability through the web-
portal. 
b. Uniformity in the form of disability certificate and UDID card across the 
country. 
c. Issue Disability Certificates not only to those who are still uncovered but 
also to persons with new types of disabilities under the RPwD Act 2016 
d. Encourage transparency, efficiency and ease of delivering government 
benefits to PwDs pertaining to  several Schemes, include Scholarship, Skill 
training, Inclusive Education, Rehabilitation, Pension, 

56 
 
e. Tracking of physical and financial progress of benefit delivery at all levels 
of hierarchy of implementation – from Village, Block, District, State and 
National levels. 
 
5.35 When asked about the benefits of UDID, the Committee have been informed as 
under:  
“Access to government schemes and benefits: The UDID card is 
recognized as a proof of disability by the government of India. This means 
that PwDs can use their UDID card to access various government schemes 
and benefits such as financial assistance, healthcare services, education, 
and employment. 
 
Easy identification: The UDID card provides a unique identification 
number to each PwD. This helps in easy identification of the person with 
disability and ensures that they receive the right kind of assistance and 
support. 
 
Accessibility: The UDID card contains information about the type and level 
of disability of the person. This information can be used to make public 
places and transportation more accessible for PwDs”.  
5.36 Asked to explain the mechanism of this scheme, the Department has submitted 
as under:- 
“The Department has instituted an online portal (www.swavalambancard.gov.in) 
where the medical authorities notified by the respective State Government have 
been on-boarded for issuance of disability certificate/Unique Disability Identity 
Card (UDID) to Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). Under Unique Disability Id 
project, disability certificates and UDID cards are issued to persons with 
disabilities mentioned in the RPwD Act, 2016. The persons in rural and far flung 
areas who don’t have internet coverage, may visit any CSC Centre for applying 
for Disability Certificate and UDID Card.   
At central level, the UDID Project is managed by the Department of 
Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (DEPwD), Ministry of Social Justice 
and Empowerment in the country. At state level, UDID project is managed by the 
State Social Welfare Departments in consultation with the State Health 
Departments. Also there is provision to appoint one state coordinator in each 
State/UT for smooth implementation of the UDID project in the country. The 
Grant is Aid for remuneration of these state coordinators is provided by the 
DEPwD”.

While no targets are set for this project, about 1.18 crore UDID has been 
generated so far through Swavalamban portal.

CHAPTER VI 
 
SCHOLARSHIPS  FOR STUDENTS WITH DISABILITIES 
 
Section 31 (1) & (2) of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 mandates 
that every child with benchmark disability between the age of six to eighteen years shall 
have the right to free education in a neighborhood school, or in a special school, of his 
choice and the appropriate Government and local authorities shall ensure their access 
to free education in an appropriate environment till he attains the age of eighteen 
years. 
 To fulfill this mandate, the DEPwD is implementing Umbrella Scheme 
‘Scholarships for Students with Disabilities’ comprising following six components with 
the objective to empower students with disabilities to study further to earn their 
livelihood and to find a dignified place in the society - 
(i) Pre-matric Scholarship (For Classes IX  and X) 
(ii) Post-matric Scholarship (For Class XI to Post-Graduate Degree/Diploma 
(iii) Top Class Education (For Graduate and Post-Graduate Degree/Diploma in 
notified institutes of excellence in education) 
(iv) National Overseas Scholarship (For Master’s Degree/Ph.D. in 
Institutions/Colleges/Universities abroad) 
(v) National Fellowship for PwDs (For M. Phil and Ph.D. in Indian Universities)  
(vi) Free Coaching schemes (For recruitment examinations for Group A, B and C 
posts and entrance examinations for admission to various technical and 
professional courses) 
6.2 The Salient Features of various Scholarship schemes are as below:- 
(i) 50per cent of the total scholarship slots available each year in Pre-matric, 
Post-matric and Top Class Education and 30 percent slots in National 
Overseas Scholarship and Free Coaching Scholarship Scheme are reserved 
for girl candidates.  
(ii) Various assistance under the Scholarship Schemes include Maintenance 
Allowance, Tuition fees, Book allowance, Disability allowance among other 
financial assistance as applicable under various components.  

64 
 
(iii) Top Class Education also provides for Reimbursement of expenses for 
purchase of a computer/ laptop and requisite aids and assistive devices.  
(iv) National Overseas Scholarship also provides contingency and equipment 
allowance. Disbursement of scholarship and reimbursement of 
miscellaneous expenses is done by M/o External Affairs (MEA) through 
Indian Mission abroad. 
(v) National Fellowship scheme provides for award of fellowships to 200 
Fellows each year; 75per cent scholars based on National Eligibility Test-
Junior Research Fellowship (NET-JRF) of UGC and remaining 25per cent 
scholars based on UGC-Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (UGC-
CSIR) NET-JRF joint test. 
(vi) Free Coaching Scheme is implemented by the Department through National 
Institutes/ Composite Regional Centers and also through Central 
Universities/Institutes, State Universities under the purview of Central 
Government and State Governments and any other government institutes 
working in the field of education/special education.  
6.3 The General Conditions of Eligibility for Scholarship Scheme are as under: 
(i) The scholarships are open only to Indian nationals.  
(ii) All the components of scholarships are open to students with benchmark 
disability i.e. 40per cent or more disability as defined in ‘Rights of Persons 
with Disabilities Act, 2016. 
6.4 Further, the applicants should have/possess:       
a) Aadhaar based biometric authentication. 
b) UDID/UDID enrolment with valid certificate of disability issued by the 
competent authority 
c) Not more than two children with disabilities of the same parents will be 
entitled to receive benefits of the scheme. In case the second child is a twin, 
the scholarship under the schemes will be admissible to the twin. 
d) Scholarship for studying in any class will be available for only one year.  If 
a student has to repeat a class, she/he would not get scholarship for that class 
for a second (or a subsequent) year.   
e) A scholarship holder under this scheme will not avail any other 
scholarship/stipend. No scholarship will be paid to the students under this 
scheme from the date he/she accepts any other scholarship/stipend.

Centre for Disability Sports  
Centre for Disability Sports now known as Atal Bihari Vajpayee Training Centre for 
Disability Sports (ABV-TCDS), Gwalior is an Autonomous body established by the 
Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (Divyangjan), Ministry of 
Social Justice & Empowerment, Govt. of India. It is a premier facility dedicated to the 
development and promotion of para-sports in India and aims to provide world-class 
training, rehabilitation, and competition opportunities for athletes with disabilities. The 
main objectives of the centre are as under  
(i) To establish a state-of-the-art international level Centre of Excellence for sports 
for Divyang sports persons (PwDs) with full accessibility as per norms. 
(ii) To create specialized sports infrastructure so that the para-sports persons can 
undergo rigorous and specialized training in the Centre. 
(iii) To ensure the participation of Divyangjan in sports activities in greater numbers 
and enable them to effectively compete in international events. 
(iv) To help build confidence and develop a sense of belonging in Divyangjan to 
facilitate their integration into Society.

world to persons with disabilities to enable them to effectively compete and win medals 
in Paralympics, Deaflympics, Special Olympics and other International events for Sports

Basketball, Table Tennis, Sitting Volleyball, Judo, Taekwondo, Wheelchair Fencing,

NATIONAL UNIVERSITY FOR DIVYANGJAN 
In the budget announcement of Hon’ble Finance Minister in FY-2015-16, 
upgradation of National Institute of Speech and Hearing (NISH) into a National 
University of Disability Studies and Rehabilitation Sciences was included. The EFC in its 
meeting held on 30th March 2016 recommended the proposal for setting up of 
University at cost of ₹382 crore. Subsequently, a Cabinet Note was circulated on 
30.06.2017, which was returned by the Cabinet Secretariat in July 2018 for review. The 
proposal was reviewed and it was decided in the Ministry to set up the University in 
Kamrup district, Assam. 
8.2 Regarding the present statusof the University, it has been informed that:- 
“The Department engaged EdCIL (India) Limited, NOIDA, to prepare a 
Detailed Project Report (DPR) for establishing the National University of Disability 
Studies and Rehabilitation Sciences in Kamrup, Assam. The final DPR has been 
submitted in March 2023, and a Public Investment Board (PIB) note was 
prepared and circulated to stakeholder ministries/departments for comments in 
June 2023. The Department of Expenditure (DoE), vide OM dated 01.11.2023, 
11.12.2023 & 10.07.2024, had made some comments/observations regarding the 
establishment of NUDSRS and given the project cost it was advised to obtain 
Delegated Investment Board (DIB) approval for the project This Department is in 
process of compliance of DoE comments regarding the NUDSRS and the proposal 
is currently under process for appraisal of DIB note. 
A three-member team comprising officials from DEPwD and CRC Guwahati
, along with the Kamrup District Administration, conducted a site visit in Novemb
er 2024 to identify the allocated land. In this regard, a joint survey report from t
he District Commissioner, Kamrup, has been received.  
Upon receipt of the Joint Survey Report from the District Commissioner, 
Kamrup, it was confirmed that the initially allocated 150 Bighas of land at 
Bondapara, BekeliMouza, included 126 Bighas within the Khatajuli Reserve Forest 
and only 24 Bighas as Government Khas land. Given the non-availability of 
alternative land under Boko Revenue Circle, a D.O. letter dated 31.12.2024 was 
issued by the Hon’ble Minister of Social Justice & Empowerment (HMSJE) to the 
Hon’ble Chief Minister of Assam, seeking intervention either for forest land 
clearance or allocation of an alternate 50-acre land suitable for the university. 

77 
 
In response, the Hon’ble Chief Minister of Assam through a D.O. letter 
dated 03.02.2025 conveyed that an alternate land under Chaygaon Revenue 
Circle, Kamrup District, has been identified for the purpose. Accordingly, the 
Department is in the process of deputing a team of officials to visit Kamrup, 
Assam, to evaluate the suitability of the proposed site before proceeding with the 
necessary land allotment formalities. Further action will be initiated based on the 
findings of the site visit”. 
8.3 The Committee emphasize the urgent need to expedite the 
establishment of the National University of Disability Studies and 
Rehabilitation Sciences in Kamrup, Assam, given the prolonged delays since 
its announcement in 2015-16. Recognizing the critical role of the university 
in advancing education, research, and rehabilitation for Persons with 
Disabilities, the Committee urge the Department to swiftly resolve land 
allocation issues, ensure timely compliance with the Department of 
Expenditure’s observations, and secure necessary approvals without further 
delay.  The Committee recommend that a clear implementation roadmap 
with defined timelines be formulated to ensure the project progress 
efficiently, enabling the university to become operational at the earliest.

APPENDIX 
STATEMENT OF OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS 
 
Sl.No Para No. Observations/ Recommendations 
1. 1.15 The Committee appreciate the proactive initiatives taken by various 
State Governments in establishing dedicated Department for disability 
affairs and significantly increasing budgetary allocations. They also 
acknowledge the innovative approach of community-based 
interventions, such as Karnataka’s village-level disability workers and 
the nationwide SugamyaYatra initiative, which fosters grassroots 
accessibility improvements. To build on this momentum, the 
Committee recommend that the Department actively collaborate with 
States to institutionalize similar models across the country, ensuring 
sustainable and community-driven disability inclusion. Additionally, a 
structured framework should be developed to document and monitor 
accessibility improvements under SugamyaYatra, enabling the 
replication of best practices nationwide. 
2. 2.15 From a thorough examination of the Demands for Grants (DFG) over 
the past three years, the Committee observe a persistent trend of 
underutilization of funds across key schemes such as ADIP, SIPDA, 
DDRS, and Scholarships, with actual expenditures consistently falling 
short of Revised Estimates (RE) and Budget Estimates (BE). The BE 
allocations were invariably reduced at the RE stage in 2022-23, 2023-
24, and 2024-25, yet the Department was unable to fully utilize even 
the reduced funds. The Department utilized 97.38per cent of RE in 
2022-23 and 93.39per cent in 2023-24. However, as of 03.03.2025, 
only ₹892.68 crore of ₹ 1167.27 crore (76.48per cent) has been spent. 
This shortfall was primarily due to disruptions from the introduction of 
the CNA and TSA models. Delays in receiving Utilization Certificates 
(UCs) from State Governments under SIPDA and a lack of adequate 
proposals from North-Eastern States further hindered funds 
utilization. While the Committee were assured by the Department’s 
representatives that the remaining 2024-25 funds would be fully 
utilized by 31.03.2025, the Committee strongly urge the Department 
to adopt targeted remedial measures and strategic interventions to 
ensure efficient fund utilization in 2025-26. Proactive engagement 
with State Governments, NGOs, and implementing agencies is 

84 
 
essential to streamline proposal submissions and disbursement 
process, particularly for skill training programs and capital 
expenditure. Furthermore, given the challenges in meeting fixed 
targets for schemes like SIPDA and DDRS, the Department should 
reassess target-setting methodologies to ensure that they are 
realistic, flexible, and aligned with performance trends. The 
Committee recommend decisive action to improve fund utilization and 
program execution in the remaining year of the 2024-26 financial 
cycle, ensuring that allocated resources effectively advance the 
welfare of persons with disabilities and that the Department 
successfully meets its commitments under various schemes. 
3. 3.20 The Committee recognize that the Assistance to Persons with 
Disabilities for Purchase/Fitting of Aids/Appliances (ADIP) Scheme is 
an important initiative aimed at enhancing mobility, independence, 
and economic empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). The 
Committee note that fund allocation and utilization under ADIP have 
shown a steady increase, with actual expenditure surpassing initial 
Budget Estimates (BE) in multiple years. In 2022-23, the BE was 
₹235.00 crore, which was revised to ₹230.00 crore, yet actual 
expenditure exceeded expectations at ₹242.29 crore. Similarly, in 
2023-24, the BE of ₹245.00 crore was significantly revised to ₹305.00 
crore, with actual spending at ₹290.60 crore. In 2024-25, the BE was 
further enhanced to ₹315.00 crore, and the Revised Estimate (RE) 
increased to ₹350.00 crore, with ₹346.27 crore utilized as of 
03.03.2025. The Committee also note that increased financial 
allocations has been ably matched by the physical 
performance/targets set under the Scheme. In 2022-23 and 2023-24, 
the target was exceeded, benefiting 2.89 lakh and 3.15 lakh 
beneficiaries, respectively. However, in 2024-25, only 2.10 lakh 
beneficiaries were assisted as of 14.02.2025, accounting for 67per 
cent of the target of 3.15 lakh beneficiaries. While the Department has 
cited the imposition of the Model Code of Conduct during General 
Elections and the transition to the TSA Model-I as reasons for the 
slower pace of the expenditure, the Committee urge the Department 
to learn from these experiences and establish contingency plans to 
ensure uninterrupted service delivery in the future. The Committee 
however express concern over the reduction of the physical targets for 

85 
 
2025-26 to 2.05 lakh beneficiaries and recommend that the 
Department continuously assess demand trends and explore 
possibilities for expanding beneficiary coverage. 
4. 3.21 The Committee appreciate the revisions in the ADIP Scheme, effective 
26.09.2024, which aim to enhance inclusivity and provide greater 
financial relief to Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). The modifications 
include higher income eligibility ceilings, increased financial support 
for assistive devices, and expanded coverage for advanced mobility 
aids and corrective surgeries. The enhancements in subsidies for 
motorized tricycles, wheelchairs, and high-end prostheses, along with 
greater financial assistance for cochlear implants and corrective 
surgeries, are expected to significantly improve accessibility and 
independence for PwDs. By reducing the financial burden on 
beneficiaries and ensuring access to better-quality assistive devices, 
these changes will promote greater social and economic inclusion. The 
Committee, however, emphasize the need for periodic impact 
assessments to evaluate whether these modifications are effectively 
improving the quality of life for PwDs, ensuring that the intended 
benefits reach the maximum number of eligible individuals. 
5. 3.22 The Committee appreciate the establishment of Pradhan Mantri 
Divyasha Kendras (PMDKs), which provide walk-in access to assistive 
aids and have benefitted 35,919 PwDs as of 2024-25. These centers 
play a crucial role in ensuring last-mile service delivery, and the 
Committee recommend their expansion to underserved regions, 
particularly in remote and tribal areas. The removal of the 
requirement for State Government recommendations in NGO 
proposals is also a welcome step toward reducing bureaucratic delays 
and ensuring the timely release of funds. However, the Committee 
stress the importance of strict monitoring mechanisms to prevent 
misuse of funds and maintain transparency in implementation. 
6. 3.23 The Committee commend the inclusion of Motorized Tricycles with Li-
Iron-Phosphate batteries and Folding Joystick Wheelchairs in the 
ADIP Scheme, with a subsidy of up to ₹50,000, as a significant step 
towards improving mobility and independence for PwDs. These 
modern assistive devices, along with enhanced financial support for 
high-end prostheses and cochlear implants, will broaden accessibility, 
lower maintenance costs, and provide financial relief to beneficiaries 

86 
 
and their families. The Committee strongly recommend that the 
Department undertake extensive outreach efforts to ensure that 
maximum eligible PwDs can avail these benefits and urge 
collaboration with research institutions and industry partners to 
continuously enhance and upgrade assistive devices, making them 
more cost-effective, durable, and efficient. 
7. 3.24 The Committee appreciate the Department’s efforts in enhancing 
accessibility and mobility for the elderly by distributing over 18,000 
assistive devices during the Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj under the 
Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (RVY). Such initiatives play a vital role in 
reaching vulnerable populations in large public gatherings and 
ensuring their comfort and dignity. To further strengthen these 
efforts, the Committee recommend that similar outreach programs be 
integrated into other major national and regional events. Additionally, 
a structured mechanism for pre-event assessment and beneficiary 
registration should be developed to ensure the distribution of need-
specific assistive devices, maximizing the impact of such initiatives. 
8. 4.17 The Committee recognize the crucial role of DDRS in providing 
rehabilitation services to Persons with Disabilities (PwDs) through 
NGOs and implementing agencies. An analysis of fund utilization 
trends over the past three years reveals significant fluctuations, with 
2022-23 and 2023-24 recording high fund utilization, while in 2024-
25, actual expenditure stood at ₹87.95 crore as of 03.03.2025, despite 
a Budget Estimate (BE) of ₹165 crore. The Committee note that the 
transition from the CNA Model to TSA Hybrid Model 1-A led to 
procedural delays in fund disbursement, impacting the pace of 
implementation. Additionally, as grants were previously processed on 
a reimbursement basis, NGOs were required to submit audited 
accounts by June 30 of the next financial year, further slowing down 
fund releases. However, with the revised DDRS guidelines effective 
from 01.10.2024, which allow Grant-in-Aid on an advance-cum-
reimbursement basis, the Committee expect that fund flow will 
improve and implementation delays will be minimized. The Committee 
recommend that the Department ensure timely release of advances, 
particularly to NGOs with strong compliance records, while also 
strengthening the capacity of implementing agencies to meet 
documentation requirements on time, ensuring efficient utilization of 

87 
 
allocated funds. The Committee also urge the Department to ensure a 
smoother transition in funding mechanisms by streamlining financial 
procedures and proactively addressing administrative bottlenecks to 
avoid such shortfalls in future fiscal years. 
9. 4.18 The Committee note that physical targets under DDRS have witnessed 
a downward revision since 2024-25, with the target reduced from 
40,000 in 2023-24 to 32,000 in 2024-25 and 2025-26. The 
Department has attributed this to the number of projects supported 
remaining largely the same over the years, with new proposals being 
considered only after the release of funds to organizations. The 
Committee emphasize that rehabilitation services for PwDs should not 
be constrained due to procedural delays in fund disbursal and 
recommend that the Department take proactive steps to expedite 
approvals for new projects. The Committee urge the Department to 
explore expanding the scope of DDRS to cover more PwDs by 
identifying underserved areas where rehabilitation services are limited 
or absent. 
10. 4.19 The Committee appreciate the Department’s efforts in introducing 
monitoring mechanisms, including video inspections of NGOs and 
time-bound forwarding of proposals under the revised DDRS. The 
provision for automatic transmission of pending proposals from the 
district levels to the State Government and further to the DEPwD if no 
action is taken within a month of submission is a commendable 
measure aimed to streamline the approval process and minimize 
administrative delays.   However, the Committee recommend strict 
enforcement of these provisions to ensure that no proposal remains 
pending for an unreasonable duration. Further, the Committee support 
the decision that organizations running both DDRS and National Trust 
schemes should choose only one to avoid duplication of services, 
ensuring that funds reach the maximum number of beneficiaries. 
11. 4.20 The Committee note that DDRCs function as decentralized 
rehabilitation units, providing early identification and intervention, 
assistive device distribution, therapy services, and skill development 
for PwDs. While DDRCs were initially planned for 325 districts, only 
269 DDRCs were set up, and currently, only 74 are functional. The 
Committee express concern over the low operational count of DDRCs 
and recommend that the Department prioritize the revival and 

88 
 
expansion of DDRCs in districts lacking rehabilitation facilities. The 
Committee also welcome the shift to online applications for Grant-in-
Aid (GIA) for DDRCs through the e-Anudaan Portal from 2024-25, 
which is expected to simplify and expedite the application process. 
12. 5.13 The Committee observe significant inconsistencies in the Budget 
Estimates (BE), Revised Estimates (RE), and actual fund utilization 
under SIPDA over the past three years. Frequent downward revisions 
at the RE stage and low actual expenditure despite available 
allocations indicate systemic inefficiencies in fund absorption. In 
2022-23, the BE was ₹240.39 crore, which was reduced by 58.4 per 
cent at the RE stage to ₹100 crore, with actual expenditure at just 
₹65.59 crore. Similarly, in 2023-24, the BE of ₹150 crore was cut to 
₹67 crore, yet actual expenditure exceeded the RE at ₹76.79 crore 
(114.7 percent of RE). In 2024-25, despite an RE of ₹111 crore, only 
₹43.90 crore (39.5 percent of RE) had been utilized as of 03.03.2025. 
These fluctuations indicate inconsistent fund planning and delays in 
fund utilization, leading to missed opportunities for enhancing 
accessibility and disability-related initiatives. The Committee strongly 
recommend that the Department strengthen its financial planning 
mechanisms by ensuring that BE allocations are realistic, demand-
driven, and reflective of actual implementation capacity.  
13. 5.14 In addition to financial inconsistencies, the Committee note a 
significant shortfall in meeting physical targets under SIPDA. In 2022-
23, against a target of 44,520 beneficiaries, 44,162 were assisted, 
reflecting 99.2per cent achievement. However, in 2023-24, the 
achievement dropped to 29,374 beneficiaries against the same target 
(65.9per cent), and as of 16.01.2025, only 11,933 beneficiaries 
(27.4per cent) had been covered out of the 43,520 target for 2024-25. 
This indicates delays in execution and ineffective implementation 
strategies. The Committee recommend that the Department enhance 
coordination with State Governments and implementing agencies to 
ensure faster approval and processing of applications, preventing 
recurring lapses in achieving physical targets. Furthermore, a robust 
monitoring framework should be instituted to track real-time 
progress, ensuring better alignment between financial allocations and 
physical achievements. The Department must identify and address 
systemic bottlenecks to ensure that SIPDA meets its intended goals of 

89 
 
accessibility, empowerment and inclusion of Persons with Disabilities. 
14. 5.15 The Committee also find that persistent challenges such as delays in 
fund disbursement, pending utilization certificates, and incomplete 
proposals have impacted the effective implementation of SIPDA. Given 
that SIPDA is the flagship scheme for PwDs, the Committee strongly 
recommend that the Department strengthen its monitoring and 
coordination mechanisms by ensuring regular follow-ups with State 
Governments and implementing agencies. The Central Project 
Monitoring Unit (CPMU) should be further empowered to enable real-
time tracking of fund utilization, proposal approvals, and physical 
progress under various sub-schemes .Furthermore, timely clearance of 
compliance documents such as Utilization Certificates (UCs) and 
pending proposals should be ensured to prevent underutilization and 
last-minute fund surrender. 
15. 5.23 The Committee note with appreciation the initiatives undertaken 
under the Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan) to 
enhance accessibility in built environments, transportation, and 
information & communication technology (ICT).However, with the 
campaign now subsumed under the “Creation of Barrier-Free 
Environment (CBFE)” sub-scheme under SIPDA, the Committee 
express concern over the absence of a dedicated budgetary allocation 
for CBFE and the continued financial support required for sustained 
accessibility initiatives. The Committee strongly recommend that the 
Department create a structured financial framework within SIPDA, 
ensuring that accessibility projects receive adequate funding without 
dependence on overall SIPDA allocations. Additionally, a long-term 
accessibility roadmap should be developed to maintain momentum 
and expand initiatives to underserved regions. 
16. 5.31 The Committee observe that the National Action Plan for Skill 
Development of PwDs (NAP-SDP) has consistently underperformed in 
meeting its physical targets, with achievements ranging from only 
12.62 per cent in 2022-23 to 32.25 per cent in 2024-25 (as of January 
2025). Given the demand-driven nature of the scheme, the Committee 
recommend that the Department strengthen its mobilization efforts, 
improve coordination with training partners, and ensure timely 
disbursal of funds. Further, efforts should be made to optimize the 
utilization of the budget allocation by addressing administrative 

90 
 
bottlenecks, improving the training infrastructure, and ensuring a 
greater alignment of courses with market demand to enhance 
employment prospects for PwDs. 
17. 5.32 The Committee however appreciate the Department's efforts in 
signing MoUs with private firms and job aggregators like Amazon, 
Youth4Jobs, and Zomato to facilitate employment opportunities for 
trained PwDs. However, with only 28,000 PwDs employed till date, the 
Committee recommend a robust strategy to strengthen employment 
linkages, including expanding industry partnerships, promoting 
entrepreneurship support programs, and ensuring sector-specific skill 
training tailored to high-demand job markets. A structured tracking 
mechanism should also be put in place to monitor the employment 
status of trained PwDs. 
18. 5.33 Noting the challenges in data tracking and monitoring of training and 
placements, the Committee welcome the introduction of the PM-
DAKSH-DEPwD Portal. However, the Committee recommend that the 
Department ensure its full operationalization with real-time data 
tracking, performance audits of training partners, and impact 
assessments of training programs. Further, a feedback mechanism 
should be established where trained PwDs can report challenges faced 
in employment, enabling timely interventions and policy adjustments 
to enhance the scheme’s effectiveness. 
19. 5.39 The Committee note that while the UDID project has successfully 
generated approximately 1.18 crore cards, its implementation 
continues to face challenges due to a shortage of medical 
professionals, inadequate hospital infrastructure, and a large backlog 
of pending applications at hospitals. To address these issues, the 
Committee recommend that the Department work in close 
coordination with State Governments to strengthen medical 
infrastructure, expedite the inclusion of private medical professionals 
in the certification process, and ensure the availability of well-
equipped facilities for disability assessment. Additionally, the 
Department should facilitate specialized training programs for medical 
authorities to improve efficiency in issuing disability certificates and 
reduce delays. 
20. 5.40 Recognizing the UDID’s critical role in enabling PwDs to access 

91 
 
various government benefits and services, the Committee underscore 
the importance of ensuring full utilization of the allocated budget for 
2025-26. This requires robust monitoring of fund disbursement, 
proactive engagement with State and UT authorities, and the timely 
clearance of pending UDID applications. A structured and time-bound 
approach should be adopted to enhance accessibility and ensure that 
all eligible PwDs receive their UDID cards without unnecessary delays. 
21. 5.41 The Committee further observe that PwDs in rural and remote areas 
face difficulties in accessing the UDID system due to limited internet 
coverage and therefore urge the Department to strengthen the role of 
Common Service Centres (CSCs) and explore the deployment of 
mobile service units for on-site registration and certification. The 
Committee would also like to recommend that awareness campaigns 
be conducted in collaboration with State Governments to ensure that 
all eligible PwDs are aware of the benefits of UDID and the process for 
obtaining it. 
22. 5.47 The Committee observe that the Scheme for Financial Assistance to 
Spinal Injury Centers (ASIC) is vital for providing treatment and 
rehabilitation to individuals with spinal cord injuries, especially those 
from economically weaker sections. However, with only eight State 
Spinal Injury Centers (SSICs) supported so far, the scheme's reach 
remains limited. To address this, the Committee recommend proactive 
engagement with State governments to expand SSICs in government 
hospitals and ensure better accessibility to specialized care. Given that 
ASIC operates under SIPDA without a separate budgetary allocation, 
the Department must prioritize its funding needs and streamline 
proposal approvals to ensure timely disbursal of grants. Strengthening 
monitoring mechanisms, expediting clearances, and raising awareness 
among stakeholders will be key to ensuring full utilization of funds and 
maximizing the scheme’s impact. 
23. 6.17 The Committee observe significant fluctuations in fund allocation, 
utilization, and target achievement under the Scholarship Scheme for 
Students with Disabilities. In 2022-23, the Revised Estimate (RE) was 
increased to ₹145.00 crore, with 97.9 per cent utilization (₹142.00 
crore). However, in 2024-25, the RE was sharply reduced by 43.9 per 
cent to ₹80.00 crore, and as of 03.03.2025, only ₹58.38 crore (72.9 
per cent of RE) had been utilized. Similarly, physical target 

92 
 
achievement dropped from 99.2per cent in 2022-23 to just 27.4 per 
cent in 2024-25(as of Jan,2025). This underutilization, despite high 
demand in previous years, indicates the need for better forecasting 
and planning. The Committee recommend that the Department adopt 
a more structured approach to fund allocation, ensuring that 
budgetary provisions align with actual demand. Additionally, efforts 
should be made to streamline the verification and disbursement 
process at the State Nodal Officer level to avoid delays and ensure 
timely financial assistance to beneficiaries. 
24. 6.18 Given the low application numbers in certain years, the Committee 
emphasize the need for a more robust awareness campaign. While 
steps have been taken, such as newspaper advertisements and 
communication with State Governments, a more proactive strategy is 
required. The Department should leverage digital platforms, 
collaborate with educational institutions, and engage disability rights 
organizations to maximize outreach. Special efforts must be made to 
target students in remote and rural areas who may be unaware of 
their eligibility for these scholarships. 
25. 6.19 To prevent recurring issues of low fund utilization, the Committee 
urges the Department to implement a real-time monitoring 
mechanism for tracking application processing and fund 
disbursement. Regular review meetings with State Nodal Officers 
should be mandated, and timelines should be fixed for application 
verification and fund release. Additionally, simplifying application 
procedures and integrating AI-based verification tools can 
significantly improve efficiency. The Committee stress that every 
effort must be made to ensure full utilization of the ₹145 crore 
allocated for 2025-26, benefiting the maximum number of eligible 
students with disabilities. 
26. 7.6 The Committee express satisfaction with the establishment and 
progress of the AtalBihari Vajpayee Training Centre for Disability 
Sports (ABV-TCDS), Gwalior, recognizing its vital role in promoting 
para-sports in India. The Centre’s state-of-the-art facilities, extensive 
sports infrastructure, and commitment to training Divyangjan athletes 
at par with international standards are commendable. The 
Committeeare pleased to note the Centre’s efforts in organizing 
national and State-level championships, talent identification 

93 
 
programs, and collaborations with reputed institutions and sports 
federations. While acknowledging the challenges in operationalizing 
the facility, the Committee are optimistic that with sustained efforts, 
ABV-TCDS will emerge as a premier institution, significantly enhancing 
India’s representation and success in international para-sporting 
events. 
27. 8.3 The Committee emphasize the urgent need to expedite the 
establishment of the National University of Disability Studies and 
Rehabilitation Sciences in Kamrup, Assam, given the prolonged delays 
since its announcement in 2015-16. Recognizing the critical role of the 
university in advancing education, research, and rehabilitation for 
Persons with Disabilities, the Committee urge the Department to 
swiftly resolve land allocation issues, ensure timely compliance with 
the Department of Expenditure’s observations, and secure necessary 
approvals without further delay.  The Committee recommend that a 
clear implementation roadmap with defined timelines be formulated 
to ensure the project progress efficiently, enabling the university to 
become operational at the earliest.
