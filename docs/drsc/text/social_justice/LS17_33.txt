Empowerment (2021-22) having been authorized by the Committee to present on 
their behalf this Thirty-Third  Report on 'Demands for Grants for the year 2022-23' 
pertaining to the Ministry of Minority Affairs.

Ministry of Minority Affairs which were laid on the Table of the House on 8th 
February, 2022. After obtaining their Budget related Documents and Explanatory

connection with the examination of the ‘Demands for Grants for 2022-23’.

CHAPTER – I 
 
INTRODUCTORY 
 
 
1.1  The Ministry of Minority Affairs was carved out of Ministry of Social Justice and 
Empowerment in January, 2006 with a vision to ensure a more focused approach towards 
issues relating to formulation of policies, schemes and programmes for the welfare of 
minority communities. The welfare of the minority includes six notified minority communities 
namely Jains, Parsis, Buddhists, Sikhs, Christians and Muslims. Since October, 2016, the 
mandate of the Ministry has been expanded to manage Haj Pilgrimage. The vision of the 
Ministry is to empower the Minority Communities and to create an enabling environment to 
strengthen the multi-social, multi-ethic, multi-cultural, multi-lingual and multi-religions 
character of our nation.  
1.2 The welfare and development schemes of the Ministry focus on poor and deprived 
sections of the minorities. Majority of Schemes have devised an eligibility criteria on 
economic basis to ensure that the benefit reaches poor and deprived sections. The 
educational Schemes cover scholarships for all levels, fellowships and interest subsidy to 
promote higher education and support for providing good quality coaching to enable 
minorities to prepare for Government and private jobs. 
1.3 The Ministry has strengthened and expanded its Scheme ‘’Seekho aur Kamao’’ in 
tune with ‘’Skill India Mission’’ and ‘’Make in India Mission’’ and has also implemented 
schemes namely USTTAD for preservation of traditional crafts/ Arts and ‘’Nai Manzil’’ to 
integrate educational bridge course with skills for economic empowerment of Minority 
Communities ‘’Nai Roshni’’ scheme provides leadership development to Minority women.

2.3 On being asked about the reasons due to which Budgetary / Revised Estimates 
for 2019-20, 2020-21 and 2021-22 could not be spent by the Ministry, the Ministry 
submitted in their written reply that : 
“Ministry is satisfied with its performance as during the year 2019-20 and 2020-
21, 95.85% and 99.84% respectively of the allocation was incurred by the 
Ministry. Ministry would have incurred much higher expenditure had the cut not 
been imposed by the Ministry of Finance at RE stage during the year 2020-21. 
As regards 2021-22, an expenditure of ₹2,342.23 crore has been incurred till 
17th Feb, 2022. The pace of expenditure remained low as the releases under 
the three Scholarship Schemes (which constitutes around 50% of the total RE 
allocation) are made mostly in the last quarter of the Financial Year when 
applications are received after two level authentication by the State 
Governments/UTs.”

PRADHANMANTRI JAN VIKAS KARYAKRAM (PMJVK) 
 
3.1 The Pradhan Mantri Jan Vikas Karyakram (PMJVK), erstwhile Multi Sectoral 
Development Programme (MsDP) launched in 2008 and restructured in June, 2013 is 
a  Centrally Sponsored Scheme(CSS), identified as Core of the Core Schemes  under 
National Development Agenda by the NITI Aayog. It is being implemented as the 
Pradhan Mantri Jan Vikas Karyakram (PMJVK) with effect from May, 2018. The 
objective of the Scheme is to develop socio economic infrastructure and basic 
amenities in the identified Minority Concentration Areas for improving the quality of life 
of the people in these areas and reduce imbalances as compared to national average.  
 
 3.2 The scheme has further been restructured with the approval of the Cabinet on 
19.01.2022 for continuation over the 15th Finance Commission Cycle. The Scheme will 
now be implemented in all Districts of the Country including 117 aspirational Districts. 
As per restructured scheme, States are to propose projects where the concentration of 
minority population is more than 25% in the catchment area (5 KM radius). With due 
certification of State Level Committee/ Central Government Organisation. Sports, 
Sanitation, Solar Energy and tourism cum skill centres projects also to be included in 
the priority sector along with Education, Health, Skill Development and Women 
Oriented Projects. The MCAs have been identified on the basis of concentration of 
minority population and backwardness in terms of identified indicators. On the basis of 
Census 2011 data, 870 Minority Concentration Blocks (MCBs), 321 Minority 
Concentration Towns (MCTs) and 109 Minority Concentration Districts HQs (MCD 
HQs) falling in 33 States/UTs have been identified.

CHAPTER-IV 
SCHOLARSHIP SCHEMES 
 
4.1 The Ministry is implementing three scholarship schemes viz; Pre-Matric, Post- 
Matric and Merit-cum-Means based Scholarships for the educational empowerment of 
students belonging to the six centrally notified minority communities.  In order to 
improve transparency in the implementation of the scholarship schemes they are being 
implemented through a new and revamped National Scholarship Portal launched by 
the Government during 2016-17.  The scholarships are transferred into the bank 
account of students through Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) mode.   
I. Pre-matric Scholarship Scheme 
4.2 The Pre-Matric Scholarship Scheme for students of the Minority Communities, 
was approved on 30th January, 2008. It is a Central Sector Scheme with 100% Central 
Government funding. The students studying in India in a government/ recognised 
private school, in Class I to X, who secure 50% marks in the previous final examination 
and whose parents’/ guardians’ annual income does not exceed ₹ 1.00 lakh, are 
eligible for award of the Pre-Matric scholarship under the scheme. Under the scheme, 
30 lakh fresh scholarships are awarded every year in addition to the renewals. 30% of 
the scholarships have been earmarked for girl students. Scholarship ranging from 
₹1,000/- to ₹ 10,700/- is awarded to every selected student. 
II.  Post -matric Scholarship Scheme 
4.3 The Post Matric Scholarship Scheme was launched in November, 2007. It is a 
Central Sector Scheme. Post Matric Scholarship is awarded for studies in a 
Government/ recognised private higher secondary schools/colleges/Universities 

 
28  
including residential higher secondary schools/colleges of India. Scholarship ranging 
from ₹. 2,300/- to ₹ 15,000/- is awarded to every selected student. 
Students who secure 50% marks in the previous year’s final examination and 
whose parents’/ guardians’ annual income does not exceed ₹2.00 lakh are eligible for 
award of scholarship under the scheme. 5 lakh Fresh scholarships are awarded every 
year in addition to the renewals. 30% of scholarships have been earmarked for girl 
students. In case, sufficient number of girl students are not available, then eligible boy 
students are given these scholarships. 
III. Merit-cum-Means based Scholarship Scheme 
4.4 The Merit-cum-Means based Scholarship Scheme is a Central Sector 
Scheme, launched in 2007 to award scholarships to students with an annual income of 
the family from all sources not exceeding ₹2.50 lakh for pursuing professional and 
technical courses at under-graduate and post-graduate levels in institutions recognized 
by appropriate authority. Under the scheme, 60,000 fresh scholarships are proposed to 
be awarded every year in addition to the renewals. 30% of these scholarships are 
earmarked for girl students, which may be utilized by eligible boy students, if an 
adequate number of eligible girl students are not available. Eligible students admitted 
in any of the 85 reputed premier institutes for professional and technical courses listed 
under the Scheme are reimbursed full course fee. Students studying in other 
institutions are reimbursed a course fee of ₹ 20,000/- per annum and in addition a 
maintenance allowance of Rs. 5,000/- for day scholar and Rs. 10,000/- for hosteller per 
annum is also admissible. The students, who have secured admission in any technical 
or professional institution, recognized by an appropriate authority are eligible under this 
scheme. In case of students admitted without a competitive examination, students 

 
29  
should have secured not less than 50% marks in the qualifying exam at higher 
secondary/graduation level in case of Fresh scholarship.

NAYA SAVERA (FREE COACHING AND ALLIED SCHEME) 
 
5.1 The “Free Coaching and Allied Scheme” of the Ministry of Minority Affairs for 
candidates belonging to minority communities was launched on 17th July 2007, to 
assist students / candidates of economically weaker sections belonging to notified 
minority communities by providing coaching for qualifying in competitive examination 
for admission in technical / professional courses such as medical, engineering etc 
and for recruitment to Group ‘A’, ‘B’ and ‘C’ services and other equivalent posts under 
Central and State Governments including public sector undertakings, banks, 
railways etc.  
5.2 This Scheme is implemented directly by the Ministry of Minority Affairs with 
100% Central assistance to the selected coaching institutes for providing free 
coaching to the students / candidates. The scheme also provides for earmarking 30% 
of the target for girls. A new component under Free Coaching & Allied Scheme was 
added from 2013-14 for focused preparation of Minority Students at Classes XI & XII 
with Science subjects viz (Physics, Chemistry, Biology and/or Mathematics). In the 
revised Scheme, a one year residential coaching programme for students who have 
passed class XII  with 75% marks and above in science subjects. The Scheme has 
been revised with effect from September, 2017, and as per the revised scheme, 
students/candidates belonging to the notified Minority Communities, having total 
annual family income from all sources not exceeding ₹6.00 lakh, are eligible for 
coaching under the scheme.

5.5 On being asked  the reasons due to which actual expenditure was substantially 
less in comparison to the revised estimates during 2019-20, 2020-21 and 2021-22 and 
initiatives proposed to be taken up to spend the funds allocation under the Scheme 
during 2022-23, the Ministry in their written submission inter-alia informed as under: 
“Under Free Coaching and Allied Scheme first instalment including 50% of 
coaching fees and 50% of stipend amount is released to the coaching 
institutions/organizations (PIAs) in advance for implementing the Scheme. PIAS are 
required to complete the coaching program and disburse the remaining amount of 
stipend to the students from their own sources.  
However, there was some delay in submission of their proposals due to various 
reasons such as problems in implementation of EAT module of PFMS which was 
introduced first time in 2018-19. Some PIAs also informed that either exams for 
Group B/C services were not held or results were not declared in time. Due to this 
PIA were not able to submit the UC and results of the coaching program in 2019-
20. Hence 2ndinstallment of 2018-19 could not be released in 2019-20.  
In 2019-20, provision for providing coaching to 9580 students was  made to 87 
PIAs with the condition that 1stinstallment for 2019-20 be released only after 
settlement of accounts of their previous program i.e. release of 2ndinstallment  
pertaining to 2017-18 and 2018-19. Since release of 2ndinstallment is linked with  
submission of various  documents including UCs, use of Expenditure Advance and 
Transfer (EAT) module, prescribed outcome of coaching program, satisfactory 
report of State government / National Productivity Council (NPC) etc.,  it takes  
considerable time to examine the  proposals and take necessary approvals of 
Competent Authority.  
Due to the reasons mentioned above, the funds allocated under the schemes 
were not fully utilized.  
In 2020-21, in addition to the above reasons, due to the ongoing pandemic, 
coaching programs were impacted for significant period during the year leading to 
lower number of beneficiaries trained under the scheme.  Coaching classes were 
disrupted in the year 2020 & 2021 due to Covid pandemic. Hence PIAs could not 
show progress of 2019-20 coaching programme to claim 1stinstallment in 2020-21. 
COVID-19 pandemic also led to delay in conducting the various examinations and 
subsequent delay in the announcement of results are some other major issues that 
led to substantial reduction in physical achievement as well as less expenditure 
under the Scheme.  
For the year 2021-22, the RE is ₹39 cr. Till 10.2.2022, ₹32.49 crore has been 
utilized and it is expected that full amount of RE of ₹39 crore will be utilized by end 
of F.Y.

5.6 On being enquired about the parameters taken into consideration to fix the target 
of students/candidates for coaching and when were they last received and the time by 
which they are likely to be revised,  the Ministry stated in their written reply: 
“Under the Scheme, all proposals, received from both Government institution or 
private institutions, are placed before the Selection Committee for its consideration 
and recommendation for empanelment of the institutions and number of allocation of 
minority students under the scheme.  
Allocation of students for the various courses are based on multiple factors such 
as infrastructure facility available at the proposed centre, experience of PIA, 
courses proposed by the PIA, previous number of allocation under the scheme, 
overall allocation in the State vis- a- vis population of minority community, number 
of students recommended by State Government etc.   
In 2017-18, PIAs were empanelled and targets were allocated for coaching for 
various courses. Performance of the PIAs is reviewed in the subsequent years 
and allocation has been made to the eligible PIAs. During 2021-22, allocations of 
5140 students have been made to 37 PIAs.  
The Scheme is due for its approval for continuation beyond April, 2022-23 and 
fresh empanelment and allocation of students will be made as per approval of 
competent authority.”

5.9 On being enquired about the revision of per capita expenditure granted to 
coaching institute and whether it is sufficient to meet the requirement of students in big 
cities, the Ministry submitted in their written reply that: 
“The Scheme was reviewed during 2017-18 and coaching fees for the various 
courses under the Scheme was increased suitably.  The coaching fees for non 
residential course were enhanced from Rs. 20,000/- to Rs. 50,000/- for six months 
courses and from ₹15,000/- to ₹30,000 and ₹20,000 for 4 months & 3 months 
courses respectively.  
  Further, the scheme was evaluated by the Indian Institute of Public Administration 
(IIPA), New Delhi during 2020-21. IIPA in its report has inter-alia recommended 
that the current fee structure needs to be brought in the range of prevailing market 
rates so that quality teachers can be hired for providing better guidance to 
beneficiary students. Accordingly, in the proposed SFC/EFC the coaching fees 
and course duration of various courses are being increased suitably which will be 
affected from next empanelment of PIAs.”

5.11 The following recommendations were inter-alia made in the Evaluation Study: 
 
(i) The monitoring system requires to be devised at the level of scheme 
implementers viz the coaching institutions, the Ministry and the District Minority 
Officers (DMO). 
 
(ii) Universities/colleges in the government sector and the private sector should also 
be integrated into the scheme. The ministry may nominate some government 
Institutions to partner as implementing agencies. 
 
(iii) The success rate informed to the Ministry by coaching centres requires a 
mandatory physical verification before releasing the II instalment. 
 
(iv) The empanelled coaching centres should be given one-time grant-in-aid (non-
recurring) for infrastructure development. The coaching classrooms, toilets, 
seating arrangements, etc. were found deficient. 
(v) As desired by most of the students receiving coaching under the scheme. The 
course duration for the coaching under the scheme needs to be extended from six 
months to eight months 
 
(vi) It is advised that the stipend should directly be remitted from the Ministry to the 
beneficiaries’ accounts without any delay. 
 
(vii) For the selection of meritorious students, the Ministry may like to conduct an 
entrance test through an institution like National Testing Agency (NTA). It would 
help the meritorious students to get the benefit of free coaching under the 
scheme. 
 
(ix) All possible media platforms, Ministry websites, and websites of the state 
governments may be used as publicity platform to increase the pool of potential 
beneficiaries.  
 
(x) The coaching fee across examinations varies. It requires to be brought to parity. 
This also requires to be brought in the range of prevailing market rates so that 
quality teachers can be hired for providing better guidance to beneficiary 
students.

5.12 The Committee note that ‘Naya Savera’ scheme was launched in July, 2007 
to assist students/candidates of economically weaker sections belonging to 
notified Minority Communities for qualifying in competitive examinations for 
admission in technical/professional courses and for recruitment of group A, B and 
C services etc.  The Committee, however find that the actual expenditure on the 
scheme has been very low during the 2019-20, 2020-21 and 2021-22 as they could 
spend only   ₹13.97 crore, ₹18.44 crore and ₹18.22 crore during these three years, 
out of BE of ₹75.00 crore, ₹50.00 crore and ₹79.00 crore respectively.  The 
Committee feel that the Department appears to be non-serious in their approach in 
the implementation of the scheme as budgetary allocation/ targets have not 
increased.  The procedural lapses identified by the Ministry for the lukewarm 
response of the scheme such as delay in submission of proposals/Utilisation 
certificates reflects poorly on the part of the Ministry.  The Committee also feel that 
perhaps the awareness on the Scheme needs to be enhanced.  Since some 
changes are expected as a result of revision in the scheme such as development 
of online portal, setting up of Project Monitoring Units, the Committee hope the 
publicity part will also be looked into so that performance of scheme would 
improve.  The Committee may like to be apprised of the outcome of the changes 
proposed in the revised scheme. 
 
5.13 In the competitive environment, the Committee find that the amount of 
stipend and the duration of coaching insufficient to meet the present day 
requirement of the students particularly in big cities.  The Committee are unable to 
understand the objective of having a provision of coaching for the period ranging 
from 3-9 months for a student belonging to economically weaker section of 

 
43  
minority community who cannot afford good quality education due to their 
economic and social backwardness.  Similarly, the stipend fixed and coaching fee 
fixed is not at par with the prevailing high coaching rates of the reputed coaching 
Institutes.  The Committee are happy to note that the current fee structure and the 
duration has been recommended for revision by the Indian Institute of Public 
Administration, in their evaluation study on the Scheme, and hence is being 
suitably  increased and will be effected from next empanelment of Project 
Implementing  Agencies.  In view of the changes proposed to be implemented from 
next empanelment of PIAs, the Committee hope that due diligence will be applied 
in the timely selection of PIAs and the criteria laid down  for selection of PIAs 
would be stringently followed.  The Committee recommend that a suitable 
methodology may be adopted for the automatic revision of fee structure  and the 
course duration of various courses including income criteria so that there is 
maximum coverage of the students.  Taking note of the various initiatives such as 
proposed online module for students and the recommendation contained in the 
evaluation study such as development of monitoring system by implementers, 
participation of Universities/colleges in the Government sector and Private sector, 
one time grant to empanelled coaching Institutes, entrance test for selection of 
students, etc., it can be expected that positive impact would occur on the outcome 
of the Schemes.  The Committee desire that the decision with regard to the 
implementation of the recommendation should be urgently taken up by the 
Ministry without any delay and ensure that these should be followed with the next 
empanelment of PIAs. The Committee would like to be apprised about the status of 
implementation including development of online modules for students alongwith 
State-wise data of beneficiaries and funds released under the Scheme for the last 
three years in the Action Taken Notes of the Ministry.

SCHEME FOR EDUCATION OF MADARSAS AND MINORITIES (SPEMM) 
6.1 Ministry of Minority Affairs is implementing a centrally sponsored Umbrella 
Scheme for Providing Education to Madarsas/Minorities (SPEMM) which comprises of 2 
sub schemes namely Scheme for Providing Quality Education in Madarsas (SPQEM) 
and Infrastructure Development in Minority Institutions (IDMI) w.e.f 01.04.2021.  The 
Scheme is implemented at National level, both the Schemes are voluntary in nature.  The 
sixteen states namely Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, 
Manipur, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Tripura, Uttar Pradesh, 
Uttarakhand, West Bengal, Sikkim, Mizoram, have availed benefits under the scheme so 
far. The Scheme has been transferred to Ministry of Minority Affairs from Ministry of 
Education w.e.f. 01.04.2021. 
 
6.2 As per guidelines of the scheme all the proposals under both the schemes are 
submitted by the State Government which are considered and approved by a PAB 
(Project Approval Board). For the FY 2021-22, eight States namely Bihar, Tripura, 
Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Uttarakhand, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh 
have sent their proposals under SPQEM while 4 States namely UP, Tripura, Manipur and 
Mizoram have sent proposals for financial assistance under IDMI.  
6.3 Under Scheme for Providing Quality Education in Madarsas (SPQEM) funds are 
provided to recognized Madrasas which are affiliated to any States or Central Board to 
encourage Madrasas to introduce modern subjects such as science, mathematics, social 
studies, Hindi and English in their curriculum so that academic proficiency is attainable 
for children studying in these institutions. Funds under SPQEM are provided for payment 
of honorarium to Madrasas teachers and other quality components such as library, 

 
 
quality intervention, smart classes and computer labs etc., in
Madrasa teachers at both elementary and secondary level and also to strengthen State 
Madrasas Boards.   
6.4 Under Infrastructure Development in Minority Institutions (IDMI) 
provided to facilitate education of minorities by augmenting and strengthening school 
infrastructure in Minority Institutions (elementary/ secondary/ senior secondary schools) 
in order to expand facilities for formal education to children of minority communities.

6.17 The Committee find that Scheme for Providing Education to Madarsas /

in Minority Institutions(IDMI) has been transferred to Ministry of Minority Affairs

note that perhaps the Ministry is yet to commence work on the ground, as only two 
Board meetings on the same have happened so far and hence no proposals under 
the Scheme for education of Madarsas and Minorities have been approved since 
the scheme was transferred from Ministry of Education. The Committee find that 
prior to the transfer of the Scheme, the proposals from 6 States under Scheme for

Committee were informed that the Project Approval Board meeting for 
consideration of SPQEM proposals was held on 30th November, 2021 and a 
proposal Board meeting for consideration of proposals under IDMI was to be held 
in February, 2022. The Committee feel that the pace of approval procedure is 
rather slow and needs to be expedited to achieve the goals of the Scheme. The 
Committee also do not find participation from a large number of States/UTs in this 
Scheme. The Committee, therefore desire that fast action may be taken so that 
there is participation of all States/UTs in the Scheme.   The Committee have now 
been informed that since the Scheme has been transferred in 2021 from the

i. The need for Project Management Unit (PMU) is essential to improve the 
efficiency of the scheme. 
 
ii. There also appears to be a justification in relaxing accreditation norms for 
Training Centers in far far-flung and remote rural and semi urban areas, 
especially in Minority Districts which do not find adequate representation on the

provided under SAK by the PIAs, it is but obvious that most of the jobs

areas. Tailoring, Lab technician and Plumbing constitute more than 75% of all

which is at the heart of Atmanirbhar Bharat, Make in India and MSMEs. The 
only way to nudge and steer the spectrum of job roles under MoMA to the 
national focus areas is to initially allocate some 25% seats for high end job roles 
of NSQF Level 5 and above, to Industry associations, reputed industries/SSCs 
or Skilling Universities on nomination basis, subject to their undertaking to 
employ minority youth in skill shadow areas, based on actual demand estimates 
in Post COVID environment.

MAULANA AZAD EDUCATION FOUNDATION 
 
8.1 Maulana Azad Education Foundation (MAEF) is an autonomous body under the 
Ministry of Minority Affairs, Govt. of India established to promote education amongst the 
educationally backward minorities. The MAEF was established in July, 1989 as a 
registered Society under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 and it is fully funded by the 
Govt. of India. The Foundation has received total Corpus Fund of ₹.1362.00 crore upto 
financial year 2017-18 from the Ministry of Minority Affairs, Govt. of India which is kept 
invested in fixed deposit with Banks and the interest accrued from the investment of the 
corpus fund shall be utilized by the Foundation for implementation of its educational 
schemes.  From the year 2018-19 onwards, MoMA has started giving Grants-in-aid to 
MAEF in place of Corpus Fund. An amount of ₹.139.50 crore has already been released 
by MoMA to MAEF as Grants-in-aid upto 31.12.2021.  
8.2 MAEF is implementing the following schemes:  
(i) Grants-in-aid to NGOs: 
Under this scheme, MAEF provides financial assistance to NGOs for 
construction/expansion of school buildings; construction of hostel buildings; 
construction/expansion of B.Ed/D.Ed. Colleges; construction of Technical 
Institutions/VTC and purchase of lab equipment; furniture etc. for Schools/ITI/VTC This 
scheme has helped small institutions to expand their infrastructure resulting in overall 
improvement in educational activities amongst the target group. It is a unique scheme 
which is implemented directly by MAEF without any intervention of State Governments or 
any outside agency. 

 
66  
(ii) Begum Hazrat Mahal National Scholarship: 
 MAEF had started this scholarship scheme in 2003-04. This was the first 
scholarship scheme at national level for meritorious girls belonging to minorities for their 
higher secondary level education i.e., for class 11th & 12th.  Now the MAEF is also giving 
scholarship to minorities girls studying in classes 9th and 10th. MAEF provides 
scholarships @ Rs.5,000/- each for classes 9th & 10th and Rs.6,000/- each for classes 
11th & 12th. Now from the current year, i.e. 2021-2022, the Scheme has been boarded 
on the National Scholarship Portal (NSP) with the objective of bringing all scheme on one 
platform and to reduce the chances of availing multiple benefits of various schemes by 
the applicants.  
(iii) Gharib Nawaz Employment Scheme: 
MAEF has launched this scheme titled Gharib Nawaz Skill Development Training 
for minorities from the year 2017-18 which has been renamed as Gharib Nawaz 
Employment Scheme form the year 2019-20. Under this scheme various short-term job-
oriented skill development courses are provided to minorities’ youth in order to enable 
them for skill-based employment. This scheme is being implemented as per common 
norms of the Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship (MSD&E) through the 
empanelled Program Implementation Agencies (PIAs). 
(iv) Other Activities of MAEF during the year 2021-22: 
Establishment of a National Institute & Hunar Hub in Alwar District (Rajasthan): 
On the request of MAEF, Government of Rajasthan has allotted 15acre land for 
establishment of proposed National Institute & Hunar Hub in Kishangarh Bas.  The 
MAEF has got the feasibility Report prepared by EdCIL for the said project. Preparation 
of the Detailed Project Report (DPR) for this purpose is under process.
