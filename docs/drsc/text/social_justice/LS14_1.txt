INTRODUCTION 
 
I, the Chairperson of the Standing  Committee on Social Justice and 
Empowerment (2004) having been author ised by the Committee to submit the 
Report on their behalf, present this Fi rst Report, of the Ministry of Social 
Justice & Empowerment on Demands for Grants, 2004-2005. 
2.         Departmentally Related Standing Committee system has been restructured in July 2004. 
Subsequently, the erstwhile Standing Committee on Labour and Welfare has been bifurcated – as 
Standing Committee on Social Justice and Empowerment and the other one is the Standing Committee 
on Labour. Under the restructured Standing Committee on Social Justice and Empowerment, there are 
two Ministries namely, the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment and the Ministry of Tribal 
Affairs. 
3. The Committee considered the Demands for Grants pertaining   to   the   
Ministry   of Social Justice  & Empowerment for the current year i.e. 2004-2005 
which were laid on the Table of the House on July 20, 2004.  Thereafter, the   
Committee   took   evidence   of   the representatives of the Ministry of Social 
Justice and Empowerment on August 10, 2004. The Committee considered and 
finalized the Report at their sitting held on August 17, 2004.  
4. The Committee wish to express their thanks to the officers of the 
Ministry   of   Social   Justice   and Empowerment for placing before them the 
detailed written notes on the subject and furnishing the information the 
Committee desired in connection with the examination of the Demands for 
Grants and tendering evidence before the Committee. 
5. For facility of reference and convenience, the observations and 
recommendations of the Committee have been printed in thick type in the body 
of the Report and have also been reproduced in a consolidated form in 
Appendix I to the Report.

INTRODUCTORY   
        
1.1 The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment have 
informed the Committee that the mandate of the Ministry is to work 
towards the mainstreaming of the disadvantaged and the 
marginalized sections of the so ciety such as, Scheduled Castes, 
Other Backward Classes, Minorities, aged persons, persons with 
disabilities, victims of substance abuse and children in need of care 
and protection. The objective is to empower the target groups 
through their educational, economic and social development and 
build self-reliance and self-esteem. The areas concerning the 
Ministry are also those where a pivo tal role is played by the State 
Governments. The State Governme nts are allocating substantial 
portions of their resources to the welfare of the disadvantaged and 
marginalized sections of the socie ty. The Ministry of Social Justice 
and Empowerment channelizes some resources to the States/UTs 
for funding specified programmes fo r the welfare of those sections. 
The Ministry also maintains regu lar interaction with the State 
GovernmentS on issues relating to empowerment of the 
disadvantaged and marginalized sections. Besides the 
implementation of different sche mes is monitored and feedback 
obtained on these aspects. The Mi nistry is also charged with the 
Constitutional obligation of ensuring effective implementation of 
laws passed from time to time for protecting the interests of the SCs, 
OBCs, Minorities and disabled persons.  
 
1.2 In order to achieve the above mentioned objectives, the 
Ministry of Social Justice and Em powerment has been divided into 
the following main Divisions:- 
 
(a) Scheduled Castes Development 
(b) Other Backward Classes  
(c) Minorities and Wakf Administration 
(d)  Welfare of persons with Disabilities  
                        (e) Social Defence including Care and Protection of Children

 During 2003-04 the BE was Rs.1410 crore which was revised

reasons:  
 

• The Ministry of Finance convey s the revised estimates on the 
basis of expenditure level upto 30 th September. Generally, the 
proposals from the State Governments and NGOs associated with 
the implementation of the schemes  of the Ministry are received 
late and so the utilization of funds by the ministry is around 30-
35% and the pace increases in th e second half of the year. The 
budget at the RE stage is redu ced for this reason. In 2003-2004, a 
cut of Rs. 120.00 crore was imposed by the Ministry of Finance. 
 
• The scheme for the Welfare of Working Children and 
children in need of care and protection (for which an amount of 
Rs. 7.00 crore was budgeted) is pe nding with the Supreme Court 
for approval. Therefore, the budgeted funds could not be 
utilized. 
 
• The funds available for the sch eme of upgradation of Merit 
for SC students could not be fully utilized, as the proposal for 
revision of the scheme could not be approved during the years. 
 
• The budget allocation of Rs. 30.00 crore was to be released to 
the Maulana Azad Education Fo undation (MAEF) during 2003-
2004 for building its corpus after obtaining the approval of the 
Expenditure Finance Committee and Cabinet Committee on 
Economic Affairs (CCEA). Me anwhile, the Department of 
Expenditure issued orders for mandatory cut on the budget of 
the Ministry, and at the RE stage, the budget of MAEF was 
reduced to Rs. 20.32 crore. However, matter for release of this 
amount could not be placed before the Cabinet Committee on 
Economic Affairs due to dissolution of 13
th Lok Sabha on 6 th 
February 2004. Therefore, only the interest equivalent of Rs. 3.57 
crore was released in 2003-2004. 
 
• Rehabilitation Council of India- An amount of Rs. 0.91 crore 
was available with the RCI as unspent balance for the year 2002-
2003 and the requirement for 2003-2004 was therefore adjusted 
accordingly. 
 
 
• Schemes arising out of the Implementation of PWD Act – An 
amount of Rs. 4.22 crore could not be spent, as the Awareness 
Generation Programme proposed  under this scheme was not 
approved by the Planning Commission. 
 

• Employment of the Handicapped – Completed proposals 
from the State Governments/UTs were not received.  
 
• Rs. 36.66 crore under the head lump sum for the benefit of 
NE region and Sikkim could not be utilized due to non-receipt of 
adequate proposals from the NE region under various schemes 
of the Ministry.

The total surrender made by the Ministry for the year 2003-2004 is

2.6 In their post-evidence reply the Ministry have informed the 
Committee that it organized a Conference of State Secretaries on 10 th 
&11th August 2004 to review the progress of all the schemes and 
programmes. They were apprised of the difficulties faced by the 
Ministry in regard to non-receipt  of complete proposals along with 
utilization certificates of previous  releases. The Ministry was given 
the feedback that the concerned de partment receives allocation of 
funds released from their State Finance Department a little late, 
which results in delay in sending the proposals. The Ministry will 
take up again the matter with the State Governments to expedite the 
matter. Besides the Ministry of Fina nce will again be requested to 
take expenditure as on 31
st December of the year while deciding RE 
for the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment keeping in view 
the special circumstances. 
 
 
2.7 The Committee note with concern that during 2003-2004, the 
Ministry’s Budget Estimate was Rs. 1410 crore which was reduced 
to Rs. 1250 crore at the Revised Estimate stage, and out of this 
reduced budget the Ministry could spend only Rs. 1182.26 crore. 
The Committee have been apprised of various reasons for 
reduction of funds at RE stage and even less spending, such as the 
Ministry of Finance making the re vised estimates on the basis of 
expenditure level upto 30
th September, late receipt of proposals 

and utilization certificates from State Governments and NGOs 
resulting in utilization of funds to the extent of only 30-35%, non-
utilisation due to pendency of schemes for revision for years, 
unspent balance with institutes due to non-filling of posts, non-
approval of awareness generation schemes, etc. Keeping in view 
the significance of the mandate of the Ministry, i.e. the upliftment 
of the most disadvantaged, marginalized and vulnerable sections 
of the society, the Committee strongly recommend that the 
Ministry should gear up its implementation and monitoring 
machinery and conduct periodic review meetings with the State 
Governments/implementing agencies and impress upon them to 
fully utilise the fund allocated under various schemes. 
 
2.8 The Committee note that the Budgetary allocation of the 
Ministry is reduced every year  at RE stage. The Committee has 
been apprised that the basis of re duction at RE stage is the pace of 
expenditure upto 30
th September, which is only 30% to 35%. The 
Committee further note that most of the schemes of the Ministry 
are implemented by the State Governments and NGOs. The State 
Governments are not able to submit their proposals along with 
the utilization certificates in time, which result in slow pace of 
expenditure and reduction at RE stage. In the opinion of the 

Committee most of the schemes/programmes of the Ministry 
relate to development and welf are of marginalized sections 
including Scheduled Castes, OBCs, Minorities and the disabled. 
The Committee, therefore, desire that keeping in view the 
practical difficulty of the Ministry of Social Justice and 
Empowerment and the interest of weaker sections, the Ministry of 
Finance should be persuaded to take a lenient view and avoid 
drastic cuts at RE stage in allocations.

(a) SPECIAL CENTRAL ASSISTANCE TO SPECIAL 
COMPONENT PLAN FOR SCHEDULED CASTES.  
 
3.1 Under the scheme 100% central assistance is extended to 
supplement the efforts of the Stat es/UTs for ensuring rapid socio-
economic development of the Scheduled Castes (SCs).  It is provided 
as an additive to the Special Component Plan (SCP) of the 
States/UTs for augmenting th eir efforts for the economic 
development of Scheduled Castes.  The objective of the SCA is to 
support SC families to enhance th eir productivity and income.  It 
focuses on maximizing the returns fr om the assets held by them by 
filling the critical gaps in the deve lopmental needs.  The SCA is   
released to the States/UTs on the basis of following criteria:- 
 
 
a) On the basis of SC Population of the  
States/UTs     : 40% 
 
b) On the basis of relative backwardness of  
the States/UTs (inverse of the State  : 10% 
per capita Domestic Product)  
 
c) On the basis of the percentage of  
SC families in the States/UTs  : 25% 
covered by composite economic  
development programmes in the plans. 
 
d) On the basis of Special Component  
Plan to the Annual Plan as compared 
to the SC population percentage in  
the States/UTs    : 25% 
 
Second instalment of SCA should be released to the States/UTs after 
ensuring expenditure of cumulative opening balances of the 

previous year and 75% utilization of the first installment for the 
current year.

7.1 Under the scheme of ‘An Integrated Programme for older 
persons’ central grant-in-aid is given to voluntary organizations 
(90:10 basis) for running old Age Homes, Day Care Centres, Mobile 
Medical Units etc., as well as fo r supporting and strengthening non-
institutional services for the aged like providing legal counselling 
assistance for the supplying pens ion, GPF, HRA, Income Tax, 
Banking Services and medical examination of their eyes for receiving 
spectacles, obtaining hearing aids an d dentures etc.  The allocation 
of funds for 2002-03 was Rs.18.27 crore against which Rs.16.46 crore 
was released.  Similarly during 2003-04 Rs.17.80 crore was allocated 
against which Rs.15.80 crore were released.  Allocation for 2004-05 is 
Rs.18.79 crore. 
 
7.2 In reply to a question the Co mmittee has been informed that 
due to general improvement in health care facilities over the years, 
there is a gradual increase in th e life expectancy.  In 1996 the 
population of persons above 60 years was 6.67% of the total 
population.  At present it has increased to 7% and expected to rise to 
8.94% in 2016.  As such, the numb er of those needing the State 
support can be expected to increase.  During 2002-03 and 2003-04 
less expenditure was incurred due to non-receipt of sufficient 
complete proposals from eligible organizations. 
 
 
7.3 The Committee note that the number of older persons who 
need State support for their care and protection is increasing year 
after year due to general improvement in health and their life 
expectancy.  The Committee observe that there are large number 
of senior citizens who are not getting adequate state support for 
their care and protection whereas the government could not fully 

utilize even the meagre budgetary provisions made for the 
purpose. The Committee desire that the exact number of older 
persons may be assessed urgently and proper awareness about the 
Government schemes for their care and protection may be created 
to enable them to avail the benefit under the scheme.  The 
performance of the NGOs engaged in this scheme should be 
strictly monitored.
