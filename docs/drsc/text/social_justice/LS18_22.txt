Recommendation (Para No.2.13) 
5. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee note that the Budget Estimates for the year 2026–27 have been 
significantly enhanced to ₹1,669.72 crore, reflecting an increase of about 30.96 
per cent over the Budget Estimates of ₹1,275.00 crore for the year 2025–26. While 
welcoming the enhanced allocation, the Committee emphasise that the increased 
outlay must lead to measurable improvements in service delivery and outreach to 
persons with disabilities. The Committee therefore recommend that the 
Department adopt a more outcome-oriented approach in implementation of its 
schemes, with clearly defined physical targets, periodic evaluation of progress and 
stronger monitoring of implementation across States and Union Territories. The 
Committee also urge the Department to prioritise expansion of assistive device 
distribution, rehabilitation services, scholarships and skill development 
programmes, particularly in the North-Eastern Region and aspirational districts. 
The Committee are of the view that such targeted and outcome-focused utilisation 
of the enhanced allocation will ensure that the increased budgetary support 
translates into meaningful and tangible benefits for persons with disabilities across 
the country”. 
 
6. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted as under:- 
 
 “The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. The enhanced 
 Budget Estimates for 2026–27 provide an opportunity to further strengthen 
 service delivery, expand outreach and improve outcomes for persons with 
 disabilities. The Department is committed to ensuring that the increased 
 allocation translates into measurable and tangible benefits through outcome-
 oriented implementation, robust monitoring and targeted interventions. Further, 
 the two new schemes introduced during 2026–27, namely Divyangjan Kaushal 
 Yojana and Divyangjan Sahara Yojana, with allocations of ₹200 crore and ₹100 
 crore respectively, are aimed at strengthening skill development and the assistive 

3 
 
 device ecosystem for persons with disabilities. Further ongoing scheme wise 
 focus areas are mentioned below:   
  DDRS  
Under the Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme (DDRS), assistance is 
provided to eligible organizations for delivering rehabilitation and support services 
to persons with disabilities. To improve outcomes and expand coverage, the 
Department is focusing on: 
(i) Strengthening monitoring and evaluation mechanisms for assessment of service 
delivery and beneficiary coverage; 
(ii) Expanding rehabilitation services in underserved regions, particularly North-
Eastern States, aspirational districts, Himalayan regions, Left Wing Extremism 
(LWE)-affected districts and border areas; 
(iii) Encouraging wider participation of eligible organizations to improve 
geographical coverage and accessibility of services; and 
(iv) Facilitating timely processing of proposals and release of Grant-in-Aid through 
digital platforms and streamlined procedures. 
The Scheme continues to provide 100% Grant-in-Aid for projects located in North-
Eastern States, Himalayan regions, LWE-affected districts and border areas to 
promote greater outreach in these regions. 
ADIP 
Under the Assistance to Disabled Persons for Purchase/Fitting of Aids and 
Appliances (ADIP) Scheme, the enhanced allocation is being utilized with a clear 
focus on expanding beneficiary coverage and improving access to quality assistive 
devices. 
The Department has adopted an outcome-oriented approach with defined physical 
targets relating to the number of beneficiaries to be covered and aids and assistive 

4 
 
devices to be distributed. Progress against these targets is regularly monitored 
through the ARJUN Portal and periodic review mechanisms. 
To strengthen outreach and service delivery, the Department is: 
(i) Conducting assessment and distribution camps across the country, particularly 
in underserved areas; 
(ii) Expanding the network of Pradhan Mantri Divyasha–Vayoshri Kendras (PMD-
VKs) to facilitate continuous access to aids and assistive devices; 
(iii) Deploying Mobile Vans and undertaking special initiatives in the North-Eastern 
Region and remote areas to improve accessibility; 
(iv) Leveraging digital platforms for real-time monitoring of beneficiaries, 
distribution activities and fund utilisation; and 
(v) Undertaking periodic reviews of implementation progress to ensure timely 
achievement of physical and financial targets. 
Scholarships 
The enhanced allocation under the Scheme is being utilized with a focus on 
expanding coverage, improving access, and ensuring timely disbursement of 
scholarship benefits to eligible beneficiaries across the country. 
 To ensure measurable outcomes, the Department has annual physical and 
financial targets and monitors progress through the National Scholarship Portal 
(NSP), which facilitates end-to-end online processing of applications, verification, 
sanction and disbursement. Regular review meetings with State Nodal Officers 
(SNOs) and other stakeholders are conducted to assess progress, address 
bottlenecks and improve the pace of verification and approval of applications. 
 The Department has strengthened its monitoring framework through periodic 
reviews of application trends, beneficiary coverage and fund utilization. 
Continuous engagement with States/UTs is undertaken to ensure timely 
verification of applications and wider dissemination of the Scheme. Special efforts 

5 
 
are being made to enhance outreach in the North-Eastern Region, aspirational 
districts and other under-served areas through awareness campaigns, targeted 
communication and coordination with educational institutions and State 
Governments”. 
BUDGET ALLOCATION AND EXPENDITURE 
7. The Committee have examined the reply of the Government and note the 
measures indicated by the Department, including the introduction of 
Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana and Divyangjan Sahara Yojana, strengthening of 
monitoring mechanisms through digital platforms, and the stated focus on the 
North-Eastern Region, aspirational districts and other underserved areas. 
While these initiatives are welcome, the Committee observe that the reply 
remains largely process-oriented and does not furnish specific outcome 
indicators or measurable physical targets against which the impact of the 
enhanced allocation of ₹1,669.72 crore for the year 2026–27 can be objectively 
assessed. 
 The Committee reiterate their earlier recommendation that the 
Department adopt a clearly defined outcome-oriented framework for all major 
schemes, with scheme-wise physical targets, beneficiary coverage goals, 
timelines for implementation, and measurable performance indicators relating 
to assistive device distribution, rehabilitation services, scholarships and skill 
development programmes. The Committee further desire that periodic outcome 
assessments be undertaken and the results placed in the public domain, 
particularly with regard to interventions in the North-Eastern Region, 
aspirational districts and other underserved areas. The Committee are of the 
view that only through measurable outcomes and periodic evaluation can it be 
ensured that the enhanced budgetary allocation translates into tangible 
benefits in the lives of persons with disabilities across the country.

Recommendation (Para No.4.18) 
8. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 
 “The Committee note that under the Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme 
(DDRS) the Budget Estimates for 2025–26 were fixed at ₹165.00 crore and 
subsequently revised to ₹145.00 crore, whereas the Actual Expenditure as on 
20.02.2026 stands at ₹88.26 crore, indicating that a substantial portion of the 
allocation remained unutilised during the year. The Committee understand that the 
release of Grant-in-Aid under the Scheme is linked to submission of audited 
accounts and other requisite documents by implementing agencies, which often 
results in delays in expenditure during the early part of the financial year. While 
appreciating the Department’s efforts to streamline processing through the e-
Anudaan portal and digital verification mechanisms, the Committee recommend 
that the Department further simplify procedural requirements and strengthen 
coordination with implementing agencies so that proposals are submitted in a timely 
manner and funds are released without avoidable delays, thereby ensuring 
smoother utilisation of funds and more effective delivery of rehabilitation services 
to persons with disabilities. 
 
 
 
 
9. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted as under:- 
“The observations/recommendations of the Committee have been noted. 
Under the Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme (DDRS), Grant-in-Aid 
(GIA) is released on a reimbursement basis after receipt and scrutiny of complete 
proposals and requisite documents as per scheme guidelines. Since implementing 
agencies require time for preparation and finalization of audited accounts after 
closure of the financial year, reimbursement proposals are generally submitted by 

7 
 
30th June of the succeeding financial year. Consequently, expenditure under the 
scheme generally gains momentum after the first quarter of the financial year. 
The Department has undertaken several measures to simplify procedures and 
strengthen coordination with implementing agencies to ensure timely processing 
and utilization of funds under DDRS, as detailed below. 
i.        A dedicated e-Anudaan portal, integrated with NGO-Darpan, Swavlamban 
(UDID) and RCI-CRR databases, has been operationalized for online submission, 
digital verification, faster scrutiny, and real-time tracking of proposals. 
ii.       The application process has been simplified through reduction in 
documentation requirements and auto-population of beneficiary and rehabilitation 
professional details via digital integration with relevant portals. 
iii. Further, advance of up to 75 % of previous year release is considered during 
the same year for which GIA has been applied by Project Implementing Agency 
(PIA). 50% is released as first instalment  and thereafter 25% is released upon 
75% utilization of initial release based on satisfactory checklist documents and 
GFR requirement”.  
 
 
Recommendation (Para No.4.19) 
10. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows: - 
“The Committee are concerned to find that several States and Union Territories 
currently do not have projects under the DDRS or functioning District Disability 
Rehabilitation Centres (DDRCs). The Committee are of the view that the absence 
of such facilities may limit access to rehabilitation services for persons with 
disabilities in these regions. The Committee therefore recommend that the 
Department take proactive steps, in coordination with State Governments, local 
bodies and credible voluntary organisations, to promote establishment of DDRS 
projects and DDRCs in such underserved areas. The people representatives should 
also be involved to generate awareness and better implementation of the scheme. 
Focused outreach and facilitation in these regions would help ensure more 

8 
 
equitable availability of rehabilitation services across the country and enable a 
larger number of Divyangjan to benefit from the Scheme”. 
 
11. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action 
Taken Reply, have submitted as under:- 
 
 “The observations/recommendations of the Committee have been noted. 
Under the Deendayal Divyangjan Rehabilitation Scheme (DDRS), Grant-in-Aid is 
demand-driven and released upon receipt of viable proposals from eligible 
implementing agencies/NGOs in accordance with the extant scheme guidelines. 
Accordingly, establishment of DDRS projects and DDRCs depends upon submission 
of proposals by eligible organizations. 
The Department is continuously making efforts to improve regional coverage under 
the Scheme, particularly in underserved States/UTs and districts where proposals 
for DDRS projects and DDRCs are inadequate, subject to receipt of viable proposals 
from eligible agencies. 
Further, under DDRS, Grant-in-Aid of up to 90% is provided for projects in general 
areas, while 100% Grant-in-Aid is admissible for projects in North-Eastern States, 
Himalayan regions, Left Wing Extremism (LWE)-affected districts and districts 
adjoining international borders, so as to promote wider outreach of rehabilitation 
services for persons with disabilities. 
The DDRS guidelines also provide for simplified, online submission and processing 
of proposals through the e-Anudaan portal, thereby facilitating easier access for 
implementing agencies and ensuring more efficient processing of proposals”.  
DEENDAYAL DIVYANGJAN REHABILITATION SCHEME (DDRS) 
12. The Committee have examined the replies of the Government in respect 
of DDRS and are not fully convinced by the responses furnished. The Committee 
note that despite the introduction of digital platforms and procedural 
simplification measures, under-utilisation of funds under the Scheme continues 
to be a recurring feature. The Committee further note that delays in submission 

9 
 
and processing of proposals, audited accounts and other requisite documents 
continue to adversely affect timely utilisation of funds and delivery of 
rehabilitation services. The Committee, therefore, reiterate their 
recommendation that the Department evolve a time-bound mechanism for 
submission, scrutiny and approval of proposals, fix accountability for persistent 
delays, and periodically assess the effectiveness of reforms introduced under 
the Scheme so as to ensure optimum utilisation of funds and timely delivery of 
services to beneficiaries. 
 The Committee are also concerned that several States/UTs continue to 
remain without DDRS projects and/or functioning DDRCs. The Committee note 
that the Department has largely reiterated the demand-driven nature of the 
Scheme without indicating any concrete strategy for expanding rehabilitation 
services in underserved areas. The Committee, therefore, reiterate their 
recommendation that the Department, in coordination with State 
Governments, local bodies, people's representatives and credible voluntary 
organisations, prepare a targeted action plan for promoting DDRS projects and 
DDRCs in uncovered districts and underserved regions so as to ensure equitable 
access to rehabilitation services across the country. 
Recommendation (Para No.5.7) 
13. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 “The Committee note that the budgetary allocation and expenditure under the 
Scheme for Implementation of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 
(SIPDA) have shown significant volatility during the last 3 financial years While the 
Budget Estimates of ₹150.00 crore in 2023–24 were reduced sharply to ₹67.00 
crore at the Revised Estimates stage, in 2025– 26 the allocation was enhanced from 
₹115.10 crore to ₹200.00 crore, indicating considerable fluctuation in financial 
planning. The Committee further note that in 2024–25 the Actual Expenditure stood 
at ₹44.16 crore against the Revised Estimates of ₹111.00 crore, reflecting utilisation 
of less than 40 per cent of the available allocation. The Committee are of the view 
that such instability and under-utilisation under an umbrella scheme meant for 

10 
 
implementing statutory obligations under the RPwD Act, 2016 may adversely affect 
the continuity of key interventions relating to accessibility, skill development and 
UDID implementation. The Committee therefore recommend that the Department 
adopt more realistic budgeting and stronger advance appraisal of proposals, along 
with closer monitoring of utilisation certificates and project readiness at the 
State/UT level, so that allocations under SIPDA are utilised in a timely and effective 
manner”. 
 
14. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted as under:- 
 “The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. 
The Department acknowledges the concerns regarding fluctuations in budgetary 
allocation and expenditure under the Scheme for Implementation of the Rights of 
Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (SIPDA). Variations in expenditure have primarily 
arisen due to differences in the number and size of proposals received from 
States/UTs, varying levels of project readiness, delays in submission of complete 
proposals and Utilization Certificates (UCs), and the demand-driven nature of 
certain components of the Scheme. 
In line with the recommendations of the Committee, the Department has initiated 
measures to strengthen planning, appraisal and monitoring under SIPDA. These 
include: 
(i) Adoption of a more realistic and evidence-based approach to budgeting, taking 
into account past expenditure trends, project readiness and implementation 
capacity of States/UTs; 
(ii) Strengthening of advance appraisal mechanisms to ensure that proposals are 
complete, technically sound and implementation-ready before approval; 
(iii) Regular engagement with States/UTs and implementing agencies through 
review meetings and consultations to improve the quality and timeliness of 
proposals; 

11 
 
(iv) Closer monitoring of submission of Utilization Certificates and physical progress 
of approved projects to facilitate timely release of subsequent instalments; 
(v) Strengthening of monitoring and evaluation mechanisms for tracking project 
implementation, fund utilisation and achievement of intended outcomes; and 
(vi) Provision of necessary guidance and handholding support to States/UTs for 
timely preparation and submission of proposals and compliance with scheme 
requirements. 
With regard to the UDID component of the said scheme, the Department continues 
to strengthen implementation through regular review of enrollment progress, 
improved coordination with States/UTs and enhanced monitoring of card generation 
and distribution activities to ensure effective utilisation of available resources. 
Similarly, under the skill development component of SIPDA, efforts are being made 
to align budgetary allocations with implementation requirements and training 
targets so as to maximise utilisation and improve outcomes. 
The Department remains committed to strengthening financial planning, improving 
utilisation of funds and ensuring effective implementation of SIPDA for achieving 
the objectives of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 in a timely and 
efficient manner”. 
Recommendation (Para No.5.8) 
15. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee observe that actual expenditure under SIPDA has frequently

(TSA) models, as well as delays in the submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs)

12 
 
States/UTs and implementing agencies through regular reviews and advance

support to States and implementing agencies to ensure their financial systems are 
fully integrated with the TSA/CNA framework. This would help ensure timely 
submission of proposals and more effective utilisation of funds for improving 
accessibility, rehabilitation services and overall empowerment of persons with 
disabilities”. 
16. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted as under:- 
“The Department has already undertaken several measures to strengthen 
coordination with States/UTs and implementing agencies and to improve utilisation 
of funds under the Scheme for Implementation of the Rights of Persons with 
Disabilities Act, 2016 (SIPDA). 
To facilitate smooth implementation of the Central Nodal Agency (CNA) and 
Treasury Single Account (TSA) framework, regular review meetings are being

guidance and handholding support to States/UTs and implementing agencies for 
integration of their financial systems with the TSA/CNA framework and compliance

In addition, efforts are being made to strengthen advance planning and project 
preparedness through early identification and appraisal of eligible projects, 
dissemination of detailed operational guidelines and continuous engagement with 
stakeholders. Digital processing and monitoring of proposals have also been 
strengthened to improve transparency, expedite approvals and facilitate timely 
release of funds. 
The Department continues to closely monitor utilisation of funds and 
implementation progress under SIPDA through periodic reviews and follow-up with 
States/UTs and implementing agencies. These measures are expected to improve 

13 
 
fund utilisation, reduce procedural delays and ensure timely implementation of 
projects relating to accessibility, rehabilitation services and empowerment of 
persons with disabilities. 
It is submitted that RE under the scheme has increased from ₹111 crore in 2024-
25 to ₹200 crore in 2025-26 and expenditure has also increased from ₹44.16 crore 
in 2024-25 to ₹157.62 crore in 2025-26. 
The Department remains committed to further strengthening coordination, 
capacity building and monitoring mechanisms to ensure effective implementation 
of SIPDA and achievement of the objectives of the Rights of Persons with 
Disabilities Act, 2016”. 
SCHEMES FOR IMPLEMENTATION OF RIGHTS OF PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES ACT 
2016 (SIPDA) 
17. The Committee note the replies of the Department. However, the 
Committee remain concerned over the recurring fluctuations in allocations and 
utilisation of funds under SIPDA, which is the flagship scheme for 
implementation of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016. While the 
Department has indicated that expenditure under the Scheme has improved 
during 2025-26, the Committee observe that significant variations in 
expenditure and utilisation have continued to persist over the years, thereby 
affecting the effective implementation of key interventions relating to 
accessibility, rehabilitation, skill development and other statutory obligations 
under the Act. 
 The Committee note the measures taken by the Department to improve 
the utilisation of funds. The Committee, desire that the Department undertake 
a comprehensive review of the structural factors responsible for recurring 
under-utilisation under SIPDA and evolve a results-based monitoring 
framework with clearly defined accountability and measurable outcomes. The 
Committee further desire to be apprised of the concrete improvements 

14 
 
achieved in project implementation, fund utilisation and coordination with 
States/UTs so as to ensure effective and timely implementation of the Scheme. 
  Recommendation (Para No. 6.6) 
18. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
“The Committee observe with concern that the physical achievements under the 
National Action Plan for Skill Development of Persons with Disabilities (NAP-SDP) 
have remained significantly below the set targets over the last three years The 
performance data reveals a widening gap: against a target of 17,000 in 2023–24, 
the Department trained 9,333 beneficiaries; this fell sharply to 836 trainees against 
a target of 15,000 in 2024–25, and further to only 2,264 beneficiaries against a 
target of 10,000 in 2025–26 (as of January 2026). While the Committee 
acknowledge the administrative challenges posed by the transition to the PM-
DAKSH-DEPwD portal and the Hybrid Treasury Single Account (TSA) model, the 
continued shortfall in achieving even 25% of the set targets is a matter of serious 
concern. With the newly announced Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana, the Committee 
recommend that the Department adopt a more robust, industry-linked approach to 
bridge the gap between training and gainful employment. It is essential that the 
Department prioritize the placement of certified trainees by strengthening 
partnerships with private employers and ensuring that training modules remain 
aligned with current market demands. The Committee further suggest that the 
targets for the 2026–27 fiscal year be monitored quarterly to ensure that the 
transition to the new Yojana results in a measurable improvement in both the 
number of beneficiaries trained and their subsequent placement in sustainable 
employment”. 
19. The Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, in their Action Taken 
Reply, have submitted as under:- 
 
 “The recommendations of the Committee have been noted. The new skilling scheme 
is being designed to address diverse skilling needs and aspirations of all types of 
Persons with Disabilities, through industry-linked and outcome-based livelihood 
models. The Department envisages to regularly monitor and review the scheme”. 

15 
 
 
NATIONAL ACTION PLAN FOR SKILL TRAINING UNDER SIPDA 
20. The Committee note the reply of the Department. However, considering 
the substantial shortfall in achievement of training targets over the past three 
years, the Committee are of the view that the reply does not adequately 
indicate how the proposed Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana will address the 
deficiencies observed under the existing programme. The Committee, 
therefore, reiterate their recommendation that the Department ensure strong 
industry linkage, measurable training and placement outcomes, and a robust 
monitoring mechanism under the new Yojana so that it results in a significant 
improvement in both skill development and employment opportunities for 
persons with disabilities. 
Recommendation (Para No. 9.9) 
21. The Committee, in their original Report, had recommended as follows:- 
 “The Committee note that the National Institutes and Composite Regional Centres 
(CRCs) play a crucial role in human resource development, research and 
rehabilitation services in the disability sector. However, the Committee observe that 
a significant gap exists between the sanctioned strength and the present staff 
strength in many of the Institutes and CRCs. For instance, institutions such as 
SVNIRTAR, Cuttack (115 against 197 sanctioned posts) and AYJNISHD, Mumbai 
(121 against 173 sanctioned posts) are functioning with substantial vacancies.

(Recommendation Para No.10.5) 
The Committee welcome the introduction of the two new schemes during 2026–27, namely 
Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana and Divyangjan Sahara Yojana, with allocations of ₹200 crore 
and ₹100 crore respectively, aimed at strengthening skill development and the assistive 
device ecosystem for persons with disabilities. However, the Committee note that the 
detailed modalities of implementation, including operational guidelines and physical 
targets, are still under formulation. The Committee are of the view that early finalisation 
of the scheme framework is essential to ensure that the budgetary allocations translate 
into tangible outcomes. The Committee therefore recommend that the Department 
expedite the finalisation of guidelines and put in place a clear implementation framework 
with measurable targets relating to skill training, employment opportunities and assistive 
device distribution, so that the schemes are operationalised effectively and deliver 
meaningful benefits to Divyangjan. 
Reply of the Government 
  
The recommendation of the Committee has been noted for compliance. The present status 
of the two newly launched schemes are given below: 
  
Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana : To ensure a meaningful scheme framework, extensive 
stakeholder consultations have been conducted. Efforts are being made to expedite the 
process and operationalise the scheme by the targeted date of 01.09.2026. The 
Department plans to implement the Divyangjan Kaushal Yojana initially in a pilot mode for 
the first year. A comprehensive proposal for the remaining four years of the financial cycle 
will be formulated based on an objective evaluation of the pilot's outcomes.

that DSY scheme is operationalised effectively and that the allocated funds translate into 
tangible and meaningful benefits for Divyangjan.
