INTRODUCTION 
  
  
            I, the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having been 
authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this 
Hundred and Forty Sixth Report of the Committee. 
. 
The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on  31.03.2003. 
  
1.3       The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the Table of the 
Rajya Sabha during its 197th session have been perused by the Committee in the light of 
the provisions of the Acts under which these were framed and also with a view to seeing 
whether these have been laid within the prescribed time-frame and properly.  The 
Committee’s observations/recommendations are contained in the report that follows.

Report 
  
I 
Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of  Rajya Sabha 
  
            During the 197th Session of the Rajya Sabha, 233 Statutory Orders were laid on 
the Table of the House by various Ministries/Departments.  The details of the Gazette 
Notifications in which these were published and the date of their laying on the Table of 
the Rajya Sabha have been indicated in the Ministry-wise statement placed at 
Annexure. 
  
2.         During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted some 
cases where there has been a considerable time lag between the date of notification of 
the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period of 
delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the Committee’s guidelines 
and whether such notifications were accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons 
for delay in laying.  In some of the cases the delay has been quite abnormal.  The 
observations/ recommendations of the Committee in respect of specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows: - 
I           Ministry of Commerce & Industry: 
            The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Commerce and Industry 
have delayed the laying of 3 of their notifications (sl. nos. 6 to 8 of the Annexure) out of 
the 8 notifications laid by them during the Session. Appropriately, notification at sl. no. 
8 could have been laid during the Budget (195th) Session of 2002 and the notifications at 
sl. nos. 6 and 7 during the Monsoon (196th) Session. The Ministry have also failed to 
append the delay statement along with these 3 notifications as has been recommended 
by the Committee. There had been cases of delay in laying by this Ministry during the 
196th and 197th Sessions also in pursuance to which the Committee had recommended 
the Ministry to take suitable remedial action. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of 
Commerce & Industry would take prompt remedial action and comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of subordinate legislation. 

  
II         Ministry of  Communication & Information Technology: 
             
The Committee regrets to note that out of the 5 notifications laid by this Ministry, there 
has been an inordinate delay of one year and nine months in case of notification at sl. 
no. 15 and 6 months in case of notification at sl. no. 17. The Ministry have even failed to 
append the delay statement in the former case where the delay has been very large. The 
Committee has come across some instances of delay in case of this Ministry in the past 
sessions also like in the 191st, 194th and 196th Sessions of the Rajya Sabha. The 
Committee would like the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology to 
take necessary corrective measures so as to ensure the compliance of the Committee’s 
recommendations in the matter of laying of subordinate legislation. 
  
III        Ministry of Environment & Forests: 
  
The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Environment & Forests did not 
comply with the schedule for laying in case of 10 out of 12 notifications laid during the 
197th Session and the delay ranging from 1 year to 4 years 4 months as shown in the 
Annexure (sl. nos. 24 to 33).  The Committee suggests that the Ministry of Environment 
& Forests should take immediate corrective action and ensure that henceforth the 
Subordinate Legislation is laid before the Parliament on time. 
  
IV        Ministry of Finance & Company Affairs: 
  
            The Committee is extremely unhappy to note that the Ministry of Finance and 
Company Affairs have not complied with the schedule for laying of the notifications as 
prescribed by the Committee in its 131st Report for the eighth consecutive session of the 
Rajya Sabha. The Ministry have laid 129 rules/regulations during the 197th Session and 
there has been delay in case of 77 ranging from 4 months to 12 months as shown in the 
Annexure and delay statement has been appended only in 8 cases.  
            The Committee, being concerned over the persistent delay in the laying of 
Subordinate Legislation by the Ministry of Finance eversince it had started examining 
this aspect from the 191st Session onwards on a regular basis, heard the Secretary, 
Ministry of Finance along with the Secretaries of different Departments under the 
Ministry on the question of non-compliance of the schedule as laid down by the 
Committee in this behalf on the 9th January 2003. During the course of the oral 
evidence, the Finance Secretary, while admitting delay in laying, assured the Committee 
that they were taking appropriate remedial action in order to avoid such delays for 
future. The Committee hopes that there would be concerted efforts on the part of 

Ministry would help to eliminate such instances of delay in laying of Subordinate 
Legislation in future.  
  
V         Ministry of Health & Family Welfare: 
  
            The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the 5 notifications laid (sl. nos. 
164-168), there has been a delay of 4 months in laying of two notifications and no delay 
statement has been annexed therewith.  There were instances of delay in a number of 
cases during the 192nd and 194th to 196th Sessions also. The Committee hopes that the 
Ministry of Health & Family Welfare would take adequate steps to ensure compliance 
with the time schedule as laid down by the Committee for the laying of Subordinate 
Legislation in future. 
  
VI        Ministry of Home Affairs: 
  
            The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of 4 months and 7 
months in case of laying two notifications (sl. nos. 171 and 174) out of 6 notifications 
laid.  The delay statement was enclosed only in one case (Sl No. 174). There were 
instances of delay in a number of cases during the 192nd and 194th to 196th Sessions also. 
Keeping in view the fact that there had been delay in laying in a fairly large number of 
cases from the 191st Session onwards of the Rajya Sabha eversince the Committee have 
taken up such a scrutiny on a regular basis, the Committee heard the oral evidence of 
the Secretary, Home Affairs on the 9th January 2003. During the course of the oral 
evidence, the Committee was assured that the Ministry were taking suitable action to 
avoid recurrence of such delays in future. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of 
Home Affairs would be able to keep to their promise.  
  
VII          Ministry of Human Resource Development: 
  
            The Committee notes with regret that there is a delay in laying in all the three 
notifications laid by the Ministry of Human Resource Development during the 197th 
Session (sl nos. 175 to 177). There had been instances of delay in laying in a number of 
cases during the 191st and 194th to 196th Sessions also. The Committee would like the 
Ministry to streamline their procedures so as to obviate such instances in future. 
  
VIII     Ministry of Information & Broadcasting :  

  
            The Committee is distressed to note that out of the 12 notifications laid during 
the 197th Session, 10 have been delayed by period ranging from 6 to 7 months.  Further, 
delay statement has been enclosed only with four notifications  (sl. nos. 181, 183, 187 & 
188).  The Committee expects that the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting would 
take appropriate steps to comply with time schedule laid by the Committee for the 
laying of Subordinate Legislation. 
  
IX        Ministry of Labour: 
  
            The Committee regrets to note that out of the 5 notifications laid by the Ministry, 
there has been delay in 2 cases (sl. nos. 193 & 194) by 5 and 6 months, respectively, and 
no delay statement has been appended. There were instances of delay in a considerable 
number of cases during the 191st, 192nd, 194th and 196th sessions also.  The Committee 
recommends the Ministry to stick to the time schedule as recommended by the 
Committee in its 135th Report in laying the   Subordinate Legislation before the 
Parliament. 
  
X         Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions:  
  
             The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel,  Public 
Grievances and Pensions have  laid 16 rules/regulations  during the 197th Session out of 
which 12 have been delayed by period ranging from 7 months to 19 months. There were 
instances of delay during the 191st to 193rd Sessions also. The Committee expects the 
Ministry to be cautious in such matters and ensure compliance with time schedule for 
laying the notifications in future. 
  
XI        Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas: 
  
            The Committee notes that two notifications laid by the Ministry of Petroleum  & 
Natural   Gas have been delayed by 9 to 10 months (sl. nos. 213 & 214).  The Ministry 
have also not enclosed the delay statement giving the reasons for the delay in 
laying.  The Committee expects the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas to exercise 
caution in such matters and ensure that the schedule as prescribed by the Committee in 
the matter of laying of Subordinate Legislation framed under various Acts of 
Parliament is followed meticulously in future. 
  

XII  Ministry of Power: 
  
            The Committee regrets to note the Ministry of Power have laid 3 notifications 
during the 197th Session out of which 2 have been delayed by 4 to 5 months (sl. nos. 215 
and 216).  The Committee expects the Ministry to be cautious in such matters and 
ensure compliance with time schedule for laying the rules/regulations in future. 
  
XIII  Ministry of Shipping:   
  
            The Committee notes that there has been an extreme delay of 2 years and 7 
months on the part of the Ministry in laying 2 of its notifications (sl nos. 230 & 
231).  There were instances of delay in a  
  
number of cases during the 194th and 196th Sessions also. The Committee would like the 
Ministry of Shipping to take remedial measure to prevent recurrence of such action and 
also ensure that henceforth the Subordinate Legislation is laid before the Parliament on 
time. 
  
3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate 
steps to avoid delay in laying of subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, 
wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of Government of India, 
particularly, the defaulting ones as enumerated above the following recommendations 
as contained in its 131st Report wherein a time schedule for the laying of subordinate 
legislation has been prescribed as follows: 
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid  (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following the date of publication of the Order 
in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the publication of 
the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of 
publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before 
the expiry of  the  Session immediately following the said Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply with this 
schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued under various Acts of Parliament 
on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
  
 4.        The Committee also reiterates its following observations made in its 135th Report 
regarding appending of delay statement where there has been delay in the laying: 

 “ The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there 
is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department should 
invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as to 
apprise the Parliament of the causes of delay.  The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this,   since   the intention is that through the 
process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identifying 
bottlenecks and devising remedial measures”.
