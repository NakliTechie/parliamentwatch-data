2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note d that there was a considerable time 
lag between issue  of Notifications and their layin g in some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 150 delayed 
Notifications, there were 116 Notifications where n o delay statement was appended to. However, 

this time also the bulk of delayed Notifications we re from the Ministry of  Road Transport and 
Highways which comes to around 41.3% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i)  Ministry of Commerce and Industry 
 (Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion) 
  
 Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of I ndustrial Policy and Promotion)  
have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 30 to 33), out of which one Notification (Sl. No. 31) has been 
delayed by 6 months. There is no delay statement appended with the Notification. 
  
 The Committee finds that Ministry of Commerce and I ndustry (Department of 
Industrial Policy and Promotion) have not only dela yed the laying of Notifications but have 
also failed to append delay statement thereto. The Committee, in the absence of delay 
statements, are not able to know the actual reasons  behind the delay in laying of 
Notifications. The Committee have clearly mentioned  in its 135 
th  Report that delay 
statements should invariably be appended whenever t here is a delay in laying of 
Notifications. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon the Ministry of Commerce and 

Industry to strictly adhere to the time schedule as  prescribed in Committee’s Report, and, 
in case of any delay in laying, a delay statement should invariably be appended thereto. 
 
 (ii) Ministry of Communications  
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
The Ministry of  Communications (Department of Tele communications) have laid 5 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 36-40). Out of these,  2  N otifications (Sl. Nos. 37 & 38) have been 
delayed. The delay period ranges from 11 months to 1 year and 3 months. All delayed 
Notifications are accompanied by delay statement. T he reason of delay given by the Ministry is 
that due to some procedural delays on account of ge tting the printing version from the 
Government Press, these could not be laid on the Table of the House. 
 
The reasons cited by Ministry of Communications (De partment of 
Telecommunications) i.e. Notifications could not be  laid because the printed copies of 
Notifications were not received from the Government  of India Press in time due to some 
procedural reasons, is an oft-repeated excuse. Many  Ministries have been citing this as a 
reason for delay, quite often. It reflects the lack adaisical approach on the part of the 
Ministry. On account of delay in getting printed co pies of Notifications from Printing Press 
so as to adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in Committee’s 131 
st    Report, they may 
obtain the G.S.R./S.O. number from the Government o f India Press in advance and print 

the same in a cyclostyle form so as to lay the Noti fication in time. This had also been 
suggested by the Ministry of Urban Development, the  controlling Ministry for Government 
of India Press. 
   
(iii)  Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
  
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 30 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 49 to 78). Six of these 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 52, 53  and 57 to 60) have been delayed from  4 months  to 1 year and 2 
although months with delay statements appended to t hem. The reason given for delay by the 
Ministry is as under:- 
 
“The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (Salar ies and Allowances and other 
terms and conditions of service of the Chairperson and other Members) Rules, 2015 are required 
to be laid before the Houses of Parliament. 
The said rules could not be laid before both the Ho uses of Parliament as constitution of 
National Company Law Appellate Tribunal was notified on 01.06.2016. 
Hence the delay.” 
 
The Committee hopes that the Ministry would avoid such instances of delay in laying 
of subordinate legislation in future and would remo ve lacunai, if any administrative or 

otherwise, causing delay. The Committee, accordingl y, also advises the Ministry to avoid 
recurrence of delays due to administrative reasons. 
 
 
(iv) Ministry of Electronics and Information Techno logy 
 (Department of Electronics and Information Technol ogy)  
 
 Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology  (Department of Electronics and 
Information Technology) have laid 6 Notifications ( Sl. Nos. 79 to 84). Only 1 Notification (Sl. 
No. 79) has been delayed by 5 months and there is no delay statement appended to it.  
  
   The Committee notes that though there has been dela y in laying of one Notification, 
no delay statement has been attached to it. The Com mittee expects the Ministry to be 
careful and responsible in these matters henceforth .  The Ministry should ensure such 
compliance in future with due caution so that such recurrence does not take place. 
 
 
 
 
(v) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Cha nge 
 

 Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change  have laid 3 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
85 - 87),  out of which 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 8 5 & 87) have been delayed. Both of the delayed 
Notifications have been delayed by 5 months to 7 Mo nths and there is no delay statement 
attached to any of these delayed notifications.  
 
Two third of the Notifications laid during the Sess ion have been delayed. The 
Committee is not happy over the delay of 5 months a nd 7 months respectively in laying of  
two Notifications  by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. Further 
there is no delay statement appended thereto giving  details as to what caused such a delay. 
The Committee, therefore, impresses upon the Minist ry to take care that such instances of 
delay do not recur in future and in case of any del ay in laying, a delay statement should 
invariably be appended them so that it is apprised of the reasons therefor. 
 
(vi) Ministry of Finance 
(a)  (Department of Economic Affairs) 
  
Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs ) have laid 21 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 88 to 108), out of which 4 Notifications have been delayed.  Out of 4 delayed Notifications,  
2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 94 & 95) have  been delay ed each by 5  months and have no delay 
statements attached to them. Another 2 Notification s (Sl. Nos. 107 & 108) have been delayed  by 

1 year and 3 months in each case with delay stateme nt appended thereto. The reasons forwarded 
by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 
 “Pursuant to the publication of the Depositories (P rocedure for Holding Inquiry and 
Imposing Penalties by Adjudicating Officer) Amendme nt Rules, 2015 and the Securities 
Contracts (Regulation) (Procedure for Holding Inqui ry and Imposing Penalties by Adjudicating 
Officer) Amendment Rules, 2015 in Gazette of India on 19.08.2015, the concerned Department 
were required to lay down the same in both the Hous es o Parliament. However, inadvertently, the 
same were not put up by the section concerned for laying down in Parliament. The same is highly 
regretted.” 
 
 The Committee does not accept the reply forwarded by the Ministry of Finance 
(Department of Economic Affairs)  that the Notifica tions could not be laid on time due to 
inadvertence is not putting up by the section conce rned. It only reflects the lack of 
seriousness on the part of the Ministry in fulfilli ng its constitutional responsibility to 
Parliament. The Committee expects the Ministry to b e more careful about laying of its 
papers and see that persons responsible for this ar e held accountable so that there are no 
such recurrences. 
 
 
(b)  (Department of Financial Services) 

 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Servi ces) have laid 23 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos.109 to 131).  Out of these 13 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 109 to 118, 121 & 126-127) have been  
delayed from  5 months to 12 months. Moreover, ther e is no delay statement appended to any of 
these delayed Notifications.  
 
 The Committee is unhappy with the delay in laying o f Notifications by the Ministry 
of Finance (Department of Financial Services) particularly Notifications at Sl. Nos. 109, 110 
and 127 where delay period spans from  a period of  5 months to one year. In the absence of 
any delay statement, Committee would not make out t he reasons of delay and suggest 
remedial measures for rectifying it. The Committee,  therefore, directs that the Ministry of 
Finance (Department of Financial Services) may appe nd delay statements whether there is 
delay in laying of Notifications and avoid recurrence of such delays in future.  
 
 (c) (Department of Revenue) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) have la id 98 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 132 to 
229) on the Table of the House. Out of these 8 Noti fications (Sl. Nos. 184-189 & 215-216) have

 The Committee observes that had the Department of Revenue exercised a bit of 
caution, they would have avoided such instances of delay as delay period is not

such instances of delay and in case there is a dela y in the laying of any Statutory 
Notifications, the same should be accompanied by a delay statement while it is  being laid. 
The Department’s attention is drawn in this regard towards recommendations as contained 
in 135 th  Report of the Committee.

(viii) Ministry of Home Affairs 
  
Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 24 Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 251 to 274). Out of  these,  
3  Notifications  have been delayed. Whereas 2 Noti fications (Sl. Nos. 262 & 263) have been 
delayed each by 9 months and are accompanied with a  delay statement, another one Notification 
(Sl. No. 256) has been delayed by 7 months and ther e is no delay statement appended to it. The 
reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
“(i) Directorate General (Fire Service, Civil Defen ce & Home Guards) Fire Adviser, 
Group ‘A’ Gazetted, Non-Ministerial Posts Recruitment (Amendment) Rules, 2016 (GSR 55); 
(ii) Directorate General (Fire Service, Civil Defen ce & Home Guards) Deputy Fire 
Adviser, Group ‘A’ Gazetted, Non-Ministerial Posts Recruitment (Amendment) Rules, 2016 
(GSR 56). 

The above Rules as published in the Gazette of Indi a were required to be laid before both 
the Houses of Parliament while it is in session lat est by July, 2016. The said rules could not be 
laid before both the Houses of the Parliament in ti me due to late receipt of the notified 
Recruitment Rules from the Government Press. 
2. The delay is deeply regretted. The above notific ations are now being laid before 
both the Houses of Parliament in the current session.” 
 
The Committee takes serious note of the fact that d elay was caused due to late 
receiving of  printed copies of Notifications from Government of India Press. Time and 
again, the Ministries have been taking recourse to this plea very frequently. This possibility  
should have been assessed well before and adequate arrangements should have been made 
in respect thereof. The Committee advises the Minis try of Home Affairs to take care so that 
such delays do not recur in future and also to reso rt to providing cyclostyled copies for 
laying after obtaining a G.S.R./S.O. no. from the Government of India Press. 
 
 
 
 
(ix) Ministry of Human Resource Development

 Ministry of Ministry of Human Resource Development  (Department of Higher Education)

 This Committee regrets to say that there is delay in laying of almost 50% of

six months to four years and   one Notification has  a delay period of more than one year.

inordinate delay. The delay period is quite astonis hing. Furthermore, there is no delay 
statement appended to any of these delayed Notifica tions which is again surprising. No 
delay statement coupled  with inordinate delay is q uite enigmatic to the Committee. The 
Committee makes a point that Ministry should have a ppended a delay statement so as to 
apprise the Parliament of the reasons of delay in l aying of Notifications on the Table of the 
House. However, the  Ministry of Human Resource Dev elopment (Department of Higher 
Education) should also strive to cut short on the e xcessive delay period in laying of the 
Notifications. The Committee reiterates its recommendations contained in its 131 
st  and 135 th  
Reports emphasizing timely laying of  Notifications   on the Table of the House and 
appending a delay statement where there has been a delay. Not appending a delay 

statement is akin to infringement of Parliament’s r ight to know the facts of the case. The 
Committee takes a serious note of the matter and urges the Ministry to take care in future.

(xiii) Ministry of Railways 
 
 Ministry of Railways  have laid 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 350 & 351). Out  of these 
1 Notification has been delayed by five months and has a delay statement appended with it. The 
reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “Notification G.S.R. No. 631 (E) was published by Government of India Press, Mayapuri, 
Delhi-110064 on 23 
rd  June, 2016. The copies of Gazette Notification cou ld not be laid on the 
Table of Parliament as there was ambiguity whether the notification is required to be placed in 
Parliament as the Railways (Amendment) Act, 2008 is  silent about its laying in Parliament. 
However, the delay in laying the Notification in Parliament may be condoned. 
 G.S.R. No. 631 (E) dated 23 rd  June, 2016 is being laid on the Table of both the Houses of 
Parliament during the ensuing Winter Session of Parliament, 2016” 
  
 In this regard the Committee would like to draw at tention of the Ministry to its 10 
th  
Report presented to the House on 24.5.1971, wherein  the Committee recommend that the 
revised formula should be incorporated not only in new legislation but also in the existing 
Acts as and when Bills to amend them are brought be fore Parliament. The Ministry should 

also incorporate the new formula in the Bills pendi ng before Parliament. This will, in the 
opinion of the Committee, bring about uniformity in the matter expeditiously.  
 
 "Every rule made by the Central Government under this Act shall be laid, as soon as 
may be after it is made, before each House of Parli ament, while it is in session, for a 
total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more 
successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of t he session immediately following the 
session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any 
modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the 
rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modi fied form or be of no effect, as the 
case may be; so, however that any such modification  or annulment shall be without 
prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule."

3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministrie s/Departments to take immediate and 
concrete steps to ensure timely and proper laying o f subordinate legislation. The Committee, 
accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all M inistries/Departments of the Government of 
India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the follo wing recommendations, as contained in its 7 
th , 
10 th , 131 
st 
, 135 
th 
, 152 
nd ,  192 nd  , 201 st , 212 th  and 213 th  Reports: 
 
 “The Committee desires that for the convenience of  the public and Members, 
Ministries should henceforth send copies of 'order'  duly authenticated by the Ministers 
concerned, incorporating therein all the correction s and amendments issued till date of 
laying such 'orders' instead of laying the uncorrec ted copy along with corrigenda. The 
Committee feels that to obviate any legal or techni cal difficulty, Ministries should examine 
whether there is any necessity of assigning a separ ate G.S.R. No. to a corrigendum at the 
time of its publication in the Gazette and whether such a corrigendum should not be 
published without assigning any G.S.R. No. The Comm ittee would, therefore, like to 
impress upon all Ministries of the Government of In dia that the present practice of laying 
the rules without incorporating corrigenda in the body of the rules is not a healthy one. The 

Committee hopes that the Ministries will ensure sup ply of only corrected copies of the rules 
for laying on the Table so that they depict a true and correct picture and serve the desired 
purpose."

 The Committee approve the following draft revised f ormula for laying of Statutory 
Rules before both Houses of Parliament which the Mi nistry of Law and Justice propose to 
incorporate in all future legislation which provide  for making of rules by the 
CentralGovernment:— 
 
 "Every rule made by the Central Government under t his Act shall be laid, as soon as 
may be after it is made, before each House of Parli ament, while it is in session, for a 
total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more 
successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of t he session immediately following the 
session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any 
modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule 
shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case 
may be; so, however that any such modification or a nnulment shall be without 
prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule." 
  

The Committee recommend that the revised formula sh ould be incorporated not only in 
new legislation but also in the existing Acts as an d when Bills to amend them are brought 
before Parliament. The Ministry should also incorpo rate the new formula in the Bills 
pending before Parliament. This will, in the opinio n of the Committee, bring about 
uniformity in the matter expeditiously.

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Committee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force of an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Mi nistry is not able to adhere to this 
time limit, it should seek extension of time for co mpleting the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
  
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are n ot complying with the Committee’s 
recommendation to complete the process of framing of subordinate legislation contemplated 
under various Acts of Parliament within a period of  six months. At the same time, the 
Committee also does not receive requests for extens ion of time for completing the rule-

making process in terms of its recommendations as a bove from most of the 
Ministries/Departments. The Committee takes a serio us note of the prevailing situation in 
which the Ministries/Departments are neither able t o complete the task of framing 
rules/regulations under different Acts within the g iven six months’ period nor do they suo-
motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. The Committee further notes 
that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/reg ulations it has taken an initiative to 
monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legi slation in respect of all legislations 
brought about by different Ministries/Departments o f Government of India from the year 
2003 onwards with a view to ensure that the framing  of subordinate legislation is not 
delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the res ponses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its 
recommendation that the Ministries/Departments shou ld ensure putting in place 
subordinate legislation positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the 
Act. The Committee further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this 
deadline, they should invariably approach the Committee for seeking extension of time. The 
Committee further recommends that in cases where th e time limit of six months is not 
adhered to, they should, while laying rules/regulat ions before the Parliament invariably lay 
a statement explaining the reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond 

six months so as to keep the Parliament apprised of  the compliance of the Parliament’s 
mandate  conferred upon the Executive.”
