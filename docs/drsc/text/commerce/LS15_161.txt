2.                During the course of scrutiny of these Notifications/Orders/Schemes, the 
Committee has noted some cases of delay upto  1 year between the date of notification of 
the Order and the date of the laying.  The annexed statement also indicates the period of 
delay in the case of notifications which have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed 
by the Committee and also whether su ch notifications were accompanied by any 
statement indicating the reason for the delay in laying.   The 
observations/recommendations of the Committee in respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows:- 
  
(i)                Ministry of  Agriculture:- 
  
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Agriculture have delayed 
the laying of all their notifications (Sl. Nos. 01-05). The delay ranges between 3 months to 
7 months.  Further, the Ministry have not appended delay statements w ith any of them. 
The Committee recommends that the  Ministry of Agriculture  should strictly adhere to 
the time schedule as recommended by it in its 131 st Report in laying of the Subordinate 
Legislation before Parliament and in case of any delay in laying of the notifications, a 
delay statement should  invariably be appended therewith. 
  
(ii)             Ministry of Commerce & Industry:-  
  
.The Committee notes with regret   that the Ministry of Commerce & Industry 
have delayed the laying of 10 notifications (Sl-Nos. 10-18,21) out of the 16 notifications 
laid by them and have not appended delay statements with any of them.The Committee 
recommends that the  Ministry of Commerce & Industry   should strictly adhere to the 
time schedule as recomme nded by it in its 131 st Report in laying of the Subordinate 
Legislation before Parliament and in case of any delay in laying of the notifications, a 
delay statement should also invariably be appended therewith. 
(iii)     Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution:- 
  
The Committee finds that the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public 
Distribution have delayed laying of 03 notifications (Sl -Nos. 36 -37,39) out of the 09 
notifications laid by them and have not appended delay statements with any of them. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public 
distribution should avoid such delays in future and append delay statement wherever the 

schedule as laid down by the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabh a in its 
131st Report is not adhered to. 
 (iv)     Ministry of Finance:- 
                
           The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Finance have delayed the laying 
of 23 of their notifications (Sl.Nos.52,57,98 -112,119-120,173,181-183) ou t of the 165 
notifications laid by them during the   Session. Further,the Ministry have not appended 
delay statements with any of them. The Committee recommends that the Ministry   of 
Finance should stick to the time schedule as recommended by it in its 131 st  Report 
regarding laying of the Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and also append delay 
statements where there is a delay in laying of the notifications. 
  
(v)       Ministry of Human Resource Development:- 
  
           The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Human Resource Development 
have delayed the laying of 03 notifications (Sl. Nos. 234 -236) out of the 06 notifications 
laid by them during the Session. Further, delay statement has been appended only with 
the notification at Sl. No. 236. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would take care in 
timely laying of the notifications and append delay statement in case where the laying of 
Subordinate Legislation is delayed. 
  
(vi)          Ministry of Labour & Employment:- 
  
               The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Labour  & Employment have 
delayed laying of 07 notifications (Sl.Nos. 240,242 -245,247 and 249) out of the 10 
notifications  laid during the   Session. In all these 07 delayed notifications, no delay 
statement has been appended therewith. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of 
Labour & Employment would continue with their efforts to avoid delay in laying of the 
notifications and in case of any delay actually taking place in laying, a delay statement 
shall be appended in all such cases.   
(vii)     Ministry of  Railways :- 
  
               The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Railways have delayed the 
laying of 03 of their notifications (Sl Nos. 266-268) out of the 04 notifications laid by them 
and have not appended delay statements with any of them. The Committee hopes that the 
Ministry would take care in timely laying of the notifications and append delay statement 
in case where the laying of  Subordinate Legislation is delayed. 
  
(viii)               Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways:- 
  
               The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & 
Highways have delayed the laying of 23 notifications (Sl. Nos. 284-294,306-317) out of the 
123 notifications laid by them during the   Session. The delay ranges from 4 months to 6 
months. Further, the Ministry have   appended delay statements with only 15 of the 
delayed notifications (Sl. Nos. 284-294,307-309,313) The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways should make all out efforts to avoid 
such delays in the laying of the notifications and in case of any delay, a delay statement 
be appended in order to apprise the Parliament of the true picture. 
  
3.The Committee further   notes that there has been delay in laying of a 
few  notifications  by the Ministries of Agro & Rural Industries (Sl. No.06), 
Communications & Information Technology (Department of Posts) (Sl. Nos 31 -32), 

Health & Family Welfare (Sl . No. 218), Home Affairs(Sl. Nos. 226,229), Power (Sl. No 
253)and Textiles (Sl No. 394).The delay ranges from 4 months to 1 year. Further, delay 
statements have not been appended with any of these delayed notifications. The 
Committee hopes that these Minis tries would take care to lay notifications in time and 
append delay statement   in cases where the laying of Subordinate Legislation is delayed. 
                                                                                                                                                      
                                                                                                          4.The Committee heard 
oral evidence tendered on the 5th October, 2005 by four Ministries/Departments, namely, 
the Ministries of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways (Department of Road Transport 
& Highways), Human Resource Development (Department of Secondary & Higher 
Education), Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (Department of Personnel & 
Training), and Information & Broadcasting which had been consistently defaulting in the 
compliance of the Committee’s recommendations regarding timely laying of 
rules/regulations framed under various  Central Acts. During the course of oral evidence, 
the Ministries were not only enquired on the delay in laying their Subordinate Legislation 
on the Table of the House but were also asked to explain their failure to append delay 
statements along with the notifications that had been delayed. The 
Ministries/Departments came up wit h some explanations that cause delay in laying but 
they could not furnish any explanation for not appending delay statements in the required 
cases. The representatives promised to take immediate corrective steps to ensure timely 
laying of Subordinate Legis lation and the appending of delay statements without fail in 
the required cases.  
  
5.In pursuance of the said oral evidence, the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & 
Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training) furnished an Action Taken Note 
detailing the steps taken by them for plugging the loopholes in the timely laying of 
Subordinate Legislation. The steps detailed included publishing of all the statutory rules/ 
orders/ notifications of the Ministry in the "Extraordinary Gazette" for obviating de lay 
in obtaining GSR Number/ Gazette copies from the Government of India press and 
introduction of a register of the notifications to be laid before Parliament which would be 
scrutinized at the level of the Deputy Secretary/Director once a week during Parl iament 
Session and once in a fortnight during inter -session period. Similarly, the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport and Highways (Department of Road Transport & Highways) 
have also issued guidelines for timely laying of Subordinate Legislation by their  
Department. It includes obtaining of 80 copies in place of one copy by the officer 
concerned, processing of notifications for laying at a single point in the Ministry and the 
monitoring of the process centrally in the Ministry at the level of the signing authority. 
The Committee recommends that the other defaulting Ministries should take similar and 
other remedial steps to facilitate the timely laying of Subordinate Legislation.  
  
6.The Committee finally impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to ta ke 
immediate steps to avoid delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation and brings to their 
notice the following recommendations, as contained in its 131 st  135th and 152nd  Reports 
:- 
  
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not 
in Session, during the Session immediately following the date of publication of the 
Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the 
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time la g between 
the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 
clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. 
All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply 

with this schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued under various Acts 
of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 
  
"The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case 
there is a delay in the la ying of Statutory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the 
notifications being laid so as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The 
Committee desires all Ministries/Departments to take a no te of this, since the 
intention is that through the process of formulation of a delay statement the 
Ministry is able to introspect, identifying bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures". 
(135th Report) 
  
“In the opinion of the Committee, every notification  should be assigned a 
distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no 
notification should be published/laid which does not carry a notification number. 
The Committee expects all Ministries/Departments of Government of India to 
exercise caution in this regard. In case where there are more than one notifications 
under a single file, the Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number 
on each notification should be suitably distinguished from one 
another.  The  Committee also impresses upon the Ministry of Urban 
Development to take care of these aspects and ensure that all rules/regulations 
published by them in the Gazette of India do carry distinct notifications/file 
numbers so that they are identifiable at later stage.” 
      (152nd Report)
