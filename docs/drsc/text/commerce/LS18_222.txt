2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that there was a considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 174 delayed 
Notifications, there were 84 Notifications where no delay statement was appended to. However, the 
bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministr y of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 48.2% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation  
  
 Ministry of Civil Aviation have laid one Notificat ion (Sl. No. 12) which is delayed by one 
year. There is no delay statement  appended thereto.  
 
 The Committee is anguished to note that Ministry o f Civil Aviation have delayed the 
laying of their only Notification by one year and t here is no delay statement attached thereto. 
There may be reason for delay in laying the Notific ation but there can’t be reason for not 
attaching a delay statement even after the lapse of  one year. The Committee therefore, 
observes  that the Ministry of Civil Aviation stric tly adheres to the time schedule as 
recommended by Committee  in its 131 
st  Report  in laying of the Subordinate Legislation 

before Parliament. Further, where there is delay in  laying of the notifications, a delay 
statement should also invariably be appended therew ith. The Committee recommends that 
the Ministry may clarify the reasons for delay in l aying and also why the delay statement has 
not been appended to the Notifications.  
  
 
(ii)  Ministry of Communications and Information Technology 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
 Ministry of Communications and Information Technol ogy (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 15 – 16) and one of these (Sl. No. 16) has 
been delayed by 12 months. There is no delay statement appended to the Notification. 
  
The Committee regrets the delay in laying of one of  its Notification on the Table of the 
House. The Committee desires the Ministry of Commun ications and Information Technology 
(Department of Telecommunications)   to have communicated the circumstances causing 
delay in laying of Notification and also to have ap pended delay statement therefor. But 
Committee finds that the Ministry of Communications  and Information Technology 
(Department of Telecommunications)   have not only delayed the laying of one Notificati on 
but also did not append any delay statement thereto . The Committee, therefor, advises the 
Ministry of Communications and Information Technolo gy (Department of 
Telecommunications)   to strictly adhere to the time schedule in laying of the Notifications and 
append a delay statement where there is delay in la ying of Notification on the Table of the 
House. The Committee recommends that the Ministry m ay clarify the reasons for delay in 
laying and also why the delay statement has not been appended to the Notifications. 
 
(iii) Ministry of Environment, Forests and Climate Change 
 
Ministry of Environment, Forests and Climate Change  have laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
19 to 25) on the Table of the House during the Sess ion. Two of them  (Sl. Nos. 20-21) have been 
delayed by 5 to 8 months and no delay statement is appended to both of these delayed 
Notifications. 
 
The Committee takes a strong view of the delay occu rred in tabling the Notifications 
in time and emphasise that the Ministry would avoid  such instances of delay in laying of 
Subordinate Legislation in future. The Committee al so impresses upon the Ministry to take 
note of the fact that in case, there is a delay in the laying of any Statutory Notifications, the 
same should be accompanied by a delay statement whi le it is being laid. The Ministry may 
clarify the reasons for delay in laying and also wh y the delay statement has not been 
appended to the Notifications. 
 
  
(iv) Ministry of Finance 
(a)    (Department of Economic Affairs) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affair s) have laid 20 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
27 to 46).  Out of these 6 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 36 – 39, 44  & 46) have been  delayed by 4 
months to 9 months. There is no delay statement appended to any of these delayed Notifications.  
 

 The Committee are not happy with the delay in layin g of Notifications by the Ministry 
of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs), partic ularly Notifications at Sl. Nos. 36 and 44 
where delay period spans from  a period of  6 month s to 9 months. In the absence of any 
delay statement, the Committee would not know the r easons of delay, whatsoever,  and 
suggest remedial measures for rectifying it. The Committee, therefore, directs the Ministry of 
Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) to append delay statements where there is delay 
in laying of Notifications and avoid recurrence of such delays in future. The Ministry may 
clarify the reasons for delay in laying and also wh y the delay statement has not been 
appended to the Notifications.  
 
(b)    (Department of Financial Services) 
 
Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Servic es) have laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
47 to 53), of which 4 Notifications have been delay ed.  Out of 4 delayed Notifications,  3  
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 50 to 52) have  been delaye d each by 5 months with delay statement 
appended thereto. One Notification which has been d elayed  by 6 months, has no delay statement 
attached to it. The reasons forwarded by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 
“The Notification no. G.S.R. 686 (E), 687 (E) and 6 88 (E) dated 17.09.2014 pertaining to 
reorganization of jurisdiction of Debt Recovery Tri bunal (DRTs) under Debt Recovery Appellate 
Tribunal (DRATs) at Chennai, Kolkata and Mumbai was  published in the Gazette of India, vide 
No. 498 dated September 25, 2014. This Department h as received copy of the Gazette Notification 
from Government of India Press late on 16.02.2015 a nd hence, there was delay in submitting the 
papers in time. However, the inconvenience caused d ue to unintentional delay in submitting the 
papers is deeply regretted and may be condoned. Due  to promptness will be ensured in the future 
while submitting the papers in Parliament.” 
 
 The reasons cited by Ministry of Finance (Departmen t of Financial Services) that the 
Notifications could not be laid down because the copies of Notifications could not be provided 
by the Printing Press in time, seems to be true as the Ministry of Finance (Department of 
Financial Services) have stated that it was uninten tional. Further, they have requested to 
condone the lapse which they have regretted and hav e also assured promptness in laying of 
papers in Parliament in future. In view of the circ umstances delineated by the Ministry  of 
Finance (Department of Financial Services), the Com mittee feels satisfied with their reply 
and decides not to recommend  further and condone  it.  
 
(v) Ministry of Home Affairs 
  
Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 8 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 141 to 148). Out of  these,  one  
Notification (Sl. No. 147) has been delayed by 5 mo nths and  there is no delay statement appended 
to it. 
 
 The Committee are not able to know the reasons beh ind this delay in the absence of 
any delay statement. Therefore, the Committee invit es attention of the Ministry of Home 
Affairs to its recommendation as contained in its 1 35 
th  Report  in this connection, where it 
has been observed to append invariably delay statem ent(s) in case of delay in laying of 
Notifications by  the Ministry. 
 
(vi) Ministry of Law and Justice 

 
The only Notification that the Ministry of Law and Justice (Sl. No. 153) have laid is 
delayed by 12 months. There is no delay statement attached to it. 
 
The Committee expects the Ministry of Law and Justi ce to lay their papers on the 
Table of the House in time. Further, if there is de lay in laying, the Ministry of  Law and 
Justice is excepted, invariably, to append a delay statement. In the instant case there is a 
delay of over one year and Committee has not been a pprised of the reasons of delay. The 
Committee, therefore, recommend that appropriate st eps may be taken to prevent any such 
delay in future for laying of Notifications on the table of the  House by the Ministry of Law 
and Justice and in case of delay, a delay statement  should invariably be appended thereto. 
The Ministry may clarify the reasons for delay in laying and also why the delay statement has 
not been appended to the Notifications.  
 
(vii) Ministry of Personnel and Public Grievances a nd Pensions 
 (Department of Personnel and Training) 
  
Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensio ns (Department of Personnel and 
Training) have laid 14 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 154 to 167). Out of these 3 Notifications have been 
delayed by 7 to 9 months. There is no delay stateme nt appended to 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 155-
156). One Notification has a delay statement append ed (Sl. No. 157) to it. The reasons for delay 
given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
“The Notification could not be laid on the Table of  the House within the specified time 
because of unintentional mistake and such a situati on will be avoided in future by being more 
vigilant, while dealing with such matters”. 
 
The Committee hopes that the Ministry would avoid s uch instances of delay in laying 
of subordinate legislation in future and would keep  off situations, intentional or 
unintentional, causing delay. The Committee have no ted the reply given by the Ministry and 
expects them to keep more vigil to avoid such unreasonable delays in future. 
 
(viii) Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas 
 
 Ministry of Petroleum and Natural have laid 2 Noti fications (Sl. Nos. 168 – 169) out of 
which one notification (Sl. No. 168) is delayed by 6 months and  no  delay statement  is attached  
thereto. 
 
The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation , that in case of delay in laying, 
Notifications should be accompanied with delay stat ement. Attention to recommendation 
contained in 135 
th  Report of the Committee is drawn here. The Ministr y of Petroleum and 
Natural Gas should take care so that repetition of such delay is avoided in future.  
 
(ix) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
 The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways have lai d 196 Notifications
 on the Table of 
the House (Sl. Nos. 170 to 365). A total of 153 Not ifications have been delayed out of which 86 
Notifications are accompanied by delay statements a nd 67 Notifications have no delay statements 
appended with them. The delay period ranges from 4 months to 18 months. The reasons given by 

the Ministry is that it took some time to complete the procedural formalities and, therefore, papers 
could not be laid in time. 
 
 The Committee take note of the fact that Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
have to lay  huge volume of Notifications on the table of the House.  The Committee, however, 
finds that percentage of delayed Notifications out of total Notifications laid is quite high i.e. as 
high as over 78%. This is simply unacceptable. Furt her, Ministry have not appended any 
delay statement with many delayed Notifications/Rul es. Ministry of Road Transport and 
Highways should see or look into the matter serious ly to eliminate recurrence of such delays 
in completing the procedural formalities. The Commi ttee desires that Ministry of Road 
Transport and Highways should adhere to the time sc hedule in laying of Notifications as 
prescribed in Committee’s 131 st  Report and append a delay statement in case there is  delay 
in laying. This has, sort of become a matter of rou tine with this Ministry, its seems. The 
Ministry may clarify the reasons for delay in layin g and also why the delay statement has not 
been appended to the Notifications. 
 
(x) Ministry of Rural Development 
 (Department of Land Resources) 
 
The Ministry of Rural Development (Department of La nd Resources) have laid only one 
Notification (Sl. No. 366) which was delayed by 6 m onths and moreover Ministry did not even 
bother to append a delay statement citing the reasons for such delay on their part. 
 
The Committee is not happy that the only Notificati on that Ministry of Rural 
Development (Department of Land Resources) have lai d, has been delayed by six months. 
Further, there is no reason given for this delay.  This is in contravention of Committee’s 
recommendation given in its 135 Report that Notific ation which are delayed should 
invariably be accompanied by a delay statement. The  Committee would like the Ministry of 
Rural Development (Department of Land Resources) to  take necessary steps to obviate such 
delays and, in case of delay in laying of Notificat ions, a delay statement should invariably be 
accompanied with them. 
 
 
 3.   Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/De partments to take immediate steps to ensure 
timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to 
the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the Gov ernment of India, particularly, the defaulting 
ones, the following recommendations, as contained i n its 131 
st 
, 135

, 152 
nd  ,   192 nd  and 201 st  
Reports: 
 
 “Orders required to be laid before the House shoul d be laid (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following t he date of publication of the Order in the 
Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Sessio n, on the date of the publication of the Order, 
during its continuance and in case the time lag bet ween the date of publication and the date 
of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear  days, before the expiry of the Session 
immediately following the said Session. All the Min istries/Departments of Government of 
India should henceforth comply with this schedule i n the matter of laying of the Orders 
issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. 
(131 st  Report) 
(Presented on 15 December 2000) 

 
 “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized tha t it is essential that in case there is a 
delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concer ned Ministry/Department should 
invariably append a delay statement along with the Notifications being laid so as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee de sires all Ministries/Departments to take 
a note of this, since the intention is that through  the process of formulation of a delay 
statement, the Ministry is able to introspect, iden tify bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135 th  Report) 
(Presented on 27 July 2001) 
 
 “In the opinion of the Committee, every notificati on should be assigned a distinctive 
number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no notification should be 
published/laid which does not carry a notification number. The Committee expects all 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India to ex ercise caution in this regard. In case 
where there are more than one Notifications under a  single file, the Ministries/Departments 
should ensure that the file number on each notifica tion should be suitably distinguished from 
one another.”

 Report) 
(Presented on 26 August 2004)  
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Commit tee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force on an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 

of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 
invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 
while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”

nd 
 Report)

  Rules/Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are laid by dif ferent Ministries on the Table of the 
House under various provisions of parent Acts passe d by Parliament. These Rules/ 
Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are published in the of ficial Gazette before being laid on the 
Table of the House. Even after they are laid, it is  very difficult to get access to these 
Notifications. The Committee recommends that such R ules/Regulations/Orders, etc. laid on 
the Table of the House should be invariably uploade d on the websites of respective Ministries 
immediately on the same day when they have been lai d on the Table of the House, for 
information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access. 
201 
st  Report 
(Presented on 14 th  December, 2012)  
 
 
The Committee observes that the whole process of en acting laws is defeated if rules 
are not framed and laid on the Table of the House i n time.  A reasonable delay could be 
understood but unjustifiably long delays should be avoided and the Ministries should work 
towards improving this position.   The Committee hopes that Ministry would strive to k eep 
the delay to the minimum and take care that Notific ations are laid on the Table of the House 
at the first available opportunity after publication of Notifications. 
  
 
The Committee would like to impress upon the Minist ry that it is their responsibility 
to ensure that copies of the Notifications are prin ted timely and for this purpose, they need to 
evolve a suitable mechanism to ensure that notifica tions are laid in time.   The Committee is 
happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  The 
Committee hopes that it would take action according ly in order to comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications. 
 
 
 

The Ministry must take appropriate step to remove l acuna, if any, administrative or 
otherwise, causing such long delay by being little proactive and prompt.  Further, the 
Ministries should take it up with the Ministry of U rban Development for late receiving of 
printed copies of the Notification.
