Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of the Rajya Sabha

During the 194" Session of the Rajya Sabha, 124 Statutory Orders were
laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha by the various Ministries/Departments.
The details of the Gazette Notifications in which these were published and the
dates of their laying on the Table of the Rajya Sabha have been indicated in the
enclosed Ministry-wise statement (Annexure). Out of these 124 Orders, the
Committee has identified eight Orders for detailed scrutiny. The Orders su
identified have been indicated in the statement.

2. During the course of general scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has
again come across a number of cases where there has been a considerable time
lag between the date of notification of thé Order and the date of its laying. The
annexed statement indicates the ‘period of delay in cases where the
Notifications have not been laid as. per the Committee’s guidelines. In some of
the cases the delay has been unusually long. The _observations/
recommendations of the Committee in this behalf are contained, Ministry-
wise, in the succeeding paragraphs: -

0) Ministry of Civil Aviation

The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Civil Aviation have
delayed the laying of both its notifications (SI. Nos. 1 and 2 of the Statement)
by four and a half months. Appropriately, the Ministry should have laid these
regulations during the Monsoon (193%) Session of the Rajya Sabha. The

Committee directs the Ministry of Civil Aviation to avoid recurrence of
such instances in future.

(ii) Ministry of Finance

The Committee is extremely unhappy to note that the Ministry of Finance
have not complied with the schedule of laying as prescribed by the Committee



in its 131% Report, for the fifth consecutive Session of the Rajya Sabha.

Unfortunately, the violation is at a larger scale this time. Out of the 49
notifications laid by the Ministry, there is a delay in laying of 44 notifications
(SI. Nos. 6 - 12, 15-25, 27-52 of the statement) of up to one year. The
Ministry of Finance had sufficient time to lay them in the Monsoon 2001
(193 Session of the Rajya Sabha and in some case even before. Further, the
Ministry has again not laid any delay statement despite this being repeatedly
pointed out by the Committee in its previous 131°, 132™, 135" and 137"
Reports on the Statutory Orders laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. The
Committee expresses its anguish that the Ministry of Finance have not
taken the Committee’s directions in this behalf seriously. The Committee
directs the Ministry of Finance to explain why they have not been able to
comply the schedule in the matter of laying as prescribed by the
Committee and why they also failed to append the delay statements.

(iii) Ministry of Home Affairs

The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Home Affairs have
delayed the laying of one of their rules mentioned at 51. No. 58 of the
statement by 4% months. There was sufficient time with the Ministry to lay
these rules during the 193 Session of the Rajya Sabha which they failed to
do. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Home Affairs shall in
future comply with the schedule prescribed by the Committee in this
behalf in its 131" Report presented to the House on 15" December 2000.

(iv) Ministry of Human Resource Development

The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Human Resource
Development has again delayed the laying of one of their regulations
appearing at 51. No. 65 of the statement by more than three months. In the
previous Session also, they had delayed the laying of two of their regulations
by over six months. The Committee impress upon the Ministry of Human
Resource Development to pay adequate attention towards the timely
laying of the subordinate legislation and obviate recurrence of such
instances.



3
(v) The Ministry of Rural Development Development

The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Rural
Development has delayed by more than a year the laying of STATUTORY
Orders mentioned at SI. Nos. 123 and 124 of the statement. The Ministry
of Rural Development has also failed to append delay statements white
laying the Statutory Orders. Attention of the Ministry of Rural
Development is drawn to this Committee’s recommendations as
reproduced hereinafter regarding the time schedule prescribed by it in
the matter of laying of subordinate legislation (131" Report presented on
15.12.2000) and the appending of delay statements in cases where there
has been delay in laying of statutory orders (135"" Report presented on
27.7.2001). The Committee would expect the Ministry of Rural
Development to comply with the schedule as prescribed by the Committee
in future.

3. Statements wherever there is a delay ip the laying

For the benefit of the various Ministries/Departments, the Committee
once again reiterates its recommendations/observations as contained in
the 131" and 135" Reports regarding the schedule of laying of
subordinate legislation and the appending of delay in the laying.

131" Report (Recommendations)

“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if
the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately

. following the date of publication of the Order in the Official
Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the
time lag ‘between the date of publication and the date of the
closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry
of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should
henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of
the orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table
of the Rajya Sabha.”



135" Report (Recommendations)

“The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is
essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory
Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department should invariably
append a delay statement along with the notifications being LAID
so as to apprise the Parliament of the causes of delay. The
Committee desires all ries/ Departments to take a note of
this, since the intention is that through the process of formulation
of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identifying
bottlenecks and devising remedial measures”.
