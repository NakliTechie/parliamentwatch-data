INTRODUCTION 
 I, the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, 
having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its 
behalf, do hereby present this Hundred and Fifty Sixth Report of the 
Committee. 
1.2 The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its 
meeting held on 12th April, 2005. 
1.3 The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the 
Table of the Rajya Sabha during its 203rd session (December, 2004) 

have been persued by the Committee in the light of the provisions of 
the Acts under which these were framed and also with a view to seeing 
whether these have been laid properly and within the prescribed time-
frame. The Committee's observations/recommendations are contained 
in the report that follows.

2. During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has 
noted some cases where there has been a considerable time lag between 
the date of notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The 
annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where 
notifications have not been laid as per the Comm ittee’s guidelines and 
whether such notifications were accompanied by a statement indicating 
the reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay has 
been quite abnormal. The observations/recommendations of the 
Committee in respect of the sp ecific Ministries/Departments are as 
follows: 
(i) Ministry of Agriculture 
 (Department of Agriculture and Cooperation) 
 The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the 4 notifications 
laid by the Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Agriculture and 
Cooperation) there has been a delay of upto 1 year in the laying of three 
notifications (Sl. Nos. 2, 3, 4 of the Annexure). The Ministry have not 
appended delay statement with any of these delayed notifications.  The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Agriculture (Department 
of Agriculture and Cooperation) should stick to the time schedule as 
recommended by it in its 131 st Report in the matter of laying of 
Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and in case of any delay, the 
delay statement must also be appended therewith. 
(ii) Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of 
Industrial 
 Policy and Promotion) 
 The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Commerce and 
Industry (Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion) have 
delayed the laying of 7 of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 
17,18,20,21,22,23,and 24) out of the 14 notifications laid by them during 
the session. The Ministry have not appended delay statement with six 
of the delayed notifications where the delay ranges from 6 months to 1 
year and three months. The Ministry have appended delay statement 
only with 1 notification (Sl. No.21) stating therein as follows: 
“The copy of the above notification could not be laid in the earlier 
session as the printed copies of the notification were not received 
in time. Subsequent session i.e. Budget Session of Parliament, 2004 
took place for one week only for vote on accounts. Next Parliament 

Session was convened during July-August, 2004 after constitution 
of the new Lok Sabha. At that time we missed th e laying down of 
the notification due to oversight. The delay is regretted”. 
 The Committee is not convinced with the grounds for delay 
furnished by the Ministry of Commerce & Industry in the delay 
statement. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would take 
necessary corrective measures to avoid delay in laying on such grounds 
and adhere to the schedule prescribed by the Committee in its 131st 
Report. 
(iii) Ministry of Communications and Information 
Technology 
 The Committee is unhappy to note that  out of the 20 notifications 
laid by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, 
there has been a delay in the laying of 15. The Department of 
Telecommunications have delayed the laying of 2 notifications (Sl. Nos. 
26, 27) out of the 4 notifications laid by them and have appended delay 
statement with both. The delay statement appended to notification 
No.414/7/99-FA dated 23rd February, 2004 (Sl. No.26) states: “....After 
notification of the Regulation in the Official Gazette, TRAI sent the 
Regulation to the Department of Telecommunications on 4th October, 
2004. Accordingly, the reason for delay in laying the Regulation on the 
Table of the House is due to delayed submission to the Government”. 
The delay in laying of notification No. 414 - 7/99 - FA dated 25th May, 
2004 (SI. No. 27) has been attributed by the Ministry to the delay in 
making available the copies of the notifications to the Government. The 
Committee hopes that the Government would take necessary corrective 
measures to avoid such in stances of delay which are, even otherwise, 
not very convincing. 
 On the other hand, the Department of Information Technology 
have delayed the laying of 13 notifications (Sl. Nos. 29 to 41) out of the 
16 notification laid by them. The Committee regrets to note that the 
delay in the laying of the notifications by the Department of 
Information and Technology range from 9 months to around a year in 
some cases and upto 1 year and 9 months in others. The Ministry have 
even failed to append the delay statement to the notifications despite so 
much delay. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of 
Communications & Information Technology should avoid such delays 
in future and append delay statement where ever the schedule as laid 

down by the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha in its 
131’t Report is not adhered to. 
(iv) Ministry of Company Affairs 
 The Committee regrets to note that there is a delay of 11 months 
in laying of one out of the five notifications laid by the Ministry of 
Company Affairs (Sl No. 47). The Committee hopes that the Ministry 
of Company Affairs would avoid such instances in future. 
(v) Ministry of Environment and Forests 
 The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Environment 
and Forests have laid 8 notifications during the ‘session and out of them 
4 (SI. Nos. 57, 60, 61, 62) have been delayed by a period of 4 to 7 months. 
Further, the Ministry have appended delay statement with only 2 of the 
delayed notifications (Sl. Nos. 61,62). The Committee observes that the 
delay in  laying of the Rules was “owing to the pre -mature sine die  
adjournment of the House.” The Committee hopes that the Ministry of 
Environment and Forests would take action at the first available 
opportunity so as to obviate such eventualities. 
(vi) Ministry of Finance 
 The Committee notes that out of the 211 notifications laid by the 
Ministry of Finance during the Winter Session, 2004, there has been a 
delay in the laying of 11 notifications (Sl Nos. 65, 66, 98 to 102, 124, 170 
to 172). The Committee further notes that the Ministry have appended 
delay statement in only two cases. While the delay in the laying of 
notification No. LEG -59/46 dated the 22nd July, 2004 (Sl. No.66) has 
been attributed to sine die  adjournment of the House, the delay 
statement appended to notification No 8/5/2004 - IF - I dated the 18th 
June, 2004 (Sl. No. 124) states as follows: 
 “Two notifications were sent to the Controller of Publications, 
Civil Lines, New Delhi vide letter dated 26th May, 2004 for 
publishing in the Gazette of India. One notification under sub -
section (1) of Section 18 of the Industrial Development Bank of 
India Act, 1964 and another notification under sub -section (2) of 
Section 21 of the said Act. 
 Ministry of Urban Development, Dep artment of Publications 
Civil Lines has forwarded one notification vide letter dated 12th 
July, 2004. It was found that only one notification under Section 18 

of the IDBI Act, 1964 had been published and the other notification 
under Section 21 of the said Act had not yet been published. A 
reminder was sent to the Controller of Publication, Civil Lines vide 
letter dated 26th July, 2004. Therefore, these notifications could not 
be laid on the Table  of both Houses of Parliament during 
Budget Session of Parliament. 
 After authentication by Minister of State (R), the notification 
will be laid on the Table of both Houses of Parliament during the 
Winter Session of Parliament.” 
 The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance would avoid 
such instances of delay in  the laying of Subordinate Legislation and 
shall keep the House informed of the reasons for delay in laying 
through delay statements in all cases. 
(vii) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
 The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Health and 
Family Welfare have delayed the laying of one notification (Sl. No. 276) 
by 9 months out of the 2 notifications laid during the Session. The 
Ministry have appended a delay statement. The Committee would like 
to draw the attention of the Ministry of Health an d Family Welfare to 
the Schedule prescribed by the Committee in the matter of laying of 
Subordinate Legislation in its 131st Report (relevant extracts 
reproduced in para 3 of the report) and hopes that the same shall be 
complied with. 
(viii) Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions 
 The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel, 
Public Grievances & Pensions have laid 26 notifications during the 
Session out of which 12 (Sl. Nos. 289 to 300) have been delayed by 
almost 1 year. The Ministry have appended delay statement with all the 
12 delayed notifications. In their delay statement appended to the 
notification at Sl. No. 300 of the Annexure the Ministry have submitted 
as follows: 
 “The Notification which was sent to the Government o f India 
Press for Publication in Part II section 3(i) of the Gazette of India 
on 5th November, 2003 was published in the Gazette on 13th 
December, 2003, i.e. during the Winter Session of Parliament, 
2003. Therefore it was not possible to lay the notificati on during 
the Winter Session of Parliament, 2003. 

 The notification was sent for laying on the Table of the Lok 
Sabha/Rajya Sabha during the Budget Session of Parliament 2004, 
which commenced from the 5th July, 2004 and scheduled to end on 
September, 2004. However, due to pre -mature sine-die 
adjournment of Parliament on 26 th August, 2004, the notification 
could not be laid on the Table of the House”. 
 The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Personnel, 
Public Grievances & Pensions could not avail of the opportunity to lay 
the Subordinate Legislation not only during the Winter Session 2003 
but also during the Budget Session 2004 which actually had 22 sittings. 
In any case, had the Ministry acted promptly they could have 
accomplished their job in the earlier part of the Budget Session 2004. 
The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensions would be meticulous in future in complying 
with time schedule as prescribed by the Committee and take prompt 
action, rather than keeping the work pending towards the end of the 
Session. 
(ix) Ministry of Railways 
 The Committee notes with regret that out of the 13 notifications 
laid by the Ministry of Railways, there has been a delay in the laying of 
11 notifications (Sl. Nos.324 to 334). The Committee expects the 
Ministry of Railways to exercise caution in the matter of timely laying 
of subordinate Legislation and ensure that the schedule as prescribed 
by it in this behalf is followed  meticulously and in case of any delay in 
the laying thereof, a delay statement must be appended there with 
without fail. 
(x) Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways 
 The Committee regrets to note that out of a total of 92 notifications 
laid by M inistry of Shipping, Road Transport and Highways, during 
the 203rd Session there has been a delay in the laying of 13 notifications 
(SI. Nos.336, 343 to 352, 424 and 426). The delay in laying of the 
notifications ranges between 5 months to 1 year. The Comm ittee 
observes that while notification No. D1201 1/1/98 - TAMP at Sl. No. 426 
of the annexure could not be laid during the Budget Session due to sine 
die adjournment of Parliament, the delay statement appended to the 
rest of the delayed notifications does not carry convincing reasons. The 
delay statement appended to one set of notifications (S.O. 751 (E), 

S.0.862 (E) to S.0.868 (E), S.0.885 (E); S.O. 579 (E), S.0.811 (E), S.0.912 
(E), S.0.934 (E), S.0.935 (E) and S.0.938 (E)) states, “It took some time 
to collect the papers and complete the procedural formalities”. For 
example the delay statement appended to G. 5.R.83 (E) dated the 28 th 
January, 2004 states, “Since the requisite number of copies of the 
printed notifications were not received in time, the sa me could not be 
laid on the Table of the House.” The Committee directs the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport and Highways to be more prompt and 
vigilant in the laying of rules/regulations within the stipulated period 
and evolve some systematic procedure to avoid such instances in future. 
3. The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to 
take immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate 
legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice 
of all Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, 
the defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 
131st 135th and 152nd Reports: 
 “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if 
the House is not in Sessi on, during the Session immediately following 
the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if 
the House is in Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, 
during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of 
publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear 
days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said 
Session. All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should 
henceforth comply with this schedule in  the matter of laying of the 
Orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the 
Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 
 “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is 
essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Order s, 
the concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay 
statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that 
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is 
able to introspect, identifying bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135th Report) 

“In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned 
a distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, 
no notification should be published/laid which does not carry a 
notification number. The Committee expects all 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India to exercise caution in 
this regard. In case where there are more than one notification under a 
single file, the Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file 
number on each notification should be suitably distinguished from one 
another. 
(152nd Report)

  SECRETARIAT 
 1. Shri Deepak Goyal, Deputy Secretary 
 2. Shri Ram Behari Gupta, Under Secretary 
 3. Ms. S. Pankajavalli, Committee Officer. 
1. The Committee took up for consideration its draft 156 th Report on Statutory 
Orders laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha during its 203rd session. While considering 
the draft report, the Committee noted persistent default on the part of certain 
Ministries/Departments in the laying of their subordinate legislation despite 
Committees’ recommendations/observations from time to time. The Committee, 
accordingly, desired that the lay ing of Subordinate Legislation by the 
Ministries/Departments over the past 5 to 6 sessions may be reviewed and the 
representatives of major defaulting Ministries/Departments could be invited before 
the Committee for clarifications. With this note, the Comm ittee adopted its Draft 
156th Report. 
2. Next, the Committee took up for consideration its draft 157 th Report on certain 
Rules and Regulations examined by it. The Committee adopted the draft report after 
some discussion. 
3. The Committee decided to present  both the reports to the House on 20 th April 
2005 and authorised its Chairman and in his absence Shri R. Shunmugasundaram 
and Shri Ravula Chandra Sekar Reddy, MPs, to present the report to the House. 
4. The Committee thereafter considered its future programme and decided to hear 
in its next meeting the representatives of the Small Scale Pharmaceuticals 
Manufacturers Association on the Drugs and Cosmetics (8th Amendment) Rules 2001. 
5. The Committee also decided to undertake a study visit to Hyderabad, Bangalore 
and Coimbatore in the last week of May 2005 in connection with the rules and 
regulations presently under its examination. 
6. The Committee then adjourned at 12.15 P.M.
