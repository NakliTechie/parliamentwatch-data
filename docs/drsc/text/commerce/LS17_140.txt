Ministry of Finance Notification S.O. No. 995(E) dated 3rd November, 2000 regarding approval of GIC as Indian
Reinsurer under section 101A(8)(ii) of the Insurance Act, 1938-Implementation of 134th Report of the Committee on
Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha.
 
      The Committee in its 134th Report presented to the House on 25th April, 2001 inter-alia dealt with the matter relating to Notification S. O. No.
995 (E) dated 3rd November 2000 issued by the Ministry of Finance (Annexure I) which had effect of restricting activities of the GIC in the area of
reinsurance only without formally amending the General Insurance Business (Nationalization) Act, 1972.  The Committee after examining the
subject matter observed as follows:
 
“that issue of notification S.O. 995(E) dated 3rd November 2000 which has the effect of restricting the activates of
GIC to the area of reinsurance, without   formally amending the parent Act was not a well considered course of
action on the part of the Ministry of Finance. In Committee’s views, the reasons advanced by the Ministry of
Finance that it is a requirement of law and the IRDA was insisting upon the issue are not convincing enough for
action in such a manner by the Ministry. The Committee is of the view that issue of notification before formally
amending the parent Act was putting the cart before the horse. The Committee also observed that the objectives of
Bill to entrust the task of Indian reinsurer to General Insurance Corporation and delink General Insurance
Corporation from its subsidiaries by carrying out necessary amendments in the GIBN Act 1972 as mentioned in the
Statement of Objects and Reason have already been brought into effect by the Ministry through the notification
followed by the administrative instructions of November 7, 2000. The effect of the notification was to take

 (iii)    After approval of GIC as the “Indian Re-insurer” four public sector general   insurance   companies   have

sector general insurance companies namely General Insurers’ (Public Sector) Association of India has been
formed on the basis of recommendation of the Poddar Committee.
         (v)   GIPSA is a voluntary association of public sector general insurance companies.  Apart from co-ordination
matter, common problems concerns and issues of the four companies are discussed and debated in GIPSA forum for
a common approach.
 
4.     The Committee, not being satisfied with the information furnished by the Ministry of Finance, decided to hear their
representatives with a view to seek further clarifications/elucidations on the subject. Accordingly, the representatives of
the Ministry of Finance along with the representatives of the GIC appeared before the Committee on December 20,
2001. During the course of the oral evidence, the Committee desired to know the present status of the Notification dated
the 3rd November 2000 when the General Insurance Business Nationalization (Amendment) Bill, 2001 was still pending
with the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance. The Committee also wanted to know from
the representatives whether the amendment in the General Insurance Business Nationalization Act, 1972 was still
necessary or the Notification issued by the Ministry was enough and if the amendment in the Act was still necessary,
then how did Government issue the Notification. In other words, the Committee wanted a specific reply from the
representatives whether any further amendment of the GIBN Act was called for under the circumstances. The
Committee also wanted confirmation from the Ministry whether the Administrative Instructions dated 7
th November

2000. Referring to this

General Insurance Business Nationalization  Act,  1972.  Earlier,  the  GIC  and  its  four  subsidiaries were
controlling the entire business of General Insurance. The four subsidiaries had the whole field of General Insurance for
competition amongst them. The situation underwent a change with the coming into the force of the IRDA Act which
resulted in opening up of the insurance sector in the private sector. Keeping in view these changes, the GIC which was a
reinsurer only in respect of the four companies had to assume the role of Reinsurer for the private companies also
coming up in the field and hence it was required that GIC be declared as a Reinsurer under section 101A (8)(ii) of the
Insurance Act, 1938. With a view to explain the basis on which Government had issued the Notification dated the 3
rd
November 2000 followed by Administrative Instructions of 7 th November 2000, the representatives stated that as per

8.        The Committee is not convinced by the comments furnished by the Ministry of Finance with regard to the basis on
which they had issued the Notification dated the 3 rd November 2000 under Section 101A(8)(ii) of the Insurance Act,
1938 as well as the Administrative Instructions dated 7 th November 2000 under Section 23 of the GIBN Act, 1972 with
a view to restrict the activities of GIC to reinsurance business and give operational and functional autonomy to four
subsidiaries in matters relating to reinsurance, investment and personnel. The Committee is not satisfied with the
correlation sought to be established by the Ministry among sections 2(7A)(c), 101A(8)(ii) of the Insurance Act, 1938
and sections 25 and 23 of the GIBN Act, 1972 for the purpose of justifying the basis for the issue of the Notification as
well as the Administrative Instructions. The Committee still feels that the action of the Ministry of Finance, Insurance
Division, declaring GIC as the Indian Reinsurer as well as grant of operational and functional autonomy to the
subsidiaries through Administrative Instructions runs contrary to the provisions of the General Business Nationalization
Act, 1972 as it stands at present. This is evident from the perusal of the General Insurance Business Nationalization
(Amendment) Bill, 2001, which is presently under consideration of the Standing Committee on Finance, Section 9(1) of
the General Business Nationalization Act, 1972 contemplates the role of “superintending, controlling and carrying on
business of general insurance” for the GIC which is sought to be changed to “carrying on reinsurance business” through
the said Amendment Bill. The Committee is unable to understand how the change effected in the role of the GIC can get
alongwith the provisions of the GIBN Act, 1972 until the amendment is carried out.The proposed insertion of a new
section 24(A) in the GIBN Act, 1972 whereby the corporation has, on and from the commencement of the General
Insurance Business Nationalization Amendment Act, 2001, been made to cease to carry on general insurance business
also establishes that certain amendments were called for in the GIBN Act before the Government could effect the
changes in
 
the role and function of the GIC. Now, when the General Insurance Business Nationalization (Amendment) Bill, 2001 is
presently under consideration of the Standing Committee on Finance, the Committee recommends that the Ministry of
Finance should not implement their notification dated 3
rd November 2000 as well as the Administrative Instructions
dated 7th November 2000 till the GIBNA Bill as introduced in the Parliament is passed.

NOTIFICATION
 
New Delhi 3rd November, 2000
 
 
      S.O. 995(E) In exercise of the power vested with the Central Government under Secretion 101A (8)(ii) of the
Insurance Act, 1938, the Central Government hereby approves General Insurance Corporation of India as the “Indian
Reinsurer” to carry on reinsurance business in India.
 
Annexure-II
 
D.O.No.8/27/200-Ins.VI dated 7.11.2000 addressed to Shri D. Sengupta
Chairman, G.I.C. of India from Shri Ajit M. Sharan, Joint Secretary
(INS), Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs.
 
        In has been decided by the Government that the General Insurance Corporation of India (GIC) shall be the “Indian
Re-insurer” under Section 101 (A) (8) (ii) of the Insurance Act, 1938.  This Section provides that-
 
        “Indian re-insurer” means an insurer specified in sub-clause (b) of clause (9)  of Section 2  who carries on
exclusively re-insurance business and is approved in this behalf by the Central Government.”
 
2.     As per requirements of the above Section, GIC will now have to restrict itself exclusively to  re-insurance business
and should cease doing direct insurance business. Presently GIC is carrying on direct business in the areas of  aviation
and crop insurance.  It also supervises and controls the four subsidiary companies in relation to the conduct of general
insurance business GIC may, henceforth, cease underwriting direct aviation and crop insurance business.   These
businesses may now be underwritten by the subsidiary companies in line with the recommendations of the Poddar 
Committee.
 
3.     GIC may take action to implement the other recommendations of the Poddar Committee to give operational and
functional autonomy to the four subsidiary companies in the following areas:
 
·
     Personnel matter
·     Investments
·     Reinsurance
 
4.     A General insurers’ (Public Sector) Association of India (GIPSA) may also be constituted as suggested by the
Poddar Committee to effect coordination among the four subsidiary companies.  This Association may be entrusted with
coordination in the areas of personnel, technical reinsurance, investment and other matters as recommended by the
Poddar Committee.

        Reply of the Government
 
        Government issued notification dated 3.11.2000 through existing power vested  under section 101A(8)(ii)  of
Insurance  Act 1935.   The  legal position and the circumstances under which the notification issued are given in the
following paragraph:
 
·     Vide notification No.S.O.770 (E) dated 29

commencing insurance business in India.  GIC, therefore, could not have reinsured the business of private insurance
companies.
 
·     For reinsuring the business of private insurance companies, it was required to be notified as “Indian Reinsurer”
under Section 101A(8)(ii) Insurance Act, 1938.
 
·     Therefore, the notification issued under Section 35 of GIBNA 1972 was rescinded and GIC was approved on
