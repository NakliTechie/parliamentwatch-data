THE TEA (DISTRIBUTION AND EXPORT) CONTROL ORDER, 2005 
 
 The Tea (Distribution and Export) Control Order, 2 005 ( Annexure I ) has been 
framed under the Tea Act, 1953 ( Annexure II ). This Order has been framed superseding 
the Tea (Distribution and Export) Control Order, 19 57, and seeks to ensure that all tea 
consignments meant for imports or exports would hav e to undergo a certification 
procedure under which the quality of tea and the co untry of its origin would be 
determined and displayed. The Tea Board has been de signated the nodal agency for this 
certification exercise. Further, the non-preferenti al certification of origin of each 
consignment of tea exports would be issued by the T ea Board or any of its designated 
agencies. The basic objective of this Order is to retain the brand equity of Indian tea.  
 
2. The Committee took up for examination of the sai d Rules and a questionnaire was 
forwarded to the  Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce & Industry on 22 
nd  
May, 2007. The Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce & Industry furnished 
their comments/clarifications on the points raised in the questionnaire (Annexure III) . 
The points raised by the Committee and the comments  of the Ministry are reproduced 
below: 
 
I  Framing of the Tea (Distribution and Export) Control, 2005  
 The Committee wanted to know about the reasons for  framing the Tea 
(Distribution and Export) Control Order, 2005 which  has been framed in supersession of 
the Tea (Distribution and Export) Control Order, 19 57, the major points of distinction 
between the Orders of 1957 and 2005, and the salient feature of the new Order? 
In response the Ministry informed the Committee tha t the new control order were 
framed in supersession of the earlier Tea (Distribu tion and Export) Control Order, 1957 
for strengthening the surveillance work of the Tea Board, to ensure standard of quality of 
tea for export/import and to maintain image of Indi an tea in the overseas market. The 
main reasons  to frame the Order and objectives thereof are as follows: 

(1) Reports of poor quality teas being imported and its  impact on domestic tea 
industry as well as on export of tea from India. 
(2) Reports of export of non-Indian tea  under a cover of a certificate of Indian 
origin tea . 
(3) Non-conformity of imported tea with the standard of  quality of tea as per ISO 
3720 leading to disrepute Indian Tea, thereby adver sely affecting India tea 
exports. 
(4) Order of the Kerala High Court exempting applicability of the Prevention of Food 
Adulteration Act for the teas stored for export. 
(5) Absence of any general rule defining “Indian Tea” f or the purpose of its export 
from India to other countries. 
 
 The salient features of the new order are as follows: 
 
(1) To ensure the standard of quality of tea meant for import /export 
(2) Issue of non-preferential certificate of origin tow ards export of tea from India by 
the agencies approved by Tea Board. 
(3) Stipulation of details of rules of origin for issua nce of certificate of Origin for 
export of tea from India to any country. 
(4) Stipulation of provision for obtaining business lic ence who will be engaged in 
business of distribution of imported tea. 
(5) Stipulation of provision towards import and distribution of tea.  
(6) Mandatory stipulation towards ensuring conformity o f tea to specification of tea 
in respect of export of tea or export of imported tea.  
 
II Rule 1(2) Short Title and Commencement :- 
 
 It shall come into force, on the date of its publi cation in the Official Gazzette, 
(except paragraph 8, paragraph 11 and clause (d), ( e), & (f) of paragraph 21 which shall 
come into force on the expiry of sixty days on and from the date of its said publication 
and paragraph 22 which shall come into force on exp iry of thirty days of said 
publication). 

 
 The Committee took note of the fact that Rule 1(2)  states that paragraph 8, 11 and 
clause (d), (e), & (f) of paragraph 21 which shall come into force on the expiry of sixty 
days on and from the date of its said publication o f the Tea (Distribution & Export) 
Control Order, 2005 and paragraph 22 on the expiry of thirty days from the date of the 
said publication. It enquired whether these paragra phs had come into force or not. It also 
asked to provide details in either case. The Commit tee also wanted to know the 
logic/objectives behind allowing more time before implementing certain provisions of the 
Order from later dates and whether those objectives have been achieved? 
 
The Ministry stated that paragraph 8, 11 and clause  (d), (e), & (f) of paragraph 21 
had come into force immediately on expiry of sixty days from the date of publication of 
the Order in the official gazette i.e. from 1 
st  April, 2005. Similarly, paragraph 22 had also 
come into force on expiry of thirty days from 1 st  April, 2005. 
 
Paragraph 8 is in relation to grant and refusal of business licence in favour of 
exporter and paragraph 11 is in relation to grant a nd refusal of non-preferential certificate 
of origin. In case of business licence to exporter it may be mentioned that under the 
earlier Order of 1957, number of firms had obtained  such licence either for temporary 
period or on permanent basis. Since nobody can expo rt tea unless he obtains licence from 
Tea Board under the Tea (Distribution and Export) C ontrol Order, 2005, there is a need 
to allow time to the intending exporter to obtain licence from Tea Board as per conditions 
stipulated in paragraph 8. It may also be mentioned  that the licensed tea exporters under 
the order of 1957, have been allowed to export tea till the validity period indicated in the 
licence granted under the said Order of 1957. This relaxation has been granted after 
obtaining approval of the Central Government.  
 
 As regards paragraph 11, clause (d), (e), & (f) of  paragraph 21 and paragraph 22, 
it was mentioned that they are new provisions. 
 The reasons for allowing more time before implemen tation of certain provisions 
is that in the case of these provisions, the export ers who had licenses under the earlier 
order needed extra time before coming within the purview of the new Order.  

 
III  Other Rules & Orders issued under the Tea Act, 195 3 
 
The Committee raised the fact that the Tea (Distribution & Export) Control Order, 
2005 has replaced the Tea (Distribution & Export) C ontrol Order, 1957. Similarly, the 
Tea (Marketing) Control Order, 1998 has been replac ed by the Order of 2003. In this 
respect what is the status with regard to other rul es/regulations/Orders framed under the 
Tea Act, 1953, like the Tea Rules, 1954, the Tea (W aste) Control Order, 1959 etc. which 
have become quite old? Whether Government would con siders reviewing these and other 
Orders under the Tea Act and update them accordingly. 
 
 The Ministry took note of the concern of the Commi ttee and informed that the 
Tea (Marketing) Control Order 1984 has been replace d by the Order of 2003 and the Tea 
(Distribution & Export) Control Order, 1957 has bee n replaced by the Order of 2005. At 
present Ministry is examining the different provisi ons of Tea Act 1953 and Tea Rules 
made thereunder so that the same can be substituted  by the new Act and Rules in view of 
liberalization and globalization era. The Committee  was informed that two committees 
have been constituted to look into different provis ions of the Tea Waste (Control) Order 
1959 and the Tea Warehouses (Licensing) Order 1989 for modification/amendment, if 
necessary. 
 The Committee feels that India occupies a leading p osition in tea export and 
therefore all efforts should be made to ensure that  it’s eminent position is 
maintained. It accordingly urges the Ministry to up date the concerned Acts and 
Orders at the earliest, if it has not been done til l now, so as to provide a strong 
statutory basis aligned to the current global scenario. 
 
IV Rule 8(4): Grant and refusal of business licence/pe rmanent licence to 
exporters- 
 The Licensing Authority may refuse to grant a perma nent business licence to any 
applicant and shall furnish him with a copy of the order so passed: 
 Provided that before passing an order of refusal a n opportunity of being heard 
shall be granted to the applicant . 

 The Committee was of the view that the rule provid es the applicant an 
opportunity to be heard before being refused a perm anent business licence along with 
furnishing him with a copy of such order. Therefore  does the Government have any 
objection in providing the reasons to be recorded in writing and issue of speaking order in 
case an applicant is refused grant of a business li cence besides giving him an opportunity 
to be heard. 
 
In reply the Department stated the following: 
For implementation of paragraph 8, the following procedure is being adopted: 
Examination of documents furnished by the applicant 
Recording of reasons in writing in case the applicant is considered to be refused to 
grant a business license and the said reasons are r equired to be communicated to 
applicant to enable him to overcome the defects so indicate in writing. 
In case the applicant overcomes the defect the busi ness licence is granted and in 
case the defects are not taken care of fully he is given an opportunity of being 
heard and on the basis of the hearing by the Licens ing Authority, final order of 
refusal, if any, is communicated to the applicant. 
 However Government of India may not have any objec tion to issue speaking 
order in which ground of refusal of license is recorded in cases of rejection. 
The Committee after considering the comments of the  Ministry recommends 
that the Order be amended to include a speaking ord er for recording the ground of 
a refusal of license in cases of rejection . It would provide more transparency to the 
working of the Order . 
 
V  Rule 8(5) :   Where an application for permanent business licence  is not refused 
under sub paragraph 4. the Licensing Authority shal l grant the applicant a permanent 
business licence in form G. 
 The Committee observed that the rule does not spec ify the time frame within 
which a permanent business licence shall be granted  to the applicant from the date of his 
applying for such licence. The Ministry stated that  time frame has not been indicated 
either in paragraph 8(2) or 8(5) in view of the pro cedure adopted by Tea Board and the 
time taken by the applicant in responding to query of the Tea Board. It may be mentioned 

that during the last two years, Tea Board did no re ceive any complaint about the delay in 
granting business licence/permanent business licenc e. The Committee accepted the 
clarification furnished by the Ministry. However as  a way of caution to prevent 
delays the Committee recommends that the Department  should make a study of the 
appropriate time within which the required formalit ies can be completed and the 
should then be incorporated in the Order.  
 
VI  Rule 10(2): Grant and refusal of Certificate of Ori gin for teas designated as 
Geographical Indications. 
 
Where an application for Certificate of Origin is n ot refused under sub paragraph 
(1) the Licensing Authority or any officer of the B oard not below the rank of Joint 
Controller of Licensing duly authorized by the Lice nsing Authority after being satisfied 
that the application is in conformity with the requ irement as laid down under paragraph 5 
and contractual obligation which the applicant has with an importer of such tea, may 
issue a Certificate of Origin of tea to the application Form I. 
In response to the Committee’s  query whether the t ime period within which a 
Certificate of Origin has to be granted by the licencing Authority or the authorized officer 
of the Board in case the same is not refused under sub paragraph (i) of Rule 10(1) should 
not be specified in the rule itself, the Ministry s tated that although the time frame in 
issuing Certificate of Origin is not mentioned the same is being issued within twenty-four 
hours from the time of receipt of application. Till  date Tea Board has not received any 
complaint about the delay in issuance of such certificate. 
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry is sues the Certificate of 
Origin within twenty four hours. However, the Commi ttee feels that it would be 
more appropriate if the same is reflected in the  O rder and would therefore 
recommend that time frame should be incorporated in the Order itself. 
VII Rule 11(2): Grant and refusal of Non-Preferential Certificate of Origin . 
 Provided that whenever the approved inspection agen cy issues a Certificate of 
Origin to any business licence, a duplicate copy of  the same along with all other 
supporting documents shall be furnished to the Lice nsing Authority or to any other 
officer of the Board duly authorized by Licensing A uthority in this regard, by the said 

inspection agency within twenty-four hours from the  time of issue of such Certificate of 
Origin. 
 The Committee enquired as to what could be the 'su pporting documents' referred 
to in the Rule: Whether Government have any objecti on for providing in the rule itself 
the documents to be furnished to the Licensing Auth ority if the same are required to be 
furnished to the Licensing Authority within 24 hours from the issue of the Certificate. 
 
 The Department clarified that paragraph 11(2) rela tes to grant of non-preferential 
certificate of origin in respect of any consignment  of tea for export based on the 
documents furnished by the applicant. While non-preferential certificate of origin is being 
issued as per Form -E the duplicate copy of the sam e is required to be forwarded to Tea 
Board. The rules of Certificate of Origin has been attached to Form -E wherein it has 
been mentioned that tea not wholly produced or obta ined in India is also eligible to cover 
by a Certificate of Origin with the word either ‘In dia Tea’ (not less than 90% by weight 
of tea) or by the words blended tea of different or igin and packed in India. The 
supporting documents based on which the agency issu es such type of non-preferential 
Certificate of Origin are required to be forwarded to Tea Board. Since the form and 
format of these documents may undergo changes from time to time it may not be suitable 
to specify them in the rule itself. 
The Committee accepts the comments of the Ministry but would like to 
recommend that the process of submission of documen ts may be made on-line and 
the forms required to be submitted may be done in s oft copy along with the scanned 
copies of the required documents. This would not on ly save time but would be more 
transparent and time saving method based on the latest technology .  
VIII Rule 12(1): Period of validity of business licence - 
Every business licence for an exporter shall be val id for a period of three years 
from the date of its issue and every business licence once renewed shall also remain valid 
for a further period of three years from the date o f its renewal unless the business licence 
is cancelled or suspended during the validity period . 
The Committee felt that the validity period of 3 ye ars for a business licence 
appeared to be short, and wanted to know whether Go vernment have any objection in 

increasing the validity period from 3 years to 5 ye ars with a further validity period of 3 
years after its renewal. 
The Committee was informed that at present there ar e two types of business 
licence (1) business licence with a validity period  of three years and (2) permanent 
business licence which is valid till the same is su spended/cancelled or otherwise. At this 
stage the validity period of the business licence i s three years within which the licensee 
has the opportunity to convert their licence to a p ermanent one on the basis of its 
performance. The permanent business licence is gran ted only to the business licence 
holder who have satisfied the eligibility criteria as indicated in paragraph 8(3) of the 
Order. In case the business licensee does not fulfi ll the said criteria, the said business 
licensee has the opportunity to renew the same for another period of three years under 
paragraph 14 of the Order. While the regular export er has converted their business 
licence to permanent business licence, the occasion al exporters have the validity period 
of three years. 
The Committee accepts the comments of the Ministry but requests it to 
ensure that no business licence holder is adversely affected  due to paucity of time . 
 
IX Rule 14(2): Renewal of business licence  
 
Every licencee being an exporter whose business lic ence has been converted into 
a permanent business licence under sub-paragraph (5 ) of paragraph 8, shall not be 
required to renew his business licence. 
The Committee raised the issue that Rule 14(2) prov ides for conversion of 
business into business licence. Under the circumsta nce, whether an exporter can keep a 
business licence in addition to his business licenc e as may be inferred from Rule 14(2). 
Does the conversion into a permanent business licen ce not render the business licence 
defunct, automatically. 
In response the Ministry stated that there is no sc ope for an exporter to keep a 
business licence in addition to his permanent busin ess licence. In fact, the exporter 
having business licence and fulfilling the criteria  indicated in para 8(3) of the Order are 
only eligible to convert his business licence to a permanent business licence. 
 
X Rule 17 (a) and (b): Fees for exporters  

 
 The Committee wanted to know whether Government hav e any objection in 
increasing the validity period from 3 years to 5 ye ars with subsequent renewal validity 
period of 3 years. 
 
 The Ministry informed that at this stage the valid ity period of the business licence 
is three years within which the licensee has the op portunity to convert his licence to a 
permanent one on the basis of its performance. 
 
The Committee took into consideration the clarifica tion furnished by the 
Ministry and also the fact that the last few years had been quite tough for Tea 
exporters. It was of the opinion that the validity period should be increased to five 
years to provide some more time to the licensee to convert his licence to a 
permanent one. 
 
XI Rule 18(2): Conditions of business licence  
 
 Where a business licensee sells or otherwise transf ers his business to another 
person the purchaser or transferee as the case may be, shall obtain a fresh business 
licence in accordance with the provisions of this Order . 
 
 Clarification was sought on whether there are any rules governing the 
sale/transfer of business from one business license e to another person; If not, what is the 
mechanism in place for regulating such sale/transfe r. In response it was informed that the 
business licence is not transferable. However, the infrastructure of a business licence 
holder is transferable as per common law. In such c ase, transferee is required to obtain 
fresh business licence as per para 8 of the Order and guidelines prepared by Tea Board. 
 
XII   Rule 18(3): Provision of Business Licence  
The Committees observed that while the Rule provide s 30 days reporting time to the 
business Licensee in case he enters into a partners hip with another person in business, no 
time period has been specified for the Licensing Au thority to grant the amended business 
licence. Further, whether any fee is charged for amending the business licence: if so, both 
the time period and amount of fees may be specified  in the Rules itself.  In reply, the 

Ministry stated that while the business licensee is  required to intimate the Licensing 
Authority in respect of change of the status of the  firm within thirty days, the amendment 
of the business licence can only be done if the doc uments to that effect are submitted to 
Licensing Authority. Generally such amendment is do ne within 7 days from the date of 
submission of all such documents. Further more, no fee is charged for amendment of 
business licence.  
 
XIII Rule 19(8) states “ Every business licensee shall carry out the directi ons of the 
licensing  Authority within the period as specified by the Licensing Authority ”  
  
 The Committee felt that the rule gives discretiona ry powers to the Licensing 
Authority for specifying the time period for carryi ng out its directions. Therefore, a fixed 
time period for the purpose of the rule may be specified in the Rule itself. 
 
The Ministry stated that the time frame for complia nce of the direction is 
dependent on the nature of directive issued by the Licensing Authority time to time. 
Specifying a fixed time period is not reasonable as  the nature of directive varies and the 
compliance time may also vary. 
 
XIV Rule 21 Explanations (2) and Rule 22 Explanations (2)  
 
 
 The Committee observed that the rule mentions the various types/mixtures of tea, 
which may render it as "adulterated”.  The Committe e, accordingly, wanted to know  the 
fate of such adulterated tea and whether there are rules governing the disposal of such 
adulterated tea. The Ministry informed that such ad ulterated tea cannot be termed as tea 
and it is considered as ‘tea waste’. Disposal of su ch tea waste is guided under the 
provisions of Tea Waste (Control) Order 1959.  
 
The Committee took note of the fact that disposal o f tea waste was been 
governed by Tea Waste (Control) Order, 1959. It was  of the opinion that lots of new 
technologies had come up since the Tea Waste (Contr ol) Order, 1959 was framed. It 
therefore, reiterates that the Ministry should take  a comprehensive view of the Tea 

Act and its concerned subordinate legislation and r edraft them in the current 
perspective .  
 
XV.  Rule 26: Power to register the export contract   
 
Licensing Authority or any Officer of the Board, not below the rank of Joint Controller of 
Licensing duly authorized by the licensing Authorit y, may register the export contract as 
per the procedure to be stipulated by the Licensing Authority from time to time before the 
shipment of tea from India under such export contract .” 
 The Committee observed that the rule gives discret ionary powers to the Licensing 
Authority in the procedure of registering the expor t contract and enquired whether there 
are any rules governing the procedure of registration of export contracts, if so, what is the 
justification for giving such discretionary powers to the Licensing Authority, which are 
liable to be misused. In response the Committee was  informed that this is an enabling 
provision. Provisions of export contracts may vary from country to country. The 
procedure for registration of such export contract can only be stipulated separately for 
different countries as and when required. The regis tration of export contract will enable 
Tea Board to ensure its compliance by the exporter.

 The Committee raised the issue that this rule give s power to the Licensing

 In response the Ministry stated that the panel of tea taster is to undertake

The Committee is happy that the Ministry has accept ed its suggestion

the process of amending the Order to incorporate th e recommendations of the 
Committee.

3. The Committee took up for consideration its draf t 201 st  Report on Statutory 
Orders Laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha during the 226th  Session. The Members noticed 
inordinate delays in the framing of rules/regulatio ns after Acts are enforced.  The 
Ministries are not adhering to the time limit of si x months in this regard and also not 
taking Committee's approval in cases of delays beyo nd six months, as recommended by 
the Committee.  Besides, the Committee also found t hat despite repeated observations 
and recommendations by the Committee in its various  Reports, Ministries/Departments 
continue to lay their notifications, etc. on the Table of the House after considerable delays 
spanning from months to years.  Around 33% delayed notifications are laid with no delay 
statement explaining the reason for delay.  And eve n when such statements are attached, 
unacceptable excuses like delays in arranging print ed copies are being given.  The 
Committee felt that the Ministries/Departments are not taking its observations and 
recommendations seriously and have a casual attitud e towards framing and laying of 
these rules/regulations - an important duty towards  Parliament.  No senior officers, it 
appears, are monitoring this exercise in the Minist ries.  The Committee's concern 
centered mainly around the serious implications of delayed framing and laying of 
notification containing rules/regulations/orders, e tc.  The Committee is constrained to 
infer that Ministries/Departments may be doing so d eliberately with a view to evading 
Parliament's scrutiny of the rules/regulations that  are delegated by it to be framed by the 
Government.  Since such rules/regulations come into  force even before they are laid 
before Parliament, any delay in laying before Parli ament, in fact, prevents Members to 
exercise their right to get them modified/annulled by moving a Motion.  And this is what 
exactly happening today.  Another disturbing proble m was delayed response to the 
Committee's communications from the Ministries, and  not acknowledging its letters, etc. 
in time.  So many cases of scrutiny of rules/regulations are pending before the Committee 
mainly due to pending responses from them for many years.  They go on seeking 
extension on one pretext or the other.  This has be come a norm than exception.  It highly 
restricts the normal flow of Committee's work and then parliamentary scrutiny.  
4. In such a situation, the Committee felt not to r emain a mute spectator; but to 
protect its right to be informed, scrutiny and surv eillance.  The Committee discussed 
possible courses of action on this issue.  Since it  amounted actually bypassing Parliament 
and disobeying its authority, it is a serious breach of privileges not only of the Committee 

but also of the House and its members, which can be invoked in this case.  To begin with, 
however, it was decided that its Chairperson should  write to all the Ministers in this 
regard.  As per its earlier decision, the Committee  has already summoned Secretaries of 
some Ministries to explain about this state of affa irs and would be calling others for this 
purpose.  The Committee also decided to apprise the  Hon’ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha 
about the same.
