2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that there was a considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 116 delayed 
Notifications, there were 33 Notifications where no delay statement was appended to. However, the 
bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministr y of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 66.37% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i)  Ministry of Civil Aviation  
  
 Ministry of Civil Aviation  have laid 4 Notificati ons (Sl. Nos. 5 to 8). Two of them (Sl. No. 
5 and 8) have been delayed by 5 months each and are  accompanied with delay statement. The 
reason given by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
  
 “The Ministry of Civil Aviation (Height Restrictio ns of Safeguarding of Aircraft 
Operations) Rules, 2015 were sent on 30.9.2015 to t he Government of India Press, Mayapuri with 
a request to provide 150 copies of the printed noti fication for record/laying on the Table of the 
House. Although the Rules were published in the Gaz ette on 1.10.2015, printed copies of the same 
were awaited from the Government of India Press and  hence the Rules could not be laid on the 
Table of the House in the last Winter Session. Subsequently, it came to notice that due to change in 
the guidelines, the physical printing of Gazette No tification of Government of India has been 
dispensed and exclusive e-publishing of the same has been started.” 
 
 The reasons cited by Ministry of Civil Aviation  i .e. Notifications could not be laid 
down because the printed copies of Notifications could not be provided by the Government of 
India Press in time, is an oft-repeated excuse. Man y Ministries have been citing this as a 
reason for delay, quite often. It reflects the lack adaisical approach on the part of the 
Ministry. On account of delay in getting printed co pies of Notifications from Printing Press 
so as to adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in Committee’s 131
st    Report, they may 
obtain the G.S.R./S.O. number from the Government o f India Press in advance and print the 

same in a cyclostyle form so as to lay the Notification in time. This had also been suggested by 
the Ministry of Urban Development, the controlling Ministry for Government of India Press.  
 
(ii) Ministry of Coal 
   
 The Ministry of Coal have laid 2 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 9 and 10) out of which one (Sl. No. 
9) has been delayed by 5 months. There is no delay statement appended with the Notifications. 
   
 The Committee finds that Ministry of Coal have not  only delayed the laying of 
Notifications but have also failed to append delay statement thereto. The Committee, in the 
absence of delay statements, are not able to know t he actual reasons behind the delay in 
laying of Notifications. The Committee have clearly  mentioned in its 135
th  Report that delay 
statements should invariably be appended to wheneve r there is a delay in laying of 
Notifications. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon the Ministry of Coal to strictly 
adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in Commit tee’s Report, and, in case of any delay in 
a laying, a delay statement should invariably be appended thereto. 
 
(iii)  Ministry of Communications and Information Technology 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
The Ministry of Communications and Information Tech nology (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 13 to 16) out of which 3 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 13 to 15)  have been delayed by 6 to 11 months . Moreover, there is  no delay statement  
appended to any of the notifications so delayed.  
     
 The Committee is of the considered opinion that had  the Ministry of Communications 
and Information Technology (Department of Telecommu nications) exercised a bit caution, 
they would have avoided such instances of delay as delay period is not exceptionally long. 
Further, a delay statement is also not appended wit h any of these. The Committee, therefore,  
reiterates its recommendation as contained in its 1 35 
th  Report i.e. to attach a delay statement, 
wherever required, and simultaneously devise methods to introspect and ensure bottlenecks. 
 
 
 

 
(iv) Ministry of Corporate Affairs  
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs  have laid 7 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 18 to 24). Only 1 
Notification (Sl. No. 19) has been delayed by 5 months and there is no delay statement appended to 
it.  
  
   The Committee notes that though there has been dela y in laying of one Notification, 
no delay statement has been attached to it. The Com mittee expects the Ministry to be careful 
and responsible in these matters henceforth.  The M inistry should ensure such compliance in 
future with due caution so that such recurrence does’nt take place. 
 
(v) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Cha nge 
 
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change have laid 3 Notifications (Sl.Nos.25 - 
27), out of these only one Notification (Sl.No.26) has been delayed and that too by 1 year. There is 
no delay statement appended thereto. 
 
The Committee expresses its deep concern over the i nordinate delay that has taken 
place in the laying of one Notification. The Commit tee  also takes serious note of the fact that 
the Ministry has not appended any delay statement w ith the delayed Notification. The 
Committee is wondering as to how in absence of any delay statement can it be ascertained 
what caused this inordinate delay. The Committee, t herefore,  recommends that the Ministry 
of Environment, Forest and Climate Change should ta ke all necessary precautions in future 
to avoid recurrence of such situations and append delay statements invariably. 
 
(vi) Ministry of Finance 
 (Department of Revenue) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) have la id 140 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 104 to 
243) on the Table of the House. Out of these 20 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 141-160) have been delayed 
by 4 to 8 months respectively and no delay statemen t have been attached to any of these delayed 
Notifications. 
 

This is just not acceptable to the Committee that 14% (20 out of 140) Notifications laid 
by the Ministry did not have delay statements. Whil e there may be a reason for the delay, 
there cannot be any reason for not furnishing reason therefor. The Ministry must understand 
the implications for not doing so regularly and tha t too in such a large number of cases. The 
Committee makes a point that Ministry should append  a delay statement so as to apprise the 
Parliament of the reasons of delay in laying of Notifications on the Table of the House. 
 
 
(vii) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
 (Department of Health and Family Welfare) 
 
Ministry of Health and  Family Welfare (Department Health and Family Welfare) have laid 
3 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 244 to 246) during the Se ssion. Out of these, only one Notification (Sl. 
No. 244) has been delayed by 4 months and has no delay statement appended to it. 
 
Although there is a delay in one of the Notificatio n and that too of 4 months, yet the 
Committee take a serious view about the delay in laying of one of its Notifications and  also its 
failure to append the delay statement along with it . The Ministry/Department must ensure to 
append a delay statement henceforth should such a situation arise . 
 
(viii) Ministry of Home Affairs 
  
Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 15 Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 247 to 261). Out of  these,  5  
Notifications  have been delayed. Whereas 4 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 248 – 249 & 252 – 253) have 
been delayed from a long period of 5 months to 2 ye ars and 8 months and are accompanied with a 
delay statement. Whereas another one Notification (Sl. No. 250) has been delayed by 8 months and 
there is no delay statement appended to it. The reasons of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
“The Notification No. 30 dated 20.08.2014 (English version) in respect of the Daman and 
Diu Panchayat (Election Procedure) Rules, 2014 was published by UT Administration of Daman 
and Diu under Section 121 (1) of ‘The Daman and Diu  Panchayat Regulation, 2012 (04 of 2012). 
The notification could not be laid on the Table of the House due to the delay in the publication of 
the Hindi translation of the notification by the UT Administration. 

The Hindi version of Notification [No.30] has now b een published on 09.10.2015 in the 
Gazette of Daman and Diu vide Notification No. 61. Hence, Notifications (both in Hindi & English 
version) is being laid on the Table of Lok Sabha/Ra jya Sabha under Section 123 of ‘The Daman 
and Diu Panchayat Regulation, 2012 (04 of 2012).” 
 
“The Notification No. 20 dated 20 
th  June, 2013 (English/Hindi version) in respect of ‘ The 
Daman and Diu Election of Upa-Sarpanch of a Gram Pa nchayat and President and Vice-President 
of District Panchayat, Rules, 2013’ was published b y UT Administration of Daman and Diu under 
Section 121(1) of ‘The Daman and Diu Panchayat Regu lation, 2012’ (04 of 2012). The 
Notification could not be laid on the Table of the Houses, due to non availability of required 
number of copies (both in Hindi/English version) from UT Administration. 
The  required number of copies of the Notification both in English/Hindi version has now 
been provided by UT Administration of Daman and Diu . Hence, it is being laid on the Table of 
Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha under Section 123 of  ‘The Da man and Diu Panchayat Regulation, 2012’ 
(04 of 2012).” 
 
 The Committee wonders as to how it has taken as lo ng as one year and six months and 
two years and eight months, respectively,  in the p ublication of the Hindi translation of the 
Notification and non- availability of required numb er of copies (both Hindi/English version) 
from UT Administration. This is simply ridiculous a nd the Committee takes a serious note of 
this. The Ministry should find some suitable mechan ism to eliminate such instances of delay. 
This shows the lackadaisical attitude and approach of the Ministry. The Committee advises 
that Ministry should impress upon their agencies to  strictly adhere to the time schedule in 
laying of papers on the Table of the House. 
  
(ix) Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas 
 
 Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas  have laid 5  Notifications (Sl. Nos. 286 to 290) 
during the Session. Out of these 2 Notifications (S l. Nos. 286 & 290) have been delayed by 7 
months to 1 year and 6 months and no delay statement is attached to both of delayed Notifications.  
   
 The Committee are pained to notice that out of 5 N otifications which Ministry of 
Petroleum and Natural Gas have laid, two Notificati ons have been delayed from 7 months to 
18 months. Further, there is no reason given for th is inordinate delay.  This is in 

contravention of Committee’s recommendation given i n its 135 Report that Notification 
which are delayed should invariably be accompanied by delay statement. The Committee 
would like the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Ga s to take necessary steps to obviate such 
delays and in case of delay in laying of Notificati ons, a delay statement should invariably be 
appended with them. 
 
(x) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
 The Ministry of Road Transport and  Highways have laid 88 Notifications
 on the Table of 
the House (Sl. Nos. 293 to 380) out of which 77 Not ifications (87.5%) (Sl. Nos. 293 to 320 and 
322 to 370) have been delayed. All the delayed Noti fications are though accompanied by delay 
statements The delay period ranges from 5 months to  13 months. The reasons given by the 
Ministry is that it took some time to complete the procedural formalities and therefore, papers 
could not be laid in time. 
 
The Committee observes that the Ministry of Road Tr ansport and Highways lays a 
large number of Notifications every Session. This i s a voluminous task being handled by the 
Ministry of Road Transport and Highways. However, t he Committee finds that 87.5% of 
total Notifications laid by the Ministry of Road Tr ansport and Highways have been delayed 
and only 12.5% of the total Notifications have been  laid in time. The Committee notes the 
reasons for delay given by the Ministry  that it to ok ‘some time’ to collect the papers and 
complete the procedural formalities,  but cannot accept such inordinate delay as of 13 months 
under the definition of ‘some time’ for laying of p apers. The Committee feels that if the 
Ministry can lay some papers  in time, it can also lay other papers likewise. Only due 
precaution is required therefor. The Committee hope s that the Ministry should, therefore,  
look into the matter seriously to eliminate recurrence of such delays in future. 
 
 (xi) Ministry of Shipping 
 
 Ministry of Shipping have laid 18 Notifications (S l. Nos. 382 to 399). Out of these 3 
Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 382 to 384) have been dela yed. The delay period is of about 4 months in 
each case and moreover no delay statement has been attached to any of these delayed Notifications. 
 

 The Committee, in the absence of delay statements,  are not able to decipher the 
reasons behind this delay in laying of Notification s. The Committee have time and again 
mentioned in its earlier Reports that delay stateme nts should invariably be appended where 
there is delay in laying of Notifications. The Comm ittee, therefore, impresses upon the 
Ministry to strictly adhere to the time schedule. The Ministry cannot just take the Committee 
for granted  
 
3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministrie s/Departments to take immediate steps to 
ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legi slation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to 
bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments o f the Government of India, particularly, the 
defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 7
th , 131 
st 
, 135 
th 
, 152 
nd  ,   192 nd  
, 201 st  , 212 th  and 213 th  Reports: 
 
 “The Committee desires that for the convenience of  the public and Members, 
Ministries should henceforth send copies of 'order'  duly authenticated by the Ministers 
concerned, incorporating therein all the corrections and amendments issued till date of laying 
such 'orders' instead of laying the uncorrected cop y along with corrigenda. The Committee 
feels that to obviate any legal or technical difficulty, Ministries should examine whether there 
is any necessity of assigning a separate G.S.R. No.  to a corrigendum at the time of its 
publication in the Gazette and whether such a corri gendum should not be published without 
assigning any G.S.R. No. The Committee would, therefore, like to impress upon all Ministries 
of the Government of India that the present practice of laying the rules without incorporating 
corrigenda in the body of the rules is not a health y one. The Committee hopes that the 
Ministries will ensure supply of only corrected cop ies of the rules for laying on the Table so 
that they depict a true and correct picture and serve the desired purpose."

(152 
nd 
 Report) 
(Presented on 26 th   August 2004)  
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Commit tee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force of an Act. The 

Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 
of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 
invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 

while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”

  Rules/Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are laid by dif ferent Ministries on the Table of the 
House under various provisions of parent Acts passe d by Parliament. These Rules/ 
Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are published in the of ficial Gazette before being laid on the 
Table of the House. Even after they are laid, it is  very difficult to get access to these 
Notifications. The Committee recommends that such R ules/Regulations/Orders, etc. laid on 
the Table of the House should be invariably uploade d on the websites of respective Ministries 
immediately on the same day when they have been lai d on the Table of the House, for 
information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access. 
201 
st  Report 
(Presented on 14 th  December, 2012)  
 
 
The Committee observes that the whole process of en acting laws is defeated if rules 
are not framed and laid on the Table of the House i n time.  A reasonable delay could be 
understood but unjustifiably long delays should be avoided and the Ministries should work 
towards improving this position.   The Committee hopes that Ministry would strive to k eep 
the delay to the minimum and take care that Notific ations are laid on the Table of the House 
at the first available opportunity after publication of Notifications. 
  
 
The Committee would like to impress upon the Minist ry that it is their responsibility 
to ensure that copies of the Notifications are prin ted timely and for this purpose, they need to 
evolve a suitable mechanism to ensure that notifica tions are laid in time.   The Committee is 
happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  The 
Committee hopes that it would take action according ly in order to comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications. 

 
 
The Ministry must take appropriate step to remove l acuna, if any, administrative or 
otherwise, causing such long delay by being little proactive and prompt.  Further, the 
Ministries should take it up with the Ministry of U rban Development for late receiving of 
printed copies of the Notification. 
 
212 
th   Report 
(Presented on  3 rd  September, 2013) 
 
 “The Committee is not inclined to accept the late availability of printed copies of 
Notifications from the Government of India Press as  the reason for delay in laying of the 
Notifications. The Committee directs all the Minist ries that in case there is a delay in 
procuring printed copies in time, they may obtain G .S.R. and S.O. numbers from the 
Government of India Press and prepare requisite num ber of copies in cyclostyled form for 
laying in the Parliament. The Committee also recomm ends that this process may be adopted 
by Ministries suo moto  on immediate basis so as to avoid such delays henceforth”.
