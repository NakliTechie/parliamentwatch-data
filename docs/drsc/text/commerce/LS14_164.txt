The Oil Mines Regulations, 1984 – Implementation of the 119th Report   of the 
Committee on  
Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha 
  
            The Committee presented its 119 th Report on the Oil Mines Regulat ions, 1984 to 
the House on the 10th July, 1998. A copy of the Report was forwarded to the Ministry of 
Petroleum & Natural Gas on the 14th July, 1998 for furnishing the Action Taken Report 
(ATR) thereon. A major recommendation of the Committee was the setting up of a multi-
member omnibus authority under an Act of Parliament, which may be known as the 
Hydrocarbons Authority of India, for overseeing the entire upstream hydrocarbons 
sector in the offshore areas, including safety of installations, health safety, and welfare of 
workers and environmental aspects. Relevant extracts from the said report are enclosed 
(Annexure). 
  
2.         The Ministry vide their letter dated the 6 th August, 1998 informed that the ATR 
was to be prepared in consultation with other Ministries for which a further additional 
period of six weeks was requested for submission of the ATR. In another communication 
dated the 14 th October, 1998 the Ministry sought further extension of time till the 31 st 
December, 1998 as they were engag ed in studying models on which the proposed 
Hydrocarbons Development Authority was to be based, as existing in countries like UK, 
Norway, Canada, U.S.A etc. 
  
3.         Thereafter, the Ministry had been seeking extension of time for furnishing the 
ATR. Subsequently, the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas vide their letter dated 
the 9 th May, 2000 informed the constitution of an Inter - Ministerial Working Group 
headed by Shri Naresh Narad, the then Additional Secretary in the Ministry of Petroleum 
and Natural Gas, for studying the upstream Regulatory Authority. The Working Group 
was expected to submit its report by the 15th June, 2000 and the same was to be examined 
in the Ministry. The Committee had then observed that the Ministry's pace of 
implementing the Committee's recommendation was extremely slow. It also pointed out 
that it was a matter of serious concern that there was neither any statutory authority in 
position nor any statutory rules/regulations in place for overseeing the offshore activities 
while the work of offering new blocks in the offshore basins was already on. It felt that 
the Ministry had failed to take note of the urgency emphasized by the Committee thereby 
rendering the Committee's whole exercise redundant. 
  
4.         The Committee heard the Secretary, the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas in 
the matter on the 17 th July, 2001. During the course of the meeting, the Committee was 
informed that the report of the Working Group, since submitted to the ministry on the 
4th April, 2001, was under consideration of the Ministry and a decision in the matter 
would be taken up after the same was examined. This would be followed by approval of 
the Cabinet. The Ministry had also stated that, in their view, there should be one 
upstream regulatory authority. 
  
5.         Thereafter, the Ministry had sought extension of time on a number of occasions, 
i.e. upto 30.11.2001, 28.2.02 and 31.5.02 for furnishing the ATN on the plea that the report 
submitted by the Working Group was still under examination. Th e Ministry was 
requested to furnish a detailed note specifically commenting on the extent to which each 
recommendation of the Committee, as contained in its 119 th report was acceptable to 
them, the action so far taken by them towards implementing the same and a definite time 
span within which they would be able to implement the Committee's recommendations. 
On the 8 th August, 2002 the Ministry informed that the Government had initiated the 

process for dismantling the Administered Price Mechanism after March 2002 and to put 
into place a Regulatory Authority for the Downstream Petroleum sector. In view of these 
developments, it had decided to process the recommendations of the Working Group on 
upstream sector. They had also contended that the proposal of the Working Group would 
also require inter-Ministerial/Departmental consultation which might took a few months 
more. Accordingly, the Ministry requested for further extension of time upto the 31 st 
October, 2002 for furnishing the ATR on the matter. On the same g round, the Ministry 
sought repeated extension of time, i.e. up to 31.01.03, 30.4.03 and 31.7.03 
  
6.      The Committee in its meeting held on the 31 st March, 2003 decided to call the 
concerned Joint Secretary, Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas to appear before the 
Chairman of the Committee, and a meeting was arranged on 25th July, 2003. Afterwards, 
the Ministry vide its communication dated the 1 st August, 2003 sought extension of time 
up to the 31 st March, 2004 to furnish the Action Taken Report, as the report of the 
Working Group was being considered in light of the progress and developments on 
dismantling of the Administered Price Mechanism and formation of regulatory 
mechanism for the downstream sector. They further submitted that the formation of 
Regulatory Mechanism for Upstream Hydrocarbons sector could be taken up only after 
such Regulatory frameworks for downstream Hydrocarbons sector has been put in place. 
  
7.         The Ministry vide  its letter dated the 14 th May, 2004 sought further extension of 
time upto the 31st August, 2004 for furnishing the ATR,  as a bill introduced in Parliament 
incorporating the report of the Working Group could not be effected/materialized  as Lok 
Sabha was yet to be formed and the bill could be t aken up after the constitution of Lok 
Sabha. Again, the Ministry vide its letter dated the 2nd September, 2004 sought extension 
of time upto the 28 th February, 2005 on the ground that the bill for establishing a 
Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board (for downstream sector) introduced in Lok 
Sabha on the 18th December, 2003 could not be taken up by the House in that session, and 
the new Government also decided that the new draft Bill should incorporate provisions 
relating to a Technical Body to advice o n technical issues as also an Appellate Tribunal. 
The Ministry contemplated to introduce the Bill for Regulatory Board for downstream 
sector in the winter session of Parliament. 
  
8.         Thereafter, several reminders were issued to the Ministry to furnish the     ATR. 
  
9.         Finally, the Ministry in its communication dated the 10th June, 2005 informed as 
under: - 
  
 “For the present, Govt. of India is not contemplating a separate and independent 
regulator for the up-stream sector”. 
  
10.       Thereafter, the Ministry were requested to furnish a detailed background note 
on the basis of which their latest decision was arrived at. 
  
11.       The Ministry vide its latest communication dated the 18th July, 2005 informed as 
under: - 
  
“The main recommendation of the Committee was to set up “Hydrocarbon Authority of 
India” in the country on the pattern followed by UK, Norway, Canada and USA for 
overseeing the entire upstream sector in the offshore areas. The recommendation of the 
committee was examined i n the Ministry, and since it required in depth study, it was 
considered appropriate to constitute a high -powered Inter-Ministerial Working Group 

to go into various aspects. The Working Group submitted its report, which was examined 
in the Ministry as well as by the Committee of Secretaries headed by the Cabinet 
Secretary. 
  
            After deliberation in detail, it has been decided not to have, for the present, a 
separate and independent regulator for the upstream sector for following reasons: 
  
Petroleum & Natural Gas Regulatory Board (PNGRB) when formed would regulate the 
petroleum operations beyond the crude oil and natural gas production phase covering 
refining, processing, storage, transportation, distribution, marketing and sale of 
petroleum and petroleum products as well as natural gas and its transportation through 
pipelines. PNGRB, would also regulate the establishment of LNG terminals and setting 
up of city or local gas distribution projects. The upstream sector is distinct from the 
downstream se ctor and has different business clientele and technical issues of a 
completely different scope and nature, apart from entailing higher risk investments. With 
the exception of Brazil, the activities of upstream sector elsewhere are generally 
controlled and regulated by the Governments themselves. 
  
As the ownership of the resources in the upstream sector is vested with the Government, 
it would be desirable and appropriate for the Government, with the help of Director -
General of Hydrocarbons (DGH) to regulate  upstream activity under Oilfields 
(Regulation and Development) Act, 1948. This act provides the legal framework for 
regulating the upstream sector.   The New Exploration Licensing Policy (NELP) for 
awarding blocks for exploration and production provides th e fiscal regime, manner of 
selection of parties and the modalities for Production Sharing contracts. DGH had been 
set up for advising the Government on technical matters like reservoir management, data 
management etc. The Ministry is of the view that the present policy and regulation regime 
for the upstream sector is adequate and DGH would regulate the upstream Hydrocarbon 
sector while the PNGRB would regulate the Downstream Hydrocarbon sector, once 
PNGRB is formed.   In view of this, the Govt. is not contem plating a separate and 
independent regulator for upstream hydrocarbon sector for the present.” 
  
The Committee considered the matter in the light of the latest information furnished by 
the Ministry. After a holistic review of the matter, the Committee note d that since the 
beginning, when the Ministry was first approached to furnish the ATR on its 
recommendations as contained in its 119th Report, the Ministry had been maintaining the 
position that an upstream regulatory authority was desirable for the upstre am 
hydrocarbon sector. The Committee was given to understand that the matter was being 
examined and an Inter -Ministerial Working Group have been set up to look into the 
matter.The Committee was approached on numerous occasions for granting extension of 
time as the report of the Working Group was under deliberation in the Ministry and, 
subsequently it was to be followed by approval of the Cabinet. While going through the 
latest information furnished by the Ministry, which in essence amounted to the Ministry 
backtracking on their earlier position on the matter which they had endorsed so many 
times.  The Committee accepts the explanation furnished by the Ministry, but at the same 
time note with regret that it should not have allowed so much time to elapse in ar riving 
at the final decision.

The Rules Framed under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 – 
Implementation of the  
151st Report of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation,  Rajya Sabha. 
             
The Committee presented its 151st Report to the House on the 22nd December,  2003. The 
Report contained recommendations, which arise out of examination of following Rules 
framed under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999:- 
  
The Foreign Exchange Management (Encashment of Drafts, Cheque Instrument & 
Payment of Interest) Rules, 2000; 
  
The Foreign Exchange Management (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000; 
  
The Foreign Exchange Management (Adjudication, Proceedings & Appeal) Rules, 2000; 
and 
  
The Foreign Exchange Management (Compounding Proceedings) Rules, 2000. 
  
 In the said report, the Committee had recommended as follows: 
  
“ The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Finance have accepted the 
Committee’s suggestions as follows: 
  
On Rule 4 of FEM (Encashment of Drafts, Cheque   Instrument & Payment of Interest) 
Rules, 2000 to prescribe a definite time frame for encashing th e draft, cheques, etc. and 
as a follow up, would be requesting the RBI to issue suitable instructions to the Banks in 
this behalf. 
  
To take necessary action to do away with the inconsistency in Schedules I   & II of FEM 
(Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000,by inserting an introductory portion in the 
English version also. 
  
(a) To delete the word “brief’ in Rule 4(9) of the FEM (Adjudication, Proceedings & 
Appeal) Rules, 2000. The Committee had objected to the provision whereby the decision 
of the Adjudicating Authority was prescribed to contain “brief reasons for the decisions”. 
  
(b) To prescribe a time limit of 180 days in Rule 6(4) of the FEM (Adjudication, 
Proceedings & Appeal) Rules, 2000 for disposal of appeals. The rules at present do not 
prescribe any time period for disposal of appeals, which was objected to by the 
Committee. 
  
            The Committee hopes that the Ministry would notify amendments in the respective 
rules as above expeditiously.” 
  
2.         Relevant extracts from the Report were forwarded to the Ministry of Finance 
(Department of Economic Affairs) on the 26th December, 2003 for furnishing the Action 
Taken Note thereon. 
  
3.         The Ministry in its communication dated the 13 th July, 2004 informed the 
Committee that work was being initiated on the Action Taken Report.  In its subsequent 
letter dated the 28 th December, 2004 they informed that they were in consultation with 
the Reserve Bank of India and the Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative 

Department).  After several reminders, the Ministry vide its communication dated the 8th 
September, 2005 furnished the Action Taken Report.  The Committee’s suggestions and 
the Ministry’s Action Taken Report thereon are as follows:  
  
1.         Committee’s Suggestions: - 
  
On Rule 4 of Foreign Exchange Management (Encashment of Draft, Cheque 
Instrument and Payment of Interest) Rules, 2000 to prescribe a definite time frame for 
encashing the draft, cheque, etc and as a follow up, would be requesting the Reserve 
Bank of India to issue suitable instructions to the Banks in this behalf. 
             
Ministry’s Action Taken Report (8.9.2005)  
  
Reserve Bank of India has issued suitable instructions to the Banks to prescribe a 
definite time frame for encashing the draft, cheque etc. (Annexure-I). 
  
2.        Committee’s Suggestions: - 
  
To take necessary action to do away with the inconsistency in Schedules I and II of 
Foreign Exchange Management (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000 by 
inserting an introductory portion in the English version also. 
  
Ministry’s Action Taken Report (8.9.2005)  
  
Notification amending Foreign Exchange Management (Current Account Transactions) 
Rules, 2000 inserting an introductory portion has been published and laid on the Tables 
of both the Houses in the Monsoon Session in August, 2005. (Annexure-II) 
  
3.        Committee’s Suggestions: - 
  
To delete the word “brief” in Rule 4(9) of the Foreign Exchange Management 
(Adjudication Proceedings and Appeal) Rules, 2000.  The Committee had objected to the 
provision whereby the decision of the Adjudication Authority was prescribed to contain 
“brief reasons for the decisions.” 
  
To prescribe a time limit of 180 days in Rule 6(4) of the Foreign Exchange 
Management  (Adjudication Proceedings and Appeal Rules) Rules, 2000 for disposal of 
appeals.  The rules at present do not prescribe any time period for disposal for appeals 
which was objected to by the Committee. 
  
Ministry’s Action Taken Report (8.9.2005) : 
  
(a) and (b) Notification amending Rule 4(9) of Fore ign Exchange Management 
(Adjudication Proceedings and Appeal) Rules, 2000, deleting the word “brief” and 
amending Rule 6(4) of the Foreign Exchange Management   (Adjudication Proceedings 
and Appeal) Rules, has been published and laid on the Table of both th e Houses on 
August, 2005 (Annexure-III). 
  
            The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Ministry of Finance (Department 
of Economic Affairs) have taken necessary action on its recommendations and notified 
amendments on the various Rules framed under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 
1999.

III 
The Railway Claims Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1989 – Implementation of 
 123rd Report of the Committee. 
  
The Railway Claims Tribunal (Procedure) Rules, 1989 framed under the Railways 
Claims Tribunal Act, 1989 were examined by the Committee and the matter was reported 
to the House on the 21 st December, 1998 in its 123 rd Report wherein the Committee had 
recommended as under : - 
            “The Committee considered the subject at its meeting held on September 8, 1998 
and noted the comments of the Ministry of Railways in respect of Rules 17, 27 (2) and (3), 
30 and 33.   As regards the rules 11(3), 22, 32(2) and 3 1(1), the Committee noted with 
satisfaction that the Ministry had accepted its suggestion.  The Committee expressed the 
desire that the Ministry of Railways should carry out necessary amendment to the 
relevant rules, where Committees’ suggestion have been accepted expeditiously.” (para 
4.4) 
  
2.         Relevant extracts from the 123 rd Report were forwarded to the Ministry of 
Railways (Railway Board) on the 22 nd April, 1999 for implementation of its 
recommendations. 
  
3. The Ministry of Railways vide their communication dated the 21 st November, 2000 
informed as under: 
“The recommendation of the Committee to change Rules 11(3), 22, 31(1) and 32(2) have 
been examined in consultation with the Legislative Department of the Ministry of Law 
and Justice. 
            Legislative Department opined that the proposed amendments in Rule 11(3) & 22 
relating to removal of defects in the application before Registrar and the summoning of 
witnesses by the Tribunal recording the reasons in writing, are free from any legal 
difficulties.  The same can be carried out. 
  
The amendment proposed in Rule 31(1) relates to passing of an order either at once or as 
soon as thereafter but not later than 30 days from the date of conclusion of evidence and 
arguments.  The amendment prescribing the time limit of 30 days imposes a bar on the 
exercise of jurisdiction, and therefore would be outside the scope of the Act. 
            Regarding Rule 32(2) it has been opined that when Review is not provided by the 
substantive Act, it cannot be provided by means of Rule.  The provision of appeal, review 
or revision are made by the substantive Act and it relates to the jurisdiction and powers 
of a Tribunal.   Rules cannot provide appeal or review if it is not provided by the 
substantive Act. 
             
Rule 11(3) & Rule 22  are being amended as suggested by the Committee.   In so far as 
Rules 31(1) & 32(2)  are concerned, the Committee may like to reconsider the proposed 
amendments in view of the aforesaid legal implications and advise.”             
4.         The Committee considered the matter at its meeting held on the 16 th September, 
2004.  The Committee noted that the Ministry of Railways have implemented all the 
recommendations of the Committee as contained in its 123 rd Report on the Railway 
Claims ( Procedure) Rules, 1989 except those relating to rules 31(1) and 32(2).   The 
Committee was not convinced with the explanation furnished by the Ministry for their 
inability to implement its recommendations and decided to pursue the matter with the 
Ministry. 

5.         Accordingly, vide this Secretariat O.M. dated the 8 th October, 2004, the Ministry 
of Railways were requested to reconsider their stand on the Committee’s 
recommendations on these two rules in the light of the provisions of the Railway Claims 
Tribunal Act. 
6.         The Ministry of Railways vide their communication dated the 24 th March, 2005 
informed that the amendments to Rules 31(1) and 32(2) had since been notified vide 
  
G.S.R 173 (E) dated the 16 th March, 2005 publishing the Railway Claims T ribunal 
(Procedure) Amendment Rules, 2005. 
  
7.         The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Ministry of Railways have 
amended the rules as per its suggestions as follows: - 
(i) Rule 11(3)    by providing that if on scrutiny an application, is fo und defective and 
defect noticed is formal in nature, the Registrar may allow the applicant to rectify the 
same in his presence, and if the defect is not formal in nature, the Registrar may allow 
the applicant to rectify defect in a week’s time, which may be extended in appropriate 
cases on the written request of the applicant. 
 
(ii) Rule 22(1)   by providing that if an application is presented by any party to the 
proceedings for summoning of witness, the Tribunal shall issue summons for the 
appearance of such witness by recording the reasons for doing so, unless it considers that 
the appearance of the witness is not necessary for the just decision of the case. 
 
(iii) Rule 31(1)   by substituting that the Tribunal, after hearing the applicant and 
respondent, shall pass an order either at once, or as soon as thereafter, as may be 
practicable but not later than twenty -one days from the date of conclusion of the 
arguments; and where is it unable to do so, it shall record its reasons for such inability; 
and  
 
(iv) Rule 32(2)   by substituting that where it appears to the Tribunal that there is no 
sufficient ground for a review, it shall reject the application and record its reasons for so 
doing.

IV 
  
The Railways (Opening for Public Carriage of  Passengers)    Rules, 2000-
Implementation 
 of 149th Report of the Committee. 
  
The Railways (Opening for Public Carriage of Passengers) Rules, 2000 were included in 
the 149th Report of the Committee presented to the House on the 19 th August, 2003. The 
Committee in the 149th Report had recommended as under: 
  
"The Committee considered the comments of the Ministry on the questionnaire at its 
meetings held on the 7 th May, 2003. The  Committee notes with satisfaction that the 
Ministry of Railways have accepted the Committee's suggestions on (i) Rule 19 
Explanation to modify the provision so as to specify the documents or types of documents 
required to be forwarded to the Commissioner for initiation of electric traction on a line; 
and (ii) Rule 24(5) to substitute the words "earliest opportunity" with suitable words so 
as to specify that the action is taken within a time frame between period of initiation of 
diversion and use of such temporary diversion to ensure prompt action on the part of the 

Commissioner. The Committee however, recommends that Rule 24(4) may be amended 
by substituting the words "responsible officer" with the words "Railway servant in 
charge of the work" in view of th eir comments. The Committee hopes that the Ministry 
would carry out these amendments expeditiously under intimation to it". 
  
2.         Relevant extracts from the 149 th Report were forwarded to the Ministry of 
Railways for necessary action on the 22 nd August, 2003. The Ministry vide their 
communication dated the 24 th May, 2004 furnished Action Taken Note on Rules 24 (4) 
and 24(5). The Committee considered the matter at its meeting held on the 7 th January, 
2005. The points referred to the Ministry, their co mments received thereon and the 
observations of the Committee are reproduced below:- 
  
I           POINT REFERRED 
  
Rule 24 (4) regarding substitution of the words "responsible officer" with the words 
"Railway servant in charge of the work." 
  
            Ministry's reply 
  
            The suggestion made by the Committee in their last para regarding Rule 24 (4) 
has been accepted by the Ministry of Railways and the same has been sent along with 
other necessary amendments to the said rules to Ministry of Law , Justice and Company 
Affairs (Legal Section) for their legal vetting so that the said rules can be published in the 
gazette. The proposed amendments are still under correspondence with the legal Ministry 
and the issue is likely to be sorted out soon. 
  
Committee’s observations (7.1.2005) 
  
            The Committee noted that the Ministry are taking necessary action with regard to 
making changes in rule 24 (4) in pursuance to its recommendations. 
  
II          POINT REFERRED 
  
Rule 24 (5) regarding substitution of the words 'earliest opportunity' with suitable 
words so as to specify that action is taken within a time frame between period of initiation 
of diversion and use of such temporary diversion to ensure prompt action on the part of 
the Commissioner. 
  
            Ministry's reply 
  
            So far as the recommendation of the Committee regarding Rule 24 (5) is 
concerned, Ministry has decided not to make any change. 
  
            Committee’s observations (7.1.2005) 
  
            The Committee was not satisfied with the comments of the Ministry on rule 24 
(5).  It took exception to the Ministry’s statement that they had “decided not to make any 
change” in rule 24 (5) and desired that the Ministry may be asked to furnish a detailed 
justification why the Committee’s recommendations in relation to rule 24 (5) are not 
acceptable to them.    
  

3.         Accordingly, a reference was sent to the Ministry on the 5 th May, 2005.   The 
Ministry of Railways furnished Action Taken Note on the 3 rd May, 2005.  The Ministry 
have amended and notified Rule 24 (4) as per the Committee’s suggestion by substituting 
the words “responsible officer” with the words “railway servant in-change of work”.  
  
            Regarding Rule 24(5), the Ministry have furnished a detailed note justifying  their 
non-acceptance of the Committee’s suggestion on the said rule which is reproduced 
below: 
  
            “The Committee’s recommendation as contained in its 149th Report in relation to 
Rule 24 (5) could not be agreed to particularly in view that the day safety of the running 
trains is the responsibility of the Ministry of Railways and not that of the 
Commissioner.  As can be seen from the Section 6 of the Railways Act, 1989, the duties of 
the Commissioner are as given below:- 
  
Inspect any railway with a view to determine whether it is fit to be opened for the public 
carriage of passengers and report thereon to the Central Government as required by or 
under this Act. 
  
Make such periodical or other inspections of any railw ay or of any rolling stock used 
thereon as the Central Government may direct. 
  
Make an inquiry under this Act into the cause of any accident on a railway.  
  
Discharge such other duties as are conferred on him by or under this Act.   
  
Therefore, keeping the above in view, the provision under Rule 24 (5) for inspection of 
the temporary diversion continuing for more than 3 days by the Commissioner has been 
kept as discretionary rather than making it mandatory.  Therefore, it has been stipulated 
in Rule 24(5) that Commissioner may inspect it at the “earliest opportunity” rather than 
fixing any definite time limit.  
  
It is hoped that the Committee would appreciate the view of the Railway Ministry”. 
  
In accordance to their explanation as given above, the Mini stry have amended and 
notified Rule 24(5) by substituting sub rule (5) of rule 24 so as to provide that “where the 
use of temporary diversion as likely to be extended to more than 3 days, the 
Commissioner shall, if he considers it necessary, take the earliest possible opportunity of 
inspecting it”. 
  
4.         The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Ministry of Railways have since 
published the Railways (Opening for Public Carriage of Passengers) (Amendment) Rules, 
2005 vide notification No. G.S.R. 44(E) dated the 27 th January, 2005 (i) modifying the 
provisions of Rule 19  Explanation so as to provide that when sanction is required for the 
initiation of electric traction on a line already opened for passenger traffic, the documents 
specified in terms (j), (k), (l), (m) and (n) of sub rule (1), item (d) of sub rule (2) and item 
(a) (vii) of sub rule 5 shall be forwarded to the Commissioner; and (ii) in case of   rule 
24(4)  by substituting the words ‘responsible officer’ with the words ‘servant-in-charge’. 
Regarding Rule 24(5), the Committee accepts the explanation furnished by the Ministry 
of Railways for non -revision of the provisions of Rule 24(5) as suggested by it.   The 
Committee, however,  notes that as a follow up to their explanation, the Ministry have 
since amended Rule 24 (5) by providing that “where the use of temporary diversion is 

likely to be extended to more than 3 days the Commissioner shall, if he considers it 
necessary, take the earliest possible opportunity of inspecting it.”

The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Slaughter House) Rules, 2001. 
  
            The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Slaughter House) Rules , 2001 framed 
under the Prevention of Cruelty to animals Act, 1960 were examined and a questionnaire 
on the subject was forwarded to the Ministry of environment & Forests for their 
comments/clarifications on the 25 th November, 2003.   The Ministry furnished  their 
comments/clarifications on the 21st September, 2004.  The points referred to the Ministry 
and the comments/clarifications received thereon are reproduced below:- 
  
1          Point referred 
  
            The legislation under which these rules have been framed is of 1960.   In what 
manner the aspects covered by these rules were being regulated prior to framing of these 
rules? 
  
            Ministry comments 
  
            Prior to this legislation, the State governments/Local bodies formulated their own 
Rules and Regulations for functioning of the slaughter houses. 
  
II          Point referred 
            Rule 2 (c) Definition 
  
            “Slaughter house” means a slaughter house wherein 10   animals are slaughtered 
per day and is duly licensed or recognized under a Central, State or Provincial Act or any 
rules or regulations made thereunder”. 
  
            Are these rules applicable to such slaughter houses where less than 10 animals are 
slaughtered per day; why these have been excluded from the defini tion of “Slaughter 
House”? 
  
            Ministry’s comments 
  
            It may not be possible to depute so many veterinary officers to conduct the proper 
ant and post -mortem examination as the number of such places where animals are 
slaughtered in twos, threes, fours or even little more may be very high and at scattered 
places.  In addition to economic considerations, proper handling and disposal of slaughter 
wastes will be a problem.  Therefore, a minimum criterion of 10 animals to be slaughtered 
has been fixed to implement these Rules effectively and check the clandestine slaughter. 
  
III.       Point referred 
            Rule 4(2) Reception area or resting grounds 
  
            “The veterinary doctor shall examine thoroughly not more than 12 animals in an 
hour and not more than 96 animals in a day”. 
  

            Is it possible to examine 96 animals in a day which normally has eight working 
hours including lunch break? 
  
            Ministry’s comments 
  
            Depending on the species, time required for the examination varies.  The number 
specified is the maximum limit.   It is not mandatory for the veterinarian that he must 
examine 96 animals in a day. 
  
IV.       Point referred 
            Rule 4(3) Reception area or resting grounds. 
  
            “The veterinary doctor after examining the animal shall issue a fitness certificate 
in the form specified by the Central Government for this purpose”. 
  
            Whether Government h ave any objection in incorporating the format of fitness 
certificate in the rules itself? 
  
            Ministry’s comments 
  
            The format has to be in conformity with international/national requirements and 
therefore, this shall need modificatio n/amendments from time to time.   Hence not 
incorporated in the Rules itself. 
  
V.        Point referred 
            Rule 6(6) Slaughter  
  
            “A curbed -in bleeding area of adequate size as specified by the Central 
Government shall be provided in a  slaughter house and it shall be so located that the 
blood could not be splashed on other animals being slaughtered or on the carcass being 
skinned”. 
  
            What are the reasons that the Central Government have been authorized to 
specify the curbed -in bleeding area?   Does this size vary with the size of the Slaughter 
House and if so, can this function not be delegated to the State Governments or other 
local authority? 
  
            Ministry’s comments 
  
            The size of the slaughter house and species of the animals to be slaughtered shall 
govern the size of curbed -in bleeding area.   The implementing agencies are State 
Governments/Local bodies.   In order to maintain uniformity throughout the country, 
power has been vested with the Central Government.   
  
VI.       Point referred 
            Rule 9(1) Inspection of slaughter house  
  
            “The Animal Welfare Board of India or any person or Animal Welfare 
Organization authorized by it may inspect any slaughter house without notice to its owner 
or the person in charge of it at any time during the working hours to ensure that the 
provisions of these rules are being complied with”.  
  

What is the frequency of such inspections? Should the rules not prescribe a mini mum 
frequency of such inspections in every slaughter house so that the inspections are uniform 
and regular? 
The words ‘any person’ appear to be vague.  Whether Government have any objection in 
specifying the minimum level/status of such officer? 
  
Ministry’s comments 
  
The Government of India is agreeable to prescribe a minimum frequency of such 
inspections after working it out with the animal Welfare Board of India (AWBI), who are 
responsible for such inspections. 
  
The Government of India has no objection in specifying the minimum level/status of the 
inspecting officers in consultation with AWBI. 
  
2.         The Committee notes with satisfaction that the Ministry have accepted its 
suggestions on point no. 6 on Rule 9(1) regarding inspection of slaughter h ouse to (i) 
prescribe a minimum frequency of inspection of slaughter houses so as to make such 
inspection uniform and regular; and (ii) to specify the minimum level/status of the 
inspecting officers.   On rest of the points, the committee accepts the Minist ry’s 
comments.  The Committee hopes that the Ministry of environment & forests would carry 
out the amendments to the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Slaughter House) Rules, 
2001, expeditiously, under intimation to it.
