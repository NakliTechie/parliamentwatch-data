2. While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note d in some cases 
considerable time lag between their notifications a nd laying. The annexed 
statement also indicates the period of delay in cas es where notifications have 
not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by the  Committee. It also shows 
notifications not accompanied by a statement indica ting the reasons for the 
delay. The observations/recommendations of the Comm ittee in respect of the 
specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: -  
 
 
 
 
 
 

 
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation 
 
 Ministry of Civil Aviation  have laid only one Not ification (Sl. No. 10) 
which has been delayed by 9 months and is accompani ed with delay statement. 
The reason given by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 
 “Though the Notification was published on 26 
th  November, 2011, 
however due to late receipt of printed copies of No tification in the Ministry, 
the approval of the Hon’ble Ministry of Civil Aviation could not be obtained in 
time for laying on the Table of the House in the Bu dget Session. Hence there 
was administrative delay in laying on the Table of the Rajya Sabha, the papers 
relating to G.S.R. 323 regarding amendment to the R ule 24 of the Aircraft Act, 
1937.” 
    
   The Committee take serious note of such a long dela y as 9 months 
and find the reasons of delay given by the Ministry  unconvincing and 
evasive. In view of the fact that this is a statuto ry obligation to lay the 
notifications/orders/rules on the Table of the Hous e in time, the situation 
should have been assessed well before and adequate arrangements were 
made in respect thereof. The Committee advise the M inistry of Civil 
Aviation to take precaution in future so that such delays do not recur and 
time schedule prescribed in Committee’s 131 
st  Report be strictly adhared 
to. 
 
(ii) Ministry of Commerce and Industry 
 (Department of Commerce) 
 
 Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of C ommerce)  have 
laid 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 12 to 16) during the  Session. Out of these 2 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 13 – 14) have been delayed by 5 months and no delay 
statement is attached to both of delayed Notifications.  
 
   
The Committee take serious note of the delay in lay ing of 
Notifications by the Ministry of Commerce and Indus try (Department of 
Commerce). Further, there is no delay statement att ached to any of these 
Notifications. This is defiance of Committee’s reco mmendation contained 
in its 135 
th  Report which requires concerned Ministries/Departm ents to 
invariable append delay statement with the Notifica tion being laid so as to 
apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The Comm ittee desire that the 
Ministry of Commerce and Industry should invariably  append delay 
statement in case of any delay actually taking plac e in laying of 
notifications and avoid recurrence of such delay in future. 
  
 
 

 
 
 
(iii)  Ministry of Communication and Information Te chnology 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
Ministry of Communication and Information Technolog y (Department 
of Telecommunications) have laid 14 Notifications ( Sl. Nos. 24 to 37). Out of 
these, 5 Notifications have been delayed. The delay  period ranges from 4 
months to 6 months. One delayed Notification (Sl. N o. 24) has no delay 
statement attached with it while other 4 Notificati ons (Sl. Nos. 26 to 29) are 
appended with delay statement. The reasons given by  the Ministry for the said 
delay is reproduced as under:-  
 
“The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) i s a statutory 
body set up under the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997. In 
exercise of the powers conferred upon it under sect ion 36 of the TRAI Act, 
1997, the Authority may, by notification, make regu lations consistent with the 
Act and the Rules made there under to carry out the purposes of the Act. Under 
section 37 of the Act, the regulations notified by TRAI are required to be laid 
before each House of Parliament. 
The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) in  exercise of the 
powers conferred by Section 36 read with sub-clause s (vii) of clause (b) of 
sub-section (1) and clause (c) of sub-section (1) o f section 11 of the TRAI Act, 
1997 notified these regulations were given for printing to Government Press on 
the same day they were issued. In spite of pursuing  with the concerned 
officials of Government of India Press, the Gazette  of India notified copies of 
the regulations were received by TRAI form the Gove rnment of India Press on 
7
th  May, 2012. The Budget Session of Parliament ended on 22 nd  May, 2012 
with last day allotted for Ministry of Communicatio n & IT being 18 th  May, 
2012. Hence the time available for laying these reg ulations in the Parliament 
was less than 15 days and the regulations could not  be laid on the Table of the 
Rajya Sabha during the Budget Session 2012 of Parliament.” 
 
The Committee notes with concern the delay of four to six months 
in laying of the Notifications mainly due to late r eceipt of printed copies 
from Government of India Press and time taken in co mpleting the 
procedural formalities in the Ministry of Communication and Information 
Technology (Department of Telecommunications). In t his context the 
Committee observes that it is the Ministry’s respon sibility to ensure that 
the Notifications are laid before the Parliament wi thin the prescribed time 
schedule and accordingly, it is for the Ministry on ly to ensure that they 
receive the same timely from the statutory bodies u nder them and 
complete all formalities of laying well before the House is adjourned. As 
for time availability being less than 15 days, it m ay be stated that had the 
Ministry been prompt, the Notifications could well have been laid even on 
the last day of the Session with the consent of the  concerned Minister. The 

 
Committee recommends that the Department of Telecom munications may 
take necessary remedial measures to ensure that pap ers are laid well in 
time  on the Table of the House. It is also impress ed upon that all those 
Notifications which have been delayed should invari ably be accompanied 
with delay statements.  
 
(iv) Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution  
 (Department of Food and Public Distribution) 
 
 Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distr ibution 
(Department of Food and Public Distribution) have l aid 8 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 41 to 48). Out of these 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 41-45) have been delayed 
by 5 to 6 months. All of these delayed Notification s have been accompanied 
with a delay statement. The reasons for delay given  by the Ministry is as 
under:- 
 
 “Delay in laying the regulations on the table of b oth the Houses of 
Parliament is due to the reason that the WDRA becam e operational only on 
25 
th  October, 2010 and the appointments were made for t he posts of the 
Chairman on 27 th  October, 2010 and Members made on 1 st  November, 2010 
and on 1 st  February, 2011 respectively. The Warehousing (Deve lopment & 
Regulations) Act, 2007 required the approval of the  WDRA Advisory 
Committee and the Central Government for all the Re gulations framed under 
the Act. The Committee was constituted on 26 th  October, 2010. The draft 
regulations were approved by the Committee on 13 th  January, 2011. The 
Competent Authority approved the same on 7 th  September, 2011 and 16 th  
February, 2012. It also took long time in vetting a ll the draft regulations and 
their Hindi translations from the Official Language  Wing of the Ministry of 
Law and Justice, and hence, the resultant delay whi ch may kindly be 
condoned.” 
 
 The Committee do not approve the reasons given by the Ministry 
of Consumer Affairs for delay in tabling of Notific ations. The Committee 
further observe that there was sufficient time with the Ministry during the 
Budget Session as the three Notifications were publ ished well before the 
Session and other two Notifications were published during the first half of 
the Budget Session. The Committee further hopes tha t Ministry in such 
cases would strive to keep the delay to the minimum  and should take care 
that Notifications are laid on the Table of the Hou se at the first available 
opportunity after publication of Notifications. 
 
(v) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 28 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 49 to 
76).  Out of these 9 Notifications have been delaye d from 4 months to 18 
months. Eight (8) Notifications (Sl. Nos. 53-55, an d 59-63) have been attached 

 
with delay statement and other Notification (Sl. No. 57) has no delay statement 
attached to it. The reasons of delay given by the Ministry are as under:- 
 
 “In exercise of powers conferred by sub-section (3 ) of Section 20 of the 
Competition Act, this Ministry had enhanced the val ue of assets and the value 
of turnover, by 50% for the purpose of Section 5 of  the said Act vide 
notification No. S.O. 480 (E) dated 04.03.2011. 
 Similarly, in exercise of powers conferred by clau se (a) of Section 54 
of the Competition Act, this Ministry in public int erest vide notification No. 
S.O. 481(E) dated 04.03.2011 exempted the “Group” e xercising less than 50% 
of voting rights in other enterprises from the provisions of Section 5 of the Act, 
for a period of 5 years and also exempted an enterp rise, whose control shares, 
voting rights or assets being acquired has assets o f the value of not more than 
Rs. 250 crores or turnover of not more than Rs. 750  crores from the provisions 
of Section 5 of the Competition Act for a period of  5 years vide notification 
No. S.O. 482(E) dated 04.03.2011. 
 The notification dated 04.03.2011 relating to exem pting an enterprise 
whose control, shares, voting rights or assets bein g acquired has assets of the 
value of not more than Rs. 250 crores or turnover o f not more than Rs. 750 
crores was subsequently amended vide notification S .O. 1218(E) dated 
27.05.2011. 
 The above said notification is still under conside ration for further 
amendment continuously from the date of its notific ation in view of the 
concerns expressed by Competition Commission of India. 
 In view of the above, the above said notifications  could not be laid in 
time on the table of both the House of Parliament a s provided by sub-section 
(3) of Section 63 of the Competition Act.” 
 
The Committee is not happy with the inordinate dela y in laying  of 
papers on the Table of the House. The reason given by the Ministry of 
Corporate Affairs that the notification could not b e laid down because of 
the amendments taking place during the period is no t convincing. The 
notifications nos. S.O. 480, 481 and 482 dated 04.03.2011 should have been 
laid in time without waiting for amendments. As it is a Constitutional 
provision to lay the notifications within a prescri bed time schedule, the 
Ministry should have taken appropriate action to la y the same on the 
Table of the House. The Notification no. S.O. 1218( E)/dated 27/5/2011 
should have followed later. The Committee, therefor e, recommends that 
adequate precaution should be exercised to avoid re currence of such 
instances in future.

3. The Committee took up for consideration its draf t 201 st  Report on 
Statutory Orders Laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha d uring the 226 th  Session. 
The Members noticed inordinate delays in the framin g of rules/regulations 
after Acts are enforced.  The Ministries are not adhering to the time limit of six 
months in this regard and also not taking Committee 's approval in cases of 
delays beyond six months, as recommended by the Com mittee.  Besides, the 
Committee also found that despite repeated observations and recommendations 
by the Committee in its various Reports, Ministries /Departments continue to 
lay their notifications, etc. on the Table of the H ouse after considerable delays 
spanning from months to years.  Around 33% delayed notifications are laid 
with no delay statement explaining the reason for d elay.  And even when such 
statements are attached, unacceptable excuses like delays in arranging printed 
copies are being given.  The Committee felt that th e Ministries/Departments 
are not taking its observations and recommendations  seriously and have a 
casual attitude towards framing and laying of these  rules/regulations - an 
important duty towards Parliament.  No senior offic ers, it appears, are 
monitoring this exercise in the Ministries.  The Co mmittee's concern centered 
mainly around the serious implications of delayed f raming and laying of 
notification containing rules/regulations/orders, e tc.  The Committee is 
constrained to infer that Ministries/Departments ma y be doing so deliberately 
with a view to evading Parliament's scrutiny of the  rules/regulations that are 
delegated by it to be framed by the Government.  Si nce such rules/regulations 
come into force even before they are laid before Pa rliament, any delay in 
laying before Parliament, in fact, prevents Members  to exercise their right to 
get them modified/annulled by moving a Motion.  And  this is what exactly 
happening today.  Another disturbing problem was de layed response to the 
Committee's communications from the Ministries, and  not acknowledging its 
letters, etc. in time.  So many cases of scrutiny o f rules/regulations are pending 
before the Committee mainly due to pending response s from them for many 

 
years.  They go on seeking extension on one pretext  or the other.  This has 
become a norm than exception.  It highly restricts the normal flow of 
Committee's work and then parliamentary scrutiny.  
4. In such a situation, the Committee felt not to r emain a mute spectator; 
but to protect its right to be informed, scrutiny a nd surveillance.  The 
Committee discussed possible courses of action on t his issue.  Since it 
amounted actually bypassing Parliament and disobeyi ng its authority, it is a 
serious breach of privileges not only of the Commit tee but also of the House 
and its members, which can be invoked in this case.   To begin with, however, 
it was decided that its Chairperson should write to  all the Ministers in this 
regard.  As per its earlier decision, the Committee  has already summoned 
Secretaries of some Ministries to explain about thi s state of affairs and would 
be calling others for this purpose.  The Committee also decided to apprise the 
Hon’ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha about the same.
