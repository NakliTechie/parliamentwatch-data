        I,      the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having been authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf,
do hereby present this Hundred and  Thirty Second  Report of the Committee
.
1.2   The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on February 7, 2001.
 
1.3     The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during its 191st session have been perused by the

Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of the Rajya Sabha
(191st Session)
        During the 191 st Session of  the Rajya Sabha, 205 Statutory Orders were laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha by the various
Ministries/Departments.  The details of the Gazette  Notifications in which these were published and the date of their laying on the Table of the
Rajya Sabha have been  indicated in the enclosed statement Ministrywise (Annexure).  Out of these 205 Orders, the Committee has identified six for
detailed scrutiny.  The Orders so identified  have been indicated in the statement.
 
2.
        During the course of general scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has come across several cases where there has been a considerable
time lag between the date of  notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The statement as at Annexure contains a specific mention of the
period of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the Committee’s guidelines.  In many cases the delay is abnormal.  The
observations/recommendations of the Committee in this behalf are contained in the succeeding paragraphs.
 
        3.     The Committee is constrained to note that the Ministry of Finance has taken an unduly long time in laying a large number of regulations
(Sl. nos 32-52 of the Annexure) issued under the Foreign Exchange and Management  Act, 1999 that too without any delay statements. As may be
seen, all these notifications were published in the Gazette  in the first half  of May, 2000 and the Ministry of Finance could have laid these
notifications in the Monsoon Session of  the Rajya Sabha,  which was there  from July 24 to August 25, 2000.  Similarly, there has been a delay in
laying of the regulations at Sl. Nos.  53, 54, 55, 66 and 71 of the Annexure concerning Nationalized  Banks  which  were  also  laid  without  any 
delay  statements. 
 
The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Finance should avoid such delays in laying of such statutory orders before the
Parliament. 
 
4.
        In case of the Ministry of Home Affairs, the Committee regrets to note that there has been an unusually long delay ranging from  one year
to five years nine months in laying of Statutory Orders (Sl. No.107-111 of the Annexure) pertaining to Union Territories of Daman & Diu and
Andaman & Nicobar Islands issued under  the Regulations framed in exercise of powers conferred under Article  240  of the Constitution  of India. 
The Committee has  taken  a  serious  note  of this delay and also the fact that the Ministry of  Home Affairs  have not appended any statement
giving reasons for delay  while laying  these Statutory Orders.  The Committee would like the Ministry of Home Affairs to avoid recurrence of such
instances for future.
                       5.     Similarly,  The Ministry of Rural Development have also delayed the laying  of Statutory Orders (Sl. 166-168 of the Annexure)
concerning Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu issued under Regulations framed in exercise of powers conferred by Article 240 of the

Constitution.  In this case also, the delay is even greater ranging from four years seventeen  days to five years ten months. The Ministry of Rural
Development have also not appended any delay statement while laying of these Statutory Orders.  The Committee takes a serious note of these
cases of extreme delay in laying of Statutory Orders before the Parliament.  The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Rural Development would
take due precaution to avoid recurrence of such instances.
               
        6.     The Committee would like to impress upon all the Ministries/Departments to avoid delay in  the laying of Statutory Orders before
Parliament. With a view to ensure  this  the  Committee  reiterates  its
observation made in its 131
st Report that Orders framed under any Central Act, should be laid on the Table of the House in compliance with the
provisions of the Act on the first available opportunity. The Committee further reiterates its following recommendation as contained in the said
report which lay down the time schedule  for the Ministry/Departments of Government of India in the matter of laying of Statutory Orders:-
 
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid  (i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following the
date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is  in Session, on the date of the  publication of  the Order,
during its continuance and in case the time  lag  between  the  date  of  publication  and  the  date  of  the
 
closing  of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session.  All the
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued
under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.”
 
7.
        While scrutinizing  the Orders the Committee also examined the aspect whether the original orders that have been laid during the Session
have been framed under the appropriate authority of the concerned Act.  The Committee notes with satisfaction that these Orders have been framed
within the parameters prescribed under the relevant Acts.
