2. During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, t he Committee has noted some cases where 
there has been a considerable time lag between the date of notification of the Order and the date 
of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates  the period of delay in cases where 

notifications have not been laid as per the schedul e prescribed by the Committee and where such 
notifications were accompanied by a statement indic ating the reasons for the delay in laying. In 
some of the cases the delay has been quite long. Th e observations/recommendations of the 
Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: -    
 
 
(i) Ministry of Agriculture 
 (Department of Agriculture and Cooperation) 
 
Out of 9 notifications (Sl. Nos. 1 to 9) laid by th e Ministry of Agriculture (Department of 
Agriculture and Cooperation)  3 notifications (Sl. Nos. 5 to 7)  were found to ha ve  been delayed, 
by four months, although these notifications were accompanied by Delay Statements. The reasons 
of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “The notifications was sent on 8 
th  November, 2010 to the Government of India Press fo r 
its publication in the Gazette of India (Extraordin ary) Part II, Section 3, Sub-Section (ii) and also 
for printing of 200 copies of each notifications fo r laying in both Houses of Parliament. 
Although, the order was published on the 8 th  November, 2010 in the Gazette of India 
(Extraordinary) but printed copies were not available/provided by the press in time. These Orders 
were required to be laid in both the Houses  of the  Parliament during the winter session, 2010. 
The same could not be laid due to paucity of time.” 

 
The Committee are not convinced by the reason given  for delay by the Ministry that 
the printed copies of Gazette Notifications could n ot be made available in time. Ministry 
should have mentioned the date when printed copies were to be given to it by the 
Government of India Press as also the efforts made by it to get the printing expedited. Delay 
could have been avoided, had adequate precaution be en taken by the Ministry in this 
regard. The Committee feel  that the Ministry of Ag riculture  did not take it with required 
seriousness. Such delays in future, particularly on  account of delay in receiving printed 
copies of notifications will not be acceptable to t he Committee. Ministry would have to 
adhere to the time schedule for notifying as well a s laying of papers as prescribed in 
Committee’s 131 st  Report. 
 
 
 
 
(ii) Ministry of Commerce and  Industry 
  
(Department of Commerce and Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion) 
 

 Ministry of  Commerce and Industry (Department of Commerce) have laid 5 notifications, 
out of which two notifications (Sl. Nos. 18 and 20)  have been delayed by 4 months and six 
months respectively. There is no delay statement appended to both of them. 
 
 Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion have  laid 9 notifications, 5 of these 
notifications have been delayed. Out of 5 delayed n otifications only 3 were accompanied with 
delay statements; 2 notifications had no delay statements appended to them. The Committee notes 
that in majority of cases delay period range from 3  to 6 months. But in two cases Sl. Nos. 28 and 
29) there is delay of one year and seven months and  2 years. The reasons of delay are given as 
under:- 
 
 “Electrical Wires, Cable Appliances and Protection  Devices and Accessories (Quality 
Control) Amendment Order, 2009 (S.O. 512 (E) of 200 9) was published in Gazette of India, 
Extraordinary, Part-II, Section 3 (ii) dated 19 Feb ruary, 2009. Later it was decided to rescind the 
said Quality Control Order No. S.O. 512 (E) of 2009 , on the request of Industry Associations as 
they faced some procedural difficulties in implemen ting this order within time limit fixed in the 
said order and therefore could not be sent to Rajya Sabha Secretariat to be laid on the Table of the 
House. The said Quality Control Order was rescinded  vide Gazette Notification No. S.O. 2058 
(E) dated 07.08.2009 Electrical Wires, Cables, Appl iances and Protection Devices and 
Accessories (Quality Control) Amendment Order, 2009. 

 After cross verification with related records, it has been observed that the subject 
notifications were not laid on the Table of the Hou ses because the requisite number of copies 
(authenticated + additional copies) as required under the procedure were not sent at the time. This 
lapse was purely unintentional and occurred due to oversight. The delay is deeply regretted and 
may be condoned. It is also ensured that such lapse would not recur in future.” 
 
The Committee have noted the reply given by the Min istry of Commerce and 
Industry (Department of Industrial Policy and Promo tion) that the notifications could not 
be laid due to inadvertence. The Committee hope tha t the Ministry would in future be 
careful about laying of its papers and see that per sons responsible for this remain vigilant 
about it. As regards reason for decision to rescind  the earlier notification on the feedback 
from the industry within no time after it was issue d, shows that required consultation were 
not done with the Industry before it was notified. This should be avoided in future because 
such delays might lead to avoidable complications.  
 
(iii) Ministry of Communication and Information Tec hnology 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
 Ministry of Communication and Information Technolo gy (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid one notification (Sl.  No. 35) which has been delayed by 3 
months. There is delay statement attached to it which reads as under:- 

 
 “The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) is a statutory body set up under the 
Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997. In  exercise of the powers conferred upon it 
under section 36 of the TRAI Act, 1997, the Authori ty may, by notification, make regulations 
consistent with the Act and the Rules made there un der to carry out the purposes of the Act. 
Under section 37 of the Act, the regulations notifi ed by TRAI are required to be laid before each 
House of Parliament. 
 The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) i n exercise of the powers conferred 
by sub-section (1) of section 36, read with sub-clauses (i), (iii) and (v) of clause (b) of sub-section 
(1) of section 11 of the TRAI Act, 1997 notified “Telecommunication Mobile Number Portability 
(Second Amendment) Regulations, 2010.” The said Reg ulation was issued on 24.11.2010. On 
receipt of the copies of the Gazette Notification o n 7 
th  December, 2010 from the Government of 
India Press, TRAI sent the Regulation to the Depart ment on 8 th  December, 2010. However, the 
Parliament was adjourned sine die on 13 th  December, 2010 and the Regulation could not be lai d 
on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during the Winter Session 2010 of Parliament.” 
 
The Committee are not inclined to accept the late r eceipt of printed copies from the 
Government of India Press as a valid reason for a d elayed laying of notifications etc. The 
Ministry of Communications and Information Technolo gy  should in future ensure that all 
its papers were laid before the House as prescribed in the Committee’s 131 st  Report. 
 

(iv) Ministry of Consumer Affairs 
 (Department of Food and Public Distribution) 
 
 Ministry of Consumer Affairs (Department of Food a nd Public Distribution have laid 7 
notifications (Sl. Nos. 45 to 51). Two of these (Sl . Nos. 49 and 50) had been delayed by three 
months and one year respectively. But the delay sta tement was appended with only one of the 
delayed notifications.  
  
   The Committee take strong exception to the Ministry ’s failure to lay the delay 
Statement. It fails to understand how in one case Delay Statement was laid and in other case 
it was not. It shows that papers before being sent for laying were not being seen by senior 
officers and it is being taken as a routine work. T he Committee feel that it amounts to 
restricting Parliament’s right to be informed about  delegated legislation and views it 
seriously. This should be avoided in future without a genuine cause.  
 
(v) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 21 notific ations and one notification (Sl. No. 53) 
has been delayed by 5 months and the delay statemen t is appended to the notification. The 
reasons of delay is given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 

 “The notification could not be laid before both th e Houses of Parliament within the 
prescribed period as the required number of copies of the notification could not be made available 
within the stipulated time frame from the Governmen t Press. Accordingly, the notifications are 
being laid before both the Houses of Parliament now”.  
 
The Committee take serious view in cases where the delay is attributed to non-
availability of the required number of copies of no tifications. Ministries have been taking 
recourse to this plea very frequently. This possibility  should have been foreseen well in time 
and adequate arrangements made in respect thereof. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
must ensure that such delay don’t recur in future.

The Committee are anguished to see that  the Minist ry of Environment and Forests 
not only had delayed the laying of 8 of its notific ations, it had also failed to lay delay 
statement that  it is required to do so. The Commit tee in this connection invite attention to 
its recommendation contained in its 135 
th  Report. The Committee have decided to take a 

serious view in such failures particularly by a Min istry like Environment and Forests that 
lays fairly large number of notifications etc. duri ng every session. The Ministry should 
know that such failure in laying papers amounts to curtailing the Parliament’s right to be 
informed about subordinate legislations. 
 
(vii) Ministry of External Affairs  
 
 The only notification that the Ministry of Externa l Affairs have laid is delayed by 3 
months and no delay statement is  appended to it.

The Ministry should appreciate the constitutional significance behind that requirement that

should take care that repetition of such delay is avoided in future.

 “Orders required to be laid before the House shoul d be laid (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following t he date of publication of the Order in 

the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Se ssion, on the date of the publication of the 
Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication and 
the date of the closing of the Session is less than  15 clear days, before the expiry of the 
Session immediately following the said Session. All  the Ministries/Departments of 
Government of India should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of 
the Orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. 
(131 st  Report) 
(Presented on 15 December 2000) 
 
 
 “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized tha t it is essential that in case there is 
a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the conc erned Ministry/Department should 
invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee de sires all Ministries/Departments to 
take a note of this, since the intension is that th rough the process of formulation of a delay 
statement the Ministry is able to introspect, ident ify bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135 th  Report) 

(Presented on 27 July 2001)

number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no notification should be 
published/laid which does not carry a notification number. The Committee expects all 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India to ex ercise caution in this regard. In case 
where there are more than one notification under a single file, the Ministries/Departments 
should ensure that the file number on each notifica tion should be suitably distinguished 
from one another.
