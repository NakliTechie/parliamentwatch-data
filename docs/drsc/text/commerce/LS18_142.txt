Report 
  
I 
Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of  Rajya Sabha 
  
  
            During the 195th Session of the Rajya Sabha, 267 Statutory Orders were laid 
on the Table of the House by the various Ministries/Departments.   The details of the 
Gazette Notifications in which these were published & the date of their laying on th e 
Table of the Rajya Sabha have been indicted in the enclosed Ministry wise statement 
(Annexure). 
  
2.         During the course of general scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has 
noted some cases where there has been a considerable time lag between th e date of 
notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the 
Committee’s guidelines.  In some of the cases the delay has been quite abnormal.  The 
observations/ recommendations of the Committee respect of specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows:- 
             
i.         Ministry of Commerce & Industry  
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Commerce and Industry have laid 
on the Table of the Rajya Sabha the Tobacco Board (Recruitment) Regulations, 1984 
as well as the Tobacco Board (Recruitment) Regulations, 1989 just because it was not 
possible for them to confirm beyond doubt whether these regulations had been laid 

before both House of Parliament earlier as the relevant records were not traceable. 
The Committee, while it appreciates the initiative of the Ministry, but, at the same 
time, it expects them to set right their records relating to the framing and the laying 
of subordinate legislation under various Acts being administered by them at-least for 
future so that such situations are avoided. 
  
ii.         Ministry of Communication and Information Technology 
            The Committee regrets to find that out of the five notifications laid by the 
Ministry of Communication and Information Technology, there has been a delay in 
case of two (sl. nos. 27 and 28 of the Annexure). The Committee expects the Ministry 
of Communication, etc. to exercise caution in such matters for future and ensure that 
the schedule as prescribed by the Committee in the matter of laying of subordinate 
legislation framed under various Acts of Parliament is followed meticulously from 
time to time. 
  
iii.        Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution 
                        The Committee regrets to note that out of 15 notifications, there has 
been a delay in laying of seven as mentioned at sl. Nos. 4,5,6,9,10, 11 and 17A of the 
Annexure. The Committee would like the Ministry of Consume r Affairs, etc. to take 
steps to avoid recurrence of such instances and ensure the compliance of the schedule 
as laid down by the Committee in the matter of laying of rules/regulations, etc. in its 
131st and 135th Reports. 
  
iv.        Ministry of Finance 
                        The Committee deeply regrets to note that for the sixth consecutive 
Session, the Ministry of Finance is coming under its particular notice due to delay in 
the laying of subordinate legislation on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. Ironic ally, the 
magnitude of the default is larger this time. The Ministry have laid 184 
rules/regulations during the 195th Session and there has been delay in case of 141 (sl. 
nos. 34, 37 39 -80, 94 -130, 135 -160, 172, 173,174, 176 -197 and 226 -227 of the 
Annexure). The Committee is even further pained to note the period by which the 
laying has been delayed which roughly averages anywhere between 8 –12 months. In 

the opinion of the Committee, calls for immediate corrective action. The Committee 
directs the Ministry of Finance to take action to prevent such lapses in the future. 
  
v.         Ministry of Home Affairs 
                        The Committee notes with regret that out of the seven notifications laid 
by the Ministry of Home Affairs during the 195 th Session, there has been a delay in 
case of five. In case of the Central Industrial Security Force Group “A” and   “B” 
Civilian Gazetted Posts Recruitment Rules, 2000, the delay is as long as of eleven 
months. The Committee would like the Ministry of Home Affairs to have a relook into 
the internal mechanism in place so as to obviate such instances for the future. 
  
vi.        Ministry of Information & Broadcasting 
The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of 1 year and 2 
months on the part of Minist ry of Information & Broadcasting in laying their only 
notification, namely, the Prasar Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) 
(Audience Research Posts) Service Regulations (Sl. No. 245). 
             
The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting would 
take due care & ensure prompt compliance of the schedule prescribed by the 
Committee for the purpose. 
  
vii.       Ministry of Labour 
                        The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Labour have laid 
two notifications during the 195th Session and there has been a delay in both the cases. 
The Ministry could have laid both the notifications during the 194 th (Winter Session 
2001) of the Rajya Sabha, but they failed to do so. The Committee hopes that they 
would take necessary action and avoid such instances. 
                          
viii.      Ministry of Shipping 
The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Shipping has laid five 
notifications during the 195th Session and three out of them were delayed by a period 
ranging from 4-8 months. The Committee expects the Ministry to take necessary and 

corrective action in order to ensure that such delays do not recur and the schedule as 
prescribed by the Committee in the matter of laying of subordinate legislati on is 
followed, strictly.  
  
3.         Besides the above, the Committee also notes with regret the isolated cases of 
delayed laying of notifications by the Ministry of Human Resource Development (sl. 
no. 36 of the Annexure), Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs (sl. no. 249 of 
the Annexure), Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (sl. no. 258 of the 
Annexure) and Ministry of Textiles (sl. no. 258 of the Annexure). The Committee 
would like these  Ministries to take note of the time schedule which is  reproduced 
hereinafter and ensure that the same is complied with meticulously for future. The 
Committee, accordingly, reiterates its observations made in its 135th Report : 
  
 “ The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential 
that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the 
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay 
statement along with the notifications being laid so as t o apprise the 
Parliament of the causes of delay.   The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intention is that 
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is 
able to introspect, identifying b ottlenecks and devising remedial 
measures”.   
  
4.         The Committee impresses again upon all the Ministries/Departments to follow 
the time schedule for laying as prescribed by it in its 131st Report as reproduced below 
:- 
  
“Orders required to be laid  before the House should be laid   (i) if the 
House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following the 
date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the 
House is in Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, during 
its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication 
and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, 

before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said 
Session.  All the Ministries/Departments of  Government of India 
should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of 
the orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the 
Rajya Sabha.”

2.         The Committee, thereafter, took up for consideration its draft 142nd Report on 
the Statutory Orders laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha during the 195 th Session. The 
Committee noted with serious concern the delay on the part of 
Ministries/Departments in la ying of the various rules/regulations before the 

Parliament. There was a reference to the delay of 17 and 12 years, respectively in the 
laying of the Tobacco Board General Regulations, 1984 and the Tobacco Board 
Recruitment Regulations, 1989 by the Ministr y of Commerce and Industry. The 
Committee took a serious view on the Ministry’s statement that it was not possible 
for them to confirm beyond doubt whether these regulations had been laid before the 
Parliament earlier due to relevant records being not trac eable. The Committee felt 
this to be a sorry state of affairs and desired that such matters alongwith other cases 
where there has been a long delay in the laying of the rules/regulations be taken up 
with the concerned Ministries. The Committee also decided  that these 
Ministries/Departments in whose cases, there has been persistent delay in laying of 
the rules/regulations despite repeated recommendations/observations by it may also 
be identified and the   Secretary incharge of such Ministries/ Departments may  be 
asked to appear before the Committee for clarifications.  
  
3.         During the course of discussion on the draft Report, there was also a mention 
about the delay in framing of the subordinate legislation under various Acts of 
Parliament. A view was expressed that there should be some definite criteria for 
ascertaining whether there has been a delay in framing of the rules/ regulations or 
not. While it was appreciated that the process of framing of subordinate legislations 
was t ime consuming and the time required for the purpose may vary from one 
legislation to another, but, there was a suggestion that the Ministries/Departments 
may also be required to indicate in the memorandum of delegated legislation 
appended to the Bill, whic h gives the areas which are contemplated to be covered 
through subordinate legislation, the approximate time which would be required for 
framing the subordinate legislation and any delay beyond this time limit could be 
pursued by the Committee as a case of  delay. The Committee desired this aspect to 
be examined. The Committee also desired the Secretariat to examine the cases where 
there has been an unusual delay in framing of the rules/regulations for its 
consideration. The draft 142nd Report was, thereafter, adopted.
