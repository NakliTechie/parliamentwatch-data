INTRODUCTION 
1.   I, the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having been authorized 
by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this Hundred 
and Sixty-Second Report of the Committee. 
1.2  The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on the 9 th 
March, 2006. 
1.3       Notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the Table of the Rajya 
Sabha during its 206 th session  (November-December, 2005) have been perused by  the 
Committee in the light of the provisions of the Acts under which these were framed and 
also with a view to seeing whether these have been laid properly and within the prescribed 
time-frame.  The Committee’s observations/recommendations are contained i n the 
report that follows.

2.                  During the course of scrutiny of these Notifications/Orders/Schemes, the 
Committee has noted some cases of delay of upto 2 years between the date of notification 
of the Order and the date of its  laying.  The annexed statement also indicates the period 
of delay in the case of notifications, which have not been laid as per the schedule 
prescribed by the Committee and also whether such notifications were accompanied by 
any statement indicating the reason for the delay in laying. The 
observations/recommendations of the Committee in respect of these specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows:- 
(i)         Ministry of Chemical & Fertilizers 
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers, 
Department of Chemicals & Petrochemicals, delayed the laying of 5 of their  notifications 
(Sl. Nos. 4-8) out of the total of 7 notifications (Sl Nos. 3 -8) laid during the 206 th session. 
The delay in all the five cases was of 6 months. Further, none of the delayed notification 
was accompanied by any delay statement explaining the reasons for delay. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers should strictly 
adhere to th e time schedule as recommended by it in its 131 st Report for laying of the 
Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and in case of any delay in laying of the 
notifications, a delay statement should invariably be appended. 
(ii)        Ministry of Finance 
The Committee notes that the Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue, laid 
81 notifications during the session. There was, however, a delay in laying in case of 8 
notifications (Sl. Nos. 97,128 -134). The period of delay in case of all these notifications  

was approximately 5 months. No delay statement was attached with any of these 
notifications. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance shall ensure timely 
laying of their notifications and adhere to the time schedule as prescribed by it in its 131st 
Report. Further, the Committee hopes that the Ministry shall keep the Parliament 
informed of the reasons for delay in the laying of their Subordinate Legislation. 
(iii)       Ministry of Home Affairs 
The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry of Home Affairs delayed the 
laying of 2 of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 156 -157) out of the total of 8 notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 153-160) laid by them during the session. Further, the Ministry did not attach any 
delay statement with these 2 notifications. The Committee has been emphasizing for the 
timely laying of the notifications and has been recommending time and again for 
appending of delay statements in all the cases of delay to keep the Parliament apprised of 
the re asons/causes of delay. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Home 
Affairs should avoid such delay in future and append delay statement wherever the 
schedule as laid down by the Committee in its 131st Report is not adhered to. 
(iv)       Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions 
The Committee notes with deep concern that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances and Pensions, Department of Personnel & Training have delayed the laying 
of 18 notifications (Sl Nos. 179, 184-197,200-202) out of the total 30 notifications (Sl. Nos. 
176-205) laid by them during the 206 th session. Further, the period of delay ranges from 
a minimum of 5 months to the maximum of 2 years. Although, the Ministry 
have  appended delay statements with 15 notifications (Sl Nos. 179,184-197) the reasons 
of delay stated therein are not convincing enough to justify delay that ranges between 1 -
2 years in the laying of the notifications. For the notifications listed at Sl Nos. 184 -189 
where the delay ranges between 1 -2 years the reason given by the Ministry is -- "Due to 
the administrative exigencies and frequent transfers of the officer dealing with the 
subject, the matter got delayed because the new incumbent needed time to acquaint 
himself with the procedure of publication,  follow up with government of India press for 
GSR copies and laying down in both the Houses of Parliament"is not justified. The 
Committee views with serious concern that inspite of its repeated recommendations 
regarding the timely laying of Subordinate Leg islation, there has not been any 
appreciable improvement in the position in case of Department of Personnel & Training. 
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and 
Pensions have again failed to adhere to the time schedule prescribed by the Committee in 
its 131 st Report. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances and Pensions to take immediate remedial steps to prevent such   instances of 
delay in the laying of their Subordinate Legislation. 
(v)        Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways 
The Committee notes with regret that out of the 274 notifications (Sl. Nos. 214 -
487) laid by the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways, Department of Road 
Transport and Highways, th ere has been delay in the laying of 70 notifications (Sl Nos. 
214,217-218,239-260,397-412,436-465 & 483). The Ministry appended delay statements in 
case of 45 notifications (Sl. Nos. 397 -412,436-465 & 483) only. Rest of the delayed 
notifications at serial Nos. 214,217 -218, 239 -260 were not accompanied by any delay 
statement. The Committee expects the Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport and 
Highways, Department of Road Transport and Highways to be more prompt and vigilant 
in the laying of rules/regulations within the stipulated period as prescribed in the 131 st 
Report of the Committee and to take corrective measures to plug the loopholes existing 
in the current procedure for laying of Subordinate Legislation.  
3.         The Committee also notes that there h as been delay in laying of one notification 
each by the Ministries of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution (Sl. No. 15), 
Environment & Forests (Sl. No. 24), Human Resource Development (Department of 

Elementary Education & Literacy) ( Sl. No. 161) a nd Department of Secondary and 
Higher Education (Sl. No. 166). Further, the notifications at Sl. Nos. 15, 24 and 166 were 
not accompanied by any delay statements. The Committee hopes that these Ministries 
would devise appropriate measures to remove every i nstance of delay in the laying of 
their notifications. The Committee further hopes that these Ministries shall be particular 
to append delay statements in all the cases of delay to keep the Parliament   apprised of 
the position. 
4.         Besides, the time ly laying of Subordinate legislation, the Committee has been 
consistently reiterating that each notification should have a distinctive number so that 
the same is identifiable. However, during the 206 th   Session, the Committee has come 
across two instances where the notifications were laid without a distinct number. These 
notifications pertain to the Ministry of Finance, Department of Economic Affairs at Sl. 
Nos. 40 & 60. The Committee reiterates that every notification should have a distinctive 
number to g ive it a separate identity. The Committee recommends that such instances 
should not be repeated and Ministries/ Departments should ensure that every notification 
is assigned a distinct number so that the same is identifiable at  later stage.  
5.         The Committee with a view to ensure timely and proper laying of Subordinate 
Legislation by Government has been monitoring the laying of Subordinate Legislation by 
various Ministries/ Departments. Accordingly, it has been making 
observations/recommendations i n case of the Ministries/Departments who have been 
defaulting in the laying of their Subordinate legislation. The Committee while carrying 
forward its monitoring exercise invited the representatives of the Ministries of Shipping 
, Road Transport & Highways  (Department of Road Transport & Highways), Human 
Resource Development (Department of Secondary & Higher Education), Personnel, 
Public Grievances and Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training), and Information 
& Broadcasting before it for oral evidence on the 5 th October 2005.   These were the 
Ministries which had been consistently defaulting in the compliance of the Committee's 
recommendations regarding the timely laying of rules/regulations framed under various 
central Acts for the past few sessions. Du ring the course of the oral evidence these 
ministries were requested to explain their in house procedure for laying along with the 
reasons for delay in laying of their Subordinate legislation as well as their failure to 
append delay statements with the not ifications delayed in laying. The representatives 
apprised the Committee of the procedural difficulties and administrative bottlenecks but 
could not furnish any explanation for not appending delay statements in the required 
cases. The representatives, howe ver, assured the Committee that they would take 
immediate corrective steps  for the timely laying of Subordinate legislation. 
6.         In pursuance of the said oral evidence, the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training) furnished an action taken 
note detailing the steps taken by them for plugging the loopholes in the timely laying of 
Subordinate Legislation. The steps detailed included publishing of all the statutory rules/ 
orders/ notifications of the Ministry in the "Extraordinary Gazette" for obviating delay 
in obtaining GSR Number/ Gazette copies from the Government of India press and 
introduction of a register of the notifications to be laid before Parliament which would be 
scrutinized at the level of the Deputy Secretary/Director once a week during Parliament 
Session and once in a fortnight during inter -session period. Similarly, the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport and Highways (Department of Road Transport & Highways) 
have also issued guidelines fo r timely laying of Subordinate Legislation by their 
Department. It includes obtaining of 80 copies in place of one copy by the officer 
concerned, processing of notifications for laying at a single point in the Ministry and the 
monitoring of the process centrally in the Ministry at the level of the signing authority.  
7.         The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting also furnished a detailed action 
taken note enlisting the corrective measures adopted by them to avoid lapses/delays in 

the laying of the ir Subordinate Legislation before Parliament. The corrective 
measures/instructions included (i) media unit to obtain a copy of the notification from the 
directorate of printing and make the same available to the joint Secretary concerned who 
shall personal ly monitor the case; (ii) laying cases to be processed on urgent basis at 
highest level; (iii) processing in the media Unit to be completed within one week; (iv) 
routing of the laying files through Parliament cell to the Minister for clearing; (v) 
maintaining of a register for monthly review of the rules/regulations required to be laid 
(vi) appending of delay statements with the delayed notifications containing valid and 
cogent reasons and   (vii) compiling of information regarding the rules/regulations 
whether or not laid on the table of both the Houses of Parliament and its review by the 
Additional Secretary on bi monthly basis. 
8.         It is a matter of regret that despite assuring the Committee of remedial action 
during the course of the oral evidence on the 5th October, 2005 the Ministries of Shipping, 
Road Transport & Highways and Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions have again 
turned out to be defaulters in the immediately following   206th session of Rajya Sabha . 
The Committee would like these Ministries to be more serious in implementing the 
corrective steps they have devised. 
The Committee recommends and hopes that all the Ministries, particularly the 
defaulting ones, to devise such in house procedures to streamline the laying of their 
Subordinate Legislation. 
  
9.         The Committee finally impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take 
immediate steps to avoid delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation and brings to their 
notice the following recommendations, as contained in its 131 st   135th and 152nd  Reports 
:- 
  
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not 
in Session, during the Session immediately following the date of publication of the 
Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House i s in Session, on the date of the 
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between 
the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 
clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. 
All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth comply 
with this schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued under various Acts 
of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 
  
"The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case 
there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the 
notifications being laid so as to  apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The 
Committee desires all Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the 
intention is that through the process of formulation of a delay statement the 
Ministry is able to introspect, identifying bottlen ecks and devise remedial 
measures". 
(135th Report) 
  
“In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned a 
distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no 
notification should be published/laid which does not carry a notification number. 
The Committee expects all Ministries/Departments of Government of India to 
exercise caution in this regard. In case where there are more than one notification 

under a single file, the Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number 
on each notification should be suitably distinguished from one another.
