Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of the Rajya Sabha

During the 192 Session of the Rajya Sabha, 88 Statutory Orders were
laid on the Table of the House by the various Ministries/Departments. The
details of the Gazette Notifications in which these were published and the date
of their laying on the Table of the Rajya Sabha have been indicated in the
enclosed Ministrywise statement (Annexure). Out of these 88 Orders, the
Committee has identified for detailed scrutiny. The orders so identified have
been indicated in the statement.

2. During the course of general scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has
noted some cases where there has been-a considerable time lag between the
date of notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed
statement indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications have not
been laid as per the Committee’s guidelines. In some of the cases the delay
has been quite abnormal. The observations/recommendations of the
Committee in this behalf are contained in the succeeding paragraphs.

3. The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Civil Aviation has
taken en unjustifiably long period of eight months for laying of the notification
containing the Aircraft (Second Amendment) Rules, 2000 (SI. No. 4 of the
Annexure). The notification containing these rules was published in the

1



2

Gazette of India on 6" July 2000. In the normal course it did have been laid
during the 190" Session (Monsoon Session-2000). However, the Ministry did
not lay the notification not only in the Monsoon Session but also in 191"
Session (Winter Session-2000). The Ministry have also not appended delay
statement while laying the same. The Committee would like the Ministry
of Civil Aviation to avoid recurrence of such instances in future.

4. The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Finance have, for
the third consecutive Session of the Rajya Sabha, delayed laying of some of
their notifications (Sl. Nos. 38, 39 and 49 of the Annexure) containing
regulations issued under the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of
Notifications) Act, 1970/80. All these notifications were published in the
Gazette between October and December, 2000, and the Ministry of Finance
had sufficient time to lay them in the 191" Session (Winter Session — 2000) of
the Rajya Sabha. The Committee, in its 131% and 13274 Reports on the
Statutory Orders laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during the 1907 and
191" Sessions, had pointed out instances of delay in laying on the part of the
Ministry of Finance and had recommended avoidance of such instances. The
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance would make special efforts
to avoid such instances of delay in future.

5. Further, the Committee also finds an instance of delay in laying ift case of
the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. The Committee notes with regret
that there has been delay of 4 months in laying of the rules pertaining to Drugs
and Cosmetics (Amendment) Rules, 2000 (SL. No. 52 of the Annexure). The
Ministry have also not appended any delay statement while laying this Rules.
The Committeé would like the Ministry to avoid recurrence of such
instance in future.



3

6. — Another instance of delay pertains to the Ministry of Law, Justice and
Company Affairs. The Committee notes with dissatisfaction that there has
been delay of eight months on the part of Ministry of Law in laying the lone
Supreme Court Legal Service Committee Rules, 2000 (SI.No.60 of the
Annexure)framed under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. The
Ministry has also not appended any delay statement while laying these Rules.
The Committee takes adverse notice of this delay and hopes that the
Ministry of Law, Justice and Company Affairs would take due precaution
to avoid recurrence of such an instance.

7. Similarly, the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pension have
also delayed the laying of three notifications (SI.Nos. 76,77 & 78 of the
Annexure). The delay period ranges from 5 to 14 months, The Committee
has taken a serious note of this delay and also the fact that the Ministry have
not appended any statement giving reasons for delay while laying these
Statutory Orders. The Committee would like the Ministry of Personnel,
Public Grievances and Pension to avoid recurrence of such instances in
the future. ‘

8. While scrutinizing the Orders the Committee also examined the aspect
‘whether the original orders that have been laid during the Session have been
framed under the appropriate authority of the respective Act. The Committee
notes with satisfaction that these Orders have been framed within the
parameters prescribed under the relevant Acts.

9. The Committee, in this context, regrets to note that some Ministries
that have delayed the laying of the Statutory Orders have failed to
append a delay statement while laying the notifications before the
Parliament. The Committee has, time and again, emphasised that it is
essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay
statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise the
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intention is that
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is
able to introspect, identifying bottlenecks and devising remedial
measures. The Committee once again impresses upon all the
Ministries/Departments to avoid delay in the laying of Statutory Orders before
the Parliament by following the time schedule as prescribed by it in its 131"
Report which is reproduced below:-

“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if
the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately
following the date of publication of the Order in the Official
Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the
time lag between the date of publication and the date of the
closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry
of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should



5

henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of
the orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table
of the Rajya Sabha.”

10. The Committee would like to take this opportunity to recommend
that Ministries, as part of their e-governance initiative, put all
subordinate legislation on their websites, preferably bilingually. The
Committee further recommends that the Ministry of Information
Technology immediately develop a standard application software with an
Internet interface for use in all Ministries, which will provide a searchable
database of subordinate legislation linked to the principal Acts
administered by the respective Ministry. Seeing the relative simplicity of
the software the Committee hopes that this will be completed within a
period of 6 months, and all Ministries will submit Action Taken replies in
this regard within this period.

E Committee considered the following memoranda:-

@ Memorandum No. 15/2001 regarding Noise Pollution
(Regulation & Control) Rules, 2000.

The Committee observed that the Ministry of Environment & Forests have
accepted its suggestions in relation to Noise Pollution(Regulation & Control)
Rules, 2000. The Committee decided that the matter Tay be reported to the
House.

(ii) Memorandum No. 16/2001 regarding Banking Service
Commission Act, 1984-Delay in framing of
Rules/Regulations — Implementation of 101% Report of
the Committee.

The Committee felt that Government should not keep an Act passed by both
the Houses of Parliament and assented to by the President in limbo for a long
time. The Committee expressed the view that the Ministry of Finance should
either enforce the Act or repeal it and desired that it the Ministry of Finance
may be asked to expedite a decision in the matter. The Committee ‘decided
that the matter may be reported.
