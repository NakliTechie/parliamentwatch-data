2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note d 
that in some cases there was a considerable time la g between 
issue  of  Notifications and their laying. The anne xed 
statement also indicates the period of delay in cas es where 
Notifications have not been laid as per the time sc hedule 
prescribed by the Committee. The Committee observed  that 
out of the total of 60 delayed Notifications, there  were 50 
Notifications where no delay statement was appended  to. 
However, the bulk of delayed Notifications were fro m the 
Ministry of  Finance (Department of Economic Affair s) 
which comes to around 43.3% of the total delayed 
Notifications. 

 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee 
in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments w here delay 
in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation 
 
 Ministry of Civil Aviation have laid 3 Notificatio ns 
(Sl. Nos. 15 – 17) during the Session, and one of t hese (Sl. 
No. 15) has been delayed by 7 months. There is no d elay 
statement appended to the Notification. 
  
 The Committee expresses its concern on  the delay 
by the Ministry of Civil Aviation in laying of one of the 
Notification on the Table of the House. The Committ ee 
desires the Ministry should  have communicated the 
circumstances causing the delay in laying of Notifi cation. 
However, the Committee finds that the Ministry have  not 
only delayed the laying of one Notification but also did not 
append any delay statement thereto. The Committee, 
therefore, advises the Ministry to strictly adhere to the 
time schedule in laying of the Notifications and ap pend a 
delay statement whenever there is delay in laying o f 
Notification on the Table of the House.  
 
 
(ii) Ministry of Communications 
 (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
The Ministry of Communications (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid 14 Notifications (Sl.  Nos. 27 
to 40). Out of these, 3 Notifications have been del ayed. The 
delay period ranges from 7 months to 14 months, app ended 
with delay statement to all of these delayed Notifications. The 

reasons given by the Ministry for the said delay is  reproduced 
as under:-  
 
 “English and Hindi version of the TRAI Regulation 
No. 7/2016 – The Mobile Banking (Quality of Service ) (2 
nd  
Amendment) Regulations, 2016 were notified on 22.11 .2016 
and 23.11.2016 respectively. The above Notification s were 
forwarded to Rajya Sabha on 23.03.2017 for laying t he same 
on the Table of Rajya Sabha. The Rajya Sabha Secret ariat 
returned the notifications saying that the date of the 
Regulation in English and Hindi versions are differ ent. Rajya 
Sabha Secretariat gave direction that if the Depart ment wants 
to lay the regulation on the Table of the Rajya Sab ha, a 
Corrigendum is required to be issued which should b e laid on 
the Table of the House. 
 Accordingly, a Corrigendum to above Regulations 
was notified by TRAI on 30.03.2017 and the same was  
received in the Department on 07.04.2017 i.e. the l ast date of 
Session of Rajya Sabha. Hence, due to paucity of ti me and 
administrative reasons, the TRAI Regulations No. 7/ 2016 
same could not be laid timely on the Table of the R ajya 
Sabha.” 
 “The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) 
Regulations No. 4/2016 – The Telecom Regulatory Aut hority 
of India (Officers and Staff Appointment) (Eleventh  
Amendment) Regulation, 2016 dated 08.06.2016 was no t in 
line with the existing Rules. Upon the clarificatio ns obtained 
from the TRAI as reflected in the Regulation No. 03 /2017- 
The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (Officers  and 
Staff Appointment) (Twelfth Amendment) Regulation, 2017 
dated 27/07/2017, it has now been decided to lay bo th the 
Regulations in the House.” 
 

 The Committee noted with serious concern that the 
Ministry of Communications (Department of 
Telecommunications) have failed to lay the Notifica tions 
in time on the Table of the House. The laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House is a statut ory 
obligation which should be fulfilled without delay.  
However, the Ministry have shown lukewarm approach 
towards their duty to the Parliament. They should 
manage the time schedule properly. The Committee, 
therefore,  recommends that the Ministry of 
Communications (Department of Telecommunications) 
should adhere strictly to the schedule as recommend ed by 
the Committee in its 131 
st  Report for timely laying of the 
Subordinate Legislation.

(iv) Ministry of Finance 
 (Department of Economic Affairs)  
 
The Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic 
Affairs) have laid 48 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 83 to  130) and a 
large number of these Notifications have been delayed i.e.  26 
delayed Notifications (Sl. Nos. 84, 87 – 88, 98, 10 0, 103 – 
105, 110 &  113 – 129) and  none of them have been attached 
with delay statement. The delay period ranges from 4 months 
to 27 months and in 7 cases, Notifications have bee n delayed 
by a period ranging from 24 months to  27 months.  
 
The Committee is not happy over such a large 
number of delayed Notifications. This is just not 
acceptable to the Committee that 26 out of 48 
Notifications which were delayed, did not even have  the 
mandatory delay statements. Further, the , delay pe riods 
were more than one year in majority  of Notificatio ns laid 
and 5 Notifications were delayed by more than 24 mo nths. 
While there may be a reason for the delay, but ther e 
cannot be any reason for not furnishing reasons for  the 
same. The Ministry must understand the implications  for 
not doing so and in such a large number of cases wh ich 
put the Ministry in poor light. The Committee also 
reiterates earlier recommendations made in its 135 
th  
Report, wherein it is laid down that it is essentia l to 
append a delay statement in case of delay in laying  of 
statutory orders. The Committee, therefore, impress es 
upon the Ministry of Finance (Department of Economi c 
Affairs) to strictly adhere to the time schedule in  laying of 
Notifications and to invariably append delay statem ent in 
each case of  delayed Notifications . The Committee 

recommends that the Ministry may clarify the reason s for 
the delay and also why the delay statement has not been 
appended to the Notification.

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the 
Committee over delay in laying of Notifications on the 
Table of the House, the Committee also express thei r deep 
concern over delay in framing of rules/regulations under 
different Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report presented 
to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended that the rule-
making process should be completed within a period of six 
months of the coming into force on an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a 
Ministry is not able to adhere to this time limit, it should 
seek extension of time for completing the rule-maki ng 
process. The recommendation of the committee reads as 
follows:- 
 

  “The Committee feels that the rule-
making process could well have been completed 
within the time limit of six months which the 
Committee has laid down for making of rules 
under an Act after coming into force of that Act. 
Where however, a Ministry does not find it possible 
to adhere to the time limit, they should inform the  
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make 
rules within the time limit and seek extension of 
time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the 
fact that the Ministries/Departments of Government of 
India are not complying with the Committee’s 
recommendation to complete the process of framing o f 
subordinate legislation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same 
time, the Committee also does not receive requests for 
extension of time for completing the rule-making pr ocess 
in terms of its recommendations as above from most of 
the Ministries/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he 
Ministries/Departments are neither able to complete  the 
task of framing rules/regulations under different A cts 
within the given six months’ period nor do they suo-motu  
approach the Committee for seeking extension of tim e. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  
timely framing of rules/regulations it has taken an  
initiative to monitor the progress of framing of 
subordinate legislation in respect of all legislati ons 
brought about by different Ministries/Departments o f 
Government of India from the year 2003 onwards with  a 
view to ensure that the framing of subordinate legi slation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the 

responses from the Ministries/Departments have not been 
prompt in this monitoring exercise undertaken on be half 
of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once 
again reiterates its recommendation that the 
Ministries/Departments should ensure putting in pla ce 
subordinate legislation positively within a period of six 
months of coming into force of the Act. The Committ ee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet 
with this deadline, they should invariably approach  the 
Committee for seeking extension of time. The Commit tee 
further recommends that in cases where the time lim it of 
six months is not adhered to, they should, while la ying
