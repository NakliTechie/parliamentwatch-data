2.         During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted some cases where there has been a considerable time lag
between the date of notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in cases
where notifications have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by the Committee and where such notifications were accompanied
by a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay has been quite long. The
observations/recommendations of the Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: -  
           
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation
 
            The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of up to eleven months on the part of the Ministry of Civil Aviation in
laying three of their notifications (Sl. Nos 2,3,& 4) and  in all the cases the Ministry have not appended the delay statements .
           
            The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Civil Aviation would avoid delay in laying of the notifications and in case of any delay
actually taking place in laying , a delay statement is invariably appended.
 
 
(ii) Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Industrial Policy & Promotion)
 
           
 
      The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Industrial Policy & Promotion) have
delayed by three months the laying of their notification (Sl. No. 6).  The Ministry have not appended any delay statement even with the
said notification. The Committee hopes that the Department of Industrial Policy & Promotion shall take necessary steps to ensure that
such delay does not recur and in case it is there then the Parliament is kept informed of the reasons therefor through delay statement.
 
(iii)       Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Department of Telecommunication)
 
            The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Department of
Telecommunication) have delayed the laying of one of their notification (Sl. 15) by seven months.  The Ministry have appended delay
statement with the notification and the reasons given by the Ministry read thus:
 
      “ The TRAI had sent the regulations to Department of Telecommunications on 21
st February, 2007, after receiving copies of the
regulations in the TRAI on 29th December, 2004 from Controller of Publication, Government of India, after notification of the regulations
in Govt. of India Gazette.  Accordingly there was a delay in laying the regulations on the Table of the House.”
           
            The Committee regrets to note that TRAI had taken around two months to forward the printed copy of the Regulations to the
Department of Telecommunication. But the latter also did not show promptness and the regulations which were received on 21.02.07 were
laid in the Rajya Sabha on 10.05.07. The Committee hope that Department of Telecommunication would take necessary precaution to
ensure that such an instance is not repeated.
 
(iv)   Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Department of Posts)
 
            The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry have delayed   laying of one of their notification (Sl No.17). The delay

(vi)       Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue)
 
            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Finance have delayed in laying their notifications (Sr. Nos.39, 40, 41,42, 43,
44, 45, , 57& 58 64), the delay ranging from 3 to 10 months.  The Ministry have not appended delay statements with the notifications at
(Sl. Nos. 57 and 58).           
 
The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) have delayed the laying of a number
of notifications. The notification at Sl No. 39 carries the date as 27.04.06 and appropriately, it should have been laid in the Monsoon
Session 2007. Having failed to do so the Department of Economic Affairs should not have waited for the second half of the Winter
Session (2007) for sending the papers for laying to the Rajya Sabha Secretariat. If this precaution had been taken, the notification could
have been laid one session earlier. The Committee recommends that the Ministries should take the first available opportunity for laying
their notification during the session.
 
 
(vii)      Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (Department of AYUSH)
 
 
 
The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of  5 months on the part of the Ministry of Health & Family
Welfare(Department of Ayush)  in laying their notification  (Sl. No. 100)
 
            The notification laid on 08.06.06 the Department of AYUSH would have safely laid the same during the Monsoon Session  or
latest in the Winter Session of Parliament, 2006 But the notification was laid only during the Budget Session 2007. The Committee
accordingly recommends that the Department of AYUSH should take necessary corrective steps to ensure that such delay dose not recur
in future. 
 
(viii)      Ministry of Home Affairs
 
The Committee notes with regret the Ministry of Home Affairs have delayed in laying four of their notifications the delay ranging
from 8 months to 10 months (Sl. Nos. 103, 104, 105 & 107) The Ministry have not appended delay  statements with any of these
notifications
 
            The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Home Affairs would avoid such instances of delay in the laying of Subordinate
Legislation and shall in such circumstance keep the House informed of the reasons for delay in laying by appending delay statements.

reads that the Notification was received by this Department on 7.9.2006 i.e. after the completion of Monsoon Session which ended on
23.8.2006.  The Papers were got authenticated by MOS (EE&L) for laying in the winter session of Parliament and sent on 18.12. 2006

 (ix) (A)    Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher Education)
 
The Committee notes with concern that the Ministry of Human Resource Development have delayed in laying notifications ranging from
5 months to 10 months.  The Ministry have also failed to append delay statements as well with their notification at (Sl. No. 114) (The
delay statement appended put forth the reason that these are being laid in the earliest Session after receipt of the documents from AICTE.
In future, all efforts will be made to ensure that these documents are laid on the Table of the House in time)
 
            The Committee notes with deep concern the inordinate delay in laying their notification of as much as 17 months.  The Committee
find that the laying of the notifications was delayed due to their late receipt in the Ministry from the AICTE. In this content the Committee
observes that it is the Ministry’s responsibility to ensure that the subordinate Legislation is laid before the Parliament within the prescribed
schedule and accordingly, it is for the Ministry only to ensure that they receive the same timely from the Statutory bodies under them .
The Committee recommends that the Department of Higher Education may take necessary remedial measures to ensure that papers are
received timely in the Ministry from the Statutory bodies.

            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Labour & Employment (Directorate General of Employment & Training)
have delayed by 5 months in laying their notification (Sl. No. 121).  Delay statement has been appended with the notification
            The Committee expects the Ministry of Labour & Employment (Directorate General of Employment& Training) to exercise
caution in the matter of timely laying of Subordinate Legislation and ensure that such delays do not recur.
(xi)       Ministry of Overseas Indian Affairs
            The Committee notes with dismay that the Ministry have exceptionally delayed the laying of their notifications (Sl. Nos 130,131).

            “The work in respect of Emigration was transferred from Ministry of Labour to Indian Overseas Affairs. During this period six

thereof, these notifications could not be laid on the Table during this period.
 
            The Committee expresses its unhappiness over the inordinate delay that has taken place in the laying of the two notifications. The
reason for delay tendered by the Ministry do not seem to be very convincing, if seen with reference to the period of delay. The Committee,
accordingly, recommends that the Ministry should take all necessary precaution for future so as to avoid recurrence of such situations.
 
(xii)      Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training)
 
            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel Public Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel &
Training) have delayed the laying of one of their notifications (Sl. No. 146) by ten months.  The delay statement appended with the said
notification reads:-
 
            “This notification had not been received in time from the Govt. of India Press due to this reason the same could not be laid on the
Table of Rajya Sabha within stipulated period.”
            The Committee would like to impress upon the Ministry of Personnel that it is their responsibility to ensure that copies of the
notifications are received timely from the Government of India Press and for this purpose they may evolve a suitable mechanism of
check/counter check so that such instances do no recur. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Personnel would take action
accordingly in order to comply with the prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of Statutory notifications.

3.     The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of
subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the Government of
India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 131
st,135th  and 152nd Reports:
        “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately
following the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the publication of
the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less
than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the Ministries/Departments of
Government of India should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued under various Acts of
Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” (131
st Report)
 
        “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial
measures”. (135
th Report)
