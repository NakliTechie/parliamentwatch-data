2. While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted in some cases considerable time lag 
between issue  of notifications and their laying. T he annexed statement also indicates the period 
of delay in cases where notifications have not been  laid as per the schedule prescribed by the 
Committee. It also shows notifications not accompan ied by a statement indicating the reasons for 
the delay. 
 

The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation 
 
 Ministry of Civil Aviation  have laid only two Not ifications (Sl. Nos. 6-7) on the Table of 
the House during the Session. There has been delay of 4 to 5 months in each case and no delay 
statement had been  attached with these Notifications. 
    
   The Committee notes with concern that Ministry of C ivil Aviation have not cared to 
append a delay statement with any of two notificati ons laid by them during winter session 
of Parliament in the year 2012. Thus the Ministry h ave flouted the Committee’s 
recommendation contained in its 135 
th  Report that a delay statement be invariably 
appended in case of delay while laying the notifications on the Table of the House.  
 
 
 
(ii) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 15 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 32 to 46).  Out of these 
3 Notifications have been delayed by 4 to 5 months.  Only one Notification (Sl. No. 41) has been 

attached with delay statement and other two Notifications (Sl. Nos. 39 - 40) did not have any. The 
Ministry has stated that the Notification could not  be laid in prescribed time  because the 
Notification being too bulky (465 pages) could not be printed by the Government Press before the 
Monsoon Session. The printed Notification was recei ved by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
only when the Monsoon Session of Parliament was already over. 
   
The Committee finds the reason given by the Ministr y of Corporate Affairs untrue. 
As stated  the notification was handed over to it b y the Government Press only after the 
monsoon Session which had started on 8.8.2012 and c ontinued upto 7.9.2012. The 
notification date is 7 th  August, 2012 and there was sufficient time for the Ministry to process 
the notification for laying. The Ministry was requi red to lay this notification during 
Monsoon Session itself there being more them 15 day s Session after it was notified. The 
Committee takes a serious note of this and directs the Ministry of Corporate Affairs to stick 
to procedure and schedule for the laying of notification in future. 
 
(iii)  Ministry of Defence 
 
The only Notification that the Ministry of Defence (Sl. No. 77) have laid is delayed by 8 
months with delay statement appended thereto. The reasons for delay given by the Ministry are as 
under:- 
 

“Recruitment Rules framed for the post of Additiona l Director General, in the Indian 
Coast Guard, Ministry of Defence were sent to the G overnment of India Press, Mayapuri for 
publication in the Official Gazette of India in Apr il, 2012. It took some time to get the published 
copies of Notification from the Government of India  Press. The sitting of the 11 th  Session of 15 th  
Lok Sabha and 226 th  Session of Rajya Sabha concluded on 07.09.2012. As  such the Recruitment 
rules could not be laid during aforesaid Session of Parliament, 2012. 
The delay in laying of papers on the Table of Lok S abha/Rajya Sabha is due to delay in 
getting published copies of the Recruitment Rules. It is requested that the marginal delay on the 
part of Ministry of Defence may kindly be condoned. 
 
The Committee finds the  reasons given by the Minis try of Defence for 8 months 
delay in laying of notification misleading. It was sent to Government Press in April, 2012  
and had it been properly tracked it would have been  laid during the Monsoon Session itself. 
Ministry did not give that dates when the printed c opies were received by it from the Press. 
As a result, it is not possible to apportion the re sponsibility. Ministry infact have tried to 
mislead the Committee and tried to lay the blame on  the Press. This is not acceptable to the 
Committee and it cautions the Ministry to be carefu l in future.  The Committee hopes that 
Ministry of Defence would take action accordingly i n order to comply with the prescribed 
schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications . 
 
(iv) Ministry of Environment and Forests 

 
 Ministry of Environment and Forests have laid 6 Not ifications (Sl. Nos. 48 to 53) during 
the Session. Out of these, two Notifications (Sl. N o. 48 - 49) have been delayed by 6 months and 
no delay statement has been appended to both of them. 
  
 The Committee notes that two of its notifications which have not only been delayed 
by six months but also do not have any delay statem ent attached to them. The Committee 
views it seriously  reiterate its recommendations ( contained in its 135 
th  Report) that in case 
there is delay in laying of Statutory Orders, the c oncerned Ministry/Department should 
invariably append a delay statement. 
 
(v) Ministry of Finance 
 (Department of Financial Services) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Servi ces) have laid 30 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 71 to 100).  Out of these 19 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 76 – 94) have been delayed. The delay 
period ranges from 17 months to 27 months. All of t hese have been attached with delay 
statement. The reasons for delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “The Copies of the Gazettee Notifications could no t be laid on the Table of the Rajya 
Sabha as printed copies of the same were not receiv ed from Regional Rural Banks in time. The 

delay has occurred as the Regional Rural Banks whic h are spread out all over the country have to 
send the Regulations to Government Press, New Delhi  for publication. Thereafter, these are sent 
back to the concerned Regional Rural Banks which th en sent it to the Ministry. The delay is thus 
in the administrative procedure/process.” 
 
 The Committee does not accept the reasons of delay given by the Ministry of Finance 
(Department of Financial Services) and expresses it s serious concern over such inordinate 
delay which ranges from 17 months to 27 months. The  Committee wonders  that how can 
administrative procedural process take 17 to 27 mon ths to receive Notifications and 
complete procedural formalities. The Committee feel s that Ministry did not take adequate 
precaution to avoid the delay and made no serious e fforts to lay the Notifications in time 
which is in contravention of its statutory obligati on to Parliament. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry must take appropriate step to remove lacuna, if any 
administrative or any other causing such long delay  and wish it  would strive to keep delay 
to the minimum by playing a more proactive role and  pursuing concerned organisations to 
act with promptness in future.  
 
(vi) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
 
 The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare have lai d 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 165 to 
171). Five of these Notifications  have been delaye d by 6 months. Out of 5 delayed Notifications 

2 notifications (Sl. Nos. 168-169) have been accomp anied with delay statement and other 3 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 165-167) have no delay statement appended to it. The reasons given by the 
Ministry are as follows:- 
 
  “……The aforesaid Notification before it could be laid on the Table of both Houses of 
Parliament  at earliest in monsoon session of Parli ament was challenged in the High Court of 
Bombay and High court of Delhi and some portion of the amendments has since been stayed by 
the Hon’ble High Courts. As the matter remained sub judice, the matter was referred to Ministry 
of Parliamentary Affairs for their advice whether under the circumstances it would be correct and 
appropriate to lay the Gazette Notification on the amendment of the Rules or it would be 
appropriate to wait till the disposal of the Court Cases. Soon after the receipt of the advice the 
process for laying the papers was initiated. 
 
 Now, the Notification G.S.R. 418 (E) is proposed t o be laid on the Table of both Houses of  
Parliament in the Winter Session of Parliament.” 
 
  “The Drugs and Cosmetics (4 
th  Amendment) Rules, 2012 under the Drugs and Cosmeti cs 
Act, 1940 were published in the Gazette of India, E xtraordinary, vide G.S.R. 575(E), dated 17 th  
July, 2012. Inadvertently, the notification could n ot be laid during the previous Monsoon Session 
of Parliament, 2012.” 
    

The Committee have noted the reason by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare 
that due to matter being subjudice, the notificatio n could not be laid in time. The reasons 
given by the Ministry seems to be justified and wel l taken. The Committee, however, are 
deeply anguished over the fact that delay in anothe r  case i.e. G.S.R. 575 (E) dated the 17 th  
July, 2012 was caused inadvertently by the Ministry . The Ministry should have been more 
vigilant to see that notification was laid within t he time schedule prescribed by the 
Committee. The Committee, therefore, impress upon t he Ministry of Health and Family 
Welfare to be watchful so that such inadvertent delay is avoided in future. 
 
(vii) Ministry of Home Affairs  
  
The Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 22 Notificat ions (Sl. Nos. 174 - 195) during the 
Session. Out of total Notifications laid 15 (68.1%)  Notifications have been delayed by 3 months 
to  5 months and moreover no delay statement has been appended to any of them.  
 
The Committee expresses its unhappiness over the la rge proportion of Notifications 
which comes around 68.1% of total notifications bei ng delayed by the Ministry. The 
Committee further takes serious note of the fact th at the Ministry even did not bother to 
provide the reasons for such delayed laying of Noti fications. In absence of any delay 
statement with any of the delayed notifications Com mittee finds it difficult to ascertain 
causes of delay. The Committee invite attention to its recommendations made in 135 
th  

Report wherein it is laid down that it is essential  to append a delay statement in case of 
delay in laying of statutory orders. The committee,  therefore, impresses upon the Ministry 
of Home Affairs to strictly adhere to the time sche dule in laying of Notifications and should 
invariably append delay statement in each case of  delayed notifications .  
 
(viii)   Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas 
 
During the Session the Ministry of Petroleum and Na tural Gas have laid total 9 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 208 to 216) on the Table of  the House.  Out of these 4 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 210, 213 - 215) have been delayed by 3 months to 8 months whereas   no delay statement 
was attached to any of these Notifications. 
  
The Committee impress upon the Ministry of Petroleu m and Natural Gas  that it is 
their responsibility to ensure the timely laying of  notifications and take appropriate action 
in order to comply with the prescribed schedule for  laying and in case where there is delay, 
the statement explaining the reasons for such dealy  should invariable be accompanied with 
them. The Committee hopes that  the Ministry of Pet roleum and Natural Gas would avoid 
such delays and would append a delay statement in all such cases of delay in future.  
 
 
(ix) Ministry of  Power 

 
The Ministry of Power have laid 6 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 217 - 222) and out of which 2 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 221-222) have been delayed.  The delay period ranges from 8 months to 9 
months, both of them are accompanied by delay state ment. The reasons for delay given by the 
Ministry is as under:- 
  
“The notification for Accounting format of DVC was published on 30.03.2012 in the 
Gazette of India. 
 
The notification for the Rule 31 and Rule 25 of DVC  was published on 29 
th  February, 
2012 in the Gazette of India. 
 
The notification for rule 21 and Rule 25 of DVC wer e published in 28 
th  February, 2012 
and received in April, 2012, the notification for a ccounting format of DVC was published 
on 30.03.2012 in the Gazette of India but received in DVC Desk in May, 2012. 
 
Inadvertently due to exigency of the works the noti fications for the revised rules for 
Accounting format of DVC, Rule 31 and Rule 25 of DVC could not be laid on the table of 
the House during the Monsoon Session.” 
 

The Committee takes serious note of the fact that N otifications were delayed 
inadvertently due to exigency of work. This means t hat priority has been  accorded to other 
works and  the work of laying of notifications has been sidelined. The Committee therefore, 
impress upon the Ministry of Power to accord top priority to laying of notifications which is 
a statutory obligation. 
  
(x) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways have la id overall 25 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 223-247) and out of which 12 Notifications  ha ve been delayed by 4 months to 1 year. 
Eleven Notifications (Sl. Nos. 223 - 226, 228-230, 240-242 & 247) have no delay statement 
appended to them. Only one notification (Sl. No. 22 7) has delay statement appended to it. The 
reason of delay given by the Ministry is that it to ok some time to collect the papers and complete 
the procedural formalities therefore, papers could not be laid in time on the Table of  both Houses 
of Parliament.  
 
The Committee does not accept the reasons of the de lay given by the Ministry of 
Road Transport and Highways that it took some time to collect the papers and complete the 
procedural formalities. The Committee wonders how c an it take one year to receive 
Notifications and to complete procedural formalities. The Committee feels that Ministry did 
not take adequate precaution to avoid the delay. Th e Committee further takes a serious 

view of the fact that even after delay of one year in certain case Ministry did not feel it 
necessary to append a delay statement so as to appr ise the Parliament of the reasons of 
delay in laying of Notifications on the Table of th e House. The Committee recommends that 
the Ministry should initiate urgent measures so as to avoid delay in completing the 
procedural formalities so that such delay can be eliminated.  
 
(xi) Ministry of Shipping 
 
The Ministry of Shipping have laid 3 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 248 - 250). Out of these 1 
Notification (Sl. No. 248) has been delayed by 2 ye ars and 1 month. There is no delay statement 
appended to it. 
 
The Committee are not happy that out of 3 Notificat ions that Ministry of shipping 
have laid, one Notification has been delayed by mor e than 2 years. Further there is no 
reason given for this inordinate delay.  This is in  contravention of Committee’s 
recommendation given in its 135 Report that Notific ation which are delayed should 
invariably be accompanied by delay statement. The C ommittee would like the Ministry of 
Shipping to take necessary steps to obviate such de lay and in case of delay in laying of 
Notifications, delay statement should invariably be accompanied with them. 
 
(xii) Ministry of Urban Development 

 
Ministry of Urban Development have laid 2 Notificat ions (Sl. Nos. 255-256) during the 
Session. Out of these, only one Notification (Sl. N o. 255) has been delayed by 9 months and no 
delay statement has been appended to it. 
 
The Committee takes a serious view not only about t he delay in laying one of its 
Notifications on the Table of the House but also fa ilure to append the delay statement with 
them. The Ministry/Department must know about the p ossible implications of failing to 
meet this requirement. It must ensure to append a d elay statement in all such cases in 
future .

3.   Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/De partments to take immediate steps to 
ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legi slation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes 
to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Department s of the Government of India, particularly, the 
defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 131 
st 
, 135 
th 
, 152 
nd  ,   192 nd  
and 201st  Reports: 
 “Orders required to be laid before the House shoul d be laid (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following t he date of publication of the Order in 
the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Se ssion, on the date of the publication of the 
Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication and 
the date of the closing of the Session is less than  15 clear days, before the expiry of the 
Session immediately following the said Session. All  the Ministries/Departments of 

Government of India should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of 
the Orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha. 
(131 st  Report) 
(Presented on 15 December 2000) 
 
 “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized tha t it is essential that in case there is 
a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the conc erned Ministry/Department should 
invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee de sires all Ministries/Departments to 
take a note of this, since the intension is that th rough the process of formulation of a delay 
statement the Ministry is able to introspect, ident ify bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135 th  Report) 
(Presented on 27 July 2001) 
 “In the opinion of the Committee, every notificati on should be assigned a distinctive 
number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no notification should be 
published/laid which does not carry a notification number. The Committee expects all 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India to ex ercise caution in this regard. In case 
where there are more than one notification under a single file, the Ministries/Departments 

should ensure that the file number on each notifica tion should be suitably distinguished 
from one another.” 
(152 
nd 
 Report) 
(Presented on 26 August 2004)  
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Committee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force on an Act. The 
committee has further recommended that in case a Mi nistry is not able to adhere to this 
time limit, it should seek extension of time for co mpleting the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 

 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are n ot complying with the Committee’s 
recommendation to complete the process of framing of subordinate legislation contemplated 
under various Acts of Parliament within a period of  six months. At the same time, the 
Committee also does not receive requests for extens ion of time for completing the rule-
making process in terms of its recommendations as a bove from most of the 
Ministries/Departments. The committee takes a serio us note of the prevailing situation in 
which the Ministries/Departments are neither able t o complete the task of framing 
rules/regulations under different Acts within the g iven six months’ period nor do they suo-
motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. The Committee further notes 
that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/reg ulations it has taken an initiative to 
monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legi slation in respect of all legislations 
brought about by different Ministries/Departments o f Government of India from the year 
2003 onwards with a view to ensure that the framing  of subordinate legislation is not 
delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the res ponses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its 
recommendation that the Ministries/Departments shou ld ensure putting in place 

subordinate legislation positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the 
Act. The Committee further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this 
deadline, they should invariably approach the Committee for seeking extension of time. The 
Committee further recommends that in cases where th e time limit of six months is not 
adhered to, they should, while laying rules/regulat ions before the Parliament invariably lay 
a statement explaining the reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond 
six months so as to keep the Parliament apprised of  the compliance of the Parliament’s 
mandate  conferred upon the Executive.”

Ministries immediately on the same day when they ha ve been laid on the Table of the 
House, for information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access.
