2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note d that there was considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of total 414 delayed 
Notifications there were 248 Notifications where no  delay statement was appended to. However, 
the bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Min istry of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 76.6% of total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Commerce and Industry  
 
The only one Notification that the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Sl. No. 7) have laid, 
is delayed by 5 months and there is no delay statement appended thereto. 
 
The Committee expressed its concern over the delay in laying of the  Notification by 
the Ministry of Commerce and Industry by  5 months.  Further, no delay statement has been 
appended giving reasons for the delay. The Committe e, therefore, recommends the Ministry 
to ensure that such delay does not recur in future and in case of such a delay in laying, a 
delay statement should invariably be appended. 
 
(ii) Ministry of Communications and Information Tec hnology  (Department of 
Information Technology) 
  
 Ministry of Communications and Information Technolo gy  (Department of  Information 
Technology)  have laid 8 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 14 to 21).  Out  of these, 4 Notifications have been 
delayed by 5 to 7 months and moreover, no delay sta tement has been appended to any of the four 
delayed Notifications. 
 

The Committee notes that four out of a total of eig ht Notifications that the Ministry of  
Communication and Information Technology  (Department of Information Technology)  have 
laid  are not only delayed by five to seven months but al so did not have any delay statement 
attached to them. The Committee views it seriously  and reiterates its recommendations, as 
contained in its 135 th  Report, that in case there is a delay in laying of  Statutory Orders, the 
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement. The Committee 
further impresses upon the Ministry that it is thei r obligation towards the Parliament to 
apprise it about the reasons for delay through dela y statement in case they are unable to lay 
any Subordinate Legislation in prescribed time. 
 
(iii) Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public  Distribution 
 (Department of Food and Public Distribution) 
 
 Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Dist ribution (Department of Food and 
Public Distribution)  have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 26 to 29) on th e Table of the House during 
the Session. Out of  these, 2 Notifications (Sl. No s. 26 and 28) have been delayed by 4  months 
each and there is no delay statement attached to both of them. 
    
 The Committee regrets the delay in laying of Notifi cations in time by the Ministry of  
Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution (Dep artment of Food and Public 
Distribution). The Committee advises the Ministry t o adhere to the time schedule in laying of 
Notifications in the manner as contained in 131 
st  Report of the Committee. The Committee 
further desires that the Ministry should invariably  append a delay statement when there has 
been delay in laying of Notifications so as to apprise Parliament of the reasons of delay.  
  
(iv) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 10 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 30 to 39). All of these 10 
Notifications have been delayed by 4 to 5 months. O nly two delayed Notifications (Sl. Nos. 38 and 
39) were attached with delay statement  whereas the   other eight Notifications (Sl. Nos. 30 to 37) 
did not have any delay statement citing the exact r easons for delay. The reason for delay given by 
the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “The Notifications relating to the G.S.R. 173 (E) dated 15.3.2013 on Companies Directors 
Identification Number (Amendment) Rules, 2013 and G .S.R. 183 (E) dated 21.2.2013 on 
Companies (Acceptance of Deposits Amendment) Rules,  2013 were sent to Government Press for 
printing on 15 
th  March, 2013 and 21 st  March, 2013. The said Notifications could not be l aid before 
both the Houses of Parliament because there was del ay in receiving the Gazette copy of the 
Notifications to the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Hence, the said Notifications got delayed.” 
 
The Committee views this delay seriously  and reite rates its recommendations as 
contained in its 135 th  Report that in case there is delay in laying of St atutory Orders, the 
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement. The Committee 
finds that laying of Notifications was delayed due to late receipt of printed copies of 
Notifications in the Ministry. The Committee observ es that it is the Ministry’s responsibility 
to ensure that Subordinate Legislation is laid befo re the Parliament within the prescribed 
time schedule and, accordingly, it is for the Ministry only to ensure that they receive the same 
timely from the statutory bodies under them.  
 

 The Committee is not inclined to accept the late av ailability of printed copies of 
Notifications from the Government of India Press as  the reason for delay in laying of the 
Notifications. The Committee directs all the Ministries that in case there is delay in procuring 
printed copies in time, they may obtain G.S.R. and S.O. numbers from the Government of 
India Press and prepare requisite number of copies in cyclostyled form for laying in the 
Parliament as suggested by Secretary, Ministry of  Urban Development, the controlling 
Ministry of Government of India Press, in its meeti ng held on 30 July, 2013. The Committee 
also recommends that this process may be adopted by Ministries suo moto  on immediate basis 
so as to avoid such delays henceforth. 
  
(v) Ministry of Culture 
 
 Ministry of Culture  have laid 2 Notifications (Sl . Nos. 40 to 41).  Both of them have been 
delayed by 2 years and are accompanied with delay s tatement. The Ministry has stated that the 
delay occurred due to certain changes required from  time to time in the draft of the Notifications

 The Committee notes the reply given by the Ministr y of Culture and cautions them 
that in future they should endeavour to strictly ad here to the schedule as recommended by 
the Committee in its 131 
st  Report for timely laying of subordinate legislation.

the delay on the part of National Cadet Corps, Ministry of Defence may kindly be condoned.” 
 
 The Committee finds that laying of Notifications w as delayed due to late receipt of 
published copies of Notifications in the Ministry. The Committee does not accept this as a

it is, therefore, the Ministry’s responsibility to ensure that Subordinate Legislation is laid 
before the Parliament within the prescribed schedul e and accordingly, it is for the Ministry 
only to ensure that they receive the printed copies  timely.  
 
 The Committee is not inclined to accept the late a vailability of printed copies of 
Notifications from the Government of India Press as  the reason for delay in laying of the

India Press and prepare requisite number of copies in cyclostyled form for laying in the

 The Committee takes note of the fact that the delay  was caused due to adjournment of 
the House. The Committee recommends that Ministries  should try to lay their papers during 
the initial period of a Session, to avoid such a si tuation in future. Such a  possibility should be 
assessed well in advance and arrangements should be  made to lay papers immediately after 
the session starts without waiting for the latter p art of the Session. The Committee advises 
the Ministry of Urban Development to ensure that su ch delays do not recur in future. The 
Ministry should take note of the fact that laying o f Notification is equally a pressing and time 
bound matter.

timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to 
the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the Gov ernment of India, particularly, the defaulting 
ones, the following recommendations, as contained i n its 131

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Commit tee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force on an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 
of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 
invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 
while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”
