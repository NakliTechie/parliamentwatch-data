2. During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee noted 
some cases where there has been a considerable time  lag between the date of 
notification of the Order and the date of its layin g. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where notifi cations have not been laid as 
per the schedule prescribed by the Committee and wh ere such notifications 
were not accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in 
laying. In some of the cases, the delay has been qu ite long. The 
observations/recommendations of the Committee in re spect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Agriculture 
 (Department of Agriculture and Cooperation) 
 
Out of 10 notifications (Sl. Nos. 1 to 10) laid by the Ministry of Agriculture 
(Department of Agriculture and Cooperation),  2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 2 and 
5)  have  been delayed by four months and 6 months respectively. There is  no 
delay statements appended to any of them  
 
The Committee expresses its unhappiness over the de lay in laying 
of Notifications. Moreover, there is no delay state ment attached thereto. 
The Committee reiterates its recommendation contain ed in its 135 
th  
Report that in case of delay in laying, Notificatio ns should invariably be 

 
accompanied with delay statement. The Ministry shou ld take care that 
repetition of such delay is avoided in future. 
 
(ii) Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers  
 (Department of Fertilizers) 
 
Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers (Department o f Fertilizers) have 
laid 4 Notifications (Sl.No.11 to 14), two of which  (Sl. No. 11 & 12)  have 
been delayed by 9 months and 8 months respectively.  There is no delay 
statement appended to any of these. 
 
The Committee regrets the delay in laying of Notifi cations by the 
Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers (Department o f Fertilizers) and 
emphasises that the Ministry should strictly adhere to the time schedule as 
recommended by it in its 131 
st  Report  in laying of the Subordinate 
Legislation before Parliament and in case of any de lay in laying of the 
notifications, a delay statement should also invariably be appended. 
 
(iii) Ministry of Civil Aviation 
 
Ministry of Civil Aviation have laid 6 Notification s (Sl.Nos.15-20), out 
of these only one Notification (Sl.No.15) has been delayed by 1 year. There is 
no delay statement appended thereto. 
 
The Committee expresses its concern over the inordinate delay that 
has taken place in the laying of the one Notificati on. The Committee  also 
takes serious note of the fact that the Ministry has not appended any delay 
statement with the delayed Notification.  In absenc e of any delay 
statement Committee is not in a position to ascerta in what caused this 
inordinate delay. The Committee, therefore,  recomm ends that the 
Ministry of Civil Aviation should take all necessar y precaution in future 
so as to avoid recurrence of such situations and ap pend delay statements 
where required. 
 
(iv) Ministry of Commerce and  Industry 
(Department of Commerce and Department of Industria l Policy 
and Promotion) 
 
 Ministry of  Commerce and Industry (Department of Commerce and  
Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion) have  laid 11 (Sl. Nos. 21 to 
31)  Notifications, out of which Department of Comm erce have laid 7 
Notifications and Department of Industrial Policy a nd Promotion have laid 4 
Notifications. 7 Notifications (Department of Comme rce(Sl. Nos. 21, 23-25) 
and  Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion [Sl. Nos. 28-30]) have 
been delayed and delay period ranges from 4 months to 11 months. There is no 
delay statement appended to any of these delayed Notifications. 

 
 
The Committee is concerned to see that  the Ministr y of Commerce 
and Industry has failed to lay delay statement that   is a statutory 
requirement. The Committee in this connection invit es attention to its 
recommendation contained in its 135 
th  Report. The Committee takes a 
serious view in such failures. The Ministry should know that failure to lay 
papers amounts to curtailing Parliament’s right to be informed about 
subordinate legislations. 
 
(v) Ministry of Communications and Information Tech nology 
(a) (Department of Posts) 
  
Ministry of Communications and Information Technolo gy (Department 
of Posts) have laid 5 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 32 to  36) . Out of these  one  
Notification ( Sl. No. 36) have been delayed by 6 m onths. There is no delay 
statement appended thereto. 
 
The Committee takes a serious view not only about t he delay in 
laying of one of its Notifications on the Table of the House but also failure 
to append the delay statement with them. The Minist ry/Department must 
know about the possible implications of failing to meet this requirement. It 
must ensure to append a delay statement in all such cases in future . 
 
  (b)   (Department of Telecommunications) 
 
 Ministry of Communications and Information Technol ogy (Department 
of Telecommunications) have laid 7 Notifications (S l. Nos. 37 to 43). All of 
these have been delayed by 5 months to 9 months. Th ere is no delay statement 
appended to four (Sl. Nos. 40-43) of these. Only 3 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 37-
39) have delay statement appended to them. The reas ons of delay given by the 
Department of Telecommunications is given as under:- 
 
 “The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) is a statutory 
body set up under the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997. In 
exercise of the powers conferred upon it under sect ion 36 of the TRAI Act, 
1997, the Authority may, by notification, make regu lations consistent with the 
Act and the Rules made there under to carry out the purposes of the Act. Under 
section 37 of the Act, the regulations notified by TRAI are required to be laid 
before each House of Parliament. 
 The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) i n exercise of the 
powers conferred by section 36, read with sub-claus es (v) of clause (b) of sub-
section (1) and clause (c) of sub-section (1) of se ction 11 of the TRAI Act, 
1997 notified “The Telecom Commercial Communication s customer 
Preference (Second Amendment Regulations, 2010.”Thi s Regulation was 
issued to amend the main regulation titled “The Tel ecom Commercial 
Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 201 0 (6 of 2010) dated 1 
st  

 
December 2010.” Main Regulation was given for printing to government press. 
However, before receipt of printed copies of the ma in regulation from 
Government Press on 21 
st  January, 2011, two amendments to the regulations 
(including this regulation) has been issued. Circum stances have changed in 
such a manner that effective date of implementation  of the regulations has to 
be changed from time to time for reasons beyond the control of TRAI. Till date 
five amendments to regulations have been issued. Fi fth Amendment to 
regulations has been issued on 18 th  March, 2011. In order to provide holistic 
and accurate information on the subject and to avoi d confusion by providing 
piece meal information to the Hon’ble Members of th e House while laying the 
copies of regulations in both houses of Parliament,  TRAI sent all the 
regulations to DoT on 7 th  April, 2011. Accordingly, the regulations are bein g 
laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
  
The Committee is not convinced by the reason given by the 
Ministry that the Notification could not be laid do wn because there were 
amendments to the Regulation taking place in the me antime. The 
Committee is of the strong view that Ministry shoul d have given priority 
to lay the main Regulation on the Table of the Hous e and amendments 
would have been tabled thereafter. 
As it is constitutional provision to lay the Notifi cation within a 
prescribed time-schedule, the Ministry should have taken appropriate 
action to lay the same on the Table of the House. T he Committee, 
therefore, recommends that adequate precaution shou ld be exercised to 
avoid recurrence of such instances in future.

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay 
in laying of Notifications on the Table of the Hous e, the Committee also 
express their deep concern over delay in framing of  rules/regulations 
under different Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report presented to the 
House on 14.9.1981 has recommended that the rule-ma king process 
should be completed within a period of six months of the coming into force 
on an Act. The committee has further recommended th at in case a 
Ministry is not able to adhere to this time limit, it should seek extension of 

 
time for completing the rule-making process. The re commendation of the 
committee reads as follows:- 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould 
well have been completed within the time limit of s ix months which 
the Committee has laid down for making of rules under an Act after 
coming into force of that Act. Where however, a Min istry does not 
find it possible to adhere to the time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the 
time limit and seek extension of time for completin g the rule-
making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India are n ot complying with 
the Committee’s recommendation to complete the proc ess of framing of 
subordinate legislation contemplated under various Acts of Parliament 
within a period of six months. At the same time, th e Committee also does 
not receive requests for extension of time for comp leting the rule-making 
process in terms of its recommendations as above fr om most of the 
Ministries/Departments. The committee takes a serio us note of the 
prevailing situation in which the Ministries/Depart ments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts 
within the given six months’ period nor do they suo -motu approach the 
Committee for seeking extension of time. The Commit tee further notes 
that in order to ensure timely framing of rules/reg ulations it has taken an 
initiative to monitor the progress of framing of su bordinate legislation in 
respect of all legislations brought about by differ ent 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India from the year 2003 
onwards with a view to ensure that the framing of s ubordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise 
undertaken on behalf of the Committee.  

 
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates 
its recommendation that the Ministries/Departments should ensure 
putting in place subordinate legislation positively  within a period of six 
months of coming into force of the Act. The Committ ee further 
recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they 
should invariably approach the Committee for seekin g extension of time. 
The Committee further recommends that in cases wher e the time limit of 
six months is not adhered to, they should, while la ying rules/regulations 
before the Parliament invariably lay a statement ex plaining the reasons 
why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed be yond six months so as 
to keep the Parliament apprised of the compliance o f the Parliament’s 
mandate  conferred upon the Executive.”

At the outset the Chairperson welcomed the new Memb ers nominated to the Committee.  
Thereafter, the Committee considered the draft 197 
th  Report  on Statutory Orders Laid on the Table 
of the Rajya Sabha during its 223 rd  Session and took note of the delay in the framing of rules/ 
regulations under the different Acts by the various  Ministries/ Departments.  The Committee was of 
the view that the Ministries/ Departments should se riously take the laying of papers in Parliament.  
The Committee decided that in cases where the time limit of six months was not adhered to by the 
Ministries/ Departments for framing the necessary s ubordinate legislation, the Ministries/ 
Departments should invariably inform the Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules 
within the time limit, justify the reasons for delay and seek extension of time for completing the rule 
making process.  The Committee, accordingly, decide d to hear the defaulting Ministries in the 
matter by calling the Secretary of the particular M inistries alongwith the Secretary, Ministry of 

Parliamentary Affairs before the Committee.  Therea fter, the Committee adopted the 197 th  Report 
unanimously.  The Committee decided to present the report to the House on the 17 th  May, 2012 and 
authorized the Chairperson and in her absence Shri Vijay Jawaharlal Darda and Dr. Janardhan 
Waghmare to present the report to the House.  
 
2. Thereafter, the Committee took up the issue of g iving retrospective effect to modifications 
in subordinate legislation as proposed by Shri K.N.  Balagopal, M.P. The Committee had 
preliminary discussion on the matter and were of th e view that the matter called for some research. 
Some Members were of the opinion that the matter ma y be referred to the Ministry of Law & 
Justice for their comments.  The Committee, however , decided to defer the matter and take it up for 
detailed discussion at its next meeting before taking a final decision.
