2.         During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted 
some cases where there has been a considerable time lag between the date of 
notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per 
the Committee’s guidelines and whether such notifications were accompanied by 
a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the 
delay has been quite abnormal. The observations/recommendations of the 
Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: 
(i)       Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers  
          (Department of Chemicals& Petrochemicals) 
  
            The Committee regrets to note that there is a delay of 6 months in the laying 
of the only notification laid by the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (Sl No. 5). 
The Ministry have appended a delay statement with the same. The Committee 
hopes that the Department of Chemical & Petrochemicals of the Ministry of 
Chemicals & Fertilizers would avoid such instances in future. 
  
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution  
            Department of Food & Public Distribution) 
  
The Committee regrets to note that there is a delay of 5 months in laying of one 
out of the five notifications laid by the Department of Food & Public Distribution 
of the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution (Sl No. 30). The 

Ministry have not appended a delay statement with the delayed notification.   The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Ministry of Consumer A ffairs, Food & 
Public Distribution would avoid such delays and would append a delay 
statement  in all such cases of delay in future. 
  
(iii)       Ministry of Finance  
(Department of Economic Affairs) 
  
The Committee notes that out of a total of   212 notifications laid by the 
Ministry of Finance (66 notifications by the Department of Economic Affairs and 
146 by the Department of Revenue) during the 207th Session, there is a delay in the 
laying of 2 notifications (Sl No. 39 & 44) by the Department of Economi c 
Affairs.  The Ministry have appended a delay statement with only one notification 
(Sl No. 44).  The reason for delay in laying the notification has been attributed to 
non receipt of the gazette copies from the Government of India Press by the Indian 
Bank.   The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance would avoid such 
instances in future.   The Committee also notes that the notification at Sl No. 97 
does not carry any distinctive number. In this context, the Committee would like 
to draw the Ministry’s attention towards its recommendation as contained in its 
152nd Report (relevant extracts reproduced later) whereby each notification must 
be allotted a distinctive number while notifying in the Gazette. The Committee 
hopes that the Ministry shall take care  of this aspect in future.    The Committee, 
however, appreciates that there is an overall decline in the instances of delay in 
the laying of notifications as compared to the position in earlier sessions and hopes 
that the Ministry of Finance would continue  with their efforts to avoid delay in 
laying of notifications and in case of any delay in their laying, shall keep the House 
informed of the reasons  for the same through delay statements. 
  
Ministry of Human Resource Development 
  
The Committee notes with  regret that the Ministry of Human Resource 
Development have delayed the laying of 3  notifications (Sl No. 269, 275 & 276) out 
of the 9 notifications laid by them.   While the Department of Elementary 
Education & Literacy have delayed the laying of one notification by 6 months, the 
Department of Secondary & Higher Education have delayed the laying of 2 of their 
notifications by 7 months and have also failed to append a delay statement.    The 
delay statement appended with the notification laid by the Departm ent of 
Elementary Education & Literacy (Sl No. 269) states the reason for delay as 
follows:- 
  
 “….The University notified the amendment on 1.8.2005 and got published the 
same in the Gazette of India Part III -Section IV on 20.8.2005.   Copies of the 
Gazette Notification were  sent by the University for laying on 10.10.2005.   These 
copies were received in the Ministry on 14.10.2005.  Copies duly authenticated by 
MOS (HRD) were received in the section on 16.12.2005.   This notification could 
not be laid in the Winter Session of Rajya Sabha due to the fact that the House 
was adjourned on 23.12.2005.” The Committee feel that the Ministry of Human 
Resource Development still had sufficient time to lay these notifications during the 
Winter Session 2005 if they had ac ted promptly.  The Committee recommends 
that Ministries should try to avail of the earliest opportunity to lay their papers 
before the Parliament rather than waiting for the later dates of the Session so as 
to avert eventualities like adjournment of the House pre mature.  The Committee 

also recommend the Ministry to invariably  append a delay statement  in all  cases 
of delay in future. 
  
(v)        Ministry of Labour & Employment 
  
The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the 4 notifications laid, the 
Ministry of Labour & Employment have delayed the laying of 2 of 
their  notifications (Sl No. 280 & 282).   Delay statement has been appended with 
both.  The delay statement for the laying of notification at Sl No. 280 states as 
under: 
  
“….at the time of la ying of the amendment incorporating a new Table ‘E’ and 
inclusion of para 39B after para  39A in the EPS, 1995 before Parliament, the Lok 
Sabha Secretariat pointed out that in all the amending rules, a footnote is required 
to be given at the end of the rul e containing therein the details of earlier 
amendments.  Thereafter, the Ministry inserted the requisite footnote by issuing a 
corrigendum to this effect and notifying the same in the Gazette on 16.8.2004, but 
a copy of the Gazette Notification was not rec eived in the Ministry.   Now the 
Notification has been obtained, which is why the delay.” 
  
The delay statement for the laying of notification at Sl No. 282 states, 
“…the copy of the Gazette Notification was received in the Ministry in February, 
2006, which is why the delay.” 
  
            The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Labour & Employment would 
take necessary corrective measures to avoid such instances in future. 
  
(vi)       Ministry of Power 
  
The Committee regrets to note that there is a delay of 6 months in laying of 
one out of the six notifications laid by the Ministry of Power (Sl No. 286). The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Power would avoid such instances in future. 
  
(vii)      Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions 
(Department of Personnel & Training) 
  
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training) have laid 30 
notifications during the Session out of which 20 notifications (Sl No. 290-309) have 
been delayed.  While some notifications have been delayed by 6 -7 months, in 14 
cases there has been a delay of 1 year and 3 months. The Ministry have appended 
a delay statement with all the 20 delayed   notifications.  In their delay statement 
appended to the notifications, the Ministry have submitted as follows:- 
  
“The notification, which was sent to Government of India Press for 
publication in Part II Section 3 (i) of the Gazette of India on 12.9.2005, was 
published in the Extraordinary Gazette on 17.9.2005.  Due to administrative 
exigencies and frequent transfers of the officer dealing with the subject, the matter 
got delayed because the new incumbent needed time to acquaint himself with the 
procedure of publication, follow up with the Government of India Press for G.S.R. 
copies and laying down in both Houses of Parliament.   The delay in laying the 

notification in time is highly regretted.   This may be laid in both Houses of 
Parliament during the current session.” 
  
The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training) could not avail of 
the opportunity to lay the subordinate legislation not only during the Budget 
Session-2005 but also during the Monsoon and Winter Sessions of 2005 thereby 
delaying the laying of the notifications by one year.  Even in cases where there has 
been a delay of 6 -7 months, the Ministry could have laid the notifications during 
the Winter Session-2005.  The Committee is not satisfied with the reasons given by 
the Ministry for the delay and expects them to be prompt and vigilant in the laying 
of subordinate legislation within the stipulated period as recommended in its 131st 
Report (relevant extracts reproduced in para 3 of the report).   The Committee 
hopes that the Ministry will evolve some systematic procedure to avoid such 
instances in future. 
  
  
(viii)       Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways  
(Department of Road Transport & Highways) 
  
The Committee regrets to note that out of a total of 204 Notifications laid 
by the Department of Road Transport & Highways of the Ministry of Shipping, 
Road Transport & Highways, there has been a delay in the laying of 16 
notifications (Sl No. 336-341, 354-361, 374-375.  The delay ranges from 3½ months 
(Sl No. 355-361), 5 months (Sl No. 354 and 376), 8 months (Sl No. 341), 11 months 
(Sl No. 338-340), 1 year ( Sl No. 374 and 375), 2 years (Sl No. 337)  and 3 years (Sl 
No. 336).  A delay statement has been appended with all the notifications which  
states, “It took time to collect the papers and complete the procedural 
formalities.  As such the notifications could not be laid on the Table of both Houses 
of Parliament during the last session.”   The Committee expects the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport & Highways to exercise caution in the matter of timely 
laying of subordinate legislation and ensure that the schedule prescribed by it in 
this behalf is followed meticulously.  The Committee recommends the Ministry to 
evolve some systematic procedure to avoid such instances in future. 
  
3.         The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take 
immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legislation. The 
Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice o f all 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the defaulting 
ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 131 st,135th  and 152 nd 
Reports: 
            “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House 
is not in Session, during the Session immediately following the date of publication 
of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date 
of the publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time l ag 
between the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less 
than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said 
Session. All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth 
comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued under 
various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 

            “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in 
case there is a dela y in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the 
notifications being laid so as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The 
Committee desires all Ministries/Departments t o take a note of this, since the 
intension is that through the process of formulation of a delay statement the 
Ministry is able to introspect, identifying bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”. 
(135th Report) 
            “In the opinion of the Committe e, every notification should be assigned a 
distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no 
notification should be published/laid which does not carry a notification number. 
The Committee expects all Ministries/Departments of Go vernment of India to 
exercise caution in this regard. In case where there are more than one notification 
under a single file, the Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number 
on each notification should be suitably distinguished from one another. 
(152nd Report)
