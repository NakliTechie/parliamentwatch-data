2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that there was a considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total of 88 delayed 
Notifications, there were 18 Notifications where no delay statement was appended to. However, the 
bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministr y of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 70.4% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Coal  
 
The Ministry of Coal have laid 2 Notifications (Sl.  Nos. 8 & 9), out of which one 
Notification (Sl. No. 9) has been delayed by one ye ar. The Ministry has appended a delay 
statement therewith and the reasons given by the Ministry are as under:- 
 
“In exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section  (1) of Section 26 of the Mines and 
Minerals (Development and Regulations) Act, 1957 (6 7 of 1957), the power of the Central 
Government exercisable by it under the proviso to s ub-section (1) of section 5 of the said Act was 
delegated to the Additional Secretary, Ministry of Coal so far as it relates to coal and lignite in 
respect of Coal Public Sector Undertakings.. 
Due to administrative exigencies and oversight, the re is an inadvertent delay in laying of 
the notification in both the Houses. The delay, the refore, was not intentional and is sincerely 
regretted. The Notifications issued subsequent to t his one viz. [G.S.R. 792 (E), dated 20.10.2015, 
G.S.R. 848 (E), dated 10.11.2015, and  S.O. 645(E),  dated 02.03.2016] were laid in the Parliament 
in time”. 
  
 The Committee directs the Ministry to avoid such i nstances of delay in laying of 
subordinate legislation in future and should keep o ff situations, intentional or unintentional, 
causing delay. The Committee have noted with concer n the reply given by the Ministry and 
expects them to keep more vigil to avoid such unreasonable delays in future. 
 
(ii) Ministry of Communications and Information Technology 

 (Department of Posts) 
 
 Ministry of Communications and Information Technol ogy (Department of Posts) have laid 
2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 13 & 14) and one of these  (Sl. No. 13) has been delayed by 5 months. 
There is no delay statement appended to the Notification. 
  
The Committee takes a serious view of the delay in laying the Notification on the 
Table of the House. The Committee wishes that  the Ministry of Communications and 
Information Technology (Department of Posts)   might have communicated the circumstances 
causing delay in laying of the Notification and als o to have appended a delay statement 
therefor. It is rather surprising to notice that th e Ministry has not only delayed the laying of 
one Notification but also has not appended any dela y statement thereto. The Committee, 
therefor, advises the Ministry of Communications an d Information Technology (Department 
of Posts)   to strictly adhere to the time schedule in laying of the Notifications and append a 
delay statement where there is delay in their laying.  
. 
 (iii) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 17 Notifica tions (Sl. Nos. 18 to 34). Out of  these,  
2  Notifications  (Sl. Nos. 28 & 29) have been dela yed for  a period of 10 months and are 
accompanied with a delay statement. The reason of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
  
 “The Competition Commission of India (Procedure fo r Engagement of Experts and 
Professionals) Amendment Regulations, 2015 and the Competition Commission of India 
(Procedure in regard to the transaction of business  relating to combinations) Amendment 
Regulations, 2015, dated the 18 
th  June, 2015 and 1 st  July, 2015 are required to be laid before both 
the Houses of Parliament. 
 These Regulations could not be laid earlier, these  are entailed financial implication and 
were under examinations in the Ministry in consulta tion with the Competition Commission of 
India. 
 Hence the delay.” 
 
 The Committee  expresses its deep concern about th e reply given by the Ministry of 
Corporate Affairs that the delay in laying of Notif ications was caused due to its consultations 

with the Competition Commission of India in the mat ter. The Committee observes that it is 
the Ministry’s prime responsibility to ensure that Subordinate Legislation is laid before the 
Parliament within the prescribed time schedule. The  Ministry should frame a suitable 
mechanism to expedite the process and avoid the undue delay henceforth. 
 
(iv) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change 
 
 Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change  have laid 11 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
35 to 45) on the Table of the House during the Session. Two of them  (Sl. Nos. 35 & 36) have been 
delayed by 5 months in each case and no delay state ment is appended to both of these delayed 
Notifications. 
 
 The Committee takes a serious view not only about the delay in laying of two of its 
Notifications on the Table of the House but also it s failure to append a delay statement with 
them. The Ministry is directed to ensure that a pro per  delay statement is appended in all 
such cases in future so as to bring to the notice of   the Parliament the causes of delay. 
 
 
(v) Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Se rvices) 
 
 Ministry of Finance  (Department of Financial Serv ices) have laid 12 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 65 to 76). Out of these, 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 68 & 76) have been delayed by 6 to 12 
months respectively and no delay statement has been  attached to both of these delayed 
Notifications.  
  
   The Committee notes with seriousness that two of th e  Notifications laid by the 
Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Servic es) were not only delayed by six to 
twelve months, respectively,  but also did not have  any delay statement attached to them. The 
Committee views it seriously  and reiterates its re commendations  as contained in its 135 
th  
Report that in case there is delay in laying of Sta tutory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a dela y statement. The Committee further 
impresses upon the Ministry that it is their obliga tion towards the Parliament to invariably 
apprise it about the reasons for delay through dela y statements in case they are unable to lay 
any Subordinate Legislation on time. 

 
 
(vi) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Departm ent of Health and Family Welfare) 
  
The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Departme nt of Health and Family Welfare)  
have laid 6 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 120-125) out of which 3 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 120, 121 & 124) 
have been delayed by 5 to 6 months. There is no del ay statement appended with any of  delayed 
Notifications. 
  
  The Committee takes a strong view of the delay oc curred in tabling the Notifications 
in time and hopes that the Ministry would avoid suc h instances of delay in laying of 
Subordinate Legislation in future. The Committee al so impress upon the Ministry of Health 
and Family Welfare (Department of Health and Family  Welfare) to take note of the fact that 
in case there is a delay in the laying of any Statu tory Notification, the same should be 
accompanied by a delay statement while it is being laid. 
  
 
(vii) Ministry of Home Affairs  
 
 Ministry of Home Affairs have laid 21 Notification s (Sl. Nos. 126 to 146).  Out of these, 10 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 135 – 141 & 144 – 146) have  been delayed by  5 to 11 months and there is  
no delay statement appended  to any of these delayed Notifications. 
 
 The Committee is constrained to note that there has been inordinate delay in  laying of 
ten Notifications by the Ministry of Home Affairs. Moreover, there is no delay statement 
appended to throw light on the factors which caused  this delay. Further they may note that 
the Committee have clearly mentioned in its 135 
th  Report that delay statements should 
invariably be appended where there is delay in layi ng of Notifications but Ministry has failed 
to do this. The Committee, therefore, impresses upo n the Ministry and reiterates its 
suggestion to strictly adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in its 135 th   Report and,  in 
case of any delay in laying, to append a delay statement therwith. 
 
 
(xi) Ministry of Human Resource Development 

  (Department of Higher Education) 
 
 The Ministry of Human Resource Development (Depart ment of Higher Education) 
have laid 4 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 149 to 152). Tw o of them (Sl. Nos. 149 & 150) have been 
delayed by 12 to 27 months with delay statement enc losed therewith. The reasons given by the 
Ministry for the said delay is as under:- 
 
 “Statute 2(4) relating to appointment of Vice Chan cellor, Statute 11 relating to constitution 
of Executive Council and Statute 13 relating to con stitution of Academic Council of the Central 
University of Kerala were amended in September, 201 3. The Statute 40 relating to School of 
Studies was repealed and Statute 41 incorporated in  March, 2015. These were required to be laid 
before both the Houses of Parliament as soon as these amendments were made. 
 It could not be laid in the Parliament earlier as the Central University of Kerala submitted 
these Gazette Notifications to the Ministry in January, 2016” 
 
 The Committee is unhappy for the delay in laying o f Notifications due to amendments 
taking place. Instead of waiting for over 12 to 27 months to lay the Notifications, the Ministry 
should have laid the Notifications on the first ava ilable opportunity. The amendments would 
have followed  suit. But the Ministry thought other wise that it would carry out the 
amendments and then  lay the Notifications. The Com mittee exhorts the Ministry to lay the 
Notifications well in time in future at the first a vailable opportunity, to avoid any delay. The 
Committee advises the Ministry of Human Resource De velopment to take care in future so 
that such delays do not recur and there are no lapses on its part. 
 
(ix) Ministry of Labour and Employment 
 
 Ministry of  Labour and Employment have laid 4 Not ifications (Sl. Nos. 154 to 157).  Out 
of these, one Notification (Sl. No. 154) has been d elayed by  12 months and has appended a delay 
statement thereto. 
The reasons cited by the Ministry for delay is  administrative reasons. 
 
 
The Committee is not convinced with the reply given  by the Ministry. The Committee 
directs the Ministry to given specific reason for t he delay as to why these Notifications were 
not laid in the House within the stipulated time.  The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry of Labour and Employment should take note of the time schedule as prescribed by 

it for laying of Subordinate Legislation and take n ecessary corrective steps so that such 
instances do not recur.  
 
 
(x) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways 
 
 The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways have l aid 62 Notifications
 on the Table of 
the House (Sl. Nos. 172 to 233) with delay of 7 to 15 months. All of them have been accompanied 
with delay statements. The reason given by the Mini stry is that it took some time to complete the 
procedural formalities and, therefore, papers could not be laid in time. 
 
The Committee does not accept the reasons of the de lay given by the Ministry of Road 
Transport and Highways that it took some time to co llect the papers and complete the 
procedural formalities, as convincing. The Committee wonders as to how can it take one year 
and three months to receive Notifications and to co mplete procedural formalities. The 
Committee feels that Ministry did not take adequate  precaution to avoid the delay. The 
Committee further takes a serious view of the fact that even after delay of one year in certain 
case the Ministry did not take any action to lay th e same at the earliest opportunity. The 
Committee finds that the reply given by the Ministr y as to the reasons of delay is not 
convincing and  appears to be evasive. Delay could have been avoided, had precaution been 
taken by the Ministry in this regard. The Committee  hopes that the Ministry of Road 
Transport and Highways would cut on such delays in future particularly on account of delay 
in completing the procedural formalities. The Commi ttee recommends that the Ministry 
should initiate urgent measures so as to avoid dela y in completing the procedural formalities 
so that such delay can be eliminated. 
 
 
(xi)  Ministry of Science and Technology 
 (Department of Science and Technology) 
 
 Ministry of Science and Technology (Department of Science and Technology) have laid 4 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 234 to 237). All of them ha ve been delayed by 2 years to 6 years and 1 
month and are appended with a delay statement. The reason for delay given by the Ministry is that 

due to circumstances beyond the control of the Depa rtment the Notifications could not be laid in 
time. 
 
The Committee are not convinced of the vague reason of delay cited by the Ministry of 
Science and Technology (Department of Science and T echnology). In Committee’s view the 
Ministry should have foreseen the things beforehand  and should have acted  promptly. The 
Committee castigates this lackadaisical approach by  the Ministry and advises them to take 
care in future in laying of Notifications.  
 
3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministrie s/Departments to take immediate steps to 
ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate legi slation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to 
bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments o f the Government of India, particularly, the 
defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 7 
th , 131 
st 
, 135

, 152

, 201 st  , 212 th  and 213 th  Reports: 
 
 “The Committee desires that for the convenience of  the public and Members, 
Ministries should henceforth send copies of 'order'  duly authenticated by the Ministers 
concerned, incorporating therein all the corrections and amendments issued till date of laying 
such 'orders' instead of laying the uncorrected cop y along with corrigenda. The Committee 
feels that to obviate any legal or technical difficulty, Ministries should examine whether there 
is any necessity of assigning a separate G.S.R. No.  to a corrigendum at the time of its 
publication in the Gazette and whether such a corri gendum should not be published without 
assigning any G.S.R. No. The Committee would, therefore, like to impress upon all Ministries 
of the Government of India that the present practice of laying the rules without incorporating 
corrigenda in the body of the rules is not a health y one. The Committee hopes that the 
Ministries will ensure supply of only corrected cop ies of the rules for laying on the Table so 
that they depict a true and correct picture and serve the desired purpose."

nd 
 Report) 
(Presented on 26 th   August 2004)  
 
 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Commit tee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 

presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force of an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 
of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 

invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 
while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”

  Rules/Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are laid by dif ferent Ministries on the Table of the 
House under various provisions of parent Acts passe d by Parliament. These Rules/ 
Regulations/Orders/bye-laws are published in the of ficial Gazette before being laid on the 
Table of the House. Even after they are laid, it is  very difficult to get access to these 
Notifications. The Committee recommends that such R ules/Regulations/Orders, etc. laid on 
the Table of the House should be invariably uploade d on the websites of respective Ministries 
immediately on the same day when they have been lai d on the Table of the House, for 
information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access. 
201 
st  Report 
(Presented on 14 th  December, 2012)  
 
 
The Committee observes that the whole process of en acting laws is defeated if rules 
are not framed and laid on the Table of the House i n time.  A reasonable delay could be 
understood but unjustifiably long delays should be avoided and the Ministries should work 
towards improving this position.   The Committee hopes that Ministry would strive to k eep 
the delay to the minimum and take care that Notific ations are laid on the Table of the House 
at the first available opportunity after publication of Notifications. 
  
 
The Committee would like to impress upon the Minist ry that it is their responsibility 
to ensure that copies of the Notifications are prin ted timely and for this purpose, they need to 
evolve a suitable mechanism to ensure that notifica tions are laid in time.   The Committee is 
happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  The 

Committee hopes that it would take action according ly in order to comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications. 
 
 
The Ministry must take appropriate step to remove l acuna, if any, administrative or 
otherwise, causing such long delay by being little proactive and prompt.  Further, the 
Ministries should take it up with the Ministry of U rban Development for late receiving of 
printed copies of the Notification. 
 
212 
th   Report

 “The Committee is not inclined to accept the late availability of printed copies of 
Notifications from the Government of India Press as  the reason for delay in laying of the

procuring printed copies in time, they may obtain G .S.R. and S.O. numbers from the 
Government of India Press and prepare requisite num ber of copies in cyclostyled form for 
laying in the Parliament. The Committee also recomm ends that this process may be adopted 
by Ministries suo moto  on immediate basis so as to avoid such delays henceforth”.
