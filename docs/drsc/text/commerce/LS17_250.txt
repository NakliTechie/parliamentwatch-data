CHAPTER I 
1.         BACKGROUND 
      Rule 204 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the 
Council of States (Rajya Sabha) provides that there shall be a Committee on 
Subordinate Legislation to scrutinize and report to the Council whether the 
powers to make rules, regulations, bye -laws, schemes or other statutory 
instruments conferred by the Constitution or delegated by Parliament have 
been properly exercised within such conferment or delegation, as the case 
may be. 
1.2.      Mandate  
            Legislations passed by the Parliament an d subsequently enacted 
after being assented to by the President of India, are implemented by the 
Executive i.e., various Ministries/Departments of Government of India. It is 
a well recognized principle that the laws enacted by Parliament cannot be 
exhaustive enough to visualize each and every situation that may arise 
during the course of their implementation and to m ake provisions for it in 
the Act in anticipation. The Central Acts, therefore, if necessary,  contain a 
provision authorizing the Government to frame detailed rules/regulations, 
etc. to enable it to implement the provisions of the Act. These 
rules/regulations framed in exercise of the powers conferred by an Act of 
Parliament are termed as Subordinate Legislation. 
Rule 209 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the 
Council of States lays down that after each rule, regulation, bye -law, 
scheme or  other statutory instrument framed in pursuance of the 
Constitution/Act or the legislative functions delegated by the Parliament to 
a subordinate authority and which is required to be laid before Parliament, 
is so laid before the Council, the Committee shall, in particular consider: 
 (i)   Whether the Order is in accord with the provisions of the 
 Constitution/Act  pursuant to which it is made; 
(ii)   Whether the Order contains matter which, in the opinion of 
 the Committee,  should more properly be dealt w ithin 
 an Act of Parliament; 
(iii)   Whether the Order contains imposition of taxation; 
(iv)   Whether the Order directly or indirectly bars the jurisdiction 
 of the Courts; 

2 
 (v)   Whether the Order gives retrospective effect to any of the 
 provisions in  respect of which the Constitution or the Act 
 does not expressly give any  such power; 
(vi)    Whether the Order involves expenditure from the 
 Consolidated Fund of India or the public revenues; 
(vii)  Whether the Order appears to make some unusual or 
 unexpected use of the powers conferred by the Constitution 
 or the Act pursuant to which it is made; 
(viii)  Whether there appears to have been unjustifiable delay in 
 its publication or laying before Parliament; and 
(ix)   Whether for any reason, the form or purport of the order 
 calls for any elucidation. 
 1.3       Statutory Requirement for laying  
             The Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha had, in its 
10th Report presented to the House on the 24 th May, 1971, inter-alia 
recommended, a standard laying formula to be incorporated not only in new 
legislation but also in existing Acts as and when Bills to amend them are 
brought before Parliament, which is reproduced below:- 
 
 "Every rule made by the Central Government under this Act shall 
be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of 
Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days 
which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive 
sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately 
following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid,  both 
Houses  agree in  making any modification to  the rule  or both 
Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall 
thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, 
as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or 
annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything 
previously done under that rule.” 
            Now, all statutes contain the above cited provision for making 
rules/regulations, for laying the same before both Houses of Parliament and 
for amending or annulling the same.  
1.4       Time limit for laying of rules/regulations 
             As per the recommendations contained in para 33 of the 1 st Report 
of the Committee, all the Orders including rules etc., are required to be laid 
on the Table of the House: - 
 
(a) within a period of 15 days after their publication in the 
 Gazette, if the House is in session; and 

3 
(b) if the House is not in session, within a period of 15 days  
 after the commencement  of  the  next  session. 
            However, in case of notifications relating to a state under the 
President’s rule, due to difficulties faced by certain Ministry, the Committee 
vide its recommendations contained in its 6 th Report (para 17 -19) relaxed 
this time period to 30 days from the date of publication of the rules, orders, 
notifications etc., in the State Gazette. 
 1.5       Motion for an nulment or modification of rules/regulations laid 
on the Table of the House 
             As per recommendations of the Committee contained in its 10 th 
Report ( cited at para 1.3 ante), after the rules/regulations are laid on the 
Table of the House, Members can move motion for modification or 
annulment of the rules/regulations. The statutory period of laying of 30 days 
might be completed in one session or two or more successive sessions. 
Further, as per the recommendation of the Committee contained in its 75 th 
Report (Para 2.5), reproduced below:- 
  
"The Committee agrees with the view of the Committee on Subordinate 
Legislation Lok Sabha (Seventh Lok Sabha) expressed in its 20 th Report, 
that laying on the Table of all Orders' in pursuance of powers delegated 
by Parliament is very significant, as it affords an opportunity to 
Members of Parliament if they so desire to move any amendment or 
modification to such 'order'; including a Motion for their annulment.”  
            Hence, a reading of both these recommendations together indicates 
that Members have a right to suggest/move motion for 
annulment/modifications in the 'Order' for a time period till one additional 
session immediately following the session in which the period of 30 days is 
completed. If the modification or annulment is carried by the Houses of 
Parliament, the rules/regulations stand annulled or modified to the extent 
agreed to by the Houses of Parliament. 
 1.6       Role of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya  
Sabha with regard to unjustifiable delay in laying 
             One of the important functions of the Committee, according to Rule 
209 (viii), is to see whether there has been unjustifiable delay in th e 
publication or laying of the rules/regulations before the Parliament. The 
Committee monitors the laying of the rules/regulations etc. on the Table of 
Rajya Sabha during its Session through its Reports. This practice started 
with the 131 st Report of the C ommittee on Statutory Orders laid on the 
Table of the Rajya Sabha during its 190 th Session, presented to the House 

4 
on 15th December, 2000. During the meeting of the Committee held on 11 th 
December, 2000, to adopt the said report, the Committee decided that  
henceforth, it will  regularly  scrutinize   the  notifications  laid  on  the 
Table  of  Rajya Sabha  from time to time and present a report to the 
Council in respect of each Session. This practice has continued since then 
and the present report is in pursuance of the said decision of the Committee.

CHAPTER II 
LAYING OF STATUTORY ORDERS ON THE 
TABLE  OF RAJYA SABHA 
               During the 256th  Session of the Rajya Sabha, (31st  January  – 11th 
February, 2022  and 14 th March – 7th April, 202 2), 439 (Four Hundred 
Thirty-Nine) Statutory Orders/Notifications relating to 
Rules/Regulations/Schemes/Bye-laws etc., framed under the 
Constitution/Acts of Parliament, were laid on the Table of the Council by 
various Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. The details of 
the G azette Notifications in which these were published and the date of 
their laying on the Table of the Rajya Sabha have been indicated Ministry -
wise, in the Statement placed at Annexure. 
2.1       While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that a 
majority of  the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, have 
ensured timely laying of the Gazette Notifications brought out by them. The 
annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where 
Notifications have not been laid as per the time schedule prescribed by the 
Committee. During the 256th Session, out of 439 Notifications that were laid 
on the Table of Rajya Sabha, 355 were laid within the stipulated time, and 
84 Notifications were delayed. Further, out of these 84 delayed 
Notifications, 37 Notifications were delaye d by a period of upto 1 year; 7 
Notifications were delayed by periods ranging b etween 1 year to 2 years; 
and 40 Notifications were delayed by more than 2 years. The Commi ttee, 
however, observes that 60 (71.42%) out of these 84 delayed Notifications, 
was accompanied by delay statement fro m the concerned Minister and 24 
(28.57%) Notifications did not have any delay statement from the 
concerned Minister enclosed along with the Notifications. The bulk of 
delayed Notifications were from the Ministry of Environment, Forest and 
Climate Change with 25 Notifications, which comes to around 2 9.76% of 
the total delayed Notifications,  and  Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers 
Welfare (Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare ) (8 delayed 
Notifications) which comes to 9.52% of the total delayed Notifications, 
some other Ministries also had a large number of delayed Notifications e.g., 
Ministry of Home Affairs (7  delayed Notifications);  Department of 
Economic Affairs  (6 delayed Notifications); Ministry of Education 
(Department of Higher Education)  (5 delayed Notifications);. Ministry of 
Defence and Ministry of  Commerce and Industry  (Department for 
Promotion  of Industry and Internal Trade ) (both are 4  delayed 
Notifications); and Ministry of Road Transport and Highways  (6 delayed 
Notifications)Hence, the Committee has examined these data in detail and 
important issues have been highlighted for guidance of the 
Ministries/Departments for the future. The following are the Ministry -wise 
details of the Notifications that were l aid during the Session, which were 
delayed:-

4.         RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE 
 a) Delay in Laying of Orders/Rules/Regulations 
     
4.1   As observed in the preceding section, t he Committee notes that 84 
delayed notifications laid by the Ministries/Departments of 
Government of India, during the 25 6th Session of Rajya Sabha, were 
delayed by various amounts of time. The Committee would like to draw 
the attention of few Ministries, which laid large number of  
Notifications  on the Table of Rajya Sabha after a considerable delay 
e.g., Agriculture and Farmers Welfare (Department of Agriculture and 
Farmers Welfare)  – 8 Notifications delayed out of a total of 10 
Notifications and 7 Notifications were delayed   by periods between  
ranging from  4 month to 1 year and 6  months; Defence – all 4 
Notifications laid on the Table  were delayed   by periods between 2 
years and 3 month to 8 years and 8  months; Education (Department of 
Higher Education  – 5 Notifications were delayed, out of a total of 24 
Notifications and all 5 delayed  Notifications were delayed   by periods 
ranging from 1 year and 7 months to 2 years  and 2 months ; 
Environment, Forest and Climate Change – 25 Notifications were 
delayed, out of a total of 31 Notifications and  these were delayed  by 
periods ranging from 9 months to 9 years and 10  months; Finance 

33 
(Department of Economy Affairs) – 6 Notifications were delayed, out of 
a total of 37 Notifications and all 6 Notifications were delayed by 
periods ranging from 2 years and 2 months  to 2 years  and 5 months; 
Department of Financial Services – 3 Notifications were delayed, out of 
a total of 16 Notifications and these delayed Notifications were delayed 
by 2  months  to 2 years and 11 months; and Home Affairs – 7 
Notifications were delayed, out of a total of 17 Notifications, 3 
Notifications were delayed by periods ranging from 4 months to 18 
years and 10 months . The Committee would like to draw special 
attention to the delays of Ministries of Environment, Forest and 
Climate Change , Department of Economic Affairs, Department of 
Financial Services, Home Affairs, Micro, Small and Medium 
Enterprises, were the amount o f delays were huge i.e., beyond 2 years 
and many of their Notifications were delayed by as much as 18 years 
and 10 months, which is quite unacceptable. The Committee feels that 
such inordinate delays reflect the callous attitude of the concerned 
Ministries and defeat the very purpose of laying such Notifications on 
the Table of the House. More importantly, this could open unwanted 
scenarios as Members have a right to annulment of the Notifications 
laid, wi thin the time period specified in the laying provisions of the 
relevant Act.  Hence, such motions for amendment/annulment of a 
Notification which is 18 years old would create an unwanted crisis. The 
Committee, therefore, once again, impresses upon all 
Ministries/Departments in general and specificall y those mentioned 
above, to avoid such delays in the future. The Committee feels that such 
delays are totally avoidable and in violation of its recommendations 
contained in its 131st Report wherein it had recommended:- 
         
        ―Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if 
the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following 
the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if 
the House is in Session, on the date of the publication of the Ord er, 
during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of 
publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 
clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the 
said Session. All the Ministries/Departmen ts of Government of India 
should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of 
the Orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the 
Rajya Sabha.‖ 
            The Committee, while reiterating its above recommendations, 
once again recommends that the Ministries of Commerce and Industry

Environment, Forest and Climate Change; Health and Family Welfare; 
Home Affairs; Power; and Road Transport and Highways should

 b) Non-attachment of ‗Delay Statements‘ to the delayed Notifications 
laid 
 4.2       The Committee feels that the most disconcerting point is that 24 
Notifications, out of a total 84 delayed Notifications , were laid during 
this Session, without any delay statement appended to them, which goes 
against the recommendation of the Committee contained in its earlier 
Report (135th Report) reproduced below:- 
        ―The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is 
essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, 
the concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay 
statement along with the Notifications being laid  so as to apprise the 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intention is that 
through the process of formulation of a delay statement, the Ministry is 
able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial measures‖. 
           The Committee recommends that all the Ministries/Departments 
must adhere to the time schedule for laying of Notifications as 
prescribed in Committee‘s 131 st Report and in case, the delay in laying 
of Notifications is unavoidable, the Ministry should attach a delay 
statement from their Minister citing cogent reasons for the delay. The 
delay statement gives an opportunity, to the Ministry to clarify the 
reasons for the delay in laying of the Notificati on but also a reason for 
the Ministry/Department to introspect and improve their functioning. 
In case this longstanding recommendation of the Committee is not 
followed, then the accountability of the Ministries to the Parliament 
and the Constitutional Mand ate of the Parliament to monitor the 
functioning of the Executive would become a futile exercise and the 
Committee has every right to take this matter very seriously. 
 
 c) Format of Delay Statements 
4.3    The Committee observes that many of the 
Ministries/Departments, whose Notifications were delayed, has not 
made any sincere effort to identify the reasons for the delay in laying 
them by citing reasons like "curtailment/sudden closure of Session, due 
to Cov id pandemic , sine-die adjournment", "due to oversight", 
'administrative reasons", "due to dela yed receipt of printed copies",  
"took some time to collect the papers and complete the procedural

Notification was sent to the Rajya Sabha/Lok Sabha on 20.09.2021, 
when there was not Parliamentary Session and again on 17.12.2021, 
which was at the fag and of the Winter Session, and hence could not be 
laid. The Committee, therefore, recommends  that the 
Ministries/Departments should keep track  of the Parliament Session 
and send their Notification for layi ng on the Table of the House and 
efforts should be made to send the Notifications during the early part of 
the Session so as to avoid last minute rush and delays. The Committee, 
therefore, strongly recommends that the Ministries/Departments 
should cite cogent, logical and self explanatory reasons for the delay in 
the laying of Notifications so that accountability of the 
Ministries/Departments to the Parliament is maintained   in its true 
letter and spirit and to ensure that, it does not remain a mere formality. 
The Committee would like to emphasize that the purpose of this Report 
is not a punitive exercise but a fact finding process to improve the 
procedure followed by the Ministries/Departments th rough the 
Committees recommendations and make the process smoot her and 
relevant. The Committee , therefore,  recommends that all the 
Ministries/Departments should put in more effort to ensure timely 
delivery of these Notifications and also to follow the guidelines issued 
by the Table Office, Rajya Sabha Secretariat to all the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India on the eve of each 
Session, so as to avoid such difficulties and also to facilitate the laying 
of Gazette Notifications on the Table of Rajya Sabha smoothly. The 
Committee would also like to re -iterate its earlier recommendations 
contained in para  2 (xii) of its 189 th Report, which is reproduced 
below:- 
              ―The Committee is not happy at the casual manner in which 
the Ministries have treated its recommendations with regard to the 
delay in the laying of the Notifications despite the Committee‘s 
reiteration in all its reports. The Committee takes serious note of the 
fact that, in most cases, the Ministries fail to append delay statements 
explaining the reasons for the delay. Therefore, the Commit tee 
reiterates, that the Ministries should take proactive measures so as to 
avoid any delay, whatsoever, in the laying of the Notifications and that 
the Committee is unable to accept the flimsy grounds given by the 
Ministries regarding the reasons for delay.‖ 
           The Committee, therefore, recommends these 
Ministries/Departments to not only ensure timely laying of 
Notifications but also follow the practice of enclosing a delay statement 
in case(s) of delays.
