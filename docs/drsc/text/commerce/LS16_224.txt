2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that there was a considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 216 delayed 
Notifications, there were 84 Notifications where no delay statement was appended to. However, the 
bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministr y of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 85.6% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of Civil Aviation  
  

 Ministry of Civil Aviation has laid five Notificat ions (Sl. No. 9 to 13) during the Session. 
Out of these two Notifications (Sl. Nos. 9 – 10) ha ve been delayed by 16 and 14 months 
respectively and no delay statement is attached to both of these Notifications. 
 
 The Committee takes a strong view of such inordina te delay in laying of Notifications 
by the Ministry of Civil Aviation which in the inst ant case, has been  delayed by over more 
than one year. Further, there is no delay statement  attached to any of these Notifications. 
This is akin to defiance of Committee’s recommendat ion contained in its 135 
th  Report which 
requires concerned Ministries/Departments to invari ably append delay statement in case of 
delayed  Notifications being laid so as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The 
Committee desires that the Ministry of Civil Aviati on should invariably append delay 
statement(s) in case of any delay actually taking p lace in laying of Notifications and avoid 
recurrence of such inordinate delay in future. 
 
  
 
(ii)  Ministry of Commerce and Industry 
 (Department of Commerce) 
 
 The Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of Commerce) have laid 3 
Notifications (Sl. Nos. 20 to 22) and out  of which  two (Sl. Nos. 20 and 21) were delayed by 12 
and 5 months respectively. One of the Notification had delay statement attached to it whereas the 
other did not have any delay statement with it. The  reasons for delay given by the Ministry are as 
under:-. 
  
 “Laying of Gazette Notification of amendment made in Agricultural and Processed Food 
Products Export Development Authority(APEDA) Act, 1985 vide S.O. 1038 (E), dated 04.04.1014 
regarding APEDA (Amendment) Rules published by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry was 
to be laid in both the Houses of Parliament. Howeve r, the notification could not be laid, within the 
stipulated time period, inadvertently. The Notifica tion S.O. No. 1038 (E), dated 04.04.2014 is 
being submitted before both the Houses of Parliament.” 
 
 The Committee directs that the Ministry must avoid  such instances of delay in laying 
of subordinate legislation in future and would keep  off situations, intentional or 

unintentional, causing delay. The Committee have no ted the reply given by the Ministry and 
expects them to keep more vigil to avoid such unreasonable delays in future.  
 
(iii) Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public  Distribution 
 (Department of Consumer Affairs) 
 
 Only one Notification that the Ministry of Consume r Affairs, Food and Public Distribution 
(Department of Consumer Affairs) (Sl. No. 32) have laid was delayed by 14 months and no delay 
statement has been appended therewith. 
 
 The Committee is not happy over the delay in layin g of the only  Notification by the 
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distr ibution (Department of Consumer 
Affairs)   by  14 months. Further there is no delay statement  appended thereto giving reasons 
as to what caused such undue delay. The Committee, therefore, impresses upon the Ministry 
to take care that such instances of delay do not re cur in future and in case of any delay in 
laying, a delay statement should invariably be appended therewith. 
 
(iv) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs have laid 17 Notific ations (Sl. Nos. 38 to 54), of which 3 
Notifications have been delayed.  Out of 3 delayed Notifications,  2  Notifications (Sl. Nos. 38 and 
40) have  been delayed each by 5 months and have no  delay statements attached to them. One 
Notification (Sl. No. 41) has been delayed  by 10 months although with a delay statement appended 
to it. The reason forwarded by the Ministry for delay is as under:- 
 
 “The notification relating to the G.S.R. No. 425(E)  dated 30.06.2014 on the Companies 
(Costs Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 is required to be laid before both the Houses of Parliament. 
 The notification relating to the G.S.R. No. 425(E)  dated 30.06.2014 on the Companies 
(Costs Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 was sent to G overnment Press for printing. Due to volume 
of pages, it took time (6 months) for printing in t he Government Press. The said notification could 
not be laid before both the Houses of Parliament du ring the Budget Session of Parliament, 2014 
due to delay in receiving the Gazette copy of said notification by this Ministry. The delay may be 
kindly be condoned.” 
 

 The Committee  expresses its deep concern about th e reply given by the Ministry of 
Corporate Affairs that the delay in laying of Notif ications was delayed due to volume of 
pages, which took time (6 months) for printing at t he Government Press.  The Ministry of 
Corporate Affairs  should, in future, ensure that a ll its papers are laid before the House as 
prescribed in the Committee’s 131 st  Report. The Ministry should frame a suitable mechanism 
to expedite the printing of papers so as to avoid undue delay.

Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs ) have laid 14 Notifications (Sl. Nos.

Notifications. 
Department of Financial Services have also laid 14 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 73 to 86). Out of

attached to it. 
 
The Committee, has time and again,  emphasised that  the Ministry should make all  
out effort for tabling of Notifications in time and  should avoid instances of delay in laying of 
Subordinate Legislation in future. The Committee al so impresses upon the Ministry to take 
note of the fact that in case, there is a delay in laying of any Statutory Notifications, the same 
should be accompanied by a delay statement while it  is being laid, as recommended in 
Committee’s 131

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of 
Notifications on the Table of the House, the Commit tee also express their deep concern over 
delay in framing of rules/regulations under differe nt Acts. The Committee in its 47 
th  report 
presented to the House on 14.9.1981 has recommended  that the rule-making process should 
be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force on an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 

Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
  
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 
of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 
invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 
while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”

immediately on the same day when they have been lai d on the Table of the House, for 
information of Members of Parliament and people at large for their easy access.

the delay to the minimum and take care that Notific ations are laid on the Table of the House 
at the first available opportunity after publication of Notifications.

happy that the Ministry has evolved an internal mechanism/ procedure for this purpose.  The 
Committee hopes that it would take action according ly in order to comply with the 
prescribed schedule in the matter of laying of statutory Notifications.
