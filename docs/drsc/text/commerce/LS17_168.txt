2.         During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted some cases where there has been a considerable time
lag between the date of notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in
cases where notifications have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by the Committee and whether such notifications were
accompanied by a statement indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay has been quite long. The
observations/recommendations of the Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows: -  
           
(i) Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Agriculture Research and   Education)
 
            The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of five months in laying one notification (Sl. No. 01) and no delay
statement was sent therewith. The Committee would like the Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Agriculture Research and  
Education) to take corrective measures so as to obviate such delay in future.
 
(ii) Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Animal Husbandry, Dairying and Fisheries)
 
      The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of seven months on the part of the Ministry of Agriculture
(Department of Animal Husbandry, Dairying and Fisheries) in laying two of their notifications (Sl. Nos. 6 & 7). In both these cases
the Ministry have failed to append the delay statement. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Animal
Husbandry, Dairying and Fisheries) would avoid delay in laying of the notifications and in case of any delay actually taking place in
laying, a delay statement would invariably be appended.
 
(iii) Ministry of Defence
 
      The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Defence have delayed the laying of one of their notification (Sl. No. 38) by
five months.  The Ministry have appended delay statement with the Notification and the reasons given by the Ministry reads: “The
delay in laying the papers has occurred due to inadvertent omission on the part of officer in the Naval Headquarters dealing with the
case. There was also a change in the officers dealing with the case. However, instructions have been reiterated to all the Departments
and Offices under the Ministry of Defence to ensure that notification etc. required to be laid on the table of the Parliament are sent to
Parliament Secretariat in time.”
       The Committee hopes that the action taken by the Ministry would avoid recurrence of such instances in future.
(iv) Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs)
 
            The Committee regrets to note that there has been delay up to 10 months in the laying of two notifications (Sl. Nos. 48 & 82). 
The Ministry have not appended delay statement with the notification at Sl. No. 48.  The delay statement appended with the
Notification at Sl. No. 48 reads : “ The Copies of the Gazette Notification No. ADVT-III/IV/23-E/2006/Exty. Dated 2
nd May, 2006
containing Rajasthan Gramin Bank (Officers & Employees) Service Regulations, 2006 could not be laid on the Table of the Rajya as
the copies of Service Regulations of Rajasthan Gramin Bank, were received on 24 th November, 2006. The notification is now being
laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during current Session of Parliament.” The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Finance
(Department of Economic Affairs) should stick to the time schedule as recommended by it in its 131st Report in the matter of laying of
Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and that they should review their internal mechanism so that the copies of the notifications
are received on time and this does not become the cause for delay in laying papers before the Parliament.
 
(v) Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue)
 
            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) have delayed the laying of three
notifications (Sl Nos. 91, 92 & 93) laid by them. The delay statement appended with the Notification at Sl. No. 91 reads: “The Copies
of the Gazette Notification No. ADVT. III/.IV/ 253G/2005/Exty., dated 10
th March, 2006 containing Jaipur Thar Gramin Bank
(Officers & Employees) Service Regulations, 2006 could not be laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha as the copies of Service
Regulations of Jaipur Thar Gramin Bank, were received in the month of September, 2006. The notification is now being laid on the
Table of the Rajya Sabha during Winter Session of Parliament.” Similar delay statements have been also appended with the
Notifications at Sl. Nos. 92 & 93. The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) would avoid such
instances in future and that the Ministry would take necessary corrective measures to avoid delay in laying on such grounds and
adhere to the schedule prescribed by the Committee in its 131st Report. .

(vi) Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (Department of Health)
          The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of upto 12 and 5 months on the part of the Ministry of Health &
Family Welfare (Department of Health) in laying two of their notifications laid by them (Sl. No.117 & 118). The delay statement
appended with the Notification at Sl. No. 117 states :-“Since the provisions of these Rules are subjudice in the matter of Mahesh Bhatt
Vs Union of India pending before the High Court of Delhi, the same could not be laid on the Table of both Houses of Parliament in
time. Further, these rules have been further amended by “The Cigarettes and other Tobacco Products (Prohibition of Advertisement
and Regulation of Trade and Commerce, Production, Supply and Distribution) (Amendment) Rules, 2006” which was notified in the
gazette of India vide GSR No. 656 (E) dated 20.10.2006. These provisions are yet to come into force. The said notifications are now
proposed to be laid on the Table of both the Houses in the current Session of the Parliament.”
      The delay statement appended with Notification at Sl. No. 118 reads:
      “ Since the requisite number of copies of printed notification were not received in time, the same could not be laid on the Table of
both Houses of Parliament in time. The said notification is now proposed to be laid on the Table of both the Houses in the current
Session of the Parliament.” The  Committee is not convinced with the grounds for delay put forth by the Ministry in case of
Notification at Sl. No. 118. The fact that an existing Subordinate Legislation is being considered for change is no ground for delaying
the laying of the same before the Parliament. Similarly, inadequate number of copies of notifications is a very common ground for
delay in laying. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Department of Health) should take
necessary corrective steps so that such instances do not recur.  
 
(vii) Ministry of Home Affairs
            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Home Affairs have delayed by eight months the laying of their
notification (Sl. No. 129). The Ministry have not appended any delay statement with the said notification. The Committee hopes that
the Ministry of Home Affairs would avoid such instances of delay in the laying of Subordinate Legislation and shall in such
circumstance keep the House informed of the reasons for delay in laying by appending delay statements. 
 
(viii) Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher Education)
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher Education) have
delayed in laying one of their notification (Sl. No. 137) by two months. No delay statement has been appended with the said
notification.. The Committee expects the Ministry of Human Resource Development (Department of Higher Education) to exercise
caution in the matter of timely laying of subordinate legislation and ensure that such delays do not recur and in case of any delay
actually taking place, a delay statement is invariably  appended while laying the notification.
 
(ix) Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (Department of Personnel and Training)
The Committee regrets to note that one Notification (Sl. No. 152) laid by the Department of Personnel and Training of the
Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions has been delayed by 17 months. No delay statement has been appended with
the said notification.  The Committee regrets to note that the Department of Personnel and Training have abnormally delayed the
laying of their notification and at the same time, the Parliament have not been apprised of the reasons for such delay. The Committee
invites attentions of the Ministry towards the Committee’s recommendations on the subject reproduced at the end of this report, for
their strict compliance.
 
(x) Ministry of Power
 
            The Committee regrets to note that one Notification (Sl. No.165) laid by the Ministry of Power has been delayed by eight
months. The Ministry have appended delay statement with the said Notification and it reads : “Copies of the above Notification were
forwarded by the Central Electricity Authority on 16.11.2006 for laying down on the table of Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha. Delay in laying
down the Regulations occurred in view of the fact that this is the first of the Regulations made by CEA under the Electricity Act,
2003. CEA has been asked to avoid such delay in future.”
 
            The Committee hopes that the Ministry would avoid such instances of delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation in future.
 
 
3.     The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of
subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the Government of
India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 131
st,135th  and 152nd Reports:
        “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately
following the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the publication
of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session
is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the Ministries/Departments of
Government of India should henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued under various Acts of
Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.”  (131
st Report)

        “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders,
the concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial
measures”. (135th Report)
 
        “In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned a distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity
and similarly, no notification should be published/laid which does not carry a notification number. The Committee expects all
Ministries/Departments of Government of India to exercise caution in this regard. In case where there are more than one notification
under a single file, the Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number on each notification should be suitably distinguished
from one another. (152nd Report)
