2.  While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee noted that there was a considerable time lag 
between issue  of Notifications and their laying in  some cases. The annexed statement also 
indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifi cations have not been laid as per the time 
schedule prescribed by the Committee. The Committee  observed that out of the total 280 delayed 
Notifications, there were 145 Notifications where n o delay statement was appended to. However, 
the bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Min istry of  Road Transport and Highways which 
comes to around 83.5% of the total delayed Notifications. 
 
The observations/recommendations of the Committee i n respect of the specific 
Ministries/Departments where delay in laying of Notifications has been noticed, are as follows: -  
 
(i) Ministry of AYUSH  
  
 Ministry of AYUSH  have laid one Notification (Sl.  No. 15) which is delayed by 5 months. 
There is no delay statement  appended thereto.  
 
 The Committee is anguished to note that Ministry o f AYUSH have delayed the laying 
of their only Notification by 5 months and there is  no delay statement attached thereto. The 
Committee observes  that the Ministry of AYUSH stri ctly adheres to the time schedule as 
recommended by Committee  in its 131 
st  Report  in laying of the Subordinate Legislation 
before Parliament. Further, where there is delay in  laying of the notifications, a delay 
statement should also invariably be appended therewith. 
  
(ii)  Ministry of Commerce and Industry 

 (Department of Commerce)  
 
Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department of Co mmerce)  has laid  one Notification  
and that has no delay statement appended thereto. The delay period is of 8 months. 
 
The Committee is not happy about the delay in layin g of the only one Notification by 
the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (Department o f Commerce) on the Table of the 
House but also failure to append the delay statemen t with it. The Ministry/Department must 
ensure that Notification to be laid on the Table of  the House should be laid in time. It also 
must ensure to append a delay statement in all cases in future. 
 
 
 
 
 
(iii) Ministry of Communications and Information Te chnology 
(Department of Telecommunications) 
 
Ministry of Communications and Information Technolo gy (Department of 
Telecommunications) have laid 5 Notifications (Sl. No. 25 to 29). Out  of these, 3 Notifications (Sl. 
Nos. 27-29) have  been delayed. There is delay of 4  months in each case and no delay statement 
has been  appended.  
 
The Committee is constrained to note  that 3 out of  5 Notifications that Ministry of 
Communications and Information Technology (Departme nt of Telecommunications) have 
laid, have been delayed. Further, there is no reaso n given for this delay.  This is in 
contravention of Committee’s recommendation given i n its 135 Report that Notifications 
which are delayed should invariably be accompanied by a delay statement. The Committee 
would like the Ministry of Communications and Infor mation Technology (Department of 
Telecommunications)  to take necessary steps to obviate such delays and in case of delay in 
laying of Notifications, delay statements should invariably be appended to them. 
 
(iv) Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
 
 Ministry of Corporate Affairs  have laid 27 Notifi cations (Sl. Nos. 36 to 62) out of which 7 
Notifications have been delayed by 4 to 6 months. T here is no delay statement  appended to 6 of 
these delayed Notifications and only one Notificati on (Sl. No. 46) delay statement is attached. The 
reason of delay given by the Ministry is as under:- 
 
 “The Notification relating to the G.S.R. No. 397 (E ) dated 12.06.2014 on the Amendment 
in Rule 3 for sub-rule (5) of the Companies (Declar ation and Payment of Dividend) Amendment 
Rules, 2014 was sent to Government Press for printi ng. The Said Notification could not be laid 
before both the Houses of Parliament during the Bud get Session of the Parliament, 2014 due to 
delay in receiving the Gazette copy of the said Not ification by this Ministry and when it was 
collected, the session was over on 14.08.2014. The said Notification got delayed and hence it may 
be kindly be condoned.” 
 
 The Committee have time and again pointed that dela y in receiving printing copies of 
Notifications from the Government Press, is a lame excuse. Ministry of Corporate Affairs 
should plan beforehand that ample time is given to printing press  so that printed copies are 

received timely. The Committee suggests the Ministr y of Corporate Affairs to take adequate 
precaution to avoid such delay in future and append delay statement where  required. 
 
 
 
(v) Ministry of Environment, Forests and Climate Ch ange  
  
 Ministry of Environment, Forests and Climate Chang e have laid 7 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
63 to 69) on the Table of the House during the Sess ion. Three of them  (Sl. Nos. 63-65) have been 
delayed by 4 months to 8 months and no delay statement is appended to any of these Notifications. 
    
 The Committee have emphasized the timely laying of Notifications on the Table of the 
House in its 131 
st  Report. Further, Committee has emphasized in its 135 th  Report that a delay 
statement should invariably be appended in case of delay in laying of Notifications. Ministry 
of Environment, Forests and Climate Change  have not only delayed the laying of 
Notifications on the Table, but also failed to appe nd a delay statement which is negligence on 
the part of Ministry of Environment, Forests and Cl imate Change  towards adhering to the 
Committee’s recommendations of another takes a serious  view of this negligence.  
  
(vi) Ministry of Finance 
(a)    (Department of Economic Affairs) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affair s) have laid 32 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
71 to 102).  Out of these, 17 Notifications (Sl. No s. 76 – 89, 91 –  92 & 96) have been  delayed by 
5 months to 8 months. There is no delay statement appended to any of these delayed Notifications.  
 
 The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry o f Finance (Department of 
Economic Affairs) have made inordinate delay in lay ing of Notifications. Neither is there a 
delay statement appended therewith to know the reas ons of such delay. The Committee 
would like the Ministry of Finance (Department of E conomic Affairs) to take corrective 
measures so as to curtail delay in future and in ca se such delay does take place it should 
invariably be accompanied by statements giving the reasons for the delay in laying.  
 
(b)    (Department of Financial Services) 
 
 Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Servic es) have laid 10 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 
103 to 112) out of which 2 Notifications (Sl. Nos. 104 – 105) have been delayed by five months 
and there is no delay statement appended to both these Notifications. 
 Another Notification (Sl. No. 109) has been delaye d by six months with delay statement 
appended thereto. The reason for delay given is as under:- 
 
“The Notification regarding amendment in Syndicate Bank Officers’ Service Regulations, 
1979 incorporating the provision of the Joint Note dated 02.06.2005 on pay revision for officers 
was published in the Gazette of India, No. 24 dated  June 14 – June 20, 2014. The Bank has stated 
that they received copy of the Gazette Notification  from Department of Publication late and hence, 
there was delay in submitting the papers in time. W hile regretting the delay, Bank has noted to 
comply with the time schedule in future.” 
 
 

The Committee notes that the Department of Financia l Services (Ministry of Finance) 
have again failed to strictly adhere to the time schedule in laying of Gazette Notifications. The 
Committee recommends that the Department of Financi al Services (Ministry of Finance) 
should put an effective mechanism in place to elimi nate such instances of delay in future, 
particularly, finalization of the work of laying of  paper by the Department of Financial 
Services. The Committee further recommends that the  Ministry of Finance should put in 
place checks and counterchecks so that such delays do not recur in future.

 The Ministry of Finance (Department Revenue) have laid 59 notifications (Sl. Nos. 113 – 
171). Out of these, 7 Notifications  have been  del ayed with delay period of 4 to 6 months and no 
delay statement is attached  with them except with two Notifications (Sl. Nos. 113 – 114 ). The 
reasons forwarded by the Ministry for delay in this case is as under:-  
 
 “The Central Board of Excise & Customs has issued two  notifications G.S.R. 335 (E) and

 The reasons cited by Ministry of Finance (Departmen t of Revenue)  i.e. Notifications 
could not be laid down because the copies of Notifi cations could not be provided by the 
Printing Press in time, is an oft-repeated excuse. It is a Constitutional provision to lay the 
notification within the prescribed time schedule an d the Ministry should have taken 
appropriate action to lay the same in time on the T able of the House. It reflects the

the Ministry to  devise suitable mechanism to avoid  such instances of delay in future,

st  Report.

The reasons for delay  given by the Ministry is  as under:- 
 
“As per instructions of the Committee on Subordinat e Legislation, Recruitment Rules, 
(PRs) are required to be laid on the Table of both the Houses of Parliament within a period of 15 
days after its publication in official gazette, if the Houses are in session and if the Houses are not in 
Session, the same should be laid as soon as possibl e after the commencement of the following 
session. 
The printed copies of the Border Security Force Com batised Assistant Sub Inspector 
(Steno) and Head Constable (Ministerial) Recruitmen t (Amendment) Rules, 2014 (PRs) were

th  August, 2014 and received in this

Secretariat on 14.08.2014 but were not accepted as the session was concluded on that day itself. 
Therefore, these Rules could not be laid on the tab le of both the Houses of Parliament due to 
insufficient time.” 
 
 Although one-eighth of the total Notifications are  delayed, yet the Committee is to see 
that statutory obligation of laying the notifications as the Table of House as prescribed by it is 
fulfilled by the Ministries. It is for the Ministri es to see that this job is performed by them 
without flaw but Ministry of Home Affairs have fail ure on this count. Therefore, Committee 
urges upon the Ministry of Home Affairs to see to i t that hurdles causing delay in laying of 
Notifications, such as conclusion of Parliament Ses sion, should have been handled 
beforehand or preparations for laying made earlier so that the situation could have been 
avoided.

, 135

, 152

 “Orders required to be laid before the House shoul d be laid (i) if the House is not in

 “In addition to the concern expressed by the Commi ttee over delay in laying of

be completed within a period of six months of the c oming into force on an Act. The 
Committee has further recommended that in case a Ministry is not able to adhere to this time 
limit, it should seek extension of time for complet ing the rule-making process. The 
recommendation of the committee reads as follows:- 
 
  “The Committee feels that the rule-making process c ould well have been 
completed within the time limit of six months which  the Committee has laid down for 
making of rules under an Act after coming into forc e of that Act. Where however, a 
Ministry does not find it possible to adhere to the  time limit, they should inform the 
Committee of the reasons why they cannot make rules  within the time limit and seek 
extension of time for completing the rule-making process.” 
 The Committee is constrained to take note of the f act that the Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India are not complying with the C ommittee’s recommendation to 
complete the process of framing of subordinate legi slation contemplated under various Acts 
of Parliament within a period of six months. At the  same time, the Committee also does not 
receive requests for extension of time for completi ng the rule-making process in terms of its 
recommendations as above from most of the Ministrie s/Departments. The Committee takes a 
serious note of the prevailing situation in which t he Ministries/Departments are neither able 
to complete the task of framing rules/regulations u nder different Acts within the given six 
months’ period nor do they suo-motu  approach the Committee for seeking extension of ti me. 
The Committee further notes that in order to ensure  timely framing of rules/regulations it 
has taken an initiative to monitor the progress of framing of subordinate legislation in respect 
of all legislations brought about by different Mini stries/Departments of Government of India 
from the year 2003 onwards with a view to ensure th at the framing of subordinate legislation 
is not delayed. The Committee, however, finds that the responses from the 
Ministries/Departments have not been prompt in this  monitoring exercise undertaken on 
behalf of the Committee. 
  
 In view of the above position, the Committee once again reiterates its recommendation 
that the Ministries/Departments should ensure putti ng in place subordinate legislation 
positively within a period of six months of coming into force of the Act. The Committee 
further recommends that in case the Ministry fails to meet with this deadline, they should 
invariably approach the Committee for seeking exten sion of time. The Committee further 
recommends that in cases where the time limit of si x months is not adhered to, they should, 
while laying rules/regulations before the Parliament invariably lay a statement explaining the 
reasons why the framing of rules/regulations was delayed beyond six months so as to keep the 
Parliament apprised of the compliance of the Parlia ment’s mandate  conferred upon the 
Executive.”
