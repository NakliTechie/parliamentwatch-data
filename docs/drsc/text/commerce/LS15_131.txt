REPORT

Laying of Statutory Orders on the Table of the Rajya Sabha (190"
Session)

It is obligatory on the part of the Ministries/Departments of
Government of India to frame and notify rules, regulations, bye-laws,
schemes or statutory instruments (hereinafter called ‘Order’)
contemplated under various Acts of Parliament under their
administrative control and also to lay the same before both the Houses
of Parliament.

2. The Committee on Subordinate legislation of the Rajya Sabha in
its very First Report which was presented to the House on March 15,
1966 showed serious concern about the timely and proper laying of
various Orders on the Table of the House. In the said Report, the
Committee prescribed the following _ Schedule for — the
Ministries/Departments of Government of India for the purpose of
laying of the Orders:-

“The Committee recommended that rules and orders required to

be laid before the House should be laid,-

(i) Within a period of 15 days after their publication if the
नि Gazette, if the House is in Session; and

(ii) If the House is not then in Session, within a period of
15 days after the commencement of the next ensuing
Session”,



~
3. The Committee did not restrict itself to the timely and proper

laying of the Orders but it also went on to deal with the question of the
timely framing of the rules/regulations contemplated under various
Acts of Parliament. The Committee in its 9" Report (Para-48) which
was presented to the House on March 24, 1971 recommended as

follows:-

“In order that Such long and inordinate delays do not take place
in future, the Committee recommend that rules and regulations
required to be made under a statute should be made as soon as
possible but in no case later than six months from the date on
which the statue comes into force”.

4. The Committee elaborated its recommendation again vide para 56
of its 10" Report which was presented to the House on May 24, 1971

and which reads as under:-

“To avoid delays the Ministries could, to start with, frame broad
but clear rules; they need not wait to frame all the rules on a
particular subject in one go especially when various
authorities/bodies have to be consulted in the matter. Such rule
could be amended or new rules added subsequently on the basis
of experience. It is recognized that rules should be as perfect as
possible, but this perfection should not be at the cost of
expedition. The Ministries should also establish the practice that
if rule are not framed within this period of six months the
Secretary or the Head of the Department at the Secretary’s level
should be informed accordingly who in turn should by means of
a comprehensive note inform the Minister concerned and obtain



E orders thereon. Such a note should invariably refer to thé
fact whether the particular matter had come within the
knowledge or comment of a Parliamentary Committee or not.
The Committee are persuaded to make this suggestion for the
simple reason that for any non-compliance or excessive delay in
compliance; with the provision of a statute the Minister is
answerable to the House. The Committee trust that if such a
course is adopted, the case of such abnormal delays will
definitely lessen”

5. During the 190" Session of the Rajya Sabha, 161 such Orders
were laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha by the various
Ministries/Departments. The details of the Gazette Notifications in
which these were published and the date of their laying on the Table of
the Rajya Sabha have been indicated in the enclosed statement
(Annexure). Out of these 161 Orders, the Committee has identified 9
for detailed scrutiny, The Orders so identified have been indicated in
the statement.

6. During the course of general scrutiny of the Orders, the Committee
noted that in certain cases there has been a considerable time lag
between the date of notification of the Order and the date of laying.
The statement as at Annexure contains a specific Mention of the period
of delay in cases where notifications were not laid on time. In many
cases the delay appears to be abnormal. The Committee regrets to point

out the following specific cases of delay with its observations:-

7. As per the laying provision, which finds place in almost all the
Acts of Parliament wherein Subordinate Legislation is contemplated,
all Orders framed under the Act are required to be laid before both the
llouses of Parliament “as soon as may be after they are made”. 4

Accordingly, the requirement of the law is that such Orders framed

under any Central Act, 'should be laid before the Parliament at the


13

earliest, There is another aspect of the matter viz. that majority of the
Orders issued under various Central Acts come into force from the date
of their publication in the Official Gazette. Keeping all this in view,
the Committee feels that it is essential that the Orders are laid on
the Table of the House in compliance with the provisions of the Act
on the first available opportunity. The Committee in the process has,
however, reviewed its earlier recommendation as contained in its 1"
Report on the subject of laying of Orders on the Table of Rajya Sabha.
The Committee now recommends that Orders required to be laid
before the House should be laid (ji) if the House is not in Session,
during the Session immediately following the date of publication of
the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session,
on the date of the publication of the Order, during its continuance
and in case the time lag between the date of publication and the
date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before
the expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session.
All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should
henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the
orders issued under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the
Rajya Sabha.

As regards the timely framing of the Orders contemplated

under various Central Acts, the Committee reiterates its earlier


14

Ministries/Departments to comply with the procedures as laid
down by the Committee in the matter of framing of various
