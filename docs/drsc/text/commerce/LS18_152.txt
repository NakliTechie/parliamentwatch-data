INTRODUCTION 
         I, the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having been 
authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this 
Hundred and Fifty Second Report of the Committee. 
1.2.   The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on the 25 th 
August, 2004. 
1.3.   The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the Table of the Rajya 
Sabha during its 200th session (November, 2003 - February, 2004) have been perused by 
the Committee in the light of the provisions of the Acts under which these were framed 
and also with a view to seeing whether these have been laid properly and within the 

prescribed time-frame. The Committee's observations/recommendations are contained 
in the report that follows.

August 25th, 2004

2.   During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted 
some cases where there has been a considerable time lag between the date of notification 
of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period 
of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by 
the Committee and whether such notifications were accompanied by a statement 
indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay 
has been quite abnormal. Th e observations/recommendations of the Committee in 
respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows:---- 
(i)    Ministry of Agriculture 
        The Committee notes that the laying of notifications at Sl. Nos.3-12 of the Annexure 
by the Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying) has 
been delayed by 1 year 3 months to 1 year 5 months four of them having been notified on 
the 10th September, 2002 and the rest on the 25th October, 2002. The regulations could 
have easily been laid during the 197 th, 198th or the 199th session of the Rajya Sabha. The 
Ministry has failed even to append the delay statements to the notifications. The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Agriculture (Department of Animal 
Husbandry and Dairying) would in future adhere to the time schedule as 
recommended by it in its 131 st Report in the matter of laying of the Subordinate 
Legislation pertaining to their Department. 
(ii)   Ministry of Civil Aviation 
      The Committee regrets to note that the Ministry of Civil Aviation have 
delayed the laying of 4 of their notifications (Sl. No. 14 -17) out of the 6 
notifications laid by them during the 200th session. The Committee recommends that the 
Ministry of Civil Aviation should stick to the time schedule as recommended by it in its 
131st Report in laying the Subordinate Legislation before the Parliament. 
(iii) Ministry of Finance 
        The Committee is unhappy to note that out of 388 notifications laid  by the Ministry 
of Finance during the 200 th session, there has been a deviation from the given schedule, 
in case of 73 notifications listed at serial numbers 64-65, 73-84, 126-135, 237-283 and 349-
350 of the Annexure. It is a matter of regret that in quit e a few of these cases the 
delay ranges between 6 months and 2 years 9 months. In percentage terms, the 
Committee regrets to note that there has been an increase in the cases of delay in laying 
of rules/regulations. While the percentage of delayed cases wa s around 6% during the 
197th session and 7.43% during 198 th session, it had risen to 15.25% during the 199 th 
session and further to 19% during the 200th session. 
        The Committee further notes that most of the notifications that have been delayed 
pertain to the Banking Division and as can be seen from the statement of reasons for delay 

which have been appended with some of these notifications, the delay has taken place due 
to delayed receipt of the notifications from the Gramin / Regional Banks or from  the 
Government Press. The Committee is unhappy to note that the Ministry of Finance have 
again failed to strictly adhere to the time schedule prescribed in its 135 th Report. The 
Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance would put effective mechanism in place. 
(iv) Ministry of Road Transport & Highways 
The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has 
considerably reduced the delay in the laying of the notifications and mostly the delay 
ranges between 6 -7 months only and  out of the 149 notifications laid by them 
during the 200th session, there is a delay in laying of 10 notifications ( Sl Nos. 500-501 and 
507-514). The Committee is happy to note that the Ministry have appended delay 
statements to all the notifications. The Committee hopes that the Ministry would continue 
with their efforts and would be able to achieve the target of timely laying of all their 
subordinate legislation. 
3.   The Committee notes that there has been a delay in laying of one notification 
each by the Ministries of Culture (Sl. No. 60). Health and Family Welfare (Sl. No. 455) , 
Home Affairs (Sl. No. 462) , Human Resource Development (Sl. No. 470), Labour 
and Employment (Sl. No. 478) , Law and Justice (Sl. No. 488) , Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensio ns (Sl. No. 489) , Shipping (Sl. No. 658) , Textiles (Sl. No. 662)  and 
Urban Development (Sl. No. 666) . The delay in these cases range from  
6 months to 5 years 10 months and in most of the cases the Ministries have not appended 
delay statement along with the notification. The Committee hopes that the above 
Ministries would adhere to the time schedule for laying of the notification and also 
append therewith a delay statement in case of any delay in laying thereof. 
4.   Besides the timely laying of subordinate legislation, the Committee have 
come across instances where some Ministries/Departments have notified their 
rules/regulations bearing identical notification number for different notifications.

5.     In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned a distinctive 
number in order to give it a separate identity. Similarly, no notification should 
be published/laid, which does not carry a notification number. The Committee 
expects all Ministries/Departments of Government of India to exercise caution in this  
regard. In case where there are more than one notification under a single file, the 
Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number on each notification 
should be suitably distinguished from one another. The Committee also impresses 
upon the Ministry of Urban Development to take care of these aspects and ensure 
that all rules/regulations published by them in the Gazette of India do carry distinct 
notification/file numbers so that they are identifiable at later stage. 

6.     The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate 
steps to avoid delay in laying of Subordinate Legislation. The Committee, 
accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the 
Government of Ind ia, particularly, the defaulting ones, the following 
recommendations, as contained in its 131th and 135th Reports: 
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not in 
Session, during the Session immediately following the d ate of publication of the 
Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, on the date of the 
publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag 
between the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Ses sion is less 
than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the said 
Session. All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth 
comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the orders issued under v arious 
Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 
“The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there 
is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department 
should invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so 
as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that through the 
process of formulation  of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, 
identifying bottlenecks and devise remedial measures”. 
(135th Report)

3.     The Committee then took up for consideration its agenda starting with the draft 
152nd Report on Statutory Orders laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during the 200 th 
Session. During the course of interaction, it was observed that the number of cases of 
delay in the laying of notifications by some of the Ministries/Departments was quite 
substantial and that there was a considerable scope for improvement. In this context, the 
Committee, however, noted that the practice of reporting to the House on the laying of 
Subordinate Legislation on the Table of the House on a sessional basis was not very old 
and that ever since the introduction of this practice, there has been considerable 
improvement both with regard to the timely laying as well as the format of the 
notifications. The Committee further noted that it had, in the past already, heard the 
Secretaries of the Mi nistries of Finance, Industry and Home Affairs on the question of 
delay in the laying of various rules/regulations pertaining to their Ministries. The 
Committee felt that there was still a substantial delay in laying of rules/regulations in a 
large number of cases and desired that an exercise may be undertaken to identify the 
defaulting Ministries/ Departments so that the matter could be taken up with them. With 
these observations, the Committee adopted the draft 152 nd Report and decided that the 
same be presented to the House on the 26 th August 2004. The Committee authorized its 
Chairman and in his absence Prof. R.B.S. Varma and Shri R.S. Gavai, Members to 
present the Report. The Committee deferred consideration of its draft 153 rd and 154 th 
Reports.
