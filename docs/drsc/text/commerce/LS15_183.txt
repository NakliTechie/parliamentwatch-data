Report on 
The Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 2006  
Originally introduced in 1995, the Banking Ombudsma n Scheme enables speedy and 
cost effective resolution of complaints of bank cus tomers relating to deficiency in 
bank services. The Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 2006 ( Annexure I ) was framed by 
the Reserve Bank of India in exercise of power conf erred by Section 35A of  the 
Banking Regulation Act, 1949. The Scheme was introd uced with the object of 
enabling resolution of complaints relating to certain services rendered by banks and to 
facilitate the satisfaction or settlement of such complaints. The Reserve Bank of India 
is mandated to ensure that all commercial banks, pr ivate banks, regional rural banks, 
and scheduled primary co-operative banks shall comp ly with the Banking 
Ombudsman Scheme, 2006.  The Scheme provides appoin tment of Chief General 
Manager or General Manager of the RBI as Banking  O mbudsmen to be located at 
places identified by the it. Chapter III of the Sch eme defines jurisdiction, powers and 
duties of Banking Ombudsmen. Chapter IV prescribes the procedure for redressal of 
grievances, grounds of complaints, settlement of co mplaints by agreement or award 
by the Banking Ombudsman. The Chapter also gives th e grounds of rejection of 
complaints and appeal against the award given by Ba nking Ombudsman before the 
Appellate Authority.    
1.  The customers can also now complain to the Banking Ombudsman against deficiency 
in almost any banking services (Annexure-II), including credit cards, after 
exhausting the channel available with the bank conc erned for resolving their 
complaints. The Banking Ombudsman tries to resolve the complaint through 
conciliation or mediation and even passes an award if it is not resolved through such 
settlement.   
2.  It was taken up for detailed examination by the Com mittee on Subordinate 
Legislation, Rajya Sabha due to the importance of t he Scheme for general public,. 
Under the Scheme, Ombudsman offices are set up at 1 5 places in different parts of 
country mainly in the State capitals ( Annexure III). Any  person who has grievance 
against a bank on any of the grounds listed in the Scheme can lodge a complaint to 

the Banking Ombudsman (BO) within whose jurisdictio n the branch or the office of 
the bank complained against is located. If the comp lainant is not satisfied with the 
award given by the BO, he can approach the Appellat e Authority for the redressal of 
his grievances.  
3.  The Scheme was further amended in May 2007 (Annexure IV). The Reserve Bank of 
India amended the Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 2006 to  enable the customers to 
appeal against the Banking Ombudsman's decision. Be fore the scheme was amended, 
the bank customers could appeal only against the aw ards given by the Banking 
Ombudsman. The appellate authority for the Banking Ombudsman Scheme is the 
Deputy Governor of Reserve Bank of India.

4.  The Committee observed that the eligibility, appoin tment and tenure of the 
Appellate Authority has not been given in the Scheme. Only the definitions given 
under Clause 3 (2) of Chapter I states that Appella te Authority meant the 
Deputy Governor in charge of the Department of Rese rve Bank implementing 
the Scheme. The Committee considers the appointment  of Appellate Authority 
was a substantive enabling provision and feels that  it should have been clearly 
mentioned as a separate Clause in Chapter IV instea d of   being included in the 
definitions. The Committee finds such omission unfortunate.

10.  The Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Aff airs) on 24 th  July, 2007  
forwarded the views of Reserve Bank of India on the  following two specific 
issues related to the Banking Ombudsman Scheme : 
 
(i)  Reasons for not appointing senior officers from scheduled commercial 
banks who are well-versed with operations of bankin g industry as Banking 
Ombudsman . 
 
The issue was examined during the recent revision o f the Banking Ombudsman 
Scheme. It was considered that as the Banking Ombudsman received complaints 
against various banks for the deficiency in banking  services, appointing serving 
senior officer from the banking sector may create a conflict of interest. 
 
Further, the Banking Ombudsmen appointed in recent past are senior officers 
(in the rank of General Manager/Chief General Manag er) with an experience of 
around 20 to 25 years in supervision/regulatory dep artments in Reserve Bank. 
The arrangement is working satisfactorily and there are no complaints regarding 
role/functioning of the Banking Ombudsman from any quarter. 
 

Under the Banking Ombudsman Scheme 2006, RBI office rs with 20 years 
working experience and having exposure to banking o perations and supervision 
are posted as Banking Ombudsman.  The experience is  that considerable 
advantage arises out of such appointments as such o fficers are able to resolve 
complaints through mediation more efficiently as ba nks against whom the 
complaints are made view this as intervention by th e regulator and usually 
redress the grievance . 
 
(ii) Rationale behind having Banking Ombudsman and Appellate Authority 
from same organization . 
 
The Banking Ombudsman Scheme is formulated, monitored, run and supervised 
by Reserve Bank since its inception in 1995. The Ba nking Ombudsman is 
treated as a separate and independent entity from R eserve Bank and there is no 
interference from Reserve Bank in the decision maki ng process by Banking 
Ombudsmen in the individual cases. Reserve Bank’s r ole is restricted to 
issuance of guidelines on the running of the scheme. 
 
Further, the Appellate Authority is Deputy Governor  of Reserve Bank. 
Whenever an appeal is filed with the Appellate Auth ority, complete position of 
the complaint is seen. The decision of the Appellat e Authority is generally 
arrived at after taking the views of the complainant, bank, Banking Ombudsman 
and that of the Legal Department of the Reserve Ban k. It is, therefore, opined 
that having Banking Ombudsman and Appellate Authori ty from Reserve Bank 
itself is fully logical and justified.

with the reply of the RBI over the pendency and not es with deep anguish that 
the Ombudsman infrastructure is not adequate and re commends to the 
Government to improve the situation at the earliest. 
 
(B) Rejection of complaints is another major concer n with about 49% of complaints 
rejected. 
 
The actual percentage of complaints that were rejected was much lower as a sample 
study of the rejected complaints showed that 42% of  such complaints were outside 
the purview of the scheme, 23% were first resort co mplaints and 24% could not be 
considered for reasons like they were from unrelate d complainants or they required 
elaborate documentary and oral evidence or they wer e pending in other fora etc.  
Thus, effectively out of the total cases rejected, redressal could not be done only in 
11% cases as deficiency of service could not be established in these cases. 
 
(C) Web based mechanism may be set up for prompt/tr ansparent redressal of 
complaints under the scheme. 
 
A Complaint Tracking Software has already been oper ational for facilitating the 
complaint redressal function of the Banking Ombudsm an Offices. The software 
facilitates easy access to complete information abo ut the complaints, correspondence 
with the banks 
. 
 
(D) Awards involving small amounts say up to Rs.500 0/- may be kept out of the 
purview of the appeal to the Appellate Authority for speedier redressal. 
 
The suggestion is acceptable, though the experience is that no such appeals have been 
received from the banks.  However, the complainants  may continue to be allowed 
to appeal regardless of the amount

( E) The number of officers of the Ombudsman may be  suitably increased from the 
existing strength of 15 to improve the geographical  reach for the customers.  
Setting up of sub offices at smaller/remote centers can also be examined. 
 
The existing number of Banking Ombudsman offices at  15 does not appear to be a 
constraint in terms of geographical reach, particul arly as the availability of Banking 
Ombudsman Scheme is well advertised by both Banking Ombudsman (BO) as well as 
banks.  A BO could be approached even through an in expensive mode like post card.  
Moreover, BO conducts awareness campaigns in distri ct and other places in his area 
and even at times holds open meetings with bank cus tomers and others at such places 
to generate awareness of the scheme.  Sometimes eve n conciliation meetings are held 
at such places so that the complainant is not put t o any inconvenience.  Further, 
conciliation meetings are not compulsory and compla ints can be resolved by 
mediation through other modes like correspondence w ith the bank and the 
complainant 
 
The Committee does not agree with the RBI on the is sue of adequacy of the BOs 
and it feels that only 15 BOs all over the country are not adequate to handle 
huge amount of pending cases. The Committee recomme nds that RBI should 
appoint adequate number of BOs at the earliest.   
 
(F) The Scheme should be publicized by the banks in  both English and 
vernacular/local languages. 
 
The Banking Ombudsman Scheme booklet is published b y RBI in English and Hindi.  
In some states, it is publicized in local languages.  The suggestion is noted and will be 
implemented in all regions. The Ministry of Finance  concurs with the comments of 
the RBI. 
 

The Committee appreciates that the RBI is in agreem ent with some of the above 
suggestions. And accordingly recommends that the RB I should implement such 
suggestions expeditiously.

12.  The Committee noted with reservation that majority of the complaints were 
against the private banks followed by SBI group. It  also observed that the number of 
e-mail and online complaints are on the rise. 40% o f the complaints received by 
Ombudsman are first resort complaints meaning that the customer directly approach 
Ombudsman without first going to the bank for redre ssal of his grievance (s). The 
Committee was informed that most of the cases went in favour of the banks. 
 
13.  The Committee was  apprehensive that the pattern of  case being decided in 
favour of Banks  could be because of the resources and institutional support 
which the Banks  had to present their cases before the Ombudsman, while 
individual customers, especially the poor, did not have adequate means to 
present their cases and were at a disadvantage. 
 
14.  The Committee was informed that complaints were ver y few against the cooperative 
banks. 
 
15.  The Committee was of the view that lower numbers of  complaints against 
cooperative banks could be because the customers we re not aware of the 
scheme. The Committee reiterated the need for incre asing awareness of the 

scheme among the bank customers. The Committee felt  that there is a need 
to senisitize the bank staff towards the needs of the customers. 
 
Observations and recommendations of the Committee  
 
16.  The Committee considers that there is proliferation  of NBFCs and Private 
Financing Companies often approved by the RBI. As s uch, the Committee 
recommends that definition of Banks as given in Cla use 3(5) of Chapter I 
should be amended to include NBFCs, Private Financi ng Companies 
approved by the RBI and the Private Companies accepting the term deposits. 
 
17.  Regarding Clause 4(1) of Chapter II, the Committee considers that in 
addition to the CGM and GM of the RBI, retired Bank  officers not below the 
rank of General Manager who are conversant with customer relations should 
also be considered for Banking Ombudsman. In this r espect the Committee 
disagrees with RBI’s reply that appointment the sen ior Bank officer as 
Banking Ombudsman may result in conflict of interests.  
 
18.  Regarding Clause 7(1) of Chapter III on the jurisdi ction, the Committee 
considers that often the case may involve more than  one Bank located in 
different territorial jurisdiction. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the above Clause read with Clause 15 (3) must be accordingly elaborated. 
 
19.  Regarding Clause 7(5), the Committee recommends tha t the annual Report 
submitted by the Banking Ombudsman to RBI should ou tline the 
shortcomings and contain recommendations for struct ural modifications in 
Banking.  
 
20.  The Committee considers that the grounds for redres sal of grievances as 
detailed in Clause 8 of Chapter IV pertain largely to retail Banking services. 
The Committee feels that Credit card has rapidly em erged as a distinct 
business. Keeping in view the large number of compl aints and litigations 

pertaining to credit card business, the Committee f eels that regulation is 
necessary for the healthy growth of this Sector. It  is necessary that there 
should be transparency about the interest rates, du es and compounding of 
such dues. The obligations of the Banks providing t he credit card services 
should be clearly defined.  The Committee recognize s the steps taken by the 
Banks in educating the customers at different stage s, however, the terms and 
conditions are not adequately and distinctly inform ed to the customers at the 
time he seeks a credit card from the Bank. Often th e credit card marketing 
business is outsourced to marketing agencies, who o ften do not adhere to 
standard fair practices. It is, therefore, realisti cally assumed that the 
unsuspecting customers need education even before a  credit card is issued. 
The Committee also notes that the credit card busin ess is mostly outsourced 
by the Banks, thereby resulting in complete loss of  interface between the 
banks and the customer. Such an arrangement also ma kes it difficult to fix 
the responsibilities.  Also, while subscribing a cr edit card has been made 
much convenient by marketing agencies, discontinuation/cancellation of card 
is much more complex and difficult. Therefore, the Banking Ombudsman 
should clearly prescribe rules for outsourcing the services in respect of credit 
card business and the eventual obligations towards the redressal of 
complaints. The Committee acknowledges that the RBI  has issued guidelines 
in respect of credit card business.  Such guideline s should be incorporated as 
a separate Clause in the Chapter IV as the ground f or redressal of 
grievances. The Committee also feels that in view o f growing complaints 
against the credit card dues, the Committee conside rs that respective banks 
should have a separate grievance redressal cell and  that the RBI should 
consider appointing dedicated Ombudsman in existing  locations to deal with 
Credit card issues in line with RBI guidelines.  
 
21.  Clause 8 (2) of Chapter IV also provides deficienci es in servicing loans and 
advances as the ground for redressal. The Committee  has noted that such a 
clause is an acknowledgement by the RBI that despit e Government’s 
guarantees and refinancing facilities, there are st ill complaints about Banks 

being unwilling to give loans under the Government- schemes. The 
Committee recommends that the Banking Ombudsman sho uld specially 
monitor the bank lendings to marginalised sections and farmers under 
Government schemes. The Committee reiterates that t hese Schemes must be 
publicized by the Banks. The Committee recommends t hat RBI should make 
survey of the expenditures made by the Banks on pro moting/publicizing the 
personal loans vis a vis   on Government schemes meant for marginalized 
sections. Such a survey should illustrate, if the b anks are more inclined to 
promote commercial personal loans instead of Govern ment schemes. The 
Committee strongly feels that Banking Ombudsman sho uld conduct a survey 
of the procedural resistance faced by the potential  beneficiaries in availing 
the schemes and make commensurate recommendations. The Committee 
feels that advances and loans given under Governmen t’s social sector 
schemes should a dealt by the Banking Ombudsman as a separate class of 
cases and should be accorded special provisions. Th erefore, the Clause 8 
should accordingly be amended.  
 
22.  Clause 8 ( 2) refers to non-observance of RBI direc tive on interest rates as 
one of the ground for redressal. The Committee find s that in most of the 
cases the interest rates are benchmarked with PLR. However, the calculation 
of PLR remains opaque and the Bank PLR rates are no t adequately 
publicized. This is particularly vital for the Gove rnment schemes that 
provide for Bank loans at concessional or susidised  rates below the PLR. As 
such, the potential beneficiaries are denied the be nefit of the Scheme. The 
Committee, therefore,  recommends that RBI and Ombu dsman should 
ensure that the Banks while publicizing the Governm ent schemes should 
specifically mention the interest rate applicable t o the scheme. The 
Ombudsman should ensure the clarity and transparenc y in interest rates 
applicable to Government schemes.  
 
23.  The Clause 9 (1) prevents a customer from represent ing his case through a 
lawyer. The Committee failed to appreciate the rati onale behind such 

disabling provision and wondered if there was a mec hanism to identify such 
distinction. The Clause though does not impose any restriction on the Bank. 
The Committee finds that on the one hand, the custo mer is required to 
represent his own case or through a representative on the other hand the 
Banks’ case will be represented by their legal cell .  The Committee is of the 
opinion that an individual customer on its own woul d always find it difficult 
to match with the resources and institutional suppo rt which the Banks have. 
These apprehensions have been duly illustrated in t he admission that most of 
the cases filed with Banking Ombudsman went in favo ur of Banks. The 
Committee also recognized the fact that a large num ber of cases were 
rejected by the Banking Ombudsman as they were firs t resort complaints 
without exploring the redressal through the grievan ce redressal of 
mechanism of the concerned Bank. The Committee cons iders that instead of 
rejecting, such cases should be taken as an illustr ation of lack of faith in the 
grievance redressal mechanism of the Banks and such  cases must be referred 
by the Ombudsman to the concerned Bank for redressa l instead of rejecting 
them outright.  The Ombudsman which rely on the rec ords, should also 
survey the grievance redressal mechanism of the Ban ks and the grounds of 
rejections at that level. The Banking Ombudsman sho uld report to the RBI 
the functioning of grievance redressal mechanisms adopted by the Banks and 
increasing number of cases resorting to appeal to t he Ombudsman against 
the Banks. The Committee, therefore, recommends tha t the Clause 9(1) 
should be accordingly amended to allow an advocate to represent the case.  
 
24.  In This respect the Committee considers it relevant  to mention that only a 
few cases against cooperative banks and RRBs have r eached the Banking 
Ombudsman. The Committee apprehends that this could  be due to lack of 
awareness or apathy among the customers towards gri evance redressal 
mechanism. The Committee, therefore, recommends tha t the Banking 
Ombudsman Scheme should be widely publicized in rur al and semi-rural 
areas. It is incumbent upon the Banking Ombudsman a s the forum of first 
appeal to ensure that the RRBs and the Cooperative banks should widely 

publicise their grievance redressal mechanism in lo cal languages. The 
Committee also requires that the Ombudsman should r eflect in their report 
to the RBI, on the issue of grievance redressal mec hanism in RRBs and 
Cooperative banks. The Committee also recommends th at the grievance 
redressal mechanism in RRBs should be decentralized  and should be 
independent of the sponsor-nationalised Bank.  
 
25.  Regarding Clause 9 (3) (c) the Committee recommends  that the rulings given 
by the Banking Ombudsman should be compiled and pub lished by the RBI 
for larger dissemination.  
 
26.  In conclusion, the Committee feels that the Banking  Ombudsman scheme 
holds immense promise for the redressal of customer  grievances. With the 
emergence of newer banking products and with Banks becoming the primary 
lending institutions alongwith other credit institu tions like NBFCs, the 
grievances too are likely to multiply. Therefore, t he mandate of the Banking 
Ombudsman too should expand accordingly. The Commit tee, therefore, 
recommends that number and locations of Banking Omb udsman should be 
increased and new banking services and credit institutions should be brought 
under their purview. The Committee recommends that the Banking 
Ombudsman should incorporate elaborate recommendati ons in their annual 
Reports to the RBI and the RBI should regularly rev iew the its Guidelines in 
light of such recommendations.  
 
27.  Appreciating that the RBI has agreed to several sug gestions, the Committee 
expects that those suggestions shall be implemented  expeditiously and also 
recommends that the current Banking Ombudsman Schem e 2006 should be 
reviewed with reference to its recommendations.

Annex  
Amendments to the Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 2006  
1. In the Banking Ombudsman Scheme, 2006 (hereinafter referred to as the ‘principle scheme’), 
clause 13 shall be substituted by the following, namely:- 
"13. REJECTION OF THE COMPLAINT   
The Banking Ombudsman may reject a complaint at any stage if it appears to him that the 
complaint made is;  
(a) not on the grounds of complaint referred to in clause 8 or 
(b) beyond the pecuniary jurisdiction of Banking Ombudsman prescribed under clause 12 (5) or  
(c) frivolous, vexatious, malafide; or 
(d) without any sufficient cause; or 
(e) that it is not pursued by the complainant with reasonable diligence; or 

(f) in the opinion of the Banking Ombudsman there is no loss or damage or inconvenience 
caused to the complainant; or 
(g) requiring consideration of elaborate documentary and oral evidence and the proceedings 
before the Banking Ombudsman are not appropriate for adjudication of such complaint. "   
2. In the principal scheme, in clause 13, sub. clause (2) shall be omitted.  
3. In the principal scheme, in clause 14, sub. clause (1) shall be substituted by the following, 
namely :-  
"(1) Any person aggrieved by an Award under clause 12 or rejection of a complaint for the 
reasons referred to in sub. clauses (c) to (g) of clause 13, may within 30 days of the date of 
receipt of communication of Award or rejection of complaint, prefer an appeal before the 
Appellate Authority ; 
Provided that in case of appeal by a bank, the period of thirty days for filing an appeal shall 
commence from the date on which the bank receives letter of acceptance of Award by 
complainant under sub. clause (8) of clause 12;  
Provided that the Appellate Authority may, if he is satisfied that the applicant had sufficient 
cause for not making the appeal within time, allow a further period not exceeding 30 days;  
Provided further that appeal may be filed by a bank only with the previous sanction of the 
Chairman or, in his absence, the Managing Director or the Executive Director or the Chief 
Executive Officer or any other officer of equal rank."  
4. In the principal scheme, in clause 12, sub. clause 8 shall be substituted by the following, 
namely :-  
"(8) An award shall lapse and be of no effect unless the complainant furnishes to the bank 
concerned within a period of 30 days from the date of receipt of copy of the Award, a letter of 
acceptance of the Award in full and final settlement of his claim.  
Provided that no such acceptance may be furnished by the complainant if he has filed an 
appeal under sub. clause (1) of clause 14."  
5. In the principal scheme, in clause 12, sub. clause (9) shall be substituted by the following, 
namely, :- 
"(9) The bank shall, unless it has preferred an appeal under sub. clause (1) of clause 14, within 
one month from the date of receipt by it of the acceptance in writing of the Award by the 
complainant under sub-clause (8), comply with the Award and intimate compliance to the 
Banking Ombudsman."  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

(Annexure-IV)  
 
The grounds of deficiency in banking service had be en enumerated in the Scheme. 
The grounds include: 
i.  non-payment or inordinate delay in the payment or collection of cheques, drafts, bills 
etc.; 
ii. Non-acceptance, without sufficient cause, of sm all denomination notes tendered for 
any purpose, and for charging of commission for rendering this service; 
iii. Non-payment or delay in payment of inward remittances; 
iv. Failure to issue or delay in issue, of drafts, pay orders or bankers’ cheques; 
v. Non-adherence to prescribed working hours; 
vi. Failure to honour guarantee or letter of credit commitment; 
vii. Failure to provide or delay in providing a ban king facility (other than loans and 
advances) promised in writing by a bank or its direct selling agents; 
viii. Delays, non-credit of proceeds to parties’ ac counts, non-payment of deposit or non- 
observance of the Reserve Bank directives, if any, applicable to rate of interest on 
deposits in any savings, current or other account maintained with a bank; 
ix. Delays in receipt of export proceeds, handling of export bills, collection of bills etc., 
for exporters provided the said complaints pertain to the bank’s operations in India; 
x. Complaints from Non-Resident Indians having accounts in India in relation to their 
remittances from abroad, deposits and other bank-related matters; 
xi. Refusal to open deposit accounts without any valid reason for refusal; 
xii. Levying of charges without adequate prior notice to the customer;  
xiii. Non-adherence by the bank or its subsidiaries  to the instructions of Reserve Bank on 
ATM/Debit card operations or credit card operations; 
xiv. Non-disbursement or delay in disbursement of p ension (to the extent the grievance 
can be attributed to the action on the part of the bank concerned, but not with regard 
to its employees; 
xv. Refusal to accept or delay in accepting payment  towards taxes, as required by  
Reserve Bank/Government; 
xvi. Refusal to issue or delay in issuing, or failu re to service or delay in servicing or 
redemption of government securities;  
xvii. Forced closure of deposit accounts without due notice or without sufficient reason; 
xviii. Refusal to close or delay in closing the accounts; 
xiv. Non-adherence to the fair practices code as ad opted by the bank; and any other 
matter relating to the violation of the directives issued by the Reserve Bank in relation 
to banking or other service. 
Complaints concerning loans and advances only in so  far as they relate to the following 
may also be filed with the Banking Ombudsman 
i. Non-observance of RBI directive on interest rates. 
ii. Delays in sanction, disbursement or non-observa nce or prescribed time schedule for 
disposal of loan applications;  
iii. Non-acceptance of application for loans withou t furnishing valid reasons to the 
applicant and non-observance of any other direction s or instructions of the RBI from 
time to time.
