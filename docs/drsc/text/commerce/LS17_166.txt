behalf, do hereby present this Hundred and Sixty-Sixth Report of the 
Committee. 
.

held on  the 5th December, 2006. 
  
1.3       The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc.  were laid on

During the course of scrutiny of these Orders, the Committee has noted 
some cases where there has been a considerable time lag between the date  
  

of notification of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed 
statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications 
have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by the Committee and 
whether such notifications were accompanied by a statement indicating 
the  
reasons for the delay in laying. In some of the cases the delay has been 
quite abnormal. The observations/recommendations of the Committee in 
respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows:-    
             
(i) Ministry of  Environment & Forests 
  
            The Committee notes with regret that out of the four notifications 
laid by the Ministry of Environment & Forests during the 208th Session, 
there has been delay in case of  one (Sl. nos.17) and no delay statement has 
been appended with it.   The Committee would like the Ministry to take 
corrective measures so as to obviate such delay in future.  
  
(ii) Ministry of Finance  
     (Department of Economic Affairs) 
  
            The Committee notes that the Ministry of Finance (Department of 
Economic Affairs) have delayed the laying of four notifications (Sl.Nos. 
27,28,68&69) out of 57 notifications laid by them during the session. In all 
these four cases the Ministry have failed to append the delay 
statement.  The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Finance would 
continue with their efforts to avoid delay in laying of the notifications and 
in case of any delay actually taking place in laying, a delay statement 
would invariably be appended. 
(iii) Ministry of Finance  
      (Department of  Revenue) 
  
            The Committee regrets to note that there is a delay of 6 months to 
11 months in laying of two notifications out of the 105 notifications laid by 
the Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) (Sl.Nos. 174 & 175) and 
the Ministry have not appended delay statement.  The Committee hopes 
that the Ministry would take necessary corrective steps in the matter. 
  
(iv)  Ministry of Home Affairs 
  
            The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the 5 notifications 
laid by the Ministry of Home Affairs there has been delay upto 10 months 
in the laying of three notifications ( Sl.Nos.189 to 191).  The Ministry have 
not appended delay statement with any of these delayed notifications 
either.  The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Home Affairs 
should stick to the time schedule as recommended by it in its 131st Report 
in the matter of laying of Subordinate Legislation before Parliament and 
in case of any delay, the delay statement must also be appended so as to 
apprise the Parliament of the reasons that caused the delay. 

  
(v) Ministry of Labour & Employment  
  
            The  Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Labour & 
Employment have delayed the laying of one notification (Sl No. 201) out of 
2 laid by them and have not appended reason for delay with it.  The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry should make efforts to adhere 
to the time schedule prescribed for this purpose by the Committee and in 
case there is delay, a statement containing the reasons for such delay is 
also laid along with the notification. 
  
(vi) Ministry of Law & Justice  
     (Department of Legal Affairs) 
  
            The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of 
seven months on the part of the Ministry of Law and Justice in laying 
in  case of one of the two notifications laid by them (Sl. No.203). The 
Ministry have appended delay statement with the Notification and the 
reason given by the Ministry reads:-“ Writ Petitions were filed in various 
High Courts in January,2006 itself.  Accordingly, a Transfer Petition No. 
92/2006 was filed in the Hon’ble Supreme Court on 2nd February, 2006, 
Subsequently, some more Writ Petitions have been filed in different High 
Courts.  The Transfer Petition was mentioned in the Hon’ble Supreme 
Court on 18th July, 2006.  On a query. the Hon’ble Court was informed 
that the service has been done on the opposite parties, decision was 
expected from the Hon’ble Supreme Court earlier.  However, now it is 
likely to be decided shortly.  Due to this, the Rules could not be laid in the 
Budget Session of Parliament”.   
            The Committee notes the   reasons furnished by the Ministry for 
delay in laying in the delay statement The Committee hopes that the 
Ministry would take care to lay their rules/regulations on time in future. 
  
(vii) Ministry of Panchayati Raj and Youth Affairs & Sports 
  
            The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry have delayed 
the laying of all the four notifications (Sl.Nos. 205 to 208 )  by them. The 
Minsitry have appended delay statement with all the Notifications, the 
reasons given by the Ministry reads.- “The Andman & Nicobar 
Administration had notified (i) The Andaman & Nicobar Islands Gram 
Panchayats (Levy of Taxes and Fees) Rules, 2002, (ii) The Andman & 
Nicobar Islands Panchayats Samitis (Levy, Assessment, Collection and 
Appropriation of Taxes, Duties, Cess, Tolls and Fees) Rules, 2002, (iii) The 
Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Grant-in-aid to Panchayati Raj Institutions) 
(3rd Amendment) Rules, 2003, (iv) The Andaman & Nicobar Islands 
(Panchayats Administration) (Amendment) Rules, 2003 on 24 April, 2002 
and 21 August, 2003.      The Andaman & Nicobar Administration had 
forwarded copies of these rules to the Ministry of Home Affairs during 
February, 2003 as soon as copies of the notifications were available after 

publication.  The Ministry of Home Affairs forwarded these Rules to 
Ministry of Rural Development during May, 2003. Hindi version of the 
Notifications mentioned above could not be sent by the Andaman & 
Nicobar Administration at that time as the same were pending with the 
Central Translation Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi.  On 
receipt of Hindi versions of the Rules, 100 copies were forwarded to the 
Ministry of Home Affairs. The Ministry further have stated that in a 
request of this Ministry for sending 40 copies of each of the Rules, the 
Andaman & Nicobar Administration got them re-printed and forwarded 
copies to the Ministry of Rural development on 12/3/2006, as sufficient 
copies were not available at that time.  The delay in sending the copies 
from November 2005 to February, 2006 was due to the delay in getting the 
copies printed."   
 The Committee feels that the cause of the delay in laying of the Rules” is 
not justified as the same could have been pre-empted by sufficient copies 
printed in time. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of 
Panchayati Raj and Youth Affairs & Sports to take immediate steps to 
prevent such instances of delay in the laying of their subordinate 
legislation in future.  
  
(viii) Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions 
        (Department of Personnel & Training) 
  
The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel, Public 
Grievances & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training) have laid 
07 notifications during the Session out of which 03 notifications (Sl No. 
217-219) have been delayed.  Notifications have been delayed upto 11 
months. A delay statement has been appended with three notifications, 
reads:- “ Due to delay in receipt of printed copies of notification from 
Government of India Press and other administrative reason, the laying of 
the notification has been delayed”. The Committee is not convinced from 
the statement giving reason for delay laid by the Ministry. The Committee 
recommends that the Ministry should make all efforts to avoid such delays 
in the laying of the notifications. 
  
(ix)       Ministry of Shipping, Road Transport & Highways  
      (Department of Road Transport & Highways) 
  
The Committee regrets to note that out of a total of 247 Notifications laid 
by the Department of Road Transport & Highways of the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport & Highways, there has been  delay in the laying 
of only one notification (Sl No. 387) by 11 months. A delay statement has 
been appended with all the notifications, reads:- “It took time to collect 
the papers and complete the procedural formalities.  As such the 
notifications could not be laid on the Table of both Houses of Parliament 
during the last session.”   The Committee expects the Ministry of 
Shipping, Road Transport & Highways to exercise caution in the matter 

of timely laying of subordinate legislation and ensure that such delays do 
not recur.  
  
3.         The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to 
take immediate steps to ensure timely and proper laying of subordinate 
legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the 
defaulting ones, the following recommendations, as contained in its 
131st,135th  and 152nd Reports: 
            “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if 
the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following the 
date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the 
House is in Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, during its 
continuance and in case the time lag between the date of publication and 
the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the 
expiry of the Session immediately following the said Session. All the 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India should henceforth 
comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued 
under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.” 
(131st Report) 
            “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential 
that in case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the 
concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay 
statement along with the notifications being laid so as to apprise 
Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that 
through the process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is 
able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial measures”. 
(135th Report) 
            “In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be 
assigned a distinctive number in order to give it a separate identity and 
similarly, no notification should be published/laid which does not carry a 
notification number. The Committee expects all Ministries/Departments 
of Government of India to exercise caution in this regard. In case where 
there are more than one notification under a single file, the 
Ministries/Departments should ensure that the file number on each 
notification should be suitably distinguished from one another. 
(152nd Report)
