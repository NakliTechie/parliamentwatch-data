INTRODUCTION 
         I, the Chairman of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, having been 
authorized by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, do hereby present this 
One Hundred and fifty fifth Report of the Committee. 
1.2    The Committee considered and adopted the Report in its meeting held on the 17th 
March, 2005. 
1.3    The notifications containing Rules/Regulations, etc. laid on the Table of the Rajya 
Sabha during its 202nd session (July --- August, 2004) have  been perused by the 
Committee in the light of the provisions of the Acts under which these were framed and 
also with a view to seeing whether these have been laid properly and within the prescribed 
time-frame. The Committee's observations/recommendations are contained in the report 
that follows.

2.     During the course of scrutiny of these Notifications/Orders, the Committee has noted 
some cases of abnormal delay ranging from 2 to 6 years between the date of notification 
of the Order and the date of its laying. The annexed statement also indicates the period 
of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the schedule prescribed by 
the Committee and also whether such notifications were accompanied by any statement 
indicating the reasons for the delay in laying. The observations/recommendations of the 
Committee in respect of the specific Ministries/Departments are as follows:---- 
(i)     Ministry of Environment & Forests 
        The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Environment and Forests have 
delayed the laying of 6 of their notifications (Sl. No. 35 -37 and 41 -43) out of the 17 
notification laid by them during the 202 nd Session. The delay ranges between 10 months 
and 1 year 4 months. Further, the Ministry have appended the delay statement with 3 
notifications (Sl. No. 41 -43) only and the re maining 3 notifications (Sl. No. 35 -37) were 
not accompanied by delay statements. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of 
Environment & Forests should strictly adhere to the time schedule as recommended by 
it in its 131 st Report in laying of the Subo rdinate Legislation before Parliament and in 
case of any delay in laying of the notifications, a delay statement should also invariably 
be appended therewith. 
(ii)    Ministry of Finance 
        The Committee is unhappy to note that out of the 320 notifica tions laid by the 
Ministry of Finance during the 202 nd Session, there has been a delay in laying of 27 
notifications listed at Sl. Nos. 51-55, 131-138, 139, 140-143, 144, 145-146, 147, 148-151 and 
152 of the Annexure. The period of delay range from a minim um of 9 months to the 
maximum of 3 years and 6 months. The Committee views with serious concern that in 
spite of its repeated recommendations regarding the timely laying of subordinate 
legislation, there has not been any appreciable improvement in the position. 

        The Committee further notes that all the delayed notifications pertain to the 
Department of Economic Affairs (Banking Division). The reasons for delay which have 
been appended with some of these notifications, such as, “copies of the notifications were 
not received in time” are also not plausible enough to justify the delay that range between 
1-3 years. The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Finance have again failed 
to strictly adhere to the time schedule as prescribed in its 13 1st Report. The Committee 
hopes that the Ministry of Finance would put an effective mechanism in place to eliminate 
such instances of delay in future, particularly on account of delay in receiving printed 
copies of the notifications. The Committee hopes th at the Ministry of Finance would be 
able to plug the loopholes so that delays do not recur in future. 
(iii)   Ministry of Information and Broadcasting 
        The Committee notes that the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting laid two 
notifications (Sl.  Nos. 392 and 393) and both were delayed by more than one year. 
Further, the Ministry did not append any delay statement with these notifications. The 
Committee recommends that the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting should take 
note of the time schedule as prescribed by it for laying of the Subordinate legislation and 
take necessary corrective steps so that such instances do not recur. 
(iv)   Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions 
        The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances 
and Pensions delayed the laying of 11 notifications (Sl. Nos. 409 -419) out of 16 laid by 
them during the 202nd Session. Further, none of the delayed notification was accompanied 
by a delay statement. The period of delay wa s also substantial, ranging between 1 to 2 
years. The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances 
and Pensions should take necessary corrective steps in the right earnest so that they are 
able to lay their Subordinate Legislation  on time. The Committee further recommends 
that the Ministry should invariably apprise the House about the reasons for delay 
through delay statement in case they are unable to lay any ‘Order’ on time. 
3.     The Committee also notes that there has been delay in laying of one notification each 
by the Ministries of Health and Family Welfare (Sl. No. 371), Home Affairs (Sl. No. 376), 
Human Resource Development (Sl. No. 386), Labour and Employment (Sl. No. 394), 
Railways (Sl. No. 444), Shipping, Road Transport and Highways (Sl. No. 538) and Urban 
Development (Sl. No. 593). In case of notification at Sl. No. 538 (Ministry of Shipping, 
Road Transport and Highways) the delay is of 6 years and 11 months. Further, the 
notifications at Sl. Nos. 386, 394 and 593 were not accompanied by delay statements. The 
Committee hopes that these Ministries would devise appropriate measures to cut on the 
delay in    laying of the notifications. They should also be particular to append delay 
statement in case the laying of Subordinate Legislation is delayed. 
4.     Besides, the timely laying of Subordinate Legislation, the Committee has been 
consistently reiterating that each notification should have a distinctive number so that 
the same is identifiable. However, during the 202nd Sessi on the Committee has came 
across three instances where the notifications were laid without a distinct number. These 
notifications pertain to the Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Sl. 
No. 19), the Ministry of Finance (Sl. No. 132) and the  Ministry Human Resource 
Development (Sl. No. 390). The Committee reiterates that every notification should have 
a distinctive number to give it a separate identity. The Committee  recommends that such 
instances should not be repeated and Ministries/ Depar tments should ensure that every 
notification is assigned a distinct number so that the same is identifiable at any later stage. 
5. The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate steps 
to ensure timely and proper laying of sub ordinate legislation. The Committee, 

accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all Ministries/Departments of the 
Government of India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the following recommendations, 
as contained in its 131st, 135th and 152nd Reports: 
“Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid  
(i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session immediately following the date 
of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in Session, 
on the date of the p ublication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the 
time lag between the date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is 
less than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the Session immediately following the 
said Session. All the  Ministries/Departments of Government of India should 
henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued 
under various Acts of Parliament on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.”    
                                                                                    (131st Report) 
“The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there 
is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department 
should invariably append a delay statement along with the notifications being laid so 
as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all 
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that through the 
process of formulation of a delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, 
identifying bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures”.                                                                                   (135th Report) 
“In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned a distinctive 
number in order to give it a separate identity and similarly, no notification should be 
published/laid which does not carry a notification number. The Committee expects 
all Ministries/ Departments of Government of India to exercise caution in this regard. 
In case where there are more than one notification under a single file, the Ministries/ 
Departments should ensure that the file number on each notification should be 
suitably distinguished from one another. 
                                                                                  (152nd Report)
