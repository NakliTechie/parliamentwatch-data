  CHAPTER I 
1.         BACKGROUND 
      Rule 204 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council of 
States (Rajya Sabha) provides that there shall be a Committee on Subordinate 
Legislation to scrutinize and report to the Council whether the powers to make rules, 
regulations, bye -laws, schemes or other statutory instruments conferred by the 
Constitution or delegated by Parliament have been properly exercised within such 
conferment or delegation, as the case may be. 
1.2.      Mandate  
            Legislations passed by the Parliament and subsequently enacted after being 
assented to by the Preside nt of India, are implemented by the Executive i.e., various 
Ministries/Departments of Government of India. It is a well recognized principle that 
the laws enacted by Parliament cannot be exhaustive enough to visualize each and 
every situation that may aris e during the course of this implementation and to make 
provisions for them in the Act , in anticipation. The Central Acts, therefore, if 
necessary,  contain a provision authorizing the Government to frame detailed 
rules/regulations, etc. to enable it to imp lement the provisions of the Act. These 
rules/regulations framed in exercise of the powers conferred by an Act of Parliament 
are termed as Subordinate Legislation. 
Rule 209 of the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in the Council of 
States lays dow n that after each rule, regulation, bye -law, scheme or other statutory 
instrument framed in pursuance of the Constitution/Act or the legislative functions 
delegated by the Parliament to a subordinate authority and which is required to be laid 
before Parlia ment, is so laid before the Council, the Committee shall, in particular 
consider: 
  
(i)    Whether the Order is in accord with the provisions of the 
Constitution/Act pursuant to which it is made; 
(ii)    Whether the Order contains matter which, in the opin ion of the 
Committee, should more properly be dealt within an Act of Parliament; 
(iii)    Whether the Order contains imposition of taxation; 
(iv)    Whether the Order directly or indirectly bars the jurisdiction of the 
Courts; 
-1- 

(v)        Whether the Order gives retrospective effect to any of the provisions 
in respect of which the Constitution or the Act does not expressly give any 
such power; 
(vi)       Whether the Order involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund 
of India or the public revenues; 
(vii)      Whether the Order appears to make some unusual or unexpected use 
of the powers conferred by the Constitution or the Act pursuant to which it is 
made; 
(viii)     Whether there appears to have been unjustifiable delay in its 
publication or laying before Parliament; and 
(ix)       Whether for any reason, the form or purport of the order calls for any 
elucidation. 
 1.3       Statutory Requirement for laying  
             The Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha had, in its 10 th 
Report presented to the House on the 24 th May, 1971, inter-alia recommended, a 
standard laying formula to be incorporated not only in new legislation but also in 
existing Acts as and when Bills to amend them are brought before Parliament, which 
is reproduced below:- 
 "Every rule made by the Central Government under this Act shall be laid, as 
soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in 
session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one 
session or in  two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the 
session immediately following the session or the successive sessions 
aforesaid,  both Houses  agree in  making any modification to  the rule  or both 
Houses agree that the rule should not b e made, the rule shall thereafter have 
effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, 
however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice 
to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.” 
            Now, all statutes contain the above cited provision for making 
rules/regulations, for laying the same before both Houses of Parliament and for 
amending or annulling the same.  
1.4       Time limit for laying of rules/regulations 
             As per the recommendations contained in para 33 of the 1 st Report of the 
Committee, all the Orders including rules etc., are required to be laid on the Table of 
the House: - 
(a)  within a period of 15 days after their publication in the Gazette, if the 
House is in session; and 
(b)  if the House is not in session, within a period of 15 days after the 
commencement  of  the  next  session. 
-2- 

            However, in case of notifications relating to a state under the President‟s rule, 
due to difficulties faced by certain Ministry, the Committee vide its recommendations 
contained in its 6 th Report (para 17 -19) relaxed this time period to 30 days  from the 
date of publication of the rules, orders, notifications etc., in the State Gazette. 
 1.5       Motion for annulment or modification of rules/regulations laid on the 
Table of the House 
             As per recommendations of the Committee contained  in its 10 th Report (cited 
at para 1.3 ante), after the rules/regulations are laid on the Table of the House, 
Members can move motion for modification or annulment of the rules/regulations. 
The statutory period of laying of 30 days might be completed in on e session or two or 
more successive sessions. Further, as per the recommendation of the Committee 
contained in its 75th Report (Para 2.5), reproduced below:- 
  
"The Committee agrees with the view of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation 
Lok Sabha (Seven th Lok Sabha) expressed in its 20 th Report, that laying on the 
Table of all Orders' in pursuance of powers delegated by Parliament is very 
significant, as it affords an opportunity to Members of Parliament if they so desire 
to move any amendment or modification to such 'order'; including a Motion for 
their annulment.”  
            Hence, a reading of both these recommendations together indicates that 
Members have a right to suggest/move motion for annulment/modifications in the 
'Order' for a time period till one additional session immediatel y following the session 
in which the period of 30 days is completed. If the modification or annulment is 
carried by the Houses of Parliament, the rules/regulations stand annulled or modified 
to the extent agreed to by the Houses of Parliament. 
 1.6       Role of the Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya  Sabha with 
regard to unjustifiable delay in laying 
  
            One of the important functions of the Committee, according to Rule 209 (viii), 
is to see whether there has been unjustifiable delay in t he publication or laying of the 
rules/regulations before the Parliament. The Committee monitors the laying of the 
rules/regulations etc. on the Table of Rajya Sabha during its Session through its 
Reports. This practice started with the 131 st Report of the Committee on Statutory 
Orders laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha during its 190 th Session, presented to the 
House on 15 th December, 2000. During the meeting of the Committee held on 11 th 
December, 2000, to adopt the said report, the Committee decided tha t henceforth, it 
will   regularly scrutinize  the  notifications laid  on   the Table  of  Rajya Sabha from 
-3- 

time to time and present a report to the Rajya Sabha in respect of each Session. This 
practice has continued since then and the present report i s in pursuance of the said 
decision of the Committee.

CHAPTER II 
LAYING OF STATUTORY ORDERS ON THE 
TABLE  OF RAJYA SABHA 
               During the 25 5th  Session of the Rajya Sabha, ( 29th November – 22nd 
December, 2021), 644 (Six Hundred Forty-Four) Statutory Orders/Notifications 
relating to Rules/Regulations/Schemes/Bye -laws etc., framed under the 
Constitution/Acts of Parliament, were laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha by various 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. The details of the Gazette 
Notifications in which these were published and the date of their laying on the Table 
of the Rajya Sabha have been indicated Ministry -wise, in the Statement placed at  
Annexure. 
2.1       While scrutinizing these Orders, the Committee note s and appreciates the 
efforts made by the Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, in so far as 
the timely laying of the Gazette Notifications brought out by them, is concerned. The 
annexed statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where Notifications have 
not been laid as per the time schedule prescribed by the Committee. During the 2 55th 
Session, out of 644 Notifications that were laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha, 464 were 
laid within the stipulated time, and 180 Notifications were delayed. Further, out of 
these 180 delayed Notifications, 92 of these were delaye d by a period of upto 11 
months; while 60 Notifications were delayed by periods ranging b etween 1 year to 2 
years; and 28 Notifications were delayed by more than 2 years. The Commi ttee, 
however, observes that 168 (93.33%) out of these 180 delayed Notifications, were 
accompanied by delay statement s from the concerned Minister and 12 (6.67%) 
Notifications did not have any delay statement from the concerned Minister enclosed 
along with the Notifications. The bulk of delayed Notifications were from the Ministry 
of Road Transport and Highways  with 54 Notifications, which comes to around 30% 
of the total delayed Notifications,  and Ministry of Environment, Forests and Climate 
Change (47 delayed Notifications)  which comes to 26.11% of the total delayed 
Notifications, some other Ministries also had a large number of delayed Notifications 
e.g., Department of Revenue ( 11 delayed Notifications); Ministry of Petroleum and 
Natural Gas  (10 delayed Notifications); Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs  (9 
delayed Notifications) ; Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (7 Notifications); 
Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade and Department of Financial 
Services (both had 5 delayed Notifications each); Hence, the Committee has examined 
these data in detail and important issues have been highlighted for guidance of the 
Ministries/Departments for the future. The following are the Ministry -wise details of 
the Notifications that were laid during the Session, which were delayed:-

RECOMMENDATIONS/OBSERVATIONS 
           At the outset, the Committee would like to commend the 
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India for putting in a good effort 
in laying a total of 644 Notifications during the 25 5th (Winter) Session. However, 
out of the Notificatio ns laid, 180 Notifications were delayed and 84 of them were 
delayed by a period of more than 11 months, some of which   were delayed by  
huge periods ranging from 2 years to 6 years and 2 months , which is 
unreasonable and need dee per introspection by  the concerned 
Ministries/Department. 
            The Committee observes that 98 Notifications, which account for (54.4%) 
of all the delayed Notifications were late by more than 1 0 months; 14  
Notifications, which come to (7.77%) of all the delayed Notifications, were late by 
9 months; 19 Notifications ( 10.55%) were late by 8 months; 1 Notification  
(0.55%) was late by 7 months; 17 Notifications (9.44%) were late by 6 months; 28 
Notifications ( 15.55%) were late by 5 months;and 3  Notifications (1.66%) were 
late by 4  months. This shows that though most of the Ministries/Departments 
have put in a good effort, more needs to be done to obviate such delays in placing 
these Notifications on the Table of Rajya Sabha.  
            The Committee observes that   some of the Ministries, which had a huge 
number of delayed Notifications, e.g., Ministry of Finance (Department of 
Revenue); Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; Ministry of Housing and 
Urban Affairs; Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas; and Ministry of Road 
Transport and Highways etc. need to review the procedure followed by them for 
laying of their Gazette Notifications and if necessary,  issue instructions to 
all their Departments and  subordinate/attached offices/organizations, impressing 
upon them the importance of laying all notifications within the stipulated time 
and if there is any delay, then to attach a delay statement, explaining cogent 
reasons for the delay and to take steps t o obviate such delays in future. The 
Committee, after considering the above data, makes the following 
recommendations and directs that these   recommendations may be circulated to 
all the Ministries/Departments for strict compliance. The Ministry of 
Parliamentary Affairs may take necessary step to co -ordinate with other 
Ministries/Departments on this serious issue.

 b) Non-attachment of ‗Delay Statements‘ to the delayed Notifications laid 
 4.2       The Committee feels that the most disconcer ting point is that 12 
Notifications, out of a total 180 delayed Notifications , were laid during this 
Session, without any delay statement appended to them, which goes against the 
recommendation of the Committee contained in its earlier Report (135 th Report) 
reproduced below:- 
        ―The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in 
case there is a delay in the laying of Statutory Orders, the concerned 
Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the 
Notifications being laid so as to apprise the Parliament of the causes of delay. The 
Committee desires that all Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the 
intention is that through the process of formulation of a delay statement, the 
Ministry is able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial 
measures‖. 
             The Committee recommends that all the Ministries/Departments must 
adhere to the time schedule for laying of Notifications as prescribed in 
Committee‘s 131 st Report and in case, the delay in laying of Notifications is 
unavoidable, the Ministry should attach a delay statement from their Minister 
citing cogent reasons for the delay. The delay statement gives an opportunity, to 
the Ministry to clarify the reasons for the del ay in laying of the Notification but 
also a reason for the Ministry/Department to introspect and improve their 
functioning. In case this longstanding recommendation of the Committee is not 
followed, then the accountability of the Ministries to the Parliame nt and the 
Constitutional Mandate of the Parliament to monitor the functioning of the 
Executive would become a futile exercise and the Committee has every right to 
take this matter very seriously.

Formula for laying of Statutory Rules 
            The Committee approve the following draft revised formula for laying of 
Statutory Rules before both Houses of Parliament which the Ministry of Law and 
Justice propose to incorporate in all future legislation which provide for making 
of rules by the Central Government:— 
  
        "Every rule made by the Central Government under this Act shall be 
laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament,

comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before 
the expiry of the session immediately following the session or t he successive 
sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule 
or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall 
thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the 
case may be; so, h owever that any such modification or annulment shall be 
without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that 
rule." 
             
            The Committee recommends that the revised formula should be 
incorporated not only in new legislation but also in the existing Acts as and when 
Bills to amend them are brought before Parliament. The Ministry should also 
incorporate the new formula in the Bills pending before Parliament. This will, in 
the opinion of the Committee, bring about uniformity in the matter expeditiously.
