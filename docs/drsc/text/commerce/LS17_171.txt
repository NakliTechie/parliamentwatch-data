statement also indicates the period of delay in cases where notifications have not been laid as per the schedule

prescribed by the Committee and whether such notifications were accompanied by a statement indicating the

(i)        Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries 
 
          The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of 11 months and 9 months, respectively, in
laying of two notifications (Sl Nos. 1 & 2) and no delay statement was sent therewith.
 
          The Committee would like the Ministry of Agro & Rural Industries to take corrective measures so as to
obviate such delay in future and in case such delays occur the notification while being laid before Parliament
should be accompanied  by statutes giving reason for the delay in laying.
    
(ii)     Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce)
 
          The Committee notes with regret that the Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce)
have delayed the laying of their notification (Sl. No. 4) by 3 months. The delay statement appended by the said
notification reads:-
 
          “The Coffee Board (Cadre & Recruitment) Rules, 1993 were framed by the Coffee Board in accordance with
the powers conferred by Rule 31 of the Coffee Board Rules, 1955.  As there was no provision under Rule 31 of the
Coffee Rules, 1955 regarding laying of the rules made thereunder, on the table of the Parliament, the same could
not be laid on the Table of each House of Parliament.
 
          However, the Rajya Sabha Committee on Subordinate Legislation were of the view that as per sub-section
(xv) of Section 48 of the Coffee Act, 1942, the rule making powers, in respect of Pay & allowances, leave and
other conditions of the service of the staff employed by the Board (other than those appointed by Central Govt.)
and other employees of the Board, are vested with the Central Govt. and as
such these rules should have been framed by the Central Govt. not by the Coffee Board.”
           The Committee regrets to note that the delay statement gives only the circumstance under which the
required rules have framed/laid but there is nothing in it to explain how the laying of the notification has been
delayed by three months.  The Committee hopes that the Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of
Commerce) would take note of discrepancy and avoid recurrence of the same in future.
 
 
(iii)              Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Department of Information
Technology)
 
          The Committee notes with regret that there has been a delay of 10 months in laying of one notification (
Sl.No.7) and no delay statement was sent therewith.
         
          The Committee recommends that the Ministry of Communications & Information Technology (Department
of Information Technology) should strictly adhere to the time schedule as recommended by it and in case there is a
delay in laying the notification should invariably be accompanied by a delay statement explaining the reasons for
such delay.

3.   The Committee impresses upon all the Ministries/Departments to take immediate steps to ensure timely and
proper laying of subordinate legislation. The Committee, accordingly, wishes to bring to the notice of all
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India, particularly, the defaulting ones, the following
recommendations, as contained in its 131st,135th  and 152nd Reports:
      “Orders required to be laid before the House should be laid (i) if the House is not in Session, during the Session
immediately following the date of publication of the Order in the Official Gazette and (ii) if the House is in
Session, on the date of the publication of the Order, during its continuance and in case the time lag between the
date of publication and the date of the closing of the Session is less than 15 clear days, before the expiry of the
Session immediately following the said Session. All the Ministries/Departments of Government of India should
henceforth comply with this schedule in the matter of laying of the Orders issued under various Acts of Parliament
on the Table of the Rajya Sabha.
(131st Report)
(Presented on 15 December 2000)
      “The Committee has, time and again, emphasized that it is essential that in case there is a delay in the laying of
Statutory Orders, the concerned Ministry/Department should invariably append a delay statement along with the
notifications being laid so as to apprise Parliament of the causes of delay. The Committee desires all
Ministries/Departments to take a note of this, since the intension is that through the process of formulation of a
delay statement the Ministry is able to introspect, identify bottlenecks and devise remedial measures”.
(135
th Report)
(Presented on 27 July 2001)
 
      “In the opinion of the Committee, every notification should be assigned a distinctive number in order to give it
a separate identity and similarly, no notification should be published/laid which does not carry a notification
number. The Committee expects all Ministries/Departments of Government of India to exercise caution in this
regard. In case where there are more than one notification under a single file, the Ministries/Departments should
ensure that the file number on each notification should be suitably distinguished from one another.
(152nd Report)
     (Presented on 26 August 2004)
