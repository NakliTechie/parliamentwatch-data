 and Forests, having been authorized by the Committee, present its 154

 Amendment *Bill 2005.  The Bill seeks to amend the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.

 Chairman, Rajya Sabha **referred the Bill, as introduced in the Rajya Sabha on the 23rd December, 2005 and pending

 Experts on the Bill.  The Committee took clause-by-clause consideration of the Bill on the 1st and 7th March, 2006.

REPORT
 
                        As per the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Bill, the Project Tiger was launched in the country on
 1st April, 1973 for conserving the endangered tiger.  Initially, 9 Tiger Reserves were covered under the project, which
 has now increased to 28 Tiger Reserves, falling in 17 States.  The Central Government provides funding support to
 projects prepared by the States in this regard.  Since then, the Project Tiger is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.  The
 implementation of the project over the years highlighted the need for a statutory authority, having legal backing with
 well-defined functions, for conservation of tigers in the country.  Despite the project ongoing for more than three
 decades, the status of tigers in the country remains critical, which necessitates the need for addressing the issue right
 from planning to administration and making provisions for dealing with cases of violation.
                        Any conservation effort to save tiger, must take care of the prevailing peculiar circumstances in the
 country, where forests are not wilderness areas but also where tribal and other people live.  On the basis of the
 recommendations of the National Board for Wild Life, a Task Force  was setup to look into the problems of tiger
 conservation in the country and to suggest comprehensive measures for its improvement.
                        The recommendations of the Task Force, inter alia, include strengthening of Project Tiger by giving it
 statutory as well as administrative powers.  It has also recommended that the report relating to Project Tiger should
 annually be sent to the Central Government for laying in Parliament so that the commitment to the Project Tiger is
 reviewed from time to time.
                        With this twin objective of tiger conservation and harmonizing the rights of the tribal people living in and
 around tiger reserves, it is proposed to amend the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, to provide for the National Tiger
 Conservation Authority responsible for implementation of Project Tiger plan for protecting endangered tigers; and to
 harmonize and facilitate the implementation of the Tiger Conservation Plans prepared by the State Governments taking
 in to account the needs and concerns of the local people.  The amendments are aimed at putting in place, a mechanism
 which will balance and manage, the livelihood needs of the local people with the imperatives of tiger conservation.
                       
The salient features of the Bill are as under :
 
(i)         The Bill seeks to constitute a National Tiger Conservation Authorityto exercise the powers conferred on, and to
 perform the functions assigned to it under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.  The National Tiger Conservation
 Authority has been proposed under the Chairmanship of the Minister of Environment and Forests.
 
Preparation of Annual Report of the Tiger Conservation Authority and submission of the audit report to the Central
 Government for laying before Parliament.
 
State level Steering Committee has been proposed in the tiger states under the Chairmanship of respective Chief
 Ministers for ensuring co-ordination, monitoring, protection and conservation of tiger, co-predators and prey animals
 within the tiger range States.
(iv)       The State Government shall, on the recommendations of the National Tiger Conservation Authority, notify an
 area as a tiger reserve. The State Government shall prepare a Tiger Conservation Plan including staff development and
 deployment plan for the proper management of tiger reserve.
(v)        No alteration in the boundaries of tiger reserve shall be made except on a recommendation of the National Tiger
 Conservation Authority and the approval of the National Board for Wild Life. No State Government shall de-notify a
 tiger reserve, except in public interest with the approval of the Tiger Conservation Authority and the National Board for
 Wild Life.
(vi)       It has been proposed that the State Government shall establish a Tiger Conservation Foundation for tiger reserves
 within the State in order to facilitate and support their management for conservation of tiger and biodiversity and, to take
 initiatives in eco-development by involvement of people in such development process.
(vii)      Some other changes of consequential and incidental nature have also been proposed in the bill.

The recommendations/observations of the Committee with regard to the principal changes proposed in the Bill are as
 follows :
 
Clause 2
I           Section 38 - O (1)(e)
The Committee recommends to replace the words ‘tiger estimation’ with the words ‘estimation of population of tiger
 and its natural prey species, status of habitats,’.
II          Section 38-  O (1)(f)
The Committee recommends the following insertions :
 
(i)         after the word ‘research’ the words ‘and monitoring’ may be inserted.
after the word ‘tiger’ the words ‘its prey and co-predators’ may be inserted.
after the words ‘socio-economic parameters’, the words ‘and their evaluation’ may be inserted.
 
III        Section 38 - O (1)(k)
 
            The Committee recommends insertion of the words ‘and its habitat’ after the words ‘conservation of tigers’.
 
IV        Section 38 - V(4)       
The Committee recommends insertion of the words ‘outside the core area’ after the words ‘tiger reserve’.
Clause 3
            V         Section 51(1) – New Proviso
The Committee sought certain clarifications on the adequacy of the penal provision proposed in Section 51 of the
 Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.  It has been informed that regarding higher punishment proposed to be provided to the
 person accused of offences, it is always made relatable to the nature of the offence committed and punishment is
 accordingly awarded commensurate with such offences.  For example, section 304 of the IPC makes provision for
 punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder and a punishment for life or up to ten years is provided. 
 Under section 307 for attempt to murder, the imprisonment may extend to ten years, whereas under section 304A,
 causing death by negligence, the imprisonment may extend to two years only.  Keeping all this in view, it seems that the
 punishment proposed in the Bill is in line with the punishment provided in the Wild Life Protection Act, 1972.  The
 Ministry of Environment & Forests, however, agreed with the views of the Committee that the amount of penalty could
 be increased keeping in view the value of the endangered tiger.
                                    The Committee, therefore, recommends that the following Sub-Sections may be inserted after Sub-
Section (1 B) of the Principal Act to make the offence more deterrent.
“(1C) – Any person, who commits an offence in relation to the core of a tiger reserve or where the offence relate to
 hunting in the tiger reserve or altering the boundaries of the tiger reserve, such offence shall be punishable on first
 conviction with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than three years but may extend to seven years, and also
 with fine which shall not be less than fifty thousand rupees but may extend to two lakh rupees, and in the event of a
 second or subsequent conviction with imprisonment for a term  of not less than seven years and also with fine which
 shall not be less than five lakh rupees but may extend to fifty lakh rupees.
            (1D) – Whoever abets any offence punishable under sub-section (1C) shall, if the act abetted is committed in
 consequence of the abetment, be punishable with the punishment provided for that offence”.
 
VI        Clause 1, Enacting Formula and Title          
 
Clause 1, the enacting formula and the title are adopted with some consequential changes, which are of consequential or
 drafting nature, namely, the figure ‘2005’ and the words ‘Fifty-sixth’ to be substituted by the figure ’2006’ and the
 words ‘Fifty-seventh’, respectively.
            The Committee adopts the remaining clauses/sections of the Bill without any amendments.
The Committee recommends that the Bill may be passed after incorporating the amendments suggested by it.

 compensation to people for loss incurred on account of wild animals are concerned, the same would be provided in the

                        The protection of tigers has reached a very critical state and if the issue is not addressed effectively there
 is a possibility of this species getting extinct.   To address the issue of poaching and illegal trade in wild life, the Task
 Force has recommended immediate setting up of an effective Wild Life Crime Bureau.  It strongly believes that if crime
 bureau has to be set-up, it should work effectively on different level, one, to strengthen the enforcement at the state
 level; two, to investigate international trade links; three, to break the crime of large poachers. Task Force has suggested
 two-tiered approach: set-up a Wild Life Crime Bureau at the Central level, with nodes in each tiger range state in which
 there will be a capacity to both investigate and follow up on thecrime. The Committee, therefore, feels that there is an
 immediate need to set up a Wild Life Crime Bureau as a statutory body under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 to
 make it effective and control the crime relating to wild life. It accordingly, recommends that this should be done on a
 priority basis at the earliest.
 
The Committee is of the view that Government may appropriately address these issues.

“(1C) – Any person, who commits an offence in relation to the core of a tiger reserve or where the offence relate to
 hunting in the tiger reserve or altering the boundaries of the tiger reserve, such offence shall be punishable on first

 extend to seven years, and with fine which shall not be less than fifty thousand rupees but which may extend to two lakh
 rupees, and in the event of a
second or subsequent conviction with imprisonment for a term not less than seven years and also with a fine not less than
 five lakh rupees but which may extend to fifty lakh rupees.
 
(1D) – Whoever abets any offence punishable under sub-section (1C) shall, if the act abetted is committed in
