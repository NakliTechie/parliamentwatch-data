REPORT
The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC), which was set up by a Government of India Resolution on August 10, 1948, is
 the apex body for formulation of policies and direction of the programmes relating to peaceful uses of atomic energy in
 electricity generation, medicine, agriculture, industry and research. The Commission implements its policies and
 programmes that are multidisciplinary in nature through the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE), which was formed on
 August 3, 1954. The Department of Atomic Energy comprises five Research Centres, five Public Sector Undertakings,
 three Industrial Organisations and three service organisations and eight fully funded institutes seven of these institutes are
 engaged in basic research. In addition, the Department’s Board of Research in Nuclear Sciences (BRNS) and the National
 Board for Higher Mathematics (NBHM) promote research in nuclear & allied fields and mathematics.
  2.       The programmes being pursued by DAE emanate from the following mandate:
 Ø      Increasing share of nuclear power through deployment of indigenous and other proven technologies, along with
 development of fast breeder reactors and thorium reactors with associated fuel cycle facilities;
Ø      Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes and carrying out radiation technology

Ø     Developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, supercomputers, advanced materials and

 3.        The programmes of the Department comprise four Sectors viz. Power, Industries, Minerals and Research &
 Development.  The financial support for the activities of the Department are provided by budgetary support under
 Demand No. 5-Atomic Energy and Demand No. 6- Nuclear Power Schemes.  The public sector undertakings of the
 Department provide Internal and Extra Budgetary Resources (IEBR) in addition to budgetary support to the capital and
 infrastructural developments.       
 4.        The Nuclear Power Sector of the Department deals with design, construction and operation of commercial power
 reactors with associated safety in all its phases.  This comprises building of Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)
 and development of Fast Breeder Reactors (FBRs) and Thorium Reactors on commercial scale.  Associated waste
 management and environment monitoring and technology development relating to operation and maintenance of the
 reactors also form part of the programmes.
 5.        Industries & Minerals Sector is involved in industrial application of technologies developed in the R&D facilities
 and includes- programmes related to nuclear fuel cycle covering design, construction and operation of industrial plants for
 refining ores, fabrication of fuel, production of heavy water, instrumentation and control, etc. needed for sustained
 operation of the power reactors; and applications of radioisotopes, radiation, laser and accelerator technology for
 development in industry, medicine, agriculture and food preservation.
 6.        Research & Development Sector provides R & D support to the Nuclear Power Programme of the Department. 
 The R & D efforts of DAE are in multidisciplinary high technology areas.  Significant achievements have been made in
 building technical capabilities in the design, construction and operation of Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR);
 exploration, mining extraction, purification and conversion of nuclear materials; production of fuel element for nuclear
 reactors; production of heavy water; health and safety research; development and application of lasers and accelerators;
 development of Fast Breeder Test Reactors and related instrumentation; reprocessing of spent fuels; waste management
 and production and use of radioisotopes, besides basic research in frontier areas of S&T, particularly radio-astronomy,
 molecular biology, condensed matter physics, computer science, etc.

13.       In reply to a query regarding the adequacy of the funds allocated to the Department at B.E. 2006-2007, the
 Committee was informed that the allocation under the Plan schemes were sufficient to meet the targeted activities of the
 Department under Demand No.5 – Atomic Energy. Although, the allocation under Demand No.6 – Nuclear Power
 Schemes was less by Rs.400 crore for investment in Equity of NPCIL, the allocations made were adequate for carrying
 out the activities and no schemes would be affected or discontinued.
 14.      The Committee was also informed that as far as the Non-Plan allocation was concerned, there was a shortfall of
 Rs.312.89 crore.  Major portion of this i.e. 83% was intended for Industries Sector where operational needs of Heavy
 Water Plants, Nuclear Fuel Complex, Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology, Fuel Reprocessing Plants of Bhabha
 Atomic Research Centre, etc. were to be met.  As the lower allocation could affect the operational requirement, the
 Department would request augmentation of funds through the First Batch of Supplementary Demands during Monsoon
 Session of Parliament 2006.
15.   The Committee notes with concern that the Non -Plan allocation has been reduced considerably by an amount of Rs
 312.89 crores which may affect the operational needs of the Heavy Water Plants, Nuclear Fuel Complex, Board of
 Radiation and Isotope Technology, Fuel Reprocessing Plants of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre etc. The Committee
 feels that any curtailment of funds in a crucial sector like atomic energy could delay the endeavor of the Department to
 increase the contribution of Nuclear Power in the total Power production and other important areas  of the
 country.

16.      In reply to a query of the Committee regarding the details of projects where funds have remained unutilized in
 2005-06 and steps taken by the Department to complete them within a specified time frame, the Committee was informed
 as follows:-
16.1     Under the Power Sector, the RE 2005-06 was reduced by Rs.927.96 crore.  Reduction was on account of less 
 requirement under the Russian Credit (Rs.724.00 crore) due to non-receipt/delay in receipt of shipments from Russia for
 the externally aided Kudankulam Nuclear Power Project.  Reduction of Rs.203.00 crore also was made under the
 investment to Bharatiya Nabhikiya Vidyut Nigam Ltd. (BHAVINI) as the procurement of high value equipment got
 delayed and the civil construction was affected by the Tsunami.  It is expected that the progress as targeted in 2006-2007
 will be fully achieved and the budgetary support is made accordingly.
 16.2    Under the Mineral sector, an amount of Rs.76.85 crore was reduced mainly due to
 scaling down of the investment in Uranium Corporation of India Ltd as they could not get the clearances for new projects
 at Andhra Pradesh and Meghalaya and reduced investment in Indian Rare Earths Ltd as they are utilizing the internal
 resources for the expenditure etc.  The clearance are expected to get materialized during 2006-07 and accordingly the
 budget provided in 2006-07.
16.3.    Under Industries sector the RE 2005-06 was reduced by Rs. 122.63 crore.  This was mainly under the various
 projects of Nuclear Fuel Complex (Rs.57.15 crore), Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (Rs.45.00 crore), Indira Gandhi
 Centre for Atomic Research (Rs.16.30 crore), Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology instrumentation packages by
 the consultancy firm for the New Zirconium Oxide and Sponge Project, Palayakayal, delay in procurement of high value
 equipment under various projects, time taken for sanctions of Mid Term Appraisal etc. have attributed to the reduction at
 RE stage.  It is expected that the projects will progress as per schedule in 2006-07. 
16.4.    Under R&D sector RE was reduced by Rs.37.95 crore mainly on account of time taken for obtaining sanctions for
 the projects undertaken in Mid Term Appraisal.  It is expected that the payment will progress in 2006-07 as per the target
 and the budget provisions are made accordingly in 2006-07.
 
17.       The Committee notes that the reason for the funds remaining unutilized in the projects have been attributed to the
 delay in receipt of equipments, delay in receipt of clearances and the longer time taken for obtaining sanctions for projects
 undertaken in the Mid Term Appraisal. The Committee feels that an in depth analysis of various factors responsible for
 lack of funds utilization should be made by the Department and wherever necessary strict checks should be incorporated
 in the administrative machinery itself to avoid funds remaining unutilized. The Committee also feels that action should be
 taken against those vendors who were responsible for delayed or inadequate supplies of equipment and machinery.
 Wherever possible, a penalty clause should be incorporated in the tender/purchase agreement to ward off any delay or
 inadequate supply of critical equipment and machinery. The Committee recommends that such remedial measures should
 be in place for future projects.

 promises to be a widely available energy source with essentially unlimited supply and manageable environmental impact.

BHABHA ATOMIC RESEARCH CENTRE(BARC)
 
24.       The Committee has been apprised that BARC is a multi disciplinary organization, pursuing comprehensive
 research and development programmes for harnessing nuclear energy and also its utility for the benefit of the society.
 These R&D efforts are concentrated in the fields of nuclear sciences, engineering & technology, basic sciences and allied
 fields. The activities are geared for exploitation of atomic energy for power generation and development of radiation
 technology and its application in the areas of agriculture, medicine, industry and research. For fulfilling the mandate,
 interaction with academic institutions and international co-operation in related advanced areas of research are being
 strengthened.  BARC continues to provide required R&D support to all other Units of the Department and provides
 necessary support to ensure national security.
 
25.       The significant activities and achievements of BARC during the last one year are Commissioning of 1kW Helium
 Refrigeration Plant.  The cold facility of Advanced Reactor Experimental Facitity (AREF) has been commissioned and
 made critical.  Indigenously developed Cobalt Teletherapy machine named ‘BHABHATRON’ installed at ACTREC       
 (Advanced Centre for Treatment, Research and Education in Cancer) and treatment of patients commenced. As part of the
 Advanced Reactor Development programme (ARDP), Integral Test Loop (ITL) facility, for Simulating AHWR(Advanced
 Heavy Water Reactor)primary and safety systems, was completed and commissioned.  Simulation facilites for  U-233
 clean-up was set up.  Work related to ADS(Accelerator Driven Sub-critical System) is in progress.  System integration for
 300 kW EB Evaporator was completed.  Assembly and installation of 10 MeV RF LINAC(LINEAR ACCELERATOR)
 was completed.  Advanced telerobotic and automation laboratory was commissioned.  Work related to KALI 5000 was

CHLORO TOXIN AND THE TREATMENT OF GLIOMAS
 
53.       The Committee understand that brain cancers are the worst forms of cancers which is  among the masses most
 difficult and expensive cancers to treat. “Gliomas” the medical name of brain cancer is not only highly invasive but any
 surgical technique has failed to eradicate the tumour permanently and other adjuvent therapies are apparently inadequate
 to treat such form of dreadly disorders of brain.
54.       The Committee, was informed by Tata Memorial Centre (TMC) that a scropion derived venom peptide which
 modified with radio-active isotopes can produce novel drug for killing cancer cells in the brain combined with lethal
 radiation.
 
 
 
55.       In response to a question on related issue the Tata Memorial Centre replied as follows:-
Given the fact that the outcome of vast majority of malignant tumours remains dismal despite rapid advances in neuro-
surgical and radio-therapeutic techniques, biological agents with novel mechanisms of actions are needed to target the
 malignant gliomas cells and improve outcome.
They further stated that chlorotoxin, a scorpion venom derived peptide, is reported to bind specifically only to glioma cells
 and not normal brain cells. It is this specificity that makes chlorotoxin  a potential agent that could carry lethal drugs
 specifically to tumor tissue with minimal effect on surrounding normal brain tissue. Chlorotoxin, which is relatively
 selectively taken up by the gliomas, inhibits glioma cell invasion and thus can be used as a bifunctional modifier
 (cytotoxic as well as radiation enhancer).
The Committee feels that Hafkiane Institute of Mumbai which is mainly researching in scorpion poison can also advise
 ACTREC research wing in the matter by supplying scorpions so that by effectively managing cholorotoxin this most
 difficult form of cancer, i.e., cancer of brain could be treated with large amount of success. The Committee feels that
 unless such new drugs are ventured into, no permanent solution to the successful treatment of cancer of the brain could be
 achieved in the near future. The Committee, therefore, recommends TMH to consider its suggestions and collaborate
 functionally with Hafkiane Institute to design an effective drug against brain cancers.
