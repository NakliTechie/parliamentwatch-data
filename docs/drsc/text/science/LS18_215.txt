REPORT 
  The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by  the Department of Space on the recommendations contained 
in the Two Hundred Sixth Report of the Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, 
Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-11) of the Department of Space, which was presented to both the 
Houses of Parliament on 22nd April,  2010. The Two Hundred Sixth Report of the Committee contained fourteen 
recommendations. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 7th 
September, 2010. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken by the 
Department of Space are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:- 
ELEVENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN (2007-12) PROPOSALS 
Recommendation  (Para 5.3) 
 The Committee recommends that in addition to continuing  and strengthening its already developed space technologies, 
the Department should also quest for mastering critical technologies and attaining further progress in its launch vehicle 
programme for successful launching of its spacecraft indigenous ly. It also recommends that the services of Antrix, the 
commercial arm of the Department, which markets India’s space services globally, must be fully utilized. The Committee hopes 
that the Department would achieve the goals set for itself during the Eleventh Five Year Plan period in the field of space 
applications for communication, broadcasting, remote sensing, launch vehicle technology etc. for the benefit of the society at 
large. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. The Department has taken up several technologically challenging projects such as development of indigenous 
cryogenic engine and stage, semi-cryogenic engine development, microwave remote sensing satellite, advanced communication 
and navigation satellites, space capsule recovery mission and critical technologies required for re-usable launch vehicles. Antrix 
has been effectively contributing in securing global reach for Indian space products and servi ces. Towards achieving the goals 
set for the Eleventh plan, the Department has made significa nt achievements in the firs t three years 2007-10 including 
Chandrayaan-l, and has drawn up firm plan of actions for the remaining period of 2010-12. 
Comments 
 The Committee is satisfied to note that the Department is doing commendable job in the field of space technologies 
and pursuing its goals with dedication. The Committee ho pes that the Department would continue utilising its 
technological capabilities for attaining its objectives and take every possible step to run its programmes as per schedule. 
BUDGETARY  ALLOCATION 
Recommendations  (Para 6.1) 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of  Space should be exempted from such ceilings in future 
and there must not be any reduction at R.E. stage for this Financial Year. The Committee also recommends that in case the 
Department requires more resources at Revised Estimates stage, it should be positively considered and desired allocation should  
be granted by the Ministry of Finance 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. The recommendations of the Committee will be brought to the notice of the concerned during the budget 
discussions. 
 
 

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and is of the view that the Department should ensure that Ministry of Finance 
releases necessary additional budgetary support to meet the revised fund requiremen ts of the programmes at R.E. stage 
so that their progress is not adversely affected due to shortage of funds. 
Recommendations  (Para 6.3) 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Sp ace should not be made to starve of funds in future and 
expects that Government will extend all budgetary supports required by the Department in future. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. The recommendations of the Committee will be brought to the notice of the concerned during the budget 
discussions. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
MANNED MISSION INITIATIVE 
Recommendation  (Para 7.2) 
 The Committee strongly recommends that the right to take final decision in Scientific and Technical matters should be 
vested with the Department of Space only and financial constrai nts should not be allowed to come in its way towards progress 
of these crucial and critical programmes. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. Based on detailed feasibility studies, the Departme nt had formulated a proposal to undertake Human Spaceflight 
Programme (Manned Mission Initiative) with the objective to develop a fully autonomous manned space vehicle to carry two 
crew to Low Earth Orbit and safe return to earth. However, considering the magnitude of efforts and technological complexities 
involved, the Department has decided to undertake the programme in a phased manner with the first phase focusing on 
development of critical technologies such as service module,  crew module, environment control and life support systems and 
crew escape module required for the Human Spaceflight Programme. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation  (Para 7.4) 
 The Committee feels that the demand for these schemes/ programmes (Chandrayaan-2 and Semi-Cryo Engine 
Development) had been made without doing proper homework and extensive and critical assessment of the actual requirements. 
The Committee feels that budgetary demands should be sought by the Department only after comprehensive analysis of the 
actual requirements for the programme. Scarce resources should not be made to be blocked by asking for ambitious allocations 
and not utilizing them subsequently. The Committee recommends that such instances, though, only a few, should be avoided in 
future by the Department. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendations of the Committee are noted. It is brough t to the notice of the Committee that the budget provisions 
made under B.E. 2009-10 were based on specific progress milestones to be achieved during the year such as establishment of 
semi-cryogenic engine test facilities at Mahendragiri, finaliza tion of scientific payloads, bread-boarding and development of 
scientific instruments, finalization of spacecraft configuration, procurement of components, etc. for Chandrayaan-2 project. 
 However, due to technological complexities involved in these projects, there was a need to revise the development plans 
during the R.E. stage with consequential reduction in budget. 

Comments 
 The Committee while taking note of the reply is of the view that for optimum utilization of allocated funds, the 
Department should plan its programmes meticulously and ensure that they are running as per their schedule. 
INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION 
Recommendation  (Para 8.1) 
 The Committee recommends that in future, in case of joint co llaboration, it must be ensured that there is a clear 
understanding between the participating countries that credit for landmark achievements should be shared equally and the 
announcement is made jointly. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. In the specific case of detection of water molecules on the moon by the Chandrayaa n-l mission, there was a clear 
understanding between the scientists of India and USA in sharing the credit for landmark achievements, and accordingly a joint 
paper was published first. Though the primary instruments that  detected water molecules on the Moon were from USA, there 
was widespread appreciation for India’s contribution among the international scientific community for accommodating these 
international payloads and in sharing the data from all the payloads, which made this achievement possible. 
 In line with the above, clear understanding would be brought to focus among the participating countries by incorporating 
appropriate clauses in all the future agreements and Memoranda of Understanding of joint collaboration entered into by the 
Department of Space. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
PARTICIPATION OF SPACE INDUSTRY PARTNERSHIP 
Recommendation  (Para 8.3) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that involvement and participation of more and more Indian industries will lead to self-
reliance and would bring down our dependence on foreign industries for equipments and technologies. Expansion of Indian 
industry would also lead to large-scale employment generation. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of 
Space should make concerted efforts in this direction. The Committee also desires that Department of Space should look 
forward to transfer more and more technologies which may be useful for civilian applications to Indian Industries. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendation of the Committee pertaining to the involvement and participation of Indian industries in 
manufacturing and development of space technology has been noted. Concerted efforts are placed for enhancing role of Indian 
industries in the satellite and the launch vehicle manufacturing. Action has been taken for the productionisation of satellite 
propulsion components through industries. Operations of the large solid booster propellant plant established at Shriharikota ha s 
also been entrusted to Indian industry. Expansion of production capabilities for satellite thermal system including heat pipes has 
been initiated. Design and development of application-specific integrated chips for space tech nology applications has been 
taken up. 
 The licensing of technologies pertaining to civilian applications has been on the rise. Specific efforts in this direction hav e 
fructified in the transfer of technologies such as Distress Al ert Transmitter, INSAT based Mobile Satellite Service Terminal, 
Indigenous Geographical Information System software, Ternary Eutectic Chloride Powder etc., to the civilian market. In 
addition, several other technologies are under process for transfer  of technology. The Department has so far transferred around  
280 technologies to Indian industries. 
 
 

Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and expresse s its satisfaction that the Departme nt has been taking  initiatives for 
enhancing role of Indian industries in the satellite and the launch vehicle manuf acturing and has so far transferred a 
number of technologies to Indian industr ies. The Committee desires that the Depart ment should keep taking such steps 
for the growth of indigenous industries. 
INVOLVEMENT OF YOUTH IN SPACE PROGRAMME 
Recommendation  (Para 8.5) 
 The Committee desires that the Department of Space should ma ke all out efforts to encourage more and more youngsters 
to join its programme. The Committee, theref ore, recommends that the Department should accord priority to this initiative. 
Along term goal should be fixed for involvement of youth in space programme. 
Action Taken Report 
 Noted. Department of Space has launched a massive outreach programme to attract youth to involve themselves in space 
activities through a series of programmes . Exhibitions highlighting the activities of Indian Space Programme are regularly 
organised at schools and colleges at district level, state level and national level. Brochures and pamphlets coinciding with th e 
launch of satellites and launch vehicles are being published for wider dissemination. Visits by students and academic 
community to various ISRO Centres are organised to provide exposure to the latest developments in space technology under 
development at ISRO Centres. Publications in easy-to-understa nd style targeting school children, attractive stickers, etc., are  
published and distributed to children visiting exhibitions and ISRO  centres. ISRO also participates in conferences, seminars at  
national and international levels by putting up exhibitions showcasing its achievements. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and woul d like the Department to continue it s efforts towards involving youth in 
space activities. 
R & D PROGRAMME 
Recommendation  (Para 8.7) 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department  should seek more resource at R.E. level under this 
programme, if need be. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendations of the Committee are noted. Additional funds required, if any, will be projected at the R.E. stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation  (Para 8.8) 
 The Committee recommends that in view of the rapid climat e change taking place, it has become absolutely necessary 
and urgent to study climatic and environmental changes and, th erefore, recommends that the National Institute of Climate and 
Environment Studies be made functional at the earliest and preferably during this financial year. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. Department of Space (DOS) had formulated a proposal for setting up National Institute for Climate and 
Environment Studies (NICES) to undertake focused research in the area of climate and environment in a coordinated manner. 
 As decided in the 114th Space Commission Meeting held on October 29, 2009, the inter-ministerial consultation was held 
on January 14, 2010 under the aegis of the Planning Commission. It was concluded inter alia in the meeting that: 

(i) NICES be temporarily located at the National Atmosphe ric Research Laboratory (NAR L), Gadanki, near Tirupati 
of DOS, in view of the available facilities; 
(ii) NICES be co-funded by DOS, Ministry of Environment and Forests (MoEF), Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) 
and Department of Science and Technology (DST); 
(iii) Objective of Regional Climate Change Modeling to be continued with Indian “ Institute of Tropical Meteorology 
(IITM); 
(iv) A climate monitoring centre be setup/ recognized with  collaboration of DOS and MoEF, which may have a climate 
data centre wherein all scientific Ministries can provide related data available with them; 
(v) National Institute of Himalayan Glaciology (NIHG) be co-funded by DST, ISRO and MoEF. 
 The Space Commission in its 115th meeting held on March 14 , 2010 endorsed the consensus arrived at in the inter-
ministerial consultation. Accordingly, the activities pertaining to National Institute for Climate and Environment Studies 
(NICES) are being pursued as a part of National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), Gadanki, near Tirupati. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and desires  that the Department should make concerted efforts for setting up of the 
proposed National Institute for Climate and Environment Studies as per its time schedule so that focused research in the 
area of climate and environment could be undertaken at the earliest. 
TELEMEDICINE 
Recommendation (Para 9.3) 
 The Committee recommends that the telemedicine network should be expanded further by covering the most backward 
States, extreme remote and inaccessible and disease-prone areas which have been left out so far. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. ISRO has taken steps to upgrade the existing satellite modem with new generation modem. The tele-medicine 
network in Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep Islands is operating with new generation satellite systems. Similar 
activity is also carried out in Jammu & Ka shmir and North Eastern region. Upgraded ha rdware in tele-medicine centres enables 
each hospital to receive Continuing Medicinal Education (CME) and to conduct awareness programs for various diseases. 
 It is planned to expand tele-medicine network in a phased manner to cover the unreached regions. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Department will take al l possible steps to expand tele-medicine 
network to reach far flung regions. 
TELE-EDUCA TION 
Recommendation  (Para 9.4) 
 The Committee, therefore, recommends that EDUSAT Programme should be expeditiously implemented in remaining 
States of the country. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. Under the EDUSAT Programme of ISRO, during the cu rrent year, a network in Assam is established. Sikkim 
EDUSAT network is under implementation. The process of esta blishing EDUSAT networks in Uttar Pradesh and Himachal 
Pradesh is initiated. 

 Under the EDUSAT Programme, ISRO is providing one hub an d 10 interactive terminals (50 terminals in case of NE 
States) to each State/ UTs. The States/ UTs have to identify user departments for utilization and expansion of EDUSAT 
networks. ISRO is working with all the State/ UT Governments in this regard. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and urges the Department to continue its efforts in this direction. 
DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT SYSTEM 
Recommendation  (Para 9.7) 
 The Committee is of the view that India has been traditionally vulnerable to natural disasters on account of its unique geo-
climatic conditions. Floods, droughts, cyclones, earthquake and landslides have been a recurrent phenomena and a lot of 
economic devastation and human loss is caused on account of these natural calamities. The Committee feels that the services of 
Virtual Private Network (VPN) which has been set up for networking the State and District level emergency control centres for 
near real time natural disasters management must be optimally utilized for rapid dissemination of information to minimize the 
human and material loss. 
Action Taken by Government 
 Noted. The satellite based Virtual Private Network (VPN) - Phase 1, established as part of their National Emergency 
Communication Plan, presently has 34 nodes. This includes 9 primary/ data provider nodes (National Remote sensing Centre, 
Hyderabad; National Remote Sensing Centre, Shadnagar; India Meteorological Department; Central Water Commission; Indian 
National Centre for Ocean Information Services; Geological Survey of India, Delhi; Space Application Centre, Ahmedabad; 
Master Control Facility, Hassan and National Institute of Disa ster Management, Delhi), 5 observation nodes (Prime Minister 
Office; Prime Minister residence; National Disaster Manage ment Authority, Delhi; Cabinet Secretariat; and Control Room 
North Block) and 20 State Emergency Operation Centres (Andhr a Pradesh; Andaman & Nicobar Islands; Arunachal Pradesh; 
Assam; Bihar; Delhi; Gujarat; Himachal Pradesh; Kerala; Maharashtra; Manipur; Meghalaya; Mizoram; Nagaland; Orissa; 
Sikkim; Tamil Nadu; Tripura; Uttaranchal and West Bengal). The hub for the network is established at Directorate of 
Coordination, Police Wireless (DCPW) premises at Samanvaya Sadan, Siri Fort Road, New Delhi. 
 The network is being utilized for the following purposes: 
(i) Transfer of value added data of the affected regions un der emergency situations like flood, cyclone, landslide and 
earth quake. For instance, during flood disaster in various parts of the country, the flood inundation maps prepared 
from the analysis of the satellite data are disseminated to the respective State Governments through the VPN for the 
relief management. During 2009, flood inundation maps prepared for flood events in Assam, Bihar, Andhra 
Pradesh, and Orissa were placed on the VPN for utilizat ion by the respective State Governments, which were 
effectively utilized by the respective States. 
(ii) During natural disasters, the Nodal Ministries/ State Governments are contacted through VPN based video 
conferencing for understanding the ground situation and to provide support as required. 
(iii) As a part of capacity building exerci se, lectures/ presentations are organised for the benefit of participants of the 
training programmes on disaster management, organized by National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM), 
Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) from time to time. 
 Further, National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC) of DOS organised five training courses at NRSC, Hyderabad, on 
“Disaster Management Support using Space Technology” for the nodal officers of the State Disaster Management Cells. 
Comment 
 The Committee notes the reply and appreciates the actions undertaken by the Department to utilize the services of 
Virtual Private Network (VPN) for networ king the State and District level em ergency control cent res during natural 
disasters.
