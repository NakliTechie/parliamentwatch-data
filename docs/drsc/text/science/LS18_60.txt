REPORT \ 
The Report of the Committee deals ·with the Action Taken by the Department of 
Biotechnology on the recommendations contained in the Fifty-fourth Report of the 
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Envi­
ronment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (1998-99) of the Department of 
Biotechnology which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on 7th July, 1998. There are seven 
recommendations contained in the Fifty-fourth Report of the Committee. Action Taken 
notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 
20th August , 1998. 
On scrutiny pf the Action Taken Report as received from the Department of 
Biotechnology, the Committee finds that all the seven recommendations have been fully 
implemented . 
The· Committee's recommendations, action tuken thereon and comments of the 
Committee on the action taken by the Department of Biotechnology are given in the 
succeeding paragraphs:-
RECOMMENDATION 
While the Committee expresses its satisfaction over various basic and produc~oriented 
R&D activities of the Department, the Committee does feel that much of the work done by the 
Department particularly which aims at benefiting farmers, weaker sections of society and 
backward regions in the country lacks publicity. The Department should try to give wide 
publicity to its work by using all modern methods of mass communication including print and 
electronic media. 
(Para 9) 
The Committee observes that the monitoring mechanism which exist today is quite lax 
because the benefit of biotechnology based programmes is not reaching a large section of our 
population, though the Committ~e is aware of the fact that the Department of Biotechnology 
can only demonstrate the technology and it is ultimately for the concerned Ministries and 
Departments to do the needed extension work to make the technology acceptable by the 
concerned sections of the society. There is a strong need to have an effective coordinating 
mechanism between the Department, socio-economic Ministries/Departments and the State 
Governments/Bodies in order to make the biotechnology based programmes reach the people. 
(Para 17) 
Action Taken by the Department: 
Department of Biotechnology can only demonstrate the technology and it is ultimately 
for the concerned Ministries/ Departments to do the extension work to make the technology 
acceptable by the concerned sections of the society. The Department has made concerted 
effort s in popularising the technologies developed through (i) exhibitions, national and 
international level, (ii) field demonstrations in farmers' fields through State Departments and 
Universities, (iii) technology transfer to industries, (iv) by involving the user ministries both 
technically and financially in its programmes to launch joint projects and (v) convening Inter­
Ministrial Steering Committees on specific aspects of nation al needs. 

During the year 1997-98 the Department has participated in about 10 exhibitions, 
assisted 85 seminars/conferences and 6 series of 18 popular lectures in various institutions. 
Ten popular monograms in English and Hindi have been published. Representatives of 
concerned user ministries have been included as members in all the programmes, having 
impact on society specially women, weaker sections of people and rural population, Several 
projects/programmes jointly funded by Central Silk Board, Central Coffee Board, Spices 
Board, ICAR, ICMR, CSIR and State Governments have been launched involving 
concerned user ministries to ensure application of specific biotechnoiogies relevant to 
different needs. Discussions are held with State S&T Councils for developing specific 
programmes. More than 20 technologies have been transferred for commercial production of 
vaccines, diagnostics in health care, nearing 40 lakhs Tissue Culture Plantlets have been 
supplied to Forest Depart111ent and 100 demonstrations in farmers' fields to popularise use of 
biopesticides, biofertilizers, allied tissue culture plants, vermicomposting etc. have been 
undertaken. Special Inter-Ministerial Steering Committees are convened to promote applica­
tion of biotechnologies in various sectors from time to time. 
Comments: 
The Committee is satisfied with the efforts being made by the Department of 
Biotechnology for popularising the research leads made and technologies developed among the 
users. The Committee, however, recommends that the Department should keep up the tempo 
of its activities. 
RECOMMENDATION 
The Committee is of the view that in order to reduce the dependence of farmers on 
pesticides and insecticides, a vigorous research is required to be pursued for the development 
of pheromones which could control the damage caused to crops by harmful insects and pests. 
The laboratory scale technology developed by the Department in this area requires upscaling 
and further test should be conducted through pilot projects so that the efficacy of the 
technology is fully demonstrated. 
(Para 13) 
Action Taken by the Department: 
The Department of Biotechnology has initiated several research projects to reduce the 
dependence of farmers on pesticides and insecticides. Four areas have been identified for 
this purpose viz. (i) Development and production of biocontrol agents for management of 
pests, diseases and weeds, (ii) Use of natural pesticides from plant sources, (iii) Develop­
ment of pheromones, and (iv) Studies on pesticides degradation as well as monitoring 
pesticides residues in the environment. 
Eight new bio-pesticides have been developed and two pilot plants are producing bio­
control agents for use of the farmers, catering to the needs of about 0.224 million ha. An 
area of 55000 ha. have been covered in various field crops for demonstrating the use of bio­
control agents. About 20,000 farmers benefited by attending the on-site training programmes 
and extension activities. The technologies for mass production of candidate biocontrol 
agents-baculovirus, parasites, predators, antagonistics, fungi and bacteria for economically 
important crops have been transferred to industries. 
About 12 potential plant species are being investigated for their insecticidal properties at 
various centres. The research includes extraction, isolation and formulation of Botanical 
pesticides for development of safe, bio-degradable and efficient insect control agents. 

The projects on various development aspects of pheromones include synthesis of 
pheromones, formulations , preparation of luresldispensors and optimization parameters for 
field trials. Pheromone s are being used the overall Integrated Pest Management package for 
monitoring the i>ests incidence in the field with a view to decide the time and rate of release 
of biocontrol agents. Pheromones of various insects have been synthesisted and field 
evaluated in number of crops e.g. cotton, gram, potato, sorghum, maize, red gram, tomato, 
cabbage, mango and other fruits. Laboratory scale process has been developed and 
optimized for their preparation. Milligram quantities are required in the field. Various 
dispensers were developed using different support materials to have slow release rate of the 
pheromone s. A technology package for INB (Inter Node Borer) - using pheromones has 
been developed . The pheromone technology is being validated in terms of its synthesis and 
bioefficacy in various fields. The up-scaling of the process for large scale synthesis of 
pheromones through pilot projects will be undertaken by the department. 
Towards studies on pesticides degradation and monitoriing of pests residues, lhe 
Department of Biotechnology has supported projects on research for food safety. Simple 
detection kits and sophisti~ated sensitive diagnostics and biosensors have been developed to 
detect the residual levels of insecticides and fungicides Consortia of microbes have also been 
developed in the form of a biofilm which could be used for process development and for 
decontamination of pesticides/xenobiotics contaminated effluents. 
Comments: 
The Committee notes with satisfaction the progress made in the field of bio-pesticides an.d 
bio-control agents and- hopes that this will help reduce the dependence of farmers on harmful 
chemicals being used as pesticides and insectic~. 
RECOMMENDATION 
The Committee recommends that if the proposed programme for the generation of 
emplo)'Dlent opportunities for women proves successful, it could be replicated in other parts of 
the country and more rural woll)en should be associated with the programme . 
(Para 19) 
Action Takeo by the Department: 
The foundation stone for the first "Golden Jubilee Women's Biotechnology Park" at 
Chennai was formally laid by His Excellency the President" of India on 29th July 1998 at 
Chennai. The Department is pursuing the project implementation. 
Comments: 
The CommiUee recommends that the implementation of project should be pursu ed 
vigorously to ensure its completion as per schedule and without any escalation of costs. 
RECOMMENDATION 
The Committee expresses its satisfaction over the fact that BIBCOL has supplied 330 lakh 
doses of OPV to National Immunization Programme upto March, 98. The Committee is of the 
view that the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare may purchase the required number of 
doses of OPV from the BIBCOL which is a public sector company. The Committee fails to 
understand as to why after signing a rate contract for procuring 120 million doses of OPV in 
June, 1997, the Ministry purchased from the BIBCOL only 27.9 million doses. 
(Para 22) 
The Committee also stresses the need for the Department to make vigorous efforts to 
produce new and improved vaccines and immunodiagnostics such as rabies, measles 
recombinent Hepatitis 'B' vaccine, etc. as has been planned under the Product Diversification 
Plan for the BIBCOL. 
(Para 23) 

Action Taken by Department: 
The EFC memo of revised cost estimates and product diversification plan of BIBCOL 
was circulated among concerned Ministries/Departments for their comments in order to 
produce BCG, Measles, T T vaccines in addition to oral ._ polio vaccine and other 
immunobiologicals to generate additional revenue to sustain its activities. Further BIBCOL 
has planned to produce rabies and Hepatitis 'B' vaccine and Immunodiagnostics in second 
phase of its diversification plan. The Department has taken up the matter with the 
Department of Family Welfare to give supply order to procure OPV from BIBCOL during 
the current financial year (1998-99) to National Immunization Programme. 
Comments: 
Reply furnished by the Department is evasive and incomplete. The Committee bad asked 
a pointed question. After signing the rate contract for procurement of 120 million doses of 
OPV in June, 1997, why only 27.9 million doses were purchased. The ·committee would like 
to have a detailed reply ,on this specific question. 
RECOMMENDATION 
The Committee wanted to know how best the assets of the Company IVCOL are being 
utilised by the Department to minimise the losses. 
(Para 24) 
Action Taken by the Department: 
The Department .has prepared a proposal for optimum utilization of assets of IVCOL, 
for the establishment of National Brain Research Centre and also to establish Biotechnology 
Integrated Complex and other new activities and projects and the same is under 
consideration at the highest level. 
Comments: 
The Committee notes the reply.
