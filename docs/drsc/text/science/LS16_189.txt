REPORT
Over the last four decades, India has come a long way in mastering the complex space
technology and using it for various developmental activities in a self-reliant manner. The ever
challenging task of space technology development and utilization, has not only graduated from
experimental and demonstration phases to an operational era, but also provided its potential to
address the national needs. Notable progress has been made in the design, development and
operation of space systems, as well as, using them for vital services like telecommunications,
television broadcasting, meteorology, disaster management support and natural resources survey and
management, which are of direct relevance for national development. The space programme has
become largely self-reliant with capability to design and build satellites for providing space services
and to launch them using indigenously designed and developed launch vehicles. The diverse roles
of space technology and services in various fronts—social, economic, commercial and strategic-
have made the space systems an important component of our national infrastructure.
2. OBJECTIVES OF INDIAN SPACE PROGRAMME
2.1. The primary objective of the Indian Space Programme is to promote the development and
application of space technology and space science for the socio-economic benefit of the nation
through establishment of operational space services in a self-reliant manner. The main thrust areas
of the programme are:—
• Satellite-based communication for various applications.
• Satellite-based resources survey and management and environmental monitoring.
• Meteorological applications.
• Development and operationalisation of indigenous satellites, launch vehicles and
associated ground segment for providing space-based services.
3. ORGANISATIONAL SET UP
3.1. The Government of India set up the Space Commission and the Department of Space
(DOS) in 1972. DOS is responsible for implementing the policies framed by the Space Commission.
These are carried out through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and the Grant-in-
aid institutions viz. National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), Physical Research Laboratory (PRL),
National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), North-Eastern Space Applications Centre
(NE-SAC), Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL) and the recently established Indian Institute of Space
Science and Technology (IlST).
3.2. The major Centres, Units and establishments of ISRO/DOS, responsible for carrying out
research and development activities as well as for undertaking various projects and programmes,
are:—
(a) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, which provides the
technology base for the country’s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development.
(b) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities at Bangalore,
Thiruvananthapuram and Mahendragiri, which is responsible for development of liquid
propulsion and cryogenic propulsion systems for satellite and launch vehicles.

2
(c) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota(Andhra Pradesh), is the
launch base. It also provides the facilities for processing of solid rocket motors.
(d) ISRO Satellite Centre (ISAC), Bangalore, which is entrusted with the prime
responsibility for satellite development.
(e) The Laboratory for Electro-Optics Systems (LEOS), Bangalore which has the
responsibility for development and production of electro-optical sensors for satellites.
(f) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad, which carries out research and
development in space applications as well as development of application payloads.
(g) Development and Educational Communication Unit (DECU), Ahmedabad, which is
involved in the conception, definition, planning and socio/techno-economic evaluation
of development communication programmes.
(h) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network (ISTRAC), with its ground stations
at Bangalore, Sriharikota, Lucknow, Bearslake(U.K.), Biak (Indonesia), Brunei and
Mauritius, which provides the telemetry, tracking and command support and spacecraft
mission management for the Indian remote sensing satellite missions, space science
missions and satellite launch vehicle programmes.
(i) Master Control Facility (MCF) with its main facility at Hassan, Karnataka and backup
facility at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh which has the responsibility for control and
operation of INSAT/GSAT/METSAT satellites.
(j) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram, which carries out research
and development in the area of inertial systems for satellites and launch vehicles.
3.3. A National Natural Resources Management System (NNRMS), with the Department of
Space (DOS) as the nodal agency, has been established to ensure optimal utilisation of natural
resources using remote sensing data in conjunction with conventional techniques. Under NNRMS,
five Regional Remote Sensing Service Centres (RRSSCs) at Bangalore, Dehradun, Jodhpur,
Kharagpur and Nagpur have been set up for execution of remote sensing application programmes
in the respective regions.
3.4 The organisations receiving Grants-in-aid under the Department of Space are:
(a) National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), Hyderabad. An autonomous institution under
the Department of Space, it is responsible for acquisition, processing and dissemination
of satellite and aerial remote sensing data. The Indian Institute of Remote Sensing
(IIRS) of NRSA, located at Dehradun imparts training in various disciplines to user
scientists.
(b) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad. An autonomous institution
supported mainly by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences.
(c) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL) at Gadanki near Tirupati. It
conducts atmospheric research.
(d) The North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC). Established as an autonomous
body under the Department of Space, it provides space technology inputs for
developmental activities in the North Eastern Region.

3
(e) The Semi-Conductor Laboratory, Chandigarh. It is a research and development society
and is entrusted with the design and development of Very Large Scale Integrated
Circuit (VLSI) devices and development of micro-electronic systems to meet the
strategic needs of the country.
(f) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST). It is an autonomous
body under the Department with the objective of creating quality human resources
tuned to suit the requirements of state-of-art space research programme. The Institute
offers graduate, post-graduate and research programmes in the area of space science,
technology and applications. lIST has started the courses from the academic
year 2007-08 around the existing infrastructure at Thiruvananthapuram.
3.5. Apart from this, the RESPOND programme of ISRO supports sponsored research activity
in space science, space applications and space technology in various national academic/research
institutions and Space Technology Cells in premier technological institutes of the country through
grants-in-aid.
3.6 The Secretariat of Department of Space and the Headquarters of ISRO are located in
Bangalore. Programme Offices at ISRO Headquarters co-ordinate the overall programmes like
satellite communication, Satellite Navigation, earth observation, launch vehicle, space science,
atmospheric science, space industry development, disaster management support, international co-
operation, publications and public relations, budget and economic analysis and human resource
development.
3.7 The detailed budget allocation of the Department of Space for the year 2008-09 was
considered by the Department-related Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the 3rd April, 2008 and the Demands for Grants of the Department has the following
provisions.
4. ELEVENTH PLAN OUTLAYS AND PROPOSALS
4.1 The Committee has been informed that the Plan outlay allocation for the Department
for Eleventh Five Year Plan (2007-2012) is Rs. 30,883 crores. The Non-plan budgetary
support is Rs. 3,000 crores. Therefore, the total budgetary allocation during Eleventh Plan is
Rs. 33,883 crores. The total expenditure of the Department during the Tenth Plan period comes
to Rs. 12,614.92 crores comprising of Plan component of Rs. 10,870 crores and Non-plan
component of Rs. 1744.92 crores.
4.2 The programmes envisaged for the Eleventh Five Year Plan include:
• Development of advanced launch vehicle systems including GSLV Mk III and
development of critical technologies required for Reusable Launch Vehicle.
• Augmentation of INSAT/GSAT system to meet the projected demand for satellite
communication services in the country.
• Establishment of Indian Regional Navigational Satellite System. (IRNSS)
• Maintaining leadership in Earth Observation system with three series of thematic
satellites viz., Resourcesat series, Cartosat series and Ocean/atmospheric series.
• Front-ranking research in space science and planetary exploration.

4
• Manned Mission Initiatives.
• Thrust areas of applications—expansion of Tele-education, Tele-medicine, Village
Resource Centres, Satellite Navigational services, Disaster Management Support and
developmental applications in the area of natural resource management.
• Human Resource Development including setting up of Indian Institute of space science
and technology, Industry Interface, academic interface and International co-operation.
4.3 The Committee hopes that the major thrust of the Indian Space programme during
the Eleventh Five Year Plan 2007-12 would not only be towards continuing and reinforcing
the space technologies already developed but also to venture into newer and greater space
challenges by further enhancing our capabilities in the advanced areas of space technology.
Efforts should also be made towards exploring the commercial prospects in the field of space
science specially in the area of Launch Vehicle Development, so that additional funds could
be generated. The Committee also hopes that the Department would achieve the goals set
for itself during the Eleventh plan period in the field of space applications for
communication, broadcasting, remote sensing, launch vehicle technology etc. which will
ultimately benefit the society at large.
5. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
5.1 The total financial outlay for the Department of Space for the year 2008-09 is
Rs. 4074.00 crores, which includes Rs. 3600.00 for Plan and Rs. 474.00 crores for Non-Plan
expenditure, as against the allocation of Rs. 3858.60 crores and Rs. 3290.00 crores at BE and RE
stages respectively, during the year 2007-08. On being asked about the reasons for the reduction
at RE stage, the Secretary of the Department explained that the amount was reduced to comply
with the revised ceilings fixed by the Ministry of Finance taking into account the overall resource
position. The reasons for the enhanced allocations sought in 2008-09 is essentially towards meeting
the following objectives:
(i) Development of advanced launch vehicle systems. (Semi cryogenic engine/stage
development and Manned Mission Initiatives.)
(ii) Augmentation of INSA T/GSAT system to meet the projected demand for satellite
communication transponders. (GSAT-9 and follow on satellites.)
(iii) Enhancing the imaging capability and providing continuity of Earth Observation Data
for various natural resource management applications. (GEO - HR High Resolution
imager for continuous observations on earth from geo-stationary orbit for Disaster
Management Support and Natural resource management applications and Altika-Argos
mission for ocean and atmospheric studies).
(iv) Establishment of Satellite Navigation and positioning services. (Indian Regional
Navigational Satellite system)
(v) Indigenous development of microelectronic devices by upgradation of the VLSI
fabrication facilities at SCL.
5.2 The Committee was informed that the Department had projected a demand of
Rs. 4462.18 crores under Plan budget for BE 2008-09. The Planning Commission, while accepting

5
the proposed programmes, suggested the Department to reprioritise the programmes within an
outlay of Rs. 3,600.00 crores in view of the overall resource constraints. This reduction has been
absorbed by suitably phasing out the expenditure on some of the new missions such as Semi
cryogenic development, advanced communication technology satellite, Earth Observation - New
missions and other initiatives without affecting any of the ongoing programmes.
5.3 The Committee notes that the Plan allocation (2008-09), which was sought by the
Department has seen a drastic reduction of Rs. 862.18 crores and the same has been
effected at the cost of some of the new but important schemes. A similar cut had to be
made at the RE stage in 2007-08 due to resource constraints. The Committee expresses
its deep anguish over the fact that even scientific departments are not spared of the
routine reduction on the ground of resource constraint and feels that this is not a healthy
trend. This also shows the degree of seriousness and significance that Government
attaches to the scientific bodies and institutions which are being put at par with other
Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the formula of routine cut should not be applied to scientific
departments.
5.4 The Committee also expresses its concern over the cuts in Plan allocations to the
Department and recommends that the Planning Commission should thoroughly examine the
proposals, before imposing cuts, as the Indian Space Programme is vital to meet the
developmental needs of the country in a self reliant manner.
6. MONITORING MECHANISMS FOR ONGOING SCHEMES
6.1 The Department informed the Committee that in order to monitor and review the progress
of all the ongoing projects/schemes and to provide overall directions, the Department has evolved
a two-tier project management structure consisting of Project/Programme Management Council and
Project Management Board/Project Executive Committee (PMC/PMB/PEX) for all the major
projects/schemes in the area of Satellites, Launch Vehicles and Space applications. Before
formulating the budget proposals, multi-level reviews are carried out at the ISRO Centres/Units
and Project Management Boards/Management Councils of all the major projects/schemes
comprehensively covering the technical, managerial, cost and schedule aspects.
6.2 The Committee was also informed that detailed and critical analysis and reviews are then
carried out by the ISRO Headquarters with the Centres/Units/Projects. The budgeted items are
reviewed in totality including Plan and Non-plan components. The ISRO Council undertakes a
rigorous review of all the budget proposals, addressing the managerial/programmatic/financial
aspects preceded by detailed technical review of the programs/projects/activities. The essentiality of
each item, the schedule-budget linkages and cash flow requirements are addressed during
these reviews. The overall budget proposals thus formulated are reviewed by Space Commission,
a high level body constituted by Government of India, before submitting proposals to the
Government.
6.3 The Committee was also informed that the projects of the Department get the funding only
through the Government of India budgetary support. The Department is provided with built-in
mechanisms of budget monitoring/control and re-appropriation by which the budgetary grant
provided during a year is fully utilised.

6
6.4 The Committee recommends the strengthening of the existing mechanism developed
by the Department to monitor the progress of the ongoing schemes before finalizing
proposals for budgetary support, so that a clear picture regarding fund requirements can be
presented to the Planning Commission and the possibility of shortage or surrender of funds
at a later stage could be avoided.
7. VILLAGE RESOURCE CENTRES
7.1 The Committee was informed that over the last three decades, satellite based.
communication and remote sensing technologies have demonstrated their capabilities to provide
services related to education, health care, weather, land and water resources management,
mitigation of impact of natural disasters, agriculture, fisheries, land and water resources
management; livestock management, interactive vocational training towards alternate livelihood, etc.
To provide these space-based services directly to the rural areas, ISRO/DOS has launched the
Village Resource Centres (VRC) programme in association with NGOS/Trusts and state/central
agencies.
7.2 The Committee was also informed that so far more than 335 VRCs have been set up in
Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Gujarat, Orissa, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh,
Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, Bihar, Delhi, Pondicherry, Assam, Uttarakhand and Nagaland
States and around 100 more are being set up. Around 40 NGOs/Trusts/Institutes/Government
Departments are associated with ISRO/DOS under this programme.
7.3 The Committee enquired as to why no VRCs have been established in the border areas of
the North-East, the North-West, Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh. In reply the Department
stated that in Jammu and Kashmir, VRCs are proposed to be set up in co-ordination with the Army,
who already have societal service programmes in these areas. However there are VRCs in the inner
areas of Nagaland, Tripura and VRCs are at the planning stage in Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh.
Further, the Department has been advised to go about expanding the VRCs in a phased manner,
since this is a purely community based project, wherein the community ultimately takes control and
hence the community has to be ready to accept the programme first.
7.4 The Committee is of the opinion that VRC is a tool which can be used for
strengthening and uniting the country in addition to its main objective of providing
information regarding various rural services. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
efforts should be made to establish VRCs in the far-flung, inaccessible, remote border areas
of the country, to help confidence-building among the population and remove the feeling of
alienation from the country.
7.5 The Committee is of the opinion that to make VRC programme a success, there
should be proper co-ordination among all the agencies connected with the programme. In
this direction the Committee recommends that local bodies across the country, district
offices and other agencies involved in planning of the VRC programmes at the rural areas
should be inter-connected, resulting into dissemination of vital information.
7.6 The Committee also recommends that special focus should be given to issues related
to self-employment and forming self-help groups through them. The Committee further
recommends that the information should be disseminated in local language so that the local
youths and people could attach themselves fully with the programme.

8. DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT SYSTEM
8.1 The Committee has been informed that the main objective of Disaster Management Support
(DMS) Programme run by the Department is to provide Space inputs and services on a timely and
reliable basis, for the management of disaster in the country. The Decision Support Centre (DSC),
set up under the DMS programme, operates on 16x7 basis during normal time and on 24x7 basis
during disasters. In the recent past, the monitoring and impact assessment was carried out for
major floods and flash flood events in 10 States and over 200 flood inundation maps were prepared
and disseminated to concerned State and Central agencies on near-real-time basis. Assessment and
monitoring of agricultural drought was also carried out for 13 States.
8.2 The Committee desired to know whether there was any co-ordination between the various
agencies, especially the Ministry of Earth Sciences, involved in the prediction of various natural
disasters like cyclones,etc. The Committee was informed that regarding atmospheric studies and
weather forecasting, coordination exists with the concerned agencies. However the basic objective
of the Department is to provide the space based observation system and the data acquired from
them to the users. In this process, when a user wants to apply that for a specific purpose, a new
software has to be developed and validated. So, the department also develops software and
validates it.
8.3 The Committee is aware that a lot of economic devastation and human loss is caused
regularly by the floods, cyclones, storms, etc., across the country. In this direction the
Committee recommends that there should be proper co-ordination among the various
agencies involved in the DMS programme, so that there is quick dissemination of
information to the District Headquarters after the data is obtained from the satellites, in
order that human and material losses can be minimised.
9. TELEMEDICINE
9.1 Telemedicine is one of the unique applications of Space Technology for societal benefit.
DOS T elemedicine programme, which started in 2001 has been connecting remote/rural/medical
college hospitals and Mobile Units through INSAT and EDUSAT to major speciality hospitals in
cities and towns. The network covers the areas of Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Andaman and
Lakshdweep Islands, North Eastern States, mainland states including tribal districts of mainland
states like Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu Chhattisgarh, Punjab, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra
Pradesh, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Rajasthan etc. Presently under ISRO telemedicine network,
about 278 hospitals are provided with this facility with 235 remote/rural/district/medical college
hospitals connected to 43 speciality hospitals with 10 Mobile Telemedicine units that have been
established.
9.2 The Committee has been informed that the telemedicine network has not been able to
establish itself in states like Uttar Pradesh and Bihar so far, due to procedural difficulties like getting
road permits for transporting the equipment and difficulty in interface with the State Government
agencies involved in establishing the network.
9.3 The Committee is of the opinion that investment in a highly capital intensive area
like Public Health may not be always possible, particularly in the backward areas of the
country. Telemedicine provides an excellent alternative. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the telemedicine network should be expanded further to include the

8
disease-prone areas which have been left out so far, specially in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.
The Department should make concerted efforts towards solving the procedural problems
through consistent persuasion and liaisoning with the State Administration.
10. CHANDRAYAAN-1
10.1 The Committee is informed that Chandrayaan-1, which is India’s first unmanned scientific
mission to moon, is targeted for launch during second quarter of the year. The final integration and
testing of the spacecraft is in progress. The spacecraft carries eleven payloads of which five are
from India and six payloads from foreign countries such as USA, Europe and Bulgaria.
Chandrayaan-1 mission features a satellite orbiting the moon at an altitude of 100 km mapping the
topography and the mineralogical content of the lunar soil. Chandrayaan-1 will also carry a Moon
Impact Probe payload for demonstrating the technology needed towards landing on the moon's
surface. The successful design, development, fabrication and installation of a 32 meter antenna to
provide tracking command and telemetry support for the Chandrayaan-l mission is a significant
achievement for the country.
10.2 The Committee desired to know the basic objective behind the programme, as similar
programmes have been conducted by other countries as well and data have been acquired by
them. The Department clarified that the main objective of the programme is to investigate
of the distribution of various minerals and chemical elements and to have a total mapping of the
moon required for conducting further studies, which is not available to the Indian scientists
presently. The countries which have conducted such programmes do not share the information with
India.
10.3 The Committee was also informed that the allocation at BE stage for the Indian Lunar
Mission - Chandrayaan-I has been decreased to Rs.78.00 crores in 2008-09, as against Rs.96.00
crores in 2007-08.
10.4 The Committee hopes that the Chandrayaan-1 mission progresses as per schedule
and the spacecraft would be ready for launch in the second quarter of 2008. The Committee
is of the opinion that the Chandrayaan-1 mission would upgrade India’s space technological
capabilities and in future help in launching manned missions to moon.
11. HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
11.1 The Committee expressed its concern over the issue of manpower depletion in the
Department and the general decline in the number of students opting for science as career. The
Ministry clarified that over the last 5 years the manpower depletion problem has assumed alarming
proportions with 700 of the scientists leaving the Department so far. The main reason for this is
the monetary benefit that the private industry offers, where salaries are four to five times of what
the Government offers. In view of this difference, a case for Special Allowance for scientists/
engineers was taken up with the Pay Commission. However it has been rejected and instead a
contract mode of hiring of scientists at market rate has proposed. The disadvantage of the contract
scheme is that it will not generate the kind of expertise and knowledge gained by working for
longer periods. The Committee was also informed that presently there is no provision for
consultancy or research work outside the organization in the Department of Space, which is
however allowed in other scientific organizations.

9
11.2 The Committee enquired from the Department whether the scientists leaving the country
went abroad or joined the local industry. The Department clarified that a majority of them are
absorbed in the local industries. There are also foreign companies which have established their
offices here and a scientist with a PhD degree and four years of experience from ISRO, is being
paid five times the ISRO salary on absorption. In this context, the Department suggested that as
the Government salaries cannot match to that of the private industry, a sort of understanding with
the industry and the Government on the wage policy may be worked out. Such an understanding
already exists in advanced countries like Europe and America, where at entry level and even after
working for 10 or 20 years, the variation in the salaries of the scientists in government sector and
that in the private industry vary by 20 or 30 per cent at best.
11.3 The Committee expresses its serious concern at the fact that the present
remuneration being paid to the scientists are not attractive enough to attract the best of
scientific talent available in the country. Further, to make matters worse; there is a high
level of attrition of scientists from the Department, who move towards the private sector
for better salaries and promotion prospects. The Committee recommends that the case for
grant of special allowance over and above the normal salaries may be taken up emphatically
with the Government, keeping in view the special requirements of the Department, and the
scientific community at large, specially in view of the tremendous contributions of the
scientists in the field of Space. The Committee also recommends that in order to retain the
scientists in the Department enhanced increment at the rate of 25 to 30 per cent may also
be seriously considered for the scientists who perform and deliver.
11.4 The Committee is of the opinion that to contain the mass exodus of scientists from
our premier institutions, formulation of a wage policy has become imperative, keeping in
view the huge difference that exists between the Public and Private sectors. The Committee
recommends that efforts must be made to work out an understanding on wages on the lines
of the one already existing in the advanced countries.
11.5 The Committee also expresses its concern over the fact that there is no separate head for
Human Resources Development whereby young and motivated people can be recruited in the
organization and also notes with concern the fact that although a provision of Rs. 75.00 crores
was made in B.E.2007-08, it was revised to Rs. 25.00 crores in R.E. 2007-08 due to under
utilization of funds and the allocation in B.E.2008-09 has been reduced to Rs. 65.25 crores.
11.6 The Committee was informed that Human Resource Development is one of the priority
areas to be taken up during the Eleventh Plan period. Though Human Resource Development (HRD)
has not been put under any specific head, yet last year, the total expenditure on HRD was nearly
Rs. 100 crores and it was spent at two levels. Firstly, at the entry level itself people are given a
four-month orientation course. Subsequently, in the middle level, programmes are conducted for
professional upgradation where trained officers impart specialized training at various centres.
11.7 The Committee was also informed that a specialized Institute viz. the ‘Indian Institute of
Space Science and Technology’ (IIST), with a view to capture the talent at ‘plus two’ level, in
order to create quality human resources in the country for ISRO has already started functioning
in a temporary campus from the academic year 2007-08 and will train people in the field of
space technology, with a guarantee that they will work for a minimum of five years in the
organisation. The reduction in allocation from Rs. 75.00 crores (B.E.2007-08) to Rs. 65.25 crores
(B.E.2008-09) was due to delay in setting up of the permanent campus for the Institute because
of delay in land acquisition.

11.8 The Committee is of the opinion that the Department has delayed the creation of a

catering specifically for space technology has been set up just a year ago, though the

SUMMARY OF RECOMMENDATIONS
1. The Committee hopes that the major thrust of the Indian Space programme during
the Eleventh Five Y ear Plan 2007-12 would not only be towards continuing and reinforcing
the space technologies already developed but also to venture into newer and greater space
challenges by further enhancing our capabilities in the advanced areas of space technology.
Efforts should also be made towards exploring the commercial prospects in the field of space
science specially in the area of Launch Vehicle Development, so that additional funds could
be generated. The Committee also hopes that the Department would achieve the goals set
for itself during the Eleventh plan period in the field of space applications for
communication, broadcasting, remote sensing, launch vehicle technology etc which will
ultimately benefit the society at large. (Para 4.3)
2. The Committee notes that the Plan allocation (2008-09), which was sought by the
Department has seen a drastic reduction of Rs. 862.18 crores and the same has been
effected at the cost of some of the new but important schemes. A similar cut had to be
made at the RE stage in 2007-08 due to resource constraints. The Committee expresses its
deep anguish over the fact that even scientific departments are not spared of the routine
reduction on the ground of resource constraint and feels that this is not a healthy trend.
This also shows the degree of seriousness and significance that government attaches to the
scientific bodies and institutions which are being put at par with other Ministries/
Departments of the Government of India. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
formula of routine cut should not be applied to scientific departments. (Para 5.3)
3. The Committee also expresses its concern over the cuts in Plan allocations to the
Department and recommends that the Planning Commission should thoroughly examine the
proposals, before imposing cuts, as the Indian Space Programme is vital to meet the
developmental needs of the country in a self reliant manner. (Para 5.4)
4. The Committee recommends the strengthening of the existing mechanism developed
by the Department to monitor the progress of the ongoing schemes before finalizing
proposals for budgetary support, so that a clear picture regarding fund requirements can be
presented to the Planning Commission and the possibility of shortage or surrender of funds
at a later stage could be avoided. (Para 6.4)
5. The Committee is of the opinion that VRC is a tool which can be used for
strengthening and uniting the country in addition to its main objective of providing
information regarding various rural services. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
efforts should be made to establish VRCs in the far-flung, inaccessible, remote border areas
of the country, to help confidence-building among the population and remove the feeling of
alienation from the country. (Para 7.4)
6. The Committee is of the opinion that to make VRC programme a success, there
should be proper co-ordination among all the agencies connected with the programme.
In this direction the Committee recommends that local bodies across the country,
district offices and other agencies involved in planning of the VRC programmes at the

(Para 7.5)
7. The Committee also recommends that special focus should be given to issues related
to self-employment and forming self-help groups through them. The Committee further
recommends that the information should be disseminated in local language so that the local
youths and people could attach themselves fully with the programme. (Para 7.6)
8. The Committee is aware that a lot of economic devastation and human loss is caused
regularly by the floods, cyclones, storms, etc., across the country. In this direction the
Committee recommends that there should be proper co-ordination among the various
agencies involved in the DMS programme so that there is quick dissemination of
information to the District Headquarters after the data is obtained from the satellites, in
order that human and material losses can be minimised. (Para 8.3)
9. The Committee is of the opinion that investment in a highly capital intensive area
like Public Health may not be always possible, particularly in the backward areas of the
country. Telemedicine provides an excellent alternative. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the telemedicine network should be expanded further to include the
disease-prone areas which have been left out so far, specially in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.
The Department should make concerted efforts towards solving the procedural problems
through consistent persuasion and liaisoning with the State administration. (Para 9.3)
10. The Committee hopes that the Chandrayaan-1 mission progresses as per schedule
and the spacecraft would be ready for launch in the second quarter of 2008. The Committee
is of the opinion that the Chandrayaan-1 mission would upgrade India’s space technological
capabilities and in future help in launching manned missions to moon. (Para 10.4)
11. The Committee expresses its serious concern at the fact that the present
remuneration being paid to the scientists are not attractive enough to attract the best of
scientific talent available in the country. Further, to make matters worse, there is a high
level of attrition of scientists from the Department, who move towards the private sector
for better salaries and promotion prospects. The Committee recommends that the case for
grant of special allowance over and above the normal salaries may be taken up emphatically
with the Government, keeping in view the special requirements of the Department, and the
scientific community at large, specially in view of the tremendous contributions of the
scientists in the field of Space. The Committee also recommends that in order to retain the
scientists in the Department enhanced increment at the rate of 25 to 30% may also be
seriously considered for the scientists who perform and deliver. (Para 11.3)
