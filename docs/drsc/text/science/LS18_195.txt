REPORT
The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science and
Technology on the recommendations contained in the One hundred and Eighty-fifth Report of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the Demands for Grants (2008-09) of the Department of Science and Technology,
which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 29th April, 2008. There were twenty-
six recommendations in the One hundred and Eighty-fifth Report of the Committee. Action Taken
notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on
21st August, 2008.
Recommendation (Para 2.2)
The Committee has been informed that the Department’s plan outlay of Rs. 1526 crores at
the BE stage last year was reduced to Rs.1270 crores at the RE stage. The Department, however,
was unable to spend even the reduced Grants during the last year. The Committee takes serious
note of the situation and recommends that the Department should be more prompt and efficient in
utilising the allocated funds. This acquires even more importance because the rise in allocation this
year, the Committee feels, is miniscule, if put in the context of inflation and economic growth. At
the same time the Committee is of the opinion that, keeping in mind the responsibilities of the
Department and the role it is expected to play in development of science and technology, the
Department should be given an increase in allocation in consonance with its requirements.
Action Taken by the Government
It is a fact that Department of Science and Technology Plan outlay was Rs.1526 crore at
the BE stage. This was reduced to Rs.1270 crore at the RE stage. Accounts for the financial year
2007-08 are under process. As per the provisional figures, expenditure of Rs.1266.18 crore took
place during financial year 2007-08. This amounts to 99.70 per cent of the budget provided at the
RE stage. The Department has successfully utilized the reduced budget at the RE stage.
Comments
The Committee expresses its concern that the funds allocated remained underutilized
leading to a reduction at RE stage in the last financial year and is of the opinion that a
comprehensive introspection by the department is required, to find out the reasons for the
under utilization and taking corrective measures to avoid such a recurrence in this financial
year.
Recommendation (Para 4.2)
The Committee welcomes the constitution of Expert Advisory Committees to monitor and
review the progress of its ongoing schemes and is of the opinion that such a step can be helpful
in prioritization and rationalisation of schemes.
Action Taken by the Government
Scientific Engineering Research Council: The Department has constituted several Expert
Advisory Committees in various areas of Science and Engineering. These Expert Committees advise
the Department in the formulation of guidelines for receiving and processing research proposals for

2
support under the scheme; the suitability and extent of grant for research programmes; make
suitable recommendations on the choice of discipline and specific fields which need increased
support for research and evaluate the progress of each research programme supported by it and
make suitable recommendations regarding its continuation or otherwise.
Survey of India (SOI): has formulated a review/monitoring committee under the
Chairmanship of Surveyor General of India wherein the progress of each and every plan schemes
is being monitored regularly.
The activities under various plan schemes, the target of which have been met/have been
concluded, have been taken out from the schemes. The available schemes have been reprioritized
as per the availability of annual plan fund. In order to continue the on-going schemes for which
budget is not available, the funds for the same are being projected in RE.
As regards the National Training Programme for Scientists/Technologists working with
Government of India, the comments of the Committee have been noted.
Drug and Pharma Research Programme, Technology Systems Group Programme, Instrument
Development Programme and Water T echnology Initiatives: The Expert Committees have been formed
for monitoring and reviewing the progress of these schemes in line with the recommendations of the
Committee. These Committees are constituted for a specific period to guide and monitor the schemes.
Science and Technology Entrepreneurship Development: All the projects under Science and
Technology Entrepreneurship Development scheme are implemented with the guidance of Expert
Advisory Committees. The guidance of the respective Committees is taken by the Department in
prioritization and rationalization of various programmes under the said scheme.
Science and Technology Communication: The programmes of Science and Technology
Communication will be reviewed through a Committee of experts as recommended by the
Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee. The review will be useful in drawing up
areas for intervention on priority and bringing more focus to the efforts.
Technology Development Board: The Government of India constituted the Technology
Development Board (TDB) in September 1996 under the Technology Development Board Act, 1995.
As per the TDB Act, the Board has 11 members consisting of 6 Secretaries to the Government
of India who are in charge of:—
(1) Department of Science and T echnology Chairperson
(2) Department of Scientific and Industrial Research ex-Officio Member
(3) Department of Defence Research and Development ex-Officio Member
(4) Department of Industrial Development ex-Officio Member
(5) Department of Rural Development ex-Officio Member
(6) Department of Expenditure (Finance) ex-Officio Member
The Board has upto 4 nominated members from persons having experience in technology
development and application, banking and finance, industry, agriculture and rural development. It
has a Secretary to the Board as a member.
TDB constitutes Committees as per its requirements.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the Department and hopes that it will
lead to speedy implementation of various schemes.

Recommendation (Para 7.2 and 7.3)
The Secretary of the Department during the presentation made to the Committee has
informed that Science and Engineering Research Council (SERC) needs an increase in allocation of
about 25 per cent in order to fulfill its objectives. The Committee, keeping in mind the huge array
of responsibilities for promotion of research in newly emerging and frontier areas of science and
engineering including multidisciplinary fields; need for encouraging young scientists to take up
challenging R&D activities and imperatives to promote basic and applied research in frontline areas
of engineering and technology in various academic and research institutions, etc., among many
others, recommends that sufficient budgetary support may be provided so that the programme
could be pursued with vigour.
The Committee has been informed that around 3000 research proposals are received by the
Department every year out of which 500-600 proposals are selected for funding. The Committee
is of the view that lack of fund should not be allowed to come in the way of funding good and
promising research proposals.
Action Taken by the Government
A Plan allocation (BE) of Rs. 415.0 crore has been made for Research and Development
Support (SERC) scheme in 2008-09 as against the previous financial year’s of Rs.344.0 crore
(BE). The support to research is enhanced by creating increased and expanded knowledge in basic
research, excellence in science and engineering, innovation and promotion of selected areas,
encouragement for industrial partnership in projects under engineering and technology, training of
manpower and encouragement to young scientists. A number of fellowships; like, Ramanna
Fellowship for performing scientists, Ramanujan Fellowships to attract outstanding scientists and
engineers from all over the world to take up scientific research in our country; and J.C Bose
Fellowships to recognize and support active, performing Indian scientists and engineers, would be
instituted in 2008-09.
The SERC of the Department has emerged as the single largest support system engaged in
promoting basic research in all areas of science and engineering and has achieved significant
success in furthering the growth of research in new inter-disciplinary areas in science and
engineering. SERC has been considered as the highest standard of Peer-Review and transparent
mechanism in promoting R&D across ‘disciplines’ and ‘institutions’. There is a genuine need of
enhancing the critical mass of scientists with quality. The Department has planned several
innovative methods to enhance the number to meet the rising challenges ahead. Programmes for
elevating group dynamics through special initiatives, mentoring, etc., are some of the immediate
steps for enhancing the number with quality. Through ‘mentoring’ the expertise and experience of
a large number of retired and near retiring scientists could be utilized. It targets an augmentation
initiative for such proposals which fall short of the required benchmark by a narrow margin. At
present, there is no constraint for funding good and promising research proposals.
Comments
The Committee is of the opinion that the present generation should be motivated
through various incentives to take up research in frontier areas of science and, therefore,
reiterates that adequate budgetary support be provided so that funds shortage does not
come in the way of promising research proposals.
Recommendation (Para 8.3)
The success of Science can be gauged by the fact as to how far it has been able to touch

4
and ameliorate the life and lot of one and all. The Committee feels that going by this yardstick a
lot more is desired to be done. The Committee is of the opinion that science should come out of
the confines of laboratories to make its outreach to the common masses and it needs to be done
in a time bound manner or a sustained basis. This becomes all the more imperative as India needs
to shed its tag of a developing nation and acquire the stature of a developed one, the sooner the
better, and it is none other but science which holds the magic stick to do wonders. DST being
the nodal Ministry, the onus lies with it. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Department of Science and Technology should coordinate with all other scientific departments and
institutions to move forward in this direction and lay more and more emphasis on S&T programme
for socio-economic development.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department of Science and Technology (DST) has good mechanism for coordinating
with other scientific departments and institutions for developing and implementing S&T
programmes for socio-economic development. For example, representatives of such departments/
institutions are members of Expert Advisory Committee and they also help DST in monitoring the
progress of such programmes.
Comments
The Committee emphasises the need for proper coordination among various agencies
involved, so as to ensure that there is no duplication and wastage of resources in achieving
the same goal.
Recommendation (Para 9.4 and 9.5)
The Committee acknowledges the important role that Technology Development Programmes
(TDP) plays in translating lab-scale research into technology mode but for which the benefit of
science cannot be reaped. The Committee, therefore, desires that Technology Development
Programmes should be further strengthened and intensified so that the advantages of new
breakthroughs in the field of science could be taken to the masses at the earliest and at an
affordable cost.
The Committee is also of the view that developing indigenous technology through joint
projects is also of utmost importance because without industry participation such projects will not
only become exorbitant but also lack the commercial dimension which is very important for
successful culmination of such projects.
Action Taken by the Government
The recommendations of the Committee have been taken note of. In this connection, it is
mentioned that the Technology Development Board is the first organization of its kind within the
Government framework with the sole objective of translating the fruits of indigenous research into
commercial products or services.
The Board plays a pro-active role by encouraging commercial enterprises to take up
technology oriented projects.
Technology Development Board aims at accelerating the development and commercialisation
of indigenous technology or adapting imported technology to wider domestic application. The board
provides financial assistance in the form of equity, soft loans or grants.

Recommendation (Para 14.3)
The Committee is of the view that in a country where poverty is rampant and substantial
part of population suffers from diseases, a continued effort in drug research is required for
providing cheaper medicines. Continued support to drug research programmes should be provided
to enable the scientists to find cheaper and effective drugs. The Committee recommends that drug
research programmes should focus on diseases which are afflicting the large populace of our
country.
Action Taken by the Government
Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Research Programme (DPRP) of DST invariably gives
importance for the R&D proposals relating to developing cheaper and effective new drugs on
diseases; such as, Tuberculosis, Malaria, Dengue, Diarrhoea, Kala-Azar, Diabetes, HIV/AIDS,
Asthma, Arthritis, Osteoporosis, Japanese Encephalitis, Amoebiasis, which are afflicting the large
population in the country. In addition, in line with the recommendation of the Committee, the
Government has approved providing grants-in-aid to Indian pharmaceutical industry for R&D
projects involving clinical trials (Phase I, II and III) to develop new drugs for neglected diseases;
such as, tuberculosis, malaria, kala-azar, filariasis, etc. The Indian pharma industry would thus be
able to develop effective and cheaper drugs for poorer society of our country.

Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that drug research programmes would be
expedited.
Recommendation (Para 14.5)
The Committee reiterates it earlier recommendation that more efforts should be made to
make alternative system of medicines successfully fill in the gap in areas where conventional cures
are not available. The Committee felt that research in traditional/alternative systems should focus
on areas where conventional cures are either inadequate or too costly.
Action Taken by the Government
The Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Research Programme (DPRP), since its inception during
1994-95, up to March 2008, had forged 88 industry institutional collaborative R&D projects for
drug development on various diseases which include 32 projects in traditional/alternative systems.
In addition, 36 national facilities for the benefit of pharma industry including industries of
traditional and alternative systems have been established in different universities/national
laboratories. The programme has extended 40 soft loans to pharma industries to undertake R&D
projects for drug development. The treatment for diseases like Psoriasis, Leucoderma, HIV/AIDS
and Rheumatoid Arthritis is either costly or inadequate in allopathic system of medicines. Research
projects have been covered under traditional system of medicines to develop drugs for such
diseases. The Committee’s recommendation is noted to give thrust in such areas.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

Recommendation (Para 16.4)
The Committee feels that the geographical reach of Science in Higher Education (SHE)
should also be extended to distant parts ofthe country like Ladakh and the Northeast and other
backward states. The Committee has also been informed that the Department was unable to
implement the ‘SHE’ scheme because it required consultation and approval of several departments.
The Committee recommends that the Department expedite the process of consultation along with
other Departments for speedy execution of proposals. Beside this the Committee also recommends
that a special effort should be made to increase the reach of the programmes to students who are
not as advantaged as in the Metros but are equally deserving and this should specially take into
consideration girl students who are unable to take advantage of opportunities.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department has carried out extensive national consultation, including with the state
educational bodies. Selections of students are being made by taking proportionally uniform
distribution among various States, including Ladakh, the Northeast and other backward states. The
EFC approved the SHE component and it is at final stages of approval by the Government. The
EFC memos for other components are at appraisal stage. The scheme would be announced in
electronic as well as in print media for wider dissemination. All efforts would be made to reach
the rural areas primarily through regional, local media/newspapers. Preliminary data studies show
that girl students have outnumbered the boys for the proposed fellowship.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the fact that the department has given due consideration
to the underprivileged students from the far flung and backward states of the country and
hopes that in future the scheme would receive wider popularization.

In the light of the serious crunch in availability of quality scientists, schemes like INSPIRE

Comments

Recommendation (Para 17.5)
The Committee is of the view that the Department should prioritize its activities and focus
on its core competencies rather than spreading its resources too thin. The top-most priority should

be laid on ‘catching them young’.
Action Taken by the Government
Developing and nurturing of human resource in Science and Technology has always been
on top of the Department’s agenda. A number of ongoing schemes have been strengthened to
attract research scholars; namely, Young Scientists/Women Scientists Fellowships; Swarnajayanthi
Fellowships and J.C. Bose and Ramanujam Fellowships for practicing scientists, Junior/Senior
Research Fellowships (JRF/SRF), etc. More recently, the fellowships of JRF/SRF have been
enhanced. INSPIRE and SHE are two noticeable and significant steps towards ‘catching them
young’.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the scheme would be able to achieve its objectives of
attracting more and more research scholars and thereby provide much needed scientific
manpower.
Recommendation (Para 18.4 and 18.5)
The Committee is of the opinion that a project for development of low cost filters for
domestic purposes should be given top-most priority as millions of households in this country, for
want of affordable filters, suffer from waterborne diseases. Integrating effective technologies to
purify drinking water supplied by civic authorities is yet another challenge to be taken by the
Department in larger interest. The Committee would also like the Department to enter into
partnership with Industry so as to make such products commercial and popular.
The Committee is also of the opinion that such water treatment technology should be made
available in large scale catering to the needs at community level and arsenic, fluoride, iron and other
hazardous minerals should be removed before it reaches households. The Committee is of the
further view that there is an urgent need to develop models which would be low cost, low power
consuming and which can benefit people of different parts of the country afflicted by the problem.

10
Action Taken by the Government
In line with the opinion of the Committee, DST has initiated a project, to be jointly
implemented by Institute of Mineral and Materials Technology (IMMT), Bhubaneshwar, Central
Institute of Plastic Engineering and Technology (CIPET), Chennai and concerned states, under
which terafil water filters would be manufactured with the help of local entrepreneurs/NGOs for
large scale demonstrations in 5 States. These would enhance the confidence of the manufacturers
for commercializing the technology.
The Department has taken initiative to set up community based plants for removal of iron,
arsenic, dissolved salts, etc., from water for providing safe drinking water to the community. In
addition to the 16 such plants as mentioned earlier in the Demand for Grants (2008-2009), 15 more
plants have been sanctioned for setting up in the State of Nagaland for iron removal. The
demonstration of the low cost house hold filter or arsenic removal has been initiated in the Balia
Distt., UP in addition to the earlier demonstration made in all the homes of the village Lalmath in
Nadia Distt., West Bengal.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the implementation and commercialization of the water
purification and water treatment technology would receive adequate support from the
department, so that pure and safe drinking water may be supplied across the country.
Recommendation (Para 19.3)
The Committee is of the opinion that an innovation infrastructure to link knowledge

effective public-private partnership model is the need of the hour for future successes of research
initiatives.
Action Taken by the Government
Necessary approval for implementation of the Innovation Clusters scheme during the
Eleventh Five Year Plan has been obtained. As recommended by the Committee various models
under Public Private Partnership would be tried during the implementation of the scheme.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 20.3)
The convergence of technology and security issues is indicative of the highly technological
world we are living in. Technology not only determines how we live our lives but also how we
protect ourselves from negative forces determined to destroy our social and economic fabric. It
is here that the Committee feels that technology has an important role to play in intelligence
gathering as well as monitoring internal security situation and it urges that the Department should
do all that is possible to achieve breakthroughs in security technology in a time bound manner so
that we could be in a position to effectively tackle the threats posed to our internal security and
improve our intelligence gathering capabilities.

11
Action Taken by the Government
Since Security Technology is a new scheme which is being introduced in the Eleventh Five
Year Plan, necessary approvals are being sought for implementation of the scheme. The
recommendations of the Parliamentary Standing Committee are well taken by the Department. The
Department would try its level best to achieve some fruitful results as outcome of this scheme to
effectively tackle the threats posed to our security.
Comments
The Committee is of the view that technology plays an important role in the
protection of society from divisive forces and hence recommends that ‘Security Technology’
scheme should be accorded top priority.
Recommendation (Para 21.4)
The Committee welcomes the symbiotic research initiative taken by the Department along with
Department of Atomic Energy (DAE). Identification of areas where an effective partnership of the
two departments can bring about an effective capacity building in the university and academic sector
for building mega faculties for basic research is of utmost importance as it is a known fact that
without progress in basic science, technological progress cannot sustain itself for very long.
Action Taken by the Government
The Department has noted the recommendations by the Committee. The EFC memorandum
is at final stages of approval.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the department will explore other
avenues of cooperation and partnership with other agencies in the area of research.
Recommendation (Para 21.5)
The Committee has been informed that the Department has developed a soil-testing kit,
which costs Rs.3000 per unit. The Committee would like the Department to popularise the kit
among farmers and rope in Industry to promote the product. The Committee also recommends that
the Department should coordinate with the Ministry of Agriculture to promote its uses among the
farmers. It also recommends that at least one soil testing kit be made available free of cost at the
Panchayat or Village, level so that even small and marginal farmers, who otherwise can’t afford
to purchase it, are not deprived of its benefits.
Action Taken by the Government
In accordance with the recommendations of the Parliamentary Standing Committee, efforts
are being made to coordinate with the Ministry of Agriculture for promoting the use of Soil Testing
Kit among the farmers and also making available the kit at Panchayat Level. Other avenues for
promoting the use of Soil Testing Kit among the farmers are also being explored.
Comments
The Committee hopes that the soil testing kit would be popularized and made
available to farmers across the country.

12
Recommendation (Para 22.2)
The Committee is of the view that the ‘Science Express’ programme of the Department has
shown encouraging results and should be further intensified and expanded to the rural and
backward areas of the country. There is need for better monitoring of the programme for crowd
management and more such expresses should be introduced so that viewers get more time while
viewing.
Action Taken by the Government
In light of the recommendation, we are working on modifying the exhibits in the Science
Express and scheduling an itinerary to cover rural and backward areas of the country. The
platforms will have better arrangements for managing the visitors and television monitors will be
installed to screen science films to utilize the waiting period of the visitors. Longer halts will ensure
viewers get more time inside the exhibition. The second phase will commence this winter (October-
November, 2008) and will have about 50 halts.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the science popularisation schemes
such as “Science Express” may be given adequate financial support.
Recommendation (Para 23.2)
After considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Science and Technology
assessing the progress of various important programmes and projects administered by it, the
Committee recommends that the budgetary allocation of Rs. 1780.00 crores under Demand No. 83
(Department of Science and Technology) as sought by the Department for the year 2008-09 may
be approved.
Action Taken by the Government
Department of Science and Technology has been allocated budget of Rs.1530 crore under
Demand No. 83 for the financial year 2008-09. There is gap of Rs.250 crore in regard to the
recommendation of the Parliamentary Standing Committee to provide budget of Rs.1780 crore.
Additional funds will be sought at the RE stage, if the level of expenditure is significant and the
requirements of more funds for the various schemes of the department is justified.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
