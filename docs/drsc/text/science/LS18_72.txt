R E P O R T
 
The Department of Science & Technology (DST) has been set up to act as nodal department for organizing, co-
ordinating and promoting S&T activities in the country.  The responsibilities    of DST include, among others to formulate
 policy statements and guidelines on S&T, and also to promote new areas of scientific research and act as a Co-ordinator of
 inter-departmental S&T efforts. The department is also responsible for providing support for basic and applied research in
 universities and other institutions and high quality research in autonomous bodies, interaction in societal programmes, and
 entrepreneurship development, Science popularisation, communication and S&T Management Information System,
 promotion of commercialisation of home grown technology, support to technology development programmes, technology
 information and assessment are also part of the mandate of the department. 
2.      Besides working in the field of international Science & Technology cooperation, its activities are also oriented in the
 undermentioned fields:
(a)  Surveying & Mapping (SOI & NATMO);
            (b) Meteorology (IMD, NCMRWF); and
(c) Technology Development and Applications (TDB).
3.      In his presentation before the Committee, the Secretary, DST mentioned that in order to promote grass root level
 invention the department has set up Innovation Foundation. Swarna Jayanti Fellowships have been initiated for outstanding
 young scientists. Funds have been earmarked for improvement of S&T Infrastructure (FIST) in universities and related
 institutions. The department is also working for Kishore Vigyanik Protsahan and Technopreneur Promotion Program (TePP)
 apart from Indian Climate Research work.  The department has also established high performance computing network
 facilities for research purposes and is in the process of strengthening Seismicity Programme.  The department is trying hard to
 modernise autonomous research institutions and to set up a National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration
 (NABL).  The department also proposes to set up a Patent Facilitating Cell to provide support to scientists and technologists
 on all aspects of patenting .The department also serves as a nodal agency in the field of International S&T Co-operation.
4.      As regard the objectives and achievements of the Department, the Committee was informed that during the 9th Plan the
 Department would lay emphasis on building and sustaining a strong science & technology base in the country, developing
 centres of excellence in the frontline areas of science & technology, and modernising the infrastructure of its autonomous
 research institutions. Under the SERC scheme new initiatives would be launched to further strengthen the S&T base in the
 country in selected areas of technological importance.  The overall approach would be to promote basic research around
 outstanding scientific groups and to a large extent in the academic sector.  The SERC would be provided more autonomy and
 flexibility for better management of R&D schemes and their proper monitoring and evaluation.  Some of the important
 initiatives under the SERC include promotion of research in less endowed universities; setting up Engineering Research
 Centres in the areas of Concurrent Engineering, Bio-Engineering, Technical Acoustics etc; providing industrial research
 fellowships to work in the industry; encouraging some of the frontier areas like biodiversity, noise control, plasma chemistry,
 micro robotics, chemical engineering sciences, modern classical optics, stress biology and immunology, boundary layer
 modelling, study of atmospheric chemistry etc.  The research areas for the engineering sciences, would be in the disciplines
 like Chemical Engineering; Materials, Mining and Mineral Engineering; Robotics; Computer Engineering; etc.  Under the

6.      National Science and Technology Manpower Information System (NSTMIS), has now proposed to set up  under the
 National Science Manpower Information System, and it is proposed to be integrated with the ongoing National Technical
 Manpower Information System of the Ministry of Human Resource Development.  An on-line access to international
 databases on S&T statistics would also be established.  In the area of technology development, a more holistic and user-
friendly approach would be adopted towards making the high technologies and products available to the society with thrust on
 technology missions, technology promotion and special technology projects through critical technology promotion, high
 technology intervention in selected export areas and promotion of NGO/society/organisation.  In respect of the Instrument
 Development Programme, it is proposed to support new projects for the development of instruments in some new areas like
 energy monitoring and management, sensors etc. besides conducting training programmes and transfer of know-how of
 instruments to industries.
7.      The new initiatives under drugs and pharmaceutical research include: drug development based on traditional systems of
 medicine like Ayurveda, Unani, and Sidha besides Homoeopathy and support to joint projects leading to drug development
 for infectious diseases.  For undertaking technology projects on a mission mode, the new areas include agro food processing,
 waterways, road transportation, electric power, telecommunication, aviation, sensors, IPR and future technology forecast for
 India for 2020.  The thrust of the S&T Entrepreneurship Development activities is on strengthening and expansion of the
 entrepreneurship development cells and the S&T Entrepreneurship Parks.  Efforts were being made to initiate rural enterprise
 development through innovative S&T based micro-enterprises in rural areas.
8.      The department under the Science communication and popularisation programme propose to impart training in S&T
 communication through short-term courses for voluntary organisations, teachers; support to popular science magazines in
 regional languages; production and screening of science films on Doordarshan; development of low-cost toys, dissemination
 of software for S&T communication/popularisation etc.  The National Resources Data Management System (NRDMS) would
 concentrate on building specialised problem oriented databases and operationalising them for real-life planning and
 administrative decision making.
9.      The Committee was informed that the activities of the State S&T Councils/Departments would be intensified so as to 
 enable them to take up major projects and its impact could be seen during the Ninth Plan.  Under the international S&T co-
operation programme, efforts would be made to strengthen bilateral and multi-lateral joint venture programmes and to expand
 and accelerate the pace of S&T co-operation with research institutions in a number of countries.  The 13 autonomous research
 institutions, being supported by the DST were to be upgraded with state-of-the-art research facilities in a phased manner.  In
 addition, funds would be provided for improvement of S&T infrastructure in universities and related institutions.
10.    The India Meteorological Department (IMD) would further intensify its high technology efforts in improving the
 accuracy of predicting severe weather, its timely warning and dissemination of its long range predictions of the monsoon. 
 The major activities proposed during the Ninth Plan include: modernisation of the observational and telecommunication
 systems, use of contemporary radar and satellite technology for better understanding of weather systems, satellite-based
 dissemination of timely warning of tropical cyclones, monitoring of seismic activities, exploitation of remote sensing data for
 agro-meteorological advisories, etc.
11.    While the Committee is happy to note that the Department  would give a thrust on consolidating the achievements made
 so far, the Committee observes that some of the areas like weather forecasting systems need a focussed attention for effecting
 improvements.  At the same time there is need to review the schemes of Swarn Jayanti Fellowships and Kishore Vaigyanik
 Protsahan and Technopreneur Promotion Programme taking into account the response received and remove the bottlenecks, if
 any, in spread of scientific temper and approach among the youth.

IMPROVEMENT OF S&T INFRASTRUCTURE IN UNIVERSITIES AND RELATED INSTITUTIONS (FIST)
32.    To start with, 15 identified departments would be selected for support, to improve quality of teaching and research
 through modernisation of laboratories and by augmenting library facilities.  Upgradation of basic research facilities would
 also be undertaken.  The support under this programme would be to the tune of Rs.50 lakhs to Rs.1 crore for 5 years.
 Subsequently, 10 identified departments would be considered for providing state-of-art equipment and laboratories
 conforming to GLP norms for conducting internationally competitive research.  At this level, the support would be in the
 range of Rs. 1-2 crores.
33.    In recent years concern has been expressed about the lack of infrastructural facilities for imparting good quality higher
 education and conducting advanced research.  As a result of the ongoing efforts, Departments in the universities and related
 institutions have made some impact on the development of teaching and research.  These University departments are now in a
 stage where they would be identified for selective strengthening of their infrastructure for post-graduate education and
 research.  It is necessary to extend such infrastructural support to other higher technical educational institutions and medical
 education institutions.  Considering all these factors, a major new programme named Fund for improvement of S&T
 infrastructure in universities and related institutions (FIST) has been initiated.  This would involve providing basic
 infrastructure and the enabling facilities and environment for promoting R&D in new and emerging areas and attract fresh
 talent.

DRUGS AND PHARMACEUTICALS RESEARCH
37.    Research for new drugs is a challenging area of research due to complexity of the biological system and disease process. 
 The scheme on Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Research was initiated in 1994-95 for promoting R&D in the drugs and
 pharmaceuticals’ sectors, development of new molecules leading to commercialisable technology towards new drug
 development, giving special thrust to the development of drugs for treatment of diseases prevalent in the Indian climatic zone
 and promoting R&D in all systems of medicines bringing together the scientific expertise existing in the country in research
 institutions and industry on a joint platform in a project mode and establishment of mechanisms and linkages so as to
 facilitate the development of new drugs by the Indian Industry and the Research Institutes.
38.    The committee was informed that the Department would continue efforts to support joint research projects for the drug
 development in the area of diseases which are of importance to our country i.e. Tuberculosis, Cancer, Gastrointestinal
 disorders like Diarrhoea, Diabetes, Hypertension, Cardio-Vascular disorders and other prevalent infectious diseases in
 particular acute respiratory infections, and food & water borne diseases etc.  Special emphasis is planned to be given towards
 development of novel drug delivery systems and "Diagnostics" for various diseases of national importance.
39.    With regard to development of " Herbal Drugs  ", the Committee was further informed that the programmes included
 standardisation of traditional medicines so far are expected to be concluded successfully. In one of the research programmes
 the active compounds in Ashwagandha, Shatavari and Amtili have been identified while in another project Proposal, a novel
 method has been standardized for the preparation of certain Asavas and Arishtas, which is more innovative and less time
 consuming.  Keeping in view the progress made in the above two projects, emphasis was also being given to development of
 " Herbal Drugs ", using traditional Indian Systems of Medicine (ISM) (Ayurvedic, Unani  & Siddha), which is widely
 practiced in India.
40.    The Committee is constrained to observe that the progress made in the field of drugs is rather slow.  It therefore
 recommends that  the Department should  speed up the pace of work in this area.  Emphasis should also be on use of our rich
 heritage of traditional system and herbal medicines.
 
SWARNA  JAYANTI  FELLOWSHIPS
41.    “Swarna Jayanti Fellowship” scheme was launched in June, 1997 on the occasion of 50 th anniversary of our
 Independence in order to support selected young scientists in the age group 30-40 to achieve world standards in basic research
 as a part of Government’s endeavour to create conditions for our scientists, technologists, academicians and industrialists to
 work in close collaboration in order to achieve integrated excellence.  In  its endeavour to strengthen basic research in frontier
 areas Government have awarded 11 Swarna Jayanthi Fellowships last year to scholars (in the age group 30-40 years) in
 different areas of science for pursuing research in their areas of specialisation.  These 11 Fellows were selected out of about
 700 applicants in the year 1998 and 6 scientists were selected in the year 1999.  The scheme covers all the requirements for
 performing at the highest level and includes in addition to an attractive fellowship of Rs.25,000/- per month to the fellows,
 provision of liberal grant for the projects including amount for purchase of equipment, research and administrative support,
 consummable, national and international travel and any other special requirement including infrastructure.

 accuracy of predicting severe weather, its timely warning and dissemination of its long range predictions of the monsoon. 
 The major activities proposed in this area during the Ninth Plan include: modernisation of the observational and
 telecommunication systems, use of contemporary radar and satellite technology for better understanding of weather systems,
 satellite-based dissemination of timely warning of tropical cyclones, monitoring of seismic activities, exploitation of remote
 sensing data for agro-meteorological advisories, etc.
46.    The Department sought an outlay of Rs.131.90 crores in the year 1998-99 at BE stage with a view to enhance the quality
 of forecast by upgrading some of the existing facilities for monitoring the Weather Forecasts.  The allocation was reduced to
 Rs.118.74 at the RE stage as some facilities could not materialize during the financial year 1998-99 due to non-receipt of
 some Capital Equipment, viz. Doppler Radars through DGS&D which were essential for improving the Weather Forecasting
 Systems in India. 
47.    The committee was informed that the Department of Science and Technology, under the Meteorology programme has
 significantly increased its budgetary support to Rs.144.39 crores for the year 2000-2001 from Rs.120.10 at R.E. stage and
 Rs.133.98 at B.E. stage during the last financial year. The increase in allocation is mainly intended to procure S-band Doppler
 Weather Radars, High Wind Speed Recorders, replacement of obsolete skopograph at International Airports, upgradation of
 INSAT-II E, Met Ground Segment and Earth Station, establishment of Seismo Telemetry Network for National Capital
 Region, upgradation of RTH computer etc. This state-of-the-art technology would enable IMD to provide accurate forecasts.
48.    The project “Upgradation of IMDPS system” for receiving and processing INSAT-2E will be commissioned.  Old
 AVHRR receiving equipment at New Delhi and 100 CWDS receivers installed in Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu coasts
 during first phase would be replaced by improved version of equipment.
49.    50 Strong Motion Accelerographs for field stations would be procured.  Mountain Met. Centre would be established at
 Srinagar and 3 Upper Air Stations under Joint Project “Development of Mountain Met in Himalayas” in collaboration with
 Ministry of Defence would also be established in the region.
50.    Seismological Telemetry System Network for National Capital Region may be commissioned apart from Augmentation
 and operationalisation of Seismological Network and National Seismological Data Centre (NSDC) under the World Bank
 assisted Project may be taken up.
51.    The Committee expresses its concern over the fact that the target for augmentation of National Data Centre and
 Modernisation/Augmentation of Air Pollution Laboratory at Pune could not be completed by the Department and hope that
 this year the department would strive hard to complete the task taken into hands.
52.    The Committee is constrained to observe that not much attention has been paid to improve the weather forecasting
 system to ensure higher degree of precision in our weather forecasts and make the country self reliant in this field despite
 Committees observations in earlier reports.  The Committee would therefore like the Department to make concerted efforts to
 perfect the system of weather forecasting as per world class standards to ensure that our forecasts do not go awry.

 Farming System, Fish culture in Hilly Areas, Water, Health and Sanitation are proposed to be  developed. An important
 project on extraction of vincristin/vinblastin (used in cancer treatment) from Vinca rosea leaves by women would be
 launched.
54.    A Coordinate Programme for women on cultivation, semi processing and extraction of natural dyes is being launched in
 different agroclimatic zones of the country. An intervention programme for prevention of florosis is also to be initiated in
 parts of the country, which are endemic to high concentration of fluoride in drinking water.   A programme on Rural
 Laboratory Technology would also be initiated at several places. Long term support to selected NGOs is proposed to be

SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY POLICY
60.    In reply to a question on the revision of the Scientific Policy Resolution of 1958, the Committee was informed that the
 Scientific Policy Resolution was still valid and did not require any major changes.  The Technology Policy Statement,
 however, requires some modifications keeping in view the changed scenario both at global and national level and the
 modifications required are under consideration.
61.    The Committee observes that the government should have a comprehensive review of the Technology Policy Statement
 with a view to decide whether to have a consolidated technological policy or different Policies- one for promotion of Science
 and the other for Technology.
SCIENCE AND ENGINEERING RESEARCH COUNCIL (SERC)
62.    The programme introduced in the year 1975 with the primary objective to promote research in new emerging and
 frontier areas of science and engineering including multidisciplinary fields and also to encourage young scientists to take up
 challenging R&D activities. The Council also helps to promote the general research capability in relevant areas of science and
 engineering taking into account the capability of the host institute.
63.    The SERC scheme is under operation for more than two decades and there have been significant contributions by
 initiating research in several advanced areas of Science & Engineering.  During the last decade has the scheme focused on
 strengthening the research infrastructure in the country particularly in the university sector in remote areas.  The new  “FIST”
 programme is likely to be operationalised during the year 2000-2001.
64.    The research projects, centres for excellence and national research facilities set up by he Department  for promoting
 research are monitored annually through group monitoring workshops and specially appointed Monitoring and Review
 Committees, for their progress and performance. These reviews are taken into account while considering continuation of the
 projects or fresh proposals from these institutions and universities. Such Review and Monitoring mechanisms exist for other
 schemes also. The activities of autonomous research institutions also come up for review and monitored by the Research
 Advisory Bodies.

 multidisciplinary, support to at about 75 professional bodies.

 scheme is implemented under the overall supervision and guidance of SERC, an apex body consisting of eminent Scientists
 from Universities, National laboratories and Industry.

 given adequate funds by the Planning Commission/Ministry of Finance for the SERC related activities.
PATENT FACILITATING CELL
71.    In the area of patent awareness, twelve workshops are planned during the year 2000-2001. Other activities like filing of
 patent applications, monthly bulletins, quarterly updates of Ekaswa-A and Ekaswa-B databases, patent searches, responding
 to queries would continue. Some improvements may be made in Ekaswa-A and Ekaswa-B. A video film on patent awareness
 is expected to be finalised and distributed to universities, R&D institutions and other agencies. Attempts are also being made
 to develop a suitable database thorough special studies, which could be easily accessed.  It is proposed to hold some
 specialised workshops for patent attorneys, examiners, etc. on topics of current science and technology, like biotechnology
 and microelectronics.
72.    In order to provide support to scientists on all aspects of patenting, a Patent Facilitating Cell has been in operation in the
 Department. A monthly bulletin called “Intellectual Property Rights” is being brought out covering a wide range of topics like
 analysis of patent, applications filed in India, national and international news and other information relating to Patent regime.
73.    The Committee commends the efforts made by the Department for spreading Patent literacy in the country by using
 various means.  However, the Committee feels that there is much more required to be done to check the theft of vast reservoir
 of traditional knowledge available in the country.

 various important programmes and projects administered by it, the Committee recommends that the Demands for Grants of
