PREFACE 
  I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment 
and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the Report on its behalf, present this Two Hundred Eighteenth 
Report on the Action Taken by the Department of Science and Technology on the recommendations contained in Two Hundred 
Ninth Report of the Committee on Demands for Grants (2010-11) of the Department of Science and Technology. 
2. The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science and Technology on the 
recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Ninth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-2011) of the Department of Science and 
Technology which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on 23rd April, 2010. 
3. There were twenty one recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Ninth Report of the Committee. Action Taken 
Notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 20th August, 2010. 
4. In the meeting held on 8th December, 2010 the Committee considered the draft Action Taken Report and adopted the 
same.

REPORT 
  The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science and Technology on the 
recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Ninth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (2010-2011) of the Department of Science and 
Technology, which was presented to both the Houses of Pa rliament on 23rd April, 2010. There were twenty-one 
recommendations in the Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received 
from the Department on 20th August, 2010. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the Action Taken by the 
Department of Science and Technology are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:–– 
Recommendation  (Para 5.2) 
 The Committee appreciates the fact that the Department has been successful in utilizing more amount during Tenth Five 
Year Plan i.e. Rs 3998.10 crores which is over and above the tota l outlay of Rs. 3400.00 crores. Accordingly, a sum of 
Rs.588.00 crores more have been utilized in various schemes over the initial total outlay provided during the Tenth Five Year 
Plan. The Committee also appreciates the fact that some new initiatives have been planned for the Eleventh Plan Period in 
addition to the strengthening of the ongoing flagship programmes. The Committee has, however, observed that against the 
approved outlay of Rs.11,028.00 crores, the actual expenditure incurred by the Department on year to year basis during the 
Eleventh Five Year Plan has been intimated as Rs 1266. 89 cr ores for the year 2007-08 and Rs .1517.12 crores during 2008-09 
of the Plan period, which seems to be on the lower side on annual basis, if the average annual expenditure in connection with 
the full utilization of the budget amount of Rs. 11,028.00 crores  is concerned which comes to approximately Rs. 2000.00 crores 
per annum. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee has appreciated increased ut ilization of budget from a total outlay of Rs. 3400.00 crores to Rs. 3998.10 
crores in the Tenth Plan period. The Comm ittee has also appreciated Department’s new initiatives as well as strengthening of 
the ongoing flagship programmes. However the Committee has observed that the actual expenditure incurred by the Department 
in the first three years of the plan period has been on a lowe r side and hence is concerned about the full utilization of the 
Department’s budget of Rs. 11,028.00 crores for the Eleventh Plan Period. The Department has planned an annual Composite 
Growth of 25-26%. Projecting the total outl ay of Rs.11,028 Crores over a five-year period with a CAGR of about 26%, would 
require the total utilization of budget within the first three years at 46-48% of total outlay. The present rate of budget abso rption 
is in the range of 46-48% of the total outlay in three years.  The Department hopes that it will utilize its full allocation of 
Rs.11,028.00 crores during the Eleventh Plan period. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Departme nt would be able to fully utilize the total approved 
outlay of the five year plans. 
Recommendation  (Para 5.3) 
 The Committee has further noted that the provision proposed under B.E. for the year 2009-10 under plan expenditure is 
Rs 1775.00 crores, which is significantly higher than the act ual expenditure made by the Department during 2007-08 and 2008-
09. Again the Committee has noted that the provision proposed  under B.E. 2010-11 is Rs. 2025.00 crores under Plan 
expenditure which is Rs. 250.00 crores more than the provision  proposed for the year 2009-10 under Plan expenditure. The 
Committee understands that this is a healthy trend as regards the allocation of funds for various schemes of the Department 
under B.E. The Committee also noted that the non-plan expenditure for the year 2009-10 was Rs 358.00 crores and the same 
has been reduced to Rs.344.00 crores at B.E. stage for the year 2010-11 i.e. a reduction of about Rs.14.00 crores under the non 
plan head. Given the aforesaid trend of expenditure and the al location of amount by the Planning Commission at B.E. stage as 
analysed in detail above, the Committee also hopes that the Department will make all out efforts to maintain its trend of 

achievements of targets set in the Eleventh plan as well. The Committee hopes that the new initiatives like INSPIRE and 
PURSE would be able to attract the younger generations towards science and the country would forge new partnerships with the 
countries who are the global leaders in science to attain the objective of transforming India into a technologically advanced hub. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee has observed that there has been a significantly  higher expenditure made by the Department in the year 
2009-10 compared to 2007-08 and 2008-09. The Committee observed that it is a healthy trend and hopes that the Department 
will make all efforts in pursuing the new initiatives during the remaining plan period. In this connection, the Department is 
taking necessary steps and actions for achieving the targets set in the Eleventh Plan period. Th e Department is continuously 
monitoring the progress being made under the new initiatives as well as the ongoing flagship programmes. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and ho pes that the Department would succeed in achieving the targets set for the 
Eleventh Plan period. 
Recommendation  (Para 6.4) 
 The Committee has noted that under B.E. (Plan) expenditure for 2009-10 initially Rs 1775.00 crores was allocated for the 
Department combining all schemes. Again during 2010-11 the Department had proposed for a Plan allocation of Rs 2300.00 
crores for entire Department out of which Rs 2025.00 cror es was actually allocated under B.E. Plan for 2010-11. The 
Committee has, therefore, observed that although the Departme nt has received an increased am ount of Rs.250.00 crores in the 
B.E. Plan outlay for the year 2010-2011 as compared to 2009-10, there has been a substantial reduction of Rs. 275.00 crores in 
the B.E.(Plan) allocation as compared to  the Proposed (Plan) allocation for the ye ar 2010-11. The Committee although agrees 
with the opinion of the Department that th is increase of Rs 250.00 crores is too limited to meet the investments made by the 
Department into new and ongoing major initiatives of the department viz. Research and Development Support including solar 
energy research, Technology Development Programmes, Aut onomous Institutions and Professional Bodies, Drugs and 
Pharmaceuticals Research, INSPIRE, Mega facilities for basic Research etc. which are of prime importance, it is however of the 
opinion that correspondingly a reduction of Rs 275.00 crores in  the B .E. Plan allocation for 2010-11 against the proposed 
demand for 2010-11 may affect the major programmes of the Department as detailed above. The Committee is of the opinion 
that curtailment of funds to the tune of Rs. 275.00 crores w ill hamper the setting up of basic infrastructure for these crucial  
projects and will be detrimental to the gr owth of science and technology in the country in the long run. The Committee 
therefore recommends that the department may take up the issu e of greater allotment of funds strongly with the Planning 
Commission at the Revised Estimate stage. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee’s recommendation for the greater allotment of funds will be strongly taken up with the Planning 
Commission and Ministry of Finance at the Revised Estimate stage.  
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
Recommendation  (Para 6.6) 
 The Committee expresses serious concern at the slow pace of expenditure in some schemes by the Department which lead 
to a reduction in allocation at the R.E. stage by the Ministry of Finance during the last financial year. The Committee is of t he 
opinion that a higher proposed allocation can only be justified if  the Department is more prompt and efficient in utilising the  
already allocated funds under various schemes. The Committee th erefore recommends that the Department should do proper 
internal planning so that maximum expenditure on its schemes could be incurred before sending the proposals for Revised 
Estimate stage. In this way the Ministry of Finance will not resort  to cuts in expenditure at the R.E. stage in future as they have 
done in the present case. 
 

Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee’s concern over the reduction in allocation at the R.E. stage by the Ministry of Finance has been noted. 
Although the Department has met the overall 50% benchmark expenditure, some schemes on account of their nature had 
performed below the benchmark. Hence, the budget reduction at RE stage could not be  avoided. Secretary (DST) has directed 
the Programme Divisions to work out Monthly Expenditure Plan (MEP) and monitors the progress of schemes and expenditure 
personally. This will ensure that all schemes are above the benc hmark expenditure at the RE Stage so that no cuts takes places 
at the RE stage. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that robust monitoring would avoid cuts at R.E. stage in future. 
Recommendation  (Para 7.3) 
 The Committee takes note of this serious lapse on the part of the Department which has lead to the non-submission of the 
Annual Report of the Department for the year 2009-10 as a result of which the Committee failed to get a Comprehensive picture 
of the performance of various schemes under the Department during the Plan period of 2009-10. The Committee fails to 
understand while other Departments under the Ministry of Science and Technology, viz. DSIR and DBT have already submitted 
their annual reports in time, why DST has failed to submit their Annual Report within the stipulated time frame. The 
Committee, therefore, strictly recommends that the Department may draw up a comprehensive time schedule for the various 
stages involved in the preparation of the Annual Report and strictly adhere to the targets set in the schedule so that the Annu al 
Reports are printed and submitted to the Committee well within the time limit. The Department may also examine and analyse 
the approach followed by the other Departments of the Ministry of Science & Technology, which have submitted their reports 
in time and adopt an effective method for the preparation of its Annual Reports for the next Plan period of 2010-11.  
Action Taken by Government 
 The recommendations of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, 
Environment and Forests with regard to non-submission of A nnual Report of the Department for 2009-10 prior to the meeting 
of the Standing Committee is sincerely regretted. This was in part due to a desire to complete some major milestones like 
notification of Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB)  and release of 1.16 lakh INSPIRE Awards. The required 
numbers of copies of the same have already been submitted to the Parliament. In future, a comprehensive time schedule for 
preparation of the Annual Report of the Department would be  drawn up so that the Report is printed and submitted to the 
Committee well in time. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and would like the Department to ensure that such lapses do not occur in future. 
Recommendation  (Para 8.4) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the core responsibility of  the Department is to promote basic and applied research in 
newly emerging and frontier areas of scie nce and build up necessary infrastructure for the purpose. The Committee appreciates 
the initiative of the Department in expanding research in infrastructural developmental activities in Universities and Higher 
Educational Institutions in States which lack necessary scientific infrastructure, through special regional packages in the Nor th 
Eastern Region, the States of Jammu & Kashmir and Bihar. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Planning 
Commission should adequately fund such special initiatives of the Department for encouraging the research and development 
activities, so that the same can be pursued with vigour.

 The Department is thankful for the opinion of the Committee that the core responsibility of DST is to promote basic and 
applied research in newly emerging and frontier areas of science and build up necessary infrastructure for this purpose. Suppor t 
of the Planning Commission for the new initiatives to strengthen research infrastructure to Universities has been forthcoming. 

Synergies are also being built with Department of Biotechnology and Ministry of Human Resource Development with similar 
objectives and programmes. The Department is hopeful of discharging the responsibility assigned by the Committee even more 
efficiently than before. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and reiterates that the States which lack in scientific infrastructure, should receive 
adequate attention of the Department a nd the Planning Commission, so that there is equitable scientific development of 
all the States.

Recommendation  (Para 8.8) 
 The Committee expresses serious concern about the fact that th e contribution of the Universities as regards publication of 
research papers/journals have decreased over the years from an in itial 50% to current figure of 15%, despite the fact that ther e 
has been incentive grant system for Universities, based on the evidence of scientific publications during the period from 1996-
2006 under PURSE programme and also despite the fact that ther e has been an overall increase in funds allocation towards 
science and technology which essentially is not a favourable in dication so far as performance of Science & Technology in the 
country is concerned. The Committee ther efore recommends that the Department should evolve a more effective incentive 
mechanism to encourage the Universities to augment their c ontribution in terms of publications of quality research 
papers/journals. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee has expressed concern over the relative decr ease in National Scheme of Publications from Universities, 
despite the launch of several new schemes and programmes like PURSE, by the Department of Science and Technology (DST). 
Several schemes of DST like Promotion of University Resear ch and Scientific Excellence (PURSE), Consolidation of 
University Research for Innovation and Excellence in Wome n Universities (CURlE), specia l packages for North Eastern 
Region, Jammu and Kashmir and Bihar are new. Results on account of the implementation of the Scheme have not yet been 
assessed. The Department plan to undertake impact assessment studies after the implementation of the new schemes for atleast 
three years. However, impact of Fund for Infrastructure Streng thening of Science and Technology has been assessed by a third 
party study on the departments availing funds. There are several positive impacts. Currently effort is being made to synergize 
the implementation of FIST with the efforts of University Grants Commission. It is relevant to point out that annual grants of 
scientific publication from India in Science Citation Index (SCI) journals is about 12% during the last three years (based on t he 
Report of Thomson Reutors) and some Universities and this report high growth rates. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like to be  apprised about the results on account of  the implementation of the various new 
schemes by the Department. The Committ ee reiterates that the Department sh ould encourage the Universities to 
augment their contribution in terms of publications of quality research papers/journals. 

Recommendation  (Para 8.9) 
 The Committee also feels that the Impact Factor of publishe d journals and research papers in the country is low as 
compared to other advanced countries who are global leaders in the field of science & technology. The Committee is of the 
opinion that the Impact Factor is correlated with the quality of the research papers/journals i.e. the degree of originality involved 
and also how it reflects about basic research on a particular issu e. Since the basic research is persistently neglected in the 
country, the Impact Factor of the Indian j ournals and research papers in science is globally languishing behind. Therefore, the  
Committee feels that more emphasis on basic research needs to be made so that international quotation from Indian journals 
could be increased in future. Apart from this, the Committee is of the firm opinion that unless Indian pioneering research 
publications/papers get world-wide online publicity, the Impact  Factor will always remain poor. The Committee, therefore, 
suggests that adequate steps may be taken to give wider interna tional publicity to Indian science journals/ research papers in 
future. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has noted the recommendations by the Committee. It may kindly be noted that all the Scientific Journals 
published by the Indian Academy of Sciences, Bangalore are being distributed in partnership with internationally reputed 
publishing houses. This is being done both electronically and print versions. 
Comments 
 The Committee desires that the recommendation of the Committee which has been note d by the Department are 
followed in letter and spirit. 
Recommendation  (Para 9.3) 
 The Committee feels that the Technology Development Programme is an initiative in the right direction as it aims at 
developing the need based indigenous technologies through joint participation. The Committee feels that the programme 
combines the competent technical inputs from the industry sector  and the inputs from the socia-economic ministries, besides 
decreasing the investment cost of individual partners. The Co mmittee appreciates that the Sola r Energy Research Initiative 
(SERI) to encourage the use of Solar Ener gy specially for the rural areas and the National Mission on Climate Change which 
addresses the pressing issue of change in climatic conditions have been accorded priority under the programme. The Committee 
feels that the Department should initiate more such programmes in the fields relevant to the socio-economic benefits of the 
common man. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department is thankful to the Committee for its appreciat ion of Solar Energy Research Initiative and Climate Change 
related programmes and the directions for more research programmes in the fields relevant to the socio-economic benefits of the 
common man. 
 The Department assures the Committee that it will strive to undertake such programmes of relevance to common man. 
The Department has initiated programmes relating to Water Technology, Security Technology, Solar Energy Research 
Initiative, potash from sea for fertilizer applications, Tech nology Initiatives for Elderly and Climate Change related 
programmes. The Department will further augment efforts to relate technology utilization by various State Governments. A 
Standing Committee under the Chairmanship of Cabinet Secretary has been constituted to coordinate the applications of 
technologies by various States and the Department has assume d the role of promoting plot demonstrations of successful 
technologies from various institutions in the country. 
 The Department has been entrusted with the responsibility of coordinating two Missions namely National Mission for 
Sustaining Himalayan Ecosystem (NMSHE) and National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change (NMSKCC) 
out of the eight National Missions under National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) launched by the Government. 
 Both the Missions have been accorded in-principle approval by the Prime Minister's Council on Climate Change. As per 
the directions of the PM’s Council the mission documents are being finalized in consultation with Ministry of Environment & 

Forests (MoEF) and Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) and al so by incorporating comments from members of PM’s Council, 
State Governments and other stakeholders. Action is underway to evolve procedures for launch of various programmes and 
schemes as outlined in the mission documents and implement deliverables of the two missions. As per the mechanism set out by 
the NAPCC, the Missions will be periodically monitored and progress evaluated by the Prime Minister’s Council on Climate 
Change from time to time. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the initiatives take n by the Department and hopes that initiatives taken by the Department 
shall result in socio-economic benefits to common man. 
Recommendation  (Para 10.5) 
 The Committee feels that in a country su ch as ours, where a large section of th e population live in abject poverty, 
technological achievements made in the laboratories would be m eaningless until such achievements addresses the cause of the 
underprivileged masses and helps in alleviating their poverty. In this direction the proactive steps taken by the Department is  
vital for the socio-economic development of the country, specially the SEED programme, which caters to the needs of the 
Tribals and the Scheduled Castes population. The Committee fails to understand as to why the allocation for this programme 
has been decreased this year despite the previous trend of increase in allocations since inception of the programme and hopes 
that in future sincere e fforts should be made to ensure that the programme is allotted enhanced funds and the same are utilized  
properly. The Committee recommends that all out efforts should also be made to popularize such programmes and involve all 
the stakeholders such as rural masses, voluntary groups and besides technical organisations for giving specialized inputs. The 
Committee also appreciates the effort of the Department in invo lving the concerned Ministries such as the Ministry of Rural 
Development and feels that such effective coordination will ensure sharing of resources between the Ministries besides ensuring 
an Effective delivery of the programme.  
Action Taken by Government 
 All efforts are being made to ensure that the achievements made in the laboratories reach, through the proactive 
beneficiary oriented schemes of the department to include Wo men, Weaker Sections, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. 
The allocation for the Scheduled Castes Sub Plan (SCSP) and Tribal Sub Plan (TSP) has been showing a progressive upward 
trend and the allocation has not been reduced. There has, howev er, been slight decrease in the allocation for the Science & 
Society Programmes in the Budget Estimate (B.E.) 2010-2011. Several new initiatives are being planned to include Women 
Technology Parks, Rural Technology Centres and once the proposals are finalized additional allocation would be done at the 
RE stage. Plans have also been drawn to enhance the coverage of the programmes with the participation of all concerned 
stakeholders, including rural masses, voluntary groups, technically viable organizations etc. 
 Department has recently got approval of the Cabinet for Na tional Innovation Foundation (NIF) to be converted as grants-
in-aid institution under Department of Science and Technology. The main objective of NIF is to give a boost to inclusive 
innovation by ensuring evolution and diffusion of grassroots innovations so as to meet socio-economic and environmental needs 
of the society. NIF seeks to scout innovations by common peop le, farmers, artisans and mechanics, etc. from all over the 
country and facilitate scaling up so as to attain and maintain technological competitiveness in the informal sector. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and reiterates that to ob tain better results the Depart ment should endeavor to 
involve all stakeholders in its programme directed towards the benefit of the society. 
Recommendation  (Paras 11.3 & 11.4) 
 The Committee notes with serious concern that the budgetary allocation for the financial years 2009-2010 and 2010-2011 
have almost remained constant, despite the fact that International S&T cooperation have registered an impressive four fold 
increase in the number of activities delivered during 2008-2009 in comparison to pervious years. The Committee also expresses 
serious concern at the fact that due to lack of budgetary support during the year 2009-2010 several new international S&T 
initiatives/agreements which were concluded, but the activities under the agreements could not be materialized due to lack of 

full budgetary support and had to be postponed to the next financ ial year i.e. 2010-2011 and this  year too the allocation has n ot 
been increased. 
 The Committee is of the opinion that retracting from an international agreement or postponing it, after it has been 
concluded sends a wrong signal to the international scientific community about the seriousness and commitment of our country 
towards such agreements. Further, International Collaboration in  S&T results in developing stronger relations both at bilateral  
and multi-lateral levels which are inevitable to maintain pace of progress and growth of S&T, besides ensuring sharing of 
expertise, cost and optimal utilization of resources. Keeping this in view, the Committee feels that full budgetary support should 
be provided by the Ministry of Finance and Planning Commission atleast for those activities for which international agreements 
have been concluded, so that during the next financial year these activities get materialized and may not languish due to lack of 
funds. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department should strongly sponsor their stand with the Ministry of 
Finance to get adequate funds to translate those activities into reality. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has noted the remarks of the Parliamentar y Standing Committee regarding the International Cooperation. 
The Department has also noted the concerns of the Committee abou t the inadequacy of the budgetar y allocation in view of the 
stepped up activities including several new initiatives/agreemen ts. The Department has adopted a new approach to connect 
national funding for research work undertaken in India under International Science and Technology Cooperation. Research and 
Development Cooperation of research in I ndia for joint research under International Science and Technology Cooperation are 
further supplemented through funds from Science and Engineering Research Council (SERC). The Department assures the 
Committee that adequate resources will be found for aggressively promoting research and development under the International 
Science and Technology Cooperation. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and reit erates that the issue of budgetary s upport for International scientific 
agreements should be actively pursued with the Planning Commission/Ministry of Finance. 
Recommendation  (Paras 12.3, 12.4 & 12.5) 
 The Committee notes with serious concern that a crucial pr ogramme such as the ‘Drugs and Pharmaceutical Research’ 
programme which was started in 1994-95 is still in search of a promoting Department/ Ministry. The Committee fails to 
understand as to why the programme is being shifted from De partment of the Science & Technology to Departments of 
Biotechnology (DBT), or A YUSH, Ministry of Health or CSIR. The Committee expresse s its serious concern at the fact that 
even after so many years of being in existence, no focused approach has been adopted towards the programme. Instead of 
shifting from one Department to another, the Committee is of the firm opinion that to achieve better results, better coordinati on 
should be evolved between the Ministries/Departments participating in the programme. 
 The Committee is of the view that in a country where poverty is rampant and substantial part of population suffers from 
critical diseases, continued support to drug  research programmes is the social resp onsibility of the government for providing 
affordable drugs/medicines to the poor masses and this responsibility cannot be diluted or absolved at any stage through 
reduced allocations. Hence instead of decreasing allocation for the programme, a focussed approach should be adopted within 
the Department of Science & Technology to ensure that the programme is able to deliver its intended results. The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that drug research programmes should be  focussed on the neglected diseases which are afflicting the 
large populace of our country with renewed vigour. 
 The Committee in its 162nd report on Dr ugs and Pharmaceuticals Research Prgramme including the herbal medicines has 
comprehensively examined the various aspects of this programme and has given cogent recommendations. The Committee 
while reiterating its recommendations would like to stress upon the urgent need to forge effective partnerships between the 
various research institutions and the pharmaceutical companie s engaged in the crucial field of drugs and pharmaceutical 
research and development, keeping in view the profound influence of R&D on the prospects and opportunities for the growth of 
the Indian Drug Industry and promotion of Industry-Institutional Collaboration of R&D in drugs and pharmaceuticals sector is 
the need of the hour. 

Action Taken by Government 
 Appropriate actions will be taken by the Department on Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Res earch Programme based on the 
recommendations of the Committee. 
Comments 
 The Committee would like to be apprised of the concrete ac tions and proposals of the Department as regards the 
Drugs & Pharmaceuticals Research Programme. The Committee would also like to know whether the programme is still 
under the control of the Department of Sc ience & Technology or has been shifte d to any other Ministry/Department as 
proposed earlier. 
Recommendation  (Para 13.6) 
 The Committee is aware that the country has witnessed an ov erall decline in general interest towards science subjects 
which has resulted in a sharp decrease in the students who ar e opting for science as a career and therefore the invaluable 
creation of the much-needed scientific and technical manpower base has suffered over the years. In this background the 
INSPIRE and SHE schemes which aims towards inculcating an inte rest towards science amongst the school children at an early 
age of 10 to 15 years is highly commendable. The Committee feel s that through such schemes the brightest students of the 
country can be nurtured and retained for re search in science streams and would eventu ally enable India to embark on ambitious 
course of scientific wealth creation. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Department has noted the observations of the Committee. The Innovation in Science Pu rsuit for Inspired Research 
(INSPIRE) scheme including Scholarships for Science in Highe r Education (SHE) scheme is progressing well in terms of 
quantity and quality. So far about 1.16 lakh schools have been covered across the country under the INSPIRE Award 
Programme. About 2800 students have been enrolled under INSPIRE Scholarship Programme. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and is of the opinion that the Department should accelerate its efforts to increase 
the enrolment of students across the country in the INSPIRE Programme. 
Recommendation  (Para 13.7) 
 The Committee also appreciates that the Department will nurture the scheme in colleges and post-graduate level too 
through its various fellowships and monetary incentives programme s, specially the ‘Inspire Faculty scheme’ wherein each Post 
Doctoral Fellow will draw a salary equivalent to an Assistant Pr ofessor of IIT with a research grant. The Committee hopes that 
the scheme, which has been given substantial budgetary support, will be initiated as per schedule and the country will be able to 
reap the benefits of the scheme at the earliest. The Committee also appreciates the innovative scheme ‘INSPIRE Internship’, 
which aims to motivate talented youth to take-up research through personal interactions with global icons of science including 
Nobel Prize Winners and recommends that such schemes should be encouraged through proper budgetary support. 
Action Taken by Government 
 All INSPIRE components including INSPIRE Internship are progressing well. 55 Science camps, covering about 14000 
students, have been organized. Implementation steps have been finalized for initiating the INSPIRE Faculty scheme. It is a 
major component in which each Fellow will draw a salary equivale nt to an Assistant Professor of  IIT with a research grant. 
Sufficient fund allocation will be ensured for successful implementation of the Scheme.  
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
 
 

Recommendation  (Para 13.10) 
 The Committee is of the opinion that the scientific departments in the various universities across the country suffer from a 
severe funds crunch and are not able to develop their infrastructure which is a serious drawback faced by the students who are 
pursuing a science course. In this direction, schemes such as PURSE have taken a right initiative by focusing on the 
infrastructure development of the Universities. The Committee r ecommends that more universities should be provided with 
grants and that such schemes should be extended to the Univer sities specially in the rural and backward areas of the country. 
The Department should also explore the possibility of expanding the scope of the PURSE funding to states, especially the states  
which have so far been left out in the Northern region, through pro-active schemes. 
Action Taken by Government 
 In order to meet diverse challenges in S&T, the Depart ment recognized the need of strengthening the existing S&T 
infrastructure support system in the universities and other related institutions. This led to the institution of a major initia tive 
titled “Fund for Improvement of S&T infrastructure in universi ties and higher educational institutions (FIST)” to rebuild the 
Science and Technology infrastructure. Th e Scheme has been conceptualized with an objective of augmentation of science 
departments either engaged in imparting PG teaching or actively involved in research activities so that more students can be 
attracted to pursue their career in higher studies in new and emer ging areas of science. Financial support is provided to acqui re 
state-of-art equipment besides basic equipment, renovation of existing infrastructure, augmentation of facilities available in the 
department and setting up of Computer centre in the department. The Program generally extends support in six broad subject 
areas of S&T i.e. Life Sciences, Physical  Sciences, Chemical Sciences, Engineering Sciences, Earth and Atmospheric Sciences 
and Mathematical Sciences through two levels of support. 
 The Department has taken initiatives to promote excellence in research in universities through schemes like PURSE while 
mounting also parallel programmes to expand the base of research in States which require special attention. Special packages 
for North/Eastern Region, Jammu and Kashmir, Bihar are some examples. PURSE type schemes in select areas where scientific 
excellence has been accomplished in various universities will be developed. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the initiatives taken by the Department as regards the FIST and PURSE 
programmes and hopes that such programmes would result in the much needed deve lopment of scientific infrastructure 
in the Universities across the country. 
Recommendation  (Para 14.4) 
 The Committee appreciates the principle of reciprocity and parity adopted by the Department as regards the use of mega-
facilities in basic research by our scientists. The initiative of  the Department will boost the confidence of our scientists an d also 
lead to the acknowledgement of our scientific expertise at an international level. The country will also be able to share the credit 
and the patents on any scientific discovery that results from international collaboration. Although the Committee appreciates t he 
partnership between the Department and the Department of Atomic Energy(DAE) and feels that such efforts can develop the 
much needed capacity building in the univer sity and academic sector for building mega  facilities for basic research, it also 
recommends that apart from DAE the partnerships with other sc ientific departments like DBT, Earth Sciences, Space and DSIR 
may also be fostered as the basic research in the country is perennially being neglected. The Committee feels that such positiv e 
activities on the part of the Department needs to be encouraged, and needs to be supported through proper budgetary support. 
Action Taken by Government  
 The Department has noted the recommendations by the Committee. The Department has identified and formed active 
groups from Universities and National labs for undertaking basic re search projects around mega facilities which are being built  
in partnership with DAE and DBT and other international bodies. Necessary financial allocations have been made and steps 
would be taken to enlarge the scope of partnerships involving other scientific departments. 
 

Comments 
 The Committee reiterates the need for fostering partnershi p with the other scientific departments to ensure that 
there is a collective effort for growth of basic research in the country. 
Recommendation  (Para 15) 
 The Committee is of the view that the Department of Science and Technology through its various research and 
development programmes is striving to uplift the standard of living in the country. Keeping in view the excellent work that the  
Department has undertaken, the kind of competence it has exhibited and the kind of core and critical programmes it has 
envisaged for the future, the Committee strongly recommends that the Department of Science and Technology should be 
properly and adequately funded and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. stage should be strictly avoided. 
Action Taken by Government 
 The Committee has observed that the depmiment is doing excellent work in various programmes for uplifting the standard 
of living in the country. The committee has strongly recommende d that the department should be properly and adequately 
funded and any kind of reduction in outlay at R. E. stage should be strictly avoided. The department appreciates the confidence  
posed by the committee and wishes to reaffirm its commitment towards the nation building exercise through its various 
programmes and activities. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the Department of Scie nce and Technology would endeavor towards better 
performance and productivi ty and ensure that its scientific achievements  result in the socio-economic development of 
the country.
