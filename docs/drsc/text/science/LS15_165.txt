REPORT 
 The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Space on the recommendations 
contained in the One hundred Sixty-first Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on 
Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2006-07) pertaining to the Department of 
Space, which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on  
18th May, 2006. There were 11 recommendations contained in the Report and the Department of Space has furnished 
the Action Taken Note in respect of all the recommendations. 
 Action Taken Note on the recommendations of the Committee and further clarifications were received from the 
Department of Space on 21st August, 2006 and 11th September, 2006, respectively. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon by the Department and Comments of the Committee 
thereon are set out in the succeeding paragraphs. 
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee commends the efforts of the Department in maintaining a record of more than 99% Budget 
utilisation during the first three years of the Tenth Five Year Plan period, which points towards the fact that the 
Department has achieved most of the goals it had set for itself through proper planning and management of funds. The 
Committee hopes that the same pace of Budget utilisation woul d be maintained in the remaining two years of the Plan 
Period to ensure that all the long-term aims are realised. (Para 16) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The observations of the Committee are noted for compliance. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
INDIAN LUNAR MISSION - CHANDRAYAAN-1 
Recommendation 
 The Committee commends the role of the Indian space scientists in mastering a frontier space technology, which 
has brought the country close to the threshold of launching lunar spacecrafts and hopes that the technologies developed 
in this mission would be fully exploited in the future for embarking on planetary missions with well thought out 
scientific objectives. The Committee also hopes that the mission would expand the scientific knowledge about the 
origin and evolution of moon, upgrade India’s space technological capabilities and provide challenging opportunities to 
the young scientists working in planetary sciences. (Para 20) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 While noting the observations of the Committee, the Department has clarified that the Department of 
Space/Indian Space Research Organisation (DOS/ISRO) in itiated Planetary Science and Exploration Programme 
(PLANEX) during 2001 with Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad as nodal institution. The PLANEX 
programme has been instituted to cater to the following: 
(a) Capacity building to attract and nurture young talented students and scholars to the field of Planetary 
Science and Research. In order to do so: 

(i)  PLANEX Workshops are organised by PRL every year on various topics related to Planetary Science 
to introduce young researchers in this area. After each Workshop, a few outstanding participants 
(Science postgraduates, research students, engineers) are selected as project associates/engineers for 
training in PLANEX programme for one to two years. Several of these have also been inducted on a 
permanent basis to participate in the Chandrayaan -1 mission. 
(ii)  Scientific research proposals from scientists working in the field of Planetary Science at various 
Universities and Institutions are also solicited, reviewed and funded under PLANEX. So far seventeen 
such groups at various Universities, IITs and Research Laboratories are actively involved and the 
investigators meet annually to review progress an d future plans. Some of these groups are already 
identified for participation in Chandrayaan-l data analysis. Research scholars are provided to the 
Principal Investigator (PIs) who also got trained in PLANEX Workshops. 
(b) ISRO has provided the funds to the PRL to initiate a state-of-the-art laboratory to function as national 
facility under PLANEX for astromaterials analysis including planetary samples, meteorites etc., 
(c)  A few young Planetary Scientists have been appointed in ISRO centres and laboratories for nucleating 
science groups in the field of Planetary Science. 
(d)  A few research projects in the field of Planetary Science in Universities are also funded under Sponsored 
Research (RESPOND) programme. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and appreciates  the challenge taken up by the Department. 
CARTOSAT -I 
Recommendation 
 The Committee appreciates the role of space scientists and the Department of Space in devising the Indian 
Remote Sensing (IRS) system, which is the largest civilian remote sensing satellite constellation in the world. The 
Committee hopes that the Department would formulate plans to sell the imageries obtained from the IRS satellites, 
worldwide on a commercial basis on a larger scale, which woul d ensure generation of intern al resources to fund future 
projects and popularisation of the achievements of the space scientists throughout the world. (Para 27) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Committee’s observations are noted. In this regard it may be mentioned that Antrix Corporation Limited, the 
commercial arm of the Department, has been continuously intensifying its marketing efforts in increasing the 
commercial sale of imageries from IRS satellites. New partners and re-sellers are being appointed along with 
independent efforts from Antrix to market data globally. At  present there are four re-se llers of Cartosat-I data in 
countries like Greece, China, Canada and Turkey. Efforts ar e on to appoint re-sellers in Australia, Brazil and Japan. 
The imageries of Resourcesat-l and the IR S-IC/ID are being sold by the reception stations at various global locations, 
USA, Germany, Iran, Russia, China, Myanmar and Taiwan. An trix will continue to enhance these efforts for larger 
share of the global market for imageries of Indian Remote Sensing Satellites. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and hopes that sustained efforts would continue to be made to increase the 
commercial sale of the imageries from IRS satellites by employing dynamic marketing strategies. 
HAMSAT 
Recommendation 

 The Committee feels that HAMSAT is India’ s contribution to the international community of Amateur Radio 
Operators and is an effort to bring ISRO’s satellite serv ices within the reach of the common man and popularise space 
technology among the masses. The Committee also feels that this satellite will play a valuable role in the national and 
international scenario by providing a low cost read ily accessible and reliable means of communication during 
emergencies and calamities like floods, earthquakes, etc. The Committee recommends that the Department should 
popularise the HAM Radio Technology through seminars and organising demonstration programmes in the 
Universities. schools etc. to stimulate technical interest and awareness among the younger generation by providing 
them with an opportunity to develop their technological projects. (Para 29 ) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department has been taking measures to popularise the use of HAM Radio Technology throughout the 
country by conducting workshop/seminars in Universities. Schools etc. In this regard, the Department had conducted 
one workshop in 2005 before launch of HAMSAT and feedback from HAMSAT was very encouraging. South Eastern 
Asian Net (SEANET) conducted an international seminar in Bangalore in 2005 after HAMSAT launch. The National 
Institute of Amateur Radio organised an International Convention in Port Blair from April 18-20, 2006. The 
Department had also released Grant-in-aid to the National Institute of Amateur Radio for conducting Seminars, etc. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply. 
EDUSAT 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is of the view that Satellites, such as the EDUSAT, can establish the connectivity between urban 
educational institutions with adequate infrastructure imparting quality education and the rural educational institutions 
that lack the necessary infrastructure. By  doing so, we can help, to a considerable extent, in meeting the aspirations of 
the rural students. The Committee, ther efore, recommends that the Department of Space should formulate plans in 
coordination with the Ministry of Human Resource Development to spread education through the EDUSAT to all parts 
of the country. (Para 32) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 After successful completion of the EDUSAT pilot phase, semi-operational phase is taken up by the Department. 
In this regard, each state is being provided with one HUB a nd few Satellite Interactive Terminals (SITs) free of cost. 
Under the national beam 8 networks have become operational to cater to IGNOU, CIET, CEC, AICTE, VIGY AN 
PRASAR (DST), SIDHI NETWORK, IDSP , INDO-US PROJECT. In this scheme , 484 SITs and 825 ROTs (Receive 
Only Terminals) have already been installed and are operational in the national beam. Under the regional beams, 20 
networks have been made operational. The number of SITs and ROTs installed in these networks are 1395 and 7347 
respectively as on date. There are 5 regional beams - Southe rn, Western, Northern, Eastern and North-eastern beams. 
The States of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat,  Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, J&K, Tripura, Nagaland, 
Mizoram, Jharkhand and West Bengal have been taken up as a first phase where the above mentioned SITs and ROTs 
have already been installed and commissioned. In the next phase, all other States will be taken up during this financial 
year. As regards formulating of plans in coordination with Ministry of Human Resource Development (MHRD), it may 
be noted that MHRD is a partner along with DOS and the concerned State Governments are also participating for the 
implementation of EDUSAT programme. The MHRD is expected to play a leading role for content availability and 
generation. The Department is pursuing with MHRD to have an exclusive set up within MHRD for effective 
implementation of EDUSAT programme in all the States and Union Territories of the country. 
Comments 

 The Committee notes the reply. It hopes the Depa rtment will take all possible steps for effective 
implementation of EDUSAT programme all over the country. 
VILLAGE RESOURCE CENTRES (VRC) 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that there is an urgent need to bridge the gap between the rural and urban population in 
terms of facilities available and the only means to accomplish the task is through technology. The concept of the 
Village Resource Centres (VRCs) is an excellent effort in this direction by the Department, which has the potential of 
transforming the socio-economic condition of rural India. Th e Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department 
should expedite its efforts towards esta blishing VRCs throughout the country, specially in  the inaccessible and under-
developed regions of the country in a planned manner, so as to cover the entire country within a fixed period. (Para 
35) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department of Space has continued its efforts to establish the Village Resource Centres (VRCs) in different 
regions of the country, specifically in the rural areas. Till date, 114 VRCs have been established in the States of Andhra 
Pradesh (2), Delhi (1), Gujarat (13), Ka rnataka (40), Kerala (22), Maharashtra (2 ), Orissa (5), Rajasthan (8), Tamil 
Nadu (12) and Uttar Pradesh (9). Another 152 VRC’s are curre ntly being established in th e States of Andhra Pradesh 
(31), Assam (13), Bihar (4), Delhi (1), Jharkhand (21), Madh ya Pradesh (10), Orissa (39), Rajasthan (12), Tamil Nadu 
(18) and Uttar Pradesh (3). 
 Further around 100 VRCs are being planned in Union Territories of Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Himachal 
Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, North Eastern States, Uttaranchal, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal. 
 Thus, by end of the year 2006-07, around 350 VRCs will be established across the different States and Union 
Territories of the country. During the 11th Five Year Plan period and beyond. It is being planned to establish around 
4000 VRCs covering rural/semi-urban blocks/taluks of the country, and setting up of a few State/regional 
HUBs/servers, as may be required, fo r catering to these VRCs. In doing so, efforts would also be placed on 
harmonising the goals of the VRCs with the related programmes of the Government. 
This initiative is envisaged to put in place a sizable numb er of VRCs in the country en suring the outreach of space 
technology based services in rural India. 
Comments 
 The Committee while noting the reply of the Departme nt is of the view that while establishing VRCs, 
better representation should the given to the rural areas of bigger and ba ckward States like Uttar Pradesh, 
Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, West Bengal and North Eastern States. 
TELEMEDICINE 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that in a developing country like I ndia where due to scarcity of resources. the possibility of 
setting up of Hospitals and even primary health centres in the villages across the country, is not always possible; 
Telemedicine facilities provide an excelle nt alternative option to provide expert medical services to remote rural areas 
by linking hospitals/health centres in remote locations via satellites with speciality hospitals at major towns and cities. 
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department shou ld carry on its efforts, in coordination with Ministry 
of Health, towards spreading the network of Tele-medicine centres in the rural areas lacking healthcare facilities.
 (Para 39) 
Action Taken by the Department 

 The Department has already taken up the matter with Ministry of Health with regard to the spreading of network 
of telemedicine centres in the rural areas lacking healthcare facilities. In this regard, a national task force formed by the 
Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Government of India with representatives from ISRO, DIT, other Hospitals and 
State Government agencies have been working towards identifying projects for establishment of Telemedicine centres 
at different hospitals/health centres in different States of India. The Task Force ha s the terms of reference for 
formalisation of national telemedicine grid, national cancer network, technology standards and aspects and continuing 
medical education with a view to provide comprehensive healthcare delivery system through telemedicine for the 11th 
Five Year Plan. 
 The Department further clarified that the National Task Force was set-up in October 2005. The National Task 
force has constituted six working groups on:- 
 (a) Standards & Inter-o peration of Systems; 
 (b) National Telemedicine Grid; 
 (c) National Cancer Network; 
 (d) Medical Education & Training Network; 
 (e) Pilot Projects & Mobile Telehealth & implementation aspects; and 
 (f) Policy aspects. 
 The reports of the working groups are being submitted in the month of September 2006. The National Task Force 
is likely to submit its report during November/December 2006. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the schedule of submission of the reports by the work ing groups and National 
Task Force would be adhered to and n ecessary follow-up action is taken expe ditiously so that the telemedicine 
network is able to deliver its benefits at the earliest possible. 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the issue has been pending with the concerned Departments for quite some time now 
and needs to be expedited. It, therefore, observes that the necessary framework for medico-legal aspects may be 
formulated at the earliest. The Ministry of Law and Justice can also be consulted,  
if necessary. (Para 41) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department under the National Task Force for telemedicine has already taken up the matter relating to the 
formalisation of necessary framework for medico-legal aspects. Further action will  be pursued with Ministry of Health 
for consulting the Ministry of Law and Justice for necessary formalisation of telemedicine practice with due 
compliance relating to medico-legal aspects. 
 The Department further clarified that a separate working group has been set up by the Ministry of Health & 
Family welfare to bring out the frame-work of Medico-legal aspects from the initial report of the Department of 
Information & Technology which will be discussed at the National Task Force working group on policy. 
Comments 
 The Committee hopes that the Departments concerned would expeditiously work towards framing of the 
medico-legal aspects so that a wider telemedicine network is available to people of the country at earliest. 

SATELLITE NAVIGATION 
Recommendation 
 The Committee is constrained to note that the Department made budgetary provision for Navigational Satellite 
System in 2005-06 without the programme being finalized and approved by the Government. This resulted in non-
utilisation of the budgetary provision of  Rs. 350 crores in BE 2005-06, which was reduced to Rs. 10 crores in RE. The 
Committee in this regard is of the opinion that necessary budgetary provision should be make by the Department only 
after a programme is finalised and approved by the compet ent authority. Till then, the Department could make a token 
provision in the budget against that programme. Necessary provision can be made at the RE stage, in case the 
programme is finalized and approved by that time. This is necessary for realistic budgeting. (Para 44) 
Action Taken by the Department 
 The suggestion of the Committee is noted. However, it may be mentioned here that the Department had made a 
provision of Rs.350.00 crores for pa rticipation in the European Galileo Program. However, the details of the 
participation and the financial aspects could not be finalised as it got into unexpected prolonged discussions with 
international agencies. 
Comments 
 The Committee reiterates its recommendation and hopes that such contingencies could be taken care of by 
following the Committee’s recommendation.

DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT 
Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that the Department can help mitigating the human and economic losses caused by disasters 
if the remote sensing data from the Earth Observation (E O) satellites were used more effectively in disaster 
management support in coordination with the local government agencies involved in relief work. In addition to disaster 
management, the satellites can be used to detect, in the fi rst place, the causes of the natural disasters like floods, 
cyclones, storms etc. to minimise the devastation. The Co mmittee also feels that there is a great potential in the 
operational application of data from the satellites, to support management of other types of disasters like oil spills, 
earthquakes, forest fires and droughts, wh ich are presently considered less signif icant and, therefore, recommends that 
earnest efforts in this direction may also be made. (Para 49) 

Action Taken by the Department 
 The Department of Space has set-up a Disaster Mana gement Support Programme u tilising the capabilities of 
space technology. Using synergistically sp ace (mainly INSAT and IRS satellites) and airborne systems in conjunction 
with the conventional technologies, the programme has been  providing space-enabled prod ucts and services towards 
supporting disaster management effort s in the country. Under the Departme nt of Space, National Remote Sensing 
Agency, Hyderabad is the nodal agency to disseminate space inputs for disaster management to the end users at the 
Centre and State levels. These space inputs are also of value in disaster warning. 
 The disaster management support services provided by Department of Space are mainly in terms of value-added 
products generated from satellite, aerial and other collateral  information, and emergency communication support. The 
information products are in the form of maps depicting dynamically the disaster impact, damages, and also 
vulnerability. 
 The disaster management support programme, since its inception, has responded comprehensively to the major 
natural disasters the country has faced including the 26th December 2004 Tsunami, monitoring of artificial lake in 
Sutlej basin that caused flash flood threat in parts of Himachal Pradesh, the recurrent floods, droughts, landslides, forest 
fires and earthquakes in different parts of the country. 
 The Department is putting efforts to develop scientific methodologies towards cyclone tracking and landfall 
prediction; storms etc. In this context, the Department has also developed Doppler Weather Radar and Automatic 
Weather Station towards supporting augmentation of the ground-based weather observations in the country. In these 
efforts, the Department is working closely with the concerned scientific departments, such as Indian Meteorological 
Department and other research institutions. 
 Detection and monitoring of oil spills is also an important activity under the DMS programme, and carried out 
whenever such events occur. 
 As regards earthquakes, in addition to damage assessment, identification of areas for rehabilitation after the 
earthquake, hazard zonation in highly earthquake-prone areas of the country, etc., are being pursued. 
 Forest fires are being monitored regularly and near real time disseminated through web. A comprehensive 
system, the ‘Indian Forest Fire Response and Assessment System (INFRAS)’ integrating multi-sensor satellite data and 
ground data through spatially and temporally explicit GIS analysis framework has been operationalised. 
 As part of the National Agricultural Drought Asse ssment and Monitoring System (NADAMS), monthly reports 
on the crop condition are being prepared at District level for 14 States, and provided to Department of Agriculture & 
Cooperation and concerned States. Efforts are also being put towards generation of information pertaining to drought 
mitigation through soil and water conservation, alternate land use planning. etc. using Earth Observation data; so also, 
in using conjunctively the products emanating from meteorological satellites, and in downscaling the information to 
reach the farmers down the line. 
Comments 
 While noting the reply of the Department, the Committ ee observes that sustained efforts should be made 
by the Department to emanate accurate and timely in formation to the concerned persons under the Disaster 
Management Support Services so that they are actual ly benefited. Though it is a challenging task, the 
Department can strive towards this goal.
