Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf,
present the Twenty Fourth Report, on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research for the year 1995-96 (Demand No. 73).

2. In its meeting held on April 20, 1995 the Committee considered the Demands for
Grants of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research on the basis of the
documents made available to it and the submission made before it by the Secretary of the
Department and the Principal Adviser, Planning Commission.

3. The Committee thanks the officers of the Department of Scientific and Industrial
Research and the Planning Commission for replying to the clarifications sought by the
Hon’ble Members and placing before it the required material to enable the Committee to

REPORT

The main thrust of the programmes and activities of the Department of Scientific and
Industrial Research (DSIR) relatés to research by industry, technology transfer, National
Information System for Science and Technology (NISSAT) and management of two public
sector undertaking, NRDC and CEL. While the short term objectives of the programmes on
research by industry and technology transfer are promotion of R&D by industry and
enhanced efficiency of technology transfer both within the country and from outside. The
long term objective of these programmes is technological self-reliance. The broad objectives
of NISSAT are to provide scientific and technological information, promote information
sharing and support development of information systems. The Department also looks after
all matters concerning the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) which
functions autonomously as a registered society and as a network of 40 laboratories and 81
field stations/extension centres/regional centres spread all over the country.

2. The Committee was informed that CSIR laboratories have responded to the changes
introduced by the industrial, economic and trade policies which were initiated by
Government in recent years to integrate the Indian economy with the global economy.
Successful technology transfers have taken place in many areas. Some of the laboratories
have entered into collaborative agreements with multinationals and internationally renowned
business houses. The laboratories work in close collaboration with Indian industry and
provide complete solutions, technology packages, turnkey plants, consultancy, etc. The
Committee was told that CSIR is also contributing significantly to the accomplishment of the
various societal programmes requiring S&T inputs. It has increased significantly its income
from sources outside direct central Government grants to it. The Secretary, DSIR informed
the Committee that besides industrial research, CSIR has commitments to support futuristic
areas and basic research.

3. The Secretary, DSIR informed the Committee that CSIR has been doing well over
the years and to highlight his point he drew the attention of the committee to the fact that in
respect of CSIR laboratories, fram 1992-93, every year the total external cash flow is
increasing at a rate of over 20%. What is particularly encouraging is that the component
from industry, which is indicative of CSIR linkages with industry, is increasing at a faster
rate. He informed the Committee that the global business of CSIR is also increasing and it is
exporting technologies to concerns like DuPont and many other well known international
firms. If one takes another measures of successful accomplishments like the number of
international patents which CSIR laboratories are filing, the product value of the goods that
are coming out as a result of R&D carried out in CSIR laboratories and in terms of basic
science and good research publications, one finds that CSIR, inspite of a lot of constraints
has been making good progress. The Secretary elaborated on this by providing the following
information to the Committee. During 1993-94, 198 patents were filed nationally and 80
were granted, which is 14 of the total patents filed in the country. Internationally, the CSIR
laboratories filed 18 patents out of which they were granted 12. With regard to the
technology development and transfer, during 1993-94, the number of processes licensed (first
time) were 35. 180 agreements were signed and a sum of Rs. 1.06 crores was earned as
royalty and premium. During that year about 2100 publications were brought out. The
Committee was also informed that the industrial production based on the CSIR knowhow is
also increasing. In monetary terms the industrial production through: CSIR knowhow during
the year 1993-94 was Rs. 2250 crores. This amount is expected to increase to Rs. 2500 crores
in 1995-96.


4. In its Thirteenth Report the Committee had made certain recommendations regarding
the modernisation of the CSIR laboratories, removal of obsolescence and the upgradation of
facilities. In that Report the Committee had also expressed the opinion that economy
measures and budgetary cuts applied by the Government from time to time should not affect
scientific and technological research that have been approved after careful consideation.
There were other aspects like providing of enough funds to the Department for meeting its
committed liabilities under non-plan sector, etc. which needed clarifications from the
Planning Commission. The Committee had, therefore, decided that the representatives of
the Planning Commission should also be requested to give their views on the
recommendations of the Committee contained in its Thirteenth Report.

5. The Committee examined the budgetary allocations of the Department for the year
1995-96.” While considering the Demands for Grants-of the Department, the Committee also
got an opportunity to review the work relating to various projects and programmes which
fall within the mandate of the Department.

6. The Principal Adviser, Planning Commission informed the Committee that no
economy measures and budgetary cuts were applied on the Department during 1994-95. No
such cuts are envisaged during the Annual Plan 1995-96 either. Regarding the modernisation
programme, it was clarified that the Annual Plan (1995-96) proposals of DSIR, (including
CSIR), were jointly reviewed by the Planning Commission and DSIR/CSIR. The plan
allocation was decided on” the basis of commitments on the various programmes and
availability of resources. With regard to the modernisation programme of CSIR laboratories,
the requirement projected by CSIR was considered during the mid-term review. Taking into
consideration the resource position, a sum of Rs. 10 crores was additionally provided for
selective modernisation during the annual plan 1995-96; this was specifically based on the
follow-up of the recommendations of the Committee. The Committee was told that,
depending on the projectisation of modernisation programme and generation of internal
resources through sponsored research, etc. suitable allocation would be considered at an
appropriate stage.

7. The Secretary, DSIR underlined the need to modernise facilities at the national
laboratories in order to carry out research of international standard and to be globaily
competitive. He informed the Committee that an in-depth exercise was initiated by all the
forty constituent laboratories of CSIR and a modernisation programme was chalked out both
in respect of equipment and facilities. The proposal was submitted as part of the Mid-term
Appraisal Document to the Planning Commission. A requirement of Rs. 100 crores was
Projected for the year 1995-96. The Planning Commission appreciated the need for
modernisation of the CSIR laboratories and suggested that CSIR should indicate its
requirements project-wise along with the financial implications so that a comprehensive view
could be taken on the proposal by the Planning Commission. Subsequently, CSIR initiated
an exercise and six specific tasks/programmes were identified which were in the area of
Catalysts, CFC Substitutes, New Drugs, Food, Wood Substitute and Coal Beneficiation.
After detailed examination the Finance Minister was approached to provide an additional
support of Rs. 40 crores to CSIR for the year 1995-96 so as to accomplish the specific tasks
and build confidence, capability and credibility of the CSIR system.

7.1 The Committee appreciates the fact that the Planning Commission has recognised the

need for modernisation of the CSIR laboratories which have a great role to play in the
changed economic climate; and for the year 1995-96 it had recommended an additional outlay
of Rs. 10 crores for selective modernisation of CSIR laboratories in relation to specific
projects. The Committee, however, is of the view that selective modernisation of CSIR
laboratories based on specific project areas whilst of value, and providing a sharper focus for
accomplishing relevant research will not gear the system to deal with the new situation of
globalization and intense international competition. A carefully planned one-time grant to
remove obsolescense and to modernise the facilities is called for. At the same time the
Committee also realises the over-all resource constraints. The Committee has noted with
satisfaction that the external cash flow generated by CSIR is increasing. The Committee is,
therefore, of the view that the Planning Commission may consider providing matching grants
equivalent to the extra budgetary resources generated by CSIR in future and a part of it can
be utilised for modernisation of its laboratories on a continuing basis. However, convinced of
the need for immediate modernisation of some of the laboratories of CSIR, the Committee
strongly recommends that a one-time grant of Rs. 50 crores may be provided for the year
1996-97 which is the last year of the Eighth Five Year Plan.

8. Last year, in its Thirteenth Report, the Committee had noted that CSIR had been
diverting funds from plan allocation to meet the expenditure in the non-plan category. The
Committee had recommended that enough funds be allocated to CSIR to enable it to meet
its committed liability under non-plan so that the need for such diversion of plan allocation
did not arise; and the plan funds could be utilised for their primary purpose.

8.1 The Secretary, DSIR informed the Committee that utilisation of plan funds for
committed non-plan liabilities has been of concern to CSIR as also to both the Planning
Commission and the Ministry of Finance. During the mid-term appraisal of the Eighth Five
Year Plan, the Planning Commission had expressed the view that the most important
drawback in the CSIR system was that the plan funds were being utilised for non-plan
commitments. Due to the efforts of the Planning Commission and DSIR the Ministry of
Finance had allocated an additional non-plan outlay to CSIR for the year 1993-94. But this
had given only partial relief to CSIR. An additional allocation of Rs. 27.79 crores was
provided for 1993-94, and for 1994-95 a sum of Rs. 16.50 crores was allocated at the R.E.
stage to partially offset the increase in committed liabilities under non-plan.

8.2 In the budget estimates for the year 1995-96 the total budgetary allocation for CSIR
is Rs. 384.85 crores out of which Rs. 233.85 crores is the non-plan component and remaining
Rs. 161 crores is the plan component. According to the Secretary, the anticipated non-plan
committed expenditure during 1995-96 is Rs. 301.50 crores. There is thus a difference of Rs.
77.65 crores between the anticipated non-plan expenditure and actua! allocation. This deficit
would be met from the internal resources of CSIR to the extent of Rs. 25 crores, and the
balance of Rs. 52.65 crores would have to come from plan funds.

8.3 The Committee takes note of the fact that despite a clear recommendation to make
sufficient provision in the non-plan outlay of CSIR to meet its committed liability, adequate
non-plan allocation has not been made in the budget estimates for the year 1995-96. The
Department will have to still divert money from its plan funds to meet its committed liability
under non-plan. The Committee would like the Department to sort out this problem with the
Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance so that plan funds are not diverted to non-
plan activities. Such a diversion adversely affects the R&D activities of the Department. DSIR
should also make an intensive study of the so-called committed non-plan expenditure to see
how this could be reduced progressively. A continuing increase under this Head is clearly
undesirable and cannot be supported in the long run.


8.4 The Committee also hopes that the Department would estimate its non-plan
commitments for the Ninth Five Year Plan well in advance following the guidelines issued by
the Planning Commission so that the unusual situation of meeting non-plan liabilities by
diverting plan funds does not arise in the subsequent years.

9. Last year this Committee had recommended that the Emeritus Scientist Scheme
should be examined from the viewpoint of funds available, effectiveness, desirability of
enlargement, etc. The Comimittee wanted to know the views of the Planning Commission on
providing additional plan budgetary support for human resource development activities,
including the Emeritus Scientist Scheme, a proposal which was sent to the Planning
Commission by the Department for being considered at the mid-term appraisal of the Eighth
Plan concerning the activities of the Department.

9.1 The representative of the Planning Commission pointed out to the Committee that
the outlay to DSIR for the human resource development programmes like Fellowships,
Scientists’ Pool, Emeritus Scientist Scheme, etc. are supported under the non-plan sector. In
addition, plan support is provided for extra-mural research. He assured the Committee that
though the Emeritus Scientist Scheme is covered presently under non-plan segment, if,
however, the Scheme is projected under plan, depending on the resource position, necessary
funds would be provided. The Secretary, DSIR, elaborating on the pointed raised by the
Committee said that there had been an increase of Rs. 5 crores in the non-plan budget
estimated for the year 1995-96 on the Scheme for Human Resource Development. On the
plan side the existing level of Rs. 4 crores had been maintained during 1995-96. The
Emeritus Scientist Scheme has been provided financial support to the extent of Rs. 60 lakhs
in 1995-96 as compared to Rs. 51 lakhs last year.

9.2 The Committee expresses its happiness over the fact that in this year’s budget of
DSIR there is an increase of Rs. 5 crores in the outlay earmarked for Human Resource
Development. The Committee hopes that with this increased allocation, the Department would
support more of such fellowships and associateships as have a definite bearing on scientific
and industrial development; there should be some emphasis in these schemes for the backward
regions and disadvantaged sections of society.

10. Answering a question on in-house R&D, the Secretary informed the Committee that
in-house R&D in industry is undertaken by the industrial units themselves with their own
finances. DSIR is the administrative department which grants recognition to in-house R&D
centres set up by industry, which on the basis of such recognition, are permitted certain
facilities and can avail of tax exemptions. He informed the Committee that about 1250
industrial units have set up R&D centres which have received recognition from DSIR. These
centres concentrate on R&D for improvement of product/process, quality improvement and
cost reduction. These centres also work on the absorption of imported technology. The
incentives and support measures presently available for encouraging R&D in industry
include: Income Tax Relief on R&D expenditure as per 1.T. Act; 125% Weighted Tax
Deduction for sponsored research programmes in approved national laboratoriesinsitutions;
Accelerated Depreciation Allowance on plant and machinery based on indigenous
technology; Price Control Exemption on production of bulk drugs based on indigenous R&D
efforts; Financial Support for R&D programmes taken up by industry either in-house or
jointly with national laboratories/institutions; and National Awards for outstanding K&D
achievements.

10.1 As regards the scheme of Weighted Tax Deduction, as per the notification of
September, 1993, approved research programmes sponsored by industry in any of the


National Laboratories functioning under CSIR, ICMR and ICAR were eligible for the
Weighted Tax Deduction under I.T. Act, 1961. By a notification issued in November, 1994
the scope of the scheme was widened to cover the laboratories of Defence Research
Development Organisation, Department of Electronics, Department of Biotechnology and
Department of Atomic Energy, Universities and Indian Institutes of Technology. The
Secretary informed the Committee that DSIR had given wide publicity to the scheme. As on
31st March 1995, four sponsored research programmes valued at Rs. 43.85 lakhs by industry
in the CSIR laboratories have been approved by DSIR and one more programme is under
consideration.

10.2 The Committee is happy that Government is providing incentives for encouraging in-
house R&D in industry; and the scheme of Weighted Tax Deduction, which originally existed,
and after being abandoned for sometime has been brought back is, no doubt, attractive to
bring about interaction between industry and the Government laboratories. Though DSIR has
given wide publicity to the Weighted Tax Deduction Scheme through newspaper advertisement
and in-house R&D Industry Update, as also through the workshopsconferences organised by
DSIR, the Committee feels that this scheme still requires more publicity and promotion. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that publicity for this scheme may also be organised
through electronic media, besides newspaper advertisements, etc. In addition DSIR will have
to organise get-togethers on specific areas/projects where industry and national R&D can
work together to mutual advantage. Major promotional efforts to bring about increasing
interaction is called for.

11. Last year the Committee had recommended that DSIR should strengthen its
linkages with socio-economic Ministrie/Departments with regard to society-related
programmes. The Committee was ihformed that dialogues have been initiated with socio-
economic Ministries/Departments, State Government agencies and NGOs with a view to
identifying societal needs which called for development of appropriate technologies and their
transfer to the beneficiary groups. The interaction has particularly been strengthened with
the socio-economic Ministries and scientific departments. These departments have provided
additional allocations, in the areas of leather, water oilseeds and pulses, bio-technology,
environment, ocean sciences, electronic materials, etc. S&T inputs to small scale sectors are
also being offered. :

11.1 The Committee notes that, with regard to society-related programmes, the
Department has set up linkages with several socio-economic MinistriesDepartments and
scientific departments: The Committee hopes that such linkages would generate various locale
specific approaches and appropriate technologies for the benefit of rural masses and society-
related programmes.

12. Programme Aimed at Technological Self-Reliance (PATSER) of DSIR aims at
promoting and accelerating the efforts of industry to undertake Research, Design,
Development, Engineering (RDDE) Projects for Technology Absorption and Technology
Development. The Committee was told that these projects have considerable commercial
potential and relate to new ‘or improved project/process technologies. Through this scheme,
strong R&D linkages have been established between industry, national laboratories and
institutions as well as user industry. While examining the allocation for this scheme the
Committee noticed an increase from Rs. 7.68 crores in the last year’s budget te Rs. 9.23
crores in the budget estimate for the year 1995-96. The Committee wanted to know the
reasons for this increase.

12.1 It was explained to the Committee that over 30 new projects have been supported


under the programme by DSIR during the year 1994-95. In addition, there are about 30 on-

" going projects of industry which have been approved by DSIR before 1994-95. Budgetary
estimates for these projects in 1994-95 under PATSER was Rs. 7.68 crores. In order to meet
further financial requirements of these on-going projects in 1995-96 amounting to Rs. 4
crores, as well as financial requirements of over Rs. 5 crores for about 30 new projects to be
supported by DSIR during 1995-96, a budget estimate of Rs. 9.23 crores was indicated under
PATSER. Financial commitment of industries for these projects has been on an average
about 70% of the Project costs.

12.2 The Committee hopes that, by supporting an increasing number of important RDDE
projects of industry having high techno-socio-commercial inputs, PATSER will be able to
enhance its thrust during 1995-96 in promoting and accelerating the efforts of industry in
technology development as also involving industry in developing and commercialising new and
improved products or processes in various sectors of economy which would strengthen
linkages between industry and national research organisations. The Committee would like
DSIR to make a study of the approach adopted by MITI of Japan for such purposes. The
Committee would also like DSIR to examine how funding from the to-be-established
Technology Development Fund (under DST) can be used meaningfully in this area.

13. The Committee wanted to know if any of the CSIR: laboratories is working in the
area of Developing Neem as contraceptive and for any other medical uses. To this, the
Secretary replied that the Central Food Technological Research Institute, Mysore; Indian
Institute of Chemical Technology, Hyderabad; and National Chemical Laboratory, Pune are
working on deyelopment of Neem-based derivatives for insect control. No CSIR laboratory
is developing Neem as a contraceptive. The National Institute of Immunology and Defence
Institute of Physiology and Allied Sciences, however, are working on its development as a
spermicidal agent. According to the Secretary, CSIR labs are developing soap nut as
spermicidal agent for use as a vaginal contraceptive and it is under Phase III clinical trials.

14. The Committee also expressed concern over the arsenic contamination in potable
water in some areas of West Bengal which is a problem of very serious magnitude. The
Committee wanted to know if CSIR could help in the matter. The Committee was told that
detection of arsenic in potable water will not be difficult and some of the CSIR laboratories
could help in detecting and assessing degree of contamination.

14.1 The Committee hopes that CSIR would ensure that its relevant laboratories provide
belp in solving the problem of arsenic contamination in potable water in some parts of West
Bengal and would provide whatever assistance would be required by the Public Health
Engineering Department of that State to chalk out a programme to tackle the problem.

15. The Committee referred to the shortage of residential accommodation for the
scientists working in the CSIR laboratories and wanted to know about the steps which are
envisaged by the Department to tackle the situation. The Secretary, DSIR explained that
residential accommodation is a major problem, particularly in the metropolitan cities. The
satisfaction level is around 26% on an average. For 1995-96 a budgetary provision of Rs.
13.5 crores has been made under the scheme on residential building.

15.1 The Committee expresses its concern over the shortage of residential accommodation
for scientists working in the CSIR laboratories. Lack of residential accommodation is a
éeterrent to attracting talented scientists to CSIR and it will even be difficult to retain
talented people if no proper accommodation is made available to them. The Committee,
therefore, feels that more funds be earmarked for this activity in the coming years. The
Department should also encourage staff members to build their own houses and Government
could consider enhancing the present limit of House Building Advance admissible to the
employees.


16. After making a detailed scrutiny of the Demands for Grants of the Department of
Scientific and Industrial Research and assessing the progress of various important
programmes and projects administered by it, the Committee recommends that the Demands
for Grants of Rs. 411.03 crores, as sought by the Department of Scientific and Industrial
Research for the year 1995-96, under demand No. 73 are in order and may be approved.

2. In the forenoon session, the Committee took up consideration of the Demands for
Grants (1995-96) of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research. First of all, the
Principal Advisor in the Planning Commission, while responding to a query from the
Committee, gave clarifications on certain aspects relating to the allocations for some of the
areas as proposed in the Demands for Grants (1995-96) of the Department of Scientific and
Industrial Research. The Secretary of the Department thereafter gave an overview of the
activities/programmes being pursued by the Department and explained how the allocations
proposed during the year 1995-96 would be utilised. The Members then sought clarifications
on various points relating to the Demands for Grants which were given by the Secretary and
other representatives of the Department. है

3. In the afternoon session, the Committee further considered the Demands for Grants
(1995-96) of the Ministry of Environment and Forests. The Inspector-General of Forests in
the Ministry of Environment and Forests, assisted by other officials, supplemented some of
the points earlier made before the Committee in its meeting held on the 6th April, 1995 and
replied to the clarifications sought by the Members.

4. The discussion on the Demands for Grants (1995-96) of the Department of Scientific
and Industrial Research and Ministry of Environment and Forests was concluded.

A verbatim record of the proceedings was kept.
5. The Committee then adjourned at 6.07 p.m.

2. The Committee took up consideration of its draft Twenty-fourth Report on the
Demands for Grants (1995-96) of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research
(Ministry of Science and Technology) and adopted the same unanimously.

3. The Committee decided to present this Report to both the Houses of Parliament on
the 12th May, 1995. The Committee authorised its Chairman and in his absence, Prof.
M.G.K. Menon to present this Report to the Rajya Sabha and Shri Oscar Fernandes and in
his absence, Shri Harchand Singh to lay the Report on the Table of the Lok Sabha.

4. The Committee thereafter took up for consideration its draft Twenty-fifth Report on
the Demands for Grants (1995-96) of the Department of Electronics and adopted the same
unanimously

5. The Committee decided to present this Report to both the Houses of Parliament on
the 15th May, 1995. the Committee authorised its Chairman and in his absence, Shri K.
Rahman Khan to present this Report to the Rajya Sabha and Shri Sharad Dighe and in his
absence, Prof. Radhika Ranjan Pramanik to lay the Report on the Table of the Lok Sabha.

6. The Committee then adjourned at 10.20 a.m.
