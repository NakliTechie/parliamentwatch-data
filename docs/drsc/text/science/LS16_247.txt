REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Science
and Technology on the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred Fortieth Report of the
Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and
Forests on the Demands for Grants (2013-2014) of the Department of Science and Technology,
which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament on 8
th May, 2013. There were twenty two
recommendations/observations in the Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the
recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 22
nd August, 2013.
Department has intimated action taken by it on twenty one recommendations but has not given
reply on the remaining one recommendation. The Department has intimated that it has accepted all
the recommendations of the Committee. The Committee, on examination of Action Taken Notes,
however was not satisfied with action taken by the Department in respect of two recommendations
and would like the Department to take further action in respect of those two recommendations as
indicated in the Report.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee
on the action taken by the Department of Science and Technology are set out in the following
paragraphs:–
Recommendation (Para 4.5)
The Committee therefore, recommends that the Planning Commission should provide
additional funds, if so required by the Department.
Action Taken by Government
No comments made by the Department.
Comments
The Committee notes with displeasure that the Department has not responded to this
recommendation of the Committee. It is, therefore, not known if the Department does not
need additional funds or if it has taken the matter with Planning Commission what has
been the results thereof. The Committee directs the Department to inform the action taken
on this recommendation and be careful in future, in preparing Action taken notes on
recommendations of the Committee.
Recommendation (Para 5.6)
The Department must keep this fact in mind that budgetary resources are very scarce and
a priority is given to the scientific Departments in the hope of better use of funds, both qualitatively
and quantitatively. It is, noteworthy, that Department’s underutilization of allocated resources for
three years in a row does not present a healthy picture of the Department. It rather reflects upon
its performance. The Committee, therefore, recommends that every possible effort should be made
to utilize the budgetary allocation optimally. The Committee finds that the phenomenon of under
utilization persists despite the claim of the Department of having a very sound and robust
monitoring mechanism. The Committee is constrained to observe that the monitoring mechanism of
the Department appears to be strong, on paper only, as it has failed to produce satisfactory and
desired result of ensuring full utilization of allocated resources, and therefore recommends that the

2
Department should further strengthen its monitoring mechanism and make it more robust to ensure
full and effective utilization of allocated funds.
Action Taken by Government
The Department is determined to implement its schemes and programmes with optimum
utilization of resources. The Budgetary allocation (Plan) and actual expenditure of the Department
during the last three financial years (2010-11, 2011-12 and 2012-13) are given below:

Though there are several new programmes that have been planned for the Twelfth Five year

The Committee takes note of the reply of the Department
Recommendation (Para 6.3)
The Committee feels that such cuts would lead to shortage of resources and may adversely
affect the progress of some of the programmes. The Committee further, notes that these disturbing
cuts have been imposed when new Science, Technology and Innovation Policy, 2013 has been
launched with much fanfare. While on one hand, the Government has given a healthy sign of
promoting science and innovations in the country but on the other hand it had not shown faith on
the scientific Departments like DST by imposing cuts on its proposed allocations. The Committee,
therefore, recommends that adequate funds should be made available to the Department at RE stage
by Planning Commission based on its requirements.
Action Taken by Government
The Department values the concerns raised by the committee for allocation of adequate
resources at RE stage. The Department has been prioritizing its programme based on the resources
available at the RE stage and would strive hard to utilize it to the fullest extent.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.

Recommendation (Para 7.10)
The Committee recommends that the key goals enshrined in the Policy document should be
realized within the given time frame particularly the goal of positioning India amongst the top five
global scientific powers by 2020 and the Department must make all out efforts to achieve these goals.
Action Taken by Government
The Department will make sincere efforts to achieve the goals enshrined in the Policy
document. A Policy Research Cell in this regard has already started functioning within the
Department. At least 4-5 Policy Research Centers, in the public-funded academic institutions will
be established to carry out policy related research in the remaining period of the Twelfth Plan.
Comments
The Committee takes note of setting up of Policy Research Cell but feels that the
Department should make sincere and focused efforts to realise the goals set in the Science,
Technology and Innovation Policy, 2013.
Recommendation (Para 9.5)
The Committee finds that Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB) has been formed
after taking into consideration the best practices and funding models of developed countries
including National Science Foundation of USA while adopting a research model which is suited to
Indian setting. The Committee while noting that research proposals for competitive grants are
subjected to two levels of appraisals expects that the Department shall implement the Basic
Research Programme efficiently based on the principles of transparency, impartiality and ethical
considerations to raise the standards of Basic research in the country.
Action Taken by Government
As observed by the Committee, the Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB) has
instituted various appraisal levels to implement the Basic Research Programme efficiently based on
the principles of transparency, impartiality and ethical considerations to raise the standards of basic
research in the country.
Comments
The Committee appreciates that the Science and Engineering Research Board (SERB)
has instituted various appraisal levels to implement the Basic Research Programme
efficiently and hopes appraisals would be made objectively and in a purposeful manner and
steps taken by the Department would lead to raising the standards of basic research in the
country.
Recommendation (Para 10.11)
The Committee feels that in order to enhance investment in R&D, the private sector’s
contribution needs to be significantly augmented. The Committee had in its 223
rd report emphasized
on the need to enhance private sector contribution to R&D by offering incentives, forming
partnership, etc. The Department in their Action Taken Replies endorsed the view of the Committee
but the desired result still eludes us. The Committee feels that somewhere we have failed in our
efforts to woo private sector and not much has been achieved even in the second year of the
Twelfth Five Year Plan period.

4
Action Taken by Government
It is an undisputed fact that if India were to achieve the goal of 2% of GDP expenditure
on R&D, private sector investment into R&D needs to be significantly augmented. This issue has
also been highlighted by the Government during formulation of Twelfth Five Year Plan.
At present, the Government is providing various indirect tax incentives to the private sector
for R&D. Some of these incentives are – 100% write off of revenue expenditure on R&D; 100%
write off of capital expenditure on R&D in the year the expenditure is incurred; weighted tax
deduction @200% for sponsored research programmes in approved national laboratories,
Universities and IITs; weighted tax deduction @ 200% on in-house R&D expenditure to companies
engaged in the business of bio-technology or in the business of manufacture or production of any
article or thing not being an article or thing specified in the list of the eleventh schedule; income-
tax exemption @175% to donations made to approved non commercial scientific and industrial
research organizations; accelerated depreciation allowance for investment in plant and machinery
based on indigenous technology. Similarly, exemptions from Customs and Central Excise duty are
available in various forms to private sector. Various types of direct incentives are also provided to
the private sector in the form of grant/loan by Department of Science and Technology, Department
of Scientific and Industrial Research, Department of Biotechnology.
A Joint Committee of Industry and Government has been constituted by the Department of
Science and Technology in 2011 to look into various aspects related to stimulation of investment
of private sector into R&D in India. The Committee has pointed out that current estimates of
private sector investment into R&D is generally limited to those incurred for direct research in a
laboratory in the form of plant and machinery, manpower, consumables and utilities and they do
not cover costs relating to translation of R&D like test-beds, design and development,
standardization, field costs, etc. The Committee was of the view that current investment of private
sector into R&D might be underestimated and the estimate may not capture all investments being
made by the non-government sector in India.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that the Department will take steps to get
investment of private sector into R&D estimated as per international norms and in case
these estimates show lower levels of private sector investment, the Department should take
steps to find out reasons as to why private sector investments into R&D are low in India
vis-à-vis investment in other developed countries despite various concessions available and
work out a potent strategy to stimulate private sector investment into R&D in India.
Recommendation (Para 10.12)
The Committee recommends that Ministry must devise innovative ways to substantially
increase private sector investment in R&D in order to make it at par with the investment of private
sector in R&D as in other developed countries. The Government may even consider making
contribution of the private sector in R&D mandatory taking a cue from South Korean experience
which reversed South Korean Government’s burden in Science and technology Sector in a decade
with a series of laws. The Committee, further, recommends that Government should, in all
situations ensure that investment in R&D gets enhanced to at least 2% of Gross Domestic Product
at the earliest so as to realize the goal of becoming a scientific super power by 2020.
Action Taken by Government
The Department is proactively working towards stimulation of investment of private sector
into R&D through various ways. The suggestion of the Committee to consider making contribution

5
of private sector in R&D mandatory may need wider consultation with the stakeholders at all levels
Government and private sector, and a consensus needs to be arrived at political level before a law
is considered to be passed by the Government in this regard. The best course would be to motivate
the private sector in increasing their share of investment into R&D voluntarily in view of the
benefits it would bring to the private sector in terms of global competitiveness.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that further steps would be taken to
generate new sources of R&D funding and motivate private Sector to invest more in R&D.
Recommendation (Para 11.10)
The Committee takes note of considerable improvement in the performance of some major
universities in their contribution to research and publications. The Committee is, however, of the
view that we shall have to put in tremendous efforts to make the quality of teaching and research
in our Institutions and Universities at par with the best in the world. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the Department should provide greater focus on the quality of teaching and
research in our Institutions and Universities.
Action Taken by Government
As observed by the Committee, there is considerable improvement in the performance of
Universities in respect of research and publications due to implementation of a few ongoing
Schemes like ‘Funds for Improvement of S&T Infrastructure in Universities and Higher Educational
Institutions’ (FIST) as well as initiation of a few new schemes in 11th plan like ‘Promotion of
University Research and Scientific Excellence’ (PURSE), “Consolidation of University Research for
Innovation and Excellence (CURIE) in Women Universities”, Special Package for North East Region
(NER), Jammu and Kashmir and Bihar. In the 12th Plan, all these ongoing efforts have been
strengthened by enlarging the Scope of Support and a greater focus towards improving the quality
both in teaching and research to a large number of institutions.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply. The reply of the Department is, however, silent on
whether the measures taken by the Department has resulted in improved quality of
teaching and research in our institutions and universities. The Committee notes with
unhappiness that despite the various measures taken by the Department, India has only
four institutions to the top 400 list and top most Indian university was placed between 226
and 250 in the global rankings. India’s prestigious Indian Institute of Technology (IITs),
were ranked in the 351-400 bracket. The Department needs to take effective measures to
improve the quality of teaching and Research in our institutions and Universities.
Recommendation (Para 11.11)
In addition, the Committee also recommends that the Department should also provide support
to improve the quality of research and teaching in the universities/Higher education Institutions based
in backward and far-flung, remote areas of the country and bring them at par with Research
Institutions/Research Laboratories of the country by the end of Twelfth Five Year Plan.
Action Taken by Government
The ongoing schemes like ‘FIST’ and Special Packages for North-East Region, Jammu and
Kashmir and Bihar are already in place to take care of strengthening infrastructure facilities at the

6
less-endowed institutes including Colleges, institutes located in remote places such as North-eastern
region, Jammu and Kashmir and Bihar State etc. While there has been a considerable impact of all
these schemes in respect of increase in publications etc., from these institutions, plans have already
been initiated in the XII Plan to widen the scope under these schemes so that more institutions
could be covered with the aim to bring them at par with other institutions in the country.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply. The Committee, however, would like the Department
to make an assessment as to the extent the measures taken by it have helped at the
ground level to improve the quality of research and teaching in the Universities in
backward, far-flung and remote areas of the country.
Recommendation (Para 12.5)
The Committee feels that in a country such as ours, where a large section of the population
lives in abject poverty, technological achievements made in the laboratories would be more valuable
if these also address the cause of the underprivileged masses and help in alleviating their poverty.
In this context, the Committee appreciates the programme undertaken by the Department for
livelihood generation and improving the quality of life of people at large particularly the scheduled
castes and scheduled tribes by providing affordable technology solutions and other scientific
interventions.
Recommendation (Para 12.6)
The Committee, however, would like to see such benefits extended further to cover more
and more scheduled castes and scheduled tribes’ population and accordingly, recommends that the
Department should further expand this programme.
Action Taken by Government
As regards to the recommendations contained in Para Nos 12.5 and 12.6, the Committee
would be happy to know that a new programme under the name Technology Application for
Livelihood Improvement (TALIM) has been initiated to cover more and more vulnerable sections
of the Scheduled Caste population.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the new initiative taken by the Department to uplift
weaker segment of the Society through Technology Application for Livelihood Improvement
(TALIM) scheme and hopes that concrete results would flow in the near future from the
programmes launched by the Department. The Committee also expects that efforts of the
Department for livelihood generation and improving the quality of life of people would boost
the interest of socially weaker segments of the society in Science and Technology and help
to raise their standards of living.
Recommendation (Para 13.7)
The Committee notes that the Department is undertaking modernisation and upgradation of
infrastructure and technical capabilities of SoI and NATMO under the programme “Modernisation
of Mapping Organisations”. The Committee, however, notes that in the year 2012-13, Department
failed to fully utilize funds made available to it under this programme. Modernisation of

7
infrastructural facilities and upgradation of data capturing and processing capabilities and technical
skills of these organizations are essential for efficient exploration and mapping of the country’s
domain and to ensure that all resources available in the country contribute in their full measures
to the progress, prosperity and security of our country. The Committee, therefore, expresses its
displeasure that the Department has not even been able to fully utilize funds meant for upgradation
of infrastructure of its two important organisations and recommends that the Department should
take all necessary measures to ensure early and successful implementation of this programme.
Action Taken by Government
The recommendations made by the Committee have been accepted by both Survey of India
(SoI) and National Atlas and Thematic Mapping Organization (NATMO). Both the organizations are
taking necessary steps to implement the recommendations and to ensure that the funds allotted to
them in the financial year 2013-14 are fully utilized.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation (Para 14.4)
The Committee notes that DISHA Programme has been launched by the Department to
encourage women to pursue a career in science research as also for augmenting the pool of
women scientists in the country and is a socially useful programme. The Department has funded
projects and provided scholarship to women scientists under this programme. The Committee,
further, notes that there has been a marginal increase in the funds allocated to the programme in
the current fiscal year and hopes that Department will make efficient use of funds allocated in a
transparent and objective manner so that objectives of the programme are achieved.
Action Taken by Government
In order to take the programme of ‘DISHA’ forward, the scheme has been modified to
enhance its outreach to all parts of the country. Another proposed modification is the provision for
one year internship of Women Scientists to facilitate collection of data by literature research,
baseline data and beneficiary requirement so that Women Scientist can generate a viable project
under the mentorship of senior scientists of the National Institutes.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply. The Committee would like the Department to carry
out periodic review of its schemes such as ‘DISHA’ and commission an independent study
to assess the actual benefits flowing from its schemes to bring gender parity in S&T
Research and Development.
Recommendation (Para 15.4)
The Committee commends the measures taken by the Department to attract and retain
Indian scientists who have settled in various parts of the world for pursuing research in India in
their respective field of expertise as well as in home country research programs and notes with
satisfaction that measures taken have led to reversal of flow of researchers. The Committee
recommends that further measures be taken to make career in science, research and innovation
lucrative enough to attract best talent available in the country.

8
Action Taken by Government
The Department has given special attention to bring back Indian brain to our country to
pursue world class research in Indian setting. The ongoing Ramanujan Fellowship of the
Department for brilliant scientists and engineers from all over the world to take up scientific
research positions in India has received utmost attention and the number of fellowships is
increasing steadily. Further, the Department has approved institution of National Jawaharlal Nehru
Science Fellowship aimed to attract scientists of global eminence to come and work in Indian
Academic and Research Institutions. These measures make career in science, research and
innovation lucrative enough to attract best talent available in the country.
As recommended by the Parliamentary Standing Committee further measure are being taken
by DST to make career in Science, research and innovation lucrative enough to attract best talent
available in the country. International Division facilitates synergies of national S&T initiatives by
contributing in building suitable recommendations under the frameworks of:
● PM Global Advisory Council of Overseas Indians (PM GAC-OI) and its associated
Steering Group on Knowledge transfer, Science and Technology and Higher Education Collaboration
and Ministry of Overseas Indian Affairs focuses on drawing upon the experience, knowledge and
skills of eminent people of Indian Origin in diverse fields.
● Ministry of Overseas Indian Affairs sponsored “Global Indian Network of Knowledge
(Global INK)”, an electronic platform for harnessing the knowledge and skills of the Diaspora for
the benefit of the country; drawing on the knowledge and expertise of Global Indians, and of
people with an affinity for India, who are outstanding in areas of Environment, Healthcare,
Innovation and Science and Technology, without relocating them.
Apart from the ongoing Ramanujan Fellowship etc., during Eleventh Plan Period, the
Department of Science and Technology initiated a new scheme titled Innovation in Science Pursuit
for Inspired Research (INSPIRE) Faculty Award under ‘Assured Opportunity for Research Career
(AORC)’. Though this scheme is primarily designed for arresting the exodus of young talents from
India, it also attracts and brings back young scientists settled abroad. INSPIRE Faculty Award has
already been offered to about 100 young scientists engaged in research outside India for carrying
out research at the Indian Universities/Institutions.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and expects that initiatives taken by the Department
would bring the eminent Indian Scientists doing research abroad back to the country. The
Committee, however, would like the Department to assess as to the extent its initiatives
have led scholars of eminence to come back and work in the country.
Recommendation (Para 16.8)
The Committee feels that the Super Computing Facility and Capacity Building and National
Geographic Information System (NGIS) programmes are of great national importance and hence
recommends that the Department should make every possible effort to successfully implement them
in a time bound manner. The Committee, further, recommends that the Department must ensure
that the allocated outlays for these two schemes are fully utilized.
Action Taken by Government
The Department would take all necessarily steps for implementation of the Scheme Super
Computing Facility and Capacity Building ; thereby ensure the utilization of allocated outlays.

9
Department of Science and Technology (DST) and Department of Electronics and
Information Technology (Deity) are jointly implementing the project of Super Computing Facility
and Capacity Building. Deity has constituted a Committee to finalize the Expenditure Finance
Commission (EFC) document.
The modalities for positioning of National Geographic Information System (NGIS) have
been discussed in the High Power Governing Council meeting. The Expenditure Finance Committee
has also recommended the NGIS project. DST is in the process of obtaining the approval of the
Cabinet for selection of Mission Director on search-cum-selection basis.
Comments
The Committee notes that procedural requirements for the setting up of these two
new programmes, such as preparation of detailed project Report, approval through
Expenditure Finance Committee and Cabinet, appointment of a full time Mission Director
etc. has been initiated.
The Committee hopes that all the further necessary proposals to take these
programmes forward would be finalized expeditiously.
Recommendation (Para 17)
The Committee hopes that Department of Science and Technology shall make prudent
utilization of funds allocated to it under Demand No. 85 for the year 2013-14 having due regard
to the recommendations made by the Committee and efficiently implement its various programmes
and schemes in the year 2013-14.
Action Taken by Government
The recommendations of the Committee is noted for prudent utilization of allocated
resources by the Department during the year 2013-14.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
