R E P O R T      
INTRODUCTION 
 
 Earth System Science deals with all the five components of the Earth 
System, viz., Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, Cryosphere, Geosphere, and 
Biosphere, and their complex interactions. The Ministry of Earth Sciences 
(MoES) holistically addresses all the aspects relating to the Earth System 
Science for providing weather, climate, ocean, coastal state, hydrological, and 
seismological services. The services include forecasts and warnings for various 
natural disasters like tropical cyclones, storm surges, floods, heat waves, 
thunderstorms, and lightning; alerts for Tsunamis and monitoring of 
earthquakes, etc. In addition, the ministry also has the mandate to develop 
technology for ocean surveys and exploration of living and non-living 
resources from the ocean and exploration of all three poles (Arctic, Antarctic, 
and Himalayas). The services provided by the ministry are being effectively 
used by different agencies and state governments to save human lives and 
minimize damages due to natural disasters. 
 
1.2 The mandate of the ministry includes overall policy, planning, 
coordination, implementation, evaluation and review of the schemes relating to 
meteorology, seismology, oceanography, ocean resources and technology, 
polar science, cryosphere and geosciences not specifically allocated to any 
other Department or Ministry.  The overarching vision of the Ministry is 
“Excelling in Earth System Science to make lives better through world class 
services for weather, climate, ocean, coastal and natural hazards; sustainable 
harnessing of ocean resources and exploration of the polar regions”.  The 
Objectives of the Ministry are as under :- 
i. To provide Weather and Climate Services for various sectors like 
disaster management, agriculture, aviation, water resources, power, 
tourism, health, air quality, public safety etc. 
ii. To undertake the Science of Climate Change- Regional Climate Change 
Scenarios. 
iii. To provide Early warnings for Hazards such as Cyclones, Heavy 
Rainfall, Heatwave, etc. 
iv. To provide Ocean Services, which include Tsunami warnings, Potential 
Fishing zone advisories, Ocean State Forecasts, Oil Spill Advisories, 
Coastal Research, and Coastal Protection. 
v. Harnessing of non-living ocean resources - Water, Energy and Minerals 
vi. Ocean living resources mapping, Marine biodiversity, Marine 
Biotechnology

vii. Survey and Exploration of Exclusive Economic Zone and Continental 
Shelf 
viii. Earthquake Detection and information, Deep Scientific drilling, Crustal 
Dynamics, Geoscience research. 
ix. Scientific exploration of the three poles (Arctic, Antarctic and 
Himalayas) and Cryosphere research. 
x. Develop deep sea technologies for sustainable use of ocean resources, 
and improve our understanding of deep sea. 
xi. R&D and development of technology leading to weather intervention 
xii. To support various Extra-Mural R&D activities, Outreach and 
Awareness activities and Capacity Building in the field of Earth 
Sciences. 
 
1.3 The policies and programs of the Ministry are being primarily pursued 
through its 10 Institutes viz., autonomous bodies, attached and subordinate 
offices.  The institutions dealing with atmospheric sciences are the India 
Meteorological Department (IMD), National Centre for Medium Range 
Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF), Noida and Indian Institute of Tropical 
Meteorology (IITM), Pune.  The institutions related to ocean science and 
technology are National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR), Goa, 
National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT), Chennai, Indian National 
Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), Hyderabad, Centre for 
Marine Living Resources and Ecology (CMLRE), Kochi and National Centre 
for Coastal Research (NCCR), Chennai. The National Centre for Earth Science 
Studies (NCESS), Thiruvananthapuram and National Centre for Seismology 
(NCS), New Delhi conduct research on solid earth and earthquake monitoring. 
Each Centre of the Ministry has a well-defined mandate to conduct research 
and development in a specific area of Earth Sciences.

5. Deep Ocean Mission 
5.1 As a Maritime nation with a coastline of about 7500 km and around 30% 
of the country's population living in coastal areas, oceans play an important 
role in the Indian economy. The Government of India’s Vision of New India by 
2030, enunciated in February 2019, highlighted the Blue Economy as one of 
the ten core dimensions of growth. With a view to exploring the deep ocean for 
resources, developing deep-sea technologies for sustainable use of ocean 
resources, and improving our understanding of the deep sea, the Government 
of India launched the “Deep Ocean Mission” at an estimated cost of Rs.4077.0 
crore for a period of 5 years. This mission mode project will support various 
priority areas illustrated in the Blue Economy policy document of the 
Government of India. Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) is the nodal 
implementing Ministry for this multi-ministerial, multi-institutional mission.   
5.2 Deep Ocean Mission (DOM) aims to explore deep-oceanic resources and 
develop technologies for their sustainable use. The Mission consists of six 
major themes, namely (i) Development of Technologies for Deep Sea Mining, 
Manned Submersible, and Underwater Robotics; (ii) Development of Ocean 
Climate Change Advisory Services; (iii) Technological innovations for 
exploration and conservation of deep-sea biodiversity; (iv) Deep Ocean Survey 
and Exploration; (v) Energy and freshwater from the Ocean and (vi) Advanced 
Marine Station for Ocean Biology. The mission involves mapping of floors of 
Deep Oceans and development of technologies like a manned submersible with 
6000m water depth rating, mining system for deep sea mining, sustainable 
utilization of deep-sea bioresources and developing engineering designs for 
offshore thermal energy-driven desalination plants. Human capacity will be 

developed in ocean biology and engineering through the translation of research into industrial applications.5.3 The financial achievements of the scheme during the last three years are as under :      Financial Year BE2022-23 650.002023-24 600.002024-25 600.00

6. Prithvi Vigyan (PRITHVI) 
6.1 Ministry of Earth Sciences is mandated to translate Science to Services 
for Society in providing services for weather, climate, ocean and coastal state, 
hydrology, seismology, and natural hazards; to explore and harness marine 
living and non-living resources in a sustainable manner for the country and to 
explore the three poles of the Earth (Arctic, Antarctic and Himalayas).  
PRITHVI Scheme deals with the study of various aspects of Ocean, Polar and 
Cryosphere with Special emphasis on the Antarctic, Arctic, Southern Ocean 
and Himalayas, Seismological and Geo-science as well as Research Education, 
Training and Outreach.  All the earlier schemes, viz. Ocean Services, 
Modelling Application, Resources and Technology (O-SMART), Polar Science 
and Cryosphere Research (PACER), Seismology and Geosciences (SAGE) and 
Research, Education, Training and Outreach (REACHOUT) have been merged 
together in the PRITHVI scheme.   
6.2 The overarching scheme of PRITHVI will holistically address all the 
five components of earth system to improve the understanding of the Earth 
System Sciences and to provide reliable services for the country.  The major 
Objectives of the Prithvi Scheme are:  
 Augmentation and sustenance of long-term observations of the 
atmosphere, ocean, geosphere, cryosphere and solid earth to record the 
vital signs of the Earth System and change

7. Mission Mausam  
7.1 This scheme has been designed to enhance India's weather and climate 
forecasting capabilities across various critical sectors, including agriculture, 
aviation, urban planning, defense, sports, and disaster preparedness. The 
mission seeks to leverage cutting-edge technologies, high-performance 
computing, and advanced modeling techniques to revolutionize weather 
monitoring, prediction, and data analysis. By advancing weather surveillance, 
incorporating next-generation radars and satellites, and utilizing artificial 
intelligence and machine learning, Mission Mausam aims to provide more 
accurate and timely forecasts, addressing the challenges of extreme weather 
events and climate change. The program also focuses on building robust 
infrastructure, fostering innovation, and ensuring that weather-related 
information reaches the public effectively, contributing to national 
development, disaster management, and climate resilience. Through its 
comprehensive approach, Mission Mausam aims to establish an integrated 
framework for advanced weather and climate prediction in India.   
7.2 The Government of India has approved the Mission Mausam with a 
financial outlay of ₹ 2000 crore with Rs. 1500 crore being additional 
allocation, and the remaining Rs.500 crore is drawn from the PRITHVI scheme 
(ACROSS sub-scheme) of the MoES, which was approved earlier.

initiative, the major components under this mission, and the expected impact 
on India's meteorological infrastructure.  In response, the Committee was 
informed that Mission Mausam aims to significantly enhance India's 
meteorological infrastructure for advancing weather and climate prediction 
capabilities and improving weather management systems. With an increased 
budget of Rs. 1329.00 crore in BE 2025-26, the mission focuses on a number 
of key objectives, as under : 
 Develop Cutting Edge Weather Surveillance Technologies & Systems. 
 Implement Higher resolution atmospheric observations with better 
temporal and spatial sampling/coverage 
 Implement Next-generation radars, and satellites with advanced 
instrument payloads. 
 Implement High-Performance Computers (HPC). 
 Improve understanding of weather and climate processes and prediction 
capabilities. 
 Develop improved earth system models, and data-driven methods (use of 
AI/ML). 
 Develop Technologies for weather management. 
 Develop state-of-art Decision Support System (DSS) and dissemination 
system for last mile connectivity. 
 Strengthen Capacity building and research collaborations. 
 

15 
 
7.6 As regards the expected impact of the mission, it was informed that 
Mission Mausam is designed to provide highly accurate forecasts across 
multiple time scales: 
(i) Short-Term (up to 72 hours): High-resolution forecasts using data 
from advanced observation systems like radars, wind profilers, and 
satellites, combined with real-time computational models for rapid 
prediction. 
(ii) Medium-Term (3 to 15 days): Enhanced weather predictions utilizing 
advanced modeling techniques and HPC resources for greater 
accuracy. 
(iii) Long-Term (seasonal to annual): The integration of Earth system 
models, data-driven methods, and AI/ML to forecast large-scale 
weather and climate patterns over months or seasons. 
 
7.7 The Committee hopes that with the requisite budget allocation at 
their disposal, the Ministry would make all out efforts to ensure that all 
the objectives of the Mission planned for the next financial year are 
achieved in a time-bound manner and allocation utilized optimally.  The 
Committee feels that by improving weather prediction accuracy, Mission 
Mausam will have a profound impact on sectors like agriculture, disaster 
management, and transportation, enabling better preparedness and more 
effective responses to climate and weather events. The mission’s integrated 
approach will help deliver more precise, timely, and actionable forecasts, 
thus contributing to improved safety, economic stability, and climate 
resilience for India. 
8. High-Performance Computing System 
8.1 The High-Performance Computing (HPC) systems deployed across 
various institutes of the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) in previous plan 
periods have been crucial in enhancing the accuracy of weather forecasts and 
advancing research and development over the past decade. These HPC systems 
have facilitated seamless data assimilation and the generation of precise 
forecasts with extended lead times from both global and regional Numerical 
Weather Prediction (NWP) models, leading to significant improvements in 
weather, climate, and ocean state predictions. The proposed scheme aims to 
establish a petaflops-scale HPC facility for MoES institutes to support 
numerical modelling for weather and climate forecasting, basic research, and 
data assimilation. It will also provide computational resources to the academic 
and R&D community, to undertake research and development and enable 
improvements in operational forecasting systems and enhance forecast skill. 
Additionally, the initiative will develop a robust computational and 
visualization environment to support state-of-the-art Big Data Analytics and

weather/climate applications and advancing AI/ML integration.

11. Use of modern technologies  
11.1 The Committee enquired from the Ministry regarding the use of new 
technologies or innovations in weather prediction and how the Ministry was 
utilizing advanced and modern technologies such as Artificial Intelligence 
(AI), Machine Learning (ML), and Big Data to improve weather forecasting 
models.   The Committee also desired to know the role of supercomputing and 
high-performance computing in improving weather predictions? 
11.2 In response to the above, the Committee was informed that the Ministry 
has taken various steps to strengthen the observational and weather forecasting 
infrastructure across the country. The Ministry adopts new technologies at 
various stages, including the augmentation of observational networks, 
numerical/AI-ML modeling, forecasting methodology, visualization, and 
dissemination of forecasts/warnings to various stakeholders.  The Ministry 
explores integrating artificial intelligence (AI) technologies into weather and 
climate forecasting systems, in addition to physics-based numerical models. 
The key initiatives, future plans, and innovative projects are as follows: 
(i) Virtual Center on AI/Machine Learning (ML)/Deep Learning 
(DL) at IITM Pune:   The Ministry has established a specialized 
virtual center at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) 
in Pune. This center focuses on leveraging AI, ML, and DL 
techniques for advancements in Earth Sciences, handling large-
datasets. It has already developed several AI/ML-based applications 
tailored for localized predictions and the analysis of weather and 
climate patterns. 
(ii) Collaborative Research Across Institutes: Institutions under the 
Ministry are actively working to incorporate AI/ML methodologies 
into their research activities and operational frameworks. This 
collaborative approach ensures a comprehensive application of AI 
technologies across Earth sciences. 
11.3 The Committee was further informed that the augmented HPC capacity 
will help in advancing the understanding of weather and climate, resulting in a 
better prediction and improved weather services for the nation. The major 
benefits include: 
 Improved weather and climate forecasts at a very high resolution and 
forecasts of extreme events at block level.  
 Carryout research to improve the indigenous dynamical models for better 
prediction. Leveraging the new HPC facility to develop models using the 
latest technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning 
(ML) to improve the last mile services to different stakeholders.   
 Improving short, medium, and long-range forecasts for monsoon.   
 Better forecast of Air Quality, and Fog forecasts for many cities in India 

21 
 
11.4 The Committee was also informed that there has been a 40 to 50 percent 
improvement in the forecast accuracy of all severe weather events like heavy 
rainfall, fog, heat/cold waves, and thunderstorms in the recent five years by the 
India Meteorological Department (IMD).  IMD has adopted new techniques 
and technology from time to time to detect, monitor, and provide timely early 
warnings for disruptive weather events. Initiatives and developments have been 
taken to improve the monitoring and forecasting of weather events by 
augmenting the observational network, numerical weather prediction models, 
and supercomputers.   Further, IMD utilizes a seamless forecasting system at 
seasonal to nowcast scale and implemented well-defined Standard Operating 
Procedures (SOPs) for monitoring & forecasting weather hazards. IMD uses a 
state-of-the-art dissemination system to share all severe weather information 
and early warnings with disaster management authorities and the general public 
through various platforms/channels for necessary preparedness and to support 
mitigation measures. It includes social media, Common Alert Protocol, Mobile 
Apps, WhatsApp, and APIs. As a result, the vulnerable population gets 
evacuated from the damage-prone areas on time to safe shelters, thereby 
reducing the human death toll to a bare minimum. 
11.5 Advanced technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI), offer 
potential for the development of solutions and improve environmental 
governance for more sustainable living.  AI algorithms process real-time 
data from weather sensors, radars, and stations, providing up-to-date 
information on weather conditions and help generate timely forecasts, 
allowing for rapid response and decision-making in various industries. 
The Committee appreciates the efforts of the Ministry to integrate 
artificial intelligence (AI) technologies into weather and climate 
forecasting systems, in addition to physics-based numerical models to 
predict extreme weather events with greater precision, enabling early 
warnings for disasters like cyclones, floods, heatwaves etc.   The 
Committee firmly believes that by combining AI, satellite data, and 
advanced analytics, the schemes of Ministry of Earth Sciences will 
improve and enhance the country's ability to make more accurate and 
efficient meteorological predictions, consequentially contributing to better 
disaster preparedness, resource management, and overall climate 
resilience.

14. The Chairman welcomed the Secretary, Ministry of Earth Sciences and his team of 
officers and asked them to give a brief presentation on the Demands for Grants (2025-26). 
Thereafter, The Secretary in his presentation provided an overview of the various mission 
mode programs and initiatives undertaken by the Ministry, particularly focusing on weather 
forecasting, ocean exploration, and high-performance computing. The discussion began with 
an emphasis on the Deep Ocean Mission and Mission Mausam. He then elaborated on the 
High Performance Computing (HPC) System, which is a critical component to enhance 
computational capabilities for weather forecasting and climate modeling. The HPC system, 
with a capacity of 20 petaflops, is being installed at two locations—Noida and Pune—with 
the hardware imported from France. The total investment for this project is Rs. 900 crores, 
spread over two years. The HPC system is expected to play a pivotal role in improving the 
accuracy of weather forecasts, particularly through the integration of artificial intelligence 
and machine learning techniques. The budgetary allocations for the fiscal year 2024-25 were 
also discussed in detail. The initial Budget Estimate (BE) was set at Rs. 3,064 crores, which 
was later revised to Rs. 3,632 crores to accommodate additional funding requirements for 
the HPC system and Mission Mausam. He explained that the revised estimate was necessary 
due to the significant financial commitments associated with these high-priority projects. 
The Committee was then informed about the advancements in weather and climate services, 
particularly the improvements in weather forecasting accuracy. The Secretary highlighted 
the significant progress made in cyclone tracking and landfall predictions, which have 
contributed to a reduction in the loss of life and property during natural disasters. The 
Committee was also informed about the development of the MausamGram app, which 
allows users to access location-specific weather forecasts up to 10 days in advance. This 
app, which covers the entire country, provides detailed information on rainfall, temperature, 
humidity, and wind speed, and has been instrumental in improving the dissemination of 

29 
 
weather-related information to the public. The Committee was also informed about the 
Ministry’s plans to install 87 radars across India to enhance weather monitoring capabilities, 
with the aim of achieving complete radar coverage of the country by March 2026. The 
Committee was also informed about the Deep Ocean Mission, which includes six verticals 
and focuses on various aspects of ocean exploration, including technology development, 
climate services, biodiversity conservation, and the exploration of deep-sea minerals. The 
mission also includes the development of a human submersible capable of reaching depths 
of up to 6,000 meters, which is expected to be operational by the end of the next financial 
year. The Committee was also informed about the research stations established in 
Antarctica, including the Maitri and Bharati stations, and highlighted the continuous 
monitoring of cryospheric components in these regions. Emphasizing the importance of 
polar research in understanding climate change and its global implications, the Committee 
was informed about the plans to expand India’s presence in the Arctic and Himalayan 
regions. As regards, Seismology and earthquake early warning systems, the Secretary 
explained the upgrades made to the seismic monitoring network, which now includes 163 
stations across the country, capable of detecting earthquakes within 45 seconds, allowing for 
timely communication of warnings to the public.  
15. Thereafter, the Chairman and Members of the Committee raised several queries such 
as monitoring mechanisms to check the progress of schemes and programmes, reasons for 
increased allocation under meteorology and oceanographic research, current status of 
installation of desalination plants at identified islands in Lakshadweep, how private sector 
participation will be encouraged in areas such as seabed mining, marine biotechnology and 
renewable ocean energy, allocation of budget for developing offshore wind and tidal energy 
projects, next phase of computational upgrades, funds allocated for AI-driven climate 
modeling, budgetary provisions for physical coastal defences such as artificial reefs and sea 
walls, timeline for expanding real-time pollution monitoring along all major coastal cities, 
integration of Mission Mausam with artificial intelligence and big data analytics for 
improved flood, drought and heat wave predictions, , robust conclusions that can be drawn 
from the concern on reservoir-induced seismicity based on  Koyna experiment, reasons for 
chronic underutilization of the funds, etc. 
16. The Secretary and other representatives of the Ministry of Earth Sciences, while 
presenting their views, also responded to some of the queries raised by the Chairman and 
Members of the Committee. The Chairman also directed the Secretary to furnish written 
replies to other queries/points/issues raised by him and other Members of the Committee, 
which could not be replied during the meeting, to the Secretariat.
