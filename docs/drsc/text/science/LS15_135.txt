   Action Taken by the Department 
NCCS has been engaged in stem cell research for the last couple of years. They are utilizing stem
cells derived from all possible sources; eg., embryonic stem (E S) cells, cord blood and adult stem cells.
Methods have been standardized for successful cr yo-preservation of adult st em cells. Some of the 
highlights of the work are as follows:   
The Centre has successfully differentiated the murine ES cells into both neural and non-neural lineages. 
The ES cell generated dopaminergic neurons do s ecrete dopamine and henc e physiologically active.
Efforts are underway to enrich this population and explore their therapeutic potential by transplanting
into parkinsonian animal models.  
The centre has developed one of the human embr yonic stem cell lines su ccessfully. The detailed
characterization of the same is underway.   
Studies are being carried out for differentiati on into Beta-cell lineage for insulin production an
d 
increasing the Beta cell mass for transplantation.   
The Centre has developed two cell-lines in different stages of transformation from brain tumors with
neural stem cell features refered to as HNGC-1 and HNGC-2.    
The Centre has developed 19 cancer cell-lines from  various tumors of neuroectodermal origin an d
characterized them extensively.

NATIONAL BRAIN RESEARCH CENTRE (NBRC), GURGAON 
Recommendation 
            The Committee commends the activities being pursued by the Centre and recommends that
NBRC should initiate the major pr ojects in the areas as  computational neuros cience, system and
cognitive neuroscience, stem cel l research, developmental neurobi ology and basic research towards
understanding of neurological and psychiatric disorder s. Besides, it should focus on modern imaging
techniques such as functional magnetic resona nce imaging, positron emission tomography, optical
imaging provide noninvasive methods  for understanding brain functions in human etc.                     
(Para 29)              
Action Taken by the Department 
As recommended by the Parliamentary Standing Committee, the National Brain Research Centre
(NBRC) Gurgaon has already initiate d major projects in the areas of computational neuroscience,
system and cognitive neuroscience, stem cell res earch, developmental neur obiology and basic research
towards understanding of neurologica l and psychiatric disorders. Besi des, it is planned to focus on
modern imaging techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging, positron emission
tomography, optical imaging for noninvasive methods for understanding brain functions in human etc.   
  
Comments 
            The Committee is aware that mo st Functional Magnetic Resona nce Imaging (FMRI) techniques
are noninvasive and do not involve the injection of radioactive materials so that a person can be imaged
repeatedly. This may allow imaging of a patient repeatedly through different disease states o r
developmental changes (ie. learning, cognitive stages of deve lopment, stages of grief recovery). It also
allows for investigations in healthy children due to the low risk. Thus, the technique may be of immense
use in understanding how a given individual’s brain functions and perhaps, in the future, making 
psychiatric diagnoses and treatment  recommendations. Perhaps the most  important clinical utility o f
FMRI for the future is that it involves only some  upgrading of conventional MRI machines and, thus,
may become widely availa ble. Similar advantages are also seen  with noninvasive techniques, such as
positron emission tomography and optical imaging. 
The Committee therefore strongl y recommends that the plan regarding focus on the modern
imaging techniques such as functional magnetic re sonance imaging, positron emission tomography and
optical imaging should be finalized and started at the earliest.

BIO-TERRORISM 
Recommendation 
            The Committee recommends that th e Department of Bio- technology should take steps to ensure 
bio-security without hampering le gitimate research or  free flow of inform ation in the field o f 
biotechnology. First, there must be an increased awareness of risk of Bio-Terrorism among scientists 
and scientific leaders.  In order to augment aw areness about security, th e Committee would like to
suggest arrangement of training and accreditation programmes th at would increase considerable
awareness and stimulate better  
communications between scientific  and national security communities .  The Committee feels that any
Bio-security policies must take  a ‘bottom up’ approach  by including direct i nvolvement of working
scientists.  The security provisions should work hand in hand with the progress in biological research.  It
is the categorized opinion of the Committee that the strategies th at block the publication of certain
research materials would deter the fr ee flow of information, impede car eers of scientists in the field o f
bio-technology and discourage new incumbents in entering the field of research in Bio-Technology. 
            The guidelines should, therefore, be developed to allow researchers to publish their work without
providing information that would be useful to Bio-Terrorists.  Universities and other scientific 
institutions as what the Committee feels should deve lop the procedures to monitor scientific activities
and design full proof action plan to prevent any strategic inform ation leakage relating to Bio-
Technology to bio terrorists who w ould in turn adversely utilize th ese information for creating Bio-
Terrorism thereby throwing considerable threat on the very existen ce of human communities by waging
biological warfare.  
            Biological weapons pose a strategic threat mirrored by no other form of attack other than nuclear
weapons.  The Committee feels that as there is no simple solution to  bio security, the Department o f
Bio-Technology must construct a syst em of checks and balances need ed to assure that the growing
power of life sciences is only used to protect life and not to destroy it.
