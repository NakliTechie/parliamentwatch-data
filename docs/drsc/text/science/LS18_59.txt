REPORT 
The Indian Space Programme, which had a modest beginning in the early sixt ies, is primarily aimed at 
promoti ng the development and application of space technology and space sciences for the socio-economic ben­
efits of the nation. Over the last three decades, the very challenging task of development of space technology and 
its utilisation, has not only graduated from establishment and expe rimental phase s to an operational era but also 
proved emphatically its potential to address the national needs. 
2. In tune with the goal of self-reliant use of space technology for national development, the main thrust 
areas of the Indian Space Programmes are:-
(a) satellite based communications for various applications; 
(b) satellite based remote sensing for resources survey, management and environmental monitoring; 
(c) meteorological applications; and 
(d) developm ent and operationalisation of indigenous satellites and launch vehicles for providing these 
space services. 
3. The Department of Space (DOS) is responsible for implementing the policies framed by the Space Commis­
sion. The policies are carried out through the Indian Space Researc h Organisation (ISRO) and the three Grant-in­
aid institutions, viz. the National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) and 
the National Mesosphere-Stratosphere -Troposphere Radar Facility (NMRF).

INDIAN NATIONAL SATELLITE SYSTEM (INSA'I) 
9. The Indian National Satellite System, which became operational with INSAT- tB, is a multi-agen cy, 
multi-purpose operatio nal satellite system which provides domestic long distance telecommunications, meteoro­
logical earth observation and data relay, nation wide direct satellite TV broadcasting to augmented TV receivers, 
nation wide radio networking, TV programme distribution and rebroadcasting to terrestrial transmitters, etc. The 
Department of Space is responsible for establishment and operation of INSAT space segment. 
9.1 The INS AT space segment at present consists of five satellites-INSAT-10, INSAT-2A, INSAT-2B, INSAT-
2C and INSAT-2DT acquired from ARABSAT Organisation, after INSAT-2D became inoperable on October 4, 
1997 following a power bus anomaly. 
9.2 The Committee was informed that during the Ninth Five Year Plan period continuation of INSAT Sys­
tem with enhanced capacity and capabilities will be an important target to meet the growing national needs and 
changing technology scenario. The Committee was also informed that it would be necessary to provide a space 
segment capacity of 135 transponders by the year 2001. Accordingly, INSAT-2E is scheduled to be launched 
during 1998-99 for communication and meteorological services. It will carry 17 C-band and lower extended C­
band transponders, a Very High Resolution Radiometer (VHRR) with an additional water vapour channel, etc. The 
sanctioned cost of the INSAT-2C D & E satellites, including launch services and acquisition of in-orbit Arabsat-C 
is Rs. 1439.80 crores and this year's financial requirement for it is kept at Rs. 58.10 crores. 
SATELLITE NAVIGATION 
l 0. Satellite navigation is a service which is expected to become operational in many advanced countries in 
the next few years. Notable among them are : United States, Japan, Europe, New Zealand, Australia and Canada. 
The International Civil Aviation Organisation has made Satellite navigation equipment mandatory on all civilian 
aircraft. To upgrade the existing Communications Navigation and Surveillance (CNS) and Air-Traffic Manage­
ment (ATM) system in India, the Airports Authority of India (AAI) is planning to make them adaptable to the 
Future Air Navigation System (FANS) through the introduction of Indian Wide Area Differential GPS (WADGPS) 
system. As a business plan AAI will phase out the existing navigational aids and will increasingly depend on 
satellite navigation by the year 2010. 
11. The Committee recommends that the Department of Space may provide aD necessary help to the Airports 
Authority of India (AAI) in setting up the satellite navigation infrastmcture to meet the national needs.

through the State Remote Sensing Application Centres.

DISASTER MANAGEMENT 
27. Disaster Management System can be broadly classified into three phases, viz. (i) pre-disaster planning, 
consisting of steps leading to disaster prevention, disaster mitigation, and disaster preparedness (ii) the response to 
the disaster comprising the disaster warning and the emergency relief aspects. and (iii) the recovery phase consisting 
of rehabilitation and reconstruction. Space-based disaster management system provides valuable inputs in all the 
three phases, when appropriately integrated with the conventional ground-based systems. 
27.1 The Committee was informed that with the object of quick dissemination of weather warnings of impend­
ing disaster from approaching cyclones, specially designed receivers i.e., Cyclone Warning Dissemination Sys­
tems (CWDSs) have been installed within the vulnerable coastal areas to disseminate cyclone warning messages 
directly to the affected areas. The Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) has deployed 250 Cyclone Warning 
Dissemination System (CWDS) in various States along east and west coast of India. IMD has planned I 00 more 
CWDS receivers to be deployed during the Ninth Five Year Plan period. 
28. The Committee feels that there is a need to increase the network density in the coastal areas for direct 
transmission of cyclone warnings weU in advance and the Department should take up the matter with the India 
Meteorological Department in this regard. The Committee recommends that an Integrated Disaster Management 
System should be established with necessary facilities like control room with the latest equipments and technologies 
including satellite data reception and communication facilities for providing near real-time monitoring of the 
disasters. 
29. On a specific question regarding the recent cyclone in Gujarat, the Committee was told by the Secretary, 
DOS that the cyclone warning systems were working almost satisfactorily at the appropriate locations and this was 
confirmed by the India Meteorological Department.

37. The Committee was infonned that M/s Antrix Corporation of the Department of Space is marketing Indian 
space products and services. So~e important achievements include marketing oflRS data (IRS data is now received 
in seven countries all over the world on commercial basis), agreements for launch of small satellites of Korea, 
Germany and a Belgian satellite which was signed recently, sale of sounding rocket to Norway, providing TIC

world. M/s Antrix and Arianspace have signed an agreement to enlarge the launch opportun ities for small satel­
lites, through Ariane and Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV). The Department has also signed an agreement 
for leasing of transponders to the International Telecommunication Satellite Organisation. India has a market 
share of I 0 per cent of remote sensing data market.

profit was of Rs. 5 crores approximately. The Committee would like the Department to take adequate steps to 
increase the market share of remote sensing data in the international space market and create further capabilities 
to sell the spin ofT technologies and services to the Indian industry. 
SPACE-INDUSTRY PARTICIPATION 
39. The Committee was told that the Indian Space Research Organisation has been encouraging active 
participation of Indian industries in the execution of space projects with a view to harnessing the potential existmg 
with the industry. The co-operative ventures with the industries have been promoted through transfer of technolo­
gies to industry, provision for technological Consultancy from space programme to industry and procurement of 
materials from Indian industry and fonning out fabrication contracts. So far, 231 technologies have been trans­
ferred to industries and more than 180 consultancy projects have been undertaken. Over 500 small, medium and 
large scale industries are involved including both Public Sector as well as Private Sector industries viz., HAL, 
BEL, BHEL, KELTRON, M/s. Godrej, L&T, Walchandnagar industries, Andlfra Sugars, etc.

40. The Committee wanted to know if there would be any effect of sanctions annou nced by the G-8 countries 
on the Department and whether any of its ongoing programme would suffer on account of these sanctions. 
40.1 The Committee was told that so far there has been no clarity on the details of the sanction and the response 
of US and European companies for the sanctions is yet to get crystallised. However, sanctions are not new to ISRO 
and space programme has placed thrust on self-relia nce through indigenous development of strategic items over 
the years. Already 23 different types of materials and about 24 types of passive electron ic components have been 
developed and qualified for use In our space programmes. Major efforts on development of critical items like 
CCDs, TWTAs, high efficiency Solar Cells, high power microwave devices, etc. are in progress. Efforts are also 
made on Identifying alternate sources for procurement for programme critical components for parallel ordering. 
(The cost impact of parallel ordering approach is estimated to be in the range of Rs. 30 crores toRs. 100 crores 
approximately). The ISRO has international cooperation agreement and joint international projects with several 
countries/space agencies like European Space Agency, French Space Agency, Gennan Aero Space Research Es­
tablishment, etc. which have not been affected.
