 REPORT 
The Action Taken Report (ATR) of the of the Department -related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change  deals with the 
action taken by  the Government on the recommendations/observations of the contained in its „Three 
Hundred Fifty Ninth Report‟ on „Demands for Grants (202 2-23) of the Department of Biotechnology‟. 
The Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 15th March, 2022.  
2. Action Taken Notes (ATNs) have been received from the Department of Biotechnology (DBT) 
in respect of all the seventeen recommendations/observations contained in the Three Hundred Fifty 
Ninth Report. The replies of the Department of Biotechnology have been categorized as follows:-  
Chapter I: Recommendations/Observations which have been accepted by the Government: 
Para Nos.:- 2.2, 2.4, 2.12, 3.6, 5.5 and 7.6 
  Total recommendations – 06 Accepted. 
  The Committee is pleased to note that the Department has accepted its six 
recommendations/observations as mentioned in Chapter-I of the Report. 
Chapter II:    Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to pursue 
in view of the Government’s replies:  
 Para Nos.:- 2.6, 2.13, 3.4, 5.4 and 5.6  
 Total recommendations – 05 Not to be pursued further.  
 The Committee is convinced with the explanations  furnished by the Department of 
Biotechnology and, therefore, does not want to pursue the recommendations further. 
Chapter III: Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the Government 
have not been accepted by the Committee: Para Nos.:- 3.8 
                           Total recommendations – 01 Not convincing reply. 
 The Committee is not convinced by the reply of the Department regarding the 
Recommendation conta ined in this Chapter and desires that the Department should 
furnish convincing action taken steps in respect of the recommendation. 
  
Chapter IV:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the Government 
are still awaited- further recommendation: 
 Para Nos.:- 3.2, 3.3, 6.5, 6.6 and 6.7  
 Total recommendations – 05 To be pursued further 
 The Committee desires that the Department should furnish pointed and detailed 
Action Taken Notes in respect of the recommendations/observations made in Chapter 
IV of the Report.

UMMID – UNIQUE METHODS OF MANAGEMENT OF INHERITED DISORDERS 
  Recommendation/Observation 
2.6 The Committee expresses its deep dissatisfaction and discontent over the execution of 
programmes of the Department under UMMID initiative. It is agonizing to note that these 
programmes, though are well planned on  the paper, lack actual ground level execution. Identification 
of rare and genetic diseases has a far reaching consequence, not just to the patient and its family but 
also on the biotechnology and bio -pharmacology research and treatment infrastructure. The  
Committee, therefore, strongly recommends the Department to identify and resolve the lacunae in 
execution and speed up its efforts to achieve the set targets for the year 2021-22.          (Para 5.4 ibid)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        
Action Taken 
2.7 Antenatal and newborn screening for genetic disorders, which account for up to 80% of the rare 
diseases, provides an opportunity for preventing disease occurrence or early management with positive 
outcomes.  Based on the above, the Department of Biotechnology has developed and implemented the 
UMMID initiative to address this major unmet need of India through establishment of NIDAN Kendras 
(genetic diagnostic centres), outreach for screening in aspiration al districts and training program for 
competence building in genetic diagnosis and counseling. This initiative, planned for long term 
sustainability, has secured a significant role in the National Policy for Rare Diseases 2021. 
2.8 Following due process of  financial and administrative approvals, the ground level 
implementation of the pilot phase of UMMID unfortunately coincided with the COVID -19 pandemic. 
The NIDAN Kendras were made fully functional even in the face of the resultant headwinds, including 
tying up of hospital resources for COVID management efforts. However, beneficiary turnout was 
highly reduced due to the pandemic and was insufficient to meet the screening targets. With the 
relaxation of COVID restrictions and following awareness campaigns by  the UMMID Centres, the 
rates of screening have started increasing. A 54% greater turnout for antenatal screening was seen in 
the 2nd half of the year 2021-22 compared to the 1st half. 
2.9 The establishment of new UMMID Centres under the phase II expansion  program has also been 
delayed due to the COVID pandemic. This is expected to be completed in FY2022 -23, and help to 
achieve the screening targets 
  Recommendation/Observation 
2.10 The Committee is also perturbed to see that the physical outreach of the three components under 
the UMMID initiative are limited to only few States of the Country. The Committee expresses its 
displeasure over this regional imbalance and recommends the Department to expedite its processes 
and engage with different stakeholders in other under-served regions as well. The Department should 
proactively reach out to District Medical Hospitals/tertiary care centre, partner with private 
hospitals/research institutes and support startups/pathologies in executing the vision of the 
Department under the UMMID initiative. The Committee also recommends that the Department should 
lay special emphasis on the North-Eastern Region and tribal belt of the country.            (Para 5.6 ibid) 
Action Taken 
2.11 The ultimate target for UMMID program throu gh DBT efforts is to have at least one NIDAN 
Kendra in each state/UT, replete with all required infrastructure for a functional genetic lab, and with 
capacity to support a screening program at each aspirational district. 
2.12 The Department is implementing  Phase II of the UMMID Initiative under which around 40 
projects covering 53 components (28 NIDAN Kendras, 21 Outreach, 4 Training centres) have been
