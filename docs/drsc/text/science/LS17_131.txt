REPORT 
  
         Establishment of the Ministry of Envi ronment and Forests in 1985 as the nodal Ministry in the
Government of India for the pl anning, promotion, coordination a nd overseeing the implementation o f
various schemes relating to environm ent and forests has been major thru st areas to effectively address
the challenges of the dete riorating environmental situation. While the Ministry is entrusted with the
responsibilities of conservation and survey of flor a, fauna, forests and wild life, its functions and
jurisdiction also encompass numerous other activities such as promotion of afforestation, regeneration of
degraded areas, prevention and control of pollution and overall protec tion of environment. The Ministry
discharges its responsibilities to integrate ecological issues with our developmental goals. By assessing
the impact of developmental pr ojects on the environment, by exte nding assistance to organizations
engaged in implementing environm ental and forestry programmes, by promoting environmental and
forestry research and by creating awareness among people about the phenomenal importance o f
environment in promoting devel opment. lts responsibilities encap sulating the creation of manpowe r
through research, extension, education and trai ning programmes, its fo cus on the spread and
dissemination of information about th e conservation of nature and its efforts for forging international
cooperation and understanding on envi ronmental issues constitute pa rt of its crucial mandate fo r
protection and preservation of ecology. 
         The activities of the Ministry are divided in five major sectors, i.e. 
(a)   Environment; 
(b)   National River Conservation Directorate;  
(c)   Forests and Wildlife; 
(d)   National Afforestation and Eco-Development Board; and  
(e)   Animal Welfare.

RESEARCH 
10.    The Research programme of the Ministry aims to promote the research in multidisciplinary aspects 
of environmental protection, conservation and development. The objective of the scheme  is to generate
information required to develop st rategies, technologies  and methodologies for better environmental
management. It also aims  at attempting solutions  to practical problems of resource management,
conservation of natural resources and eco regeneration of degraded areas. Further, the scheme also seeks
to strengthen facilities to facilitate research and scientific man-power development. During 2004-05, the 
allocation for the Research Programme  is made in respect of lndian Council of Forestry Research &
Education (lCFRE), Dehra dun and Indian Plywood. l ndustries Research Instit ute (lPlRI), Bangalore.

17.    The Committee is constrained to note that fore st resources are steadily depleting and deforestation
is increasing unabatedly. Taking note of this alarming situation, the Committee is of the view that the
active and willing participation of  local communities is a pre-requis ite for the success of any forest 
regeneration programme. Therefor e, the Committee recommends that  the Joint Forest Management
(JFM) programme should be vigorously pursued by  involving women and tr ibals to protect and
regenerate degraded forests. Sec ondly, in the absence of any direct benefit and suffic ient authority fo r
the local community, there would be very little incenti ve for them to participate in any activity of the
Ministry. 
18.    The Committee recommends that the rural pe ople, particularly, women and the tribal community
have an intimate knowledge of fore st products, their specie s, their growth char acteristics, utility,
medicinal value, etc. They are also  well informed about the species th at need to be planted in a given
locality to satisfy specific requirements of fuel, fodder, timber, and other non-wood forest products. This
knowledge should be utilized under the JFM for the larger cause of environmental protection and benefit
of the community. 
19.    The Committee recommends that the policy of increasing forest cover should be implemented only
in forest deficient States so that a balanced proporti on of forest cover can be maintained in all parts o f
the country.

25.    The Committee has observed that given the pres ent state of Wildlife Cons ervation in India, there
are a lot of work to be done in th e field to give the word ‘Conservation’ a real meaning. Over the years, 
the Committee has noted that the basic requirements for Conservation of Wildlife have not been fulfilled
to its best by the Ministry of E nvironment & Forests and neither much importance has been attached fo r
the preservation of endangered species of animal and flora which constitute the heart of nature in India.
