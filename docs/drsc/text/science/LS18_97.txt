R E P O R T
 
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Ocean Development on the
 recommendations contained in the Eighty-eight Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on
 Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2001-2002) of the Department of Ocean
 Development which was presented to the Rajya Sabha on 20th April, 2001 and laid on the table of Lok Sabha on the 20 th
 April, 2001.  There are eighteen recommendations contained in the eighty-eight Report of the Committee. Action Taken
 notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department on 23rd October, 2001.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the
 Department of Ocean Development are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR 2001-02
Recommendation 
The Committee observes that in view of considerable reduction in the Budgetary support at RE level last year, it
 should be possible for the Department to manage with the reduced budgetary support this year.  To that end the
 Committee brings on record the assurance given by the Department in reply to Question 1 of the Questionnaire (See
 Appendix) that the present outlay will not hold up the execution of any programme.
                                                                                                                                  (Para 14)
Action Taken by the Department
The Department is grateful for the concern shown by the Committee with regard to the reduction in the budgetary
 support for the current year.  The Department would like to re-assure the Committee that the present outlay will not hold up
 the execution of any programme.
Comments:
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation
In the context of delay in the Commissioning the Technology Demonstration Pilot Plant for extraction of metals
 from Poly Metallic Nodules at Hindustan Zinc Ltd., at Udaipur, HZL (U), which  was to be commissioned in
 October, 2000 and the Pilot Plant which was expected to be commissioned off Tuticorin coast in September-October,
 2000, the Committee observes that such delays result in cost escalations.  Besides, they reflect on the Departments’
 efficiency to plan things meticulously.  Such delays need to be avoided.
                                                                                                            (Para 15)
Action Taken by the Department
It is submitted that the initiation of the project was delayed for few months due to delay in the finalization of the contract
 for the erection & commissioning of the plant by HZL (U). The plant is now scheduled for commissioning in October, 2001
 without any cost escalation.
As regards the commissioning of the floating OTEC Plant off Tuticorin, there were delays in completing the two main
 components viz., the plant barge and the sea water system. These delays were due to delayed supply by vendors and some
 teething problem during fabrication.  Finally, however, when the sea water systems were being deployed there was a mishap
 resulting in loss of the pipelines. This has caused a set back to the project which is now expected to be commissioned only
 by mid 2002.  The Committee’s directions have been noted and every effort would be made to ensure completion of projects
 as per schedule.

NON-PLAN PROGRAMMES
Recommendation
As regards the difficulties being faced by the Department on the non-plan side with regard to the payments to the
 Shipping Corporation of India for the operation of the Research Vessels “Sagar Kanya” and Sagar Sampada”, while the
 committee is all out for reduction of non-plan expenditure, but in view of the seriousness of the expenditure for Research
 Vessels, the Committee feels that it is a kind of committed expenditure and the Department should not be compelled to
 dither on its commitments, the Committee recommends that the budgetary support on this count be suitably augmented now
 or at least at RE stage or else the research vessels may remain idle and consequently the research effort may suffer which
 would not be healthy development.                                                                                                                            (Para 19)
Action Taken by the Department
The non-plan BE 2001-02 for FORV Sagar Sampada is Rs. 10.31 crore.  Taking into consideration the mandatory need
 for dry dock repairs of the vessel during 2001-02 and the critical need to upgrade the scientific facilities on board, the BE
 under FORV non-plan head 2001-02 need to be enhanced by at least Rs. 1 crore at the RE stage.  The non-plan BE 2001-02
 for ORV Sagar Kanya is Rs. 13.12 crore.   
Comments
The Committee observes that the Department should have indicated the action taken for augmenting the budgetary
 requirements.  The Department should now take up the matter with the Ministries/Departments concerned for suitably
 augmenting the budgetary support.

DRUGS FROM SEA
Recommendation
The Committee observes that the programme regarding development of six marine organisms showing anti-
diabetic/antidiarrahoeal; anti-hyperlipidaelmic, anti-anxiety, anti-cholesterol cum anti-oxidant; anti-bacterial cum anti-fungal
 and larvicidal activities is more or less at the same stage as was at the last year with no signs of the end product coming to
 market in the near future.  In these circumstances the Committee does not find any justification in the new projects on herbal
 drugs being undertaken.  Unless the drugs are developed expeditiously and brought into markets, the market forces could
 defeat the purpose of developing the drugs and render the whole project infructuous and futile.
                                     (Para 34)
Action Taken by the Department
Out of the organisms identified for anti-diabetic, anti-diarrhoeal, anti-hyperlipidaemic, anti-anxiety, anti-cholesterol cum
 anti-oxidant, anti-bacterial cum anti-fungal and larvicidal activities, substantial progress has been made in the development
 of marine organisms identified for anti-diabetic, anti-diarrhoeal and anti-hyperlipidaemic activities besides continuing with
 the ongoing exploratory activity.  Their activities have been revalidated in repeat collection and regulatory sub-acute
 toxicity studies in two rodent species. The organisms have been found to be safe and devoid of any side effect.  The
 regulatory sub-acute toxicity studies in primates are in progress and are likely to be completed by the end of 2001.
The regulatory  pharmacological studies have also clearly indicated that anti-diabetic, anti-diarrhoeal and anti-
hyperlipidaemic preparations are devoid of any other physiological effects.  After toxicity studies in monkeys tie-up with
 pharmaceutical will be explored in the early part of 2002.  The clinical trials are expected to be initiated after obtaining the
 statutory permissions of the Drugs Controller General of India sometime in 2002.
It may also be pointed out that 13 organisms have further been identified for various therapeutic activities and work on
 them is being done vigorously.  It is expected that out of these 3-4 molecules will be identified for further development of
 potential drugs in future.
Comments
While the Committee notes the reply, it would like to impress on the Department the need to develop and market the end
 product at the earliest without circumventing the process/procedures or prescribed legal requirements.

OCEAN OBSERVATIONS AND INFORMATION SERVICES (OOIS)
Recommendation
While the Committee notes with satisfaction that the programmes under the Ocean Observation Information Service have
 been successful, it observes that the indigenisation of Data buoy technology and Operation and maintenance of 12 data buoy
 system by National Data Buoy Programme is yet to be achieved.  The Committee observes that the upgradation of existing
 tide gauge network both in terms of software and sensors redeployment of existing current meter and deployment of new
 current meter array  along the equator would take place during this financial year.  Similarly the Committee would
 appreciate if under the programme Ocean Dynamics and Modelling (INDOMOD), validation of Coupled Atmospheric
 Ocean Model (CAOM) to provide forcing parameter for ocean general circulation model and sensitivity test for CAOM for
 prediction of storm surges associated with tropical cyclone along the Indian coast would be conducted and validated.  The
 experiments to determine the sensitivity for development of hybrid coupled model for prediction of Indian monsoon regions
 have also to be achieved.
                                                                                                                              (Para 49)
Action Taken by the Department
During the 9
th Plan period DOD formulated a programme on Indian Ocean Modelling (INDOMOD) with the objective to
 develop ocean state forecast of the Indian seas.  Indian Institute of Sciences, Bangalore, Indian Institute of Technology,
 Delhi, Centre for Mathematical Modelling and Computer Stimulation, Bangalore, Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology,
 Pune, National Institute of Oceanography, Goa and Cochin University of Science and Technology, Kochi are undertaking
 programmes to develop the numerical model and necessary infrastructure to achieve the goal.  During the last 3-4 years
 significant progress has been made in the improvements to the prediction of storm surge, development of model for real
 time forecast of oceanic conditions and prediction of spatial distribution of SST, Current and Biological Productivity, etc.
To provide forcing for driving an ocean model, realistic stimulation of surface wind stresses on the monthly and seasonal
 time scales over the oceans in the monsoon region using atmospheric general circulation models (GCMs).  This has been
 achieved using 3 different GCMs.  It has been validated the wind stresses against observed stresses.  We are now using the
 stimulated wind stresses to drive an ocean model in the monsoon region.  This SSTs output by the ocean model from this
 experiment will be validated against available SST observations.  This will tell us how realistic is our stimulated
 atmospheric forcing.

COASTAL COMMUNITY PROGRAMMES
Marine Instrumentation (Shore to Fishing Vessel Communication System)
Recommendation
The Committee recommends that the Department of Ocean Development should ensure the development of the shore to
 vessel communication system and their transfer and installation in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture which
 would be helpful for fishermen going for fishing in high seas.
                                                                                                                            (Para 51)
Action Taken by the Department
As indicated in the report of the Committee in para 50, the Ministry of Agriculture is the nodal agency for fishermen
 welfare and their safety at sea.  The fisheries division under the Department of animal Husbandry & Diary Development in
 the Ministry of Agriculture is understood to have initiated some programmes during the 9
th Plan period for upgrading the
 mechanized fishing sector by helping them to acquire the craft and gear, navigational, communication and life saving
 equipment while they go out for fishing at sea.  The Department of Ocean Development (DOD) and Ministry of Agriculture
 are already interacting on this issue.  DOD would provide the requisite information/ technological inputs, etc., to the
 Ministry of Agriculture for implementation of this programme.  The shore to vessel communication  system set up in Tamil
 Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and South Goa, has already been transferred to the respective state Governments.  In
 Andaman & Nicobar Islands, the Port Management Board of the A&N Administration has already been entrusted with the
 responsibility of managing this programme.  In West Bengal & Orissa, Pondicherry and North Goa, the system will be
 transferred to the respective states and union Territory during the current financial year.

 transferred to all the states within the time so that  the same can be used for benefit of fishing communities.
ISLAND DEVELOPMENT
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that the Department should carry out further research on formulation of appropriate feeds
 for the enhancement of juvenile lobsters and develop the larval rearing technology in Indian spiny lobster which was taken
 up during 1999-2000.  The Department should also initiate research in the area of identification of pathogenic organisms
 and development of methodology for eradication of these pathogens using chemotherapeutents.  The technology for
 fattening of lobsters and mud-crab at Andaman & Nicobar Islands and lobsters in the main land should also be disseminated
 to the coastal community and selected entrepreneurs.
                                                                                                                     (Para 53 )
Action Taken by the Department
Formulation of an appropriate palletized feed for fattening of juvenile lobsters based on the various biochemical analysis
 carried out on the  composition of lobster tissue and feed  substrates earlier in the research laboratory of NIOT, is already
 underway.  The feed thus formulated will be fortified with B-carotene, which is responsible for retaining the pigmentation in
 lobsters.  This study has already been initiated and the results obtained through this study will be compared with live feeds
 and a commercially available penaeid prawn feed.
Under the larval rearing programme, for the first time in India, the lobster larva (phyllosoma) has been reared up to 82
 days under controlled conditions.  Further experiments have been initiated to induce moulting in Phyllosoma by
 administering various hormones responsible for moulting.  The requisite infrastructure facilities for this purpose have
 already been created at the onshore hatchery of Tamil Nadu fisheries Department near Chennai.
The Department through NIOT is already addressing the issue of identification of pathogenic organization and
 development of methodology for eradication of these pathogens from lobster culture systems.  The problem of pathogens
 during fattening and larval rearing has been successfully overcome by employing a wide range of chemotherapeutants and
 antibiotics.  In addition to that, application of probiotics in disease control is also being experimented upon.
