INTRODUCTION
The remarkable achievements of Indian Space Programme since independence of India have
demonstrated India’s technological capability and strength in the world. These Space Programmes are
implemented and executed by the Department of Space since its establishment in June, 1972. The programmes
framed by the Space Commission are carried out through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)
and the Grant-in-aid institutions, i.e., Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National Atmospheric Research
Laboratory (NARL), North Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC), Semi Conductor Laboratory (SCL)
and Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) with Department of Space Secretariat and the
Headquarters of ISRO in Bengaluru, Karnataka.
1.1 The Programme offices at ISRO Headquarters co-ordinate the overall programmes including
Satellite Communication, Satellite Navigation, Earth Observation, Launch Vehicle, Space Science,
Atmospheric Science, Space Industry Development, Disaster Management Support, International Co-
Operation, Publications And Public Relations, Budget and Economic Analysis and Human Resource
Development.
1.2 The successful completion of intended six month period by Mars Orbiter Spacecraft in its orbit
around Planet Mars, making India the first country to achieve total success in its maiden attempt to explore
the planet of Mars, is one of the most remarkable achievements of the Department of Space. Besides, launch
of GSLV -MK II carrying the indigenous Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS), ASTROSAT-India’s first Multi
wavelength Space Observatory, IRNSS -ID, IRNSS -1E and IRNSS -1F and GSAT -15 carrying
communication satellite and GAGAN payload and other missions and programmes are the highlights of the
achievements of Department of Space in the financial year 2015-16.
OBJECTIVES OF INDIAN SPACE PROGRAMME
2. The primary objective of the Indian Space Programme is to promote the development and
application of space technology and space science for the socio-economic benefits of the nation through
establishment of operational space services in a self-reliant manner. The main thrust areas of the programme
are:
 Satellite-based communication and navigation for various applications.
 Satellite-based resources survey and management and environmental monitoring.
 Meteorological applications.
 Space Science Research and Planetary Exploration; and
 Development and operationalisation of indigenously built satellites, launch vehicles and associated
ground segment for providing these space-based services.
2.1 The major centres, units and establishments of DoS/ISRO, responsible for carrying out R&D activities
as well as for undertaking various projects and programmes, are as under:-
(i) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala provides the technology
base for the country’s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development;

2
(ii) Liq uid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities at Bengaluru, and Valiamala/
Thiruvananthapuram is responsible for development of liquid propulsion and cryogenic
propulsion systems for satellite launch vehicles;
(iii) ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC), erstwhile LPSC, Mahendragiri has the responsibility for
assembly, integration and testing of storable and cryogenic liquid rocket engines and stages,
space craft thrusters testing in sea level and high altitude conditions, storage of liquid and
cryogenic propellants and production of liquid hydrogen etc.
(iv) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, is the launch
base and also provides the facilities for processing of solid rocket motors;
(v) ISRO Satellite Centre (ISAC), Bengaluru is entrusted with the prime responsibility of the satellite
development;
(vi) Laboratory for Electro-Optic Systems (LEOS), Bengaluru, is responsible for development and
production of electro-optical sensors for satellites;
(vii) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad, Gujarat carries out R&D in space applications
as well as development of application payloads;
(viii) Development and Educational Communication Unit (DECU), Ahmedabad is involved in the
conception, definition, planning and socio/techno-economic evaluation of developmental space
applications;
(ix) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad, Andhra Pradesh is responsible for satellite
data acquisition and processing, data dissemination, aerial remote sensing and decision support
for disaster management;
(x) Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), Dehradun, Uttrakhand is a premier training and
educational institute set up for honing skills of trained professionals in the field of Remote
Sensing, Geoinformatics and Global Positioning System (GPS) Technology for Natural
Resources, Environmental and Disaster Management;
(xi) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network (ISTRAC), with its ground stations at
Bengaluru, Sriharikota, Lucknow, Bearslake (USA), Biak (Indonesia), Brunei, Mauritius, Port
Blair and Thiruvananthapuram provides the telemetry, track and command support and
spacecraft mission management for Indian remote sensing satellite missions, space science
missions and satellite launch vehicle programmes;
(xii) Master Control Facility (MCF) with its main facility at Hassan, Karnataka and backup facility
at Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh have the responsibility for control and operation of INSAT/GSAT/
METSAT satellites; and
(xiii) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala carries out research and
development in the area of inertial systems for satellites and launch vehicles.
2.2 A National Natural Resources Management Systems (NNRMS), with DoS as the nodal agency,
has been established to ensure optimal management/utilisation of natural resources using remote

3
sensing data in conjunction with conventional techniques. Under NNRMS, five Regional Remote
Sensing Service Centres (RRSSCs) at Bengaluru, Dehradun, Jodhpur, Kharagpur and Nagpur have
also been set up for the execution of remote sensing application programmes in the respective regions.
With effect from December 2009, four out of five RRSSCs have been redesignated as Regional
Remote Sensing Centres (RRSCs), while RRSSC, Dehradun has been merged with Indian Institute
of Remote Sensing (IIRS).
2.3 The mandate of the organisations receiving Grants-in-aid from Department of Space is as
follows:
(i) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous institution supported mainly
by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences;
(ii) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL), set up at Gadanki near Tirupati,
conducts atomospheric research;
(iii) The North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC), established as an autonomous body
under the Department of Space, provides space technology inputs for developmental activities
in the North-Eastern Region;
(iv) The Semi-conductor Laboratory, Chandigarh, an R&D society, is entrusted with the
design and development of V ery Large Scale Integrated Circuit (VLSI) devices and
development of micro-electronic systems to meet the strategic needs of the country;
and
(v) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), an autonomous body under the
Department, aims at creating quality human resources tuned to suit the requirements of state-
of-the-art space research programme. The Institute offers graduate, post-graduate and doctoral
programmes in the area of space science, technology and applications. Initially, IIST started
the courses from the academic year 2007-08 with the existing infrastructure at
Thiruvananthapuram.
2.4 The ANTRIX Corporation Limited, Bengaluru, established in September, 1992, a wholly owned
Government of India company under the administrative control of Department of Space, is a marketing arm
of ISRO for promotion and commercial exploitation of Space products, technical consultancy services and
transfer of technologies developed by ISRO. It also facilitates development of Space related capabilities in
India.
2.5 Besides, the RESPOND programme of ISRO supports sponsored research activities in space science,
space applications and space technology in several national academic/research institutions and Space
Technology Cells in premier technological institutes of the country through grants-in-aid.

GEOSTATIONARY SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLE (GSLV)
10. Geosynchronous Satellite launch V ehicle (GSLV) is a three-stage vehicle with solid, liquid and cryogenic
upper stage, designed to place 2 ton class of communication satellite in the Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit
(GTO).
10.1 The fi rst flight of GSLV took place from SDSC-SHAR on April 18, 2001 by launching 1540 kg
GSA T-1. Eight more launches have taken place, GSLV D2 on May 8, 2003 (GSAT – 2 1825 kg), GSLV-F01
on September 20, 2004 (EDUSAT 1950 kg), GSLV – F02 on July 10, 2006, GSLV – F04 on September 2,
2007 (INSAT – 4CR 2130 kg), GSLV – D3 on April 15, 2010, GSLV – F06 on December 25, 2010, GSLV
– D5 on 5January, 2014 and GSLV-D6 on 27August, 2015.
10.2 It was informed by the Department that GSLV -D6 with indigenous Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS),
successfully launched GSAT-6, an advanced communication satellite of 2117 kg into GTO and the launch
signified a major step in perfecting highly complex cryogenic propulsion technology and also achieving self-
reliance in launching 2 ton class communication satellites into GTO.
10.3 GSLV-MK III is the next generation launch vehicle of ISRO capable of launching 4 ton class of
satellite to Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO). It is a three stage vehicle with two solid motor strap-ons
(S200), a liquid propellant core stage (L110) and a cryogenic stage (C25). The solid motors and liquid
propellant core stages were successfully flight tested in the sub-orbital GSLV -MK III X mission during
December, 2014.
10.4 The Committee desired to know the progress made in the development of Cryogenic Stage (C 25).
The Department informed that it has undergone a series of ignition tests in 2015, including a flight
duration test for 635 sec. and an endurance test for a duration of 800 sec and on February 19, 2016, the
second cryogenic engine was successfully tested for the full flight duration of 635 sec. The activities
related to the integration of the engine with the Cryogenic Stage are in progress and the Cryogenic Stage
is expected to be hot tested in the second half of 2016, before it is inducted into the GSLV MkIII launch
vehicle.
10.5 The Committee feels that GSLV-MK III is a successful accomplishment of the Department
exhibiting India’s might in the world in launching next generation launch vehicle with indigenously
developed cryogenic engine. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the testing of cryogenic
engine and its induction into the GSLV-MK III launch vehicle should be speeded up and completed
by the Department within its scheduled time frame.

TRISONIC WIND TUNNEL PROJECT
11. Trisonic Wind Tunnel facility is planned to be set up at Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre,
Thiruvananthapuram for meeting the test requirements of future launch vehicle development.
11.1 The Committee desired to know the details of the project’s application and its significance. The
Department informed that the project is for wind tunnel testing which is an integral part of the design and
development process in aerospace industry and the data generated by testing provide aerodynamic
characterisation which form major inputs for the structural design of satellite launch vehicles.
11.2 The Department further informed the Committee that even in an age of advanced computer simulation,
aerospace engineers still rely on the testing of physical models to verify the computer data and establish
baseline aerodynamic information. Space vision for the future emphasizes the enhancement of launch vehicle
capability, reusable launch vehicle and human rated launch vehicle. Demand on wind tunnel testing and
computations for aero characterisation for re-entry and human rated vehicle are of higher magnitude than
the expendable launch vehicle. Generation of more reliable, high quality experimental aerodynamic data
through testing in more than one wind tunnel facility mainly for complex missions involving humans is
essential to improve safety and reliability.
11.3 The Committee was informed by the Department that during the processing, a revision in scope was
envisaged to include the facility for the simulation of jet-on effects and therefore the revised project report
has been submitted for financial sanction and is under process.
11.4 The Committee had, in its 255
th Report on Demands for Grants (2015-16) of Department of
Space, considered the subject and highlighted that budget provision for the project in 2014-15 could
not be utilised due to the inability of the Department to obtain necessary approvals. The Committee
had therefore recommended that the approval for the project should be obtained at the earliest.

9
The Committee is, however, constrained to note that despite the recommendation, the financial
approval is still awaited which is delaying the programme. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that the Department should exhibit its zeal and obtain all the approvals at the earliest so that the
programme takes off without further delay.
MANNED MISSION/HUMAN SPACE FLIGHT PROGRAMME
12. The objective of Human Space flight programme is to undertake a human space flight mission to
carry a crew of two to Low Earth Orbit (LEO) and return them safely to a predefined destination on earth.

SATELLITE NA VIGATION PROGRAMME
14. Satellite Navigation (SATNAV) is a programme that includes activities such as GAGAN and Indian
Regional Navigation Satellite System (IRNSS). GAGAN is the first satellite-based augmentation navigation
system over Indian region and it aims to provide Satellite -based Navigation services with accuracy and
integrity required for civil aviation applications and to provide better Air Traffic management over Indian
Airspace. Establishment of an independent Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System (IRNSS) is going to
provide accurate position information service to users in India as well as the region extending up to 1500 km
from its boundary, which is its primary service area.
14.1 The Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System (IRNSS) is planned to be a constellation of 7
satellites aimed at providing position accuracies similar to Global Positioning System (GPS) in a region
centred around India with a coverage extending upto 1500 km from India. IRNSS will provide two types
of services namely, Standard Positioning Service (SPS) for common civilian users and Restricted Service
(RS) for special authorised users and is expected to provide a position accuracy of better than 20 m in the
primary service area. The IRNSS system mainly consists of Ground Segment, Space Segment and User
Segment.
14.2 Segment of IRNSS consists of seven satellites with three to be put in geostationary orbit and four in
inclined Geosynchronous orbit. IRNSS – 1A, the first of the seven satellites of the IRNSS Constellation,
was successfully launched onboard PSLV-C22 on July 01, 2013 and is functioning satisfactorily. IRNSS-1B
and IRNSS-1C were also launched successfully in 2014. IRNSS – 1B was launched by PSLV-C24 on April
4, 2014 and IRNSS-1C on board PSLV-C26 on October 16, 2014.  The satellites, IRNSS-1D was successfully
launched on 29
th March 2015 and IRNSS -1E on 20 th January, 2016. Also, IRNSS -1F was launched on
March 10, 2016.
14.3 The Committee desired to know the status of launch of IRNSS -1F and IRNSS -1G and about the
realization of IRNSS constellation by the end of 2016. In response, the Department informed the Committee
that IRNSS -1G is planned to be launched during April, 2016 and with these launches, the IRNSS constellation
is planned to be completed by April 2016.
14.4 The Committee desired to know the progress made with regard to the ground control centres specially
the primary control station at MCF and backup control centre in Bhopal. The Department informed that all
primary facilities for managing IRNSS constellation and the required ground stations and control centres at
MCF, Hassan and Bhopal are ready and the new Spacecraft Control Centre at MCF, Hassan is planned to be
ready by end March, 2016. The Department also informed the Committee that the additional ISRO Navigation
Centre (INC) and IRNSS Network Timing Centre at Lucknow are planned to be ready by September 2016
and April 2017, respectively.
14.5 The Committee congratulates the Department on the successful launch of IRNSS-1G . The
Committee is of the view that with this launch, India will have an independent robust navigation
system which is a significant achievment for the country. The Committee, however, recommends
that the Department should make efforts to complete the additional ISRO Navigation Centre (INC)
and IRNSS Network Timing Centre at Lucknow in scheduled time frame which will assist in
functioning and operation of navigation system.

NATIONAL SPACE ACT OF INDIA
21. The Committee desired to know whether any Space law was under consideration of the Department
and the benefits for bringing such a Law in India. The Department informed the Committee that it is in the
process of formulating a Draft Bill on ‘National Space of India’ and that this law is intended to support
overall growth of the Space activities in India with higher order or participation of various stake-holders and

20
agencies under Public/Non Governmental private sector. The provisions for licensing & authorization,
continuing supervision and regulation of space activities by Public/Non-Governmental private sector stake-
holders, by the Central Governmental are included in the proposed legislation.
21.1 On its benefits in commercial use of Space, the Department also informed that the proposed legislation
inter-alia, would support commercialization of space activities in terms of realization of space systems and
provision of space systems based services in various application areas like SATCOM, Navigation, Remote
Sensing etc. by various stake holders in the country.
21.2 The Department further informed that consequent to the clearance of the Space Commission, the
draft contents of the proposed Act is being put up for seeking the approval of Hon’ble Prime Minister as
Minister-in-charge of the Department of Space for proceeding with the process of enactment as per the
Parliamentary Procedures and upon approval, Public opinion/comments on the draft contents of proposed
law would be invited and thereupon, Inter-Ministerial consultation process would be taken up through Draft
Cabinet Note. These steps may take about 9 to 12 months or more for submission of the Bill to the Cabinet/
Parliament.
21.3 The Committee feels that a National Space Act of India would be beneficial for the overall
growth of Space activities facilitating participation of both public and private sectors, enforcing
commercialization including better delivery of space services and supervision and regulation of
space related activities. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Government should take
all necessary action for the enactment of the law at the earliest.

RECOMMENDA TION/OBSERV ATIONS — A T A GLANCE
FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE OF THE DEPARTMENT
The Committee takes note of the fact that the cuts in the budgetary grants at the R.E. stage
in financial years 2013-14 and 2015-16 were above `````1600 crore and `````1400 crores respectively which
results into delays and disruption in launching of crucial programmes of strategic importance.
However, in 2015-16, the allocation was reduced by ````` 428.75 crore. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the cuts in the grants at R.E. stage should be avoided so that the Department
could continue with work as per the priorities set by it without any obstacles of resource crunch. In
addition the Department should also be provided adequate funds to undertake all its critical
programmes and schemes in right earnest. (Para 4.1)
BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2016-17
The Committee strongly recommends that the Government should positively consider the
requirement of additional funds for the Department in order to facilitate undertaking works of
strategic importance. (Para 5.2)
PHYSICAL ACHIEVEMENTS OF THE DEPARTMENT IN 2015-16
The Committee takes note of the achievements of the Department in 2015-16. Launching
of ASTROSAT, India’s first space observatory, near completion of Indian Regional navigation
Satellite System and launching of some of the important communication satellites has
demonstrated India’s potential to become a world leader in Space Technology. The Committee
strongly feels that such achievements should not only be continued but should also be sustained
and improved so as to make India self-reliant, self independent and successful in its space
missions. (Para 6.1)
POLAR SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLES
The Committee strongly recommends that a crucial programme of PSLV launches should not
be subjected to resource crunch. Considered as a work horse, PSLV have been extremely successful
in launching most of the significant satellites of India and hence, the Committee recommends that
the programme should not face reduction in budgetary allocation at R.E. stage which would cause
disruption and delays in launches. (Para 9.5)
GEOSTATIONARY SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLE (GSLV)
The Committee feels that GSLV-MK III is a successful accomplishment of the Department
exhibiting India’s might in the world in launching next generation launch vehicle with indigenously
developed cryogenic engine. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the testing of cryogenic
engine and its induction into the GSLV-MK III launch vehicle should be speeded up and completed
by the Department within its scheduled time frame. (Para 10.5)
The Committee feels that the adequate budgetary allocation to an important programme
like GSLV-MKIII Development should always be made available to the Department to ensure
expeditious completion of the programme. Since a reduced allocation has been made for the
programme in 2016-17, the Committee recommends that no cuts in budgetary grants in the
21

22
programme should be made at the R.E. stage in 2016-17 so that the progress of the programme is
not adversely affected. (Para 10.8)
TRISONIC  WIND TUNNEL PROJECT
The Committee had, in its 255 th Report on Demands for Grants (2015-16) of Department of
Space, considered the subject and highlighted that budget provision for the project in 2014-15 could
not be utilised due to the inability of the Department to obtain necessary approvals. The Committee
had therefore recommended that the approval for the project should be obtained at the earliest. The
Committee is, however, constrained to note that despite the recommendation, the financial approval
is still awaited which is delaying the programme. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Department should exhibit its zeal and obtain all the approvals at the earliest so that the programme
takes off without further delay. (Para 11.4)
MANNED  MISSION/HUMAN SPACE FLIGHT PROGRAMME
The Committee notes that the pre-project activities are still in progress and critical technologies
like Crew Escape System (CES) and Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) are
in the process of testing and validation. The Committee, therefore, recommends that such critical
technologies should be completed on a priority basis within a specified time frame without which
Manned Mission/Human Space Flight Programme will not be accomplished. (Para 12.4)
The Committee takes note of the Department’s inability to utilize the funds and its failure to
make best use of the funds available to it for completion of the project in 2015-16, as a result of
which the funds were reduced at R.E. stage. The Committee recommends that the Department
should take the programme seriously and should make full utilization of the funds made available
to it. (Para 12.5)
RADAR IMAGING SATELLITE – RISAT – 1A
The Committee is of the view that RISAT-1A is an important satellite having applications in
agriculture, forests and in disaster management and therefore its launch is significant for its
contribution in the said fields. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Department should
take required steps for the completion of the programme within its scheduled time frame.
(Para 13.3)
The Committee notes that inspite of having augmented the budget allocation for the project
at R.E. 2015-16 to ````` 10.20 crore to meet the requirements towards initial milestone payments for
procurement for long lead electronic equipments, the Department has not been able to utilise any
amount till February, 2016.  The Committee recommends that the Department should make sure
that all necessary efforts are made to achieve the desired targets of the project in 2016-17 and there
is no underutilisation of funds. (Para 13.4)
SATELLITE NAVIGATION PROGRAMME
The Committee congratulates the Department on the successful launch of IRNSS-1G . The
Committee is of the view that with this launch, India will have an independent robust navigation
system which is a significant achievment for the country. The Committee, however, recommends

23
that the Department should make efforts to complete the additional ISRO Navigation Centre (INC)
and IRNSS Network Timing Centre at Lucknow in scheduled time frame which will assist in
functioning and operation of navigation system. (Para 14.5)
SPACE SCIENCES - ASTROSAT MISSION
The Committee notes that the budget allocations for the Astrosat Mission, which was reduced
during 2014-15 and 2015-16, at R.E. stage could not be fully utilized by the Department. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should go all-out to avoid underutilisation
of the allocated funds. (Para 15.2)
INDIAN LUNAR MISSION – CHANDRAYAAN-2
The Committee notes the increased allocation to the programme in 2016-17 and recommends
that the Department should utilise the allocated budget in an optimal manner so that the Orbiter,
Lander and Rover modules of the mission are realized without any delay. (Para 16.3)
The Committee is of the opinion that completing the mission Chandrayaan-2 indigenously is
both a challenge and an opportunity before the Department and with its completion, India’s capability
in launch technology will be proved. However, the Committee notes that the mission is far behind
the targeted schedule and therefore, recommends that the Department should take steps to speed
up the stages of development of the project and complete the designs and testing of the components
to ensure its targeted launch by first half of 2018. (Para 16.6)
NASA ISRO SYNTHETIC APERTURE RADAR MISSION (NISAR)
The Committee notes that the allocation for NISAR was reduced to nearly half at R.E. stage,
out of which only a meagre amount of ````` 1.91 crore could be utilised by the Department upto February
2016. The reasons furnished by the Department indicated that either the Department could not
realistically plan the various stages of the programme or could not meet the schedule of events of
the project. The Committee recommends that the Department should monitor the progress of the
programme through an institutional mechanism and ensure that the funds allocated for the project
during 2016-17 are utilised optimally. (Para 17.5)
SATELLITE FOR SAARC  COUNTRIES
The Committee notes the progress achieved in obtaining concurrence and signing of crucial
agreements with some SAARC countries i.e. Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, and Nepal. The Committee,
however, notes that agreement with other countries, alongwith Afghanistan, are still being pursued
and recommends that the other countries should be actively pursued so that concurrence from all
SAARC members is obtained at the earliest and the funds earmarked for the project are realised.
(Para 18.4)

The Committee notes the application of the spin-off technologies in the sectors of agriculture
and irrigation, which are the backbone of India’s economy, and feels that the Department has the

growth of the economy of the country at large. The Committee recommends that the Department
should make more efforts in enlarging space applications and in pursuing projects which involve
societal and economical benefits. (Para 19.7)
