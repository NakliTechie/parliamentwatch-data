REPORT 
The Action Taken Report (ATR) of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing 
Committee on Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change  deals with the 
action taken by the Government on the recommendations/observations of the contained in its „Three 
Hundred Sixty Second Report‟ on „Demands for Grants (2022-23) of the Department of Space‟.  The 
Report was presented to Rajya Sabha and laid on the Table of Lok Sabha on the 15th March, 2022. 
2. Action Taken Notes (ATNs) have been received from Department of Space in respect of all 
the fifteen recommendations/observations contained in the „Three Hundred Sixty  Second Report ‟. 
The replies of the Department of Space have been categorized as follows:- 
Chapter I:        Recommendations/Observations which have been accept ed by the 
Government:  
Para Nos.:- 2.7, 2.8, 2.10, 3.3, 3.4, 4.3, 6.3, 7.3, 7.4, 8.3, 9.1, 9.2 and 9.3  
 Total recommendations – 13 accepted. 
The Committee is pleased to note that the Department has accepted its  thirteen 
recommendations/observations as mentioned in Chapter-I of the Report.  
Chapter II:       Recommendations/Observations which the Committee does not desire to 
pursue in view of the Government’s replies:  
Para Nos.:- 2.4 and 5.3  
Total recommendations – 02 Not to be pursued further. 
The Committee is convinced with the explanations furnished by the Department 
and, therefore, does not want to pursue the recommendations further. 
Chapter III: Recommendations/Observations in respect of which replies of the Government 
have not been accepted by the Committee: ‘Nil’ 
Chapter IV:  Recommendations/Observations in respect of which final replies of the 
Government are still awaited: ‘Nil’

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS/RECOMMENDATIONS OF THE COMMITTEE 
Recommendations/Observations 
1.23 The Committee notes that the Department of Space has been making efforts in mapping the 
glacial lakes which span over more than 2.5 hectare in size and the said exercise has been 
completed in respect of the Ganga basin and Sutlej river and the data has already been complied in 
the atlas form. The Committee is of the view that it will be of great importance if an inventory about 
the glacier and glacial lakes in the Himalayan region is documented on yearly basis. This is crucial 
in light of many studies suggesting that there has been rapid increase in the number of glacial lakes 
due to retreat in the glaciers caused by the impact of g lobal warming and keeping into 
consideration the possible large scale flooding and destruction that could happen in future.  
Therefore, role of the Department, in the matter becomes all the more pivotal and significant in 
light of the fact that many such gl aciers are physically inaccessible for ground reporting. The 
Committee also feels that with the modern technology and better resolution satellite imagery the 
task of glacier lakes outburst monitoring can be done in a more scientific and robust manner. The 
Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to initiate action in this regard in a time bound 
manner and to make best use of its remote sensing services for the purpose. Adequate budgetary 
allocations should also be made for the same.                                                               (Para 9.1 ibid)  
 
 
 
 

8 
 
Action Taken 
1.24   i. As an implementing agency under the National Hydrology Project (NHP) of M inistry of 
Jal Shakti, National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC)/ ISRO mapped Glacial lakes of size 
greater than 0.25  ha in Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river basins of Himalaya 
(including transboundary catchment flowing into Indian region), along with associated 
characteristics describing geometry, hydrology, topographic, etc., using Resourcesat -2 
LISS4 MX data. 28,043 such lakes have been mapped across these basins, and 
corresponding atlases have been published. Mapping and attribute assignment of  these 
Glacial lakes took nearly 3 years. 
 ii.  
(a) Glacial lake formation is a slow process and generally extends over long duration, 
except in cases like landslide dammed lakes. Considering the dynamicity of the 
glacial lakes and availability of usable (due to issues such as cloud, snow cover, 
shadow etc.) high resolution satellite data, it is desirable to ha ve Glacial lake 
inventory updation at periodicity around 3-5 years. 
Periodic updation of Glacial lake database can be taken up by the following 
Organisations: 
o Central Water Commission/NWIC 
o State Disaster Organisations/State Remote Sensing Centres 
NRSC/ ISR O would build capacity for the concerned Organisations and extend 
technical handholding. 
(b) In case of Glaciers, the dynamism (changes in glacier surface area; volume etc.) may 
not be perceivable on an annual basis, even on high resolution satellite data. Mos t of 
the Himalayan glaciers are covered with rock debris partially in their ablation zones 
which makes it difficult to monitor the changes in ice surface area of glaciers. 
Glacier mapping is practically done by visual interpretation, which would take more 
than a year for covering the entire Himalaya, using High resolution satellite data.   
Also, usable (cloud/snow -free) high resolution satellite data for the entire Himalaya 
may not be available every year.  
Monitoring of Glacier mass balance can also be done using geodetic methods such as 
DEM differencing. This has been done by many foreign researchers, which has 
established thinning of glaciers in the Himalaya. These assessments have been done 
for decadal or coarser time intervals.  
Glacier inventory and ch ange assessment have been done by Space Applications 
Center (SAC)/ ISRO over different time periods in the Himalaya (Annexure-I). 
Based on these experiences and scientific knowledge, it is proposed that inventory of 
Glaciers in the Indian Himalayan regions  would be required at time intervals of 10 -
15 years. This could be taken up by SAC/ ISRO, on demand from the concerned 
Ministry/ Department; such as Ministry of  Environment, Forests and Climate 
Change, Ministry of Jal Shakti etc.   
 
 
 

9 
 
iii. 
(a) Glacial Lake Monitoring by Central Water Commission (CWC)/NWIC 
o Central Water Commission (CWC) is mandated by Government of India to provide 
Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) early warning. 
o NRSC/ ISRO imparted necessary training & technical handholding to Morphology & 
Climate Change Directorate, Central Water Commission, during 2015 for monitoring 
of glacial lakes using satellite data. Morphology & Climate Change Directorate, 
CWC is internally carrying out the monitoring Glacial lakes (>50ha) using satellite 
data since 2016 and monthly reports are web published 
(http://www.cwc.gov.in/glacial-lakeswater-bodies-himalayan-region). 
o NRSC/ISRO further conducted one -week training to CWC Officials in 2021 on 
“Monitoring of Glacial Lakes using Satellite Remote Sensing Data. Updat ed Glacial 
lake data base (size GT 0.25a) prepared by NRSC/ ISRO under NHP is also shared 
with CWC for use in monitoring of glacial lakes. 
o Hence, it is suggested that CWC may continue to monitor glacial lakes using updated 
glacial lake database provided by NRSC/ ISRO. 
(b) Glacial Lake Monitoring by State Organisations /State Remote Sensing Centres 
o State Government Departments located in Himalayan region can monitor glacial 
lakes concerned to their geographical region on regular basis for better and efficient 
management of glacial lake floods.  
o NRSC/ ISRO can build capacity and extend technical handholding for the concerned 
State Organisations on monitoring of glacial lakes using satellite data. 
o It is also informed that the Power Organisations such as NHPC, SJVNL , etc. are also 
gearing up for periodic monitoring of Glacial lakes using satellite data 
(c) GLOF studies at ISRO 
Glacial lake outburst flood modelling of prioritised glacial lakes are being done by 
NRSC/ ISRO, under NHP. 
Further, SAC/ISRO would carry out rese arch on developing method to identify potential 
areas around the lakes which could create temporary damming, and could lead to GLOFs.

research potential.

Annexure-I 
Glacier inventory was carried out by SAC/ISRO over Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra basins 
using IRS P6  satellite data of (AWiFS and LISS -III sensor) for the periods; 2004 -2007 and 2007 -
2010. During Phase-I of the project, inventory was generated for all the glaciers in the Indus, Ganga 
and the Brahmaputra basins encompassing the Himalayas, Trans -Himalaya and Karakoram regions 
on 1: 50,000 scale using Resourcesat -1 satellite data of 2004 –2007 period. The results show that 
there are 32, 392 glaciers covering 71,182 sq. km area in the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra basins 
draining into the Indian territory. Duri ng Phase-II, the work was extended to map glaciers of West 
Indus basin which are draining into neighbouring countries. Thus the total glacier inventory data for 
all the three Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra basins show that there are 34919 glaciers covering 7 5, 
779 sq. km area. An updation of the Glaciers‟ inventory is proposed systematically; selectively over 
Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra basins with the help of collaborating agencies in respective states 
with recent satellite images. 
Glacier monitoring has be en carried out for select Glaciers using satellite data at SAC/ 
ISRO, which may represent the general trend of the Glacier dynamics. Changes in area of glaciers 
for the time period of 1962 – 2001/04/05 were mapped for 2630 glaciers. Glacier extents for 196 2 
were taken from Survey of India (SOI) topographical maps on 1:50,000 scale as reference and that 
of 2001/04/05 timeframe were taken from satellite data. This study showed a total loss of 13.4 % in 
area of glaciers. In continuation, monitoring of change i n extents of 2018 glaciers was carried out 
from IRS LISS III data of year 2000/01/02 and 2010/2011. The monitoring showed that 87% of the 
glaciers had no change, 12% have retreated and 1 % glaciers have advanced. This gives a net loss of 
20.94 sq. km in th e total area of 10,250.68 sq. km for all the monitored glaciers mapped in the year 
2000/01. Net change in glaciated area varies from region to region.  
Further, 5234 glaciers were monitored between the years 2001 and 2017/2018 across 
Himalayan –Karakoram ( H-K) region from Kashmir to Sikkim using IRS LISS III and limited 
Landsat data. These 5234 ice bodies included 3435 debris free glaciers also. In Karakoram region 
(north of Indus river), 2058 glacier bodies were checked and mapped. In this region, gain in area 
(0.056 %) has been observed in the area coverage of 17902.95 Km 2. The loss observed in rest of 
Himalayan region varies from 0.751 % for sub -basins south of Indus river (or in rest of Jammu and 
Kashmir and Ladakh states), 2.32 % in Himachal Pradesh or in Chenab and Sutlej basins, 0.84 % in 
Uttarakhand, 2.164 % in parts of Nepal, and 2.147 % in Sikkim or Tista sub -basin. SAC/ ISRO has 
taken up glacier monitoring using recent satellite images.
