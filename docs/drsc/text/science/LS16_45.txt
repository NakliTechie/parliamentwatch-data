REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Ocean Development on the recommendations
 contained in the Thirty-fifth Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology,
 Environment and Forests on the Demands for Grants (1996-97) of the Department of Ocean Development which was presented to the
 Rajya Sabha on 4th September, 1996. There are 6 recommendations contained in the Thirty-fifth Report of the Committee. Action
 Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee, were received from the Department on 16th January, 1997.
On scrutiny of the Action Taken Report as received from the Department of Biotechnology, the Committee finds that two
 recommendations have been fully implemented. The Committee in regard to its recommendation Nos. 23 & 26 recommends that the
 Department should make out a strong case for enhance allocation and improve its own resource generation wherever feasible. In
 regard to recommendation No. 30 the Department should take steps to complete the survey expeditiously. The Committee in regard to
 its recommendation No. 28 observes that the Department may continue with the existing system of charting the vessel for its
 Antarctica operation.
The Committee's recommendation, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the Department
 of Ocean Development are given in the succeeding paragraphs:-
RECOMMENDATION
"The Committee feels that the Department should make out a strong case for enhancement in its allocations in the revised
 estimates for the current year".
(Para No. 23)
Action Taken 
by the Department
The B.E. for 1996-97 for the Department was approved as follows:
Plan: Rs. 52.00 crores
Non Plan: Rs. 14.83 crores
The Department 
vide its communication No. DOD/52-IFD/28/96 dated 17.10.1996 requested Ministry of Finance for the
 following enhanced allocation at RE. stage for 1996­97:-
Plan: Rs. 58.47 crores
Non Plan: Rs. 19.77 crores
A meeting was taken by Secretary (Expn.) with the Financial Adviser of the Department on 22.10.1996 wherein it was desired by
 Secretary (Expn.) that the Department should endeavour to generate a saving of Rs. 10 crores under Plan so that R.E. for 1996-97 is
 reduced to Rs. 42 crores in view of overall resource constraints being faced by the Govt. of India. An exercise was made to locate
 savings under Plan side. However, it was found that Rs. 51.55 crores will be the minimum requirement to carry out the essential
 components of the various programmes. Accordingly, the Department sent a revised proposal requesting for the allocation of Rs.
 51.55 crores on the Plan side at RE. 1996-97. However, Finance Ministry have communicated the following size of the RE. for the
 Department for 1996-97.
Plan: Rs. 45 crores
Non Plan: Rs. 19.38 crores

RECOMMENDATION
Exploitation of Krill, Resources
"Besides, the Department should endeavour for commercial exploitation of the krill fishery and other such resources in the
 Antarctic waters because of its demand in the overseas markets."
(Para No. 25)
Action taken by the Department
The Department took up the first expedition to Antarctic waters for exploration of the potential of krill and other Marine Living
 Resources, with a view to assess the economic viability of future commercial exploitation of these resources. Two representatives of
 Fishing Industry also participated in this cruise to have a first hand information on the potential of krill and other living resources in
 the Antarctic waters. The product development from the krill stock harvested and brought to the main land is in progress. Some of the
 frozen, dried and canned products have already been produced and test marketed. The first exclusive Antarctic cruise for assessment
 of krill and other living resources was confined only to fishing area 58 of the Antarctic waters and that too in the summer months. For
 establishing the commercial feasibility to exploit krill, there is a necessity to undertake such studies even during the winter season,
 which will require a larger and sophisticated vessel with onboard processing facilities and a fish hold capacity of over 500 to 800
 tonnes at minus 40° C. The preliminary assessment worked out so far indicates that at present it is not commercially viable.

RECOMMENDATION
Polymetallic Nodules Programme
"Considering the potential of the benefits that could accrue for the future industrial programmes, the ,Committee feels that in view
 of the need for India to be among the developed nations in the high technology in the Ocean sector and from the long term strategy
 and geo-political consideration, it is essential to continue technology development efforts for exploration and exploitation of the
 polymetallic nodules so that we do not lag behind other pioneer investors and enable the country to begin commercial exploitation
 when economic feasibility is established. Here also the Committee is of the view that the Department's original demand of Rs. 11
 crores should be met in full whereas the Planning Commission has sanctioned only a sum of Rs., 9 crores."
(Para No. 26)
Action taken 
by the Department
As a Pioneer Investor and Member of the Council of International Seabed Authority under investor's category, Department has
 structured its programmes towards development of techniques and pilot plants for assessing techno-economic feasibility of deep sea
 mining and extractive metallurgy along with survey and environmental impact analysis.
The Department sought 'an ,allocation of Rs. 12.28 crores for PMN Programme at the RE. 1996-97 out of total proposed outlay of
 Rs. 58.47 crores. However, in view of the reduction of Plan allocation for the Department from Rs. 52.00 crores to Rs. 45.00 crores,
 we have been constrained to keep the allocation for PMN Programme at the B.E. 1996-97 level of Rs. 9.00 crores.
COMMENTS:
The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendations and would like the Department to make out a strong case for the enhanced
 allocation.

ACQUISITION ON AN' ICEBREAKERIMULTIPURPOSE OCEANOGRAPHIC RESEARCH VESSEL
The Department related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests
 in its 35th Report on Demands for Grants 1996-97 of the Department of Ocean Development has recommended that
 the Department should work out the economics of all the alternatives to acquire, including the purchase of a new vessel
 and plan its strategy accordingly, and further that the Committee should be apprised of economics of all the proposals.
The specifications of an Icebreaker-cum-Multipurpose vessel was drawn up by an Expert Group constituted by the
 Department and was circulated in the international market in 1994. However, no offer was received which conform'
 precisely to our specifications. In the meantime, in view of the then political scenario in Russia, an Expert Group
 visited Russia for a market survey. The Expert Group had a meeting with the Chief Expert of the Russian Federal

 Services for Hydro-Meteorology and Environmental monitoring, Research and Expedition Fleet. The Expert was
 clearly of the opinion that a ship, as per our specifications, will not be available in the market and needs to be specially
 designed and built and may cost anything between US $s 150 to 300 million, depending upon various fitments.
 Accordingly, the alternative of buying a second hand vessel capable of Antarctic voyage and thereafter, with
 modifications for other oceanographic research was pursued. However, Russia could not offer such a second hand
 vessel meeting our requirements.
Following the recommendations of the Parliamentary Committee, yet another attempt was made to explore the
 availability of any suitable second hand vessel.
The results of our enquiry are as follows:
1.    There is a vessel under construction in Germany and will cost around 76 million DM or in other words around
 Rs. 150 crores. The requirements of any modifications for our work has not been explored.
(b) Two old vessels, namely, (1) CARIBOO and (2) LIVONIA are available. The CARIBOO would cost around
 US $s 8 million (around Rs. 30 crores); whereas LIVONIA may cost 20 million US $s (around Rs. 70 crores);
(c) The offers. of these second hand vessels received do not indicate the helipad specifications and modifications
 may include helipad construction also. Further, as per the present Antarctic requirements, the vessel should be
 capable of accommodating around 65 persons in each Expedition. These vessels do not meet our requirements.
2. Against the above background, the broad economics are as follows:-
(a) Considering the lowest offer of a second hand vessel of around Rs. 30 crores only, even without any
 modification requirements, would mean an additional annual maintenance charges of atleast Rs. 10 crores per
 annum, based on the experience of ORV Sagar Kanya.
(b) . The Antarctic Expeditions leave in December and return to India towards the end of March. If it has to be
 used for oceanographic survey, the same has to be carried out between April and October. During this period,
 the period of monsoons also overlap.
(c) Even assuming that some oceanographic work is possible, the ship will have to be mobilised after proper dry
 docking etc. and made· ready for the Antarctic Expedition. It is likely that about one month of ship time will
 be lost in the process and additional expenditure incurred. In other words, in practical terns, the vessel would
 be utilised only for Antarctic expeditions.

Present Arrangement for Antarctic Expeditions:-
The Department has entered into a three year charter covering upto 1997-1998 season. The total charter cost per annum is around
 Rs. 9 crores approximately.
The chartering of a vessel providing their own officers and crew. The Department's budget is limited and the present budget does
 not allow for purchase of new vessel costing around Rs. 150 crores or more. In fact the present budget does not also allow even
 investments of the order of Rs. 30 crores besides additional operational and maintenance costs.     
On the other hand chartering of a vessel on an average of around Rs. 10 crores per annum would meet our Antarctic requirements
 and does not cost any additional investment on employment, maintenance, operational costs, spares, dry docking, etc. Acquisition of a
 second hand vessel and modifications cannot make it all purpose and will have limited functions only. In the event the future Antarctic
 programme involves going to Rose Sea or other difficult areas of Antarctica, it would be necessary to deploy an Icebreaker rather than
 Ice Class vessels, such as CARIBOO 
or LIVONIA.
Taking the overall considerations, as desired by Secretary DOD, a joint meeting of JS, JS & FA and Adviser was convened to
 examine this issue. The meeting arrives at the following conclusions:
(a)    Purchase of an Icebreaker, as per our specifications, is not economically viable.
(b)    (b) the option of a purchase of second hand vessel and further modifications will not prove to be more cost effective than the
 present arrangement for chartering the vessel for the Antarctic Expedition;
(c) The Department already has ORV Sagar Kanya and FORV Sagar Sampada. In addition, it has recently commissioned a CRV
 Sagar Paschmi. Another CRV is expected to be commissioned very soon.
