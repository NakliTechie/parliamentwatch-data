5. The Ministry of Environment and Forests is responsible for planning, promotion and co­ordination
 of environmental and forestry programmes. The main activities of the Ministry are conservation and
 control of pollution, afforestation and regeneration of degraded areas and protection of environment.
 These tasks are sought to be fulfilled through environmental impact assessment; eco-regeneration;
 assistance to organisations implementing environmental and forestry programmes; enactment of
 environmental legislation; formulation of environmental policies; promotion of environmental and
 forestry research, extension, education and training to augment the requisite manpower;
 dissemination of environmental awareness among all sectors of the country's population .
.
6. India has a land frontier of 15,200 kms. and its sea coast runs to 6100 kms. It is the second
most populous and seventh largest country in the world. The total geographical area of the country is
 3.28 million Sq. Kms. The total forest area in the country is 0.76 million Sq. Kms. which constitutes
 23.2% of the total geographical area. The percentage of actual forest of geographical area is 19.5.
 According to the National Forest Policy, 1988 33% of the total geographical area of the country
 should be under forest cover.
6.1 The Committee has been informed that Government propose to achieve the national goal-
minimum of 33% of the total land area of the country under forest cover. If, for some unavoidable
 reasons, any State cannot achieve this norm, the forest cover should not be less than 20 per cent
 of the total land area in the State and those districts which have less than 1 per cent of the
 geographical area under the forest should achieve 2% during the Ninth Plan period. In the hilly
 regions; the aim would be to maintain atleast 60% of the land area under tree cover.
6.2 The Committee has also been informed that special efforts would be made to contain the
 continued trend of diversion of forest land for non-forest activities. All existing forest-based industries
 would be encouraged to raise on their own the raw material needed by them. Private sector
 participation is sought to be encouraged for afforesting degraded and denuded lands.

21.1 In case of Delhi alone levels of carbon monoxide at all the traffic intersections are higher than
 the prescribed standards. It appears that the city is getting poisoned with every passing day due to

21.2 The major sources of air pollution are the vehicles, thermal power plants, industries and burning

 November, 1996 it went up by 3000 micrograms. The situation in the city has already reached a
 saturation point as six' out of every 10 residents of Delhi are suffering from Asthma or respiratory
 diseases. Besides, over 7000 people in Delhi die a premature death due to air-pollution according to
 a non-­government organisation, Vatavaran while quoting medical practitioners.
21.3 The steep increase in Vehicular population has resulted in corresponding rise in pollution emitted
 by vehicles. Major sources of Pollution are exhaust from Petrol driven, automobiles, lead fumes

ANIMAL WELFARE BOARD OF INDIA
26. Animal welfare board of India was set up in 1962 at Madras under the provision of Prevention of
 cruelty to Animal Act 1960. The board consists of 28 members representing Government of India,
 the veterniary profession, municipal bodies, practitioners of modern and indigenous systems of
 medicine, animal welfare organisations of the country and humanitarians apart from two members of
 Rajya Sabha and four members of Lok Sabha. The board is an autonomous body under the
 administrative control of the Ministry of Environment and Forests.
26.1 Animal Welfare Organisations alongwith the board take steps to generate public awareness. A
 WBI receives grant-in-aid from Government of India and in turn provides assistance to various
 organisations in the country for rescue/shelter homes, animal birth control, vehicles, medicines and
 veterinary care etc. A WBI also imparts training to representatives of various Animal Welfare
 Organisations and public in animal care, laws and various other disciplines. Under the provisions of
 Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 Government of India reconstituted a Committee for the
 purpose of Control and supervision of experiments on animals.
26.2 The Animal Welfare Board of India proposes to undertake approximately 35,000 sterlisation
 operations for animals birth control in 6 metros and also proposes to set up state advisory boards
 for animal welfare in the remaining States apart from amendment of Prevention of Cruelty to Animals
 Act, 1960.
27. The Committee observes that the final installment of grant of Rs. 75,000/- sanctioned for the year
 1994-95 under non-plan was actually received by the Board on 6th April, 1995 
i. e. in the next
 financial year 1995-96. Likewise, the 'grant of Rs. 1,96,00/- sanctioned for the year 1995-96 was
 also released in the next financial year 1996·97 i.e. on 6th April, 1996.
28. The Committee recommends that in future there should be a timely release of funds preferably
 within one month from the date of commencement of the next financial year. Further, as the Animal
 Welfare Board· of India is funding non-Governmental organisations which have no other sources of
 income, the delay in the release of funds often results in mass starvation of animals, inadequacy of
 medicines for their treatment and the municipal stress.

(i) To control forest fires with a view to protect and conserve forests,

PROJECT ELEPHANT
38. During the 1980s, the seriousness of the increasing impacts of human intervention in wildlife
 habitats became significantly noticeable in terms of fragmentation and degradation of the forests in
 the country. The conflict of interests and confrontation between the population of human beings and
 domestic stock 
vis-a-vis wild animals also became manifested in terms of gradual increase in direct
 confrontations, particularly so in the case of the wild elephants and the human population in and
 around their habitats. The seriousness of the concern .was voiced by eminent wildlife
 conservationists and naturalists for the plight of the wild elephants in their natural forest habitats and
 resulted in the constitution of a Task Force by the Government of India for looking into the problems
 and issues involved and to suggest future strategy for ensuring the long term conservation of viable
 population of wild elephants in India, without ignoring the welfare of the human communities residing
 in an around the forest habitats. The Centrally Sponsored Scheme "Project Elephant" was launched
 in February, 1992. The object of the scheme is to provide assistance to the major elephant States
 in achieving the:-
(i) Long term conservation and protection of viable population of wild elephants in their natural
 habitats in the country;
(ii) Conservation, protection and where necessary, restoration of the elephant range and traditional
 corridors used by the elephant population;
(iii) Development of a viable mechanism to ensure inter-state, regional and national level coordination
 in protecting and conserving the elephant and its range; and
(iv) Reduction of man-elephant conflict and development of compassion and tolerance of people
 towards elephants.
38.1 Project Elephant is in its initial stages and would continue as a Centrally Sp::msored Scheme
 during the IX Plan. Unlike the tiger, the elephant does not confine itself to limited areas that can be
 declared as National Parks or Sanctuaries and provide the species a very high level of protection.
38.2 Another scheme Project Elephant was started in 1991-92 based on a task force report. A
 budget of Rs. 19 crores has been provided for the scheme for a period of five years which has
 subsequently been increased to Rs. 23 crores. During last five years of the project, the States have
 been provided assistance to the tune of Rs. 17.84 crores. An amount of Rs. 3.95 crores has been
 released to the 9 States in the last financial year upto December, 1996 against the revised estimate

44. The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act was enacted in 1977 to provide for the

 local authorities, with a view to augment the resources of the Central Board and the State Boards
 for the prevention and control of water pollution constituted under the Water (Prevention and Control
 of Pollution) Act, 1974.'

 the Act was to reduce the rebate from seventy-five per cent earlier to twenty-five per cent and even
 this rebate is not available to the water consuming industries, if the consumption of water is in
 excess of the minimum prescribed quantity for specified industries or in the event of their failure to
 comply with the standards laid down under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. A subsequent
 amendment to the Act was made in January, 1992 to include cotton, synthetic, semi-synthetic, fibres
 as well as processing of milk, meat, hides and skins, all agricultural products and their waste. As a
 result of these amendments, the cess collection has increased substantially and is likely to increase
