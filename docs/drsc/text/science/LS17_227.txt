REPORT
The progressive development of science and technology since Independence reflects the
saga of a large developing nation struggling to establish an equitable society with reasonable quality
of life standards for its people within a stable democratic set-up. While, the focus during 1950-
1970 was on self-reliance, it shifted to technology denial driven science and technology during the
period 1971-1999 and post 1991 endeavor has been for IP generation and global competitiveness.
In the coming two decades, focus would be on attaining global leadership in science and
technology at organizational, as well as, individual level. During the ancient times, India was fairly
developed in the field of science and mathematics. Scientists such as Aryabhata, Sushrut, Charak
and Brahmagupta were some of the prominent figures of ancient India who contributed to the
advancement of ancient Indian science. However, advances made during the ancient times got
somehow lost in the medieval ages and during the British Colonial Rule.
1.2 Although, scientific development was initiated in 1942 with the establishment of CSIR,
major steps in our march towards revitalisation of the scientific traditions of ancient India were
taken by Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru for whom science was not only a tool for economic development
but also a means of emancipation of man, and the scientific temper represented true religion which
alone could lead to the qualitative transformation of a stagnant society and a tool to solve the
problems of hunger and poverty. A number of specialized national laboratories under CSIR were
opened which after seventy years have now grown into a large network of 37 national laboratories,
39 Outreach Centers, 6 units and 3 innovation complexes carrying out industrial research in diverse
scientific disciplines dealing with a wide variety of technologies.
1.3 Developments since Independence have no doubt had a significant impact on the nation’s
techno-economic growth with scientific and technological research organisations playing major role
in it. The Department of Scientific and Industrial Research is one of the three Departments of
Ministry of Science and Technology, formed in 1985 with a view to promote industrial research,
technology development, transfer and its utilization. DSIR has two autonomous organizations viz.
Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) and Consultancy Development Centre (CDC)
and two public sector undertakings viz. National Research Development Corporation (NRDC) and
Central Electronics Limited (CEL). The Department also provides institutional host facilities to Asia
Pacific Centre for Transfer of Technology (APCTT).
1.4 The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology,
Environment and Forests considered the detailed Demand for Grants (2012-13) of the Department
of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) on 12
th April, 2012.
2. MANDATE
2.1 The primary endeavour of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) is
to encourage industry to increase their share in R&D expenditure, support a larger cross section
of small and medium industrial units to develop state-of-the-art globally competitive technologies of
high commercial potential, catalyze faster commercialization of lab-scale R&D, enhance the share
of technology intensive exports, strengthen industrial consultancy and technology management
capabilities and establish user friendly information network to facilitate scientific and industrial
research in the country. The Department also acts as an administrative link between the
Government of India and the CSIR society.

3. MECHANISM TO MONITOR AND REVIEW THE ONGOING SCHEMES
3.1 The Committee has been informed that progress of projects of the Department is monitored
at various levels. At the external level the Department has been reviewing the progress of on-going
schemes by involving an external agency or outside experts at the beginning of the Five Year plan.
The external Monitoring Committees review progress at Head Quarter level. Director General, CSIR
further reviews the overall progress of the projects from Head Quarters from time to time. During
the mid-term appraisal of Eleventh five year plan, CSIR started the Cluster concept of participative
planning and participatory monitoring and proposes to continue with the same in the Twelfth Five
Year Plan. The dynamic mode of project operation is also in place to ensure that all programmes
can be repositioned and reoriented to meet the desired objectives. Compilation of comprehensive
knowledge base information on Clusters; and project-wise white papers for appropriate
benchmarking are additional features for the monitoring of the schemes during the Twelfth Five
Year Plan.
3.2 Department’s performance has been measured through a third party review set up by the
Performance Management Division of the Cabinet Secretariat through a mechanism of Results
Framework Document (RFD), since its inception in 2009-10. Schemes, programmes and activities
are defined in quantitative terms and achievements made get reviewed by Ad hoc  Task force
consisting of former Secretaries of Government of India. The Committee has been informed that
the Department has achieved high composite value scores of 95.84% in 2009-10 and 95.97% in
2010-11.
3.3 The Committee is happy to note that the Department has put in operation a
monitoring mechanism which has helped it to make almost full utilisation of allocated
funds. The Committee appreciates the fact that the Department achieved a very high
composite value scores of 95.84% and 95.97% consecutively in two years with Excellent
grade which reflects the degree to which the DSIR has been able to accomplish success in
its programmes and schemes. The Committee, however, hopes that the Department
continues and maintains this performance in future as well.

4.4 The Committee is surprised to note that while the Plan outlay earmarked for the Department
for the Eleventh Five Year Plan was Rs.9000.00 crores, the actual allocation made was curtailed
to Rs.6959.00 crores – a cut of Rs.2041.00 crores, more than the outlay for any one financial year
during the plan. The Committee observes that against the total outlay of Rs. 9000.00 crores for
entire plan allocation, the B.E. allocation was to the tune of Rs. 7150.00 indicating a shortfall of
Rs. 8150.00 crores. It is all the more surprising for the Committee because the Department has
been efficiently utilising the allocations made to it throughout the entire Plan period. The
Department was in fact able to utilise over 99 per cent of the allocated funds.
4.5 The Committee expresses its anguish over such a huge cut resorted to by the Planning
Commission in plan outlays of the Department charged with the onerous responsibility of
using scientific innovation and interventions for catalyzing the industrial growth making
possible provisions of affordable health care for all and improving the living standards of
people at the bottom of economic pyramid. The Committee is of the opinion that such a step
on the part of Planning Commission is not justified. The Committee, therefore, recommends
that such cut measures should be avoided in future and it must be ensured that the entire
outlay for the T welfth Five Year Plan is actually made available to the Department.

ACHIEVEMENTS OF CSIR
CSIR, informed the Committee that it has made major strides in terms of its contributions
in varied areas such as affordable healthcare, sustainable energy, development of technologies for
industrial competitiveness, providing knowledgebase for strategic sector, and finding holistic
solution for societal welfare, etc. The Committee was particularly informed of the following
achievements made by CSIR in various spheres of its activities:–
5.1.1  A. Research Papers and Awards
CSIR published 4433 research papers in Science Citation Index (SCI) journals of national
and international repute during 2010 contributing to average 12% of the National SCI
publications. The average Impact Factor per paper stood at 2.28 during 2009 and 2010. CSIR
scientists have also bagged prestigious fellowships. In 2011, 37 fellows of Indian National
Science Academy, 23 fellows of Indian Academy of Engineers and 34 Shanti Swarup Bhatnager
Awardees were from CSIR. CSIR has bagged award for “Highest number of Patents in the
Country” instituted by Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion (DIPP), Ministry of
Commerce and Industry, for an Indian institute securing highest number of Indian patents in the
year 2009. It has also won Thomson and Reuters Innovation Award 2010 for “Most Innovative
Hi-Tech Academic Institution in the Country”. CSIR was granted 1282 foreign and 1507 Indian
Patents during the Eleventh Plan.
5.1.2 While the Committee lauds CSIR winning a number of prestigious awards at the
national level, the Committee desires that CSIR scientists should strive hard to make their
presence felt even at the international level by winning prestigious international awards as
well. The Committee further desires that with a good number of patents, CSIR should make
efforts to help Indian Industry develop more and more globally competitive products, for
that alone will determine usefulness of patents developed by CSIR scientists.
5.2 B. CSIR Technologies for National Capacity Building
5.2.1 CSIR has developed certain useful technologies for National Capacity Building namely
CNM5 (a five seater all metal civil aircraft designed and developed in Public-Private Partnership
mode with NAL and Mahindra Aerospace); Carbon Fibre Technology (an important strategic raw
material for the fabrication of advanced composite materials; Technology for recovery of Sulphate
of potash (SOP), a premium fertilizer with highest nutrient value of 68%; Head up Display
(a component of Cockpit Display). ATBS (Acrylamido Tertiary Butyl Sulfonic Acid) Technology,
etc.
5.2.2 CSIR has also significantly helped in building up of technology portfolio aimed at socio-
economic impact. Some notable successes in the field of Agriculture are development of tractors
Swaraj, Sonalika and now Krishi Shakti a 10 horsepower tractor for just Rs. One Lakh.
“RISORINE” for the treatment of tuberculosis, Prostalyn for the treatment and remedy of prostate
problem, Memory Enhancer, Caerulomycin, new contraceptives, Anti-malarial formulation,
Technologies for enhancing potability of Water, Streptokinase, Natural Streptokinase, Recombinant
Streptokinase, Clot-specific Streptokinase, Development of New-Generation Thrombolytic
Molecules, etc. are some of the important products developed in the field of healthcare. In addition

5
to this, CSIR Laboratories have developed significant knowledgebase on water, ranging from source
finding to mapping of water resources, from quality assessment to enhancing potability of water
and from recycling to waste water treatment.
5.3 C. Technology Transfer
Technology Transfer has been one of the focus areas of CSIR during the Eleventh Plan for
catalyzing the Industrial Growth. An outstanding example of Public-Private-Partnership that will
ultimately benefit the mankind is a unique deal with Nostrum Pharmaceuticals for world-wide
licensing of clinical development of new generation thrombolytic molecules. CSIR will be receiving
over 150 million US$ through various milestone payments and royalties. Other significant examples
are design and development of clot specific protein that displays plasminogen activation property;
new anti-ulcer drug; development of technology for Caerulmycin for its novel indication of immuno
suppression.
5.4 D. CSIR 800
Under CSIR 800, a programme which aims at improving the quality of life of the people
of India at the base of the economic pyramid through appropriate S&T interventions and developing
and providing innovative R&D based products and processes which would be affordable, CSIR has
designed and developed an eco-friendly dual powered rickshaw named ‘Soleckshaw’, driven partly
by pedal and partly by electric power supplied by a battery that is charged from solar energy. A
novel variety of Ashwagandha with high root yield of 15 quintal/hectare has been developed and
released to farmers which would help farmers with more earnings. A high yielding cultivar of
Lavender developed by CSIR has proved to be an excellent alternate crop for cultivation by farmers
in the state of Jammu & Kashmir.
5.5 E. Other Achievements
Other significant achievements of the CSIR are setting up of Academy of Scientific and
Innovation Research (AcSIR), CSIR Tech. Pvt. Ltd. for valorizing CSIR intellectual Property. The
Open Source Drug Discovery (OSDD) is an innovative programmes. It is an alternative model of
innovation to make healthcare affordable to the common man. OSDD got appreciated in the
prestigious scientific journal ‘Science’.
5.7 F. Ayurgenomics
Ayurgenomics, an integrative approach of Ayurveda and Genomics for discovery of
predictive markers for preventive and personalized medicine has been opened by the CSIR as a new
line of research. CSIR has played a major role in protecting the traditional knowledge by creating
a Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL). The TKDL contains information in 5 international
languages, i.e. English, Japanese, Spanish, French and German in 34 million pages concerning 2.26
lakh medicinal formulations in Ayurveda, Unani and Siddha. TKDL is recognized globally as a model
for protection of traditional knowledge.
5.8 G . TAPSUN
A Mega Project TAPSUN has been conceptualized in partnership with Ministry of New and
Renewable Energy (MNRE), CSIR led R&D initiatives on solar energy with number of
complementary and complimentary approaches are being implemented under the umbrella
programme T echnologies and Products for Solar ener gy utilization through Networks (T APSUN).

5.9 H. CSIR Innovation Complexes
CSIR Innovation Complexes is a new initiative for setting up Innovation Complexes focused
at translational research in diverse domains equipped with the state-of-the-art facilities in plug and
play mode innovation complexes and would help catalyze innovation in their regions of operation.
5.10 I. Technopreneur Promotion Programme
Technopreneur Promotion Programme (TePP), India’s largest network programme
supporting independent innovators and start-up firms aims at providing opportunities to all the
innovators across the country. It tries to increase 34 outreach centres and a hundred innovation
managers network to newer locations/states. This helps innovators to take benefit from TePP
progrmme for nurturing their innovations to convert into workable models/prototypes.
5.10.1 Achievements of TePP in 2011 were providing support to 400 projects of individual
innovators; commercialization of TePP Innovations; herbal medicine for treatment of Asthma; A V AZ,
an augmentative and alternative device for children suffering from Cerebral Palsy and development
of environment friendly Printing Ink, etc.
5.10.2 The Committee appreciates achievements of the Department both in high-end technology
and technologies for the societal benefits particularly in affordable healthcare during the
Eleventh Five Year Plan. The Committee, however, feels that a lot more needs to be done if
the country is to emerge as a technology driven economic superpower. The Committee,
therefore, desires that the Department should further intensify and galvanise its efforts during
the Twelfth Plan to make India a truly technology and innovation driven society.
6. THRUST AREAS OF TWELFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN
6.1 While the Eleventh Five Year Plan objective of DSIR was faster and inclusive growth and
substantial progress towards both the objectives was made, the Twelfth Five Year Plan, with the
starting year of 2012-13 aims at faster, sustainable and more inclusive growth. The goal of DSIR
in the Twelfth Five Year Plan is prosperity of the people while protecting the planet. To attain the
above objective, DSIR programmes in the Twelfth Five Year Plan shall focus on building an
innovative economic system in the country and supporting innovations in Medium, Small and Micro
Enterprises (MSME) clusters.

7. BUDGETARY ALLOCATION FOR THE TWELFTH FIVE YEAR PLAN
7.1 When the Committee enquired about the total budgetary allocation made for Twelfth Five
Year Plan for the Department, it was informed that the Planning Commission was yet to
communicate the budgetary allocation for the Twelfth Five Year Plan.
7.2 The Committee finds it quite surprising that even when the Twelfth five Year Plan has
started, Planning Commission is yet to finalise the Twelfth Plan allocations. The Committee
feels that such a delay could affect not only the planning and distribution of resources of the
Department but also the physical and financial targets set by the Department. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that Planning Commission should finalise the allocations
for the Twelfth five year plan without any further delay so that the Department is clear on
its long term and short term targets to be achieved during the plan period.

9.1 ‘A’ PROGRAMMES OF THE DEPA RTMENT OF  SCIENTIFIC AND INDUSTRIAL
RESEARCH (DSIR)
9.1.1 The Committee has been informed that Technology Promotion, Development and Utilization
(TPDU) Programme constituted one of the major plan programmes of DSIR during the Eleventh
Five Year Plan which aimed at promoting industrial R&D; development and commercialization of
technologies; acquisition, management and export of technologies; promotion of consultancy
capabilities; facilitation of technology information to support R&D, etc. It had four major sub
schemes viz.
(i) Industrial R&D Promotion Programme
(ii) T echnology Development and Demonstration Programme (TDDP)
(iii) T echnopreneur Promotion Programme (TePP); and
(iv) Technology Development Utilization Programme for Women.
9.1.2 When the Committee found that no budgetary allocation has been made for TPDU for the
year 2012-13, it enquired from the Department, whether the programme had been discontinued and
if so, what were the reasons for that?
9.1.3 The Department clarified that it had decided to discontinue the programme since it had
run for two plan periods i.e. Tenth and Eleventh Plan and the Department felt the need to
introduce innovative mechanisms for achieving significant outcomes in terms of establishment of
a large number of knowledge based enterprises and making the MSME Sector globally
competitive. TPDU has, therefore, been replaced by four new schemes in the T welfth Five Year
Plan focusing on nurturing innovations among individuals, startup companies, MSMEs and
industries for achieving inclusive growth in industry and economy. The most significant
component of TPDU i.e. TePP outreach centre activity would however continue as a part of the
new scheme called “PRISM”.
9.1.4 The Committee is aware that investment of over Rs. 10000 crores in R&D had been
reported under this programme and DSIR had reported innovations in various fields like
development of Fuel Cell Bus; E-waste Recycling & Precious Metal Recovery; Indigenous
Dialysis Device; Novel plant tissue culture medium from bio-waste – Fly Ash; Herbal
Medicine for treatment of Asthma; and A V AZ – Device for Cerebral Palsy through this
programme. The programme has now been scrapped as need for more innovative mechanism
was felt for achieving significant results. The Committee hopes that four new schemes now
introduced would be able to produce significant and much better results than obtained under
TPDU and for the purpose, the Committee recommends that Department should effectively
monitor these schemes so that money invested in these schemes is not only adequately
justified but also provides the desired results.

9.1.6 NEW SCHEMES OF THE DEPARTMENT
9.1.7 DSIR pro poses to pursue the following four new Plan Schemes during the Twelfth Five
Year Plan, namely:–
(i) Promoting Innovations in Individuals, Start-ups and MSMEs (PRISM),
(ii) Patent Acquisition and Collaborative Research and Technology Development (PACE),
(iii) Building Industrial R&D and Common Research Facilities (BIRD) and
(iv) Access to kn owledge for Technology Development and Dissemination (A2K +).
A brief analysis of the objectives, targets set and allocation made for the schemes for the
year 2012-13 is given below:–
(i) Promoting Innovations in Individuals, Start-ups and MSMEs (PRISM)
Under the scheme, innovative proposals of MSMEs shall be supported; CSIR – Cluster
Innovation Centres (CICs) promoted by National Innovation Council shall be supported for
providing innovative solutions; proposals from individual innovators/incubates shall be supported;
and support shall be extended to approved Technopreneur Promotion Programme (TePP) projects,
spilling over from the Eleventh five year plan.
Some of the significant objectives of the schemes include:–
• To create an innovation eco-system in India for growth of MSMEs;
• To create industry-CSIR Cluster Innovation Centres (CICs) for strengthening
competitive advantages of MSMEs;

14
• To support innovative proposals of innovators/researchers (students/scientists/
consultants/grassroots innovators) formulated by CICs;
• To convert innovative ideas or inventions into demonstrable working models/
prototypes/processes;
Targets envisaged under PRISM Scheme for 2012-13 are to establish 10 cluster innovation
centres and to support around 200 innovative proposals from MSME clusters; to establish 5 DSIR
Outreach Centres and to support around 75 innovative proposals; to identify and support 2 startup
companies in focused areas in collaboration with SIDBI; and to complete 40 ongoing TePP
projects. An allocation of Rs 25 crores has been made under this scheme as against the projected
demand of Rs. 220 crores. The Department, however, has informed the Committee that it would
have to significantly tone down these targets in view of reduced allocation made for the scheme.
(ii) Scheme on Patent Acquisition and Collaborative Research and Technology Development
(PACE) –
This scheme aims at providing support to Indian industries to acquire Intellectual Property
at early stage from overseas or within the country and add value to the acquired IP and focus on
Public-Private-Partnerships (PPP) to create enabling environment for collaborative research between
Industry and Universities/Public Funded Research Institutions.
Some Significant Objectives of this scheme are:–
• To focus on Public Private Partnership (PPP) and create enabling environment for
collaborative research between Industry and Universities/Public Funded Research
Institutions;
• To develop a dynamic database on existing expertise and IPs available in the
Universities/Public Funded Research Institutions to facilitate tie-ups with industry for
PPP;
• To promote commercialization of lab scale technologies of the Universities/Public
Funded Research Institutions;
• To strengthen tie-ups between Universities/Public Funded Research Institutions and
Industry to formulate collaborative projects for development of new products and
processes;
Main targets to be achieved under this scheme are to support 3 collaborative projects of
industry and institutions; to assist industry in acquiring one technology and adding value for
development and demonstration. An outlay of Rs 10 crores has been allocated against the projected
demand of Rs. 160 crores under this scheme. The Committee was informed by the Department
that in view of reduced allocation under the schemes, targets have to be toned down.
(iii) Building Industrial R&D and Common Research Facilities (BIRD)
BIRD scheme aims at recognizing and encouraging in-house R&D units of industry as well
as NGOs, Universities and other institutions (designated as Scientific & Industrial Research
Organisations) through various support mechanisms and implementing fiscal incentives announced
by the Government from time to time.
Some of the significant objectives of the scheme include:–
• To promote and nurture research and development in industry with a view to enhance
industry‘s share in national R&D expenditure;

15
• To establish at least 10 privately operated facilities for industrial R&D;
• To cover areas where Indian industry will be required to compete internationally in the
next 10 years;
The criteria for granting recognition to in-house R&D of industry and SIROs, which include
research publications and patents, shall focus on commercialization of patents into useful products
and services.
Some major targets to be achieved under this scheme include processing of around 240
applications for grant of recognition and registration to new in-house R&D units; Scientific and
Industrial Research Organisations (SIROs); Public Funded Research Institutions (PFRI)and around
775 applications for renewal of recognition and registration to in-house R&D units, Scientific and
Industrial Research Organisations (SIROs); Public Funded Research Institutions (PFRI); and to
initiate the process of setting up at least one common facility centre.
Under the scheme, Rs 25 crores has been allocated against the projected demand of 60.50
crores. Lower allocations, the Department informed the Committee, shall have an adverse impact
on the outcomes of the schemes.
(iv) Access to Knowledge for Technology Development and Dissemination (A2K+) –
Under this scheme, Science, technology and innovation related international journals from
major publishers shall be made accessible to 1500 in-house R&D units of industry and 600 SIROs
and techno-entrepreneurs.
The specific objectives of the scheme would be to subscribe to science, technology and
innovation related international journals and make it accessible to in-house R&D units of industry, SIROs
and techno-entrepreneurs; to sponsor industrial technology related studies and disseminate them widely;
and to organize, support and participate in national and international conferences, seminars &
workshops, exhibitions etc. to share the knowledge and success stories with stake-holders.
Main targets envisaged under A2K+ Scheme for 2012-13 are to initiate the process of
subscription to international science journals and making them accessible to in-house R&D units
and SIROs, to initiate 5 technology related studies and organize 5 conferences/exhibitions and to
support ongoing Technology Development and Demonstration Programme (TDDP) Projects. An
allocation of Rs 24.50 crores has been made against the projected demand of Rs. 56.50 crores.
The Committee was informed that targets set for 2012-13 under the schemes would be lowered
in view of significant reduction in allocation under the scheme.
9.1.8 The Committee feels that in view of budgetary constraints, there is a need for the
Department to devise innovative ways for funding these schemes. Public Private Partnership
(PPP) model, levy of appropriate user charges, funding from other beneficiaries, sharing of
profits arising from commercial exploitation of patents funded under scheme, etc. are some
of the known ways in which requisite funds for implementation of scheme can be generated.
The Committee hopes that the Department would be able to devise innovative ways of
funding the scheme and achieve the targets set for 2012-13 under these schemes. The
Committee, however, feels that Government should, to the extent necessary, provide
adequate funds for these schemes at RE stage, so that target set for 2012-13 under these
schemes get realised in toto .

I. NATIONAL LABORATORIES SCHEME
The Scheme Covers R&D projects in suprainstitutional Network, Inter-agency and facility
creation modes. CSIR, during the Twelfth Plan, proposes to set up five new institutions either in
the physical or in virtual mode, namely CSIR-Institute of Synthetic and System Biology (CSIR-
ISSB); CSIR- Fourth Paradigm Institute (CSIR-4PI); CSIR-Institute of Bio-Mimetic Materials
(CSIR-IBMM); CSIR-Network Institute of Solar Energy (CSIR-NISE); and CSIR-Network Institute
of Manufacturing Technology (CSIR-NIMT).
It also proposes to undertake R&D programmes across the biological, chemical,
engineering, information and physical science clusters. Different categories of projects include
supra-institutional projects (involving individual laboratories),network projects (involving multiple
CSIR laboratories), supra-institutional network projects (involving as well the non-CSIR
participation in identified set of projects), large mission projects, cross-cluster projects (that are
formulated to overarch multiple clusters) and facility creation projects. In addition, few mega
projects have been proposed.
The Department has informed that under this scheme, for 2012-13 it has set a target of
developing 10 advanced products/technologies either singly or in trans-cluster mode through
biological, chemical, engineering, information and physical sciences clusters. Establishment of five
new institutions and formulation of projects and securing relevant approvals for setting up of CSIR
outreach centres and CSIR special centres; operationalization of new projects under the EMPOWER
and RISK programmes; and augmenting core competency in identified R&D areas, appropriate
steps to augment R&D infrastructure in CSIR laboratories and to develop energy efficient green
campuses etc. are other targets under the scheme.
The Committee observes that National Laboratories scheme has been allocated an
outlay of Rs. 2146.00 crores for 2012-13 as against the projected demand of Rs. 2312.68
crores were allocated to this scheme at B.E. Stage in 2011-12 which was reduced to
Rs. 2225.91 crores at R.E. stage. The Committee finds that 2012-13, outlay under this
scheme is lower by Rs. 166 crores as compared to B.E. 2011-12 and by Rs. 79.91 crores
vis-à-vis  R.E. 2011-12. The Committee apprehends that this reduction might be because of
the fact that the Department failed to fully utilise the allocated outlay last year with
underutilisation being to the tune of Rs. 86.77 crores. However, when the Committee
enquired about the reasons for lesser allocation this year as compared to the last year,
the Department clarified that the decrease is due to overall low allocations during
2012-13 and initiation of new schemes for the T welfth Five Year Plan for which, though,
desired allocations have not been made, some budget had been diverted, from National
Laboratories to new schemes.
The Committee, in its 208
th report, had recommended that the network of National
Laboratories should be extended to all the states of the country, with at least one laboratory
in each state so that there is equitable scientific progress in each state. The Committee had
also recommended that though the National Laboratories are doing a lot of ingenious work
as regards Research and Development in various scientific spheres but most of its
achievements are not reaching the common man. In fact, the Committee had felt that the
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research could justify an increase in the budgetary
allocation only if substantial benefits reach the common man and had accordingly
recommended that the Department should focus towards transfer of technology from the
National Laboratories to the society.

IV. NEW MILLENNIUM INDIAN TECHNOLOGY LEADERSHIP INITIATIVE (NMITLI)
The NMITLI aims at creating alliance with the industry through Public Private Partnership
Schemes (PPP schemes). The Department informed that during the Tenth Plan New Millennium
Indian Technology Leadership Initiative (NMITLI) has created a brand image and is viewed today
as a benchmark of PPP schemes which is being emulated by various other government
departments. The partners especially from the industry were of the view that industries, which
collaborated NMITLI projects, have highly benefited not only in terms of the hard objectives and
achievements in the project wherein they participated but also in terms of other associated gains
such as high quality technical inputs etc.
The Committee finds that this programme was allocated Rs. 70 crores at B.E. 2011-12
which was reduced to Rs. 50.00 crores at R.E. 2011-12. At B.E. 2012-13 the allocated contect
were again enhanced to Rs. 60.00 crores. Noting that programme is to be run on PPP model,
the Committee hopes that the Department would effectively leverage the funds allocated to
obtain desired outcome under the scheme.
The Committee has been informed that the reason behind NMITLI Scheme not performing
as expected is that the country is not able to recognize and appreciate the concept of PPP for
Research and Development for R&D. In addition, there is no enabling mechanism to develop
seamless team between industry and publicly funded institutions. It has further been informed that
the Department proposes to broaden the NMITLI scheme in the Twelfth Plan as per the Cabinet
Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) approval.
The Committee is of the view that the Department should take necessary steps to
dispel the apprehensions about PPP mode and make sincere efforts to ensure success of this
model.

V. CSIR-800 SCHEME
Twelfth Five Year Plan goal of CSIR 800 is to measurably augment the incomes and
improve the quality of the lives of one million rural citizens geographically spread across the
country, in accordance with stated Government of India targets and relevant UN-Millennium
Development Goals. Strategic implementation of the program will focus on CSIR’s new initiative
of 24 geographically distributed CSIR Technology-enabled Villages (TECHVIL) chosen because of
their high proportion of families living below the poverty line.
Following are the main Objectives of the schemes:–
• Transform life in TECHVIL [24 CSIR Technology-Enabled Villages].
• Showcase TECHVIL to enroll 1 million citizens in adjoining communities to the benefits
of technology.
• Formalize TECHVIL experiences into a spectrum of replicable small entrepreneurial
business models to cover the remaining 800 million citizens at the base of the Indian
economic pyramid.
Stating the targets for 2012-13, the Department has informed that the CSIR-800 would have

a new platform for innovation in the domain of affordable healthcare. The Department has informed

output to the public at large. CSIRs Open Source Drug Discovery (OSDD) initiative has achieved
significant success during the Eleventh Plan.
This new scheme covers three specific programmes viz.
(i) Open Source Drug Discovery Programme (OSDDP);
(ii) Distributed Organic Chemical Synthesis (DOCS) Programme; and
(iii) Science 3.0: Open Innovation, Crowd Sourcing and Open Source Knowedgeware.

enabling mechanisms for contract research, consultancy, joint (collaborative) research, loan of
equipments and facilities and personnel exchange, etc.
The Committee is happy to learn that CSIR has implemented all the
recommendations of Abid Hussain Committee. The Committee notes that share of public
funding for R&D at 2-3 per cent of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is at par with
international norms. However, the share of private funds for R&D at only 3 per cent of
GDP in our country is very low as compared to international norms of 1.3-1.4 of GDP. In
France, Industry performs over 63 per cent (of all R&D) and funds around 54 per cent
projects while this figure is 69 per cent in Japan. In India, contribution of industries is
estimated at around 25 per cent of national R&D expenditure which needs to be enhanced
to at least 50 per cent in the Twelfth plan. The Committee feels that the Department should
give more and more incentives and form more partnerships with the private sector
companies of India in order to infuse more funds in R&D sector. In this regard, the
Committee fully supports the recommendations of the Planning Commission in its ‘approach
paper on Twelfth Five Year Plan—Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth’ wherein
it has suggested on interaction of Public S&T Institutions with CSIR at large.

disease and in India, the government in collaboration with a private company was doing research
on the disease and a generic drug which would reduce cost of medicine had been thought of.
50. He further suggested that Parliament should take a policy decision to declare cancer as a
strategic disease to facilitate research on the disease and availability of drugs at affordable costs.
CSIR’s reverse engineering capability should come up as an alternative to reduce the cost of cancer
drug. However, the Secretary mentioned that for making a complete new discovery in the field of
cancer, it would take another 15 years.
51. The Secretary also intimated the Committee about the production a low cost HIV drug as
compared to international prices with the help of a private medical company.
52. Regarding Scientific manpower in the country, the Secretary appreciated the visionary leadership
of Pt. Nehru for putting up CSIR laboratories with the support of Dr. Bhatnagar. He further, mentioned
that highest return on investment in science and technology was still at a dismal figure of 0.80 to 0.89
per cent and suggested that a cess may be levied by the government, similar to the education cess and
such a move would help in creation of substantial budget for infusion in science and technology. He
further stated that development in science and technology would definitely augment India’s health care
capabilities, energy capabilities, water, food quality and nutrition capabilities.
53. The Secretary then requested the Chairman of the Committee to undertake a study visit to
one of the CSIR laboratories at Palampur, to have first hand experience of the achievements of the
CSIR laboratories.
