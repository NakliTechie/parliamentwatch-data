69th Report on Demands for Grants (1999-2000)of the Department
of Scientific and Industrial Research
 
The Department of Scientific and Industrial Research which forms a part of the Ministry of Science and Technology
 comprises of activities of Departmental Schemes viz. Research and Development by Industry (RDI), Programme
 Aimed at Technological Self Reliance (PASTER), Scheme to Enhance the Efficacy of Transfer of Technology
 (SEETOT), National Information System for Science and Technology (NISSAT) and management of the public sector
 undertaking, National Research Development Corporation (NRDC) and Central Electronics Limited (CEL). The long
 term objective of the programme on Research by Industry and Technology Transfer is technological self-reliance and
 the short term objectives are promotion of R&D by Industry and enhancing efficacy of technology transfer, both
 domestic and foreign. Issues addressed in these programmes include identification of technological gaps, making
 investment in R&D by Industry attractive and productive, development and commercialisation of domestic
 technologies through. In – house R&D in industry and R&D by public funded laboratories coupled with industry. The
 Department also looks after matters concerning the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) which
 functions as a registered society and has a network of 40 laboratories and 81 field stations/extension centres/regional
 centres located all over the country that undertake R&D in various science and technology areas and disciplines.
2. The Secretary explained the achievements of the National Laboratories under the CSIR, in the fields of Aerospace,
 Biology and Biotechnology, Chemicals, Drugs and Pharmaceuticals, Earth resources and Natural hazards mitigation,
 Ecology and Environment, Food and Food Processing etc. which have tangible impact on the socio-economy sectors,
 during the Eighth Five Year Plan and informed about the new projects which the Department proposes to undertake
 during the year 1999-2000.
3. The Secretary also gave a brief presentation on the recent earthquake having an epicentre in the Chamoli region of
 Uttar Pradesh. The Committee enquired about the efforts made by the Department to arrest the damage and
 rehabilitation plans. The Committee specifically wanted to know whether any lead has been made for scientifically
 predicting earthquake. The Committee was informed that within CSIR a National Geophysical research Institute,
 Hyderabad and Central building Research Institute, Roorke are working for minimising the damage after an earthquake.
 As regards, predicting earthquakes, it was stated that as of today, there is no scientifically accepted method available to
 predict earthquakes accurately enough to be of help in saving human lives as there are no well established precursors
 which precede earthquake. A member pointed out that such an event in China was once preceded by strange behaviour
 of animals which led an elder to forecast that an earthquake was about to come and city was got vacated which helped
 minimise the damage. However, it is possible today to broadly work out the earthquake hazard in terms of accelerations
 that may occur at a given region based on the previous earthquake records. Then, on the basis of local geology and
 several other factors, it is possible to carry out mico zoning which indicates the kind of acceleration that would be
 expected in areas at a scale of a few hectares to a few square kilometres. Thereafter retrofitting of vulnerable structures
 can be done to save non engineered structures.

8. Asked about success rate of various research projects being pursued in various laboratories working under the CSIR
 and the steps the Department has taken to maximise gains from R&D programmes/activities, the Committee was
 informed that on an average, from around 2000-3000 new R&D ideas/concepts, only one becomes a commercial
 success, and another four may make it to the market-place, as by very nature R&D output is highly risky and uncertain.
 The success rate in respect of projects varies from one sector/discipline to another and even within a sector in the niche
 areas dealt with, it was stated that on an average CSIR is able to commercialise around 3-10% of its in-house R&D
 projects, which is an unenviable record anywhere in the world, CSIR is seeking to maximise its gains by forging
 strategic alliances and linkages with other constituents in the innovation chain e.g. financial institutions, industry,
 engineering firms etc. CSIR has in place a three-tier project monitoring system for getting optimum output and returns.

RESIDENTIAL BUILDING:
10. The allocation for Residential Buildings has been kept at Rs.15.30 crore against Rs.16.37 crores and Rs.14.87 crore
 respectively in B.E. and R. E. of last year. Housing has been identified as one of the major constraints in attracting
 talented scientists and retain them in CSIR, yet adequate efforts have not been made by the Department to address to
 the problem as is reflected in the budget allocation. The Committee wanted to know reasons for non- utilisation of full
 allocation of Rs.16.37 crore made during last year which led to reduced allocation of Rs.14.87 crore at RE stage. It has
 been stated that CSIR has accorded Intellectual Property & Technology Management the highest priority and sought to
 make saving in the other schemes to enhance the allocation on housing. Thus CSIR had to generate Rs.1.5 crore saving
 from Residential Buildings Head which has been diverted to Intellectual Property and Technology Management
 (IP&TM). In view of higher Housing Building Advance as recommended by the Fifth Pay Commission to the
 employees and also the incentives announced by the government for the housing sector, such as liberal financing of
 housing loans a short term reallocation was resorted to.
11. "The Committee observes the Department should endeavour to attract talent to the CSIR laboratories by
 providing additional amenities, of which housing remains an important area. The Committee is of the view that
 adequate provisions should have been made for IP&TM so that diversion of funds from other schemes should
 not have been resorted to.
12. The total allocation for Programme Aimed at Technological Self Reliance (PATSER) a programme involving
 absorption, adaptation and demonstration of technology was Rs.10.00 crores in the BE of last year which was reduced
 to Rs.6.50 crores at the RE stage. The allocation for this programme has been increased to Rs.7.80 crores in the BE of
 current year. The Committee enquired into the reasons for variations in the allocation at different stages. The
 Committee was informed that an allocation of Rs.10.00 crores was made at BE 1998-99 under the Programme Aimed at
 Technological Self Reliance (PATSER), however, on stock taking of the projects on the pipeline, this was reduced to
 Rs.6.50 crore at RE stage. The first half of the year 1998-99 was a period of consolidation and implementation of
 streamlined procedures, and for technical and financial approvals of projects under PATSER, which were
 recommended by the Committee. During the second half of 1998-99 various proposals were scrutinised and processed
 for financial approvals and the Departments expect that the allocation of Rs.6.5 crores at the RE would be utilised. The
 department has in hand a number of proposals for support under PATSER and it accodingly sought an allocation of
 Rs.7.8 crore . Considering that the procedures have been streamlined during 1998-99 the allocation of Rs.7.8 crores for
 1999-2000 would be fully utilised.
 
The Committee notes the position.

 traditional knowledge that is to have a CD-ROM on the wealth of the country. The Department is taking steps to
 digitalise the tribal knowledge, the ayurvedic practice, text books in Sanskrit and has been able to make policy
 initiatives on a global scale

National S&T Human Resource Development
16. CSIR supports the development of highly qualified R&D manpower at the national level through grant of Junior and
 Senior Research Fellowships and Research Associateships. CSIR has introduced a scheme to promote youth leadership
 in science this year with a view to attract young school going talent to science in order to encourage bright students
 opting for science subjects. CSIR also supports External Research through grants to Universities with the objective of
 improving the scientific and engineering research in the country.
17. It has been stated by CSIR that after the successful completion of their studies, young scientists generally prefer to
 work abroad. This diversion of talent affects the research activities in our country and efforts are being made to stop
 this trend. The Committee was informed that the extent of brain drain is not limited to young scientists . Even people
 engaged in jobs go away because of attractive emoluments. It is therefore necessary that employees in CSIR need better
 scales to stop this kind of diversion.
18. The Committee observes that in order to encourage talent in the field of research it is necessary that all the
 technical personnel including those engaged in promotion of science or work related to science are given
 attractive pay scales so that they can sustain their interest in the work assigned to them. This should include
 furnished houses and other incentives that are necessary keeping in view their status etc. This would arrest brain
 drain. The Committee is also of the view that age of superannuation of Senior Scientific Staff in CSIR should be
 brought at par with that prevailing in IITs, IISs and Universities which would also help in restricting the
 external and internal brain drain.
DRUGS AND PHARMACEUTICALS
19. Some of significant achievements made in this sector is discovery and development of a new molecule centchroman
 by CDRI as a new non-steroidal once a week female oral contraceptive which had been introduced in the National
 Family Welfare Programme. Centchroman had been found effective as a therapeutic for other ailments and diseases.
 Besides, Piperine isolated from an indigenous plant, had been developed by RRL (Jammu) as a novel bioenhancer that
 enabled the dosages of the common anti-TB drugs, particularly Rifampicin, to be reduced by at least 50% resulting not
 only in the reduction of cost of medication but more importantly in the reduced side effects such as hepatotoxicity and
 neurotoxicity. The know-how had been transferred to industry. In addition to this Brahmi: ‘Bacopa monnieri’ extract
 from Brahmi had been standardised & validated as a learning and memory enhancer by CDRI and licensed to the firm
 which had commenced its manufacture and marketing. Besides, CSIR has developed novel and cost-effective processes
 for over 30 drugs as well as four drug intermediates which had been licensed and most of them have also been
 commercialised viz; Anti-AIDS: AZT (Azidothymidine); Anti-viral: Acyclovir; Anti-cancer: Etoposide, Ondansetron
 and Leuprolide (LHRH agonist); Anti-bacterials: Norfloxacin, Ciprofloxacin, Azithromycin, Sulbactam, Sultamicilin
 tosylate; Anti-malarial: Mefloquine; Cardio-Vascular: Enalapril, Nadolol, Metoprolol and Felodipine; Analgesics &
 anti-inflammatory: Ketorolac; Anti-allergic: Astemizole and Citrizine; Anti-ulcer: Omeprazole and Lansoprozole; Anti-
glaucoma: Betaxolol and ‘S’ Timolol and anti-inflammatory: S-ibuprofen and S-flurbiprofen; antihypertensive: SS-
enalapril.
20. Drugs and pharmaceutical industry is very crucial for India and the sector cannot grow without innovations
 and R&D. The Committee is constrained to observe that the quantum of investments for R&D in this sector by
 the Industry is far from satisfactory. The Committee recommends the Department to take initiatives to
 encourage industry to increase their R&D input to bring out new technologies which are superior & cheaper
 than the existing ones.

FOOD AND FOOD PROCESSING
21. The object of food processing is primarily done to improve and safeguard the agricultural productivity reducing
 wastage of perishable food items like fruit and vegetables and for improving food availability for domestic markets and
 for exports . This would ensure supply of fruit and vegetables during off season when the crop for those products does
 not grow.
22. As regard the achievements of Department in this sector, the Committee was informed that CSIR was designated as
 the nodal agency for the Mini Mission on Post Harvest Technology of the Technology Mission on Oil Seeds and
 Pulses. CSIR has been participating in the Mission through its laboratories CFTRI, IICT and RRLs (Trivuvanthapuram
 & Jammu). Its achievements were in the field of Oil palm processing technologies including a screw press of 5 tonnes
 FFB/hr capacity; Improved expeller of 1 TPD, 10 TPD and 50 TPD capacities; a 10 TPD solar-cum-waste energy drier
 for mustard/toria/rape seed; designs of chemical stabilizer for rice bran oil of 15 kg/batch and 25 kg/batch capacities;
 single roll decorticator of 1 T/hr capacity for dehulling of groundnut; twin roll decorticator of 6 T/hr and 1 T/hr
 capacities for dehulling of sunflower seed; improved process for dehulling of black sesame seed (240 kg/hr capacity);
 designs of Mechanised pulse Mill of 100 kg/hr capacity and hand Operated Daal Mill of 40-50 kg/hr capacity. Besides
 above, inhouse activies had resulted in development and transferring of several processes to industry and for their
 commercial utilisation which include Food processing & Post Harvest Technology. Optimisation of process for clean
 and sterilised – chilli, pepper and turmeric; spice flavour concentrate and spice paste; dry green pepper and white
 pepper without using chemicals; food packages for export of fresh mango, sapota, pineapple and pomegranate;
 modified atmosphere packging of vegetables for export and in the field of Food Additives: Stabilisation of alpha
 amylase; optimised conditions for maximum extraction of enzyme from mouldy bran; development of processes for
 microbial production of phytase, catalase and enzymes for degradation of aflatoxin and pesticides; protein hydrolysate
 preparation from selected oilseeds proteins for high functionality and use in specialty food.
23 The Committee is of the view that since the Indian diet consists largely of cereals and much of the food value is
 lost on account of refinement , the department should aim its activities for preserving and adding value to the
 food items while at the same time preserving the natural and traditional tastes.
FRUIT AND VEGETABLES
24. Despite being a major producer of fruit and vegetables, the country has very low share in the world trade of
 processed fruit and vegetables. Asked about the efforts made in the field of processing fruit and vegetable, the
 Committee was informed that Central Food Technological Research Institute has pioneered technological innovations
 in the processing of fruit and vegetables. Some of the leads made are – Technology protocols for post harvest handling
 preservation, storage, packing and marketing of fresh fruit; concentration of tropical fruit juices; dehydration techniques
 for tropical fruit using state of art drying system; and enzyme clarification for tropical fruit pulps etc.
25. In order to minimise the post harvest losses and create employment in the rural areas it is necessary to
 develop simple processing technologies. The Committee therefore recommends that Department should give
 thrust to its activities in this field and strive to develop and commercialise more techniques like Modified
 Atmosphere Storage packaging which could be extended to practically all the fruit and vegetables.
Industrial Toxicology Research Centre (ITRC)
26. It has been stated that ITRC has undertaken in-house research in the areas of Preventive Toxicology, Predictive
 Toxicology, Environmental toxicology and Inhalation Toxicology. The projects undertaken during 1997-98 and 1998-
99 include (I) Toxicological studies of petroleum products and chemicals; (ii) Risk assessment of dietary and
 environmental chemicals; (iii) Developmental and reproductive toxicity of chemicals; (iv) Effect of environmental
 pollutants on bio-diversity and environmental detoxification and (v) Inhalation toxicity of pyrethoroids, insoluble
 materials and intervention strategies.
27. It was also informed that CFTRI, Mysore and ITRC, Lucknow have been conducting awareness programmes on
 detection of adulterants in foods both for rural and urban people through schools, villages, panchayats etc.

Leather Technologies:
29. India has the highest cattle population in the world, but the value addition is not satisfactory. India’s share in exports
 of leather products is meagre 4 percent. A Leather Technology Mission for sustainable Development of the Indian
 Leather Sector was assigned to CSIR and the implementation task to CLRI, to bridge the technological gap between the
 decentralised and organised sectors of the Indian leather industry keeping the environment in the forefront. The other
 partners in the Mission were Department of Biotechnology, Science & Technology, KVIC and several NGOs. The
 Mission inter-alia sought to help industry achieve the targets set for annual growth rate of 25% and raise India’s share
 in the global leather trade to 10% . Of the 120 projects planned for development and implementation more than 70
 programmes had been initiated covering eleven States of the country with the objective to augment the availability of
 quality hides and skins; evolve a technology grid for a balanced development of the sector; provide extension services
 of rural and small-scale units for adoption of cleaner technology and for upgradation of the processing techniques;
 initiate a campaign for quality and standardisation; enable harmonious blending of traditional and new skills through
 innovative training and HRD programmes; evolve and implement appropriate technology systems; study and identify
 the most suitable organisational structures to integrate the development of rural, semi-urban and urban sector.
30. The Committee expresses its concern on the increasing trend of Non-Plan expenditure by the department over the
 years 1995-96, 1996-97, 1997-98 and 1998-99. It was stated that the increase in Non-Plan expenditure was due to the
 committed liabilities towards expenses of salaries for the existing employees, pension for the retired employees and
 increase in stipends to Research Fellows and Associates, basically due to the implementation of the recommendations
 of Central Fifth pay Commission in/by CSIR. Another reason for the increase was cost escalation on basic
 infrastructure inputs such as electricity, communication charges, travel expenditure due to decline in the value of rupee
 for purchasing imported consumables & chemicals and hence the essential expenses had increased despite austerity and
 economy measures from time to time and close monitoring of costs in CSIR. Thus there was rise under the Non Plan
 expenditure to the tune of Rs.77.13 crore and Rs.149.19 crores during 1997-98 and 1998-99. Out of these, the impact of
 the emoluments related expenditure was Rs.65.5 crores and Rs.126.0 crores for the year 1997-98 and 1998-
99.respectively.
31. The Committee was informed that allocation of Rs.531.46 crores under non-plan provided this year would not be
 sufficient to meet the commitments and a short fall of Rs.40.49 crores under committed heads is anticipated this year
 which includes Rs.12.83 crore under salaries, Rs.17.30 crore under pensionary expenses and Rs.4.76 under advances.
 The Department proposes to project the exact shortfall including the additional anticipated DA payments to the
 Ministry of Finance at R.E. stage Thus, the shortfall under committed expenses is likely to be made up by additional
 budgetary support under non-plan from government, and the short fall under R&D expenses is proposed to be met from
 the internal resources of CSIR.
32. The Committee is constrained to observe that inspite of its earlier recommendations contained in its
 Thirteenth, Twenty Fourth, Thirty Sixth, Fifty Second, Fifty Sixth reports Department has failed to make
 sufficient provision in the non-plan outlay of DSIR, to meet its committed liability, yet a shortfall to the tune of
 Rs.40.49 crores is anticipated in this sector during the current financial year. The Committee suggests that
 Department should make out a strong case to meet its committed liabilities under the non-plan sector by
 providing additional budgetary support at R.E. stage so that there is no need under any circumstances to divert
 funds earmarked for R&D work and other plan activities to non-plan uses.
33. After making a detailed scrutiny of the Demands for Grants of the Department of Scientific and Industrial
 Research and assessing the progress of various programmes and projects administered by it, the Committee
 recommends that the Demands for Grants of Rs.820.46 crores, as sought by the Department of Scientific and
 Industrial Research for the year 1999-2000, under Demand no.74 are in order and may be approved.
