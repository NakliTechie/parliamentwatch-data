of promoting new areas of Science and Technology and act as a nodal department for

2. With the total budgetary support of Rs. 654.63 crores, the Department of Science 
and Technology proposes to lay emphasis on building and sustaining a strong science and 

technology base in the country, developing centres of excellence in the frontline areas of 
science and technology, and modernising the infrastructure of its autonomous research 
institutions . Under the Science and Engineering Research Council Programme, new 
initiatives would be launched to further strengthen the S & T base in the country in 
selected areas of technological importance. The research areas for the engineering sciences 
would be in the disciplines like Chemical Engineering; Materials Mining and Mineral 
Engineering; Robotics; Computer Engineering etc. The overall approach would be to 
promote basic research around outstanding scientific groups and to a large extent in the 
academic sector. The SERC, it is stated, would be provided more autonomy and flexibility 
for better management of R & D schemes and their proper monitoring and evaluation. 
Some of the important initiatives include promotion of research in less endowed 
universities; setting up Engineering Research Centres in the areas of Concurrent 
Engineering, Bio-Engineering, Technical Acoustics etc.; providing industrial research 
fellowships to work in the industry; encouraging some of the frontier areas like 
biodiversity, noise control, plasma chemistry, micro robotics, chemical engineering sciences, 
modern classical optics, stress biology and immunology, boundary layer modelling, study of 
atmospheric chemistry etc. The research areas for the engineering sciences, would be in the 
disciplines like Chemical Engineering; Materials, Mining and Mineral Engineering; 
Robotics; Computer Engineering etc. The Department would focus on setting up a few 
major sophisticated facilities and Centres in the country to pursue frontline R&D, such as 
electron spray . mass spectrometer, radioactive ion beam, multi-Ge V Hadron heavy ion 
accelerator, low energy accelerator, confocal microscopy facility etc. under the programme 
of Intensification of Research in High Priority Areas (IRHPA). 
3. As a part of the National Science and Technology Manpower Information System 
(NSTMIS), a National Science Manpower Information System would be taken up and 
integrated with the ongoing National Technical Manpower Information System of the 
Ministry of Human Resource Development. In the area of technology development, a 
more holistic and user-friendly approach would be adopted towards making the high 
technologies and products available to the society. The thrust would be on technology 
missions, technology promotion and special technology projects through critical technology 
promotion, high technology intervention in selected export areas and promotion of NGO/ 
society/organisation. Under the Ir.strument Development Programme, it is proposed to 
support new projects for the development of instruments in some areas like energy 
monitoring and management ·sensors etc. besides conducting training programmes <.tnd 
transfer of know-how of instruments to industries. The new initiatives under drugs and 
pharmaceutical research would include: drug development based on traditional systems of 
medicine like Ayurveda, Unani and Sidha besides Homoeopathy and support to joint 
proejcts leading to drug development for infectious diseases. The mission mode would 
include the new areas li\<e agro food processing, waterways, road transportation, electric 
power, telecommunication, aviation, sensors, IPR and future technology forecast for India 
for 2020. 
4. The thrust of the S&T Entrepreneurship Development act1V1t1es will be on 
strengthening and expansion of the entrepreneurship development cells and the S&T 
Entrepreneurship Parks. Besides , ef'forts would be made to initiate rural enterprise 
development through innovative S&T based micro-enterprises in rural areas. 
5. Likewise. the efforts unde r the science communication and popularisation 
programme are aimed at covering mo·e beneficiaries which will include training in S&T 
communication through short-term courses for voluntary organisations, teachers, support to 
popular science magazines in regional languages; production and screening of science films 

on Doordarshan; development of low-cost toys, dissemination of software for S&T 
communication/popularisation etc. The National Resources Data Management System, 
· (NRDMS), it is stated, will concentrate on building specialised problem oriented databases 
and operationalising them for real-life planning and administrative decision making. The 
.activities of the State S&T Councils/Departments are to be intensified so that the States are 
able to take up major projects. Under the International S&T cooperation programme, 
efforts would be made to strengthen bilateral and multi-lateral joint venture programmes and 
to expand and accelerate the pace of S&T cooperation with research institutions in a number 
of countries. The 13 autonomous research institutions, being supported by the DST are to be 
upgraded with state-of-art research facilities in a phased manner. In addition, funds are 
proposed to be provided for improvement of S&T infrastructure in universities and related 
institutions. 
6. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) will further intensify its high technology 
efforts aimed at improving the accuracy of predicting severe weather, its timely warning and 
dissemination of its long range predictions of the monsoon. The major activities proposed in 
this area during the Ninth Plan include: modernisation of the observational and 
telecommunication systems, use of contemporary radar and satellite technology for better 
understanding of weather systems, satellite-based dissemination of timely warning of tropical 
cyclones, monitoring of seismic activities, exploitation of remote sensing data for agro­
meteorological advisories, etc.

IMPROVEMENT OF S&T INFRASTRUCTURE IN UNIVERSITIES AND RELATED 
INSTITUTIONS (FIST) 
17. In recent years concern has been expressed about the Jack of infra structural facilities 
for imparting good quality higher education and conducting advanced research. As a result 
of the ongoing efforts, Departments in the universities and related institutions have made 
some impact on the development of teaching and research. These University departments 
are now in a stage to be identified for selective strengthening of their infrastructure for post­
graduate education and research. It is necessary to extend such infrastructural support to 
other higher technical education institutions and medical education institutions. Considering 
all these factors, a major new programme named Fund for improvement of S&T 
infrastn:rcture in universities and related institutions (FIST) has been thought of. This would 

involve providing basic infrastructure and ~he .enabling facilities and environment for 
promoting R&D in new and emerging areas and attract fresh talent in those areas. Special 
attention needs to be paid to the following:-
• Optimal infrastructure facilities such as, adequate laboratory space; uninterrupted 
electric supply, cold rooms, water supply etc. Modernisation of laboratories for 
teaching and research, Refurnishing/establishing environmentally compatible and safe 
laboratories, that will meet GLP standards, among other things; 
• Acquisition of essential equipment and up-gradation of existing facilities and support 
for maintenance of sophisticated equipment; 
• Ready access to i!lformation systems: Net-working and ready access to computational 
facilities data bases, scientific journals, etc. 
18. The Committee was informed that the objective of this new initiative is to 
strengthen R&D infrastructure in the Academic/Research Institutions. The · programme 
would identify active University/Academy .Departments through a peer review mechanism 
including on-site visits and the funding would be at two levels. The scheme would provide 
basic infrastructure and facilities for promoting R&D in new and emerging fields of S&T 
which would also help in attracting fresh talent in such Departments, 'fPe proposals 
supported · under the programme would be monitored annually or bi-annually through an 
appropriate mechanism. 
19. An Advisory Board of Experts would be set up for assisting the Department in the 
identification and selection of the Departments in Universities/higher Educational 
Institutions for receiving financial support under the scheme and also for its monitoring and 
review from time to time. An empowered Committee will make the final selection of the 
Departments for providing support under . the scheme. 
20. The Committee observes that our universities will have to play a meaningful role as 
providers of high calibre human resources and as repositories of the nation's intellectual 
wealth in the S&T sector. In order to meet these diverse challenges, there is an urgent need 
for strengthening/revampin-g the existing S&T support systems in the universities a~d other 
educational and related institutions; in terms of infrastructure, quantum of funding as well as 
operational structures and mechanisms. . 
21. The Committee is distressed to note that the infrastructure necessary to promote 
scientific .temper is totally non-existent in our institutions of learning be . they schools and 
colleges despite the fact that some of them are charging exorbitant fees· and receive 
undisclosed alTlounts of donations. ·what to speak of the infrastructure, some of these 
institutions do not have even the basic amenities · such aS drinking water and neat and clean 
toilets. There appears to be sheer lack of will on the part of learning institutions which 
appear to have a poor management system. How such i-nsti~utions would promote scientific 
temper. 
22. The Committee feels that the time has come to have a system of incentives which 
could be linked to the provision of facilities as are necessary for promoting congenial 
atmosphere for learning and at the same time promote scientific temper. 
23. The Committee notes with satisfaction the new initiative to strengthen R & D 
infrastructure in the Academic/Research Institutions. The Committee would like to emphasize 
that the cost of imparting the research education should be low and within easy reach of 
trainees/students. If necessary some 'learn and earn' programmes may be devised under 

which trainees may be provided part time employment restricted to certain hours per week to 
support themselves. 
24. The Committee is of the view that an amount of Rs. 75 crores proposed to be spent over 
the next four years for providing assistance to selected Departments of the Universities and 
institutions of higher learning for improving the infrastructure in science and technology sector 
and to improve the standard of research appears inadequate. Taking into consideration the 
number of institutions which would be in need of such attention the Committee recommend s that 
the grant needs to be at least doubled.

53. The Depar tment sought an outlay of Rs. 131.90 crores in the year 1998-99 at BE 
stage with a view to enhance the quality of forecast by upgrading some of the existing 
facilities for moni toring the Weather Forecasts. The allocat ion was redu ced to Rs. 118.74 at 
the RE stage as some fucilities could not materialize during the financial year 1998-99 due to

latest state-of-the-art equipment. 
54. Asked about the degree of precision in our weather forecast s as compared to 
western countries like UK and USA and whether our weather forecasts lack precision as 
compared to those countries, the Comm ittee was informed that the overall percentage 
accuracy of different types of short range weather forecasts i.e. forecast s valid upto 3 days 
including cyclone warn ings issued by IMD is of the order of 80%. Infact , the accuracy of the 
cyclone warnings with respect to its landfall points and associated disastrous weather is even ·· 
more and considered reasonably good. It was further stated that taking into account 

the excellence of cyclone forecasting by IMD, the World Meteorological Organization 
(WMO) has recognised New Delhi as a Regional Special ised Meteorological Centre on 
Tropical Cyclones alongwith Tokyo (Japan) and Miami (U.S.A.) 
55. The Long Range Forecast (LRF) of monsoon rainfall over the country has been 
found quite accurate every year since 1988 onwards when the indigenously developed LRF 
model was introduced by IMD. IMD is constantly striving to develop different LRF models 
for regional basis forecasts for different agroclimatic regions. 
56. UK & USA being extra-tropical countries are having more systematic weather 
phenomena as compared to the countries of tropical belt such as India. The tropical 
weather, which is dominated by meso-scale convective process, is difficult to forecast. Even ' 
in the case of countries like USA. there have been errors in forecasting stom1 tracks. 
Despite these limitations, India has been recognised by the developed coutnries including 
WMO that IMD's forecasting capabilities are one of the best in the world. 
57. In reply to a question whether it was a fact that some of the tragedies resulting from 
cyclones and hail storms etc. particularly in places like coastal areas of Andhra Pradesh 
could have been averted had our weather forecast been precise and timely; the Committee 
was informed that the cyclones are detected as soon as they form in the Indian seas with the 
help of INSA T series of satellites and constantly remain under surveillance till they approach 
Indian Coastline where they fall, in the grip of an overlapping chain of cyclone detection 
radars installed on east and west coasts of the country. No cyclone goes undetected with 
present system of observational netwnrk. 
58: The tragedies do occur sometimes bacause tropical cyclo111.:s particularly those of 
very severe intensity are natural phenomena with ext remely high potential for damage to 
property and Joss of life. The system is being constantly modernised with the latest state-of­
the-art technologies. Every effort is made by the State and Central Government agencies 
responsible for mitigation to reduce loss of life. 
59. Notwithstanding what was stated about the weather forecasts, the Members 
expressed their concern about the degree of precision in our weather forecasts. Besides, as a 
result of emission of gases global warming was attr ibuted to be the cause behind the changes 
in the weather cycle and the phenomenon of unusual rains. 
60. The Committee observes tbat there is need to ensure higher degree of precision in our 
weather forecasts . The budgetary allocations may be utilised to that end. The higher 
allocations may be considered in case the Department comes up with new plans and 
programmes aimed at ensuring higher degree of precision in weather forecast by IMD.
