Action taken by  the Department  
  
            Disposal of vitrified high level wast e in geological formations has been adopted as a third and
final step of Indian strategy for management of radioactive high le vel waste (HLW) in our three step
approach. The first two steps of this strategy are 
  
1.                  Immobilisation of high level waste in solid form with the objective of  volume reduction 
and reducing the possibility of migration of radionuclides. 
2.                  Interim storage for a period of about 30 years to cool the waste a nd ensure integrity o f
conditioned waste package  by continuous surveillance. 
  
Many of  developed countries in  the world are also following the above three step strategy fo r
management of high level waste.  
  
Disposal of vitrified high level wa ste in deep bore holes or the se a bed is not planned due to the
following reasons. 
  
1.                    Disposal in deep bore holes (about 3000 me tres) is being considered basically  fo r 
disposal of spent radiation sources in  countri es where geological dis posal facility is not 
being planned due to either small size of their atomic energy programme or absence of it. 
In this concept, deep bore holes are planne d and waste is proposed to be disposed and 
sealed with no provision of retrieval. 
2.         In disposal in deep sea bed, there is  a large possibility of loss of control of the disposed
waste and, hence, it is not being considered now. 
  
We agree with the recommendation of the Co mmittee and share the vi ew that disposal o f
vitrified high level waste in deep bore-hoes or in the sea bed would be very costly.

Recommendation 
  
            For minimizing the high level nuclear waste volume for pe rmanent storage and eventually
determining what happens to the ma terial when it leaks out of th e tank, the Committ ee feels, it is
necessary to decrease the waste volume by evaporating as much water as possible from the crusty
sludge.  This will make the transport of the waste easier and storage less costly.   
  
(Para58)
Action taken by the Department  
  
The practice of decreasing the volume of high le vel waste by evaporation is being followed in
our  spent fuel reprocessi ng plants as well as in high level waste vitrificati on facilities. Evaporation by
concentration in specially designed thermo-syphon evaporators is an integral part of the basic flow sheet
of all these plants. We are, thus, able to reduce the waste volumes substantially. 
  
As regards the storage of HLW in  the liquid form, this is done in high integrity stainless steel
storage tanks with cooling and instrumentation provisions. Our experience of HLW storage in tanks over
last more than three decades  is excellent from safety point of view with not a single instance of leakage
of any of the tanks. 
  
In order to avoid transportation of HLW, care  has been taken during the plant design by co-
locating reprocessing and vitrification plants. Transfer of waste among reprocessing plant, waste storage
tanks and vitrification plant is done through underground shielded piping system to avoid radiation
exposure to operation personnel. 
  
Thus, aspects of volume reduction of  HLW, its safe storage and tran sfer are an in tegral part o f
the design of our facilities and their safe performance has been demonstrated during last more than three
decades of operations.

Recommendation  
  
            The Committee has been given to understand that  the Universal Extraction, or UNEX, process 
which is being studied in USA by Idaho National Engineering and Environmental Laboratory (INEEL)
engineers which is a subsidiary of US Department of Energy has developed the first demonstrated
technology of its kind capable of removing multiple radio active elements from high level nuclear waste
in one step.  Just a sprinkling of radioactive el ements turns volume of a largely harmless waste into
‘high level radioactive waste’ subject to rigorous and expensive storage standards.   
  
            Such waste is a byproduct of nucle ar energy and weapons developm ent and usually contains a
mixture of intensely radioact ive fission products like Stront ium-90, Cesium-137 and long lived 
radioactive elements like plutonium and Americiam plus hazardous and toxic materials. Separating most
of the radioactive elements  from the other materials can shrink volume of high-leve l waste, reduce the 
total disposal cost and minimise potential harm to the people and the environment surrounding it.  The
Committee is of the opinion that an extractant is required that works in one step and removes radioactive
strontium, cesium and other Actinides at once. 
  
            Using the UNEX process, trimming th e volume of high level waste at least twenty fold, each
gallon of waste shrinks to less than a cup and the twenty fold vol ume reduction leads to a corresponding
reduction in disposal costs.  The ma jority of the waste left over afte r a   UNEX sepa ration is far less
expensive to treat and store than the highly radioactive portion. 
  
            The Committee would like to suggest that this unique UNEX process of squeezing the volume of
enormous nuclear waste to  size less than a cup should be studi ed to depth by DAE and Atomic Energy
Regulatory Board (AERB) so that the benefit of the reduction of disposal cost could be enjoyed by the
Department as an economy measure for treating, managing and storing all types of radioactive waste e.g.
low level, intermediate level and high level nuclear waste etc., and a subsequent  feasibility report could
be submitted before the Committee for perusal and examination, partic ularly keeping in view the cost
effectivity aspect  of the project. 
( Paras 70, 71, 72 & 73)
  
Action taken by the Department 
  
In various countries several solvent extraction process have been developed for the separation o f
long-lived radionuclides, Stront ium-90 and Cesium-137 from acidic radioactive waste solution. These 
processes use different types of solvent employing solvent-extraction equipment of different designs.  
  
Universal Extraction (UNEX) process being studied by Idaho Nati onal Engineering and
Environment Laboratory (INEEL), USA employs a large number of  centrifugal  contactors  in a cascade
manner. In this process,  a universal solvent containing Ch roninated Co balt Di carbollide (ChCoDic) 
with polyethylglycol (PEG) to remove 137-Ce sium and Strontium-90 and C arbamoylmethyl Phosphine 
Oxide (CMPO ) to remove long-lived radionuclides is used. 
  
India has developed extens ive expertise during last  four decades in extr action  of uranium and
plutonium from acidic solutions gene rated during spent fuel reprocessi ng. The solvent used is 30%  tri-
butyl phosphate (TBP) + dodecane  in pulse agitated  columns. Plants  based on this solvent extraction
technology are in o peration at Tromba y, Tara pur and Kal pakkam from 1965 onwards. Presentl y,
