Report
 
India is a land of one of the oldest civilizations of the world with rich cultural heritage, thriving economy and civic life. It has
 been a fountainhead of ancient knowledge and wisdom. The traditions of Science & technology in India are over 5000 years old. The
 resurgence of science in India came as an offshoot of the Nationalist Movement, towards the end of 19th century and the early decades of
 the 20 th century. Since 1947 a renaissance was witnessed with the emergence of India as a politically independent nation and as it
 pursued a programme of employing modern Science and Technology for national development. A few exceptionally talented and
 committed scientists and teachers of that period laid the solid foundation of Indian science by their zeal, dedication ad sacrifice. 
2.         Post independence, conscious efforts were made to enhance and modernize the scientific infrastructure in the country by
 establishing a series of national laboratories, institutes of advanced technical education, universities etc. Science & Technology inputs
 are made an intrinsic part of the planning process and development strategies. Many institutions were created soon after independence to
 promote basic research and technological progress.
3.         As a result of these pragmatic policies, India has turned from a largely agrarian society to a highly industrialized country today.
 Due to systematic exploitation of Science & Technology for socio-economic development and scientific innovations, the country has
 been successfully transformed from a food starving country at the time of independence to a food-surplus nation.
4.         To give direction to the promotion of Science & Technology in the country the Department of Science & Technology was
 established with the primary objective of promotion of new areas of Science and Technology and to play a role of a nodal Department

SEISMOLOGY (MISSION MODE PROJECT)
16.       The Department has a mandate to provide earthquake information to the country.  The India Meteorological Department (IMD)
 which is a part of Department of Science & Technology maintains the National Seismological Network in the country.  During the last
 few years several important projects have been implemented.
17.       The proposal envisages strengthening of seismological network in the Himalayas and its contiguous region (Indo-Gangetic-
Brahamputra plains) with state-of-the-art robust earthquake monitoring systems for fine scale mapping of the earthquake hazard areas and
 ascertaining the seismogenic sources.  The proposal also envisages deployment of strong motion accelerographs and dense network of
 GPS.

                        DRUGS AND PHARMACEUTICALS
                        29.       The Committee was informed that the Department recognizing the profound influence of R&D on the prospects
 and opportunities for the growth of the Indian Drug Industry initiated the scheme on Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Research
 in 1994-95 for promoting Industry-Institutional Collaboration R&D in drugs and pharmaceuticals sector. The programme
 aims at enhancing capabilities of institutions and the Indian Drugs & Pharmaceuticals Industry towards development of
 new drugs in all systems of Medicine.  The specific objectives of this scheme are:
            To synergise the strengths of publicly funded R&D institutions and Indian Pharmaceutical Industry in developing drugs in areas of
 national relevance.
            To create an enabling infrastructure, mechanisms and linkages to facilitate new drug development.
            To stimulate skill development of human resources in R&D for drugs and pharmaceuticals.              
30.       The Committee was informed that during the current year, the Department plans to continue efforts to support collaborative
 research projects leading towards drug development in the area of disease which are of importance to our country like Tuberculosis,
 Leprosy, Malaria, Gastro-intestinal disorders like Diarrhoea, Diabetes, Hypertension, Cardiovascular disorders, Skin disorders like
 Leucoderma, Psoriasis and other prevalent infectious diseases. Besides, development of new drug delivery systems and Veterinary drug
 development a total number of sixteen proposals were considered for financial support during the year. Ten proposals were
 recommended by the Expert Committee for funding including nine Industry-Institutional collaborative research proposals and one
 National facility.
31.       Three new collaborative projects have already been sanctioned after signing of agreements and are related to development of a
 novel herbal ectoparasiticidal product ( a veterinary product), Study of safety and efficacy profile of “Kodiveli”-a siddha medicine in use
 for the management of rheumatoid arthritis and development of a novel controlled/sustained release formula for cardiovascular &
 inflammatory diseases.
32.       Besides, seven proposals are likely to be sanctioned during the year, are related to development of novel TB vaccine candidates,
 Designer Polymers for Tailored Oral Drug Delivery systems, study of molecular mechanism of action of Cardoguard and its efficacy in
 the regression of ventricular hypertrophy, Efficacy and Safety evaluation of a Siddha medicine HIVS-2003 for HIV/AIDS, Development
 of herbo mineral and plant based disease oriented immuno-modulator formulations, increase efficacy of herbal medicines and
 standardization and modernization of ayurvedic/herbal formulations using modern techniques.
33.       The emerging economic, trade and intellectual property scenario, nationally and internationally, necessitates a qualitative change
 in the industry, to drive Indian pharmaceutical industry away from manufacturing only known drugs to discover and commercialize new
 molecules.  The availability of trained scientific manpower of high quality, S&T infrastructure, accumulated knowledge of traditional
 medicinal system and large bio-diversity of our country offer a great opportunity to our industry.  However, at present, most Indian drug
 firms, due to their small size compared to international operators and limited resources, are not in a position to invest in a viable new
 drug development programme.  At the same time, several Indian academic institutions and national laboratories have established
 impressive infrastructure and developed excellent expertise in selected areas of drug development. 
34. The Committee observes that most of the drugs developed or available in markets for diseases like malaria, tuberculosis etc. are no
 longer effective as the disease parasites has become resistance to drugs, and this poses a challenge to the scientific community to develop
 more effective drugs. The Committee recommends that Department to look at the repository our traditional medicine which may have
 answers to many of the challenges.

th Plan, the Department has initiated the Instrument Development programme in mission

            Analytical / Optical instrumentation;
            Medical Instrumentation;
            Industrial Instrumentation; and
            Imaging Techniques and Instrumentation.
