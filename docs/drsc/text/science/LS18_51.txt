REPORT 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and 
Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the report on its behalf, present 
this Fifty-first Report of the Committee. This Report deals with the Demands for Grants 
pertaining to Department of Biotechnology (Ministry of Science & Technology) for the year 
1997-98 (Demand No. 76). 
2. In the meeting of the Committee held on the 15th April, 1997, the representatives of 
the Department of Biotechnology gave an overview of the. various activities of the 
Department and the Committee sought clarification on the various aspects of the functioning 
of the Department of Biotechnology. 
3. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on 8th 
May, 1997. 
4. The world has witnessed spectacular advances in the fields of biotechnology during 
the last 44 years after the discovery of the structure and DNA by Watson and Crick, in 
1953, for which they were awarded Nobel Prize in Medicines and Physiology. The field was 
regarded as the greatest individual enterprise of the humankind. It has revolutionised the 
industrial scenario, especially, the pharmaceutical industry all over the world, particularly in 
technologically advanced countries. Biotechnology is a science which uses the living 
organisms to make or modify a product-to improve plants or animals and microorganisms for 
specific uses. It aims at harnessing the genetic diversity in the living organisms for 
manufacture of useful products and production of strains of microbes, plants and animals 
with improved qualities. 
5. The Department of Biotechnology, estabished in 1986, is expected to give a greater 
impetus to the development of biotechnology in the country. The objectives of the 
Department include development of products and processes and technologies whose large­
scale applications would result in societal and economic benefits. 
The Mandate of the Department of Biotechnology includes:-
• Support R&D and Manufacturing in Biologicals 
• Identify and set up Centres of Excellence for R&D 
• Promote large-scale use of Biotechnology 
• Integrated Programme for Manpower Development 
• Establishment of infrastructural Facilities to support R&D and Production 
• Evolve Biosafety Guidelines 
• Promote University-Industry intemr.tion 
• To serve as Nodal Point for specific International Collaborations 
• Manufacture and application of cell-based vaccines 
• Responsibility of National Institute of Immunology (Nil), National Centre for Cell 
Science (NCCS), Centre for DNA Finger ~rioting and Diagnostics, (CDFPD) and 
International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology (ICGEB)

43. Considering the enormous biological wealth of the country and the biodiversity 
which exists, the application of biotechnological tools as well as enhancing the knowledge on

among the students, teachers and general public of the development and potential 
application of Biotechnology in various sectors. Also posters showing· Biotechnology 
processes and concepts as visual learning aid specially for school children, besides bringing· 
out biotechnology columns in popular science magazines. 
43.2 The scheme of popular lectures by experts was started in institutions and 
universities, which are engaged in Biotechnology Research activities, in order to bring 
general awareness of basic concepts, recent advances and potential application of 
biotechnology among the students of schools, colleges and universities as well as the 
members of the general public. 
43.3 The Department of Biotechnology also participated in exhibition like International 
Crop Science Congress, "Agrotech Exhibition", "lndi-2010" and "Science & Culture Expo" 
to demonstrate various immunodignostic kits, plant raised through tissue culture techniques, 
bio-fertilisers, bio-pesticides. 
43.4 Apart from this, partially/fully supported seminar&lsymposia are conducted by the 
department to bring together young scientists to interact with each other.

Biotechnology, but feels that little is known about the activities of the Department. There is

the targeted sections of the society so that they can make use of its activities and benefit 
from its programmes. The Committee, therefore, observes that there is need to focus in that 
area and the Department should devise ways to communicate its activities in a layman's 
language. 
45. After Considering the Demands for Grants of the department of Biotechnology and 
assessing the progress of various programmes and projects admiriistered by it, the Committee 
recommends that the Demands for Grants of Rs. 113.87 crores as sought by the Department 
of Biotechnology for the year 1997-98, under demand No. 76 may be approved.
