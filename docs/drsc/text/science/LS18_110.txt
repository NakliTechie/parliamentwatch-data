R E P O R T
 
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research on the recommendations contained in the One Hundred Third Report of the Department-related
 Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests on the Demands for Grants (2002-2003) of the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research which was presented to the
 Rajya Sabha on 14
th May, 2002.  There are fourteen recommendations and one observation contained in the One Hundred Third Report of the Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the
 Committee were received from the Department on 20th August, 2002.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the action taken by the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research are set out in the succeeding paragraphs: -
NATIONAL LABORATORIES
Recommendation
            The Committee notes with satisfaction the various activities in which CSIR laboratories are conducting research.  However, the pricing and the terms at which the technology is transferred to industry needs
 to be looked into afresh with a view not only to retain the competitive edge for the industry but it should also generate royalty for the Government.  From the experience gained in respect of ASMON being
 marketed by only one company, which was not able to cope with the demand, the Committee feels that the marketing rights in respect of new drugs entering the market should be with more than one company to
 avoid inconvenience to customers.
(Para 11)
Action Taken By The Department
CSIR has recently revised the guidelines for Technology Transfer and Utilisation of Knowledgebase taking into consideration developments on the IPR, economic and technological fronts.  This would help in
 retaining the competitive edge as well as generate additional royalty. CSIR has noted the observations.  It is being ensured that the new herbal drug will be licensed to reputed concerns who have large scale
 production capacity so that the new drug is available in sufficient quantity throughout the country.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.
 
NATIONAL S&T HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
Recommendation
The Committee observes that in order to encourage talented persons to take up research, they should be encouraged with remunerative schemes and wherever applicable attractive pay scales so that a suitable
 environment congenial to work culture is provided wherein they can sustain their interests in scientific research.  They should also be provided state of art laboratory facilities as this also would prevent them from
 going abroad and thus, reducing brain drain.   The Committee is also of the view that the efforts be made to utilize the services of scientists who have since retired by involving them in projects which were aimed
 at inculcating scientific temper among students and this would help utilize  their talents.  This could be done through re-employment.  Infact the scientists should be allowed to work irrespective of the age of
 retirement, as long as they feel they can meaningfully contribute in the interest of the society and the country.
(Para 14)
Action Taken By The Department
The observation of the Committee has been noted.  CSIR has revised its Recruitment and Assessment policy for its scientific personnel recently which has a provision for providing higher salary  to exceptionally
 outstanding scientist at the time of selection.  CSIR laboratories have state of art facilities built over the years which are available to the scientists for their scientific pursuits.
CSIR presently has a few schemes, to utilise the services of retired scientists viz. Emeritus Scientists Scheme, Bhatnagar Fellows Scheme. In addition there is a provision to engage retired scientists as consultants
 for Business Development. Govt. of India recently have made provision in the rules to utilise the services of eminent scientists upto the age of 64 years. In keeping with spirit of recommendation of the Committee
 CSIR shall make efforts to utilise these provisions as recommended by the Committee.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
 
INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY & TECHNOLOGY MANAGEMENT (IPTM)
Recommendation
The Committee notes with satisfaction the increasing pace of applications for patents and the resultant folio increase.  The Committee would like the Department to simultaneously concentrate its efforts on the
 utilization of these patents by either transferring the technology to the industry or by the Department itself for commercial exploitation before they get outdated.
(Para 17)
Action Taken By The Department
The continuous encouragement and support from the Hon’ble Committee has helped CSIR to occupy the first position in the country in terms of filing of patents.  It has tied up with some of the well-known Indian
 and foreign companies for commercialisation of its technologies.  It would make further efforts to enhance the realisation of value from these licenses.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply with satisfaction and the position achieved by the Department, in filing of patents.  Before transferring the Patents for commercial exploitation the Department should
 disseminate the advantages in order to create more interest among the potential users for the commercial exploitation of patents.

NEW MILLENNIUM INDIAN TECHNOLOGY LEADERSHIP INITIATIVE (NMITLI)
Recommendations
The Committee feels that department should take up such technology projects as were important to the country and strive to bring out the best quality technologies at flexible costs.  Efforts should be made to
 expeditiously complete the project with regular monitoring by competent authorities/panels appointed for the purpose.
(Para 20)
Action Taken By The Department
The concern of the Committee is well founded and will be duly addressed to. Presently under the scheme 15 proof- of- concept projects have been launched involving publicly funded institutions, academia and
 private industries.  A  two tier monitoring mechanism viz Steering Committee and Project specific Monitoring Committee  periodically keeps track of the progress of the projects.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
 
INFRASTRUCTURAL RENOVATION AND REFURBISHMENT
Recommendation
The Committee agrees that the Infrastructure Renovation and Refurbishment is essential in the present day context.  Infact this should have been done much earlier in pursuance of recommendations of the
 Committee in the context of modernization of laboratories.  The Committee has time and again reiterated that progressive modernization or renovation was a continuous process and the Department should also
 ensure that the laboratories that were being taken up for refurbishment should be as per world standards.
However, it is constrained to observe that during 9
th plan an allocation was made for modernization of laboratories and in 10th plan they made an allocation for renovation & refurbishment which included
 strengthening of foundation and revamping of electrical wiring and better communication connectivity.  The Committee observes that both the programmes were similar in nature and being analogous could have
 been taken together.
(Para 23)
Action Taken By The Department
The first phase of modernization as approved by the government on the recommendations of the Hon’ble Parliamentary Committee undertaken during the Ninth Plan largely dealt with Modernisation and
 Upgradation of R&D equipment and facilities only and not with infrastructure renovation. The present emphasis is on the Infrastructure Renovation and Refurbishment (IRR) including IT infrastructure. The IRR
 is being taken-up in a phased manner.  The necessary proposals are being processed for seeking appropriate approvals.
Comments
            The Committee reiterates that IT infrastructure should be upgraded to the world class level as then only our Scientists would be able to have knowledge about the scientific achievements world wide and
 thus compete with prevalent standards.
 
NATIONAL RESEARCH DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION (NRDC)
Recommendation
The Committee observes that the Public Sector Undertakings (PSU) are no longer considered the model employer as they were once considered to be.  They are now hot bed of inefficiency, corruption and lack
 competitive edge.  At the same time in some enterprises the employees are being asked to forego their allowances, being denied of even their legitimate dues like Provident Fund.  The Committee feels this is not a
 good state of affairs so long it tends to deny them of their legitimate dues.  But in the wake of this it has become necessary that the PSUs prepare themselves to the changing scenario, gain optimum efficiency and
 increase their profits.  This may save them from the processes of disinvestments at least for a while. 
(Para 28)
Action Taken By The Department
The comments of the Committee that – “PSUs must prepare themselves to the changing scenario, gain optimum efficiency and increase profits” are taken note of. The public sector undertakings viz. National
 Research Development Corporation (NRDC) and Central Electronics Ltd. (CEL) have taken the following initiatives in this regard :
NRDC is transforming its image from a mere technology transfer organisation to an organisation which provides a wide range of technology related information. It has established  “Intellectual Property Rights
 Consultancy Division” which provides IPR related consultancy, training, etc. It is focusing on export marketing activities of indigenous technologies for increased returns and profits.
CEL proposes to introduce an attractive voluntary retirement scheme to down size its manpower by about 20%. During the tenth five-year plan, CEL plans to focus on solar photovoltaics and strategic electronics to
 improve business, efficiency of operations and profitability.
Comments
            The Committee notes the reply.
AEROSPACE
Recommendation
The Committee commends the efforts made by the Department for its activities on design, development and fabrication of civil aircrafts.  The Committee feels that the technologies must be transferred to industry
 for commercial production as these small aircrafts can be of immense use in remote & hilly areas where construction of large runways is not feasible.  These can also be used by defence establishment to deliver
 food & ammunition to remote hill regions.
The Committee is constrained to observe that the development of Hansa and Saras has been going on for a considerable period.  The Committee on its visit to NAL in September, 2000 was informed that Saras was
 expected to fly in 2001.  However, during the course of presentation before the Committee the Secretary, DSIR has stated that it is scheduled for 2002.  The Committee feels that the project particularly the Saras
 Project has been there for a quite long time and such delays should be avoided as they result in cost overruns in addition to losing technological importance. 
(Para 31)
Action Taken By The Department
Efforts are being made to manufacture Hansa aircrafts through a joint venture. The technology to manufacture the fibre glass airframe of Hansa has already been transferred to a small scale industry.  Four Hansa
 aircrafts under DGCA certification have already been fabricated by NAL in association with M/S Taneja Aerospace & Aviation Ltd. and three of these have been supplied to flying clubs and one to IIT Kanpur for
 joint flight experimental research. 
The work on SARAS project actually commenced in August/September 1999 and the first prototype was to be test flown 18 months from the initiation of the project. During the development process certain
 technological problems were encountered which is common in a project of this complexity.  These problems required new technologies to be developed  which took extra time. Further due to US sanctions many
 components could not be procured from western sources leading to development of alternatives which took some time.  These technological difficulties and procurement problems have now been overcome and all
 efforts are being made to test fly the first prototype by year end.

ECOLOGY & ENVIRONMENT
Recommendation
The Committee appreciates the efforts made by the Department in abating the pollution.  It feels that data generated regarding the various forms of pollution should be supplied to agencies like pollution Control
 Boards so that they can utilize the same in ensuring pollution free environment.  Besides, Industries should also be supplied with newer technologies developed for pollution control, as their acceptance and
 application of these technologies was essential for control of pollution. 
(Para 35)
Action Taken By The Department
It is informed that NEERI is already submitting data on National Air Quality status to the Central Pollution Control Board and Water Quality to CPHEEO and Rajiv Gandhi National Drinking Water Mission.
 NEERI also generates data on air, water, biological, noise, land, socio-economic, cultural and health environments for several developmental project locations as a part of Environmental Impact and risk
 Assessment studies.  The data is provided and reviewed by state Pollution Control Boards and State Environment & Forest Departments. To mitigate the adverse impacts due to industrial developments, NEERI
 provides newer and cost effective technologies for implementation during pre-construction, construction and operational phases of the projects. Through various publications, information are disseminated on
 pollution control and prevention technologies.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the studies being conducted for assessment of pollution and to mitigate the adverse impact due to it. The committee however, feels that by active involvement of voluntary agencies, Non
 governmental organizations etc. the public awareness can be created for reducing various forms of pollution and the acceptance of newer technologies for reduction in pollution.
 
FOOD AND FOOD PROCESING
Recommendation
The Committee reiterates its earlier recommendation that Department should take initiative to impress upon the State Governments concerned to waive sales and trade tax so that they reach the maximum
 population.  The   Committee also feels that that Indian exports especially edible products were not able to compete in international markets due to reasons such as unscientific packing which results in leakage or
 decay of products.  This  could be prevented by applying best packaging methods.
The Committee appreciates the development of enriched snacks.  Efforts should be made to make available these snacks in the schools especially in areas where young students suffer from malnutrition as well as
 under nutrition. 
(Para 39)
Action Taken By The Department
Through discussions and otherwise the CFTRI has impressed upon the state Governments to exempt payment of sales and trade taxes from the clients. It feels that all the State Government may take a uniform
 policy decision in sales & trade exemption to all the small and medium scale industries involved in processing of foods. As an initiative from the Ministry of  Food Processing supported by technical inputs from
 CFTRI, Hon’ble Finance Minister has announced the tax exemption  on process fruits & fruits products last year.
            In view of the competition in International market the fresh agri materials such as mango, letchi, banana etc., which go in bulk packaging, the exporters have adopted the Controlled Atmospheric Packaging
 (CAP) and Modified Atmospheric Packaging (MAP) to enable transportation by ship.  Earlier, the consignments had to be transported by air.  APEDA have also adopted packaging techniques developed by
 CFTRI and are putting these to use in a big way. 
The processed foods, canned, tetrapacked or similar aseptic packaging materials including flexible pouches and products such as masala mixes, pickles and other traditional materials which are normally exported
 in containers such as bottles, polythene covers etc.,  packaging need to be improved.  Assistance in nutritional labeling along with statutory and regulatory declarations is being improved so that product enjoys
 confidence of the importing countries.  A Nodal Codex Laboratories is being established at CFTRI, for introduction of traditional foods into the Codex Alimentarius to increase export potential of small, tiny and
 micro food industries.
CFTRI has developed the enriched snacks and the same are being distributed through schools.  For example, High Protein Upma a ready mix has uniform distribution of required ingredients and characteristic
 flavour, taste of semolina and the added spices.  The product being rich in protein will help in utilizing in the nutrition feeding programme for school children.
Suruchi Meetha is another low cost nutrient supplement.  The product is easy to prepare and has a shelf-life of about 6 months.  Suruchi is ready to eat burfi like product is also enriched with several vitamins as
 well as minerals.  The product contains a combination of cereals and oilseed flours and jaggery.  It has formed as an ideal product for school feeding programmes in the country.In recent years food fortified with
 vitamins and other nutrients have found flavour in various countries and their production has been on the increase.   Such foods are useful as supplements to the diet of children, invalids, convalescents, etc. 
 CFTRI has developed a biscuit,  with rich composition of proteins, vitamins and calcium under the name Nutro Biscuits.  The biscuits are also fortified with thiamine, riboflavin, nicotinic acid, vitamin A and D to
 improve its nutritious value.  Use of Nutro Biscuits in diet therapy for treating cases of nutritional oedima syndrome and malnutrition in children has been found to be effective.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
 
ESTABLISHING GENETICALLY MODIFIED FOOD REFERRAL FACILITY
Recommendation
The Committee is of the view that adequate nutritional and toxicity studies should be conducted before the introduction of genetically modified foods in the market.  All the same it should be left to the
 farmers/users whether to adopt these keeping in view their tastes & preferences.
(Para 41)
Action Taken By The Department
DSIR appreciate the concern of the committee on introduction of genetically modified foods with market.  CSIR, recognizing that large number of Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOS) are in development and
 likely to come in market, is setting up a Genetically Modified Foods Referral Research Facility in the Tenth Plan.  The objective is to set up state-of-art analytical laboratory for testing GM foods and products.
  The detection and quantitation of GMO’s in food materials and processed foods derived there from will be by DNA based methods.  These methods include the isolation of DNA from the food material,
 amplification of the DNA by PCR techniques and then analysis by agarose electrophoresis. Quantitation will be carried out using real time PCR.  Internationally recognized standard GM reference materials will
 be used to validate the methods and over a period of time, these reference materials and probes would emanate from such a facility indigenously.

HEALTH CARE, DRUGS & PHARMACEUTICAL
Recommendation
The Committee is happy to note the success of the herbal drug Asmon, but is constrained to observe that the rights to manufacture this drug had been exclusively given to one company, which could not cope with
 the demand.  The Committee feels that if adequate study of marketing had been done before giving the exclusive rights, the situation as obtaining, could have perhaps been avoided.  Nevertheless, the Committee
 notes with satisfaction that Asmon plus would be launched shortly and the manufacturing/marketing rights would be given to more than one company keeping the interest of the users in view. 
(Para 44)
Action Taken By The Department
IICB has been working on the formulation of a new and highly effective herbal drug for asthma and it is ready for licensing. Keeping in view the larger interest of the users concerned.  It is being ensured that the
 new herbal drug will be licensed to reputed concerns who have large scale production capacity so that the new drug is available in sufficient quantity throughout the country.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.  However, it recommends that so as to ensure the availability of these drugs better marketing techniques be also applied to make it available even in remote parts of India.  The
 Indian Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Ltd., a public sector undertaking, which was reportedly on the verge of closure/disinvestment could be utilised for commercial exploitation of the formulation and marketing of
 the drug if it has not been finally wound up.
HOUSING & CONSTITUTION
Recommendation
The Committee observes that the quality and cost of construction were two important requisites in the field of construction.  It is necessary to develop low cost alternatives which the private sector alone can do. 
 But the technology therefor had to be developed & transferred to that sector by our laboratories/institutions after extensive research.  The laboratories etc. should therefore be geared up to develop cheaper &
 durable alternatives.
(Para 46)
 Action Taken By The Department
The suggestion of the Committee is being implemented.  The Central Building Research Institute in association with eleven other CSIR laboratories is gearing up to develop and transfer alternative cost-effective
 material and construction technologies.  An Inter-lab networked project on Developing New Building Construction Materials during the Tenth Plan has been initiated.  The areas to be addressed by these labs shall
 include inorganic materials, organic materials, process technologies, performance and durability and environmental studies.  It is also informed that CBRI has been in the forefront of R&D in providing cost
 effective and durable alternatives to the private sector, NGO’s Govt. Departments, rural and urban poor etc.  Some of the building materials and construction technologies developed and transferred to the building
 industry are : Quality Clay Bricks, EPS Door Shutter, High Draught Brick Kiln, Vertical Shaft Lime Kiln, Gypsum Calcinator, Precast R.C. plant, Precast Channel Units for flooring/roofing, Thin precast RC lintel
 and sunshades, Under-reamed pile foundation, Fire retardant and water resistant thatch roof, Fire resistant door, and Low cost latrine etc.
Comments
The Committee appreciates the Department for developing these technologies.  However, there is a need for better marketing techniques for propagation and adoption of these techniques.  The Department should
 gear up accordingly.
SCHEME TO ENHANCE THE EFFICACY OF TRANSFER OF TECHNOLOGY (SEETOT)
Recommendation
The Committee feels that the technology, which is transferred to industry, should be in respect of quality products only. 
(Para 51)
Action Taken By The Department
The observation of the Committee regarding technology transfer to industry, only in respect of quality products is very appropriate.  Technologies are transferred to industry generally from universities, national
 laboratories and other research establishments through licensing, transfer of know-how, collaborative technology development between university/R&D laboratory and industry, etc.  Technology development and
 transfer is promoted and supported by the government  under a number of departmental schemes.  While appropriateness and quality of products are essential gradients in technology transfer, department would
 specifically address this issue in future as per the advice of Committee.Comments The Committee notes the reply.
 
NATURAL INFORMATION SYSTEM FOR SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY (NISSAT)
Recommendation
The Department should endeavour to strengthen the library network with the help of latest IT tool & techniques. 
(Para 55)
Action Taken By The Department
The views expressed by the Committee to strengthen the library network with the help of latest IT tools and techniques are well taken.  NISSAT has been promoting the adoption and use of emerging technologies,
 tools and techniques to provide services through library networks.
With the availability of Internet technology, NISSAT has been promoting and supporting development and dissemination of web-based information resources apart from the physical networks.  The library
 networks at Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Mysore, and Pune have established websites for providing web based information access facilities for the dissemination of Indian information on various sectors.  The libraries
 under each network have also developed the databases relating to their own collections and operation and are being hosted on websites.  These networks through the websites provide services like resource sharing,
 flash news bulleting, training programmes, free email consultancy in library automation, website design, bibliographic information, databases of intellectual assets of the city, search facility, city information, etc. 
 These are continuously being updated and efforts will be made to strengthen the delivery system as recommended by the Committee.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
