INTRODUCTION
 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by
 the Commitlee to submit the report on its behalf, present the Tenth report on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the
 Department of Space for the year. 1994-95 (Demand No. 88).
2. The Committee held three meetings on 4,5 and 15. April, 1994. In the meeting held on April 4, while considering the
 Demands for Grants of the Department of Space, the Committee sought detailed clarifications on various projects and
 programmcs initiated by the Department. On its sitting held on April 5, the Secretary of the Department replied to the
 clarifications sought by the Hon'ble Members. The Committee thanks the Secretary of the Department of space for answering
 queries raised by Hon'ble Members and places on record its appreciation for other officers of the Department who provided
 the required material to the Committee in order to enable it to undertake detailed scrutiny  of the Demands for Grants of the
 Department of Space.

REPORT
1. The Indian Space programme is geared to achieve to goal of self-reliant use of space technology for national development
 Its main thrust areas are: (a) satellite communications for various applications: (b) Satellite based radio and television,
 broadcast and networking: (c) Satellite remote sensing for resources survey and management, environmental monitoring and
 meteorological services: and (d) development and operationalisation of indigenous satellite and launch vehicles for providing
 these space services.
2. In his presentation while delineating the salient features of the Indian Space Programme, the Secretary of the Department of
 Space (DOS) assured the Committee that the Department of Space will continue to endeavour to make the best possible use of
 space technology in building a modern India by taking the benefits of space applications to the common man and to the
 remotest comers of the country. The Department has achieved significant success in the development and application of
 Space technology in such vital areas as telecommunications, television broadcast meteorological services including disaster
 warning and in providing information related to agriculture, forestry, water resources and mineral. Besides highlighting the
 achievements of the Department during the year 1993-94, the Secretary of the Department informed the Committee about the
 programmes/projects which the Department intends to undertake during the year 1994-95.
3. After listening to the presentation made by the Secretary of the Department, the Committee examined the budgetary
 allocations made for various programmes failing within the mandate of the Department. While examining the Demands for

7. The Committee sought further clarifications on the budgetary allocations for the various progmmmes/projccts/centres/units,
 etc. The Secretary, Department  of Space gave detailed replies and explained to the Committee that the Department of Space
 follows the concept of Zero Base Budgeting. Every year, very detailed reviews are carried out at different levels in the
 organisation, to arrive at the requirements of funds for each of the programme, scheme, etc. taking into account the progress
 achieved in the past, targets and schedules envisaged for the future and the minimum essential needs of cash flow for the
 current year. This review ensures proper prioritiisation  of the activities. For every project, the annual cashflow requirements
 are worked out based on the total cost and development schedule. In the initial period, the Secretary of the Department
 informed, the expenditure picks up slowly, followed by larger requirements in the subsequent period. The expenditure then
 tapers off towards the end of the project period. The committee was, satisfied by the explanation furnished by the Secretary of
 the Department and was happy to note that none of the programmes/projects of the Department is going to suffer adversely
 and this includes even those programmes for which there have been made comparatively lower allocations for the year 1994-
95.
POLAR SATELLITE LAUNCH VEHICLE (PSLV)
8. The development of PSLV is an important component of the overall space proramme for acquiring indigenous capability for
 launching IRS satellites (100 kg. class) into Polar sun synchronous orbit. The Committee wanted to know whether a sum of
 Rs. 0.91 crores would be sufficient for taking care of the preparations for the next flight of PSLV D2 which is slated to be
 launched in 1994-95. The Secretary of the Department explained to the Committee that out of the sanctioned cost of Rs.
 414.96 crores for this project,          Rs. 409.58 crores had already been spent up to March 1993. He further informed that the
 PSLV D2 systems have already been realised and the launch is expected during 1994. With regard to the continuation of
 PSLV beyond D2 a separate project has been approved for PSLV-D3 at a cost of Rs. 48.00 crores. Rs. 5.00 crores have been
 provided in the budget for 1994-95 under the head PSLV-D3/D4.

9. The first development flight of PSLV took place on September 20, 1993 but could not place the IRS-IE satellite into the
 orbit as the flight became sub-orbital. When the Committee asked the reasons of the failure and expenses involved the
 Secretary of the Department said that the first flight of the PSLV was itself almost a success. He informed that the failure
 analysis committee set up by the Government after reviewing the entire gamut of flight results achieved from PSLV-D1
 launch, has concluded that "all the stages and their sub-systems have performed very well except for the software
 implementation error. The correction for the same, as well as other minor recommendations of the Expert Committee which
 went into the failure of PSLV-D1, have been incorporated. The total cost involved was about Rs. 45.00 crores. The Secretary
 told the Committee that through the successful development of PSLV, India will be for the first time, able to realise a state-of-
the-art satellite launch vehicle which will not only prove commercially viable in the global market but also pave the way for
 the realisations of 60% of the GSLV systems. In view of the details furnished by the Department, the Committee feels that the
 first developmental flight of the PSLV was, in fact, not a total failure as all the major rocket motors worked perfectly and
 practically all the secondary control motors worked very well. Though the mission of injecting IRS-IE into sun-synchronous
 orbit could not be achieved, most of the technologies developed for the PSLV were however, proven. The committee hopes
 that gaining from the findings of the failure analysis committee, the Department would initiate suitable remedial steps so as to
 achieve success in launching its next flight of PSLV for which preparations are already on.
 
Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV)
 
10. The Department is also working for the development, of the GSLV which is capable of launching 2,500 kg. class
 communication satellites into geostationary transfer orbit. About the progress of this project the Committee was informed that
 the work relating to the subsystem configurations and the requirements for the augmentation of the PSLV mobile service
 tower of SHAR are finalised. Also, actions have been initiated for hardware fabrication using Indian industries. The civil
 works at SHAR have already commenced and detailed design reviews of various systems have also been completed. The
 liquid hydrogen production facilities are established and commissioning trials are in progress. A sum Rs. 256.67 crores has
 been spent up to March 1993 towards fabrication, of the subsystem for  S125 Booster stage, L 40 strapons, Liquid Second
 Stage, Avionics, Stage Auxiliary systems, Control Power Plant, Test Facilities and Augmentation of the launch complex,
 including the milestone payments made to Russia for the cryo Engine and stage.
 
Cryo-technology
 
11. The Committee expressed its anxiety over the realisation of cryo technology indigenously and wanted to know about the
 present state of the programme. The Secretary of the Department informed that one of the objectives of the, Indian Space
 Programme is to achieve the capability to launch a satellite into geosynchronous orbit. Achievement of this capability is
 crucial for the propose of maintaining services like telecommunications, weather forecasting and broadcasting. As the country
 aims at launching several INSAT class of satellites in near future, it is necessary to develop our own cost-effective launches.
 For this, acquisition of hardware and  technology pertaining to cryogenic engine is of vital importance. Development of
 cryogenic technology has taken about 10 to 15 years in the developed countries and involves a huge investment.  It was,
 therefore, thought that if the segments containing cryo engine and related technology were obtained from other countries,
 India could acquire greater capability in about 5 years with hardly any risk of failure. This would also avoid delays. In 1986,
 with an allocation of Rs. 16.30 crore project activity started for developing cryo technology for GSLV and a one tonne class
 sub-scale engine using liquid oxygen and gaseous hydrogen was successfully fired. Studies have aim been undertaken in
 various related disciplines such as design of turbo pump, fabrication of milled thrust chamber, etc. In August 1990, the Space
 Commission approved a plan for the development of cryo engines and related technology as part of the GSLV programme
 relying on  indigenous  capabilities. At the  same time,  GLAVKOSMOS  of  the Soviet Union offered the technology to India
 including supply two stages for Rs. 235 crores. This was approved as the offer was within the scope of the originally
 envisaged GSLV project cost. In May 1992, however, USA imposed sanctions on ISRO and GLAVKOSMOS for a period of
 two years. ISRO and GLAVKOSMOS pointed out that the agreement did not come under the purview of MTCR and the use
 of cryogenic upper stage, as part of the GSLV, was only for launching geosynchronous satellites for peaceful purpose. Up to
 October 1993, the agreement with GLAVKOSMOS was progressing satisfactorily, but due to the pressure of US on Russia,
 GLAVKOSMOS invoked the force majaure clause on October 16,1993, as a result of which the transfer of technology and
 training stopped. Further negotiations between the GLAVKOSMOS and ISRO have now resulted in a modified agreement
 approved by both the Governments. Apprising the Committee of the details of the modified contract, the Secretary of the
 Department told that, in addition to the originally planned two cryo stages, two more flight stages and two ground models
 would be supplied in lieu of the technology transfer within the original agreement cost of Rs. 235 crores. ISRO has also
 exercised the option to buy 3 additional cryo stages at a cost of US $ 3 million each as negotiated in the original contract. The
 Committee was also informed that, for this programme, Government has approved an outlay of Rs. 245 crores after taking
 into account the provision already available for GSLV. The first indigenous built cryo stage, as informed by the Secretary of
 the Department, is expected in the year 1998-99. The Committee feels that with the experience and knowledge that the

Groundwater targeting
 
12.       Remote sensing is an important aspect of space technology which has proved useful in many areas such as flood
 monitoring, mapping forest cover and wastelands, drought assessment, groundwater targetting, fisheries forecast, etc. Several
 remote sensing application projects are being carried out by       the Department of Space and the user Departments as part of
 the Remote Sensing Application Mission (RSAM). The Committee evinced keen interest in application projects in the areas
 of groundwater targetting and fisheries forecast. As regards use of remote sensing in water resource management, the
 Committee was told that use of Space based imageries are of great importance in conservation and optimal use of water in
 India.  These are: predication of snow melt runoff; identification of ground water potential both in terms of quantity and
 quality; assessment of causes of pollution of water; sedimentation, etc.  It was also explained to the Committee that by the
 range of information obtained from Space imageries on types of soil, geomorphology, water, and wasteland. Watershed
 characteristics land use, agricultural, meteorological and forestry information, etc. a long-term strategy for land and water
 conservation can be chalked out to combat drought. The Secretary of the Department also informed  the Committee that under
 the Integrated Mission for Sustainable Development (IMSD), pilot studieson land and water resources in some drought prone
 districts were undertaken. Encouraged by the results,        this study was recommended to be extended to additional districts in
 the country which are perennially affected by drought, floods; as also certain other areas which fall within hill and tribal
 regions. These  districts have to be covered under three phases. In Phase-1, 6 districts have been covered for which data have
 already been generated and implementation taken up.* 15 Districts are proposed to be covered in Phase-II for which Action
 Plans am being finalised.** In Phase III, 136 districts are proposed to be covered for which study has been initiated and
 Action Plans would be finalised within a period of 3-4 years. *** The Committee was also informed that
 hydrogeomorphological maps obtained from remote sensing imagery have been utilised for identification of ground water
 potential zones for all the districts in the country on a mapping scale of 1:250,000 under the National Drinking Water Mission
 and these maps were given to the Ministry of Rural Development. For some of the problem districts, large scale maps have
 also been prepared. This activity of Department has led to a great success in identifying ground water. The Committee was
 told that as against the success rate of 40-45% achieved by the traditional method, borewells drilled on the basis of satellite
 data have led to a success rate of 90-95%. It proves the efficacy and commercial viability of the Space based data in this
 regard.While commending the efforts of the Department initiated under the IMSD which cover at present 45% of the
 geographical area of the country, the Committee feels that the Government should make available more funds through public
 finance institutions like banks, insurance companies etc. so that more area can be covered under this programme in view of
 the efficacy and the cost-effectiveness of the technology.
 
Potential Fisheries Zones (PFZs)
 
13. The Committee was informed that data derived from satellite imageries are also utilized to identify Potential Fisheries
 Zones (PFZs). The PFZs charts are prepared from the sea surface temperature data derived from satellite imageries. The PFZ
 information obtained from satellite imageries is, at present, disseminated through the AIR, in the local language, for the
 benefit of fishermen; and it has been found that generally fishermen who make use of this information are able to increase
 their catches by 2 or 3 times. The Department agreed with the Committee that adopting method for identifying the
 phytoplankton concentration would further improve the accuracy of prediction of PFZs. The Department of Space is
 coordinating its efforts with the Department of Ocean Development to develop an ocean colour monitor payload which can be
 incorporated on board the IRS Satellites. This could help in identifying phytoplankton which is the main source of food for
 many types of fishes. The Committee, while appreciating the efforts of the Department in the area of making available
 satellite based data for identifying PFZs, feels that the Department may also evolve a suitable programme to effectively tap
 the potential of space based information for deep-sea fishing. The Committee recommends that the Department should accord
 priority to the designing and developing the ocean colour monitor payload which could be put on board future IRS satellite as
 this would lead to more accurate predictions about PFZs.

Linkages with Industries
 
15. Establishing linkages with industries has been one of the major thrusts of the Department of Space. The Department has
 transferred several technologies to Indian industries. The Committee wanted to know as to what specific actions have been
 initiated to strengthen the linkages with industries. The Secretary of the Department informed that, from the very beginning,
 concerted efforts have been made to involve the Indian industry, both in the private and public sector, in the Space
 programme . There are, at present, about 500 industries, most of them in the private sector, contributing to the space
 programme. Flow of funds from space programme to the industry is to the tune of Rs. 2.50 crores annually. Major space
 hardware for PSLV and GSLV, which includes hardware for the Cryogenic segment have been contracted to industry,
 including those in private sector such as L&T, Walchand Industries, Godrej, NOCIL, Andhra Sugar etc. So far, 216
 technologies/processes developed under the space programme have been transferred to industry. The expertise built up, and
 the infrastructure established, in these industries are now being commercially exploited. The Committee was further told that
 the Department had earned Rs. 15 lakhs in 1991-92, Rs 19 lakhs in 1992-93 and Rs. 25 lakhs in 1993-94 from royalties and
 nominal technology fee from recipients of the spin-off technologies. The Committee notes that though the earnings of the
 Department on this score appear to be meagre, this has, however, got a multiplier effect. The Committee is happy to note that
 the money so earned is being ploughed back for R & D and technology development.
 
Linkages, with Universities
 
16.  Space science research under the Indian Space Programme encompasses a wide spectrum of activities including study of
 cosmic rays, astronomical investigations, using space and ground based systems, study of meteorites and lunar samples and
 physical observations of sun. Another major branch of activity relates to the study of the earth's atmospheric system through
 rockets, balloons and orbiting space systems. When Committee asked if there was any programme for starting post-graduate
 courses in atmospheric sciences in Universities, the Secretary, Department of Space told that apart from what is already
 ongoing, post-graduate courses and research in atmospheric sciences have been taken up by the Gujarat University in
 Ahmedabad, Kerala University in Thiruvanthapurarm and SV university Tirupati, which draw support from the Centres of the
 Department of Space located at these places. About 100 universities participated actively in the workshop on atmospheric
 sciences, organised at Tirupati recently, as part of the utilisation programme of MST (Mesosphere-Stratosphere-
Troposlghere) Radar. The Committee was further informed that under the sponsored Research Programme-- (RESPOND) the
 Department of Space has been promoting research projects in space technology/science/applications since 1976. So far, more
 than 250 projects have been funded in 75 Universities from all parts of the country. Three space technology cells have already
 been established at Bangalore, Madras and Bombay for ensuring advanced research in some of the critical areas. Efforts have
 been made to establish space technology cells in IIT, Kharagpur and BIT, Ranchi. Space Science Education Centre has been
 established at Ahmedabad. The Punjab State Remote Sensing Centre has been established as part of the Punjab agricultural
 University. Under the scheme for promoting remote sensing technology in educational curricula, both at college and school
 level, action has been taken jointly with UGC and NCERT. Under the IRO Geosphere-Biosphere programme, Universities are
 being encouraged to work in several related research areas. Jadavpur University has been involved in some Space science and
 technology (including Onboard processor development) areas. The Committee, while appreciating the efforts of the
 Department in promoting academic linkages, emphasises that there is a need to cover more universities at least by starting
 post-graduate courses in space/atmospheric sciences with a view to preparing more and more scientists who could assist in the
 area of space R & D.

GRAMSAT
 
GRAMSAT is a satellite system which intends to provide useful information to the people particularly in the rural
 areas by broadcasting development oriented programmes in the field of education, rural development, health, etc. It is
 envisaged under this programme to provide 6 to 8 TV channels for this purpose. The Committee noted that the department
 could utilize a sum of Rs. 10 lakhs  which was only one-tenth of the total amount provided in the budgetary outlay for this
 programme for the year 1993-94. For the year 1994-95 budget estimates for this programme are kept again at Rs. 1.00 crore.
 The Committee wanted to know as to how the Department proposes to utilise the funds provided for this project.  The
 Secretary, Department of Space informed that since the GRAMSAT project is yet to be defined, and formally sanctioned by
 the Government, the budgetary provision is only notional. While it is possible to define the satellite now, it is to be ensured
 that the user ministries (Rural Development, Human Resources Development, Information & Broadcasting, Health etc.) are
 geared up with facilities and software on the ground for its utilisation. Efforts are under way in this regard. The Committee is
 of the view that the GRAMSAT project can play a meaningful role in the lives of our people, particularly those living in the
 remote and rural areas. The Committee, therefore, would like the Department of Space to give full information about the
 potential of this project to the Ministries/Departments (both at the Centre and the States), which are concerned with
 socio­economic development. Such a contact would enable the Department to develop proper formats for the utilisation of
 the GRAMSAT system, when it is implemented, hopefully soon.
 
Second launch pad
 
18. The committee noted that Rs. 3.00 crores provided for the development of second launch pad and common facilities at
 Sriharikota for PSLV and GSLV launches could not be utilised during the year 1993-94. Budgetary outlay for the purpose for
 the yea
r 1994-95 has been kept at Rs. 2.00 crores. The Committee enquired about the non-utilisation of the amount sanctioned
 during the year 1993-94 for developing a second launch pad. The Committee was informed that to meet the immediate
 requirement of the GSLV, the Department had decided to augment the existing PSLV launch pad by adding the Cryogenic
 fluid handling facility. However, in view of the increasing number of launches per year, the need to have a second launch pad
 cannot be ignored. The Committee, therefore, strongly feels that due priority should be accorded to the development of the
 second launch pad at SHAR.
 
Housing for staff
 
19. The Committee asked the Department as to why the percentage of satisfaction of housing for scientific and technical staff
 was only 29.8% and why the funds provided for this purpose for the year 1993-94 could not be utilised fully. The Secretary,
 Department of Space informed the Committee that the aim of the Department at present is to provide at least 50% satisfaction
 for its staff. The funds provided, however, could not be utilised fully because longer time was taken in planning the activity in
 procuring the necessary statutory clearances. The Committee is of the view that providing accommodation near the work
 place attracts high quality talents and increases the efficiency of personnel working in any organization. The Committee,
 therefore, feels that the Department should make earnest efforts to achieve the target of providing 50% satisfaction for its
 employee within a reasonable period of time.
