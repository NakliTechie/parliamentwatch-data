REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Space on
the recommendations contained in the One Hundred Eighty-ninth Report of the Department-Related
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the
Demands for Grants (2008-2009) of the Department of Space, which was presented to both the
Houses of Parliament on 29th April, 2008. The One Hundred Eighty-ninth Report of the Committee
contained fifteen recommendations. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee
were received from the Department on 29th September, 2008.
The Committee’s recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee
on the Action Taken by the Department of Space are set out in the succeeding paragraphs:—
ELEVENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN (2007-12) OUTLAYS AND PROPOSALS
1. Recommendation (Para - 4.3)
The Committee hopes that the major thrust of the Indian Space Programme during the
Eleventh Five Year Plan 2007-12 would not only be towards continuing and reinforcing the space
technologies already developed but also to venture into newer and greater space challenges by
further enhancing our capabilities in the advanced areas of space technology. Efforts should also
be made towards exploring the commercial prospects in the field of space science specially in the
area of Launch Vehicle Development, so that additional funds could be generated. The Committee
also hopes that the Department would achieve the goals set for itself during the 11th plan period
in the field of space applications for communication, broadcasting, remote sensing, launch vehicle
technology etc. which will ultimately benefit the society at large.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. The Eleventh Five Year Plan, besides strengthening the already established operational
space capabilities, also envisage development of advanced communication technologies, Microwave
Remote Sensing, advanced propulsion systems, Human Space Flight and planetary exploration
which are intended to enhance India’s space technology capabilities in advanced areas Expansion
of Space Commerce in the areas of launch services, Transponder capacity leasing Remote Sensing
Data Services, spacecraft hardware and other value added services are also planned. The
Department will thrive to achieve the targets set for Eleventh Five Year Plan.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and appreciates the efforts undertaken by the
Department to enhance Indian Space technology capabilities. It also hopes that the
Department would make every effort to achieve the targets set for the Eleventh Five Year
Plan as per schedule.
BUDGETARY ALLOCATION
2. Recommendation (Para - 5.3)
The Committee notes that the Plan allocation (2008-09), which was sought by the
Department has seen a drastic reduction of Rs.862.18 crores and the same has been effected at

2
the cost of some of the new but important schemes. A similar cut had to be made at the RE stage
in 2007-08 due to resource constraints. The Committee expresses its deep anguish over the fact
that even scientific departments are not spared of the routine reduction on the ground of resource
constraint and feels that this is not a healthy trend. This also shows the degree of seriousness and
significance that Government attaches to the scientific bodies and institutions which are being put
at par with other Ministries/Departments of the Government of India. The Committee, therefore,
recommends that the formula of routine cut should not be applied to scientific departments.
3. Recommendation (Para - 5.4)
The Committee also expresses its concern over the cuts in Plan allocations to the
Department and recommends that the Planning Commission should thoroughly examine the
proposals, before imposing cuts, as the Indian Space Programme is vital to meet the developmental
needs of the country in a self reliant manner.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. The recommendations of the Committee will be conveyed to the Planning
Commission during Performance review Meeting.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and urges the Department to ensure that due
cognizance of the recommendations made by the Committee in this regard is taken at R.E.
stage for releasing required budgetary support.
MONITORING MECHANISMS FOR ONGOING SCHEMES
4. Recommendation (Para - 6.4)
The Committee recommends the strengthening of the existing mechanism developed by the
Department to monitor the progress of the ongoing schemes before finalizing proposals for
budgetary support, so that a clear picture regarding fund requirements can be presented to the
Planning Commission and the possibility of shortage or surrender of funds at a later stage could
be avoided.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted for compliance.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and reiterates that the Department must strengthen
its monitoring mechanism and should monitor its ongoing programmes/projects for its
implementation in a time bound manner.
VILLAGE RESOURCE CENTRES
5. Recommendation (Para - 7.4)
The Committee is of the opinion that VRC is a tool which can be used for strengthening
and uniting the country in addition to its main objectives of providing information regarding various
rural services. The Committee, therefore, recommends that efforts should be made to establish

3
VRCs in the far-flung, inaccessible, remote border areas of the country, to help confidence-building
among the population and remove the feeling of alienation from the country.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. As on date 417 Village Resource Centres (VRCs) are set up in different States and
Union Territories of the country, in association with selected partner agencies viz., NGOs, Trusts,
Academic institutions and Government. These include 13 VRCs in Assam, 1 in Meghalaya, 8 in
Nagaland, 17 in Sikkim and 4 in Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Further expansion of VRCs is in
progress.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and hopes that sustained efforts would continue to
be made by the Department to set up more Village Resource Centres in different States and
Union Territories.
6. Recommendation (Para - 7.5)
The Committee is of the opinion that to make VRC programme a success, there should be
proper co-ordination among all the agencies connected with the programme. In this direction the
Committee recommends that local bodies across the country, district offices and other agencies
involved in planning of the VRC programmes at the rural areas should be inter-connected resulting
into dissemination of vital information.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. The VRCs set up by Department of Space are primarily managed and operated by
NGOs, Trusts, V oluntary Agencies and Government Agencies who organize necessary linkages
among various players including local bodies connected with the programme and services.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
7. Recommendation (Para - 7.6)
The Committee also recommends that special focus should be given to issues related to
self-employment and forming self-help groups through them. The Committee further recommends
that the information should be disseminated in local language so that the local youths and people
could attach themselves fully with the programme.
Action Taken by the Government
The variety of activities carried out by the VRCs encompass management of land and water
resources; agriculture development; supplementary education, training and skill development for
livelihood support; healthcare and social empowerment, including forming self-help groups. The
interactive programmes carried out by VRCs and dissemination of information by them are
necessarily in local languages.
Comments
The Committee while taking note of the reply is of the view that while establishing
Village Resource Centres, more representation should be given to the remote areas.

4
DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPPORT SYSTEM
8. Recommendation (Para - 8.3)
The Committee is aware that a lot of economic devastation and human loss is caused
regularly by the floods, cyclones, storms, etc., across the country. In this direction the Committee
recommends that there should be proper co-ordination among the various agencies involved in the
DMS programme so that there is quick dissemination of information to the District Headquarters
after the data is obtained from the satellites, in order that human and material losses can be
minimized.
Action Taken by the Government
The Disaster Management Support (DMS) Programme of the Department is conceived and
developed in close coordination with the concerned Central and State Ministries/Departments and
District Authorities, such as, Ministry of Home Affairs; National Disaster Management Authority;
National Institute of Disaster Management; Department of Agriculture and Cooperation; India
Meteorological Department; Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services of Ministry of
Earth Sciences; Central Water Commission; and District Authorities. All the disaster management
support related data and information generated using satellite and aerial survey data by the
Department are disseminated to these agencies on near real-time basis, through an exclusive
Decision Support Centre set up for the purpose at NRSC, Hyderabad.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
TELEMEDICINE
9. Recommendation (Para - 9.3)
The Committee is of the opinion that investment in a highly capital intensive area like Public
Health may not be always possible, particularly in the backward areas of the country. Telemedicine
provides an excellent alternative. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the telemedicine
network should be expanded further to include the disease-prone areas which have been left out
so far, specially in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. The Department should make concerted efforts
towards solving the procedural, problem through consistent persuasion and liaisoning with the State
administration.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. Expansion of Tele-medicine program is progressing well and Telemedicine facilities
have been established in 310 hospitals as on August, 2008. Telemedicine facilities in North East
(28) and Orissa (9) cover the disease-prone areas also. Discussions have been initiated with the
State Governments of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar for introduction of Telemedicine systems.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and would like the Department to continue its efforts
towards establishing telemedicine network in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar as both these States
are disease prone and require better and suitable healthcare facilities. The Committee would
also like to be informed about the progress of discussion in the matter.

CHANDRAYAAN-I
10. Recommendation (Para - l0.4)
The Committee hopes that the Chandrayaan-I mission progress as per schedule and the
spacecraft would be ready for launch in the second quarter of 2008. The Committee is of the
opinion that the Chandrayaan-I mission would upgrade India’s space technological capabilities and
in future help in launching manned missions to moon.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. The integration and checkout activities of Chandrayaan-I spacecraft have been
completed. The spacecraft is undergoing environmental testing and then onwards, it will be
transported to launch site at Sriharikota for launch onboard PSLV-C11. The launch campaign
activities for PSLV-C11 is also in advanced stages.
Comments
The Committee expresses its satisfaction that Chandrayaan-I mission is progressing
as per schedule and hopes that it would improve Indian space technological abilities once
it has been launched successfully.
HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
11. Recommendation (Para - 11.3)
The Committee expresses its serious concern at the fact that the present remuneration being
paid to the scientists are not attractive enough to attract the best of scientific talent available in
the country. Further, to make matters worse, there is a high level of attrition of scientists from
the Department, who move towards the private sector for better salaries and promotion prospects.
The Committee recommends that the case for grant of special allowance over and above the normal
salaries may be taken up emphatically with the Government, keeping in view the special
requirements of the Department, and the scientific community at large, specially in view of the
tremendous contributions of the scientists in the field of Space. The Committee also recommends
that in order to retain the scientists in the Department enhanced increment at the rate of 25 to 30%
may also be seriously considered for the scientists who perform and deliver.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. The matter has been referred to the Government.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
12. Recommendation (Para - 11.4)
The Committee is of the opinion that to contain the mass exodus of scientists from our
premier institutions, formulation of a wage policy has become imperative, keeping in view the huge
difference that exists between the Public and Private sectors. The Committee recommends that
efforts must be made to work out an understanding on wages on the lines of the one already
existing in the advanced countries.
Action Taken by the Government
Extract sent to Ministry of Labour.

6
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
13. Recommendation (Para - 11.8)
The Committee is of the opinion that the Department has delayed the creation of a
specialised manpower base for its growing space programme as the only specialised Institute
catering specifically for space technology has been set up just a year ago, though the Department
was set up in the year 1972. However, though delayed, it is a welcome step. The Committee feels
that the Department should be pro-active in initiating such projects.
14. Recommendation (Para - 11.9)
The Committee is also of the opinion that the possibility of introduction of Space Science
as a subject at the graduate/higher secondary level may be explored to generate the interest of the
younger generation in this field and more such institutes should be set up by the Department across
the country to train scientists/engineers, to create a manpower base of dedicated scientists.
Action Taken by the Government
Noted. At the higher secondary level, the subject related to Astronomy , Astrophysics and
Space Science have already been introduced in 10th, 11th and 12th standards in CBSE. Some of
the Universities are also running MSc. Programme with specialization in Atmospheric and Space
Science. Further, as a part of space science promotion, the National Advisory Committee on Space
Science (ADCOS) has taken steps to strengthen space science education in ten selected universities
as Phase-l across the country by initiating space science research activities, offering Fellowships
to MSc. and M. Tech. programs in Atmosphere and Space Sciences and setting up experimental
facilities in space science research such as Automatic Weather Stations, Microwave radiometer,
GPS systems, spectral radiometers etc.
Comments
The Committee expresses its satisfaction on the initiatives taken by the Department
to strengthen space science education under the supervision of the National Advisory
Committee on Space Science (ADCOS). The Committee desires that Department should
further enhance its potential in the field of space science education and expand its activities
in various other universities apart from selected ones.
