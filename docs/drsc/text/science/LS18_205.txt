REPORT 
 
The Department of Biotechnology, was set up as an Independent Board in 1982 
and became a full fledged Department under Ministry of Science & Technology in 1986. 
The vision of the Department inter-alia includes creation of biotechnology tools and 
technologies that address the problems of agriculture productivity, food production, 
nutrition security, health care and environmenta l sustainability affecting the large section 
of the society, providing  new and emerging pr oducts and services at affordable prices, 
generating employment opportunities and ma king India globally competitive in the 
emerging bio-economy. The Department seeks to achieve its vision by facilitating 
emergence of a system that promotes ex cellence and sustains innovation through 
implementation of novel schemes/programmes and institutional mechanisms. 
2. The main mandate of the De partment of Biotechnology is: 
To evolve policies and integrated progr ammes in biotechnology and ensure their 
implementation and monitoring.  
(i) To identify specific programmes of Research and Development and 
manufacturing in biologicals and biotechnology and oversee the initiation and 
pursuit of related research and manufacturing activities.  
(ii) To identify, set up and support Centres of Excellence for Research and 
Development in biotechnology and ensure pr oper dovetailing of their activities as 
per national priorities and objectives.  
(iii)To act as a screening, advising and approving agent of the Government with 
regard to import and transfer of ne w technologies for the manufacture of 
biologicals, biotechnological products and their intermediates.  
(iv) Evolve safety guidelines for biotechnology Research and Development and 
manufacturing in India.  
(v) To act as the central agency for the impor t of genetically manipulated materials, 
culture, cells, specimens, tissues and biotech products including DNA and RNA 
of any type or size and for promoting their production in the country 
(vi) Serve as the inter-ministerial and in teragency nodal point for all specific 
international bilateral and multilateral Research and Development collaborations 
and agreements in the area of biotechnology; act as the nodal point for all 
technology transfers in the area of biotechnology.  
(vii) Manufacture and ensure application of  recombinant, cell-based and DNA 
vaccines, diagnostics and other biotechnological products.  
(viii) To evolve programmes for Human Res ource Development in the area of 
biotechnology.  
(ix) Serve as an administrative and impl ementing Department of agencies, 
commissions, boards, etc. specifically formed by the Government for fulfilling the 
national objectives in biotechnology and also to serve as the nodal point for Bio-
informatics including training and cr eation of infrastr ucture, collection, 
dissemination and exchange of information relating to biotechnology.  

 
(x) Matters relating to following institutions:

3. MECHANISM TO MONITOR AND REVIEW THE ONGOING SCHEMES  
3.1 In response to a query from the Committee regarding the mechanisms of the 
Department to monitor and review the progress of its ongoing schemes before 
formulating/finalizing proposals for budge tary support, the Department informed 
that the progress of ongoing schemes /projects are monitored and reviewed biannually or 
annually by 20 subject specific Task Forces and high level expert committees besides site 
visits and annual reports. The Department also  has a mechanism for internal assessment 
of schemes/projects implemented vis-a-vis 11th Five Year Plan proposal and overall goals 
and new initiatives are undertaken within the scope of overall  goals. Proposals for 
budgetary support are finalized on the recomme ndations of the Task Forces/expert group 
and internal assessment beside s considering current international trends and national 
needs in different sectors in terms of R&D, technology development and institutional 
requirements.  
4. BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS AND FOCUS AREAS FOR THE 
ELEVENTH FIVE YEAR PLAN PERIOD 
4.1 The total budgetary allocation made for the Department for the 11th Five Year 
Plan is RS.6389.00 crores. The 11 th plan proposal takes stock of two decades of the 
activities of the Department and provides a framework for the future within which 
strategies and specific actions to promot e biotechnology can be taken. It focuses on 
cross-cutting issues such as human resour ce development with skills for tasks from 
discovery to markets; schemes re-entry of our scientists abroad; promotion of R&D in 

small and medium size companies; newer ways of collaborating with large companies; 
establishing centres of excellence with partnership with hospitals, industry and other user 
agencies; remodeling life science departments in universities/institutions; establishment 
of technology management centres; promoting dynamic biotech regional clusters; 
interdisciplinary networks; academic and industry interface; infrastructure development 
in terms of laboratories for services a nd manufacturing; expansion of existing 
autonomous institutions for translational re search; establishment of new institutions, 
technology platforms, biotechnology parks and incubators; setting up of biotechnology 
regulatory authority; and public communication and participation.  
4.2 The 11
th Five Year Plan also aims to chalk out the path of progress in sectors such 
as agriculture and food biotechnology, industrial biotechno logy, medical biotechnology, 
bio-engineering, nanobiotechnology, biofuels and bioener gy, IT enabled biotechnology, 
bioresources conservation and bioprospect ing, environment resilent technologies; 
intellectual property & patent law, international cooperation. The schemes and 
biotechnology interventions during 11th Five Year Plan are exp ected to provide tools and 
technologies that address the problems of agriculture productivity, food production, 
nutrition security, health car e and environmental sustaina bility by providing new and 
emerging products and services at affordable  prices, generate employment opportunities 
and make India globally competitive in the emerging bio-economy.  
4.3 While the Committee appreciates the ab ove goals set by the Department for 
the 11th Five Year Plan 2007-2012, it note s with concern that the trend in 
expenditure incurred so far during the 11 t h     Plan period is not very encouraging.  
During the last three financial years, i.e., 2007-08, 2008-09, 2009-10  around 43 per 
cent of the total budgetary allocation has been spent by the end of March, 2010.  
Thus, the Department will have to spend the remaining 57 per cent of the total 11 th 
Five Year Plan allocation in the next  two years.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that there must not be any laxi ty in utilizing resources allocated to it 
and the trend in utilization of resources should be uniform and consistent during 
the entire Plan period.

5.5 When the Committee enquired from the Depa rtment as to why the total budgetary 
allocation of Rs. 1024.00 crores at the B.E. le vel was reduced to Rs. 925.90 crores at the 
R.E. stage last year and also that why a similar trends of reduction at R.E. stage are found 
under some other programmes, such as S upport to Autonomous R&D Institutions, 
Research and Development, Grand Challenge  Programme for Promotion of Excellence 
and Innovation, etc.  and whether such a reducti on is due to underutilization of budgetary 
allocations provided for those programmes or  it is due to some other reasons.  The 
Department replied that even when it was ab le to incur an expenditure of more than 54 
per cent of B.E. allocations as against the ta rget of 50 per cent, th e Ministry of Finance 

exercised a mandatory cut of 10 per cent of B. E. outlay resulting in reduced allocation at 
R.E. stage.   The same reason holds good for the reduction of funds at R.E. stage for other 
programmes.  Hence the reduction at the R.E.  level is due to a policy decision taken by 
the Ministry of Finance and not  because of under utilization of funds by the Department.  
The Committee expresses its dismay over the fact that despite the track record of 
utilization being as high as 98.55 per cent, 90.15 per cent and 99.82 per cent during 
the last three years respectivel y, the Department has to face mandatory cut off       
10 per cent of B.E. under most of the pr ogrammes.  The Committee is of the view 
that such routine cuts should not be effected in scientific Departments as it 
adversely affects not only research and development, but also scientific and 
technical advancement of the nation.  Th e Committee, therefore,  recommends that 
the Department of Biotechnology should be exempted from such routine cuts and 
the entire allocation as requested by  the Departme nt for the year 2010-11 at B.E. 
stage should be released at the R.E. level also.  The Committee also recommends 
that the Department should vigorously take up this matter with the Planning 
Commission so as to ensure that no routine reduction is resorted to at the R.E. level. 
5.6 The Committee expresses its serious con cern over the drastic cut in the Plan 
allocation for the Department.  Even in ca se of allocation for priority areas where 
realistic projections were made by the De partment, there has been a cut which has 
forced the Department to reprioritize and reassess the expenditure on new 
initiatives.  The Committee is of the view th at unless there is substantial increase  in 
the allocation, the Department will not be able to translate their dream of making 
India to take a giant leap in innovation and research because growth is closely 
linked with investments made.   
5.7 As regards the minimum requirem ent of Rs.200.00 crores more for the 
research & development, public-private partn ership and infrastructure sectors; the 
Committee suggests that the department shou ld take up the issue emphatically with 
the Planning Commission and  give a compre hensive note  providing cogent reasons 
and details of the funds required under each sector. 
6. The Committee fails to understand the ra tionale behind not fulfilling the projected 
demand of the Department, especially, when the Committee takes note of the 
achievements made by the Department in the 10 th Five Year Plan.  During the 10th Five 
Year Plan period, biotechnology industry has been growing @ 40 per cent and in 2006-07 
exceeded $2.0 billion annual turnover.  The social impact of biotechnology has also been 
substantial as is evident from performance in vaccines, diagnostics, insect resistant 
cotton, medical devices and a number of bi o therapeutics in the Indian market and 
exported to many countries.  Th e 10th Plan period investme nt has been very productive 
and as informed by the Department the cost Vs. total pooled output is about 1:7 and 
productivity per scientist funded Vs . total pooled output is about 1:6.   The Committee 
feels that as against the backdrop of such a brilliant performance by the 
Department of Biotechnology it is sheer injustice with the Department if  Finance 
Ministry fails to provide total budgetary support at le ast for strategic programmes 
being undertaken by the Department. The Committee, therefore, strongly 
recommends that brilliantly performing  Department should be rewarded and 
encouraged by providing total budgetar y support as per their demand.  The 

Committee, therefore, also recommends that  the financial requirements of the 
Department must be met by the Ministry of Finance at the R.E. level.   
7. SUPPORT  TO  AUTONOMOUS  R& D  INSTITUTIONS 
7.1 The Autonomous Institutions have b een established by the Department to 
demonstrate the increasing emphasis on research and process-product development. 
These institutions generate a new syner gy towards capturing research leads and 
converting them into products. Presently there are 14 existing Autonomous institutions 
and 6 new institutions have been proposed. 
7.2 When the Committee enquired about th e increase  in allocation made 
under the programme of 'Support to Autonomous R&D Institutions' from 
Rs.281.10 crores at B.E. stage in th e year 2009-2010 to Rs.330.10 crores this 
year; the Department informed that  this is due to implementation of action plan of 
six new institutions recently approved by the Government in terms of construction, 
procurement of equipments, appointment of manpower, establishment of interim 
facilities etc.  
7.3 The Committee in its 190
th report on the Demand for Grants (2008-2009) 
had strongly recommended that the Department should draw a reasonable time 
frame for establishing new autonomous in stitutions and devise a mechanism to 
monitor their progress periodically so as to avoid time and cost overruns.  However 
the Committee is constrained to note that th e six new institutions are yet be to set 
up.  The Committee expresses its dissatisfaction at the undue delay in the obtaining 
of the necessary Government approvals and reiterates its recommendation for 
expediting the setting up of the proposed si x institutions, so that they could  be 
completed as per schedule and the new institutions could start functioning at the 
earliest and become centres of excellence.  
7.4. The Committee also recommends that  the Department should focus on 
research and development in bio energy a nd bio fuels, partic ularly, the green 
sources of energy which is badly required today in view of the climate change & 
global warming.  The network programme on biobutanol and green diesel should, 
therefore, be launched at the earliest, so as  to expedite the early breakthrough in 
this regard.

10.    BIOTECHNOLOGY FOR SOCIETAL DEVELOPMENT  
10.1 The programme aims at demonstration of proven biotechnological techniques for 
application to be benefit of SC/ST population, women and rural areas for training to skill 
development, self employment and income generation. The projects are implemented by 
the self help group, NGOs in partnership with  local universities/in stitutions and Krishi 
Vigyan Kendras. About 200 projects are ongoing annually benefitting about 10000 
people. The areas in which activities are undertaken currently  include mushroom 
cultivation, production of fruit and vegetable,  use of biofertilisers and biopesticides, 
utilization of local biores ources for example lantana as  a substitute for bamboo, 
enhancement of agricultural productiv ity, aquaculture, integrated farming, 
vermicomposting, goat farming, quails farming, health care, organic farming, sustainable 
animal husbandry practices, off-season ve getables cultivation, pulse cultivation, 
cultivation of medicinal & aromatic plants etc.  
 

10.2 The Committee was informed that "Biotechnology for Societal Development" has 
been allocated Rs.10.00 crores at B.E. and Rs.9.40 crores at R.E. in the year 2009-2010. 
This year the allocation has b een raised to Rs.13.00 crores. The Department clarified that 
allocation has been increased during 2010- 2011 due to commitments in the ongoing 
projects and for setting up a public communi cation system for biotechnology involving 
State Agricultural Universities focusing on agriculture biotechnologies.  
10.3 The Committee is of the opinion that the benefits of science and technology 
should percolate to the lowest strata of  society and help in upliftment of rural 
masses and women. The Committee is also of the view that popularization of Proven 
and field tested technologies will help the target rural population in their skill 
development, employment and income ge neration in the field of agriculture, 
sericulture, production and manufacture of biopesticides and biofertilisers. In 
health sector too, such projects result in creating awareness on nutrition including 
traditional food and healthcare. The Committee therefore recommends that such 
programmes which involve local people through self help groups and result in 
overall societal benefit should be encourage d by the Department  and efforts should 
be made to ensure that bene fits of such programmes reach the various parts of the 
country. 
11. GRAND CHALLENGE PROGRAMMES 
11.1 The objective of the Grand Challenge Programme is to support biotechnology 
interventions which can bring about significant value addition, cost effectiveness and 
competitiveness in products and processes. Seven such programmes have been launched 
in the areas of Microbial pr ospecting for genes and molecu les, Vaccine Grand Challenge 
Programme, Food Science and Nutrition, A ccelerated Molecular Breeding, Biodesign, 
Gemomics and Bioenergy and Biofuels. The overall objective of Vaccine Grand 
Challenge is to accelerate development of candi date vaccine such as Rotavirus, Cholera, 
Typhoid, Rabies, Anthrax, Malaria, dengue, T uberculosis and Japanese Encephalitis. 
Microbial prospecting involves discovery of genes and molecules in drug development for 
anti-cancer, diabetics, inflammation and infection with participation of 12 institutions and 
screening at private sector facility.  
11.2 While apprising the Committee of th e upto date actual achievements the 
department stated that Micobial prospecti ng programme for genes and molecules in drug 
discovery & development and Bio-fortifi cation programmes of wheat, rice and maize 
using molecular breeding has already been started, while 4 Universiy/IIT departments 
have been supported for starting integrated food science and nutrition research.  
11.3 The department also informed the Co mmittee that the allocation for "Grand 
Challenge Programme" was substa ntially increased from Rs.38.00 crores at R.E. stage in 
the year 2009-2010 to Rs.54.00 crores in B.E 2010-2011. The Department also informed 
that the enhancement in 2010-2011 is due to ongoing commitments in 5 grand challenge 
programme on microbial prospecting, vaccines, biodesign, food science and nutrition and 
genomics. Further, as a follow-up of the midterm evaluation by the Planning 
Commission, a major challenge programme na mely crop breeding by design shall be 
implemented as mega project through settin g up of molecular breeding platform in 
collaboration with Indian Council of Ag ricultural Research/S tate Agricultural 
Universities. Under the biodesi gn program, it is also proposed to establish a Centre for 
Biodesign addressing diagnostics and medical devices in healthcare.  

11.4 When the Committee enquired as regards the development of new medicines, the 
department explained that presently eleven drug s are being developed by the department    
and funds to the tune of Rs.1000.00 crores  are required for this purpose. The                     
public-private partnership pattern is being followed for the drug development. One of the 
outstanding drugs which have been developed is the ‘oral insulin’ for persons suffering 
from diabetes, which will eliminate the requirement for injections. 
11.5 The Committee is aware that global research towards discovery of vaccines 
and medicines for diseases afflicting the western countries find precedence over the 
diseases which are commonly found in the developing countries  such as Cholera, 
Typhoid, Rabies, Malaria, dengue, Tuberculosis.  It is therefore the responsibility of 
the scientific departments to concentrate on drug discovery & development for the 
aforesaid diseases which afflict millions of  people across the country and also make 
them available to the poor pe ople at affordable prices.  The Committee therefore 
recommends that the Department should accelerate the development of vaccines 
and medicines through its Vaccine Gr and Challenge Programme and ensure 
participation of both premier government sc ientific and the private sector research 
institutions in this regard. 
12. PUBLIC- PRIVATE PARTNERSHIP  
 The Committee desired to know about the efforts made by the Department to 
encourage and involve industrie s in the field of biotechnolo gy.  The Department replied 
that it is running a programme, named, Pu blic Private Partnership which has two 
components, i.e., Small Business Innovative Research Initiative (SBIRI) and 
Biotechnology Industry Partners hip Programme.  The Secretary further informed that 
Department have now 300 companies while it had only 50 companies 6 years ago.  He 
further informed the Committee that actually concentration is being made on building a 
lot of young companies in the country and DBT had moved into the field.  The 
Committee while appreciating the efforts ma de by the Department so far in this 
fields, however, recommends that the Depar tment should make concerted efforts to 
involve more and more Indian  industries and encourage young entrepreneurs to 
give a boost to the area of biotechnology which as already emerged as promising and 
fast growing sector for feeding the economic development of the country. 
13. PROJECTS /SCHEMES FOR THE BENEFIT OF THE NORTH-EASTERN 
AREAS AND SIKKIM 
13.1    The main objectives of the progra mme has been human resource development 
at all levels in healthcar e, agriculture and bioresour ce biotechnology; upgradation of 
research facilities in universities, colleges of agriculture and medicine; implementation of 
North Eastern relevant product oriented R&D projects such as swine fever network for 
vaccine and diagnostic development and de monstration of proven technology for the 
benefit of society to promote skill development, entrepreneurship and self-employment.  
13.2 The department informed that the total allocation made for provision for 
projects/schemes for the benefit of "North Eastern Areas and Sikkim" has been increased 
from Rs.90.20 crores at R.E stage in the year 2009-2010 to Rs.120.00 crores in                 
2010-2011. Therefore, out of the total alloca tion of RS.1200.00 crores of plan budgets, it 
is mandatory to allocate Rs.120.00 crores for North Eastern Region.  
 

13.3 The Department informed the Committ ee that considerable efforts through 
various interactive meetings with Vice-Chancellor of universities, Director of institutions, 
colleges and non-profit organizations in the North-eastern region to  implement various 
projects so far with variable success rates.  
 The main challenges faced by the Depart ment for utilization of this mandatory 
budget of 10% of Gross Budgetary Support(GBS) have been following:  
(i) The fund absorption capacity in biotechnology in North Eastern Region 
and Sikkim is highly limited due to severa l factors such as lack of skilled human 
resource, no biotech industri al activity and other social factors. Year after year 
spending 10% GBS is practically not feasible and even if it is spent somehow, it is 
at the cost of certain high quality projects and initiatives in  biotechnology which 
have far ranging benefits irrespective of region. 
  (ii) The Department has no adequate human and institutional resources to 
mobilise expenditure in North East in biotechnology under such challenging 
conditions described above. Therefore, DBT has drawn up to collaborate and 
partner with other agencies such as ICAR, CSIR and ICMR to strengthen the 
programme implementation.  
  (iii) The Department would like to take  up the matter with the Department of 
North Eastern Region (DONER) to limit annual allocation at RS.80.00 crores 
instead of 10% of GBS for expenditure with high success rates.  
13.4 The Committee is of the opinion that mandatory 10% allo cation of total 
budget to Northern-Eastern Region would be more beneficial for the Region. The 
Committee therefore recommends that instea d of thinking of limiting the annual 
allocation to Rs.80.00 crores, the Department should make efforts towards creation 
of skilled human resource  and setting up institutional resources of the Department 
to ensure that the funds a llocated are fully utilized, specially when the region is in 
dire need of  the funds for its development. The Department should also plan 
towards cooperation with other scientific departments to ensure the much needed 
growth in this region.   
14. The Committee is of the view that th e Department of Biotechnology through 
its various research and development progra mmes is striving to uplift the standard 
of living in the country.  Keeping in view the excellent work that the Department has 
undertaken, the kind of competence it has exhibited and the kind of core and critical 
programmes it has envisaged for the future, the Committee strongly recommends 
that the Department of Biotechnology shou ld be properly and adequately funded 
and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. stage should be strictly avoided.
