REPORT 
 The report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) on 
the recommendations contained in the one hundred and seventy-sixth Report of the Department-related 
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands for 
Grants (2007-08) of the Department of Atomic Energy, which was presented to both the Houses of Parliament 
on 26th April, 2007. There were eleven recommendations in the One Hundred Seventy-sixth Report of the 
Committee. Action Taken notes on the recommendations of the Committee were received from the Department 
on 27
th July, 2007. 
 The Committee’s recommendations, Action Taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the 
Action Taken by the Department of Atomic Energy are set out in the succeeding paragraphs. 
PLAN OUTLAY 2007-08  
1.  Recommendation 
 The Committee observes that the Department has in formed the Committee regard ing a similar reduction 
of Rs. 312.89 crores in the non-plan allocation under Demand no.5, during the Demands for Grants for the year 
2006-07, and the Department had raised its concerns on the operational needs of the Heavy Water Plants, 
Nuclear Fuel Complex, Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology, Fuel Reprocessing Plants of the BARC. 
The Committee had accordingly recommended in its 160th Report (recommendation no. 15) that any 
curtailment of funds in a crucial sector like Atomic En ergy could delay the endeavor of the Department to 
increase the contribution of nuclear power in the tota l power production and in other important areas of the 
country.   (Para 11.2) 
 The Committee expresses its serious concern at the fa ct that the issue has not been resolved and has 
arisen again and there has been reduction of Rs.443. 88 crores in the non-plan allocation under Demand  
no.5 for year 2007-08; which is even more than the earlier reduction of Rs. 312.89 crores during the year 2006-
07.   (Para 11.3) 
 The Committee strongly feels that any curtailment of funds for operational and maintenance reasons and 
any institute especially like BARC, which is involved in high-end and innovative research areas, slows down 
the pace of sustained growth attained over the years in the field of atomic en ergy. The Committee therefore 
strongly recommends that the Department should take up the issue of persistent reduction in the non-plan 
allocation (Demand No.5) with the Ministry of Finance expeditiously and impress upon them the urgent need 
for increased allocation to enable the Department to maintain the progress in field of nuclear energy.  
 (Para 11.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 During the year 2007-08 there is a shortfall of Rs . 443.88 crore in the Non-plan allocation under Demand 
No.05-Atomic Energy. As recommended by the Committee, the matter was taken up with Ministry of Finance 
to impress upon the urgent need for increased allocation of funds. Ministry of Finance has informed that a 
detailed review will be carried out in mid-year and the actual requirement of funds would be assessed and 
augmentation of funds done through the Supplementary Demands for Grants, if considered necessary.  

Comments 
 The Committee is not satisfied with the reply of the Department. The Committee feels that if the 
required funds are provided at th e budget estimate stage itself, th e need for more funds through 
supplementary grants would not aris e. The Committee reiterates its con cern on the issue and is of the 
view that since the Ministry of Finance has suggeste d, this issue may be forcefully taken up with them 
during the Supplementary Demands for Grants. 
NUCLEAR POWER PROJECTS UNDER CONSTRUCTION  
2.  Recommendation 
 The Committee commends the fact that Kaiga-3 has a ttained criticality in February, 2007, which is one 
month ahead of the actual/anticipated date of completion i.e. March, 2007. The Committee also commends the 
Department for the progress made on the construction of  the remaining five nuclear power units, which are 
progressing as per schedule. The Committee is of the opini on that in view of the severe shortage of power 
being faced by the country presently, nucl ear energy is fast appearing to be an inevitable answer to sustain the 
power requirements of the country. The Committee therefore desires that the pace of construction should be 
maintained for the ongoing power projects and in future efforts should be made to complete the planned 
projects within the targeted dates. (Para 12.3) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 One of the main objectives of the Department is to  pursue rapid nuclear power capacity addition to meet 
the long term energy requirements of the country through its three stage nuclear power programme. It has been 
the endeavour of Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd. (NPCIL) to ensure completion of all projects within 
their targeted date, even in the face of constraints. Th is has been achieved in Tarapur Atomic Power Projects 3 
and 4 also. The pace of construction of ongoing projects will be maintained and efforts are being made to 
ensure completion of the project under construction. 
 While NPCIL activities have proceeded as per progr amme, some activities beyond control of NPCIL, are 
foreseen to have a potential of delay. These are being dealt with at the highest level. Efforts are also on to 
contain the impact of such activities on project completion dates.  
Comments 
 The Committee is of the view that Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd. (NPCIL) should 
anticipate the possible causes for de lays and make efforts to adhere to  the time schedule for completion 
of Nuclear Power Projects. 
MONITORING MECHANISMS FOR ONGOING PROJECTS  
3.  Recommendation  
 The Committee desires that the existing reviewing mechanisms developed by the Department to monitor 
and review the progress of the ongoing schemes/projects before formulating/finalizing proposals for budgetary 
support should be strengthened to eliminate the scope of shortage or surrender of funds at a later stage.  
 (Para 13.3) 
Action Taken by the Government 

 The Department has taken further steps to strengthen the existing reviewing mechanisms to monitor and 
review the progress of the ongoing schemes/projects to eliminate the scope of shortage or surrender of funds at 
later stage. The recommendations of the Committee has been  brought to the notice of all Heads of Constituent 
Units/Autonomous Bodies and Public Sector Undertakings of the Department to take necessary action. 
Comments 
 The Committee appreciates the steps taken by the De partment to strengthen the existing reviewing 
mechanisms to monitor and review the progress of the ongoing schemes/projects. 
UNDER UTILIZATION OF FUNDS IN THE YEAR 2006-07 
4.  Recommendation  
 The Committee notes that the reasons attributed by the Department for the underutilization of funds for 
the project during the year 2006-07 are mainly administrative in nature viz. delay in finalization of contracts 
for supply of equipments and materials, delay in ge tting statutory clearance from various agencies etc. The 
Committee is of the opinion that these administrative problems can be easily resolved through proper planning 
and coordination with the agencies in volved in supplying equipments or granting clearances for the strategic 
projects of the department. The Committee also recommends that the department should ensure strict 
compliance of the terms agreed to by the agencies supplying equipments, to avoid procedural delays.   
 (Para 14.5) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 In order to ensure full utilization of funds, the Department has taken adequate advance action. All the 
schemes executed by the Department are reviewed by Project-coordinators from time to time and monitored 
for the physical and fiscal progress. For major items, completion schedule of milestone from the 
contractors/suppliers are being obtained for strict co-ordination and follow up. Project schedules are also 
prepared for each project being executed and are reviewed  at appropriate level. During the review, the critical 
activities, if any, in execution of projects are highlight ed and efforts are made to remove the constraints. Co-
ordination with all the authorities concerned are also taken up appropriately to reduce the delay. Actions are 
taken in advance for procurement of items and also identification of possible suppliers for various items are 
being carried out to avoid any delay in execution of the projects. 
 As regards recommendation of the Committee to the De partment to ensure strict compliance of the terms 
agreed to by the agencies supplyin g equipment to avoid procedural delay it is stated that whenever any 
agreement is entered into with vendors/contractors for supplying items or awarding contract, liquidity damages 
and risk purchase clauses are appropriately incorporated in the purchase orders to ensure strict compliance of 
the terms agreed by the suppliers. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the steps taken by the Department to  ensure full utilization of funds 
and to avoid procedural delays. Ho wever it feels that the Department  should make maximum effort to 
remove the constraints which comes in the way of execution of the projects. 
 

ASSISTANCE TO UNIVERSITIES/INSTITUTIONS/AWARENESS PROGRAMMES 
5.  Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that by investing substantially in research in the field of nuclear science at 
University level and even at undergraduate level, helps in attracting more students and developing their interest 
towards nuclear science. In this direction the Committee recommends that more universities should be brought 
under the proactive system of funding and efforts should be made towards inculcating the interest of students 
in the science stream at higher secondary level in the field of Atomic Energy. (Para 15.4) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Department has been actively involved in synergizing research programmes with Universities. As a 
part of this, DAE and Mumbai University and DAE and Pune University have an ongoing MoU under which 
students are selected for Ph.D. programme and fellowships are awarded by DAE. The Ph.D. work is pursued 
using the facilities at BARC and Universities. In addition, scientists from the Department have been 
collaborating with a large number of Universities and the joint research pr ojects are funded through the Board 
of Research in Nuclear Sciences of DAE. To attract young students to careers in science and to enhance to 
quality of training in University system, DAE has recently proposed a National Institute for Science Education 
and Research at Bhubaneswar and in  collaboration with Mumbai Univ ersity has created a Centre for 
Excellence in Basic Sciences. Both these institutions will provide quality undergraduate / graduate training in a 
post-graduate environment, involving faculty from proximate national institutes. The students, after higher 
secondary level will pursue a five year integrated M.Sc . Degree in various science disciplines. This is yet 
another effort to build brand institutes for science education similar to IITs for Engineering. 
 Homi Bhabha Centre for Science Education (HBCSE) organises camps and preparation of students 
throughout the country to participate in International Science Olympiads in Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, 
Biology and Astronomy. 
 Homi Bhabha Centre for Science Education (HBCSE) of TIFR runs a programme called National 
Initiative for Undergraduate Science (NIUS). Under this programme select group of students studying for 
B.Sc. at various colleges in the country and International Olympiad medal winners spend their vacation at 
HBCSE and are exposed to lectures, demonstrations and experimental work in addition to each student being 
mentored throughout their study through internet communication, thus keeping them interested in scientific 
research. 
 Every year, students from various Colleges / Universities from all over the country are given specialized 
training in DAE Organizations in the advanced area s of Chemistry, Physics and Biological disciplines. 
Collaborative projects with renowned academic instit utes / Universities are being carried out by providing 
research funding. Department supports students of Graduate level from BITS who are regularly undergoing 
their Practice School session. In order to expose students to frontiers in science, Department runs a Young 
Scientist Research Programme during summer at Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT). 
Short term training is given to engineering undergraduates during summer. M.Tech students from several 
universities carry out their one year project works at RRCAT. Department has organised several summer 
schools on Beam Physics for students selected from vari ous universities in the country, to promote accelerator 
science. RRCA T is involved in the DAB Graduate Fellowship Scheme (DGFS) under which selected M.Tech 
students from various IITs do project works in areas  of relevance to DAB un der the guidance of RRCAT 

scientists. A.M.Tech (Lasers &Applications) course has been started in Devi Ahilya Vishwa Vidyalaya 
(DAVV) Indore under the aegis of National Lase r Programme. Under a MoU between RRCAT and DAVV, 
scientists of the Centre are actively involved in teaching, guiding project work and evaluation activities. 
Students who have ‘completed this programme are contributing to laser related R&D work in several 
laboratories. RRCAT took proactive m easures to promote nuclear resear ch at Mangalore University by 
establishing a variable energy electron accelerator, Microtron. This facility has helped in carrying out frontline 
research in areas of radiation physics and related interdisciplinary areas of science & technology. In Mangalore 
University, accelerator physics  is presently being taught as optional subject for M. Sc. Physics students. In 
collaboration with DAB-UGC, Consortium for Scientific  Research (CSR), university researchers use beam 
lines of Indus-I at RRCAT for their re search work. Science day is observe d annually by arranging visit of 
students of science stream from various schools to inculcate interest in science and to familiarize them with the 
activities of the Department. In order to inculcate the awareness among students, several exhibition and quiz 
programmes on nuclear science are also being conducted regularly for the students and special interaction 
programmes are being held for teachers of colleges and schools for developing interest in science among the 
student community. 
Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the various steps ta ken by the Department for public awareness in 
nuclear science. 
6. Recommendation  
 The Committee notes that only a miniscule amount of Rs. 0.40 crores and Rs. 2.00 crores has been 
allocated for Public awareness and Neighbourhood We lfare Programmes and exhibitions respectively. The 
Committee feels that there is an urgent need to develop interest and awareness of the masses and especially the 
interest of the new generation towards the subject of Atomic Energy in general and to dispel the negative 
impression of the public towards nuclear power. The Committee is of the opinion that the department should 
allocate more funds towards mass awareness programmes in the field of nuclear science. (Para 15.5) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The figure of Rs. 0.40 crore and Rs. 2.00 crore allocated for Public Awareness and Neighbourhood 
Welfare Programmes and exhibitions respectively refer only to the budget allocation from the Secretariat of 
Department of Atomic Energy. In addition to the above, all the Constituent Units of DAE including R&D 
Units, Industrial Units, Public Sector Undertakings, Autonomous Bodies have their own expenditure towards 
Public Awareness and Neighbourhood Development programmes. Department has taken up various activities, 
regularly and in a systematic manner to disseminate authentic information and to remove misapprehensions 
about nuclear power by taking up exhibitions and seminars in school/scientific/technical fora, technical visits 
of media personnel to nuclear power stations /sites and workshops etc. 
 The Department in association with various Universities as well as Science popularization societies like 
Indian Association of Nuclear Chemists and Allied Scientist (IANCAS), Indian Physics Association (IPA), 
Marathi Vigyan Parishad and Hindi Vigyan Sahitya Parishad have been regularly organizing public awareness 
programmes, exhibitions, lectures, Kisan melas, popular lectures in State language or Hindi for college and 
school students. 

 These activities are primarily aimed at (l) Generating interest and attracting young school and college 
students to take up Research in Nu clear Science as a career; (2) Acquaint ing students, farmers, general public 
and members of the electronic and prin t media with the achievements of the Department in the fields of 
applications of radioisotopes and radiation technology to society at large and more specifically in the field of 
agriculture, industry and medicine. Advan ces in other related areas of research e.g. biotechnology, 
biopesticides, biofertilizers and bioremediation through the R&D activities are also projected; (3) Allaying the 
fears in the mind of people about nuclear radiation by informing through audiovisual aids and lectures the 
significance of dose to radiation effects and lack of significant health effects e.g. cancer incidence, genetic 
effects etc. among the people living in high background radiation area of the Monazite belt of Kerala. Special 
emphasis is laid on natural radiation exposure, its magnitude, variation and the strict limits enforced by 
regulatory agencies and the excellent  track record of nuclear power in dustry in India; (4) Conveying the 
changing energy resource scenario leading to inevit ability of the nuclear optio n as a cleaner source;  
(5) Creating awareness about dissemina ted approach to the problem of environmental pollution––the zero 
garbage wealth from waste aspect of Nisargaruna biogas plants being promoted by BARC. 
 In the last four years, Department supported two campaigns by Marathi Vigyan Parishad, Mumbai to 
motivate young students at IX to XII standards to take  up research in nuclear scie nce as a career. About 100 
lectures were organized during each campaign in Marathi in more than 50 locations in rural Maharashtra. 
Likewise special symposia in Hindi have been held at different locations outside Mumbai. Last three of them 
were at Shillong, Varanasi and Jabalpur. Topics related to nuclear energy and application of radiations were 
the main themes. Hindi Vigyan Sahitya Parishad, which is very active in this regard derives financial as well as 
manpower (voluntary) support from the Department. 
 Need to further augment these activity is well recogn ised and Department will strive to work in that 
direction. 
Comments 
 The Committee notes the reply and reiterates its re commendation that a greater sensitivity to the 
needs to reach out to young people be addressed with a view to broad base recruitment. 
7. Recommendation  
 The Committee feels that diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals are an important constituent of the various 
diagnostic tools used for the detection of life threateni ng diseases like cancer, thalassemia, tumors etc, and 
therefore desires that efforts should be made by the Department to produce the second variety of radio 
pharmaceuticals in the country itself, which would decrease our dependence on imports. (Para 16.3) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 Efforts to produce the Radionuclides required in the country are continuously made in order to be self-
sufficient. Apart from I-131, P-32, Cr-151, several other isotopes being regularly produced at Bhabha Atomic 
Research Centre and supplied through Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology to the users for both 
diagnostic and therapy applications. Newer isotopes such  as 1-125, Lu-177 are also now produced for use in 
nuclear medicine. Apart from reactor produced isot opes, BARC has commissioned a medical cyclotron and 
introduced Fluorine-18 based radiopharmaceuticals, furode oxyglucose to the users in Mumbai for diagnosis 
and management of cancer. The cyclotron is in operation regularly from March 2003, and over 1300 batches of 

[F-18]-FDG has been produced and supplied to Radiation Medicine Centre, Tata Memorial Hospital, Jaslok 
Hospital, Hinduja Hospital and Bombay Hospital. Over 10,000 patients-studies have been done.  
[F-18]-Sodium fluoride is available for bone-imaging  by PET. Two other [F- 18]-radiopharmaceuticals are 
ready for patient trials. Indigenous synthesis of precur sor chemicals (presently imported) has been done and 
evaluation is in progress. In the last two years four other cyclotrons have come up in the country and staff from 
these centres have received training at our cyclotron facility Isotopes for medical use produced in cyclotrons of 
higher energy will be realized after commissioning of the proposed 30 MeV cyclotron at Kolkata. The Kolkata 
cyclotron being a larger one can produce several radioactive isotopes including 
124I, 123I, 201TI, 64Cu etc. These 
are useful in making new and improved radiopharmaceuticals for diagnosis of a variety of diseases. 
 In short, Department is taking concerted efforts for making second varieties of radiopharmaceuticals 
envisaged in the Committee’s Report, for decreasing the dependence on imports of the same by production of 
adequate amount of the isotopes.  
Comments 
 The Committee while taking note of the effort s taken by the Departme nt for making second 
varieties of radiopharmaceuticals are also of the view that priority should be given to the projects where 
self reliance can be achieved. 
INDO-UNITED STATES JOINT CIVILIAN NUCLEAR ENERGY AGREEMENT 
8. Recommendation 
 The Committee feels that with the fast depleting conventional sources of  energy, the agreement with the 
United States would result in a quantum jump in our nuclear energy generating capacity and would provide the 
much needed energy security to the country which is essential for the country’s growth. The Committee desires 
that the cooperation in nuclear resear ch activities should also increase and India should be able to join the 
international mainstream and occupy its rightful place among the top countries in the nuclear community. The 
Committee also desires that the Department should ensure that in future, the country is able to pursue it nuclear 
energy activities, whether civilian or military, as per it own national interests. (Para 17.7) 
Action Taken by the Government 
 The Government is having discussions with friendly countries such as the USA, France and Russia with a 
view to enhance bilateral cooperation in the field of nuclear energy and to open-up international trade in civil 
nuclear energy with India. Officials have already negotiated text of an agreement with the USA and this is 
being further discussed within the Government. In addition, during the visit of French President Chirac to 
India, a declaration for cooperation in nuclear energy was signed on 28th February, 2006. Similarly, a 
memorandum of the intent to set up nuclear power plants in India was signed between DAE, India and Federal 
Atomic Energy Agency, Russia on 25th January, 2007. The purpose of all these instruments 
(agreement/declaration/Memorandum of intent) is to ensure that there is a significant additionality in nuclear 
energy generating capacity in the country. However, while negotiating various instruments, it has been ensured 
that we are able to carry on with our indigenous programme as per our national interests without any 
hindrance. 
 

Comments 
 The Committee takes note of the action taken by the Department. However, the Committee would 
like to emphasize that national int erest should be kept in mind and it should be ensured that there is no 
obstruction to the indigenous nuclear Programme.
