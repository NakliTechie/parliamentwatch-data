REPORT
I. the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to
 submit the report on its behalf, present this 41st Report of the Committee. This report deals with the 'Mid-Term Appraisal' of the programmes
 and functioning of the Department of Science and Technology.
2. In the meeting of the Committee held on the 7th February, 1997, the Secretary and other officers of the Department of Science and
 Technology gave an overview of the various activities of the Department and the Committee sought clarifications on various aspects of the
 performance of the Department particularly in the fields of Basic Research and Allied-­areas and their financial aspects.
3. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the Officers of the Department of Science and Technology for their cooperation and
 answering the queries raised by Members on various aspects on programmes initiated by the Department.
4. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on the 5th March, 1997.
 
5. The Secretary, Department of Science and Technology, in his presentation before the Committee, gave an overview of the thrust areas in
 which the Department is engaged in pursuance of the national objective of making scientific and technological progress for attaining self-
reliance. Over the years, the Department and autonomous institutions under the Department of Science and Technology have been making
 consistent efforts in formulation of policy statements and guidelines on Science and Technology; promotion of new areas of scientific research;
 Co-ordination of inter-departmental Science and Technology efforts; Support basic and applied research in universities/labs/institutions;
 International Science and Technology co-operation; Support high quality research in autonomous bodies; Catalysing Science and Technology
 interaction in societal programmes, enterpreneurship development; Science popularisation, communication and Science and Technology
 Manage­ment Information system; Promotion of commercialisation of home grown technology; support to technology development
 programmes, technology information and assessment; providing scientific services in the areas of Surveying and Mapping through its
 autonomous organisations e.g. Survey of India (SOl) and National Atlas Thematic and Mapping Organisations (NATMO); Meteorology
 through India Meteorological Department and National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) and Technology
 Development Board (TDB).
6. The Committee examined the performance of the Department during the current year and its major thrust areas identified in the Ninth
 Five Year Plan in the light of the presentation made by the Secretary, Department of Science and Technology.
7. The Committee feels that the expectations of people regarding timely returns on investments made in Science and Technology and their
 egalitarian distribution amongst various sections of the society have not met with desired success. Besides, Ministry should sincerely
 endeavour to achieve cost effective and quality oriented competitiveness in the industrial field. It is equally necessary to reduce the time lag in
 the transfer of technology from the laboratory to its commercial exploitation in the field. There is need for an urgent effort to that end.
 
7.1 The Committee observes that out of Rs. 242.55 crores of the Plan Budget, about Rs. 150 crores would be spent on basic research
 and on autonomous institutions. The rewards of research are not commensurate with the amounts being put in. The Committee feels there
 is urgent need to review the research projects keeping in view the national requirements and the likely returns from the research projects. '
In the case of Technology Board, the expenditure upto December, 1996, 'has been only Rs. 3 lakhs whereas expenditure of Rs. 4.62
 crores has been spent on international co­operation. Against the allocation of Rs. 34 crores as Plan expenditure for India Meteorological
 Department an amount of Rs. 5.53 crores has only been spent. The Committee is not satisfied with the low level of fund utilisation in
 respect of various programmes.
8. The Committee observes that the time lag between the clearance of the projects by the Programme Advisory Committee and release
 of funds needs to be reduced considerably. To that end, if necessary, a Technological Financial Board could be constituted to monitor the

9. The Secretary, while explaining the slow pace of the functioning of Technology Development Board (TDB) informed the
 Committee that the Board came into existence in the last week of September, 1996, and immediately after wards there were several
 meetings with the industry in which the Board spelt out the quantum and type of finances and help that could be rendered to the industry in
 ,carrying ;out new collaborations in various fields with them. The Board has 50 odd proposals and all of them are presently under
 evaluation through a rigorous procedure and until they are thoroughly examined and scrutinised, the finances would not be released.       
9.1 The Committee was not satisfied with the explanation regarding the delay in finalisation of the project under consideration of
 Technology Development Board and felt that the progress was far from satisfactory. The system needs to be improved upon to ensure that
 the programmes are designed in such a way that the Board can sanction the projects within the stipulated period.
10. The Secretary informed the Committee that in the field on international co­operation, there are three types of programmes v;z
.,
 Integrated long-term programme of co­operation in Science and Technology with India and Republic of Commonwealth of Independent
 States (CIS), programmes of co and operation with the developed countries and lastly technical co operation with the developing countries.
 Among the foremost areas in which the Government have co operation with the CIS countries are material technology, Bio-technology.
 energy-related issues and information technology. The Secretary further informed that they were facing some problems because of severe
 financial crunch existing in CIS countries whereas with developed countries they have equal partnership.
10.1 The Committee is of the view that the scientists 
of CIS countries today are migrating to developed countries. The Department
 should devise some programmes whereby their services could· be utilised productively. The  Committee recommends that the· Department
 should not only bear all the expenses of the scientists who give us technology and blue-print, but also should devise a compensation
 package for them.
11. The Secretary, informed about the functioning of the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and stressed that the Scientists of
 this Department were successful in making a long and definite forecast of seasonal rainfall i.e., Monsoons. for last nine years. Earlier IMD
 were giving forecast for the entire country. Since 1988 they have started concentrating even for smaller areas.
 
11.1 The Committee appreciates the efforts of India Meteorological Department in making reasonably accurate forecasts about the
 monsoons but at the same time is of the view that the Department can do much better and can improve its functioning so that our forecasts
 are fairly accurate. More so, in case of natural Calamities so, that ample and\timely relief operations can be provided to the. affected people.
 
12. The Committee is satisfied with the performance of the Department of Science and Technology in the case of societal programmes.
 Nevertheless, the Committee feels that much still remains to be achieved in this area. The Committee therefore observes that an integrate(l
 programme for the development of ' tribal people, particularly In the North-Eastern region of the country, needs to be drawn up on the lines
 of one Lunched in the Patalkot area of district Chindwara in Madhya. Pradesh recently.
