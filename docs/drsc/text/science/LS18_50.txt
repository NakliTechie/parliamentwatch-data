REPORT
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment &
 Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to submit the Report on its behalf, present this 50th Report on the Demands for
 Grants pertaining to the Department of Space for the year 1997-98 (Demand No. 92).
2. The Committee in its meeting held on 8th April, 1997, had the benefit of a presentation made by the Secretary, Department of
 Space on Demands for Grants. Members of the Committee sought clarifications and explanations from the Secretary on different
 aspects.
3. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the Secretary and other officers of the Department of Space for placing before
 it the requisite material and answering the queries raised by members on various aspects of the Space Programmes to enable it to
 scrutinise the Demands for Grants.
4. The Committee considered and adopted a draft report at its meeting held on 28th April, 1997.
5. The Department of space is responsible for carrying out the policies framed by the Space Commission. The programmes are
 implemented through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), the National Remote Sensing Agency (NRSA), the Physical
 Research Laboratory (PRL), the National Mesophere-Stratosphere-Tropospare Radar Facility (NMRF) and other agencies. The two
 main national systems that have been established, namely, the Indian National Satellite System(INSAT) and National Natural
 Resource Managemen System (NNRMS) are co-ordinated and managed by the Secretary level INSA T Co-ordinate Committee
 (ICC) and the Planning Committee of NNRMS(PC-NNRMS), respectively. The Committee provides guidelines for the evaluation
 of the system and also overseas the progress of remote sensing applications for progressive uses in the country. NNRMS Standing
 Committee (NNRMS-SC), set up with user Ministries and Departments, ensure coordinated functioning of different NNRMS
 elements.

11.The Committee was informed that during the discussions, in respect of Annual Plan 1997-98, the Planning Commission had
 agreed for a minimum net Plan Outlay of Rs. 1200 crores. However, in view of severe budgetary limitations, the Planning
 Commission could allot only a net Plant Outlay of Rs. 990 crores (Gros8-'-Rs. 992.55 crores). While communicating this, the
 Planning Commission had also mentioned that depending on the progress of expenditure on approved schemes and the over all
 resource situation, the position regarding additional allocation of funds to the Department of Space might be reviewed during the
 course of the year.
11.1 The Committee finds that against the allocation of Rs. 761.77 crores (RE) for the year 1994-95 stage the actual
 expenditure was Rs. 759.08 crores. Likewise in 1995·96 against the allocation of Rs. 918.56 crores (RE stage) the actual
 expenditure was Rs. 917.88 crores. In terms of percentage the utilisation works out to 99.65% for 1994·95 and 99.93% for 1995-96.
 Details in respect of 1996·97 are not yet available. In view of satisfactory utilisation of the expenditure the Committee recommends
 that the additional requirements of funds of the department of Space be met in full so that Spare Programmes do not suffer for want
 of funds.

13. The Committee has been informed that the Eighth Plan accomplishments of the Department of Space in different fields
 were as follows:-
Satellite Communications:
INSAT-2A and 2B launched in July 1992 and July 1993 respectively have enhanced the INSAT Space Segment Capacity,
 facilitating introduction of Metro Channel, rural network, search and rescue service and additional regional channels. INSAT-2C
 launched in December, 1995 provides additional capability vis Ku band transponders for business communication, mobile satellite
 services and extended coverage of C-Band transponders for reception of TV Programme beyond Indian boundaries thus catering
 population from South­ East Asia to the Middle-East. the transponders in the INSA T system have increased from 14 to 70 and
 will further increase to 93 with the launch of INSAT-2D by the end of May/early June, 1997. The cumulative monthly utilisation of
 INSAT transponders for TV and radio networking has increased to 6860 and 13000 hours respectively. The INSAT derived
 services such as telecommunication and television, radio-networking, development and communication, weather forecast disaster
 warning, search and rescue, etc. have also recorded phenomenal growth. The INSA T \based meteorology data collection continues,
 to be an important element of country's meteorological  system. It has also been stated that the Disaster Warning System (DWS) too
 continues to provide timely and invaluable cyclone alert services using 200 DWS receivers installed in eight States. A satellite
 based interactive communication system (I-way video and 2-way audio) has been successfully used to create awareness and provide
 training to adult education functionaries, rural development functionaries, teachers, students, industrial workers, banking staff, etc.
Earth Observation Systems
13.1 IRS-IB, launched in August, 1991 was pressed into operational service combined with IRS-I, launched in March, 1988,
 they provide vital remote sensing data for the National Natural Resources Management System (NNRMS). Utilising the launch
 opportunities from the developmental flights of PSL V, application specific low cost, quick turn-around time satellite missions
 called IRS-P series of satellite were identified. Accordingly, IRS-P2 and IRS-P3 satellites were launched and operationalised in
 1994 and 1996. These satellite complement the data requirements of the user community which was not hitherto available.
IRS-IC, most sophisticated civilian satellite all over the world today, was launched in December, 1995. It has unique
 capabilities being harnessed for a wide range of new applications in the areas of agriculture, cartographic mapping and terrain
 modelling. One up in the series IRS-ID is scheduled to be launched in the current year from Sriharikota. IRS Satellite Constellation
 has become an international system recently', Data from these satellites is being received already by ground stations in USA and
 Germany; some more stations are expected to start' reception in the near future. Commercial agreements are underway for reception
 and marketing of the data worldwide.
SPACE  SCIENCES
14. The National MST Radar facility has been fully commissioned and is being used by the scientific community for advanced
 research in the area of atmospheric sciences. Sounding rocket experiments have been conducted with pay loads development by
 Indian and foreign scientists. The SROSS-CI and SROSS-C2 satellites launched by ASLV in May, 1992 and May, 1994
 respectively are providing useful data in the area of aeronomy and astronomy.
LAUNCH VEHICLE SYSTEMS
15. Successful completion of ASL V programme, development of PSL V and successful flights of PSL V, progress in
 development of GSL V and implementation of agreement with Russia for cryogenic stage and starting of indigenous; cryogenic
 upper stage project were some of the highlights of the Eighth Plan.
15.1 During the Plan period three flights of ASLV were scheduled. However, ASLV-D3 mission 'of May, 1992 proved a
 turning point in the programme. Subsequently, after detailed studies the Department has decided to concentrate its efforts in the
 development of PSL V and, GSL V and to discontinue ASL V which have served immensely as a low cost flying test bed for
 evaluating complex technology. The maiden flight of PSL V took place on September 20, 1993. However, an error in
 implementation of on-board software resulted in a sub-­orbital flight. After the corrective actions second flight took place on 15th
 October, 1994 which successfully placed the IRS-2 satellite weighing 804 kg. in 817 Kms. polar sub- synchronous ·orbit (SSPO).
 With further improvements and higher pay load of 922 kg. the PSLV-D3 was flown on March 21, 1996 with IRS-P3. PSLV has
 now been declared operational. Under the PSL V continuation programme three more flights of PSL V are planned.

15.2 Geosynchronous Satellite Lauch Vehicle (GSLV) is a three stage vehicle, configured with maximum utilisation of PSL V
 modules and infrastructure created for its development. It uses a cryogenic upper stage which was planned to be procured along
 with technology from Russia. However, due to geopolitical reasons, the technology transfer agreement had to be modified into a
 supply contract under which the first cryo stage was revised and consequently the first developmental flight was rescheduled for
 1997-98. It has entered the hardware realisation and qualification phase.
Cryogenic Upper Stage
15.3 After the modification of the agreement with Russia, a separate cryogenic upper stage project for indigenous development
 was initiated. Significant progress. has been achieved in the project, approved in 1994, which was not· envisaged in the Plan.
 Designs have been completed and fabrication activities have commenced. Establishment of test facilities is in an advanced stage.
16. After the completion of the -Eight Five Year Plan the Department of Space has come to conclusion that barring some minor
 slippages or deliberate postponements most of the programmatic targets and missions planned have been accomplished and, in
 some cases, even exceeded. Establishment of UN centre for Space Science and Technology Education in Asia-Pacific were
 additional accomplishments not envisaged under the Plan. Indigenous development of cryogenic upper stage for GSL V was
 initiated though not originally envisaged under the Plan.
17. As already stated in the foregoing paragraphs the expenditure utilisation by the Department has been nearly cent percent.
 An outlay of Rs. 12,200 crores is envisaged for the Ninth Five Year Plan period, the non-Plan component of Rs. 1200 crores and
 plan component of Rs. 11,000 crores. With the confidence achieved by the Department in meeting the set targets during the eighth
 Plan period, the space missions for the Ninth Five Year Plan have been worked out during the year through a series of interactive
 process of interactions with experts and end beneficiaries. Accordingly, the Ninth Plan envisages continuation of development and
 applications of space technology for the social economic benefit of the country adopting an application driven approach while
 efforts would also be made towards developing and harnessing advanced technology in a self-reliant manner.
17.1 The Ninth Plan targets envisage providing services in newer and renovative areas of space applications using state of art
 remote sensing and communication satellites. Scientific investigation in the areas of astronomy including the study of earth and its
 environment would be further pursued. The Department hopes to continue its efforts towards commercialisation of India's
 technological capabilities through marketing space hardware and services in the global market. The Department seeks to achieve
 the space programme objectives by strengthening its partnership with Indian industry, academia and the user community.
17.2 The Department of Space has planned the 16 satellite missions and 11 indigenous launch . vehicle missions during the
 Ninth Five Year Plan.
18. The Committee feels satisfied with the achievements during the Eighth Plan period.
The Committee is also satisfied with the utilisation of expenditure during 1994-95 and 1995­96. While the details of utilisation of
 expenditure for 1996-97 are not yet available, the Committee hopes that the utilisation of expenditure during 1996-97 would also
 be cent- ­percent. Keeping in view the immense importance of the programmes proposed to be undertaken by the Department of
 Space during the Ninth Plan period, the Committee recommends that the adequate funds should be made available to the
 Department so that the space programmes do not suffer.
