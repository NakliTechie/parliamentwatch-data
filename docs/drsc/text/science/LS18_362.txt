REPORT  
1.1 The Government of India has set up the Space Commission and the Department of Space 
(DOS) in 1972.  The DOS is responsible for implementing the policies framed by the Space 
Commission.  These are carried out through the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), 
Indian National Space Promotion & Authorization Centre (IN -SPACe) and the Grant -in-aid 
institutions viz., Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National Atmospheric Research 
Laboratory (NARL), North -Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE -SAC), Semi -Conductor 
Laboratory (SCL) and Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST).  
1.2 The Secretariat of the Department of Space and the Headquarters of  ISRO are located in 
Bangalore.  The Programme Offices at ISRO Headquarters co -ordinate the overall programmes 
like Satellite Communication, Satellite Navigation,  Earth Observation, Space Transportation 
Systems, Space Science and Planetary Exploration, Climate &  Atmospheric Programme, 
Disaster Management Support, National Natural Resources Management System (NNRMS), 
International   Co-Operation, Capacity Building, Media & Public Relations, Budget & Economic 
Analysis and Human Resources Development.  
Major Centres / Units of DOS/ISRO: 
1.3 The major Centres, Units and establishments of DOS/ISRO, responsible for carrying out 
research and development activities as well as for undertaking various projects and programmes, 
are as follows: 
a) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VS SC), Thiruvananthapuram, provides the technology 
base for the country‟s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) development; 
b) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with its facilities located at Bangalore and 
Thiruvananthapuram, is responsible for development  of liquid propulsion and cryogenic 
propulsion systems for satellite and launch vehicles; 
c) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC -SHAR) at Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, is the 
spaceport of India providing launch base to indigenously built launch vehicles.  Also, i t 
provides the facilities for processing of solid rocket motors; 
d) Human Space Flight Centre (HSFC) is created with the responsibility to act as the lead 
centre for Human Space Flight Program. This centre is responsible for the execution of 
Gaganyaan programme that envisages sending a crew of astronauts to space and bring back 
safely to earth.   
e) U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC), Bangalore, is entrusted with the prime responsibility for 
development of satellites;      
f) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ahmedabad,  carries out research and development in 
space applications as well as development of application payloads; 
g) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), Hyderabad is responsible for satellite data 
acquisition and processing, data dissemination, aerial remote sen sing and decision support 
for disaster management.  

 
2 
 
h) The Laboratory for Electro -Optics Systems (LEOS), Bangalore has the responsibility for 
development and productionisation of electro optical sensors for satellites; 
i) ISRO Propulsion Complex (IPRC) located a t Mahendragiri has the responsibility for 
assembly, integration and testing of storable and cryogenic liquid rocket engines and 
stages, spacecraft thrusters testing in sea level and high altitude conditions, storage of 
liquid and cryogenic propellants and production of liquid hydrogen etc.; 
j) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking & Command Network (ISTRAC), with its ground stations at 
Bangalore, Sriharikota, Lucknow, Bearslake,  Biak (Indonesia), Brunei and Mauritius 
provides the telemetry, tracking and command support and  spacecraft mission management 
for the Indian remote sensing satellite missions, space science missions and satellite launch 
vehicle programmes; 
k) Master Control Facility (MCF) with its main facility at Hassan, Karnataka and backup 
facility at Bhopal, Madhya  Pradesh has the responsibility for control and operation of 
INSAT/GSAT/METSAT satellites; 
l) Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), Dehradun is a premier training and educational 
institute set up for developing trained professionals in the field of Remot e Sensing, Geo -
informatics and  GPS Technology for Natural Resources, Environmental and Disaster 
Management.   
m) Development and Educational Communication Unit (DECU), Ahmedabad,                    is 
involved in the conception, definition, planning and socio /techno-economic evaluation of 
development communication programmes; 
n) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram, carries out research and 
development in the area of inertial systems for satellites and launch vehicles; 
Organisations receiving Grants-in-aid: 
a) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous institution supported 
mainly by the Department of Space, carries out research in space sciences. 
b) The National Atmospheric Research Laboratory (NARL) set up at Gadanki near Tirupati , 
conducts atmospheric research. 
c) The North -Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE -SAC) established as an autonomous 
body under the Department of Space provides space technology inputs for developmental 
activities in the North-Eastern Region.  
d) The Semi -conductor Laboratory (SCL) located at Chandigarh is a research and 
development society and is entrusted with the design and development of Very Large Scale 
Integrated Circuit (VLSI) devices and development of  micro-electronic systems to meet 
the strategic needs of the country.  
e) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) is an autonomous body under 
the Department with the objective of creating quality human resources tuned to suit the 
requirements of state-of-art space research programme.  The Institute offers graduate, post -
graduate and doctoral programmes in the area of space science, technology and 
applications. The annual intake of the Institute is about 150-200 students.   
 

 
3 
 
Government Companies under DOS:  
a) The Antrix Corporation Limited, Bangalore, established in 1992, is a wholly owned 
Government Company for marketing space products and services.  
b) The NewSpace India Limited (NSIL), Bangalore, is another commercial arm of Indian 
Space Research Organization, with the primary objective of enabling Indian industries to 
scale up high-technology manufacturing and production base for Indian space programme. 
NSIL was established on March 06, 2019 as a wholly owned Government Undertaking / 
Central Public Sector Enterprise, under the administrative control of Department of Space.  
Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Center (INSPACe) 
1.4 Recently, the space sector was opened up to promote, handhold, regulate and authorise 
private enterprises and start-ups to undertake space activities by creation of an independent nodal 
agency under Department of Space - the Indian National Space Promotion and A uthorization 
Center (IN-SPACe).  This will enhance the contribution of space technology towards bo osting 
space economy within the country. 
1.5 Major projects for which allocations are sought include GSLV Continuation programme, 
GSLV Mk III continuation, PSLV -Continuation, Gaganyaan, RLV -TD Orbital re -entry 
experiment, Semi Cryogenic Engine & Stage Deve lopments, Development of Small Satellite 
Launch Vehicle (SSLV), Launch pad for SSLV, PSLV Integration Facility (PIF), Augmentation 
to Solid Motor Production Facilities (ASMP), Trisonic Wind Tunnel Facility Project, NETRA, 
INSAT-3 series, GSAT -20, GSAT -22/23/24, GSAT -30/31/32, IDRSS, TDS -01, NASA -ISRO 
Synthetic Aperture Radar Mission, Oceansat -3/3A, Resourcesat -3 Series, RISAT -1A&1B, 
HRSAT constellation, Navigational Satellite Systems (IRNSS -1J-1N), Aditya -L1, XpoSat, 
Chandrayaan-3, Space Docking Experiment are already approved and work is progressing to 
realise as per the targeted schedules.

2.3 Responding to the query of the Committee about the fiscal performance of the 
Department during the year 2021-22, it was submitted that an amount of  Rs.13,949.09 crore was 
allocated in BE 2021-22 for the Department of Space.   During the second wave of the pandemic, 
the activities of ISRO Centres/Units were affected during the first quarter owing to lockdowns.  
Further, the supply chain for electronic components, materials was also disrupted as the 
pandemic affected the global s uppliers as well.  It was brought to the notice of the Committee 

 
5 
 
that a fter a detailed assessment of the impact of the pandemic on the space activities and the 
cash-flow requirements for various projects/programmes activities, the Revised Estimates 2021 -
2022 was reduced to Rs.12,642.00 crore.  
2.4 The Committee observes  that the Department of Space was allotted Rs.13,919.09 
crore in BE 2021 -22 which was reduced to Rs.12,642.00 crore at  RE stage 2021-22.  The 
top three areas that suffered a drastic cut at RE 2021-22 stage are (i) Space Technology; (ii) 
Space Application; and (iii) Space Sciences.  Given the significance and importance of these 
three Central Sector Schemes/Projects  in the overall growth and development of the 
country, t he Committee desires to know the  reasons for poor  utilisation of the allocated 
amount.   
2.5 In response to a query of the Committee on the projected demand vis -à-vis actual 
allocation in BE 2022 -23, it was informed that the Department had made a projection of 
Rs.15,622.00 crore for BE 2022 -23 against which an amount of Rs.13,700.00 crore has been 
allocated in BE 2022 -23.  It was also  informed that the Department  has re -prioritised its 
projects/programmes/schemes in order to comply with the ceilings communicated by  Ministry of 
Finance.  It was submitted that while re -prioritising, due care has been taken to ensure that on -
going projects/programmes are not unduly affected.  Depending on the progress on on -going 
missions and facility build -up, the Department would app roach Ministry of Finance for 
additional funds at RE stage and through the mechanism of supplementary Grants. 
2.6 On the issue of roadmap prepared by the Department for the utilisation of the budgetary 
allocation of the year 2022-23, the Department shared the following details with the Committee 
about the projects/programmes/activities proposed for the year 2022-23:- 
a) Realisation of flight hardware, testing infrastructure, crew training and progress 
towards first unmanned flight under Gaganyaan Programme. 
b) Realisation of operational flights of PSLV, GSLV, GSLV Mk III towards providing 
launch services for Earth Observation, Communication, Navigation and Commercial 
missions. 
c) Development and flight testing of Small Satellite Launch Vehicle (SSLV) for 
providing on-demand launch services for small satellites.   
d) Provide continuity of Earth Observation data with improved spatial and spectral 
resolutions for natural resources management, cartography, oceanography, disaster 
management support and weather forecasting. 
e) Realization and launch of NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar Mission. 
f) Realisation of replacement satellites for maintaining the NavIC constellation to 
provide space-based navigational services in the country. 
g) Realization of Space Science missions such as Chandrayaan-3, Aditya-L1, Xposat 
h) Realization communication satellites viz. GSAT -20, GSAT -22/23/24, GSAT -32, 
IDRSS, Technology Demonstration Spacecraft (TDS-01) 
i) Grant-in-aid to the five Autonomous Bodies functioning under the Department. 
j) Pursue advanced technology developments such as semi -cryogenic engine and stage 
development, re-usable launch vehicle – orbital re-entry experiment,  next generation

2.7 The Committee makes note of the roadmap prepared by the Department for the 
year 2022-23 with a hope that the Department would strive hard to ensure that the targets 
set for the year 2022 -23 are achieved and the budgetary support provided to the 
Department would be sufficient to sustain its major projects/schemes/programmes without 
any difficulty.   
2.8 The Committee while scrutinising the targets set and progress achieved by the 
Department during the year 2021 -22, observes that the Department had set the target of 
realisation and launch of Geo -Imaging Satellite (GISAT) for agriculture and disaster 
management applications which could not be achieved as the launch could not succeed .  
Likewise, the launch of India ’s first solar observatory Spacecraft, Aditya -L1, on -board 
PSLV was envisaged during  the year 2021 -22 which has now been targeted during 2022 -
23. The Committee further notes that the Department had a plan to launch                         
the 3rd generation Oceanographic satellite – Oceansat-3 on-board PSLV in the year 2021-22 
which has now been rescheduled to 2022 -23. The Committee, therefore, recommends that 
the Department should strive hard to ensure that the newly set timeline for these 
components are honoured and further delay should not happen. 
2.9 Responding to the query of the Committee on the proposed utilisation of the increased 
allocation of Rs.193.00 crore in BE 2022 -23 under the head „Indian Space Research 
Organisation (ISRO) Head Quarters‟, the Department has submitted that the allocation is mainly 
to cater to recruitment drives planned during the financial year. 
2.10 The Committee hopes that the recruitment drives as intended by the Department 
would be completed in a time bound ma nner.  The Committee also desires the Department 
to furnish its updated manpower status in the Action Taken Notes.

9. General Observations/recommendations of the Committee 
9.1 The Committee notes that the Department of Space has been making efforts in 
mapping the glacial lakes which span over  more than 2.5 hectare in size and the said 
exercise has been completed in respect of the Ganga basin and Sutlej river and the data has 
already been complied in the atlas  form.  The Committee is of the view that it will be of 
great importance if an inventory about the glacier and glaci al lakes in the Himalayan 
region is documented on yearly basis .  This is crucial  in light of many  studies suggesting 
that there has been rapid increase in the number of glaci al lakes due to retreat in the 
glaciers caused by the impact of global warming and keeping into consideration the 
possible large scale flooding and destruction  that could happen i n future .                                                             
Therefore, role of the Department , in the matter becomes all the more pivotal and 
significant in light of the fact that many such glaciers are physically inaccessible for ground 
reporting.  The Committee also feels that with the modern technology and better resolution 
satellite imagery  the task of glacier lake s outburst monitoring can be done in a more 
scientific and robust manner.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to 
initiate action in this regard in a time bound manner and to make best use of its  remote 
sensing services for the purpose.  Adequate budgetary allocations should also be made for 
the same. 
9.2 The Committee further notes that the existing Institute/Centres of the Department 
are stationed in major cities and the presence of the Department appears to be negligible as 
far as the remote locations of the country are concerned.  The Committee feels that with 
the increasing demand of space based activities in various sectors, it would be justifiable to 
have Institutes/Centres spread across the country.  The Committee, therefore, recommends 
the Department to explore possibilities of establishing Institutes/Centres in these 
underserved areas so as to strengthen its base in the entire country. 
9.3 On the issue of providing assistance/su pport by the Department to the space 
industry based startups in the country , the Committee appreciates the efforts being made 

 
16 
 
by the Department in this direction.  The Committee, however, feels that in order to give 
impetus to the budding startups , more incentives may be offered by the Department which 
would have far reaching impact on the ground level .  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends the Department to identify the startups that would play a greater role in the 
overall growth and development of the space ecosystem in the country  and help them 
appropriately through incentives.

significant in light of the fact that many such glaciers are physically inaccessible for ground 
reporting.  The Committee also feels that with the modern technology and better resolution 
satellite imagery the task of glacier lakes outburst monitoring can be done in a more 
scientific and robust manner.  The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to 
initiate action in this regard in a time bound manner and to make best use of its remote 
sensing services for the purpose.  Adequate budgetary allocations should also be made for 
the same.                                                                                                                           (Para 9.1) 
 The Committee further notes that the existing Institute/Centres of the Department 
are stationed in major cities and the presence of the Department appears to be negligible as 
far as the remote locations of the country are concerned.  The Committee feels that with 
the increasing demand of space based activities in various sectors, it would be justifiable to 
have Institutes/Centres spread across the country.  The Committee, therefore, recommends 
the Department to explore possibilities of establishing Institutes/Centres in these 
underserved areas so as to strengthen its base in the entire country.                         (Para 9.2) 
 On the issue of providing assistance/su pport by the Department to t he space 
industry based startups in the country, the Committee appreciates the efforts being made 
by the Department in this direction.  The Committee, however, feels that in order to give 
impetus to the budding startups, more incentives may be offered by t he Department which 
would have far reaching impact on the ground level.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends the Department to identify the startups that would play a greater role in the 
overall growth and development of the space ecosystem in the country  and help them 
appropriately through incentives.                                                                                  (Para 9.3)
