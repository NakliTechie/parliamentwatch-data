INTRODUCTION 
  
I, the Chairman of the Commi ttee on Science and Tec hnology, Environment and Forests, have been
authorised by the Committee to present the report on its behalf, present this 14th Report of the
Committee. This Report deals with  the Detailed Demands for Grants  (1994-95) of the Department o f
Electronics. 
  
2 The Committee held in all three meetings on 6 th , 7 th  and 22 nd April, 1994 (Appendix). In the 
meeting held on the 6th April, 1994, the Secretary and other repres entatives of the Department o f
Electronics gave a brief account of the allocations on different activiti es/projects of the Department as
proposed in its Detailed Demands for Grants and Members sought clarificati ons thereon. On the 7th
April, 1994 the Committee discussed in detail the Demands for Grants of the Department on the basis of
the documents made available to it and the submissions made befo re it by the Secretary and othe r
officials of the Department and the officials of the Department replied to the clarifications sought by the
Members. 
  
3. The Committee considered and adopted this report at its meeting held on the 22nd April, 1994. 
  
4. The Committee expresses its appreciation for the officials of the Department for their cooperation
and making available the material whenever required by the Committee.

In the modern world if there is any area, which has affected our life most, it is the field of electronics
and information technology and all th e related disciplines that underpin it. There is hardly any area o f
human endeavour where electronic ga dgets have not reached; and ther e is hardly any industry, which
does not depend for its efficiency and productivity on the application of electronics in its different
manifestations. Innovations are taki ng place in this field on a revol utionary, continuing and breath-
taking basis, and the world over. Computers, communicati ons, control systems, consumer, industrial,
entertainment, defence and space el ectronics are all surging ahead. Th e Department of Electronics has
been vested since the early 1970s w ith the responsibility of formulat ion and implementation of national
policies in this sector, promoti onal measures for the development of appropriate new technologies
through R&D Programmes and coordinati on of the various appl ications of electronics. The aims, which
this Department seeks to achieve, are two-fold namel y, to bring the benefits of electronics to every walk 
of life and to every part of the country; and to develop the Indian Electronics as a major global player. 
  
2. In its sittings spread over two days, the Committee had occasion to examine the Detailed
Demands for Grants of the Department for the ye ar 1994-95 at some lengt h, particularly from the 
broader angle of its mandate and objectives. In this process the Committee has made an evaluation of the
plans and programmes of this Department in the larger framework of the Eighth Five Year Plan.

5. In comparative terms, the Pl an allocation of Rs. 140.10 crores for 1994-95 is less by about Rs. 24 
crores as compared to the year 1993-94. It was stated  that the major reason for this is that, during the 
year 1993-94, there was an allocation of a substantial amount to  the tune of Rs. 75 crores through
supplementary grants for partiall y meeting expenditure on rebuild ing of the Semiconductor Complex
Limited (SCL) Mohali. In the year  1994-95 a budgetary provision of Rs . 27.18 crores has been kept fo r 
this purpose. Therefore, if the amount of Rs. 75 crores made ava ilable to SCL th rough supplementary
grants and the amount of Rs. 27.18 crores earmarked for SCL duri ng the year 1994-95 is excluded, then 
an increase of about Rs. 24 crores is reflected in the budgetary estimates for 1994-95 in the Plan sector. 
6. In the course of the submissi ons made by the Secretary and othe r officials of the :Department
before the Committee, the constant refrain was that the Department's  projected dema nd for its various
projects, both ongoing as well as new, had either been met only partially by the Planning Commission or
no allocations at all had b een made, particularly for new projects.  It was mentioned that for Technology 
Missions theprojected demand of th e Department during the Eighth Plan  was for an amount of Rs. 519
crores against which a meager am ount of Rs. 25 Crores was allocate d. Similarly, sixteen new projects
for 1994-95 involving a budgetary s upport of Rs. 19.87 crores were submitted, which related to: 
providing employment opportunities for women in electronic sector; R&D in agri-electronics; Computer 
Graphic Development; and Survey of IT industry in India. No allocations whatsoever were made for any
of these projects. 
7. The Committee, however, did not f eel satisfied with these arguments . It was clearly visible to the
Committee that even during the ye ars 1991-92 and 1992-93, the Department  had not been able to fully 
utilise the funds allocated for di fferent projects/scheme s. For example, duri ng the year 1991-92, the 
utilisation was Rs. 123.10 crores ( both Plan and Non-Plan) as agai nst the allocation of Rs. 137.25 
crores; and in 1992-93, it was Rs. 87.12 crores against Rs. 104.61 crores. In so far as the year 1993-94 is 
concerned, the same trend of under utilisation of al located funds appears to be continuing, though signs
of marginal improvement are there.  The reasons given by the Department  for the shortfalls in expendi-
ture in most of the cases are th at the saving was due to insufficie nt number of R&D projects of the
requisite quality (which the Committee found to be very surprising, cons idering the contrary situation in
most other areas that the Commit tee has examined, and this appear s to be a reflection of the poo r
promotional attitudes towards R&D in the Department) or release of less funds owing to delay in getting
approval of the competent authority (which again th e Committee found to be su rprising, considering the
powers available to scientific depa rtment). The Committee feels that in some of the cases there may be
genuine reasons for non-utilisation or under-utilisation of allocated f unds, but if this becomes recurring
feature in respect of use of budgeta ry allocations every year, it does not portray the Department in a
good li ght and will surel y result in some kind of hesitation to provide enhanced allocations in

This, in the view of tile Committee is not corr ect. The Committee also feels that the progress o f

23. R & D is an important area in the field of Electronics and the Committee wanted to know from
the Secretary of the Department as to how much of the total amount was being spent on R & D projects
as a whole. The Committee was informed that the total amount on R & D projec ts earmarked during the
year 1994-95 was to the t une of Rs. 67.43 crores. Th e Secretary also informed  the Committee that the
Department looks on R & D projects  from two angles. Firstly, whatever technology is developed should
be used. In other words, only thos e projects are recommended which have a ready user. Secondly, the
Department expects the contribution to  come from either user Department  or a personal us er so that the
small capital of the Department acts as a catalyst to attract investment . It is not clear to the Committee
why this argument has not been applied to the STQC programmes. 
  
24. A question was posed to the Secretary as to whether the Department was getting the right type of
projects from the educational institutions. The repl y was that the Department  has started a number o f
programmes and interacted  with Electronic Industry Associati ons such as ELCINA, CETMA, IEEMA.
The Department has got Proposals from Punsami indu stry for aluminum capac itors and other proposals
are also coming. This, of course, does not relate to educational instituti ons. It was then stated that the
Department as a matter of fact wants to put in every professional college teaching Science & Technol-
ogy as well as engaged in R &D one computer at least to link it with the Telecom Network and Satellite
Communication; every college will th en have the facility of documen t transfer and the access to data
base. Of the amount allocated for        R & D 60% of the Department's  budget goes to meet the
requirements of applied research and 40% to fundame ntal research. The project s are selected such that
India has an advantage parallel pr ocessing and Photonics etc. The in tention of the Department is to
strengthen the country's infrastructure in order to apply indigenous technology effectively. 
  
25. The Committee feels that out of  the total Plan outlay of the Department for the year 1994-95 
which is Rs. 140.10 crores, almost 50%  appears to be spent on R & D projects, but the performance o f
the Department up till now in the R & D sector has not been ver y commendable. The exce ptions are
Page 11 of 1914th Report of Committee on S&T
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/science/14threport.htm

major activities like on parallel processing SA MEER, HVDC. No major projects in the hardware
sector appear to be on the anvil as may reassure the country about Department's playing the role o f
makingIndia a global player in term s of either productivity  or competitiveness. As pointed out by the
Secretary of the Department himself Electronics is a strategically important area and as an instrument o f
economic development; it has, therefore, to be taken up as a national priority. The Department, however,
has failed to realise its objectives, only partly because of lack of resour ces. Largely this is because of a
lack of vision relating to the role  of technology in area of hitech with rapid change and obsolescence
where there is need for self reliance. The Department, no doubt, has endeavoured to establish linkage
with industry but the industry does not seem to have  been very enthusiastic about using the technology
development efforts of the Department. On the contrary the response fr om Government Mini-
stries/Departments such as Rural Development, Water Resources, Public Enterprises, Women and Child
Development, Family Welfare, Agri culture, to use its inputs seem s to be more encouraging. The
Secretary of the Department made a forceful plea before the Committee that the Department had requisi-
te technological inputs and if an amount of Rs. 1000 cr ores is spent on steel, cement, fertilisers, paper,
etc. return would be as much as 5000 crores of rupees. If that is so  then the Committee is of the view
that the Department must make an analysis as to where it has"  gone wrong. This is, however, not to
belittle Department's achievements such as of the Centre for Materials for Electronics Technology (C-
MET), a society under the Depart ment, which include the Successful Development of Silicon
Tetrachloride for Optical Fibre A pplications, POC-13 for Solar Cell  Applications, Barium Carbonate
and Strontium Carbonate for Electro Ceramic Industry. Efforts are now under way to transfer these tech-
nologies to the Industry. By the turn of the century the import of Electronics material is expected to be at
the level of 40% as compared to the present level of 80%. Besides, as many as 17 projects are now under
development. 
26. In the course of the scrutiny of the budgetary demands of the Department it has come to the
notice of the Committee that the allocations during the year 1994-95 for Rural Electronics an  R & D 
Agricultural Electronics have been substantially brought down as compared to those for these
programmes during the year 1993-94. Rs. 50 lakhs we re allocated during 1993-94 for Rural Electronics 
whereas in 1994-95, the amount allotted is just  Rs. 10 lakhs. Similarly, for R & D Agricultural
Electronics Rs. 20 lakhs were earma rked during 1993-94 and no provision has altogether been made in 
the Budget Estimates for 1994-95. Conf ronted on this question, it was explained that the Department 
had projected a demand of Rs. 1.60 crores for Rural Electronics ag ainst which indications were given at
the time of discussions with the Pl anning Commission that the provision wi ll be kept at the level of last
year viz. Rs. 50 lakhs but finally it was reduced to Rs. 10 lakhs. None theless, quite a few proposals are,
at present, stated to be under Financial Adviso r's consideration, which would require funds to be
released. However, with the provision of Rs. 10 lakhs as it stands on date, th e Department may have to
take a decision either not  to support these proposal s or to suitably enhance the budgetary provision. As
regards the R &D Agricultural Electronics, the De partment submitted to the Committee that the
Planning Commission applied its own inter-se priority and decided not to allocate any amount for this
programme in 1994-95. The Committee fails to understand as to how th e Department would achieve its
objective of bringing the benefit of electronics to ev ery walk of life and every part of the country if the
rural sector is ignored in  this way and even smaller allocations  are not given. The Committee therefore,
strongly recommends that in the matt er of allocations the Electronics projects for the rural sector should
be given high priority. 
  
27. The Committee has observed that among the ne w projects submitted by the Department to
Planning Commission one related to employment opportunities for women in electronic sector for which
an allocation of Rs. 10 crores was sought. In this c ontext, a point was raised about the serious crisis
being faced by 13 women's co-operati ves in Kerala which had an em ployment force of 700 persons and
there was fear of their becoming jobless because selling their product was a big problem, there being no
buyers. The Secretary expl ained the position by saying that the solution to the problem lay in an
encouraging private companies which had a foot-hold in the market to use the product of such women's
Page 12 of 1914th Report of Committee on S&T
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/science/14threport.htm

co-operatives as an input and for that it was necessa ry to give incentives to such private companies. 
For example, in China and Japan large and ancillar y industries work together and the necessity is to
evolve similar strategies in our country. In so far as women's co-operatives in Kerala are concerned, they 
have enjoyed excise duty concession, but it has lately  been withdrawn for the reason that they started
producing under some other brand na me. The matter has been taken up by the Department with the
Ministry of Finance. The Committe e feels that it is a laudable obj ective to make women a strong wor k
force in the electronics se ctor, and pursuant to that objective it is all the more necessary that suitable
linkages are established with th e industries to use the produce being manufactured by them. This
proposition can be viable and mean ingful only if due patronage on duty concessions are given to such
co-operatives. 
  
28.  The Department of El ectronics has not only to make an anal ysis of the problems and define the
reasons for the difficulties encount ered, but play an important promot ional role in achieving solutions.
While the Department of Electronics seems to have a large technical staff of high quality, the impression
that the Committee has gathered in its discussion is more of efforts of an administrative nature, as would
be expected from a regular Governme nt department concerned with i ndustry rather than with science
and technology. There is need to cha nge this approach. The Department  also does not have a full time
Secretary in contrast to the earli er period when this area had a high powered Electronics Commission.
The Committee notes that the funding for majo r areas of R & D under National Radar Council,
Technology Development Council, Na tional Microelectronics Council  (NMC), EMDC is relatively
small. 
  
29. The Department has allotted the re sources available to it significantly on infrastructural facilities
rather than on R&D and links with  sectors powerful in electronics like DRDO , ISRO, Department o f
Atomic Energy appear to be wea k. The Performance Budget does not i ndicate in clear terms the actual
utilisation of allocated funds which would give a picture as to how the money has been spent and results
achieved. It gives just details of the project and does not highlight the actual physical targets laid out and
achieved. The Department of El ectronics aims at India becomi ng a significant global player in
electronics. The Committee does not see in the progr ammes of the Department  presented to it, any
integrated approach to achieve this. In the absence of such a vision  and major self-reliant strength in the 
country progress towards this is unlikely. The Commit tee would urge Government  to lay greater stress
on this area of opportunity.
