REPORT
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment & Forests, having
 been authorised by the Committee to present the report on its behalf, present this Forty-Seventh
 Report of the Committee. This Report deals with the detailed Demands for Grants (1997-98) of
 the Department of Ocean Development.
2. In its sitting held on April 7, 1997, the Committee discussed in detail the Demands for Grants
 of the Department on the basis of documents made available to it and the submissions made
 before it by the Secretary and other officials of the Department in the course of their oral
 evidence.
3. The Committee wishes to express its thanks to the Officers of the Department of Ocean
 Development for their cooperation and answering the queries raised by Members on various
 aspects on programmes initiated by the Department.
4. The Committee considered and adopted the draft report at its meeting held on the 22nd April,
 1997.
5. The Department of Ocean Development was set up in 1981. The programme of the department
 are directed towards development and use of Science and Technology for the sustainable and
 environment friendly exploration and exploitation of marine resources for the socio-economic
 benefit of the country. The Programmes of the Department are based on Ocean Policy Statement
 of the Government besides the UN convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOs). It is a nodal
 department for organizing, coordinating and promoting ocean development activities in the country.
 The activities of the Department can be grouped in the following major heads:
Polar Sciences
2.  Marine Living Resources
3.  Marine Non-liying Resources
Marine Environment and Coastal Zone Management
Ocean Observation and Information Services
  Marine Research and Manpower Development
 International Programmes

ANTARCTIC RESEARCH PROGRAMME: 
14. Much has already been mentioned about the Antarctic Programme and its scientific expeditions
 on annual basis. The first Indian research station, Dakshin Gangotri was commissioned in 1983
 and replaced in 1988 by the indigenously designed second Indian permanent station 'Maitri' which
 has adequate infrastructural facilities for conducting scientific research of contemporary nature in
 the icy continent.
14.1 Even while using Antarctica as a platform for conducting scientific experiments, India has
 always recognised the importance of preserving the pristine nature of this remote and unique
 continent. In order to uphold this commitment, India, an original not any of the Protocol on
 Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty, has ratified this protocol in April, 1996.
14.2 During the period 1997-98, the Seventeenth Indian Antarctic Expedition would be launched to
 continue contemporary research in polar science with special emphasis on International
 collaboration.
14.3 The board scientific activities planned to be taken include-Continuation of studies on ozone,
 trace gases and aerosols of the Antarctic atmosphere using various state-of-the-art equipment and
 Archieval of meteorological parameters that can be employed for climate modelling and prediction
 apart from in situ geological and air-borne geophysical investigations of the Central Dronning
 Maudland area.
14.4 The Department also propose to experiments in solar terrestrial phenomenon like optical
 aurora and geo-magnetic pertubations and Survey and assessment of living resource potentials of
 Antarctic waters.

POLYMETALLIC NODULES PROGRAMME:
 
16. In 1987, Indian became the first Pionear Investor and started the Polymetallic Nodules
 Programme. The International Seabed Authority allocated 150,000 sq. km. in the Central Indian
 Ocean Basin.
16.1. The Committee was informed that at the current state of technology, land based resources
 availability and market demand, it is not economical to go in for exploitation of minerals from
 deep sea. The international Studies indicate a scope for such exploitation in 2010 or so. The
 Department plans to develop and demonstrate adequate technical capabilities in all technical
 areas of Polymetallic nodules exploitation, so that India would be ready to embark upon a
 commercial venture when it is economically viable and would not lag behind other pioneer
 investors in the world.
16.2. The Committee was further apprised that it was a national programme with international
 commitments. The four major activities under this programme were (i) Survey and exploitation of
 poly-metallic nodules; (ii) Development of mining of nodules;

DRUGS FROM THE SEA
 
18. The Committee was informed that though· it was an ongoing programme on development of
 drugs which was till now being funded tinder Research and Development Programme. The
 programme was scheduled for three stages. Stage-I was implemented with a total outlay of Rs.
 1.72 crores; Phase II was initiated in 1993-94 involving a total expenditure of Rs.4. 78 crores and
 would be implemented over a period of three years. The Committee was further informed that by
 the end of second phase of the project in 1995-96, over 450 different species of potential flora
 and fauna had been collected and identified and about 1200 samples were subjected to biological
 screening for ability in treating different investigations have led to identification of human
 diseases. These are 17 most promising organisms possessing anti-diabetic, anti-viral, anti-
amoebic, anti-anxiety, anti-amalarial, anti­fertility, anti-hypertensive, anti-inflammatory,
 hepatoprotective activities.
 
18.1. During 1996-97, Phase-III of the project has been initiated as the next sequential step for
 drug development. During this phase, 5 out of the 17 most potential marine organisms
 possesssing anti-viral, anti-amoebic, anti-diabetic, anti-anxiety and larvicidal properties, were taken
 up on priority basis for extracting 1-2 herbal drugs by the end of Phase- III. It is expected that
 by 1998-99, one or two herbal drugs would be available for marketing, after completion of
 regulatory pharmacological and toxicological studies.

COMPAS PROGRAMMES
 
20. The Department is operating a programme namely Coastal Ocean Monitoring and Prediction
 Systems (COMPAS) since 1991. The COMPAS programme at present deals with the chemical,
 biological and microbiological parameters and would continue at the present level as far as the
 chemical and biological parameters are concerned. During 1991-98, microbiology programmes
 would be expanded in corporating therein physical oceanographic studies in order to make the
 programme more comprehensive. Besides, pollutants would be monitored in ecologically sensitive
 areas like Gulf of Kutch wherein large-scale developmental activities are being planned. The
 Planning Commission has approved an outlay of Rs. 4 crores for 1997-98 whereas anticipated
 expenditure would be Rs. 8.5 crores. Under this programme, levels of pollution would be
 monitored at 77 locations along the coastal areas of the country. Data in respect of 25
 parameters including nutrients, heavy metals, pesticides, pathogenic bacteria were being collected
 in 0.25 km. transact in the sea at these locations with the help of 11 Research Institutions. The
 programme was being monitored by a Steering Committee headed by the Chairman of Central
 Pollution Control Board and all the Coastal State Pollution Control Boards (PCBs) are represented
 in the Committee. Periodical exchange of data collected upto the year 1996 reveals that the sea
 approx, beyond 2 km. except off Mumbai is clean. In case of Mumbai the sea approx. beyond 5
 km. is clean.
20,1 It has further been revealed that the pollution levels in Versova, Mahim, Kakinada canal and
 Vishakapatnam harbour in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Orissa are areas of concern. The pollution
 in these areas have reached significant levels.
20.2 The data and information relating to the rising trends of pollution at the observed locations
 were periodically brought to the notice of the concerned State Pollution Control Boards who are
 empowered under the Water Act (1974) and Environment Protection Act (1976) to take preventive

POLLUTION IN BOMBAY HIGH
 
20.3 In the Bombay high, ONGC is carrying out exploration and exploitation of oil and natural gas.
 The area is periodically monitored by ONGC. The data collected by ONGC during 1994 and 1996
 indicated no significant levels of pollution in this area. During 1997-98, DOD plans to monitor the
 levels of pollutants in the Bombay High area using its newly acquired Coastal Research Vessel
 Sagar Paschimi.
 
21. The Committee is alarmed over the magnitude of marine pollution and the inadequate steps
 taken to prevent as well as control the same. The Committee finds it difficult to believe the data
 furnished to the effect that in case of Mumbai, which has been described as the commercial
 capital of country, the sea beyond 5 Kms. is clean. An aerial view of the sea surrounding
 Mumbai suggests that which travelling towards Mumbai High for about 30 to 40 minutes the layer
 of pollution and the lean sea was visible even to the naked eye. The Committee therefore finds
 that the data furnished by the Department in respect of marine pollution unreliable. The
 Committee express its unhappiness towards the problem of marine pollution and would like the
 Department to take urgent steps to deal with the same. The Committee should be apprised of
 the fresh assessment.
 
NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF OCEAN TECHNOLOGY
 
22. National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT) was established in 1993 with a view to develop
 technology in Ocean Sector activities. The Budget allocation for NIOT has been substantially
 increased for the year 1997-98 to Rs. 7.50 crores against Rs. 0.80 crores allocated at the stage
 of revised allocation for 1996-97.
22.1 The Committee was informed that the increased allocation of Rs. 7.5 crores to NIOT for
 1997-98 is mainly due to the proposal to take up the pilot activity on Ocean Thermal Energy
 Conversion (OTEC) at Rs. 3.0 crores and for Land and Building at Rs. 2.0 crores. These two
 together amount to Rs. 5.0 crores. The remaining Rs. 2.5 crores is for other 3 mission activities

22.2 Another thrust area in the projects of the Department is National Institute of Ocean

25. The Committee was informed that in the field of shore to vessel communication system the.
 Department had introduced a pilot project on shore to vessel communication system under the
 Marine Instrumentation Programme in 1991 as the fishermen in the sea experienced several
 difficulties while they were in distress. The department envisaged setting up of the shore stations

 informed that even phase-I took 3-4 years as walkie-­talkie sets were not given in time. The
 problems faced by the Department were technical as well as administrative. Mere provision of
 sets would have served no purpose if proper training was not imparted to fishermen. Fisheries
 Departments of each State periodically monitor the status and operation of these facilities.
