REPORT
INTRODUCTION
Ever since the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) came into existence on August 3, 1954, it has
been engaged in advancing peaceful uses of nuclear technology across India. Successfully completing
the six decades of its existence, the department has been applying radiation technologies in the domains
that matter most to the common man: agriculture, health care, industry and basic research. The current
mandate of the Department, on which its programmes are based, extends to:-
• Increasing share of nuclear power through deployment of indigenous and other proven
technologies, along with development of fast breeder reactors and thorium reactors with
associated fuel cycle facilities;
• Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes and carrying out
radiation technology applications in the field of medicine, agriculture and industry;
• Developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, supercomputers, advanced
materials and instrumentation, and encouraging transfer of technology to industry.
• Support to basic research in nuclear energy and related frontier areas of science; interaction
with universities and academic institutions; Support to research and development projects
having a bearing in DAE's programmes, and international cooperation in related advanced areas
of research; and
• Contribution to national security.
2. MAJOR PROGRAMMES
2.1. The vision of the DAE is to empower India through technology, creation of more wealth and
improving standard of living of its citizens. To realise its vision, the Department has been engaged in the
design, construction and operation of nuclear power/research reactors and the associated nuclear fuel
cycle technologies across the country. Over the years, the Department has been able to establish a
network of institutions engaged in R&D as well as industrial activities and acquired expertise in all
aspects of the nuclear fuel cycle.
2.2 At present, Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) is spearheading India's Nuclear
Power Programme with 21 Nuclear Power Reactors in operation, with total installed capacity of 5780
MW at seven locations. Of these reactors, the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Project-1 (KKNPP-1) of 1000
MWe commenced commercial operation from December 31, 2014. The second unit KKNPP-2 is also
in advanced stage of commissioning. First pair of indigenously designed 700 MWe Pressurised Heavy
Water Reactors (PHWRs) at Kakrapar in Gujarat followed by second pair at Rawatbhata in Rajasthan are
under construction. Work on the reactors to be launched in the third stage is being pursued in right
earnest.
2.3 DAE is also developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, super computers,
advanced materials and instrumentation and encouraging transfer of technology to the industry, which

2
could contribute to the national prosperity. A wide range of technologies have been developed to come
to the present stage of harnessing nuclear energy and efforts are continuing to develop such technologies
further with future requirements in mind. Technologies to be developed for future applications include
those necessary for building accelerator driven sub-critical reactor system (ADS), high temperature
reactor systems and fusion technologies. Several applications of radiation and isotope technologies for
improving the quality of life have been developed and efforts on this front are to be continued.
2.4 The Department is also engaged in the development of radiation technologies and their applications
for better crop varieties, techniques for crop protection, radiation-based post harvest technologies,
techniques for radio diagnosis and radio therapy of diseases particularly cancer, technologies for safe
potable water, better environment and industrial growth.
2.5 DAE contributes to the enrichment of knowledge domain by pursuing basic research in nuclear
energy and related frontier areas of science; interaction with universities and academic institutions;
support to research and development project having a bearing in DAE's programmes, and international
cooperation in related advanced areas of research.
2.6 The Department of Atomic Energy has mastered a host of highly complex technologies particularly
in the field of medical radiology, nuclear agriculture, desalination, food irradiation, radio astronomy,
supercomputing, particle accelerators, plasma and cryogenics. True to the visions of Dr. Homi J. Bhabha
and Jawaharlal Nehru of envisaging the usage of atomic energy to make a difference to the social life
of Indians, the DAE should now strive to scale it up and take it to the next level. The Committee feels
that the share of nuclear power which accounts for only 3.45 per cent of India's total electricity
generation, needs to be increased to the higher proportions.
3. ORGANISATIONS/INSTITUTIONS/CENTRES/PSUS
The DAE, over the years, has established a vast network of institutions and evolved into a broad-
based organisation comprising research centres, industrial units, public sector undertakings and fully
aided autonomous institutions along with service organisations. These organisations are engaged in
research, technology development and commercial operations in the areas of nuclear power and associated
technologies, radiation technologies and their applications, basic research and academic programmes to
support, sustain and carry forward its avowed objectives and activities. So far, it has established 107
installations spread across several States of the country. The organisations/institutions under the ambit
of DAE, category-wise, are as follows:
• Research Centres: Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC)- Mumbai; Indira Gandhi Centre
for Atomic Research (IGCAR)- Kalpakkam; Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology
(RRCAT)- Indore; Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC)- Kolkata; and Atomic Minerals
Directorate for Exploration and Research (AMD) - Hyderabad; Global Centre for Nuclear
Energy Partnership (GCNEP), Gurgaon.
• Industrial Organisations: Heavy Water Board (HWB) - Mumbai; Nuclear Fuel Complex (NFC)-
Hyderabad; and Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology (BRIT)-Mumbai.
• Service Organisations: Directorate of Purchase & Stores (DPS)- Mumbai, Directorate of
Construction, Services and Estate Management (DCSEM)- Mumbai; and General Services
Organisation (GSO)- Kalpakkam.

4. GRANTS
There are two Grants, i.e., Grant No. 4 (Atomic Energy) and Grant No. 5 (Nuclear Power
Schemes) under which Demands for Grants of the DAE are provided. Grants for six research Centres,
three industrial Organisations, three Public Sector Undertakings; Aided Institutions; three Service
Organisations; Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB), etc. are covered under Grant No. 4. Grant No.
5 comprises of budgetary allocations mainly for NPCIL and the Bharatiya Nabhikiya Vidyut Nigam
Limited (BHAVINI) besides Bhabha Atomic Research Centre for Waste Water Management facilities and
Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research for Prototype Fast Breeder Test Reactor.

7.3 The trend of underutilisation of approved BE outlay is perceptible every year. The Committee finds
that in none of the two years i.e. 2012-13, 2013-14, the DAE has been able to utilise fully the BE
allocation. While the Plan allocations for the year 2012-13 and 2013-14 were `4,601.73 crore and
`5,163.86 crore respectively, for Atomic Energy the actual utilisations were to the tune of ` 2,539.75
crore and ` 3,473.18 crore respectively, thus, leaving ` 2,061.98 crore and `1,690.68 crore unutilised
respectively.
7.4 Similarly, BE Plan allocation of ` 970.00 crore in 2014-15 was reduced to ` 562.60 crore at RE
stage. The Department, while explaining the reason behind the reduction in allocation, stated that even
though huge commitments under the Plan had already been incurred and expenditure has also been
booked, Ministry of Finance has reduced the budgetary allocation at RE stage keeping in mind the overall
availability of resources at their level.
7.5 Like-wise in the Power Sector under Demand No. 5, the Committee finds that the actual utilisation
of allocated resources was far less than the approved allocation. While in the year 2012-13 and
2013-14, the Budgetary Support allocation under Plan Head was ` 998.27 crore and ` 716.14 crore
respectively, the actual utilisation in the corresponding years stood at ` 571.88 crore and 581.62 crore
respectively.
7.6 It is surprising to note that BHAVINI which is entrusted with the task to plan, execute and operate
an integrated programme of Fast Breeder Reactors - the second stage of the Nuclear Power Programme,
could utilise ` 571.88 crore only as against the approved outlay of ` 998.27 crore under Plan Head in
2012-13, thus leaving ` 426.39 crore underutilised in the year 2012-13. The situation slightly improved
in the year 2013-14 where the Department could utilise ` 581.62 crore as against the approved Plan
outlay of ` 716.14 crores. A comparison of the BE, RE and Actuals of Plan Head the last two years,
i.e., 2012-13, 2013-14 reveals that the Actuals always remain far less than the BE suggesting that BE
allocation does not present a realistic picture of the likely utilisation of allocated resources; although the
Department claims that the approved outlay is likely to be fully utilised by the end of the financial year.
Based on the above inferences, it can be safely concluded that the actual utilisation of budgetary support
under the Plan Head is really low.
7.7 The Committee feels that the budgetary restriction imposed by the Ministry of Finance, as
pointed by the DAE, was not the only reason for reduction in allocation at RE stage in 2012-13,
2013-14 and 2014-15, under-utilisation of outlay by the Department might have also played a role
in reduction at R.E. level.
7.8 The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department needs to improve its capability
to utilise the allocated resources particularly in the Plan Head through strict monitoring at
regular intervals of the physical and financial performance of the projects and obviate the
possibility of any reduction at Revised Estimate Stage. It is felt that considering the strategic
importance of the institutions of the Department of Atomic Energy, financial cuts should be
minimized and should not be insisted upon if strategic programmes were to be delayed as a result
thereof.

focussed but not confined to the development of futuristic nuclear reactors, fabricating nuclear fuels,
reprocessing of spent fuel, waste management, robotics, super-computers, lasers, accelerators, human
genomes, nano-technology, and super-conductivity. Other activities pursued by BARC include R&D for
application of radiation technologies (non-power application) in the areas of food preservation, radiation

12. VARIABLE ENERGY CYCLOTRON CENTRE (VECC)
12.1 The Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC) Kolkata is a premier research institute in the country
in nuclear sciences with excellent infrastructure for advanced R&D in the field of accelerator technology.
This Centre indigenously developed the first large accelerator in the country, the room temperature
cyclotron with K= 230 during seventies. It was stated that the Centre is installing a medical Cyclotron
both for research and medical purposes, which will serve as a major research facility for nuclear physics
and related fields.
12.2 VECC has been running a Regional Radiation Medicine Centre at Thakurpukur, which is the only
governmental nuclear medicine centre in the North Eastern region, providing state-of-the art facilities in
nuclear imaging at an affordable cost. The Committee has been given to understand that VECC has
continuously increased its support and involvement at the Regional Radiation Medicine Centre at the Saroj
Gupta Cancer and Research Institute at Thakurpukur which started with providing facilities for
Radioimmunoassay (RIA) tests. There is a shortage of nuclear medicine centres in eastern India. The
few that are there are private commercial organisations where the charges for nuclear imaging are
exorbitant. Thus, the patients from low income groups who need to undergo nuclear imaging come to
the Regional Radiation Medicine Centre which is the only governmental nuclear medicine centre in the
region.
12.3 The Department informed that around 200 patients undergo in-vitro RIA studies every month at the
Saroj Gupta Cancer Centre and Research Institute (SGCCRI). In collaboration with the SGCCRI, a
facility for High Dose Radio-Iodine-131 Therapy of Cancer of Thyroid patients is being operated regularly.

16

12.4 The Department has informed that India hosts two Tier-2 centres of the Large Hadron Collider Grid
(LCG) namely Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC) and Tata Institute of Fundamental Research
(TIFR). VECC is one of the two Tier-2 Centres of the Large Hadron Collider Grid other being the TIFR.

laboratory of the ALICE-India Collaboration, which consists of 13 Institutes and Universities in the
country.

14. INDIAN RARE EARTHS LIMITED
14.1 Indian Rare Earths Limited (IREL) is engaged in mining and production of beach sand minerals and
rare earth compounds. Some of the minerals produced by IREL find use in the Nuclear Power Programme
while the others have wide ranging industrial applications. The main minerals separated by IREL from
the beach sands at its three Units at Chavara, Kerala, Manavalakurichi, Tamil Nadu and Chatrapur, Odisha
are ilmenite, rutile, zircon and monazite. IREL also produces uranium, thorium, and rare earth salts.
14.2 IREL was involved in the production of Rare Earth compounds from monazite processing plant at
its unit in Rare Earths Division (RED), Aluva, Kerala from 1952 to 2004. About 1.47 lakh tons of
monazite had been processed by IREL during this period. While deposing before the Committee, the
Secretary, DAE informed that market conditions for RE products are extremely volatile and the domestic
demand for Rare Earth products could never exceed more than 10 per cent of the annual production.
As a result, about 90 percent of the production of IREL has usually been exported.
14.3 Explaining the efforts made by the Department to increase domestic production of rare earth
including monazite and whether there was any proposal to encourage the private Indian companies to
enter the field, the Department informed that in order to take advantage of the current demands of Rare
Earth in the International market, IREL has set up a 10,000 tons per annum monazite processing plant
at its unit in OSCOM, Odisha which will be producing about 11200 tpa of Mixed Rare Earth Chloride
(MRCL). Commissioning of the plant is in progress and IREL was awaiting clearance from AERB for
commercial plant operation. About 50 per cent of MRCL produced will be transported to IREL's plant
at RED, Aluva for production of separated Rare Earth of various purities including very high pure Rare
Earth oxides.
14.4 The Secretary further added that Monazite is an associated mineral found along with other beach
sand minerals. IREL has set up plant facilities for producing about 10,900 tpa of +96 per cent monazite
at its three units located in Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Odisha.
14.5 When asked about the reason behind the Department of Atomic Energy not permitting Indian
domestic companies that have resources and technical knowhow to assist in the exploration of Uranium

19
and other rare earth metals to enter the field, the Secretary informed that as regards Monazite, because
of its association with Thorium and Uranium, the DAE has not permitted to pass the contract to any
other private company. The Indian Rare Earths Limited was able to separate Monazite for the purpose
of separation of thorium. However, he added, there was no proposal to encourage private sector
companies to enter the field of commercial exploitation of Uranium, Thorium, Monazite and other rare
earth metals. Uranium, thorium and monazite are prescribed substances as per the Notification No. SO
61 (E) dated 18.01.2006 issued under the Atomic Energy Act, 1962. Monazite is also an atomic mineral
as per the First Schedule (part- B) of the Mines & Minerals (Development & Regulation) Act 1957.
14.6 The Committee understands that India has second largest Rare Earths reserves after China
and the prices of Rare Earths have increased significantly in the recent past. The Committee
feels that the Department needs to seriously explore the possibilities of increasing the production
of Rare Earths and if possible by involving private sector domestic companies forming joint

15. INDIA BASED NEUTRINO OBSERV ATORY PROJECT (INO)
15.1 The India-based Neutrino Observatory (INO) Project is a multi-institutional effort aimed at building
a world-class underground laboratory with a rock cover of approx.1200 metres for non-accelerator
based high energy and nuclear physics research in India.
Physical Performance
15.2 As regards budgetary allocation for pre-project activities, it was informed that ` 85.00 Crores have
been allocated as part of the project budget for pre-project activities and according to the Department,
the amount is sufficient to complete the pre-project activities. Informing about the timeframe for the
project, it was stated that the pre-project activities could be completed after getting necessary approvals
in about one-and-a-half-year's time.
15.3 Regarding pre-project activities started for the observatory, it was informed by the Department that
construction of the boundary wall at Madurai as well as the chain link fencing at the INO site have been
completed. Water pipeline has been laid to bring water to the project from the nearby river. Various
detector components to construct the Engineering Module at Madurai have been ordered. The construction
of the road to the INO project site has been taken up by the Tamil Nadu Highways Department. There
is temporary disruption in these activities due to the Orders of the Hon'ble High Court and lack of some
clearances by the Tamil Nadu Government. The pre-project activities can be completed by 31 March,
2016 subject to clearances by the Hon'ble High Court and the Tamil Nadu Government.
15.4 The Committee hopes that all the pre-project activities, construction of the underground
and surface facilities will be completed as per the schedule by 2016 and the Iron Calorimeter
(ICAL) detector will be completely ready in four years.
15.5 The project includes construction of an underground laboratory and associated surface facilities at
Pottipuram in Bodi West hills of Theni District of Tamil Nadu; construction of a Iron Calorimeter (ICAL)
detector for studying neutrinos and setting up of National Centre for High Energy Physics at Madurai,
for the operation and maintenance of the underground laboratory, human resource development and

20
detector R&D along with its applications. The underground laboratory, consisting of a large cavern of
size 132m X 26m X 20m and several smaller caverns, will be accessed by a 2100 m long and 7.5 m
wide tunnel.
15.6 The initial goal of INO is to study neutrinos. The ICAL detector is designed to address some of
the key open problems in a unique way. Over the years, this underground facility is expected to develop
into a full-fledged underground science laboratory for other studies in physics, biology, geology, hydrology
etc. Development of detector technology and its varied applications are an important aspect of the
project.
15.7 In response to the query relating to the likely benefits of INO, it was informed by the Department
that the primary goal of the INO is to study the properties of the fundamental particle called" neutrino"
which is the most exciting area of research in particle physics after the discovery of Higgs Boson. In
the light of Neutrino research having attracted serious attention of the scientific community world over,
the project will have an international standing and will have a good potential in bringing out scientific
findings of very high impact. The project will also lead to Human Resource Development in basic science
research and strong industrial base in detector technology. Science students across the country will have
the opportunity to develop sophisticated detectors and electronics using their own hands.
15.8 Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai is the host institute for INO project. All
INO related activities will be coordinated through the INO centre, which is to be located at Madurai.
This centre will be responsible for the operation and maintenance of the underground laboratory, human
resource development and detector R& D. The construction of the underground and surface facilities
is expected to be completed in four years and the entire ICAL detector will be completed in the next
three years. Preliminary data taking is expected to start in 2020. The budgetary outlay earmarked for this
purpose is ` 1583.05 Crores.
15.9 The Committee takes note of the joint initiative taken by the Department of Atomic Energy
and the Department of Science & Technology, in setting up the India-based Neutrino observatory
for cutting-edge research in Neutrino studies. The Committee recommends that necessary
budgetary provisions should be made for the research facility in particle physics.
15.10 The Committee hopes that observatory in years to come will turn into an observatory of
excellence providing high quality research facility in Neutrino studies.
15.11 Disposal of construction debris of INO has been one of the controversial issues amongst the local
people around Madurai that has drawn attention of the media. When Committee asked about the manner
in which the Department was going to dispose the construction debris without affecting the natural
resources in neighbourhood areas, the Department informed that the tunnel excavation debris are hard
charnockite granite rock pieces which is used in construction. The excavated rock will be the property
of Tamil Nadu Forest Department. The rock debris will be temporarily stored in a storage yard within
the project site. This storage yard will be protected by dry stone masonry wall, all around except for
entry and exit points, to prevent contamination of any nearby water sources. Therefore, it is not likely
to affect the natural resources in neighbourhood areas. The Department also informed that the project
site is at least 2 km away from the nearest village and it has been proposed by the State of Tamil Nadu

21
that the disposal of this material may be entrusted to TAMIN Ltd, Salem and the sale proceed may be
remitted to Tamil Nadu Forest Department.
15.12 Dispelling the doubts about the length of the tunnel that is required for the INO project, the
Department informed that the tunnel is only 1.9 km long. This tunnel will be a horizontal tunnel just like
road tunnels and there is no question of harming the mountain due to the construction of a 2 km long
tunnel. Geo-technical studies were carried out by Geological Survey of India before finalising the details
of the tunnel.
15.13 The Committee, while taking note of the explanation given by the Department on disposal
of construction debris and utility of the ICAL detector, would like to emphasise that grievances
and apprehensions of the local people need to be dispelled. For that purpose, a proper public
outreach programme to educate the local people about potential benefits of Indian-Neutrino
Observatory should be started in local language and through the social media tools.

POWER SECTOR
The Committee finds that the projections of the Department, particularly, regarding setting
up of nuclear power plants are prone to time and cost over-run. NPCIL in 2014-15 had planned
to produce 34,828 Million Units. However , upto December, it could produce only 27,284 Million
units. Similarly, the operating performance of NPCIL in 2013-14 was not very impressive. Similarly,
as per the Mid-Term Appraisal document, the Fast Breeder Reactor at Kalpakkam was supposed
to be commissioned by March, 2012, finally that reactor achieved criticality on 31 December 2014.
Target for Kudankulam unit-2 was rescheduled from September 2014 to January 2016. The
Committee understands the hurdles were faced by the Department but it is of the view that
considering the enormous energy challenge and the grim energy situation of the country, the
Department has to work harder and needs to be fully supported by all concerned in this regard.
(Para 18.10)
The Committee is of the view that the Department needs to accelerate the pace of construction
of nuclear reactors and reduce the average construction time taken from seven-eight years to
five years. Currently, India has an installed capacity of 5,780 MWe of nuclear power, including
the 1,000 MW of electricity being generated by recently inducted Unit-I at Kudankulam. The
1,000 mw Unit-II at Kudankulam is also slated to begin power production soon. Five nuclear
reactors with a total capacity of 3,300 mw are under various stages of construction. In addition,
work on 19 new reactors, with a cumulative generation capacity of 17,400 MW, is scheduled to
begin in the next few years. If all these start producing electricity between 2025 and 2027, the
total installed capacity would still come to only 27,840 MW. However, the Integrated Energy
Policy in 2006 had projected a target of 63,000 MW of nuclear energy by 2032. This still leaves
the huge gap of 35160 MWe to be covered. (Para 18.11)
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should mobilise its resources
and intensify its efforts to ensure that the proposed nuclear project are completed and commissioned
within the scheduled deadline in order to achieve the target of producing 63,000 MW of electricity
through nuclear energy by 2032. (Para 18.12)
