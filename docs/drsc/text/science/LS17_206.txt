REPORT 
 
Despite being a developing economy, I ndia has effectivel y developed space 
technology and has applied it su ccessfully for its rapid de velopment and today it is 
offering a variety of sp ace services globally. India has come a long  way in mastering 
the complex space tec hnology in a self-reliant manner . The ever challenging task 
of space technology devel opment and its utilis ation, has gradua lly graduated from 
experimental and demonstrat ion phases to an operation al era, commendable and 
notable progress has been ma de in the design, development and operation of space 
systems, as well as, using them for vital services like telecommunications, 
television broadcasting, meteorology, disaster management support and natural 
resources survey and management, which are of direct relevance for national 
development. The space programme has become largely self-reliant with capability 

to design and build satellites for providing space services and to launch them using 
indigenously designed and deve loped launch vehicles. Th e diverse roles of space 
technology and services in various fro nts - social, economic, commercial and 
strategic - have made the space systems an important component of our national 
infrastructure.  
 
2.  OBJECTIVES OF INDIAN SPACE PROGRAMME 
2.1. The primary objective of the Indian Space Programme is to promote the 
development and application of space technology and space science for the                        
socio-economic benefit of the nation thr ough establishment of operational space 
services in a self-reliant manner. The main thrust areas of the programme are: 
 Satellite-based communication for various applications. 
 Satellite-based resources survey and management and environmental monitoring. 
 Meteorological applications 
 Development and operationali sation of indigenous satell ites, launch vehicles and 
associated ground segment for providing space-based services. 
3. ORGANISATIONAL SETUP 
3.1. The Space Commission and the Department of Space (DOS) were set up in 1972.  
DOS implements the policies framed by th e Space Commission.  These are carried out 
through the Indian Space Resear ch Organisation (ISRO) and th e Grant-in-aid institutions 
viz., Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), National Atmospheric Research Laboratory 
(NARL), North-Eastern Space Applicati ons Centre (NE-SAC), Semi-conductor 
Laboratory (SCL) and Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST).  
3.2 The major Centres, Units and establishments of DOS/ ISRO, responsible for 
carrying out research and development activities as well as for undertaking various 
projects and programmes, are :- 
(a) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, which provides 
the technology base for the country’ s indigenous launch vehicle (rocket) 
development. 
(b) Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC),  with its facilities at Bangalore, 
Thiruvananthapuram and Mahendragiri, which is responsible for development 
of liquid propulsion and cryogenic propulsion systems for satellite and launch 
vehicles. 
(c) Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) at Sriharikota (Andhra Pradesh), 
is the launch base.  It also provides th e facilities for processing of solid rocket 
motors. 

(d) ISRO Satellite Centre (ISAC), Bangalor e, which is entrusted with the prime 
responsibility for satellite development.      
(e) The Laboratory for Electro-Optics Syst ems (LEOS), Bangalore which has the 
responsibility for development and produc tion of electro-optical sensors for 
satellites. 
(f) Space Applications Centre (SAC), Ah medabad, which carries out research 
and development in space applications as well as development of application 
payloads. 
(g) Development and Educational Comm unication Unit (DECU), Ahmedabad, 
which is involved in the conception, definition, planning and socio/techno-
economic evaluation of development communication programmes. 
(h) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRS C), Hyderabad is responsible for 
satellite data acquisition and processi ng, data dissemination, aerial remote 
sensing and decision support for disaster  management. (The erstwhile NRSA, 
an autonomous society, was converted into NRSC as a Government entity 
w.e.f. 1st September 2008);  
(i) ISRO Telemetry, Tracking & Command Network (ISTRAC), with its ground 
stations at Bangalore, Sriharikota,  Lucknow, Bearslake(U.K.), Biak 
(Indonesia), Brunei and Mauritius, whic h provides the telemetry, tracking and 
command support and spacecraft mission ma nagement for the Indian remote 
sensing satellite missions, space scien ce missions and satellite launch vehicle 
programmes. 
(j) Master Control Facility (MCF) with its main facility at Hassan, Karnataka and 
backup facility at Bhopal, Madhya Prad esh which has the responsibility for 
control and operation of INSAT/GSAT/METSAT satellites. 
(k) ISRO Inertial Systems Unit (IISU), Thiruvananthapuram, which carries out 
research and development in the area of  inertial systems for satellites and 
launch vehicles. 
3.3 A National Natural Resources Manage ment System (NNRMS), with the 
Department of Space (DOS) as the nodal agency, has been established to ensure optimal 
utilisation of natural reso urces using remote sensing data in conj unction with 
conventional techniques. Under NNRMS, five Regional Remote Sensing Service Centres 
(RRSSCs) at Bangalore, Dehradun, Jodhpur, Khar agpur and Nagpur have been set up for 
execution of remote sensing application pr ogrammes in the respective regions.  With 
effect from December 2009, the RRSSCs have  been redesignated as Regional Remote 
Sensing Centres (RRSCs) and amalgamated with NRSC).      
3.4 The organisations receiving Grants-in- aid under the Department of Space are: 

(a) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Ahmedabad, an autonomous 
institution supported mainly by the Department of Space, carries out 
research in space sciences. 
(b) The National Atmospheric Research La boratory (NARL) set up at Gadanki 
near Tirupati, conducts atmospheric research. 
(c) The North-Eastern Space Applications Centre (NE-SAC) established as an 
autonomous body under the Department of Space provides space technology 
inputs for developmental activities in the North-Eastern Region.  
(d) The Semi-conductor Laboratory located at  Chandigarh is a research and 
development society and is entrusted with the design and development of 
Very Large Scale Integrated Circu it (VLSI) devices and development of 
micro-electronic systems to meet the strategic needs of the country.  
(e) The Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST) is an 
autonomous body under the Department w ith the objective of creating 
quality human resources tune d to suit the requirements of state-of-art space   
research programme.  The Institute offers graduate, post-graduate and 
doctoral programmes in the area of space science, technology and 
applications.  IIST has started the c ourses from the academic year 2007-08 
around the existing infrastructure at Thiruvananthapuram.  
3.5 Apart from this, the RESPOND programme of ISRO supports sponsored research 
activity in space science, space applications and space technology in various national 
academic/research institutions and Space Technology Cells in premier technological 
institutes of the country through grants-in-aid.  
3.6 The Secretariat of Department of Space and the Headquarters of ISRO are located 
in Bangalore. Programme Offices at ISRO Headquarters co-ordinate the overall 
programmes like satellite communication, Satellite Navigation, earth observation, launch 
vehicle, space science, atmospheric science, space industry development, disaster 
management support, international co-operation, publications and public relations, budget 
and economic analysis and human resource development.  
3.7 The Antrix Corporation Limited, Banga lore, established in 1992, is a wholly 
owned Government Company for marketing space products and services.

4. MONITORING  MECHANISMS FOR ONGOING SCHEMES 
4.1 The Department informed the Committee that in order to monitor and review the 
progress of all the ongoing projects / scheme s and to provide overall directions, the 
Department has evolved a two- tier project management stru cture consisting of Project / 

Programme Management Council and Projec t Management Board / Project Executive 
Committee (PMC/PMB/PEX) for all the major projects / schemes in the area of Satellites, 
Launch Vehicles and Space applications.  Before formulating the budget proposals, 
detailed and critical analysis  and reviews are carried out by the ISRO Headquarters with 
the Centres/Units/Projects. The budgeted items  are reviewed in totality including Plan 
and Non-plan components. The ISRO Council undertakes a rigorous review of all the 
budget proposals, addressing the managerial/ programmatic / financ ial aspects preceded 
by detailed technical review of  the programs/ projects / activities.  The essentiality of 
each item, the schedule-budget linkages and cash flow requirements are addressed during 
these reviews.  The overall budget proposal s thus formulated ar e reviewed by Space 
Commission, a high level body constituted by Gove rnment of India, before submitting 
the proposals to the Government.

6.       BUDGETARY ALLOCATION 
6.1 From the information furnished by the Department of Space on the budget 
approved and actually utilized during the last three years, i.e., 2007-08, 2008-09 and 
2009-10,  the Committee finds that there has been a consistent reduction in the outlay at 
the revised estimates level.  The total budgetary allocation of Rs. 3858.80 crores, 4074.07 
crores and 4959.04 crores at Budget Estimates (B.E.) level during the financial years 
2007-08, 2008-09 and 2009-10 respectively have  been reduced to Rs. 3290.09, Rs. 
3499.04 and Rs.4167.04 in the respective years at the R.E. Stage.  When the Committee 
enquired from the Department as to whether the reduction at the R. E. Stage  has been 
made due to underutilization of funds allocated at B.E. level or because of some other 
reasons, the Department stated that the allocat ion have been reduced to comply with the 
ceilings fixed by the Ministry of Finance during Revised Estimates.  The Department 
further stated that in fact  it projected budgetary requirements of Rs. 5119.98 crores for 
revised Budgets 2009-10 taking into account th e programmatic aspects and spendability.  
The Committee is surprised to note that desp ite the actual expenditure  being as high as 
99.63 per cent, 99.84 per cent during th e financial years 2007-08 and 2008-09 
respectively and the R.E. allocation expected to  be fully utilized in the Financial Year 
2009-10.  The compulsory reduction by fixing ceilings by Ministry of Finance is not at all 
justified.  The Committee expresses its concern over such a pr actice adopted by the 
Ministry of Finance, especially for the scie ntific departments like Department of Space 
which has done our country proud by landmark achievements.  The Committee, 
therefore, recommends that the Department of Space should be exempted from such 
ceilings in future and there must not be any reduction at R.E. stage for this 
Financial Year.  The Committee also recommends that incase the Department 
requires more resources at Revised Estimates stage, it should be positively 
considered and desired allocation should the granted by the Ministry of Finance. 
6.2  The Committee has been informed that  the Department of Space has projected a 
budgetary demand of Rs. 7485.56 crores for the year 2010-11 at the Budgetary Estimates 
stage.  However, the total financial outlay allocated fina lly is Rs. 5778.00 crores which 
includes Rs.5000.00 crores for plan and Rs. 778.00 crores for non-plan expenditure.  The 
Committee finds that in net term s, there is an increase of Rs. 819 crores this year as 
against the last year allocation of Rs. 4959.00 cr ores at the B.E. stage.  The Committee 
notes with concern that this increase is quite  negligible if inflationary trends are taken 
into consideration. The Committee, therefore, expresses its serious concern over non-
adequate allocation of budgetary provisi ons for the Department of Space and 
recommends that sufficient budgetary provisions  should be made for the Department in 
order to enable it to carve a niche in space technology and space applications.   

6.3  When the Committee enquired from the Department as to whether the budgetary 
allocations made for 2010-11 are sufficient to  carry out the planned activities of the 
department,   it was informed that funding for some of the new projects/schemes, such as, 
Cartosat-3, TES-Hyp, GISAT, GSAT-13 a nd follow on Missions and Human Space 
Flight Programme have been phased out to subsequent years in order to contain the 
funding within the allocated funds.  Th e Committee understands that these new 
projects/schemes which are basically meant for the purpose of earth observation are 
going to suffer due to lack of funds which is  not at all a healthy scenario if such a 
dynamic and performance oriented department  like Space is made to suffer from the 
shortage of funds.  In such a situation th e possibility of desp eration and lack of 
motivation among scientists due to resource crunch cannot be ruled out.  The 
Committee, therefore, recommends that th e Department of Space should not be 
made to starve of funds in future and expects that Government will extend all 
budgetary supports required by the Department in future. 
7. MANNED MISSION INITIATIVE  
7.1 The Committee finds that in 2009-10, the Manned Mission Initiative/ Human 
Space Flight Programme was allocated Rs. 238 crores at B.E. stage which came down to 
Rs. 30 crores at R.E. stage.  When the Committee desired to know the reasons for the 
drastic reduction of allocation at R.E. stag e, it was informed that the Department 
submitted the Budget Report for the Human Space Flight Programme which had been 
reviewed and cleared by Space Commission, as well as, by a high level committee 
chaired by the Deputy Chairman, Planning Commission.  However, it was indicated 
through the subsequent discussions that th e Human Space Flight Programme could be 
taken up in phases and the first phase shoul d focus on developing critical technologies, 
such as, crew module, service module, crew escape system, environment control and life 
support systems.  The R.E. provision, therefor e, has been reduced in view of the pre-
project activities.  When the Committee enquired about the im portance of this 
programme from the Secretary, Department of  Space it was informed that it is a very 
crucial and important programme because ma ny countries, like U.S.A., Russia, China, 
Japan, etc., are looking at the ha bitat in Mars which is consid ered a better place for life, 
with full of water, land bearing clouds, atmosphere, pressure, etc. by 2030-2035.  Moon 
becomes an inter-mediary point where they should put a habitat and have the propulsion 
system made and go up.  The Committee fails to understand that when the Space 
Commission, the highest policy making body of the Department of Space has given ‘a go 
ahead’ to this programme, why stumbling blocks were created at the Government level?  
Planning Commission should not come in the way of conception an d realization of a 
programme only because it is instrumental in providing allocations for the Department.   
7.2  The Committee is of the view that Human Space Flight Programme is a very 
important one which should not only be take n up in right earnest,  but also be made 
successful at the earliest, so that India is not left far behind in the race of making a habitat 
in mars.  The Committee strongly recommends that the right to take final decision in 
scientific and technical matters should be vested with the Department of Space only 
and financial constraints should not be allowed to come in its way towards progress 
of these crucial and critical programmes. 
7.3 It has been observed that higher allocations are made on certain 
schemes/programmes at Budgetary Estimates level 2009-10, but they remain unutilized at 

the R.E. stage owing to some reasons or th e other. Provision of Rs . 155.00 crores for the 
Semi-Cryogenic Engine Development and its re duction to 36.00 crores, allocation of Rs. 
90 crores for Indian Lunar Mission Chandr ayan I & II and its re duction to Rs. 19.50 
crores at R.E. stage are some of the gl aring instances. When the Committee enquired 
about the reasons for such a gross underutilization of funds under the above programmes, 
the Department replied that in  case of semi cryogine engi ne Development the provision 
has been reduced at R.E. stage because the project was approve d in January, 2009.  
Secondly, due to complexities of the technolo gies and iterative process involved in 
evolving the engine design, th e specification details of th e engine and engine test 
facilities took more time than anticipated.  For Indian Lunar Mission, the Department 
stated that more than 30 experimental pr oposals received for conducting scientific 
investigations on moon onboard are being currently evaluated by an Expert Committee to 
finalise the scientific payloads to be flow n up Chandrayan-2. Pendi ng the finalization of 
the payloads and their specifications/in terface details, the activities related to 
procurement of components/materials and sub-sy stem fabrications has been re-scheduled 
to next year.  Hence, the budget during the current year has been reduced at R.E. Stage. 
7.4  The Committee feels that demand for these schemes/programmes had been made 
without doing proper homework and extensive and critical assessment of the actual 
requirements.  The Committee feels that budgetary demands should be sought by the 
Department only after comprehensive analys is of the actual requirements for the 
programme.  Scarce resources should not be ma de to be blocked by asking for ambitious 
allocations and not utilizing them subsequently.  The Committee recommends that 
such instances, though, only a few should be avoided in future by the Department.   
8. INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION 
8.1 The Committee is pleased and proud to no te that India is be ing recognized today 
as one of the fast growing and a leading country in the field of space programmes.  A 
number of developed countries  like, U.S.A., U.K., Russia,  France, Germany, etc., are 
seeking cooperation and actively participati ng in our space programmes.  India’s maiden 
mission to Moon, the Chandrayan-I is a classic example of international cooperation with 
its international payloads  – including Moon Mineralogy, Mapper of NASA  which 
identified presence of water molecules on the Moon.  While the Committee extends its 
compliment to the Indian scientists for su ch a path breaking and landmark achievements, 
it expresses its displeasure over the way cred it for this has been taken by others.  An 
impression has been given through the mass media that it is basically a finding conducted 
by foreign scientists and not by the Indian scientists.  When the Committee enquired from 
the Department, the circumstances under which such an impression has been created, the 
Secretary, Department of Space explained that  NASA put two instruments, i.e., (i) Moon 
Mineralogy Mapping, (ii) Microw ave Payload, onboard Chandray an.  In fact, they were 
the primary instruments which found water on the Moon.  He further explained that based 
on an arrangement of the instruments that were carried by other countries on Chandrayan, 
we had investigated from our side.  It was a joint paper which was written by the 
American and Indian scientists.  The Secretary further added that the first press statement 
was issued in the U.S. and then it was done  here.  This was done standing between the 
two because the prime instruments were from  the U.S.  The Committee, however, is not 
convinced by the argument given by the Secretary.  The Co
 mmittee recommends that 
in future, in case of joint collaboration, it must be ensured that there is a clear 

understanding between the participatin g countries that credit for landmark 
achievements should be shared equally and the announcement is made jointly.   
  PARTICIPATION OF SPACE INDUSTRY PARTNERSHIP 
8.2 The Committee enquired from the Depart ment whether it has evolved any policy 
for involvement and participation of Indian i ndustry in manufacturing and 
development of space technology.   The Secretary, Department of Space informed the 
Committee that it has been the stated policy, ri ght from 1970s that the services Indian 
industry will be used to the maximum possible extent.  It was further informed that as on 
today the Department has association with mo re than 500 small, medium and large-scale 
industries and about 60-65 per cent of our budge t allocation goes to th e Indian industry 
both in public and private sectors.   
8.3  The Committee appreciates the efforts ma de by the Department in this direction, 
however, it feels that in this buoyant space market, there are great opportunities for space 
technology and Department of Space should invol ve and nurture Indian  industry to play 
leading role as establis hed manufacturer and supplie r in the global market.  The 
Committee is of the opinion that involvement and participation of more and more 
Indian industries will lead to self-reliance and would bring down our dependence on 
foreign industries for equipm ents and technologies.  Ex pansion of Indian industry 
would also lead to large-scale employmen t generation.  The Committee, therefore, 
recommends that the Department of Space sh ould make concerted efforts in this 
direction.  The Committee also desires th at Department of Space should look 
forward to transfer more and more technolo gies which may be us eful for civilian 
applications to Indian Industries.   
  INVOLVEMENT OF YOUT H IN SPACE PROGRAMMES  
8.4 The Committee enquired from the Department about the status of interests 
enthusiasm and zeal among youth towards joining space programme and take up space 
science as a career.  The Secretary inform ed the Committee that a few years ago the 
position was that about 5 to 15 percent youngsters  used to leave after training.  But now 
on the one hand Chandrayaan Mission has given a lot of encouragement to the youngsters 
to get into this programme and on the ot her hand Sixth Pay Commission has given 20 
percent extra payment based on organizationa l performance and about 10 percent on the 
individual performance. This marks a pay packet  which is 30 percent more than the other 
agencies.  As such there has been no attrition in the last two years.  The Secretary further 
informed the Committee that we are seeing large number of applicants for our induction 
programme now.  
8.5 While the Committee expresses its sati sfaction over the current indicators of 
involvement of youth in space programmes, it desires that the Department of space 
should make all out efforts to encourage  more and more youngsters to join its 
programme.  The Committee, therefore, r ecommends that the Department should 
accord priority to this init iative.  A long term goal shou ld be fixed for involvement  
of youth in space programmes. 
R&D PROGRAMMING

areas of relevance to the space program me. RESPOND also suppo rts basic research

8.7 But the Committee finds that for such an important programme the allocation this 
year has been reduced to Rs. 15.00 crores as  against Rs. 16.50 crores  last year at the 
Revised Estimates level.  The Committee feels that the budget allocated for this 
programme is not sufficient as the Development should further intensify and strengthen it 
so that adequate quality human resources are created to meet the future requirements.  
The Committee, therefore, recommends that  the Department should seek more 
resource at R.E. level under this programme, if need be.   
8.8 It has been informed to the Committee th at the Department of Space envisages to 
setup an institute to carry out focused research in climate change and environment. When 
the Committee desired to know about the progr ess made in this regard the Department 
stated that this idea has b een mooted jointly with the Ministries of Environment & 
Forests, Science and Technology and Department of Space.  It was further informed that 
this proposal has to be got cleared in the inter Ministerial Comm ittee.  The Committee 
observed that no allocation whatsoever had be en made under this programme during the 
year 2009-2010.  This year also only a token provision of Rs. 1 crore has been made.  
The Committee fails to understand as to w hy such an important programme on climate 
change is not being taken seriously.  The Committee feels that when the adverse effects 
of global warming and climate change have  started manifesting in many forms, the

National Institute of Climate Change and Environment Studies be  made functional 
at the earliest and preferably during this financial year.

  SPACE APPLICATIONS 
9. TELEMEDICINE   
9.1 Telemedicine is one of the unique app lications of Space T echnology for societal 
benefit.  DOS Telemedicine programme, which started in 2001 has been connecting 
remote/rural/medical college hospitals a nd Mobile Units thr ough INSAT and EDUSAT 
to major specialty hospitals in cities and towns. The Committee has been informed that 
the telemedicine network has been further e xpanded to cover the areas of Mobile Tele-
health and Continuing Medical Education. Th e network covers the areas of Jammu & 
Kashmir, Ladakh, Andaman and Lakshdweep Islands, North Easter n States, mainland 
states including tribal districts of mainla nd states like Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu 
Chhatisgarh, Punjab, West Bengal, Orissa, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Jaharkhand and 
Rajastan.   
9.2 Presently, the ISRO telemedicine ne twork covers, about 382 hospitals. 306 
remote/rural/district/medical college hospitals connected to 60 specialty hospitals. 
Sixteen Mobile Telemedicine units have also  been established. The Mobile Telemedicine 
units cover diverse areas of Opthalmo logy, Cardiology, Radiology, Diabetology, 
Mammography, general medicine, women and child health. 
9.3 Under the programme, the Department of Space provides Telemedicine systems 
software, hardware and communication equipment as well as give satellite bandwidth. 
State governments and the speciality hospitals give funds for the infrastructure, 
manpower and maintenance. The Committee is of the opinion that this is a very useful 
and workable proposition as far as providing quality health care facilities in remote and 
far flung areas of the country is concerned. The Committee recommends that the 
telemedicine network should be expanded further by covering the most backward 
states, extreme remote and inaccessible and disease-prone areas which have been 
left out so far.  
  TELE-EDUCATION 
9.4  EDUSAT launched in 2004 is India’s firs t thematic satellite dedicated exclusive 
to education services, which is already providing a wide range of educa tional delivery 
modes including one way TV broadcast, in teractive TV, Video conferencing, Computer 
conferencing and Web based instructions at present there are more than 5617 interactive 
classrooms and 46541 receive only classr ooms thus making it a total of around 52158 
classrooms.  About 15 to 18 lack stude nts are being benefitted through EDUSAT 
annually and its networks have been setup in 24 states.  The Committee feels that this is a 
very path breaking educationa l programme which has the pote ntial to go a long way in 
raising the literacy rate of the country.  The Committee therefo re recommends that 
EDUSAT programme should be  expeditiously implemented in remaining states of 
the country.  
 DISASTER MANAGEMENT SUPPORT SYSTEM 
9.5 The Committee has been informed th at the main objective of Disaster 
Management Support (DMS) Programme run by th e Department is to provide timely and 
reliable space inputs and servi ces to the DMS in th e country and is a vital area of space 
applications.  The major initiatives planned during 11
th five year Plan period includes 
realization of a National Data Base for Emergency Management, Impact mapping and 
monitoring support for Disaster events, Satellite based communication support for 

Disaster Management, strengthening of early warning systems and development of tools 
and techniques for decision support systems for Disaster Management.  
9.6 The work on realisation of a National Data Base for Emergency Management has 
been initiated.  Besides creation of data laye rs, several application tools like river-watch 
tool, flood hazard information system, flood disa ster decision support system, etc., have 
been developed. An Airborne Synthetic Aper ture Radar (ASAR) has been developed for 
aircraft based surveys as a part of disaster management support system and demonstration 
flights have been completed. Towards emergency communication for disaster 
management activities, a Virtual Private Ne twork (VPN) has been set up with 29 Nodes 
networking the State and District level emerge ncy control centres with Ministry of Home 
Affairs for near real time management of natura l disasters.   As a part of impact mapping 
and monitoring support, over 160 Flood Inundati on maps were generated covering the 
flood disaster events in Andhr a Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Ori ssa, Uttar Pradesh and West 
Bengal States.   The Government have also approved setting up of dedicated aerial survey 
facilities consisting of exclus ive aircraft, sensor instruments and onboard real time data 
processing facilities to support Disaster Management System in the country.  
9.7     The Committee is of the view that India has been traditionally vulnerable to 
natural disasters on account of its unique geo-climatic conditions. Floods, droughts, 
cyclones, earthquakes and landslides have been a recurrent phenomena and a lot of 
economic devastation and human loss is caused on account of these natural 
calamities. The Committee feels that the s ervices of Virtual Private Network (VPN) 
which has been set up for networking the St ate and district leve l emergency control 
centres for near real time natural disaster s management must be optimally utilized 
for rapid dissemination of information to minimize the human and material loss. 
  Keeping in view the excellent and ou tstanding work that the Department of 
Space has undertaken, the kind of competen ce that the Department has exhibited 
and the kind of core and critical programme s that they have en visaged for future, 
the Committee strongly recommends that the Department of Space should be 
properly and adequately funded and any kind of reduction in outlay at R.E. Stage 
should be strictly avoided.
