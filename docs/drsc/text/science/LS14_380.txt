on Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change deals with the action taken by  
the Government  on the recommendations/observations of the  Committee contained in its ‘Three 
Hundred Seventy Third Report’ on ‘Demands for Grants (2023 -24) of the Department of Atomic

Lok Sabha on the 20th March, 2023.

OVERALL BUDGETARY DETAILS FOR THE FI NANCIAL YEAR 2022 -23 (BE, RE &

Recommendations/Observations  
1.1 From Chart 1, t he Committee observes a general  increasing trend in BE allocations for the 
Department. While the BE allocation for 2021-22 was at Rs.27,795 crore, it has been increased by 
about 21 percent in BE 2023 -24. Similarly, for  the past two years,  i.e. 2021 -22 and 2022-23, the 
Department has been getting an additional allocation at the RE stage. The proposed BE allocation for 
2023-24 was Rs.42,092.20 crore but the Department was allocated only  Rs.35,262.49 cro re in 2023 -
24. This is a shortfall of Rs.6,829.71 crore.                                                 (Para 2.6 of the 373rd Report) 
Action Taken 
1.2 As observed by the Hon'ble Committee there has been  a consistent rise  in the BE allocations  
commensurate with the activities of the Department. There are budgetary cuts in the BE allocations in 
each year, however, the  Department has been  sanctioned additional  funds through Supplementary 
Demands for Grants at RE stage. The current shortfall of  Rs. 6,829.71 crore alongwith any further  
additionality, based on the budgetary requirements,  shall be sought at RE stage. 
 
Recommendations/Observations 
 
1.3 From the submission of the Department the Committee feels that the Department may  face 
challenges especially in its initiatives relating to Nuclear Recycle Board (NRB), Tata Memorial Centre  
(TMC), Nuclear Power  Corporation of India Limited  (NPCIL), Heavy Water  Board (HWB) and 
Bhabha Atomic Research  Centre (BARC).                                                                        (Para 2.11 ibid)

crore over and above  the BE 2023-24 quoted by the Department  and recommends that the Department

timely and e ffective completion.  The initiatives of the Department under TMC, NPCIL  and BARC, 
amongst others, are crucial to the energy needs of the country  as well  as in  many healthcare and 
societal applications.  It is also necessary to ensure tha t the activities  of the Department do not suffer 
due to any lack of funds as it will have a bearing on India's strategic nuclear capacity.  
                                                                                                                                    (Para 2.12 ibid)

Recommendations/Observations  
2.1 From Chart 2 which depicts the share of Revenue and Capital allocation in BE 2023 -24 of the 
Department, across its various key sectors, t he Committee  observes that while the  share of revenue 
and capital expenditure allocations is almost  equal in the  total budget, capital  expenditure is  almost 
twice that of revenue for the Power Sector. On the other hand,  revenue expenditure is more than thrice  
of the capital expenditure  allocation under R&D Sector.                          (Para 2.7 of the 373rd Report) 
 
Action Taken 
2.2 Power Sector: The Department  provides Gross Budgetary Support under Capital Division for 
Power Sector  towards – Loans and Investments in PSUs.  Whereas, the Department  provides limited 
budgetary support under Revenue Division for Operation &  Maintenance purpose for e.g. Fuel 
Inventory, Interest on Heavy Water Pool Management etc. A  major portion of Revenue expenditure is 
incurred by the Power Sector  PSUs through internal sources. The Budgetary allocations under Capital 
Division (Loans and Inve stments) have increased due to the upcoming  Projects. Hence,  the Capital 
expenditure is  almost twice of the Revenue for the  Power Sector i.e.  NPCIL (Rs.6364  crore), 
Investments in NPCIL (Rs.3046  crore) and Loans to BHAVINI (Rs.200 crore). 
 
2.3 R&D Sector: The revenue expenditure of R&D sector comprises of Establishment expenditure 
(Rs.4410.80 crore) of all R&D Units viz. BARC, IGCAR, AERB, AM D, DCSEM, GSO, RRCAT and  
VECC. Grants to Autonomous Bodies and Aided Institute s (Rs.3240.40  crore) and Contribution to 
IAEA & Anunet charges (Rs.62.43 crores). As the employee strength in R&D units is 65% of the total 
staff strength of the Department, the establishment expenditure are commensurately on the higher side. 
Further, O&M expenditure of the  running Pl ants/R&D Reactors is also booked under  the Revenue 
Division. The allocations under the Capital  Division of R&D Sector are towards the ongoing  R&D 
Projects of BARC, IGCAR, RRCAT, VECC, GSO, AERB, DAE, DCSEM, and AMD as  per the 
budgetary requirements. Hence, the allocations towards revenue expenditure  are more than  thrice of 
the Capital expenditure under R&D Division. 
 
Recommendations/Observations  
2.4 The Committee observes and highlights the fact that even though India's rank is 6th in terms of 
numbers of nuclear power reactors being operated, we are still at number 11 and 14 when it comes to 
nuclear power produced and installed capacity respectively. This  is indicative of  the fact that the 
production capacity  per power plant is low. The Committee, therefore, recommends the Department to 
study the reasons for  this anomaly and also apprise the Commi ttee of  its findings. Further, the 

7 
 
Committee recommends that the  Department devices a  strategy to install higher production capacity 
power units so that the unit cost comes down and nuclear power becomes more affordable.    
                                                                                                                                           (Para 2.27 ibid) 
 
Action Taken 
2.5 The small  size (200-220 MW) reactors were set up  in the early days of  the nuclear power 
programme in the country due to the limited capacity of the then existing regional electricity grids. 
Following the imposition of  international technology denial  and embargo regime in 1974, India placed 
thrust on achieving self-reliance in this high technology area. India mastered Pressurized Heavy Water 
Reactor (PHWR) technology,  evolved a standard 220 MW PHWR design and  setup a number of fully 
indigenous 220 MW reactors. With the evolution of the capacities of the Indian electricity  grids, a 540 
MW PHWR was designed in the 1980s and the first  540 MW PHWR commenced operation in 2005.  
Following the successful operation of the 540 MW size PHWRs, a larger  unit size - the indigenous 700 
MW standard design has been evolved and  the first  700 MW PHWR is  connected to  the grid, 
generating power.  Fifteen more reactors of 700MW size  PHWRs are under construction/pre-project 
activities, to be progressively commissioned  by 2031-32. These reactors will form the mainstay of the 
rapid expansion of  nuclear capacity in the  country in the near future. Large size reactors which were 
setup in some countries of the world in the 1980s and 1990s  could not be setup in India due to the 
technology denial  and embargo regime. However, with the fruition of international cooperation in 
nuclear energy and entering into of relevant agreements, large size reactors of 1000 MW capacity are 
being setup at Kudankulam in cooperation with Russian Federation . Discussions are also in progress 
with France and the USA to arrive  at project  proposals to  setup large size reactors at  Jaitapur, 
Maharashtra and Kovvada, Andhra Pradesh respectively. 
 
Recommendations/Observations

proper utilization of  funds and that the Department should  take extra effort to ensure optimum 
utilization under the three heads 'BARC', 'IGCAR'  and 'Nuclear Fuel Complex'.             (Para 2.30 ibid)

Recommendation/Observation 
4.1 The Committee observes from Chart 11 that while during the years 2020 -21, 2021 -22 and 
2022-23 the revenue generated and the gross budget of the Department has constantly increased, the 
same trends is not seen in terms of percentage  profit (% of Gross budget). The Commi ttee is aware of 
the fact that the Department is engaged in a sector that requires intense scientific and technical 
resources in terms of research, development and deployment and hence relationship between budget 
spent, revenue generated and profits earned may not be as linear as it is in the classical sense. The 
Committee would however like to nudge the Department in strategizing ways of increasing the revenue 
and profit margins and move towards higher self -sustenance in financial terms.  
(Para 6.3 of the 373rd Report) 
Action Taken 
4.2 Department has been trying to increase the revenue through: 
a) Revision in the rate of Heavy Water and Fuel bundles based on the revised Cost Estimates 
b) Increase in production commensurate with the completion of new Reactors 
c) Diversification of activities of  HWB 
 
4.3 Further, there  are certain fictitious receipts which the Department has  proposed for  
discontinuation in Government Account. This shall ensure the Department to prepare a more realistic 
budget. Matter is under consideration with Ministry of Finance.
