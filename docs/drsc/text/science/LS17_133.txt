NATIONAL MISSION ON BAMBOO APPLICATIONS  
Recommendation 
        Bamboo is an important resource in the Soci o-economic functional cont ext. Apart from its 
traditional uses, it has various new applications as an alternative to rapidly depleting wood resources. To
extend the utilization of bamboo, consideration shoul d be given to promote or upgrade the existing
research and development institutions assisting the small-scale bam boo industries. The Committee feels 
that this mission mode programme should introduce new techniques for utiliz ation of bamboo, which
would promote the use of  bamboo and also generate good em ployment opportunities. The Department
through its institutes should take the new techniques to cooperatives/industries where it can be put to use
for commercialization.(Para 28) 
Action Taken by the Department 
        The National Mission on Bamboo Appl ications (NMBA), forma lly launched on 25 Novembe r
2004, is one of the key initiatives of the Department of Science a nd Technology (DST) for the Tenth
Plan. It is structur ed as a Technology Mission, and impleme nted by the Technology Information,
Forecasting and Assessment Council (TIFAC). 
        Bamboo is a fast growing and woody grass.  Hardy and adaptable, it grows throughout the country.
It is a strong and highly renewable material, and its usage an integral  part of the country’s cultural, 
social and economic traditions. In many parts of the country th e use of bamboo is ubiquitous.
Communities have put the material  to functional yet aesthetic use,  their skills having evolved ove r
centuries. Even today, by far the most important us es of bamboo are in the every day lives of people.
There is dependence, and a corresponding respect fo r the material; people are repositories of knowledge
about bamboo and its usage. 
        In recent years, a range has emerged of products and applications that  use bamboo in newer and
value-added ways. These have been developed thro ugh the application of scie ntific and engineering
skills; many of them bu ild on traditional usage, in the proce ss enlarging and enriching traditional
livelihood patterns. 
        The provision of employment and opportunity has been accorded the highest priority in the agenda
of the Government, and in the National Comm on Minimum Programme (NCMP). One of DST’s 
commitments to the NCMP, the Mission has been ta sked with creating the basis for enlarging the
bamboo sector, and with supporting the efforts of the Government  of India towards augmenting
economic opportunity, income and employment. 
        The core application and thrust areas of the Mission include wood s ubstitutes and composites,
structural and constructi onal applications, bamboo shoots, propa gation and cultivation, machinery and
process technologies, industrial products and energy. 
        The Mission has carried out property testing and assessment of growing stock, and a matching with
end use. Based on this testing and assessment the Mission has provi ded to the Planning Commission a
recommendation for a focus on 15 co mmercially significant species , an objective starting point fo r
action. A series of assessment, doc umentation and knowledge gatheri ng exercises have been taken up.
These include technology and process testing and docum entation preparation of data bases. A range o f
training manuals, designed in user  friendly formats have been co mmissioned. A comprehensive and
annotated bibliography of bamboo li terature will shortly be  issued, to meet the needs as well of the
research community. 
        In the area of wood substitutes and composites, a 60,000m2 per annum capacity plant is being
established with Mission support at Guwahati to manufacture co mposites/flooring boards. Mat based
and glass fibre composites have  been developed, and are ready for commercialisation. Technology
packages are available for bamboo based plywood substitutes, roofing material and panels. 
        The Mission is workin g to develop ran ges of efficient, sturd y and inexpensive toolin g and
Page 5 of 12133rd REPORT OF COMMITTEE ON SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY
7/16/2008http://10.21.217.2/book2/reports/science/133rdreport.htm

processing machinery, suited for Indian conditi ons and species, to reduce drudgery, improve
productivity and reduce wastage. At the same time process tec hnologies are being validated, and
converted to techno-economic packages for use by entrepreneurs. Primar y processing equipment,
indigenously developed, and suited to Indian bam boo has been prototyped, test ed and validated. It is
now available from a growing range of manufacturers. 
        The Mission supports a pplication oriented research and deve lopmental activity, utilising bamboo
for constructional applications. It also seeks to also demonstrate the virtues of combining bamboo with a
range of material - locally available and appr opriate, newly developed and non-conventional. The 
Mission supports innovation with  different construction techniques, and sets benchmar ks of quality o f
construction, functionality, strengt h and safety and aesthetics. Mode ls of low cost housing for rural
areas, utilising bamboo and bamboo composite material, have been developed, and demonstrated. 
        Bamboo shoots carry the potential of va lue added economic activity at the entrepreneurial and
community level through cultivation, processing and packaging. Th e Mission has developed technology
packages for bamboo shoot processing at the indus trial scale, and for co mmunity/ village level
enterprise. Two processing units, at Dimapur and Jorhat respecti vely, are being established. The
community level technology package has been demo nstrated at 24 locations , and has received an
overwhelming response. 
        In most application areas there is a re quirement of adequate quantities of quality bamboo the
quality being defined by species, maturity, bamboo characteristics,  harvesting technique and post-
harvest treatment. The Mission has de veloped guidelines for intensive cultivation, intended to achieve
productivity levels of over 20 tonnes per hectare, and for a range of commercially significant species. 
        Good quality plant material and in suffic ient quantity is a necessary condition if a large-scale 
programme of high yielding  plantation is to be taken up. Bamboo seeds are available infrequently, and
at long intervals, and traditional vegetative propagation practices will not suffice to meet the quality and
quantity aspects of such a programme. 
        There have been breakthroughs in the commer cial availability of high quality tissu e cultured plant
material, and, for the first time, an  export order of 50,000 tissue cultured  plants of Bambusa balcooa has
been received, by a NMBA supported unit in Hosur. 
        Gasification of bamboo can produce en ergy and a range of valuable by-products, including 
charcoal and activated carbon. It can reinforce a commitme nt to clean, renewabl e and cheap electricity
and thermal energy. It ca n utilise waste generated by processing ope rations, substitute the use of fossil
fuels, and lower operating costs. This  is particularly suitable for of f-grid and remote locations, and to 
meet captive industry and utility needs. Several gasification units are in the proc ess of being inducted in
the field, with capacities ranging from 5 Kwe to 1 MWe. 
        Working with tiny and small scale entrepre neurs, the Mission is also supporting the establishment
of mechanised units for sliver and stick making, and for a range of  products using whole and woven
bamboo. 
        Multi-disciplinary in its approach, and mult i-dimensional in its range of activities, the National 
Mission on Bamboo Applications is poised to provide a new impetus to the bamboo sector. 
Comments 
        The Committee is satisf ied with the objectives of the missi on and hopes that by the end of Tenth
Five-Year Plan there would be a marked increase in  the use and application of bamboo in the range o f 
products produced in the country.

Action Taken by the Department 
        In order to improve the forecasting system on weather in general and monsoon rainfall in particular,
India Meteorological Department (IMD) has al ready taken concerted ef forts for improvement in
observing system particularly on da ta collection, exchange and proce ssing. It is also making continued
and systematic efforts to upgrad e its technology as an ongoing progr amme. Some of the developments
are listed below:— 
1.     Recent initiatives for improvement of Weather Forecasting in IMD 
        For implementing a dynamical prediction model for long range foreca st of southwest monsoon,
IMD has started, in 2003, a co llaborative project with Indian Instit ute of Science, Bangalore. Under this
project an atmospheric General Ci rculation Model (GCM) with a mixe d-layer Ocean will be used fo r 
prediction of mons oon rainfall. Required compu ting facilities have been pr ocured at IMD, Pune. The
latest seasonal dynamical model of  NCEP, USA has also been offi cially obtained for this work.
Currently the model is being installed and tested. 
        Under ISRO - GBP programme a collaborat ive project has been initiat ed to use Regional Climate
Model (RCM) for long range predic tion of monsoon rainfall. SAC Ahme dabad is the nodal institute fo r
this project. IMD is also participating in this pr oject for validation of the model and its application in
long range forecasts. Various institutions like IIT Delhi, IISc Bangalore are also involved in this project. 
        On August 2, 2004 Hon’ ble Minister of State for Science and Technology convened a meeting o f 
scientists of IMD and other res earch organizations like IIT-Delh i, IITM Pune, IISc. Bangalore, 
CMMACS Bangalore etc. to assess the weather forecasting capabilities in our country. A strong need for
an integrated approach to synerg ise the expertise of these organiza tions was felt necessary to improve
our capabilities in weat her forecasting with particular empha sis on Southwest Monsoon rainfall. Action
in this regard has already been initiated. A Co mmittee under the chairmanship  of Prof. S.K. Dube,
Director, IIT Kharagpur has been set up recently whic h will synergise the forecasting activities of these
organisations.
