INTRODUCTION
 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised
 by the Committee to submit the report on its behalf, present the Twelfth Report on the Demands for Grants pertaining
 to the Department of Science and Technology (Ministry of Science and Technology) for the year 1994-95 (Demand No.
 71).
 
2. In its meeting held on April 12, 1994, the Committee considered the Demands for Grants of the Department of
 Science and Technology on the basis of the documents made available to it and the submission made before it by the
 Secretary and the other representatives of the Department.
 
3. The Committee expresses its thanks to the officers of the Department for replying to the clarifications sought by the
 Hon'ble Members and for placing before it the required material to enable the Committee to scrutinise the Demands for
 Grants of the Department of Science and Technology.
 
4.            The Committee considered and adopt the report at its meeting held on April 21, 1994.

REPORT
 
The Department of Science and Technology (DST) has a broad mandate of promoting science and technology on
 an overall basis in the country. This Department, inter alia, deals with the formulation of policy statements and

 guidelines on science and technology and also looks after their implementation. Promoting new areas of science is one
 of the major activities of the Department. Since the Department  of Science and Technology is the nodal Department in
 so far as promoting scientific activity in the country is concerned, it has to coordinate its endeavors with a host of
 scientific organisations, institutions and autonomous bodies which operate in the country. It also runs science and
 technology related programmes under schemes of bilateral and multilateral cooperation with other countries and foreign
 agencies. The Department deals with the promotion of ventures, which involve commercialisation of technologies
 developed indigenously. Through India Metrological Department (IMD), Survey of India (SOI) and National Atlas
 Thematic Mapping Organisation (NATMO), the Department provides scientific services.
 
2. The Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology, in his presentation, informed the Committee about the
 thrust areas of the Department and gave an account of the activities of the Department ,undertaken during the year
 1993-94. The Committee was particularly informed about the society related programmes, expansion of seismological
 network, strengthening linkages with other scientific organisations, establishing coordination with other Ministries and
 Departments including the socio-economic Ministries/Departments, promotion of home-grown technologies and
 supporting new R&D projects. The Committee was told that two Mission Mode projects - one on ‘Sugar Production
 Technologies' and the other on 'Advanced Composites' are being implemented by the Department. The Committee was
 also given to understand that for the year 1994-95, the Department intends to derive its guiding principles from the
 Sixth Report of the Committee on Science & Technology, Environment and Forests. These guiding principles would be
 to vigorously sustain support to scientific research, enuciate programmes which are of societal relevance and establish
 greater linkages with various Ministries/Departments including the socio-economic Ministries/Departments. The
 Department would also promote effective coordination. The Secretary of the Department especially made a reference to
 the new fund which would be created by transferring the 5 per cent cess on payment of royalty for imported
 technologies which is presently collected under the Research & Development Cess Act and would be administered by
 the Department of Science and Technology to help the indigenously developed technologies to reach the stage of
 commercial application. This new fund would be termed as the' Fund for Technology Development and Application'.
 
3. After listening to the presentation made by the Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology, the
 Committee examined the budgetary allocations of the Department for the year 1994-95. * While considering the
 Demands for Grants of the Department, the Committee got an opportunity to review the working of various projects
 and programmes which fall within the mandate of the Department of Science and Technology.
* For summary of the Demands for Grants, see Schedule.
4.     The Committee expressed its happiness that  Government  had  decided  to  set up a Fund for Technology
 Development and Application' into which the cess collected under the Research and Development Cess Act, 1986
 would be put. This Fund would be administered by the Department of Science and Technology and utilised to help
 indigenously developed technologies to reach the stage of commercial application. The Committee wanted to know as
 to how much money is likely to accrue to the Department through this new Fundduring the year 1994-95 and how the
 Department proposes to utlise it. The Committee was informed  that the Government is considering the proposal to
 amend the Research and Development Cess Act, 1986 so as to enable it to reappropriate the cess collected. The Fund
 would be utilised to meet the expenditure incurred in providing equity capital and/or any other form of financial
 assistance to research and development institutions and industrial concerns which would attempt commercial
 application of indigenous technology or adapting imported technology to wider commercial application. The
 Committee was told that the amount collected initially may not be very large, but if more and more technologies are
 imported which is a possibility under the liberalised economic policies, naturally the amount collected for this Fund
 would also increase. In a period of  five years ending March, 1993, the total collection of cess has been about Rs. 150
 crores. The Committee is of the view that the proposed new Fund would certainly act as a catalyst in promoting
 indigenously developed technologies reach the stage of commercial application and would result in a greater interface
 between scientific institutions and industry. The Committee would, however, like the Department to do proper analysis
 about the use of this Fund and the manner in which it would utilise the proposed Fund for Technology Development
 and Application. The Committee feels that the  Department would not treat this Fund just as an additional resource and,
 therefore, proper thrust and direction would be provided to the utilization of this Fund. A proper institutional
 mechanism to ensure this is called for.

6. The Committee is firmly of the view that the remaining outlay of Rs. 76.20 crores for the next two years of the Eighth
 Plan period is hardly sufficient for running the various activities of the Department. Due to the fast changing economic
 scenario and in view of the greater efforts being made towards globalisation of Indian economy, this nodal Department
 which deals with the promotion of new areas of science and technology needs to be strengthened properly. The
 Committee noted that considering the recommendations of this Committee made in its Sixth Report, the Government
 has increased the allocation for the Department in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 by 19 per cent over the
 budget estimates for the year 1993-94, From an outlay of            Rs. 188.80 crores (plan) for the year 1993-94, the
 outlay in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 has been kept at Rs. 225 crores (plan).
 
7. The Committee noticed that under plan allocation there existed a gap between the outlay allocated and the utilisation
 during the year 1991-92, 1992-93 and 1993-94. About the year 1993-94, it was explained to the Committee that the
 total allocation made to the Department was to the tune of Rs. 351 crores(B.E.). This included Rs. 10.2 crores which
 were transferred to Ministry of Urban Development for civil works of IMD, SOI and NATMO. Taking into account the
 net supplementary Grants of Rs. 1.6 crores approved in March, 1994, the total funds available would work out to Rs.
 352.6 crores, and on the basis of the expenditure incurred, the Committee was told that the Department could almost
 fully utilise the total allocation. While taking note of this explanation furnished by the Department, what was observed
 by the Committee is that for the last three years the Department has not been fully able to utilise the plan component of
 the funds and there is excess utilisation in the non-plan component which normally reflects the running cost of the
 existing set up. The Department should evolve proper strategies to fully utilise the plan outlay, as this would lead to the
 promotion of scientific activity.

92, as there had been slippage in delivery of certain radars which were subsequently received in 1992-93. The reason for
 under utilisation in 1992-93 was due to delay in the receipt of the new computer for the Northern Hemisphere Analysis
 Centre, which had since been received in 1993-94. It was clarified to the Committee that savings by NCMRWF in

 as it was felt by the Expenditure Finance Committee that there was scope for reduction in the area of construction and
 construction cost. It took some time to re-examine the proposal and revise the costs through an export committee. The
 Committee was informed that the Expenditure Finance Committee has not approved the proposal and the Department
 would be able to show some progress in this matter. As regards anxiety expressed by the Committee about the
 installation and upkeep of the Cray computer, the Committee was told that it continues to operate from the IMD
 complex at Lodhi Road and the absence of the new building has not hampered the working of the NCMRWF centres.
 The Committee hopes that during the year 1994-95, IMD, SOI and NCMRWF would be able to utilise fully the plan
 component of the budget estimates earmarked for these organisations for the year 1994-95.
9. Mass Employment Generation through S&T (MEGSAT) can be an important programme under which training is
 imparted to various identified trades with the help of S&T experts and institutions for creating technical manpower. The
 programme also aims at generating jobs on a sustainable basis. In the year 1993-94 a sum of Rs. 3.50 crores was
 provided for this programme but in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 the allocation has been reduced to only
 Rs. 50 lakhs. The Committee wanted to know the reasons for this drastic reduction in the allocation for this programme.
 The Committee was informed that the MEGSAT programme was discussed by the Department of Science and
 Technology with the Planning Commission and it was decided that any new employment generation programme like
 MEGSAT needs to be integrated with the existing employment generation programmes like TRYSEM, IRDP, JRY, run
 by the Ministry of Rural Development. Recognising the fact that by the application of science and technology skills can
 be improved, the Department of Science and Technology should co- ordinate its activities with the Ministry of Rural
 Development and other Departments so as to explore the possibilities of employment generation particularly for the
 rural youth, women and weaker sections of society by providing greater S&T inputs into the existing employment
 generation programmes.
              
10.Under the head 'Drugs an Pharmaceuticals Research' in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95, a provision of Rs.
 10 crores has been made, but the Committee found that there         was no allocation made under this head last year. The
 Committee, therefore, wanted to know why this allocation has been made in the budget estimates for this year. The
 Committee was informed that the Department of Science and Technology is giving emphasis on the support of        
 Life Science activities along with the Department of Biotechnology and the Council of Scientific           and Industrial
 Research. According to the Department, due to the emerging global scenario, it would be necessary for the country to
 prepare itself in a big way by stepping up capabilities for indigenous drug design and development. For achieving this
 purpose, a provision for Rs. 10 crores has been made in the budget estimates for this year. The Committee was also
 informed that a committee consisting of Secretary, Department of Science and Technology, Secretary, Department of
 Biotechnology and Director-General, CSIR would guide the activities in this area. One or more of the important
 national institutions like the laboratories of CSIR and industries would also be involved in this programme. The
 Committee is concerned that when major institutions and infrastructure in this area exists in CSIR, DBT and ICMR
 why Department of Science and Technology should get involved. Any capabilities under DST which are marginal at
 present should be made available to these agencies. DST should concentrate on areas where serious gaps exist on the
 S&T scene. The Department may also made efforts to attract industry to work in developing drugs and pharmaceuticals
 from basic research to product.
 
11. The Committee observed that in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 for the Technology Information
 Forecasting and Assessment Council (TIFAC), a sum of Rs. 7.16 crores has been allocated while in the revised

 estimates for the year 1993-94, this Council got Rs. 3.99 crores. In view of this increased allocation for TIFAC, the
 Committee wanted to know whether the Department has some special programmes to be undertaken by the TIFAC
 during the current year. The Committee was informed that TIFAC was set up to undertake technology forecasting,
 technology, and assessment and to disseminate technology information. The Department has initiated Mission Mode
 Projects based on the reports prepared by the TIFAC. Seven Joint Project Management Boards have been set up in
 areas like advanced composites, aviation products and services, instrumentation, liquid crystals display system,
 electronics technology, leather techn61ogy and Chemical/membrane and polymer technology. Over the past few years
 TIFAC has generated a number of reports which provide forecasting and market surveys and also assess a few areas of
 technology. 13 projects in specific areas like CFC substitutes, CO based-chemicals, high performance substitutes of
 kerosene, etc. have been identified. The Committee, however, noted that several of these areas are being handled by
 CSIR on a priority basis, for example, leather, CO based- chemicals, CFC substitutes, etc. Some special initiatives
 which would be followed by TIFAC pertain to the areas of small scale industries, export sector, surface engineering,
 biochemical technologies, etc. Following the recommendation of the present Committee made in its Sixth Report, a
 number of specialised link programmes with Departments, national laboratories, industries and industry associations
 have been formulated in the form of multi-institutional projects. Further, at the instance of Planning Commission,
 specific home-grown technologies are beige supported and it is proposed to strengthen this activity during the year
 1994-95. The Committee feels that TIFAC should confine itself to its basic objectives which relate to technology
 assessment and technology forecasting. If the role of TIFAC is widened, there is a possibility that it may lose its
 original direction and may start functioning as a project manager; in particular, the Committee noted in this regard the
 project management role of TIFAC in the project on advanced composites. The Committee is of the view that TIFAC
 can make a great impact in promoting science if it clearly follows its original objectives. The Committee feels that,
 being the nodal agency for promoting S&T, the Department of Science and Technology should bring about flexibility
 in the operation of the scientific departments and that there should be transparency in t operation of TIFAC. The
 Committee noted that funds allocated for TIFAC were far great than support provided to many major aided institutions.
 
12.  Societal programmes of the Department of Science and Technology aim at providing
the benefit of science and technologies directly to the people. The Committee has, however observed that in regard to
 some of the society-related programmes there are other institution which too are looking after similar types of
 programmes. In order to avoid duplication  supporting such programmes the Committee is of the view that the
 Department of Science and Technology may consider transferring some of the programmes to the specialise      
 institutions or concerned bodies, and the money thus saved can be utilized for promoting other thrust areas of the
 Department. The Committee strongly supports the S&T programme       undertaken by the Department because it is
 interested in having a very strong S&T base for the country but it would like to point out that the Department should
 regroup its activities or shed some of its load so as to concentrate on thrust areas that are not being dealt with by    
 other agencies.
 
13. The Department should also promote indigenous technologies which exist in the country which are highly useful
 and very cheap. By strengthening this area the Department would not only create Societal impact but would also be
 able to generate employment. The Department of Science and Technology should make efforts to create a strong
 technological base in the country relying on its technological traditions. The Department should investigate areas where
 India had a strong technological base and replace and upgrade some of the old technologies so that with less investment
 India can gain competitive technological edge over other countries.
 
14. Through science and society programme, the Department of Science and Technology makes the use of S&T for
 societal development, which particularly aims at influencing the lives        of weaker sections, tribals and women. The
 other component of this programme relates to the popularisation of science and entrepreneurship development .   
 through the intervention of S&T. The Committee was given an account of the activity undertaken by the Department
 during the last year and a recent successful experiment conducted by the department in this area was the Children’s
 Science Congress which was every popular among children.  The Department is also thinking of setting up of a
 National Children’s Science Academy. The Committee feels that
many of the ills which beset Indian society today are largely due to the unscientific attitude. Creating scientific temper
 among masses is, therefore, a great responsibility of the Department of Science and Technology. This is an area of great
 importance.

5. The Committee noticed that for the Mission Mode on Cyclone Warning  and Seismic Observation in the budget
 estimates for the year 1994-95, there is only a marginal increase of Rs.  15 lakh over the last year's budget estimates.
 The Committee, therefore, wanted to know whether with this meager increase the Department would be able to run this
 programme in Mission Mode, particularly when the Department intends of re-orient its approach after the earthquake in
 the Marathwada region. The Committee was told that under the Mission Mode Project, a sum of Rs. 2 crores has been
 provided in this years budget against Rs. 1.85 crores provided last year. While this Mission Mode Project will be with a
 specific intention of enhancing the capabilities of seismic observations, there is also a provision of Rs. I crore under
 seismicity. Thus total provision under seismology for the year 1994-95 comes to Rs. 3 crores. IMD also incurs some
 expenditure on seismology in maintaining its national network. The Committee was informed that as a post-budgetary
 development, a World Bank loan of 7.5 million US dollars for improving the seismic monitoring set-up in peninsular
 India has been approved. The Department also intends to strengthen its seismic network in the Himalayas and the
 North-Eastern Region. It intends to initiate the programme of micro-zoning of selected cities and locations in the
 country as this would lead to reduction of loss of life and damage to property. The Committee observes that the whole
 area of seismicity has two aspects: one relates to seismology and the other to the disaster mitigation efforts. In the area
 of seismology, the Department should upgrade - its technology. The instruments which are awfilable to the Department
 for observing seismological activities have to be upgraded as also the manner in which the data is processed and
 analysis made. The Department should also spend a part of its funds in imparting knowledge to the people as to how
 they can cope with the disaster and how the loss of life and damage to property can be minimized. The Committee feels
 that some major efforts are required to be made by the Department in the area of earthquake prediction like the
 Department has done in the case of Cyclone warnings and there is tremendous need for software development. The
 Committee is of the view that the Department needs to evolve an  integrated disaster prediction and mitigation
 programme for the entire country.
 
16. The Committee was informed that under the instrument development programme, the Department aims at planning,
 co-ordinating and implementing programmes leading to indigenous development and production of instruments. The
 allocation made for the instruments development in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 is Rs. 2 crores. The
 Department is also making an attempt to get some part of the funding from industry. The Committee agrees with the
 Department that it is a very vital area for modernising the industry in the country and needs adequate financial support.
 
  17.       The Committee wanted to know the reasons for increase from the revised estimates of Rs. 45 lakhs for the year
 1993-94 to Rs. 1.20 crores in the budget estimates for the year 1994-95 in the area of National Coordination of Testing
 and Calibration Facilities (NCTCF). It was explained to the Committee that the increase in the provision has been made
 with a view to developing and establishing a national accreditation and wants to initiate new system of accreditation in
 order to promote our exports. The Committee felt that there was need for an integrated look at the areas of standards
 quality control, reliability evaluation, accreditation of facilities, national testing capabilities, etc. There were widely
 disparate investments in different areas, for example, Department of Electronics, Department of Science and
 Technology, etc. These need to be reviewed. A strong programme in this area is called for with the thrust on exports
 and global competitiveness.
 
18. The Committee is of the view that Science and Society programmes, Seismicity, Instrument Development and the
 National Coordination of Testing and Calibration Facilities are vital areas which require to be strengthened and,
 therefore, recommends that at the time of doing mid-term appraisal of the Eighth Five Year Plan, the Government may
 consider enhancing the allocation for these four programmes suitably.
 
19. The Committee noted the vital role that the Department of Science and Technology plays in the matter of bilateral
 and multilateral international cooperation and in particular, successful programmes that have been embarked upon with
 the former Soviet Union (now Russia and the new initiatives with the other Republics), Germany and the USA.
 
20. The Committee noticed that in the area of policy formulation anti related aspects for the science sector much
 headway has not been made but the Committee noted that the Government has recently constituted the Cabinet
 Committee on Science and Technology and hopes that it will start functioning soon and the matters pertaining to the
 setting up of Science Advisory Council to the Cabinet Committee on Science and Technology, Statement of
 Technology policy or a science and technology approach to the post GATT period would receive its prompt attention as
 these measures are vital from the viewpoint of our national development.

       2.  The Committee took up for Consideration the Detailed Demands for Grants  (1994-95) of the Department of
 Science and Technology. The Secretary of the Department briefly          explained for the benefit of the Members the
 allocations on different activities/projects of the Department as proposed in its Detailed Demands lot Grants. The
 Members then sought       clarifications from him other officials of the Department on various points relating to the      
 Demands for Grants. Thereafter the Secretary and other officials concerned replied to the       clarifications Sought by
 the Members.
 
3.     The Committee concluded its deliberations on the Demands for Grants for the Department of Science and
 Technology.
A verbatim record of the proceedings was kept.
 
4.            The Committee than adjourned at 5.40 P.M. to meet again at  3.00 P.M. on Friday, the 15 April, 1994.

2. The Chairman, at the outset, informed the Committee about the sad and untimely demise of Shri Ram Prakash
 Chaudhary, a sitting Member of Lok Sabha. The Committee expressed condolences over his death, with all Members
 standing and observing two minutes silence as a mark of respect to the memory of the departed.
 
3. The Committee then took up for consideration its draft Twelfth and Thirteenth Reports on the Detailed Demands for
 Grants ( 1994- 95) of the Departments of Science & Technology and Scientific & Industrial Research, respectively and
 adopted the same unanimously.
 4. The Committee decided to present these Reports to both the House of Parliament on the 22nd April, 1994. The
 Committee authorised its Chairman and in his absence, Shri W. Kulabidhu Singh to present these Reports to the Rajya
 Sabha. The Committee also authorised Shri E. Ahamed and in his absence, Prof.Radhika Ranjan Pramanikor             
 Shri Jitendra Nath Das to lay these Reports on the Table of the Lok Sabha, along, with Committee's Eighth, Ninth,
 Tenth and Eleventh Reports which could not be laid on the Table of the House despite being put in the Agenda on the
 l8th, 19th and 21st April, 1994 because of abrupt adjournment of the House on each of these days.
 
5.         The Committee then adjourned at 9.55 A.M. to meet again at 10.00 A.M. on Friday, the 22nd April 1994.
