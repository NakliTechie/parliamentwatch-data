 planning, promotion, coordination and overseeing the implementation of various environmental and forestry programmes.
 The Ministry’s mandate also includes  environmental management to promote health considerations, to focus on poverty
 alleviation by enhancing access of the poor to natural resources for livelihood, and to heighten the awareness regarding
 environmentally sound living process by focusing on nature-man synergy.  The Ministry has been designated as the nodal
 agency in the country for the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP), and the International Centre for
 Integrated Mountain Development, and looks after the follow-up of the United Nations Conference on Environment and
 Development (UNCED). Within the overall framework of its mandate, the main activities of the Ministry are conservation
 and control of pollution, afforestation and regeneration of forests in degraded areas, protection and conservation of forests
 and wildlife and protection of environment.
The tasks assigned to the Ministry are achieved through environmental impact assessment, eco-regeneration,
 assistance to organizations implementing environmental and forestry programmes, promotion of environmental and
 forestry research, extension, education and training for capacity building, dissemination of environmental information,
 international co-operation, creation of environmental awareness among all sections of the country’s population.  These
 objectives are supported by a set of legislative and regulatory measures aiming at preservation, conservation and
 protection of environment.  Some of these are the Wildlife  (Protection) Act, 1972, the Water (Prevention and Control of
 Pollution) Act, 1974, the Forests (Conservation) Act, 1980, Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, the
 Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, National Forest Policy, 1988 the Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991, a National
 Conservation Strategy and Policy Statement on Environment and Development, 1992, a Policy Statement on Abatement
 of Pollution, 1992, the National Environment Tribunal Act, 1995, the National  Environment Appellate Authority Act,
 1997, the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 2001. The Bio-Diversity Act 2002 has been passed and Rules 2004 have
 been finalized.
 
The following are the broad objectives of the Ministry:
i)
                    Conservation and survey of flora, fauna, forests and wildlife,
ii)                   Prevention and control of pollution,
iii)                 Afforestation and regeneration of degraded areas,
iv)                 Protection of the environment, and
v)                  Ensuring the welfare of animals.

POLLUTION FROM COAL BASED METHANE AND CLEAN COAL TECHNOLOGIES
 
11.       Among the fossil fuels, coal has acquired the dubious distinction of being the dirtiest one.  Such an attribute is on
 account of severe environmental damage and acute pollution problems caused during mining operation, processing of the
 mineral till its end use and pollution arising out of coal based wastes.
 
12.       Land subsidence in underground mines, ugly scars of land in abandoned open cast mines, emission of fly ash
 during combustion of coal and huge quantities of ash generated from boilers of coal based power plants and industrial
 units are among the hosts of multi-facial problem associated with handling and subsequent productive use of coal. 
 Emission of carbon dioxide, an important component of green house gases (GHGs) and global warming is another
 emerging concern linked with the burning of coal.
 
13.       The Committee is of the opinion that although the coal constitutes a major source catering to the country’s energy
 needs, possibly with proper technologies and new initiatives for better management, it is possible to reduce the hazards of
 coal production which is being otherwise caused due to extraction of this mineral without proper planning.  Through
 scientific mining practices followed by land reclamation, beneficiation for ash reduction at source, improvement in
 combustion methods, use of efficient electrostatic precipitators, bag filters for trapping of fly ash emission and effective
 management of ash including its utilization for bricks, cement and such other purposes, many of the problems can be
 prevented and controlled.
 
14.       The Committee feels that Central Pollution Control Board should lay down the environmental guidelines for coal
 mining activities prescribing the use of beneficiated coal with lower ash content in power plants and lay down specific
 norms for emission and effluents from coal combustion system.
 
15.       In India out of the total land area of 3.29 million sq. km., only 0.45% area, that is, about16,000 sq. kms. is coal
 bearing  and out of this coal bearing area, active coal mining area is about 2500 sq. kms.  The Committee feels that
 maximum land degradation in coal mining is caused by open-cast mining.  Since the productivity is higher in open cast
 sector, this pace of growth cannot be sustained for long since the stripping ratios will increase the mining operations
 which may consequently run into  land access and other environmental problems.

16.       Air pollution in coalmines is chiefly due to the fugitive emission of particulate matter and gases including methane,
 sulphur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen.  The mining operations like drilling, blasting, movement of the heavy earth
 moving machinery on haul roads, collection, transportation and handling of coal, screening, sizing and segregation units
 are the major sources of such emissions. Underground mine fire is also a major source of air pollution in some of the
 coalfields.
 
17.       High levels of suspended particular matter increase respiratory diseases such as chronic bronchitis and asthma
 while gaseous emissions contribute towards global warming besides causing health hazards to the exposed population
 
18.       Methane emission from coal mining depends on the mining methods, depth of coal mine, coal quality and
 entrapped gas content in coal seams.  The proper management of methane emission should be one of the important factors
 which should be handled by the miners and mining authorities to reduce its emission into the atmosphere.
 
19.       The major source of water pollution in the coal mines is the carry over of the suspended solids in the drainage
 system of the mine sump water and storm drainage and in some of the coal mines acidic water is also found in the
 underground aquifers.  In addition, waste water from coal preparation plant and mine water are other source of water
 pollution which requires immediate control.
 
20.       The main sources of noise pollution, are blasting, movement of heavy earth moving machine and drilling and coal
 handling plants and the effective monitoring of all these issues are necessary for reducing noise pollution.
 
21.       Subsidence of surface takes place due to extraction of coal by underground mining.  Subsidence is exhibited by
 cracks on surface and lowering of land in the worked out areas compared to surroundings.  The surface is rehabilitated by
 dozing and sealing  of cracks followed by plantation of trees.  The subsided areas with medium sized depressions are ideal
 for developing water pools and sustain green vegetation and also to meet the water needs of local people.  The Committee,
 therefore, recommends that the mined areas, which are backfilled, should be rehabilitated for development of vegetation. 
 In the quarried areas water reservoir is developed for water harvesting.  The big voids created by open-pit mining can
 cause land degradation which, the Committee feels, could be gainfully utilized to serve water reservoirs which will
 provide moisture for vegetation in the surrounding areas and the water could be used for domestic supply after necessary
 treatment.
 
22.       It is understood that major coal projects are required to obtain Environmental Clearance from the Government. 
 Mitigative/control measures are identified to reduce the adverse impacts on environment.  It helps to integrate
 environmental considerations into mining and related operations right from the planning stage.  For this purpose, the
 project proponent needs to prepare an Environmental Impact Assessment Report and an Environmental Management
 Plan.
 
23.       The Committee is of the opinion that for abatement of the pollution problems associated with the coal mines, the
 Central Pollution Control Board needs to develop National Environmental Standards and Code of practice for coal mines
 which should be given due consideration by the Ministry of Environment & Forests and given a legal framework.
 
24.       The Committee is of the opinion that supply of  high ash laden coal to power plants not only poses environmental
 problems but also causes poor plant performance and high cost for ash disposal. The Committee, therefore, strongly
 recommends that Ministry of Environment and Forests should evolve a mechanism for suggesting measures for improving
 the quality of coal supplied to the power plants.
a)         Particularly power plants located beyond 1000 kms. from pit head; and
b)         Power plants located in critically polluted areas, Urban sectors and in ecologically sensitive zones.

26.       The Committee has been given to understand by Regbional Research Laboratory that most widely studied
 microorganism for removal of pyritic sulfur from coal is Thiobacillus ferrooxidans, a Gram-negative, acidophilic,
 mesophilic, chemoautotrophic, rod-shaped bacterium.  The Committee feels that greater researches in this direction by
 RRL could lead to cost effective  microbial processes that can be implemented in pilot plant commercially considering
 some factors like capacity of reactor for treatment of coal.  The Committee has been further given to understand that
 certain radiation techniques have been reported to be effective for removal of sulfur from coal.  The gamma radiolytic
 pretreatment by ionising gamma rays from Cobalt-60 removes organic sulfur from coal leaving the hydrocarbon matrix of
 coal unaffected. 
 
OPTIMAL HYDRO-CARBON ENERGY MANAGEMENT THROUGH CLEAN DEVELOPMENT
 MECHANISM (CDM)
 
27.       In the wake of implementation of Kyoto Protocol in February, 2005 stabilization of greenhouse gas concentrations
 in the atmosphere at a level that would prevent dangerous anthropogenic interference with the climate system appears to
 be one of the challenging subjects before the country in the current global environment scenario.  The Committee would,
 therefore, like to invite the attention of the Ministry of Environment and Forests in conjunction with the oil sector
 that investment in low emissions technologies is the demanding need of the hour and the problem should be
 addressed in such a fashion that long term national emissions reduction should be made one of the priority areas.
 
28.       In order to give an effective implementation of the above proposal of the Committee, the following model may be
 considered by the Ministry of Environment & Forests:
·
        The solubility of additional carbon dioxide in the ocean surface water decreases with rising concentrations. 
 Thus, the relative oceanic uptake of carbon dioxide is reduced at higher concentrations.
·        The land vegetations may serve as an additional medium term sink for anthropogenic carbon emissions.
·        The radio-active greenhouse forcing grows only logarithmically with increasing carbon dioxide and Green
 House Gases (GHG) emissions.
 
29.       The environment friendly performance objectives and mechanisms for tracking progress in achieving the prime
 objective of the aforesaid model for the growth of the oil industry on the one hand and socio-economic sustainability on
 the other, the implementation of socio-environmental policies involving oil companies and the Ministry of Environment &
 Forest requires combination of technological advances and innovative approaches.
 
30.       The Committee is of the opinion that for addressing the key issues pertaining to the transition of a carbon based
 economy to a hydrogen economy, the Ministry of Environment and Forests needs to promote the following:
§
         Promote more rapid dissemination and use of efficient, existing commercial technology in strategic and
 environmentally sensitive areas of the country.
§         Encourage the development and use of innovative, currently non-commercial technologies.
§         Enhance the ability of all oil sector companies to achieve and utilize advanced and efficient technologies, and
§         Improve scientific understanding by addressing well-known uncertainties, in the adoption and implementation of
 efficient technologies.
 
BOTANICAL SURVEY OF INDIA: METHODS OF CONDUCTING SURVEY AND
 STRUCTURE OF SURVEY REPORT

 habitat (terrestrial epiphytic, epiphyllous, saprophyte, parasite,  aquatic etc.) local name uses, if any, abundance and such
 features which will not remain in herbarium specimens  as colour of the flower/leaf, bark, etc., fragrant, height/girth and

PROJECT ELEPHANT
51.       Project Elephant was launched in February, 1992 to assist States having free ranging populations of wild elephants
 to ensure long term survival of identified viable population of elephants in their natural habitat.  The Project is being
 implemented in 13 States, viz Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Meghalaya,
 Nagaland, Orissa, Tamilnadu, Uttaranchal, Utter Pradesh and West Bengal.  States are provided financial as well as
 technical assistance in achieving the objectives of the Project.  Help is also provided to other States with small population
 of elephants for the purpose of census, training of field staff and mitigation of human elephant conflict.
            The main activities of the project are as follows :-
i)
                    Strengthening of measures for protection of wild elephants from poachers.
ii)                   Development of scientific and planned management for conservation of elephant habitats and viable
 population of wild elephants in India.
iii)                 Ecological restoration of existing natural habitats and migratory routes of elephants of elephants.
iv)                 Promotion of measures for mitigation of human – elephant conflict in problem areas and moderating pressures
 of human and live stock on crucial elephant habitats.
v)                  Eco-development and veterinary care.
vi)                 Research on issues relating to elephant conversation.
vii)               Public education and awareness programme and capacity building  of field staff, mahouts and veterinarians.
 
52.       The Committee feels that Project Elephant is a major project of the Ministry but getting less importance. The tusk
 is as precious as tiger skin and not immune from Poachers. Therefore, the Committee recommends that Elephants should
 also be protected from the poaching and effective strategy should be evolved for their preservation. In view of decline in
 Tiger population in Sariska, the Committee suggests the Ministry to undertake an evaluation of Elephant population and
 take corrective measures accordingly.
 
53.       The Committee recommends that sufficient allocation must be provided to various States for habitat management
 mitigation of human-elephant conflict, payment of ex-gratia relief for loss of human life and crops caused by wild
 elephants, strengthening of anti-poaching measures, capacity building of the field staff, registration of domesticated
 elephant using microchips etc.
 
54.       The Committee recommends that endangered species need to be preserved while the population of other animals
 should be sustainably managed.  The Ministry should generate data and information on maintaining a sustainable
 population of each animal for their scientific management.  Research initiatives in the field of sustainable use of wildlife
 products are needed.  The Ministry should cover not only wildlife research and training but also the socio-economic
 aspects of wildlife conservation.
 
55.       While the Committee is concerned about the protection of wildlife in the Protected Areas, it feels that wildlife
 should also be protected outside the Protected Areas and sufficient allocation should be made for the purpose.

 rural water bodies.

i)

 entering the lake.

 approach etc. depending upon the site conditions.
iii)                 Catchment area treatment and lake beautification which may include bunding fencing, creation of
 facilities and public recreation and entertainment(children park, boating etc.)and public area.
iv)                 Public awareness and public participation.

 considered by the Ministry of Environment & Forests:
·
        The solubility of additional carbon dioxide in the ocean surface water decreases with rising concentrations. 
 Thus, the relative oceanic uptake of carbon dioxide is reduced at higher concentrations.
·        The land vegetations may serve as an additional medium term sink for anthropogenic carbon emissions.
·        The radio-active greenhouse forcing grows only logarithmically with increasing carbon dioxide and Green
 House Gases (GHG) emissions.
(Para 28)

 objective of the aforesaid model for the growth of the oil industry on the one hand and socio-economic sustainability on
 the other, the implementation of socio-environmental policies involving oil companies and the Ministry of Environment &
 Forest requires combination of technological advances and innovative approaches.
 (Para 29)

 economy to a hydrogen economy, the Ministry of Environment and Forests needs to promote the following:

         Promote more rapid dissemination and use of efficient, existing commercial technology in strategic and
 environmentally sensitive areas of the country.
§         Encourage the development and use of innovative, currently non-commercial technologies.
§         Enhance the ability of all oil sector companies to achieve and utilize advanced and efficient technologies, and

 efficient technologies.

 including norms for designing survey report would also like to suggest that the Ministry  should examine and review the

 also strengthen the infrastructure and technical capabilities of the universities providing forestry education with necessary
 grants. The Ministry should also organize seminars and workshops, besides conducting special courses on various forestry
 themes for the academic advancement of the foresters and those interested in the field of forestry.

27.       The Committee expresses its serious concern over the sudden disappearance of the Tigers from Sariska Tiger
 Reserve. The Committee feels that the negligence of Forest staff coupled with the large scale poaching has cost the
 country dear. Conditions in most of the national parks are more or less same, posing a clear danger to protected animal
 species. Poaching is not a new phenomenon but the poachers are now more advanced with latest weapons and very
 powerful communication network, making the lackadaisically managed tiger reserves easy picking. In contrast, forest
 guards are usually equipped with a wooden stick and most of the times without any means of communication. Taking note
 of this alarming situation, the Committee is of considered opinion that a Special Task Force at the central or state level
 with the involvement of para-military forces must be constituted to combat the menace of poaching of wild-life.
(Para 49)
28.       Moreover, the Ministry should also involve the villagers living in and around National parks/Tiger reserves to
 prevent the poaching as they are aware of the tentative movement of the poachers but because of poachers’ threats or any
 other compulsion, they refrain from coming out openly to help the forest Department to catch the poachers.  The
 Committee strongly recommends that all vacant positions should be filled immediately and at no time any tiger reserve in
 the country should be left with the junior officers.
 
(Para 50)
29.       The Committee feels that Project Elephant is a major project of the Ministry but getting less importance. The tusk
 is as precious as tiger skin and not immune from Poachers. Therefore, the Committee recommends that Elephants should
 also be protected from the poaching and effective strategy should be evolved for their preservation. In view of decline in
 Tiger population in Sariska, the Committee suggests the Ministry to undertake an evaluation of Elephant population and
 take corrective measures accordingly.
(Para 52)
30.       The Committee recommends that sufficient allocation must be provided to various States for habitat management
 mitigation of human-elephant conflict, payment of ex-gratia relief for loss of human life and crops caused by wild
 elephants, strengthening of anti-poaching measures, capacity building of the field staff, registration of domesticated
 elephant using microchips etc.
(Para 53)

 should be sustainably managed.  The Ministry should generate data and information on maintaining a sustainable
 population of each animal for their scientific management.  Research initiatives in the field of sustainable use of wildlife
 products are needed.  The Ministry should cover not only wildlife research and training but also the socio-economic
 aspects of wildlife conservation.
(Para 54)

34.       The Committee is also of the view that the Ministry should formulate an effective strategy to consider all activities,
 current and potential,that can influence forests and related social, economic, and environmental outcomes.  More focussed

 competitive private sector participation in the sector.  More focused interventions are required for maintaining natural
 forests for eco-system protection and sustaining the global and local forest values.

 Education in the formal educational curriculum and for this purpose, it should vigorously interact with the University
 Grants Commission (UGC), the National Council for Educational Research and Training (NCERT), the Ministry of

 to ensure integration of environmental concept into the curriculum. Moreover, talented persons should be encouraged with
 remunerative schemes and attractive pay scales to do research on environment related issues.

 policy makers, etc., and also for exchange of information and knowledge on different aspects of ocean science and

 help the Ministry in building up a skilled human resource base in environmental science, besides augmenting existing
 infrastructure facilities.

43.       The Committee is of the view that 90% of the country’s medicinal plants occur in forest habitats.  The most
 important strategy for conserving the medicinal plant gene pools is to establish a nation wide network of forest gene banks
 (average size 200-500 ha in tropical forest and 50-100 ha in high altitude forest) across the different forest types and
 altitudinal range in all bio-geographic regions of the country.  In view thereof, the Committee recommends that Ministry
 should urgently initiate programmes or in-situ conservation of genetic diversity. The Committee also feels that
 conservation of genetic diversity and documenting the entire traditional medicinal plants should be undertaken in such a
 manner so as to plan for development of traditional medicinal plants.  Besides, the Ministry, in collaboration with CSIR,
 DST, DBT and Ministry of Rural Development, should support community owned enterprises based on medicinal plants. 
 (Para 98)
44.       TBGRI is the only organization in India, which maintains a 300 acre conservatory garden for the wild tropical plant
 genetic resources of India, besides a well integrated multidisciplinary R&D system dealing with conservation,
 management and sustainable utilization of tropical plant resources.  The Institute is ideally located at the foothills of the
 Western Ghats.  The institute has 121 acres of forest land with hills and hillocks, portions of evergreen, decidous, marshy
 and riverine vegetation in addition to grass lands and rocky areas.  All of them to large extent provide different
 microclimatic conditions for different species.  The Institute also have a well-organized Arboretum, Palmetum,
 Bambusetum, Orchidarium, Medicinal plant garden and Field Gene Bank of wild plant collections to which new additions
 are being carried out from the topical areas of the country.  The Institute has about 20 major projects at operational stage
 on survey and inventory conservation and sustainable utilization of plant genetic resources in India.  TBGRI is accredited
 to UNCED and enjoys the membership of Botanic Gardens Conservation International.  The Institute is the coordination
 and National Database Center for All India Coordinated Research Project on Ethnobiology sponsored by the Ministry of
 Environment and Forests, Government of India.  TBGRI also harbours a well-established herbarium of vascular plants
 and fungi and a group of well-trained taxonomists.
(Para 100)
45.       Keeping in view above the facts, the Committee considers it as an institute of national importance and, therefore,
 recommends that the TBGRI should be elevated to a national institute under the administrative control of Ministry of
 Environment & Forests.
(Para 101)
