INTRODUCTION
 
            I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the report on its
 behalf, present this Eleventh Report of the Committee. This Report deals with the Detailed Demands for Grants (1994-95) of the Department of Ocean Development.
2. In its sitting held on April 8, 1994, the Committee discussed in detail the Demands for Grants of the Department on the basis of the documents made available to it
 and the submissions made before it by the Secretary and other officials of the Department in the course of their oral evidence.
3. The Committee considered and adopted this report at its sitting held on the 15th April, 1994.
4. The Committee expresses its appreciation for the officials of the Department for their co-operation and making available necessary material for consideration of
 Demands for Grants.
NEW DELHI 
 April 15, 1994
 
SYED SIBTEY RAZI

REPORT
            The Department of Ocean Development has proposed an outlay of Rs. 59 crores, which includes a Plan component of Rs. 46 crores and Non-plan component of
 Rs. 13 crores for the year 1994-95. This represents an increase of just Rs. 2.59 crores over that of the year 1993-94. In relative terms, there has been an increase of Rs. 3
 crores in plan outlay, which gets reflected in higher allocations being made for Marine Satellite Information Service, National Ocean Information System and National
 Institute of Ocean Technology vis-a-vis those made for these programmes during the year 1993-94; whereas in the Non-plan outlay, the expenditure is projected Rs. 41
 lakhs less. The comparative figures in terms of allocations for various programmes of the Department of Ocean Development for the years 1992-93, 1993-94 and 1994-
95 are given in    Annexure-I.
 
2.    The Committee before examining the outlay of the Department for the year 1994-95, vide Demand No. 87, went into the projected demand figures of the
 Department for the Entire Eighth Five Year Plan and the allocations actually approved therefore by the Planning Commission, as also their utilisation by the
 Department during the first two years of this Plan period. The committee finds that for the Eighth Five Year Plan (1992--97), the Department had projected an outlay of
 Rs. 656.05 crores to pursue its various programmes and activities against which a plan outlay of just Rs. 130 crores has been approved. Going by, the allocations for the
 first three years of the Eighth Five Year Plan period, the Department has already been allotted Rs. 39, Rs. 43 and Rs. 46 crores, which means that Rs. 128 crores out of
 an approved Plan outlay of Rs. 130 crores have already been given to the Department, leaving just Rs. 2 crores for it to meet its expenditure during the remaining two
 years of this Plan period. A pointed question was, therefore, raised in the course of the oral evidence of the Secretary of the Department and other officials concerned
 before the Committee on April 8, 1994 as to how the Department would manage its activities with this meager amount, in response to which it was explained that on
 being approached with the problem of allocation of resources for the ongoing programmes, the Planning Commission has assured the Department that its requirements
 will be considered at the time of finalisation of Annual Plan based on the performance in the previous years.
 
3.    The Committee notes that the Plan allocations for the Department for the first three years of the Eighth Five Year Plan have been notably of a high order given the
 total plan outlay approved for the Department for the entire Eighth Five Year Plan. Having thus been left just Rs. 2 crores for the remaining two years of the Eighth
 Plan, the Department cannot have a clear idea about its future shape in terms of availability of resources and therefore of the policy it should pursue on a long-term
 basis. The Committee is of the view that this uncertainty about the future financial outlay not only puts a question mark on the fate of on­going projects but also deters
 the Department to take up any set of coherent initiatives and, therefore, recommends that Department's priorities should be examined in right perspective and
 appropriate allocations be made leaving no scope whatsoever for any ambiguity about the Department's future planning.
4.    Another issue gone into by the Committee in the initial stage was the utilisation of allocated funds by the Department for the years 1992-93 and 1993-94. In both
 these years, the Department was unable to fully utilise the allocated amounts; during 1992-93 as against an approved Plan outlay of Rs. 39 crores, the utilisation was
 only to the tune of Rs. 31.18 crores, whereas during 1993-94 the amount spent provisionally was Rs. 36.74 crores as against an outlay (Plan) of Rs. 43 crores. The
 position for 1992-93 in real terms in respect of utilisation, however, does not appear to be so bad in view of the fact that an amount of Rs. 5.73 crores was transferred
 from Plan to Non-Plan to meet the liability of payment of arrears to the Shipping Corporation of India for maintenance and operation of the Department's research
 vessels ORV Sagar Kanya and FORV Sagar Sampda. This means that Rs. 45.52 crores was utilised during the year 1992-93 as against an allocation of Rs. 48.17 crores,
 both Plan and Non-Plan. In so far as 1993-94 is concerned, the detailed reason for under-utilisation item-wise is given in the Statement at Annexure-H. A persual of this
 Statement would show that quite large sums of money have been reappropriated to other schemes and there has been under-spending in respect of Exhibitions & Fairs
 and Marine Instrumentation. The Committee feels that while this process of reappropriation may be warranted under certain special circumstances to meet some
 immediate needs, it should not to be encouraged as a matter of practice, and a system has to he evolved whereby the amounts earmarked for particular projects are spent
 only on those projects. Besides, it also looks somewhat inexplicable that the Department on the one-hand projects very high demands and on the other it is not able to
 fully utilise the smaller allocations for different schemes.
5.      After going into the preliminary issue of Eighth Five Year Plan projections, actual allocations for the first two years of this Plan period and their utilisation by the
 Department, the Committee examined the details of the Budgetary demands of the Department for the year 1994-95 and of some of the projects for which allocations
 have been sought. The Committee has noted that of a total budgetary Plan outlay of Rs. 46 crores for 1994-95, a major part has been given to a few ongoing
 programmes, namely, Antarctic Research (Rs. 16 crores), Polymetallic Nodules Programme (Rs. 9 crores), Marine Satellite Information Service (Rs. 4.96 crores),
 Coastal Ocean Monitoring and Prediction System (Rs. 1.35 crores) and Rs. 5.80 crores have been earmarked for the National Institute of Ocean Technology. A total
 figure of Plan allocations for these programmes comes to Rs. 37.11 crores and rest of the amount of about Rs. 9 crores, is spread over a number of small programmes.
6.      Two new major initiatives which form an important component of the entire programming structure of the Department for the year 1994-95 are the ones relating
 to-(i) setting up of a Marine Oceanarium in Goa; and (ii) Marine Data Buoy Programme for which a token provision of Rs. 10 lakhs each has been made in the Budget

 a joint project between the Department of Ocean Development and the Andaman Administration.
7.    The programme is in a tentative stage at present and the Committee would like to have full details before commenting on it. The other new major initiative of the
 Department is for Marine Data Buoy Programme which envisages deployment of MET-ocean Data Buoys at select locations on the Indian coast for collection of
 oceanographic data relating to winds waves, ocean currents, sediment transport and other important information which are necessary for establishment of a data base for
 use by agencies like Port Trusts, ONGC, GS and research institutions working in the Marine sector. For this programme a committee of experts has been constituted
 and feasibility report is under consideration. The Committee felt that this programme should be integrated with the international Global Ocean Observing System
 (GOOS) and the facilities to be created under it.
 
8.     Antarctic Research has been at the centre stage of the Department's activities since 1981. This programme is on an ongoing basis and is related to international
 treaty obligations. During the year 1994-95, the 14th Antartica expedition is proposed to be launched with a whole range of scientific experiments being part of its
 mandate. Of a total budgetary provision of Rs. 16 crores for Antarctic Research Rs. 7 crores are to be spent on chartering  running of Ice-breaker ship. This being a
 substantial amount being incurred every year on recurring basis, the Committee posed a question to the Department as to why it did nothing of purchasing a vessel of its
 own, specially when an indication to this effect was available from the provision of Rs. 25 lakhs made in the budget for the year 1994-95 for acquisition of an Ice-
breaker. In this context, the Department's attention was also drawn to the fact that the two oceanographic research vessels presently available with the Department,
 namely, ORV Sagar Kanya and FORV Sagar Sampada were, as admitted by it, ageing and there was need for their replacement. Instead of any budgetary provision
 being made for purchase of vessels for replacement of these ageing vessels, a provision of quite an high amount of Rs. 11.70 crores has been made just for their
 operation and maintenance. It was clarified by the Department that it proposes to acquire a multi-purpose vessel which would be useful for the Antarctica expedition
 and will also be utilised for oceanographic research work during the intervening period. For this specification have been drawn up and an inquiry has been floated in the
 global market to ascertain the availability of such ships, which can be modified to the country's requirements and the cost of such ships. It is only after getting this
 information that the actual requirement of funds can be projected. On being asked as to what would be the estimated cost of this multi-purpose vessel, it was explained
 by the Secretary of the Department that its cost would not be less than Rs. 75 crores, and that the Department was examining the possibility of arriving at an
 arrangement whereby a vessel could be acquired on some kind of lease, the probable terms of which would be that the Department would pay only an annual fee over a
 period of 20 years.
 
9.     The Committee feels that the proposal for acquisition of a multi-purpose research vessel that can also function as Ice-breaker, on lease basis, which would
 eventually confer ownership rights on the department, is a good one and should have engaged the attention of the Department earlier. In this context a valid point was
 made by the Secretary of the Department that the annual remmittance by way of lease fee for this type of vessel ought to be less than the expenditure which the
 department is at present incurring annually both in terms of maintenance of its vessels and the Antarctic. If the probable cost of this vessel is spread over a period of 20
 years, the net amount payable per annum excluding other liabilities by way of interest etc., would be to the tune of about just 4 crores of rupees, and taking into account
 the expenditure presently being incurred on hiring of a vessel for Antarctic Research, the preposition appears to be quite viable. The Committee recommends that the
 Department should expeditiously work out the modalities of the purchase of such a multi-purpose research vessel on lease basis after making a correct assessment of its
 needs and the financial implications involved therein. The committee would like to know the area of research that such a vessel could be used for and whether the
 replacement of FORV Sagar Sampada is envisaged.
                                                                                                           
10.     Another programme undertaken by the Department as part of its international commitments under the Law of the Sea Convention is the Polymetallic Nodules
 Programme Deep Sea-bed Exploration. This programme has three basic components (a) survey and exploration of the mining area, (b) development of technologies for
 collection and transportation of nodules from the mine site to the processing site; and (c) metallurgical processing of the nodules. The Committee was apprised that this
 programme has undergone thorough revision and the process of re-organisation of this programme undertaken in 1993-94 would be continued, with the emphasis being
 more on lower depth mining in the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) than on Deep Sea-bed mining. In this context attention of the Department was drawn to the
 committee's recommendation as contained in its Seventh Report to the effect that the possibility of involving the private sector in this programme might be examined so
 that the exploitation of the non-living resources could be more fruitful. It was explained by the Secretary of the Department that quite a few private sector/ institutions
 have already been involved in the development of mining system, some of which are M/s Tractors India Limited, a subsidiary of the Larsen & Toubro, Bombay, M/s
 B.M. Macro System, Madras and the NECO Industries, Calcutta.
            The Committee would be interested in seeing the private sector come into the programme not as contractors but as partners. In response to another query from
 the Committee as to why the allocations for the Antarctic Research and Polymetallic Nodules Programme have been reduced in the Budget estimates for 1994-95, the
 reply of the Secretary was that this has been done as part of deliberate strategy because the policy of the Department is to economise on long term programmes linked
 with international obligations and to spend more money on society and technology related programmes which have greater relevance to the Exclusive Economic Zone.
 The Secretary, however, clarified that reducing the expenditure from 40% to 34% would in no way have negative impact on the outputs of these programmes, because it
 was only a question of economising in the number of people who go on the Antarctica expeditions, and economising on the expenditure 'relating to the Antarctic Study
 Centre. The Committee is of the view that for any long term continuity in Antarctic research including analysis of data from our expeditions as well as from other
 sources, the Antarctic Study Centre has great significance; the Department could present a detailed report on the objectives and progress concerning this Centre. The
 Committee also wanted to know the reaction of the Department to the international forecasts that the commercial mining of Polymetallic Nodules cannot be
 economically feasible till 2010 and whether the Department thought that after 2010, mining of the Nodules will become cheaper and be commercially viable. The reply
 given was that the projections based on the studies carried out by the Australians and the French suggest that Deep Sea-bed Mining for Polymetallic Nodules will not be
 economically viable till 2010, but it will be too early to say whether mining of the nodules will become cheaper and be commercially viable beyond 2010 as this will be
 guided by market considerations and technologies available at that point of time.
11. It came to the notice of the Committee that the Department has been spending much in excess of the allocated amount on Marine Satellite Information Service
 continuously since 1991-92, and accordingly the Committee wanted to know as to what are the reasons therefor? Similarly, the Committee also wanted to know as to
 whether, going by the past trend the amount of Rs. 4.96 crores for which a provision has been made in the Budget Estimates for 1994-95 would be sufficient to meet the
 Department's requirements or the same phenomenon of excess spending will continue. The Committee was told that for 1991-92 and 1992-93, the Marine Satellite
 Information Service (MARSIS) project was in the formative stages of evolution. The potential for dissemination of fishing zone information has been realised greatly
 with and help of user interaction, and there has been greater demand for the services than was expected for these two years. Encouraged by this response the
 Department came up with and E.E.C. proposal for an amount of Rs. 17.75 crores for the four-year period starting from 1993-94 as per the E.F.C. proposal was Rs. 4.15
 crores and the actual allotment was only Rs. 3.66 crores. However, the shortfall was later on met by reappropriation, and an amount of Rs. 4.37 crores was spent in

13. The Committee has noted that insofar as the programmes mentioned at 1, 2, and 3, above are concerned, the Department's requirement is exactly in the same
 proportion in which a demand was made to the Planning Commission and against which allocations of Rs. 86 lakhs, 1 crore and 10 lakhs were made. In other words the
 Department has emphasised the need for this much minimum amount to effectively run these programmes. The requirement of Rs. 3 crores for development of pay load
 for Ocean Satellite appears to be new one following submission of a Project Report by the Joint Study Team of the Departments of Space and Ocean Development.
 While the programme on Marine Instrumentation aims to develop a communication system for fishermen, the other programmes intend to generate data on various
 ocean related parameters essential for protection, preservation and conservation of marine environment as well as for the effective management of our Coastal Zone.
 The Committee feels that the Department should be encouraged to take up the above new initiatives to the full extent and should not suffer for want of adequate
 financial resources for these programmes. The Committee feels that with regard to the Coastal Research Vessel, there should be coordination with the Geological
 Survey of India, which has significant responsibility in the Coastal Zone and continental shelf with regard to non-living resources.
 
14. It was brought to the notice of the Committee that in order to augment the resources available for technological development, it has tried to enter into bilateral
 technical collaboration agreements with a few countries, namely, Norway, Germany and Japan. An Indo-Norwegian Programme for collaboration in the field of sea-bed
 mining is actively being pursued. The Government of Japan has been approached for financing of two coastal research vessels for monitoring of marine pollution in
 India's EEZ. A programme for study of environmental impact of seabed mining has also been formulated as part of the Indo-German Collaboration Programme. While
 the Committee appreciates the efforts of the Department to mobilise external funding for its projects through these bilateral programmes, it feel that the Department
 should give serious thought to evolving fund generating projects with greater outside participation so as to make Department's schemes economically viable and self-
sustaining.

16. As compared to the year 1993-94, the allocations for international obligations have come down from 40.56% to 34.02% during the year 1994-95 and similarly the
 allocation for basic research and manpower has also come down from 14.51% to 9.20%. There has, however, been an increase of allocations by about 8% for
 Technology Related Programmes and by over 4% for Society-Related Programmes. The policy of the Department is now to gradually reduce spending on long term
 ongoing programmes and to enhance budgetary provision for technology and society related programmes. There is now perceptible shift towards Society related
 programmes like Marine Remote Sensing Information Service, Monitoring of Marine Pollution, shore to vessel communication system, aquaculture programmes in the
 Islands and monitoring and modelling of sea level variations, which gets reflected in 4% increase in budgetary allocations this year (1994-95). The Committee feels that
 monitoring of Marine Pollution and Prevention of degradation of marine environment is an area where the Department needs to concentrate more and widen the area of
 coastal monitoring with the help of vessels for the purchase/manufacture of which a provision of Rs. 1 crore for advance payment has been made in the Budget
 Estimates for 1994-95.
 
17. It came to the notice of the Committee that the Department has reduced the Budget heads from 22 to 15 during the year 1994-95 and accordingly the Committee
 wanted to know the reasons therefore. The reasons given by the Secretary of the Department was that this has been done at the behest of the Planning Commission
 because every time the Department sought Plan allocations, a query was invariably made as to what are the programmes which have been closed and what are the
 programmes which have been regrouped. The Secretary, however, underscored the point that even in reducing the Budget heads they have tried to ensure that the thrust
 areas which the Department has identified in the course of the year 1994-95 are not jeopardised. This led to a point being raised as to whether the reduction in the
 number of budget heads would not be tantamount to doing away with transparency which is now-a-days becoming the keynote of Government's liberalisation policy.
 The clarification given by the Secretary was that the case was not like that as only those areas where there is some commonality budget heads have been merged. Two
 examples cited by him were that the International Geosphere and Bio-sphere Programme, which was an international type of programme, has been merged with
 international co-operation and the Budget head of Assistance to Universities has been brought under Manpower Training.
18. The Committee appreciates that the Department has enhanced socially relevant and technology innovative programmes in important fields of the Ocean sectors and
 plans to take up new initiatives in priority areas like Marine Pollution Monitoring, Development of Communication Systems, National Data Buoy Progamme and
 launching of Ocean Satellite which are of great relevance to the coastal communities. The collection, processing and dissemination of data and information under these
 programmes is an important constituent of the DOD National Ocean Information System which is a vital tool for the integrated management of our coastal zone. The
 Committee is, however, of the view that the Department as it is at present does not have great strength or capabilities relating to marine technologies; those exist in

STATEMENT SHOWING THE REASONS FOR UNDER
 UTILISATION DURING 1993-94 ITEM WISE
 

11th Report of Committee on S &T

Out of Rs. 43 crores provided for 1993-94, the Department utilised Rs. 36.74 crores (provisional figures). Major variations have occurred in respect of the following
 programmes-
 
(1) Polymetallic Nodules Programme :
 
            As the entire programme is under restructuring based on the recommendations of two expert committees, about Rs. 4.9 crores has been reappropriated to other
 schemes like National Institute of Ocean Technology where the requirement is more immediate, and surrendered Rs. 1.58 crores.
 
(2) Antarctic Research Programme :
 
Out of an allotment of Rs. 17.5 crores, the Department spent Rs. 13.7 crores on the programme. Apart from economising on the programme to the extent of Rs. 1 crore,
 an amount of Rs. 1.6 crore has been reappropriated for more immediate, needs and the rest surrendered.
 
(3) Exhibition & Fairs:
 
Out of an approved allotment of Rs. 1.00 crore, the Department spent Rs. 18 lakhs only as the Trade Fair Authority of India could not make available the permanent
 pavilion in Pragati Maidan.
 
(4) Marine Instrumentation:
 
             Under the head Marine Instrumentation, an amount of Rs. 86 lakhs has been provided m 1993-94. The Department utilised Rs. 21 lakhs and surrendered Rs. 65
 lakhs. This is because of non supply of required number of walkie-talkie sets by the Marine and Communication Electronics (MACE) Ltd., Visakhapatnam. The
 supplier tried for customs duty exemption for import of vital component and this has delayed the process of supply of sets.
 
(5) National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT) :
 
Approved • Rs. 0.05 crores    Utilised : Rs. 4.64 crores
 
As acquisition of vital equipment could not be postponed any further, an amount of Rs. 4.6 crores was reappropriated from the Polymetallic Nodules Programme and
 Antarctic Study Centre to NIOT.
 
(6) Marine Satellite Information Service (MARSIS) :
 
As against an allotment of Rs. 3.66 crores for MARSIS programme, the utilisation was Rs. 4.38 crores. The allocation was less than the required amount of Rs. 4.18
 crores as per the year-wise expenditure projected in the EFC. This necessitated reappropriation and a further amount of Rs. 20 lakhs was provided to Jawaharlal Nehru
 Technological University, Hyderabad for conducting studies on Bio-optical modelling for Generation of Chlorophyll Data.
