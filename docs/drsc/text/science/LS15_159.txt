 and wildlife, prevention and control of pollution, afforestation and regeneration of degraded areas, protection of the
 environment and ensuring the welfare of animals. These tasks are sought to be fulfilled through environmental impact
 assessment, eco-regeneration, assistance to organizations implementing environmental and forestry programmes,

            REASONS FOR SHORTFALL IN UTILISATION OF THE OUTLAYS
 
18.       The broad reasons for shortfall in the utilisation of the outlays during 2005-06 were as follows:
Delays in approvals/investment approvals of schemes and other procedural delays including delay in receipt of
 proposals from implementing agencies. 
Non receipt of sufficient proposals/detailed project reports and delays in submission of utilization certificates.
Delays in appraisal process of sanction/completion of procedural requirements.
In many schemes, implementing agencies are state governments/ Forest Departments whose performance depends on
 timely release of matching grants.
Under e-governance the shortfall was due to the methodical and well-defined process of selection of consultant adopted
 by the Ministry with the assistance from National Institute for Smart Governance (NISG) for SR specifications and
 software development.
Delays in tender/land acquisition /litigation process by the project implementing agencies in river conservation projects.
 Non utilization of externally aided component of river conservation plan was on account of time taken by states in
 appointment of consultants for various sewerage and non sewerage works. The actual physical works are likely to start
 in 2006-07. 
 
19.       The Committee observes that the reasons cited by the Ministry for shortfall in utilization of the budgetary
 outlays during 2005-06 are mainly attributed to delay in getting approvals, appraisal, finalizing of contracts and non-
receipt of sufficient proposals and other such procedural delays. This reflects the lackadaisical approach on the part of
 Ministry to implement its programmes on timely basis. The Committee is of the view that the Ministry should make
 every effort to avoid such procedural delays for effective and timely implementation of the schemes. It needs to have a
 pro-active approach in this regard.

RESEARCH
23.       The Research programme of the Ministry aims at promoting research in multi-disciplinary aspects of
 environmental protection, conservation and development. The objective of the scheme is to generate information
 required to develop strategies, technologies and methodologies for better environmental management. It also aims at
 attempting solutions to practical problems of resource management, conservation of natural resources and eco
 regeneration of degraded areas. Further, the scheme also seeks to strengthen facilities to facilitate research and
 scientific man-power development. During 2006-07, the allocation for the Research Programme is made in respect of
 Indian Council of Forestry Research & Education (ICFRE), Dehradun and Indian Plywood Industries Research Institute
 (IPIRI), Bangalore. ICFRE was set up in 1986 with a view to achieve the national goals of conserving existing forest
 area and increasing productivity of forests. In addition to ICFRE, following five institutes in different eco-geographic
 regions of the country take care of the research needs of the regions :
Institute of Wood Science and Technology, Bangalore.
Institute of Forest Genetics & Tree Breeding, Coimbatore.
Institute of Deciduous Forests, Jabalpur.
Institute of Arid Zone Forestry Research, Jodhpur.
Institute of Rain & Moist Deciduous Forests Research, Jorhat.
           
24.       The budgetary allocation for research programmes of the Ministry has been supported with an allocation of Rs.
 70.94 crores during 2006-07 as compared to last year’s Rs. 66.66 crores in BE Stage.  The Ministry informed that
 during the year 2005-06, 67 new research projects were initiated and research activities were carried out in 337 ongoing
 projects by ICFRE. 29 research projects were completed by IPIRTI, Bangalore. The increased allocation will be
 utilized for undertaking a few more research projects by ICFRE in consultation with the stakeholder State Forest
 Departments and industries and other stakeholders by IPIRTI.    
           
25.       The Committee feels that the scope of forestry research should cover not only biological and technological
 aspects (forestry, forest products, conservation, wildlife), but also a wide spectrum of economic, environmental,
 sociological and policy research since the continuous and sustainable development of forestry would depend on
 research inputs in crucial areas and expanding the knowledge in various fields. Moreover, Universities providing
 forestry research should be given grants-in-aid for strengthening their infrastructure and technical capabilities.
 Opportunities should also be provided for furthering the academic advancement of foresters/scientists/academicians in
 the field of forestry. The Committee would also like to know the achievements made out of these researches
 particularly during 2005-06 in which a number of research projects were undertaken/completed.

GREGARIOUS FLOWERING OF MULI BAMBOO
61.       The specter of gloom is haunting the North East region once again with reports of the natural phenomena
 occurring in different parts of the north-east India. Experts at the Rain Forest Research Institute (RFRI), Jorhat has
 anticipated another gregarious flowering of Muli bamboo between 2005-07 over an area of 18,000 sq. kms in India’s
 north-eastern states of Mizoram – the epicentre, Tripura, Manipur, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya and in parts of
 Assam. Manipur’s Tamenglong district is already reeling under a rodent menace, the outcome of bamboo flowering.
62.       For the people of the region, the flowering of the bamboo blossoms means that the inevitable famine is not far
 behind. As per records, flowering of this very species of the bamboo (Muli bamboo) led to famines in 1862, 1881,
 1911-12 and 1959. The 1959 famine claimed between 10,000 and 15,000 lives in Mizoram, Tripura, Manipur and
 Barak Valley of Assam. Based on this, the projections have been made that the next flowering cycle is expected to
 occur during 2004-2007. The phenomenon can be explained as a chain of events occurring in the following sequence.
 The gregarious flowering of bamboos produces large quantities of seeds, which in turn causes sudden population
 explosion in rats. However, the quantity of seeds available for rats diminishes soon on the germination of seeds after
 the rains. The resultant short supply of bamboo seeds on the one hand and a large population of rats on the other, makes
 rats heading towards the farmlands in the adjoining areas and causing wide spread loss to the crops. Such a chain of
 events has the potential to cause famine. 
63.       Most species of bamboo in India — home to the world’s largest bamboo forests — flower simultaneously every
 50 years, then the bamboo germinates its seed and dies subsequently. In previous years, the simultaneous production of
 seeds by millions of bamboo plants has caused a surge in the number of seed-eating rodents. The rodents then move to
 nearby paddy and potato fields in search of food, with a devastating effect on staple crops. Rats literally thrive on the
 seeds of bamboo flowers. Experts say the rodents multiply at a tremendous rate as they get enough food to sustain a
 large population from the bamboo flowers and seeds. The flowers and seeds also enhance the estrogen level of rodents,
 leading to a spurt in the rodent population.
64.       The gregarious flowering of muli bamboo is a natural ecological phenomenon having high social and economic
 implications.  The Committee, therefore, feels that the Ministry of Environment & Forests should collaborate with the
 State Governments to gear up for the impending catastrophe, since the magnitude of the disaster could be very large.
 The Committee recommends that the focus areas in this regard should be harvesting, regeneration of the area and
 rodent control.

WILD LIFE PRESERVATION
           
67.       Under the Wild Life Preservation Programme, the Ministry has sought a budgetary allocation of Rs. 142.98
 crores for the year 2006-2007. The major Centrally sponsored schemes under this Programme are Project Tiger, Project
 Elephant and Development of National Parks and Sanctuaries. Out of this budgetary allocation of Rs.142.98 crores,
 Rs.29.50 crores has been sought for Project Tiger for the year 2006-2007, while for Project Elephant it is Rs. 13.09
 crores and for Development of National Parks and Sanctuaries, Rs.49 crores have been sought. These schemes aim not
 only at protecting the endangered species of wild life, but the entire eco-system.
           
68.       Although the sum sought for budgetary allocation is quite impressive, the basics of wild life conservation cannot
 be successfully accomplished unless certain ground realities are improved.
           
69.       In this connection, the Committee while examining some of the important recommendations in the Report of the
 Tiger Task Force appointed under the direction of the Prime Minister of India following the Tiger fiasco in the
 protected reserve of Sariska in Rajasthan has noted that about 18.2 per cent of the posts for field staff i.e., forest guards,
 foresters and rangers across 28 Tiger reserves are vacant. Among these, the vacancy in the post of guards is
 comparatively higher than other field staff position.
 
70.       The Task Force in its Report has also observed that the training position for foresters and guards is far from
 satisfactory in most of the reserves. 
 
71.       Keeping in view the aforesaid observations made by the Tiger Task Force in its Report, it is observed that the
 basics of the wild life conservation demand for extremely devoted, trained and high quality performance of existing
 staff alongwith strong law enforcement.
           
72.       Another factor, which the Committee feels is very important, is to provide special package for staff, amenities
 and incentives like hard area allowance, free rationing etc. for the forest protection guards combined with provision for
 basic infra-structure, such as wireless sets, cell phones, vehicles etc. so that these could be effective incentives to boost
 wild life conservation. Fast track courts for quick judicial settlements of wild life crime cases, in the opinion of the
 Committee, can also do great justice towards conservation of endangered species in India like Tigers, Elephants,
 Rhinos, etc.
           
73.       For mitigation of human- tiger conflict, the minimisation of livestock killings is an effective countering method
 towards socio-ecological constraints which may stand in the way of wild life conservation. To minimise the ill-effects
 of livestock killings on people, education and awareness programmes related to ecology and  behaviour  of   tiger, 
 genesis  of  tiger menace, possible   mitigation  strategies, need to be framed for  the villagers in the important areas
 with a package for effective  and quick compensation in genuine cases and effective participation of the village
 populace at the ground level which can immensely contribute for wild life conservation of endangered species.
