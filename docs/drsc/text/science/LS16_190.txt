PREFACE
I, the Chairman of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and
Technology, Environment and Forests, having been authorised by the Committee to present the
Report on its behalf, present this One Hundred Ninetieth Report of the Committee. This Report
deals with the detailed Demands for Grants (2008-2009) of the Department of Biotechnology
(Ministry of Science and Technology) (Demand No. 85).
2. In the meeting of the Committee held on 1st April, 2008, the Secretary and other Officers
of the Department of Biotechnology gave an overview of the various activities of the Department
and the Members sought clarifications on various aspects of the performance of the Department
to enable it to scrutinise the Demands for Grants.
3. The Committee expresses its thanks to the Officers of the Department for replying to the
clarifications sought by the Members and placing before it the required material to enable the
Committee to scrutinise the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology.
4. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its meeting held on the 23rd April, 2008.

REPORT
The Department of Biotechnology which was established as an Independent Board in
1982, and became a full-fedged Department in 1986 has completed more than two decades of
existence. During this period the focus has been on enhancing the knowledge base and generating
highly skilled human resource, nurturing leads of potential utility and bringing bio products to the
market place. In the area of HRD, the Department has implemented an integrated programme to
generate adequate and appropriately trained manpower. The Department has established about 35
Biotech facilities during the Tenth Five Year Plan for production and supply of biological
reagents, culture collections and experimental animals which are available to scientists, industries
and students. An extensive Bio-Informatics Network covering 65 Institutions spread all over the
country has been established. Some of the Important achievements in the field of Crop
Biotechnology include transformation technology for rice for salinity and drought tolerance,
molecular marker for wheat quality trades; Bt novel Cotton Technology; Transgenic virus
resistant Tomato; GM Mustard with higher yields, etc. In the area of Animal Biotechnology a
technology for recombinant anthrax vaccine has been developed and transferred. In the field of
Medical Biotechnology, development of new technologies for affordable vaccines for Malaria,
Tuberculosis, Cholera, HIV , Filariasis, etc. have been undertaken. Stem Cell Research is also
being promoted for basic and translational research. In the area of Environmental Biotechnology,
several technologies are ready for commercialization like Hioremediation of mine spoil dumps;
technology for mangrove afforestation, etc. Various technologies have also been developed for
manufacture of nutrient supplements, health food, fruit juice processing, oil and fat modification,
ensuring food quality/safety etc.
1.1 The National Biotechnology Development Strategy was approved by the Government of
India during the current year. The strategy is an outcome of a two-year-long nationwide
consultation process with multiple stakeholders including concerned ministries, universities,
research institutes, private sector, civil society, consumer groups, non-government and voluntary
organizations and international bodies. The draft strategy, which was posted on the web, received
over 300 comments from all sections of the society. The strategy, while enabling the full utilization
of currently available opportunities in manufacturing and services, will lay a strong foundation for
discovery and innovation, effectively utilizing navel technology platforms with potential to
contribute to long terms benefit in agriculture, animal prductivity, human health, environmental
security and sustainable industrial growth.
1.2 The autonomous institutions like National Institute of Immunology, New Delhi, National
Centre for Cell Science, Pune, and Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and diagnostics, Hyderabad, etc.
under the fold of DBT have made significant achievements. Four International and ten Indian
patents were granted to National Institute of Immunology. The repository at the National Centre for
Cell Science is the only repository that houses human and animal cells in India. The Centre has
a total of 155 publications and has obtained 6 US and 3 Indian patents. The Department has
supported setting up of biotech parks at Lucknow and biotech Incubation Centre at Hyderabad,
Bangalore, Kochi, Chandigarh and Solan. The Department has launched Small Business Innovation
Research Initiative (SBIRI) to boost Public Private Partnership in the country.

2. BUDGETARY ALLOCATIONS AND FOCUS AREAS FOR ELEVENTH PLAN PERIOD
2.1 The total budgetary allocation made for the Department for Eleventh Five Year Plan is
Rs. 6389.00 crores. The Eleventh plan focuses on cross-cutting issues such as human resource
development, smarter re-entry for our scientists abroad, new and varied models of creating
innovation-friendly micro-environment, R&D in small and medium size companies, newer ways of
collaborating with large companies, establishing centre of excellence, remodeling life science
departments in universities/institutions, establishment of technology management centre, promoting
dynamic biotech regional clusters, interdisciplinary networks, academic and industry interface
infrastructure development lab and manufacturing promotion of industry and trade expansion of
autonomous institutions and establishment of new institutions, biotechnology parks and incubators,
regulatory mechanisms, public communication and participation. The Eleventh plan also aims to
chalk out the path of progress in sectors such as agriculture and food biotechnology, industrial
biotechnology, medical biotechnology, bio-engineering, nano-biotechnology, bio-informatics and IT
enabled biotechnology, bio-resources, environment, intellectual property and patent law, international
cooperation and of course in meeting the basic needs of the society.

4. BIOTECH FACILITIES AND PROGRAM SUPPORT
4.1 The Committee was informed that the strength of biotechnology development in the country
lies in up scaling a number of proven technologies-diagnostic, vaccines, products, and processes
for fine-tuning and large-scale production. While Indian industry is strong in product development
and marketing for commercial benefits, biotechnology in India still lacks necessary infrastructure
for R&D in molecular modeling, protein engineering, drug designing, immunological studies, pre-
clinical studies, clinical trials, etc. The public investment in major equipment required for research
and development in biotechnology should be directed so as to maximize access not only within the
institution but also for other institutions and industry both in public and private sectors for optimum
utilization of resources.
4.2 It was further informed that the Department has been supporting establishment of Biotech
Facilities in Institutes/Universities to undertake production and supply of biological, reagents,
culture collections and experimental animals. These facilities also conduct regular training
programmes for capacity building in the areas of instrumentations, handling of small animal house,
bio-processing, microbial taxonomy and molecular biology.
4.3 Till the end of the Tenth Plan (2002-07) the Department had established about 35 facilities
across the country. A major review at the end of Tenth plan on the performance and relevance of
these facilities was carried out. Most of the facilities are now operated by the host institutions and
their agencies while others were continued for support through remodeling and up gradation to suit
contemporary needs. New facilities are being continuously established keeping in view the National
Biotechnology Development strategy of the Department.
4.4 Allocation made for ‘Bio-Tech Facilites’ has shown an increase from Rs. 22.50 crores at
BE and RE stages in Budget 2007-08 to Rs.25.00 crores in Budget 2008-2009. The Department
has informed that the increase is due to implementation of a newly proposed programme in the
Eleventh Five Year Plan aimed at remodeling/reengineering of university departments of Life
Sciences for taking up new research agenda for global competitiveness. The programme envisages
faculty induction, upgradation of postgraduate laboratories/fellowships, providing common
sophisticated instrumentation facilities.
4.5 The Committee is of the view that remodeling/reengineering of university
departments of Life Sciences is of utmost importance in order to remain globally
competitive. The Committee expresses its concern over the lack of infrastructure for R&D
in molecular modeling, protein engineering, drug designing, immunological studies, pre-
clinical studies, clinical trials, etc. as without a strong R&D base success of the Department
will remain short-term and would fail to sustain itself in the long run. Therefore, the
Committee recommends that infrastructure for R&D needs to be built up adequately to
achieve the desired targets fixed in the Eleventh Plan period.

7. HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT
7.1 An exercise to formulate model undergraduate and postgraduate curricula in life sciences
and in translational science keeping in view, future needs, new PG teaching programmes in the
areas of food and nutrition biology, clinical pharmacology, bioenterprise management, biofinancing
and regulatory efforts shall be initiated. M.D/Ph. D programmes will be supported in some medical
colleges/ institutions. At least five star undergraduate colleges in biotechnologies/life sciences will
be in place. Few teacher and technician training centres will be setup. The existing programmes
like Ph.D., Post-doctoral Fellowships and others will be scaled up. Besides continuing
and expanding the fellowship, need based new fellowships to promote innovation will be
instituted.
7.2 The Committee has been informed that the country produces lesser number of PhDs,
in comparison to other developing countries like Brazil and China except in engineering.
This has grave implications for Scientific Research in future. The committee recommends
that this situation be rectified so as to create a good research base in the country and that
the Department must play a pro-active role in this regard. The number of PhDs is bound
to be less unless and until students are initiated in the subject at a young age. The
Department should evolve a suitable long-term mechanism for the purpose. But the
Committee feels that this would not be possible as the allocation under this programme is

Rs. 30 crores as against Rs. 70 crores proposed. The Committee is of the opinion that

8. PUBLIC-PRIV ATE PARTNERSHIP
8.1 Allocation made for ‘Public-Private Partnership’ has been enhanced from Rs. 43.56 crores
at RE stage in Budget 2007-08 to Rs.60.00 crores in Budget 2008-09. The Department has
informed that as proposed in the Eleventh Five Year Plan, the department will be launching a new
Public-Private Partnership (PPP) scheme ‘Biotechnology Industry Partnership Programme (BIPP)’
aiming at development of futuristic technologies and to address un-met national priorities in
agriculture and healthcare. Besides, it is proposed to establish 'Biotechnology Industry Research
Assistance Council ‘(BIRAC)’ for managing PPP programmes of the department, technology
management and licensing and catalyzing growth of small and medium enterprises.
8.2 The Committee is of the opinion that financial and technical support should be given
to individual innovators as good innovations many a times come from the unorganised
sector.The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should do all needful in
spreading the culture of knowledge based entrepreneurship.

research.

International Centre for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology till its member countries start

10.2 The Committee desires that the Centre should continue its research work in the
areas of Hepatitis, Malaria, recombinant gene product, plant molecular biology, plant

9
resistance, plant transformation, etc which will help for the development of Biotechnology

11. LUMPSUM PROVISION FOR PROJECTS/SCHEMES FOR THE BENEFIT OF NORTH
EASTERN REGION AND SIKKIM
11.1 Allocation made for ‘Lumpsum provision for projects/schemes for the benefit of
North Eastern Region and Sikkim’ has been hiked from Rs. 68.30 crores in RE stage in Budget
2007-2008 to Rs. 90.00 crores in Budget 2008-2009. The Committee has been informed that
the increase in the allocation is due to compulsory allocation of 10% of GBS of the Annual
Plan 2008-2009 of the Department. During 2007-2008, the Department was informed of the
non-exemption from this allocation at the final stages of budgetary exercise in the Ministry of
Finance leaving no scope for planning the schemes/programmes in advance. Even though,
Secretary, DBT and senior officials organized several meetings during the year 2007-2008 with Vice
Chancellors/Directors of the Universities/Institutions, adequate proposals could not be generated for
effective utilization of funds. However, the department has launched a major programme in tissue
culture propagation of spices and tress of North Eastern Region and set up bioinformatics units
in selected universities/institutions/ colleges.
11.2 The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should take serious and
sincere steps to make this scheme successful by not just allocating funds but also creating
capacity to absorb these funds. The Committee feels that this scheme should be seen as an
oppurtunity to involve local communities and provide them employment.
12. OTHER SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH
12.1 Plan Outlay for ‘Other Scientific Research’ has been enhanced from Rs. 614.70 crores at
RE stage in 2007-08 to Rs. 810.00 crores in Budget 2008-09. The Committee has been
informed that the expenditure as on 10.03.2008 is Rs. 554.00 crores (90 percent) as against
Rs. 614.70 crores. The new schemes includes Grand Challenge Programmes in vaccine
development and breeding crops by design; upgradation of university departments of life sciences;
new programmes for Promotion of Excellence and Innovation; and strengthening Public-private
partnership and establishment of university-industry incubators. New R&D projects in agriculture,
healthcare, RANi technology and nano-technology .
12.2 The Committee has been informed that while 7000 patents were filed globally, there
were only two patents from India, ‘The reason being lacunae our institutional design and
in our strategy for getting cutting-edge scientists,’ The Committee emphasizes the need to
improve improve incentive structure so as to attract good scientists and make efforts for
the same. The Committee is of the view that post-doctoral fel1owships are very low and
need to be increased to retain our scientists in the country.
12.3 The Committee is further of the view that in a dynamic and competitive
environment, quick decision-making is the key to success. Owing to involvement of several

10
ministries in decision-making process, initiation and implementation of schemes gets
considerably slowed down in our country. It is, therefore, required that a better coordination
at different levels of decision-making is established so that opportunities are not lost due
to undue delays.
12.4 After considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology and
assessing-the progress of various important programmes and projects administered by it, the
Committee recommends that the budgetary allocation of Rs. 919.00 crores under Demand
No.85 (Department Biotechnology) as sought by the Department for the year 2008-09 may
be approved.

additional fund at the RE stage so that the gap between the projected demand and the
actual allocation can be bridged so as to enable the Department to establish new
autonomous institutions. The committee is also of the view that the well thought out
projected demands of scientific departments should be honoured and accepted by the
Planning Commission, more so when the Department has the capacity and the readiness to
utilise the additional funds. (Para 3.2)
2. The Committee also strongly recommends that the Department should draw a
reasonable time frame for establishing new autonomous institutions and devise a
mechanism to monitor their progress periodically so as to avoid time and cost overruns.
(Para 3.3)
3. The Committee is pleased to know that the current trend of expenditure indicates
that there are no budget heads of the schemes under which the funds will be underutilized.
The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should continue its good efforts for
optimum utilization of funds. (Para 3.5)
4. The Committee desires that the reorientation of some of the above programmes,
which could not be completed, should be given a sharper focus and more efforts should be
made for their completion so as to avoid wastage of scarce resource owing to unnecessary
delays. (Para 3.7)
5. The Committee is pleased to know that the Department has been successful in
meeting its targets set for the Tenth Plan. Growth rate of 40% is encouraging and thus the
Committee desires that the Department should continue the good work during the next plan
period also. (Para 3.9)
6. The Committee is of the view that remodeling/reengineering of university
departments of Life Sciences is of utmost importance in order to remain globally
competitive. The Committee expresses its concern over the lack of infrastructure for R&D
in molecular modeling, protein engineering, drug designing, immunological studies, pre-
clinical studies, clinical trials, etc. as without a strong R&D base success of the Department
will remain short-term and would fail to sustain itself in the long run. Therefore, the
Committee recommends that infrastructure for R&D needs to be built up adequately to
achieve the desired targets fixed in the Eleventh Plan period. (Para 4.5)
7. The Committee is of the opinion that State Agriculture University should be given
required support to start interdisciplinary translational research centres so as to enable
them to emerge as solution providers for local agricultural needs. The Committee
recommends that the Department engages itself in exploring the possibilities for the use of
barren land for agricultural or horticultural purposes with the help of bio-technology, as this
would help bring a vast unutilised area under productive cultivation in the country. The
Committee would be happy to see progress in potential application of nano-biotechnology in

12
agriculture, medicine and environment. Special emphasis needs to be paid for use of
technology in preserving our environment which has seen large scale degradation. This
would go a long way in conservation of lakes which are getting polluted everyday. The
Committee would also like the Department to establish specialized virus research centres
to address viral biology, pathogenesis, biomarkers, etc. within a reasonable time-period.
(Para 5.2)
8. The Committee would also like the Department to focus and channelise its R&D
activities on programmes for using algae and jatropha as a source of biodisel and producing
ethanol from cellulose because the sources of fossil fuel are depleting fast but yet constitute
the major source of energy. This should be done in a targeted manner so as to make early
breakthroughs. (Para 5.3)
9. The Committee would like the Department to disseminate proven and field tested
technologies to help the rural population in their skill development, employment and
income generation in the field of agriculture, sericulture, etc. This has a potential not only
to provide self-employment and raise farm incomes but also to check migration to the
cities, which are already under stress. Self Help Groups along with other civil society groups
need to be roped in big way to make such programmes reach tribal, women and other
weaker sections of the society. (Para 6.4)
10. The Committee has been informed that the country produces lesser number of PhDs,
in comparison to other developing countries like Brazil and China except in engineering.
This has grave implications for Scientific Research in future. The committee recommends
that this situation be rectified so as to create a good research base in the country and that
the Department must playa pro-active role in this regard. The number of PhDs is bound to
be less unless and until students are initiated in the subject at a young age. The
Department should evolve a suitable long-term mechanism for the purpose. But the
Committee feels that this would not be possible as the allocation under this programme is
Rs. 30 crores as against Rs. 70 crores proposed. The Committee is of the opinion that
adequate resources should be earmarked for Human Resource Development. (Para 7.2)
11. The Committee is of the opinion that financial and technical support should be given
to individual innovators as good innovations many a times come from the unorganised
sector.The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should do all needful in
spreading the culture of knowledge based entrepreneurship. (Para 8.2)
12. The Committee welcomes the idea of spreading the scheme to Universities by setting
up university- incubators in selected universities based on previous performances in life
sciences. This will not only bring the universities in the ambit of professional research but
also mobilize contract assignments from small and medium industries and promote
academia-industry interaction thus helping universities to get more exposure for good
rersearch. (Para 9.2)
13. The Committee desires that the Centre should continue its research work in the
areas of Hepatitis, Malaria, recombinant gene product, plant molecular biology, plant
resistance, plant transformation, etc which will help for the development of Biotechnology
in our country. The Committee is of the opinion that there is a lot to learn from the
developed countries in the field of genetic engineering to address the health needs of our

13
country. The Institute can play a pivotal role in coordinating scientific and technological
exchanges with the west so as to obtain vital inputs for our health research. (Para 10.2)
14. The Committee is of the opinion that the Department should take serious and
sincere steps to make this scheme successful by not just allocating funds but also creating
capacity to absorb these funds. The Committee feels that this scheme should be seen as an
opportunity to involve local communities and provide them employment. (Para 11.2)
15. The Committee has been informed that while 7000 patents were filed globally, there
were only two patents from India. The reason being lacunae in our institutional design and
in our strategy for getting cutting-edge scientists. The Committee emphasizes the need to
improve the incentive structure so as to attract good scientists and make efforts for the
same. The Committee is of the view that post-doctoral fellowships are very low and need
to be increased to retain our scientists in the country. (Para 12.2)
16. The Committee is further of the view that in a dynamic and competitive
environment, quick decision-making is the key to success. Owing to involvement of several
ministries in decision-making process, initiation and implementation of schemes gets
considerably slowed down in our country. It is, therefore, required that a better coordination
at different levels of decision-making is established so that opportunities are not lost due
to undue delays. (Para 12.3)
17. After considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Biotechnology and
assessing the progress of various important programmes and projects administered by it, the
Committee recommends that the budgetary allocation of Rs. 919.00 crores under Demand
No.85 (Department Biotechnology) as sought by the Department for the year 2008-09 may
be approved. (Para 12.4)
