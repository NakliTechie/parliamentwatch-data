INTRODUCTION
The Department of Scientific and Industrial Research is one of the three Departments of the Ministry
of Science & Technology. The mandate of Department of Scientific and Industrial Research includes promotion
of industrial research for indigenous technology promotion, development, utilization and transfer.
1.1 The primary endeavour of DSIR is to promote R&D by the industries; support industrial units to
develop state-of-the-art globally competitive technologies of high commercial potential; catalyze faster
commercialization of laboratory-scale R&D; augment technology transfer capabilities; enhance the share of
technology-intensive exports in overall exports; strengthen industrial consultancy and establish a user-friendly
information network to facilitate scientific and industrial research in the country.
1.2  The DSIR has two public sector undertakings viz. National Research Development Corporation
(NRDC) and Central Electronics Ltd. (CEL) and two autonomous organizations viz. Council for Scientific
and Industrial Research (CSIR) and Consultancy Development Centre (CDC).  The Department also provides
host facilities and assistance to Asian and Pacific Centre for Transfer of Technology (APCTT) as the focal
point in the country.
1.3 The Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment
and Forests considered the Demands for Grants (2016-17) of the Department of Scientific and Industrial
Research in its meeting held on 31
st March, 2016.

3.2 The Department submitted before the Committee that the CSIR has been under tremendous financial
strain. Initiating the 12th Five Year Plan, the Planning Commission communicated to the Department of
Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) the Gross Budgetary Support (GBS) of ` 17,896.00 crore. Out of
the said GBS indicated, CSIR was allocated ` 17,400.00 crore and DSIR was allocated ` 496.00 crore for
the R&D activities to be pursued under various schemes of the 12th Five Year Plan. CSIR has received only
` 6,767.93 crore – a mere 38.9% of its total Plan Funds demand until the 4th (2015-16) year of the 12th FYP

the original allocation of ` 17,400.00 crore.
3.3 CSIR, thus, has been facing great challenges in pursuing the envisaged activities. Even under on-
going National Laboratories schemes, CSIR was required to either shelve or prune the R&D activities,
while it could make only token allocations for the proposed societal programmes such as CSIR-800. New
Schemes such as CSIR Scheme for Open Innovation; CSIR Initiative for Inclusive, Participative, and
Collaborative Research & Development; and National Civil Aircraft Development Programme could not
be materialized.
3.4 CSIR’s contribution for S&T Human Resource development in the country is significant. However,
in view of the increase in Research Fellowship amount w.e.f. 01.10.2014, and to meet the shortage of funds,
the number of doctoral and post-doctoral fellowships was considerably reduced. The funds for the National
S&T Human Resource Development scheme continue to be a constraint.

of ` 500 crore for the aforesaid Plan activities during FY 2016-17. This will not only help CSIR overcome the

5. RESULT FRAMEWORK DOCUMENT (RFD)
5.1 Result Framework Document (RFD) has been introduced by the Cabinet Secretariat’s Performance
Management Division since 2009-10, wherein the project performance of all scientific departments of the
Government is evaluated by a third party, appointed by the Performance Management Division of the Cabinet
Secretariat. One of the most important features of RFD is that under it, schemes, programmes and activities
are defined in quantitative terms and the achievements of the projects are reviewed by the Adhoc Task Force
consisting of former Secretaries of Government of India.
5.2 In the year 2013, the Committee had noted decline in RFD performance of the Department for the
years 2011-12 and 2012-13. Department of Scientific and Industrial Research had intimated that performance
of the Department of RFD score was expected to improve with the approval of 12
th Five Year Plan schemes
and implementation thereof.
5.3 The RFD score of the Department during the last five years has been as under:

5.4 The Committee enquired from the Department about the steps taken to arrest the declining trend of
its RFD Score during the last few years and the results thereof. In response, the Department intimated that
the RFD score for Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) for 2014-15, as assessed by the
Department, is 79.95 which is a measurable increase over the previous year. It was also submitted that the
RFD for the year 2014-15 was prepared by the Department but it was not evaluated by the Performance
Management Division (PMD) of the Cabinet Secretariat. RFD for the year 2015-16 was not initiated by
PMD in view of adoption of other mechanisms of performance review by the present Government e.g.
PRAGATI (Pro-Active Governance And Timely Implementation), eSamiksha, a real time, on-line system for
monitoring of follow-up action on the decisions taken during the presentations made by different Ministries/
Departments to the Hon’ble Prime Minister etc.
5.5 The Committee has noted the performance of DSIR evaluated by third party under Result
Framework Document (RFD) and finds that performance is declining continuously. From a score of
96 % in 2010-11, it has decreased to 80% in 2014-15. The Committee strongly disapproves such a
trend. The Committee also notes that RFD for the year 2015-16 was not initiated by PMD in view of

9
adoption of other mechanisms of performance review by the present Government e.g. PRAGATI
(Pro-Active Governance And Timely Implementation), eSamiksha, a real time, on-line system for
monitoring of follow-up action on the decisions taken during the presentations made by different
Ministries/Departments to the Hon’ble Prime Minister etc. In view of this, the Committee would
like to suggest that timely planning, approval and execution of the various programmes of the
Department may be ensured in order to perform better on this account. The Committee would also
like to be informed about the performance of the Department under PRAGATI (Pro-Active

6. AUTONOMOUS INSTITUTIONS Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)
6.1 The Council of Scientific & Industrial Research, constituted in 1942 is an autonomous body. The
detailed functions assigned to the Council are as follows:
(i) Promotion, guidance and coordination of scientific and industrial research in India, including
the institution and financing of specific researches;
(ii) Establishment or development and assistance to special institutions or departments of existing
institutions for scientific study of problems affecting particular industries and trade;
(iii) Establishment and award of research studentships and fellowships;
(iv) Utilization of the results of the researches conducted under the auspices of the Council towards
the development of industries in the country and the payment of a share of royalties arising out
of the development of the results of researches to those who are considered as having contributed
towards the pursuits of such researches;
(v) Establishment, maintenance and management of laboratories, workshops, institutes and
organizations to further scientific and industrial research and to utilize and exploit for
purposes of experiment or otherwise any discovery or invention likely to be of use to
Indian industries;
(vi) Collection and dissemination of information in regard not only to research but also to industrial
matters generally;
(vii) Publication of scientific papers and journals;
(viii) To enter into arrangements with foreign scientific agencies and institutions for exchange of
scientists, study tours, training in specialized areas of science and technology, conducting joint
projects, providing technical assistance in the establishment of scientific institutions and for
other matters consistent with the aims and objectives of the Society;
(ix) To do all other such things as the Society may consider necessary, incidental or conducive to
the attainment of the above subjects, etc.
The CSIR has been striving to perform these functions in accordance with the priorities laid down in
Five Year Plans and perceived needs of the nation from time to time.
6.2 The Council of Scientific & Industrial Research is known for its cutting edge R&D knowledge base
in diverse S&T areas. Having pan-India presence, CSIR has a dynamic network of 38 National Laboratories,

10
39 Outreach Centres, 3 Innovation Complexes and 5 units. CSIR’s R&D expertise and experience is embodied
in about 4000 active scientists supported by about 7000 scientific and technical personnel.
6.3 CSIR covers a wide spectrum of science and technology from radio and space physics, oceanography,
earth sciences, geophysics, chemicals, drugs, genomics, biotechnology and nanotechnology to mining,
aeronautics, instrumentation, environmental engineering and information technology. It provides significant
technological intervention in many areas with regard to societal efforts which include environment, health,
drinking water, food, housing, energy, leather, farm and non-farm sectors. CSIR is the Nation’s custodian
for Measurement standards of Mass, Distance, Time, Temperature, Current etc. CSIR has created and is
the custodian of Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) which is a powerful weapon against unethical
commercial exploitation of Indian traditional knowledge.  CSIR maintains Microbial Type Culture collection
(MTCC) and Gene Bank.
6.4 CSIR has been pursuing cutting edge science and advancing new knowledge frontiers. The scientific
staff of CSIR only constitutes about 3-4% of India’s scientific manpower but they contribute to 9.61% of
India’s scientific outputs. In 2013 and 2014, CSIR published 5086 and 5824 papers in SCI Journals with an
average impact factor per paper of 2.86 and 2.97 respectively. As per preliminary estimates, CSIR has
published 5743 research papers in International SCI journals during 2015.
6.5 The Committee notes and appreciates the efforts of CSIR in publishing large number of
research papers in International SCI journals every year. However, the Committee observes that
5743 research papers published in International SCI journals during 2015 also includes a large
numbers of articles, reviews, proceeding papers, editorial materials, letters etc. In view of this, the
Committee would like to suggest that CSIR’s main focus should be on original research work.

16. SCIENTIFIC VISION FOR THE FUTURE AND GENERAL OBSERV ATIONS &
RECOMMENDATIONS
16.1 The Committee during its meeting held on 31
st March, 2016 had opined that development of the
country at any phase is always linked with technology and technology happens when there is advancement
in science. Hence science, technology and development are all proportional to each other. A nation who is
not able to prosper on these grounds would never be able to sustain the lives there and may have to solely
depend on other nations for the basic requirements. Such is the influence of science and technology for the
development of a nation. A nation’s development and prosperity is judged to a large extent by the status of
science and technology of that nation.
16.2 The Committee enquired from the Department about its scientific vision for the future i.e. for
the next coming 10-15 years for making our nation a strong force in the field of science & technology
as compared to the developed nations of the world and making self-reliant in every aspect as the
Committee feels that scientific interventions is the key for societal and overall development of the
nation.

22
16.3 In response, the Department informed the Committee that R&D involves treading the un-treaded
path. It involves risk, more so when the aim is to develop a technology which can be globally benchmarked.
CSIR has been undertaking challenge driven R&D aimed at developing the desired products/technologies
and has thus been continuously striving to address the unmet needs through its unique S&T interventions
and thereby contribute to the socio-economic development in the country. It has been providing significant
technological intervention in many areas which include environment, health, drinking water, food, housing,
energy, specially chemicals & petrochemicals, glass & ceramics, medicinal plants & plants of economic
value, leather, mining, metals & minerals, machinery & instrumentation, strategic sectors including aerospace
etc. The journey of CSIR over the years has been quite satisfying.
16.4 The government is making all efforts to position our country as a global leader and to make Indian
industry globally competitive. In the present scenario of rapid globalizations, when we are facing challenges
nationally and internationally of rapid technological advancements along with an unpredictable obsolescence
of even the recently developed S&T interventions, CSIR is building policy and action interventions focusing
on “Technology Innovation and Translational Research” to achieve National Goals. Some of the initiatives
include:
(a) Leveraging Science, Technology and Innovation (STI): Focused R&D efforts through: CSIR
Mission-mode Projects; Translational Research Projects; etc. It is proposed to earmark 60%
of CSIR budget for translational research and technology innovation and mission mode
projects;
(b) Positioning STI Reward System: in order to encourage, motivate and sustain quality deliverables
emphasis is being laid up on “Responsive Science, Technology and Innovation” and “Outcome
Driven Performance” and giving the achievers “Performance Linked Rewards”;
(c) Enhancing  Industry Partnerships  and  last  mile linkages:  Through innovative PPP models,
Technology Parks and Incubation Centers across CSIR Laboratories, Skill Development
Initiatives; Institutional linkages with stakeholder ministries, MSME Clusters etc. The objective
is to accelerate technology deployment at scales.
(d) Measures to enhance Industry Contribution to Science R&D: Policy interventions to encourage
and broaden incentives to industry spending on R&D. Focus will be on developing seamless
and time bound technology transfer mechanisms.
16.5 Through the aforesaid endeavours, CSIR is committed to deliver on the National Missions for enhancing
Self-reliance, Self-sustainability and thus accelerating Socio-Economic Development in the country.
16.6 The Committee has taken note of the response of the Department and feels that the
Department of Scientific and Industrial Research, Ministry of Science and Technology plays a pivotal
role in the promotion of Science & Technology in the county. New initiatives in the field of S&T play
a significant role in the development of a nation, be it in the field of Aerospace Science & Engineering,
Agro, Food Processing & Nutrition Technology, Biology and Biotechnology, Healthcare, Marine
Sciences & Engineering, Chemicals & Petrochemicals, Ecology & Environment, Energy, Electronics
& Instrumentation, Mining, Metrology and Housing, Road & Construction, etc. All the great scientific
discoveries and technological achievements in our country have brought the societal development
and grown economic status and have created many new ways for the new generations to grow in the
technologically advanced environment. The Committee is of the view that although the Department

23
has undertaken many new initiatives & measures for achieving excellence in the field of Science &
Technology, a lot more needs to be done and achieved in this area. In order to successfully meet the
challenges of the future, the Committee recommends that the Department should undertake
meaningful initiatives to establish strong foundations for a leadership role in the world in various
areas of Science & Technology in order to complete with the developed nations like USA and China.
To achieve this goal, the Committee further recommends that adequate allocation of funds be made
available to the Department in order to fulfil its vision into reality.
16.7 The Committee observes that investment in Research and Development (R&D) is decreasing.
It is only about 0.48% of our GDP whereas in some other countries, it is 2-3%. It is also a matter
of concern that all over the world, as per information available with the Committee, about 66 per
cent of R & D funding comes from the private sector and about 25-33 per cent comes from the
public sector. In India, the ratio is reverse. In view of this, it is essential that representatives of
private sector/industries are get involved in order to contribute more in R & D in the country.
Therefore, the Committee recommends that investment in R&D must be increased so that research
work may be taken up at large scale and more and more representatives of industries/companies
should be motivated for making investment in R&D of the Scientific Ministries/Departments of the
country.
16.8 The Committee also feels that even though good amount of research work is being done, it is
not actually percolating down for the use of society. It is just good on the paper. Further, the
Committee also observes that a large number of patents have been obtained in India and abroad,
but only few of them have been transferred in sustainable technologies. Therefore, the Committee
recommends that efforts should be made by the Department for making use of various research
works for societal development and transferring patents into sustainable technologies.
16.9 The Committee observes that similar kind of research in various fields is going on in the
Scientific Ministries/Departments of the country especially in the Departments of Science &
Technology, Biotechnology, and Space and Ministry of Earth Sciences etc. For example, same type of
devices like electronic machine for checking blood pressure, blood sugar and milk adulteration is
also developed by the Department of Science and Technology (DST) besides Department of Scientific
and Industrial Research (DSIR). In view of this, the Committee feels that this shows some sort of
lack of co-ordination and consultation between the various Scientific Ministries/Departments of the
country. Therefore, the Committee recommends that a mechanism should be evolved for having a
constant and broad consultation/co-ordination amongst the various scientific Ministries/ Departments
about the areas/fields of research so that time, energy, money, manpower and brain of the country
may be utilized optimally.

for utilizing the allocated funds so that optimum utilization of allocated funds may be ensured.
