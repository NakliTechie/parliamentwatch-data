REPORT 
I, the Chairman of the Committee on Science and Technology, Environment & Forests 
having been authorised by the Committee to present the report on its behalf, present the 
Thirty-sixth Report, on the Demands for Grants pertaining to the Department of Scientific 
and Industrial Research for the year 1996-97 (Demand No. 75). 
2. In its meetings held on 13th and 22nd August, 1996 the Committee discussed in detail 
the Demands for Grants of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research on the 
basis of documents made av·ailable to it and the submissions made before it by the Secretary 
of the Department and the other representatives of the Department. 
3. The Committee expresses its thanks to the officers of the Department for replying to 
the clarifications sought by the Hon'ble Members and placing before it the required material 
to enable the Committee to scrutinise the Demands for Grants of the Department of 
Scientific and Industrial Research. 
4. The Committee considered and adopted the Report at its meeting held on 
September 3, 1996. 
5. The thrust of the programmes and activities of the Department of Scientific and 
Industrial Research (DSIR) is on the activities of Council of Scientific and Industrial 
Research (CSIR)- a premier Science and Technology (S&T) agency with a network of 40 
national laboratories, two complexes and 81 Extension and Regional Centres spread all over 
the country, Technology Promotion, Development, Utilisation and Transfer (TPDU) and 
National Information System for Science and Technology (NISSAT). 
6. Other major programmes of the Department include Research and Development by 
Industry (RDI) consisting of in-house R & D in Industry, Scientific and Industrial Research 
Organisations (SIROs) and Fiscal ·Incentives for Scientific Research; Programme Aimed at 
Technological Self Reliance {PATSER) consisting of development of new or improved 
technologies. Development of speciaVcustom built capital goods, absorption and adaptation 
of improved technology, studies and interactions concerning Technology Evaluation and pre­
feasibility reports of major sectors/products; Scheme to Enhance the Efficacy of Transfer of 
Technology (SEETOT) consisting of National Register of Foreign Collaborations (NRFC), 
Industrial Technology, Transfer and Trading in Technology (TA TT), Linkages with 
International Organisations including Asian and Pacific Centres for Transfer of Technology 
(APCIT) and Promotion and Support to Consultancy Services (PSCS) including the 
Consultancy Development Centre (CDC) and two Public Enterprises viz. National Research 
Development Corporation (NRDC) and Central Electronics Limited (CEL) engaged in the 
commercialisation of indigeneously developed technologies. 
6.1 The Committee was informed that CSIR laboratories have responded to the radical 
change and the liberalised economic policies introduced by the Government to integrate the 
Indian economy with globalisation. Consequently, successful technology transfers have taken 
place in many areas. The efforts of CSIR towards globalisation of its activities have resulted 
in entering into collaborative agreements, licensing of its technologies, and signing of MOUs 
\•.ritb international organisations like Du Pont Agricultural Products, USA; FMC 
Corporation, Commonwealth Science Council, Zymogenetics, USA; Silesia SA, South 

Africa; Chinese ~eronautical Establishment, Beijing; University of New South Wales. 
Australia; University of Lancaster, UK and Special Design Bureau, Moscow. Among the 
major industry and economy oriented programmes, those related to Drugs, Chemicals, 
Environment, Metallurgy, Agrotechnologies, Leather, Electronics, Transportation, Materials 
and Wood Substitute have made significant contributions. The collaborative efforts of 
Central Institute of Medicinal and Aromatic Plants (CIMAP) and Central Drug Research 
Institute (CDRI) on arteether are about to lead to the release of a drug for the control of 
cerebral of chloroquin resistant malaria in the market. 
7. The Secretary, DSIR informed the Committee that CSIR has made significant, 
contributions in R & D and technical services resulting in industrial and socio-economic 
Development. CSIR has also achieved excellence in basic research and is creating a strong 
infrastructure in terms of S&T Manpower in the country. The attention of the Committee 
was invited towards cash flow through sponsored research and consultancy in respect of 
CSIR laboratories which was to the tune of Rs. 177 crores in 1995-96 i.e. an increase of 
Rs. 40 crores over 1994-95. If one takes into account the accomplishments like the number 
of international patents which CSIR laboratories were filling, the product value of the goods 
that were coming out as a result of research and development carried out in CSIR 
laboratories and in terms of basic science and good research publications, one could 
conclude that CSIR laboratories inspite of constraints have been making good progress. The 
Committee was informed that during 1995-96, technologies generated and patent filing 
increased by about 25%. The industrial production based on the CSIR knowhow has also 
gone up. In monetary terms the industrial production through CSIR knowhow during the 
year 1994-95 was Rs. 2600 crores. 
8. Budget Estimates of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR) for 
the year 1996-97 provide Rs. 438.97 crores, of which Rs. 186.00 crores constitute the plan 
component and Rs. 252.97 crores the non-plan component. The Committee examined the 
budgetary allocations of the Department for the year 1996-97•. While considering the 
Demands for Grants of the Department, the Committee also had an opportu ~1ity to review 
the work relating to some of the projects and programmes which fall within the purview of 
the Department. 
9. On being asked about· the justification for 98% of DSIR's budget being spent as 
grants-in-aid to various organis-ations and departments, the Committee was informed that out 
of the total budgetary support of Rs. 438.97 crores to DSIR, Rs. 412.88 crores goes to 
CSIR, which being an autonomous body, receives budgetary support in the form of grants­
in-aid. The Secretary, explained that under the general financial rules any money given to 
autonomous bodies or registered societies is termed as grants-in-aid. The Secretary also 
informed that as Director General of CSIR, he is also the head of CSIR and therefore the 
fund that goes to DSIR gets transferred to CSIR, and its budget is treated as grants-in-aid in 
Parliamentary, legal and financial terms. The Committee was also informed about the issue 
of financing the laboratories and monitoring mechanisms prevailing in the Department of 
CSIR has 40 laboratories and 80 extension centres through which the research and 
development is carried out. Therefore, bulk of the expenditure is spent on funding research 
within their own laboratories. Another means of funding research is through extramural 
research, the principal of extramural research is to generate manpower in the country. 
Under this scheme examinat ions are conducted for selecting candidates who prepare for 
Ph.D., Junior Research Fellowship and Senior Research Fellowship. Besides, there is a 
•for headwise details see schedule. 

research scheme under which funds are given to graduates within the universities having linkage 
with CSIR laboratories. 
10. Asked about the monitoring mechanism the Secretary informed the Committee that the 
allocation to the laboratories is strictly monitored on the basis of specific projects having socio­
economic bearing. Since last year specific performance indicators for individual laboratories 
have been devised for optimal utilisation of the money. Accordingly, grants are linked to 
specific principles and targets. There also exists a close interaction with industry which joins to 
undertake research and development. Linkage with industry acts as a strong mechanism for 
monitoring as they are interested in achieving goals through their research. 
The Committee is of the view that with the increasing cross-linkages between industry and 
scientific research institutions/universities there is a strong possibility that it may lead to 
exclusive priority to Research and Development which may yield to immediate results. In the 
process research in pure or basic science is likely to be relegated to the background. The 
Committee therefore, observes that DSIR must put in concerted efforts to check the emergence of 
any such trend which may impair the importance of research in fundamental sciences. Besides, 
the Ministry should thoroughly monitor the projects. 
11. The Secretary, DSIR, underlined the need to modernise facilities at the national 
laboratories in order to carry out research of international standard and to be globally 
competitive. The Committee was informed that the Planning Commission had agreed to provide 
for modernisation an amount of Rs. 40 crores for 1996-97 at the time of the plan di..;cussion. 
However, the Government has pegged the current year's plan allocations for all the 
departments to the last year's level. Thus CSIR was allocated Rs. 181 crores only under the plan 
head. The Committee was apprised that the Finance Minister recognising the need for 
modernisation of the infrastructure of the laboratories has proposed in his budget speech a 
matching one time grant for the modernisation of CSIR and ICAR laboratories. However, the 
matching grant proposed by the Finance Minister would be available only from the financial 
year 1997-98 onwards. The CSIR would thus not have any plan allocations for modernisation in 
the ptesent year. The Secretary informed that a point regarding modernisation of equipment in 
the CSIR laboratories was also stressed during the meeting of the CSIR Society, which was 
presided over by the Hon'ble Prime Minister and also attended by the Finance Minister. 
The Committee observes that the yardstick adopted by the Finance Ministry ignores the 
imperative need of some of the laboratories of CSIR which need to update themselves to 
international level of research. The Committee therefore recommends that the G,)Vernment 
should restore the agreed plan allocation ofRs. 40 crores for modernisation at the earliest, say, at 
the stage of Revised Estimates for 1996-97. 
12. The Secretary, DSIR, informed that the committed liability for salary, pension and 
contingencies expenditure was expected to go up from that of 1995-96 by Rs. 32 crores in 
1996-97. Further, in 1995-96, out of the total estimated expenditure of Rs. 215 crores on pay and 
allowance Rs. 50 crores was met from the plan funds. As the Planning Commission has 
disallowed any expenditure or pay and allowance to be met from the plan funds and in'-' ted that 
the same should be met from non-plan. CSIR would be faced with a shortfall of Rs. 82 ores on 
non-plan side. 
The Committee, in its Thirteenth Report and Twenty-Fourth Report had noted that CSIR 
had been diverting funds from plan allocation to meet the expenditure in the non-plan areas. The 
Committee had recommended that enough funds be allocated to CSIR to enable it to meet its 
committed liabilities under non-plan so that the need for such diversion of plan allocation does not 
arise; and the plan funds could be utilised for their primary purpose. 

The Committee would· like to reiterate its earlier view (Twenty-Fourth Report) that 
despite a dear recommendation to make sufficient provision in the non-plan outlay of CSIR to 
meet its committed liabUity, adequate non-plan allocation has .not been made in the budget 
estimates for the year 1996-97. The Department is thus left with no alternative but to divert 
money from its plan {upds to meet its committed liabilities in non-plan areas. The Committee 
would like the Department to take up the problem with the Planning Commission and the 
Ministry of Finance so that plan funds are not diverted to non-plan activities. Such a 
diversion adversely affects the pace of R&D activities of the Department. The Committee 
therefore recommends that adequate budgetary support is made available to the Department 
at the RE stage to provide the Department with aaequate funds to meet its ·committed 
Jlabllities and thus obviate the need for diversion from plan funds. 
At the same time the Committee hopes that the Department would project its non-plan 
commitments for the Ninth Five Year Plan well in advance following the guidelines issued by 
the Planning Commission so that non-plan anomaly does not arise again. 
13. The Budget Estimates for 1996-97 for National Laboratories have shown a reduction 
from Rs. 317.53 crores in the previous year to Rs. 315.33 crores. The reduced allocation is 
of Rs. 2 crores on non-plan side. The Committee observes that the reduced allocation may 
hamper the pace of research and recommends that remedial steps should be taken say, at 
revised estimates stage to restore the allocation to last year's level. 
14. The Secretary informed· the Committee about CSIR's Technology Mission Mode 
Programmes. The mission mode programmes formulated in 1994-95 include Leather 
Technology Mission for · Sustainable Development and Clean Coal Technologies. The 
Leather Mission has been operationalised with the objective of improving the quality of 
leather produced at the grass-root level, to mitigate drudgery and improve the working 
conditions of deprived sections of the people, who are employed in tiny and small scale 
leather sectors. The Coal Mission is yet to be formally launched. Besides, CSIR has 
identified two other programmes that it proposes to implement under the Mission Mode i.e. 
development and upscaling of wood substitute materials for the building industry and 
technologies for upgrading quality of food. CSIR also continued to participate in and 
provide significant S&T inputs to two National Technology Mission Programmes viz., Rajiv 
Gandhi National Drinking Water Mission and Technology Mission on Oil Seeds and Pulses. 
The Committee observes that the Technology Mission Mode combines technology with a 
human face, CSIR should vigorously pursue the Programmes and initiate steps to launch the 
Mission on Clean Coal Technologies without further delay. 
15. It was stated that the DSIR scheme "Programme Aimed at Technological Self 
Reliance" (PA TSER) aims to promote and accelerate the efforts of industry to undertake 
Research, Design, Development and Engineering (RDDE) Projects for Domestic 
Technology Development as also for absorption and upgradation of imported technology. 
The Committee was informed that these projects have considerable commercial potential and 
relate to new or improved products/process technologies. Through this scheme, strong R&D 
linkages have been established amongst manufacturing industry, Government R&D 
laboratories and institutions as well as user/customer organisations in both the public and 
private sectors. 
It was stated that there were 57 on-going projects supported by DSIR requiring 
additional outlay of about Rs. 5 crores during 1996-97. ·Besides. about 30 new projects are 
proposed to be supported during 1996-97 involving an outlay of about Rs. 7 crores during 

1996-97. Of these new projects, 10 projects have already been approved involving an outlay of 
about Rs. 3 crores. Thus in all, there would be outflow of around Rs. 12 crores during 1996-
97. The Secretary also apprised the Committee that as the PA TSER programme is a jointly 
funded Research and Development project in industry, additional funds can be tapped from 
the venture capital funds which have been set up through public sectors .like lOBI, IFCI, and 
ICICI. This year about Rs.lOO crores has been set apart as venture capital money by lOBI. 
The Committee hopes that, by supporting an increasing number of important Research, 
Design, Development and Engineering (RDDE) projects of industry having high techno-soda­
commercial inputs, PA TSER will be able to promote and accelerate the efforts of industry in 
technology development as also involved industry in developing and commerdalising new and 
improved products or processes in various sectors of economy which would strengthen linkages 
between industry and national research organisations. The Committee feels that the allocation 
under the head is inadequate in view of the thrust of the Department in revenue generation 
through projects undertaken by the industry and the Committee recommends that the steps 
should be taken at RE stage to get the projected allocation. 
16. Asked about the policy framework for transfer of technology to oth~r countries, the 
Secretary apprised the Committee about the development of 'Artificial Heart Valve' through 
indigenous research. This artificial valve has been development Chittartirunal Institute of 
Medical Sciences and Technology, Thiruvananthapuram through funding from NRDC. This 
valve is certified for 80 million operations for a 40 year life time of a human being. The valve 
has been patented internationally and leading companies like Pfizer of Switzerland and Bayer 
of Germany are prepared to pay substantial amount for transfer of this technology but the 
matter is being reviewed in terms of national interest and commercial interests. 
The Committee is of the view that in view of the growing demand for export of our 
technology the Department should consider entering into joint ventures retaining maijority of 
the share for itself. Besides, steps be taken to protect our intellectual property rights. 
17. The public enterprises namely, National Research Development Corporation 
(NRDC) and Central Electronics Limited (CEL) attached to the Department qf Scientific and 
Industrial Research are engaged in important activities in the commercialisation of 
indigenously developed technologies. NRDC is involved in the transfer of technology from the 
laboratory to the industry and the market. NRDC acts as a technological intermediary while · 
CEL is involved in manufacturing and marketing process in Electronics, with its emphasis on 
indigenous technology, inducted both from its in-house developments and from National 
Laboratories , for its production programme in diverse high-technology areas of national 
importance. 
Asked about provision of adequate funds for Public Sector Enterprises, the Secretary 
informed the Committee that the provision is adequate and sufficient. The total investment in 
Central Electronics Limited (CEL), is to the tune of Rs. 6.10 crores ,consisting of an outlay of 
Rs.3.05 crores for equity and an outlay of 3.05 crores for loans. There is an additional , 
provision of Rs. 1 crores as support for research and development to CEL. Budgetary 
Support for NRDC is Rs. 0.40 crores as NRDC doesn't manufacture any products . 
The Committee is of the view that investments should be made in such a way that the 
public enterprises should be self-supporting and resource generating so that the provision for a 
loan Rs. 3.05 crores is avoided. Besides the Committee recommends that the time gap between 
the transfer of technology from laboratory to market should be minbnu.m to maximise the fruits 
of scientific research. 
18. While the Committee seeks to ensure that the Department of. Scientific and Industrial r 

Research is not starved of funds in order to carry out its scientific and research programmes, 
the attention of the Committee has been drawn to some of the audit observations as have 
appeared in the Report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India for years ending 
31st March, 1993 (No. 6 of 1994) and 31st March~ 1994 (No. 6 of 1995) in respect of 
Laboratories under CSm. The objection relates to time overrun ranging from 3 to 36 months 
for in-house projects, physical non-verification of stores, idle equipment lying for want of 
inadequate manpower, unutUised equipment and the amount of demurrage being paid on 
imported consignments. The Report also mentions about the amounts lying outstanding from 
the sponsors which is to the tune of Rs. 30.71 lakhs in case of National Geophysical Research 
Institute. 
The Committee would like the Department to attend to the audit observations and report 
to the Committee unless they have been attended to in the meantime. The Commlttee 
recommends that DSIR should take steps to ensure that the Project Monitoring and 
Evaluation is effective in each organisation under DSIR so that process of Project based 
Budgeting is monitored. 
19. Asked about the outcome of CSIR's study at Jadavpur University, Calcutta on 
development of technology for Arsenic removal, the Secretary informed the Committee that 
a process has been developed for the removal of arsenic from water by first passing it 
through a composition comprising a coprecipitating agent, an oxidising agent and activated 
carbon followed by filtering through a special water filter candle. The process has resulted in 
reducing arsenic in ground water with an efficiency of better than 96%. Patents have been 
filed for the invention in India and Bangladesh. Three hundred sets of the system have been 
given to the PHED .of Government of West Bengal for field trials and evaluation and their 
reports are awaited. The Committee notes with satisfaction the contribution made by DSm to 
assist the Public Health Engineering Department of the State aimed at tackling the problem of 
Arsenic removal from water. 
20. The Committee had earlier (in its twenty fourth report) expressed its concern over 
the lack of adequate housing facilities in CSIR. Asked about the progress in this regard, the 
Secretary, DSIR informed the Committee that CSIR was fully seized of the problem and 
had accordingly set up a Committee specifically for the purpose. This Committee of CSIR 
has since submitted its report which was under examination of CSIR. The salient features of 
the recommendat~ons envisage enh~ncing the HBA ceiling upto Rs. 8 lakh, hiring/leasing of 
500 houses in endeavour to achieve accomodation satisfaction level of 70% in remote areas, 
60% in towns and 50% in metropolitan cities over the IXth Five Year Plan. CSIR has 
accordingly souglit Rs. 150 crore for this activity in the ~Xth Five Year Plan. As housing was 
a pre-requisite to attracting talented scientists the Committee recommends that CSIR should 
take action in a stipulated timeframe and endeavour to reach satisfaction level of housing on 
priority basis.
