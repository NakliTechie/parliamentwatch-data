REPORT
The Report of the Committee deals with the Action Taken by the Department of Atomic Energy on
the recommendations contained in the Two Hundred and Ninety-ninth Report of the Department-related
Parliamentary Standing Committee on Science and Technology, Environment and Forests on the Demands
for Grants (2017-18) of the Department of Atomic Energy, which was presented to both the Houses of
Parliament on 31st March, 2017. The Committee had in all made thirty seven recommendations/observations
in the report. Action Taken Notes on the report was received by the Committee on 14th August and
4th December, 2017.
The Committee's recommendations, action taken thereon and comments of the Committee on the
Action Taken by the Department of Atomic Energy are set out in the following paragraphs:–
Recommendation
The Committee notes with satisfaction the achievements of the Department of Atomic Energy in its
62 years of operation. In addition to its considerable achievements in the nuclear power generation, the
Department has also produced useful spin-off applications for the benefit of the society and industry.
Keeping in view the Department's success, the Committee is confident that the Department will be able
to fulfil Ten Point Vision outlined by NITI Aayog for the Department. (Para 3.7)
Action Taken
The Department accepts the observations of the Honourable Committee. The Department is working
towards fulfilling the Ten Point Vision outlined by NITI Aayog.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
PERFORMANCE IN THE XII FIVE YEAR PLAN
Physical performance in XII Five Year Plan
Recommendation
The Committee feels that this phenomenon of underutilization of allocated outlay is a very serious
matter towards which the Department needs to focus its attention in a more professional manner and
initiate concrete measures. Merely, reiterating that the Department has already in place a robust monitoring
mechanism would not do any good particularly in view of the fact that underutilization of allocated
budgetary resources has turned out to be a recurring phenomenon. It becomes all the more imperative,
as underutilization of allocated resources provides the Ministry of Finance with an alibi to make less
allocation in the next year and also allows them to use it as a tool to reduce the approved BE outlay
at the RE Stage.
The Committee is of the considered view that the Department can justify its demand for additional
resources only when it is able to first utilise the entire resources allocated to it. The Committee, therefore,

2
recommends that the Department of Atomic Energy must make sincere efforts by way of meticulous
planning, timely implementation and stricter monitoring of schemes/projects. (Para 7.8 and 7.9)
Action Taken
The recommendation of the Committee has been brought to the notice of Heads of Constituent Units
in the Department for meticulous planning and reporting through D.O. letter dated 18.5.2017 for timely
implementation and monitoring.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and would like the Department to constantly monitor the
utilisation of funds.
Recommendation
The Committee also notes that the Department could not get allocations as per the ratio of outlay
approved for the XII Five Year Plan. If the total approved outlay of ` 107186.73 crore for the entire
XII Plan period be evenly distributed among the five Annual Plans, the average allocation comes to
approximately ` 21437.34 crore per Annual Plan. The Committee finds that for 2016-17, the last year
of the Plan period, the plan allocation at BE level was less by ` 5675.46 crore compared to average
allocation per year and if it is seen in the perspective of the RE allocation, this gap further widens to
` 11318.50 crore which is almost the half of the annual allocation during the XII Plan period. The
Committee is aghast to find that for past five years, allocations for Externally Approved Projects could
not be finalised by NITI Aayog/Ministry of Finance. The Committee taking a serious view in this matter
feels that by doing this, Ministry of Finance has made a mockery of approved outlay for the XII Plan
period. (Para 7.10)
Action Taken
The recommendation of the Committee has been brought to the notice of Ministry of Finance for
their kind consideration.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply.
Recommendation
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Ministry of Finance must desist from such practices
and allocate budgetary resources in the Annual Plans in proportion due to the Department or else the
very purpose of long term allocations gets defeated. (Para 7.11)
Action Taken
The recommendation of the Committee has been brought to the notice of Ministry of Finance for
their kind consideration.
Comments
The Committee takes note of the reply and would like the Department to pursue the matter
with Ministry of Finance for full disbursement of the allocations made.

THE GURUSHIKHAR OBSERV ATORY FOR ASTROPHYSICAL SCIENCES (GOALS)
Recommendation
The Committee feels that these issues must be taken up with the Ministries of Urban Development
and Environment, Forest and Climate Change by the Department to make sure that in the interest of
scientific observation, GOALS Observatory is allowed to perform its duties properly. (Para  15.11)
The Committee feels that such a valuable observatory dedicated to the cause of astrophysical
sciences and capable of making the country proud of its achievements, fully deserves all necessary
assistance from the Department. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department of Atomic
Energy must render all the help required by the Gurushikhar Observatory for Astrophysical Sciences
(GOALS) of Bhabha Atomic Research Centre. It also recommends that efforts should not only be made
to meet its requirements after MACE telescope is fully functional but attempts should also be made to
grant expeditious sanction for providing more facilities, so that, the Observatory could be able to excel
further in pursuit of its objectives. (Para 15.12)
Action Taken
Status of GOALS, Mount Abu
The TACTIC (TeV Atmospheric Cherenkov Telescope with Imaging Camera) is operational at
GOALS (Gurushikhar Observatory for Astrophysical Sciences), Mount. Abu (24.60 N, 72.70 E, 1300m
above sea level) for more than fifteen years. The telescope is based on the imaging atmospheric
Cherenkov technique and is used to record images of extremely short duration atmospheric Cherenkov

9
flashes during clear and moonless nights. TACTIC deploys a large light collector and a 349-pixel imaging
camera based on Photo Multiplier Tubes (PMTs).
During 2016-17, we have used TACTIC to observe various galactic and extra-galactic very high
energy (VHE) gamma-ray sources namely Crab Nebula, Markarian 421, Markarian 510, 1ES1215+305,
1ES2344+514 and NGC1275. A total of around 425 hours of data has been recorded during this period.
Comments
The Committee notes that the Department has not furnished any response to the observations/
recommendations in the above paras and would appreciate an affirmative commitment by the
Department in the Action Taken Note.
C-INDUSTRIES AND MINERALS SECTOR
Indian Rare Earths Ltd. (IREL)
Recommendation
Rare Earths are important resources for use in high-tech applications in various strategic sectors
such as defence, atomic energy, space, oil, green energy and electronics, etc. The Committee is of the
view that it is high time that strategies should be adopted to achieve self-reliance in critical strategic
resources like Rare Earths and in the light of its importance, develop a roadmap for exploration and

Action Taken
The beach sand mineral sector has already been opened for private participation from the year 1998
having one of the major objectives of setting up value added industry to meet the requirement of domestic
market.
Aligning further with such objectives, IREL has envisaged setting up of a rare earth based theme
park which would inter alia include demonstration of home-grown technologies from BARC in the field,
skill development centre and state-of-the art laboratory to analyze the raw materials and finished products.
Entrepreneurs will be invited to set up RE based products value chain industries by up scaling the pilot
plant technologies to commercial scale.

Recommendations
While noting the development of Non-Power Applications and socially useful technologies, the
Committee is concerned with the slow pace at which these non-power applications and socially useful
technologies developed by the Department are trickling down to the targeted groups in particular and
society in general. This is primarily because Scientific Departments have not made any proactive combined
effort to create a dedicated agency and portal for popularisation of socially useful technologies and
other technologies ready for commercialisation. However, DAE has made some good moves in this
direction with Board of Radiation and Isotope Technology. The Committee is aware the DAE has tied
up with a private company, CSIR tech for marketing of socially and industrially useful technology
developed by the Department. (Para 21.6)

their use on a large scale which requires propagation and popularisation of these technologies.
(Para 21.10)

strategy to aggressively market its non-power applications and socially beneficial technologies such as

Gamma radiation processing Plan, including its technology on food preservation, molecular medicine
and healthcare, through Board for Technology Transfer and Collaboration Division and other possible
mechanisms so that more and more people get benefitted. AKRUTI Programme of the Department should
also spread its activities outside Maharashtra. (Para 21.11)

BHARTIYA NABHIKIYA VIDYUT NIGAM LTD. (BHA VINI)
Recommendation
The Committee feels that BHAVINI has an important role to play in bringing energy security to the
country. The Committee is of the view that if India has to successfully realise the three stage Nuclear
Energy Programme and thereby tap huge thorium reserves of the country, Department has to make all
out efforts for expediting the commissioning and ramp up the capacity of Fast Breeder Reactor.
(Para 24.11)
Action Taken
The recommendation of the Honourable Committee is noted.
The Committee observes that there has been inordinate delay in commissioning of Prototype Fast
Breeder Reactor at Kalpakkam. As per the original schedule, the project was to be commissioned in
September, 2010 which was later rescheduled to September, 2014. The goalpost was again shifted to
September, 2016 and later on to October, 2017. The Committee is disappointed to note that despite a
Steering Committee headed by the Secretary himself conducting regular meetings for monitoring the
activities of PFBR, such meetings have not yielded any concrete results as the completion schedule has
been delayed considerably due to various constraints resulting in delay in achieving criticality. While
appreciating the complexities involved in the project and extreme caution exercised by the Department,
the Committee hopes that the Department will expedite the remaining work so that the reactor is
commissioned by October, 2017 without any further delay. (Para 24.12)
Action Taken
The recommendation of the honourable Committee is noted. Presently, all construction activities and
total equipment erection and installation have been completed in Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR).
A number of mandatory pre-requisites tests viz. Integrated Leak Rate Test of Reactor Containment
Building, commissioning of handling flasks for demonstrating the maintainability of Primary Sodium
Pumps, Pressure hold test of Reactor Assembly and Main Vessel and integrated pre-heading of sodium
lines have been completed.
Preparatory jobs are in progress to test the Reactivity control mechanism and Fuel handling equipments
in high temperature environment in Reactor Assembly.

16
Presently, purification of secondary sodium is completed and filling of Secondary sodium main circuit
is in progress. This will be followed by sodium filling in main vessel and loading of fuel sub-assemblies
towards approach to first criticality of the reactor by October 2017.
Comments
The Committee notes the reply and recommends that there should be no let up in the
efforts of the Department to ensure timely completion of the first-of-its kind Fast Breeder
Reactor.
OUTREACH  PROGRAMME
Recommendation
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department and its Public Sector Units such as
Uranium Corporation of India Limited, and Indian Rare Earths Limited should prepare a comprehensive
outreach programme with a calendar of events using social media platforms, radio, television, etc. meant
for students and general public and most importantly for all the elected representatives right from district
level i.e. Members of Zila Panchayats, Legislative Assemblies and Councils and Members of Parliament.
(Para 25.2)
The Committee is aware that some steps in this direction have already been taken by the Department.
Setting up of Hall of Nuclear Energy is one such small step in this direction. The Committee is also aware
of public awareness campaigns done by NPCIL in and around its nuclear reactors. However, it still
believes that a lot more needs to be done for management of unfounded fears among the people and
negative perception against possible adverse fallouts of nuclear energy. (Para 25.3)
The Committee strongly feels that elected representatives can play an important role in making their
constituents aware of non-power applications and spin-off technologies developed by the Department and
its Public Sector Units which have the potential to serve the society in numerous ways and ultimately
create a positive image about nuclear energy in the mind of Indian public at large. (Para 25.4)
Action Taken
The need for Public Awareness on Atomic Energy has been realized since inception of the Department
of Atomic Energy (DAE) and since then it has a dedicated wing for public awareness, the Public
Awareness Division (erstwhile Publicity Division).
2. DAE has been continuously promoting awareness about the peaceful uses of atomic energy
(as in power production, applications of radiation technology in the areas of healthcare, food
and agriculture, industry, water, environment etc.) with prime focus on benefits to the society
and also keeping the public abreast with current trends and developments in the field.
3. In view of the expanding programmes of DAE, and technological advent and digital India, an
urgent need was felt to enhance the scope and quantum of public awareness activities further
by setting up a project called Societal Outreach Program Cell (SOPC) in the Department of
Atomic Energy. SOPC is mandated to spread awareness on the benefits flowing out to the
society from the programmes of the Department by using Social Media Tools.

17
4. DAE has organized and participated in 60 exhibitions in different regions of the country; 36
seminars/workshops for students, academia, general/rural public, members of the media and
NGOs have been conducted; made over 06 video films and multimedia presentation. Recently
DAE has organized a work shop for the benefit of Journalists at BARC from various newspapers/
Electronic Media and also a guided tour to the Tarapur Atomic Power Station.
5. DAE organizes the All India Essay Contest in Nuclear Science and Technology (started during
the Birth Centenary year of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru) every year. It brought out several
publications on peaceful uses of atomic energy during the last 3 years. Most of the publications
have been done in various local Languages wherever relevant.
6. Various activities to improve and augment visibility of such programmes among general public
are being carried out by the Societal Outreach Programme Cell (SOPC) of DAE. These include
outreach via different media like television, print media, social and digital media, conduct of
workshops, etc. DAE chalked out various Public Outreach programmes for the benefit of
stake holders across the country.
7. As a part of DAE's public outreach activity, a studio interaction / interview of Dr. Sekhar Basu,
Secretary, DAE on Doordarshan has been produced, which has been broadcasted on Doordarshan
National channel and Lok Sabha TV . This studio interaction touches upon different domains
of nuclear energy including the Indian energy scenario, societal and spin-off technologies of
DAE, human resource development and future plans etc. and it also includes a question-
answer session of Secretary, DAE with mass media students.
8. Further, two page advertorials of DAE have been released in "Shubh Yatra" - the in-flight
magazine of Air India and in "Rail Bandhu" - the on-board magazine of Indian Railways, which
are impactful print media options aimed at passengers of Air India and the Indian Railways.
The campaign was received positively among a wide cross-section of passengers including
Government officials, dignitaries like Hon'ble Members of Parliament, State Legislative Assemblies
and other political figures and members of civil society.
9. In addition, DAE's advertisement campaigns were carried out on Lok Sabha Television (LSTV)
during the Parliament Sessions over a period of three months during January 2017 to March
2017, which are quite popular among the parliamentarians and has a large viewership across
the country, especially during the Parliament Sessions. In order to create a positive image
about nuclear energy in the minds of elected representatives, it is further planned to have site
visits for them.
10. Enhanced use of social media is an important activity of the Societal Outreach Programme Cell
(SOPC) of DAE. As a part of this, DAE had launched its official Facebook page at
www.facebook.com/dae.connect. Recently, DAE has also augmented its presence on social
media by launching its Twitter handle @DAEIndia.
