4. The Department of Ocean Development was established ia 1981 as a part of Cabinet 
Secretariat. It became a separate Department in 1982. It was assigned the role of a Nodal 
Department for organizing, coomittating and promoting Ocean Development activities in tJ;le 
oountry. The cbject of the Department was to formulate and implement programmes in 
Ocean Science and Technology with long term scientific, technological and economic 
benefiti. The Headquarteri of the Department are at New Delhi with attached operating 
offices based at Goa and Cochin. In all, the Department has three attached offices and one 
autonomous organization. 
S. The major programmes of this Department, which are being implemented at present 
comprise: Antarctic Research Programme; Polymetallic Nodules Programme (PMN); Explo­
ratioE of living and non-living Resources; Coastal Zone and Island Programme (MARSIS, 
COMAFS ·etc.); Natipnal Institute of Ocean Technology; Marine Research and Manpower 
Development; Internatio.1al Law including Treaty and Programmes relating to international 
co-opernti<Jn. The Programmes of the Department are based on Ocean Policy Statement of 
the Government besides the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). The 
Programmes are directed to explore and exploit vast potential of marine resources, hitherto 
hidden undet the mysterious ocean nnd sons, for the socio-economic benefit of the society 
and to protect and preserve \he marine environment.

Institutes/Laboratories, the cost of which is met by Department of Ocean Development as

10. Explaining the importance of the Antarctic Research Programme, it was stated that 
the programme had scientific and geopolitical significance on the one hand and had to carry 
out international treaty obligations on the other. Asked about the need for an allocation of 
Rs. 23 crores. (Rs. 20 crores approved- Annexure-H), it was stated that it had been at the 
centre stage of Department's activities since 1981- the year of iLs first expedition. India is 
one of the 46 members of Antarctica Treaty System (ATS) assuming the status of one of the 
26 consultative members of ATS and adheres by UN Convention of the Law of the Sea. The 
country ranks as a frontrunner in the world in this programme having led as many as 15 
expeditions and has a permanent station 'MAITRI' in Antarctica. The Department needed 
the budgetary support of Rs. 23 crores. However, a sum of Rs. 20 crores has been allocated 

which includes funds for an efpedition in the year end to Antarctica and finalising of phase-I 
of Antarctic Study Centre (ASC), Goa. The ASC comprising logistics, administrative and 
residential blocks would be completed during the current year. The phase-II for setting up of 
scientific laboratories and other allied support system would be taken up in succeeding years. 
Asked about the reappropriation of the amount of Rs. 17.5 crores in 1993-94 to Rs. 16 
crores and about the utilisation of Rs. 45 lacs allocated for the Centre in 1995-96, the 
Secretary stated that the allocations could not be utilised due to impediments in 
constructions activities caused by perennial garbage dumping at the site by Goa Municipal 
Corporation. The Committee was informed that for the first time the Department launched 
a special cruise to explore and assess krill fishery resources in Antarctic waters. The 
expedition was joined by three experts from Poland besides representatives of Indian Fishery 
Institutes. The commercial exploitation for krill is also being explored. The Scientists 
displayed the samples-species of krill to the Committee. 
11. Asked about the reasons for non-acquisition of the multi-purpose research vessel 
which can be used as oceanographic research vessel and can also function as ice-breaker so 
as to be used in Antarctic expeditions as well it was stated that the major part of allocation 
made under the Antarctic Research Programme in 1994-95 alone, an amount of Rs. 7 crores 
against the allocations of Rs. 16 crores was meant for the rentals/chartering of ice-breaker 
ship. In_ 1996-97, a sum of Rs. 10.5 crores out of Rs. 20 crores is proposed to be spent on 
chartering alone. In 1993 the research group, set up for this purpose, submitted its Report 
stating that such a vessel would cost Rs. 100 crores and take three-four years to acquire. The 
report also maintained that the Department could explore acquisition of a second hand ship 
for ready availability if it was economically viable. Accordingly, an enquiry was floated 
which attracted responses from South Africa, Germany, Ukraine, Finland, Norway and 
Denmark , The offers did not confirm to Department's specifications. In the meantime the 
Department entered into a 3 year (1995-98) contract for chartering of vessel with a Norway 
based company eventually reducing the charter cost which was coming high on an annual 
basis. The delegation sent to Russia to study a second hand vessel could not identify one 
which would suit the Department's requirements.

managing and maintaining . Mere provision of sets would have served no purpose if proper 
training was not imparted to fishermen . Fisheries Departments of each state periodically 
monitor the Status and operation of these facilities. Now in Phase-11 a total of 12 shore 
stations would be operationalised in 1996-97 with distribution of 130 sets of walkie-talkie in 
each state. The demonstrations t@ fishermen would be taken care of in the current year.

18. On the question of future prospects of Prawn Culture Project in Andaman and 
Nicobar Islands , the Secretary explained that the Islands had approx. 1000 ha. of bracki sh 
water area potential to be brought under prawn cultu(e. The production of a low 300 kg/hal 
crop was assigned to the reason of acidic nature of the brackish water ·soil in Andaman 
Islands . The department, thereafter, sanctioned a project to Marine Products Exports 
Development Authority (MPEDA} in 1990 for R & D for prawn culture in the Islands . The 

team identified 15 ha. of land at Dollygunj which was acquired from private owners for a 
consideration of Rs. 32 lakhs. The yield was 2 tonlha/crop. Subsequently it varied from 
0.02 tonlha/crop to 1.82 tonlha/crop. Imported feed with a good Food Conversion Ratio 
(FCR) was used but bad soil could not enhance the production. The produce was sold in the 
Island at a lesser rate than what prevailed at mainland due to poor transportation facilities . 
At present training to fishermen is being imparted on prawn culture. Low production target 
has been fixed at 1 tonlhalcrop till 1997. Replying further to a question by Members, it was 
stated that this project has also trained fishermen in Tamil Nadu. About 2000 entrepreneurs 
have been identified. 
19. Proposals for a new project Marine Acquaria were initiated as early as 1994-95 in 
the B.E . of that year. Asked as to why against an allocation of Rs . 10 lakhs each in the B.E. 
of 1994-95 and 1995-96, a sum of Rs. 1 lakh and 5 lakhs respectively was actually utilised it 
was stated that the Department was promoting the establishment of Oceanaria on a Build, 
Own, Operate , Maintain (BOOM) basis. The Department had floated a global enquiry to 
this effect and there was no response due tO non-attractiveness of the site proposed in Goa . 
Thereafter, the Government of Goa decided to allocate 32,000 sq. mtr. land on Miramar 
Beach , Panjim , Goa. It is proposed to spread awareness on marine environment in Indian 
habitats with its diverse flora and fauna. Moreover, it would display species diversely found 
in AndaJllans , Gulf of Mannar etc . which were unique in character. Four foreign and one 
Indian company took interest in the project. The Indian firm responded and would do so in 
collaboration with an Australian Company. The firm has also been conveyed about awarding 
of the contract to it by the Government of Goa. The project would be a tourist attraction 
too. The Department hopes to finalise the proposal in 1996-97 for setting up of an 
Oceanarium . 
20. On the question of a new scheme 'Drugs from the Sea', the Secretary submitted 
that the allocation in B.E. for 1996-97 provide a sum of Rs. 2 crores . Though it was an 
ongoing programme on development of drugs which was till now being funded under 
Research and Development Programme, it would be separated from 1996-97 onwards . The 
programme, was scheduled for three stages. Stage-1 was implemented with a total outlay of 
Rs. 1. 72 crores; Phase - II was initiated in 1993-94 involving a total expenditure of Rs. 4. 78 
crores and would be implemented over a period of three years . Over 450 different species of 
potential flora and fauna were .collected from indian coastline and over 1200 samples were 
subjected to biological screening for some major activities. So far 17 organisations have been 
identified which possess the requisite experience in the field. Five organisms identified and 
evaluated in Phase-II would be given top priority for det ailed evaluation leading to 
development of new drugs in anti- diabetic, anti-viral, anti-amoebic, anti-anxiety and lavicidal 
fields. The Phase-III of the programme will be initiated during the year 1996-97. During this 
phase refinements of techniques to isolate bio-active substances from marine organisms 
would be continued.

21. The Department of Ocean Development l)as sought budgetary support for a sum of 
Rs. 66.83 crores in the budget estimates for 1996-97. The Plan component in the budgetary 
support is for Rs. 52 crores and the non-Plan component it Rs. 14.83 crores. The total 
budgetary support is at the level of budgetary estimates for 1995-96. The budget support for 
1995-96 which has considerably gone down at the stage of revised estimates had stood at 
Rs. 57.79 crores of which the Plan component totalled Rs. 44 crores and non-Plan 
component was Rs. 13.79 crores. In the process, the programmes which have suffered 

monetarily are the National Ocean Information System where the allocation has gone down 
from Rs. 80 lakhs in 1995-96 to Rs. 50 lakhs in the current year, International Co-operation 
where the budgetary support has gone down from Rs. 50 lakhs in the revised estimates for 
1995-96 to Rs. 40 lakhs in the current year. Likewise, the Coastal Research Vessel would 
receive only Rs. 3.10 crore against Rs. 4.5 crore in the revised estimates of 1995-96. Here, 
the current year's provjsion is exactly half of what was sought in the budget estimates of 
1995-96. The Department of Ocean Development continues to lay emphasis on some 
programmes like Antarctic Research Programme, Polymetallic Nodules Programme, 
Oceanographic Research Vessel, Fishery and Oceanographic Research Vessel (FORV) and 
Marine Satellite Research· Information and Coastal Ocean Monitoring and Prediction System 
(COMAfS). 
22. It has been stated by the Department that it had projected its demand originally for 
Rs. 63.80 crores but the Planning Commission agreed for a sum of Rs. 57.75 crores. 
Subsequently, however, the Department was informed that the outlay finally approved by 
the _Planning Commission stood at Rs. 52 crores. With the reduction in the outlay, the 
Department has been compelled to shift some of its activities to the next financial year. The 
Department has proposed to manage with the existing allocation by rescheduling some other 
activities. 
23. The Committee feels that the Department should make out a strong case for 
enhancement in its allocation in the revised estimates for the current year. 
24. As regards the Antarctica Research Programme, the Department has originally 
sought Rs. 23 crores . The Planning Commission had first agreed to provide Rs. 20.20 crores 
but it finally approved an outlay of Rs. 20 crores. India's foray in the Antarctica commenced 
with the launching of the first expedition in 1981. Thereafter a number of multi-disciplinary 
and multi-institutional scientific expenditions have beeri sent to the Antarctica regularly on 
annual basis. The first research station in the Antarctica, "Dakshin Gangotri" was 
commissioned in 1983 and was functional until 1987. In 1988 the settting of Indian permanent 
station "Maitri", with modern infrastructural facilities for conducting temporary scientific 
research was established in the Schirmachar Oasis. Dakshin Gangotri Station is being 
maintained as a supply base. Sdentists from diverse fields drawn from national laboratories, 
institutions and universities are P.rovided with adequate opportunities to carry out experi­
ments in all major branches of polar science. The ·logistic support is extended by various 
wings of the Defence Services and Laboratories of the Defence Research and Development 
Organisation. The programme has thus become one of international Prestige and is therefore 
necessary that the programme is allowed to continue with adequate budgetary support. 
25. The Committee therefore recommends that the Department should get what it had 
originally projected i.e. a sum of Rs. 23 crores. Besides, the Department should endeavour for 
commercial exploitation of the Krill ftshery and other such resources in the Antarctic waters 
because of its demand in the overseas markets. 
26. The Polymetallic Nodules (PMN) Programme is another national programme with 
international commitments. It consists of three components "Survey and exploration of 
polymetallic nodules in the Central Indian Ocean Basin (CIOB)", "Development of 
Technologies for mining of nodules " and "extraction of metals from sea bed". The 
programme is reviewed and guided by the PMN Board of Management. Considering the 
potential of the benefits that could accrue for future industrial programmes, the Committee 
feels that in view of the need for India to be among the developed nations in the high 
technology in the Ocean sector and from the long-term strategy and geo-political considera-

tions, it is essential to continue technology development efforts for exploration and exploitation 
of the polymetallic nodules so that we do not lag behind other pioneer investors and enable 
' the country to begin commercial exploitation when economic feasibility is established. Here 
also the Committee is of the view that the Department's original demand of Rs. 11 crores 
should be met in full whereas the Planning Commission has sanctioned only a sum of Rs. 9 
crores. 
27. The Committee had recommended in its 11th and again in 19th Report on Demands 
for Grants (1994-95 and 1995-96) that the Department should make vigorous efforts to acquire 
a multi-purpose vessel/ice-breaker ship expeditiously to obviate the possibility of escalation in 
its cost in case the matter is delayed any further. Such a project would call for increased 
financial allocations for the Department during this plan period. The Committee was apprised 
that an Expert Group constituted for the purpose recommended in September 1993 that the 
new ship may cost Rs. 100 crores keeping in view our requirements in respect of the design of 
construction and it may take 3 to 4 years to time to get the same. Therefore, the ready 
availability of the suitable second-hand ship may be explored for the purpose. The Committee 
has now been given to understand that the offers made by South Africa, Germany, Ukraine, 
Finland, Norway and Denmark in response to the global enquiry did not conform to our 
detailed specifications, the Department has evaluated in the meantime, the option of 
chartering a vessel for a period of 3 years instead of annual charter which could result in 
reduction of charter cost. Accordingly, Department has entered ;-into a contract with M/s. 
Reiber Shipping Company, Norway for a three year charter. They have stated that an official 
delegation which visited Russia for a on-the-spot market survey in January, 1995 could not 
identify a suitable second-hand vessel meeting our requirements. 
28. The Committee feels that the Department should work out the economics for all the 
alternatives to acquire including the purchase of a new vessel and plan its strategy 
accordingly. The Committee should be apprised of economic of all the proposals. 
29. The marine instrumentation has received an allocation of Rs. 2 crores· in the budget 
estimates for 1996-97 against the budgetary support of Rs. 1 crore last year. The allocation 
covers shore to fishing vessel communication which is a reliable means of passing messages. 
SOS signals and data with the help of a fixed shore station and a hand held VHF radio 
telephone on board the fishing craft. The system would help the traditional and small-scale 
fishermen who are venturing for fishing for a range of about 40 kms. from the shore and had 
no means to communication from the fishing craft and vice-a-versa. With the advancement in 
the communication technology it has become possible to provide such facilities to the 
fishermen for ensuring their safety at sea. 
30. The Committee therefore recommends that expeditious steps be taken to select a 
suitable model of walkie-talkie set and the other technology to cover maximum number of 
fishermen within a short span of time across the country. 
31. The Committee is of the view that the extraction of drugs from the ~ea has immense 
potential. By now more than 100 pure compounds have been isolated and characterised 
some of which posses interesting biological activities while others even though biologically 
not promising possess interesting structures. The Department has taken up standardisation 
and introduction of new bioassay methods for violating anti-viral activity both IN VITRO 
and IN VIVO, casting hepatoprotective agents, anti-diabetic agents, anti-filarial agents, anti­
fungal agents using multi-fungal infection models. 
32. The Committee recommends that the Department should put in sustained effort in 
tapping this potential by involving universities/institutes. The Committee is happy to note that 

the number of active/modern organisms have been screened. The Committee hopes that in the 
near future it will be possible to commercially exploit the area for development of drugs from 
medical substances obtained from sea.
