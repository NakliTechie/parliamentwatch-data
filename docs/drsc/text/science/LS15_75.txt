R E P O R T
           
      Information Technology (IT) has tremendous potential to become an engine of growth and productivity improvement for
 all sectors of the economy and for the country.  With the continued thrust on liberalization, in order to make IT products
 cost effective and  price competitive, the  use and penetration of computers in the society is fast expanding with special
 emphasis on multi-lingual technologies.
2.   Ministry of Information Technology was set up on 15 th October, 1999 to be the nodal institutional mechanism for
 facilitating all the initiative in the Central Government, the State Government, Academia, the Indian private sector and
 successful Indian IT professionals abroad.  With a view to accelerate the Internet revolution in India, emphasizing the
 creation of useful contents in Indian languages, IT-enabled services, IT education, electronics and computer hardware
 manufacturing and exports, silicon facility, e-Commerce and Internet based enterprises, the Ministry has been entrusted the
 task of implementation of a comprehensive action plan to make India an IT Superpower in the early part of the next century
 and achieve a target of $ 50 billion in software exports by 2008.
      The Ministry has been entrusted with the following responsibilities:-
(i)            Policy matters relating to IT;
(ii)        Promotion of knowledge based enterprises, Internet, e-commerce, IT education and IT based education;
(iii)            Development of Electronics;
(iv)            Development of Microelectronics and Silicon facility;
(v)            Promotion of Information Technology and Standardization;
(vi)            National Informatics Centre (NIC); and
(vii)            Electronics and Computer Software Export Promotion Council (ESC)
In order to make India an IT Super Power by the Year 2008, the vision translates into a mission-‘Move works to India’. 
 The Vision objective are:
(i)            Creation of Wealth
(ii)            Employment Generation
(iii)            IT led Economic Growth
The role of the Ministry of IT will be of a
(i)            Pro-active facilitator
(ii)            Pro-active motivator
(iii)            Pro-active promoter
(iv)            Spread of IT to masses speedily and

NATIONAL INFORMATICS CENTRE (NIC)
5.   National Informatics Centre (NIC) which was set up in 1977, is a premier S&T organization of the Government of India
 in the field of Informatics Services and Information Technology (IT) applications in India. NIC was a constituent unit under
 the erstwhile Department of Electronics and then brought under the Planning Commission in 1988. It is now a part of the
 Ministry of Information Technology.  Its main objective is to provide computer support to Central Government
 Departments, State Government Departments, District Administration and new districts being created by the State
 Governments.  NIC has set up a satellite based computer communication network for providing E-mail, INTERNET, File
 Transfer access to  National & International databases etc.  NIC has set up a Local Area Networks(LAN) in Central
 Government Departments and State Government Secretariats.  NIC has been training the Government employees on a
 regular basis to enable them to use MIS databases.  Training is provided upto district level.  It is the only Government
 Organization in India, at present, offering network services over C-band and Ku-band VSATs, Wireless Metropolitan Area
 Networks and Local Area Networks (LANs) with NICNET gateway for Internet resources, so as to promote  social,
 economic, scientific and technological activities, and also for “macro-economic adjustment programme” of the
 Government.
5.1Since its inception, NIC has been propagating “IT-led Development” facilitating rapid economic growth and social
 transformation in India, by setting up a countrywide satellite based VSAT network (NICNET), first of its kind among the
 developing countries, linking about 540 District Administrations, 25 State Secretariats, and 7 NCT/UT Administrations. 
 NICNET links extend to Andaman Islands, Lakshadweep & Minicoy Island , Ladhakh Region, Indian High  Commission
 (Mauritius), and B.P. Koirala Institute of Medical Sciences in Kathmandu (Nepal).  NIC provides the state-of-the-art IT
 solutions to information management, information dissemination, and decision support requirements of the Central as well

5.3In some of the offices there is a large accumulation of hardware such as computer terminals, keyboards and related
 material such as transformers etc. which contained software, which is not in use now because of the upgradation of
 technology.  This material is now lying as junk for so many years with no action for its removal in sight.   Unless urgent
 steps are taken for the disposal of the hardware, which appears to have been rendered obsolete, it may pose an
 environmental hazard, more so because of the rapid advances being made in this field.  When asked about removal of this
 material lying in the Government offices, the Committee was informed that the NIC has set up a Obsolete Declaration
 Committee to identify obsolete equipments and outdated software and recommend their disposal.  It has also set up an
 Auction Committee to expedite the auction of the items recommended as obsolete by the Obsolete Declaration Committee.
5.4The Committee notes the reply of the Ministry that the provision of Rs.142.17 crores for the NIC for the year 2000-01 is
 sought to be utilized mainly towards its on-going activities.  If it be so, the Committee fails to understand as to how NIC
 will catch up with rapid advances being made in this field as a result of technical innovations.  The Committee therefore
 observes that the Ministry should review its priorities and, if necessary, come out with additional financial projections at
 R.E. stage.  The Committee feels that financial constraints should not be allowed to stay in the way of IT culture being
 spread in the country or in the matter of gaining supremacy in the field.

 TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT COUNCIL PROGRAMME
6.   The Technology Development Council (TDC) programme  seeks to support the new initiatives responding to changing
 developmental needs of the electronics sector keeping in line with the liberalized industry policy framework.  The
 programme aims to promote and support research, design, development and engineering in the areas of computer and
 computer communications, control and instrumentation, consumer electronics telecommunications and broadcasting.
6.1When asked about the disproportionate variations in respect of TDC programme, the committee was informed that the
 original provision during 1999-2000 for TDC programme was Rs.8.00 crores (6.80 crores as grants-in-aid and Rs.1.20
 crores as loan), which was reduced to Rs.4.00 crores in R.E. (Rs.3.30 crores as grants-in-aid and Rs.0.70 crores as loan). 
 The reduction in provision has been in view of new project proposals from some of the implementing organisations having
 not materialized and delay in release of funds from some of the ongoing projects owing to funds already available with
 some of the institutes against the earlier releases.  The provision for 2000-01 has been proposed at Rs.5.00 crores (Rs.3.00
 crores as grants-in-aid and Rs.2.00 crores as loan)  which would be utilized mainly towards the ongoing projects and  new
 projects.
 
6.2The Committee observes that it is necessary to ensure perfect co-ordination among the    project implementing agencies 
 supported under TDC programme.  This could be reflected only when allocation made during the period under review is
 utilized fully.

 Under the programme, generic technologies applicable to all industry in general as well as industry specific technologies for
 example meant for paper, textile and steel industries are developed and implemented.  System engineering projects are also
 taken up to enhance productivity, conserve energy and improve quality.

 Committee was apprised that the B.E. for 2000-2001 has again been increased to Rs.3 crores as seven ongoing projects
 would be completed in the current year and six other projects would be nearing completion by 2000-2001.   New project
 initiatives have been taken in the area of Coal Mining and Food Industry.

NATIONAL INFORMATION INFRASTRUCTURE(NII)
8.   Information Infrastructure is vital for the country and has a tremendous potential for bringing rapid transformation in our
 work culture and thus  the development of the country.   Therefore,  the Ministry  has initiated a focused attention on
 National Information Infrastructure (NII) with the aim of promoting use and proliferation of  Information Technology. 
 Thus, the objective of  the programme is to demonstrate the use of contemporary tools in Information Technology,
 enhancing overall efficiency in work, reduce delays in decision making, promote optimal utilization of manpower resources
 by improving access to information and Information Technology tools and  demonstrate the concept in action to other
 users.  Some of the sectors proposed to be covered under the programme include, literacy, education and life-long learning
 leading to increase in employment opportunities, better health care, environment friendliness, opportunities for rural
 populace to acquire new skills etc.  A number of initiatives have also been taken in the area of multi-media promotion,
 electronic governance and ATM test bed.
8.1The allocation for NII during 2000-2001 has been kept at Rs.11.00 crores as against Rs.10.00 crores in B.E. and Rs.6.00
 crores in the R.E. of the year 1999-2000.  The Committee was informed that the enhanced allocation for the NII programme
 during 2000-2001 is because of the requirement of the network security and also towards new test-beds/applications
 projects and promotional measures  towards e-commerce implementation, online learning amongst others.  Three new 
 projects are also in progress for development of e-commerce technology, tourism information system and creation of
 information infrastructure for Sikkim Government.  Certain new projects in the field of telephony overy internet, networks
 security, e-commerce applications and web enabled database management are being initiated. 
8.2The Committee notes that an allocation of Rs.10 crores was made for NII at B. E. stage of the year 1999-2000 which was
 reduced to Rs.6 crores at the R.E. of that year and it has again been increased to Rs.11 crores in 2000-01.  In view of  the
 variations in the allocation, it appears that either the Ministry is not following realistic approach towards the programme or
 there has been   utilization of funds due to  other reasons.  The Committee therefore reiterates its earlier observation made in
 its 67
th Report and would like to emphasize that having once drawn up the programmes, the Ministry should aim at full
 utilization of the budgetary provisions.    
8.3  The Committee observes that logging into the Internet in Delhi through telephone numbers announced by NIC and in
 the North-eastern region is next to impossible in view of poor telecom infrastructure.  A decision was taken by VSNL to set
 up an International gateway at Guwahati and it was to be commissioned in 1999.  Till date, the element of uncertainly hangs
 about the project and there appears no physical progress.  The Committee therefore recommends that the International
 gateway should be commissioned within a definite time frame.   Efforts should also be made to improve accessibility to the
 internet through telephone network as an individual subscriber may not have the advantage of LAN.
PUBLIC SECTOR ENTERPRISES

 Complex Ltd. (SCL) and Electronics Trade and Technology Development Corporation Ltd. (ET&T) under the Ministry of
 Information Technology.

 networking facilities management and hardware support.  In the first half-year of 1999-2000, CMC has made the best ever
 performance with a revenue of Rs.183 crores with a growth rate of 23%.  In 1999-2000 CMC’s MoU rating with
 Government of India has been “Very Good”.  CMC stocks are also performing well and recently ICRA, the leading credit
 rating agency has awarded “A1+ “ rating to CMC for its commercial programmes.  Besides, the Government has also given
 clearance to CMC for the enhancement of its equity.  This would provide CMC the required funds to meet its further
 expansion plans.

 keeping in view the larger   national considerations of self reliance. SCL’s broad programmes, inter-alia, are to design,
 develop and manufacture new VLSLs to meet the requirements of Telecom sector, Watch & clock industry and other
 industrial electronic market; and to cater the requirements of strategic sector viz. Space and Defence by development of
 Applications Specific Integrated Circuits.  The Company has also just completed the development of 0.8 micron CMOS

SOFTWARE/HARDWARE EXPORT PROMOTION
11.The programme aims at the creation of basic facilities for development and export of software to data links and promote

 Rs.17,000 crores during 1999-2000 from Rs.10,940 crores during 1998-99, registering a growth rate of over 55%. Domestic
 Software industry is likely to go up to Rs.7,300 crores during 1999-2000 from Rs.4,900 crores  during  1998-99.  The 
 schemes  broadly  covered by  the  programme are : -  a)  Software Infrastructure (STPs); b) Quality Assurance,
 Certification and Information Security Management; c) Development support for software (R&D); and Export Promotion
 Campaigns and Markets Intelligence studies.

 export in the country.
