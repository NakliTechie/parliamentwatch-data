REPORT
Nuclear technology is perhaps one of the most contentious and controversial technologies
in the world which, notwithstanding the fact that this is highly useful and beneficial for the people,
has not got the credit it deserves. It is notorious for its potential to cause devastating and
catastrophic consequences, lesser known as source of clean and green energy and very little
known for its useful applications in the field of health, agriculture, industry and basic research.
2. Evolution of atomic energy programme in India can be traced back to 1948 when Atomic
Energy Commission was set up for formulation of policies and direction of the programmes in the
country. To implement its policies and programmes, the Department of Atomic Energy came into
being on 3
rd August, 1954 with Dr. Bhabha, the Father of Nuclear Programme in India, as its
Secretary. The atomic energy programme in our country which was initiated in a modest manner with
the objective of peaceful uses of atomic energy to meet our energy requirements, has now grown
manifold and has become multi-dimensional and multi-disciplinary in nature. Besides power
production, development of non-power applications of nuclear technology for use in agriculture,
healthcare, industry, water, food preservation, radiation processing of material, care for environment,
isotope hydrology, etc. for societal benefits is being undertaken in an emphatic manner. The first
commercial nuclear power programme began in 1969 with the operation of Tarapur Atomic Power
Station and currently Atomic Energy caters to about 3 per cent of the country’s total energy demand.
The Department has, over the years, evolved a broad-based organisation consisting of 6 Research &
Development Centres, 5 Public Sector Undertakings, 3 Industrial Organisations, 3 Service
Organisations and 9 Aided Institutions. Besides, through Board of Research in Nuclear Sciences
(BRNS) and National Board of Higher Mathematics, Department of Atomic Energy promotes research
in nuclear and allied fields and mathematics respectively. There are two deemed Universities under the
ambit of the Department of Atomic Energy, namely, Homi Bhabha National Institute and Tata Institute
of Fundamental Research. Academic Programmes are run by 4 Research & Development Units, viz.
Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC), Mumbai; Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research
(IGCAR), Kalpakkam; Raja Ramanna Centre for Advance Technology (RRCAT), Indore; Variable
Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC), Kolkata and 6 Grants-in-Aid Institutions viz. Saha Institute of
Nuclear Physics (SINP), Kolkata; Institute of Physics (IOP), Bhubaneswar; Institute for Plasma
Research (IPR), Gandhi Nagar; Institute of Mathematical Science (IIMSC), Chennai; Harishchandra
Research Institute (HRI), Allahabad and Tata Memorial Centre (TMC), Mumbai under the umbrella of
the Homi Bhabha National Institute. Academic programmes of National Institute of Science Education
and Research (NISER), an Autonomous Body under the Department of Atomic Energy are also
conducted under the aegis of Homi Bhabha National Institute. Academic Programmes include Ph.D,
Integrated Ph.D., M.Tech., M.Phil., M.Sc. (Engineering) and Post Graduate Diploma in all science
and engineering disciplines, etc.

4.8 When the Committee enquired from the Department the reasons for allocated funds
remaining unutilized during the Financial Year 2010-11 and 2011-12, the factors enumerated by the
Department were mostly of administrative nature. For example, explaining the reasons for less
expenditure in 2010-11, the Department stated that the funds remained unutilized in Research &
Development Sector, mainly due to delay in procurement of machinery and equipments for the
projects of BARC, IGCAR, RRCAT, VECC, etc.; delay in award of contract for new campus of
BARC; late receipt of clearance of Ministry of Environment and Forests for construction activities,
etc. Under Industry Sector, the reasons for less expenditure varied from delay in projects of
Nuclear Recycle Group; delay in construction work of housing project for Heavy Water Plant,
Manuguru; delay in sanction of Fast Breeder Reactor Fuel Cycle Facility Project, etc. Under Mineral
Sector, underutilization of allocated funds was stated to be mainly due to delay in sanction of
Uranium Corporation of India Ltd. Projects. Similarly, under Power Sector, the main reasons for
underutilization was the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor Project which encountered technological
difficulties during manufacture and assembly together with delay in finalization of orders due to
non-availability of suppliers. Reasons for underutilization under various sectors of the Department
during the year 2011-12 are no different from the reasons given for the underutilization of funds
for the year 2010-11. The Department stated that under Research & Development Sector funds
remained unutilized due to delay in infrastructure projects of TIFR, BARC and project of
International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor (ITER) and Global Centre for Nuclear Energy
Partnership. Under Industry Sector, the reasons were delay in receipt of sanction for Fast Breeder
Reactor Fuel Cycle Facility etc., while, under Mineral Sector, delay in sanction of UCIL Projects,
inter-alia, was the main reason for non-utilisation of allocation. Slow progress of Prototype Fast
Breeder Reactor project, mainly contributed to the underutilization of allocated resources under
Power Sector.
4.9 On an analysis of the above reasons, the Committee finds that the grounds given by the
Department for underutilization of allocated funds are mainly of administrative nature and feels that
these could have been overcome by the Department by acting in an efficient, effective and
professional manner. It is also constrained to observe that the reasons which led to underutilization
of funds during the year 2010-11 have been allowed to persist even in the year 2011-12 and it is
only the Department which is to be held responsible for not addressing those issues in right
earnest. The Committee does not seem inclined to be convinced by the reasons given by the
Department and feels that the resources which are allocated to the Department are very scarce and

7
a scientific Department like Atomic Energy must make full use of these resources. Blocking or not
utilizing the scarce resource some how affects overall development of the country as these
resources could very well have been earmarked for the other welfare schemes/projects for the
people of the country. The Committee, therefore, recommends that Financial Year 2012-13,
being the first Financial Year of the XII Five Year Plan, the Department must ensure that
the allocation made to the Department is fully utilized not only during the current year but
also throughout the entire Plan period.
4.10 The Committee was, however, informed that reduced allocation of Non-Plan expenditure
in Demand No. 4 to the extent of Rs. 984.67 Crores, if not restored would affect the production
of Heavy Water and Nuclear fuel besides affecting O&M of various R&D units of the
Department. Similarly, the shortfall of Rs. 377.94 Crores in Demand No. 5, if not restored will
affect the import of nuclear fuel. The Committee takes note of adverse consequences arising
from drastic cut in the Non-Plan allocation under Demand No. 4 & 5 and recommends that Non-
Plan allocation be suitably enhanced at R.E. stage to avoid adverse impact arising from reduction
of Non-Plan allocation.
4.11 The Committee feels that one of the main reasons behind underutilization of allocated
resources is lack of effective and strict monitoring mechanism to review the progress of its on
going schemes/programmes from time to time. The Committee had, in its 207
th Report on Demands
for Grants 2010-11 of the Department of Atomic Energy expressed its serious concern over the
underutilization of funds under different sectors despite an elaborate and comprehensive monitoring
mechanism adopted by the Department and had recommended that the Department needed to
further strengthen the mechanism for monitoring of ongoing schemes for their effective
implementation and elimination of any scope of surrender of funds at a later date due to non-
utilisation/underutilization. The Department had in its Action Taken Note claimed that the monitoring
mechanisms developed by the Department are being effectively used to eliminate the scope of
shortage or surrender of funds at later stage. It had further informed that the recommendations of
the Committee had been brought to the notice of all Head of Constituent Units/Autonomous Bodies
and Public Sector Undertakings of the Department and also to all Members concerned with the
monitoring mechanisms with a request that all necessary efforts were taken at their end to further
strengthen the monitoring system to achieve full utilisation of Plan Funds. However, the Committee
finds that the trend of utilisation in all the 4 Sectors for the year 2011-12, shows that the
phenomena of underutilization still continues as is evident from Chart No. III.
4.12 When the Committee enquired about the monitoring mechanism put in place by the
Department to monitor and review the progress of its ongoing schemes/programmes and the steps
taken to further strengthen the monitoring mechanisms, it was informed that every scheme was
being examined at the level of Constituent Units, in Internal Working Group of Respective Sectors,
Department of Atomic Energy and finally at the level of Atomic Energy Commission, whenever
necessary before it was sanctioned. It was further informed that multifaceted activities of the
Department had recently been grouped into the following 4 major groups, apart from those in
strategic areas:
(a) Nuclear power and associated fuel cycle;
(b) Basic research and advance technologies development with special reference to
nuclear science & technology;
(c) Obligation of radiation and Radio isotope including spin off technologies for societal
benefits; and
(d) HRD and international collaborations.

8
4.13 The Committee was further informed that Apex Committees had been constituted for each
of these 4 major areas to effect enhanced synergy and coherence in each of these activities, broad
planning and periodical monitoring. While, 3 out of the 4 Apex Committees were headed by
Secretary of the Department, the 4
th one on HRD and international collaborations was headed by
Principal Advisor of the Department.
4.14 The Committee observes that these Apex Committees were constituted in December, 2010
and despite their existence there had been no significant progress in the improved utilisation of
allocated funds, particularly, under Plan Heads during the Financial Years 2011-12. The Committee,
therefore, feels that the existing monitoring system is not able to ensure full utilisation of funds
and, therefore, recommends that the monitoring mechanism needs to be so strengthened and
revamped as to preclude any possibility of underutilization/ non-utilisation of allocated funds on
almost similar grounds year after year.
4.15 Key to the budgetary documents 2012-13 provides that Result Framework Document
system is being implemented in various Ministries/Departments in a phased manner since 2009-10.
Performance management determines the performance index based upon the agreed objectives,
policies, programmes and projects/schemes. When the Committee enquired from the Department as
to whether the Result Framework Document system is being implemented in the Department, it
evaded a direct reply and informed the Committee that the performance of various Constituent
Units is periodically evaluated by a Committee comprising of senior scientists. For the last 3 years,
the Committee has been chaired by Prof. P. Rama Rao, Former Secretary, Department of Science
& Technology with Prof. S.K. Jain, Former Secretary Department of Science & Technology and
Prof. Sukhatme, Former Chairman, Atomic Energy Regulatory Board as Members which evaluates
the performance of Research & Development Institutions. The performance of Industrial Units is
evaluated by a separate Committee, headed by Dr. V .K. Iya, Former Director, Isotope Group,
BARC. The other Members of this Committee are Shri V .P. Raja, Chairman, Maharashtra Electricity
Regulatory Authority; Shri S. Sharma, Former Chief Executive, Heavy Water Board and Shri T.K.
Mukherji, Former CMD, Indian Rare Earths Ltd. In addition to these, ongoing projects are
evaluated by separate Committees consisting of Chairman Atomic Energy Commission and Head of
Units in the Department. A monthly report on the performance of all the Units is submitted to Prime
Minister.
4.16 The Committee finds that silence on the issue of preparation Result Framework Document
shows reluctance on the part of the Department to prepare the same. The Committee fails to
understand the reasons for its reluctance when other scientific Departments like the Department of
Scientific and Industrial Research and Department of Biotechnology have been preparing the Result
Framework Document since its inception in 2009-10. The Committee, therefore, recommends that
the Department should also strive to prepare the Result Framework Document to objectively assess
its performance index.

4.19 RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT CENTRES
4.20 Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC)
4.20.1 Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, which is almost synonymous with the atomic energy
programme of our country is an internationally renowned multidisciplinary and multi-scale activity
research & development organisation engaged in comprehensive research & development
programmes aimed at harnessing nuclear energy and its utility for the benefit of the society. Some
of the prominent activities of BARC include entire spectrum of fuel – cycle activities, developing
futuristic nuclear reactors, fabricating nuclear fuels, reprocessing of spent fuel, waste management,
robotics, super computers, lasers, accelerators, human genomes, nanotechnology, super-
conductivity, etc. BARC is also actively involved in developing applications of radiation technologies
for the purpose of food preservation, radiation mutation breeding of varieties of pulses, groundnuts
and other seeds, radio isotopes for healthcare, trouble shooting in industries, etc. While, its main
campus is located at Mumbai, it has other campuses at Tarapur, Kalpakkam and Mysore and a new
campus is being set up at Visakhapatnam.
4.20.2 BARC was allocated an amount of Rs. 2554.00 crores both Plan and Non-Plan at B.E. level
for the year 2011-12. However, it could utilize Rs. 2408.00 crores at the R.E. level, thus, showing
an underutilization of Rs. 146.00 crores. When the Committee enquired about the reasons for
underutilization of allocated outlay, the Department of Atomic Energy came out with the plea that
the provision of Rs. 80 crores for composite contract for development of facilities at Atchutapuram
Mandal – Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh could not be utilized as it had to be retendered, Micro
Nano Engineering Initiative, could not be taken up, construction of New Engineering Halls at
Visakhapatnam Campus of BARC remained pending for want of approval of Department of Atomic
Energy, provision for Establishment of Infrastructural Facility and Production Capacities at Rare
Materials Plant (RMP), Mysore was reduced by Rs. 40.00 crores keeping in view the status of
ongoing composite contracts and the provision made for setting up of Uranium Oxide facility at
Kalpakkam was reduced by Rs. 30 crores due to slow progress of the work. The Committee finds
that all these grounds cited by the Department are of routine administrative nature which could
have been avoided by the Department as delay in completion of infrastructure projects leads to time
and cost overrun and affects research & development work. The Committee feels that it is a
matter of concern that as against the B.E. allocation of Rs. 1190.00 crores under Plan Head,
BARC could utilize only Rs. 1015.00 crores, thus, leaving a gap of Rs. 175.00 crores. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that these sort of loopholes which lead to
underutilization of funds should be plugged in future.
4.20.3 The allocation for BARC for the year 2012-13 has been increased by Rs. 68.70 crores in
comparison to the B.E. allocation for the year 2011-12. While, the increase as against the R.E.
allocation for the year 2011-12 amounts to Rs. 214.70 crores. The Committee was informed
that the increase in provision for the year 2012-13 is mainly for the development of
facilities at BARC, Visakhapatnam and for setting up of integrated nuclear projects at
Tarapur. The Committee is happy to note that allocation for BARC, a prestigious research
& development institution, has been increased notwithstanding its poor performance in
terms of financial achievements. It however, recommends all out efforts be made to fully
utilize the outlay and that no excuse whatsoever should be allowed to come in its way.
4.20.4 The Committee is happy to note that BARC is actively pursuing research in nuclear
agriculture by using genetic mutation of crop plants using ionizing radiation aimed at
improved yields, resistant to disease which has resulted in production of several new crop
varieties. Over 38 new crop varieties produced by BARC have thus far been released for
use by farmers. The mutant of black gram produced by BARC accounts for more than 90

11

thus far, in this direction are not enough to meet the challenges posed before us. The
Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should intensify its efforts in
developing more and more high yielding disease resistant crop varieties not only of pulses,

4.20.5 The Committee is also of the view that there is need for popularizing new high

4.21.2 An increase of Rs. 431.30 crores has been made for IGCAR as against the Budget

4.22 Raja Rama nna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT), Indore

4.24 MINERAL SECTOR
4.25 Uranium Corporation of India Ltd. (UCIL)
4.25.1 UCIL is a Pubic Sector Undertaking of the Department of Atomic Energy whose specific
mandate is mining and processing of Uranium ore to produce Uranium concentrate for nuclear
power generation. To attain the above objective UCIL is engaged in design & construction of
underground mines, ore processing and other related activities.
4.25.2 Supply and availability of Uranium forms a critical component of expansion of nuclear
power programme of our country. Non-availability of fuel owing to shortage of Uranium continues
to be a major constraint in enhancing nuclear energy production. Worse, operation of PHWRs
fuelled with domestic Uranium is not able to attain full capacity factor. As per the Annual Report
– 2011-12 of the Department, the reactors operating with domestic fuel supply registered capacity
factor of about 67 per cent till December 31, 2011. However, the Committee is surprised to note
that out of B.E. allocation of Rs. 154.00 crores for the year 2011-12, UCIL under Major Head 4861
and Rs. 487.00 crores under Major Head 12861 was drastically reduced at R.E. stage to only
Rs. 25.00 and Rs. 274.00 crores respectively apparently due to non-utilisation or underutilization
of funds by UCIL. When the Committee enquired about the reasons for gross utilisation of funds
by UCIL, it was informed that it was due to delay in sanctioning of the Tummalapalle Expansion
Project and Uranium Project at Gogi. The Committee understands that sanctioning of the project
is an internal matter of the Department, which could have been obtained in time as such a vital
project on which expansion of nuclear power programme so heavily depends cannot be made to
suffer due to procedural reasons. The Committee expresses its serious displeasure over such
a lackadaisical approach of the Department.
4.25.3 The allocation for Uranium Corporation India Ltd. for this year under Major Head 4861 has
been increased from Rs. 154.00 crores B.E. last year to Rs. 216.00 crores this year, but under
Major Head 12861, it has been slightly reduced from Rs. 487.00 crores to Rs. 467.00 crores. The
Department has stated that the provision has been made in anticipation of sanction of the

14
Tummalapalle Expansion Project and Uranium Project at Gogi and also the new projects envisaged
under Twelfth Plan. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the budgetary allocation made
this year must be fully utilized and the Department should not come out with procedural excuses
next year. The Committee also recommends that the new potential heavy mineral zones identified
by Atomic Minerals Directorate in new areas must also be exploited so as to extract as much
Uranium ore as possible and for this purpose, the Department should prepare a time bound Action
Plan.
4.25.4 The Committee also expresses its serious concern over the fact that the Lambapur Project,
Nalgunda District, Andhra Pradesh and Kylleng Pyndengsotiong Mawthabah (KPM), West Khalsi
Hills District, Meghalaya, which are being pursued from Tenth Five Year Plan onwards have not
yet been set up owing to resistance by the local people. The Committee understands that large
amount of money has already been spent on these Projects. The Committee fails to understand as
to why for so long the Department has not been able to amicably sort out the problems with the
local people. The Committee feels that the concern of the people about health hazard posed by
Uranium mining needs to be allayed with extensive public awareness and outreach programmes
instead of standing firm on the notion that Uranium mining does not pose any health hazards and
a number of health surveys conducted confirm this fact. The fact that Uranium mining does not
cause health hazard needs to be appropriately conveyed to the people so as to convince them and
win their confidence. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should
pay due attention on the above observations of the Committee.
4.26 Atomic Minerals Directorate, for exploration and Research (AMD)
4.26.1 AMD, a research & development centre of the Department carry out survey, prospecting

“Augmentation of Airborne and Ground Geophysical Capabilities”. It was further stated that during
Twelfth Plan, an outlay of Rs. 112.00 crores only is envisaged under this Project as against

4.26.2 The Committee, although satisfied with the explanation given by the Department for the

4.30.8 While the Committee appreciates the NPCIL motto of ‘Safety first and production next’ and

has come when the Department must enhance its nuclear energy capacity in a big way to help the
country sustain its economic growth. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the
Department must not leave any stone unturned to ensure that nuclear power generation
capacity is given a big boost as the country cannot afford any laxity on this front any more.

projects for which allocation has been earmarked must be completed in the same financial
year, so that, the allocated outlay are fully utilized in that year itself. The Committee also
expresses its serious concern over such sort of delay for a matter and crucial project like
fast reactor fuel cycle facility which is badly required for the second stage nuclear power
programme and recommends that such procedural delays should not be allowed to come in

13. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Centre should not only try to utilize
the allocated resources this year but also accomplish the task of development of software
for hearing impaired and medical cyclotron project within the stipulated targeted period.
(Para 4.23.3)
14. The Committee, therefore, recommends that accomplishment of third stage nuclear
power programme using thorium as fuel for power generation on commercial basis, research
on Compact High Temperature Reactor (CHTR) and on Hydrogen energy needs to be
intensified and results achieved in a time bound manner. (Para 4.23.4)
15. The Committee expresses its serious displeasure over such a lackadaisical approach
of the Department. (Para 4.25.2)
16. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the budgetary allocation made this year
must be fully utilized and the Department should not come out with procedural excuses next
year. The Committee also recommends that the new potential heavy mineral zones
identified by Atomic Minerals Directorate in new areas must also be exploited so as to
extract as much Uranium ore as possible and for this purpose, the Department should
prepare a time bound Action Plan. (Para 4.25.3)
17. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should pay due
attention on the above observations of the Committee. (Para 4.25.4)
18. The Committee, therefore, recommends that AMD should make efforts to cover
those areas of the country to identify prospective mineral resources which have been left
untouched so far. (Para 4.26.2)
19. The Committee, therefore, recommends that TMC should, in a time bound manner,
try to establish its Centres in other parts of the country with high cancer case rates.
Simultaneously, it should also makes sincere efforts to develop tertiary level cancer care
facilities in each State/Region, so that, patients need not travel from remote and far flung
areas all the way to Mumbai. The Committee also recommends that adequate financial
allocation should be provided to the Centre, so that, it could not only undertake research
in the field of cancer, but also expand its activities and reach in different parts of the
country. The Committee expresses its displeasure over the reduction of allocation for the
Centre in RE. 2012-13 which is Rs. 248.15 crores as against the RE. Rs. 289.78 crores last
year and recommends that adequate budgetary support needs to be provided to the Centre

so that it is able to cater to the needs of the cancer patients, especially the poor through

20. The Committee expresses its concern over underutilization of funds by these
prominent institutions as it would adversely affect our preparations/advancement in the
field of nuclear science. (Para 4.28.3)
21. The Committee is hopeful that the Department would give due consideration to the
recommendations of the Committee and take all necessary measures to obviate the
possibilities of underutilization of funds under various schemes. (Para 4.28.4)
22. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department should make all out
efforts to make the prototype fast breeder reactor operational by targeted date of
completion, i.e ., September, 2014 and start its commercial operation by 2014-15 as
scheduled. (Para 4.30.5)
23. The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department must not leave any
stone unturned to ensure that nuclear power generation capacity is given a big boost as the
country cannot afford any laxity on this front any more. (Para 4.30.8)

11. The Chairman after welcoming Secretary, Department of Atomic Energy and his team of
officials to the meeting of the Committee informed that the Committee proposed to discuss the
functioning and Demand for Grants of the Department for the year 2012.
12. Expressing happiness over the growth of nuclear power generation by 39 per cent in the
year 2011 as compared to the previous year Chairman mentioned that with several reactors under
construction share of nuclear power in total power generation would rise, significantly in years to
come.
13. Thereafter, Chairman desired to know the future roadmap drawn by the Department to
augment share of nuclear power to rid the country of chronic power shortage. Secretary was also
asked to apprise the Committee of the results of stress test undertaken in the wake of Fukushima
incident and the steps taken to ensure full safety of nuclear power reactors. He was also asked
to appraise the Committee of the new applications of atomic energy in the fields of agriculture,
food technology and healthcare, thrust areas of the Department for the XII Five Year Plan and
Financial Year 2012-13, budgetary allocations made for the year 2012-13, targets set for the year
and whether the allocations are sufficient to carry out the programmes/schemes and if not, detailed
justification for the increased allocation. Thereafter, Chairman asked the Secretary to start his
presentation.
14. The Secretary, first of all, thanked the Committee for the opportunity given to the
Department to present the work done by the Department. Thereafter, he made a power point
presentation. Intervening during the power point presentation, the Committee enquired about the
achievements, aims and objectives of Tata Memorial Centre (TMC). Director, TMC informed the
Committee that the primary aim of TMC was to give cost effective treatment to cancer patients.
He informed that while the cost of the medicine that came from the West for treatment of cancer,
TMC has formulated medicines and interventions which could give the same kind of benefits at the

25
cost of Rs. 100 only. Secondly, the Centre provided radiation therapy at a cost which was 50 per
cent of the cost of any other treatment available in the market. TMC had made yet another
achievement by documentation of the cancers which were contributing to death in our country,
according to which 40 per cent of death was tobacco related, 25 per cent infection related and
another 25 per cent was related to obesity.
15. Responding to the issues raised by Chairman, the Secretary, on the safety aspect of nuclear
power reactors, emphasised that the Department gave maximum emphasis on safety in terms of
new design, in terms of running a plant and continuously monitoring the parameters to see that
a nuclear power reactor was always in safe operation. He further added that now there would be
an international review of the safety issues. India would become a member of the Operation
Science and Advisory Team (OSAT) of International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) and this agency
would submit their report  to the Government and to the regulator and not to the operator. This
report would also be made public. Several reviews initiated in the wake of Fukushima disaster
unequivocally prove that all of the system in our nuclear power reactors are in perfect order. AERB
has also constituted a separate Committee to assess the safety features of different types of
reactors. AERB had suggested some new safety measures which are being implemented. Secretary
further added that Fukushima disaster occurred as the decay heat could not be taken out of the
reactor. For instance, if it was a 100  mw reactor, after complete shutdown, it would be producing
about 1 mw and hence the heat thus developed had to be taken out. To take the heat out normally,
we had power supplies of 4 different categories, i.e., from category 1 to 4, but if all the power
supplies failed taking out heat would be done by certain passive means, which we had in
corporated in all our reactors. The second thing was that stress test undertaken to check the
capacity of our reactors to withstand natural calamities like earthquake etc., showed that for all
our reactors the margin for each equipment and each building was 3 to 4.5 times more. Briefly
to say that whatever intensity of earthquake was known to the area in which the reactor was
located even if 3 times more than that level occurred our nuclear reactor was safe.
16. Asked about the uranium reserves in the country, the Secretary informed that the total
reserve of uranium that we had could not support more than 15000  MW capacity and, therefore,
we had to import uranium from outside. He further added that the import of uranium would not
be perennial and after next 30 to 40 years, our Plutonium and Thorium reserves would be adequate.
17. Replying to the question of reduction in budgetary allocations as compared to the last year,
the Secretary informed that they required lesser allocation for many of the new Projects in the first
year of the XII Plan period. The Committee further wanted to know in what manner water
purification system developed in Bhabha Atomic Research Centre was different from the systems
developed by other scientific Departments like DSIR and Earth Sciences and whether the
Department had any coordination with them. Secretary replied that Department of Atomic Energy
had been doing water purification through multi stage flash evaporation process while in the DSIR,
it was mostly by the membrane process. Department of Atomic Energy too had a membrane
process and it had been doing membrane development by itself.
