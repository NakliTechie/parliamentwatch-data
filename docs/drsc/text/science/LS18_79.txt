79th Report on the Information Technology Bill, 1999
 
 The Chairman, Rajya Sabha in pursuance of the rules relating to the Department-related Parliamentary Standing
 Committees, referred* the Information Technology Bill, 1999**, as introduced in the Lok Sabha on 16 th December,
 1999 and pending therein, to the Standing Committee on Science & Technology, Environment & Forests for
 examination and Report.
2. In the present day world it has been the electronics and now the Information Technology (IT) which influence our life
 the most. There is hardly any area of human endeavour where electronic gadgets have not reached. There is barely any
 industry which does not depend, for its efficiency and productivity, on the application of electronics in its different
 manifestations. Technology has emerged as the vehicle of rapid change in the work culture and the way mankind
 communicates in the century. India has been no exception to this phenomenon. Whether in the field of
 telecommunications, roads, ports or air travel, the hallmark has been more efficient and cost effective technology. Thus
 from slow-moving majestic automobiles the country has moved to smart and fast technologies which have given us
 much more advanced vehicles, coal guzzling steam locomotives which have been replaced by electric traction engines.
3. Information Technology is a product of information age and technology. It is a vehicle for future development,
 opportunities, challenges and competition that enables faster collection and dissemination of information. In the history
 of civilization, no work of science has had so comprehensive an impact on human development as IT. Undoubtedly, IT
 has been the greatest changing agent of this century and promises to play its role even more dramatically in the times to
 come. IT is changing every aspect of human life, breaking old barriers and building new inter connections in the
 emerging global village and becoming the chief determinant of progress of nations and that of individuals. IT is being
 meaningfully employed in railways and airlines, banking, insurance, weather forecasting, radio astronomy, molecular
 biology, aerodynamics, etc. thereby reducing the fatigue and increasing the productivity and efficiency. And in
 education too, IT has a vital role. A large variety of software is available to cater to self-learning and evaluation on
 various subjects.
4. IT is a broad term covering the entire gamut of managing and processing information. Computer hardware, software
 and internet are the tools for the systems that have been designed, developed, supported or managed by IT
 professionals. The discovery of computers and subsequent development of the technology popularly known as
 information technology is one of the most significant achievements of the 20
th century. The role of IT as an instrument
 for progress and development has been acknowledged widely and is expected to bring in major social and economic
 benefits for the mankind and accelerate the process of development.

5. Andhra Pradesh is an example of how the States in the country can incorporate IT in the day-to-day functioning of the
 Government and re-engineer the manner in which services are provided to citizens. Under its ‘One-stop non-stop’
 approach, a pilot project called Twin cities Integrated Network Service (TWINS) integrated 18 services of six
 departments over one counter. It provides fast and easy access to Government services, makes the access to
 Government much simpler and can also cut down on corruption. It would be naive, of course, to suggest IT as the
 ultimate panacea for corruption, which may find ingenious ways to beat new technology.
6. Today, among the major Asian industrialized countries, India has abundant technically skilled manpower towards
 global IT development. IT is the fastest emerging industrial segment in the country. During the last decade, IT sector
 has registered an unprecedented growth. All the renowned global brands have either established production facilities in
 the country or are present in the market through technical/financial collaborations, thus giving consumer a wider choice
 of products in terms of features, quality and prices.
7. A special Task Force on Information Technology and Software Development involving several agencies at Central
 and State levels has been constituted with a view to keep pace with the latest technology developments abroad. To that
 end Government have taken two major initiatives, one, the formation of IT Ministry at the Central level and second the
 establishment of a Rs.100 crores National Venture Fund for software and IT industry. The IT industry is thus expected
 to play a lead role and serve as a nodal point to facilitate further development of IT industry.
8. Member countries of the United Nations have recognised the need for "Uniformity of laws applicable to alternatives
 to paper-based methods of communications and storage of information". A model law on e-commerce was adopted in
 1996 by the U.N. Commission on International Trade Law (UNCITAL) and the U.N. General Assembly recommended
 to its members in 1997 to give due consideration to this Model Law while enacting or revising their respective Laws.
 The IT Bill, 1999 is a sequel to this U.N. resolution. Malaysia has introduced new comprehensive legislation whereas
 countries like USA, European Union, Singapore, Korea, Japan, UK and Germany have brought out multiple
 amendments to the in existing laws as well as enacted new legislation. Italy, Japan, South Korea, Sweden and UK have
 also introduced Digital Signature laws recently.
9. In India, an Inter-Ministerial Standing Committee under the Chairmanship of the Secretary to the then Department of
 Electronics was set up on 15
th September, 1997 to take stock of draft legislation and formulate a draft Bill on electronic
 data transfer and transactions having regard to international practices. The interim report of the Standing Committee
 recommended amendments in the existing core laws such as the Indian Evidence Act, 1872; the General Clauses Act.,
 1897 and the Indian Penal Code and that there should be a single comprehensive cyber law covering digital signature,
 data protection and computer offences. Hence, the Information Technology(IT) Bill.
10. The IT Bill provides a peripheral framework for e-commerce regime, which comprises of 93 sections divided into
 13 chapters. It includes four schedules which lay down the relative amendments sought to be made to the Indian Penal
 Code, the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, the Banker’s Evidence Act, 1891, and the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The
 13 chapters respectively deal with preliminary matters, digital signature, electronic governance, attribution,
 acknowledgement and dispatch of electronic records, secure electronic records and secure digital signatures, regulation
 of certifying authorities, digital signature certificates, obligations of subscribers, penalties and adjudication, the cyber
 regulations appellate tribunal, offences, network service providers not to be liable in certain cases and miscellaneous
 provisions.
11. The Committee observes that the Bill provides: -
i. to facilitate electronic communication by means of reliable electronic records;
ii. to facilitate electronic inter-action in trade and commerce, eliminate barriers to electronic commerce resulting
 from uncertainties over writing and signature
requirements, and to promote the development of the legal and business
infrastructure necessary to implement secure electronic commerce;

 drafting of the Bill.
14. In the meetings of the Committee, Chairman, Telecom Commission made a presentation on the subject matter of the
 Bill and expressed their concern on the issue of "Convergence" of the Telecommunication, Broadcasting and
 Information Technology, as intended to be covered under the Bill. According to him, the convergence of technologies
 will result in the dilution of the boundaries between different sectors and it may be difficult to distinguish the computer
 networks’ as defined in the Bill from the telecom network. Therefore, the issues and concerns relating to security and
 privacy of information in the Bill would be more or less similar to the concerns in the area of telecommunications and
 distribution of encrypted TV or Radio programmes. The Secretary, Ministry of Information & Broadcasting also
 echoed the same apprehension. The Chairman, Telecom Commission also suggested for inclusion of word "voice" in
 the definition of "information" as given in clause 2(u) of the Bill to avoid any overlapping with the provisions of the
 Indian Telegraph Act; and to fix a time limit for retention of electronic records in clause 7 of the Bill. In response to the
 latter, Member Secretary, Law Commission of India submitted that the provision of Public Records Act will mutatis
 mutandis apply to this Act. Moreover, the electronic records automatically start vanishing after 5-6 years.
15. The Representatives of the Indian Music Industry and the National Association of Software and Service Companies
 (NASSCOM) also appeared before the Committee on the subject matter of the Bill. Both the representatives expressed
 the need for deletion of clause 78 of the Bill as the same appears to be in conflict with the provisions of the Copyright
 Act. Moreover, in view of the defence of due diligence and knowledge being available under clause 78, representatives
 were of the view that it was difficult to book the violators as clause 78 merely required a Network Service Provider
 with knowledge of an offence to prove that it exercised due diligence to prevent the commission of the offence. The
 Indian Music Industry submitted that the tests of lack of knowledge or of due diligence as stipulated in clause 78 of the
 IT Bill were not precise and objective and a Network Service Provider (NSP) or intermediary could rely upon clause 78

 deletion. Furthermore, the representative emphasized on the need to train Police Officials in handling cyber crimes as
 the concept of cyber law is of recent origin.
16. The Committee in its meeting held on 4
th May, 2000 had the benefit of the presentation made by the Commissioner
 of Police(CP), Delhi. The CP stated, inter alia, that in order to prevent cyber crimes, all Internet Service Providers
 should allow internet access through the digital telephone only which would enable to keep records of caller
 identification in the respective server. He also expressed concern on the mushrooming of Cyber Cafes from where a
 criminal mind could easily cause damage in economic terms. In order to provide solution, he suggested insertion of a
 provision regarding identification of particulars of the user through ration card, electoral card, passport, driving license,
 PAN Card etc. An obligation should be imposed on the ISP to maintain a record the users and provide details of every
 cyber activity. The Commissioner of Police informed the Committee that though at present not much police force is
 equipped with cyber knowledge yet initiatives have already been made and various training courses were going on to
 educate the police officials. The Commissioner, of Police, however disagreed with clause 77 which deputes an officer
 of the rank of Deputy Superintendent Police or above to investigate the offences under the Bill. According to him what
 was required is the knowledge of computer, not the rank and even an Inspector or Sub-Inspector could be competent to
 investigate the offences if he possessed the knowledge of the computer. The Commissioner of Police submitted that the
 officers of the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police were already over burdened and by assigning the job of
 investigation to them, little time would be left for him to attend to other work. After hearing the Commissioner of
 Police, the Committee suggested for recruitment of persons possessing the knowledge and experience in computer and
 also create special task force to tackle cyber crimes.
17. The Committee had the benefit of the views of the Member Secretary, National Commission to Review the Working
 of the Constitution and the representatives from the Department of Economic Affairs and the Reserve Bank of India
 Board on the subject matter of the Bill.
