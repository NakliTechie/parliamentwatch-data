5.   Asked about the increase in allocation to Topographical Surveys in the year 2001 –2002 to Rs.93.10 crores as against Rs.59.89
 crores in B.E. stage and Rs.54.45 crores during R.E. stage of 2000-2001 and the reason for no allocation being made to development
 project surveys, the Department informed the Committee that the marked difference in the B.E. 2000-2001 & B.E. 2001-2002 under
 Topographical Surveys and Surveys of Development Projects was on account of the merger of both minor heads under Topographical
 Surveys.
6.   In order to meet the national requirement, including that of Defence Forces the responsibility of Survey of India was expanded after
 1962 to provide and update topographical coverage on various scales in the entire country.  Accordingly, a large number of
 Topographical Units were raised to complete this task in the scheduled time, keeping in view peace and war and internal security
 duties.  Simultaneously, under the development programmes, the State Governments had projected their mapping requirements to
 Survey of India for major engineering projects such as Steel Plants, Power & Irrigation Projects, etc. In view of the demands of various
 States, Survey Parties were also formed on regional basis for undertaking Extra-Departmental jobs under the caption “Surveys of
 Development Projects”.  There was thus no functional requirement to operate both the heads separately.  The merging of provision of
 “Surveys of Development Projects” in B.E. 2001-2002 into “Topographical Surveys” has been proposed to ensure smooth functioning.

 ‘Foreign travel expenses’ was mainly on account of the increased bilateral and multilateral activities under international cooperation

SCIENCE & ENGINEERING COUNCIL (SERC)
8.   The Committee was informed that the SERC mechanism was under operation since the last twenty-five years. The scheme has been
 focusing on strengthening the research infrastructure in the country during the last decade. The new “Fund for the Improvement of
 Science & Technology Infrastructure”(FIST) programme, which has operationalised during the current year, would lay emphasis on
 evolving coordinated programmes in advanced areas through organization of brain storming sessions. The emphasis would be on
 supporting more Research projects in the university sector to tap the expertise available in the universities.  The areas include plant
 sciences, soft condensed matter physics, lasers & quantum optics, boundary layer modeling, crystal engineering, and Earth Sciences
 applications for societal needs, geophysical exploration, Mathematical Sciences etc.
9.   The allocation for Multi-disciplinary Research in Science and Technology (SERC) has been increased to Rs.87.10 crores for the
 year 2001-2002 from Rs.69.50 crore in B.E and R.E of the year 2000-2001. Asked about the reasons for increased allocation, the
 Secretary informed the Committee that the original budget provision in respect of SERC was Rs.75 crores, out of which Rs.7.5 crores
 had been separately shown under provision for North Eastern region and Sikkim. This has been restored to the SERC budget. He
 further stated that the B.E for 2001-2002 includes Rs.85 crores under plan and Rs.2.1 crores under non-plan. The increase therefore is
 only Rs.10 crores under the plan outlay.
10.  The Committee has been given to understand that SERC is one of the Major R&D programmes of the Department,
 Promoting multi-disciplinary research in Science and Engineering providing support to individual research scientists,
 establishing core groups and centers of excellence and setting up national R&D facilities. Further, the SERC has approved a
 large number of important programmes requiring additional funds. In view of the above and also to take care of the increased
 cost of sophisticated equipment, foreign exchange fluctuations, costly consumables and chemicals etc., the Committee
 recommends that the allocation needs to be suitably augmented.
TECHNOLOGY INFORMATION, FORECASTING AND ASSESSMENT COUNCIL (TIFAC)
11.  The Technology Information, Forecasting, and Assessment Council (TIFAC) is a society registered under the Department of
 Science and Technology. The main objectives of TIFAC include generation of Technology Forecasting/Technology
 Assessment/Techno Market survey reports providing technology related information, promotion of technology and evolving suitable
 mechanism for testing of technology, technology transfer and commercialisation as well as patent information.
      TIFAC has brought out over 250 well-researched, specialized reports – both in breath of technologies covered (Agriculture to
 Advanced Sensors) as well as depth (Technology Assessment and Status in India/Abroad, recent/potential markets, R&D opportunities
 etc.)     
      TIFAC reports have covered all the major sectors, which are of relevance to India.  The reports have also focused on specific
 products, processes & on their applications.
      In order to cater to the fast changing technological scenario in India and abroad, TIFAC reports also address the cutting edge and
 frontier technologies, technologies that may take 506 years to mature.
12.  The Department aims speed up implementation of the Technology Vision 2020 Mission projects. The systems approach technology
 aims to increase agricultural productivity. Besides, up gradation of Science and Engineering colleges is also proposed to be taken up.
 Mission REACH aims at creating centers of excellence in selected Departments at par with best available standards with a view to
 available attract industries to solve targeted problem in a time bound manner. The project aims at designing and developing prototypes
 for commercial production.
13.The Committee is of the view that in the wake of globalization and opening up of global markets, new technologies would
 flood Indian markets. Therefore, TIFAC must strengthen its forecasting/assessment of technologies so as to compete with the
 technologies coming from abroad.  Besides, the technologies proposed to be developed should be cost effective. Efforts should
 also be made to make the scientists aware of the need for taking patents. Knowledge about Procedures for applying a patent as
 also other formalities required being completed should be included, if not already available in the Department’s web page
 along with facility for downloading of necessary forms.  The web site should be updated periodically. The information about
 the patents applied must also be made available, if not already available, in the web sites.  Sometimes, the web sites are not
 easily accessible.  This needs to be looked into.

 Technology upgradation programme and (b) Vision technology (c) Gold Technology upgradation programme and (d) Laser

      Under Glass Technology upgradation programme, upgradation of technology, processes, product and applications of special glass
 (optical as well as ophthalmic glass) and consumer glass were initiated; efforts were also being made to develop technologies to make
 the present production process more cost effective and to enable utilization of glass scrap. The programme also seeks to facilitate
 creation of production facilities for ophthalmic glass to meet domestic and global demands; quality auditing and regulation through an
 appropriate law/act prevent production of substandard glass.
      In regard to consumer glass, efforts were focused on developing technologies aimed improving the technology and product quality
 so that the existing units become more productive and efficient .Eco friendly furnaces and improving glass melting pots were also
 developed.  
      Vision Technology programme aims at developing preventive, diagnostic and curative technologies and instrumentation for
 application amongst a large number of patients. It aims to effectively prevent /delay in onset of cataract and to retard the progression of
 cataract. The Gold Technology Programme aims to promote hallmarking activity.  Appropriate instrumentation was being developed
 for accurate assessment of gold content.  Development of abrasion resistant colourless weatherproof clear surface finishes had also
 been undertaken for value addition to the existing products.       
      In Laser application, Laser awareness programme was initiated for educating and making aware the children and population at large
 about the potential of lasers.  An advanced application of lasers in pattern recognition has been  identified  and  equipment for finger
 print  identification was being developed.  This technology would have many applications in Defence, Quality control in industries,
 Robotic vision and Artificial intelligence.
15.  The Committee is of the view that given the size of population and the use of ophthalmic glass, The country must aim to
 achieve self sufficiency in glass manufacture under vision Technology, the time schedule should strictly be adhered to and tests
 should be conducted before they are transferred to be used on patients, as the cases of failure of cataract operations have made
 many patients reluctant in adopting new techniques.  Laser technologies for curing cataract and other eye disorders must be
 strengthened and technologies developed should be available at affordable prices. 
SCIENCE  & TECHNOLOGY COMMUNICATION & POPULARIZATION PROGRAMME
16.  The thrust of the programme is to popularize Science & Technology, stimulation of Scientific and Technological temper among the
 people, take steps to provide support and coordination of Science & Technology popularization activities in the country.
17.  Under the programme Science & Technology for the visually challenged a variety of software and a science magazine for the blind
 in both Braille and audio cassettes and development of kits/teaching and learning aids were to be developed. Department aims to
 promote voluntary blood donation and a special software “Rudhirnet” was developed which would be made available to various blood
 banks. A programme on Environment and sustainable development would continue.
18.  The National Resource Data Management System (NRDMS), it is stated, would concentrate on building specialized problem
 oriented databases and operationalising them for real life planning and administrative decision-making.  It proposes to create new
 NRDMS databases centres for better coverage in states of Tamilnadu, Nagaland, UP and Assam, develop and disseminate spatial data
 management tools for integrated rural development, formulation of new R&D projects in areas of watershed management; land use
 planning, energy planning and infrastructure development. The Department also proposes to undertake studies to identify landslides
 prone areas in North Eastern states for detailed geological and geo-technical investigations.     
      While the Department proposes to continue the on-going programmes, it also proposes to extend contact programmes for school
 children to R&D labs and S&T organizations, visit to industries and S&T establishment and lecture demonstration programme in
 science would to extended to different parts of the country for stimulating creativity amongst children.
      Besides, programmes related to celebrations of National Technology Day would be extended to different states/UT of the country.

 agricultural practices and their improvement. A special programme on animal husbandry was proposed for smaller animals. It also
 proposed to sensitize and encourage scientists/NGOs to work with tribal communities.

 has been established between the observatory at Bhojbasa and R.D.C. SASE, Chandigarh.  The communication link was used for
 transmitting manually observed snow and meteorological data to R.D.C. SASE for archival of both manually observed and the
 automatic data.  Attempt is being made to make the data available to all users through internet.

14.The Committee feels that given the significance of Vigyan Prasar in popularizing Science & Technology the
 recommendations of the Finance Committee for the enhancement of allocation of funds to Rs.2.00 crores should not have been
 ignored. The Committee accordingly recommends allocation for Vigyan Prasar be increased to Rs. 2.00 crores.
      The Committee is of the view that, efforts should be made to strengthen publication activities and publications should be
 carried out in more & more Indian languages.  Besides, in view of the role played by HAM radio during the recent Gujarat
 earthquake as well as Orissa cyclone, it should be popularised among people so that they are encouraged to depot it as hobby. 
 Besides, equipment for HAM radio be made available at cheaper prices.
(Para 48)
15.In recommending the enhanced budgetary allocation for Vigyan Prasar the Committee has been guided by the imperative
 need to spread and inculcate scientific temper. The programme should be suitably modified on the basis of experience gained
 and the feedback received from the participants and others.  Shortage of funds should not be allowed to stand in the way of
 such programmes.
(Para 49)
16.The Committee notes the work being done by the S&T wings in Indian missions abroad.  The Committee would have
 appreciated some more details about the complement of staff, budgetary support provided to these S&T wings.  Likewise
 whether there are any plans to open more of such wings in the Missions located in some other countries.  The Committee will
 consider their functioning in greater details at a later stage.  The Committee have been receiving journal on S&T in Germany,
 published monthly from S&T wing in Berlin.  The Committee expects that such journals brought out by other missions be
 made available to it. 
(Para 51)
17.While considering the Demands for Grants of the Department of Science & Technology and assessing the progress of various
 important programmes and projects administered by it, the Committee found the discrepancy in the total amount of the Demands for
 Grants for Department of Science & Technology.  The Expenditure Budget 2001-02 (vol.2) shows that the total Demand for Grants as
 sought by DST is Rs.823.97 crores whereas the document containing detailed Demands for Grants gives a different version of the same
 i.e. Rs.829.62 crores.  When asked to comment thereon the Department clarified that this is due to receipts from various resources.
       The Committee therefore recommends that the Demands for Grants of Rs.829.62 crores, as sought by the Department of Science
 and Technology, for the year 2001-2002 under demand No.68, may be approved.
(Para 52)
