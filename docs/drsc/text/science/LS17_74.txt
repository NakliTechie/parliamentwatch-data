R E P O R T
 
 
      Biotechnology is a fascinating field of science.  Mankind has used biotechnology since ancient times for wine making
 and baking.  The modern science has uncovered the scientific truth behind these processes.  It has also multiplied the
 applications of biotechnology manifold in several areas of human welfare.      Biotechnology has emerged as the
 technology, which is set to shape the future by promising food security for the world, disease resistance and control,
 sustainable environmental options and thus a more healthier world scenario and ensure optimum utilisation of the scarce
 resources such as land.  The term “Biotechnology” was first described in 1920, although the origin of biotechnology could
 be traced beyond 6000 B.C. Biotechnology involves methods employed to living systems to produce/design or modify
 certain products for selective applications.  It has since made significant advances  and has come out with products that are
 of immense use in the most important areas of health, agriculture and industry. 
2.   The challenges in the modern biology and biotechnology in the next millenium are enormous in terms of research,
 product and process development and commercial exploitation thereof.  Adequate skilled human resources are also crucial. 
 The field of biotechnology in India has already opened opportunities for commercial exploitation in high value Biologicals,
 new generation vaccines, diagnostics in human and animal health care, value addition to agricultural crops through
 nutritional improvements, pests and disease resistance. Water and salinity stress resistance are the agenda for the coming
 years towards food and nutritional security.  Solutions for environmental protection through phytoremediation,
 bioremediation and microbial technologies may be available for treatment of degraded soils, enhanced oil recovery and
 pollution remediation and monitoring.  Therefore, Biotechnology provides a tremendous scope in the form of modern tools
 and techniques for improvement of human life through poverty alleviation, upgradation of skills and self-employment to
 large sections of society  in the rural areas to provide hem sense of social security.
3.   The Committee was informed that during the year 2000-2001 a consolidated effort would be made for application of
 biotechnology for the uplift through skill development of rural population especially women and weaker sections of the
 society and that, these  initiatives would be  pursued.  The efforts made in the first three years of 9
th plan have culminated
 into transfer of about 27 applications of biotechnology, filing of 41 Indian patents and granting of 7 Indian patents and 6
 U.S. patents.
4.   The Department   envisages consolidation of many activities during the third year of the current five year plan,
 operationalisation and speedy implementation of various programmes and supporting new initiatives.  The department is
 consciously moving towards product development in addition to basic research, as is evident from  technology transfers that
 have taken place in  the previous year.  A few  more diagnostics are expected to be taken up by the industry next year.

AGRICULTURAL BIOTECHNOLOGY
8.   The Committee was informed that drought and salinity are two major problems  which tend to limit plant growth and
 make soils unproductive.  A novel gene p5cs (phyroline-5-carboxylate synthase) has been reported to be a functional gene
 and over expression of this gene in transgenic tobacco results in an increased production of proline. Consequently, the
 transgenic plants produced were found to be tolerant to drought and salinity. A programme to develop transgenic indica rice
 (cv.Rasi) with p5cs gene was initiated two years ago at Madurai Kamaraj University (MKU), Madurai.
9.   A protocol for high frequency plant regeneration from embyogenic calli has been confirmed by PCR analysis.  The
 transgenic plants with desired traits would be generated in coming two years and  subjected to field evaluation.
10.The concept of organic farming is capable of  ensuring good soil health, food security and  reduce  environmental 
 pollution.  The  Committee  was  informed  that  the  department  had  started
 
national network programme on integrated nutrient management for sustainable maintenance of soil health through organic
 farming.  The efforts were being made to consolidate use of various biofertilisers and biopesticide technologies in the
 farmers’ field or specific cropping system.  This would ensure optimum use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides.  The
 technologies would help in maintenance of soil health as well as ensure environmental protection.  Various programmes on
 organic farming, specially using vermi compost have also been started in farmers’ field, demonstration to SC/ST, weaker
 sections, rural and women population.
11.The Committee feels that with the rising  costs of chemical fertilisers and the accompanying  risks of degradation of soil
 fertility because of increasing resource of the fertilisers, the role of biofertilisers and biopesticides is becoming significant. 
 The Committee recommends that the Department should encourage  the use of biofertilisers and biopesticides by educating
 the farmers though print & electronic media and field demonstrations, so as to achieve higher consumption levels of the
 bioproducts and with a view to increase their yields.

MEDICAL BIOTECHNOLOGY
15.In the area of medical biotechnology, the Department proposes to lay  emphasis on the development of diagnostics,
 vaccines for prophylactic and therapeutic use, drug delivery systems, production of important biological/biomolecules and
 creation of infrastructures in hitech biomedical areas in order to undertake further research.  During the year 1999-2000 the
 Department  has considered 42 new projects besides monitoring 55 ongoing projects and the National Jai Vigyan
 
Science and Technology Mission on development of New Generation technologies for Vaccines were initiated..
16.The department has been funding research for the development of indigenous diagnostics for major communicable and
 non-communicable diseases.  The basic purpose has been to develop easy to use, affordable and accurate test systems for
 Indian strains which could be converted into appropriate kits with the help of industries for large scale use.  The Committee
 was informed that after reaching  the level of prototype systems, the department is endeavouring  to upscale these
 technologies. The Committee was apprised of the achievements made in the area of major communicable and non-
communicable diseases which include HIV/AIDS, Japanese Encepalitis, Hepatitis, Tuberculosis, Cholera, Shipella, 
 Rotavirus,  Helicobacter pylori, Leishmaniasis Malaria etc
17.The Committee recommends that besides supporting and developing indigenous diagnostics for major communicable and
 non-communicable diseases, the department should simultaneously endeavour to find market for indigenous products, and
 strive for better qualities to ensure priority for indigenous products over the imported ones.
HUMAN GENETICS AND GENOME ANALYSIS
18.The “Human Genetics & Genome Analysis” programme was initiated during 1994 with the objective to identify, map
 and characterise genes related to genetic disorders prevalent in India for diagnosis, prevention and management of genetic
 disorders and to develop new methods of the analysis and interpretations of genomic DNA sequences. Genetic diagnosis-
cum-counselling units established by the department have been providing molecular diagnosis and counselling for the
 common genetic disorders such as betathalassemia, Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD), haemoglobinopathies and
 neurogenetic disorders prevalent in the country, to the affected families.
19.The Committee was informed that since the inception of 14 genetic diagnosis cum counselling units, about 11,000
 affected families have benefitted from prenatal diagnosis and counselling for major genetic disorders an a population of
 1,700 tribals screened for these disorders.
20.While the Committee commends the efforts made by the Department in providing counselling and diagnostics services
 for the common genetic disorders prevalent in the country to the affected families, the question remains how many people
 are aware of such facilities.  Such programmes and facilities should be named in a language which people can understand. 
 The symptoms of such disorders should be publicised as also the programmes and facilities.   The Committee therefore
 recommends that the Department may provide wide publicity to the programme by involving print as well as electronic
 media so that the benefits of these activities of the Department could percolate to the underprivileged and weaker sections
 of the society.

NATIONAL JAI VIGYAN MISSION ON DEVELOPMENT OF NEW GENERATION VACCINES
25.The Committee was informed that the Department has initiated the programme based on several leads obtained from the
 R&D projects funded by the department earlier.  The objectives of the programme include generating candidate vaccine(s)
 for diseases like cholera, rabies, Japanese encephalitis, tuberculosis, malaria and HIV.  The programme has been grouped
 under three categories.  Category (A) includes those ‘candidate vaccines’ where potential research leads are available and
 which now require clinical trials or generating pre-clinical data.  Under this category, vaccines for cholera and rabies are
 being attempted.  The programmes under category (B) are related to available leads on candidate vaccines where efficacy
 and immunogenicity need to be worked out.  The target vaccines are tissue culture-based Japanese encephalitis, DNA
 vaccines, epitope vaccinogenes of Japanese encephalitis and synthetic candidate malaria vaccine.  Under category (C),
 programmes requiring sustained R&D efforts with the possibility for development of candidate vaccine(s).  Under this
 category, efforts would be made to produce candidate prophylactic anti-HIV and DNA vaccines for tuberculosis.  It is
 expected that at the end of the programme at least 2-3 vaccines will be available for upscaling and use to control/contain the
 infections while others would reach the stage of the clinical trials.
26.As regards the activities of the vaccine production unit, namely, Bharat Immunologicals & Biologicals Coprn. Ltd.
 (BIBCOL) set up by the Department for the production of viral vaccines, the Committee was informed that BIBCOL is
 formulating Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV) from imported bulk since January 1996 and is supplying it to the National
 Immunisation Programme.  During the year, the company has formulated 175 million doses of OPV, a part of which was
 supplied to UNICEF.  When enquired about the proposed diversification plan for production of BCG, Measles and Tetanus
 toxoid vaccines as submitted by BIBCOL, the Committee was informed that the proposed plan has been finalised from
 BIBCOL side and the same is being submitted to the operating agency namely BIFR.

 successful vehicle for transfer and exchange of knowledge, information, technology packages and references in the country
 involving 8-10 thousand scientists.  Ten Distributed Information Centres and 38 Sub-Distributed Information Centres

 interactive graphics based molecular modeling and other biocomputational needs.  The entire network has emerged as a
 very sophisticated scientific infrastructure for bioinformatics involving state-of-the art computational and communication
 facilities.  The computer communication network, linking all the bioinformatics centres, is playing a vital role in the success
 of the bioinformatics programme.  Database development, R&D activities in bioinformatics, human resource development

NATIONAL INSTITUTE OF IMMUNOLOGY
31.The Committee was informed that the Institute has excelled in the pursuit of rigorous fundamental research and emerged
 as a centre of excellence in basic immunology, infectious diseases and molecular design.  As regards the most recent
 achievements of the Institute, the Committee was informed that a major fundamental discovery concerning pathogenesis,
 which concerns survival of bacteria in phagocytes, has been reported in the Journal of Cell Biology. The complete genome
 of an Indian isolate of JEV(GP78) has been sequenced revealing its evolutionary closeness to the Chinese SA14 isolate.  A
 novel immunomodulatory use of an Indian medicinal plant product (NII-60) to prevent septic shock received a US patent
 and was transferred to an Indian Pharmaceutical industry for commercialisation.  A transgenic mice expressing the hepatitis
 B virus X  gene has been generated.  In a novel strategy, genes for different stages of plasmodium falciparum have been
 stitched and expressed in baculovirus system.  The resultant proteins elicit good immune response in experimental animals.

 tremendous potential of biodiversity, scientific and technical manpower and existing infrastructure it is likely to become an
 important global player in biotechnology in the next 10 years or so.  Keeping the mandate of the Department in view the
 Committee feels that sufficient funds need to be provided to the Department so that it can carry out its  mission effectively.
(Para 7)
2.   The Committee feels that with the rising  costs of chemical fertilisers and the accompanying  risks of degradation of soil
 fertility because of increasing resource of these fertilisers, the role of biofertilisers and biopesticides is becoming
 significant.  The Committee recommends that the Department should encourage  the use of biofertilisers and biopesticides
 by educating the farmers though Print & Electronic media and field demonstrations, so as to achieve higher consumption
 levels of the bioproducts and with a view to increase their yields.
(Para 11) 
3.   The Committee feels that with a view to replenish  the depleting forest cover and improve the environment  the role of
 tissue culture technique  has assumpted importance.  The committee observes that the techniques if proved successful in
 case of ongoing projects, may be replicated in  case of value based forest trees, horticultural crops and plantation crops.
(Para 14)

4.   The Committee recommends that besides supporting and developing indigenous diagnostics for major communicable
 and non-communicable diseases, the department should simultaneously endeavour to find market for indigenous products,
 and strive for better qualities to ensure priority for indigenous products over the imported ones.
(Para 17)
5.   While the Committee commends the efforts made by the Department in providing counselling and diagnostics services
 for the common genetic disorders prevalent in the country to the affected families, the question remains how many people
 are aware of such facilities.  Such programmes and facilities should be named in a language which people can understand. 
 The symptoms of such disorders should be publicised as also the programmes and facilities.   The Committee therefore
 recommends that the Department may provide wide publicity to the programme by involving print as well as electronic
 media so that the benefits of these activities of the Department could percolate to the underprivileged and weaker sections
 of the society.
(Para 20)
6.   The Committee notes with satisfaction the achievements made by the Department in the field of food Biotechnology. 
 The Committee however feels, that much more efforts are needed in the field to create awareness about the progarmme.
(Para 22)
7.   The  Committee  notes  with satisfaction the efforts made by the Department for upliftment of SC/ST population of
 country, Women and rural poor. The Department has launched Biotechnology based programme  for the welfare of SC/ST
 population through proven technologies.  It facilitates SC/ST population to upgrade their skills and create additional job
 avenues or help in improving their health status.  The main activities are cultivation and distillation of medicinal and
 aromatic plants, vermicomposting, spirulina production, fish and poultry  farming,  production and  application of  various
 biofertilisers  for  better  soil  health,
biological control agents for plant pests and survey of genetic disorders and counseling.  Forty-three projects have been
 pursued in 19 states and union territories, which benefited about 15,000 families.  The Committee observes that benefits of
 such programmes should be equally distributed among all the intended sections of the society.
(Para 24)
8.   The Committee commends the efforts made by the Department of Biotechnology in the field of bioinformatics, which
 are expected to yield multidimensional results in variety of areas.  The Committee, however, recommends that the
 Department should make all out efforts to ensure that biological data created in agricultural and other sectors be
 disseminated in a way that it also reaches the persons working at grassroots levels and desiring to make use of it, so as to
 ensure optimum use of the data created
(Para 29)
9.   The Committee is satisfied with the work being done by the National institute of Immunology.  However it observes that
 there are  certain unexplored areas in the field of  Immunodiagnostics and other diseases, where the institute can focus its
 research activities.  The Committee has been made to understand that 80% of all the R&D and scientific research is
 supported by the Government, and a smaller portion is funded by the Research Institutes/Industry. The Committee
 recommends that  Department should strive to raise funds  by directly collaborating with the Industry for providing
 additional support to Research activities.
(Para 34)
10.The Committee is of the view that Agriculture is not really sustainable anywhere on account of loss of top soil and
 excessive use of pesticides and fertilizers besides growth of population at a rapid pace.  Hence the need for higher
 productivity and for nutritious food. The object can be achieved by changing the plant characteristics by genetic
 modification.  The Committee impresses upon the Department to work with full vigour in this area and efforts should be
 made to cover  value based crops under its ambit which may solve the problem of under nourishment  at the glassroot levels
 of the society.
(Para 37)
