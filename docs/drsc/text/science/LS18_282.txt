INTRODUCTION
The Department of Atomic Energy (DAE), came into being on August 3, 1954, and has been engaged
in the development and deployment of nuclear power technology, application of radiation technologies in the
field of agriculture, health care, industry and basic research.  The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) is the
apex body for formulation of policies and direction of the programmes.  The Commission implements its
policies and programmes through the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE).  The Programmes of DAE have
been divided into four sectors viz.  Research and Development, Industries, Minerals and Power Sector.
The current mandate of the Department, on which its programmes are based, extends to:-
z Increasing share of nuclear power through deployment of indigenous and other proven
technologies, along with development of fast breeder reactors and thorium reactors with associated
fuel cycle facilities;
z Building and operation of research reactors for production of radioisotopes and carrying out
radiation technology applications in the field of medicine, agriculture and industry;
z Developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, supercomputers, advanced
materials and instrumentation, and encouraging transfer of technology to industry.
z Support to basic research in nuclear energy and related frontier areas of science; interaction
with universities and academic institutions; Support to research and development projects having
a bearing in DAE’s programmes, and international cooperation in related advanced areas of
research; and
z Contribution to national security.
2. MAJOR PROGRAMMES
2.1 The vision of the DAE is to empower India through technology, creation of more wealth and improving
standard of living of its citizens. To realise its vision, the Department has been engaged in the design,
construction and operation of nuclear power/research reactors and the associated nuclear fuel cycle
technologies across the country.  Over the years, the Department has been able to establish a network of
institutions engaged in R&D as well as industrial activities and acquired expertise in all aspects of the nuclear
fuel cycle.
2.2 The share of nuclear power in the total electricity generation from April, 2015 to February, 2016 is
about 3.3%.  The Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) in the country has kept pace with
the total electricity generations with the share remaining around 3 to 3.5%.  A substantial increase in the
share is planned by augmentation of the installed nuclear power capacity.
2.3 At present, NPCIL is spearheading India’s Nuclear Power Programme with 21 Nuclear Power Reactors
in operation, with total installed capacity of 5780 MW at seven locations.  Locations (including RAPS-1 of
100 MWe owned by the Government).  The reactor units are: 4 at Tarapur (Maharashtra), 6 at Rawatbhata
(Rajasthan), 2 at Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu), 2 at Narora (Uttar Pradesh), 2 at Kakrapar (Gujarat), 4 at Kaiga
(Karnataka) and 1 unit at Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu).  The Kudankulam unit-1 has started commercial
operation since December 31, 2014 and Kudankulam Unit-2 is in commissioning stage and likely to commence

2
commercial operation in financial year 2015-16.  The construction work is also under progress on 4 PHWR
of 700 MW Unit, 2 at Kakrapara, Gujarat, 2 at Rawatbhata, Rajasthan.
2.4 The DAE is also developing advanced technologies such as accelerators, lasers, super computers,
advanced materials and instrumentation and encouraging transfer of technology to the industry, which
could contribute to the national prosperity.  A wide range of technologies have been developed to come to the
present stage of harnessing nuclear energy and efforts are continuing to develop such technologies further
with future requirements in mind. Technologies to be developed for future applications include those necessary
for building accelerator driven sub-critical reactor system (ADS), high temperature reactor systems and
fusion technologies.  Several applications of radiation and isotope technologies for improving the quality of
life have been developed and efforts on this front are to be continued.
2.5 The Department is also engaged in the development of radiation technologies and their applications for
better crop varieties, techniques for crop protection, radiation-based post harvest technologies, techniques
for radio diagnosis and radio therapy of diseases particularly cancer, technologies for safe potable water,
better environment and industrial growth.
2.6 The DAE contributes to the enrichment of knowledge domain by pursuing basic research in nuclear
energy and related frontier areas of science; interaction with universities and academic institutions; support
to research and development project having a bearing in DAE’s programmes, and international co-operation
in related advanced areas of research.
2.7 The Committee notes with satisfaction that in the last sixty two years of its existence, the
Department of Atomic Energy has mastered a host of highly complex technologies particularly in
the field of medical radiology, nuclear agriculture, desalination, food irradiation, radio astronomy,
supercomputing, particle accelerators, plasma and cryogenics.  True to the visions of Dr. Homi J.
Bhabha and Jawaharlal Nehru of envisaging the usage of atomic energy to make a difference to the
social life of Indians, the DAE should now strive to scale it up and take it to the next level.  The
Committee feels that the share of nuclear power which accounts for only 3.3 per cent of India’s
total electricity generation, needs to be increased to the higher proportions and efforts should be
made in this direction.
3. ORGANISATIONS/INSTITUTIONS/CENTRES/PSUs
3.1 The DAE, over the years, has established a vast network of institutions and evolved into a broad-
based organisation comprising research centres, industrial units, public sector undertakings and fully aided
autonomous institutions along with service organisations. These organisations are engaged in research,
technology development and commercial operations in the areas of nuclear power and associated technologies,
radiation technologies and their applications, basic research and academic programmes to support, sustain
and carry forward its avowed objectives and activities.  So far, it has established 107 installations spread
across several States of the country. The organisations/institutions under the ambit of DAE, category-wise,
are as follows:
z Research Centres: Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) – Mumbai; Indira Gandhi Centre
for Atomic Research (IGCAR)– Kalpakkam; Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology
(RRCAT) – Indore; Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC) – Kolkata; Atomic Minerals
Directorate for Exploration and Research (AMD) – Hyderabad; and Global Centre for Nuclear
Energy Partnership (GCNEP), Gurgaon.

10. ATOMIC ENERGY REGULATORY BOARD (AERB)
10.1 The Atomic Energy Regulatory Board was constituted on November 15,1983 by exercising the powers
conferred by section 27 of the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 (33 of 1962) to carry out certain regulatory and
safety functions under the Act.  The regulatory authority of AERB is derived from the rules and notifications
promulgated under the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Environmental protection Act, 1986.  The mission
of the Board is to ensure that the use of ionizing radiation and Nuclear Energy in India does not cause undue
risk to health of people and the environment.
Monitoring of Radio Active Substances and Radiation Generating Units Outside
10.2 The Committee desired to know the manner in which Atomic Energy Regulatory Board was monitoring
the use of radioactive substances and radiation generating Plants outside nuclear installations to avert accidents
like Mayapuri, Delhi. In response thereto, it was informed that the existing regulations, viz, Atomic Energy
(Radiation Protection) Rules, 2004 and Atomic Energy (Safe Disposal of Radioactive Wastes) Rules, 1987,
specifies the rules to be followed for safe handling, use and disposal of radioactive material as well as their
regulation. Some of the requirements of these Rules were violated in the case, which resulted in the disused
radioactive source of a high hazard category finding its way in the public domain causing the untoward
incident.

12
10.3 The ‘ Atomic Energy (Safe Disposal of Radioactive Wastes) Rules-1987 clearly spell out the
requirements, roles and responsibilities of various parties. In addition to the abovementioned Rules, AERB
has developed and issued Safety Codes, Standards and other regulatory documents, specifying the regulatory
and safety requirements to be followed in the handling, use and disposal of radioactive materials and
radiation generating plants equipment. In accordance with the provisions of the Rules, AERB claims to
have a well-established regulatory system for licensing, periodic review of safety status, regulatory
inspections and enforcement with respect to the radiation facilities (the facilities using radioactive sources
and radiation generating equipment for medical, industrial and research applications, which are outside
the nuclear installations.
10.4 The radiation facilities using radioactive sources of higher category (Category 1& 2) have a high
hazard potential, and if not well regulated they can lead to incidents like the one happened at Mayapuri, New
Delhi in 2010. The Mayapuri incident was primarily due to violation of the regulatory and safety requirements
related to decommissioning/safe disposal of the disused source by the Delhi University. The source in
question, a Gamma Chamber (Category-1 source)was in disused state for well over 20 years. This was a
‘legacy source’ with the Delhi University, which they procured at a time before the regulatory framework
was established.
10.5 The lower category sources (category 3, 4 & 5) are of lower to negligible hazard potential and the
possibility of them resulting in such incidents is very minimal. The radiation generating equipment like Linear
accelerators used in Medicine and Industry, diagnostic X-ray equipment used in hospitals and to some
extent in the industry are unlikely to cause similar incidents, if handled properly.
10.6 The Committee desired to know the measures taken by AERB for further strengthening the regulatory
mechanism for the radiation facilities for ensuring safety in the use of radiation sources in the country and
maintaining a tight control and traceability in their procurement, installation, operation and final disposal.  In
response thereto, it was informed that following six are the measures taken by the Board are for ensuring
safety:-
(i) Strengthening of licensing  of radiation facilities.
(ii) Maintaining inventory of the Radiation facilities.
(iii) Strengthening of Regulatory Inspections.
(iv) Monitoring and follow up of discussed sources for safe disposal.
(v) Periodic Safety Status Review and
(vi) A wareness Programme.
10.7 AERB has also specified the requirements for all the licenses to promptly report to the Board, any
incident of loss/theft or misplacement of radiation sources, so as to enable AERB to facilitative prompt
action for launching investigations/recovery of the sources.

which was reduced to ` 12.00 crore at RE 2015-16. When asked about reasons for reduction in allocation

14. INDIA BASED NEUTRINO OBSERV ATORY PROJECT (INO)
14.1 The India-based Neutrino Observatory (INO) Project is a multi-institutional effort aimed at building a
world-class underground laboratory with a rock cover of approx.1200 metres for non-accelerator based
high energy and nuclear physics research in India. The project is a joint initiative of Department of Atomic
Energy and DST with DAE as lead agency.
14.2 The project includes construction of an underground laboratory and associated surface facilities at
Pottipuram in Bodi West hills of Theni District of Tamil Nadu; construction of a Iron Calorimeter (ICAL)
detector for studying neutrinos and setting up of National Centre for High Energy Physics at Madurai, for
the operation and maintenance of the underground laboratory, human resource development and detector
R&D along with its applications. The underground laboratory, consisting of a large cavern of size 132m X
26m X 20m and several smaller caverns, will be accessed by a 2100 m long and 7.5 m wide tunnel.
14.3 The initial goal of  INO is to study neutrinos. The ICAL detector is designed to address some of the
key open problems in a unique way. Over the years, this underground facility is expected to develop into a
full-fledged underground science laboratory for other studies in physics, biology, geology, hydrology etc.
Development of detector technology and its varied applications are an important aspect of the project. The
primary goal of the INO is to study the properties of the fundamental particle called “neutrino” which is the
most exciting area of research in particle physics after the discovery of Higgs Boson.

19
14.4 Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai is the host institute for INO project. All INO
related activities will be coordinated through the INO centre, which is to be located at Madurai. This centre
will be responsible for the operation and maintenance of the underground laboratory, human resource
development and detector R& D. The construction of the underground and surface facilities is expected to
be completed in four years and the entire ICAL detector will be completed in the next four years. Preliminary
data taking is expected to start in 2020.

15. LASER INTERFEROMETER GRA VITATIONAL-W AVE OBSERV ATORY (LIGO)
15.1 Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) is world’s largest night time Astronomy
Telescope is being set up in the USA by several international partners. Further, it was informed that a new
advanced Laser Interferometer Gravitational-wave Observatory (LIGO) will be set up in India also. This
will be the third such detector in the world, the other two being in the United States. Recently, the Union
Cabinet has accorded in-principle approval for the same.
15.2 Gravitational Waves have drawn the attention of whole world this year.  37 Indian scientists have
contributed towards the recent discovery of gravitational waves. Steps have been taken by the gravitational
wave community of the country for forging international scientific collaborations and developing human
resource in this field. For this, a pan-Indian consortium, Indian Initiative in Gravitational-wave Observations
(IndIGO) was formed consisting of researchers from premier institutions and universities of the country.
IndIGO consortium has representation in the Gravitational Waves International Committee (GWIC) which
works to facilitate international collaboration and cooperation in the construction, operation and use of the
major gravitational wave detection facilities world-wide. Indian scientists are participating in the international
LIGO Science Collaboration (LSC), through IndIGO.

17. CONTRIBUTION TO ASTROSAT MISSION
17.1 The ASTROSAT programme is aimed at design, development, fabrication and launch of an astronomical
observatory of cosmic studies.  It is envisaged to be a national observatory for astronomical observations by
researchers in India.
17.2 ASTROSA T satellite was launched by ISRO and put into orbit on 28 September 2015. Tata Institute of
Fundamental Research of DAE has developed 3 of the 5 major instruments on Astrosat namely Large Area
X-ray Proportional Counters (LAXPC); Soft X-ray Telescope (SXT); Cadmium-Zinc-Telluride Imager
(CZTI). In addition to this, Inter University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics, Department of Science
& Technology has also developed to instruments on ASTROSAT.
17.3 ASTROSA T MISSION is aimed at design, development, fabrication and launch of India’s first multi
wavelength astronomical observatory of cosmic studies.  ASTROSAT is aimed at studying celestial sources
in X-ray, optical and UV spectral bands simultaneously.  All the data are received regularly and are archived
at Indian Space Science Data Center (ISSDC).
17.4 Informing about the progress of the satellite the Department has stated that the satellite is functioning
well.  All functions of various detectors on-board were found to be normal and its performance continues as
per specifications. Charge Particle Monitor (CPM) was the first detector to be operational on 5th and  6th
October, 2015, followed by the Cadmium Zinc Telluride Imager (CZTI), the hard X-ray detector. The Soft
X-ray imaging telescope (SXT) onboard Astrosat saw its first light on October 26, 2015. Calibration of
SXT using cosmic X-ray sources was started. The analysis software is also being upgraded. The Large
Area X-ray Proportional Counter (LAXPC) detector was fully operational on October 19, 2015 and its first
light observations were carried out for one week. In the next few months all instruments will be ready to
make observations of interesting objects in the universe.
17.5 The Committee congratulates the Department particularly Tata Institute of Fundamental
Research, Mumbai for successful operationalization of  Large Area X-ray Proportional Counters
(LAXPC); Soft X-ray Telescope (SXT); Cadmium-Zinc-Telluride Imager (CZTI) of ASTROSAT and
hopes that India’s first  national observatory for astronomical observations will yield desired results
and will be of great help to the researchers in India.

recommends that the Department should make a firm resolve, mobilise its resources and intensify
its efforts to ensure that all proposed nuclear projects are completed and commissioned within the
scheduled deadline. (Para 18.15)
BHARATIYA NABHIKIYA VIDYUT NIGAM LTD. (BHA VINI)
The Committee is of the view that setting up of Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor, the second
stage of the Indian Nuclear Power Programme is crucial for energy security of the country.
The Committee, therefore, recommends that the Department must make the Prototype Fast
Breeder Reactor Unit 2 operational by revised target of completion, i.e. first quarter of financial
year 2016-17 and start its commercial operation as scheduled. (Para 19.4)
The Committee recommends that the Department should make concerted efforts to tap Nuclear
Energy to surmount the rising energy need of emerging India.  The Department at the same time
must do away with the deficiencies in the realm of safety framework so as to make the nation safe
from nuclear accidents. (Para 19.5)
